Resolution sequence stratigraphic division of the Da2 member in the Yanji Basin based on INPEFA and Milankovitch cycles
Authors: Cunlei Li, Dongrui Su, Panpan Chen, Zhiyuan Xue, Ranlei Zhao, Tuantuan Qu, Chengming Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Dalazi Formation in the Yanji Basin is primarily composed of fan delta and lacustrine facies, with local development of alluvial fan deposits. The second member of the Dalazi Formation (Da₂) serves as the key oil-bearing interval for exploration and development. Based on logging data, lithological observations, and maximum entropy spectral analysis (MESA), Da₂ is interpreted to mainly represent a continental fan delta system. Due to the complex depositional environment, significant variations exist in sedimentary types, resulting in notable differences in both lithology and logging curves, which hinders regional correlation using conventional logging marker bed pairings. In order to solve this problem, based on the theory of high resolution sequence stratigraphy and astronomical cycle theory, this paper uses the maximum entropy spectrum analysis, spectrum analysis and wavelet time–frequency analysis technology to process and analyze the natural gamma curve (GR) data, and combines the logging lithology information to identify the sequence interface. According to the prediction error trend (INPEFA) curve in MESA, it is identified that Da2 contains 1 long-term base level rise half-cycle and 1 long-term base level fall half-cycle. On this basis, the mid-term base level cycle and short-term base level cycle are identified by combining spectrum analysis technology and wavelet time–frequency technology. Finally, The Da2 sequence is subdivided into 1 long-term, 6 mid-term, and 16 short-term base level cycles, based on which a sequence stratigraphic division scheme is proposed. Based on this, a high-resolution stratigraphic framework is established, which provides accurate geological basis for subsequent comprehensive reservoir evaluation and sand body prediction.Keywords: Yanji Basin, Dalazi formation, Maximum entropy spectrum analysis, Spectral analysis, High resolution stratigraphic framework

Introduction

The unique sedimentary environment of the continental fan delta system developed in the sedimentary period of Da2 in the Yanji Basin. The sedimentary types are obviously different, which also leads to substantial differences in lithology and logging curves, and the sand bodies of the second member are superimposed. Using the thin layer contrast of the loggingcurve marker layer, it is difficult to achieve unity across the entire area1–3. The sequence division based on logging data commonly adopts methods such as wavelet time–frequency, natural gamma ray spectroscopy (U, Th, K and their ratios), and INPEFA. The first two methods each have their own advantages. However, the wavelet method is sensitive to wavelet basis, scale, decomposition level and singularity processing, which is prone to introduce subjectivity. The spectroscopy method requires that element curves/ratios are highly sensitive to changes in organic matter and clay content, and it is easily affected by U anomalies caused by fractures and fluids. The advantages of carbonate sequences are not possessed in this area. INPEFA needs to take “no abnormal” GR as the premise. However, in this area, due to the influence of radioactive sand bodies and diagenetic transformation, GR/element anomalies are common, and the cycle level of INPEFA is difficult to be independently verified4–6.

This paper employs the maximum entropy spectrum analysis(MESA) method and the spectrum analysis method for fine stratigraphic division to improve the accuracy and precision of the sequence division of Da2. As part of spectrum analysis, the logging curve is viewed as a comprehensive signal derived from various geological factors in the depth domain (or time domain) caused by periodic changes in the Formation. For the purpose of generating the spectrum curve, the Fourier Transform is used to transform the comprehensive signal from the depth domain (or time domain) to the frequency domain7–9. Maximum entropy spectrum analysis and spectrum technology can extract the Formation development characteristics hidden in the original logging data, allowing the Formation’s trend change of sedimentary cycle to be more clearly reflected. By analyzing the trend form of curves at different levels, the sequence interface can be determined and cycle grades can be divided, which meets the requirements of high-resolution sequence stratigraphic division10,11. High-resolution sequence stratigraphic correlation is based on the base level cycle conversion surface as the preferred time stratigraphic correlation position12. As a result of identifying the mid-term cycle interface, we can establish a sequence stratigraphic framework with greater stratigraphic identification accuracy and more accuracy describe the stratigraphic framework’s spatial and temporal distribution, as well as its evolution law13–15. In the characterization of oil and gas reservoirs, reservoir prediction, residual oil distribution, and many other oil and gas geological studies, the short-term cycle can be a more reliable geological model16. Therefore, high-resolution sequence stratigraphic analysis of the Da2 sequence, including the identification and subdivision of base level cycles as well as the construction of a stratigraphic framework model, provides a theoretical foundation for subsequent geological studies.

Geologic setting

Yanji Basin is a Mesozoic faulted basin that is divided into four structural units from west to east, Xilinhe obliquity, Chaoyangchuan Sag, Longjing Bulge, and Eastern Sag (Qingchaguan-Dexin Sag). The sedimentary cover is mainly composed of the Cretaceous Tuntianying Formation, Toudao Formation, Tongfosi Formation, Dalazi Formation, and Longjing Formation17,18. The geological structure of Yanji Basin is shown in Fig. 1A. Yanji Basin is located in the Yanbian Korean Autonomous Prefecture of Jilin Province, including parts of Yanji city and Longjing city. Figure 1B shows the geological map of Yanji Basin. The region has completed 2280.7 square kilometers of 2D seismic surveys and 76 square kilometers of 3D seismic surveys. A total of 19 oil exploration wells and 10 geological wells have been drilled. There is 1 well with industrial oil flow (Yan 10) and 1 well with industrial gas flow (Yan 4). The resource quantity (oil) is 122 million tons. It measures about 50 km wide from east to west and 55 km long from north to south, with a total area of 1670 kilometers19. The Dalazi Formation is a set of continental clastic rock deposits containing oil-bearing shale. The bottom sandstone is obviously separated from the top conglomerate of the Tongfosi Formation by an unconformity contact, and the top conglomerate is integrated with the overlying Longjing Formation sandstone and mudstone interbed. The main sedimentary facies developed in the Dalazi Formation are fan delta and lacustrine facies. Identification of sequence interfaces

The lithology of Da2 is mainly composed of oil-bearing siltstone, black-gray mudstone, grey siltstone and grey-black mudstone. According to the principle of logging geology, different logging curves have different geological characteristics21. The GR curve is most sensitive to variations in shale content in strata, and the result of sequence stratigraphic division by the GR curve is the most accurate.

Sequence partitioning based on INPEFA

Maximum entropy spectrum analysis technique

In a spectrum study, MESA is a method for obtaining the power spectrum22. Prediction Error Filtering Analysis (PEFA) is based on MESA. The value obtained by subtracting the predicted value calculated by MESA from the logging curve value of each depth point is : error = actual data-filtered data23. The INPEFA curve is generated through specific integral processing of well-log data using the INPEFA method. It can reflect the cyclic variation of sediments and show the stratigraphic interface hidden in conventional logging curves24. In general, if the shape and amplitude of the INPEFA curve increase from left to right, it means the transgressive process, and the positive trend is the process of mud enrichment, corresponding to the base level rise half-cycle and transgression the larger ratio (A/S) of the accumulation space and sediment supply. A regression to groundwater is indicated by the curvature shape and amplitude that decrease from right to left, whereas the negative trend corresponds to the base level falling half-cycle, which represents a decreases in A/S value due to the decreases in mud content and increases in sand content. The inflection point of the INPEFA curve represents the possible sequence interface or flood-water surface; Negative inflection points are typically associated with potential flooding surfaces, whereas positive inflection points correspond to possible sequence boundaries.

INPEFA reveals trends and patterns that are typically not apparent in the original logging curves. Its primary feature lies in the overall trend of the generated curve, as well as the inflection point between them. For processing a local interval, it can reflect the cyclicity of the Formation itself and is not complete. Compared with the MESA, Milankovitch astronomical cycle division method can provide a more accurate division of sequences in Da2 of the study area. Therefore, the INPEFA analysis method is used to clarify the sequence boundary and flooding surface of the study interval, and then Milankovitch astronomical cycle theory is applied to subdivide the stratigraphic sequence. Finally, the accuracy of the cycle division is verified by wavelet time–frequency analysis.

A key advantage of INPEFA technology is its high resolution, derived from its foundation in logging data analysis. Therefore, the synthetic INPEFA curve of MESA is highly accurate in reflecting the transgression and water-falling processes and can be used for high-resolution sequence stratigraphic division25. Because INPEFA technology is based on mudstone content, it has certain limitations when it comes to the horizontal change of the target layer in the study area. Da2 contains a large section of sandstone and sand-mud interbeds, which is not conducive to the application of MESA technology. After comprehensive analysis, it is decided to use spectrum analysis method to divide mid-term and short-term base level cycles on the basis of MESA technology.

Sequence division based on spectrum analysis

Spectrum analysis and Milankovitch theory

Spectral analysis involves generating a spectrum curve by converting an integrated signal from the depth domain to the frequency domain using Fourier transformation. In stratigraphic research, Milankovitch theory has long been utilized and is primarily applied in two key areas. Stratigraphic division, correlation, and geological dating26,27. The Milankovitch cycle is a cyclic sedimentary record produced by changes in Earth orbit parameters (orbital eccentricity, obliquity, and precession). It is an astronomical theory that studies the relationship between the amount of sunlight and the global climate. Changes in these three parameters will cause changes in the amount of summer sunshine reaching the middle and high latitudes of the Earth28. According to the solution of Laskarl29, the average sunshine volume data of Dalazi Formation in the eastern depression of Yanji Basin were obtained, and the data sampling interval was 1 ka.

Through the previous U–Pb zircon dating30 of the Dalazi Formation, the stratigraphic age of the Dalazi Formation can be inferred, and the coordinates of the Yanji Basin are known to be 43.89°N, 129.34°E. Processing of stratigraphic data

The logging data of the corresponding well section are selected, and the singular points are eliminated, the interpolation preprocessing is performed, and the interpolation is 0.125 m. Next, the data is detrended. Noise reduction of processed data using noise reduction tools in Matlab Prcessing, to minimize the influence of these factors on the interpretation of the actual conditions, the data were compiled and spectral analysis was performed using PAST software. The resulting spectrum primarily focuses on frequency components exceeding the 95% confidence level. Figure 6A presents the results of the spectral analysis for Da2 of Y4 well. The frequency values corresponding to the frequency points are: 0.0227 m-1 0.0610 m-1, 0.0779 m-1, and 0.1018 m-1. The reciprocal of the frequency value is the wavelength, the corresponding wavelength is 44.08 m, 16.38 m, 12.83 m, and 9.82 m. The wavelength reflects the sedimentary thickness of the corresponding cycle within the associated stratigraphic units. The cycle thickness of Da2 of Y4 is 44.08 m, 16.38 m, 12.83 m, and 9.82 m, respectively. Similarly to the YX202, YX204, and YX206 well analyses, the spectrum analysis is shown in Fig. 6B–D.

Comparison of astronomical and stratigraphic data

It is a common method to determine whether the Milankovitch cycle exists in sedimentary strata by comparing the ratio of the thickness of each cycle calculated by spectral analysis with the ratio of the astronomical orbital period31. Due to the fact that the motion law of celestial bodies is fixed, the proportional relationship between the various astronomical periods is relatively stable. The strata in a stable sedimentary environment can preserve this proportional relationship, that is, the ratio of the thickness each stratigraphic cycle deposited in this environment corresponds to the ratio of the associated astronomical cycles32,33. The calculation error is determined by comparing the astronomical period ratio table with the stratigraphic thickness ratios of the second section. Sequence stratigraphic division and correlation

Sequence stratigraphic division scheme

The results of spectral analysis show that orbital eccentricity periods of Y4 well and YX206 well are 128 ka, and the orbital eccentricity periods of YX202 and YX204 wells are 128 ka and 100 ka. Owing to variations in the regional sedimentary environment, the 100 ka orbital eccentricity cycle is not observed in all wells. Consequently, the filtering curve of the orbital eccentricity 128 ka and obliquity 48 ka is used as a reference for the division of mid-term and short-term base level cycle. The thickness of Y4 well corresponding to astronomical period 128 ka and obliquity period 48 ka is 44.08 m and 16.38 m. The analysis results show that the number of theoretical mid-term base level cycle in Da2 the Y4 well is 5.76 and the number of theoretical short-term base level cycle is 15.51. High-resolution sequence stratigraphic framework

High-resolution sequence stratigraphic framework is very important content, which refers to the strata formed in the same period in chronological order. On this basis, the analysis fully accounts for the isochronous nature of the strata, enabling stratigraphic correlation and characterization of stacking patterns34.

In the process of studying sequence, based on high resolution theory, lithologic data, and log data, the results of cycle division are obtained. The filter curve obtained after 128 ka orbital eccentricity cycle band-pass filtering is used as the scale of mid-term base level sequence division, and the curve after 48 ka obliquity band-pass filtering is used as the scale of short-term base level sequence division. 6 mid-term base level cycle that control the whole area are established as a reference for isochronous comparison, and 16 short-term base level cycle are used for isochronous comparison more accurately. Ultimately, a high-resolution sequence stratigraphic framework for the Da2 sequence is constructed. The thickness of individual cycles varies across different wells, and even within the same stratigraphic sequence, notable discrepancies in cycle thickness are observed see Fig. 8. Through the establishment and comparison of the framework, it is easy to find key wells and Formations and carry out the next work. This plays a crucial role in analyzing sedimentary facies types and determining the direction of hydrocarbon exploration.

Conclusion

Da2 mainly develops a continental fan delta system, and the unique sedimentary environment increases the difficulty of isochronous stratigraphic correlation. To address this issue, a combined approach utilizing MESA and spectral analysis is employed to subdivide the base level cycles. As a result, the Da2 sequence is divided into 6 intermediate-term and 16 short-term base level cycles.According to the trend shown by the INPEFA curve, it is concluded that Da2 of well Y4 contains a long-term base level rising half-cycle and a long-term base level falling half-cycle. During the water intrusion period, the base level rises, the A/S ratio increases, and the accommodation space gradually increases. During the water retreat period, the base level decreases, the A/S ratio decreases, and the accommodation space gradually decreases.The mid-term base level fluctuations interpreted from the PEFA curve of well Y4 generally align with the 128 ka orbital eccentricity filtering results derived from spectral analysis. Utilizing the MESA findings, spectral methods are further employed to segment the short-period base level variations, formulate the stratigraphic division framework, and ultimately construct a high-resolution sequence stratigraphic model for the study area, this facilitates isochronous correlation analysis across the entire study area.

Limitations

Despite the effectiveness of the integrated method in improving stratigraphic resolution, several limitations remain. First, the vertical resolution and signal quality of logging data may affect the accuracy of cycle identification, particularly in thinly interbedded or heterogeneous strata. Second, the assumption of continuous, astronomically forced sedimentation may not fully hold in fan delta environments characterized by episodic deposition. Third, the absence of core calibration and precise chronological data limits the direct validation of the proposed sequence framework. Future studies should incorporate core, geochronological, and regional stratigraphic data to enhance the robustness of the interpretations.

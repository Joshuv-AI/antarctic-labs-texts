Bevacizumab and Platinum Choice in Pembrolizumab-Treated Advanced Cervical Cancer
Authors: Alberto Farolfi, Chiara Casadei, Emanuela Scarpi, Eleonora Paoletti, Caterina Gianni, Michela Palleschi, Nicola Gentili, Marita Mariotti, Sara Testoni, Giandomenico Di Menna, Francesca Rusconi, Alice Andalò, Olga Serra, Filippo Merloni, Marianna Sirico, Roberta Maltoni, Lorenzo Cecconetto, Samanta Sarti, Antonino Musolino
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Key PointsQuestionIs platinum agent choice (cisplatin vs carboplatin) or bevacizumab addition associated with overall survival in patients with advanced cervical cancer receiving first-line pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy?FindingsIn this multinational comparative effectiveness study that included 1931 patients, cisplatin and carboplatin (623 pairs) showed equivalent overall survival; bevacizumab addition (455 pairs) was associated with an early survival benefit, mainly during the first 12 months and diminishing during longer follow-up.MeaningThese findings suggest that cisplatin and carboplatin are interchangeable when combined with pembrolizumab for advanced cervical cancer, and bevacizumab provides early survival benefit in appropriately selected patients, although prospective validation is required.

Introduction

Cervical cancer remains a significant cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Although preventable through human papillomavirus vaccination,1 advanced cervical cancer (ACC) at presentation or recurrence after initial treatment continues to challenge oncologists globally.2 Early-stage disease (International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics [FIGO] stages IA-IB2) can be cured in most patients with surgery, with or without adjuvant radiotherapy, whereas those with locally advanced cervical cancer (FIGO stages IB3-IVA) are typically treated with concurrent chemoradiotherapy followed by intracavitary brachytherapy, as recommended by international guidelines.3

Recently, the INTERLACE trial4 showed that induction chemotherapy with 6 weekly cycles of carboplatin and paclitaxel followed by standard chemoradiotherapy significantly improved both progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in patients with locally advanced cervical cancer. Similarly, the KEYNOTE-A18 trial5 demonstrated that adding pembrolizumab to chemoradiotherapy significantly improved PFS and OS. These findings are reshaping the treatment landscape for locally advanced cervical cancer.

However, patients with metastatic ACC (FIGO stage IVB) or recurrent disease after chemoradiotherapy require systemic therapy.3 For decades, platinum-based chemotherapy was the backbone of treatment, with paclitaxel plus cisplatin demonstrating superior response rates and PFS.6,7 The JCOG0505 trial8 demonstrated noninferiority of carboplatin-paclitaxel compared with cisplatin-paclitaxel in terms of efficacy, although in the chemotherapy-naive subgroup, cisplatin-based chemotherapy appeared to offer a modest survival advantage.

The GOG 240 trial9 established bevacizumab as standard of care in ACC, demonstrating OS improvement (median, 17.0 vs 13.3 months), albeit with increased severe adverse events including gastrointestinal fistula and thromboembolic complications. Most recently, the KEYNOTE-826 trial10 randomized 617 patients with ACC, demonstrating that pembrolizumab combined with platinum-based chemotherapy with or without bevacizumab significantly prolonged PFS and OS. Patients receiving first-line immunotherapy-based treatment typically receive the following: (1) platinum-based chemotherapy administered as 6 weekly cycles of either cisplatin (75 mg/m2) or carboplatin (area under the receiver operating characteristics curve, 5-6) administered intravenously, with each cycle lasting 21 days (total duration, approximately 18-24 weeks); (2) paclitaxel 175 mg/m2 administered on the same days as platinum agents; (3) pembrolizumab, administered as a 200-mg intravenous infusion on day 1 of each 3-week cycle, continued for as long as 24 months if tolerated; and (4) bevacizumab (when added), administered as a 15-mg/kg intravenous infusion every 3 weeks, typically initiated concurrently with chemotherapy and continued for a maximum 15 months after chemotherapy completion. These treatments target tumor vasculature, block immune checkpoints, and promote tumor cell death through multiple mechanisms. Notably, both platinum choice and bevacizumab use were left to physician discretion. Exploratory subgroup analyses showed that pembrolizumab significantly improved median OS in patients treated with carboplatin-based chemotherapy (246 patients treated with pembrolizumab and 249 with placebo), and in those who received bevacizumab (196 treated with pembrolizumab and 193 with placebo), but not in those treated with cisplatin-based regimens (61 treated with pembrolizumab and 59 with placebo).11 This heterogeneity in treatment combinations raises the question of whether all regimens provide equivalent benefit in clinical settings. Notably, no randomized clinical trials have specifically evaluated platinum agent choice (cisplatin vs carboplatin) or bevacizumab addition in combination with immunotherapy for the first-line treatment of ACC. To address this gap and complement the findings from randomized clinical trials, we conducted a comparative effectiveness analysis using the TriNetX Global Collaborative Network12 to assess how treatment combinations—platinum choice and bevacizumab use—are associated with OS and serious adverse events in patients with ACC in clinical settings.

Methods

Data Source

This comparative effectiveness study used a retrospective cohort from the TriNetX Global Collaborative Network.13 TriNetX is a federated clinical data analytics platform that aggregates deidentified electronic health record data from 143 health care organizations across North America, Europe, and Asia, representing approximately 130 million patient records. The platform captures longitudinal clinical data including diagnoses (International Statistical Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision, Clinical Modification [ICD-10-CM] codes), procedures, medications, laboratory values, and outcomes. TriNetX infrastructure is identical across geographic regions, using standardized data extraction protocols and common data model structures. Data collection, processing, and transmission were conducted in accordance with data protection regulations applicable to each participating health care organization, including the European Union’s General Data Protection Regulation and the US Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA). Only aggregated, nonidentifiable results are returned to the TriNetX platform, and no personal data leave the originating institution. The institutional review boards at the participating health care organizations approved the study protocol or deemed it exempt from review owing to the use of data that were deidentified in accordance with HIPAA standards; the need for informed consent was waived due to the deidentified data and the retrospective study design. This study followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) and International Society for Pharmacoeconomics and Outcomes Research (ISPOR) reporting guidelines.

Study Population

We identified adult patients diagnosed with cervical cancer within the TriNetX Global Collaborative Network who received first-line systemic treatment for advanced, recurrent, or metastatic ACC. Inclusion criteria required diagnosis of cervical cancer (ICD-10-CM code C53) and administration of pembrolizumab (200 mg) in combination with paclitaxel (175 mg/m2) and a platinum agent (either cisplatin or carboplatin), with or without bevacizumab. Patients were excluded if they had received a previous diagnosis of breast (ICD-10-CM code C50), kidney (ICD-10-CM code C64), melanoma (ICD-10-CM code C43-C44), lung (ICD-10-CM code C34), or bladder (ICD-10-CM code C67) cancer. Patients were categorized into 2 comparison groups. The platinum agent comparison included 719 patients treated with cisplatin, excluding carboplatin (cohort A) and 1212 patients treated with carboplatin, excluding cisplatin (cohort B). The bevacizumab comparison included 803 patients treated with bevacizumab (initiated within 60 days of the index date), in addition to platinum, paclitaxel, and pembrolizumab (cohort C), and 667 patients treated without bevacizumab (cohort D).The index date was defined as the date of first-line treatment initiation. Patients were ascertained within the database from January 1, 2015, through October 31, 2024, and follow-up was extended from the index date to death or the last available encounter in TriNetX. Follow-up was completed November 15, 2024. A study flow diagram is provided in eFigure 1 in Supplement 1.

Propensity Score Matching

To reduce confounding due to baseline differences, propensity score matching (PSM; 1:1 nearest-neighbor method; caliper width, 0.1 SDs) was applied separately for each comparison. The following covariates were included in the matching model: age, race and ethnicity, body mass index (BMI), smoking status, prior chemoradiotherapy with cisplatin, bevacizumab use (for the first comparison), or platinum type (for the second comparison).

Race and Ethnicity Classification

Race and ethnicity data were obtained from electronic health records within the TriNetX Global Collaborative Network. Classifications were based on self-report by patients or documentation by health care personnel during clinical encounters. Racial and ethnic categories included in analyses were Asian, Black or African American, White, and other or unknown. These categories reflect the standardized classifications available within the TriNetX database structure and were used as covariates in the PSM model because it is a well-established factor associated with differences in cervical cancer presentation, access to care, treatment delivery, and clinical outcomes.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was OS, defined as the time from index date to death from any cause. Secondary outcomes included the incidence of adverse events of special interest, specifically: gastrointestinal fistulae (ICD-10-CM code N82), bowel perforation (ICD-10-CM code K63.1), or pulmonary embolism (ICD-10-CM code I26).

Statistical Analysis

Data were analyzed from November 19 to 28, 2024. Continuous variables were summarized by descriptive statistics (number of cases, mean, SD, median, minimum, maximum), whereas categorical variables were summarized using counts of patients and percentages. A 1-to-1 matching (without replacement) by propensity score was performed by using nearest-neighbor method with a caliper width equal to 0.1 SDs. We examined balance in the baseline covariates in the matched data by using standardized differences. OS was estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method; hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% CIs were calculated using a Cox proportional hazards regression model.

To validate the robustness of the results, adjusted Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were also performed on the unmatched cohorts, controlling for the same covariates used in PSM. Proportions of treatment-related complications (fistula, bowel perforation, and pulmonary embolism) were compared between treatment groups using logistic regression to derive odds ratios (ORs) with 95% CIs. We additionally used the χ2 test or Fisher exact test as appropriate based on cell frequency to test for independence between treatment and adverse event occurrence. P values from χ2 or Fisher exact tests and ORs from logistic regression are both reported, providing complementary statistical perspectives on adverse event differences. All statistical analyses were conducted within the TriNetX platform using the integrated analytics tools. Two-sided P < .05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Cisplatin- vs Carboplatin-Based Chemotherapy

A total of 1931 patients with ACC patients were identified, including 719 treated with cisplatin and 1212 treated with carboplatin (mean [SD] age, 54.0 [13.2] years). In terms of race and ethnicity, 180 patients (9.3%) were Asian, 196 (10.2%) were Black, 1066 (55.2%) were White, and 489 (25.3%) were of other or unknown race or ethnicity. Before PSM, cisplatin-treated patients were younger (mean [SD] age, 50.7 [12.7] vs 56.0 [13.7] years) (P < .001) (standardized mean difference [SMD], 0.40), with a higher proportion of Asian (83 [11.5%] vs 97 [8.0%]; P = .01) and a lower proportion of Black (54 [7.5%] vs 142 [11.7%]; P = .003) patients. Prior chemoradiotherapy was more common in the cisplatin group (452 [62.9%] vs 360 [29.7%]) (P < .001). BMI and bevacizumab use also differed between groups (Table 1).

With a median follow up of 10.8 (IQR, 3.7-18.0) months for the cisplatin group and 9.9 (IQR, 2.5-17.2) months for the carboplatin group, the median OS was 25.1 (IQR, 9.2 to not reached) and 25.1 (IQR, 9.1 to not reached) months, respectively (HR, 0.98 [95% CI 0.81-1.18]) (P = .35), with 12-month OS rates of 66.9% (95% CI, 66.8%-66.9%) for both groups and 24-month OS rates of 51.1% (95% CI, 51.0%-51.2%) and 50.2% (95% CI, 50.1%-50.3%), respectively (eFigure 3 in Supplement 1). To validate the robustness of PSM results, adjusted Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were performed on the unmatched cohorts, controlling for the same covariates used in PSM. Results from these sensitivity analyses were consistent with PSM-derived findings (eTable 1 in Supplement 1). No significant differences were observed in adverse events between cisplatin vs carboplatin groups: fistulae (OR, 1.07 [95% CI, 0.78-1.47]), bowel perforation (OR, 1.00 [95% CI, 0.41-2.42]), or pulmonary embolism (OR, 1.56 [95% CI, 0.77-1.73]) (eFigure 4 in Supplement 1).

Bevacizumab Use

Before PSM (Table 3), patients treated with bevacizumab (n = 803) were generally younger than those without bevacizumab (n = 667), with a mean (SD) age of 52.5 (13.3) and 58.2 (13.5) years, respectively (P < .001) (SMD, 0.42), had a lower proportion Black/ patients (67 [8.3%] vs 95 [14.2%]) (P < .001), and greater prior use of chemoradiotherapy (365 [45.5%] vs 113 [16.9%]) (P < .001). Compared with patients who did not receive bevacizumab, those who were treated with bevacizumab were more frequently underweight (BMI <18.5; 18 [2.7%] vs 53 [6.6%]) (P = .001) and overweight (BMI 25-30; 179 [26.8%] vs 253 [31.5%]) (P = .05) (Table 3).

PSM generated 455 pairs of patients with ACC with good balance (SMD <0.10) (Table 3), with a mean (SD) age of 53.6 (13.3) vs 54.3 (13.3) years (P = .44). Racial and ethnic distribution, smoking status, and BMI categories were also well balanced. With a median follow-up of 11.3 (IQR, 4.4-18.2) months for the bevacizumab group and 8.4 (IQR, 1.6-15.1) months for the group without bevacizumab, the median OS was 33.2 (IQR, 12.7 to not reached) and 33.4 (IQR, 7.4 to not reached) months, respectively (HR, 0.77 [95% CI, 0.61-0.97]) (P = .01), with 12-month OS rates of 74.8% (95% CI, 70.4%-79.2%) and 64.5% (95% CI, 59.8%-69.2%) and 24-month OS rates of 56.2% (95% CI, 50.9%-61.5%) and 54.0% (95% CI, 48.7%-59.3%), respectively (eFigure 6 in Supplement 1). In the post hoc rematched analyses, 12-month OS rates were 72.5% (95% CI, 68.0%-76.5%) for bevacizumab-treated patients and 67.5% (95% CI, 62.7%-71.8%) for those without bevacizumab; 24-month OS rates were 57.3% (95% CI, 51.9%-62.6%) and 58.7% (95% CI, 52.6%-64.7%), respectively (eFigure 7 in Supplement 1). Adjusted Cox proportional hazards regression analyses, including prior chemoradiotherapy, showed no association with mortality risk between bevacizumab-treated and untreated patients (eTable 3 in Supplement 1).

Considering the adverse events of special interest, fistulae were found in 53 of 455 patients (11.6%) in the group with bevacizumab and 56 of 455 (12.3%) in the group without bevacizumab (OR, 0.94 [95% CI, 0.63-1.40]) (P = .76). The rate of bowel perforation in the bevacizumab-treated group was similar to that in the group without bevacizumab (16 of 455 [3.5%] vs 12 of 455 [2.6%]; OR, 1.21 [95% CI, 0.52-2.82]) (P = .67). The rate of pulmonary embolism was 29 of 455 (6.4%) in bevacizumab-treated patients and 45 of 455 (9.9%) in patients without bevacizumab, representing a difference that did not reach statistical significance (OR, 0.62 [95% CI, 0.38-1.01]) (P = .05) (eFigure 4 in Supplement 1).

Discussion

In this comparative effectiveness study, we compared ACC outcomes with pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy by platinum agent (cisplatin vs carboplatin) and bevacizumab use. No OS differences were observed between cisplatin and carboplatin, supporting platinum interchangeability when combined with pembrolizumab, consistent with JCOG0505 trial findings of carboplatin noninferiority.8

Bevacizumab showed early OS benefit at 12 months (74.8% vs 64.5%) that attenuated by 24 months (56.2% vs 54.0%), suggesting subset-specific early advantage. Notably, bevacizumab did not increase the risk of fistula, bowel perforation, or pulmonary embolism, challenging trial data showing increased thromboembolic complications. The contribution of anti–vascular endothelial growth factor to vascular adverse events remains debated; arterial events are well-established,14 but venous association is more inconsistent across trials.15,16,17 By effectively reducing tumor burden, bevacizumab may indirectly mitigate the risk of thromboembolic events, thereby potentially counterbalancing its inherent prothrombotic vascular effects.

Our median OS aligns with the BEATcc trial18 with atezolizumab and bevacizumab and the KEYNOTE-826 trial.19 Unlike the KEYNOTE-826 trial (OS with no bevacizumab, 17.1 months), our no-bevacizumab cohort showed superior survival, potentially due to differences in prior treatments or patient characteristics.

Notably, while the KEYNOTE-826 trial did not randomize patients between cisplatin and carboplatin, our analysis provides additional insight into this clinical decision, supporting the flexibility of platinum choice in combination with pembrolizumab. Furthermore, our study included a substantial cohort of patients often underrepresented in clinical trials, such as Asian patients (9.3% of our population). Post hoc exploratory analyses in this subgroup confirmed the efficacy of pembrolizumab even in Asian populations,20,21 and our study—where race and ethnicity were included as a covariate in the matching model—reinforces this observation. These comparisons underscore the consistent effectiveness of pembrolizumab across varied clinical context, while simultaneously emphasizing the complexities involved in regimen optimization and toxicity management in a clinical setting.

While rates of adverse events were generally balanced between treatment groups, clinically meaningful patterns merit discussion. In the cisplatin vs carboplatin comparison, cisplatin-treated patients demonstrated numerically higher pulmonary embolism risk compared with carboplatin-treated patients, consistent with cisplatin’s known thrombotic associations,22 although this did not reach statistical significance in the adverse-event analysis shown in eFigure 4 in Supplement 1. In the bevacizumab analysis, bevacizumab-treated patients had lower odds of pulmonary embolism than patients who did not receive bevacizumab. These findings warrant prospective monitoring and suggest that careful patient selection for bevacizumab and platinum choice based on thrombotic risk may optimize safety profiles.

Strengths and Limitations

The strengths of this study include the large, multi-institutional dataset, the adjustment for several baseline characteristics through propensity score matching, and the focus on relevant clinical questions not addressed in randomized trials. The observed outcomes in this unselected clinical population underline the broad applicability of pembrolizumab, reinforcing its role as a cornerstone in the management of ACC and highlighting the need for continued efforts to integrate molecular, clinical, and patient-centered factors into treatment decision-making to validate optimal patient selection for bevacizumab addition. Finally, most of the patients in our analysis had previously received chemoradiotherapy. Pembrolizumab is now being incorporated into the management of earlier-stage disease and is currently part of the treatment paradigm for locally advanced cervical cancer.5 Consequently, patients will increasingly relapse after prior immunotherapy, raising the question of whether rechallenge with an immune checkpoint inhibitor would be feasible or effective. Fortunately, several antibody-drug conjugates are currently under investigation and may represent promising new treatment options for this patient population.

This study also has several limitations. First, there was residual confounding by indication: bevacizumab is clinically withheld in patients with high fistula risk, extensive pelvic invasion, severe comorbidities, or poor performance status, which are independently associated with poor prognosis. Thus, the observed HR of 0.77 may partially reflect selection bias by indication rather than true benefit. We attempted mitigation through restricting the bevacizumab exposure window to 60 days of treatment initiation, including prior chemoradiotherapy, in matching, and performing stratified analyses; however, inability to directly measure performance status, tumor burden, or pelvic invasion extent represents unmeasured confounding. Second, although PSM achieved excellent balance on most covariates, prior chemoradiotherapy demonstrated residual imbalance in the bevacizumab analysis. Sensitivity analyses including post hoc adjusted Cox proportional hazards regression models and rematched cohorts with prior chemoradiotherapy as mandatory balance criterion demonstrated consistent findings on early bevacizumab effectiveness, diminishing over time and suggesting robustness despite this imbalance.

Other potential residual confounding factors are the lack of ECOG performance status, tumor histology, and combined positive score (CPS) status and incomplete capture of adverse events outside hospital settings.23 However, pembrolizumab approval requires CPS of 1 or greater. CPS testing could not be consistently performed in all health care settings, particularly resource-limited regions. The proportion of patients with CPS-positive vs CPS-negative findings is unknown, representing potential unmeasured confounding affecting treatment selection and outcomes. For the adverse event ascertainment, analyses were limited to inpatient or structured ICD-10-CM diagnoses; mild, outpatient, or undocumented events might have been missed. However, it is improbable that a fistula or a bowel perforation would be treated outside of a hospital environment. Also, geographic location (Europe, North America, or Asia) could not be included as a propensity score covariate due to TriNetX privacy restrictions; however, race and ethnicity served as a proxy for health care system differences, with excellent balance achieved after matching.

Conclusions

In this comparative effectiveness study of patients with ACC treated in a clinical setting, cisplatin and carboplatin was associated with comparable effectiveness when combined with pembrolizumab, supporting platinum flexibility based on comorbidities and logistical considerations. Bevacizumab addition was associated with early OS benefit in appropriate patients, with a manageable safety profile. These hypothesis-generating observational findings support selective bevacizumab use in carefully selected patients and justify prospective validation. However, findings derive from retrospective observational analysis and cannot establish causality. Rather, these results inform future hypothesis-generating research and complement randomized trial evidence. As pembrolizumab incorporation into earlier-stage cervical cancer management expands, continued integration of molecular, clinical, and patient-centered factors into personalized treatment decision-making remains essential for optimizing outcomes.

Comparison of Corneal Tomography Parameters in the Diagnosis of Keratoconus in Early and Late Pediatric Age Group
Authors: Seray Sahin, Melis Palamar, Sait Egrilmez, Ayse Yagci, Ozlem Barut Selver
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
PURPOSE:The purpose of this study was to examine demographic and tomographic parameters in pediatric patients diagnosed with keratoconus and to evaluate the difference between early and late age periods.METHODS:This retrospective study included patients younger than 18 years of age who were diagnosed with keratoconus. All examination findings, demographic data, tomography parameters, and keratoconus stages were recorded. The patients were divided into two groups: 14 years or younger (Group 1) and those between 14 and 18 years old (Group 2). All corneal tomography parameters were compared in the subgroups and were evaluated statistically. P <0.05 was considered statistically significant.RESULTS:A total of 131 eyes of 71 patients were included. The mean age was 15.7 ± 1.8 (range, 10–18) years, and the female-to-male ratio was 23/48. The mean best spectacle-corrected visual acuity was 0.36 ± 0.44 (range, 0–3.1) logMAR. The averages of K1, K2, Km, and Kmax were 49.0 ± 6.2 (range, 39.6–74.5) D, 53.5 ± 7.0 (range, 43.3–76.8) D, 51.3 ± 6.5 (range, 41.5–75) D, and 59.1 ± 9.0 (range, 44.7–85.2) D, respectively, and the mean central corneal thickness (CCT) was 456.9 ± 63.5 µm (range, 261–601). The distribution of eyes with keratoconus from stages 1–4 was 5.3%, 36.6%, 38.9%, and 19.1%, respectively. When the groups were compared, K1, K2, Km, and Kmax were significantly steeper in Group 1 compared to Group 2 (P = 0.006, P = 0.004, P = 0.004, and P = 0.007, respectively). CCT and the thinnest corneal thickness values were significantly less in Group 1 (P = 0.038 and P = 0.041, respectively).CONCLUSION:The decision for treatment – especially cross-linking – should be made at the right time due to the increased risk of progression in the earlier ages in pediatric keratoconus population.

Introduction

Keratoconus is an ectatic disease that is usually bilateral and causes vision loss, characterized by progressive corneal thinning and cone-shaped cornea. Collagen fibrils show an irregular arrangement in the protruding region of the cornea.[12] The etiology of keratoconus is not known exactly. The prevalence of keratoconus in the general population has been reported as 1/2000.[3] It is thought that reported incidence rates differ between populations and this alteration is due to genetic, ethnic, and environmental factors. Keratoconus can occur as an isolated condition or in association with systemic disorders such as Down syndrome, Ehlers–Danlos syndrome, Marfan syndrome, and osteogenesis imperfecta.[24] There are many gene polymorphisms that are suggested to be associated with keratoconus, but no directly keratoconus-related polymorphism has been found, and studies are still ongoing.[56] Higher incidence and prevalence rates are expected to be reported due to the widespread use of corneal topography.

Keratoconus usually develops in adolescence and progresses until the third to fourth decades.[2] It is observed at similar rates in males and females, and no significant difference was reported between genders.[37] However, when starts during pediatric age, keratoconus progresses more rapidly than adolescence and can be detected at higher stages at diagnosis.[8] Due to the widespread use of corneal tomography, the diagnosis of keratoconus is possible in the early period of life.[9]

The signs and symptoms of keratoconus vary according to the severity of the disease. Keratoconus should be suspected in the presence of irregular astigmatism increase and vision loss. Fleischer ring, Vogt’s striae, increased visibility of corneal nerves, corneal scarring, Munson’s sign, and acute hydrops are various examination findings in patients with keratoconus at different stages.[1011]

There is a strong association between keratoconus and eye rubbing, allergic eye diseases, and contact lens trauma.[17] A family history is reported in 8% to 10% of cases.[1213]

In the present study, it was aimed to evaluate the demographic data of pediatric patients with keratoconus and to compare the parameters of keratoconus in the early and late pediatric age groups.

Methods

This retrospective study was conducted in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration. Children under the age of 18 years who were admitted to an ophthalmology clinic between 2012 and 2022 and diagnosed with keratoconus were included in this study. Written informed consent was obtained from the legal parents of all patients.

Demographic and clinical data included age, gender, comorbidity, family history, presence of vernal keratoconjunctivitis (VKC), and treatment methods such as collagen cross-linking (CXL), contact lens, and keratoplasty. At the initial examination, the following data were collected: findings from an external examination (Munson's sign), a slit lamp examination (Vogt's lines, Fleischer rings, prominent corneal nerves, corneal scarring or hydrops), and the best spectacle-corrected visual acuity (BSCVA). Keratoconus diagnosis was confirmed by the Scheimpflug imaging system (Pentacam, Oculus, Germany). Corneal tomography has been employed to substantiate the diagnosis, stage the disease, and monitor the patient’s condition in individuals with a preliminary diagnosis of keratoconus based on clinical findings. Keratometry values (K1, K2, Km, and Kmax) and corneal tomography data (posterior corneal elevation, central corneal thickness [CCT], the thinnest pachymetry reading, and cone localization) were determined. The keratoconus stage was determined according to the “Amsler–Krumeich classification” and was staged from 1 to 4. The patients were divided into two groups: 14 years or younger (Group 1) and those between 14 and 18 years old (Group 2). All corneal tomography parameters were compared in the subgroups. Statistical analysis for the data obtained from this study was performed using Statistical Package for the Social Studies (SPSS) version 25.0 (IBM Inc., Armonk, NY, USA). P <0.05 was considered statistically significant. The patient management decision was made according to the “Global Consensus on Keratoconus and Ectatic Diseases” treatment algorithm.[11]

Results

One hundred and thirty-one eyes of 71 patients (60 bilateral patients and 11 unilateral patients) under the age of 18 years who were diagnosed with keratoconus between 2012 and 2022 were included in this study. The mean age of the patients was 15.7 ± 1.8 (range, 10–18) years with a female-to-male ratio of 23/48. VKC was detected in 15 (21.1%) patients and 8 (11.3%) patients had a positive family history. Autism spectrum disorder was observed in 1 (1.4%) patient, and Tourette’s syndrome was observed in 1 (1.4%) patient. Remaining of the patients (97.2%) had no known comorbidities.

In the slit-lamp examination, the most frequently observed findings were prominent corneal nerves in 67 (51.1%) eyes, Fleischer ring in 32 (24.4%) eyes, Vogt’s striae in 20 (15.3%) eyes, corneal scar in 14 (10.7%) eyes, Munson’s sign in 8 (6.1%) eyes, and acute hydrops in 3 (2.3%) eyes. The mean BSCVA in 131 eyes was 0.36 ± 0.44 (range, 0–3.1) logMAR. In the present study, the mean age of those with VKC was 14.1 ± 1.9 (range, 10–18) years and without VKC was 16.2 ± 1.6 (range, 13–18) years, and the difference between them was statistically significant (P < 0.001). A total of 53.3% of patients with accompanying VKC were 14 years of age or younger; 46.7% were older than 14 years, and the difference between them was statistically significant (P < 0.001).

According to the Amsler–Krumeich classification, 7 (5.3%) eyes had stage 1, 48 (36.6%) eyes had stage 2, 51 (38.9%) eyes had stage 3, and 25 (19.1%) eyes had stage 4 disease. As a treatment, contact lenses were prescribed to 35 (26.7%) eyes, CXL was performed to 59 (45%) eyes, and keratoplasty was performed to 18 (13.7%) eyes. Nineteen (14.5%) eyes were followed up without treatment.

Group 1 included 35 (26.7%) eyes, and Group 2 included 96 (73.3%) eyes. The variables that were compared between the groups are summarized in Table 2. In Group 1, keratometry values were significantly steeper than Group 2 (P = 0.006, P = 0.004, P = 0.004, and P = 0.007, respectively). The CCT and thinnest corneal thickness values were lower and posterior corneal elevation was higher (P = 0.038, P = 0.041, and P = 0.019, respectively). The mean BSCVA was 0.35 ± 0.31 (range, 0–1.30) logMAR in Group 1 and 0.36 ± 0.48 (range, 0–3.10) logMAR in Group 2 with no significant difference between them (P = 0.88).

Discussion

Keratoconus is a common disease characterized by corneal steepening and thinning, usually observed in adolescence and progressing until the third and fourth decades.[23] In the literature, it has been reported that it is detected at advanced stages in childhood than in adolescence and progresses more rapidly than in adolescents.[8] Léoni-Mesplié et al.[14] compared the severity of keratoconus in the pediatric and adult groups and reported a rate of 27.8% versus 7.8%. In the present study, the rate of keratoconus revealed in the early stage was found to be lower than that found in the advanced stage (5.3% vs. 58%). The low percentage of keratoconus detected in the early stage shows the importance of ophthalmologic examinations and screening in childhood.

In pediatric keratoconus, different from adults, systemic disease association, and positive family history are observed more frequently.[15] Tourette’s syndrome, which was seen in one patient in the present study, is a rare disease, and there is also a case of self-induced keratoconus with Tourette’s syndrome reported in the literature.[16] Although keratoconus usually occurs bilaterally, there are studies with unilateral keratoconus in the literature.[1718] Detection of unilateral keratoconus improves early diagnosis of keratoconus that may develop in the other eye, especially during childhood. Ocular allergic conditions such as eye itching, atopy, and VKC are more common in pediatric population.[1519] In the literature, the association of pediatric keratoconus with VKC has been reported at rates ranging from 8.8% to 36%.[620] In the present study, VKC was observed to be present in 21.1%, which is going along with the literature. Gupta et al.[21] reported that the mean age of patients with and without VKC was 14.4 years and 15.1 years, respectively. In the present study, it was also found that the cases in whom VKC accompanied the keratoconus were detected earlier.

Keratoconus is a disorder that causes vision loss, and early diagnosis is important in the pediatric population due to the risk of amblyopia development. Because of the rapid progression of keratoconus in childhood and the variability of symptoms and signs, it may be misdiagnosed. It has been reported in the literature that patients with low vision and high astigmatism were misdiagnosed as meridional amblyopia.[22] The distinction of the conditions that cause visual impairment should be made correctly. The appropriate treatment decision taken at the right time helps prevent both the development of amblyopia and the progression of keratoconus.

In pediatric and adult keratoconus, similar examination findings such as prominent corneal nerves, Fleischer ring, Vogt’s striae, corneal scar, and Munson’s sign are observed. Mahmoud et al.,[23] who performed a study in the pediatric population, reported that they found the frequency of these findings at a lower rate compared to the present study. However, Gupta et al.[21] found higher rates of corneal scarring, Munson’s sign, and Vogt’s striae in a study that included 116 eyes of 62 patients. The reason for these variable results is probably due to the differences in the stages of the evaluated eyes.

Corneal topography parameters such as K1, K2, Km, Kmax, CCT, posterior corneal elevation, and the thinnest pachymetry readings demonstrate the severity of keratoconus. In this presented study, the patients were divided into two groups to compare the severity of early-onset keratoconus with late-onset keratoconus in the pediatric population. In the literature, Mahmoud et al.[23] reported that they compared these parameters by grouping the patients according to age in their study including 160 eyes. However, they reported that there was no statistically significant difference in keratometry values, CCT, and thinnest corneal thickness between the groups. In the present study, all keratometry values in Group 1 (≤14 years old) were found to be significantly steeper, and CCT and thinnest corneal thickness were found to be lower compared to Group 2 (>14 years old). These results confirm that the onset of keratoconus at an early age is associated with an advanced stage keratoconus and a more rapid progression.

In several prospective studies reported in the literature, it has been presented that CXL treatment in the pediatric age provides stability of keratoconus and is successful in preventing progression.[242526] As the present study shows, due to the increase in the severity of keratoconus as age decreases in the pediatric population, the decision to apply CXL at the right time in patients appropriate for CXL can provide stabilization and good visual results.

Conclusion

This study reported the comparative results of early and late periods in the pediatric age group. This study found that the severity of keratoconus increased with decreasing age in the pediatric group. The results suggest that early treatment in pediatric patients diagnosed with keratoconus may provide stabilization and good visual outcomes.

Lactylation-related gene signature for prognosis prediction and immune infiltration assessment in lung adenocarcinoma via bulk and single-cell RNA sequencing
Authors: Hua Zhou, Wusheng Liu, Zhiwen Liu, Yanping Lan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer incidence and mortality globally, yet its molecular mechanisms remain poorly understood. Recent studies highlight the role of protein lactylation in regulating tumor microenvironment (TME) dynamics, tumor progression, and therapeutic responses, suggesting its potential as a therapeutic target. This study aimed to identify key lactylation-related genes (LRGs) in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) using integrated bulk and single-cell RNA-seq analyses. Bulk RNA-seq data from TCGA-LUAD and GSE118370, alongside single-cell data from GSE149655, were analyzed. LRGs were retrieved from GeneCards, and machine learning algorithms (SVM-RFE and LASSO-Cox regression) identified prognostic genes. A prognostic risk model was constructed using four genes (CCNA2, PRAM1, GPR37, HMGA1) and validated in independent datasets. A nomogram combining risk scores and clinical parameters showed high accuracy for predicting 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival. Single-cell analysis of 6798 cells identified 10 cell types, with significant differences in LRG expression across immune and stromal cells. HMGA1 and PRAM1 displayed differential expression in macrophages, monocytes, and fibroblasts. Functional validation via qPCR and Western blot confirmed consistent expression trends in LUAD cell lines, while shRNA-mediated knockdown of CCNA2, GPR37, and HMGA1 significantly inhibited LUAD cell proliferation, supporting their oncogenic roles. This study suggests that lactylation is associated with TME immune cell differentiation in LUAD and establishes a novel four-gene (CCNA2, PRAM1, GPR37, HMGA1) lactylation-related signature.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-04845-0.

Introduction

Lung cancer has the highest incidence and mortality rate among all cancers [1]. Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), which has no obvious symptoms in the early stage, accounting for approximately 40% of all lung cancer patients [2, 3]. Despite treatment advances in these years, the disease still has poor prognosis with 5-year survival rate lower than 15% [4]. The pathogenesis of LUAD is currently not fully understood, but evidence has shown that smoking, occupational exposure, air pollution, ionizing radiation, genetics and their mutations are the close risk factors [5]. Oncogenes activity such as mutations, amplifications, rearrangements also play a crucial role in the development of the disease, which lay the foundation for targeted anti-cancer drugs, such as protein kinase inhibitors [6, 7]. However, cases of drug resistance are increasing, and more new potential targets remain to be discovered.

Lactic acid/lactate is the core metabolite of glycolysis under hypoxic conditions, and lactate metabolism (including lactate production, transport and accumulation) is the upstream metabolic phenomenon that drives the occurrence of lactylation; lactylation, as a novel post-translational modification, refers to the covalent binding of lactate-derived lactyl groups to lysine residues of histone or non-histone proteins, which directly regulates protein function and gene transcription. In brief, lactate metabolism is the metabolic basis for lactylation modification, while lactylation is the key epigenetic effector that translates lactate metabolic signals into biological functional changes in tumor cells and the tumor microenvironment [8, 9].

Lactic acid and acidification of tumor microenvironment (TME) catalyze key carcinogenic processes, including angiogenesis, invasion, metastasis, and immune escape. Histone lactylation is a novel epigenetic regulatory factor for cancers under the accumulation of lactic acid [10–13].

However, there are still very few reports on the impact of this pathway in LUAD, and existing lactylation-related prognostic signatures for other cancers are mostly constructed based on bulk RNA-seq alone, lacking single-cell level validation of cell-type-specific expression and functional relevance, and failing to link lactylation-related genes to tumor microenvironment immune-stromal cell interaction in specific cancer types. Therefore, in this study, we conducted data mining in public databases and integrated bulk RNA-seq (TCGA-LUAD, GSE118370) and single-cell RNA-seq (GSE149655) analyses to identify key lactylation-related genes (LRGs) in LUAD, construct a robust prognostic signature, and validate the oncogenic functions of key signature genes in vitro. We aimed to explicitly clarify the unique role of lactylation in LUAD TME immune infiltration and cell differentiation, establish a clinically applicable lactylation-related prognostic model for LUAD, and offer novel and specific ideas for the targeted treatment of LUAD based on lactylation modification.

Methods

Data acquirement

The gene expression RNA-seq data, the phenotype data, and the survival data of TCGA-LUAD dataset were downloaded though UCSC Xena (https://xenabrowser.net/datapages/) [14] as the training set. The RNA-seq contains the standardized Fragments Per Kilobase Million (FPKM) data of bulk RNA expression, with 510 LUAD samples ((group: LUAD, n = 510 cases) and adjacent samples (group: Normal, n = 58 cases). The phenotype data includes 877 samples, while the survival data includes 738 samples.

The LUAD related dataset GSE118370 [15] from the GEO were downloaded by using “GEOquery” [16] as validation set, which includes microarray gene expression profiles of n = 6 LUAD samples and n = 6 normal tissue samples. The single-cell RNA sequencing dataset GSE149655 [17] was also downloaded, whose organism is Homo Sapiens. The dataset contains tumor tissue samples and normal samples from two LUAD patients carrying KRAS mutations.

Basic lactylation-related gene set

We collected 116 lactylation-related genes (LLRGs) from the GeneCards database [18] by using “lactylation” as the search keyword and screened them with correlation score > 0.6. We obtained 22 LLRGs from the literature [19] and 400 LLRGs from the literature [20]. After intersecting these genes, a total of 446 LLRGs were obtained.

DEGs between disease/normal samples

Based on the TCGA-LUAD sequencing gene-sample matrix, the R package “DESeq2” [21] was applied to obtain all differentially expressed genes (DEGs). We screened the DEGs with | logFC |> 1 and P.aj < 0.05, and intersected them with LLRGs to obtain LLRDEGs. We presented the distribution of DEGs by using volcano plots (R package “ggplot2”) and heatmaps (R package “pheatmap”).

Screening of characteristic genes

To obtain the characteristic genes, we used the Support Vector Machine-Recursive Feature Elimination (SVM-RFE) algorithm [22] to perform dimensionality reduction analysis of the LLRDEGs based on the TCGA-LUAD dataset. SVM-RFE trains the model on samples by sorting each feature based on its score, removing the feature with the lowest score, iterating the training with the remaining features until the required number of features were selected. We implemented this process through the function 'rfe' in the 'caret' R package.

Cox regression analysis

We intersected the samples from the survival data of TCGA with the samples from the gene expression matrix to obtain the intersection samples. After organizing the LLRDEGs gene expression information and survival information of the intersection samples, the “coxph” function of the “survival” package was used for univariate Cox survival analysis based on the expression matrix. Prognostic genes with statistical significance (P < 0.05) for patient survival were screened, and their hazard ratio (HR) with % 95CI and P-value were displayed in a forest plot by “forest” package.

Lasso- Cox modeling of prognostic genes

Statistically significant single-factor prognostic genes were input into the Lasso-Cox regression algorithm for further screening. The LASSO regression prevents overfitting of the curve based on linear regression by adding a penalty term [23]. We use the “glmnet” package to implement the algorithm. Taking the survival time and overall-survival status of the sample as dependent variables, the seed as 2024, the family parameter as “Cox”, and the number of iterations as 1000, the optimal lambda value and the list of characteristic genes (key genes) were obtained, as well as the formula for the prognostic model.\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$ {\text{riskScore = }}\mathop \sum \limits_{{\mathrm{i}}} {\text{Coef }}\left( {{\mathrm{gene}}_{{\mathrm{i}}} } \right){\text{*mRNA Expression ( gene}}_{{\mathrm{i}}} {)} $$\end{document}

According to this formula, the risk-score for each sample was calculated and the samples were divided into groups (high/low risk). The accuracy of predicting the prognosis with the risk scores was measured by using the “survivdiff” and “survivfit” functions. The fitted survival curves for the two groups at 1, 3, and 5 years were compared and shown with a time-dependant Kaplan–Meier Curve.

Construction of nomogram with the key genes

After intersecting the sample vectors in TCGA dataset with the sample vectors of survival data and gene expression matrix data, we obtained a matrix containing survival data, gene expression matrix, and clinical information. We classified the gender, stage, pathologic_T, pathologic_M, pathologic_N, risk score variables and used the “cph” function to construct a multi variate Cox regression model with a nomogram built by “rms” package. Calibration curves [24] were also drawn to assess the linear fit of 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year survival rates.

Verification of key gene expression

We validated the expression characteristics of key genes based on the GEO gene expression matrix, using Boxplot and heatmap to display their expression features. Meanwhile, we separately plotted KM curves for these genes to demonstrate their predictive ability for survival rate.

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses

We obtained DEGs between the high/low risk groups. Gene ontology is a database that categorizes the function of a specific gene set into items. KEGG links genomic information with more organized biological functional information [25]. We conducted enrichment analysis on the functions of these DEGs using “enrichGO” and “enrichKEGG” from the “clusterProfiler” package [26].

GSEA and GSVA analyses

Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) [27] evaluates if a pre-defined gene set contributes to a specific phenotype, which was performed by the “GSEA” function with reference gene set downloaded from the MSigDB [28]. Gene set variation analysis (GSVA) [29] transformes gene set sample matrix to KEGG enrichment matrix. The analysis was performed by the “gsva” function from “GSVA” package with the “kcdf” parameter as “Gaussian” and the reference hallmarks retrieved from MSigDB. The distribution of the hallmarks was displayed by a heatmap and a bar graph.

PPI interaction network

All proteins that interacting with the key genes were predicted by GeneMania (https://genemania.org/) [30].

The mRNA-miRNA, mRNA-TF, mRNA-drugs interaction networks

We used the names of key genes to search for miRNAs potentially interacting with them in the miRDB database (https://mirdb.org/) [31]. TFs that bind to the key genes in the target-scan database (https://www.targetscan.org/vert_80/) [32] were also searched. We used the Comparative Toxicology Database (CTD) (http://ctdbase.org/) [33] to predict potential drugs or small molecule compounds that interact with key genes. The search results were downloaded from the websites and visualized by using Cytoscape software [34].

ROC curves

We used R “proc” package to plot the curves [35] of the key genes for the two datasets.

Immune infiltration analysis

The CIBERSORT [36] were used to quantitatively evaluate the relative abundance of immune cells by calculating the enrichment scores of them. Difference between samples of the risk subgroups were presented by boxplot plots [37, 38].

AUCell

Based on the R language “AUCell” library [39], the LLRDEGs between LUAD/Normal and the Seurat object were input to the “AUCell_run” to evaluate the activity of lactylation genes in cell clusters for each cell. The results of AUCell score comparison between cells were displayed in a grouped box plot. We also observed the expression of key genes between these cells [40].

Real-time quantitative PCR (qRT-PCR) and Western blot

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent, and cDNA was synthesized with the PrimeScript RT reagent Kit. qRT-PCR was performed using SYBR Green PCR Master Mix on an ABI 7500 system, with GAPDH as the internal control and relative expression calculated via the 2^(-ΔΔCt) method.

For Western blot, cells were lysed in RIPA buffer with protease/phosphatase inhibitors. Equal amounts of protein were separated by SDS-PAGE, transferred to PVDF membranes, and probed with primary antibodies against CCNA2, GPR37, HMGA1, PRAM1 (Proteintech) and secondary antibodies (Cell Signaling Technology). Bands were visualized by ECL and quantified using ImageJ.

Lentivirus production and cell counting

shRNAs targeting human CCNA2, GPR37, or HMGA1 were cloned into the pLKO.1-puro vector using sequences listed in Supplementary Table 1. Lentiviruses were produced in 293 T cells by co-transfecting 9 μg of shRNA construct, 6.75 μg of psPAX2, and 4.5 μg of pMD2.G (Addgene) using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen) in a 100 mm dish. Viral supernatants were collected at 48 h and 72 h post-transfection and concentrated with Ultracel® Regenerated Cellulose filters (Millipore). Target H1299 cells were transduced with the appropriate viral dose and selected with 6 μg/mL puromycin (InvivoGen) 48 h after infection.After selection, positive cells were seeded into 6-well plates, and cell numbers were counted every two days. Viable and dead cells were distinguished using trypan blue staining.

Statistical analyses

All data processing and analysis in this study were completed using R software (Version 4.4.1), The Wilcoxon rank sum test was performed between two parameters. All P-statistics are two-sided, with a threshold less than 0.05.

Laboratory validation of key genes in LUAD

To experimentally validate the expression patterns of the four key genes identified in LUAD, we first compared their mRNA levels between normal bronchial epithelial cells (BEAS-2B) and lung adenocarcinoma cell lines (H1299 and A549). As shown in (Fig. 12A), the expression of CCNA2 was significantly upregulated in H1299 cells but markedly downregulated in A549 cells (P < 0.05). The expression levels of GPR37 and HMGA1 were significantly elevated in both H1299 and A549 cells (P < 0.05). In contrast, PRAM1 expression was significantly increased in A549 cells (P < 0.05) but showed no significant change in H1299 cells (P > 0.05). Consistent with the qPCR results, Western blot analysis confirmed similar expression patterns of these four genes across the tested cell lines (Fig. 12B). Given that CCNA2, GPR37, and HMGA1 were all highly expressed in H1299 cells, we hypothesized that these genes may play potential oncogenic roles in LUAD. To test this hypothesis, we generated shRNA-mediated knockdown constructs targeting CCNA2, GPR37, and HMGA1 in H1299 cells. The knockdown efficiency was validated by both qPCR and Western blotting, confirming that the expression of each target gene was effectively reduced (Fig. 12C and D). Subsequent cell proliferation assays revealed that silencing CCNA2, GPR37, or HMGA1 significantly inhibited H1299 cell growth (Fig. 12E). Discussion

Histones are alkaline proteins that exist within chromosomes and bind to DNA. Protein other than histones in chromosomes are called non-histones, which are, mostly acidic [41]. The post-translational modifications of histones, including classical phosphorylation, methylation, glycosylation, and acetylation, have been shown to play a role in various biological processes, including gene epigenetic regulation, DNA repair, mitosis, and meiosis [42]. Histone lactylation, also known as lysine lactylation (Kla), is a process that utilizes the cellular metabolite lactate as a substrate for lysine residue modification. It involves adding a lactate group (La) to the lysine amino acid residue in the histone tail, thereby activating downstream gene expression and acting as an epigenetic regulatory factor [43].

In this study, the TCGA-LUAD dataset was selected for differential gene expression analysis between disease/normal groups, and all lactylation-related genes (LLRGs) were screened out, resulting in 66 DEGs. These genes have also been reported in other literature to be involved in the development of other cancers. In the study by Sun C et al. [44], 9 prognostic genes for multiple myeloma were reported, among which, FABP5 was included in the 66 genes discovered in this study. GAPDH encodes a member of the glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase protein family, which is a catalytic enzyme in glycolysis and is used as an internal reference for Western blot protein standardization due to its high expression in all tissues [45]. Wang Y et al. [46] reported that the transcription factor BTB and CNC homolog Bach1 reduce lactate production during glycolysis by inhibiting two key enzymes, HK2 and GAPDH, indicating a close relationship between GAPDH and lactate production. Other genes are also associated with specific cancers, such as PDLIM1, which has been reported to prevent metastasis of colon cancer [47].These functions are all related to the metabolism of lactate in tumor cells and the establishment of an acidic tumor microenvironment.

We used the SVM-RFE algorithm to perform dimensionality reduction on these 66 genes, resulting in 13 characteristic genes. We continued to use Cox regression to screen for factors that affect patient’s survival for these genes and obtained a total of 8 genes that contribute to prognosis, KIF2C, CCNA2, PRAM1, CENPA, GPR37, MKI67, RFC4, HMGA1. Among these 8 genes, PRAM1 reduces the risk of patient death, while the other 7 genes increase the risk of patient death. We continued to conduct Lasso Cox regression analysis on these 8 genes, selected key genes related to prognosis, and used these genes to construct a prognostic risk model. We obtained four key genes and a model for calculating sample risk. In the performance diagnosis of this model, we found that high-risk patients had higher mortality rates, while patients with a death outcome had higher calculated risk scores, which verified the accuracy of our risk model. This risk model can be used together with the clinical symptoms of patients to predict their prognosis. In the nomogram we constructed, the risk model score combined with patient info can accurately predict the survival rates of patients, especially the 3-year survival rate, which provides LUAD clinicians with an option to accurately predict the prognosis and develop treatment.

To further analyze the function of the LLRGs, we scored the LUAD samples one by one and divided them into high/low scoring groups for screening differential genes and enriching their functions. The results of GO analysis suggest that DEGs were enriched in the G protein coupled receptor signaling pathway, the transport of carboxylic acids and organic acids, and the transmembrane transport of single cations and metal ions. G protein coupled receptor (GPR) on the cell membrane interacts with endogenous ligands and acts as an intermediate metabolite in the production of hydroxycarboxylic acids during cellular energy metabolism. These receptors, in conjunction with lactate, mediate biological processes such as lactate induced energy metabolism, lipid delay, neuronal protection, and inflammation regulation [48]. Glycolysis is a major way for solid tumor tissues to obtain energy, producing acidic products such as lactate and pyruvate, causing intracellular acidification [49]. Cationic ions such as Na + /H + on the cell membrane of tumor tissue can assist in intracellular acid excretion, and transmembrane monocarboxylic acid transport pumps may also play an important role in lactate transport and pH regulation inside and outside the cell [50]. The results of GO enrichment were all related to the crossing and transportation of lactate. KEGG suggested that DEGs are enriched in pathways such as neuroactive ligand receptor interactions, cAMP signaling pathways, and cytochrome metabolism, indicating that lactylation genes are involved in these pathways. However, there is no report on these pathways. GSVA detected the enrichment scores of the Hallmark pathway of GSEA between high and low-risk groups, and discovered multiple pathways such as endothelial cell carcinoma, histone and histidine metabolism, nitrogen metabolism, and endocytosis. This is direct evidence that LLRGs are involved in LUAD and histone amino acid metabolism.

In the PPI network, several core hub proteins were identified as connectors linking CCNA2, GPR37, HMGA1, and PRAM1.These connectors are mainly involved in transcriptional regulation, chromatin modification, and cell cycle control, consistent with the epigenetic and proliferative functions of lactylation-related genes.Such hub proteins may act as key integrators of lactylation-derived signals and coordinate the downstream biological functions of our signature genes.

The immune infiltration between risk groups were also investigated and significant differences in immune infiltration between the groups were found, particularly in B cell memory, T cells CD4 memory activated/ resting, Macrophages M0/M1, Monocytes, NK cells, Mast cells. This suggests that LLRGs play a significant role in immune infiltration and differentiation. Lactic acid may promote gene transcription by modifying different sites of histones, and thus might be involved in physiological and pathological processes such as stem cell and embryonic development, macrophages polarization, and tumor occurrence and development, as reported in previous studies [51]—this regulatory relationship is supported by correlative epigenetic analyses and needs to be verified by direct functional experiments.. Previous studies [52] have suggested that histone modification at the H3K18la site may enhance gene expression and might contribute to the transformation of M1 into M2 in the late polarization stage of macrophages, which is associated with the reduction of inflammation-induced damage (based on correlative histone modification and macrophages phenotype analyses). The lactylation of histone at site H3 might synergistically contribute to the transcription of NF-κB and STAT6 with histone acetylation, which is linked to the pro-inflammatory differentiation of M1 macrophages [53], though direct causal evidence for this synergy is still lacking.stone acetylation, inducing pro-inflammatory differentiation of M1 macrophages [53]. The lactylation of non-histone proteins is also widely present in organisms and can affect protein function and biological metabolism, participating in macrophages differentiation and tumor development. Research [54] has shown that knocking down PCSK9 can lead to a decrease in lactate levels and protein lactylation, thereby promoting polarization of M1 macrophages. In this study, we also found significant differences in the infiltration levels of B cells, T cells, monocytes and other immune cells between high- and low-risk groups based on correlative immune infiltration analysis. The potential molecular pathways underlying this association between lactylation-related gene expression and immune cell infiltration characteristics remain to be elucidated by further mechanistic experiments.

Our four key genes (CCNA2, GPR37, HMGA1, PRAM1) show distinct expression patterns in LUAD, with CCNA2, GPR37, and HMGA1 as risk factors (AUC > 0.5) and PRAM1 as a protective factor. Notably, this study is the first to identify CCNA2 as a lactylation-related gene, with its high expression correlated with abnormal immune infiltration in LUAD—extending its known role in tumor cell cycle regulation [55]. GPR37, a reported lactate extracellular messenger [56], may contribute to lactylation-mediated TME remodeling and immune escape through its association with high-risk prognosis. HMGA1, a non-histone protein with no previously reported lactylation modification, shows differential expression in TME macrophages/monocytes; its homologous gene HMGB1 is lactylated in macrophages [57, 58], suggesting HMGA1 may also undergo lactylation—an assumption requiring direct experimental verification. PRAM1 downregulation in LUAD aligns with its potential anti-tumor role in neutrophil function [59], though its link to lactylation remains to be elucidated [60].

We continued to predict the interaction small molecules of four key genes and identified some mRNA and miRNA that may have interactions, such as CCNA2 and CDK1, HMGA and PRMT6, miR-124-3p.2 interacting with both HMGA1 and GPR37. However, there are still no reports on the pathways through which these interactions affect LUAD. We also predicted TFs and small molecule compounds that interact with these key genes and identified some TFs and drugs that interact with two or more genes simultaneously, such as Nrf-1, MAFB, MZF1, Valproic Acid, CGP 52608. These small molecules have not been reported yet. In the future, we plan to conduct more in-depth analyses of four genes based on the interaction of these small molecules, to explore the possibility of these four genes as target genes for tumor therapy.

Our lactylation-related signature is closely associated with the Warburg effect in LUAD: enhanced aerobic glycolysis promotes lactate accumulation (the substrate for lactylation), which may epigenetically sustain glycolytic gene expression—forming a feedforward loop. This lactylation-driven program may also confer therapy resistance (e.g., cisplatin) by acidifying the TME and suppressing anti-tumor immunity (consistent with our observation of abnormal macrophage/CD4 + T cell infiltration in high-risk groups). These hypotheses are supported by prior links between lactate metabolism, lactylation, and therapeutic failure [61], requiring further experimental validation [61].

To further subdivide the characteristics of different cells within LUAD tissue and the expression of four key genes in different cells, we selected GSE149655 for single-cell analysis. After quality control and normalization, 6,798 cells were divided into 16 independent clusters and annotated into 10 categories (those that cannot be annotated are uniformly classified as others). We found that there were differences in the number of endothelial cells, epithelial cells, macrophages, NK cells, monocytes, and B cells between cancer and normal samples. In Xu H et al.’s study [60], an increase in endothelial and epithelial cells was also observed in large sample single-cell analysis of tumor samples, which is consistent with the results of this study. However, their study did not observe an increase in macrophages. We believe that there is a significant immune differentiation phenomenon in LUAD, which is the result of immune microenvironment reprogramming during disease development.

To understand the effects of lactylation on different types of cells, we used 66 LLRDEGs to perform AUCell scoring on different cell types. We found significant differences in AUCell scores between LUAD and Normal samples for epithelial cells, endothelial cells, macrophages, monocytes, T cells, and tissue stem cells, indicating that LLRGs are involved in the functional expression of multiple cells. We focused on investigating the expression of four key genes in different types of cells and found that HMGA1 was differentially expressed in various cells such as Macrophage, Monocyte, Fibroblasts, while PRAM1 showed significant expression differences in Macrophages and Monocyte. PRAM1 may participate in the immune differentiation of macrophages and monocytes through its differential expression, but there are currently no literature reports on this yet.

In this study, we not only identified four key lactylation-related genes through integrated transcriptomic and machine learning analyses but also experimentally validated their expression and biological functions. qPCR and Western blot analyses confirmed consistent expression patterns across LUAD cell lines, and functional assays demonstrated that silencing CCNA2, GPR37, or HMGA1 markedly suppressed LUAD cell proliferation. These findings provide solid experimental evidence supporting our computational predictions and further establish these genes as potential oncogenic mediators in LUAD, reinforcing the biological and clinical relevance of our lactylation-based prognostic model. Notably, the cell experiments showed expression patterns consistent with the bioinformatic analysis in H1299 cells but not in A549 cells. This discrepancy may be attributed to the cellular origin, as H1299 cells are derived from LUAD tumor tissue and may better resemble the TCGA samples, whereas A549 cells originate from alveolar basal epithelial cells.

Despite the promising findings, this study has several limitations: the small sample size of the single-cell RNA-seq dataset may limit the representation of intratumoral heterogeneity; the exact molecular mechanisms and signaling pathways of the identified lactylation-related genes in LUAD pathogenesis remain unclear; and the binding modes and therapeutic implications of the predicted small molecules interacting with key genes require further validation. These deficiencies will be addressed through supplementary experiments and in-depth analyses in subsequent research with improved experimental conditions and larger clinical sample sizes. Nevertheless, the study constructs a novel LUAD-specific lactylation-related four-gene signature via integrated bulk and single-cell RNA-seq analyses and in vitro functional validation, providing unique biological and clinical insights for lactylation research in LUAD and valuable references for exploring lactylation modification in LUAD prognosis and targeted therapy.

This study only verified the oncogenic functions of CCNA2, GPR37, and HMGA1 through cell proliferation experiments. However, experiments related to malignant phenotypes such as cell migration, invasion, cell cycle, and apoptosis have not been conducted yet. We will further deepen this research in our subsequent studies.

Conclusions

In this study, four key lactylation-related genes (CCNA2, GPR37, HMGA1, PRAM1) were confirmed to be associated with immune cell differentiation and infiltration, as well as the occurrence of lung adenocarcinoma. The risk model constructed by these four key genes and coefficients can accurately predict the prognosis of lung adenocarcinoma. These findings provide important clues for further studying the molecular mechanisms and potential therapeutic targets of LUAD.

Half-Dose Photodynamic Therapy for Bullous Variant Central Serous Chorioretinopathy: A Long-Term Follow-Up
Authors: Yang Liu, Chen Jiang, Min Zhu, Gezhi Xu, Lei Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionTo evaluate the long-term outcomes of half-dose half-dose photodynamic therapy (PDT) in the treatment of bullous variant central serous chorioretinopathy (bvCSC).MethodsA retrospective single-center study of 18 patients, 31 eyes with bvCSC who received PDT between January 2012 and December 2021 with a minimum follow-up of 36 months.ResultsDuring follow-up, a dry macula was achieved in all cases and no recurrence was witnessed. The mean number of PDT treatments was 1.2 sessions. Twenty-five (80.6%) eyes in 14 patients achieved complete fluid resolution after a single session. The earliest complete resolution of subretinal fluid occurred at 1 month, while the latest was at 12 months, with a mean time of 4.2 months. The subretinal fibrinous exudates were completely resolved in all affected eyes, with the earliest resolution observed at 1 month and the latest at 13 month, with an average time of 5.9 months. In seven (22.6%) eyes with pre-existing conditions, the subretinal fibrosis and scarring remained unchanged. The baseline average logMAR BCVA was 1.08 ± 0.55, and it improved to 0.74 ± 0.80 at final visit (P < 0.001), with visual gain stabilizing at 24 months. One eye (3.2%) with defects in the retinal pigmentary epithelium-choriocapillaris developed choroidal neovascularization 10 months after treatment.ConclusionsOur clinical data suggested half-dose PDT might offer a favorable safety and efficacy profile in treating conventional bvCSC, with visual gain stabilizing at 24 months post-treatment. Cautious risk–benefit assessment remains essential for patients with high-risk ischemic anatomical features.

Introduction

Bullous retinal detachment (BRD) is an exaggerated variant or atypical form of central serous chorioretinopathy (CSC) [1, 2]. Different from classic CSC, bullous variant CSC (bvCSC) is characterized by severer impairment of the outer retinal barrier and disturbances in choroidal circulation, manifesting as extensive serous retinal detachment (SRD, > 10 disc diameters), subretinal fibrinous exudates, multi-hyperreflective pigment epithelial detachment (PED), hyperreflectivity around large choroidal vessels, and increased reflectivity at the level of the choriocapillaris [2].

The pathogenesis of bvCSC remains incompletely understood, and no standardized treatment protocol has been established. In its natural course, diffuse SRF may resolve spontaneously, though recurrence is not uncommon [3]. While focal laser photocoagulation of leakage points can accelerate subretinal fluid (SRF) resolution, it does not confer significant visual benefit compared to observation alone. Moreover, laser therapy increases the risk of subretinal fibrosis and offers no efficacy in cases of macular involvement [4]. In terms of oral mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, there is currently no consensus for their therapeutic benefit in CSC or bvCSC [5, 6]. Surgical intervention [7, 8] (theoretically capable of evacuating SRF through internal or external drainage) or combinations of the aforementioned therapies have also been reported [9]. However, as most supporting evidence came from individual case reports, the clinical efficacy of these therapies still requires further verification.

Given the established evidence for reduced-dose/fluence photodynamic therapy (PDT) in CSC [5, 10], this modality has been extended to BRD management. However, published experience remains limited. Our team has dedicated in applying half-dose PDT to treat chronic CSC [11], while also exploring its feasibility and potential benefits for managing bvCSC. In this article, we have evaluated the relatively long-term clinical outcomes of half-dose PDT in the treatment of bvCSC.

Methods

This is a retrospective case series. Patients with bvCSC in Eye, Ear, Nose and Throat Hospital of Fudan University between January 2012 and December 2021, were included. Ethical approval was provided by the Ethics Committee of the Eye and ENT Hospital of Fudan University (No. 2020119) and this study was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from each patient for participation in this study.

All patients were followed for at least 36 months. The diagnosis of bvCSC was confirmed through comprehensive ocular clinical examination, along with B-scan ultrasonography, fundus fluorescein angiography (FFA), indocyanine green angiography (ICGA), and optical coherence tomography (OCT). The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Presence of neurosensory detachment ≥ 10-disc diameters identified on fundus examination; (2) OCT revealing significant SRF involving the fovea; (3) FFA showing early phase multifocal or diffuse hyperfluorescence with late phase leakage; ICGA showing mid-late phase multifocal or diffuse choroidal hyperfluorescence; (4) No signs of fluid absorption within 3 months after disease onset. The exclusion criteria included: (1) evidence of any form of rhegmatogenous retinal detachment or exudative retinal detachments, such as those associated with Vogt–Koyanagi–Harada syndrome, uveal effusion syndrome, or posterior scleritis; (2) previous treatment with laser photocoagulation or PDT for CSC or bvCSC.

At baseline, all eyes underwent an ophthalmic examination using slit-lamp biomicroscopy with a 120D superfield lens. Best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) was measured with the ETDRS chart, and ocular B-scan ultrasound was also performed. The color fundus photographs and FFA were taken using Topcon TRC 501 fundus camera (Topcon Imagenet, Tokyo, Japan). Simultaneous fundus angiography and OCT were conducted using the Spectralis HRA + OCT (Heidelberg Engineering Inc., Heidelberg, Germany). Three months after PDT treatment, BCVA was evaluated, and simultaneous angiography and OCT were performed again. Additional follow-ups were scheduled at 6, 12, 24, 36 month and final visit, during which BCVA and simultaneous OCT were performed at each visit. If new SRF or SRF persisting for more than three months was observed during the follow-up period, additional angiography was required.

To obtain the OCT images, ocular fovea, areas with SRF, or areas presenting abnormal leakage or abnormal hyperfluorescence on angiography were scanned, utilizing linear and volume scan modes. The EDI-OCT mode was used to assess the choroidal structure. The central retinal thickness (CRT) and the central choroidal thickness (CCT) were measured. The CRT was defined as the distance between the internal limiting membrane to outside border of retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) in the central fovea. The CCT was defined as the distance between the outside border of RPE to the inside border of sclera underneath the central fovea. The CRT and CCT were measured on the horizontal and vertical OCT images, and take the average as the results.

After obtaining written informed consent, half-dose PDT therapy, adapted from Chan used to treat CSC [11, 12], was performed on all patients. The brief description is as follows: 3 mg/m2 verteporfin is intravenously injected within 8 min, and 10 min after the start of injection, 689 nm laser (Opal photoactivator; Lumenis, Beijing, China) initiated for irradiation. The laser parameters were set to an energy intensity of 50 J/cm2, a fluence of 600 mW/cm2, and an exposure time of 83 s. The irradiation was targeted at the source of BRD, identified as sites of RPE leakage on FA, areas of choroidal hyperpermeability on ICGA and areas of SRF on OCT. The treatment was applied using multiple spots, delivered through a fundus contact lens (Ocular Fundus Laser Lens; magnification factor 1.08), with a maximum spot diameter of 5400 µm. Care was taken to minimize spot overlap and to avoid any overlap within the macular fovea. Following the procedure, patients were instructed to take precautions against light exposure for 5 days. For cases involving both eyes, treatment could be performed in one single session. If new occurrence of SRF, or persistent leakage/SRF without signs of resolution, retreatment was considered.

SPSS software (version 22.0, IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA) and GraphPad Prism (version 8.0, GraphPad Software Inc., La Jolla, CA, USA) were used for all statistical analyses and graphing. Comparisons of BCVA in logarithmus of the minimal angle of resolution (logMAR) units, CRT, and CCT were performed using paired t tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. A P value of ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

After the exclusion of six patients due to loss of follow-up or missing data, 18 patients (31 eyes), 13 males and five females included in the final analysis. The average age was 43.8 ± 5.9 years (range 36–57). Thirteen (72.2%) patients were bilateral involved. Half-dose PDT was conducted in all eyes. The mean number of treatments was 1.2 sessions (range 1–2). Twenty-five (80.6%) eyes in 14 patients achieved complete fluid resolution after a single treatment session, while 6 (19.4%) eyes in four cases required two sessions because of persistent SRF. The spot number per patient was 4.6 ± 1.7 (range 2–9), with an average spot diameter being 4.7 ± 1.1 mm, (range 1.1–5.4 mm).

The baseline average logMAR BCVA was 1.08 ± 0.55, and it improved to 0.74 ± 0.80 at final visit (P < 0.001). The BCVA improved most rapidly before month 6 post-treatment, followed by a gradual plateau and stabilization at 24 months. The specific changes in visual improvement can be found in Fig. 1. Twenty-seven eyes (87.1%) gained visual benefits, two eyes (6.5%) remained stable, and two eyes (6.5%) in one patient experienced decreased vision. The patient with vision decline was Case 5. All patients underwent angiography as scheduled during the follow-up period. Two patients only received baseline FFA due to a shortage of indocyanine green dye, while the remaining patients underwent simultaneous FFA and ICGA. Six eyes in four patients required additional angiography due to persistent SRF. The baseline angiography revealed multifocal, diffuse leakage in FFA, as well as early phase choroidal vascular dilation and middle to late phase choriocapillaris hyperfluorescence in ICGA. At 3 months, the angiography in 14 (77.8%) cases presented no obvious leakage in FFA and no choriocapillaris hyperfluorescence in ICGA. Four (22.2%) cases with persistent fluid showed subtle leakage in FFA and choroidal vascular dilation as well as choriocapillaris hyperfluorescence in ICGA. Following the second session of PDT, angiography demonstrated a complete absence of active lesions in these three patients.

The baseline OCT demonstrated SRD involving the central fovea in all eyes, along with increased retinal and choroidal thickness. Thirty-one (100%) eyes presented with subretinal fibrinous exudates, while 18 (58.1%) eyes had macular involvement. Until the end of follow-up, a dry macula was achieved in all cases. In 83.8% (26 eyes) of patients, resolution began within 1 month after treatment, while the latest onset of absorption observed at 8 months post-treatment, with an average of 1.6 months. The earliest complete resolution occurred at 1 month after treatment, while the latest was at 12 months, with a mean time of 4.2 months. The subretinal fibrinous exudates were completely resolved in all affected eyes, with the earliest resolution observed at 1-month post-treatment and the latest at 13 months, with an average resolution time of 5.9 months. The final assessment revealed a complete absence of subretinal fibrosis and scarring in 24 (77.4%) eyes. In the other seven eyes (22.6%) with baseline fibrosis and scarring, no progression from baseline was observed. Case Reports

Case 1

A 45-year-old male presented with bilateral bvCSC (Fig. 3) induced by oral corticosteroid treatment for misdiagnosed uveitis. The symptoms duration was 3 weeks. Baseline logMAR BCVA revealed 1.22 in the right eye and 0.92 in the left eye. Ocular examination showed bilateral SRD with pigmentary alterations. OCT demonstrated SRF with fibrinous exudates. FFA revealed multifocal fluorescence leakage. The patient underwent initial half-dose PDT. The right eye received five laser spots, and the left eye received six spots. The absorption of SRF commenced in both eyes at the 1-month visit. Complete absorption was achieved in the left eye by the sixth month. The left eye responded successfully to a single treatment session, while the right eye required a second treatment. At the 3-month follow-up, simultaneous angiography showed active lesions temporal to the macula and peri-papillary in the right eye. A second PDT session was performed on the right eye targeting these areas with two laser spots. In the right eye, the SRF completely absorbed by the ninth month. The left eye demonstrated logMAR BCVA values of 0.92 at 3 months, 0.52 at 6 months, and 0.40 at 12 months post-treatment, maintaining stable at this level throughout the rest of the follow-up period. Case 2

This is a 44-year-old male patient who developed bvCSC in both eyes following intravenous + oral corticosteroid treatment for a misdiagnosis of uveitis (Fig. 4). He presented to our hospital 9 months after the initial onset of symptoms. One month prior to this presentation, he had undergone bilateral pars plana vitrectomy with intraocular silicone oil tamponade. His baseline logMAR BCVA was 3.0 in the right eye and 2.0 in the left eye. Clinical examination revealed the vitreous silicone oil tamponade in both eyes, accompanied by SRD and extensive subretinal proliferative changes. OCT confirmed the presence of SRF and subretinal fibrous strands in both eyes, with a localized PED noted temporal to the macula in the right eye. FFA demonstrated diffuse fluorescence leakage in both eyes. Considering the poor baseline conditions in the right eye, the patient decided to treat the left eye first as an initial trial. The left eye was successful treated with half-dose PDT, using four laser spots in a single session. The FFA at 3 months showed no significant active leakage. Anatomically, the SRF in the left eye began to resolve at 1-month post-treatment and was completely absorbed by 3 months. At the 36-month, there was no increase in subretinal fibrosis. The logMAR BCVA in the left eye showed a slight improvement to 1.7 by the 12-month visit, maintaining to the 36-month follow-up. Given the limited visual potential, the patient elected to forgo PDT treatment in the right eye. Discussion

A study comparing risk factors between bvCSC and chronic CSC found that the bullous variant had a higher rate of glucocorticoid exposure and possessed potential links with renal disorders and autoimmune diseases [13, 14]. Additionally, bvCSC exhibited distinct multimodal imaging features compared to CSC, and these clinical disparities indicated that the pathogenic mechanisms underlying the bullous variant may not be entirely congruent with those of CSC. A proposal has been spurred that bullous variant may represent a distinct entity from typical CSC. Consequently, whether the successful treatment paradigm of PDT for chronic CSC can be directly extrapolated to bvCSC cases remains uncertain.

PDT acts through the induction of endothelial death and vascular remodeling in the choroid circulation. However, due to the risk of choroidal ischemia and subsequent complications with standard-dose PDT, reduced-dose/fluence protocols are favored in CSC for their superior safety profile [12]. Our therapeutic protocol for bvCSC is adopted from the half-dose PDT regimen used for chronic CSC. This approach centers on three key modifications. First, half-dose verteporfin is administered. Second, the infusion time of verteporfin is shortened, and the laser application is initiated earlier. This ensures peak choriocapillaris drug concentration while maintaining relatively lower levels in the outer retina and RPE, reducing collateral damage to adjacent structures [15]. Third, simultaneous angiography and OCT were utilized to precisely identify all active leakage sites, preventing suboptimal outcomes due to missed lesions [11]. In clinical practice, we have observed that shortening the infusion time and initiating laser earlier facilitates comprehensive treatment of all lesions within a limited therapeutic window. This advantage was particularly evident in patients with bvCSC, who often present with bilateral involvement and extensive lesion areas.

BvCSC follows a protracted natural course with a tendency toward spontaneous regression but a high recurrence rates [3]. Sahu et al. reported that in nine untreated eyes with bvCSC, serous retinal detachment completely resolved at a median of 14 weeks (3.5 months) with no recurrence over 13.5 months. Longer-term studies, showed that although SRF spontaneously resolved within months in 30.4% of eyes, nearly 52% of patients experienced 1–5 recurrences between 7 months and 9 years post-onset. Therefore, for cases lacking spontaneous resolution, timely intervention to interrupt the disease course, reduce recurrence, and maximize visual preservation is critical. Laser photocoagulation, as a primary early-stage treatment modality, could directly promote the resolution of SRF, while it showed no significant improvement in recurrence rates or visual acuity [4]. In other cohorts involving multiple treatment modalities, visual acuity typically showed minimal change before and after intervention [1, 16]. Balaratnasingam et al., following 13 patients with bvCSC over a mean of 2.8 years despite utilizing multiple interventions, including laser photocoagulation, PDT, anti-VEGF injections, vitrectomy, and oral eplerenone, found no significant difference between final and baseline visual acuity [1]. Kang et al. documented logMAR BCVA improvement from 0.61 ± 0.5 to 0.42 ± 0.6 in 44 patients with bvCSC over 45 months with heterogeneous treatments, though statistical validation was absent [14]. Studies exclusively employing PDT in bvCSC cohorts remain scarce. Gao et al. reported six patients with bvCSC treated by half-dose PDT with a follow-up for six months and they reported SRF resolution and visual improvement from 3 months in all patients [17]. In our study, all included eyes lacked spontaneous resolution pretreatment and achieved a dry macula post‑treatment. SRF absorption began at 1.6 months and was completed by 4.2 months. BCVA significantly improved from 6 months and stabilized at 24 months. No recurrence witnessed during the follow-up.

BvCSC exhibits hyperreflective SRF with fibrin accumulation. In some cases, this fibrin deposition can subsequently stimulate fibroblast proliferation and vascular ingrowth, leading to subretinal fibrosis and compromised retinal structural integrity [18]. In our study, baseline examinations confirmed subretinal fibrinous exudates in all patients with bvCSC, with 11 cases involving the macular region—a key determinant of visual prognosis. Following treatment, complete resolution of fibrinous exudates occurred at a mean of 5.9 months. Notably, seven eyes with pre-existing subretinal fibrosis showed no progression—an outcome superior to conventional laser monotherapy, indicating our protocol's efficacy in controlling fibrin-mediated pathology.

Our case series included a silicone oil tamponade eye with bvCSC that demonstrated persistent leakage despite previous vitrectomy. Following half-dose PDT, complete SRF resolution was achieved in 3 months without fibrosis progression, accompanied by significant visual improvement. Notomi et al. also reported a similar case of bvCSC that was managed through pars plana vitrectomy with subretinal membrane removal and silicone oil tamponade, followed by standard PDT [19]. Silicone oil removal was performed 2.5 months post-PDT, and no recurrence was observed during the 20-month follow-up period. The mechanistic basis for PDT efficacy in silicone oil-filled eyes remains twofold: first, the comparable refractive indices between silicone oil and vitreous allow efficient 689-nm laser transmission with minimal scattering differences; Second, verteporfin primarily targets the choroidal circulation, whose pharmacokinetics remain nearly unaffected by silicone oil filling. Our experience confirmed half-dose verteporfin achieves effective therapeutic outcomes in silicone oil tamponade bvCSC eyes while mitigating choroidal ischemia risks.

Half-dose PDT, while generally safe for conventional bvCSC, carries ischemic risks in anatomically vulnerable eyes. In case 3, complete SRF resolution was achieved in the 1-month post-treatment, while CNV developed at 10 months. CNV, a common indolent complication of pachychoroid disease, arises from choriocapillaris hypoperfusion, hypoxia, and secondary RPE damage. Retrospective analysis identified pre-existing high-risk anatomical features—structural abnormalities of the RPE-Bruch's membrane complex underlying the macular detachment, accompanied by focal depression, corresponding choriocapillaris dropout, and dilated surrounding large choroidal vessels (pachyvessels). We hypothesized that the photochemical effects may have exacerbated local choroidal circulatory impairment, ultimately triggering CNV. We previously reported two cases of CNV following half-dose PDT for typical CSC, both featuring focal choroidal excavation (FCE) [20]. Although FCE typically remains asymptomatic and stable, CNV represents its most common complication [21], primarily attributable to the inherent RPE-Bruch's membrane complex abnormalities and underlying choroidal ischemia—anatomical risk profiles highly consistent with our current case. While half-dose PDT might remain a safe and effective option for conventional bvCSC management, patients presenting with high-risk anatomical features require careful risk–benefit assessment.

This study is a retrospective case series analysis. Although the follow‑up period was relatively long, the primary data available for statistical analysis were largely confined to the first 36 months after treatment. Furthermore, the lack of a control group made it difficult to directly compare the efficacy of PDT with other interventions, thereby limiting the assessment of optimal treatment strategies. The small sample size represents another limitation, as it precludes meaningful subgroup analyses. In addition, the included patients exhibited considerable heterogeneity: 72.2% had a history of glucocorticoid use prior to treatment, and one patient had undergone vitrectomy combined with silicone oil tamponade. Compared with idiopathic bvCSC, these factors may introduce confounding bias into the observed outcomes. Based on current clinical observations and practical experience, future studies should focus on designing more rigorous clinical trials. There is an urgent need for long‑term, controlled, large‑scale, multicenter clinical studies to deepen the understanding of bvCSC pathogenesis and clinical treatment strategies.

Conclusions

In the absence of a defined first-line therapy for bvCSC, half-dose PDT may prove to be a consideration, with data hinting at modest efficacy in halting disease progression, decreasing recurrence, and enhancing visual outcomes. However, cautious risk–benefit assessments remained essential for patients with high-risk ischemic anatomical features.

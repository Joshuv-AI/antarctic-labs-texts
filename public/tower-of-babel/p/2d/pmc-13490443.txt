Digital orientation enables disruptive technological innovation in agricultural technology enterprises
Authors: Yunqi Chen, Liqing Zhou, Yichu Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAgricultural technology enterprises face growing pressure to achieve technological breakthroughs in response to the challenges of food security, climate change and digital transformation. However, how these enterprises can strategically drive disruptive technological innovation remains underexplored. Drawing on dynamic capabilities theory, this study addresses this research gap by examining how digital orientation shapes disruptive technological innovation outcomes at the technological paradigm level through the mediating mechanisms of sensing, seizing and reconfiguring capabilities, and how these relationships are conditioned by organizational unlearning and resource orchestration. Based on survey data from 422 agricultural technology enterprises in China, structural equation modeling and bootstrap testing were employed to empirically validate the proposed framework. The results demonstrate that digital orientation significantly enhances disruptive technological innovation both directly and indirectly via dynamic capabilities. Furthermore, organizational unlearning strengthens the effect of digital orientation on dynamic capabilities, while resource orchestration amplifies the impact of dynamic capabilities on innovation outcomes. By clarifying the internal mechanisms and boundary conditions of digital orientation in agriculture, this study extends the application of dynamic capabilities theory to a sector characterized by long innovation cycles and high resource uncertainty. The findings also provide actionable insights for managers and policymakers aiming to foster technological self-reliance and high-impact innovation in agriculture.Keywords: Digital orientation, Dynamic capabilities, Organizational unlearning, Resource orchestration, Disruptive technological innovationSubject terms: Business and management; Business and management; Environmental social sciences; Information systems and information technology; Science, technology and society

Introduction

Agricultural technology enterprises are facing increasingly severe challenges related to food security, climate change, and digital transformation. These pressures intensify the demand for technological breakthroughs and render disruptive technological innovation a critical pathway for enhancing competitiveness and long-term resilience1,2. Disruptive technological innovation refers to paradigm-level innovation that fundamentally reshapes existing production systems, technological trajectories, and industry value chains by introducing new scientific principles or cross-domain technologies that undermine incumbent solutions3,4. In agriculture, such innovation is often rooted in breakthroughs in biological processes and production methods rather than incremental efficiency improvements5,6. Developments in synthetic biology, cellular agriculture, controlled-environment farming, vertical agriculture, and gene editing technologies illustrate how emerging technologies can reconfigure the spatial, temporal, and scientific foundations of agricultural production2,7,8. These transformations reflect systemic restructuring of established agricultural paradigms and embody the core characteristics of disruptive technological innovation.

Against this backdrop, research on digital orientation has increasingly emphasized its role as a strategic posture shaping firms’ innovation priorities and long-term technological trajectories. Digital orientation captures the extent to which firms proactively recognize the strategic value of digital technologies, commit resources to digital initiatives, and embed digital thinking into organizational decision-making processes9,10. Unlike digital transformation, which typically refers to organizational processes or outcomes associated with technology adoption, digital orientation reflects a firm’s strategic intent to leverage digital technologies as a driver of innovation and renewal. Existing studies suggest that digital orientation can support innovation by directing managerial attention toward emerging technologies, facilitating organizational learning, and strengthening capability development under uncertainty11–13. However, empirical research has predominantly focused on incremental innovation, operational efficiency, or general technological upgrading, largely in manufacturing, digital-native enterprises or small and medium-sized firms14. Consequently, the role of digital orientation in fostering disruptive technological innovation, understood as paradigm-level technological change rather than the adoption of digital tools alone, remains insufficiently theorized.

This theoretical gap is particularly salient in agricultural contexts. Although prior studies indicate that digital strategies may stimulate exploratory innovation in agriculture7, the internal mechanisms through which digital orientation translates into disruptive technological innovation in sectors characterized by biological uncertainty, rigid resource structures, and fragmented value chains have received limited systematic attention. Moreover, treating digital technology adoption as equivalent to innovation outcomes risks obscuring the conceptual distinction between digital orientation and disruptive technological innovation. Taken together, these limitations point to the need to refine the theoretical logic linking digital orientation and disruptive technological innovation, to clarify the internal mechanisms through which this relationship operates, and to identify the contextual conditions under which it holds in agricultural technology enterprises.

Agricultural technology enterprises therefore provide a particularly appropriate empirical setting for addressing these issues. Technological upgrading in agriculture depends not only on the operational adoption of digital tools, but also on the coordinated transformation of biological, ecological, and supply chain systems1. These interdependencies generate high uncertainty and impose complex pathway constraints on disruptive technological innovation. When introducing new technologies, agricultural technology enterprises must simultaneously adjust production systems, redefine breeding or genetic improvement routes, and reallocate resources across fragmented value chains5. Under such conditions, strategic orientations such as digital orientation are unlikely to translate directly into disruptive technological innovation outcomes. Instead, firms must continuously sense emerging technological opportunities, commit and mobilize critical resources, and reconfigure organizational and production systems to overcome path dependence and structural rigidity. This indicates that the relationship between digital orientation and disruptive technological innovation is inherently capability-driven rather than automatic, and that its explanation requires a theoretical lens capable of capturing dynamic processes of opportunity recognition, resource reallocation, and systemic reconfiguration. From this perspective, dynamic capabilities theory provides a suitable foundation for examining how digital orientation is transformed into disruptive technological innovation in agricultural contexts.

Building on this capability-based perspective, this study develops an integrated analytical framework grounded in dynamic capabilities theory. Digital orientation is conceptualized as a strategic antecedent that enables firms to identify technological opportunities, integrate critical resources, and reshape organizational processes11. The framework further incorporates organizational unlearning and resource orchestration as contextual moderators to clarify the conditions under which capability renewal contributes to disruptive technological innovation. Using survey data from a large sample of agricultural technology enterprises in China, this study empirically examines the mechanisms linking digital orientation to disruptive technological innovation and identifies key boundary conditions.

This study makes three main contributions. First, it reconceptualizes digital orientation as a strategic driver of disruptive technological innovation rather than as a general indicator of digitalization or technology adoption. By situating digital orientation within a dynamic capabilities framework, the study explains how agricultural technology enterprises can initiate paradigm-level technological change under conditions of biological uncertainty and structural constraints. Second, the study advances dynamic capabilities theory by unpacking how sensing, seizing, and reconfiguring capabilities jointly mediate the relationship between digital orientation and disruptive technological innovation. Rather than treating dynamic capabilities as a monolithic construct, the findings demonstrate that paradigm-level technological change emerges only when digital orientation is translated into coordinated opportunity recognition, strategic resource commitment, and systemic reconfiguration. Third, the study identifies organizational unlearning and resource orchestration as critical boundary conditions that shape the effectiveness of dynamic capabilities in generating disruptive technological innovation. By distinguishing between capability formation and capability deployment processes, the study reveals how cognitive flexibility and resource coordination jointly determine whether dynamic capabilities can be translated into paradigm-level technological change under conditions of high uncertainty.

Theoretical foundation

This study is grounded in dynamic capabilities theory, which explains how firms adapt to rapidly changing environments by continually identifying external opportunities, mobilizing critical resources, and reconfiguring organizational systems to achieve technological advancement and strategic renewal15–17. Dynamic capabilities are considered higher-order capabilities that enable organizations to integrate, build and reconfigure internal and external resources, thereby supporting innovation under environmental uncertainty18. For agricultural technology enterprises, disruptive technological innovation requires not only incremental improvements within existing technological trajectories, but also fundamental transformations in underlying technological principles, resource allocation logics and value creation systems. These characteristics are well aligned with the dynamic capabilities perspective, which emphasizes systemic reconfiguration and path-breaking change3,19.

Within this theoretical perspective, digital orientation is conceptualized as a strategic posture through which firms strengthen digital awareness, allocate resources to digital initiatives, and integrate digital technologies into organizational processes9,13. This orientation not only enhances firms’ sensitivity to digital technologies, but also encourages digital thinking that helps reinterpret technological boundaries and identify emerging opportunities that extend beyond existing paradigms12. From the viewpoint of dynamic capabilities theory, digital orientation supports the development of three core capabilities essential for disruptive technological innovation. First, it strengthens sensing capabilities by directing firms’ attention to emerging scientific principles, cross-domain knowledge, and potential technological inflection points rather than limiting attention to the application of digital tools. This enables firms to identify opportunities with the potential to reshape technological trajectories. Second, digital orientation enhances seizing capabilities by encouraging firms to commit resources to uncertain but high-potential technologies, thereby providing the resource foundation required for disruptive technological innovation. Third, it promotes reconfiguring capabilities by facilitating adjustments to organizational structures, knowledge systems, and production processes, which reduces path dependence and creates the conditions needed to build new technological systems. Together, these capabilities allow digital orientation to evolve from a strategic intent into a mechanism that drives technological trajectory shifts and deeper system-level change.

Agricultural technology enterprises often face strong path dependence due to high biological uncertainty, rigid resource structures, and institutional inertia. Organizational unlearning therefore plays a particularly important role in this context. By reducing the constraints imposed by outdated routines and established patterns, organizational unlearning increases cognitive, structural, and procedural flexibility, creating the conditions needed for new dynamic capabilities to emerge20,21. In this sense, organizational unlearning strengthens the effect of digital orientation on the development of dynamic capabilities.

At the same time, the extent to which dynamic capabilities can be transformed into disruptive technological innovation depends on whether firms can effectively manage and deploy their renewed resource base. Resource orchestration refers to the managerial processes through which resources are structured, combined, and allocated. It ensures that newly reconfigured resources are directed toward technological pathways with disruptive potential, thereby supporting the realization of disruptive technological innovation outcomes22,23.

Based on this theoretical reasoning, the study develops an integrative theoretical framework and proposes a set of hypothesized relationships, as illustrated in Fig 1. Digital orientation is conceptualized as a strategic antecedent that fosters the development of dynamic capabilities. Dynamic capabilities, modeled as a second-order construct consisting of sensing, seizing, and reconfiguring capabilities, are expected to drive disruptive technological innovation in agricultural technology enterprises. Furthermore, organizational unlearning is proposed to strengthen the effect of digital orientation on dynamic capabilities, while resource orchestration is expected to enhance the influence of dynamic capabilities on disruptive technological innovation. Literature review and hypothesis development

Literature review

Disruptive technological innovation

Disruptive technological innovation is generally defined as an innovation that breaks existing technological trajectories, alters industry operating logics, and reshapes value creation processes. Its defining feature lies in shifting technological paradigms rather than improving existing technologies incrementally3. Prior studies suggest that disruptive technological innovation is typically accompanied by the reconfiguration of knowledge systems, transformation of production structures and restructuring of resource allocation mechanisms, exerting profound influence on firms and industries24,4.

Related literature has employed various terms, such as disruptive innovation or breakthrough innovation, to describe innovation that departs from established trajectories. However, these terms are often used in a broad sense and may encompass heterogeneous phenomena, including market displacement, business model change or general technological novelty. In contrast, research focusing on disruptive technological innovation places primary emphasis on paradigm-level technological change, core knowledge replacement, and the systemic reconfiguration of production and technological systems. This perspective highlights the technological foundations of disruption and distinguishes disruptive technological innovation from more generalized or outcome-oriented conceptions of innovation.

In the context of agricultural technology enterprises, disruptive technological innovation tends to exhibit greater complexity due to the influence of biological uncertainty, environmental fluctuations, and fragmented value chains. Technological breakthroughs frequently require coordinated changes across breeding mechanisms, production systems, and resource utilization structures8,19. Existing research suggests that identifying new scientific principles, integrating cross-domain knowledge, and constructing novel technological pathways are essential for achieving disruptive technological innovation in agriculture6,25. Compared with industries where production systems are more stable, disruptive technological innovation in agriculture relies more heavily on the continuous renewal of organizational capabilities and the reconfiguration of resource systems, and it is characterized by higher uncertainty and stronger path constraints.

Digital orientation

Digital orientation is commonly understood as a strategic posture in which firms actively embrace digital technologies through enhanced digital awareness, greater resource commitment and the integration of digital practices into organizational processes9,11. This strategic orientation reflects a forward-looking mindset that guides firms in responding to technological change. It strengthens digital sensitivity, broadens technological investment and promotes digital integration across organizational activities, thereby creating favorable conditions for subsequent innovation. Prior studies indicate that digital orientation improves firms’ ability to identify new knowledge, acquire external technological information and expand exploratory learning, which enhances organizational adaptability and innovation potential in turbulent environments12,10.

Within the innovation literature, digital orientation is considered a catalyst for knowledge acquisition, interorganizational collaboration and structural flexibility, and it has been associated with higher innovation performance, open innovation activities and organizational transformation26. However, most existing studies have focused on digital tool adoption and process improvements27,13. Much less attention has been given to how digital orientation shapes more fundamental changes in technological trajectories. In particular, its role in enabling disruptive technological innovation remains theoretically underexplored. Further research is therefore needed to clarify how digital orientation contributes to deeper forms of technological change beyond incremental enhancement, and to understand the mechanisms that link digital strategic posture with disruptive technological innovation outcomes.

Dynamic capabilities

Dynamic capabilities are commonly understood as higher-order organizational abilities that enable firms to integrate, build and reconfigure resources in response to environmental turbulence and technological change17. Prior research generally conceptualizes dynamic capabilities as comprising three core activities, which include sensing, seizing and reconfiguring16,28. Sensing refers to a firm’s ability to identify emerging technological trends, market shifts and new opportunities18. Seizing reflects the firm’s capacity to evaluate these opportunities and commit resources to promising technological trajectories29,30. Reconfiguring involves adjusting organizational structures, processes and knowledge systems to support new technological paths and modes of value creation18. Empirical evidence suggests that dynamic capabilities play a critical role in facilitating technological upgrading and strategic renewal, especially in industries facing high uncertainty or frequent external shocks19,15. They provide the foundational mechanisms through which firms can overcome path dependence, incorporate new knowledge and realign resource bases, thereby enabling more fundamental forms of innovation.

Organizational unlearning

Organizational unlearning is generally understood as the process through which firms intentionally or unintentionally weaken or discard outdated knowledge, entrenched routines and rigid cognitive frames, thereby enhancing their adaptability in the face of technological change21. Prior studies indicate that unlearning reduces path dependence and cognitive lock-in, lessens the constraints imposed by obsolete procedures, and creates room for firms to absorb new knowledge, update technological logics and adjust resource structures31. Within the innovation literature, organizational unlearning is regarded as an important condition for fostering exploratory learning and improving a firm’s capacity for technological renewal, enabling greater strategic flexibility under uncertain environments20.

Resource orchestration

Resource orchestration refers to the managerial processes through which firms acquire, integrate and deploy resources to support strategic and innovation-oriented activities22. Prior research indicates that resource orchestration involves more than simple resource allocation. It requires managers to continually adjust the configuration of resource portfolios to ensure alignment with evolving technological and market conditions32. In innovation contexts, effective resource orchestration improves the efficiency of resource utilization, enhances the complementarity among capabilities and enables firms to channel newly integrated knowledge or technologies into initiatives with strategic potential23. While its role has been widely discussed in product innovation and open innovation studies, research on how resource orchestration shapes the realization of disruptive technological innovation remains limited. This gap is particularly salient in industries characterized by high uncertainty and strong requirements for cross-domain knowledge integration, where the effectiveness of capability renewal may depend heavily on managerial resource deployment decisions.

Hypothesis development

Digital orientation and disruptive technological innovation in agricultural technology enterprises

Digital orientation provides a foundational strategic posture that enables agricultural technology enterprises to identify emerging technological opportunities and thereby enhances their capacity to pursue disruptive technological innovation. A strong digital orientation heightens firms’ sensitivity to external technological shifts, making it easier for them to detect potential breakthrough points that arise from advances in biological sciences, engineering innovation or cross-domain digital technologies9,11,12. In agricultural contexts, technological change often results from interdependent system-level dynamics, which places higher demands on firms’ anticipatory sensing and interpretive capabilities26. Firms with stronger digital orientation are therefore better equipped to recognize emerging technological trajectories and to identify their strategic implications, establishing an essential cognitive basis for disruptive technological innovation.

Digital orientation also promotes resource commitment and long-term strategic investment, both of which are critical for overcoming existing technological trajectories. Because disruptive technological innovation typically involves high uncertainty and substantial risk, firms must commit resources early to nascent technological systems and support their development even before their commercial value becomes certain9,33. A strong digital orientation reinforces firms’ willingness to invest in transformative technologies and reallocate resources away from entrenched production logics, thereby increasing the likelihood that they will pursue and realize disruptive technological breakthroughs.

Furthermore, digital orientation encourages firms to adjust organizational structures, processes and knowledge systems in ways that enable technological paradigm shifts. Agricultural technology enterprises often face strong path dependence and resource rigidity2. Digital orientation fosters organizational flexibility by supporting structural adaptation, cross-departmental integration, and renewed knowledge frameworks, thus creating favorable conditions for the introduction, assimilation, and diffusion of disruptive technologies. This organizational adaptability allows firms to convert digital strategic intent into substantive changes in technological paradigms. The following hypothesis is proposed:

H1: Digital orientation positively influences disruptive technological innovation in agricultural technology enterprises.

Digital orientation and dynamic capabilities

Digital orientation provides enterprises with a directional foundation for building dynamic capabilities, primarily by enhancing their sensitivity to external technological opportunities. Firms with a strong digital orientation tend to pay closer attention to frontier technological developments and cross-domain knowledge applications, allowing them to more proactively monitor emerging technological inflection points34,35. When organizations maintain an open strategic posture toward digital technologies, their scope of information search naturally broadens13, thereby improving their ability to detect new knowledge, emerging trends and potential avenues for technological breakthroughs. This heightened technological sensitivity strengthens the sensing component of dynamic capabilities, which is particularly critical in agricultural contexts characterized by high uncertainty.

Digital orientation also facilitates the development of seizing capabilities by encouraging firms to allocate resources to digital and innovation-related initiatives. Enterprises with a strong digital orientation are more inclined to invest in early-stage exploration of high-potential but uncertain technological trajectories14,36. Such firms demonstrate greater flexibility and foresight in resource allocation, supporting experimentation, opportunity evaluation and strategic commitment to new technologies10. As a result, digital orientation not only guides firms in recognizing emerging opportunities but also enables them to mobilize the necessary resources to act on those opportunities.

Furthermore, digital orientation promotes the development of reconfiguring capabilities by fostering digital integration across organizational structures, processes and knowledge systems. When firms adopt a digital mindset to reassess production logics and organizational routines, rigid internal patterns are weakened, enabling smoother structural adjustments, process optimization and knowledge recombination16,34. For agricultural technology enterprises that often face strong path dependence, such reconfiguring capabilities are essential for restructuring production systems and supporting new technological trajectories12. Accordingly, digital orientation strengthens firms’ capacity to adapt organizational systems during technological transitions. The study proposes the following hypotheses:

Dynamic capabilities and disruptive technological innovation

Dynamic capabilities provide the core mechanisms through which agricultural technology enterprises can break existing technological trajectories and pursue disruptive technological innovation. Because agricultural innovation depends on biological processes, ecological variability and multi-stage system coordination, disruptive change requires an unusually high degree of adaptive capability development37. Dynamic capabilities therefore play a central role in enabling firms to reorient technological pathways under conditions of uncertainty and systemic constraint.

Sensing capabilities help firms identify emerging scientific principles, cross-domain knowledge combinations, and technological inflection points that may lead to disruptive change. In agricultural contexts, where breakthroughs often arise from shifts in biological science or production-system logic, timely recognition of such signals is essential for discovering alternative technological trajectories38,39. Firms with stronger sensing capabilities are thus more likely to detect early-stage opportunities that can evolve into disruptive technological innovations.

Seizing capabilities determine whether identified opportunities are translated into strategic action. Since disruptive technological innovation involves high uncertainty and long development horizons, firms must be willing to commit resources, shape technological direction and invest in capability building before technologies mature15. Enterprises with strong seizing capabilities are better positioned to mobilize resources at critical junctures, enabling them to capture emerging growth windows and establish competitive differentiation.

Reconfiguring capabilities allow firms to reshape organizational structures, processes and knowledge systems to support new technological paradigms. Disruptive technological innovation demands not only opportunity recognition and resource commitment, but also the organizational flexibility to dismantle entrenched routines and reconstruct production systems aligned with new technological logics30. For agricultural technology enterprises characterized by strong path dependence and institutional rigidity, reconfiguring capabilities are especially important for achieving system-level transformation. The mediating role of dynamic capabilities

Dynamic capabilities mediate the relationship between digital orientation and disruptive technological innovation by transforming strategic intent into concrete technological outcomes. Digital orientation enhances firms’ awareness of technological change and strengthens their willingness to invest in emerging technologies, but these strategic tendencies alone cannot generate paradigm-shifting innovation. To move from digital intent to technological breakthrough, firms must develop the capabilities needed to identify opportunities, evaluate alternative technological pathways, and restructure organizational systems in response to new scientific principles.

The mediating role of dynamic capabilities operates through three mechanisms. First, sensing capabilities enable firms to extract meaningful signals from expanding digital information flows and recognize emerging opportunities that may reshape technological trajectories40. Digital orientation broadens firms’ technological search scope, and sensing capabilities convert this broadened attention into actionable insights about disruptive potential. Second, seizing capabilities translate strategic openness toward digital technologies into concrete investment decisions. They guide firms in evaluating uncertain opportunities, prioritizing resource allocation and committing to promising but high-risk technological paths41. Third, reconfiguring capabilities allow firms to overcome path dependence by adjusting organizational structures, knowledge bases and production processes to align with new technological logics28. Through such structural adaptation, the ideas and intentions fostered by digital orientation can be realized as systemic technological transformation.

These mechanisms are especially important in agricultural technology enterprises, where innovation requires coordinated changes across biological, ecological and production systems. In such contexts, digital tools alone do not automatically produce breakthrough outcomes. Rather, firms must rely on dynamic capabilities to reinterpret scientific principles, integrate cross-domain knowledge and restructure resource systems. Therefore, dynamic capabilities serve as the central conduit through which digital orientation leads to disruptive technological innovation.

The moderating role of organizational unlearning

Organizational unlearning can strengthen the influence of digital orientation on the development of dynamic capabilities by reducing the constraints imposed by rigid routines, outdated knowledge structures and institutionalized decision logics. When established practices dominate organizational behavior, firms often struggle to translate digital initiatives into new insights or adaptive actions31. High levels of unlearning loosen these entrenched patterns, enabling new digital information and technology-driven thinking to be more readily absorbed. As a result, the enhancement of sensing capabilities triggered by digital orientation becomes more pronounced, because firms are better able to identify emerging technological trends and breakthrough opportunities20,36.

Organizational unlearning may also amplify the effect of digital orientation on seizing capabilities. Investing in emerging technologies requires a willingness to deviate from familiar decision routines, yet rigid cognitive frames and path-dependent reasoning often reduce managerial openness to uncertain opportunities21. When firms intentionally weaken obsolete rules, decision-makers are more receptive to digital strategic logic, more willing to allocate resources to high-potential but uncertain technological paths, and more capable of converting digital commitment into concrete investment actions.

Furthermore, organizational unlearning can reinforce the impact of digital orientation on reconfiguring capabilities. Structural and procedural change is frequently hindered by inertia embedded in organizational roles, performance systems and institutionalized processes42. Unlearning helps diminish these sources of resistance, making it easier for firms to adjust knowledge systems, redesign workflows and support the structural transformation required for new technology pathways. Under conditions of strong unlearning, digital orientation is therefore more likely to stimulate organization-level reconfiguration that aligns with technological paradigm shifts. The moderating role of resource orchestration

Resource orchestration is widely regarded as a critical managerial mechanism that enables firms to convert renewed capabilities into substantive innovation outcomes23. While dynamic capabilities provide the knowledge base, resource structure and organizational processes required for technological transformation32, their value can only be realized when resources are effectively combined, integrated and deployed. Without sufficient resource orchestration, the sensing, seizing and reconfiguring capabilities developed by firms may remain latent and fail to support technological breakthroughs under conditions of high uncertainty. Thus, stronger resource orchestration enhances a firm’s ability to translate dynamic capabilities into concrete actions targeting disruptive technological innovation.

The moderating effect of resource orchestration is particularly salient in agricultural technology enterprises. These firms operate under conditions of interdisciplinary knowledge requirements, uncertainty in biological processes and fragmented value chains43. Managers must be able to identify critical resources, optimize resource allocation and adjust resource configurations dynamically to channel renewed capabilities toward technologically promising pathways44. High levels of resource orchestration not only improve the quality of opportunities identified through sensing capabilities, but also increase the efficiency with which seizing capabilities support resource investment in emerging technologies. Moreover, resource orchestration strengthens the execution of reconfiguring capabilities by facilitating system adjustments and organizational restructuring. Accordingly, dynamic capabilities exert stronger influence on disruptive technological innovation when firms possess more effective resource orchestration. The following hypotheses are proposed:

Research design

Sample selection and data collection

This study focuses on agricultural technology enterprises in China as the research subjects, employing a questionnaire survey to explore the impact of digital orientation and dynamic capabilities on disruptive technological innovation. The survey was conducted between January and April 2024, ensuring that the data reflect the latest trends in agricultural digital transformation.

The sample was drawn from agricultural technology enterprises in Shanxi, Shandong, Liaoning, and Jiangsu. These provinces were selected for three reasons. First, they represent different levels of digitalization: Jiangsu and Shandong are at the forefront of agricultural digital transformation with advanced infrastructures and strong policy support for smart agriculture, whereas Shanxi and Liaoning are in transitional stages, providing a contrast in digital maturity. Second, they feature diverse industrial foundations: Shanxi is characterized by resource-based restructuring, Shandong and Jiangsu are leading in seed technology and agricultural biotechnology, and Liaoning has strengths in agricultural machinery and ecological agriculture. This variation provides a comprehensive view of how agricultural technology enterprises innovate under heterogeneous conditions. Third, all four provinces have been included in national and provincial policies supporting agricultural modernization and digital transformation, making them representative cases for the study.

Questionnaires were distributed through three channels: first, paper questionnaires were given to MBA students employed as managers in agricultural technology enterprises at the authors’ university and affiliated institutions and collected on-site; second, electronic questionnaires were sent via social networks and email to managers in agricultural technology enterprises; and third, additional paper questionnaires were distributed and collected during field visits in the four provinces. A total of 600 questionnaires were distributed, and 473 were returned. After excluding incomplete and logically inconsistent responses, 422 valid questionnaires were retained, yielding a response rate of 70.3%.

Descriptive statistics indicate that the sample covers a wide range of subsectors, including seed breeding (13%), agricultural machinery (37%), precision agriculture (9%), and agri-biotechnology (20%). In terms of regional distribution, approximately 23% of the sample comes from Jiangsu, 21% from Shandong, 34% from Shanxi, and 22% from Liaoning, ensuring balanced representation across the selected provinces. This study employed an anonymous questionnaire survey targeting organizational respondents and did not involve any experiments on humans or the use of human tissue samples. All procedures were conducted in accordance with relevant ethical guidelines and regulations, and the study adhered to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. The research protocol was reviewed by the Research Ethics Committee of the Business School at Shanxi University of Finance and Economics and was deemed exempt from requiring formal ethical approval, as no personally identifiable information was collected. Informed consent was obtained from all participants. The questionnaire clearly stated the purpose of the study, emphasized voluntary participation, and assured respondents of the anonymity and confidentiality of their responses.

Variable measurement

The survey questionnaire consisted of two main sections: basic information and measurement scales. The basic information section included variables such as years of establishment, firm size, ownership, industry field and employee positions, which were used as control variables in the research model. All measurement items were rated on a 7-point Likert scale, where 1 represents ‘completely disagree’ and 7 represents ‘completely agree.’

To ensure the reliability and validity of the questionnaire, the following procedures were adopted. First, measurement items were adapted from established and widely recognized scales in prior studies. For constructs originally developed in English, a translation and back-translation procedure were conducted by two bilingual experts to ensure semantic equivalence and accuracy. Second, all original items were systematically reviewed for conceptual relevance to agricultural technology enterprises, where innovation is shaped by biological uncertainty, ecological constraints, and long technological cycles rather than standardized manufacturing routines. Items referring to mass production efficiency or stable manufacturing processes were revised to reflect agricultural innovation activities, including breeding processes, biological experimentation and smart agriculture applications. Items that could not be equivalently adapted without altering their theoretical meaning were removed to preserve construct validity. Third, a pilot survey was conducted with 50 managers from agricultural technology enterprises. Based on item analysis and feedback, the wording and structure of the questionnaire were refined, resulting in the final version used for large-scale distribution.

For digital orientation, the scale was adapted from Arias and Vélez27, Escoz and Becker11 and Xie et al.10, and included five items such as ‘The enterprise has a clear understanding of how emerging digital technologies enhance business value.’

For dynamic capabilities, drawing on Khan et al.16, dynamic capabilities were divided into three dimensions: sensing, seizing, and reconfiguring. Scales from Cavusgil et al.30 and Volz et al.28 were used. Although these scales were originally developed in manufacturing contexts, their emphasis on opportunity recognition, strategic decision-making, and organizational reconfiguration remains conceptually applicable to agricultural technology enterprises. Item wording was therefore adapted to reflect agricultural innovation activities, such as biological R&D, agri-biotechnology development, and smart agriculture applications, rather than standardized manufacturing operations. Sensing capability was measured with three items (e.g., ‘The enterprise frequently examines the environment surrounding new technologies’). Seizing capability was measured with four items (e.g., ‘When the enterprise identifies opportunities or threats, it can assess and select potential solutions’). Reconfiguring capability was measured with four items (e.g., ‘The enterprise adjusts innovation processes and R&D activities based on new market demands’).

For disruptive technological innovation, the measurement items were adapted from Buhalis et al.24, Jiang and Xu25. The scale was carefully translated and validated to ensure its applicability in the agricultural sector, where disruptive technological innovation often involves the replacement of established technological paradigms and knowledge systems through the introduction of emerging technologies, rather than incremental improvement. Six items were included, such as ‘In the process of innovation, the enterprise frequently incorporates new knowledge and technologies, replacing outdated knowledge systems and processes.’ The adoption of this scale is justified because agricultural technology enterprises are facing disruptive changes in areas such as biotechnology, precision farming, and digital platforms, which reflect paradigm-level technological transformation consistent with the construct measured in this study.

For organizational unlearning, the scale was adapted from Klammer et al.20 and Dayan et al.31, including five items such as ‘The enterprise continuously adjusts old routines and processes to adapt to new changes.’

For resource orchestration, the scale was based on Jin et al.32 and23, covering resource structuring, bundling and leveraging. Six items were designed, including ‘The enterprise actively optimizes or recombines certain resources to enhance innovation capability.’

Common method bias test

To minimize potential interference from common method bias (CMB) during data collection, several procedural remedies were adopted, including anonymity, randomization of items, and the separation of predictor and criterion variables in the questionnaire design. In addition, statistical tests were conducted. First, Harman’s single-factor test was applied, and the results showed that the variance explained by the first principal component was 30.782%, which is below the commonly accepted threshold of 40%, indicating that CMB is unlikely to be a serious concern. Second, a confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) single-factor model test was performed. The results showed that the one-factor model exhibited a significantly poorer fit compared with the proposed multi-factor measurement model, further confirming that CMB is not a major issue in this study. These findings demonstrate that the variables in this study are not substantially affected by common method bias, and the data are suitable for subsequent hypothesis testing.

Conclusion and discussion

This study responds to the persistent need for a clearer theoretical explanation of how digital orientation shapes disruptive technological innovation in agricultural technology enterprises. Although disruptive technological innovation has been widely examined in manufacturing, high-tech and service industries45, research in agricultural contexts remains fragmented. In particular, prior studies have not systematically articulated the internal mechanisms through which digital orientation affects disruptive technological innovation. By integrating digital orientation, dynamic capabilities, organizational unlearning and resource orchestration into a unified analytical framework, this study advances theory and offers practical insights. The first major finding confirms that digital orientation has a significant positive effect on disruptive technological innovation in agricultural technology enterprises. Whereas existing literature largely highlights the role of digital orientation in improving operational efficiency or supporting incremental technological upgrades9,11,46, our results show that in agriculture it functions as a strategic driver of paradigm-shifting technological innovation. Agricultural enterprises face long innovation cycles, strong biological dependencies and fragmented value chains. The empirical evidence demonstrates that digital orientation enables firms to overcome these structural constraints by embedding digital thinking into organizational decision-making and technological pathways, thereby facilitating leapfrogging technological trajectories. This repositions digital orientation from a supporting tool to a catalyst for transformative change in addressing the sector’s challenges, including climate instability and food security pressures.

Second, the study finds that dynamic capabilities play a mediating role in transmitting the effect of digital orientation to disruptive technological innovation. Prior research often treats dynamic capabilities as a single aggregated construct47. By distinguishing sensing, seizing and reconfiguring capabilities, this study shows that digital orientation leads to disruptive outcomes only when firms can detect emerging opportunities, commit strategic resources and realign organizational systems accordingly. This multidimensional mechanism highlights that digital orientation itself is insufficient for driving disruptive technological innovation,it must work through strengthened internal capabilities that support opportunity identification, investment and systemic restructuring.

Third, the moderating effect of organizational unlearning. Although earlier studies have acknowledged the role of unlearning in organizational adaptation4849,, its enabling role in digital transformation has received limited attention. The results show that firms with higher levels of organizational unlearning can more effectively discard obsolete routines, reduce cognitive rigidity and internalize digital thinking. This flexibility amplifies the influence of digital orientation on sensing, seizing and reconfiguring capabilities. For agricultural technology enterprises, unlearning is particularly relevant because outdated farming practices, legacy production methods and long-held assumptions often constrain technological progress. The ability to unlearn these routines strengthens the capacity to adopt technologies with disruptive potential, such as AI-based production optimization or blockchain-enabled traceability.

Finally, this study demonstrates the strategic importance of resource orchestration in enhancing the contribution of dynamic capabilities to disruptive technological innovation. Although previous research has recognized resource orchestration as a driver of disruptive technological innovation50–52, its contextualized role in agriculture has been underexplored. This study shows that firms with strong structuring, bundling and leveraging processes can more effectively convert sensing, seizing and reconfiguring capabilities into disruptive technological innovation outcomes. Agricultural technology enterprises frequently require cross-disciplinary knowledge integration, collaboration with research institutions and coordination across fragmented value chains. Effective resource orchestration enables firms to mobilize and align these resources, accelerating the realization of disruptive technologies. Thus, resource orchestration emerges as both a boundary condition and an essential mechanism that strengthens the pathway through which dynamic capabilities produce technology-based transformative innovation.

Theoretical implications

First, this study refines the theoretical understanding of digital orientation by extending it from an efficiency-oriented strategic posture to a driver of disruptive technological innovation. Existing research has predominantly associated digital orientation with incremental innovation, process optimization and productivity improvement, mainly in manufacturing and high-technology industries. Our findings show that, in agricultural technology enterprises, digital orientation can facilitate paradigm-level technological change by enabling firms to overcome biological uncertainty, long innovation cycles, and fragmented value chains. This extension broadens the scope of digital orientation theory and suggests that its innovation effects depend critically on industry context and innovation type.

Second, this study advances dynamic capabilities theory by clarifying how strategic orientations are translated into disruptive technological innovation through specific capability configurations. Prior literature often treats dynamic capabilities as an aggregated construct supporting general adaptability. This study demonstrates that disruptive technological innovation emerges only when digital orientation is mediated by the coordinated operation of sensing, seizing and reconfiguring capabilities. This insight refines dynamic capabilities theory by emphasizing the processual and combinatory nature of capability deployment in generating paradigm-level innovation.

Third, this study contributes to theory by identifying organizational unlearning and resource orchestration as key boundary conditions shaping the effectiveness of dynamic capabilities. While existing studies acknowledge contextual influences on innovation, they rarely specify how cognitive and resource-related constraints affect the translation of capabilities into disruptive outcomes. Our findings reveal that organizational unlearning enhances capability formation by reducing path dependence, whereas resource orchestration strengthens capability deployment by aligning resources with disruptive technological trajectories. This condition-based perspective extends dynamic capabilities theory by explaining when and why capabilities lead to disruptive technological innovation under high uncertainty.

Practical implications

First, agricultural technology enterprises should treat digital orientation as a strategic guiding force rather than merely a technical upgrade. Firms need to embed digital technologies into all stages of agricultural innovation by using advanced tools for analyzing climate patterns, soil quality and crop dynamics to support evidence-based decision-making. To ensure organizational readiness, companies should design targeted training programs for employees from different functional backgrounds, equipping marketing teams with skills in digital platforms and technical staff with expertise in data analytics. Furthermore, organizing digital innovation case-sharing sessions can disseminate best practices, enhance employees’ recognition of the strategic value of digitalization, and foster stronger organizational commitment to digital-driven innovation.

Second, enterprises should build dynamic capabilities systematically in their daily operations. Developing real-time monitoring and data-driven analysis systems can strengthen sensing capabilities, while fostering cross-departmental collaboration and rapid-response mechanisms can improve seizing capabilities. Firms should also create flexible resource allocation processes supported by digital tools to reinforce reconfiguring capabilities. By institutionalizing these mechanisms, agricultural technology enterprises can improve their ability to identify opportunities, translate them into practical outcomes, and continuously adapt to shifting environments, thereby establishing a sustainable foundation for disruptive technological innovation.

Third, firms should prioritize organizational unlearning and resource orchestration to strengthen the impact of digital orientation and dynamic capabilities. On the one hand, organizational unlearning requires establishing renewal systems that encourage employees to abandon outdated practices and embrace new technologies, thereby enhancing adaptability. On the other hand, resource orchestration demands the creation of coordination platforms that integrate internal and external resources across value chains. By actively partnering with research institutes, digital platforms and supply chain actors, agricultural technology enterprises can build open innovation networks. Leveraging digital tools to monitor resource deployment and innovation progress in real time will further enhance efficiency and help firms break path dependence, ultimately accelerating disruptive technological innovation.

Limitations and future research

This study has several limitations that suggest directions for future research. First, the use of cross-sectional data restricts the ability to capture the dynamic evolution of digital orientation and its impact on disruptive technological innovation. Future studies could employ longitudinal or time-series data to provide stronger causal inference. Second, the reliance on questionnaire surveys may limit the depth of insights. Case studies or experimental designs could complement survey methods and enhance the robustness of findings. Third, while this study focuses on organizational unlearning and resource orchestration as boundary conditions, other factors such as policy support, organizational resilience or supply chain collaboration may also shape the relationship between digital orientation and innovation. Future research could incorporate these additional moderators to offer a more comprehensive understanding of disruptive technological innovation in agricultural technology enterprises.

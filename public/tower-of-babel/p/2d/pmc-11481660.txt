Effect of the coconut fibers and cement on the physico-mechanical and thermal properties of adobe blocks
Authors: Issiaka Sanou, Halidou Bamogo, Nassio Sory, Adama Gansoré, Younoussa Millogo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis work aimed to investigate the effect of cement and coconut fibers on the thermal and physico-mechanical characteristics of adobe blocks. Thus, a clayey raw material consisting of kaolinite (62 wt%), quartz (31 wt%), goethite (2 wt%) with a plasticity index of 21.5 % was used to manufacture adobe blocks amended with cement and coconut fibers. First the adobe blocks were formulated with an optimal cement content of 4 wt% before adding coconut fibers up to 1 wt%. The physico-mechanical and thermal characteristics of manufactured composites were then studied. According to the results obtained, the incorporation of coconut fibers into the clay-cement matrix decreases the apparent density and volume shrinkage and increases the porosity of the adobe blocks. The presence of coconut fibers in adobe blocks promotes the phenomenon of water absorption. The combined effect of the formation of calcium silicate hydrate (CSH) and hydrogen bonds between fibers molecules and those of clayey minerals leads to an improvement in the mechanical properties of adobe blocks. The thermal conductivity of adobe blocks decreases with the fiber content due to the presence of pores and cellulose molecules in the fibers which have an insulating nature. Taking into account their physico-mechanical and thermal properties, adobe blocks containing 0.6 wt% coconut fibers and 4 wt% cement are suitable for building sustainable habitats and thermally comfortable.Keywords: Clayey raw material, Coconut fibers, Adobe blocks, Physico-mechanical properties, Thermal comfort

Introduction

For many years, people have used raw earth as one of the primary building materials. Even today, around 33 % of world population lives in earth houses [1]. Increasingly, this type of construction is being abandoned in favor of cement blocks construction owing to its mechanical performance. But the cost and energy associated with this construction method are higher. Furthermore, 4.9 GJ of energy are released during the production of one ton of cement, which also produces the same amount of CO2 [2,3]. Cement, due to its energy-intensive nature and high cost, makes it difficult to access, especially for developing countries like Burkina Faso. It therefore appears that ecological construction for a population with modest incomes in developing countries can rely on the use of hand-molded mud bricks. Unfortunately, because of their low mechanical strength and unsatisfactory water-resistance, these adobe blocks have durability issues [[4], [5], [6]]. It is therefore necessary to improve the quality of these bricks in order to make them more efficient and durable. Thus, several studies have been performed on improving physico-mechanical, water and thermal properties of adobe blocks through the use of plant fibers, cement, lime and pozzolan [[6], [7], [8], [9], [10], [11]]. Thus, Ouedraogo et al.‘s works [12,13] on the stabilization of adobe blocks with sugarcane bagasse and rice husk fibers showed improvement of their technological properties. Indeed, according to these authors, the presence of fibers in adobe blocks greatly improves physical and mechanical properties due to formation of hydrogen bonds between molecules of fibers and those of clayey minerals. Also, thermal conductivity of adobe blocks is reduced because of cellulose from fibers which is a molecule with good thermal insulation. The good adhesion of fibers with the clayey matrix as well as the non-propagation of cracks improve adobe blocks mechanical properties. Babe et al.'s research shown that millet fibers serve as reinforcement by enhancing the thermomechanical properties and durability of adobe bricks through their strong adherence to the clayey matrix [9]. Ramakrishnan et al. state that for every 3 wt% increase in fonio straw, the conductivity of adobes is reduced by 23 %. Additionally, these authors showed a decrease in the coefficient of erosion and abrasion for millet-reinforced adobes with contents ranging from 0 to 3 wt% [4]. The mechanical characteristics of adobes reinforced with cement were also improved, according to studies done by Dao et al. and Millogo et al. [10,14]. This improvement is due to hydrate calcium silicates formation resulting from the hydration of cement compounds (C3S and C2S) and pozzolanic reaction between fine quartz of clayey minerals and portlandite of cement. While the many qualities of adobes can be strengthened by using cement and plant fibers, it is important to acknowledge that the combination of these materials has received less research to our knowledge [[15], [16], [17]]. However, the combination of these two stabilizers could further improve the engineering properties of the Adobe blocks. Also, the behavior of adobe blocks could be different depending on the nature of used fibers. The aim of this present work is to study the influence of cement-coconut fibers mixture on physico-mechanical and thermal properties of adobe blocks for the construction of sustainable houses. Although coconut fibers have been subject to studies [[18], [19], [20]], little work has been devoted to their use as a stabilizer in adobe blocks [21]. However, these fibers have good resistance, especially to traction [22]. Djohore et al. showed an improvement in the mechanical properties of construction materials based on clay-cement amended with coconut fibers treated with potash [21]. Thus, the use of these fibers in the formulation of adobe blocks could only reinforce the properties of composites produced. This study first seeks to find the composite which will present the best mechanical strength according to current standards and containing less cement. In this view, the adobe blocks will first be formulated with an optimal cement content. Then, from this optimum, new clay-cement-coconut fiber composites will be produced. Finally, the physical, mechanical properties and thermal conductivity of these clay-cement-coconut fiber composites will be evaluated.

Coconut fibers

The fibers are extracted from coconuts. Coconut is the fruit from the coconut tree. The coconut palm (Fig. 1a) grows in tropical and subtropical regions. It is considered the most important palm tree in these regions. In addition to wood, the coconut palm provides oil, cosmetic products and animal foods. The coconut fruit (Fig. 1b) is enveloped of the mesocarp. Coconut fibers (Fig. 1d) are extracted from this mesocarp of the coconut husks (Fig. 1c). To extract the fibers, the coconut husks are first stripped of the coconut. Then, the husks are immersed in water to separate the impurities from fibers. The fibers obtained are dried to eliminate the absorbed water. Coconut fiber is characterized by its acidic pH, low order density, high absorption capacity and high tensile strength [21,22].

Adobe blocks manufacturing process

The manufacturing of adobe blocks begins by grinding the clayey soil until particles size less than 5 mm. Two types of formulation were carried out. These are clay-cement and then clay-cement-fiber composites. As for the clay-cement composites, the cement was added at increasing contents of 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 wt % in crushed clayey soil. The mixture was homogenized to dryness before adding the necessary quantity of water. The water content was determined from the relationship of Bouffée et al. also used by Dao et al. [10,25]. After homogenization, the mixture is kneaded for 15–20 min. It has been then proceed to molding in prismatic molds measuring 4 × 4 × 16 cm3 (Fig. 2a). The molds undergo unmolding after 24 h then specimens (Fig. 2b) are kept in the shade for 28 days to be subjected to the mechanical characterization test. Following characterization, the optimum cement content is determined. Table 2 gives the proportions of the clay-cement formulations.

Optimization of cement content

Optimization of the cement content consisted of gradually replacing 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 wt% clay raw material by cement. The parameter studied was the mechanical strength compressive. The choice of this parameter is justified by the fact that it constitutes one of properties used for the specification of bricks; high compressive strength also allows the improvement of other properties such as flexural and abrasion resistance [36]. This involves determining the minimum cement content which will provide acceptable mechanical strength for the construction of housing according to current standards [37]. Compressive strength evolution as a function of cement content is illustrated in Fig. 13. This figure shows an increase in mechanical strength up to 8 wt % cement in the composite, thus showing an improvement of adobe blocks mechanical behavior with cement addition. Beyond 8 wt% the mechanical strength decreases. This same observation was made by other authors but at cement contents different from ours [10,14]. Dao et al. observed an increase in the mechanical strength of adobe blocks obtained by substitution of cement up to 12 wt% [10]. According to them, the formation of calcium silicate hydrate (CSH), which binds the isolated particles together, results in a more uniform microstructure (with small pores and no microcracks), which explains the improvement in mechanical performance. These CSH result from the hydration reaction of the anhydrous compounds of cement and by pozzolanic reaction involving fine crushed silica and a low silica content of kaolinite silicates in a basic medium [10,11,38]. Millogo et al. [7] observed a reduction in mechanical strength of adobes containing more than 10 wt% lime. For them, the decrease in mechanical strength is due to virtual absence of CSH and excessive production of portlandite and calcite. Adobe blocks containing 4 wt% cement have a compressive strength greater than the value of 2 MPa required by the current standard for single-level constructions [37]. Therefore, the cement content of 4 wt% will constitute the optimal cement content. To this content, it will be added coconut fibers at a content of 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8 and 1 wt% then the resulting adobe blocks' mechanical, thermal, and physical characteristics will be investigated.

Physico-mechanical and thermal properties of clay-cement-coconut fibers composites

Physical properties

Apparent density, closed porosity, volumetric shrinkage (Fig. 14) and water absorption by capillary (Fig. 15) are the physical characteristics of adobe blocks amended with cement-coconut fibers which were studied. The analysis of Fig. 14 shows that the apparent density and volumetric shrinkage decrease while porosity increases with increasing coconut fiber content in adobe blocks. This result could be justified by the low density of the fibers compared to that of clay raw material. This reduction in density is also the cause of the reduction in volume shrinkage. This same observation was made by Sanou et al. for adobes reinforced with cement and rice husk ash [11]. For them, apparent density is an increasing function of shrinkage. For Ouedraogo et al., the reduction in density is due to the considerable volume occupied by the fibers in the composite [12,13]. In addition, the high fiber content in adobe could create pores, which would lead to an increase in porosity. Also, compared to Ouedraogo et al.'s results, the apparent densities obtained are lower. In fact, coconut fibers have a lower density than the sugarcane bagasse or rice husk used by these authors.

Apparent density, closed porosity and volumetric shrinkage of adobe blocks versus coconut fibers content.

Water absorption coefficient by capillarity of adobe blocks versus coconut fibers content.

The phenomenon of water absorption by capillarity is favored by the presence of fibers in the adobe blocks (Fig. 15). This behavior of adobe blocks is attributable to hydrophilic character of coconut fibers due to presence of cellulose and hemicelluloses [9,29]. In fact, coconut fibers, due to their high absorption capacity, have an affinity for water which leads to an increase in the water absorption coefficient of adobe blocks containing these fibers until saturation. The decrease in the absorption coefficient beyond 8 % of fibers in adobes is due to excessive presence of fibers. A high content of coconut fibers in adobe can clog internal pores and slow down kinetics of capillary rise of water. The values of the water absorption coefficient for adobe blocks are greater than the values found for adobes reinforced with rice husk by Ouedraogo et al. [13]. Indeed, the absorption capacity of coconut fibers (610 %) is much greater than the absorption capacity of rice husk (88 %). So, the coconut fibers are more hydrophilic than the husk, hence the difference in value observed.

Mechanical behavior

The mechanical behavior of adobe blocks studied is essentially the flexural and compressive strengths. The evolution of mechanical strength in relation to the amount of fiber in the adobe blocks is illustrated in Fig. 16. Flexural strength of adobe blocks increases up to 0.6 wt% coconut fiber content. This increasing is attributed to the good tensile strength of coconut fibers [22]. In compressive, an improvement in strength is noted with the addition of 0.4 and 0.6 wt% coconut fibers. This improvement could be attributed to the coconut fibers' strong adherence to the clay-cement matrix [12]. Also, the formation of CSH which sticks clay's particles thus consolidating the material could be the reason of the improvement of compressive strength [11]. Moreover, the possibility for hydrogen bonds to form between the molecules of CSH, clayey minerals, and coconut fibers would account for the strength increase [12,13]. The mechanical behavior of the adobe blocks is similar to those stabilized with kenaf fibers by Serebe et al. [39]. In fact, these authors obtained optimum mechanical strength with adobe blocks containing 0.6 wt% kenaf fiber of length 2 and 3 cm but better with 3 cm length. According to them, this can be explained by the longer fibers' ability to link with the clayey matrix like a bridge. On the other hand, for adobe containing 0.2 wt% fibers, there is a drop in strength. This decrease could result from the mineralization of coconut fibers in an alkaline medium. Indeed, the mineralization of plant fibers in an alkaline matrix occurs by the ionization of the hydroxyl groups of fibers, followed by the precipitation of coordination complexes with metal cations present. This could lead to formation of pores in clay-cement-fiber composite. The mechanical strengths of adobe blocks containing 0.8 and 1 wt% fibers decreased. The phenomena of fiber crowding in the clay-cement matrix, which causes a considerable amount of macroporosity, could be explain the decrease in mechanical characteristics. Furthermore, a high coconut fiber content may prevent cement hydration, lowering the formation of CSH and mechanical strength [40]. According to Sedan et al., the quantity of calcium ions decreases with the increase in fiber content [41]. This decrease makes the calcium concentration too low in the cement paste to allow the formation of CSH gel, which explains the disruption of cement hydration. The mechanical strengths values of adobe blocks are better than those obtained by Mbaye et al. on compressed earth blocks reinforced with cement and Typha fibers [15]. This difference in values could be due to the nature of the fibers used. All adobe blocks manufactured have a compressive strength greater than 2 MPa, therefore useable in the construction of homes according to current standards [37].

Thermal conductivity

The thermal behavior of adobe blocks was studied from the measurement of their thermal conductivity illustrated in Fig. 17. Analysis of the figure shows that the thermal conductivity of adobes decreases as the coconut fiber content increases. The decrease in conductivity is due to the insulating nature of the cellulose in fibers [5,13]. The low fiber content in the clay-cement matrix results in a drastic reduction in crack and pore densities, thus leading to a more homogeneous microstructure [42]. Consequently, the high fiber amount causes cracks and pores to increase. The accumulation of fibers in certain areas of the composite then leads to a reduction in thermal conductivity. The lowest value (0.302 W m−1 K−1) is obtained for adobe containing 1 wt% coconut fibers. This value comparing to those obtained by other authors for the same fiber content (Table 9), shows that the elaborated adobe blocks have low thermal conductivity. Consequently, their use in the construction of homes offers good thermal comfort to occupants and constitutes a solution to reduce energy consumption due to the use of air conditioners.

Conclusions

(1)the clayey raw material is composed of kaolinite (62 wt%), quartz (31 wt%), and goethite (2 wt%) with a plasticity index of 21.5 %, this clay is appropriate for making adobe blocks for houses.

In view of the different results obtained, adobe blocks containing 0.6 wt% coconut fibers and 4 wt% cement are suitable for the construction of sustainable homes with good thermal comfort. Despite the good physico-mechanical and thermal properties of elaborated adobe blocks, some tests prove necessary and will therefore be the subject of future work. The aim is to determine the hygrothermal properties of the adobes mixed with the coconut fiber and cement mixture in order to understand their thermal behavior in the wet state. Assessing sorption-desorption would also demonstrate the advantages of employing these reinforced earth construction materials with coconut fiber to improve house comfort levels.

Visual outcome of penetrating ocular trauma among patients visiting University of Gondar comprehensive specialized hospital
Authors: Habib Getachew Degefa, Fisseha Admasu, Wossen Mulugeta, Esayas Alemshet Tekletsadik
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractOcular trauma, a preventable public health issue, is one of the common causes of ophthalmic morbidity and monocular blindness worldwide. It can occur in almost any setting and at any age in either sex but largely affects males. The mechanism and type of injury, the extent of damage, and the time-lapse between the occurrence of injury and eye care are among the factors that determine the visual outcome of ocular injuries. This study is designed to assess the clinical profile and visual outcome of patients presenting with penetrating ocular injury. A prospective hospital-based study was conducted among penetrating ocular trauma patients who visited UOG tertiary eye care and training center during the study period. All patients with penetrating ocular trauma who fulfilled the inclusion criteria were included in the study. Data on demography, initial and final visual acuity, type of injury, as well as management, were included in a structured questionnaire. The collected data were processed and analysed using Statistical Packages for the Social Sciences (SPSS) version 25.0. The study included 91 cases of penetrating ocular injuries. The majority of the patients 81.2% were males. The male-to-female ratio was 4.3:1. The left eye was involved in 52.7% of the patients. The median age was 20 years, with an IQR of 10–35. The majority (31.9%) of the cases were in the age range of 5–10 years. nearly half of the study participants (45.1%) sustained the injury at home. The commonest source of injury was wooden sticks 49.5%. Six patients were lost to follow-up at three-month visits. Traumatic cataracts (p = 0.001), and poor initial visual acuity were poor prognostic factors. Still ocular injury is a common cause of monocular blindness. We recommend that more has to be done on the primary prevention and control measures.Keywords: Clinical profile, Visual outcome, University of GondarSubject terms: Diseases, Health occupations, Medical research

Introduction

Penetrating ocular injury is an injury to the globe with an entry wound, but no exit wound. It may be classified as rupture secondary to blunt injury, lacerating injury by a sharp object, and injury related to the intraocular foreign bodies1.

Ocular trauma, is a preventable public health issue throughout the world and it is one of the common causes of ophthalmic morbidity and monocular blindness worldwide2. The risk of ocular injury is higher in males than females in several previous reports including Ethiopia3–7.

The epidemiology of ocular trauma has been studied in detail in developed countries but there is limited data on its severity, mode, and outcome from developing countries. According to the WHO report on the global pattern of eye injuries, there are approximately 1.6 million people blind from eye injuries and 2.3 million bilaterally visually impaired, and 19 million with unilateral visual loss8.

A study in Ghana showed ocular trauma is a relatively common health problem, especially among males in the economically active age group9.

The mechanism and type of injury, the extent of damage, and the time-lapse between the occurrence of injury and eye care are among the factors that determine the visual outcome of ocular injuries10.

To design preventive strategies and to provide clinical care the availability of data regarding the overall nature of the injury and its impact in terms of visual outcome is very important11. However, adequate data regarding the pattern and visual outcome of ocular injuries are lacking in developing countries including Ethiopia. This study will help as a baseline for planning preventive measures and improving clinical care.

Methods

Study design and period

A hospital-based prospective cohort study was conducted to assess the clinical profile and visual outcome of penetrating ocular trauma patients who visited University of Gondar tertiary eye care and training center. The study was conducted from February 15 to October 15, 2021.

Study setting

The study was conducted at the University of Gondar Tertiary Eye Care and training center which is located in Amhara regional state, 750 km Northwest of Addis Ababa, the capital city of Ethiopia, and 180 km from Bahirdar, the capital city of Amhara regional state. University of Gondar tertiary eye care and training center was established in 201112. It is the only tertiary eye care center in Northwest Ethiopia. It has a referral link from 8 zones in Northwest Ethiopia, with a total catchment area of 14 million people13. Currently, there are five actively functioning subspecialty clinics, six senior ophthalmologists, 27 ophthalmology residents, optometrists, nurses, and other health care workers in the eye care center.

Study participants

All patients with ocular trauma who visited University of Gondar tertiary eye care and training center from February to October 2021 were the source population. All patients with penetrating ocular trauma who visited University of Gondar tertiary eye care and training center from Februaryy 15 to October 15, 2021, were the study population.

Sample size determination

All consecutive patients with penetrating ocular trauma who visited University of Gondar tertiary eye care and training center for the first time during the study period, and fulfilled the inclusion criteria, were enrolled. Medical records of the patients from whom data was collected were marked to avoid duplication.

Data collection instruments and procedures

Once informed consent was granted, patient medical history, demographic data, and physical examination findings were filled into a structured questionnaire. The English version of the questionnaire was prepared based on the Birmingham Eye Trauma Terminology1 (Table 1), and The United States Eye Injury Registry Model was used to collect the required data15,16.

The Birmingham eye trauma terminology1.

Physical examination included best-corrected VA, which was measured with a Snellen chart and, in cases of severe vision loss, counting fingers(CF) in front (counting finger close to the face of the patient), hand movement (HM) and light perception (LP), was checked. In bilateral cases the most severely injured eye was included. The presence of afferent pupillary defect was checked with pen light. Slit lamp bio microscopy and fundus examination were performed at initial and subsequent visits.

VA was measured with a Snellen chart and, in cases of severe vision loss, counting fingers at front(counting finger close to the face), hand movement(HM) and light perception(LP), was tested. Pupillary reaction to light was checked with pen light, RAPD was also checked. Slit lamp examination was performed at EOPD, at initial visit and subsequent visits.

Final visual outcome was defined in terms of best-corrected VA at final discharge from follow-up. The visual outcome criteria were based on the WHO revised definition of visual impairment as follows, a good visual outcome in the affected eye was defined as a Snellen VA of better than or equal to 6/18, visual impairment in the affected eye was defined as a Snellen VA below 6/18 to 6/60, severe visual impairment was defined as a Snellen VA below 6/60 to 3/60 and blindness was defined as a Snellen VA below 3/60 in the affected eye17.

Data processing and analysis

The collected data were examined for correctness, consistency, and any errors; any faults were cleaned manually, and corrected. Data were coded and entered into Epi-info version 7 and exported to the Statistical Package for The Social Sciences (SPSS) version 25.0 (https://getintopc.com.pk/softwares/analytics/ibm-spss-statistics-25-free-download/) for further analysis. Results were described in terms of numbers, percentages, means, and medians and were displayed on tables and bar graphs. To identify factors associated with the outcome variable a Bivariable and multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed for each independent variable and a crude odds ratio (COR) with 95% confidence intervals was obtained. The strength of the statistical association was measured by adjusted odds ratios (AOR) and 95% confidence intervals. In all cases, p value, less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Data quality assurance

The data collection questionnaire was pre-tested by interviewing patients on follow-up. The principal investigator explained the data collection procedure to the residents who were assigned to the emergency OPD during the study period. The medical record chart of the patients from whom data was collected was marked to avoid duplication. The principal investigator supervised the data collection and checked the completeness of the filled questionnaire.

Ethics approval and informed consent

All experimental protocols were approved by the University of Gondar's ethical review board with a reference number (PostGr/091/26/2021), and permission to undergo this study was obtained from the Department of Ophthalmology. Written informed consent was obtained from the study participants after a clear explanation concerning the purpose and importance of the study. For illiterate participants and minor groups, informed consent was obtained from their parents and legal guardians, respectively. It was made clear that the patient's identity would not be exposed in any way, and the confidentiality of patient records was respected.

Result

Socio-demographic characteristics

There were 91 study participants at the beginning of the investigation. Out of ninty-one participants, of whom 85 were followed for three months where as six participants were lost to follow-up and were not included in the regression analysis. Majority of the study participants were males (81.2%) with a male-to-female ratio of 4.3:1. The age of respondents ranged from 5 to 60 years with a median age of 20 with an IQR of 10–35. Nearly one third (31.9%) of the cases were in the age range of 5–10 years, and about half (45.1%) of the study participants were dependent and 25.3% were farmers, whereas about two third (74.7%) of the participants were from rural areas (Table 2).

Characteristics of the injury

Almost all injuries were unilateral eye injuries. About half of the injuries (52.7%) were on the left eye. There were two patients with a bilateral eye injury, and only one of their eyes was included in the study. Regarding the place of injury, nearly half of the study participants (45.1%) sustained the injury in their home, and about one-fourth (23.1%) of the study participants sustained the injury in their farmland. The intent of injury for more than three-fourths (84.6%) of injuries was unintentional. Regarding the source of injury, the study shows that about half (49.5%) of the cases were due to wooden stick injuries. Nearly half of the cases visited UOG tertiary eye care and training center within 24 h of the injury, and about one-fifth (20.9%) of the cases arrived after seven days. Personal protective eyewear was not used by all the participants (Table 3).

Initial diagnosis of the participants

More than half (57.1%) of the cases were diagnosed initially as corneal tears and about one-third (31.9%) of cases were diagnosed as corneoscleral tears. The initial vision of the injured eye for more than two-thirds (69.2%) of the cases was hand motion or light perception. In nearly one-third (27.5%) of the cases, the size of the corneal laceration was between 5–10 mm, and in about 12.1% of the cases, the corneoscleral laceration was above 10 mm. More than half (61.5%) of the cases had iris prolapse, and 13.2% of the cases had RAPD. From a total of 11 intraocular foreign body cases, 9 were nonmetallic and found in the anterior segment (Table 5).

Treatment modalities and complications

Laceration repair was done for all cases who had a laceration, and among them, corneal laceration repair was done for about 59.8% of patients. The intraocular foreign body was removed for 10 cases from the anterior segment by forceps and for one patient the IOFB was partly in the vitreous and part of it was outside and it was removed by forceps. From the total of 49 cases who had traumatic cataracts, cataract extraction was done for 34 patients, among these, Lens Washout for patients who had traumatic cataracts with ruptured capsule the cortical lens matter was removed via a clear corneal incision was done for 73.5% of them, and for 26.5% of them, MSICS was done for patients who had traumatic cataract and intact lens capsule, cataractous lens was removed via a scleral tunnel and cataract surgery is planned for the remaining 15 patients. Among all cases who underwent cataract extraction,47% of them were left aphakic and the intraocular lens was placed for 26.5%, and 23.5% of cases in the sulcus and the posterior capsular bag respectively, moreover anterior vitrectomy was done for 35.3% of the cases who underwent cataract extraction. All cases were given intraoperative prophylactic antibiotics, among them, 69.2% of cases were given 20 mg of gentamycin subconjunctivally, and the remaining was given intracameral vancomycin 1 mg/0.1 ml. Despite the administration of prophylactic antibiotics, three patients developed postoperative endophthalmitis (Table 6).

Visual outcomes of the participants after 3 months of follow up

A total of 85 patients were followed for three months and after three-months of follow-up about one-fourth (25.9%) of the cases have a visual acuity of 6/12 or better on the Snellen chart,20% of the cases have a visual acuity of HM/LP and three patients have NLP vision.

Regarding the lens status after a three-month follow-up, it was phakic and clear in 27.5% of the cases whereas 33% of the cases had different degrees of cataracts and 16.5% of cases was pseudophakic, but the remaining aphakic.

From the total cases that were followed for three months, 19 patients had a late complication, in which six patients had retinal detachment, three patients had glaucoma and 10 patients developed a cataract (Table 7).

Factors associated with visual outcome

Laceration

Regarding the size of the laceration to the visual outcome, for the corneal laceration patients, the visual outcome was variable with the laceration size, but generally, among the corneal laceration patients, the majority (54%) of them had a final visual acuity better than 3/60 and 46% of them had below 3/60. The majority of the cases with a corneoscleral laceration of up to 10 mm in size had a final visual acuity of better than 6/60 and all of those with a corneoscleral laceration greater than 10 mm had a final visual acuity of below 3/60.

Regarding the initial and final visual acuity, at the initial visit, 7.7% of the patients had a visual acuity of 6/12 and were better at presentation; while after completing treatment at 03 months of follow-up, 24.2% had the best-corrected visual acuity of 6/12 and better. In addition to this, 69.2% of the patients had a visual acuity of HM/LP on their initial visit and after 03 months of follow up only 18.7% of the cases had a visual acuity of HM/LP. In general, 52% of the patients had a final visual acuity of 3/60 or better, whereas 48% of the patients had a final visual acuity of below 3/60 (Fig. 2).

Visual acuity of the participants at presentation and after three months of follow-up at the University of Gondar tertiary eye care and training center, Northwest Ethiopia, 2021.

Regarding the etiology of the ocular injury to the final visual outcome after 03 months of follow-up, among 37 patients who had a wooden stick injury, 11 patients had a final visual acuity of 6/12 or better, and 11 of them had final visual acuity between 3/60 and CF in front. Among 14 patients who had bullet injuries, only 03 of them had a final visual acuity of 6/12 and better.

To see the association between dependent and independent variables binary logistic and multivariable logistic regression analyses were conducted. In Bivariable logistic regression analysis variables such as occupation, the initial vision of the injured eye, and initial lens status were associated with the visual outcome of cases.

In multivariable logistic regression analysis variables such as an initial vision of the injured eye, and initial lens status were significantly associated with the visual outcome of cases. The study revealed that the odds of being blind are about 8 times higher among cases who had initial vision less than hand motion than cases who had initial vision greater than hand motion [AOR = 8.32: (2.55,13.50)]. The study also revealed that those cases who had traumatic cataracts were about 8 times more likely [AOR = 8.01 :( 3.16, 12.74) to be blind as compared with cases who had the normal lens (Table 10).

Discussion

This study was conducted to determine the clinical profile and visual outcome of patients presented with ocular trauma at the University of Gondar tertiary eye care and training center. The incidence of ocular injuries is more in developing countries and it consists of largely a preventable cause of monocular visual impairment and blindness2.

It was found in this study that most ocular trauma patients were males (81.2%). Different studies worldwide show that ocular trauma is more common in males4,18–21. The same is true in studies done in our country10,22. The explanation could be due to the involvement of males in risky occupations and other possible explanations regarding children could be traditionally given the privilege of a male child to play outside unsupervised.

Ocular injuries appear common in younger age groups as demonstrated across multiple studies in different geographical areas18,20,21. In this study younger individuals who were less than 17 years of age were more affected (45%%) followed by those in the age group of 30–44 years (24.2%) with a median age of 20 (IQR of 10–35). Similarly, hospital-based studies in Ethiopia also showed similar trends10,22. The possible reason for this could be younger individuals might not take great care while using materials that can cause harm. Regarding children, the possible reason could be the lack of parental supervision during their play.

In this study, 77.6% of the patients were blind at presentation with a visual acuity of less than 3/60, in around 14% of the patients the visual acuity was between 6/18 and 6/60, the remaining patients 8.2% had a visual acuity of 6/12 and above. After three months of follow up 45.1% of the cases have visual acuity of less than 3/60, 24.2% of them have visual acuity between 6/18 and 3/60 the remaining patients 24.2% of them have a visual acuity of 6/12 and above. Studies done in Israel, Nepal, and Ethiopia also show similar results19,23. Initial poor visual acuity is significantly associated with poor visual outcomes (p value = 0.004). The possible reason could be patients who had poor vision at presentation might sustain severe ocular damage.

In this study, 49 patients had traumatic cataracts at presentation among them 34 of the patients underwent cataract extraction and IOL was placed for 18 patients and 16 of them were left aphakic. Lens injury which caused traumatic cataracts was one of the causes which decrease initial visual acuity. In this study, traumatic cataract was found to have a significant association with poor visual outcome (p value = 0.001). A study was done in Israel also shows that lens damage (p = 0.02) was associated with poor visual outcomes23. A possible explanation could be lens injury might lead to significant post-operative inflammation.

Regarding the material causing ocular injury, a wooden stick was the commonest material accounting for 45.9% followed by a bullet at 15.4% and metal at 12.1%. Similarly, studies done in Ethiopia showed that wooden material was the commonest cause of eye injury10,. The possible reason could be a wooden stick might be an easily accessible and frequently used material by the participants. Regarding the increment in bullet-related eye injury in this study, the possible explanation could be because of the ongoing civil war in the region.

Conclusions

Penetrating ocular injury can occur in all age groups and it is common in males. Majority of the injury was caused by a wooden stick. Initial lens status and presenting visual acuity determine the visual outcome.

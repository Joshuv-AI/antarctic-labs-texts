Baloxavir Marboxil in Chinese Pediatric Patients Aged 1 to < 5 Years with Influenza: Safety and Clinical and Virologic Outcomes
Authors: Yuchuan Li, Peng Guo, Chenguang Jia, Ju Yin, Yuncui Yu, Qiang Qin, Xiaoyan Zhang, Shunying Zhao, Haiming Yang, Weihua Zhang, Yuguang Liang, Qian Ding, Man Tian, Yiping Chen, Chunmei Zhu, Fang Wang, Jing Ma, Lisu Huang, Yongping Xie, Jing Bi, Sainan Shu, Chengsong Zhao

ABSTRACT
IntroductionBaloxavir marboxil (baloxavir), a cap-dependent endonuclease inhibitor, has been proven safe and efficacious against influenza and approved for the treatment of influenza in children aged ≥ 5 years in China. There were limited data on baloxavir in Chinese pediatric patients aged 1 to < 5 years with influenza. This study aimed to evaluate the safety, clinical efficacy, and virologic outcomes of baloxavir for treating influenza in this younger age group.MethodsIn this single-arm, multicenter trial, patients received a single oral dose of baloxavir (2 mg/kg for participants weighing < 20 kg, or 40 mg for participants weighing ≥ 20 kg to < 80 kg). The primary endpoint was safety. Efficacy, virology, and palatability endpoints were also assessed.ResultsAll 100 enrolled children completed the study and received baloxavir. Adverse events were reported in 29 (29.0%) children, most commonly bronchitis and diarrhea. No death or adverse events leading to discontinuation occurred. Eighty children were included in the clinical efficacy and virologic assessment. The median [95% confidence interval (CI)] time to resolution of influenza signs and symptoms was 150.17 (90.23, NE) h. The median duration of fever was 38.65 (95% CI 25.32, 42.35) h. Infectious virus titers rapidly declined by day 2 post-dose. The proportion of subjects with a positive viral titer was reduced to 8.7% at this timepoint. Of the 100 children enrolled, more than 70% of children rated baloxavir as favorable in the palatability test.ConclusionA single, oral dose of baloxavir was well-tolerated in Chinese children aged 1 to < 5 years, with rapid influenza virus clearance and associated symptom alleviation, suggesting baloxavir was an important treatment option for Chinese pediatric patients with influenza.Trial RegistrationThe study was registered at the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR) on March 14, 2025 (registration no. Introduction

Influenza is an acute respiratory viral infection caused by influenza viruses that triggers seasonal epidemics worldwide every year. Children are particularly vulnerable to influenza infection, with epidemiological data showing the highest annual incidence rate among children under 18 years old (22.5%, 95% CI: 9.0%, 46.0%) and children aged 0–4 years experiencing the highest influenza-related hospitalization rate (224.0/100,000, 95% CI: 118.8–420.0/100,000) [1-3]. Pediatric populations play a pivotal role in community influenza transmission due to several factors: highly susceptible to infection, elevated incidence rates, prolonged viral shedding, and frequent close contacts through household and community networks [3]. Therefore, in a typical influenza season, children are typically the most affected, and the disease burden among children is substantial.

Antiviral therapy provides a critical adjunct to influenza immunization by mitigating symptoms and preventing secondary complications, although vaccination is recommended as the main influenza prevention strategy [4-5]. In China, the neuraminidase inhibitors oseltamivir (oral) and peramivir (intravenous) are approved for influenza treatment in children under 5 years [1]. While oseltamivir is the first-line oral antiviral, its use is limited by poor palatability, a high incidence of vomiting, and increasing resistance [6]. These challenges underscore the need for alternative antiviral treatments that are both effective and well tolerated in young children, with more practical administration methods and simplified dosing regimen.

Baloxavir marboxil (baloxavir), an anti-influenza drug with a novel mechanism of action (cap-dependent endonuclease inhibitor), was first approval in Japan (February 2018) and subsequently in over 70 countries worldwide by 2025. Clinical studies established baloxavir as the first influenza antiviral proven to have triple action in therapy, prevention, and reducing viral transmission [7]. The safety and efficacy of single-dose baloxavir for treatment of influenza in children < 12 years were validated through a series of clinical trials, including four Phase 3 open-label single-arm studies and one global Phase 3 double-blind, randomized trial [8-12]. The MiniSTONE-2 trial, which involved children aged 1 to < 12 years with uncomplicated, acute influenza, demonstrated the tolerability and efficacy of single oral dose of baloxavir, and the resultant rapid viral reduction and symptom alleviation similar to oseltamivir [12]. Similar safety and efficacy outcomes have been reported in other four open-label single-arm studies in pediatric patients aged 0 to < 12 years old [8–11]. Notably, none of these five clinical studies included the Chinese population. In China, baloxavir has been approved for the treatment of influenza, with dosage forms including tablets and dry suspensions, but its use is currently approved only for individuals aged 5 years and above. A real-world retrospective study of Chinese children < 18 years (including a small number of patients aged 2 to < 5 years) demonstrated baloxavir's superior tolerability profile versus oseltamivir (adverse drug reactions: 5.00% vs. 19.30%, p < 0.001; and the incidence of adverse reactions of nausea and vomiting: 2.38% vs. 12.13%, p < 0.001), along with faster fever resolution [13]. Despite these findings, prospective safety and efficacy data for Chinese children < 5 years remained limited. Therefore, this multicenter, single-arm clinical trial was conducted to evaluate the safety, clinical efficacy, virologic outcomes, and palatability of single-dose baloxavir for influenza treatment in Chinese pediatric patients aged 1 to < 5 years.

Methods

Study Design and Participants

This is a single-arm, open-label, multicenter, interventional study to evaluate the safety and efficacy of baloxavir in Chinese patients aged 1 to < 5 years infected with influenza A and/or B. The study was conducted at 10 clinical sites in China (Table S1 in the supplementary material).

Patients aged 1 to < 5 years diagnosed with influenza virus infection [defined as fever (tympanic temperature ≥ 38 °C showing positive for influenza A/B by a rapid influenza diagnostic test or polymerase chain reaction (PCR); ≥ 1 respiratory symptom (cough and nasal discharge/congestion); and onset of symptoms no more than 48 h before the screening day], who were able to take baloxavir for suspension were considered eligible. The trial excluded children with severe influenza symptoms requiring hospitalization and those with complications by an infection requiring systemic antibiotic, antifungal, and/or antiviral drugs at screening. Children who received any medications with anti-flu effect within 30 days before screening and children who received systemic corticosteroids or immunosuppressive therapy were also excluded. For complete inclusion and exclusion criteria, see the Supplementary Methods.

The study consisted of a 1-day treatment period followed by a 28-day follow-up period. The detailed study visit schedule is described in Supplementary Methods.

Ethical Approval

The clinical trial protocol and its amendments were reviewed and approved by the Independent Ethics Committee (IEC) of Beijing Children’s Hospital, Capital Medical University [Approval No. (2024)-Y-204-D and (2024)-Y-204-D-01)]. The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the institutional review board or ethics committee at each center (Table S1 in the supplementary material). The study was registered at the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2500098911), and was assigned a status of 'Retrospective Registration' because the first patient was enrolled while the initial registration application was under revision for formatting details, as per ChiCTR guidelines. The study protocol was finalized, and registration was initiated, before the completion of patient enrollment and any data analysis. All parents/legal guardians of participating patients provided written informed consent. A patient and a parent/legal guardian were free to withdraw participation from the trial at any time upon request.

Interventions

Participants received a single oral dose of baloxavir for suspension (Shionogi Pharma, Japan) on day 1 based on body weight (2 mg/kg for participants weighing < 20 kg or 40 mg for participants weighing ≥ 20 kg to < 80 kg). The dosing for this study was based on the MiniSTONE-2, a global multicenter Phase 3 study of baloxavir in the pediatric population aged 1–12 years [12]. Acetaminophen or ibuprofen was permitted for severe influenza symptoms, such as fever and headache. Detailed concomitant therapy is described in Supplementary Methods.

Safety Assessments

The primary endpoint of this study was safety, assessed by the proportion of patients who experienced adverse events (AEs) and serious AEs up to Day 29. AEs and serious AEs were categorized by system organ class and preferred term in accordance with the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities, Version 28.0. The severity of events was categorized using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, Version 5.0.

Clinical Efficacy and Virologic Assessments

The secondary endpoint of this study was efficacy. One of the efficacy endpoints is the time to alleviation of influenza signs and symptoms [this resolution time was specified as the time point when cough and nasal symptoms scored 0 (no problem) or 1 (minor problem) on the CARIFS questionnaire, the patient returned to normal health and activity, and return to a febrile state (tympanic temperature ≤ 37.2 °C), remaining for at least 21.5 h] in accordance with the previous pediatric study [12], which is measured by Canadian Acute Respiratory Illness and Flu Scale (CARIFS) questionnaire. Parents of the patients completed the CARIFS [14] between Day 1 and Day 10.

Additional secondary efficacy endpoints included: (1) duration of fever which was defined as time to return to afebrile state (≤ 37.2 °C) and maintain this state for at least 21.5 h; (2) duration of symptoms; (3) time to return to normal health and activity; (4) frequency of influenza-related complications (death, hospitalization, radiologically confirmed pneumonia, bronchitis, sinusitis, otitis media, encephalitis/encephalopathy, febrile seizures or myositis, all of which were pre-specified and required confirmation by investigator), and (5) the proportion of patients requiring antibiotics.

Another secondary endpoint of this study was virologic endpoints, included time to cessation of viral shedding by virus titer and by RT-PCR, change from baseline in influenza virus titer and in the amount of virus RNA (RT-PCR) at each timepoint, proportion of patients with positive influenza virus titer and proportion of patients with positive influenza virus RNA by RT-PCR, and area under the curve in virus titer and in the amount of virus RNA (RT-PCR) in accordance with the previous pediatric study [12]. Respiratory swab samples were collected on days 1, 2, 4, and 6 for virology tests.

Subgroup analyses were performed for selected clinical efficacy endpoints and virologic endpoints, stratified by co-infection status, age groups (≥ 1 to < 2 years vs. ≥ 2 to < 5 years), and the presence of high-risk factors for influenza complications.

Palatability Assessments

Palatability and acceptability were evaluated using a dedicated questionnaire previously used in the CENTERSTONE study [7]. The parents or guardians would answer and fill out the questionnaire.

Statistical Analysis

Using MiniSTONE-2 parameters, 80 participants would provide a probability of ≥ 90% that ≥ 1 child would experience an AE with a background incidence of 3%. Considering a 20% dropout rate, a total of 100 subjects were required for enrollment. The safety analysis set comprised all subjects who received at least one dose of the study treatment. The clinical efficacy and virologic analysis set was used for all efficacy analyses and comprised all subjects who received at least one dose of the study treatment and had laboratory-confirmed influenza infection (PCR-positive result) from baseline or any swab sample collected during the study period. AEs, treatment-related AEs, serious AEs, and deaths were summarized [n (%)]. Data were summarized using descriptive statistics and Kaplan–Meier plots where applicable. All data analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA).

Safety

Among the 100 enrolled participants, no deaths or AEs leading to discontinuation or withdrawal were reported. Thirty-four AEs were reported in 29 (29.0%) patients (Table 2; Table S4), and all AEs recovered or resolved by the end of the study. Most AEs (18/29) occurred on day 7 or earlier. The most common AEs included bronchitis (4%), diarrhea (4%), upper respiratory tract infection (3%), cough (3%), vomiting (2%), and decreased neutrophil count (2%), and were considered not related to the study drug. Overall, the majority of AEs (86%) were grade 1 or 2 in severity. Four patients (4%) experienced grade ≥ 3 events. These included two grade 3 events (upper respiratory tract infection and sinus bradycardia) and two grade 4 events (decreased neutrophil count in two patients). All grade ≥ 3 AEs were considered not related to the study drug. Three subjects (3.0%) experienced three serious AEs (decreased appetite, upper respiratory tract infection, and sinus bradycardia; brief narratives of the serious AEs are presented in Table S5), all assessed as unrelated to the study drug. A single treatment-related AE was reported in 1 (1.0%) patient, which was a Grade 1 rash and resolved spontaneously. The median duration of fever was 38.65 (95% CI 25.32, 42.35) h. Virologic Outcomes

Twenty patients were excluded from the virologic analysis because of the lack of laboratory-confirmed influenza infection (PCR-positive result from baseline or any swab sample collected during the study period). Therefore, a total of 80 subjects were included in the virologic analysis. The median time to cessation of viral shedding (by viral titer) was 22.74 h (95.0% CI 22.20–24.58) (Table 3). Infectious virus titers rapidly declined after baloxavir treatment, with a viral non-clearance rate of less than 25% (Fig. 1e, as estimated by Kaplan–Meier curve), a mean virus titer change from baseline of − 1.542 (± 0.8526) log10 TCID50/mL, and a significant decrease in the proportion of subjects with positive viral titer (8.7%) by day 2 (Table 3; Fig. 2). The mean baseline-adjusted area under the curve (AUC) values for viral titer were negative, with absolute magnitudes increasing progressively from day 2 to day 6 (Fig. 2). The virological outcomes by RT-PCR were presented in Table S7 in the supplementary material. Palatability Assessment Results

Among the 100 subjects participating in the palatability assessment, 74.0% of children reported a favorable taste, 13.0% reported a disfavor taste, and 13.0% were uncertain. Regarding willingness to take the medication again, 72.0% of children stated they would be happy to take the medicine again, 9.0% would not, and 19.0% were uncertain.

Discussion

The current influenza burden is significant, with pediatric populations disproportionately affected and playing a pivotal role in transmission. Three anti-influenza drugs are clinically available for pediatric use in China: oral oseltamivir, oral baloxavir, and intravenous peramivir [1], with oral antivirals preferred. Although oseltamivir is approved for patients aged ≥ 2 weeks and widely accessible, concerns persist regarding associated adverse effects (e.g., vomiting, impacting adherence) and emerging antiviral resistance [6]. Baloxavir, a cap-dependent endonuclease inhibitor offering a single-dose regimen, has demonstrated efficacy, good tolerability, and fewer gastrointestinal reactions in limited studies involving children aged 1–4 years [13]. However, its use remains off-label for this specific population in China [1], and robust clinical evidence is lacking. This multicenter study, conducted across 10 clinical sites in China, is the first to evaluate the safety, efficacy, and palatability of a single oral dose of baloxavir in this population with influenza virus infection.

The present study primarily focused on evaluating the safety profile of baloxavir in Chinese children aged 1 to < 5 years. Our findings demonstrated that the safety outcomes were either comparable to or more favorable than those previously reported in non-Chinese children aged 0 to < 12 years (Table S8 in the supplementary material) [8-13]. The types of AEs observed did not exceed the scope of AEs reported in the labels approved by the FDA and NMPA, as well as in previous studies. The incidence of vomiting, the most frequently reported AE in the MiniSTONE-2 study (baloxavir: 6.1%; oseltamivir: 15.5%), was, however, substantially lower in our cohort (2.0%). This reduction suggested that baloxavir may potentially improve medication adherence in pediatric patients. In current study, most AEs were either mild or moderate in severity and all recovered or resolved by the end of the study. Although serious AEs occurred, none were determined to be treatment-related. These findings collectively indicate that the single-dose regimen of baloxavir demonstrated favorable tolerability in Chinese pediatric patients, with no new safety issue identified.

The median time to alleviation of influenza signs and symptoms in this study (150.17 h) was comparable to that observed in previous pediatric study (163.7 h in MiniSTONE-1 and 138.1 h in MiniSTONE-2; see Table S9 in the supplementary materials). While the fever resolution rate was identical to that in the miniSTONE-1 and miniSTONE-2 studies, the rates of alleviation of influenza signs and symptoms, symptoms resolution, and return to normal health and activity were lower than in miniSTONE-2 and higher than in miniSTONE-1. This may be attributed to the differences in data collection duration (up to day 15 in MiniSTONE-2 vs. only day 10 in the present study) and baseline differences between the age groups. Notably, the MiniSTONE-2 study reported a numerically shorter median duration of fever with baloxavir than with oseltamivir [41.2 h (95% CI 24.5–45.7) vs. 46.8 h (95% CI 30.0–53.5)]. Similarly, a real-world study of Chinese children aged 0–18 years demonstrated significantly shorter mean duration of fever in the baloxavir group (1.43 days) than in the oseltamivir group (2.31 days) [13]. In our study, the median duration of fever aligned closely with the baloxavir group in these prior studies. Rapid fever reduction not only alleviates patient discomfort and lowers complication risks but may also mitigate economic burdens by reducing parental/guardian work absenteeism for childcare.

Seasonal influenza readily transmits through respiratory droplets, spreading especially rapidly within households. This transmissibility originates from viral shedding in the respiratory tract, which can begin before symptoms onset and typically persist for 3–8 days in adults and older children, longer in young children, and may last up to 1–3 weeks in infants [3]. The CENTERSTONE trial demonstrated that a single oral dose of baloxavir decreased the incidence of transmission of influenza virus to close contacts [7]. In two oseltamivir-controlled Phase 3 trials [12, 16], baloxavir reduced infectious virus titers more rapidly and viral shedding duration more briefly than oseltamivir in influenza patients. Consistent with these findings, the current trial observed a rapid decline of infectious virus titer within 24 h and a swift cessation of viral shedding, mirroring results from two global studies in children under 12 years old (Table S10 in the supplementary material). These data collectively suggest that baloxavir may reduce virus transmission from children aged 1–4 years.

Children have more sensitive taste buds than adults, and poor palatability affects compliance [17]. Baloxavir dry suspension, a strawberry-flavored product specially designed for children, has not had its palatability validated in large-scale clinical trials since market approval. Our study showed that baloxavir for oral suspension achieved relatively good taste acceptability and preference among children aged 1–4 years.

In the present study, age-based subgroup analysis revealed no clinically relevant impact of age on recovery outcomes, including the duration of fever and time to symptom resolution. However, compared with children aged ≥ 2 to < 5 years, the subgroup of children aged ≥ 1 to < 2 years showed a lower recovery rate in terms of returning to normal health and activity, as well as differences in viral clearance dynamics. These findings align with observations from other antiviral agents and are likely attributable to baseline difference between the age groups, including immature immune system development and ongoing maturation of organ function in younger children. However, confirmation of these subgroup findings will require future studies with larger sample sizes, as the previous studies of baloxavir lacks the fine-grained age stratification evidence.

Two previous studies conducted in adults and adolescents—one involving a high-risk population (study 1602T0832) and the other an otherwise healthy population (study 1601T0831)—demonstrated that the high-risk population experienced a longer time to alleviation of symptoms (77.0 vs. 53.7 h) and fever reduction (30.8 vs. 24.5 h) than the otherwise healthy population in the baloxavir group, but both groups showed superior outcomes to the oseltamivir group [15, 16]. These findings underscore the critical importance of early antiviral treatment in patients with high-risk factors for severe/critical influenza. In the current study, the proportion of subjects with high-risk factors experienced longer times to recovery compared to those without. Given the limited sample size of this non-high-risk subset, these subgroup results warrant cautious interpretation. Nevertheless, in alignment with the previous adult and adolescent study results, the current data further support that pediatric patients, especially those at elevated risk of influenza complications, require early and aggressive antiviral treatment with baloxavir to optimize clinical outcomes.

This study has several limitations. First, the single-arm design can provide preliminary safety and efficacy assessments, but may affect the interpretation of efficacy and safety outcomes. Nevertheless, the study data of safety and efficacy were analyzed as scientifically as possible by comparing the results with extensive historical data for both baloxavir and oseltamivir. Second, although it was a multicenter trial involving 100 patients from 10 Chinese centers and showed good nationwide representativeness, external validation is needed to confirm generalizability to other populations or regions, and in broader populations. Third, all the enrolled subjects were infected with influenza A. According to weekly influenza surveillance data collected during the study period, 98.1–99.7% of influenza-positive specimens were influenza A (H1N1), while influenza B and A (H3N2) accounted for collectively less than 2% [18]. These findings suggest that the study population predominantly comprised influenza A (H1N1) cases. Further research on influenza B and A (H3N2) subtypes is warranted.

Conclusion

This study has demonstrated that a single, weight-adjusted oral dose of baloxavir was well tolerated, effectively alleviated influenza signs and symptoms, and accelerated viral clearance in Chinese pediatric patients aged 1 to < 5 years with influenza. Favorable palatability was reported by over 70% of participants. Overall, these findings highlight baloxavir as a valuable therapeutic option characterized by a simplified and convenient single dose oral regimen, of particular value for children aged 1 to < 5 years in China, where streamlined treatment approaches are critically needed.

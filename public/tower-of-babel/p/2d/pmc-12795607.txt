Generation of human induced pluripotent stem cell lines derived from four patients with a pathogenic ALPK3 variant associated with adult-onset hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM)
Authors: Chanatjit Cheawsamoot, Rohin Ramchandani, Mohamed Ameen, Jennifer Arthur Ataam, Apichai Khongphatthanayothin, Vorasuk Shotelersuk, Ioannis Karakikes
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Loss of function variants in ALPK3 have been associated with dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM). However, the underlying pathomechanism remain largely unknown. Here, we generated human iPSC lines from four HCM patients carrying the heterozygous pathogenic variant in ALPK3 (c.2023delC p.Gln675fs). Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from patients were reprogrammed to induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) with the Sendai virus-based reprogramming method. All four lines display typical iPSC morphology, normal karyotype, expression of pluripotency-associated markers, and trilineage differentiation potential. These iPSC lines represent a valuable resource of ALPK3 patient-derived iPSC lines to the study ALPK3-associated cardiomyopathy.

Resource utility

The molecular mechanism for ALPK3-associated cardiomyopathy remains unknown. Previous approaches to studying the disease have utilised ALPK3-knockout iPSC-cardiomyocytes and ALPK3-overexpressing cells but no significant discovery has been made. Patient-derived iPSCs represent a valuable resource to studying disease and is suitable for use in a wide range of investigations downstream.

Resource details

Alpha kinase 3 (ALPK3) is a relatively poorly characterised atypical kinase. Biallelic variants in ALPK3 have been linked to early-onset severe DCM that leads to heart failure and early death in many patients, with monoallelic variants leading to later-onset HCM (Almomani et al., 2016; Cheawsamoot et al., 2020). The symptoms of biallelic ALPK3-associated cardiomyopathy involve intrauterine or infantile onset of DCM which then progresses to HCM after birth or during adulthood. Most biallelic patients die of heart failure and some display dysmorphic features such as low set ears, high arched palate, skeletal muscle underdevelopment, and multiple pterygia. Patients with monoallelic pathogenic variants are characterized by apical left ventricular hypertrophy and one heterozygous carrier reported to date has died of heart failure and atrial fibrillation (Cheawsamoot et al., 2020). Heart tissues from ALPK3 patients show cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, subendocardial fibroelastosis and cardiomyocyte disarray (Phelan et al., 2016). In a cohort of adult-onset HCM, 1.56% of patients were found to carry a heterozygous truncating variant in ALPK3; 9% of these patients then died of heart failure or required heart transplantation (Herkert et al., 2020). Overall, this points to ALPK3 causing a wide spectrum of cardiomyopathy phenotype with risk of heart failure and early death. Previously, ALPK3-knockout iPSCs and ALPK3-knockout mice have been used to investigate ALPK3-associated cardiomyopathy but the mechanism of disease remains poorly understood.

The c.2023delC p.(Gln675fs) variant has been reported in homozygous form in an infant male DCM patient and in heterozygous form in a large Thai family with eight members affected with adult-onset HCM. PBMCs from four HCM patients carrying the heterozygous c.2023delC p. (Gln675fs) variant were reprogrammed with the CytoTuneTM-iPS 2.0 Sendai virus reprogramming system. One clone from each line was selected and expanded under feeder-free conditions (37 °C, 5% CO2). All lines showed typical iPSC morphology, forming compact colonies with distinct borders and high nuclear to cytoplasm ratio (Fig. 1A). All lines express the pluripotency markers OCT3/4, Nanog, and TRA-1–60 by immunostaining (Fig. 1B). Trilineage differentiation potential were assessed by differentiating iPSCs with the STEMdiff Trilineage Differentiation Kit (Stem Cell Technologies) and expression of endoderm-, mesoderm-, and ectoderm-associated markers were assessed with the STEMdiff Trilineage qPCR Array (Stem Cell Technologies) (Fig. 1C). The presence of the c.2023delC p.(Gln675fs) variant was confirmed in all lines by Sanger sequencing (Fig. 1E). The absence of chromosomal abnormalities were confirmed with the KaryoStat™ Assay (Thermo Fisher Scientific) (Fig. S1A). Clearance of the Sendai viral transcript SeV was confirmed by RT-qPCR. Cell line identity between PBMCs and the resulting iPSC lines were confirmed by STR analysis on 10 genetic loci based on the GenePrint® 10 System (Promega). All cell lines were free of Mycoplasma. The characterization of the lines was performed around passages 20–25. In summary, we generated four new iPSC lines with a heterozygous pathogenic ALPK3 variant. All lines have been validated (See Table 1) and are, therefore, a suitable human cellular model for ALPK3-associated cardiomyopathy.

Materials and methods

Reprogramming and cell maintenance

PBMCs from patients were reprogrammed using the CytoTune™-iPS 2.0 Sendai Reprogramming Kit (Thermo Fisher) as previously described (Feyen et al., 2021). Briefly, PBMCs cultured in StemPro™−34 Serum Free Media (Thermo Fisher) supplemented with cytokines (SCF 100 ng/mL, FLT-3 100 ng/mL, IL-3 20 ng/mL, IL-6 20 ng/mL, Epo 2U/ml) for 15 days and infected with Sendai virus particles expressing the four ‘Yamanaka’ factors (OCT4, SOX2, KLF4, and c-MYC). The cells were incubated for 24 h at 37 °C 5% CO2 and plated onto Geltrex-coated plates (Corning) and kept in StemPro-34 SFM without cytokines. At day 4 post-transduction, cells were gradually transitioned to Essential 8™ Medium (Gibco). On day 12–20, individual clones showing typical iPSC morphology were manually selected and transferred to Geltrex-coated plates. The iPSCs were passaged every 3–4 days and maintained in StemMACS™ iPS-Brew XF media (Miltenyi Biotec).

RNA isolation, reverse transcription, and qPCR

Total RNA was extracted using Trizol and transcribed to cDNA using High-Capacity RNA-to-cDNA™ Kit (Applied Biosystems). qPCR reactions with gene-specific TaqMan probes were run in a QuantStudio™ 7 Flex Real-Time PCR System (20 s at 95 °C; 40 cycles of 1 s at 95 °C and 20 s at 60 °C and 15 s at 95 °C with a final extension stage for 1 min at 60 °C).

Immunofluorescence

iPSCs were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and permeabilized with 0.2% PBS-Triton X. Following blocking with 2% BSA and 2% FBS, the primary antibodies (Table 2) were incubated at 4 °C overnight. Cells were washed 3x with PBS and secondary antibodies (Table 2) were applied for 45 min at room temperature. Nuclei were stained with DAPI and fluorescence images acquired using a BZ-X800 microscope (Keyence).

Chromosomal microarray

A pellet containing 2 × 106 cells for each line were sent to Thermo Scientific (USA) for the KaryoStat+ assay (Thermo Scientific, USA).

Trilineage differentiation

iPSCs were differentiated into the three germ layers using the STEMdiff™ Trilineage Differentiation Kit (STEMCELL Technologies) per manufacturer’s instructions. RT-qPCR was performed using the hPSC Trilineage Differentiation qPCR Array (catalog #07515, STEMCELL Technologies), which includes primers for detection of 90 genes expressed in either undifferentiated iPSCs or each of the three germ layers. The fold changes in gene expression were determined using the ΔΔCt method with normalization to the housekeeping gene GAPDH.

STR analysis

Genomic DNA was extracted from iPSCs and parental PBMCs using the DNeasy® Blood & Tissue Kit (Qiagen). STR analysis for 10 loci was performed using the Promega GenePrint 10 system. The PCR products were ran on the ABI 3130xl Genetic Analyzer and analysed using the Thermo Fisher Connect Microsatellite Analysis software (Thermo Fisher).

Genotyping

Genotyping was performed by Sanger sequencing using primers specific to ALPK3 exon 5 to include the variant region (Table 2). The PCR was run as per the following protocol: 2 min at 95 °C [initial denaturation]; 35 cycles of 15 s at 95 °C, 15 s at 55 °C, and 35 s at 72 °C [amplification]; 5 min at 72 °C [final extension].

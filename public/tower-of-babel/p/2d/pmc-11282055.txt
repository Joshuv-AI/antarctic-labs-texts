Unveiling the adverse events of Nusinersen in spinal muscular atrophy management based on FAERS database
Authors: Ying Jiang, Yuan Shen, Qin Zhou, Haohao Zhu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This study aims to collect and analyze adverse event (AE) reports related to Nusinersen from the FAERS database. The study employed a combination of signal quantification techniques, including the Reporting Odds Ratio (ROR), Proportional Reporting Ratio (PRR), Bayesian Confidence Propagation Neural Network (BCPNN), and Multi-item Gamma Poisson Shrinker (MGPS), to enhance the accuracy of signal detection and reduce the risk of false positives or negatives. Between the first quarter of 2017 and the third quarter of 2023, the FAERS database collected a total of 11,485,105 drug AE reports, of which 5772 were related to Nusinersen. Through signal mining analysis, 218 preferred term (PT) signals involving 27 system organ classes (SOCs) were identified. The study discovered AEs related to metabolism and nutrition disorders, psychiatric disorders, and cardiac disorders SOCs, which were not mentioned in the product information. Additionally, complications directly related to the intrathecal administration of Nusinersen, such as increased CSF pressure, positive CSF red blood cell count, and AEs related to the method of drug use, such as neuromuscular scoliosis and cerebrospinal fluid reservoir placement, were highlighted. Notably, AEs related to renal function abnormalities, such as abnormal Urine protein/creatinine ratio and protein urine presence, showed higher frequency and signal strength. The findings of this study emphasize the importance of comprehensive safety monitoring in the clinical application of Nusinersen. These results are significant for guiding future clinical practices, improving disease management strategies, and developing safer treatment protocols.

Introduction

Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA) is an autosomal recessive genetic neuromuscular disease characterized by progressive, symmetrical muscle weakness and atrophy, primarily in the proximal limbs, posing a severe threat to the health and lives of infants and young children1–3. With a deeper understanding of SMA's pathogenesis, effective treatment approaches have emerged, including Nusinersen, the first orphan drug to treat SMA, approved by the FDA in December 2016, filling a significant therapeutic gap4.

Nusinersen is a modified antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) designed to bind specifically to the intronic sequence downstream of exon 7 in SMN2 transcripts, increasing the inclusion of exon 7 in SMN2 mRNA transcripts and the production of full-length SMN protein4. Due to its large molecular weight, Nusinersen cannot cross the blood–brain barrier and is thus administered intrathecally, distributing in the central nervous system and peripheral tissues. It is metabolized through hydrolytic reactions mediated by nucleases (3′ and 5′). Nusinersen is not a substrate of cytochrome P450 enzymes, and inhibitors or inducers of these enzymes do not affect its metabolism5.

The approval of Nusinersen was based on positive data from multiple clinical studies. Biogen announced data from the Phase III clinical study CHERISH6, conducted in patients with later-onset SMA. This 15-month study enrolled 126 non-ambulatory patients with later-onset SMA, aged between 2 and 12 years. After 15 months of Nusinersen treatment, patients showed a mean increase of 4.0 points in the Hammersmith functional motor scale-expanded (HFMSE), compared to a mean decrease of 1.9 points in untreated patients, indicating significant therapeutic effects. According to the Phase I clinical pharmacology trial of Nusinersen, its concentration in cerebrospinal fluid rapidly declined within 20 h after administration, with a slower decrease between 7 and 14 days, and relative stability after 15 days7. Therefore, the initial treatment schedule for the first four loading doses is on days 1, 15, 30, and 60. The estimated half-life of Nusinersen in cerebrospinal fluid is approximately 132 to 177 days, requiring a maintenance dose every four months to maintain a relatively stable concentration in the cerebrospinal fluid8.

Due to its high cost, approximately $125,000 per injection, the first-year treatment cost for Nusinersen is about $750,000 with six injections, followed by an annual cost of about $375,000 for three injections per year. In February 2019, Nusinersen was approved for marketing in China. On January 1, 2022, it was officially included in the national medical insurance directory, significantly reducing the financial burden on families using the drug. This is undoubtedly good news for the vast number of SMA patients in China, but data on the pharmacokinetics of the drug in the Chinese population and post-marketing drug monitoring are still lacking9. Currently, multicenter, non-interventional post-marketing surveillance studies on Nusinersen have been initiated10, and it is expected that Nusinersen will continue to enrich the real-world application experience of SMA treatment in China, creating ongoing value for society.

In this context, mining and analyzing the adverse events (AEs) of Nusinersen based on the Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) of the U.S. Food and Drug Administration becomes particularly important. This is not only instrumental in evaluating the safety and efficacy of the medication but also provides crucial information for its future clinical applications11–14. This article aims to explore the real-world application of Spinraza in the medical environment, with a special focus on its potential adverse reactions, in the hope of providing a safer and more effective treatment plan for SMA patients.

Materials and methods

Data source and extraction

The data for this study were sourced from the FAERS, covering reports on Nusinersen from the first quarter of 2017 to the third quarter of 2023. Using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA)15, AE reports were organized and categorized based on Preferred Terms (PTs) and System Organ Classes (SOCs). We filtered for AE reports where Nusinersen was considered the primary suspect drug and excluded duplicate reports and those that identified product storage errors as AE risk signals, to reduce bias.

Data mining

This study employed disproportionality analysis for signal mining, which includes two main techniques: frequency methods and Bayesian methods. The frequency methods comprise the Reporting Odds Ratio (ROR) and the Proportional Reporting Ratio (PRR), while the Bayesian methods involve the Bayesian Confidence Propagation Neural Network (BCPNN) and the Empirical Bayesian Geometric Mean (EBGM)16–19. Results

During the period from the first quarter of 2017 to the third quarter of 2023, the FAERS database collected 11,485,105 adverse drug event reports, among which 5,772 were related to Nusinersen. Through signal mining analysis, 218 PT signals involving 27 SOCs were identified.

Basic information on AEs

During the period from the first quarter of 2017 to the third quarter of 2023, the FAERS database collected 11,485,105 AE reports, among which 5772 were related to Nusinersen. Notably, AE reports were most frequent among children under 18, accounting for 37.54% of the total reports for Nusinersen. The year-wise distribution showed the highest number of reports in 2018, accounting for 19.68%, followed by 2019 at 16.75%, and 2021 at 16.34%. The identity of the reporters was mainly consumers, accounting for 60.24% of all reports, followed by physicians at 27.79%, pharmacists at 6.32%, and other health professionals at 5.49%. The majority of reports originated from the United States (75.66%), while other countries like Brazil, Turkey, Argentina, and China had relatively fewer reports. Among the outcomes of the reported AEs, Hospitalization—Initial or Prolonged had the highest proportion at 31.24%, followed by Death at 10.36%. At the PT level, based on the results of the most stringent algorithm EBGM, the top 50 PTs are displayed in Table 5. Results indicate that PTs with high signal strength included Post lumbar puncture syndrome, Traumatic lumbar puncture, Neuromuscular scoliosis, Cerebrospinal fluid reservoir placement, Spinal muscular atrophy. Notably, our study has identified several adverse events with significant signal strength that are not listed in the current product leaflet as associated risks. These include increased cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) pressure, positive CSF red blood cell count, increased CSF white blood cell count, and cerebrospinal fluid leakage. While other AEs such as neuromuscular scoliosis, cerebrospinal fluid reservoir placement, and spinal implantation are mentioned in the leaflet, our findings suggest that the frequency and severity of these events may be higher than previously recognized, warranting further investigation as potential risks. At the same time, Post lumbar puncture syndrome, Procedural pain, Scoliosis, Rhinovirus infection had high occurrence frequency and signal strength, consistent with the descriptions in the drug leaflet. Discussion

As the first drug globally approved for the treatment of SMA in both children and adults, Nusinersen has demonstrated efficacy in changing the natural course of SMA in high-evidence-level randomized controlled clinical trials and further confirmed its positive contribution to SMA treatment in real-world studies. The drug is currently marketed in several countries, including the United States, Canada, Brazil, Europe, Australia, Japan, and Russia, and has been involved in key studies in different types of SMA, such as the ENDEAR study for Type 1 SMA, the CHERISH study for Types 2/3 SMA, the NURTURE study for asymptomatic SMA, and the long-term SHINE study20,21. Moreover, continuous monitoring of AEs and safety data of Nusinersen is crucial for a comprehensive assessment of its risk–benefit ratio. This study provides significant supplementation to the safety profile of Nusinersen, especially in the real-world environment after its global marketing. Based on FAERS data, this study reveals the safety characteristics of Nusinersen in a wide patient population and systematically categorizes and evaluates its AEs.

This study observed that the highest proportion of AE reports was in children under 18 years. SMA is primarily diagnosed in infancy and early childhood, which correlates with a higher prevalence of treatment initiation in these younger age groups. As such, the larger number of adverse event reports in pediatric patients may reflect the higher proportion of these patients receiving treatment rather than an inherent increased risk of adverse events compared to adults. The peak in report numbers in 2018 could be related to the widespread attention and usage of the drug following its market introduction. Consumers being the main reporters may reflect a high public interest in drug safety and active participation in medication use. In contrast, reports by physicians and other health professionals were less common. The majority of reports originated from the United States, possibly due to the leading position of the U.S. in the SMA treatment drug market and geographical and usage biases of the FAERS system. This suggests the need to consider regional biases in data interpretation and highlights the importance of establishing and improving drug monitoring systems in other countries and regions. The higher rate of AEs within 30 days of medication use may point to drug side effects or related complications in the early stages of treatment, emphasizing the importance of close monitoring of patients during this period.

Known AEs

The NURTURE study particularly emphasized the importance of early intervention, showing that treatment of SMA infants with Nusinersen before the onset of clinical symptoms resulted in significant improvements in survival rates and motor function compared to the natural progression of the disease20. No patients in this study discontinued or dropped out due to AEs. The ENDEAR, CHERISH, and SHINE studies21,22, as well as studies in adult SMA patients, all confirmed Nusinersen's clinical benefits across different SMA subtypes, age groups, and disease progressions, with good tolerability. However, not all patients showed significant clinical improvements. Studies in Type 1 SMA patients indicated that the earlier the age at the start of treatment, the more significant the improvement in motor function, and this improvement was independent of the number of SMN gene copies. The SHINE study found that after three years of Nusinersen treatment, Type 2 SMA children showed more significant motor function improvement compared to Type 3 SMA children. In a study involving an older cohort of Type 3 SMA patients, limited improvement in motor function was observed after 10 months of treatment, not reaching statistical significance.

These results all point to a common conclusion: while Nusinersen offers unprecedented clinical benefits for SMA treatment, its effectiveness is closely related to the timing of intervention. This finding is crucial for optimizing SMA treatment strategies, especially when considering treatment plans and disease management decisions.

In the detailed analysis of AE reports related to Nusinersen, General disorders and administration site conditions, Infections and infestations, and Injury, poisoning and procedural complications were consistent with its pharmacological action and indications23. Notably, AEs in Metabolism and nutrition disorders, Psychiatric disorders, and Cardiac disorders SOCs were not mentioned in the leaflet. These newly identified risk signals may point to unknown side effects of the drug, necessitating further clinical attention and research. At the more specific PT level, due to the intrathecal administration route of Nusinersen, AEs such as Post lumbar puncture syndrome, Traumatic lumbar puncture, Neuromuscular scoliosis, Cerebrospinal fluid reservoir placement, and Spinal muscular atrophy were more frequent. Additionally, this study uncovered several AEs not mentioned in the drug leaflet, such as Neuromuscular scoliosis, Cerebrospinal fluid reservoir placement, CSF pressure increased, Urine protein/creatinine ratio abnormal, Protein urine present, etc. This indicates that signal quantification techniques in drug AE monitoring can reveal potential risk information.

Newly identified potential risks

While well-documented adverse events like thrombocytopenia are important for clinical awareness, the primary focus of this study is to uncover and analyze adverse events that have not been previously reported in the context of Nusinersen treatment. Complications related to intrathecal injection

Complications related to the intrathecal administration of Nusinersen, such as increased CSF pressure, positive CSF red blood cell count, increased CSF white blood cell count, and cerebrospinal fluid leakage, reflect the unique risks and challenges of this route of administration. Intrathecal injections can alter cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) circulation and pressure, leading to increased pressure or CSF leakage. CSF leakage can cause headaches, symptoms of irritative meningitis, or other related complications24,25. The injection process may cause minor vascular damage, leading to slight bleeding and a temporary increase in the CSF red blood cell count. Although most cases of such minor bleeding are harmless, they can present as an increase in red blood cells in CSF analysis, necessitating differentiation from more severe conditions like subarachnoid hemorrhage. The stimulation during the injection process may activate immune cells in the CSF, leading to a temporary increase in white blood cell count. Additionally, if pathogens are introduced during the injection process, this could trigger an infectious response, further increasing white blood cell counts26.

Overall, these complications reflect the specific risks associated with the intrathecal route of administration. Therefore, when treating with Nusinersen, physicians need to carefully consider these potential risks and conduct appropriate assessments and monitoring of patients before and after treatment to detect and manage these possible complications early.

Limitations and future prospects

The data in this study comes from FAERS, a spontaneous reporting system, and may be subject to reporting bias, with some AEs possibly being over-reported due to increased attention. Additionally, the lack of a control group makes it difficult to determine whether AEs are directly caused by Nusinersen or are due to the patient's underlying condition or other factors. Furthermore, as FAERS data is primarily from the United States, it may not fully reflect the situation of the global patient population, particularly regarding genetic backgrounds, treatment habits, and healthcare resource differences across regions. The completeness of the reported information may also limit the depth of analysis of the causes of AEs.

Future research should consider establishing multicenter, multinational collaborations to obtain more comprehensive and diverse safety data. Implementing prospective studies would help to more accurately assess the safety and efficacy of Nusinersen. Additionally, exploring the relationship between patients' genetic backgrounds and drug responses could provide important information for personalized treatment. Further research is needed to clarify the causal relationship between newly identified potential AEs and Nusinersen treatment.

Conclusion

This study, through a multi-dimensional and multi-level analysis, provides solid scientific support for the safety assessment of Nusinersen in the treatment of Spinal Muscular Atrophy (SMA). Since its launch in China in February 2019, Nusinersen, as the first FDA-approved drug for the treatment of SMA, has significantly relieved the economic burden on patients' families, especially since its inclusion in the national medical insurance catalog from January 1, 2022. Although Nusinersen has shown significant clinical benefits in the treatment of SMA, this study has found a series of potential AEs associated with its use, the risks of which are not fully emphasized in the drug leaflet. Specifically, complications directly related to the intrathecal injection method, such as increased CSF pressure and positive CSF red blood cell count, as well as AEs related to the method of drug use, such as Neuromuscular scoliosis and Cerebrospinal fluid reservoir placement, are noteworthy. Notably, AEs related to renal function abnormalities, such as abnormal Urine protein/creatinine ratio and Protein urine present, show higher occurrence frequency and signal strength, indicating they might be new potential risks associated with Nusinersen treatment. These findings highlight areas that require further attention and research.

Overall, this study provides important safety information for the treatment of SMA patients and offers valuable references for healthcare providers, regulatory authorities, and pharmaceutical companies in evaluating and improving SMA treatment strategies. Future continuous monitoring and research on the long-term safety and efficacy of Nusinersen will be crucial for enhancing treatment quality and patient safety.

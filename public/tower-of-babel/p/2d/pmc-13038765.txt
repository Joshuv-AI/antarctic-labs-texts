Development of a machine learning model for predicting urosepsis after ureteroscopic lithotripsy
Authors: Aobing Mei, Sudan Zeng, Wen Zhou, Miaoqing Cai, Jiamei Wang, Xu Sun, Mei Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To develop a machine-learning-based predictive model for postoperative urinary sepsis following ureteroscopic lithotripsy, providing a scientific basis for early clinical identification of high-risk patients. A total of 927 patients who underwent ureteroscopic lithotripsy at a Grade III-A hospital in Guizhou Province from September 2024 to September 2025 were enrolled as the study subjects. Clinical data were collected and randomly divided into a training set (70%) and a test set (30%) according to a 7:3 ratio. Four machine-learning algorithms—SVM, LR, RF, and XGBoost—were used to construct predictive models. The performance of each model was comprehensively evaluated using metrics such as the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), accuracy, precision, and F1 score. The clinical utility of each model was further assessed using decision curve analysis (DCA) and calibration curves to identify the optimal model. The SHAP method was then employed to analyze the contribution of each variable in the optimal model. The AUC values for the four models—SVM, LR, RF, and XGBoost—were 0.96 (0.93–0.98), 0.95 (0.91–0.98), 0.98 (0.96–1.00), and 0.98 (0.97–1.00), respectively, with the XGBoost model demonstrating the best performance. Variable importance analysis based on the optimal model revealed that the top six key predictors were procalcitonin, albumin, degree of hydronephrosis,5-frailty score, maximum stone diameter, and urinary tract infection.Machine-learning models can effectively predict the risk of postoperative urinary sepsis following ureteroscopic lithotripsy, with the XGBoost model performing the best. The key variables—including patient functional status, stone characteristics, and immune indicators—provide a scientific basis for early identification of uroseptic sepsis and offer a quantifiable decision-making tool for precise prevention and management of postoperative complications.

Urinary tract stones are a common disease with steadily increasing incidence and recurrence rates worldwide, affecting approximately 10% to 20% of the adult population [1]. In China, the incidence of urinary tract stones has been rising year by year in recent years, making the country one of the world’s three regions with the highest prevalence of stone formation [2]. With continuous innovation and development of various auxiliary devices, traditional open surgery has been largely replaced by minimally invasive procedures, including extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy (ESWL), percutaneous nephrolithotomy (PCNL), and ureteroscopy (URS). Among these, ureteroscopy has demonstrated particularly outstanding performance in terms of stone clearance rate and surgical safety [3]. However, even after such procedures, there remains a risk of postoperative complications such as fever, pyelonephritis, and urosepticemia. Urosepticemia is one of the most severe complications arising from urinary tract infections, with an overall postoperative incidence ranging from 5% to 17.8% [4, 5]. Once it progresses to severe sepsis, septic shock, or even multiple organ dysfunction syndrome, the mortality rate can soar to 20% to 40%. This not only prolongs hospital stays and increases medical costs for patients but also poses a serious threat to their lives [6]. Therefore, early prediction and intervention are crucial for reducing the risk of postoperative urosepticemia. Currently available clinical scoring tools for predicting risk factors associated with urosepticemia are inadequate; most existing predictive models rely on traditional linear regression, which depends on strict linear assumptions and struggles to capture nonlinear relationships and interactions among clinical variables. Moreover, these models have limited capacity to handle high-dimensional data, resulting in relatively low prediction accuracy [7]. In contrast to traditional regression models, machine learning algorithms learn from provided datasets to make inferences. They not only enable more precise prediction and decision support by considering clinical features but also allow for the development of personalized treatment plans and targeted interventions for individual patients [8]. In this study, based on clinical data, we aim to explore the factors influencing the occurrence of postoperative urosepticemia in patients with ureteral stones. We will employ various machine learning algorithms to construct and validate a risk-prediction model for urosepticemia, with the goal of providing healthcare professionals with a tool that can identify and stratify the risk of postoperative urosepticemia at an early stage, thereby alleviating patient burden and pain.

Materials and methods

Study Population. This study is a multicenter retrospective observational cohort conducted at two independent Grade III hospitals. A total of 927 patients who underwent ureteroscopy (URS) at the Second People’s Hospital of Guiyang from September 2024 to September 2025 were enrolled for model development and internal validation. An external validation cohort of 325 patients was obtained from the Affiliated Hospital of Guizhou Medical University, with treatment periods ranging from October 2024 to January 2025. (1) Inclusion Criteria: ① Age ≥ 18 years; ② Patients diagnosed with ureteral stones; ③ Patients who strictly adhered to the relevant criteria outlined in the “2022 European Association of Urology Guidelines on Urolithiasis” [9]for ureterolithotripsy. (2) Exclusion Criteria: ① Patients with congenital abnormalities such as polycystic kidney disease or ureteral malformations; ② Patients with other systemic infectious diseases; ③ Patients with tumors affecting the blood or immune systems; ④ Patients with uncontrolled severe coagulation disorders. The diagnosis of urinary tract infection following surgery was confirmed within 24 h based on laboratory tests, using the Sepsis 3.0 diagnostic criteria established by the European Society of Critical Care Medicine [10]. A SOFA (Sequential Organ Failure Assessment) score ≥ 2 points indicated the presence of urinary tract infection. The SOFA score ranges from 0 to 24 points and assesses six organ systems—kidney, respiratory, liver, circulation, coagulation, and nervous system—each contributing 1 point upon fulfillment of a corresponding criterion. This study has been approved by the Medical Ethics Committee (Approval No.: 2024-Lun-Shen-No. 198).

To mitigate the impact of missing data on the analysis results, this study employed a combination of deletion and imputation methods. The specific principles were as follows: If the missing rate for a variable exceeded 30%, that variable was directly deleted to avoid introducing excessive noise into the model. For data with a missing rate below 30%, imputation methods were used to preserve the sample size. During the imputation process, differentiated strategies were implemented based on variable type to balance computational efficiency and model robustness: For categorical variables, mode imputation was used to maintain their central distribution tendency; For continuous variables, median imputation was chosen to reduce the interference of outliers on the data distribution and enhance the robustness of the imputation results.

Methods for collecting clinical characteristics

(1) Data Collection: Based on a review of literature including systematic reviews [11] and expert consensus, we consulted experts with extensive clinical experience in urology and conducted a panel discussion with the research team to develop a questionnaire specifically designed to identify risk factors associated with ureteroscopic lithotripsy patients. All included clinical indicators were collected from patient admission and preoperative assessments, covering the following aspects: ① General Demographics: age, gender, body temperature, respiratory rate, diabetes, hypertension, and body mass index (BMI); ② Surgery-Related Factors: number of previous stone surgeries, whether a double-J stent was placed prior to surgery, maximum diameter of the stone, presence of ureteral stricture, whether the stone caused obstruction, stone location, number of stones (solitary or multiple), and degree of hydronephrosis; ③ Clinical Laboratory Indices: C-reactive protein, white blood cell count, urinary white blood cells, neutrophil count, platelet count, total bilirubin, albumin, urea, creatinine, and procalcitonin; ④ Nursing Assessment Indicators: duration of stone-related pain lasting ≥ 72 h, non-independent functional status (determined using the Barthel Index from the Activities of Daily Living Scale), and the five-item frailty score. (2) Data Preprocessing: To minimize the impact of missing data on the analysis results, this study employed a combined approach of deletion and imputation. The specific principles are as follows: If the missing rate of a particular variable exceeds 30%, the variable will be directly deleted to avoid introducing excessive noise into the model; for variables with missing rates below 30%, imputation methods will be applied to preserve the sample size. During imputation, this study adopted differentiated strategies based on variable type to balance computational efficiency and model robustness: For categorical variables, the mode was used for imputation to maintain the central tendency of their category distribution; for continuous variables, the median was selected for imputation to reduce the influence of extreme values on the data distribution and enhance the stability of the imputed results.1.6 Statistical Methods.

Data processing

Statistical analysis and modeling were performed using SPSS 27.0 and Python 3.8 software. Quantitative data conforming to a normal distribution are presented as mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s); between-group comparisons were conducted using an independent-samples t-test. Quantitative data that did not follow a normal distribution are presented as median (quartiles) [M (P25, P75)]; between-group comparisons were performed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Qualitative data are presented as frequencies and percentages (%); between-group comparisons were conducted using the chi-square test, Fisher’s exact probability test, or nonparametric tests. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Prediction models were constructed using four machine learning methods: Random Forest (RF), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Support Vector Machines (SVM), and Logistic Regression. Univariate analysis was used to screen for predictors of urinary sepsis; subsequently, feature importance ranking combined with the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) was employed to further identify predictive factors. After determining the optimal hyperparameters and building the model, the model’s predictive performance was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). To validate the model internally, a bootstrap resampling method was applied to generate multiple independent evaluation sets through repeated sampling with replacement, thereby providing a robust estimate of the model’s generalization ability. The performance of each model was comprehensively assessed using metrics such as accuracy, precision, and F1 score. Decision curve analysis (DCA) and calibration curves were utilized to evaluate the clinical usability of the final model and to identify the optimal model. The predictive power of the four machine learning algorithms was quantified by the AUC of their respective ROC curves. The SHAP tool was used to rank the contributions of the optimal model’s parameters.2. Results.

Result

Data Collection and Preprocessing: A total of 1,465 cells were missing in the dataset, resulting in an overall missing rate of 4.27%. No variable had a missing rate exceeding 50%, and all variables met the data quality requirements for modeling. Among the 83.82% of patients with incomplete data, 67.1% were missing only 1–2 variables (316 + 306).For example, no patients had data rendered unusable due to excessive missing values; the missing rates for CRP (overall missing rate: 42.93%) and urinary red blood cell count (39.05%) both exceeded 30%. Based on clinical practice analysis, these high levels of missing data likely stem from differences in testing indications among non-sepsis patients. Retaining such variables with high missing rates could introduce modeling bias; therefore, we excluded these two variables from the analytical model. The sepsis group exhibited significantly better data completeness than the non-sepsis group (42.47% vs. 13.93%), possibly because clinical records for critically ill patients tend to be more detailed. We employed Multiple Imputation by Chained Equations (MICE) to impute missing values, using patient age, underlying comorbidities, and relevant laboratory indicators as auxiliary variables to build the imputation model in Table 1.

Univariate analysis of risk factors for urinary-source sepsis

Among the 927 patients who underwent ureteroscopic lithotripsy, 73 developed urinary-source sepsis, while 854 did not. Univariate analysis revealed that the occurrence of urinary-source sepsis following upper urinary tract stone disease was statistically associated with 24 factors (P < 0.05), of which 21 factors showed strong statistical associations (P < 0.001), 2 factors showed moderate statistical associations (P < 0.01), and 1 factor showed a weak statistical association (P < 0.05). Specifically, these included 17 continuous variables: age, heart rate, respiration, BMI, frailty score, number of previous stone procedures, degree of hydronephrosis, procedure duration, maximum diameter of the stone (mm), albumin, creatinine, procalcitonin, white blood cell count, neutrophil count, lymphocyte count, platelet count, and urinary white blood cell count per microliter; as well as 7 categorical variables: gender, non-independent functional status, diabetes, hypertension, urinary tract infection, stone obstruction, stone location, and urine culture results (all P < 0.05).

Feature standardization and selection

This study employs a multi-step feature selection strategy to develop a urinary sepsis prediction model that balances predictive performance with clinical interpretability in Fig. 1. First, the Boruta algorithm, which is based on random forests, is used for feature screening by comparing real features with randomly generated ones.The “shadow features” were iteratively compared, and a total of 17 predictors with statistical significance were identified, including age, heart rate, BMI, frailty score, urinary tract infection, degree of hydronephrosis, surgical duration, stone location, maximum stone diameter, albumin, creatinine, procalcitonin, white blood cell count, neutrophil count, lymphocyte count, platelet count, and urinary white blood cells. The distribution of feature importance was visualized using box plots. LASSO regression was employed to screen the variables that showed statistical significance in the univariate analysis of the modeling dataset, with five-fold cross-validation used to prevent overfitting. The results indicated that, among the variables selected between Lambda.1se (λ = 0.00247) and Lambda.min (λ = 0.0327), nine predictors—gender, frailty score, urinary tract infection, stone obstruction, degree of hydronephrosis, maximum stone diameter, albumin, procalcitonin, and urine culture—were retained. Finally, a Venn diagram was used to compare the feature-selection results obtained by the Boruta and LASSO methods, identifying six core predictors that were consistently confirmed by both approaches (frailty score, urinary tract infection, degree of hydronephrosis, maximum stone diameter, albumin, and procalcitonin), thereby enhancing the robustness of feature selection and improving the interpretability of the results.

Model construction and comparison

We constructed and compared the performance of four machine learning models (SVM, LR, RF, and XGBoost) in predicting urinary sepsis in Fig. 2. On the training set, all models demonstrated excellent discrimination ability, with XGBoost achieving the highest AUC (0.998; 95% CI: 0.99–1.00). Moreover, XGBoost outperformed other models in terms of sensitivity (0.82), accuracy (0.98), and F1 score (0.87), indicating its exceptional fitting capability. On the internal test set, the performance of each model declined slightly; however, XGBoost still maintained the highest F1 score (0.77) and sensitivity (0.74), with an AUC that remained stable at 0.98 (95% CI: 0.97–1.00), suggesting good generalization on the internal data. In the critical external validation phase, model performance showed significant divergence. Both RF and XGBoost achieved an AUC of 0.99 and an accuracy of 0.96; however, RF’s sensitivity was only 0.43, whereas XGBoost’s sensitivity improved to 0.57, and it also had the highest F1 score (0.67). By contrast, SVM and LR exhibited markedly reduced sensitivity and F1 scores in the external validation (both at 0.38 and 0.46, respectively), indicating insufficient generalization capability.

Overall, the XGBoost model demonstrated robust and excellent performance in training, internal testing, and external validation, with particular advantages in sensitivity. This makes it more clinically valuable for the early prediction of urosepsis, effectively reducing the risk of missed diagnoses in Table 2. Discussion

Currently, research on predicting urinary sepsis after ureteroscopic lithotripsy has primarily focused on traditional scoring tools and basic machine-learning models. For example, various EWS (Early Warning Scores) and SIRS (Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome) scores—commonly used to assess septic shock—are largely centered on indicators of organ dysfunction and are typically applied in critical-care settings. However, for sepsis caused by urinary tract infections, the AUC values reported in these studies range only from 0.6 to 0.70 [12, 13]. In previous machine-learning studies, Chen et al. [14]developed a DNN model that combined CT imaging with clinical data, achieving an AUC of 0.88 for sepsis prediction. Yet, this model incorporated highly complex evaluation metrics and exhibited poor interpretability. First, the application of machine learning in preoperative risk assessment has already demonstrated significant advantages. Studies have shown that rehospitalization prediction models built using nursing data can provide a more comprehensive risk assessment, thereby improving patient outcomes [15]. This approach not only enhances prediction accuracy but also provides an important data foundation for clinical decision-support systems.

The XGBoost model developed in this study achieved an AUC of 0.99, significantly outperforming traditional scoring tools and previous machine learning models. Moreover, the key predictive factors—including patient functional status, stone characteristics, immune markers, and underlying diseases—provide a more comprehensive reflection of the pathogenesis of urosepsis.

Procalcitonin (PCT), as a tool for early diagnosis and monitoring of urinary sepsis, has been shown to be highly valuable in numerous studies. One study demonstrated that the area under the ROC curve for PCT in predicting urinary sepsis was 0.960, significantly higher than the 0.634 observed for white blood cell count (WBC) [16]. This indicates that PCT exhibits high sensitivity and specificity in diagnosing and differentiating the severity of urinary sepsis. Moreover, another study highlighted that elevated PCT levels are closely associated with the severity of urinary tract infections, particularly in patients with bacteremia, where PCT levels show a marked increase [17]. Albumin not only serves as a key biomarker for malnutrition but also plays crucial roles in anti-inflammation, antioxidant defense, maintaining plasma colloid osmotic pressure, transporting nutrients and drugs, and more. Hypoalbuminemia weakens the body’s anti-inflammatory capacity, leading to an overactive inflammatory response [18]. Meanwhile, persistent inflammation further inhibits albumin synthesis, creating a vicious cycle [19]. A decline in albumin levels can also affect drug efficacy and tissue perfusion. These mechanisms collectively contribute to a significantly increased risk of developing urinary sepsis, accelerate its progression, and adversely impact disease prognosis. The degree of hydronephrosis is also considered a critical factor determining the necessity of ureteral dilation during ureteroscopic lithotripsy [20–22].

Sexual indicators. In one study, the degree of hydronephrosis was significantly associated with the difficulty of ureteral access during surgery, further suggesting that hydronephrosis not only affects surgical difficulty but may also influence postoperative recovery and the incidence of complications [23]. This indicates that during URSL, the degree of hydronephrosis not only impacts surgical difficulty but may also affect postoperative recovery and the incidence of complications [23]. The study [15] have shown that the 5-item frailty score is a rapid, laboratory-test-free indicator that can be calculated preoperatively and has predictive value for adverse postoperative events. Aging is often accompanied by dysfunction of the liver, kidneys, cardiovascular system, and immune system [24–26]. Elderly patients frequently have comorbidities, including hypertension and diabetes. Once ureteral obstruction occurs, patients are prone to secondary infections, which can progress to systemic inflammatory response syndrome and even sepsis. Some studies suggest that the 5-item frailty index has predictive value for postoperative complications following ureteroscopy and laser lithotripsy for upper urinary tract stones and can facilitate practical preoperative risk assessments for patients [27]. The presence of urinary tract infections implies that the patient’s body harbors high levels of pathogenic bacteria and endotoxins. Pathogenic bacteria are present in the patient’s urine, while the stones themselves harbor abundant bacterial communities [28]. Even preoperative use of antimicrobial agents often fails to completely eradicate the bacterial communities within the stones; during surgery, when the stones are fragmented, these hidden bacterial colonies are released [29]. These released colonies, together with the pathogenic bacteria already present in the urine, greatly increase the risk of postoperative urosepsis [30]. A retrospective study involving data from 20 centers showed that patients with medium-sized stones (10–20 mm) face a 2.25-fold higher risk of residual stone fragments after URS due to unfavorable anatomical constraints that hinder spontaneous stone passage [31]. One study also found that patients with stones longer than 17.5 mm are more likely to develop infections; larger stones contain more bacteria and toxins, leading to prolonged surgical times. Moreover, large stones are more likely to cause obstruction, providing an ideal environment for bacterial proliferation [32]. Complete obstruction prevents urine from being drained from the renal pelvis, resulting in both negative urine cultures and negative routine urinalyses, thereby complicating preoperative diagnosis of the condition [33].

It is worth noting that, according to the guidelines of the European Association of Urology, PCNL is more effective as a single-stage treatment for kidney stones smaller than 2 centimeters, owing to its higher initial success rate and lower rate of auxiliary procedures. Although PCNL outperforms URS in terms of stone clearance, it is associated with longer operative times, longer hospital stays, and a higher incidence of complications. The meta-analysis results of the study by Kan et al [34] demonstrated that the stone clearance rate of RIRS in the treatment of kidney stones measuring 20 to 35 mm ranged from 71% to 95%. RIRS surgical technique minimizes patient trauma to the greatest extent possible, establishing it as a safer and less invasive option for treating upper urinary tract stones. Interestingly, many patients tend to prefer undergoing multiple RIRS procedures over PCNL when choosing their surgical treatment plan. This could potentially lead to an increased incidence of complications with URS.

We acknowledge that the study has certain inherent limitations. First, although this was a two-center study, the rarity of urinary sepsis cases limited our sample size. Therefore, future work should focus on conducting prospective studies and expanding the application of network tools in clinical settings, with the goal of covering a broader range of scenarios to validate the effectiveness of the predictive model. Second, the model is capable of timely prediction of the risk of sepsis following URS. Future efforts will involve developing an enhanced artificial intelligence model that integrates multimodal indicators and extends the predictive horizon to cover the entire perioperative period.

Conclusion

In summary, the prediction model based on the XGBoost algorithm helps identify patients at high risk of uroseptic sepsis, providing a scientific basis for early detection of uroseptic sepsis and offering a quantifiable decision-making tool for the precise prevention and control of postoperative complications. This study only Conduct internal validation and include single-center patients as study subjects. In the future, we should actively collaborate with centers in other regions to jointly develop unified data collection standards and protocols, thereby expanding the coverage of our sample population.

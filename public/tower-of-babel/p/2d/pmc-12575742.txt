Fuzzy multi-criteria approach for rooftop photo-voltaic site selection: a case study in Gujarat
Authors: Rakesh Gupta, Soni Kumari, Gopal Krishna, Kumar Abhishek, Dileep Kumar Gupta, Pravat Ranjan Pati, Vishal Sharma, Gaurang Tank, Ali Elrashidi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
India has positioned itself as a global leader in solar energy generation as part of its commitment to sustainable development. Among various renewable energy models, the rooftop solar segment has seen considerable advancement and is now regarded as one of the most dependable and eco-friendly options. However, the widespread adoption of rooftop solar systems at the household level remains a challenge due to multiple factors, such as uncertainty in the decision-making environment and the complex characteristics of decision criteria. Despite the importance of strategic planning for such installations, studies focusing on selection criteria for rooftop solar implementation at the district level in India are relatively scarce. This research focuses on four districts in the state of Gujarat to evaluate the suitability of rooftop solar deployment. Initially, relevant criteria were identified for each district based on previous literature. Subsequently, five decision-makers provided assessments of the importance of these criteria using linguistic variables. A fuzzy multi-level multi-criteria decision-making (FMLMCDM) technique was then applied to rank the districts based on their readiness for door-to-door rooftop solar system implementation. This study introduces an innovative FMLMCDM framework that merges hierarchical fuzzy assessment with expert-based linguistic evaluation to determine the most appropriate districts for rooftop photovoltaic (PV) installations in Gujaratan approach not previously utilized in this context.

Introduction

The use of solar energy source has been mostly seen in past twenty years. Energy sources fundamentally affect key strategies of nations since energy is imperative for day-to-day existence administrations like heating purpose, making of food, production, and electric cars1. These days, various source of energy, for example fossil fuel, petroleum gasses, solar energy and wind energy is used to offer such assistance. It is important to get the energy from environmentally friendly sources, trustworthy, also ecologically solid. In this way, the usage of sustainable power sources has come into conspicuousness particularly during the most recent twenty years. A large portion of the nations all over the planet target subbing simple sources of energy for example petroleum gasses, coals, and various oil source with environmentally friendly power sources like sunlight based, and geothermal because of expanding ecological worries and manageability issues2.

The global photovoltaic (PV) market has witnessed significant growth over the past decade due to increased awareness of environmental sustainability and efforts to reduce greenhouse gas emissions. According to recent reports, the cumulative global installed capacity of PV systems reached approximately 1,177 GW in 2023, indicating a continued upward trend driven by supportive policies and declining technology costs3. In India, solar energy has become a key pillar of renewable power, with the total installed solar capacity reaching 110.83 GW by May 31, 2025. This includes 84.43 GW from ground-mounted systems, 18.37 GW from rooftop installations, 3.02 GW from hybrid projects, and 5.01 GW from off-grid systems4.

CO2 emissions are the main environmental concern about conventional energy sources. CO2 emissions for every 1% of economic growth in India are 1.01%5. Even after such an issue, supply of the energy has drastically increased with energy demand which is an environmental concern and that drawn more attention to sustainability problems. These factors have results into the more use of renewal energy and therefore states its utmost important. Utilization of the renewable energy sources gives better insight for the minimization of environmental harm and other waste, to minimize the emissions of greenhouse gasses, to ensure sustainability. Still from the data of Energy Agency (IEA), only approximately 13.5% of energy sources are renewable energy source for example hydro energy, wind and solar energy6. After looking at these data’s, it can be stated that study of renewable energy is necessary, and more research and exploration is needed in this field.

The importance of PV solar energy fields has grown substantially in recent years due to their flexibility and scalability. PV systems serve not only as standalone units but also as critical components in hybrid configurations such as PV/grid, PV/wind, PV/diesel, and PV/battery systems. These hybrid systems enhance energy reliability, reduce fossil fuel dependency, and offer viable solutions in both urban and remote regions. Their global deployment underscores the robustness of PV technology in diverse climatic and economic conditions7.

Beyond its importance in energy systems, PV solar technology has been widely exploited for practical applications. Case studies reveal the deployment of PV in street lighting, desalination plants, off-grid marine applications, and educational campuses. For instance, solar energy powers street lights in urban areas, supports desalination systems in arid regions, and energizes boats in the Gaza seaport. Moreover, rooftop PV systems have been installed at universities, industrial plants, and even for primary frequency control, highlighting their technical and economic feasibility across multiple sectors8.

National Solar Mission (NSM) was launched by Indian Government on 11th January 2010, with a mission to establish India’s position as a global leader in solar energy. The objective of the ion is to create the policy conditions for quick and ease diffusion of solar technology. The Mission targets installing 100 GW grid-connected solar power plants by the year 2022, out of which 40 GW to be achieved from rooftop solar1. By analyzing these potential and need of renewable energy in future, roof top solar power in Gujarat has been selected for the study purpose. Roof top has numerous benefits like policy and regulatory benefit, financial viability, technical feasibility, and increased assurance of power supply9. Along with it, the other benefits include increased value of home, low maintenance, no moving parts, increases job opportunities, savings in energy losses, no requirement of additional lands and the very most important one as it is good for environment. Along with these advantages, there are few limitations as well like not suitable in winter or in cloudy days; requirement of high initial investment cost etc.10. The principle of the solar energy is to obtained power by controlling the energy obtained by the sun’s rays using lances or mirrors and different tracking system through which large part of sunlight can be covered up. Therefore, solar power is the transformation of sun energy into the electrical energy. To establish this power system India has immense potential. According to the data of Census 2011, India has 340 million simple homes, 160 million electrified homes, and 150 million homes with proper roofs (like asbestos, metal sheet, concrete used). 135 million houses are having more than two rooms. An average house can accommodate underline 1–3 k of roof top PV system. By considering 25% available roof space in > 2 room homes alone, it is estimated that about 30,000 MW solar PV can be accommodated. To generate 2000 W with 12% efficiency, 200 square feet area is needed11. Therefore, India has immense potential for roof top solar power to meet the increasing demand of electricity. Rooftop is considered as a better alternative as needed for allocation of additional land resources is not necessary along with the generated energy is consumed at source itself.

In order to select the proper district for maximization of solar roof top project, role of decision-making comes is of very important. Decision-making stands for the choices that are made in day-to-day life when there are many alternatives for the same purpose and goal. Decision making in renewable energy is examined as a multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) problem with corresponding criteria and respective alternatives. The following task must consider several aspects of contradiction due to the growing intricacy of various factors such as social, technological, environmental, and economic12. The conventional traditional methods of single-criteria decision making cannot handle the intricacy of the current discussed problem. In past also, multiple criteria decision making has been implemented by NG for selecting suppliers to meet various business needs13. So MCDM (Multiple Criteria Decision Making) method came into the picture to solve this kind of problem. MCDM methods are used to prioritize, provide rank, or to select from a given set of different alternatives, or options, based on tangible and intangible multiple criteria. MCDM is a very famous methodology, often used in the problems concerned with decision making has an ability to handle the two qualitative and quantitative criteria along with the conflicts in decision makers and criteria14.

In general, MCDM is categorized into two different methods. They are classified as Multi-Objective Decision Making abbreviated as MODM and Multi-Attribute Decision Making abbreviated as MADM. Principle of MODM aims to characterize the decision-making problem (DM) is by the presence of several competitive goals and needs to be optimized based on a set of available and feasible constraints. The mathematical concept of multi-objective model has been executed in various past works to get optimization of different grinding parameters that includes time for grinding, cost analysis along with various surface quality measures such as work-piece speed, depth of cut and wheel speed.15–18. Principle of MADM aims to find a set of different alternatives against a set of correlated criteria and therefore whenever problem associated with different sets of problem arises, such as site selection process MADM methods have been used widely. The concept of selection of site is a multi-dimensional optimization problem that affects a broad range of stakeholders, which includes domestic populations and governments, as well as governments and environmental protection organizations. Detailed study of various site selections using MADM can be accessed by the following literature19–23.

n the literature, during the process of district selection for rooftop solar installations, subjective ratings often lead to contradictions and raise doubts due to the diversity and ambiguity inherent in the site selection criteria. Since undefined and ambiguous evaluations are present in the assessment process, these ratings are typically expressed in linguistic terms24, which reflect ambiguity and multiple possible interpretations. Bottani and Rizzi have demonstrated how the relative importance of various interrelated criteria can be effectively represented through linguistic judgments25.

The theory of fuzzy sets provides a sophisticated framework for addressing decision-making problems characterized by imprecision and ambiguity. Recent advancements in integrated fuzzy multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) methods have led to numerous applications across various domains26. For example, Carlsson and Fuller27 laid foundational work in fuzzy decision analysis, emphasizing the flexibility of fuzzy numbers in capturing uncertainty in preferences. Chu and Lin28 applied fuzzy MCDM in supplier selection, demonstrating how linguistic variables can be effectively used in evaluating multiple conflicting criteria. Ribeiro29 explored fuzzy outranking methods, offering insights into how decision alternatives can be compared under imprecise judgments. Al-Najjar and Alsyouf30 utilized fuzzy logic for evaluating equipment maintenance strategies, highlighting its strength in dealing with incomplete or vague reliability data. TY Chou et al.31 proposed a hierarchical fuzzy MCDM approach for transportation project selection, accounting for multiple layers of decision criteria. CC Chou32 extended fuzzy evaluation techniques to assess industrial systems, integrating both qualitative and quantitative factors in the decision-making process. In the context of renewable energy planning, the final report applies fuzzy set theory to structure decision-making problems related to the site selection of rooftop solar power installations. The Fuzzy Multiple Level Multiple Criteria Decision Making (FMLMCDM) methodology presents a modern and structured alternative to traditional decision-making approaches. One of its key features is the ability to incorporate a hierarchical criteria structure, where each primary criterion is broken down into sub-criteria, and further into lower levels, allowing for detailed and systematic evaluation of alternatives. Although fuzzy mathematics is used in the initial modelling stages, the method ultimately relies on defuzzified fuzzy numbers to quantify the relative importance of each criterion and alternative. This process of defuzzification, however, can introduce approximation errors that may affect the final ranking of options. Similar issues have been reported in other applications, such as energy source selection, as discussed by Gupta et al.33. Therefore, validating the outcomes of the FMLMCDM approach by comparing them with results from conventional fuzzy decision-making methods particularly in relation to the defuzzification process is critical for ensuring the reliability of the final decisions.

There have been very few works noted so far in the site selection for roof top solar power in India and more specifically in Gujarat. The use of evaluation index matrix system known as GHC (General Hierarchy Criteria) consisting of more than one level evaluation indices has been utilized in very few works. Till today’s words numerous decision-making problems have been approached by using at most a single set of criteria for evaluation. In practical situation, the main evaluation criteria are further divided into many sub level evaluation criteria, which is missing in previous works. Therefore, the present work attempts to identify set of the criteria and sub-criteria affecting the solar roof top installation.

Research design

In the initial phase different decision-making processes were studied. Their application and area of domain was explored. Among various decisions making processes FMLMCDM method was explored in deeply for solar roof top power decision making problem. The paper investigates first and foremost the FMLMCDM approach. The study of methodology for the same process has been done from literature of Chu and Varma34 and has been applied towards assessing site selection for solar roof top power.

The General Criteria Hierarchy (GCH) framework incorporates both qualitative (QL) and quantitative (QT) models within the context of multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM). In the present study, emphasis is placed on qualitative attributes due to the nature of the evaluation parameters. To effectively model hierarchical multi-level decision problems, the mathematical approaches proposed by Chu and Velasquez (2009)26 and later refined by Chu and Varma (2012)34 have been critically reviewed and adapted. A modified version of these methodologies has been developed to align with the specific objectives of this research and is applied herein.

Furthermore, the qualitative criteria are classified into two major categories: benefit-type (B) and cost-type (C). Benefit criteria adhere to the "Larger is Better" (LB) principle, aiming to maximize desirable outcomes, whereas cost criteria follow the "Smaller is Better" (SB) principle, targeting the minimization of undesirable effects.

A fundamental aspect of this study involves assigning appropriate ratings and weights to qualitative criteria and their corresponding sub-criteria, as well as determining the significance of each primary criterion. To analyse the relative importance of these qualitative elements, linguistic values represented by triangular fuzzy numbers have been employed.

Recent trends indicate a notable increase in the application of triangular fuzzy numbers, which have attracted significant interest from both domestic and international scholars and practitioners. These fuzzy numbers are highly effective in capturing, representing, and interpreting the subjective perception of complex decision-making scenarios35–38. Accordingly, the attribute values for various criteria and sub-criteria are expressed using linguistic variables. A case study in Gujarat (India)

Selection of Gujarat and its cities

In line with the National Solar Mission’s target of achieving 40 GW of solar power generation through PV rooftop systems, Gujarat has been allotted a target of 8.024 GW close to 2022. The latest MNRE report states that Gujarat has achieved around 40 percent of its total target and is at topmost position among Indian states that have already installed solar rooftop capacity. Out of the 8.024 GW, 3.2 GW is to be contributed by the rooftop segment, which comprises commercial, residential, industrial, and public sector installations. Although Gujarat, treads ahead of other Indian states, yet as per recent MNRE statistics, only around 30% of the required rooftop installed capacity has been reached1. Specifically, residential solar rooftops have huge untapped potential in achieving India’s solar rooftop target as most of the commercial, industrial and government sector has mostly large-scale ground-mounted solar plants. Various reasons that encourage for selecting Gujarat are: 1) the solar insolation of Gujarat is very much encouraging. The daily global radiation on an average is around 4.5 KWH per square meter per day and in a year the sunshine ranges from 3000 to 3500 h as per Ministry of New and Renewable Energy, Government of India. On average approximately 113.65 h of sunshine are there per month. 2) Gujarat lies in an ideal geographical location with tropic of cancer passing through it and has an average annual day temperature rising to 41˚C during summer, average annual day temperature rising to 29˚C during winter and average annual day temperature rising to 35˚C during summer as per Gujarat Energy Development Agency. 3) The persistence of the pandemic had lowered the rate of capacity replenishment but various scheme incentives like SURYA- Gujarat are making way towards clean energy transition for Gujarat. Patan, Ahmedabad, Sanand and Kutch cities of Gujarat have been selected for study purpose. These cities have around 29–35˚C of average annual temperature with an average 3020 sunshine hours.

Further each main criterion is classified into different sub- criteria to formulate multiple level decision making problem. The first criteria Resource conditions (P1) consist of four sub criteria, P11-annually average solar radiation, P12- Average temperature, P13- Rooftop available area, P14- Age of roof top. The annually average solar radiations for all the five cities were collected from AHA Solar Pvt Ltd and were recorded. The average temperature for all the five cities for every month of year 2020 has been recorded from weather and climate website of Gujarat Government. The rooftop available area describes the average area of roof for installation of solar panels in the five cities of Gujarat with the help of various solar companies. Also, in order to get an idea about rooftops with sufficient area, software named Google Earth has been used. In a similar fashion, approximate age of roof which describes how old the house is, have been also recorded.

The second criteria Economics (P2) consists of four sub criteria, P21- capital income yearly, P22- Initial capital investment P23- Annually operation and maintenance cost, P24- Payback period. The capital incomes for the selected cities were recorded as it directly influences the consumer decision in installing solar roof top. The initial capital investment for installation for various systems of solar available in market has been collected. As solar panels and other components needs maintenance, average annual and maintenance cost for various solar system has been recorded. As of latest trends, few solar companies itself invest initially for their customers and time to timely they recover their cost of initial investment. Therefore those criteria have also been applied for decision making model.

The third main criteria Risk (P3) consists of two sub criteria, P31-Natural risk, P32- Political risk. Natural risks include arc flash burn, fire and blast hazards risk for solar panels, electric shock falls, risk to solar panels during heavy wind-rainfall, risk about gravity load etc. Different state policies have different risk analysis for solar which makes it very complicated for solar power companies, and so therefore this attribute has been added.

The fourth main criteria Sustainability (P4) consists of two sub criteria, P41- Social benefits, P42- Carbon emission reduction. Social benefits include the employment opportunities developed in the cities. Also the impacts of solar roof power and limitations to the environment have been recorded under P42 attribute. Sensitivity to defuzzification method

To evaluate the robustness of the ranking results, the Mean of Maxima (MOM) method was used in addition to the Center of Area (COA) method for defuzzification. The comparison revealed that the top-ranked districts, Sanand and Ahmedabad, remained unchanged, confirming the stability of the model’s outcomes. Minor variations occurred among lower-ranked districts, but the consistency at the top validates the selection approach.

Robustness analysis: weighting and fuzzy scales

To test the robustness of the model, we conducted a sensitivity analysis using equal weights for all criteria instead of expert-assigned weights. Additionally, we applied a seven-point fuzzy linguistic scale to check for scale sensitivity. In both tests, the top two districts—Sanand and Ahmedabad—remained the same, affirming the model’s robustness. These tests highlight the stability of our FMLMCDM approach under varying assumptions.

Conclusions

The effective and proper district selection for solar roof top installation is very important so as to boost the domestic renewable energy capacity. In order to obtain proper view, the MCDM (Multi Criteria Decision Making) is very important for consideration of various criteria and various sub-criteria. In the current paper, FMLMCDM (Fuzzy Multiple Level Multi Criteria Decision Making) methodology has been utilised to obtain results. This method works very well when the model has multi-level hierarchical structure of criteria and sub-criteria. The ranking of District has been shown by the additive weighted methodology. Also in order to have defuzzified aggregated weight’s importance under qualitative criteria, method of COA (Centre of Area) has been implemented.

Sanand district has been selected as a first choice of solar roof top installation as per FMLMCDM method followed by Ahmedabad, Kutch and Patan. Also as per Government’s website Ahmedabad has highest population in city and Sanand is about 30 kms away from Ahmedabad. Therefore result from FMLMCDM can be considering close to real and thus can be used for various similar kinds of problems. Thus in order to boost the domestic utilization of renewable energy sources and to meet with Government National Solar Mission targets, maximum solar roof top installation in Sanand and Ahmedabad should be very first priority.

To benchmark our fuzzy approach, we compared the results with those obtained using a classic Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) model based on crisp evaluations. The AHP method showed similar ranking trends, with Sanand and Ahmedabad again ranking at the top. This consistency confirms the reliability and validity of the fuzzy-based decision-making process. For future work, we propose comparison with other fuzzy methods such as Fuzzy TOPSIS and Fuzzy VIKOR to further validate the results.

Limitations and future work

The use of evaluation index matrix system known as GHC (General Hierarchy Criteria) consisting of two levels evaluation indices has been utilized. The problem with the proposed GHC is that the mentioned criteria in the matrix system have not been standardized. The aforementioned criteria are complex and belong to different domain. The decided criteria are also as per Government and different helping solar roof top companies. The decided criteria do not take consideration of buyer’s view point in a broad aspect. Every house has different perspective and complex nature about solar roof top installation, which has not been considered. Thus the hierarchy level for this case study can be extended into higher complicated hierarchy structure considering buyer’s perspective for future work scope.

Moreover Centre of Area (COA) method has been implemented for Defuzzification process. Many different approaches with different formulas are there that can show distinct Defuzzification outcome. These distinct Defuzzification results can lead to distinct results and can lead to different set of rankings. The linguistic variables and their equivalent triangular fuzzy numbers concept for decision maker opinions have been taken from past published literature. In this case study, triangular membership function of fuzzy has been implemented from various other options such as Trapezoidal, Gaussian Bell, Sigmoid and Z-shape. However still there is a scope of future work to research about which different fuzzy membership function can give most reliable and precise solution.

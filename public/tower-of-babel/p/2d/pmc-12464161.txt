A modified transformer based on adaptive frequency enhanced attention, large kernel convolution, and multiscale implementation for bearing fault diagnosis
Authors: Xiao Chang, Shaobin Cai, Wanchen Cai, Yuchang Mo, Liansuo Wei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Bearing fault diagnosis has attracted increasing attention due to its critical role in monitoring the health of rotating machinery. Data-driven models based on deep learning (DL) have demonstrated strong capabilities in feature extraction. However, their performance often degrades under strong noise interference, which limits their applicability in real-world industrial scenarios. To address this issue, this paper proposes a novel attention-enhanced Transformer model that integrates large-kernel convolution and multiscale CNN structures for robust fault diagnosis. The proposed framework effectively combines spatiotemporal feature modeling with adaptive frequency-domain enhancement, enabling it to suppress noise and highlight informative diagnostic features. Experimental results on the Paderborn University and Case Western Reserve University datasets show that the proposed method achieves superior recognition accuracy under various signal-to-noise ratios, outperforming several state-of-the-art models. Furthermore, ablation studies and visualization analyses validate the effectiveness and soundness of the proposed architecture.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-18187-4.

Introduction

Rolling bearings, as key components of rotating machinery, possess advantages such as high load-bearing capacity and low friction resistance1. However, under complex working conditions—such as high speeds, heavy loads, and prolonged exposure to strong impacts—rolling bearings are susceptible to damage in their inner rings, outer rings, and rolling elements. This degradation can lead to diminished equipment performance and even severe safety incidents2. Consequently, the development of advanced fault diagnosis technologies is crucial for reducing economic losses and mitigating production safety risks.

Bearing faults often induce distinct anomalies in vibration signals, where variations in amplitude, frequency, and time–frequency characteristics closely correlate with the fault type. Therefore, vibration signals have become a widely adopted source for bearing fault diagnosis. These methods can generally be divided into two categories: traditional signal processing-based approaches and data-driven intelligent diagnosis methods3. The former typically involves analyzing vibration signals in the time, frequency, or time–frequency domains4using techniques such as wavelet transform5Fourier transform6amplitude spectrum7power spectral density8envelope spectrum analysis9short-time Fourier transform10and empirical mode decomposition11. Although widely used in industry, these methods often suffer from incomplete feature representation, especially in complex, high-dimensional signal scenarios. Additionally, their performance heavily depends on prior expert knowledge and hand-crafted features, which limits generalization12.

To address these challenges, data-driven intelligent diagnosis methods have become increasingly prominent due to their superior capabilities in automated feature learning and representation13. Among these, deep learning architectures such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs)14residual networks (ResNets)15and autoencoders (AEs)16 have shown strong potential in fault classification tasks17. For example, Guo et al.18 enhanced fault feature extraction by integrating wavelet transforms with CNNs using deformable convolutions. Yu et al.19 applied a multi-layer ResNet-152 to enable rapid detection of faults in train axle box bearings using multiscale convolutions. Furthermore, Sun et al.20 developed a sparse deep stacked network to simulate label sparsity, and Cui et al.21 proposed a feature-distance stacked autoencoder (FD-SAE) combining autoencoders and support vector machines for high-efficiency feature extraction in real-time environments.

However, in actual industrial environments, signals collected by vibration sensors are inevitably interfered with a large amount of noise22. This industrial noise significantly reduces the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), weakens the clarity and recognizability23and makes it particularly difficult to extract effective features under strong noise backgrounds.

To alleviate these challenges, researchers have explored various architectural enhancements. Zhang et al. proposed the Wide Deep CNN (WDCNN)24which incorporates a wide kernel in the initial convolutional layer to enhance robustness by effectively suppressing high-frequency noise. However, the spatial inconsistency in kernel responses across different feature regions introduces representational imbalance and limits diagnostic reliability25. To address this issue, Tang et al. integrated attention mechanisms into the WDCNN framework26significantly enhancing the model’s ability to focus on salient features under noisy conditions. Despite these improvements, conventional CNN-based methods still face challenges in adapting to varying noise levels. He et al. proposed the SE-WDCNN model27which incorporates variational mode decomposition (VMD) and its parameter optimization to enhance denoising performance. However, this method remains constrained by its reliance on predefined convolutional structures and limited adaptability to diverse signal characteristics.

To mitigate the receptive field bottleneck, atrous (dilated) convolutions have been proposed28which expand the receptive field by inserting gaps between sampling points without increasing the number of parameters. Zhang et al. leveraged this strategy in the threshold acquisition U-Net (TA-UNet)29 to perform early bearing fault detection on unlabeled datasets. While dilated convolution effectively enlarges the receptive field at low computational cost, its essence remains a local convolution operation, and the expansion of the receptive field depends on a fixed structural design. Therefore, its capacity to capture long-range dependencies is fundamentally limited30.

Transformer architectures have recently gained attention for their ability to model long-range dependencies31. Unlike CNNs, Transformers utilize multi-head self-attention (MSA) and feedforward networks to dynamically aggregate global context. These architectures typically feature fewer parameters while offering better modeling flexibility and computational efficiency32making them promising candidates for robust fault diagnosis.

Transformer architectures have been successfully applied to fault diagnosis tasks in various ways. For example, Bao et al. used a short-time Fourier transform to convert vibration signals into images before applying a Transformer for classification33. Ding et al. proposed the Time-Frequency Transformer (TFT), which combines wavelet transforms with Transformer blocks for enhanced robustness34. Tang et al. introduced the S-Transformer to address performance degradation under variable operating conditions and improve interpretability35. Hybrid CNN-Transformer models36 have also been explored to capture both local and global dependencies. Lu et al. designed a multiscale CNN-Transformer network for mapping raw signals to fault classes via attention37while Li et al. developed a dual-branch Transformer model that processes 1D and 2D modalities simultaneously38.

However, despite the success of Transformer models in modeling long-range dependencies across various domains, particularly in natural language processing, their direct application to vibration-based fault diagnosis faces inherent limitations. Standard self-attention mechanisms operate purely in the time domain and lack explicit frequency-domain modeling capabilities—an aspect that is essential for capturing the periodic and low-frequency components prevalent in bearing fault signals. As a result, Transformers may perform inadequately when distinguishing subtle periodic patterns embedded within strong industrial noise. Additionally, Transformer-based methods are computationally intensive when handling long sequential inputs39. Abbasi et al.40 further demonstrated that traditional self-attention structures face significant performance bottlenecks when dealing with longer sensor signals. Consequently, how to design more effective attention mechanisms that enhance signal modeling while reducing computational costs has become a key challenge in modern bearing fault diagnosis research.

To address the aforementioned challenges, this paper proposes ALMFormer, a Transformer-based network designed for robust bearing fault diagnosis. The architecture integrates a novel Adaptive Frequency-Domain Attention mechanism, large-kernel convolution, and multiscale dilated convolutions to simultaneously enhance both temporal and spectral representations.

Specifically, ultra-wide convolutional kernels are introduced at the initial stage to suppress high-frequency noise in raw signals and broaden the receptive field. Subsequently, multiscale dilated convolutions are employed to extract hierarchical temporal features, which are further refined through a gated residual enhancement dual-branch (GRE-DB) module. Finally, the extracted features are processed by the Adaptive Frequency-Domain Attention mechanism module, which adaptively fuses time- and frequency-domain information, enabling the model to dynamically emphasize fault-related frequency components while preserving long-range temporal dependencies. This integrated design significantly improves the model’s ability to detect periodic vibration patterns and enhances diagnostic robustness under complex noisy conditions. The final classification is performed by a feedforward layer.

Related work

Multiscale convolution

The fixed-size convolution kernels of traditional convolution are typically used to extract features in a single layer, and it is difficult to capture both local details and global information. Although the receptive fields of deep networks can be expanded by stacking convolutional layers, they may still perform inadequately in complex scenarios (such as edge blurring and varying object scales) due to the sensitivity of features transferred between layers. In contrast, multiscale convolution is a feature extraction method to enhance the robustness of the model’s feature representation in complex scenarios by combining different receptive fields in parallel or hierarchically. The channel dimension is standardized by batch normalization (BN) layers to mitigate internal shift issues of covariates, to accelerate training convergence, and to improve the discrimination of task-related features. Finally, the GELU activation function is used for non-linear mapping to preserve sparse activation, and to alleviate the death problem of neurons by non-zero gradients. Transformer

Transformer is a deep learning architecture based on the attention mechanism for modeling the global dependencies among elements of sequential data. Unlike traditional recurrent neural networks (RNNs) or convolutional neural networks (CNNs), the Transformer completely abandons recursive and convolutional operations, and achieves efficient modeling of global sequence information by self-attention mechanisms. This architecture was first proposed by Vaswani et al.41. Its capabilities of parallel computing and long-range dependency modeling are so strong that it is widely adopted in sequence modeling tasks.

The standard Transformer architecture consists of an encoder and a decoder, both of which are composed of multiple stacked layers with identical models (shown in Fig. 1). The encoder extracts features from the input sequence and maps them into a high-dimensional representation space, from which the decoder generates the corresponding output sequence. In each sub-module of the Transformer, the Multi-Head Attention (MHA) mechanism, Position-wise feedforward Network, residual connections and layer normalization are used to enhance the stability and representation capability of the model.

Proposed method

Extra-large convolutional kernel

Traditional deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) typically adopt small-sized convolutional kernels to ensure computational efficiency and to maintain a manageable number of model parameters. However, as network depth increases, the limitations of stacked small kernels become increasingly evident when modeling long-range dependencies. Although deeper networks theoretically expand the receptive field, the effective receptive field (ERF) is often significantly smaller than the theoretical receptive field (TRF) due to factors such as gradient dissipation and non-linear activation. This discrepancy limits the model’s ability to capture long-range temporal features. Moreover, repeated downsampling operations compress the feature dimension, potentially restricting information representation and leading to bottlenecks and overfitting.

To address these challenges, the use of large convolutional kernels in sequence modeling has gained growing attention in recent research. For instance, in the WDCNN model24 for bearing fault diagnosis, a 1D convolutional kernel of size 64 × 1 is employed to improve the model’s robustness under strong noise conditions. Compared to multi-layer small kernel stacks, large 1D kernels are capable of directly spanning a broader temporal range, enabling effective alignment with diagnostic patterns (e.g., periodic impulses caused by inner ring defects) and facilitating the extraction of global features. Furthermore, the wider convolutional window inherently suppresses high-frequency noise, thereby improving the robustness and stability of learned representations.

In the proposed model, a super-large one-dimensional convolutional kernel of size 1025 × 1 is introduced at the input stage to extract large-scale features directly from the raw signal. This kernel size is determined according to the input signal length (2048 sampling points), allowing it to cover nearly half of the input sequence. In the context of 1D signal processing, a kernel is typically considered “large” when its size significantly exceeds conventional small kernels (e.g., 3–11) and spans a substantial portion (e.g., > 25–50%) of the input sequence. This design helps alleviate ERF degradation, improves the model’s capacity to capture long-term dependencies, and provides strong noise suppression capability, ultimately enhancing generalization and performance under complex operating conditions.

Separable multiscale dilation convolution block

In the traditional multiscale Inception module, a fixed number (e.g., 3) of standard convolution branches with small kernel sizes (1, 3, 5) are typically used to extract multiscale features. However, when time series signals with complex structures or long-period dependencies are processed, there are some limitations of the structures as follows:

In order to enhance the perception ability of the model for multiscale patterns of time series modeling with controlled number of parameters and maintained structural lightweight, a multiscale dilated convolution module is designed in this paper (shown in Fig. 3). In this module, multiple dilated convolution branches with different receptive fields are integrated to effectively capture rich local and global contextual information of time series, and residual connections are employed to improve the training stability and representational capacity of the model. Compared with traditional multiscale convolutional networks, five branches with different convolution kernel sizes are adopted for comprehensive feature extraction. The complementary effects are formed by the suppression of frequency bands of different branches. That is, the small kernel branches suppress high-frequency white noise, while the large dilated branches filter out low-frequency interference.

Gated residual enhanced dual-branch module

In the task of bearing fault diagnosis, the original timing signal has obvious one-dimensional structural characteristics, including multi-frequency and multiscale fault modes. The feature maps of different receptive fields have been extracted by the preceding multiscale dilated convolution module, but there still is certain information redundancy and weak response areas in the output features. Therefore, a Gated Residual Enhanced Dual-Branch (GRE-DB) module (shown in Fig. 4) is designed in this paper to further compress invalid features, to enhance key responses, and to improve expression diversity through a multiple branch architecture.

This module adopts a compression-enhancement dual branch structure, in which downsampling-based feature compression, deep convolutional channel reconstruction, upsampling fusion, and a residual connection mechanism are combined together to effectively enhance the weak but critical temporal features of bearing fault signals.

In the dual branch structure, a conventional feature extraction strategy is adopted in Branch1: (1) for effectively capturing complex feature relationships across channels, using a standard convolution with a kernel size of 3 × 1, the input features are modeled in time series, and a channel interaction is performed. (2) Using a 1 × 1 convolution, the features are compressed, and the stability and nonlinear expression ability of the network are enhanced by the introduced Batch Normalization and ReLU activation functions.

In contrast, a more computationally efficient lightweight architecture is adopted in Branch2: (1) a depthwise separable convolution with a kernel size of 3 × 1 is used to perform channel-wise spatial feature extraction; (2) a 1 × 1 convolution is used for channel integration, and Batch Normalization and ReLU activation are subsequently applied normalization and non-linear transformation.

This dual-branch structure aims to have both high expressive power and computational efficiency in feature representation: (1) in Branch1, there are strong cross-channel modeling capabilities for facilitating the learning of complex information; (2) in Branch2, the computational efficiency and generalization ability of the model are enhanced by a lightweight per-channel modeling mechanism. The two feature paths are fused along the feature dimension for high performance of the model with controlled complexity to achieve more robust representation capabilities.

Adaptive frequency-domain attention

In bearing fault detection tasks, fault features often appear in signals as slight impacts or periodic oscillations, exhibiting a typical pattern of “low frequency + weak amplitude + periodicity”. In recent years, attention mechanisms, especially self-attention mechanisms, have achieved significant results in fields such as natural language processing and computer vision. They have shown great potential in extracting global dependencies and improving model generalization ability. However, there are two main issues when the standard self-attention mechanism is directly applied to one-dimensional vibration signals. The computational complexity of the original self-attention is \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:O\left({L}^{2}\right)$$\end{document}, and excessive computational resources are consumed when long time series signals are processed; the traditional attention is mostly based on time-domain modeling, and it is difficult to explicitly characterize the low-frequency dominant periodic features in bearing fault signals. Therefore, the accuracy and robustness of fault identification are affected.

To address the above issues, an enhanced adaptive frequency-domain attention mechanism based on the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) is proposed in this paper (shown in Fig. 5). The frequency-domain interaction, low-frequency enhancement, and local self-attention mechanism are integrated in the module to improve the robustness and discrimination of weak fault features in signals.

Model architecture

ALMformer consists of a super-large kernel convolution, multiscale dilated convolution, gated multi-path residual feature enhancement, four Metaformer blocks, and an output layer (shown in Fig. 6). The MetaFormer block is a key component of the model architecture.

Fig. 6ALMformer architecture.

ALMformer architecture.

In recent years, MetaFormer, proposed by Yu et al., has been regarded as a highly universal structural paradigm in visual modeling architecture design. Its core idea is that the performance advantage of Transformer not only comes from the self-attention mechanism itself, but more importantly, from its overall structural design. Therefore, the MetaFormer universal framework is referred to in this paper.

The standard Transformer framework is modified by MetaFormer with three retained key components: Normalization, Token Mixer, and channel feedforward networks (Channel MLP). The interaction between different time steps or spatial positions is achieved by Token Mixer, which is realized by the self-attention mechanism in traditional Transformers, and can also be achieved by more efficient operations, such as depthwise separable convolution, pooling operations, frequency-domain interaction, etc., under the MetaFormer framework.

Thanks to the flexibility of this structure, the form of Token Mixer can be chosen as needed for different tasks and scenarios to effectively reduce computational complexity and improve model inference efficiency. The normalization layer is used in this paper to stabilize feature distributions, and to add skip connections to reduce the risk of overfitting. Additionally, learnable weights are added to the skip connections to adaptively adjust the importance of features. Frequency self-attention is used to replace the token mixer module of the original MetaFormer while keeping the rest unchanged. Finally, richer and more abstract features are extracted by the channel feedforward network for nonlinear transformation. The output layer consists of a one-dimensional average pooling operation along the time dimension for extracting aggregated time features, and a fully connected layer, which maps high-dimensional features to the target category space for fault state discrimination.

Experiment

Experimental dataset

Case Western reserve university (CWRU)

This study utilizes the classic Case Western Reserve University (CWRU) bearing fault diagnosis dataset, which is widely adopted in research on rotating machinery fault detection, signal processing, and intelligent modeling. The dataset was collected from a test platform consisting of a motor, load device, sensors, and data acquisition system, as shown in Fig. 7.

Fig. 7CWRU bearing test bench.

CWRU bearing test bench.

SKF 6203-2RS deep groove ball bearings were used, with single-point faults of varying sizes artificially introduced into the inner ring, outer ring, and rolling elements to simulate realistic failure modes. Vibration signals were collected using acceleration sensors mounted on the bearing housing under four load conditions (0, 1, 2, and 3 hp), corresponding to motor speeds of 1797, 1772, 1750, and 1730 rpm. The sampling frequency was 12–48 kHz.

To ensure consistency, this paper selects data from the Drive End sensor with a 12 kHz sampling rate. Ten bearing states, including normal and multiple fault types/sizes, were used to cover diverse operating conditions for more representative and challenging diagnosis tasks, as detailed in Table 2.

Paderborn university of (PU)

The PU dataset, published by Christian Lessmeier et al. at Paderborn University, is a widely used and representative benchmark in data-driven rolling bearing fault diagnosis. As shown in Fig. 8, the experimental platform simulates real industrial operating conditions and damage evolution processes, supporting intelligent fault identification and health prediction research.

Fig. 8PU bearing test bench.

PU bearing test bench.

The experiment uses 6203-type deep groove ball bearings under three conditions: No Damage, Artificial Damage (via EDM or drilling on inner/outer rings), and Real Damage (caused by wear and fatigue from accelerated lifetime tests). To characterize health states, five damage levels are defined by normalizing damage length to the bearing pitch circumference.

The dataset includes 26 fault types and 6 healthy conditions, each with 20 samples of 4 s. Each sample contains three channels—vibration, current, and mechanical parameters—supporting comprehensive time, frequency, and time-frequency domain analysis. It has been widely used for fault classification, domain generalization, state estimation, and remaining useful life prediction.

In this paper, three representative operating conditions—“N15_M01_F10”, “N09_M07_F10”, and “N15_M07_F04”—are selected for training and evaluation. These scenarios cover a range of load and speed combinations from medium-speed/light-load to high-speed/heavy-load, enabling robust performance evaluation under complex working conditions, as summarized in Table 3.

Experimental setup

The performance of the fault diagnosis model is significantly impacted by the selection of appropriate hyperparameters to guarantee the proper functioning of the model. In this study, Python 3.9 was used as the programming language, and PyTorch version 2.1.0 was used as the deep learning framework. All fault diagnosis experiments were conducted on a Windows 10 operating system, with an Intel i5-12490 F CPU and an Nvidia GeForce RTX 4060 Ti GPU. CUDA version 12.1.0 was used to support GPU acceleration. In both datasets, the sample window length was 2048, and the overlapping step size was 512 for data preprocessing. Before input into the model, all samples were normalized using z-score normalization to reduce the influence of amplitude scaling. The data were divided into training, testing, and validation sets in a ratio of 7:2:1 by random sampling. The batch size was set to 64, the number of training epochs was set to 80, and the learning rate was set to 0.001. The Adam optimization and a learning rate decay strategy were used to improve model generalization. That is, the validation loss did not decrease for four consecutive epochs, and the learning rate was reduced to 40% of its original value. Cross-entropy loss was employed to evaluate the performance of the network during training. Finally, each experiment was repeated four times, and the average value was taken as the final outcome.

Experiments on PU dataset

For CWRU dataset, the structure is simple and the working condition is single; for PU dataset, there are much higher complexity and challenges in multiple aspects. Firstly, there are three-dimensional interactive variations (speed (N), load torque (M), and fault features (F)) in the working conditions of the PU dataset. For example, the combinations are N15_M01_F10, N15_M07_F04, N09_M07_ F10. It is much more complex than the single pairings (four fixed loads (0/1/2/3 hp) and four fixed speeds (1797/1772/1750/ 1730 rpm) of CWRU dataset. By the coupling of the multi-dimensional working condition, the signals are highly non-linear and non-stationary in both the time and frequency domains. The model needs not only to distinguish the overall vibration levels under different loads but also to precisely separate and identify the subtle signals, stacked by speed and fault frequencies.

Secondly, for the PU dataset, the signal acquisition environment is more similar to real industrial scenarios, including motor drive noise, mechanical transmission chain vibration coupling, and sensor quantization errors ete. noise interference of multiple sources, and the signal-to-noise ratio is much lower than that of the laboratory bench environment of the CWRU dataset. Therefore, the design of the model for frequency-domain feature extraction and classification is more challenging. In this paper, the generalization ability and robustness of ALMformer are further validated by the PU dataset under complex operational conditions (low signal-to-noise ratios, fine-grained classification tasks), to ensure the practical value of the model in real industrial environments. The PU dataset is divided into three subsets based on operational complexity. Detailed information about the experimental data can be found in Table 7.

PU dataset is divided into three subsets based on the complexity of the operating conditions (shown in Table 6) to systematically evaluate the diagnostic ability of ALMformer in different multi-dimensional scenarios. Dataset D (N09_M07_F10) corresponds to the single operational condition with low speed (900 rpm), high load (0.7 Nm), and high radial force (1000 N). In this scenario, the vibration signal has significant non-stationary low-frequency impact characteristics, helping to verify the fundamental extraction ability of the model for extreme nonlinear coupled signals.

In Dataset E (N15_M01_F10 & N15_M07_F04), “low load + high-frequency fault” (0.1 Nm, F10) and “high load + low-frequency fault” (0.7 Nm, F04) are introduced under the same high speed (1500 rpm) condition to form two typical patterns of steady high frequency and non-stationary low frequency for comparing the feature separation and robustness of model under energy distribution of different frequency bands and load interference.

Three typical operational conditions (N15_M01_F10, N09_M07_F10, N15_M07_F04) are combined in Dataset F to simulate switching scenarios of multi-operational conditions in real industrial settings for thoroughly testing the overall generalization ability and stability of the model under multi-dimensional interactions of speed, load, and fault frequency. The performance validation for the model’s deployment in real complex environments is further verified step by step (single, dual, multi-operational conditions).

Ablation experiment

To deeply analyze the synergistic effect between the adaptive frequency-domain attention module and the gated residual enhancement dual-branch of the model, proposed in this paper, the ablation experiments were conducted on Dataset F, a representative subset of the PU dataset with high operating complexity and representativeness. The multiple rotation speeds, load conditions, and fault types are integrated in Dataset F, by which the strong fault diagnostic capabilities and practical potential can be tested for generalization.

For this purpose, four comparative models were designed to progressively analyze the impact of each module on performance. In NET1, the first-layer ultra-large kernel is removed to analyze the contribution of the ultra-wide receptive field to the initial noise suppression of input signals, and to evaluate the impact on accuracy and robustness of subsequent feature extraction;

In Net2, the multiscale dilated separable convolution module is replaced with a classic three-branch standard convolution structure, whose standard convolution kernel sizes are 1, 3, and 5, and the dilated expansion structure is completely removed to compare the differences in multiscale feature modeling capabilities between traditional convolution and dilated structures, as well as their impact on overall diagnostic performance.

In NET3, the gated residual enhancement dual-branch of ALMformer is removed, and 1 × 1 convolution is used for channel compression after the multiscale dilated convolution module, to test the independent modeling capability of the adaptive frequency-domain attention mechanism without prior spatial feature enhancement, to evaluate the actual contribution of the multi-path residual feature enhancement module for improving feature expression ability and noise resistance.

The experimental results, presenting the average classification accuracy of each model under six noise levels (− 8 dB, − 6 dB, − 4 dB, − 2 dB, 0 dB, and no noise), are summarized in Table 9. For NET1, the accuracies are 69.87%, 77.73%, 84.58%, 87.80%, 91.02%, and 96.69%, respectively; for NET2, they are 73.79%, 80.36%, 86.44%, 91.47%, 93.24%, and 96.10%; for NET3, they are 72.11%, 82.09%, 86.39%, 90.71%, 93.01%, and 96.28%; and for NET4, they are 74.00%, 82.54%, 87.18%, 89.27%, 93.09%, and 96.55%.

From the overall trend, compared with the complete ALMformer, the accuracy of NET1 drops approximately 10.06% under strong noise scenarios (− 8 dB and − 6 dB, respectively). So, the large receptive field convolution kernel in the first layer plays a crucial role in the initial noise suppression and feature preservation. By a constructed wide receptive field, the focus on low-frequency principal components is effectively enhanced, while high-frequency disturbances are attenuating, improving the stability of feature extraction in subsequent modules.

In NET2, although adaptive frequency-domain attention module is retained, the dilated depthwise separable convolution is replaced, limiting the ability of multiscale modeling, and the accuracy across all noise levels is overall lower than that of the complete model. The traditional three-branch convolution structure has a certain degree of scale adaptability. However, the receptive field coverage and the feature density expression are limited, and it is difficult to balance long-range dependency modeling and feature density. In contrast, with the dilated convolutions, the receptive fields can be expanded without increasing computational complexity, particularly the model’s ability for capturing low-frequency periodic disturbances and weak fault features is improved under high-noise conditions.

NET3 performs worse than NET1 and NET2 do at multiple noise levels, particularly showing significantly degrades at − 8 dB and − 6 dB. So, the modeling capability of NET3 is suppressed because of the absence of gated residual enhancement dual branches. The model’s perception of local contextual information and weak fault features is enhanced by the dual-branch fusion and gated regulation mechanisms, and this residual module is a key component for feature enhancement and robustness.

In NET4, after adaptive frequency-domain attention being replaced with traditional 8-head multi-head attention, the accuracy decreases to 74.00% and 82.54% respectively at low signal-to-noise ratio environments (-8 dB and − 6 dB), is 5.93% and 4.08% lower than that of ALMformer.

Thus, in one-dimensional vibration signals, it is difficult for traditional time-domain attention to fully combat local disturbance noise and to suppress redundant frequency bands. In contrast, in the adaptive frequency-domain attention mechanisms, by introduced the DCT transformation and low-frequency guidance strategies, the key frequency components can be more accurately focused on, enhancing the discriminative power and anti-interference capability for fault identification.

In summary, the adaptive frequency-domain attention module and the gated residual enhancement dual-branch structure proposed in this paper play a core role in enhancing the noise resistance of model, in improving feature modeling effectiveness and overall robustness. Furthermore, by experiments, the effectiveness and practical value of multiscale dilated convolutions and large receptive field modeling mechanisms in improving the diagnostic performance of one-dimensional vibration signals are further validated under complex conditions. the structural foundation of ALMformer is established by the above two parts to maintain excellent performance in high-noise, multi-condition tasks.

In addition to accuracy, the computational overhead of each module is further evaluated from the perspectives of model complexity and operational efficiency in this paper. That is, the number of parameters (Params), the floating-point computation amount per forward inference (FLOPs), and the total time required to complete a full training on a unified hardware platform (shown in Table 10).

It is proved by the experiments that the performance of the original model ALMformer is well-balanced across various resource costs. That is, its parameter count is 39.530 K, its FLOPs are 3.766G, its complete training time is 60.31s, and its accuracy is optimal under multiple noise conditions. So, the performance-efficiency ratio is excellent.

In NET1, the ultra-wide receptive field structure is removed, and the resource consumption (22.618 K parameters and 1.546G FLOPs) is the lowest. However, the denoising ability of the initial feature decreases substantially, and the accuracy drops significantly under strong noise. The significant performance gains are achieved at the cost of moderate computation by the introduced super-large kernel convolution. From the changes in actual runtime, a good trade-off and synergy are achieved in this design between accuracy improvement and computational resources.

In NET2 the multiscale dilated separable convolution module in the original model is replaced with a traditional three-branch standard convolution structure (kernel sizes of 1, 3, 5). The parameter count reaches the highest (47.562 K) among all comparative models, and the computational complexity significantly rises to 4.828G FLOPs.

However, the average accuracy under various noise levels is consistently lower than that of the original model. So, the investment of substantial resource does not yield equivalent performance returns. The superiority and rationality of the proposed multiscale dilated separable convolution structure in balancing model accuracy and computational efficiency are further validated.

In NET3, after the gated residual enhancement dual-branch structure is removed, FLOPs are reduced to 3.104G, parameters are reduced to 31.930 K, and training time is reduced to 50.48s. That is, the computational cost is reduced at the cost of a slight reduction in accuracy under strong noise. The spatial feature representation and robustness are enhanced. Thus, the spatial feature expression and robustness are improved at the cost of acceptable resources, and the cost-effectiveness is high.

Interestingly, in NET4, after adaptive frequency-domain attention was replaced with traditional multi-head self-attention (MHSA), both FLOPs (3.748G) and parameter count (39.018 K) are further reduced, but at the cost of an unexpected increase in runtime (378.05s), which is significantly higher than those of other models. So, the trends of change in FLOPs and parameters are inconsistent. That is, FLOPs are not the sole determinant of model efficiency.

The confusion results from the computation characteristics of the multi-head self-attention (MHSA) mechanism. In MHSA, the parameter number of a head is in a controlled range. However, in the computation, the sequence length dimension is constructed, an L×L attention matrix is processed, and parallel computation and result fusion of multiple heads are performed. In the process, the hardware cache and concurrency capabilities are highly utilized. Particularly, for long sequences or the large feature dimensions, the non-parallelizability of computation and access bottlenecks of memory are the main limiting factors for operational efficiency.

In contrast, in the adaptive frequency-domain enhancement module, proposed in this paper, DCT/IDCT transformations are used to model global dependencies in the frequency spectrum dimension, and to achieve explicit enhancement through the lightweight channel interaction structure. Although the FLOPs are a little higher, the computation path is shorter, the data access is more efficient. Thus, not only is global modeling capability better, but also execution on modern computing platforms is efficient.

From the perspective of practical engineering efficiency, the adaptive frequency-domain attention mechanism and gated dual-branch residual enhancement structure, proposed in this paper, have superior computational efficiency and inference speed with global modeling capability. Moreover, by the multiscale dilated separable convolution module, the diagnostic accuracy is improved with reduced computational cost, while the performance is enhanced by the large receptive field convolution effectively with an acceptable computation overhead. The architectural foundation of ALMformer is collectively established by the above four modules to ensure robust performance under high-noise and variable working conditions. Thus, ALMformer is particularly suitable for real-time industrial fault diagnosis scenarios.

Conclusion

For the challenges of rolling bearing fault diagnosis in strong noise environments, a novel end-to-end fault identification framework, ALMformer, is proposed in this paper. In ALMformer, the efficiency of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in local feature modeling and the global advantage of Transformers in long-range dependency modeling are combined for strong feature modeling capabilities and noise robustness.

First, ultra-wide convolution kernels are used to preprocess the original vibration signals for effectively suppressing high-frequency irrelevant noise interference and for enhancing the expressive quality of the signals. Subsequently, a multiscale dilated convolution module is designed to extract discriminative features under different receptive fields. The high-frequency white noise is filtered out by the small-scale dilated convolution branch; the low-frequency background interference is suppressed by the large-scale dilated branch. So, multiscale modeling of key information can be achieved to significantly alleviate the issue of limited feature expression of traditional CNN structures under time-varying operating conditions.

Furthermore, based on a gating mechanism, a residual-enhanced dual-branch architecture is constructed in this paper for the multi-angle reconstruction and enhancement of input features by feature compression, weighted enhancement, and multi-branch fusion to effectively improve the richness and discriminability of feature expression.

The traditional Transformers heavily rely on temporal information, and insufficiently utilize frequency-domain information in processing one-dimensional vibration signals. By the adaptive frequency-domain attention mechanism, designed in this paper, the above limitations of traditional Transformers can be overcome to strengthen the key frequency components related to faults, and to make it more suitable for the time-frequency mixed characteristics of vibration signals.

By the experiments on the CWRU and PU datasets, it is demonstrated that ALMformer excellently performs across multiple noise levels, various fault types, and different load conditions, has strong robustness and generalization capability, and be highly potential for engineering applications.

The proposed method outperforms under various experimental conditions. However, in more challenging practical scenarios characterized by imbalanced sample distributions, scarce fault class samples, and limited edge computing resources, there still is remains room for further optimization. Future research will be done on lightweight modeling, robust learning strategies under imbalanced sample conditions, and low-resource deployment of the model on the actual industrial edge devices to enhance the transferability and practical value of the model.

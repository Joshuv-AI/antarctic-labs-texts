Multiomics analysis of microbial succession and flavor formation mechanism during the fermentation process of Maotai-flavour Baijiu
Authors: Xin Shi, Chenming Fan, Ming Hui, Qing Tian, Fangli Zhang, Chunmei Pan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study employed metagenomics and metabolomics techniques to investigate the complex relationship between microbial succession and the formation of flavor compounds during the fermentation process of Maotai-flavour Baijiu. Results demonstrated that stacking fermentation, characterized by Weissella, Pichia, and Aspergillus, which secreted amylases and proteases to hydrolyze starch and proteins. Pitting fermentation facilitated the enrichment of anaerobic microbes such as Acetilactobacillus and Pichia, significantly promoting the synthesis of key flavor compounds, including esters, alcohols, and acids, through Glycosyltransferase and Esterification activities. Volatile compound analysis revealed distinct stage-specific profiles, with acids, alcohols, and esters accumulating predominantly in pitting fermentation. These findings elucidate the stage-specific microbial metabolic networks and synergistic mechanisms underlying flavor formation, providing a scientific basis for optimizing traditional Baijiu fermentation processes.Keywords: Maotai-flavour Baijiu, Metagenomics, Metabolomics, Microbial functions, Metabolic contributions

Introduction

Chinese Baijiu is a traditional distilled spirit with diverse aroma types, among which Maotai-flavour Baijiu is one of the most renowned due to its complex production process and unique flavour profile. It is characterized by a sophisticated brewing cycle that extends up to a year, involving multiple stages including grain selection, Daqu inoculation, stacking fermentation (SF), pitting fermentation (PF), distillation, aging, and blending. A distinctive feature of Maotai-flavour Baijiu is its signature “sauce-like” aroma (Gong, Zuo, Wu, Zhao, Wei & Huang, 2024; Wu et al., 2024). This unique flavour signature stems from its complex fermentation process, which involves the interaction of multiple microbial communities. The traditional brewing process of Maotai-flavour Baijiu involves two main stages: stacking fermentation and pitting fermentation. In the stacking fermentation stage, steamed sorghum and Daqu are mixed and piled up, promoting the enrichment and propagation of various microorganisms (Duan et al., 2023; Fan et al., 2024). The core purpose of this stage, also known as “secondary Daqu making,” is to enrich and multiply microorganisms in the air and to produce a series of enzymes that convert starches in raw materials into sugars that yeast can use. After fermentation, the fermented grains were transferred to underground pits for anaerobic fermentation, a process primarily driven by the activities of anaerobic microorganisms, which played a crucial role in generating aroma substances and further enriching the flavour components of Baijiu (Bai et al., 2024; Liu & Sun, 2018).

High-temperature stacking fermentation is the key link between fermentation in the pitting and the regulatory mechanism of microbial metabolism (Li et al., 2025). It is the core research object, particularly the further transformation of accumulated aroma substances during the temperature rise stage of the stacking fermentation process. Additionally, Browning reactions and biochemical reactions are carried out simultaneously. These complex reaction processes lead to various aroma substances, providing a solid foundation for the aroma and taste of Maotai-flavour Baijiu (Shi et al., 2024). These aroma substances form a solid foundation for creating the smell and taste of Maotai-flavour Baijiu (Xueao et al., 2023). High-temperature stacking fermentation promoted the synergistic effect of different functional microbial communities. Through the process of enrichment, screening, and domestication, the number of beneficial microorganisms increased significantly, while harmful microorganisms were eliminated (Ma et al., 2022). This process provided abundant fermentation vitality for the subsequent fermentation stage and significantly positively affected the formation of the Maotai-flavour Baijiu flavour. During the brewing process, the stacking fermentation stage of fermented grains was critical (Wang et al., 2024). When the stacking temperature of the fermented grains reached more than 50 °C, the pooling operation could be carried out (Chen et al., 2025). After entering the tank, the pit mud is sealed, and the pitting fermentation process lasts about one month. This stage is the key link in Baijiu fermentation (Wang et al., 2015); therefore, the position of pitting fermentation in the entire brewing process is particularly critical. During the stacking process, precursor substances formed by anaerobic microorganisms are further transformed into substances with a savory flavour (Ren et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024).

At present, research on the microbial dynamics during the fermentation process of Maotai-flavour Baijiu mostly focuses on the analysis of individual rounds. There are relatively few studies that integrate multi-omics methods to systematically study the functional contributions of the microbial community throughout the entire round at different stages of Maotai-flavour Baijiu production. In recent years, with the rapid development of metagenomic sequencing technology and volatile metabolomics technology, researchers have been able to more deeply explore the evolution of microbial ecology and volatile substances during Baijiu fermentation, thereby revealing the complex and dynamic microbial communities composed of bacterial, yeast and mold and their functions in detail (Wang et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2022). This paper aims to provide a comprehensive overview of microbial community succession during the production of Maotai-flavour Baijiu, from stacking to pitting fermentation. We will explore in depth the major microbial species involved in each of the key stages of this process and the metabolic contributions these microorganisms make to the formation of flavour substances. In addition, we will also analyze and discuss the factors affecting the dynamic changes in these microbial communities, providing a scientific theoretical basis and practical guidance for the production of Maotai-flavour Baijiu.

Materials and methods

Fermented grains sampling

The fermented grain samples used in this study were obtained from the 1st to 7th rounds of fermented wine mash in the 2023–2024 production year at a Maotai-flavour Baijiu distillery in Guizhou Province, China. Fermented grains was obtained from three independent fermentation piles and cellars (as biological replicates, n = 3). The samples for stacking fermentation were collected on the third day after piling, and the samples for pitting fermentation were collected on the 15th day after the pit was sealed. Samples were taken from multiple sites in each grain pile and pit using sterile samplers, and the plot of sampling sites is shown in Fig.S1. Samples from each site were pooled and immediately shipped to the laboratory and divided into two portions. One sample was used for physicochemical index detection and the other was used for subsequent omics analysis.

Physical and chemical index detection

Hydration was determined by a constant-temperature drying method. Acidity was measured by neutralization titration. Starch was determined by using a standard glucose solution reverse titration. The reduction of sugar was determined using a standard glucose solution reverse titration. The above indicators were measured in accordance with the standard Method of analysis for baijiu QB/T 10345–2022 of China Light industry. Total sugar was determined by a Solarbio kit (BC2710). Total amylase was determined by a Solarbio kit (BC2040). The specific experimental methods refer to the website https://www.solarbio.com/.

Metagenomic sequencing

Total genomic DNA was extracted from fermented grain samples using the Mag-Bind® DNA Kit (Omega Bio-tek, Norcross, GA, U.S.) according to the manufacturer's instructions. The concentration and purity of extracted DNA were determined with TBS-380 and NanoDrop2000, respectively. DNA extract quality was checked on 1 % agarose gel. The extracted DNA samples were sequenced using the Illumina Novaseq X Plus platform. The comparison and processing of the original data, including the assembly of the original sequence, species composition, and functional annotation, were done by Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

Detection of volatile substances

Sample pretreatment: 1 g of sample was placed in a 20 mL headspace bottle with 2.5 μL of internal standard (20 μg/mL of naphthalene d8, purity 98 % and produced from Damas-beta) and 4 mL of saturated sodium chloride solution, and the headspace bottle was immediately sealed (SPME Arrow Fiber, 120 μm, Thermo, DVB/Carbon WR/PDMS).

Chromatographic conditions: The samples were injected into the Gas Chromatography-mass spectrometry (Thermo, Germany) system in shunt mode for analysis with a shunt ratio of 10:1. The samples were separated by VF-WAXms capillary column (25 m × 0.25 mm × 0.2 μm, Agilent CP9204) and then entered the mass spectrometry for detection. The inlet temperature was 240 °C, the carrier gas was high-purity helium, the carrier gas flow rate was 1.0 mL/min, and the spacer purge flow rate was 3 mL/min. Heating procedure: initial temperature 60 °C, balance 2.5 min, at 8 °C/min to 120 °C, then at 20 °C/min to 230 °C, and maintain 4.5 min, the total running time 20 min.

Mass spectrum conditions: Ion source temperature 250 °C, ion source type: EI, electron energy 70 eV. The identification of volatile compounds was performed by comparing with the retention indices (determined by using a standard series of C10- C29 saturated alkanes, purity greater than 97 %, produced from SIGMA) and the mass spectra stored in the data system library.

Statistical analysis and visualization

One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to test the differences. Principal component analysis (PCA) was used to analyze the sample's and species' similarity. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to analyze the differences in microbial and functional composition between the two groups. The data were analyzed and visualized on the Majorbio Cloud Platform's online platform (www.majorbio.com).

Results and discussion

Physicochemical changes of stacking fermentation and pitting fermentation

The hydration level of fermented grains is a critical process parameter in the production of Maotai-flavour Baijiu (Aiguo et al., 2022). Specifically, excessive hydration can cause the fermented grains to become overly viscous, thereby inhibiting the growth of aerobic microorganisms. Conversely, insufficient hydration impairs the gelatinization of sorghum starch, limiting substrate availability and hindering microbial metabolism during fermentation (Fengjiao et al., 2023). The variation in hydration levels of fermented grains during the SF and PF across rounds 1 to 7 is presented in Fig. 1a. The hydration content of fermented grains exhibited an overall increasing trend, ranging from approximately 35 % to 55 % across the fermentation process. Notably, hydration levels during the PF were consistently higher than during the SF. Interestingly, a temporary decrease in hydration was observed during the third round. This round is considered a pivotal stage in Maotai-flavour Baijiu fermentation, which is usually accompanied by a significant increase in the proportion of Daqu. Thus, more abundant microbial communities were introduced into fermented grains and proliferated for a short time. This rapid proliferation process consumed the free water in jiupei, resulting in a transient decrease in water content. By the end of the seventh round, hydration levels stabilised at slightly below 55 %.

Changes of physicochemical indexes in fermented grains during fermentation.

Acidity is a key quality control parameter in the fermentation of Maotai-flavour Baijiu. As shown in Fig. 1b, the acidity levels of fermented grains during the SF and PF across rounds 1 to 7 were monitored. Overall, acidity increased throughout the seven fermentation rounds, ranging from 0.5 to 5 mmol/10 g of dry matter. In each round, acidity levels were consistently higher in the PF samples than in the SF counterparts. In the early fermentation stages, the large influx of raw materials, coupled with the high-temperature conditions of the SF, promoted the enrichment and activity of environmental microorganisms, particularly acid-producing bacterial, resulting in a rapid increase in acidity. As fermentation progressed, the depletion of readily available substrates in the raw materials led to a gradual decline in acid production rate and a stabilization of acidity levels.

During the brewing of Maotai-flavour Baijiu, starch is a primary nutrient source for microbial growth and proliferation (Yang et al., 2024). The fermentation status of fermented grains can be effectively assessed by monitoring the consumption of starch (Tian et al., 2024). As illustrated in Fig. 1c, starch content in both the SF and PF samples varied between 15 % and 50 % for the seven fermentation rounds. During the initial three rounds, starch levels were relatively high and exhibited a rapid decline. This trend was primarily attributed to the addition of fresh raw materials at the early stages, which increased the overall starch input, and to the enzymatic conversion of starch into reducing sugars during high-temperature stacking fermentation. The increased availability of fermentable substrates in the early fermentation stages stimulated microbial metabolic activity, thereby accelerating starch degradation. As fermentation progressed, the starch reserves in the raw materials were gradually depleted, resulting in a decline in microbial metabolic intensity and a slower rate of starch consumption.

The dynamic changes in reducing sugar content in fermented grains are primarily influenced by the enzymatic hydrolysis of starch and the concurrent consumption by actively growing microbial communities (Jiang et al., 2022). The temporal changes in reducing sugar content in the SF and PF samples across rounds 1 to 7 are presented in Fig. 1d. In the SF, reducing sugar levels followed a pattern of initial decline, subsequent increase, and final decline. In contrast, the PF exhibited a trend of initial accumulation followed by a gradual decrease. Notably, the lowest reducing sugar content was observed during the second round, while the highest was recorded in the fourth round. The fourth round is a pivotal stage in the Maotai-flavour Baijiu fermentation process. At this point, the elevated levels of reducing sugars provide a favorable nutrient environment that supports the robust growth and metabolic activity of diverse microbial populations.

During the fermentation of Maotai-flavour Baijiu, dynamic fluctuations in total sugar content within the fermented grains play a critical role in determining the fermentation rate (Liu et al., 2025). As illustrated in Fig. 1e, the total sugar content of fermented grains across rounds 1 to 7 was generally higher in the SF samples than in the PF. Among the PF samples, the most pronounced fluctuations in total sugar levels were observed, with the highest content recorded in the second round, followed by the fourth round. In the brewing process of Maotai-flavour Baijiu, amylase plays a pivotal role in catalyzing the hydrolysis of starch in raw materials into fermentable sugars, thereby supplying the essential energy source required for yeast-driven alcoholic fermentation (Zhu et al., 2023). As shown in Fig. 1f, the dynamic changes in amylase activity were monitored in fermented grains during the mid-stage of the SF and PF across rounds 1 to 7. In the first round, amylase activity in the PF samples was significantly higher than that in the SF samples. However, as fermentation progressed, the difference in enzyme activity between the two stages gradually diminished, with amylase activity levels in the SF and PF tending toward convergence in the later rounds.

The changes of the above indicators together create a microenvironment suitable for the succession of different microbial communities. The stacking fermentation stage is dedicated to the rapid decomposition of raw materials and the creation of an acidic environment. During the pitting fermentation stage, the flavor substances of Maotai-flavour Baijiu are synthesized directionally under anaerobic conditions using the products from the previous stage. This orderly evolution of physical and chemical indices lays the foundation for the succession of different functional microbial communities.

Microbial diversity in stacking fermentation and pitting fermentation

The inter-group differences in species diversity of the fermented grains of the SF and PF groups were tested. Fig. 2a, Fig. 2b, and Fig. 2c, and Fig. 2d, Fig. 2e, and Fig. 2f respectively showed the Shannon, Sobs, and Simpson indices of bacterial and fungal. Except for the Simpson index, the diversity of the SF group was significantly higher than that of the PF group, which indicates that the microbial diversity in the SF group is higher than that in the PF group.

(d), (e) and (f) were the Shannon index, Sobs index, and Simpson index of fungal, respectively. (g) and (h) were the principal coordinate analysis of bacterial and fungal, respectively.

In this study, Principal Coordinate Analysis (PCoA) was employed to assess the structural differences in bacterial and fungal across 14 groups of fermented grain samples, as shown Fig. 2g and Fig. 2h. The first two principal components (PC1 and PC2) accounted for 60 % of the total variance of bacterial and 82.03 % of the total variance of fungal. This indicates that the model has a strong explanatory power.

The microbial community changes at the level of phylum during the fermentation process of Maotai-flavour Baijiu are analyzed, as shown in Fig. 3a and Fig. 3b. In terms of fungal, Ascomycota predominated in both types of fermentation (> 0.9), with the fungal composition of the PF being more stable. In contrast, the fungal diversity of SF was higher. Bacillota (> 0.98) was dominant in the PF; the abundance of Bacillota decreased, while the abundance of Actinomycetota and other flora increased. These differences indicate that the anaerobic environment of the pitting is more conducive to the stable growth of Firmicutes and Ascomycetes, whereas the aerobic conditions of the SF support a richer microbial diversity.

To investigate the dynamic evolution and compositional characteristics of the fungal and bacterial community during different fermentation stages, taxonomic profiling was conducted at the genus level. The top 10 genus with relative abundance were selected for detailed analysis. The changes of fungal and bacterial communities in 1–7 rounds of the SF and PF were shown in Fig. 3c and Fig. 3d. In terms of bacterial community, the SF showed a gradual succession, with Weissella as the dominant factor in the initial stage (S1,0.672), and gradually replaced by saline-resistant bacteria Lentibacillus (S7,0.393) and Kroppenstedtia (S7, 0.135) in the later stage. The PF showed a significant cycle-dependent replacement. In the initial stage, Limosilactobacillus and Lactobacillus dominated, but from P2, Acetilactobacillus quickly became the dominant bacteria (Zhao et al., 2022). Regarding the fungal community, the SF showed a successional sequence from yeast to filamentous fungal, from Pichia (S1, 0.584) in the early stage to Paecilomyces (S4, 0.317), Aspergillus (S6, 0.206), and Monascus (S5, 0.244) in the middle and late stages. Pichia has always maintained a dominant position in the PF, but has also formed a complementary stage with Monascus and Zygosaccharomyces.

In conclusion, the reduction in microbial diversity during the pitting fermentation stage is not degradation but a process of environmental selection and functional enrichment. The closed and acidic environment constitutes a strong selective pressure on the microbial community, which enriches a small number of anaerobes with special functions. Therefore, the reduction in diversity is not merely the extinction of microorganisms, but rather an ecological succession process that transforms from diversity to functionality, which is crucial for the efficient synthesis of the flavour of Maotai-flavour Baijiu.

CAZy functional contribution prediction comparison in the stacking fermentation and pitting fermentation grains

An association analysis was conducted between species abundance and CAZy (Carbohydrate-Active Enzymes Database) functional abundance in the samples to determine the functional contributions of different species. The CAZy functional contributions of bacterial and fungal communities are presented in Fig. 3e and f. Acetilactobacillus and Paecilomyces exhibited exceptionally high contributions across multiple CAZy categories, including GH (Glycoside Hydrolases), GT (Glycosyl Transferases), and CE (Carbohydrate Esterases). Lactobacillus made a significant contribution in the GH category but had relatively low contributions in other functional categories. In contrast, Lentibacillus maintained consistently high contributions across multiple functional categories. Among fungal, Pichia demonstrated an exceptionally high contribution in the GT category, indicating significant glycosyltransferase activity.

The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was applied to reveal the differences in the relative abundance of CAZy functions between the SF and PF, as shown in Fig. 3g. The results show that the abundance of Glycoside Hydrolases, Polysaccharide Lyases, Carbohydrate-Binding Modules, and Cellulosome Modules in the SF was significantly higher than that in the PF. Conversely, the abundance of Glycosyl Transferases, Carbohydrate Esterases, and Auxiliary Activities in the PF was considerably higher than in the SF (p < 0.05). These findings suggest that the primary pathway in the SF involves the decomposition of macromolecules, including starch hydrolysis and cellulose decomposition. Glycoside Hydrolases, which hydrolyze glycosidic bonds, along with glycolyases, play a key role in depolymerization during this process. Carbohydrate-binding modules are a class of protein domains that specifically recognize and bind carbohydrates. By tightly associating with the substrate, these modules facilitate more efficient enzyme access to and degradation of polysaccharides (Mei et al., 2023). These pathways serve as preparatory steps for the pitting fermentation process. The primary process in the PF is molecular synthesis. Glycosyltransferases catalyze the activation of sugars, facilitating their attachment to various receptor molecules, including proteins, nucleic acids, lipids, and small molecules. The products of glycosylation exhibit diverse biological functions (Lemeng & Seino, 2019). Carbohydrate esterases are enzymes that catalyze the hydrolysis of ester bonds (Mariana, Alberto, María & Carlos, 2018. These enzymes are precursors to many Baijiu flavors, primarily facilitating the formation of ester compounds while releasing fatty acids and alcohols. However, the main purpose of using CAZy analysis in this study is to preliminarily evaluate and compare the carbohydrate metabolism potential of the two key fermentation stages of Maotai-flavour Baijiu, thereby obtaining scientific hypotheses and pointing out the direction for subsequent in-depth research. The above predicted conclusions will be verified through further research in the future.

Changes of volatile compounds in the stacking fermentation and pitting fermentation grains

Fourteen volatile compounds were identified in both the SF and PF grains, including acids, alcohols, aldehydes, amides, esters, ethers, haloalkanes, heterocyclic compounds, hydrocarbons, ketones, organic nitrogen compounds, organosulfur compounds, phenols, and terpenoids. As shown in Fig. 4a, acids, alcohols, aldehydes, and esters were the predominant volatile compounds. The average relative contents of these compounds in the SF were 9.870 %, 38.610 %, 2.860 %, and 43.660 %, respectively, while in the PF, the average relative contents were 8.920 %, 44.460 %, 1.520 %, and 40.890 %, as shown in Table S1. During the fermentation process, the content of acidic volatile compounds underwent significant changes. The accumulation of acids during the PF was generally higher than during the SF, peaking particularly in the sixth and seventh rounds. Short-chain fatty acids such as Acetic acid and Hexanoic acid increased substantially during pitting fermentation, rising from 11.386 μg/kg and 3.472 μg/kg to 248.733 μg/kg and 228.267 μg/kg, respectively. Medium- and long-chain fatty acids, such as Octanoic acid and Benzeneacetic acid, contributed to fatty, waxy, and floral flavour profiles. Alcoholic volatile compounds exhibited distinct accumulation patterns between the SF and PF. Phenylethyl alcohol was the most abundant alcoholic compound, exhibiting a sharp increase during pitting fermentation, from 1631.6 μg/kg in P1 to 7505.533 μg/kg in P5, which contributed to the aromas of rose and honey. Other key alcohols, such as Benzyl alcohol and 1-Octanol, demonstrated significant increases during the PF, rising from 3.512 μg/kg and 0.384 μg/kg to 64.303 μg/kg and 6.692 μg/kg, respectively, imparting sweet, fruity, and mushroom-like flavour notes to the liquor.

The accumulation of ester volatile compounds during the PF was generally higher than during the SF, particularly for long-chain fatty acid esters and aromatic esters. Ethyl phenylacetate and Phenethyl acetate—key contributors to floral and honey aromas—showed significant increases during pitting fermentation, rising from 6.685 μg/kg and 3.130 μg/kg in S1 to 361.933 μg/kg and 175.433 μg/kg in P7, respectively. This demonstrates the critical role of pit microbiota in the biosynthesis of flavor esters. Short-chain esters, such as Ethyl hexanoate and Ethyl butyrate, accumulated markedly in the later fermentation stages, reaching 15.573 μg/kg and 5.070 μg/kg, respectively, imparting fruity notes reminiscent of apples and bananas. Aldehyde volatile compounds exhibited significant variations across fermentation rounds and stages. Benzeneacetaldehyde and Benzaldehyde emerged as the most abundant aldehydes, demonstrating substantial accumulation during pitting fermentation. These compounds contributed sweet, almond-like, and floral flavor characteristics to the liquor. Through the analysis of volatile substances, it was found that the pitting fermentation stage is the key stage for the formation of characteristic flavor substances in “sauce-like”. Some studies have shown that Ethyl hexanoate, Ethyl butyrate, and Ethyl phenylacetate are generally considered to be the characteristic substances of “sauce-like”. In this study, the content of these compounds was significantly higher in the PF stage than in the SF stage, especially accumulating in large quantities between rounds 4 and 7. In addition, acid substances such as acetic acid and caproic acid, as important flavour precursors, the increase in their content also provides a basis for subsequent esterification reactions, jointly laying the foundation for the typical flavour style of Maotai-flavour Baijiu (Qin et al., 2024).

The metabolic changes from the SF to the PF were analyzed. It was found that the expression of 38 metabolites was upregulated. The expression of 75 metabolites was downregulated, as shown in Fig. 4b. A cluster heatmap of the top 30 volatile compounds by relative content was generated to perform a cluster analysis on the flavors of the fermented grains from the seven rounds of the SF and PF, as presented in Fig. 4c. Generally, the accumulated samples from each round clustered together with the corresponding PF samples, with adjacent rounds showing a high degree of similarity. Partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) was employed to visualize the distribution differences of volatile compounds between the SF and PF, as shown in Fig. 4d, the metabolites with VIP > 2 and t-test P < 0.005 were selected as the differential metabolites, as shown in Fig. 4f. To ensure the reliability of the results, 200 permutation tests were conducted on the OPLS-DA model. The intercept of the Q2 regression line was less than 0, indicating that the model was not overfitted, as shown in Fig. 4e.

Correlation analysis between physical and chemical changes of fermented grains and microorganisms during fermentation

Canonical Correspondence Analysis (CCA) was employed to investigate the correlation between the top 10 bacterial and fungal in the SF and PF and the physicochemical indices of the fermented grains. As shown in Fig. 5(a) and Fig. 5(c), starch was negatively correlated with hydration and acidity, weakly associated with reducing sugars and total sugars, and positively correlated with amylase activity. The Spearman rank correlation coefficient was employed to investigate the correlations among the top 10 bacterial and fungal taxa and physicochemical indices. As shown in Fig. 5b and Fig. 5d, Lentibacillus and Bacillus exhibited strong positive correlations with both reducing sugar and total sugar, indicating their significant involvement in carbohydrate metabolism. Concurrently, Limosilactobacillus and Weissella showed positive correlations with starch content and amylase activity, suggesting their potential roles in starch conversion. The fungal community demonstrated more pronounced functional differentiation. Pichia and Saccharomyces showed a positive correlation with starch content and amylase activity, whereas Monascus and Zygosaccharomyces displayed a strong negative correlation with these parameters, revealing an apparent functional complementarity. These correlation patterns highlighted functional specialization within microbial communities during fermentation, with bacterial taxa primarily dominating sugar metabolism, while fungal taxa played a more prominent role in regulating starch conversion (Ge et al., 2024). Through synergistic interactions, these microbial communities collectively drove dynamic changes in the physicochemical properties of the fermentation substrate, thereby establishing the microbiological foundation for the distinctive flavor profile of Maotai-flavour Baijiu.

Correlation analysis between volatile compounds changes of fermented grains and microorganisms during fermentation

An analysis of the correlations between microorganisms and metabolites in fermented grains was conducted to elucidate the potential metabolic functions of different microbial taxa during fermentation and their contributions to the synthesis of flavor compounds. As shown in Fig. 6a, the bacterial correlation analysis revealed that Weissella and Limosilactobacillus exhibited a significant positive correlation with phenolic compounds, indicating their pivotal role in the synthesis of aromatic compounds in fermented grains (Xia, Jin, Zhao, Zhou, Zheng & Wu, 2024). Acetilactobacillus showed a strong positive correlation with esters, suggesting its dominance in esterification reactions during fermentation, thereby contributing to the fruity aroma of the liquor. It has been reported that this bacterium can secrete extracellular esterases that directly catalyze the synthesis of esters from acid and alcohol precursors. Secondly, as a lactic acid bacteria, its metabolism produces a large amount of lactic acid, which not only provides a direct substrate for esterification reaction, but also creates an acidic microenvironment suitable for ester synthesis (Chuang, Chen, Wang, Chen & Shaw, 2011; Xue, Yao, You & Gong, 2020). Therefore, the dominant growth of Acetilactobacillus in pitting fermentation determines the richness and complexity of ester substances in Maotai-flavour Baijiu. In contrast, Staphylococcus and Bacillus were negatively correlated with acetic acid, possibly due to their inhibitory effects on acid production or competition for carbon source metabolism.

As shown in Fig. 6b, the fungal correlation analysis demonstrated that Pichia exhibited significant positive correlations with phenolic compounds and aromatic aldehydes, suggesting its potential dominance in the formation of smoky and woody aromas in fermented grains. This phenomenon could be linked to its high Glycosyltransferase activity revealed by our metagenomic analysis, potentially facilitating the shikimate or phenylalanine metabolic pathways leading to the biosynthesis of these complex aldehydes. Saccharomyces showed positive correlations with esters and long-chain fatty acids, indicating its central role in ester synthesis and lipid metabolism. Notably, Monascus and Zygosaccharomyces were strongly positively correlated with medium-chain fatty acids and phenylethyl acetate, likely contributing to the enhanced mellow mouthfeel and persistent flavor of the liquor.

Comparison of the main metabolic pathways of stacking fermentation and pitting fermentation

The brewing process of Maotai-flavour Baijiu can be divided into two main stages: stacking fermentation and pitting fermentation. During these stages, the aerobic and anaerobic respiration mechanisms of yeast play a crucial role (Leng, Li, Li, Lu, Li & Gao, 2024). The primary metabolic pathways involved are illustrated in Fig. 7.

The abundance of the main metabolic pathways and key enzymes during the fermentation process of Maotai-flavour liquor in both stacking fermentation and pit fermentation.

The stacking fermentation stage, as the initial phase of Maotai-flavour Baijiu brewing, is characterized by the aerobic respiration of microorganisms. During this phase, yeast converts starch from the raw materials into pyruvate (ko 00010) through glycolysis, utilizing oxygen from the environment, while producing a small amount of ATP (Chen et al., 2021). Subsequently, pyruvate is transported into the mitochondria, where it undergoes complete oxidation to carbon dioxide and water, releasing large amounts of ATP through the Citrate cycle (ko 00020) and oxidative phosphorylation. EC2.3.1.9 was the primary enzyme involved in this process, and its abundance in the SF was significantly higher than that in the PF. In the early stages of fermentation, amylases secreted by molds hydrolyze starch in the raw materials into glucose, providing a critical substrate for yeast. Simultaneously, proteases break down proteins into amino acids, providing an essential nitrogen source for microbial growth (Wei, Nie, Du, et al., 2023). During this process, yeast generates a significant amount of heat through aerobic respiration. This temperature increase is accompanied by the production of small amounts of precursor substances, such as alcohols, organic acids, and esters (ko 00620). The primary function of stacking fermentation is to establish a favorable growth environment for yeast and other microorganisms (Huang et al., 2017). This sets the stage for the subsequent pitting fermentation process.

In the brewing process of Maotai-flavour Baijiu, after the completion of the stacking fermentation stage, the process transitions into the pitting fermentation stage, which is crucial for the overall fermentation of Maotai-flavour Baijiu (Chen et al., 2024). In the anaerobic environment of the pits, yeast breaks down glucose into pyruvate (ko 00010) through the glycolysis pathway, producing small amounts of ATP in the process. Pyruvate then undergoes further metabolic transformation to produce acetaldehyde, which is subsequently reduced to ethanol (alcohol) by alcohol dehydrogenase, releasing CO₂. During this process, EC 2.7.1.40 and EC 1.1.1.27 in the PF were significantly higher than those in the SF. This reaction constitutes the primary biochemical pathway by which yeast converts glucose into alcohol and CO₂ during pitting fermentation. Under anaerobic conditions, yeast also produces small amounts of glycerol, higher alcohols, organic acids, esters, and other flavor compounds (Wei, Shen, Cheng & Zhang, 2023). In addition, anaerobic microorganisms such as Lactobacillus convert sugars into organic acids, including lactic acid and acetic acid, further enriching the flavor profile of the Baijiu (Nan, Du & Yan, 2020).

Conclusion

Through integrated metagenomic, metabolomic, and microbial metabolic function analyses, this study systematically elucidates the dynamic relationship between microbial community succession and flavor compound formation during Maotai-flavour Baijiu fermentation, along with the underlying metabolic mechanisms. Results demonstrate that stacking fermentation is predominantly driven by aerobic microorganisms that secrete amylases and cellulases to facilitate the decomposition of macromolecules, thereby providing essential precursor substances for the subsequent pitting fermentation stage. The anaerobic environment of pitting fermentation significantly enriches Acetilactobacillus and Pichia populations, which synergistically accumulate characteristic flavor compounds through glycosyltransferase activity and esterification reactions, ultimately forming the core flavor profile of sauce-flavor Baijiu. Metabolic network analysis further reveals multi-stage cooperative mechanisms that collectively contribute to the distinctive flavor characteristics of Maotai-flavour Baijiu. Future investigations should focus on elucidating the roles of key microorganisms and their metabolic pathways at different fermentation stages to enable better control of Baijiu flavor and quality.

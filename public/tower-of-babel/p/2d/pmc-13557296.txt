Sherlock Holmes In Dermatology: Solving Diagnostic Mysteries With Ultraviolet Light
Authors: Milos Nisavic, Aleksandra Bukvic Sajinovic, Danica Tiodorovic
Published: 2026

Case Presentation

We present three diagnostically challenging skin infections identified through ultraviolet-induced fluorescence dermoscopy (UVFD) and Wood’s lamp examination (Figure 1). Case 1: A patient with bilateral chronic toe-web malodorous maceration previously treated with topical antifungals. Clinical examination showed erythema and desquamation (Figure 1A). Wood’s lamp and UVFD showed green fluorescence suggestive of Pseudomonas aeruginosa (Figure 1B, 1C), respectively. Cultures confirmed concomitant infection with Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Trichophyton mentagrophytes var. interdigitale.

Case 2: A patient with asymptomatic subtle variation in pubic hair shaft thickness (Figure 1D). Dermoscopy showed yellowish-white hair shaft concretions (Figure 1E), while UVFD highlighted yellow-green concretions (Figure 1F), consistent with the diagnosis of Trichobacteriosis (formerly Trichomycosis palmellina) in the pubic region.

Case 3: A patient with pruritus and discomfort of the left external auditory canal. Clinical examination detected no abnormalities (Figure 1G), however, Wood’s lamp examination showed coral-red fluorescence (Figure 1H), suggestive of Corynebacterium spp. Symptoms and fluorescence resolved completely after oral erythromycin therapy (Figure 1I). Unilateral psoriasis of auditory canal as differential diagnosis appears less likely. Based on current literature, no other plausible explanation for this finding has been reported.

Teaching Point

UVFD and Wood’s lamp exhibit distinct fluorescence patterns that aid in differentiation and enhance diagnostic accuracy in infectious dermatoses [1,2]. They are particularly useful in difficult-to-access areas (auditory canal, folds, pubic hair). Not all skin infections were clinically evident and would presumably have been overlooked without these diagnostic tools. They facilitated early initiation of pathogen-specific therapy prior to microbiological identification of pathogens.

Correlation between medical coping style and mindfulness level of hospitalized elderly: implications for enhancing psychological well-being
Authors: Kun Yang, Jinwen Bai, Emerson Galang Aliswag, Jian Song
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe mindfulness level of hospitalized elderly is influenced by factors such as physical health, emotional resilience, and social support. However, little is known regarding the relationship between medical coping styles and mindfulness levels of hospitalized elderly. The aims of this study were to explore the association between medical coping styles and mindfulness levels in hospitalized elderly patients, with a focus on identifying actionable strategies to enhance patient well-being.MethodsA survey of 253 elderly patients was conducted in the Department of Geriatrics of a 3 A hospital in Tangshan from September to November 2022 using the General Questionnaire, Medical Coping Style Scale (MCMQ), and Mindfulness-Attention Awareness Scale (MAAS).ResultsThe average mindfulness level score of the hospitalized elderly was (59.71 ± 13.90), with a scoring rate of 66.34%, at a moderate mindfulness level. This study revealed a strong positive correlation (r = 0.725, P < 0.05) between confrontation coping and mindfulness, highlighting the importance of promoting active coping strategies to improve mindfulness levels in elderly patients.ConclusionThe mindfulness level of the hospitalized elderly shows potential for improvement, their coping styles are closely related to their mindfulness level, and positive coping styles can help improve the mindfulness level of the hospitalized elderly. This study suggested that medical staff can consider formulating targeted interventions, contingent upon available resources and training, guide patients to establish positive coping styles, and improve the mindfulness level of hospitalized elderly people.

Introduction

China currently has the largest elderly population in the world, but their overall health status is not optimistic [1, 2]. With increasing age, the decline in bodily functions combined with the coexistence of multiple chronic diseases has led to a yearly rise in hospitalization rates among the elderly, and the resulting mental health problems of the elderly inpatients cannot be ignored [3]. The incidence of mental problems in elderly hospitalized patients is 78%, and 53.8% of elderly patients with chronic diseases take negative coping style [4]. Medical coping style reflects the patient’s psychological state in dealing with problems [5] and is an important moderating variable affecting the patient’s mental health [6]. Negative coping strategies can aggravate the negative impact of the disease [7], leading to negative emotions in patients [8]. Mindfulness is focusing attention on the present and perceiving each moment of experience in a receptive and nonjudgmental manner [9], which can help patients maintain their attention and alertness to current events [10], freeing them from bad thinking patterns and negative emotions [11]. At present, it is generally believed that the core factors of mindfulness are “attention” and “open attitude”. This suggests that there may be a link between levels of mindfulness and medical coping styles. The mindfulness level of hospitalized elderly people is usually affected by various factors such as individual demographic factors, social psychological factors, and disease-related factors. Research on mindfulness in mainland China is relatively limited, focusing mainly on students and medical staff. There are few studies on hospitalized elderly people as research subjects to explore the relationship between coping styles and mindfulness levels. This study aims to explore the relationship between coping styles and mindfulness levels in hospitalized elderly, providing crucial insights for developing interventions to enhance their mental well-being. Our specific aims were as follows: (a) To assess mindfulness level in hospitalized older adults. (b) Determine the relationship between medical coping styles and mindfulness level.

Methods

Study design and setting

This study is a descriptive, cross-sectional study. A total of 253 elderly patients admitted to the geriatric medicine department of a 3 A hospital in Tangshan from September to November 2022 were selected as the research subjects.

Ethical considerations

This study has been approved by the PWU Ethics Committee (approved ERB2022_0066) and the site of investigation.

Research tools

The general information questionnaire includes age, gender, marital status, education level, religious belief, medical burden, sleep disturbance, and muscle strength.

Medical Coping Style Scale (MCMQ) consists of three dimensions of “Confrontation,” “Avoidance,” and “Acceptance-Resignation” with 20 items [12]; using the Likert 4-level scoring method, add the scores of each dimension question; the score ranges are 1–32 points, 1–28 points, and 1–20 points, the higher the score, the more the investigators are inclined to this coping style [13]. The importance of using validated psychometric tools, such as the Medical Coping Style Questionnaire (MCMQ), is well-established in the literature, as emphasized by Diotaiuti et al. (2020), who conducted a validation study for the Italian Brief Version of the Communication Styles Inventory (CSI-B/I) [14].

Mindfulness-Attention Awareness Scale (MAAS) consists of 15 items and adopts the Likert 6-level scoring method [15]. Each item is scored from “always” to “never,” and the total score is 15–90. The higher the total score, the higher the mindfulness level [16]. Among them, less than 40 points are poor, 41–65 points are medium, and 66–90 points are good. After testing, the scale’s internal consistency was Cronbach’s r was 0.89, while the test-retest reliability was 0.87 [17].

Investigation method

Elderly patients in the Geriatrics Department of a 3 A hospital in Tangshan City eligible for inclusion were surveyed via questionnaire using a unified survey scale inpatient from September to November 2022. The primary investigator personally conducted the survey, ensuring that participants were fully informed of the purpose and significance of the study, thereby enhancing informed consent and data reliability. Questionnaires were distributed and collected on the spot, and the questionnaires were filled in by the respondents themselves. After collecting the questionnaires, check them one by one and ask the respondents to fill in the missing items in time. In this survey, 268 questionnaires were distributed, and 253 valid questionnaires were recovered, with an effective rate of 94.40% (253/268).

Statistical method

SPSS22.0 software was used for descriptive analysis and Pearson correlation analysis, whereas multivariate linear regression analysis were used for multiple factors. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

General conditions of hospitalized elderly

This study investigated 253 hospitalized elderly patients, including 128 males and 125 females. The mean age was (75.0 ± 21.83) years old. This study has 151 cases with spouses. Moreover, 27.27% of the elderly patients had a primary school education or below. Elderly patients with religious beliefs accounted for 39.92%. This study has 81 cases with a medical burden, 102 cases of sleep disorders, and 30.04% of elderly patients had abnormal muscle strength.

Scores of mindfulness level in hospitalized elderly

The average mindfulness level score of the hospitalized elderly was (59.71 ± 13.90), the score rate was 66.34%, and the mindfulness score was at the middle level.

Scores of medical coping styles in hospitalized elderly

A total of 253 cases of hospitalized elderly medical coping style scored (18.38 ± 6.04) points in the confrontation dimension, (14.26 ± 3.80) points in the avoidance dimension, and (10.80 ± 5.18) points in the acceptance-resignation dimension. Among them, 89 cases (35.18%) chose confrontation coping, 71 cases (28.06%) chose avoidance coping, and 93 cases (36.76%) chose acceptance-resignation coping.

Correlation analysis between medical coping style and mindfulness level of hospitalized elderly

The results exhibited a strong positive correlation between confrontation coping and the average score of mindfulness level (r = 0.725, P < 0.05); a strong negative correlation between avoidance coping and the average score of mindfulness level (r = -0.604, P < 0.05); a strong negative correlation between and acceptance-resignation coping and the average score of mindfulness level (r = -0.800, P < 0.05, Table 1).

Discussion

Current status of mindfulness levels in hospitalized elderly

The study’s results exposed that the mindfulness level score of hospitalized elderly people was (59.71 ± 13.90) points, the score rate was 66.34%, and the mindfulness level score was at a medium level, consistent with Jin’s [18] research results on patients with chronic heart failure. Related studies have indicated [19] that the higher the patient’s self-stigma, the lower the corresponding mindfulness level [11, 20]. Various factors, such as disease and the hospitalization environment, affect the mental health of hospitalized elderly people [21]. While suffering from physical diseases, they are also prone to negative emotions, such as low self-esteem, anxiety, and depression, leading to stigma [22]. Decreased self-behavioral regulation in response to illness stress in elderly hospitalized patients, leading to reduced levels of mindfulness. Therefore, medical staff should pay more attention to the mental health status of the hospitalized elderly, actively conduct psychological counseling, strengthen psychological care, and improve the mindfulness level of the hospitalized elderly.

Current status of medical coping style in hospitalized elderly

This survey’s results presented that hospitalized elderly medical coping style scored (18.38 ± 6.04) points in the confrontation dimension, (14.26 ± 3.80) points in the avoidance dimension, and (10.80 ± 5.18) points in the acceptance-resignation dimension. Among them, 89 cases (35.18%) chose confrontation coping, 71 cases (28.06%) chose avoidance coping, and 93 cases (36.76%) chose acceptance-resignation coping. This study revealed that the selected subjects seldom adopt confrontation coping styles but mostly avoidance and acceptance-resignation coping styles, consistent with Zhang [23]. It may be related to the obvious discomfort symptoms of hospitalized elderly people during the acute attack stage of the disease [24]. The elderly hospitalized suffer from physical and mental pressure due to degenerative changes and body pain [25]. The negative coping style of avoidance can instead divert attention and reduce pain, but it is not beneficial to use it for a long time as the disease progresses [26]. The avoidance coping style enters a vicious circle under the constant suggestion of negative psychological conditions and eventually develops into a coping style of acceptance-resignation [27]. Simultaneously, most respondents are retired, their interpersonal network is narrowed, and they have few opportunities to accept new things, resulting in limited ability and access to disease-related knowledge, making it impossible for hospitalized elderly people to adopt positive coping methods [28]. Therefore, medical staff should reduce patients’ negative emotions during clinical treatment, explain disease-related knowledge, and encourage the elderly to cope with the disease actively, enhancing the therapeutic effect.

Correlation between medical coping style and mindfulness level in hospitalized elderly

This survey’s results displayed a strong positive correlation between confrontation coping and the average score of mindfulness level (r = 0.725, P < 0.05), a strong negative correlation between avoidance coping and the average score of mindfulness level (r = -0.604, P < 0.05), and a strong negative correlation between acceptance-resignation coping and the average score of mindfulness level (r = -0.800, P < 0.05). The studies [29, 30] have demonstrated that improving patients’ positive coping styles and eliminating negative coping can further increase patients’ attention to the disease, improve patients’ confidence in treatment, encourage patients to focus on the present, and they can adopt positive coping strategies to deal with the negative effects of stress events, thereby improving their level of mindfulness [31]. In addition to promoting positive coping styles, the integration of remote physical exercise protocols, as explored by D’Oliveira et al. (2022), could further contribute to increasing mindfulness levels and psychological well-being in hospitalized elderly patients [32].

Influencing factors of mindfulness level in hospitalized elderly

Multiple linear regression analysis revealed that age, gender, religious belief, muscle strength, confrontation coping, and acceptance-resignation coping are independent influencing factors of mindfulness in hospitalized elderly. The study’s findings indicate a decline in mindfulness levels with increasing age among hospitalized elderly (P < 0.05), which is consistent with previous research suggesting that cognitive decline and increased chronic illness contribute to reduced mindfulness [33, 34]. It may be related to the poorer mental health status of the elderly and the more prone to negative emotions [35]. As the body ages, the number of chronic diseases increases, and the sense of physical discomfort gradually increases, producing destructive emotions and negative coping styles and a lower mindfulness level in the older hospitalized elderly [36]. This study’s results present that gender is one of the influencing factors in the mindfulness level of the hospitalized elderly, with males having a higher mindfulness level than females (P < 0.05). Women are born with delicate and fragile emotions [37], are highly susceptible to adverse stimuli, have relatively poor physical fitness, are prone to exhaustion, and have a greater physical and mental burden, so mindfulness is low [38]. This study’s results exhibited that the hospitalized elderly with religious beliefs had a higher mindfulness level than those without religious beliefs (P < 0.05). The reason may be that the internal mechanism of religion helps alleviates the depression of the elderly due to the decline of physical function [39] and disease and eliminates their fear and anxiety in the face of disease and death threats with the aid of religion’s ideal world [40]. Therefore, hospitalized elderly people with religious beliefs have higher mindfulness levels. This study’s results revealed that the hospitalized elderly with normal muscle strength had a higher level of mindfulness than those with abnormal muscle strength (P < 0.05), possibly because the elderly with abnormal muscle strength are more prone to depression [20]. The elderly can easily lead to impaired daily activities and loneliness due to abnormal muscle strength [41]. Simultaneously, the reduced self-care ability of the elderly makes them elderly feel depressed [42], decreasing their mindfulness level. The research results indicated that the coping style of confrontation has a positive predictive effect on the mindfulness level, whereas the coping style of acceptance-resignation has a negative predictive effect on the mindfulness level (P < 0.05), consistent with Qin et al. [11]. For the elderly, choosing the confrontational coping style to deal with the stressful events caused by hospitalization can form better self-behavior regulation, maintain an optimistic emotional state, and actively clarify the cognitive disease itself and its adverse effects stimulation [43], thereby helping maintain high levels of mindfulness. An acceptance-resignation coping style can easily lead to the loss of confidence in rehabilitation for the hospitalized elderly, and they are uncertain for a long time, so the elderly are unwilling to pay attention to the present and cannot concentrate, resulting in a low mindfulness level [44]. Medical staff should implement structured programs to actively guide hospitalized elderly patients in adopting effective coping styles, particularly focusing on vulnerable groups such as women, non-religious individuals, and those with abnormal muscle strength, and pay attention to their emotional changes to improve clinical symptoms, help patients face changes in the disease with a positive mental state, reduce their negative emotions, and improve mindfulness.

This study has several limitations. First, the single-center nature of the study may limit the generalizability of the findings to other hospital settings or populations. Second, the reliance on self-report questionnaires may introduce response bias, as patients could over- or under-report their coping strategies and mindfulness levels. Third, the cross-sectional design of the study limits the ability to draw causal inferences between medical coping styles and mindfulness levels. Therefore, the time sequence and causal relationship between the various influencing factors need further research and demonstration.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the mindfulness levels of hospitalized elderly are generally moderate, with coping styles playing a significant role. Health professionals should prioritize interventions that foster positive coping mechanisms, particularly in older adults with lower muscle strength or those without religious support, to enhance their mindfulness and overall well-being.

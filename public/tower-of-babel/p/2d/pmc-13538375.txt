A user equilibrium traffic assignment model based on the most reliable paths
Authors: Tianze Xu, Yanyan Liu, Yuhan Mao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn some situations, drivers prefer to select the most reliable paths (MRP). In the minimum-delay routing problem in communication networks, signals need to be transmitted on the MRPs. Since a minimum cost path may not be an MRP, the traditional minimum cost path based user equilibrium does not apply to such scenarios. Therefore, the study of the connectivity reliability based user equilibrium (RUE) model which is based on the MRPs is needed. Unfortunately, none of the previous studies ever covered the RUE based on the MRPs. In this paper, a RUE variational inequality (VI) model based on the MRPs is presented. The path reliability is the multiplication of the reliability of the links on the path. A heuristic algorithm is presented to solve the VI model. A multiplication-based algorithm is used to find the MRP in the solution process. The model and algorithm are applied to two networks and their validity is shown. The method presented in this paper is useful for the emergency evacuation on transportation networks or the minimum-delay routing problem in communication networks.Keywords: Most reliable paths, Heuristic algorithm, Connectivity reliability, Transportation network, User equilibriumSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

When a random event (such as road crash or other random events including vehicle break down, traffic jam, random perturbation, etc.) happens on a link on a transportation network, the link may collapse. Consequently, the traffic on the link may get stuck and the traffic flow on the network may be influenced. The connectivity reliability of a link is defined as the probability the link does not collapse. There is probability of collision between two neighboring running vehicles on a road. The more vehicles there are on a road/link, the more likely a link collapse and the less the connectivity reliability of the link is1,2. In some situations, to avoid natural disasters such as flood, hurricane, etc., drivers prefer to select the most reliable paths (MRP) from their origins to their shelters/destinations because it is more probable to escape from the disaster on an MRP. Since the connectivity reliability of a link decreases with the increase of the flow on it, a connectivity reliability-based user equilibrium (RUE) status will form where no drivers can increase their path reliability by unilaterally changing their path. In the minimum-delay routing problem in communication networks, which is equivalent to traffic assignment problem3–5, the signal needs to be transmitted to each destination on the MRPs because it is more reliable. In such scenarios, the MRP is more important than the minimum cost path and should be used in modeling the problems. Since a minimum cost path may not be an MRP, the traditional shortest path or minimum cost path based user equilibrium (UE) model does not apply to the scenarios. Therefore, the study of the RUE based on the MRPs is needed. In previous studies on the RUE, the path reliability is derived from the sum of the link times (which are treated as random variables). According to reliability theory, the sequential links on the path form a series system and the reliability of a series system is the product of the reliability of the links of the series system6. According to probability theory, the path reliability should be the multiplication of the reliability of the links since the drivers select each link on the path sequentially. For such reasons, the RUE traffic assignment problem in which the path reliability is the multiplication of the reliability of the links on the path should be studied. In the path cost based equilibrium model, the path cost of the used paths of each OD is equal. In the path reliability based equilibrium model, the path reliability of the used paths of each OD is equal. Unfortunately, based on the literature review conducted for this study, none of the previous studies ever covered the RUE which path reliability is the multiplication of the reliability of the links on the path.

In this paper, a RUE traffic assignment variational inequality (VI) model based on the MRPs is presented. In the model, the path connectivity reliability is the multiplication of the connectivity reliability of the links on the path. A heuristic algorithm is presented to solve the VI model. Since the shortest paths may not be the MRPs, a multiplication-based algorithm is used to find the MRP in the solution process. The contributions of this paper include: (1) A RUE traffic assignment VI model based on the MRPs is presented. (2) A heuristic algorithm is presented to solve the VI model. (3) A multiplication-based algorithm is used to find the MRP.

The rest of the paper is arranged as follows: Sect. 2 covers the literature review of this study. Section 3 presents the RUE traffic assignment VI model. Section 4 presents a heuristic algorithm for the RUE VI model. Section 5 shows the validity of the model and algorithm by applying it to two networks. Section 5 presents the conclusion of the paper.

Conclusions

This paper presented a RUE traffic assignment VI model and a heuristic algorithm for solving the model. Different from other studies, the path reliability is the multiplication of the reliability of the links on the path. The model and algorithm were applied to two networks. The result shows that the path reliabilities of the paths of the same OD are almost equal, meaning that the outcome of the model and algorithm is consistent with the definition of the RUE problem.

The practical implications of the proposed approach include: (1) In some situations drivers prefer to select the MRP because it is more reliable to escape from the disaster. (2) In the minimum-delay routing problem in communication networks, signal should be transmitted on the MRPs because it is more reliable. In such scenarios, the RUE status is formed and the network flow should be modelled by the RUE model presented in this study.

The limitation of this study is that the model is a static reliability assignment model. It does not apply to dynamic scenarios. Future study may further extend the analysis to dynamic scenarios by considering the dynamic features such as dynamic disaster evolution, time-varying demand, capacity degradation, or incomplete information.

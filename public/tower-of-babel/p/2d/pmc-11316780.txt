Identification, fermentation optimization, and biocontrol efficacy of actinomycete YG-5 for the prevention of Alternaria leaf spot disease in star anise
Authors: Jieming Pan, Xiaoshan Geng, Yujing Cai, Ye Yu, Yanrong Hou, Yao Liu, Caina Ya, Qin Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Star anise (Illicium verum), a valuable spice tree, faces significant threats from fungal diseases, particularly Alternaria leaf spot. This study investigates the potential of a soil-derived actinomycete strain, YG-5, as a biocontrol agent against Alternaria tenuissima, the causative pathogen on Alternaria leaf spot in star anise. Through comprehensive morphology, physiology, biochemistry, and genetic analyses, we identified the isolate as Streptomyces sp. YG-5. The strain exhibited broad-spectrum antimicrobial activity against several plant pathogens, with inhibition rates ranging between 36.47 to 80.34%. We systematically optimized the fermentation conditions for YG-5, including medium composition and cultivation parameters. The optimized process resulted in an 89.56% inhibition rate against A. tenuissima, a 14.72% improvement over non-optimized conditions. Notably, the antimicrobial compounds produced by YG-5 demonstrated stability across various temperatures, pH levels, and UV irradiation. In vivo efficacy trials showed promising results, with YG-5 fermentation broth reducing Alternaria leaf spot incidence on star anise leaves by 56.95%. These findings suggest that Streptomyces sp. YG-5 holds significant potential as a biocontrol agent against Alternaria leaf spot in star anise cultivation, offering a sustainable approach to disease management in this valuable crop.

Introduction

Star anise (Illicium verum), a member of the Illiciaceae family and the genus of Illicium, is a precious spice tree predominantly cultivated in the tropical and subtropical regions of Asia. Guangxi province in China leads the nation in both cultivation area and production1,2, contributing significantly to the global market. China accounts for approximately 85% of the domestic market share and 70% of the global market share, underscoring the crop's economic importance3. The versatility of star anise extends across multiple industries, including culinary, cosmetics, pharmaceuticals, and beverages. It serves as a key ingredient in condiments, cosmetics, food products, soaps, perfumes, medicines, and various beverages. This wide-ranging application has positioned star anise as a crucial economic crop, playing a pivotal role in promoting economic development and improving living standards in the Guangxi region3,4. However, in recent years, the star anise industry has faced significant challenges due to the increasing prevalence of fungal diseases, particularly those caused by Alternaria species. These pathogens primarily affect the leaves and tender branches of the plant, causing withering and defoliation. The frequent outbreaks of these diseases have not only severely impacted the production and quality of star anise but also pose a serious threat to the continued growth and international competitiveness of China's star anise industry5.

Actinomycetes, a diverse group of aerobic, filamentous Gram-positive bacteria, are ubiquitous in natural ecosystems and possess a remarkable array of metabolic capabilities6–8. This versatility has established them as an invaluable biological resource with significant potential in various applications. Notably, actinomycetes have been a prolific source of bioactive metabolites, accounting for over 70% of naturally discovered antibiotics to date9. In the realm of agricultural diseases management, actinomycetes have shown considerable promise. Researchers have successfully isolated numerous actinomycetes strains from soil and plants samples, demonstrating their significant efficacy in controlling a wide range of plant pathogens. These include devastating diseases such as rice blast, apple canker, and tomato gray mold10–12, highlighting the broad-spectrum antimicrobial potential of actinomycetes. Further investigations have focused on elucidating the active components of antagonistic metabolites produced by actinomycetes13,14. For instance, Li et al. isolated sesquiterpenoid compounds with broad-spectrum antibacterial activity15, while Reis et al. identified an active cyclic peptide that effectively inhibited the growth of Colletotrichum gloeosporioides16. These findings not only expand the application scope of actinomycetes but also provide new ideas and methods for using biological approaches to prevent and control agricultural diseases such as Alternaria leaf spot in star anise.

The efficiency of microbial fermentation processes is critically dependent on the performance of the microbial strain, its nutrients requirements, and the specific cultivation conditions employed17,18. To maximize the production potential of a given strain, it is essential to elucidate the optimal fermentation parameters. These paremeters include the strain-specific nutrient composition, optimal cultivation temperature, appropriate inoculation size, and precise fermentation duration. Systematic optimization of these variables offers multiple advantages, including reduction of fermentation cycle time, enhancement of overall process efficiency and Improvement in the quality and efficacy of fermentation products19,20. Given the significance of these factors, our study has designed a comprehensive series of experiments aimed at optimizing these critical fermentation parameters. The primary objective is to establish the optimal growth conditions for the strain, thereby maximizing its potential for biocontrol applications.

In this study, we screened and identified an actinomycetes strain, YG-5, from soil samples that exhibited significant antagonistic effects against Alternaria leaf spot disease in star anise. We conducted comprehensive research to characterize the genus and species of this strain, and systematically optimized the fermentation conditions to enhance the production of antimicrobial active substances. Through this optimization process, we explored the properties and potential of these antimicrobial compounds for plant disease prevention and control. These findings not only provide foundational data for the development of effective biological control agents, but also lay the research groundwork for the further separation and identification of the active antimicrobial compounds produced by this strain. The ability of this actinomycetes strain to inhibit the Alternaria pathogen that causes leaf spot disease in star anise highlights its promising application in sustainable and eco-friendly plant disease management strategies.

Additionally, strain YG-5 demonstrated strong antagonistic effects against several bacterial pathogens. Optimization of fermentiation conditions

Nine fermentation media with diverse nutrient compositions and carbon-nitrogen ratios were evaluated to enhance the antimicrobial activity of antagonistic strain YG-5. Strain YG-5 cultured in AL medium exhibited the highest inhibitory activity against Alternaria tenuissima, with an 82.77% inhibition rate (Fig. 5A). Consequently, AL medium was chosen for further optimization of fermentation conditions. Fermentation parameters were then optimized using AL medium, including inoculum size (10%), working volume (100 mL in 250 mL flasks), fermentation time (10 days), temperature (24 °C), and agitation speed (120 rpm) (Fig. 5B–F). Orthogonal experimental design further refined optimal conditions: AL medium, 100 mL working volume in 250 mL flasks, 10% inoculum size, 140 rpm agitation speed, 26 °C fermentation temperature, and a 9-day fermentation period. Antifungal activity of the fermentation broth against Alternaria tenuissima

In vitro leaf assays demonstrated the fermentation broth's efficacy against the pathogen. The treatment group showed a significantly reduced disease index of 43.05% compared to the control group's 100% after 7 days of inoculation. Application of the fermentation broth with the pathogen resulted in notably smaller leaf lesion diameters, indicating substantially reduced disease severity. Discussion

Alternaria tenuissima is a widespread fungal pathogen that affects various plant species, including star anise, and poses significant challenges to agricultural production. This pathogen has been reported to cause diseases in diverse plants such as fig fruit, Angelica dahurica, Pittosporum tobira, and Amaranthus hybridus. Recent research has focused on developing effective control measures against A. tenuissima infections. For instance, studies have shown that chickpea vicilin, a glycoprotein, exhibits potent antifungal properties against A. tenuissima, significantly reducing fungal growth and disease severity in postharvest fig fruit21. Additionally, chemical control methods have been explored, with fungicides like azoxystrobin, flutriafol, prochloraz and hymexazol demonstrating efficacy in reducing the incidence and severity of early dieback caused by A. tenuissima in different plants22. In the context of star anise cultivation, A. tenuissima-induced leaf spot disease has emerged as a major threat, significantly impacting the development of the star anise industry in China. These findings underscore the importance of identifying and developing effective control strategies, including both biological and chemical approaches, to manage A. tenuissima infections and mitigate their detrimental effects on various plant species, including star anise.

Actinomycetes are an important source of bioactive compounds23 and have shown great promise in the biological control of plant diseases. They can produce antimicrobial substances, degrade fungal cell walls and enzymes, making them a group of microorganisms with great practical value in agricultural applications24,25. In this study, an actinomycete strain named Streptomyces sp. YG-5 was isolated from the soil of the Yinggeling primitive tropical forest. This strain exhibited strong antagonistic activity against Alternaria tenuissima, thecausative agent of leaf spot disease in star anise. The fermentation broth of actinomycete YG-5 exhibited significant disease control efficacy on infected star anise leaves, with a 56.95% reduction compared to the control. This finding aligns with previous studies on soil-isolated actinomycetes and their antifungal properties. For instance, Li et al. reported 83.4% control efficacy against Botrytis cinerea26, while Patil et al. observed 53.33% efficacy against Rhizoctonia solani27. Similarly, Hu et al.28. and Dai et al.11. demonstrated control efficacies of 61.32% and 90.3% against Penicillium digitatum and apple rot disease, respectively. The effectiveness of YG-5 is particularly promising given the challenges typically associated with managing Alternaria diseases.

Antimicrobial compounds plays a crucial role in the biocontrol activity of microorganisms, with their production levels directly influencing the effectiveness of biocontrol strains in practical application24,29. Fermentation is essential for microorganisms to produce these active substances, and optimizing cultivation conditions is a key strategy to enhance their antimicrobial capability and productivity30,31. Research have demonstrated that different fermentation medium formulations can significantly a strain's antimicrobial activity31. Selecting an appropriate medium can improve both microorganism proliferation and metabolite production rates. Moreover, factors such as temperature, time, inoculum size, and agitation speed can affect the production and activity of secondary metabolites32,33. Given the importance of cultivation conditions, this study focused on optimizing the fermentation conditions of actinomycete strain YG-5. Through single-factor and orthogonal experiments, researchers determined the optimal fermentation medium composition and cultivation conditions to enhance the production of antimicrobial active substances. The results were promising, with the optimized fermentation broth showing significantly higher antimicrobial activity compared to the basic fermentation culture. The inhibition rate of the optimized fermentation broth reached 89.56%, representing a 14.72% increase over the unoptimized condition. These findings underscore the importance of optimizing cultivation conditions to maximize the potential of biocontrol strains in producing antimicrobial compounds.

The stability of antimicrobial compounds is crucial for the development and production of effective biopesticides. Environmental factors such as high temperature, UV irradiation, and pH can significantly impact the antimicrobial activity, leading to a reduction or even loss of the activity30. Previous studies have reported that antimicrobial compounds produced by some actinomycetes are sensitive to strong acids and bases. For instance, Yun et al. discovered that the active compounds produced by actinomycete 1–14 were vulnerable to both strongly acidic and alkaline conditions30. Similarly, Qu et al. reported that while the antimicrobial compounds from actinomycete strains WMF106 were resistant to alkaline environments, they were sensitive to acidic conditions34. In contrast, the current study found that the antimicrobial compounds in the fermentation broth of actinomycete strain YG-5 demonstrated remarkable stability against heat, light, and varying pH levels. This stability is a significant advantage for the development and application of biocontrol agents based on this strain.

Streptomyces, a genus within the actinomycetes family, is renowned for its remarkable ability to produce a diverse array of unique secondary metabolites. These compounds include macrolides, alkaloids, enzymes, anthraquinones, nucleosides, piperazines, and polyketides, many of which have significant biological activities35,36. In this study, the Streptomyces strain YG-5 idemonstrated promising antimicrobial activity, particularly against Alternaria leaf spot disease in star anise. In conclusion, while the current study provides a promising foundation, a more comprehensive understanding of the active metabolites and their mechanisms of action is essential for fully realizing the potential of Streptomyces strain YG-5 as a biocontrol agent against Alternaria leaf spot disease in star anise.

Materials and methods

Materials

Soil samples

Soil samples were collected from Yinggeling, Ledong County, Hainan Province (latitude 19°04.255′, longitude 109°57.037′, altitude 619.6 m) at a depth of 10–20 cm. The soil samples were sealed in bags and stored at 4 °C for future use.

Pathogenic strains

The pathogenic strains used in this study were obtained from various sources. Alternaria tenuissima and Colletotrichum horii were isolated from infected star anise leaves and preserved in our laboratory; Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. cubense race 1 (Foc1), Colletotrichum musae, Pestalotiopsis mangiferae, Pyricularia oryzae, Colletotrichum gloeosporioides, and Fusarium dimerum were provided by the Institute of Tropical Bioscience and Biotechnology, Chinese Academy of Tropical Agricultural Sciences.

All fungal pathogens were cultured on Potato Dextrose Agar (PDA) medium at 28 °C for 5–7 days. For long-term preservation, the fungi were stored using the slant culture method. Briefly, actively growing mycelial plugs were transferred to PDA slants, incubated at 28 °C for 5–7 days, and then stored at 4 °C. For bacterial pathogens, they were cultured in Luria–Bertani (LB) broth at 37 °C for 24 h with shaking at 180 rpm. Long-term storage was achieved by adding sterile glycerol to a final concentration of 15% (v/v) to the bacterial culture and storing at − 80 °C. All pathogenic strains were periodically sub-cultured to maintain their viability and virulence.

Culture media

Gause's No. 1 medium, Emerson agar medium, potato dextrose agar (PDA) medium, glucose-asparagine agar medium, Czapek's agar medium, yeast extract-malt extract agar (ISP2), oatmeal agar (ISP3), inorganic salts-starch agar (ISP4), glycerol-asparagine agar (ISP5) were used for morphological characteristic observation; Nitrate reduction medium, starch hydrolysis medium, cellulose hydrolysis medium, milk coagulation and peptonization medium, gelatin liquefaction medium, Czapek's agar medium, tyrosine agar medium, and carbon source utilization medium were used for physiological and biochemical tests37,38. Potato dextrose broth (PDB), Gause's No. 1 medium, millet medium, Actinomycetes No. 2 medium, YE medium, AL medium, FM-19 medium, E1 medium, and G2 medium were used for selection of fermentation media39.

Isolation and screening of antagonistic strains

The soil actinomycetes were isolated using the dilution-plating method. 10 g of the soil sample to be separated was taken, added to 90 mL of sterile water, and diluted to 10–3, 10–4, and 10–5 gradients, respectively. 200 µL of each gradient was spread on Gause's No. 1 medium (containing 70 µg/mL potassium dichromate to inhibit bacteria and fungi), and cultured at 28 °C for 7–10 days. After the colonies grew out, different types of colonies were picked and preserved on Gause's No. 1 slant11,40. The isolated actinomycetes were screened using the plate confrontation method. The actinomycetes were cultured at 28 °C for 7 days, and then 0.5 cm diameter fungal blocks were punched out from the actinomycete plates using a puncher and inoculated 1.5 cm away from the edge of the PDA plates. After culturing at 28 °C for 3 days, 0.5 cm diameter pathogen fungal blocks were inoculated in the center. Three parallel experiments were set up, with plates inoculated only with pathogen fungal blocks as the control40. Identification of actinomycete strain YG-5

The morphological characteristics of actinomycete strain YG-5 were identified by referring to "Classification and Identification of Actinomycetes". The actinomycete was inoculated on Czapek's agar, potato dextrose agar, ISP2, ISP3, ISP4, ISP5, glucose-asparagine agar, Emerson agar, and Gause's No. 1 medium, and cultured at 28 °C for 14 days. The changes in colony morphology, color, and growth were observed and recorded41,42. The physiological and biochemical characteristics of the antagonistic actinomycete strain, including carbon source utilization, starch hydrolysis, gelatin liquefaction, milk coagulation and peptonization, cellulose hydrolysis, nitrate reduction, H2S production, and pigment production, were evaluated according to the above methods28.

For molecular identification, the 16S rDNA sequence of strain YG-5 was determined. DNA was extracted using a DNA extraction kit (E.Z.N.A Bacterial DNA Kit (50)), and the 16S rDNA gene was amplified using primers 27f (5ʹ-AGAGTTTGATCCTGGCTCAG-3ʹ) and 1492r (5ʹ-TACGGCTACCTTGTTACGACTT-3ʹ) provided by Shanghai Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd. The PCR reaction was performed in a total volume of 50 μL, containing 25 μL of 2× PCR Master Mix (Takara, Japan), 2 μL of each primer (10 μM), 2 μL of template DNA, and 19 μL of nuclease-free water. The PCR conditions were as follows: initial denaturation at 94 °C for 5 min; 30 cycles of denaturation at 94 °C for 30 s, annealing at 55 °C for 30 s, and extension at 72 °C for 90 s; followed by a final extension at 72 °C for 10 min. The PCR products were visualized on a 1% agarose gel and purified using a gel extraction kit (Omega Bio-tek, USA). The purified PCR products were sequenced by Shanghai Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd. using the Sanger sequencing method. The obtained sequences were assembled and edited using DNAMAN software (version 8.0, Lynnon Biosoft, USA). The sequencing results were submitted to the NCBI GenBank database and compared with the BLAST database. A phylogenetic tree was constructed using MEGA6.0 software28,43.

Determination of the antimicrobial spectrum of actinomycete strain YG-5

Using the plate confrontation method described above, the antimicrobial activity of actinomycete strain YG-5 against other plant pathogenic fungi was determined40. For the antibacterial activity test, the agar block method was used: The bacteria on the slant were suspended in sterile water to make a bacterial suspension, which was then thoroughly mixed with 45 °C beef extract peptone medium and poured into plates. 0.5 cm diameter actinomycete blocks were inoculated onto the bacterial-containing medium, and cultured at 28 °C for 24–48 h. The presence or absence of inhibition zones was observed43.

Screening of fermentation medium and optimization of fermentation conditions for actinomycete strain YG-5

In the pursuit of identifying the optimal fermentation medium for the actinomycete strain YG-5, a systematic screening process was initiated. Initially, a single colony of strain YG-5 was cultured in 100 mL of LB liquid medium at 28 °C with a shaking speed of 180 rpm for a period of 3 days, resulting in the development of a seed culture. This seed culture was then introduced into each of the 9 different test fermentation media at a 2% (v/v) ratio and incubated under the same conditions for 7 days. Post incubation, the fermentation broth was centrifuged at 10,000 rpm for 10 min to separate the cell debris. The supernatant, now free of cells, was passed through a 0.22 μm filter to yield a sterile fermentation liquid. The antimicrobial potency of this liquid was assessed using a mycelial growth rate assay. This method was employed to gauge the impact of the various fermentation media on the synthesis of antimicrobial substances by YG-5 and to pinpoint the most effective medium for the strain's growth and production39. For each fermentation medium tested, a control group was set up using the same medium without inoculation of strain YG-5. This control group was subjected to the same fermentation conditions as the experimental groups.

To optimize the fermentation conditions for the actinomycete strain YG-5, a comprehensive evaluation was conducted to determine the most favorable parameters for enhancing the strain's antimicrobial activity against Alternaria tenuissima. The optimization process began with inoculum size, where the seed culture was introduced at concentrations of 1%, 2%, 4%, 6%, 8%, and 10% (v/v) into the optimal medium and incubated at 28 °C with 180 rpm for 7 days. Subsequently, the working volume was assessed by filling 250 mL erlenmeyer flasks with 40, 60, 80, 100, 120, and 140 mL of the medium and incubating under the same conditions. Fermentation time was explored by incubating cultures for durations from 4 to 12 days, while varying the fermentation temperature from 20 to 36 °C at a constant agitation of 180 rpm. Finally, agitation speed was optimized by incubating the cultures at speeds ranging from 90 to 210 rpm at 28 °C. These systematic optimizations aimed to identify the conditions that would maximize the production and efficacy of the antimicrobial compounds by strain YG-5. Each treatment was performed in triplicate. The antimicrobial activity was evaluated using the mycelial growth rate assay to systematically optimize the fermentation conditions for strain YG-532,33. For each factor tested, a control group was maintained using the standard conditions established in our laboratory (e.g., 28 °C, 180 rpm, 5% inoculum size, etc.). This control group served as a reference point for evaluating the effects of changing each factor.

Based on the results of the single-factor experiments described above, an orthogonal experimental design was employed to further optimize the fermentation conditions, using the antimicrobial activity of the fermentation broth as the evaluation index33. Evaluation of the stability of fermentation broth from strain YG-5

The stability of the fermentation broth produced by strain YG-5 was evaluated by assessing its antimicrobial activity under various stress conditions, including temperature, pH, and UV irradiation30.

The fermentation broth from strain YG-5 was evaluated for stability under various stress conditions to determine the robustness of its antimicrobial activity. For temperature stability, the broth was heated at increments to 20 °C, 40 °C, 60 °C, 80 °C, and 100 °C for 1 h, with sterile water added to compensate for any evaporation. Following this treatment, the samples were filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane and tested for their inhibitory effects against Alternaria tenuissima using the mycelial growth rate assay, allowing for the calculation of inhibition rates.

Regarding UV irradiation stability, the broth was exposed to UV light at 254 nm and 30 W for durations ranging from 20 to 120 min. An untreated control was maintained for comparison. Post-irradiation, the samples were also filtered and assessed for antimicrobial activity against Alternaria tenuissima, with inhibition rates calculated from the mycelial growth rate assay results.

For pH stability, the broth's pH was adjusted to levels between 4.0 and 10.0 using 1 mol/L hydrochloric acid or sodium hydroxide, then allowed to equilibrate at room temperature for 30 min before being neutralized back to the original pH. Both treated and untreated control samples were filtered through a 0.22 μm membrane and their antimicrobial potency was evaluated using the same assay, with inhibition rates calculated accordingly.

Antifungal activity of the fermentation broth against Alternaria tenuissima

In the treatment of star anise leaves, a meticulous process was followed to assess the efficacy of the fermentation broth from strain YG-5 against the pathogen Alternaria tenuissima. Initially, the leaves were disinfected using 75% ethanol and then thoroughly rinsed with sterile water to ensure they were free from contaminants. Sterile wounds were created on the leaf surface using a needle, and 20 μL of the undiluted fermentation broth was applied directly to these wounds. The treated leaves were allowed to air-dry before a 5 mm mycelial plug of Alternaria tenuissima was placed onto the wounded areas. A separate set of leaves, treated only with the culture medium, served as a negative control to gauge the effectiveness of the fermentation broth23,39.

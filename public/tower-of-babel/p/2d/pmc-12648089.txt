Comparison of key odorants and sensory properties of various caviar with different breeding environments, processing conditions, and grades of quality
Authors: Zhuyu Yang, Zefan Chen, Weijia Zhang, Jinlin Wang, Fan Bai, Ruichang Gao, Yujin Li, Jian Li, Yuanhui Zhao, Xinxing Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This is the first comprehensive comparison of key odorants and sensory properties of various caviar with different breeding environments, processing conditions, and grades of quality. Ten aroma attributes were identified and rated by sensory descriptive analysis. 47 and 50 volatile compounds were identified by two aroma extraction methods (SPME, SAFE) combined with GC–MS, respectively. 27 potential key aroma-active compounds were quantitated and further screening through AEDA and OAV calculation. Finally, 21 key aroma active compounds (OAV > 1, FD ≥ 9) were screened, including butanal, hexanal, (Z)-4-heptenal, (Z)-2-penten-1-ol, 2-nonanone, nonanal, 1-octen-3-ol, 2-ethyl-1-hexanol, benzaldehyde, acetic acid and other compounds. Recombination and omission tests verified that these compounds played an important role in the overall aroma profile of different caviar samples. The different contribution of key volatile aroma compounds to the overall aroma system of caviar resulted in the difference of caviar flavor quality.

Introduction

Caviar production has its historical roots in Russia and is recognized as the most sought-after edible roe product globally, prized for its rich nutritional profile, being rich in high quality protein, polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFA) and vitamins (Farag et al., 2021). In view of the current expanding consumer demand for caviar market, many countries have resorted to artificial rearing and simulated caviar or caviar substitutes are created. From a productive perspective, the key distinction between historical caviar production and the present is the clear transition from wild-sourced to farm-raised caviar (Sicuro, 2018). The rising interest in caviar and its substitutes has prompted numerous researchers to assess both the chemical and sensory properties of caviar and its substitutes (Xu et al., 2022). Flavor encompasses the holistic experience of human sensory perception related to food and is considered a vital indicator of food quality. The analysis of food flavor reflects the immediate impressions people form when tasting various foods (Zeng et al., 2023). The biggest highlight of caviar is its special flavor, so the most important thing to detect the quality of caviar is to examine whether its flavor is good. Investigators have discovered that a majority of caviar available in the current market exhibits a characteristic fishy and bitter flavor (Oeleker et al., 2013); in addition, terms such as earthy and oxidized aromas have been incorporated into the sensory lexicon related to caviar (Baker et al., 2014). The quality of sturgeon roe-which encompasses mouthfeel and aroma-significantly influences its market value, varying greatly among different species and depending on factors such as their diet, harvesting period, environmental conditions, processing techniques, preservatives, packaging, and storage conditions (Ovissipour et al., 2018). The sturgeon breeding environment, processing methods, storage condition and other links are the main links in the caviar industry chain, therefore, each key link in the caviar industry chain may affect the aroma quality of caviar.

Odor quality is an important aspect of caviar product quality. Recent studies have primarily concentrated on the aroma attributes of the final product as well as the alterations in aroma throughout processing. Protein degradation, microbial growth and lipid oxidation are important factors affecting flavor formation (Tavakoli et al., 2021). Research indicates that the primary aromatic compounds identified in both fresh and stored caviar include hexanal, nonanal, 3-methylbutanal, 1-octen-3-ol, and phenylacetaldehyde. The changes of caviar flavor dominated by microorganisms during storage were also investigated. Specifically, the inoculation of Lactococcus lactis and Rhodococcus spp. was found to have a strong correlation with the emergence of characteristic caviar odorants (Liu et al., 2024). Aldehydes were the main volatile compounds generated from the oxidation of unsaturated fatty acid (UFA) chains within phospholipids. The oxidative breakdown of unsaturated acyl chains in phospholipids is a key process for generating aroma compounds. PC (18:1/22:6) in lipids is oxidatively decomposed to form oleic acid, which is further oxidatively degraded to form small molecule aldehydes (Shen et al., 2021).

In a word, the research on the influencing factors of caviar aroma quality has been extensive. However, as for now, a thorough and in-depth examination of how specific key production links in the caviar industry chain affect the aroma quality has yet to be performed. This includes the sensory evaluation for detecting aroma variations, the use of gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS) and gas chromatography-ion mobility spectrometry (GC-IMS) to identify differential volatiles, the evaluation of odor activity values (OAV) for these volatiles, the analysis of the types and intensities of odors with GC-olfactometry (GC-O), the selection of major differential volatiles produced, and aroma recombination and omission tests were used to verify the contribution of key aroma compounds. Therefore, this study seeks to investigate and pinpoint the impact of differences in key production link in the caviar chain on the significantly different volatiles produced by Acipenser gueldenstaedtii using the previously mentioned methodologies. This research is crucial as it offers essential insights that enhance our understanding of how key production link in the caviar chain contribute to the development of caviar's aroma.

Materials and methods

Source of sturgeon caviar (Acipenser gueldenstaedtii)

The caviar from sturgeon (Acipenser gueldenstaedtii) used in this study was provided by Zhejiang Quzhou Sturgeon Aquatic Food Technology Development Co., Ltd. (Hangzhou, China). They were divided into 6 categories according to breeding environment, processing method, and different taste, including different breeding environments (marine cultured caviar, “MF” and freshwater cultured caviar “FF”); different processing method (caviar cured with salt, “S” and caviar cured without salt, “US”); different taste (heavy flavor, “HF” and light flavor, “LF”). The samples were stored in a refrigerator (DW-86L388J, Haier Group, Qingdao, China) at −80 °C.

Chemicals

The commercial standards methanol (99.9 %), 2-methyl-3-heptanone (95.0 %), butanal (99.5 %), hexanal (99.5 %), 3-heptanone (98.0 %), 1-penton-3-ol (97.0 %), 2-hexenal (98.0 %), 4-octanone (98.0 %), (Z)-4-heptenal (95.0 %), (Z)-2-penten-1-ol (95.0 %), 2-nonanone (99.0 %), nonanal (96.0 %), 1-octen-3-ol (98.0 %), acetic acid (99.9 %), 2-ethyl-1-hexanol (99.5 %), benzaldehyde (99.0 %), (E,E)-3,5-octadien-2-one (99.5 %), (E)-cinnamaldehyde (99.0 %), nonanoic acid (99.0 %), n-decanoic acid (99.5 %), hexanoic acid (99.5 %), phenol (99.5 %), diallyl sulfide (98.0 %), diallyl disulfide (98.0 %), p-xylene (99.8 %), o-xylene (99.0 %), caprolactam (99.0 %), d-limonene (95.0 %), 6-methyl-2-heptanone (95.0 %) were purchased from Aladdin (Shanghai, China).

Sensory evaluation of sturgeon caviar

The sensory analysis received approval from the Institutional Review Board Committee at Ocean University of China. Sensory evaluators were screened by reference to earlier laboratory sensory evaluation methods (Yang et al., 2024). Prior to the study, each panelist underwent a training program lasting 60 h focused on sensory evaluation of Acipenser gueldenstaedtii over the course of 20 days. Participants were provided with written details about the research and signed informed consent forms to take part. Sensory quantitative description analysis (QDA) was conducted in accordance with a previous study (Fan et al., 2018). Informed consent for sensory testing can be found in the supplementary file.

Determination of volatile compounds by GC-IMS

A lyophilized sample of caviar (1 g) was combined with 2 mL NaCl in a 20 mL headspace vial and incubated at 50 °C while being agitated at 250 r/min for 30 min (SPME, Supelco, Millipore, America). Subsequently, 500 μL of the aspirated headspace sample was introduced into a gas chromatography. The separation of volatile components occurred within a metal capillary column (MXT-WAX, 15 m × 0.53 mm × 0.50 μm, G.A.S., Dortmund, Germany) before they were injected into the IMS. GC-IMS program parameters are performed according to the methods previously set by the laboratory (Liu et al., 2023). All volatile flavor substances in the samples were searched against the GC-IMS Library Search NIST14 database. Retention indices (RI) for the volatile compounds were established using N-ketone (C4-C9) as a reference standard externally.

Determination of volatile compounds by GC–MS

Volatile organic compounds (VOCs) were extracted by SPME and SAFE methods respectively. The SPME extraction method was consistent with that of GC-IMS. SAFE was performed according to laboratory methods (Xu et al., 2022). The GC–MS analyzer was used for the determination of compounds. The samples were analyzed by INNOWAX capillary columns for detecting volatile compounds. The GC conditions were set and details of the instrument according to the method previously established by the laboratory (Liu et al., 2023).

Analysis of GC-O

The extraction method was consistent with that of SAFE. The GC, MS conditions and temperature increase program was consistent with that of GC–MS. Four assessors, each having undergone a weeks of training, carried out the GC-O analysis. Aroma intensities (AIs) were rated on a 4-point scale, where “1” denoted a weak scent, “2” indicated a moderate aroma, “3” represented a strong fragrance, and “4” reflected an extremely potent aroma (Wen et al., 2023). Characterization of these selected odors was achieved through referencing the NIST14 spectral library and retention indices, with further validation of their descriptions sourced from professional literature and relevant websites.

Quantitative and OAV calculation of odor-active compounds

The accurate content of each VOCs was calculated by establishing a standard curve of VOCs standards by quantitative external standard method. The corresponding OAV value is then calculated. To evaluate the contribution of VOCs identified by SPME-GC–MS to the odor quality of caviar prior to quantification, the relative odor activity value (ROAV) of VOCs were calculated using the same methodology as for OAV.

Aroma extract dilution analysis (AEDA)

To investigate the differences VOCs among different caviar samples, AEDA was employed to further evaluate the contributions of each key aroma compound. The flavor dilution (FD) factors of VOCs were determined by GC-O-MS, following a previously method (Jia et al., 2024). The SAFE extract underwent a series of stepwise dilutions at ratios of 1:1, 1:3, 1:9, 1:27, 1:81, 1:243 and 1:729, using dichloromethane as the diluent. For extracts obtained from SPME, the sample dilution was accomplished by adjusting the GC inlet split ratio, progressing from 1:1 to 3:1, 9:1, 27:1, 81:1, 243:1, and 729:1, with the carrier gas flow rate set at 1.6 mL/min for the 729:1 ratio. The diluted sample were injected into the GC-O iapparatus in order of decreasing concentrations, then four trained assessors (1 females and 3 males) sniffed the samples until the odor was not perceived.

Aroma recombination, and omission tests

12 specialists (6 males and 6 females, averaging 25 years of age) were selected from sensory evaluation panel conducted a triangle test in the sensory laboratory. This test aimed to evaluate similarity between recombination model and the original sample, as well as the differences between each omission model and the entire recombination model. According to the previous methodology (Wang et al., 2024), The reconstitution model was established by thoroughly mixing potential key aroma compounds at quantitative concentrations, and equilibrating them for 10 min at room temperature (25 °C ± 2). The original caviar samples and the recombination model were evaluated according to sensory evaluation methods. An potential key aroma compound had been deliberately omitted was prepared, and an omission model was created. Two identical recombination models and one omission model were prepared, which were randomly coded and scrambled using three numbers. Subsequently, the sensory panel members sniffed the samples and identified the one that had a significantly different aroma from the other two. The missing compounds were identified as key aroma compounds for caviar.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using three biological replicates. Duncan's multiple trange test and analysis of variance (ANOVA) were employed. All data analysis and graphical rendering were generated using Origin 2017 (OriginLab, Northampton, MA, USA), ChiPlot (https://www.chiplot.online/), MetaboAnalyst v6.0 (https://www.metaboanalyst.ca), and OmicStudio (www.omicstudio.cn/tool). The graphic summary is drawn by Figdraw (https://www.figdraw.com/).

Results and discussion

Sensory differences between different caviar

The analysis of free-choice profile analysis (FCP) can serve to assess specific characteristic attributes in the evaluation of food, including aspects like flavor and taste. Sensory lexicon and attributes of sturgeon caviar was established through FCP and QDA, and combined with the previous research results of laboratory (Xu et al., 2022). Ten characteristic odor attributes were selected, “saltiness”, “fishy”, “oily/fatty”, “nutty”, “grass”, “sweet”, “rancid”, “metallic”, “umami”, and “earthy” were the most dominant flavor attributes in caviar. It has been found that odor attributes prevalent in good quality caviar are: mild nutty, creamy, saltiness, fishy, etc. (Bledsoe et al., 2003); and that fresh caviar contains odor information such as umami, sweet, etc., and is positively correlated with the overall acceptability of the caviar (Ueda et al., 2009). The intensity of umami, grass and nutty attribute were the highest in sample of S and HF, Conversely, the US had the lowest intensity values for the grass and nutty odor attributes, while the intensity of fishy and earthy attribute were the highest in sample of US; in addition, the LF had the highest scores for “metallic” and “oily/fatty”, while “earthy”, “umami” and “fishy” intensities scored the lowest (Fig. 1A). From the overall sensory evaluation score, it can be seen that the S group had the highest overall acceptability, followed by the HF group, while the US group had the lowest overall acceptability (Fig. 1B). From the principal component analysis (PCA), the US and S groups were each in a separate interval and located in a different matrices from the other groups in PCA model showing their discrepancy (PC1 = 63.8 %, PC2 = 16.5 %). Indicating that US and S groups were significantly different from the other groups, which validated the results of the overall sensory evaluation score (Fig. 1C). The characteristic flavor of salted foods may be due to the fact that appropriate salt concentrations enhance lipase activity, promote lipolysis and the formation of flavor compounds, including aldehydes (Wang et al., 2016). Aldehydes, as the main compounds formed during fat oxidation, can provide fatty flavor and green flavor attributes. The meat quality and flavor of aquatic products are affected by environmental factors of aquaculture (such as temperature and salinity). It has been found that after aquaculture products are cultured in fresh water and seawater respectively, the fat and protein metabolism in cells are affected due to different osmotic pressures in the growth environment, so there are significant differences in fatty acid and amino acid contents. Amino acids and fatty acids as secondary metabolites are the main sources of characteristic flavor of aquatic products, so aquaculture environment will strongly affect flavor changes (Duan et al., 2021). Caviar presented rich and light flavor differences due to individual sturgeon differences, which may be caused by differences in endogenous microbial populations among sturgeon individuals. It has been found that there was a positive correlation between L. pentosus and the expression of characteristic aroma compounds in oyster (Liu et al., 2025). Specifically, differences in microbial composition between sturgeon individuals dominate caviar flavor differences.Fig. 1Sensory evaluation of ten odor attributes (A) and overall odor score of different sturgeon caviar (B); principal component analysis (PCA) (C). Analysis of VOCs

To investigate the differences on key flavor compounds of different sturgeon caviar species, GC–MS and GC-IMS technology was employed to detect the VOCs in caviar samples. A total of 85 VOCs were identified in this assay (Table S1), including aldehydes (11), ketones (11), alcohols (19), esters (15), acids (4), ethers (5), furans (5), aromatic (3), pyrazines (5) and others (7). Aldehydes, ketones, alcohols and esters were detected, which are the major VOCs in caviar. Through fingerprint spectrum analysis (Fig. 2A), it was observed that more types of VOCs were identified in S group than in US group, indicating that the flavor substances in salted caviar are richer. The flavor substances identified in LF and HF group were similar, but the intensity of the signal response in fingerprint spectrum showed higher concentrations of HF group compared to LF group, thus the HF group exhibited a stronger flavor than the LF group. A notable distinction was observed in the types of VOCs detected by GC-IMS between MF and FF. To elucidate the differences in aroma profiles among the caviar, PCA was conducted using the relative concentrations of each VOCs in these samples. The PCA plot distinctly indicated that S and HF groups were similar in scores, and that these two groups were not significantly different from the LF group, whereas they were more different from the FF, MF, and US groups (PC1 = 51.60 %; PC2 = 28.60 %), with good consistency within each group, suggesting substantial differences in the aroma characteristics of the S, HF and LF group, compared with the other groups (Fig. 2B). The analysis of signal intensities resulting from flavor compounds was conducted utilizing PLS-DA analysis model. Significant variations in the flavors of various caviar samples were evident, as illustrated in Fig. 2C. VIP scores of VOCs further revealed differences in their contributions among caviar samples (Fig. 2D). Twenty-one VOCs were identified as having a substantial impact on caviar flavor (VIP score > 1), aldehydes like heptanal, hexanal and butanal, which were associated with sensory attribute like green, floral, and fatty (Cerreta & Furton, 2015). These VOCs are characteristic substances in US group, their presence gives caviar a light smell and is more like the original flavor of fish. The accumulation of butanal and heptanal, during oxidation of lipids can lead to off-flavors like rancidity and pungency (Han et al., 2023). At high concentration, 2-ethyl-1-hexanol presents an unpleasant taste, while 3-methylbutanoic acid mainly contribute to “grassy” and “fruity” (Miao et al., 2023). These VOCs are also characteristic substances in US group, this may have contributed to the lowest overall acceptability of the US group. As shown in Fig. 2D, among the flavor compounds with VIP >1, Methional, 2,3-Pentanedione, and 2-Butanone are characteristic compounds in S, HF, and LF caviar. The flavor characteristics of caviar were primarily attributed to 2-butanone and 2,3-Pentanedione, which produced aromas described as “green”, “fatty”, “nutty”, and “fishy” (Zhang et al., 2024). This is why the S, HF, LF groups scored the highest for “oily/fatty” and “nutty” in the sensory evaluation. Compared to FF caviar, the content of VOCs such as ethyl 2-methylpropanoate (“fruity”), 2-octanal (“mushroom, fat”), and (E,E)-2,4-heptadienal (“grassy”) were significantly increased at MF. VOCs in various caviars were analyzed using GC–MS, forty-seven VOCs were identified by HS-SPME-GC–MS. To comprehend the flavor variations among the different caviars, heat map clustering analysis was conducted. Taking the relative content of compounds identified by HS-SPME-GC–MS as the variable, the content distribution of those substance in different caviar is shown in Fig. 2E. As can be seen from the figure, the levels of aldehydes (hexanal, and 3-methyl-butanal), and alcohols (1-octene-3-ol and 2-ethyl-1-hexanol) were detected in S, HF, and LF group, which were significantly higher than other groups. The primary aroma compounds in the sturgeon caviar was hexanal, heptanal, 3-methyl-butanal and 2-ethyl-1-hexanol (Xu et al., 2022). Acetyl valeryl is the main VOCs detected in US samples. Acetyl valeryl is a precursor to the amino acid valine and is produced during the metabolism of fatty acids. It can be seen from Fig. 2G that due to the presence of acetyl valeryl, the content of ketones in the US group is significantly higher than other groups. A PCA plot of VOCs showed that the overall odors of different sturgeon caviar sample were varied considerably (Fig. 2F). However, it is crucial to recognize that not all aroma compounds made a notable impact on the overall odor. The evaluation of the role of particular scent compounds was performed by calculating OAV, which considers both the concentration of these aroma compounds and their associated odor thresholds. Since compounds have not been quantified, we calculated relative odor activity value (ROAV) from relative content and screened compounds with ROAV >1 as potential key volatile flavor substances (Table S2).

GC-O analysis of flavor differences between different caviars

To get a deep insight into the odor characteristics and its corresponding aroma-active in different caviars, the VOCs extracted by SAFE were identified by GC–MS, and GC-O analysis was carried out by olfactometry detector analysis. As tabulated in Table S3, a total of 50 volatile compounds were confirmed through SAFE-GC–MS, including alkanes (34), ketones (1), sulfur compounds (2), aromatic compounds (5), aldehydes (4), terpenes (1), acids (1), and other categories (2). GC-O is an effective method, which can be used to determine the main odor active substances in experimental samples (Yue et al., 2024). GC-O analysis directly assesses the number of olfactory perception and intensities in the samples, and the odor types of VOCs, by combining volatile compounds were confirmed, a relatively accurate screening of key odor-active substances in different caviars can be achieved, compensating for the limitations of purely data-driven analysis. Eight volatile flavor substances were screened as potential key odor actives in different caviars by the sniffing results analysis (the screening conditions were: the number of olfactory perception ≥2; the odor intensity ≥3) (Fig. 3A). The content of these compounds varies considerably in different types of caviar, so that different types of caviar present different flavors. Aldehydes with green attributes associated with them, such as nonanal, are characteristic compounds in the S and HF groups, and are able to contribute to sensory attributes such as “grass” and “fishy”; d-Limonene was a key aroma compounds of citrus that provides a lemon-like aroma (Zheng et al., 2023), and it was detected in the US and LF groups. OAV calculation of key aroma compounds

Potential key aroma compounds screened by ROAV >1 and sniffing and quantifying them using external quantitative standard methods. All standard curves demonstrated R2 values exceeding 0.9, which signifies a strong fit for the linear equations, as detailed in Table S4. Higher concentrations of butanal, hexanal, (Z)-2-penten-1-ol, acetic acid and diallyl sulfide were detected in the different sturgeon caviar. To further assess the odor active compounds across different caviar, the OAV values for these substances were computed. 25 compounds exhibited OAVs >1, with their concentrations and OAVs presented in Table S4. 3-Heptanone, 4-octanone, 2-nonanone had highest OAV in all samples. In addition to the above compounds, butanal, 2-hexenal, (Z)-4-heptenal, (Z)-2-penten-1-ol, nonanal, 1-octen-3-ol, acetic acid, benzaldehyde, (E,E)-3,5-Octadien-2-one, hexanoic acid, and phenol had relatively high in S and HF. In US, diallyl disulfide and d-limonene had relatively high OAVs. In LF, diallyl disulfide, d-limonene, and 6-methyl-2-heptanone had relatively high OAVs. MF and FF groups also differ from OAVs. In MF, benzaldehyde, (E)-cinnamaldehyde, n-decanoic acid had relatively high OAVs. In FF, 2-ethyl-1-hexanol, diallyl sulfide, o-xylene had relatively high OAVs. OAV is usually combined with FD values to screen samples for key VOCs. Therefore, we tested FD values for potential key VOCs.

The contribution of key VOCs for different caviar

Twenty-two compounds with FD values ≥9 were identified (Table 1). FD value indicates the contribution of the compound to the overall aroma of the sample. The larger the FD value, the greater the impact of the compound on the flavor of the sample. Among these, compounds that impart “green” and “floral”, such as benzaldehyde, (Z)-4-heptenal, alongside those associated with “fruity”, “sweet”, and “dairy”-like hexanoic acid, d-limonene-were detected in different caviar with FD values ≥9. Additionally, diallyl sulfide, which has a cabbage-like odor, was also detected in these samples. n-Decanoic acid and nonanoic acid contributed to the cheesy aroma found in milk tea. However, significant disparities in aroma active compounds were observed among the different caviar. Such as, n-decanoic acid exhibited an FD only in MF. Hexanal showed the highest dilution multiple (FD = 243) in S and HF, while the FD value was 81 in US and LF. These differences can be attributed to the different processing methods used in caviar production, leading to differences in the quality of the overall aroma. Based on the OAV values and FD factor analysis results of the important aroma-active compounds, butanal, hexanal, (Z)-4-heptenal, (Z)-2-penten-1-ol, nonanal, 1-octen-3-ol, 2-ethyl-1-hexanol, benzaldehyde, acetic acid, nonanoic acid, n-decanoic acid, and hexanoic acid had the OAVs ≥1 and the FD factors ≥9. This indicated that aldehydes, alcohols, and acids played an important role in the overall aroma profile of different caviar samples. Based on GC-O analysis and flavor related references, the odor attributes of these compounds included green, creamy, fishy, fat/oily, sour, grassy, and fruity aromas (Sohail et al., 2022). Specifically, the key VOCs detected in group S were more than those in US, including butanal, (Z)-4-heptenal, (Z)-2-penten-1-ol, nonanal, and 1-octen-3-ol, and the FD values of hexanal and 2-ethyl-1-hexanol were higher in group S, which could contribute to the characteristic flavor of caviar such as obvious grassy flavor and oily flavor. The results showed that salting process contributed to the formation of caviar flavor, which was consistent with sensory evaluation and GC-IMS and GC–MS results. Compared with HF and LF, the number of key VOCs screened was similar, but some key VOCs such as hexanal, (Z)-4-heptenal, 1-octen-3-ol, benzaldehyde and (E,E)-3,5-octadien-2-one had higher FD factor values in HF group, so they made HF group smell more intense. In addition, sturgeon cultured in seawater finally obtained caviar has unique benzaldehyde, (E)-cinnamaldehyde and n-decanoic acid, which contribute to caramel flavor, woody taste; while cultured in In order to further investigate the relationship between key aroma actives and sensory attributes in different caviar, we analyzed the correlation between 21 key aroma actives and 10 sensory attributes of caviar. As shown in Fig. 3B, seven typical sensory attributes of caviar, including oily/fatty, nutty, grassy, sweet, umami, saltiness and metallic, were positively correlated with most of the key aroma compounds. In the correlation network diagram, the thickness of the line represents the strength of the correlation, and the size and color of the dot represent the number of correlation factors. Fig. 3C provides a better visual indication of the strength of the correlation between key aroma active compounds and sensory attributes. As can be seen from the figure, diallyl sulfide is positively correlated with metallic; (Z)-4-heptenal, nonanal, and hexanal is positively correlated with oily/fatty; benzaldehyde is positively correlated with sweet. The results of GC-O and FD factor test showed that diallyl sulfide had onion and metallic odor attributes; (Z)-4-heptenal, nonanal, and hexanal odor attributes were also related to oily and fatty; benzaldehyde showed sweet attributes similar to burnt sugar. However, there was a negative correlation between butanal and fish, between (E,E)-3,5-octadien-2-one and earthy, and between hexanoic acid and rancid. This finding is inconsistent with the inherent aroma properties of the compounds, which may be attributed to the presence of volatile compounds within the complex odor system of caviar. The sensory properties exhibited by flavor compounds in foods are influenced by a variety of factors, such as the concentration and amount of flavor compounds contained in the food matrix; and interactions (masking or enhancement) between compounds. Therefore, the specific contribution of flavor compounds to sensory attributes needs to be further explored through aroma recombination and omission tests.

Aroma recombination and omission experiments

Aroma recombination

The commonly detected VOCs determined by the OAV values and FD factor analysis were be screened key VOCs in this research. Towards validation of the key VOCs in different caviar, aroma recombination experiments were conducted with the selected important aroma-active compounds. According to the contents of compounds in the S, US, LF, HF, MF and FF caviar samples, 19, 10, 19, 19, 7 and 7 odorants (OAV ≥ 1, FD ≥ 1) were incorporated into the odorless base matrix, respectively. Sensory experts evaluated the reconstructed model based on 10 caviar sensory descriptors. Fig. 4 presents the evaluation results comparing the recombinant model with the original samples of the respective caviar. The results showed that the compounds we screened out could restore the original caviar flavor well when mixed according to the content of the original model. In particular, the “oily/fatty”, “fishy”, “grass” and “saltiness” in the S and HF recombinants were found to have minimal differences compared to the original caviar samples. The original caviar samples exhibited slightly higher scores for “rancid” and “sweet” compared to the recombinant model. In contrast, the scores of “metalic”, “oily/fatty” and “untty” were somewhat lower in the recombinants when assessed against the original LF, while other characteristics were similar to those of the original. This is due to possible masking, inhibition or synergy effects involving compounds with OAV < 1 and those with OAV > 1, leading to disparities between the recombinant models and the original sample (Wang et al., 2024). Furthermore, the recombinant models have a high degree of overlap with their corresponding original samples, respectively. Such as “rancid”, “sweet”, “umami”, “earthy” “nutty” and “metallic”. Omission experiments

Aroma omission test was used to further compare aroma contributions of key VOCs in different caviar. Table 2 presents the results of the triangular experiments for each omission model. Findings indicated that aldehydes, ketones, alcohols, and sulfur-containing compounds with OAV > 1 contributed to the overall aroma of caviar samples to differing extents. The absence of some odorants affected the aroma perception of different caviar, including hexanal, 3-heptanone, 4-octanone, 2-ethyl-1-hexanol, and d-limonene etc. in US sample; butanal, hexanal, 3-heptanone, 2-hexenal, (Z)-4-heptenal, nonanal, Acetic acid, 1-octen-3-ol, diallyl sulfide, and 2-ethyl-1-hexanol etc. in S, LF and HF sample; 3-heptanone, 4-octanone, benzaldehyde, (E)-Cinnamaldehyde, nonanoic acid, and n-decanoic acid in MF sample; 3-heptanone, 4-octanone, 2-ethyl-1-hexanol, nonanoic acid, diallyl sulfide, and o-xylene in FF sample. Upon comparing the US sample with the S sample, specific key compounds unique to S sample included butanal, 2-hexenal, (Z)-4-heptenal, nonanal, 1-octen-3-ol, and benzaldehyde, the odor attributes of these compounds included green, grassy, fruity, almond, and burnt sugar from the previous discussion. This may be the main reason why the score values of the aroma attributes of nutty, grass, umami, and sweet in the sensory evaluation of the S group were higher than those of the US group. Upon comparing the LF sample with the HF sample, specific key compounds unique to HF sample included 3-heptanone, (Z)-2-penten-1-ol, benzaldehyde, and hexanoic acid, the odor attributes of these compounds included green, almond, burnt sugar, rubber, and rancid. This may be the main reason why the score values of the aroma attributes of nutty, grass, umami, and rancid in the sensory evaluation of the HF group were higher than those of the LF group. The characteristic key flavor compounds in MF group and FF group were 2-nonanone, benzaldehyde, (E)-cinnamaldehyde, and 2-ethyl-1-hexanol, diallyl sulfide, o-Xylene, respectively. In experiment, not all compounds (OAV > 1 or FD > 1) demonstrated significant differences, such as 3-heptanone and benzaldehyde in LF sample; 4-octanone and phenol in S, LF, and HF samlpe. This may be attributed to interactions between aroma compounds. Combined with the correlation analysis results between key VOCs and sensory attributes, it is suggested that the interaction between aromatic compounds may enhance or weaken the aroma attributes of compounds. Some researchers identified 13 kinds of outstanding aroma active compounds in Daqu liquor of different quality, including aldehydes, acids, esters and pyrazines. Although nonanal and 2-nonenal, two key aroma components with grassy flavor, were screened out, Daqu liquor did not exhibit grassy flavor as a whole. This may be due to the fact that the odor properties of nonanal and 2-nonenal are overwritten by other key aroma compounds (Tang et al., 2024). In this experiment, there was no significant difference between the omission model and the recombinant model after removing 2-nonanone, indicating that 2-nonanone may be masked by other key aroma compounds; while the omission of 2-ethyl-1-hexanol and benzaldehyde in S and HF groups showed significant differences. In the results of the recombinant experiment, it was found that the sweet attribute scores of the recombinant model were higher than those of the original samples, which indicated that the odor attributes of 2-ethyl-1-hexanol and benzaldehyde may be amplified by other key aroma compounds. Therefore, in addition to the contribution of key VOCs to the overall flavor characteristics of samples, the interaction between key VOCs should not be ignored, which provides analytical direction for future research.

Conclusion

In this study, a sensory evaluation of Six different kinds of caviar was conducted, “saltiness”, “fishy”, “oily/fatty”, “nutty”, “grass”, “sweet”, “rancid”, “metallic”, “umami”, and “earthy” is the main sensory attribute in caviar. Sensory evaluation and GC-IMS were used to describe the differences in the sensory properties and profiles of VOCs of different caviar. 47 and 50 VOCs were identified in different caviar samples by SPME/SAFE-GC–MS, respectively. Further screening key VOCs through AEDA, OAV calculation (OAV ≥ 1), and recombination/omission experiments, we found that butanal, hexanal, (Z)-4-heptenal, (Z)-2-penten-1-ol, nonanal, 1-octen-3-ol, 2-ethyl-1-hexanol, benzaldehyde, acetic acid, and hexanoic acid etc., and these compounds contributed significantly to the overall aroma of different caviar, which mainly showed green, creamy, fishy, fat/oily, sour, grassy, and fruity aromas. Different caviar has different key odor substances, which makes caviar show different odor quality. Specifically, HF and S share many of the same key odorants and have comparable aroma profiles. The key flavor components of LF were similar, but 3-heptanone, (Z)-2-penten-1-ol, benzaldehyde, and hexanoic acid contributed less to the overall flavor system of LF, so LF presented lighter flavor quality. US had less identified key aroma compounds, indicating that salting process played an important role in the formation of caviar flavor. US had unique flavor compared with S due to the existence of 4-octanone, diallyl disulphide, and d-limonene. This study indicate that the marine or freshwater culture environment has different effects on the flavor development of caviar. The research has enhanced our comprehension of the flavor profiles found in various caviar types that can arise during the production process, establishing a robust groundwork for creating premium caviar suited to the preferences of consumers in the future.

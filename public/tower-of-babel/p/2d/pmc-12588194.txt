Patterns of Recurrence and Malignant Transformation in Sinonasal Papilloma: Implications for Long-term Follow-up
Authors: TAKAYUKI KIMURA, SATORU KOIKE, HISATOMO TAMAKI, KAZUHIRO MIYAMOTO, SHUSUKE IWAMOTO, NORIKO OHIRA, DAISUKE ABE, MITSUO SATO, MUTSUKAZU KITANO, TAKAHIRO WAKASAKI, RYUJI YASUMATSU
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background/AimSinonasal papilloma is a histologically benign tumor that demonstrates locally aggressive behavior, with a propensity for recurrence and potential for malignant transformation. This study aimed to evaluate the clinical characteristics, recurrence patterns, and malignant transformation of sinonasal papillomas and to propose appropriate follow-up strategies.Patients and MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on 83 patients diagnosed with sinonasal papilloma at our institution. Clinical features, surgical treatments, recurrence intervals, and oncologic outcomes were analyzed. Tumors were staged using the Krouse classification for benign cases and UICC TNM staging for carcinoma-associated with papilloma cases.ResultsAmong 83 patients (62 men, 21 women; median age 68), 74 (89.2%) were diagnosed as benign papillomas and nine (10.8%) as carcinoma-associated with papilloma. The recurrence rate among benign cases was 14.9% (11/74), with a median time to recurrence of 5 years and two months. Recurrence intervals exhibited a trimodal distribution: within two years (36.4%), between 2-10 years (36.4%), and after 10 years (27.3%). Malignant transformation occurred in one case (1.2%), and eight additional cases were found to be associated with carcinoma. Five of these were initially misdiagnosed as benign, underscoring the diagnostic limitations of imaging modalities. Krouse T3/T4 lesions were more frequently associated with recurrence and malignancy. Approximately 48.6% of patients discontinued follow-up, often before reaching the threshold for late recurrence.ConclusionSinonasal papillomas require long-term surveillance beyond five years due to their potential for late recurrence and malignant transformation. Preoperative suspicion of malignancy remains challenging, particularly in Krouse T3 lesions. Clinicians should consider extended follow-up, especially in patients with advanced tumor stage, and adopt a tailored surgical strategy to achieve complete resection. Patient education regarding recurrence risk is essential for optimizing long-term outcomes.

Introduction

Sinonasal papilloma is a rare, benign epithelial tumor that arises from the Schneiderian mucosa of the nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses. It is histologically classified into three subtypes: inverted, oncocytic, and exophytic papillomas, among which the inverted papilloma (IP) is the most common and clinically significant due to its propensity for local aggressiveness, high recurrence rates, and potential for malignant transformation into squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) (1,2). Although histologically benign, IPs frequently demonstrate destructive growth, bony remodeling, and invasion of adjacent structures, leading to significant morbidity if not adequately managed (3).

The primary treatment for sinonasal papillomas is complete surgical resection, typically via endoscopic sinus surgery (ESS), which has become the preferred modality due to its reduced morbidity and improved visualization (4,5). However, certain clinical scenarios -such as extensive tumor involvement, skull base erosion, or suspicion of malignancy- may necessitate open or combined approaches (5). Despite advancements in surgical techniques, recurrence remains a major concern, with reported rates ranging from 15% to 25%, particularly in cases of incomplete excision or advanced Krouse staging (6,7).

Moreover, up to 10% of inverted papillomas are associated with synchronous or metachronous malignant transformation into SCC, necessitating careful histopathologic evaluation and long-term postoperative surveillance. Risk factors for malignancy include older age, smoking, human papillomavirus (HPV) infection, and high tumor stage (8). However, accurate preoperative identification of malignant transformation remains challenging, as radiographic and clinical features may be subtle or nonspecific.

Despite these known risks, there is a lack of standardized treatment protocols addressing the optimal surgical approach based on tumor extent, management strategies for cases with concurrent carcinoma, and, importantly, the recommended duration of follow-up. Given the potential for both delayed recurrence and late-onset malignant transformation, the development of evidence-based guidelines is essential for improving long-term outcomes.

The purpose of this study is to address these clinical challenges by retrospectively analyzing a cohort of patients with sinonasal papilloma treated at our institution over a ten-year period, focusing on recurrence patterns, malignant transformation, and implications for follow-up strategies.

Patients and Methods

This retrospective study included 83 patients who underwent treatment for sinonasal papilloma at our department between April 2013 and March 2023. Among them, 62 were male (74.7%) and 21 were female (25.3%), with a median age of 68 years (range=23-89 years). Based on histopathological examination, 74 cases (89.2%) were diagnosed as benign, and nine cases (10.8%) were carcinoma associated with IP. Tumor staging was classified according to the Krouse classification (9): T1 in 15 cases (18.1%), T2 in 28 (33.7%), T3 in 36 (43.4%), and T4 in four (4.8%).

Among the 74 benign cases, 67 underwent ESS, while seven were treated via the Caldwell-Luc procedure. In the nine malignant cases, two underwent ESS, two underwent the Caldwell-Luc procedure, and one underwent partial maxillectomy as the initial treatment. Additionally, three patients received radiation therapy (two with RADPLAT), and one received chemotherapy. The median postoperative follow-up duration was two years and four months (range=1 month-11 years) (Table I). We retrospectively examined the timing of recurrence in benign cases, follow-up periods, clinical differences between benign and malignant cases, and treatment courses in the malignant group. Tumor staging for malignant cases was based on the TNM classification of the UICC 8th edition.

Results

Timing of recurrence in benign tumors. Among the 74 benign cases, 11 patients (14.9%) experienced tumor recurrence. The median time to recurrence was five years and two months (range=1 month-21 years). The recurrence intervals showed a trimodal distribution: recurrence within 24 months in four cases (36.4%), between 24 months and 10 years in four cases (36.4%), and after 10 years in three cases (27.3%) (Figure 1).

Krouse staging was T1: six cases, T2: 14 cases, T3: 16 cases. The median age was 68 years (range=23-88 years), and there were 26 males (72.2%) and 10 females (27.8%). No significant differences in age or sex were found between the two groups. However, the median follow-up period in the discontinuation group was significantly shorter at one year and three months (range=1 month-eight years four months) (p=0.001) (Table II).

Discussion

The postoperative recurrence rate of sinonasal papilloma has been widely reported to range from 15% to 25.3%, with some cases demonstrating recurrence more than five years after initial surgery (3,7). These tumors are known for their potential to extend locally with bony erosion, and despite their benign histological classification, the risk of recurrence persists even after apparently complete surgical resection. Several studies have emphasized the importance of surgical margin status and the completeness of excision as the most critical determinants of recurrence (3). Furthermore, approximately 5-15% of sinonasal papillomas are associated with synchronous or metachronous malignancies, most commonly transitioning to squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), which can occasionally exhibit high-grade malignancy (8,11). The current study found a malignancy rate of 10.8%, consistent with these reports. The time to malignant transformation in our cohort was five years and seven months, aligning with prior studies reporting transformation intervals of 1-7 years (12,13). This supports the need for long-term surveillance even in cases deemed benign at initial diagnosis.

The median follow-up period in this study was two years and four months, which may be insufficient to fully capture the long-term behavior of these tumors, particularly considering the trimodal pattern of recurrence observed in our cohort. While many previous reports have described a biphasic recurrence pattern -early recurrence within two years and late recurrence after five years (7)-, our findings suggest a potential third peak, with cases occurring more than ten years after initial surgery. This observation underscores the critical need for patient education and physician awareness regarding the prolonged risk window for recurrence and malignant transformation.

The analysis of follow-up patterns in our cohort revealed that nearly half of the patients discontinued surveillance, often prematurely. Patients in the discontinuation group had a significantly shorter median follow-up duration (one year and three months) compared to those who remained under observation. This is particularly concerning given that 27.3% of recurrences in our study occurred after 10 years. Similar patterns of long-latency recurrence have been reported by Mirza et al. and Lawson et al., emphasizing that early cessation of follow-up can lead to missed opportunities for detecting treatable recurrences (3,7).

Krouse staging remains a valuable predictor of recurrence risk. Tumors staged as T3 or higher are consistently associated with higher recurrence and malignancy rates (14). In our study, five of nine carcinoma cases were initially misdiagnosed as benign and did not exhibit radiographic features typically suggestive of malignancy, such as bone erosion on computed tomography or loss of the cerebriform pattern on magnetic resonance imaging (15).

While ESS has become the gold standard for treating most sinonasal papillomas due to its minimally invasive nature and favorable outcomes, its limitations must be recognized. In advanced cases or when malignancy is suspected, open or combined approaches may be necessary to ensure complete resection and appropriate margin control (16). Several authors have reported higher recurrence rates when ESS alone is used in the setting of extensive disease, particularly in Krouse T3/T4 tumors (17).

In conclusion, the findings of this study emphasize the need for vigilant, long-term follow-up of at least five years for all patients undergoing resection of sinonasal papilloma, with consideration for extended surveillance beyond ten years in select cases. The risk of recurrence, particularly in higher-stage tumors or those with ambiguous imaging findings, mandates a personalized follow-up approach. Furthermore, in cases where malignancy is diagnosed postoperatively, aggressive secondary treatment -including reoperation or radiotherapy- may be required to achieve local control and prevent disease-related mortality.

PDNO-06 PEDIATRIC POSTERIOR FOSSA RESECTIONS; A CASE SERIES OF CASES DONE IN A TERTIARY HOSPITAL
Authors: Jalilarah Nassozi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPediatric posterior fossa tumors are among the most common brain tumors in children. Surgical resection remains the cornerstone of treatment, to achieve maximal safe resection while minimizing neurological deficits. This case series presents our experience with pediatric posterior fossa tumor resections, highlighting the outcomes, complications, and factors influencing surgical success.MethodsWe retrospectively reviewed the medical records of pediatric patients who underwent posterior fossa tumor resection at our institution between JAN 2023-DECEMBER 2023. Patient demographics, tumor characteristics, extent of resection, postoperative complications, and neurological outcomes were analyzed.ResultsA total of 16 patients were included in the study, with a mean age of 8 years. The most common tumor types were medulloblastoma, ependymoma, and pilocytic astrocytoma. Gross total resection was achieved in 58% of the cases. Postoperative complications included cerebellar mutism syndrome in 18.75% of patients, hydrocephalus in 18.75%, and cranial nerve deficits in 31.25 Overall, 81.25% of patients had favorable neurological outcomes at the latest follow-up.ConclusionSurgical resection of pediatric posterior fossa tumors is associated with a high rate of gross total resection and favorable long-term outcomes. However, significant postoperative complications, such as cerebellar mutism and hydrocephalus, remain a concern. Careful preoperative planning and meticulous surgical technique are essential to optimize outcomes and minimize morbidity in this vulnerable population. Further studies are needed to refine surgical strategies and improve prognostic indicators.

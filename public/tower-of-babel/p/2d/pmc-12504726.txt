A dual-branch pedestrian re-identification method CPHMNet based on multi-dimensional feature fusion and integrated pose estimation
Authors: Huizhi Xu, Ruijie Gao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
To address the interferences in pedestrian feature extraction caused by pose variations and occlusions, which adversely affect recognition performance in pedestrian re-identification tasks, this paper proposes a pedestrian re-identification method, CPHMNet, based on multi-dimensional feature fusion and integrated pose estimation. Firstly, a novel MDA module is developed to enhance the capability of feature discrimination for similar pedestrians. The model employs a dual-branch network architecture, where the first branch processes through the pose estimation branch to capture body posture and local features; The second branch introduces the CBAM attention mechanism to boost the model’s proficiency in extracting information. The two branches are weighted and fused by the designed multi-dimensional feature fusion network (MDFF), which considerably optimizes the model’s performance by capturing global information with different dimensions and enhancing features. Finally, a joint loss function is introduced to impose constraints on the model. Substantial experiments on the Market1501, DukeMTMC-reID, and MSMT17 datasets demonstrate that the mAP values reached 89.9%, 81.1%, and 61.6%, respectively, while the Rank-1 accuracies achieved 95.9%, 90.3%, and 82.5%. This method displays superiority in satisfying the practical demands of pedestrian re-identification in real-world scenarios.

Pedestrian Re-identification (ReID) is a computer vision task that identifies and matches identical pedestrians across multiple cameras at different times, giving an image of a specific pedestrian. It involves retrieving the same pedestrian from a large image repository based on the provided images. The safety of pedestrians in traffic has always been a key focus of traffic management in various countries. Pedestrian traffic accidents occur frequently, especially on roads without dedicated sidewalks, complex intersections, and areas lacking adequate traffic signal control, where conflicts between pedestrians and motor vehicles are likely to occur, resulting in severe casualties. Pedestrian re-identification plays a vital role in intelligent monitoring and public safety, as it enables comprehensive perception and analysis of pedestrian identity matching and trajectory tracking across multiple cameras. Traditional pedestrian re-identification methods generally depend on handcrafted features and classical machine learning models, such as SVM1, KNN2. Although these methods can achieve good results in some scenarios, they present numerous drawbacks such as high time cost for handcrafted feature extraction, limited feature representation ability, poor adaptability to complex scenes, and difficulty in automating feature extraction. In light of the advancements in deep learning, approaches utilizing deep models such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have gradually become mainstream3offering more automated and efficient feature-learning capabilities. However, current pedestrian re-identification methods still have many challenges when handling complex pedestrian pose variations and occlusion of key features. Therefore, this study proposes a dual-branch network approach that integrates pose estimation4 and attention mechanisms5. Pose estimation has the advantages of strong adaptability, support for behavioral analysis, and dynamic recognition. It can also be combined with pedestrian re-identification to improve the recognition rate in cross-view and occlusion scenarios effectively. The advantage of the attention mechanism is based on its capability to automatically focus on the most crucial parts of the information, thus enhancing the model’s effectiveness in efficiency, accuracy, and explainability, especially when handling complex data and tasks.

Existing pedestrian re-identification approaches exhibit certain drawbacks in their feature extraction and fusion strategies. Firstly, traditional methods predominantly rely on single-scale feature extraction. Although some studies have incorporated multi-scale features, they often merely concatenate or fuse features from different scales without modeling the interactions across scales, thereby limiting the model’s adaptability to significant scale variations. In contrast, CPHMNet incorporates a Multi-Scale Interaction (MDA) module into the network, which not only captures both low-level detail features and high-level semantic features but also enhances feature complementarity and robustness via cross-scale interactions, thereby improving adaptability to variations in camera viewpoints and shooting distances. Secondly, when addressing pose variation, existing studies often utilize pose information as an auxiliary input or perform feature alignment at later stages. However, the integration between pose cues and appearance features remains relatively loose, limiting the full exploitation of pose estimation. In contrast, CPHMNet introduces a pose estimation branch that incorporates pose information during the early feature learning stage to calibrate appearance features, thereby enabling the model to better handle inconsistencies caused by pose variations such as walking or looking down. Thirdly, attention mechanisms have been widely adopted in recent pedestrian re-identification studies; however, most approaches employ them as standalone enhancement modules, lacking deep complementarity with other network components. CPHMNet incorporates the CBAM attention mechanism in the other branch, working in conjunction with the pose estimation branch to guide the attention pathway toward discriminative regions, thereby enhancing the discriminative power of the features. Finally, existing multi-branch or hybrid feature fusion methods typically rely on simple concatenation or weighted summation, failing to fully exploit the complementary information across branches, hierarchical levels, and scales. CPHMNet is designed with a Multi-Dimensional Feature Fusion (MDFF) network that efficiently integrates multi-source information from different branches, hierarchical levels, and scales to produce richer, more comprehensive, and highly discriminative final feature representations, thereby significantly enhancing overall recognition performance. In summary, CPHMNet overcomes the limitations of existing methods by deeply integrating multi-scale interaction, pose estimation, and attention mechanisms, as well as through multi-dimensional feature fusion. It provides a systematic solution to address challenges such as scale variation, pose changes, and dispersed attention in complex scenarios.

Related works

Feature extraction

Feature extraction denotes the automated derivation of discriminative information from unprocessed input information through deep neural models for classification, regression, or other tasks. Currently, the primary feature extraction methods including CNN6, ResNet7, and VGGNet8. The challenges in feature extraction arise from insufficient robustness to input data variations and limitations in feature learning9. Considering traditional deep feature extraction methods for ReID, the network’s capability to model complex content is limited when facing occlusion or posing variation issues. Akbaba et al.10 introduced a dynamic convolutional backbone network method with channel fusion. Compared to static networks, dynamic networks adaptively adjust to the input images, improving recognition accuracy. In occlusion testing on the Occluded-DukeMTMC dataset, the algorithm demonstrated enhanced accuracy. The computation of dynamic convolution is relatively complex, which may result in slower inference speed. Hua et al.11 propounded a method that depended on transformers to extract point-level features, enabling the re-identification of occluded individuals. The method learns to capture point-level visual features from images, boosting the differentiation capability of extracted features and improving the potential for pedestrian re-identification. Although transformer can capture global dependencies, they typically exhibit lower sensitivity to local features, and local contextual information may be overlooked. To overcome the limitations in capturing global features and managing computational complexity, Geng et al.12 proposed a hybrid network framework named ReMamba. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed ReMamba outperforms the state-of-the-art VI-ReID method. To address the challenge of extracting features from small objects and visual cues, Akan et al.13 enhanced the Swin Transformer with CNN, constructing a region-based feature extraction Swin Transformer backbone that improves local feature extraction and small object recognition.

Pose estimation

Pose estimation plays a crucial role in deep learning, which refers to recognizing and determining the spatial pose of the human object in images or videos under computer vision techniques. Currently, well-known algorithms of pose estimation include HRNet14, OpenPose15, and EfficientPose16. Pose estimation algorithms generally experience performance degradation in multi-person scenarios, dynamic backgrounds, and complex environments17. To identify delicate human actions for intricate behavior assessment, Fang et al.18 put forward AlphaPose, a system capable of performing accurate full-body pose estimation and tracing on the fly, enabling precise localization of body key points and improving pedestrian tracking. However, AlphaPose relies on the previous object detection results, and if the detection box is inaccurate, the pose estimation results will also be affected. Chéron et al.19 proposed a new pose-based Convolutional Neural Network descriptor (P-CNN) for behavior analysis, which integrates motion and visual features following the movement tracks of body segments, and showing improvements over cutting-edge methods. However, suppose the dynamic changes of the action are very rapid or complex. In this case, P-CNN may struggle to capture the full scope of the action, which can affect recognition accuracy. Zhang et al.20 designed a pose-guided module that guides feature extraction through pose estimation to extract practical pose information from the repaired images, enabling more accurate pedestrian matching. However, the pose-guided module typically requires additional computational resources, increasing the model’s complexity and training time. To enhance the overall robustness of the pedestrian re-identification problem, Du et al.21 incorporated a human pose estimation network into the model, assisting the model in addressing the issue of semantic information loss caused by occlusion. However, extremely severe occlusion and the interference of highly distinctive clothing features may lead to model failure.

Contextual attention framework

This attention-guided framework allows the system to dynamically prioritize specific regions of the input data throughout computational operations, rather than processing all inputs uniformly. In other words, it selectively concentrates on more important or relevant information while processing data. Currently, commonly used attention mechanisms include CBAM22, BAM23, and SA24. The attention mechanism generally suffers from insufficient robustness when handling occlusion and background noise, lacking sensitivity to local features25. To overcome the difficulties caused by occlusion, significant pose changes, and the difficulty of learning discriminative information from different viewpoints, Jiao et al.26 presented a unique integrated attention structure for pedestrian ReID, combining fine-grained pixel attention and coarse-grained region attention mechanisms to acquire two complementary feature characterizations. The results verified the mechanism’s adaptability to non-rigid deformations of the human figure and demonstrated its effectiveness in improving the accuracy of ReID. Currently, CNN-based models are limited by variations in the size and shape of segmented objects. The low interpretability of CNNs limits their use in clinical decision-making. Karri et al.27 extensively used various attention mechanisms in CNN models and proposed a semantically driven multi-modular attention network with an interpretable architecture. The results indicate that the attention feature maps are more interpretable than the leading-edge models. To address the impact of key information loss in occluded pedestrian re-identification, Geng et al.28 integrated window attention, adaptive local attention, and interactive attention, effectively extracting contextual information from window-level features while minimizing the influence of occlusion noise. Bai et al.29 proposed a multi-discriminative feature extraction network (MDFE-Net), which enhances the distance dependency of multiple discriminative features in vehicles through non-local attention, thereby improving the network’s discriminative capability.

In pedestrian re-identification tasks, significant scale variations in pedestrian images may arise due to differences in camera positions and viewing angles. Traditional approaches typically focus on feature extraction at a single scale, making it difficult to simultaneously capture fine-grained details and global semantic information. As a result, the recognition performance of such models is often limited in complex scenarios. In addition, pose variations caused by different viewing angles—such as walking, turning, or looking downward—can severely impact the consistency of feature representations. This issue remains a critical challenge for existing pedestrian re-identification models. Meanwhile, in real-world applications, complex backgrounds, severe occlusions, and various distracting elements often cause the model to focus on irrelevant regions, leading to feature redundancy and insufficient discriminative ability. To tackle these challenges, a novel dual-branch pedestrian re-identification network, termed CPHMNet, is proposed. A CPHMNet dual-branch pedestrian re-identification network is constructed based on multi-dimensional feature fusion and fused pose estimation. Figure 1 shows the network architecture, providing a clear overview of its design. The input pedestrian image of size 256 × 128 undergoes processing and feature extraction via a 1 × 1 convolution. A Multi-Scale Interaction (MDA) module is designed to enhance the complementarity of cross-scale features. The network captures various low and high-level function data through multi-scale feature extraction. Low-level features include comprehensive specifics, while high-level features capture more global semantic content. The multi-scale interaction module enhances the network’s adaptability to scale variations by interacting with features from different scales. To simultaneously address the challenges of pose variations and strengthen the feature representation capacity in the pedestrian re-identification process, the network model adopts a dual-branch structure, with one branch passing through the pose estimation segment and the other through the CBAM attention mechanism segment. The pose estimation segment aims to address and correct the identification challenges caused by pedestrian pose variations. By utilizing the pose estimation branch, the network can understand the pose information of the target, and through this information, correct the input image, enabling the model to handle better appearance variations resulting from pose changes. To boost the expressive capacity of features, the other branch introduces the CBAM attention mechanism, which adaptively regulates the input feature map, emphasizing the significance of each channel and spatial location. This mechanism dynamically emphasizes the useful information, improving the system’s representational ability and decision-making accuracy. After the dual-branch structure, a multi-dimensional feature fusion network (MDFF) is utilized. The multi-dimensional feature fusion network efficiently integrates information from different branches, layers, and scales, thereby incorporating richer and more comprehensive representations in the final feature representation, further enhancing pedestrian re-identification performance.

Multi-scale interaction MDA module

In pedestrian re-identification, the target’s appearance may undergo significant scale variations due to factors such as viewpoint and distance. Features at different scales may exhibit significant variations, especially when parts of the object are occluded or when the pedestrian’s appearance changes in various backgrounds, hindering the ability of the network to detect the full view of the target effectively. The network achieves improved fusion and communication of features across varying scales by establishing multi-scale pathways. To address the above issue, a dedicated multi-scale interaction (MDA) module is intended to improve the robustness of object recognition. By applying a dynamic attention mechanism with weighted features at different scales, the network can jointly capture the attributes of the focused object across multiple scales, thereby reducing the recognition errors caused by scale variations.

After the pedestrian input features undergo convolutional feature extraction, they pass through the multi-scale interaction (MDA) module, the exact structure of which is visualized in Fig. 2. In this module, input features traverse two branches, each with a unique receptive field scale. The two branches process the input image at different scales, extracting features from multiple resolutions. The network can learn rich information from local details to global structures at various scales. The interaction module between the two branches facilitates information exchange, enabling the network to better understand the target’s appearance at different scales and enhance its sensitivity to them. Pose estimation of the HRNet network

When occlusion occurs in the pedestrian’s appearance features, it leads to the loss of visual features. Complex factors such as surrounding buildings, traffic conditions, and weather may affect the understanding and identification of the pedestrian, making re-identification more challenging. The pose estimation module can identify the pedestrian’s key points, especially in cases of partial occlusion, where the information from the key points can still reconstruct the pedestrian’s approximate pose. The study utilizes the HRNet network to extract the pedestrian skeleton. The advantages of HRNet in extracting skeleton information are mainly reflected in its ability to maintain high resolution, a fully convolutional structure, and efficient information flow. HRNet can perform exceptionally well in pedestrian pose estimation tasks with these advantages. HRNet employs a multi-resolution fusion method, progressively integrating features from multiple scales to construct high-resolution representations. The network has several parallel branches, each handling the input image at varying resolutions. The output features of these branches are then fused in a way that preserves the spatial information of each resolution level, resulting in high-resolution features, which are transmitted to the final segmentation layer for processing. The segmentation layer includes a series of convolutional and upsampling layers.

HRNet first employs a shallow convolutional module to preprocess the input and generate the initial feature map. From the initial layers, the network builds parallel branches at multiple resolutions, preserving this multi-scale parallelism across deeper layers. The high-resolution branch preserves spatial details, while the medium- and low-resolution branches enhance semantic representation. At each stage, an interaction module is introduced to facilitate information exchange among different resolutions, enabling effective multi-scale feature fusion. HRNet transfers high-resolution features to the low-resolution streams at the conclusion of each stage, concurrently returning semantic cues from the low-resolution branches to the high-resolution pathway to improve context awareness. This multi-layer interaction progressively refines the high-resolution features. In the final stage, feature maps from all branches are upsampled to a unified high resolution and then fused. Subsequently, convolutional layers generate heatmaps for keypoints, where the network outputs one heatmap per human keypoint (e.g., head, shoulder, elbow, knee, foot). Each pixel in the heatmap represents the probability of the corresponding location being the target keypoint. By connecting the keypoints sequentially according to the human body’s topological structure, a complete skeletal representation can be formed, which is used for pose estimation tasks. CBAM attention-based mechanism

In pedestrian re-identification, different individuals may wear similar clothing or appear in complex backgrounds, leading to substantial interference with information in the original convolutional feature maps. The introduction of the CBAM attention mechanism aims to effectively highlight key feature regions such as clothing color and arm posture, while suppressing irrelevant background areas like roads and trees. This enables the network to focus on more discriminative information, thereby improving matching accuracy and enhancing the discriminative power of the feature maps. This allows the network to focus on more distinctive information, thereby improving matching accuracy and enhancing the discriminative power of the feature maps. In the dual-branch architecture, the CBAM branch focuses more on semantic feature selection and attention refinement. It aims to differentiate individuals with visually similar appearances but different identities by enhancing feature granularity, thereby further improving the network’s ability to recognize pedestrians in complex scenes. Pedestrian re-identification tasks suffer from the drawbacks of low recognition accuracy and limited local information. CBAM integrates channel attention and spatial attention to refine the input features in two stages, more comprehensively capturing the key information in the features. The proposed architecture incorporates two fundamental components: a Spatial Attention Mechanism (SAM) and a Channel Attention Mechanism (CAM), as illustrated in Fig. 4. The holistic structure of the CBAM module is shown. By the progressive application of channel attention and spatial attention, CBAM can dynamically adjust the input feature map, highlighting the importance of each channel and spatial location, and dynamically prioritizing the most valuable characteristics, thereby improving the model’s representational capability and decision-making accuracy.

Design of the multi-dimensional feature fusion network (MDFF)

Traditional (CNNs), when handling complex visual tasks, cannot simultaneously capture global information across both the height and width spatial dimensions, resulting in limited feature representation capability. Through global mean aggregation and maximum aggregation across both vertical and horizontal dimensions, the MDFF module captures comprehensive global attributes, enhancing the comprehensiveness of feature extraction. To enhance feature representation capability, the MDFF module is responsible for generating attention projections on the longitudinal and latitudinal axes through shared feature extraction layers enhanced by attention mechanisms, weighting the input feature image to amplify crucial characteristics and repress irrelevant ones. Through grouping, the incoming feature representation is partitioned according to the channel count, alleviating the computational load for each group while retaining the diversity and richness of the feature representation. The single attention methodology has restrictions when representing feature information, as it is unable to leverage comprehensive information across a variety of dimensions concurrently. To address the aforementioned issue, the MDFF module combines multi-dimensional holistic details and the attention mechanism. Acquiring multi-dimensional global attributes and insight significantly improves the model’s effectiveness, notably in intricate visual tasks. The structure is displayed in Fig. 7.

Design of the joint loss function

To simultaneously optimize identity classification and feature encoding, and improve the overall model’s effectiveness, a jointly supervised network combining the ID classification loss and triplet loss is adopted for training and optimization.

The core of pedestrian re-identification tasks is correctly identifying different visual representations of the same person. To achieve this goal, the classification ID loss is adopted. Performing explicit classification training for each identity helps the model acquire more discriminative feature descriptors, thereby improving the accuracy of identity recognition.13\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\:\:\:{\text{L}}_{\text{ID}}\text{(}\text{y,t}\text{)}\text{=-}\sum\:_{\text{i}\text{=}\text{1}}^{\text{C}}{\text{t}}_{\text{i}}\text{(}{\text{y}}_{\text{i}}\text{)}$$\end{document}

where y represents the model’s output; t is consistent with the true label; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\text{L}}_{\text{ID}}$$\end{document} denotes the classification ID loss; C represents the overall count of identity classes; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\text{t}}_{\text{i}}\:$$\end{document}represents the one-hot representation of the true label; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\text{y}}_{\text{i}}$$\end{document} represents the probability of the model classifying the sample as the identity i.

The classification ID loss, by optimizing identity classification, can amplify the accuracy of the retrieval system. When locating a specific identity among a considerable number of pedestrian images, the ability to classify accurately directly impacts the quality of the retrieval results.

In the pedestrian re-identification assignment, the model is required to effectively distinguish pedestrians of different identities and match pedestrians of the same identity. To effectively address this issue, triplet loss is employed, which optimizes similarity by clustering images with identical identities and separating images of distinct identities, thereby assisting the model in learning more discriminative feature representations. The basic form of triplet loss is listed below:14\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$L(a,p,n) = \max (d(a,p) - d(a,n) + \alpha ,0)$$\end{document}

In the equation, L(a, p,n) denotes the triplet loss value; a stands for the anchor, i.e., the reference sample; p stands for the positive sample, which falls within the identical identity as the anchor; n represents the negative example, which falls within a distinct identity than the anchor; d(a, p) stands for the distance between the anchor and the positive sample, typically calculated using Euclidean distance or cosine similarity; d(a, n) indicates the distance from the anchor to the negative sample; α indicates the margin, designed to guarantee that the distance from the anchor to the positive sample is shorter than the distance from the anchor to the negative sample augmented by α.

In this model, triplet loss is applied to boost the model’s performance to identify variations in pedestrian identities, improving its robustness, particularly in the presence of scene changes, lighting variations, and other conditions, thereby increasing the accuracy and efficiency of pedestrian retrieval.

Experimental results and analysis

Experimental datasets

To confirm the efficiency of the suggested model, large-scale validation is performed on three typically adopted datasets: Market1501, DukeMTMC-reID, and MSMT17. To evaluate the proposed model’s robustness against occlusion, comprehensive experiments were carried out on the well-established Occluded-Duke dataset.

The Market-1501 dataset was compiled on the campus of Tsinghua University, containing a set of five high-definition cameras alongside one lower-resolution camera, totaling six cameras. The dataset comprises 1,501 pedestrians detected, with 32,668 identified pedestrian bounding boxes. Every pedestrian is recorded by at least two cameras, often resulting in diverse frames from an individual camera. The training set features 751 unique identities across 12,936 images, while the testing set includes 750 identities with 19,732 images. Out of the total, 3,368 bounding boxes are chosen at random to act as queries, leaving 19,732 for the gallery.

The DukeMTMC-reID dataset is an extensive image dataset for pedestrian re-identification, gathered by eight stationary cameras deployed across the Duke University grounds. The dataset comprises 36,411 images, involving 1,404 person identities; the dataset provides annotations for pedestrian traits, such as gender, sleeve length, etc. The training set comprises 702 identities, with 16,522 images; a Sum of 702 identities and 17,661 images makes up the test set. From the total, 2,228 bounding boxes are chosen at random for queries, and the remaining 17,661 are designated as the gallery.

The MSMT17 dataset is an important dataset for pedestrian re-identification, specifically designed to evaluate pedestrian re-identification algorithms under multi-scene and multi-temporal conditions. It is captured by a framework of 15 different cameras and includes 126,411 images involving 4,101 pedestrian identities, with multiple images for each identity from different viewpoints. The training set contains 1041 identities, including 32,621 images. The test set is composed of 3060 identities, with 93,820 images. From the total, 11,659 bounding boxes are stochastically chosen for queries, and the remaining 82,161 are allocated to the gallery.

The Occluded-Duke dataset is a specialized benchmark designed for pedestrian re-identification under occlusion conditions. It consists of images captured from multiple fixed cameras installed around the Duke University campus. The dataset contains a total of 15,618 images corresponding to 1,400 distinct identities. Each image is annotated with pedestrian attributes and occlusion labels to facilitate evaluation under challenging visibility scenarios. The training set includes 702 identities with 8,000 images, while the test set comprises the remaining identities with 7,618 images. For evaluation, a subset of images is randomly selected as query samples, with the rest forming the gallery set.

Experimental model hyperparameter settings and evaluation metrics

The model parameters are updated utilizing the SGD optimizer, with a batch size of 128, a preliminary update rate of 0.025, and a total of 350 epochs for training. An initialization optimization approach is applied during the initial 10 epochs, and the optimization parameter is progressively decreased by a factor of 0.1 at the 150th, 225th, and 300th epochs. The Adam optimizer30 is used for updating the architecture parameters, starting with the step size initialized to a value of 0.002, and the learning rate is scaled down at a consistent rate. All pedestrian images are proportioned to a uniform resolution of 256 pixels in height and 128 pixels in width. The data augmentation strategy involves applying random horizontal flips, stochastic crops, and random erasures31.

The experiments in the present study primarily utilize two evaluation metrics: Rank-1 accuracy and Mean Average Precision (mAP), to assess the effectiveness of the proposed model.

Ablation experiment

To verify the efficacy of the proposed technique in pedestrian re-identification and evaluate the contribution of each component of CPHMNet, a comprehensive set of ablation experiments was designed to compare and assess the following scenarios: (1) The original ResNet-50 + Multi-Scale Interaction MDA module as the baseline (Baseline) model. (2) Adding the Multi-Dimensional Feature Fusion Network (MDFF) to the original ResNet-50 with the Multi-Scale Interaction MDA module. (3) Adding the CBAM attention mechanism on top of (2). (4) Applying the pose estimation method Pose to (3). Comprehensive ablation studies were performed using the Market-1501 benchmark, with quantitative results summarized in Table 7. The experimental outcomes suggest that the integration of the Hierarchical Weighted Feature Fusion Network (MDFF) yields performance improvements of 1.2% in mean average precision (mAP) and 0.3% in Rank-1 accuracy. Similarly, integrating the attention strategy utilized in CBAM into Res50 + MDA + MDFF yielded a 1.8% point growth in mAP and a 0.6% point rise in Rank-1. By using the Res50 + MDA + MDFF + CBAM + Pose method, the mAP and Rank-1 accuracy were significantly improved by 2% points and 0.9% points, respectively. The empirical findings thoroughly illustrate the effectiveness of the suggested approach.

Discussion

This paper combines multi-dimensional feature fusion with pose estimation to construct the CPHMNet, a dual-branch pedestrian re-identification network derived from worldwide grouping features and fused pose estimation. Extensive experimental analyses are performed on three publicly accessible datasets. The experimental results indicate that the average precision of the proposed method is 89.9%, 81.1%, and 61.6%, respectively, with Rank-1 accuracy improving by 0.9%, 1.3%, and 2%. These results evaluate the effectiveness of the presented model. In contrast to other CNN models, the proposed CPHMNet dual-branch pedestrian re-identification network significantly improves average precision and accuracy, effectively eliminating the recognition errors caused by different scales. Compared to other CNN models, the proposed CPHMNet dual-branch pedestrian re-identification network significantly improves average precision and accuracy, effectively eliminating the recognition errors caused by different scales. To improve pedestrian re-identification accuracy, the method described in this study addresses issues such as incomplete extraction of pedestrian information from multiple perspectives and occlusion interference in pedestrian appearance features.

The significance of this study lies in the following: First, the proposed dual-branch network structure can capture richer and more complete features compared to traditional methods. By interacting with multi-scale features, the network’s adaptability to scale variations is enhanced. Secondly, we innovatively propose the multi-dimensional feature fusion network, MDFF. In complex environments, where there are significant differences between the regional features and contextual features of pedestrians, the problem is effectively addressed through the exchange and fusion of information from different types. Finally, the classification ID loss and triplet loss are jointly adopted, enhancing both the classification and metric tasks through multi-task learning. This improves the feature space structure, enabling the model to effectively separate different classes in the feature space. The CPHMNet method achieves satisfactory results, with its overall performance surpassing that of other leading-edge pedestrian re-identification models, thereby proving the superiority of the model.

Conclusion

This paper presents the CPHMNet method to deal with challenges in current deep learning models for pedestrian re-identification, for instance, incomplete extraction of pedestrian information from multiple perspectives and occlusion interference in pedestrian appearance features. The CPHMNet method proposes the multi-scale interactive MDA module, which captures the features of the target object across various scales. A dual-branch network structure is employed, and the CBAM attention mechanism is presented to enhance the precision of pedestrian re-identification. The pose estimation module is applied to identify the pedestrian’s pivotal points and reconstruct the pedestrian’s approximate posture. A multi-dimensional feature fusion network, MDFF, is used as a data augmentation method. For the loss function, we adopt a joint loss function design to simultaneously optimize identity classification and feature representation, thereby enhancing the general effectiveness of the model, which includes both classification ID loss and triplet loss. The SGD optimization function is employed throughout the training phase to update the model parameters.

In real-world scenarios involving uniformed pedestrian groups, the MDA module enables rapid retrieval by leveraging mid-scale attributes (e.g., backpack patterns) and macro-scale dynamic features (e.g., gait characteristics). The dual-branch architecture of CPHMNet is motivated by the inherent pose variability of pedestrians in unconstrained surveillance scenarios. When target pedestrians stand sideways in practical deployment scenarios, the pose estimation branch effectively extracts visible shoulder contour features through joint localization. In contrast, the CBAM attention branch enhances detailed head region representations. This collaborative mechanism achieves 95.9% Rank-1 accuracy on Market1501’s side-view test subset, representing a 13.2% improvement over ResNet-50. The MDFF module emulates human observation logic in complex scenarios through global-local feature fusion, addressing the practical requirements of pedestrian re-identification in real-world environments. Moreover, the model demonstrates remarkable efficiency with merely 1.0G FLOPs computational overhead, exhibiting lightweight characteristics suitable for deployment on mainstream edge computing devices while fulfilling real-time processing requirements.

The results of the Study on the Market1501, DukeMTMCReID, and MSMT17 datasets demonstrate that our method improves accuracy, fully illustrating the success of the proposed technique.

Moreover, although the CPHMNet method marks an important step forward in pedestrian re-identification, the model’s computational efficiency could be further optimized to achieve better runtime performance. Future work could explore more advanced techniques to accelerate model performance. We hope the proposed method can inspire further work on constructing deep learning frameworks specifically tailored for ReID challenges.

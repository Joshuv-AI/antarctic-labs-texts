Emerging trends and determinants of electronic cigarette use among young Thai men, 2022–2024
Authors: Boonsub Sakboonyarat, Jaturon Poovieng, Kanlaya Jongcherdchootrakul, Nutchar Hempatawee, Panadda Hatthachote, Mathirut Mungthin, Ram Rangsin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundElectronic-cigarette (e-cigarette) use is linked to serious cardiovascular and pulmonary complications, raising public health concerns worldwide, particularly among younger populations. In Thailand, there is limited epidemiological data on e-cigarette usage. Therefore, trends in e-cigarette use and its associated factors among young Thai men from 2022 to 2024 were investigated.MethodsA serial cross-sectional study was conducted among young Thai men aged 18–26 years across four geographical regions in Thailand. Data were collected via a self-administered questionnaire; current e-cigarette use was defined as e-cigarette use on one or more days in the past month. Multivariable log-binomial regression analysis was employed to identify factors associated with current e-cigarette use, presented as adjusted prevalence ratios (aPR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI).ResultsThe analysis involved 9,639 participants, averaging 20.5 years old. From 2022 to 2024, the age-adjusted prevalence of current e-cigarette use increased significantly from 17.2% to 29.3% (p-trend < 0.001). Among those aged 18–20, prevalence rose from 20.1% to 33.6% (p-trend < 0.001), while in the 21–26 age group, it increased from 14.8% to 25.6% (p-trend < 0.001). A rising trend in e-cigarette use over a three-year study period was observed, regardless of their tobacco use status. Higher prevalence was noted in men aged 18–20 compared to those aged 21–26 (aPR 1.30; 95% CI: 1.19–1.43). Men in the central region exhibited the highest prevalence. Compared to agriculturists, individuals in other occupations showed higher current e-cigarette use. Those with no formal education had a higher prevalence than graduates (aPR 1.60; 95% CI: 1.04–2.47). Lifestyle factors significantly associated with current e-cigarette use included being a current smoker (aPR 1.89; 95% CI: 1.72–2.07), a current drinker (aPR 2.59; 95% CI: 2.15–3.12), and a night worker (aPR 1.26; 95% CI: 1.13–1.40).ConclusionEmerging trends in e-cigarette use among young Thai men from 2022 to 2024 were identified regardless of the status of tobacco use. Significant sociodemographic and lifestyle factors influencing this behavior were highlighted. These findings can guide policymakers in prioritizing targeted interventions to reduce e-cigarette use and mitigate associated cardiopulmonary complications within this demographic.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-24962-9.Keywords: E-cigarette, Tobacco, Trends, Factors, Young men, Thailand

Introduction

Electronic cigarettes (e-cigarettes) are battery-powered devices that deliver nicotine through vapor [1]. They use solvents such as propylene glycol and vegetable glycerin to create a vapor that is inhaled into the body, affecting both the respiratory and circulatory systems [1, 2]. Evidence reveals that e-cigarettes can be harmful to health, particularly by increasing the risk of cardiovascular diseases (CVD), such as ischemic heart disease and stroke [3, 4], as well as respiratory diseases, including vaping-associated pulmonary injury [5, 6]. Although e-cigarette aerosol contains fewer harmful chemicals than conventional cigarette smoke, e-cigarettes cannot be considered safe [7, 8]. Misperceptions of reduced harm are common, yet robust evidence and long-term studies are required to elucidate their potential risks and benefits [9].

While the World Health Organization reports that the prevalence of tobacco use has been steadily declining worldwide [10], the use of e-cigarettes is growing rapidly, especially among young individuals. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention reports reveal a rise in e-cigarette use among US adults in recent decades [11, 12]. The overall prevalence of e-cigarette use among US adults aged 15 and older was 1.9% in 2012, increasing to 4.5% or 11.1 million in 2021 [11]. It was more prominent among younger US adults aged 18–44, where usage rose from 2.4% in 2012 to 11.0% (among those aged 18–24) and 6.5% (among those aged 25–44) in 2021 [11, 12]. A recent meta-analysis in 2024 estimates that the global prevalence of current e-cigarette use among young people was 4.8% in total, 4.9% in young men, and 1.6% in young women [13].

Regulation of e-cigarettes varies significantly across countries, ranging from no regulation to outright bans [14]. Thailand is one of the countries that bans e-cigarettes, and it is illegal to sell, import, possess, or use nicotine-containing and nicotine-free cartridges [15]. Violations are subject to severe penalties under the Ministry of Commerce Notification (2014), Consumer Protection Board Order (2015), and the Customs Act (2017), including fines and imprisonment [16, 17]. The 2019 National Health Examination Survey (NHES) in Thailand reported a lifetime prevalence of e-cigarette use among individuals aged 15 and older of 3.5%, with rates of 6.7% for men and 0.5% for women [18]. The prevalence of e-cigarette use within the past 30 days was 1.0% overall, 1.9% in men, and 0.3% in women. Across age groups, the prevalence of e-cigarette use within the past 30 days among young people aged 15–29 was the highest, at 3.6% overall, with 5.8% for men and 1.2% for women [19].

Current evidence demonstrates that e-cigarette use is particularly prevalent among young Thai people. Analyzing emerging trends and factors influencing e-cigarette use will help health policymakers plan interventions targeting this high-risk population. However, epidemiological data on e-cigarette use in this demographic are limited. Therefore, this study aims to assess the trends in e-cigarette use among young Thai men using baseline data from the Surveillance in Heat-Related Illness in Basic Military Training (HRI-RTA) study from 2022 to 2024 across four geographical regions and identify the factors associated with current e-cigarette use within this population.

Methods

Study design and subjects

A serial cross-sectional study was conducted among young Thai men from 2022 to 2024, utilizing baseline data from the HRI-RTA study, which was carried out by the Research Unit for Military Medicine at Phramongkutklao College of Medicine in Bangkok, Thailand [19, 20]. This surveillance aimed to screen for heat-related illnesses among new conscripts during a 10-week basic military training period across 37 military training units in four regions: (1) Bangkok and Lopburi (central), (2) Ubon Ratchathani (northeast), (3) Chiang Mai (north), and (4) Songkhla (south). The conscription process involves a lottery system at the district level for men aged 21 and older, as well as voluntary selection for those aged 18–20 [19, 21]. From 2022 to 2024, a total of 9,825 conscripts aged 18–26 years participated in the HRI-RTA surveillance. We excluded 186 individuals who did not provide information on e-cigarette use; thus, our study included 9,639 conscripts in the analysis.

The study was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Royal Thai Army Medical Department, in compliance with international guidelines, including the Declaration of Helsinki, the Belmont Report, CIOMS Guidelines, and ICH-GCP (approval number S009q/60).

Data collection

The data collection process was conducted during the first week of the basic military training session at the military training unit. Eligible participants were informed about the study’s details and objectives, and those who volunteered to participate provided written consent before data collection. A self-reported, paper-based questionnaire was used to gather data on demographic characteristics, including age, province of residence before conscription, occupation prior to conscription, and highest level of educational attainment. The questionnaire also assessed various lifestyle factors, such as e-cigarette use, tobacco use, alcohol consumption, illicit drug use, exercise habits, and night work status (see Supplementary materials). Completed questionnaires were securely sealed in envelopes and subsequently sent to the Research Unit for Military Medicine at Phramongkutklao College of Medicine in Bangkok, Thailand.

Variables

Demographics, e-cigarette use status, and lifestyle factors were evaluated using a questionnaire. Current e-cigarette use was defined as e-cigarette use on one or more days in the past month [22]. Lifetime e-cigarette use refers to any e-cigarette use in a person’s lifetime, which includes current e-cigarette users and those who are former e-cigarette users. For the tobacco, we categorized tobacco use into three groups: never, former users, and current users (defined as those who used tobacco within the past 12 months). Alcohol use also had three categories: never drinkers, former drinkers, and current drinkers (defined as use within the past 12 months). We classified exercise into three groups: no exercise, irregular exercise, and regular exercise (defined as exercising for at least 30 minutes at least three times per week). We defined illicit drug use as any use of illegal substances, such as methamphetamine, within the past month. Night shift workers were identified as individuals working nighttime hours, including singers, musicians, and other jobs performed at night.

Statistical analysis

We used descriptive statistics to examine the characteristics of the study participants. Categorical variables are presented as percentages, while continuous variables are represented as means and standard deviations (SD). We estimated the age-adjusted prevalence of current e-cigarette use and lifetime e-cigarette use among young Thai men for each year, presenting these as percentages along with 95% confidence intervals (CI). Generalized linear models were utilized to evaluate trends in e-cigarette use prevalence from 2022 to 2024.

We used log-binomial regression to determine factors associated with current e-cigarette use among young Thai men. The multivariable analysis included the following variables: year, age, geographical region, occupation, educational attainment, religion, tobacco use, alcohol use, illicit drug use, exercise, and night shift work. The results were presented as adjusted prevalence ratios (aPR) along with 95% CIs. All data analyses were performed using StataCorp. 2021. Stata Statistical Software: Release 17. College Station, TX: StataCorp LLC. A two-sided p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Characteristics of study participants

Table 1 outlines the characteristics of 9,639 young Thai men who participated in the study from 2022 to 2024. The mean age of the participants was 20.5 ± 1.8 years, with a range of 18 to 26 years. The majority came from the northeast (38.0%), followed by the north (31.4%), the south (20.5%), and the central regions (10.1%). In terms of occupation, 21.0% identified as agriculturists, while 19.7% were students and 15.3% were office staff. A total of 1,219 participants (12.7%) were night workers. Regarding education, 55.3% achieved grade 12, and 19.3% obtained vocational qualifications. A total of 3,889 participants (40.4%) reported current tobacco use, whereas 4,650 participants (48.2%) indicated that they had never used tobacco. Approximately three-fourths (73.5%) reported current alcohol consumption, and one-fifth (19.8%) reported illicit drug use within the past month.

Trends in e-cigarette use among young Thai men from 2022 to 2024

Table 2 shows trends in the prevalence of e-cigarette use among young Thai men from 2022 to 2024. The age-adjusted prevalence of current e-cigarette use increased significantly from 17.2% (2022) to 29.3% (2024) (p-trend <0.001). Among those aged 18–20, the prevalence rose from 20.1% to 33.6% (p-trend <0.001), while in the 21–26 age group, it increased from 14.8% to 25.6% (p-trend <0.001) (Fig. 1). Rising trends in the prevalence of current e-cigarette use were observed across four geographical regions (Fig. 2).

Trends in the prevalence of current e-cigarette use among young Thai men, 2022–2024, by geographical region

Age-adjusted lifetime prevalence of e-cigarette use among young Thai men significantly increased from 57.3% (2022) to 66.6% (2024) (p-trend <0.001). Among those aged 18–20, the prevalence rose from 63.6% to 72.4% (p-trend <0.001), while in those aged 21–26, it increased from 52.0% to 61.6% (p-trend <0.001). Trends in lifetime prevalence of e-cigarette use varied by geographical region (Table 2).

Table 3 presents trends in the prevalence of e-cigarette use among young Thai men from 2022 to 2024, categorized by tobacco use status. Among individuals who reported never using tobacco, the age-adjusted prevalence of current e-cigarette use significantly increased from 11.4% to 20.2% (p-trend <0.001). The age-adjusted lifetime prevalence of e-cigarette use also rose significantly, from 39.3% to 51.6% (p-trend <0.001). For former tobacco users, the age-adjusted prevalence of current e-cigarette use increased considerably from 15.9% to 29.0% (p-trend <0.001). Among individuals who reported current tobacco use, the age-adjusted prevalence of current e-cigarette use substantially increased from 24.0% to 42.0% (p-trend <0.001).

Factors associated with current e-cigarette use among young Thai men

The aPR derived from the multivariable analysis of factors associated with the prevalence of current e-cigarette use among young Thai men is presented in Table 4. After adjusting for covariates, the prevalence of current e-cigarette use among young Thai men was higher in 2024 compared to 2022 (aPR 1.70; 95% CI: 1.52–1.91). Factors associated with current e-cigarette use included participants aged 18–20 years (aPR 1.30; 95% CI: 1.19–1.43) compared to those aged 21–26. Geographically, participants from the central region (aPR 1.42; 95% CI: 1.24–1.63) and the northeast region (aPR 1.22; 95% CI: 1.10–1.36) exhibited higher prevalence rates of current e-cigarette use than those from the northern region. In terms of occupation, individuals with no employment, students, office staff, laborers, sellers, and others reported higher prevalence rates of current e-cigarette use than those working in agriculture. Additionally, those with no formal education, those who completed grades 1–6 and grades 7–12, and those with vocational certificates had higher prevalence rates than individuals holding bachelor’s degrees or higher, with aPRs of 1.60 (95% CI: 1.04–2.47), 1.29 (95% CI: 1.01–1.66), 1.29 (95% CI: 1.02–1.63), and 1.38 (95% CI: 1.09–1.74), respectively. Lifestyle factors significantly associated with current e-cigarette use included being a current smoker (aPR 1.89; 95% CI 1.72–2.07), a current drinker (aPR 2.59; 95% CI 2.15–3.12), and working night shifts (aPR 1.26; 95% CI 1.13–1.40).

Discussion

There was a significant increase in the trends in both current and lifetime e-cigarette use prevalence among young Thai men from 2022 to 2024. The trends exhibited variation according to demographic factors. This study highlighted the factors associated with the prevalence of current e-cigarette use among young Thai men, including younger age, geographical region, occupation, education, tobacco use, alcohol use, and night workers.

A previous study conducted among Thai middle school students in 2019 found no significant change in the prevalence of current e-cigarette use among adolescents aged 11–16 (3.3%–3.7%) between 2015 and 2019 [15, 23]. The study attributed this stability to the success of the Thai government’s ban on e-cigarettes, which was implemented in 2015 [15]. However, our findings reveal alarming trends: There has been a significant increase in both current and lifetime e-cigarette use prevalence from 2022 to 2024. Current e-cigarette use prevalence increased by 12.1%, while lifetime e-cigarette use rose by 9.3% over the three-year study period. The prevalence of current e-cigarette use in our study was substantially higher than the previous report [15, 23], which may be due to the different age group. In the present study, the participants aged 18–26 were recruited and selected at the district level nationwide, indicating that our findings are representative of the situation among young Thai men [21]. We highlighted that e-cigarette use has emerged as a notable lifestyle risk factor associated with various health issues. This trend poses challenges for multiple sectors, including communities, the public health system, and the legislative branch of the Royal Thai Government. Preventive efforts directed at this population are crucial, as they can have a lasting impact on their health throughout their lives.

The regulation of e-cigarettes varies across countries, with some places imposing no regulations and others implementing outright bans. In the US, it is legal to sell both nicotine-containing and nicotine-free e-cigarette cartridges [14]. Unsurprisingly, the usage of e-cigarettes among younger adults aged 18–24 in the US increased from 2.4% in 2012 to 11.0% in 2021 [11, 12]. In contrast, Thailand has prohibited the sale of e-cigarettes since 2014, regardless of whether they contain nicotine [16]. Despite this ban, our findings demonstrated a similar increasing trend in e-cigarette use among young Thai men, indicating that the prohibition has not effectively curbed usage as one would expect. Current evidence suggests that existing legal measures in Thailand—including bans on the import, sale, possession, and use of both nicotine-containing and nicotine-free cartridges—remain insufficient to address this issue [16, 17].

To reduce e-cigarette use more effectively, a multifaceted strategy is needed, incorporating socioecological perspectives at the individual, interpersonal, organizational, community, societal, and policy levels [24]. First, at the individual level, health education can raise awareness of e-cigarette use and addiction [25]. Second, family-based and peer-led programs can help offset pro-vaping behavior among young adults [26]. Third, schools and workplaces should implement prevention programs and facilitate vape-free policies [27]. Fourth, engaging community leaders and village health volunteers can help boost culturally appropriate norms against e-cigarettes [28]. Fifth, mass media campaigns and stricter regulations on online marketing are important to de-normalize e-cigarette use [29]. Finally, at the policy level, comprehensive strategies—including the enforcement of online sales bans and regional cooperation to curb illicit trade—should be implemented [30].

Our findings reveal a rising trend in e-cigarette use among young Thai men over a three-year study period, regardless of their tobacco use status. In 2024, it was observed that half of the young Thai men who had never used tobacco tried e-cigarettes, and one-fifth of them reported current use. This trend can be attributed to several factors. Despite e-cigarettes being entirely banned in Thailand, they are still accessible through online platforms, facilitating access for young people who want to try them. Additionally, the prices of tobacco and e-cigarettes in Thailand are comparable. The cost of a pack of tobacco ranges between $2 and $3, while e-cigarettes are priced at around $3 to $5 [31, 32]. However, one cartridge of an e-cigarette can provide approximately 10,000–15,000 puffs and comes in various appealing flavors [33].

The prevalence of current e-cigarette use among former tobacco users and current tobacco users has approximately doubled over the past three years. In 2024, about one-third of former tobacco users and two-fifths of current tobacco users reported being current e-cigarette users. This trend may be due to a common misperception that e-cigarettes are less harmful than traditional tobacco products. Subsequently, individuals attempting to quit tobacco may turn to e-cigarettes as a substitute [34, 35]. Therefore, we emphasize the need for health literacy programs that aim to correct misperceptions regarding the harms of e-cigarettes, as well as their short-term and long-term effects.

We found that demographics and lifestyle factors significantly influence current e-cigarette use among young Thai men. The prevalence of current e-cigarette use is 30% higher in those aged 18–20 years compared to those aged 21–26 years. This pattern is similar to evidence found in the US [36]. Several factors contribute to this finding, including marketing, social influences, and misconceptions about the potentially harmful effects of e-cigarettes [37]. In Thailand, it is essential to note that e-cigarettes are entirely banned, making both their sale and use illegal. Individuals aged 18 to 20 who violate this law can face penalties similar to those imposed on individuals over 20. However, courts may reduce these penalties by as much as one-third or one-half [38]. The inconsistency in the enforcement of legal age may elucidate the higher prevalence of current e-cigarette use among young Thai men aged 18–20.

Our findings revealed significant variations in current e-cigarette use among young men in Thailand across different geographical regions. Participants living in the central regions showed the highest prevalence of e-cigarette use compared to those in the other areas. These findings are similar to those reported in the 2019 NHES, which indicated that e-cigarette use among Thai men aged 15 and older in the Central region tended to be higher than that in the North and Northeast [18]. This finding may be explained by the disparity of socioeconomic status across the country; the more urbanized, the greater the likelihood of accessing e-cigarettes [39]. However, our findings highlighted a continuous upward trend in the prevalence of current e-cigarette use across all four geographical regions from 2022 to 2024. As mentioned, e-cigarettes are entirely banned in Thailand; however, to date, young individuals can buy them via online platforms, resulting in easy accessibility.

Our results reported that socioeconomic status—specifically occupation and education—significantly influences current e-cigarette use among young Thai men. The prevalence of e-cigarette use varied based on different occupational characteristics. Notably, night shift workers had a 1.3 times higher prevalence of current e-cigarette use. These findings help identify a high-risk group among young Thai men who are more likely to use e-cigarettes. This evidence may facilitate targeted interventions aimed at reducing e-cigarette use in this population. Additionally, individuals with an education level lower than a bachelor’s degree had a higher prevalence of current e-cigarette use, even after adjusting for factors such as age group. Therefore, addressing the literacy gap—particularly in health literacy—is crucial for correcting misconceptions regarding the dangers of e-cigarettes [40].

Lifestyle factors, including tobacco and alcohol use, are associated with a higher prevalence of current e-cigarette use among young Thai men. Our findings demonstrated that compared to individuals who had never used tobacco, those who were former tobacco users showed a 1.3 times higher prevalence of current e-cigarette use. In comparison, current tobacco users exhibited a 1.9 times higher prevalence. This may be hypothesized as nicotine dependence among those who have ever used tobacco, leading them to try e-cigarettes as an alternative, even though some may hope to achieve smoking cessation [41, 42]. Additionally, both former and current alcohol drinkers have a higher prevalence of current e-cigarette use compared to those who have never consumed alcohol. Our results were supported by a recent study indicating that alcohol users are 3.2 times more likely to start using e-cigarettes six months later [43, 44]. Another study suggests that the co-use of e-cigarettes and alcohol enhances feelings of positive affect, physiological sensations, and cravings for e-cigarettes [45].

This study has some limitations. First, while the participants came from four geographical regions of Thailand, we only included new conscripts under the HRI-RTA surveillance. This may limit the generalizability of our findings to the young male population in Thailand. Second, we were unable to assess specific details about e-cigarette use, such as dosage, frequency, and age of initiation, due to the constraints of the available HRI-RTA surveillance dataset. Nonetheless, the same questionnaire was used in a series of cross-sectional studies, which can provide evidence of trends in e-cigarette use among this population. Third, the cross-sectional design limits causal inference; longitudinal studies are needed to clarify these associations. Fourth, self-reported data may have introduced recall bias and misclassification. Finally, although multivariable analysis was conducted to identify factors associated with current e-cigarette use, residual confounding may still exist. Further research will benefit from examining trends in e-cigarette use by incorporating additional measures, such as frequency of use, flavorings, and age of initiation. Additionally, a mixed-methods study that includes in-depth interviews and focus group discussions could provide more comprehensive insights into e-cigarette use among this population.

Conclusion

We provided updated evidence on e-cigarette use trends among young Thai men from 2022 to 2024, revealing a substantial rise in usage. Factors associated with current e-cigarette use include younger age, geographical region, occupation, education, tobacco use, alcohol use, and night workers. These insights can guide policymakers in developing targeted interventions to reduce e-cigarette use and mitigate cardiopulmonary health issues among high-risk populations in the future.

Aerobic exercise alleviates allergic airway inflammation by suppressing circMETTL9-mediated formation of macrophage extracellular traps⋆
Authors: Haixia Wang, Ting Gao, Bin Ma, Yuanmin Jia, Hui Wei, Danyang Li, Junlian Gu, Ou Chen, Shouwei Yue
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Emerging evidence suggests that aerobic exercise exerts beneficial effects on asthma. Previous studies have demonstrated that cell communication can drive the formation of macrophage extracellular traps (METs). However, the potential of aerobic exercise to mediate communication between airway epithelial cells and macrophages, thereby influencing MET formation, remains unexplored. Our data reveal that the upregulation of circular RNA METTL9 (circMETTL9), derived from methyltransferase-like protein 9 (METTL9) in airway epithelial cells, promotes the formation of METs. Notably, aerobic exercise was found to downregulate the expression of circMETTL9, thereby facilitating communication between airway epithelial cells and macrophages and inhibiting METs formation. Mechanistically, circMETTL9 and insulin-like growth factor binding protein 3 (IGFBP3) compete for binding to the DEXDc domain of eukaryotic translation initiation factor 4A3 (EIF4A3), which regulates METs via the C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 12(CXCL12) -C-X-C chemokine receptor type 4(CXCR4) signaling axis. This study provides robust molecular evidence supporting aerobic exercise as a foundational pulmonary rehabilitation strategy for lung protection in asthma. Targeting circMETTL9, mimicking this exercise-mediated pathway, represents a promising therapeutic approach. These insights offer a direct mechanistic rationale for refining exercise-based rehabilitation protocols and developing novel targeted therapies.

Introduction

Asthma is one of the most prevalent respiratory diseases, affecting an estimated 300 million individuals globally, including over 30 million in China [1,2]. Current therapeutic strategies primarily rely on pharmacological interventions, which can be effective but may lead to significant adverse side effects, such as osteoporosis [3]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to identify scalable and cost-effective adjunctive therapies for pulmonary rehabilitation to improve clinical outcomes. The Global Initiative for Asthma (GINA) endorses aerobic exercise as a component of asthma management [4]. However, exercise can also trigger asthma symptoms, particularly exercise-induced bronchoconstriction, which may diminish an individual's tolerance for physical activity and their willingness to engage in aerobic exercises. Despite these challenges, aerobic exercise is believed to provide benefits in asthma management by modulating inflammatory and immune responses [[5], [6], [7]]. However, the precise mechanisms underlying these effects remain incompletely understood, highlighting the necessity for further research to elucidate the regulatory pathways through which aerobic exercise can improve asthma outcomes.

Normal communication between airway epithelial cells and immune cells is crucial for maintaining lung homeostasis [8]. When stimulated by environmental factors or allergens, airway epithelial cells become damaged, which prompts the recruitment of a significant number of macrophages to the surrounding airways [9]. As the most abundant immune cells in the lungs, macrophages play crucial roles in coordinating various responses essential for homeostasis and defense against pathogens. They contribute to preserving both the integrity and responsiveness of the airway epithelium [10]. Recent investigations have shown that macrophages can produce extracellular traps. Macrophage extracellular traps (METs) represent a novel mechanism of macrophage immunity, functioning by releasing a DNA meshwork containing myeloperoxidase (MPO), neutrophil elastase (NE), and citrullinated histone H3 (CitH3), among other components [11]. METs are implicated in tissue damage, inflammatory progression, and autoimmune diseases [12]; however, evidence regarding their role in asthma remains limited. The importance of intercellular communication between airway epithelial cells and macrophages in asthma pathogenesis, as well as the potential mediating role of aerobic exercise in this process, warrant further investigation.

Circular RNAs (circRNAs), which are formed through back splicing and lack both a 5′ cap and a 3′ poly(A) tail, have attracted considerable attention due to their high stability, abundance, and conservation [13]. CircRNAs function as multifunctional regulators in cellular processes—including but not limited to acting as miRNA sponges [14], protein scaffolds/decoys [15], translators of functional peptides [16], and modulators of transcriptional programs [17]—with spatiotemporal specificity that critically influences physiological homeostasis and disease pathogenesis [18]. Emerging evidence indicates that circRNA expression in the airways is implicated in asthma pathogenesis, with specific circRNAs that regulate the proliferation, migration, and inflammation of airway smooth muscle and bronchial epithelial cells [19]. Aerobic exercise has been demonstrated to positively impact circRNA expression in cardiovascular, neurological, and diabetic diseases [[20], [21], [22]]. However, the gap in the current study is whether aerobic exercise can regulate circRNA to inhibit METs formation to alleviate allergic airway inflammation. Thus, this study innovatively explores whether aerobic exercise can mitigate allergic airway inflammation by mediating the expression of circMETTL9 to inhibit MET formation, presenting a novel perspective in asthma research.

In this study, we firstly clarify the impact of aerobic exercise on MET formation by mediating communication between airway epithelial cells and macrophages, which represents a novel discovery in asthma research. We identified a circRNA derived from methyltransferase-like protein 9 (METTL9) in airway epithelial cells. Mechanistically, we demonstrate that circMETTL9 and insulin-like growth factor binding protein 3 (IGFBP3) compete to bind to the DEXDc domain of eukaryotic translation initiation factor 4A3 (EIF4A3). The circMETTL9/IGFBP3 pathway in airway epithelial cells activates the CXCL12-CXCR4 axis, thereby driving MET formation. This study provides new insights into pulmonary rehabilitation interventions and targeted therapies for asthma.

Materials and methods

Animals

The male C57BL/6J mice utilized in this study were six weeks old, weighed 22 ± 2 g, and were maintained under standard laboratory conditions. These conditions included a regulated 12-h light/dark cycle, a controlled temperature maintained at 22 ± 1 °C, and a relative humidity ranging from 40 % to 60 %. All animal experiments were conducted in the animal laboratory of Shandong University. The animal experiments conducted within this study adhered strictly to the guidelines established by the National Research Council for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals in Biomedical Research. Additionally, the experimental protocol received approval from the Ethical Committee of Shandong University's School of Nursing and Rehabilitation (approval number: 2021-D-01).

Chronic allergic pulmonary inflammation model

The mice were allocated into four distinct groups, each consisting of six animals: the control group (C), the asthma group (A), the exercise group (E), and the asthma + exercise group (AE). The A group and AE group were administered aluminum hydroxide-diluted OVA at a dose of 10 μg per mouse via intraperitoneal injection on days 0, 14, 28, and 42, following a previously established protocol [23]. From day 21 to day 54, the mice in these groups were exposed to a 1 % OVA aerosol for 30 min, three times per week. In contrast, the mice in the C group and the E group were exposed to saline under the same conditions. Mice are anesthetized with 1 % pentobarbital sodium via intraperitoneal injection. Euthanasia is performed by tail vein injection of a 3 % pentobarbital sodium solution.

Protocol for aerobic exercise

From days 21–23, the mice underwent treadmill exercise for 15 min at a speed of 0.2 km/h. On days 25 and 53, a maximal exercise test was performed, initiating with a 5-min warm-up at 0.2 km/h. The treadmill speed was subsequently incrementally increased by 0.1 km/h every 2.5 min until the mice reached exhaustion, which was defined as the inability to continue running despite the application of 10 mechanical stimuli. Between days 28 and 52, the mice underwent treadmill training for 4 weeks. During this training, the mice ran at a speed equivalent to 50 % or 75 % of their maximum exercise capacity for 60 min, five times per week, as previously described [5,23,24].

Isolation of bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF)

BALFs were isolated following previously described procedures [25]. Twenty-four hours after exposure to the final OVA or saline solution, the mice were anesthetized through the intraperitoneal administration of 1 % pentobarbital (0.2 mL/mice). Subsequently, a left bronchoalveolar lavage was conducted after the right lung was ligated. The specific methodology involved exposing the trachea at the neck, making a T-shaped incision in the epiglottic cartilage, inserting a modified 18G endotracheal tube, and then inhaling and collecting PBS through repeated lavage using a 1 mL syringe (0.4 mL per lavage, repeated three times) to ensure a recovery rate exceeding 80 % in each instance. The collected lavage fluid was stored in 1.5 mL Eppendorf tubes and kept on ice for subsequent supernatant preparation.

RNA sequencing

Shanghai Biotechnology Corporation conducted the RNA microarray analysis. Total RNA was extracted from the lung tissues of three OVA-induced mice and three healthy control mice via the RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen). For RNA-seq, strand-specific libraries were prepared via the TruSeq Stranded Total RNA Sample Preparation Kit (Illumina). the samples were quantified via a Qubit 2.0 fluorometer (Life Technologies), and the insert size was verified via a 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent). Following the dilution of the library to 10 pM, clustering was achieved via cBot, and sequencing was conducted on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. Shanghai Biotechnology Corporation was responsible for both library construction and sequencing.

Histology and cell staining

The lungs of the mice were fixed in 10 % formalin, dehydrated, embedded, and sectioned. The formation of METs was detected via SYTOX Green (catalog #S7020, Invitrogen, USA). Immunofluorescence staining was carried out with antibodies against EIF4A3 (1:200, catalog #17504-1-AP, Proteintech, Chicago, USA), CitH3 (1:200, catalog #A18298, ABclonal, China), MPO (1:100, catalog #EM1901-19, HUABIO, China), and Cluster of Differentiation 68 (CD68) (1:200, catalog #DF7518, Affinity, China), as previously described [25]. The stained sections or cells were observed under a light or fluorescence microscope (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan), and the results were quantified via ImageJ software.

Cell culture and cell lines

The BEAS-2B cells were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA). The cells were cultured in Dulbecco's modified Eagle's medium (DMEM) (Macgene, Beijing, China), supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum (Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin. Each cell line was maintained at 37 °C in a 5 % CO2 humidified chamber. These cells were treated with IL-13 (100 ng/mL, Novoprotein, China).

siRNA, plasmid construction, and cell transfection

To investigate the functional roles of the target genes, siRNA constructs and overexpression plasmids were developed. Specifically, siRNAs targeting circMETTL9, EIF4A3, and IGFBP3 were acquired from Generalbiol (Chuzhou, China). The overexpression plasmid for circMETTL9 was sourced from GenePharma (Shanghai, China), whereas those for IGFBP3 and EIF4A3 were obtained from Generalbiol (Chuzhou, China). Additionally, FLAG-tagged gene expression vectors encoding full-length EIF4A3 and targeted mutants (DEXDc and HELICc) were provided by Generalbiol (Chuzhou, China). The cells were transfected with the siRNAs using the Lipofectamine 2000 kit (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA), following the manufacturer's instructions. A comprehensive list of all the sequences used in this study is provided in Table S1 of the Additional file.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs)

To assess the expression levels of CXCL12, ELISA kits specifically for mouse CXCL12 (catalog #KE10049, Proteintech, Chicago, USA), and for human CXCL12 ELISA Kit (catalog #RK00266, ABclonal, China) were utilized according to the manufacturer's protocols.

RNase R treatment

Total RNA was analyzed by RT-qPCR after incubation with 2 U/μg RNase R (catalog #R0301, Geneseed, China) for 0, 10, 20, 30, and 40 min at 37 °C.

Actinomycin D assays

BEAS-2B cells were plated in six-well plates and exposed to 2 μg/mL actinomycin D (catalog #50-76-0, Merck, Germany). The cells were collected at specified time points, and the stability of circMETTL9 and METTL9 was analyzed via RT-qPCR.

Nuclear-cytoplasmic fractionation

Nuclear and cytoplasmic RNA were extracted from BEAS-2B cells via the FastPure Cytoplasmic Nuclear RNA Purification Kit (catalog #EK-1315-50T, ECOTOP SCIENTIFIC, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions. RT-qPCR was performed to quantify the expression of circMETTL9, U6, and GAPDH.

RNA FISH immunofluorescence microscopy

BEAS-2B cells were fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde for 15 min, treated with pepsin (1 % in 10 mM HCl), and rinsed with PBS. The cells were dried and incubated with a 20 nM fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) probe in a hybridization buffer at 73 °C for 5 min to denature them. Hybridization was carried out overnight at 37 °C. Before imaging, the slides were cleaned, dried, and stained with DAPI (catalog #F32202/50, RNA FISH SA-Biotin, GenePharma, China). The RNA FISH probe was designed and synthesized by GenePharma (Shanghai, China). The sequences are listed in Table S2 of the Additional file.

RNA pull-down assays

The probe, designed to target the cyclization site of circMETTL9 with a reverse complementary sequence, was synthesized with biotin labeling and LNA modifications by GenePharma (Shanghai, China). This same probe was used for both RNA-FISH and RNA pull-down experiments. For the RNA pull-down assays, the biotin-labeled circMETTL9 probe was incubated with lysed Beas-2B cells. The cell lysates were then incubated with streptavidin-conjugated agarose magnetic beads (Cat#20164, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) at standard temperature. Proteins that interacted with the probe were ultimately identified by Western blot analysis.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays

A Magna RIP RNA-Binding Protein Immunoprecipitation Kit (catalog # 17–700, 17–701, and 17–704, Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA) was used to perform RIP assays. Approximately 1 × 10^7 cells were lysed in RIP lysis buffer and stored at −80 °C. Specific antibodies were incubated with magnetic beads for 30 min at room temperature with rotation. The cell lysates were quickly thawed and centrifuged at 14,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C, after which the supernatants were and supernatant was immunoprecipitated with antibody-coated beads overnight at 4 °C. The beads were washed six times, and the coprecipitated RNAs were purified. qRT-PCR was performed to detect the enrichment of circMETTL9 in different groups.

RT-qPCR

As previously described, RT-qPCR was employed to assess gene expression [26]. Briefly, total RNA was extracted via the TRIZOL reagent (catalog #15596026, Invitrogen, USA). Subsequently, A HiFiScript cDNA Synthesis Kit (catalog #CW2569M, Cwbio, China) was used to transcribe the extracted RNA into cDNA. RT-qPCR was conducted via the UltraSYBR One-Step RT-qPCR Kit (catalog #CW0659S, Cwbio, China) on an RT-qPCR detection system (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA), with cDNA serving as the template. The initial mRNA concentrations were normalized to those of GAPDH as a control. All primers were obtained from Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China. The expression levels of the target genes were calculated using the 2^-ΔΔCT method. The primer sequences are provided in Table S3, an Additional file.

Western blot analysis

For Western blot analysis, protease and phosphatase inhibitors were added to RIPA lysis buffer (catalog #P0013B, Beyotime Biotechnology, China) to homogenize the lung tissues and BEAS-2B cells. Next, tissue or cell proteins were extracted through centrifugation at 4 °C for 25 min at 12,000 rpm. The proteins were then electrophoresed and transferred to a polyvinylidene difluoride membrane (catalog #24937-79-9, Millipore, MA, USA) after being mixed with loading buffer and heated to 95 °C for 5 min. The membranes were blocked with 5 % nonfat milk for 1 h and subsequently incubated with primary antibodies against FLAG, IGFBP3, and EIF4A3 (Proteintech, China) overnight at 4 °C. The immunoreactive bands were visualized via an enhanced chemiluminescence detection kit (catalog #BL520A, Biosharp, China) and identified with a horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody (1:2000, Proteintech, China). Densitometric analysis was performed via Tanon Image Quant 4.2 software (Shanghai, China).

In vitro transwell coculture

Transwell cell coculture chambers (Corning, 0.4 μm) were used to cultivate THP-1 cells and BEAS-2B cells. Specifically, THP-1 cells were plated in the upper chamber and differentiated into macrophages by stimulation with 100 ng/mL phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (PMA) for 48 h (catalog #16561-29-8, MedChemExpress, USA). Simultaneously, BEAS-2B cells were plated in the lower chamber and stimulated with 100 ng/mL IL-13 for 48 h. Next, the cells were cocultured for an additional 24 h, with the upper chamber containing fresh 1640 complete medium and the lower chamber containing fresh complete DMEM.

Adeno-associated virus (AAV)

To investigate the in vivo effect of circMETTL9, AAV lung vectors mediating circMETTL9 overexpression were constructed by Obio Technology, Shanghai. These vectors specifically target airway epithelial cells and increase the expression level of circMETTL9 in these cells. The AAV vectors were administered via nasal drip at a dose of 1E+11 vg per mouse.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted via GraphPad Software version 8. All the data are presented as the means accompanied by their respective SDs. The normality of the distribution of the data was assessed via the Shapiro-Wilk test. To compare the differences among various groups, a one-way ANOVA was used. In instances where comparisons were made between two groups, either the independent sample t-test was applied, depending on the data characteristics. Furthermore, to assess the linear relationship between circMETTL9 and IGFBP3, Pearson correlation analysis was performed. Significance was determined at the level of P < 0.05, whereas P > 0.05 was considered not statistically significant.

Results

Aerobic exercise reduces METs in OVA-Induced Chronic Allergic Airway Inflammation

To investigate the beneficial effects of aerobic exercise on chronic allergic airway inflammation, we established a mouse model of allergic airway inflammation through aerobic exercise intervention (Fig. 1A). The co-immunofluorescence assay was used to detect markers of METs (CitH3 and MPO) and a macrophage marker (CD68). The results indicated substantial recruitment of macrophages around the airways and a significant increase in MET markers compared with the control group. Interestingly, aerobic exercise was found to reduce macrophage infiltration and decrease the formation of METs (Fig. 1B). Western blot analysis of CitH3 and MPO protein expression in lung tissues confirmed these findings (Fig. 1C). Subsequently, we verified the back-splice junction site and the stability of circMETTL9. circMETTL9 was back-spliced from exons 2, 3, and 4 of the METTL9 gene, spanning 586 nucleotides in circPrimer 2.0. Sanger sequencing of the RT-PCR product confirmed the formation of the back-splice junction, with the junction site located between the G and T bases (Fig. 2D). In this study, we designed two sets of primers to characterize circMETTL9: divergent primers for amplifying circular transcripts and convergent primers for detecting linear transcripts. The divergent and convergent primers we designed for circMETTL9 are illustrated in Fig. S1. The results indicated that circMETTL9 could only be amplified from complementary DNA (cDNA), while its linear form could be amplified from both genomic DNA (gDNA) and cDNA (Fig. 2E). To assess the stability of circMETTL9 in comparison to METTL9, we conducted an RNase R degradation assay. As illustrated in Fig. 2F, the linear transcripts of METTL9 were rapidly degraded following RNase R treatment, while circMETTL9 exhibited resistance to degradation for up to 40 min. This finding was further corroborated by experiments involving actinomycin D treatment (Fig. 2G).

The function of circRNAs is closely linked to their intracellular localization. We demonstrated that circMETTL9 is primarily localized in the cytoplasm of BEAS-2B cells (Fig. 2H and I). RNA FISH analysis further revealed that circMETTL9 is expressed in airway epithelial cells and that aerobic exercise reverses the OVA-induced increase in circMETTL9 expression (Fig. 2J and K).

Taken together, circMETTL9, identified through bioinformatics methods, is regarded as a key circRNA that may mediate the beneficial effects of aerobic exercise in asthma.

To explore the effect of circMETTL9 in airway epithelial cells on METs, we conducted loss-of-function and gain-of-function experiments by transfecting BEAS-2B cells with siRNAs or overexpression plasmids targeting circMETTL9 (Fig. S2B). Immunofluorescence staining revealed that overexpression of circMETTL9 in BEAS-2B cells drove METs, whereas its knockdown inhibited MET formation (Fig. 3D). Western blot analysis confirmed these findings, showing that overexpression of circMETTL9 in BEAS-2B cells increased the protein expression of CitH3 and MPO in macrophages, while knockdown of circMETTL9 significantly inhibited these markers (Fig. 3E). These results collectively demonstrate that circMETTL9 in airway epithelial cells plays a critical role in driving METs formation.

We focused on the genes whose expression was downregulated in the OVA group, selecting IGFBP3, which exhibited the most significant differential expression among the down-regulated genes. Previous studies have also reported that IGFBP3 is involved in allergic airway inflammation [28]. To determine whether aerobic exercise regulates IGFBP3 expression, we assessed IGFBP3 expression in lung tissue through Western blotting and RT-qPCR. Our data showed that IGFBP3 levels were significantly decreased in Group A compared to the control group, while IGFBP3 levels were markedly increased in the AE group compared to Group A (Fig. 4C). To explore the regulatory role of circMETTL9 in IGFBP3 expression, we performed loss - and gain-of-function experiments, utilizing Western blot and RT-qPCR to measure IGFBP3 mRNA and protein levels. We found that both mRNA and protein expression levels of IGFBP3 were significantly upregulated compared to the control group. Conversely, when circMETTL9 was overexpressed, the expression levels of IGFBP3 were markedly downregulated at both the mRNA and protein levels compared to the control group (Fig. 4D). Our results demonstrate that circMETTL9 negatively regulates IGFBP3 expression at both the mRNA and protein levels.

To investigate whether IGFBP3 in airway epithelial cells can drive METs, we established a co-culture system involving airway epithelial cells and macrophages. Initially, we transfected BEAS-2B cells with siRNAs or overexpression plasmids targeting IGFBP3 (Fig. S2C–D). Overexpression of IGFBP3 in BEAS-2B cells resulted in a decrease in CitH3 and MPO expression in macrophages compared to the control group. Conversely, the knockdown of IGFBP3 in BEAS-2B cells had the opposite effect (Fig. 4E). To further demonstrate that the circMETTL9/IGFBP3 pathway in airway epithelial cells can drive METs, functional rescue assays were conducted. The results from Western blot and immunofluorescence were consistent. Overexpression of IGFBP3 inhibits METs, but this inhibition is reversed by overexpression of circMETTL9 (Fig. 4F and G).

Collectively, our findings suggest that IGFBP3 mediates aerobic exercise's positive effects on asthma. Additionally, IGFBP3, as a downstream target gene of circMETTL9, regulates the formation process of METs.

The CXCL12-CXCR4 axis mediates communication between airway epithelial cells and macrophages

Macrophage-derived chemokines serve as critical mediators of cellular crosstalk between airway epithelial cells and macrophages, particularly through their chemotactic recruitment function [29]. To systematically explore potential chemokine candidates driving this interaction, we conducted a comprehensive literature review and identified seven key macrophage chemokines: CCL1, CCL4, CCL5, CCL17, CXCL12, CXCL13, and CXCL14 [30,31]. Notably, subsequent network co-expression analysis using the STRING database revealed a direct association between CXCL12 and IGFBP3 (Fig. 5A), suggesting their potential synergistic role in intercellular communication. Given that ligand-receptor interactions constitute a central mechanism for cell-to-cell signaling [32], we further employed the CellphoneDB database to predict binding partners of CXCL12. This analysis highlighted the specific binding of CXCL12 to the CXCR4 receptor (Fig. 5B), a pathway of particular interest due to its established involvement in asthma pathogenesis [33]. First, we directly stimulated macrophages with CXCL12 and observed the formation of METs. The results of SYTOX Green staining showed that stimulation of macrophages with 100 ng/mL CXCL12 for 24 h significantly increased METs compared to the control group (Fig. 5C). Furthermore, Western blot results indicated that CXCL12 significantly increased the protein expression of CitH3 and MPO compared to the control group (Fig. 5D). Next, loss-of-function and gain-of-function experiments demonstrated that IGFBP3 negatively regulates the expression of both CXCL12 and CXCR4. ELISA and RT-qPCR analyses consistently revealed that knockdown of IGFBP3 in BEAS-2B cells markedly increased CXCL12 levels in the supernatant and upregulated CXCL12 expression in BEAS-2B cells, while simultaneously enhancing CXCR4 expression in cocultured macrophages. Conversely, IGFBP3 overexpression in BEAS-2B cells suppressed their expression (Fig. 5E). Subsequently, functional rescue experiments confirmed that the circMETTL9/IGFBP3 pathway activates the CXCL12-CXCR4 signaling axis. Importantly, the activating effect of IGFBP3 knockdown on the CXCL12-CXCR4 axis was rescued by simultaneous circMETTL9 knockdown (Fig. 5F). Similarly, the inhibitory effect of IGFBP3 overexpression was reversed by circMETTL9 overexpression (Fig. 5G).

These data provide direct evidence that IGFBP3 serves as a target gene of circMETTL9 to regulate the CXCL12-CXCR4 axis, thereby driving METs.

Next, we further identify the potential physical binding between circMETTL9, IGFBP3, and EIF4A3. We observed that circMETTL9 and IGFBP3 co-localize with EIF4A3 in the cytoplasm of BEAS-2B cells (Fig. 6H). As shown in the results of RIP assays, enrichment of circMETTL9 and IGFBP3 in the anti-EIF4A3 group compared with the anti-immunoglobulin G (IgG) group (Fig. 6I). The overexpression of circMETTL9 significantly reduced IGFBP3 enrichment via EIF4A3, while the knockdown of circMETTL9 had the opposite effect (Fig. 6J). RNA pull-down silver staining revealed bands at 40–55 kDa, suggesting that EIF4A3 binds to circMETTL9 (Fig. 6K). The binding of circMETTL9 and EIF4A3 was demonstrated by RNA pull-down and western blotting (Fig. 6L). These data not only demonstrate that circMETTL9 and IGFBP3 bind directly to EIF4A3 but also suggest that circMETTL9 and IGFBP3 competitively bind to EIF4A3.

To pinpoint the functional domains of EIF4A3, the protein structural domain of EIF4A3 was obtained from the STRING database, revealing that it consists of the DEXDc and HELICc domains. We created three FLAG-tagged vectors to determine which domain interacts with circMETTL9 or IGFBP3 (Fig. 6M). CircMETTL9 primarily binds to the DEXDc domain as shown in the RNA pull-down assay (Fig. 6N). The consistent result was further validated by RIP-qPCR (Fig. 6O). Similarly, RIP-qPCR revealed that IGFBP3 also binds primarily to the DEXDc domain of EIF4A3 (Fig. 6P).

Taken together, these data strongly suggest that circMETTL9 and IGFBP3 competitively bind to the DEXDc domain of EIF4A3. Increased binding of circMETTL9 to EIF4A3 inhibited the effect of EIF4A3 on IGFBP3 transcription and translation.

Discussion

Aerobic exercise is widely recognized for its beneficial effects on patients with respiratory diseases, as it improves respiratory function and enhances quality of life [35,36]. However, there are limited studies investigating how circRNAs mediate the beneficial effects of aerobic exercise on allergic airway inflammation. To our knowledge, this is the first study to elucidate the mechanisms by which aerobic exercise mediates cellular communication between airway epithelial cells and macrophages through the circMETTL9/IGFBP3/CXCL12/CXCR4 pathway, reducing MET formation. Our results provide compelling evidence that aerobic exercise serves as a crucial adjunct intervention for pulmonary rehabilitation in asthma.

Previous studies have primarily focused on macrophage polarization rather than the role of METs in the pathogenesis of asthma [37]. Notably, this study is the first to demonstrate that macrophages recruited to allergic airways release a substantial amount of extracellular traps. Interestingly, aerobic exercise inhibits the formation of METs. METs represent an immune response involving the release of extracellular DNA fibers by macrophages, which serve to immobilize and eliminate certain microorganisms [38]. Research has revealed that aberrant METs play a crucial role in respiratory disease development [39]. METs, for instance, can intensify neutrophilic airway inflammation in severe asthma cases [40]. However, existing findings are inconsistent. A study indicates METs rise during acute asthma attacks and decline in chronic, severe cases [41]. Thus, further research is needed to clarify METs' role in asthma pathogenesis.

With the rapid advancement of next-generation sequencing and bioinformatics technologies, circRNAs implicated in diverse biological processes have been identified [42]. Emerging data suggest that circRNA is involved in the proliferation, migration, and inflammation of airway smooth muscle and bronchial epithelial cells in asthma [43,44]. However, the role of circMETTL9 derived from METTL9 in the pathogenesis and progression of asthma has not been fully explored. In this study, circMETTL9 was found to be significantly upregulated in asthma. It has been fully demonstrated that circMETTL9 promotes the proliferation, apoptosis, and oxidative stress of airway epithelial cells in vivo and in vitro. Previous studies did not investigate the role of circMETTL9 in asthma. Only two studies have explored the proinflammatory and proliferative effect of circMETTL9 in traumatic brain injury and colorectal cancer [45,46], which was in line with our findings. Notably, aerobic exercise reduced the expression of circMETTL9, which in turn alleviated the pathological features of asthma. This has not been found in previous studies. This study fully explored the beneficial effect of circMETTL9 in airway epithelial cells in mediating aerobic exercise, and future studies can target circMETTL9 for drug development to achieve the effect of asthma treatment.

Previous studies have mainly focused on the function of circRNAs as miRNA sponges, and the mechanism of interaction between circRNAs in the cytoplasm and RBPs located on introns upstream or downstream of coding regions has been widely concerned by recent studies [47]. However, there is a lack of studies to analyze the interaction mechanism between circRNA and RBP in asthma. The results of our study demonstrate for the first time that circMETTL9 and IGFBP3 competitively bind to the DEXDc domain of EIF4A3. EIF4A3 was ATP-dependent RNA helicase and involved in pre-mRNA splicing as the component of the spliceosome [48]. EIF4A3 was found to be involved in a variety of post-transcriptional events, including mRNA transport and translation [49], which was also well confirmed in this study. EIF4A3 was found to increase IGFBP3 mRNA and protein expression in the present study. IGFBP3 is a significant circulating species that forms a large ternary complex with IGF, an acid-labile subunit, and 75–90 % of circulating IGF-I [50]. IGFBP3 is broadly implicated in controlling the division, growth, and proliferation of various cell types. Previous studies have shown that IGFBP3 can reduce airway remodeling and suppress lung inflammation and hyperresponsiveness in asthmatic mouse models [28,51].

Cell-cell communication is pivotal in the initiation and progression of diseases [52]. In this study, the circMETTL9/IGFBP3 pathway in airway epithelial cells activates the CXCL12-CXCR4 signaling axis to mediate communication with macrophages and drive METs in vivo and in vitro. Previous studies have found that the pathways implicated in MET induction include the activation of NADPH-oxidase, peptidyl arginine deiminase (PAD), and early secreted antigenic target-6 (ESAT-6) [[53], [54], [55]]. Interestingly, our study innovatively discovered that the binding of receptors and ligands would trigger METs. Sun et al. reported that blocking the CXCL12-CXCR4 axis inhibited neutrophil recruitment and neutrophil extracellular traps [56]. These findings suggest that the induction of extracellular traps in different immune cells may occur through similar pathways.

Among the limitations of our study, exercise is a prevalent predisposing factor for asthma, prompting the selection of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise. Consequently, the findings of our study are confined specifically to moderate-intensity aerobic exercise. Additionally, the study exclusively involved male mice, necessitating further research to validate these results in female animals. Finally, Cell-to-cell communication is typically bidirectional. However, our study only revealed that airway epithelial cells drive METs. Future studies can delve deeper into the impact of METs on the function of airway epithelial cells.

Conclusions

This study is the first to suggest that aerobic exercise through regulating the circMETTL9/EIF4A3/IGFBP3/CXCL12/CXCR4 pathway reducing macrophage aggregation and METs. Notably, the CXCL12-CXCR4 axis was identified as a mediator of communication between airway epithelial cells and macrophages, driving MET formation. These results indicate that aerobic exercise plays a pivotal positive role in asthma and identifies circMETTL9 as a crucial therapeutic target, providing a significant molecular foundation for future research endeavors.

Numerical study of local meshless collocation method in one-dimensional convection and diffusion problems
Authors: Kehong Zheng, Chunbo Xing, Yingying Lan, Enran Hou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This paper proposes a local meshless collocation method based on global radial basis function (G-RBF) for one-dimensional convection-diffusion equations. The method transforms the equation into a steady-state form through time forward Euler discretization and uses local G-RBF interpolation with only three adjacent collocation points for spatial approximation. Numerical experiments have shown that this method has significant advantages in solving problems with different Péclet numbers. It not only has high accuracy, but also exhibits stable performance against numerical perturbations. It can effectively alleviate undesired numerical oscillations, notably reduce the condition number of discrete equations and cut down computational overhead, delivering a numerically reliable tool with competitive computational resource utilization for high-precision simulation of complex transport processes.

1. Introduction

The convection-diffusion equation is widely applied in fields such as environmental science, energy development, fluid mechanics, chemical engineering, and biomedical engineering, describing the transport process of matter or energy within fluids.1,2 This equation combines two mechanisms: convection and diffusion, enabling it to accurately depict complex physical phenomena such as pollutant dispersion, heat transfer, and drug distribution. 3

The mathematical characteristics of the convection-diffusion equation are determined by the dimensionless Péclet number. 4 When the Péclet number is small, diffusion dominates, and the equation exhibits a parabolic form. When the Péclet number is large, convection dominates, the equation is prone to numerical oscillations, necessitating the use of special numerical methods such as the characteristic method and streamline diffusion method for solution. In the field of numerical computation, various methods have their own strengths. The finite element method achieves a balance between ease of operation and computational efficiency with its classic and mature algorithmic logic. 5 The finite difference method takes its outstanding conservation properties as its core advantage, while its scheme system possesses good compatibility. 6 The finite volume method also exhibits excellent conservation performance, and its scheme can be compatible and adaptable with the finite difference method. 7 The meshless method breaks free from the constraints of traditional grids, demonstrating outstanding adaptability to large deformation scenarios; the spectral method is characterized by high-precision solution, performing exceptionally well in dealing with smooth solution problems.8–10

In scientific research fields such as predicting the movement of pollution plumes and assessing the time required for water quality to meet standards, the advection diffusion equation plays a vital role. However, it is virtually impossible to obtain an exact analytical solution that varies with time in various practical transport problems.11,12 The vast majority of researchers often turn to numerical methods as an alternative to analytical solutions for the convection-diffusion equation. Currently, a variety of numerical methods have been developed and are used to solve such equations in various fields.13,14 Bai 15 has proposed a novel grid-mesh duality principle for the development of new numerical algorithms. Aatish 16 has proposed a meshfree radial point interpolation method for simulating pollutant transport under rate-limited adsorption conditions. Swetha 17 has developed a weak form meshless method known as the Local Radial Point Interpolation Method, which utilizes radial basis functions (RBF) for approximation or interpolation to solve groundwater flow problems in confined aquifers. Guo 18 has proposed a second order BDF and θ scheme for computing approximate solutions to Sobolev models, based on an efficient local mesh-free technique. Hossein 19 has proposed a novel mesh-free local integral equation numerical method for solving two-dimensional and three-dimensional Poisson equations. Soheil 20 has proposed a novel artificial diffusion method to circumvent the instabilities associated with standard finite element approximations of the convection-diffusion equation. Luiz 21 presented four typical cases involving transient (nonstationary) heat conduction equations and evaluated them. The meshless local Petrov Galerkin method was employed, and comparisons were made between different approximation functions: moving least squares, radial point interpolation and interpolated moving least squares. Semenov 22 discusses modelling methods for the Control Volume Finite Element Method for solving convection-diffusion equations on nonuniform triangular meshes and proposes a method for handling interfaces between nonuniform elements within the CVFEM framework. Liu et al. 23 proposed an RBF-PU method framework based on augmented polynomials for multi harmonic spline radial basis functions, which improves stability by introducing a super viscous term and solves convection diffusion equations on closed surfaces. Chou et al. 24 proposed the meshless approximate finite volume method (MAFVM), which relies on the locally tightly supported radial basis function differential meshless approximation technique and combines the classical upwind scheme and TVD method in unstructured grids. It can accurately solve high Peckley number convection diffusion problems on conventional or severely distorted grids. Saeb et al. 25 conducted research on the upwind moving least squares approximation method for convection dominated problems and applied it to the framework of a mixed discrete least squares meshless method.

This paper proposes a novel local mesh-free method for solving the convection-diffusion equation. The paper is organized as follows: Section 2 outlines the computational workflow for the convection-diffusion equation, including time discretization and the local G-RBF coordination method. Section 3 employs several numerical comparison examples to validate that the algorithm outperforms baseline methods and that the equation is suitable for the algorithm. Section 4 summarizes the main findings of this study.

2. Methodology

2.1. The convection-diffusion equation

The convection-diffusion equation is a mathematical model that describes how physical quantities (such as heat or concentration) are governed by both directed flow (convection) and random diffusion (diffusion) in one-dimensional space. It forms the basis for understanding complex transport phenomena. For the spatial variable x and the time variable t, the equation is (1) ∂c∂t=D∂2c∂x2−u∂c∂x+R where D∂2c∂x2 is the diffusion term, u∂c∂x is the convection term, R is the source-sink term, D is the diffusion coefficient, u is the convection velocity.

The convection-diffusion equation plays a crucial role in simulating river water quality and controlling pollution, as well as in addressing issues such as saltwater intrusion in estuaries and coastal areas. However, to determine whether convection or diffusion is more significant in a specific problem, researchers have introduced a new dimensionless number, i.e., the Péclet number ( Pe ). When Pe≫1 (very large), the effects of convection are the primary consideration. When Pe≪1 (very small), the effects of diffusion are the primary consideration. The definition of Pe is (2) Pe=uhD where h is the feature length.

Clearly, to find a numerical solution to (1), one must take into account both the initial conditions and the boundary conditions; the initial conditions impose constraints on the time dimension, whilst the boundary conditions impose constraints on the spatial domain, and together they ensure the well posedness of the equation.

The mathematical expression for the initial condition is straightforward (3) c(x,t=0)=c0(x),a≤x≤b and boundary conditions. (4) c(x,t)=c1(x,t),x={a,b},t>0

Here, c0(x) and c1(x,t) are prescribed smooth functions.

2.2. Time discretization

To facilitate subsequent analysis, an appropriate method must be employed to perform an equivalent transformation on Equation (1). Specifically, the time term is first discretized, thereby converting the original transient equation into a time independent steady state form. In this paper, a time-discretization scheme is selected that offers high computational efficiency, particularly in parallel computing schemes.

Denote ts=sΔt , where s=0,1,...,M and Δt=L/M is the time step. The total duration is in the range [0,L] . Using the forward Euler difference scheme for first-order time derivatives, the time derivative term on the left-hand side of Equation (1) can be discretized as26,27 (5) ∂c(x,t)∂t=c(x,ts+1)−c(x,ts)Δt+Ο(Δt)

By combining the above time discretization scheme with a local mesh-free method, the system of equations (6) derived from equations (1), (3) and (5) can be solved (6) cs+1−csΔt=D∂2cs+1∂x2−u∂cs+1∂x+R. where cs+1=c(x,ts+1) , cs=c(x,ts) .

2.3. The local G-RBF collocation method

Since Equation (6) falls within the category of parabolic partial differential equations, the interpolation method based on RBF can serve as an effective means for its numerical solution. This method is also one of the classic application paradigms within the RBF mesh free numerical methodology.

However, the systems of interpolation equations constructed by traditional RBF interpolation methods often suffer from excessively high condition numbers. This shortcoming not only reduces the stability of the numerical solution but also significantly increases the time complexity and computational cost of the overall process. To effectively alleviate this bottleneck, this section proposes to integrate a local interpolation strategy into the G-RBF interpolation method. Unlike global interpolation schemes, local interpolation methods require only a small number of neighboring configuration points around the interpolation point to complete the interpolation approximation. The resulting interpolation system exhibits significant sparsity, which effectively improves the behavior of the system of equations and reduces computational overhead.

To ensure the generality of the method, let us denote a set of interpolation points within the interval [a,b] as xi(i=1,2,...,N) . For any given central point xi , we need to select its nearest local neighbors xli(l=1,2,...,k) . In the one-dimensional case, the nearest-neighbor local template typically takes k=3 . This will be verified in the numerical examples. It addresses the high condition number pain point in traditional global G-RBF systems, maximizes matrix sparsity while retaining the high-precision advantage of radial basis functions. This setup fully leverages the sparsity of local interpolation schemes to effectively circumvent ill-conditioned systems, improving computational efficiency while highlighting the method’s simplicity and ease of implementation. The core meaning of ‘local’ here refers to each center point xi corresponding to a subset comprising k nearest-neighbor local points. These subsets overlap with one another and satisfy {xil}l=1k⊂{xi}i=1N .

For the local approximation at the central point xi∈{xil}l=1k , one can obtain the local approximation formulation by (7) c¯(xi)=∑l=1kλilG(xi,xil), where G(xi,xil)=e−ε2(xi−xil)2 is the G-RBF with shape parameter ε in Equation (7) and λil is the unknown coefficients.

From the above local approximation, one can obtain the following local interpolation system by collocation of all interpolation points within the interval [a,b] (8) c¯=(c¯(x1)c¯(x2)⋮c¯(xN))=(G1k0⋯00G2k⋯0⋯0⋱000⋯GNk)(λ1λ2⋮λN)=Gkλ, where Gik(i=1,2,...,N) is a local matrix obtained from Equation (7).

For Equation (6) in terms with boundary condition equation (4), a global interpolation method based on a local G-RBF interpolation scheme may be employed to perform a discrete approximation of the equation at each discrete node xj within the computational domain.

Specifically, Equation (6) can be numerically approximated by interpolation at each node xj (9) D∂2cs+1(xj)∂x2=cs+1(xj)−cs(xj)Δt+u∂cs+1(xj)∂x+R.

The boundary condition Equation (4) is performed at point xj (10) c(xj)=c1(xj).

Rewrite Equation (9) to (11) Lcs+1=f, where Lcs+1=DΔt∂2cs+1(xj)∂x2−uΔt∂cs+1(xj)∂x−cs+1(xj) and f=−cs(xj)+RΔt .

One can approximate Equation (11) using (12) Lcs+1(xj)=∑l=1kλlLG(xj,xl),j=1,2,...,k.

Similar to Equation (8), we have (13) Lcs+1=(Lcs+1(x1)Lcs+1(x2)⋮Lcs+1(xN))=(LG1k0⋯00LG2k⋯0⋯0⋱000⋯LGNk)(λ1λ2⋮λN)=LGkλ.

For boundary condition Equation (10), L is an identity operator. This is a global sparse system. 28 After obtaining coefficients λ=[LGk]−1f from Equations (11) and (13), one can calculate the general solution of the interest domain by using Equation (7).

3.4. Discussions

From the above-analysis, it can be seen that the proposed method is deeply coupled with the forward Euler time discretization scheme, and the parallel computing performance under this integrated framework is more outstanding. Without the need to introduce additional artificial diffusion terms, it can effectively suppress numerical oscillations in strong convection scenarios with Péclet numbers up to 2000.

Numerical experiments have shown that the average absolute error of this method in convection diffusion equilibrium scenarios is as low as 1.8e-7, which is one order of magnitude higher than the accuracy of existing control methods such as FD and DMM, while significantly reducing computational costs.

In subsequent research, we will further explore the applicability of the proposed method to more complex situations, including nonlinear problems, 35 high-dimensional systems, 36 and problems with high-sensitivity parameters. 37

4. Conclusions

The locally meshless collocation method based on G-RBF proposed in this paper has achieved superior performance in numerical simulations for solving one-dimensional convection-diffusion equations. This method first transforms the equation into a steady-state form through forward Euler discretization in time, and then approximates it spatially using only local G-RBF interpolation containing three adjacent configuration points. It avoids the problems of poor stability and high computational cost caused by the high number of system matrix conditions in traditional G-RBF methods. Numerical experiments show that this method exhibits high accuracy under different Péclet numbers, where its AAE is much lower than existing methods. In addition, this method effectively suppresses numerical oscillations and the system matrix has sparsity during the calculation process, significantly improving computational efficiency. In summary, the local meshless collocation method is an efficient, stable, and highly accurate numerical tool, suitable for high-precision simulation of complex transport processes.

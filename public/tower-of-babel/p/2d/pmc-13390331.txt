Simultaneous quantification of donepezil and memantine by HPLC–refractive index detection for novel drug delivery systems
Authors: Baljinder Singh, Souha H Youssef, A H M Musleh Uddin, Yunmei Song, Sanjay Garg
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAlzheimer’s dementia is often treated with a combination of memantine and donepezil, which is more effective than monotherapy. Despite its clinical relevance, simultaneous determination is challenging due to the absence of a chromophore in memantine. Herein, a simple, accurate, and sensitive High-Performance Liquid Chromatography–refractive index method is reported for the first time for the simultaneous quantification of memantine and donepezil for routine analysis. The method employed a C18 column with a mobile phase of 1% diethylamine in water: methanol (50:50, v/v), adjusted to pH 6.0 using dilute glacial acetic acid. Optimized conditions (flow rate 0.8 mL/min, injection volume 20 µL at 40 °C) yielded a linearity ranging between 2.5 and 30 µg/mL (R² = 1) for both the drugs. Limits of detection and quantification were 0.194 and 0.589 µg/mL for Dnp and 0.237 and 0.718 µg/mL for Mem. Validation confirmed precision, robustness, and stability. The method was successfully applied to determine the amount of drug release from the intranasal nano-colloidal preparations, along with optimized sample preparations giving analyte recoveries within range without any interference from the formulation excipients. The derivatization exhibited acceptable selectivity with no interfering peaks from excipients, demonstrating its suitability for routine analysis. Introduction

Dementia due to Alzheimer’s is the most common in elderly patients, accelerating with time [1]. The management of Alzheimer’s disease (AD) includes medications that can improve the symptoms and slow down the decline in cognitive functioning of the brain. The FDA-approved first-line therapy for AD is cholinesterase inhibitors such as donepezil, galantamine, and rivastigmine, and the N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) receptor blocker memantine [2].

The combination therapy of memantine (Mem) and donepezil (Dnp) is commonly prescribed in moderate to severe AD and has been proven to be effective [3, 4]. The current treatment for AD primarily involves the use of a combination of Mem and Dnp (more effective and superior to monotherapy [5]) in oral dosage forms. However, these oral formulations have gastrointestinal side effects, especially in elderly patients [6]. Therefore, intranasal formulations could provide an alternative route offering targeted brain delivery and other advantages, including rapid absorption, ease of administration, and enhanced bioavailability [7]. The intranasal drug carriers should allow for controlled drug release profiles and prolonged retention time at the mucosal site, which mucoadhesive-based hydrogels could achieve [8]. These bases undergo in-situ gelling via a phase transition triggered by physiological environments, such as pH and temperature.

Mem (3,5-dimethyl-1-aminoadamantane) is a small molecule shown in Fig. 1A that acts as an NMDA receptor antagonist by modulating glutamate levels, thereby reducing excessive neuronal excitability [9]. The absence of a chromophore in its structure results in less effective detection due to negligible ultraviolet (UV) absorbance; therefore, refractive index (RI) detectors [10] can be used in the HPLC analysis of memantine [11, 12]. Dnp (2-[(1-benzylpiperidin-4-yl)methyl]-5,6-dimethoxy-2,3-dihydroinden-1-one) is a piperidine derivative, as shown in Fig. 1B, that exerts its therapeutic effect by inhibiting the acetylcholinesterase (AChE) enzyme and preventing the breakdown of the neurotransmitter acetylcholine [13]. Unlike Mem, Dnp has a chromophore, which allows for detection with UV-based methods [14].

The chemical structure of memantine (A) [15] and donepezil (B) [16]

Several analytical methods have been reported in the literature for the quantification of Mem and Dnp, either individually or in combination. Analytical techniques such as HPLC–UV/DAD [14, 17, 18], LC-MS/MS [19–21] Fluorometric methods [22] have been reported for the estimation of Dnp, leveraging its inherent UV-absorbing chromophore. In contrast, Mem, which lacks a chromophore, has been quantified using different derivatizing agents such as 9-fluorenylmethyl chloroformate (FMOC) [12, 23], 1-fluoro-2,4-dinitrobenzene (FDNB) [24], 1-chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene [25], dansyl chloride [26, 27] derivatization-based UV methods [12, 28], or by HPLC-RID [11]. However, the derivatization adds complexity, time consumption, and variability. Although UV-based methods are suitable for Dnp, they are not reliable for simultaneous detection, including Mem, due to negligible UV absorbance. While LC-MS/MS offers high sensitivity and requires sophisticated instrumentation less suitable for routine simultaneous analysis of both the drugs.

Therefore, A simple, cost-effective, and derivatization-free method for the simultaneous estimation of both drugs was developed and validated according to ICH Q14 guidelines and validated as per ICH Q2(R1) [29]. This study addresses the analytical gap by presenting a direct and sensitive method with multiple advantages, including multi-analyte quantification, enhanced sensitivity, elimination of derivatization, short analysis time (20 min runtime), and low injection volume (20 µL). The developed method was used to determine Mem and Dnp contents in the formulated intranasal hydrogel and release samples. The greenness and applicability of the developed HPLC method were comprehensively evaluated using different tools, including Analytical GREEnness Metric Approach and Software (AGREE), Green Analytical Procedure Index (GAPI), and Blue Applicability Grade Index (BAGI).

Experimental

Materials

Memantine and donepezil were purchased from Combi Blocks USA. HPLC-grade methanol was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (NSW, Australia). Chitosan, Poloxamer 407, and Poloxamer 188 were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (Australia). Ultrapure Milli-Q water (18.2 mΩ) was obtained from a Millipore Milli-Q system (Sartorius, Germany). Diethylamine, glacial acetic acid, and absolute hydrochloric acid were purchased from Merck Ltd, Australia.

Instrumentation

An HPLC instrument (Shimadzu Corporation, Kyoto, Japan) equipped with a binary pump (LC-20AD), a degasser (DGU-20A3), an autosampler (SIL-20 A HT), and a Refractive index detector (RID-10 A) was utilized for the evaluation of Mem and Dnp. For interpretation and data collection, LabSolutions software (version 5.92) was used. A pH meter (Thermo Scientific, ORION STAR A111) was used for all pH measurements. LabSolutions software (version 5.132) was used for data processing and collection.

Chromatographic parameters

The mobile phase was composed of a 50:50 (v/v) mixture of methanol and ultrapure water containing 1% diethylamine, adjusted to pH 6.0 with 1 M glacial acetic acid. The flow rate was 0.8 mL/min, with a run time of 20 min, and an injection volume of 20 µL. The column used was a Kinetex 100 Å, C18 (250.0 × 4.6 mm; 5 μm), which was maintained at 40 °C. An RI detector was used for the analysis. The detector temperature was set at 40 °C with a sensitivity of 512. The sample and reference cells were purged with the mobile phase for 60 min each.

Preparation of standard solution

Accurately weighed amounts of both Mem and Dnp (10 mg) were transferred to a 10 mL volumetric flask containing 6 mL of the mobile phase as a diluent, and the mixture was sonicated for 10 min to obtain complete dissolution. The volume was then made up to 10 mL with the mobile phase. The stock solution was stored at 4°Ctill further use.

Method validation

Linearity and range

From the working stock solution containing both Mem and Dnp (1 mg/mL), further dilutions in a concentration range of 2.5 to 30 µg/mL were prepared using the mobile phase as a diluent. The prepared concentrations were analyzed using optimized HPLC-RID chromatographic conditions, and the obtained peak areas were plotted against the corresponding concentrations of each drug. The coefficient of determination (R2) and percentage recoveries were calculated.

Specificity

The specificity of the method was evaluated to confirm the absence of any co-eluting peaks with Dnp and Mem in in-vitro release media (Phosphate-Buffered Saline pH 7.4) and the mobile phase.

Precision and accuracy

Three standard solutions were prepared at three concentration levels: low (5 µg/mL), intermediate (15 µg/mL), and high (30 µg/mL), followed by injection into the HPLC system for analysis, and the percentage recoveries were determined (n = 6).

For precision, the same concentrations (5, 15, 30 µg/mL) were injected at different time points on the same day (intraday precision) and over three consecutive days (inter-day precision) for analysis. The relative SD was calculated for all data points.

Limit of detection (LOD) and limit of quantification (LOQ)

LOD is the lowest concentration of an analyte that can be detected but not necessarily quantified, and LOQ is the lowest concentration of an analyte that can be quantitatively detected with accuracy and precision. From the linearity concentration range (2.5–30 µg/mL), the standard deviation and slope were calculated using the LOD and LOQ equation from Shrivastava et al. [30].

Robustness

Method robustness was evaluated by slightly changing chromatographic conditions like temperature, pH, and mobile phase composition. The results were presented as % recoveries and relative standard deviation % (RSD).

Stability

To assess the stability of the drugs at different temperatures, a solution containing 30 µg/ml of both drugs was prepared and stored at refrigerated temp, room temperature, and 37 ˚C for 28 days, and samples were analyzed, and recovery was calculated.

Hydrogel formulation preparation and method application

Preparation of hydrogel

The poloxamer-chitosan-based hydrogel formulation shown in Table 1 was prepared using the cold method [31]. Firstly, the required amounts of poloxamer 407 and poloxamer 188 were added to pre-refrigerated triple-distilled water and kept overnight for complete dissolution and form a transparent solution. Separately, in another beaker, the weighed amount of chitosan (0.1% w/v) and the drug were added to the required amount of 1% acetic acid solution (v/v) and kept overnight at 4˚C. Finally, both the prepared solutions were mixed under continuous stirring to obtain a clear gel, which was stored in the refrigerator before use.

Drug content determination

Accurately 500 µL of the intranasal formulation was transferred into a volumetric flask and was added with mobile phase up to the 10 ml mark. The solution was sonicated for 15 min to ascertain complete dissolution of the formulation in the mobile phase, which was filtered through a syringe filter of 0.22 micron, and analyzed using the method.

Drug release studies in an in vitro model

Approximately 200 mg of the formulation was weighed into a presoaked dialysis bag, then properly sealed. The formulation containing the dialysis bag (MWCO 12,000 KDa) was placed into 50 ml PBS pH 7.4, placed in an incubator at 37 ˚C at 100 rpm for 9 h [32]. At different time points, 10 ml volume was replaced with fresh media, and the samples collected were analyzed using the developed HPLC method.

Results and discussion

Analytical method development

Mem and Dnp are used in combination for the management of AD, necessitating a combination detection method capable of analyzing both drugs simultaneously.

To develop an optimal chromatographic method for the simultaneous detection of both drugs, various mobile phase compositions and parameters were systematically evaluated. Given the relatively high pKa values of both drugs, mobile phases in a buffer pH range of 5.0–7.0, containing different ratios of methanol and diethylamine (DEA) buffer, were tested. The pH of the mobile phase plays a significant role in the protonation of basic drugs and leads to better peak resolution and less tailing in the pH range 5 to 7 (Supplementary Fig. 6–10). Furthermore, diethylamine (DEA) was incorporated in concentrations ranging from 0.5% to 1.0% (v/v) to improve peak shape and minimize tailing, particularly due to the basic nature of the analytes. Among the tested conditions, a mobile phase consisting of methanol and 1% diethylamine buffer in a 50:50 (v/v) ratio at pH 6.0 provided the best results in terms of peak resolution, symmetry, and baseline stability when using a refractive index detector. Additionally, different flow rates from 0.5 to 1.0 mL/min were evaluated. The optimization data is provided in the supplementary sheet. Final flow rate (0.8 mL/min) was found to offer the optimal balance between analysis time, peak shape, and resolution, as shown in Fig. 3, and was therefore selected for the final method. A C18 Kinetex column was used to achieve isocratic elution, with average retention times (RT) of 6.7 min for Dnp and 9.8 min for Mem. The chromatogram of the blank mobile phase has been provided in Supplementary Fig. 16.

Sustainable analytical performance profile of the developed method

The analytical method was developed with environmental impact in mind, aiming to replace and/or minimize the use of hazardous chemicals, reduce waste, and conserve energy, while maintaining analytical performance [33–35]. Unlike conventional studies that rely solely on greenness metrics, this work adopts a multidimensional sustainability assessment by integrating environmental impact (AGREE, GAPI) with method practicality and applicability (BAGI), providing a more comprehensive evaluation of analytical performance.

Methanol was used as the main solvent, in addition to water, for drug extraction from intranasal formulations, and the mobile phase, glacial acetic acid (pH adjustment), and dimethylamine (1% of the mobile phase) were also used in minimal quantities to optimise peak resolution and system suitability parameters. Based on the GlaxoSmithKline solvent guide, methanol and acetic acid received environmental impact scores of 9 (green) and 8 (green), out of 10, respectively, with water (10) representing the benchmark for the least environmental impact [36]. The use of non-green diethylamine was inevitable; however, only the smallest volume was employed, thereby limiting its environmental burden.

Descriptive evaluations of analytical method greenness, while informative, remain subjective and often insufficient for comparison across methodologies. Consequently, several quantitative greenness assessment tools have been introduced to provide structured, criterion-based evaluation frameworks. Many of these tools provide numerical scores and colour-coded charts, which facilitate thorough evaluation and easily highlight strengths and weaknesses of the assessed methods [37, 38]. In the present study, the greenness was assessed by AGREE and GAPI [39] tools, and the practicality of the developed method was evaluated using BAGI [40] tools. Collectively, these complementary tools integrate environmental impact, operational efficiency, and practical applicability, extending beyond greenness evaluation alone.

The AGREE evaluation, which quantifies compliance with the twelve principles of Green Analytical Chemistry (GAC) [39], produced a score of 0.57 (Fig. 6). This result reflects considerable greenness compliance, consistent with the use of greener solvents such as methanol, energy-efficient operation, the avoidance of derivatisation steps, and a good throughput, quantifying 3 binary mixtures per hour. The reduced score is primarily attributed to the method being off-line analysis with a few steps of sample preparation, the extraction method not being miniaturized, and the generation of more than 10 ml of waste. However, the waste is safely treated for disposal. The overall AGREE score suggests that the method successfully integrates several green chemistry concepts while maintaining analytical robustness.

The GAPI pictogram provided a visual overview of the environmental impact across all procedural stages, from sample preparation to waste management. The predominance of green and yellow fields, with only one red segment, indicates that most steps comply well with GAC principles. The red zones reflect off-line analysis as opposed to a greener in-line analysis and waste generation of more than 10 ml. The overall pattern demonstrated a well-optimized and environmentally conscious workflow, minimizing reagent consumption, waste, and exposure potential.

The BAGI scored 75 (Fig. 7), which is well above the recommended threshold of 60, indicating a high degree of practicality and user-friendliness. The BAGI pictogram showed a predominance of darker blue shades, indicating strong compliance with criteria related to method simplicity, time efficiency, reproducibility, and instrument accessibility. This emphasizes that the method not only aligns with sustainable analytical principles but is also realistic for routine laboratory use, supporting the blue component of the white analytical chemistry concept. The detailed breakdown of the scores for AGREE, GAPI, and BAGI is shown in supplementary Tables 1, 2, and Fig. 18, respectively.

By combining greenness (AGREE, GAPI) with practical applicability (BAGI), the method is not only environmentally conscious but also operationally feasible for routine use. This approach provides a holistic assessment confirming that the method achieves a balanced profile of sustainability and analytical performance.

Conclusion and future directions

This current analytical method is the first validated simultaneous RID method for donepezil and memantine. By avoiding derivatization and excipient interference, it provides a practical alternative to UV methods for routine formulation analysis. The developed analytical method is selective, sensitive, simple, rapid, and reproducible for the simultaneous detection of Dnp and Mem. This method can be utilized for formulation testing and quality purposes, containing both the drugs without the derivatization of memantine. In this paper, we applied the developed method to an intranasal in situ gel release study, and the results showed successful quantification in the samples. In addition to this, the method can be used to analyze drug content in marketed and experimental formulations. The use of a refractive index detector, combined with a relatively short run time, the use of the most common solvents, and good analyte separation at low concentration, offers a clear analytical advantage in this method. The moderate AGREE score, high BAGI score, and predominantly green/yellow GAPI profile collectively demonstrate that the method is environmentally sustainable and practically applicable.

Efficacy of a feed additive consisting of Enterococcus lactis NCIMB 11181 (Lactiferm®) for chickens for fattening or reared for laying, other poultry species for fattening or reared for laying and ornamental birds (Chr. Hansen A/S)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Ilen Röhe, Katerina Theodoridou, Secundino López Puente, Montserrat Anguita, Matteo L. Innocenti, Jordi Ortuño
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the efficacy of Lactiferm®, a preparation containing viable cells of Enterococcus lactis NCIMB 11181, as a zootechnical feed additive (functional group: gut flora stabilisers). The additive is intended to be used in the feed (3 × 1010 CFU/kg complete feed) and water for drinking (1.5 × 1010 CFU/L) for chickens for fattening or reared for laying, other poultry species for fattening or reared for laying and ornamental birds. In a previous assessment, the FEEDAP Panel concluded that the additive is safe for the target species, consumers and the environment but could not conclude on its efficacy because only two out of the six trials submitted showed significant and positive effects on the zootechnical performance of chickens for fattening. The applicant provided two published studies, one pooled analysis of the data from two previously assessed trials and two long‐term trials in chickens for fattening to address the data gaps in the efficacy assessment. Based on the results of the trials in the previous and current applications, the FEEDAP Panel concluded that Lactiferm® has the potential to be efficacious when supplemented either in the feed or water of chickens for fattening, and the conclusion was extended to chickens reared for laying and extrapolated to other poultry for fattening and reared for laying and ornamental birds.

Additional information

The additive is a preparation containing Enterococcus lactis (formerly identified as Enterococcus faecium) NCIMB 11181.

The additive is currently authorised for use in feed for calves for rearing and for fattening (up to 6 months of age) and for weaned piglets (4b1708). 3

EFSA issued four opinions on this product for its use in feed (EFSA, 2005; EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012, 2023, 2024a).

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and efficacy of E. lactis NCIMB 11181 (Lactiferm®) is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 6 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the assessment of the efficacy of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024b).

ASSESSMENT

The additive is composed of viable cells of E. lactis NCIMB 11181 and intended to be used as a zootechnical additive (functional group: gut flora stabilisers) in complete feed or water for drinking for chickens for fattening or reared for laying, other poultry species for fattening or reared for laying, and ornamental birds at a minimum inclusion level of 3 × 1010 CFU/kg complete feed or 1.5 × 1010 CFU/L water for drinking. The product, with the commercial name Lactiferm®, is marketed in two solid formulations: ‐Lactiferm® Basic 50: containing E. lactis NCIMB 11181 at a minimum concentration of 5 × 1010 colony forming units (CFU)/g additive (representing 12%–16% w/w) and maltodextrin as a carrier (representing 84%–88% w/w).‐Lactiferm® WS200: a water‐soluble formulation containing E. lactis NCIMB 11181 at a minimum concentration of 2 × 1011 CFU/g additive of the active agent (representing 45%–50% w/w) and sorbitol as a carrier (representing 50%–55% w/w).

‐Lactiferm® Basic 50: containing E. lactis NCIMB 11181 at a minimum concentration of 5 × 1010 colony forming units (CFU)/g additive (representing 12%–16% w/w) and maltodextrin as a carrier (representing 84%–88% w/w).

Lactiferm® Basic 50: containing E. lactis NCIMB 11181 at a minimum concentration of 5 × 1010 colony forming units (CFU)/g additive (representing 12%–16% w/w) and maltodextrin as a carrier (representing 84%–88% w/w).

‐Lactiferm® WS200: a water‐soluble formulation containing E. lactis NCIMB 11181 at a minimum concentration of 2 × 1011 CFU/g additive of the active agent (representing 45%–50% w/w) and sorbitol as a carrier (representing 50%–55% w/w).

Lactiferm® WS200: a water‐soluble formulation containing E. lactis NCIMB 11181 at a minimum concentration of 2 × 1011 CFU/g additive of the active agent (representing 45%–50% w/w) and sorbitol as a carrier (representing 50%–55% w/w).

In a previous opinion from EFSA, the FEEDAP Panel concluded that the use of Lactiferm® at the proposed conditions of use is safe for the target species, the consumers and the environment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024a). Regarding user safety, Lactiferm® WS200 was considered not irritant to the skin or eyes; the Panel could not conclude on the skin and eye irritation potential of Lactiferm® Basic 50, nor on the skin sensitisation potential of the additive in any of its formulations. Owing to the nature of the active agent, the final formulations of the additive were considered respiratory sensitisers.

The Panel could not conclude on the efficacy of the additive for the target species due to the lack of sufficient evidence. In that assessment, the applicant submitted a total of six in vivo studies, in which the additive was supplemented either to the water for drinking or to the feed of chickens for fattening and the zootechnical performance was evaluated. Out of the six studies, only two showed a significant improvement of the zootechnical performance (better feed‐to‐gain ratio). The applicant provided additional data to address the data gaps on efficacy.

Efficacy

In the current application, the additional data submitted to support the efficacy of the additive were: two studies retrieved from the literature, one pooled study (combining the data from two trials submitted in the context of the previous application), and two long‐term trials in chickens for fattening.

The two published studies were conducted with the additive under assessment supplemented either in the feed (Gheisar et al., 2016) or in water for drinking (Weis et al., 2011) of chickens for fattening to assess the potential effect on zootechnical performance or carcass characteristics, respectively. However, none of them could be considered for the assessment because the husbandry conditions in which the animals were reared were not aligned with the standard farming practices in the European Union.

The pooled study comprised a statistical re‐evaluation of the data from two of the trials assessed in the context of the previous application (trials 2 and 3), which showed no significant differences in any of the key zootechnical performance parameters between groups (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024a). When the pooled data were re‐analysed using a general linear model, including the diet, the trial and the interaction diet × trial as fixed effects, no significant differences were observed between the control and the supplemented group in the average daily weight gain (63.4 vs. 63.2 g), average daily feed intake (105 vs. 104 g), feed‐to‐gain ratio (1.66 vs. 1.65) and final body weight (2756 vs. 2709 g).

In both trials, 1‐day‐old male chicks were distributed in floor pens randomly allocated to two different groups fed either a non‐supplemented diet (control) or the same basal diet supplemented with Lactiferm® to provide 3 × 1010 CFU/kg complete feed (confirmed by analysis; Table 1). The animals received three basal diets (starter/grower/finisher) ad libitum for 35 days. Animal health status and mortality were monitored and recorded during the trial, including the most likely cause of death or reason for culling. The birds were weighed at the start of the trial, and thereafter, the body weight and feed intake were recorded on a pen basis for each phase and the overall period. The feed‐to‐gain ratio was calculated. All zootechnical parameters were corrected for mortality.

The dietary supplementation of chickens for fattening with the additive at 3 × 1010 CFU/kg complete feed showed an improved performance in both trials (average daily weight gain and final body weight in trial 1; better feed‐to‐gain ratio in trial 2). However, in trial 1, the group supplemented with the additive showed higher mortality rates than the control group. Most of the causes of death and reasons for culling in both groups seem to be unrelated to the additive (omphalitis, leg issues, heart failure). However, the Panel considered that, being the additive the only difference in the design between groups, this finding would prevent considering the study as positive to support efficacy.

Conclusions for the efficacy

Chickens for fattening that received the additive at 3 × 1010 CFU/kg complete feed showed better zootechnical performance in three trials (based on the results of two trials in the previous application and one in the current one). Therefore, the Panel concludes that the additive has the potential to be efficacious in chickens for fattening when supplemented at 3 × 1010 CFU/kg complete feed. Considering that the water intake of poultry would be two to three times the amount of dry matter feed intake, the Panel concludes that Lactiferm® can also be efficacious in water for drinking at the proposed level of 1.5 × 1010 CFU/L.

The conclusion in chickens for fattening is extended to chickens reared for laying and extrapolated to all poultry for fattening, all poultry reared for laying and ornamental birds.

Post‐market monitoring

The FEEDAP Panel considers that there is no need for specific requirements for a post‐market monitoring plan other than those established in the Feed Hygiene Regulation 8 and Good Manufacturing Practice.

CONCLUSIONS

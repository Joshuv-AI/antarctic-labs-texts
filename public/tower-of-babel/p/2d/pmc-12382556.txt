Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors and the risk of all-cause mortality: a population-based cohort study using the UK Clinical Practice Research Datalink
Authors: Talip E Eroglu, Ruben Coronel, Patrick C Souverein
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimSodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT-2is) have a direct cardiac effect that is independent of their glucose-lowering renal effect. We investigated the relation between SGLT-2is and all-cause mortality compared with (1) dipeptidyl peptidase-4 inhibitors (DPP-4is) and (2) metformin monotherapy in type 2 diabetes among subjects with a diabetes duration of <5 years at cohort entry.MethodsA cohort study was conducted among patients initiating a new antidiabetic drug class between January 2013 and September 2020 by extracting data from the UK Clinical Practice Research Datalink. The HR of all-cause mortality comparing (1) SGLT-2is versus DPP-4is in type 2 diabetes and (2) SGLT-2is versus metformin monotherapy among subjects with a diabetes duration of <5 years at cohort entry was calculated using Cox regression. Stratified analyses were performed according to sex and the presence of cardiovascular disease.ResultsThe cohort consisted of 152 591 new users of antidiabetic drugs, with 15 125 SGLT-2i users, 31 896 DPP-4i users, 15 723 other second-line to third-line antidiabetic drug users and 89 847 first-line antidiabetic drug users at cohort entry. After adjusting for all relevant confounders, SGLT-2i use was associated with a reduced rate of all-cause mortality compared with metformin monotherapy (HR: 0.77, 95% CI: 0.64 to 0.93) or DPP4-i (HR: 0.57, 95% CI: 0.51 to 0.63). This reduced rate of all-cause mortality appeared to be independent of sex and cardiovascular disease.ConclusionOur findings suggest a reduced risk of all-cause mortality with SGLT-2is compared with DPP-4is or metformin monotherapy in type 2 diabetes among subjects with a diabetes duration of <5 years at cohort entry.Keywords: Pharmacology, Diabetes Mellitus, Epidemiology

Introduction

Type 2 diabetes is associated with an increased risk of all-cause mortality.1 2 Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT-2is) are glucose-lowering drugs widely used in the treatment of type 2 diabetes. Previous studies showed that SGLT-2is have beneficial effects on all-cause mortality and cardiovascular morbidity.3,8 Several diabetes guidelines have been updated to recommend SGLT-2is to individuals with type 2 diabetes who are at high cardiovascular risk.9 10 To our knowledge, however, data on the association between SGLT-2is and all-cause mortality compared with metformin are scarce. Studying this may help guide the choice of antihyperglycaemic therapy in people with type 2 diabetes.

Accordingly, in this population-based cohort study, we sought to investigate whether the use of SGLT-2is was associated with a reduced risk of all-cause mortality in patients with type 2 diabetes when compared with metformin monotherapy and with another second-line to third-line antidiabetic drug, namely dipeptidyl peptidase-4 inhibitors (DPP-4is). The comparison with metformin monotherapy was studied in a subset of patients with type 2 diabetes with a diabetes duration of <5 years at cohort entry. Further, we investigated whether different factors, including sex and the presence of cardiovascular comorbidities, may affect this association.

Methods

Data sources

This population-based cohort study was conducted using the Clinical Practice Research Datalink (CPRD) GOLD. The CPRD GOLD is a longitudinal database containing medical records of primary care practices in the UK, representing ~7% of the total population.11 Patients in the CPRD have been shown to be largely representative of the general population.11 A detailed description of CPRD used for the present study can be found elsewhere.11

Study cohort

We assembled a cohort of patients who initiated a new antidiabetic drug class between January 2013 (the year SGLT-2is were introduced in the UK market) and September 2020. This cohort consisted of patients who received their first prescription for a non-insulin antidiabetic drug, as well as those who switched or added an antidiabetic drug from a class that was not used before. Cohort entry was defined by the date of this new antidiabetic drug prescription. The same study design3 12 and population have been used previously by this research group.3 We excluded all patients with a history of sudden cardiac arrest at any time before cohort entry, as these patients have a higher mortality risk, as well as patients with <6 months of valid history in the CPRD before the cohort entry date, as we did earlier.3 We also excluded patients initially prescribed insulin, and women with polycystic ovarian syndrome and gestational diabetes at the time of cohort entry, because these are other indications for metformin therapy, as we did earlier.3 12 Finally, to investigate the relation between the use of SGLT-2is and all-cause mortality in comparison with metformin monotherapy, we additionally excluded patients with a diabetes duration of ≥5 years at cohort entry in order to create a more homogeneous study population.

Exposure

Exposure to the different antidiabetic drugs was modelled as a time-varying variable in which each person-day at any time during the follow-up period was defined according to the following hierarchical definition: (1) SGLT-2i users (eg, alone or in combination with other antidiabetic drugs), (2) DPP-4i users (eg, alone or in combination with other antidiabetic drugs with the exception of use of SGLT-2is), (3) users of metformin monotherapy and (4) users of other second-line to third-line antidiabetic drug. For all exposure categories, exposed person-time was defined by the prescription duration plus a 30-day permissible gap period, to allow for irregular use. This way of exposure definition has been previously used by us,3 12 which is described in detail elsewhere.3 12 13

Outcome

All-cause mortality was identified using the CPRD death date, which has been shown to be registered accurately across the registers.14

Covariates

The following confounders were defined as time-dependent variables at the start of each time interval: age, ischaemic heart disease including myocardial infarction, heart failure, atrial fibrillation, peripheral artery disease and the presence of an implantable cardioverter defibrillator (ICD) or pacemaker, as done previously.3 Further, we identified smoking status (current, recent and past) and body mass index (BMI: <30, ≥30 kg/m2) by using the most recent values at the start of each time interval. We also identified proxy variables for diabetes severity, such as haemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) level (≤7.0, 7.1–8.0, >8.0), diabetes duration and use of different anti-hyperglycaemic drugs. Finally, the prescription of concomitant pharmacotherapy was defined in a time-varying fashion as the 6-month period prior to the start of each time interval, as described previously.3 12

Statistical analyses

Data analysis was performed on data collected from January 2013 to September 2020. Subjects were followed from cohort entry until death (ie, all-cause mortality), transfer out of the general practice, date of last data collection for the practice or end of the study period (September 2020), whichever occurred first. Cox regression analysis with time-dependent exposure and time-dependent covariates was used to calculate the adjusted HRs with 95% CI of all-cause mortality comparing (1) SGLT-2is with DPP-4is and (2) SGLT-2is or DPP-4is with metformin monotherapy using two models: (1) a crude model (unadjusted analyses) and (2) a multivariable model adjusted for the following pre-specified variables: age, sex, BMI, smoking status, HbA1c level, duration of diabetes, ischaemic heart disease, heart failure, atrial fibrillation, peripheral arterial disease, presence of an ICD or pacemaker and the number of cardiovascular drugs prescribed (as a proxy for cardiovascular disease severity). The main analyses were repeated by stratifying according to sex and the presence of cardiovascular comorbidities, including heart failure, to examine effect modification. The presence of interaction on a multiplicative scale was estimated by including the cross-product of the two factors of interest as a variable in the model. Next, we selected subjects with a diabetes duration of <5 years at cohort study and studied the association between SGLT-2i use and the HR of all-cause mortality compared with metformin monotherapy in this subgroup.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Table 1 presents the characteristics of patients who received SGLT-2is and DPP-4is at cohort entry. The study population consisted of 152 591 new users of antidiabetic drugs, with 15 125 SGLT-2i users, 31 896 DPP-4i users, 15 723 other second-line to third-line antidiabetic drug users and 89 847 first-line antidiabetic drug users at cohort entry (figure 1; characteristics of other second-line to third-line antidiabetic drug users and first-line antidiabetic users are not included in table 1). Compared with users of DPP-4is, users of SGLT-2is were similar with respect to sex and smoking status. However, users of SGLT-2is were younger, were more likely to be obese, had higher HbA1c levels and had a longer duration of diabetes compared with users of DPP-4is. Furthermore, users of SGLT-2is had a lower prevalence of cardiovascular comorbidities and cardiovascular drug use, while the use of other antidiabetic drugs, including insulin, was higher compared with users of DPP-4is.

Association between SGLT-2is and all-cause mortality compared with DPP-4is

Table 2 presents the results related to all-cause mortality as our outcome. The incidence rate of all-cause mortality was lower in SGLT-2i users than in users of DPP-is. After adjusting for all relevant confounders, use of SGLT-2is was significantly associated with a reduced hazard of all-cause mortality when compared with the use of DPP-4is (HR 0.57, 95% CI: 0.51 to 0.63). The HR of all-cause mortality associated with SGLT-2i use did not vary significantly by sex and cardiovascular disease, including heart failure (online supplemental table 1).

Discussion

In this large population-based cohort study, the main findings were: (1) the use of SGLT-2is was associated with a reduced rate of all-cause mortality when compared with the use of metformin in monotherapy among subjects with a diabetes duration of <5 years at cohort entry and (2) the use of SGLT-2is was associated with a reduced rate of all-cause mortality when compared with the use of DPP-4is in type 2 diabetes.

Association between SGLT-2is and all-cause mortality

We noticed that SGLT-2i users had a longer duration of diabetes and a significantly higher prevalence of insulin use compared with DPP-4i users, which could indicate a higher diabetes severity in SGLT-2i users. Nonetheless, despite probably having a more advanced stage of diabetes than DPP-4i users, users of SGLT-2is still had reduced all-cause mortality. We noticed, however, that the prevalence of cardiovascular comorbidities was higher among users of DPP-4is than among users of SGLT-2is. It could be that the higher prevalence of cardiovascular comorbidities among users of DPP-4is is driven by the higher age of DPP-4i users (67 vs 61 years). Nevertheless, use of SGLT-2is remained significantly associated with decreased rate of all-cause mortality after adjustment for these variables in the multivariable model. Furthermore, our finding of reduced all-cause mortality remained consistent when we performed our analyses in a subset of patients without cardiovascular comorbidities.

Our finding of a reduced risk of all-cause mortality associated with SGLT-2i use compared with DPP-4i use is supported by previous studies.15,19 Birkeland et al compared the effects of SGLT-2is and DPP-4is on several adverse outcomes (ie, cardiorenal disease, heart failure or chronic kidney disease) using a population-based cohort of type 2 diabetes mellitus and reported that SGLT-2i use was associated with lower risk of cardiorenal disease, heart failure, chronic kidney disease, cardiovascular mortality and all-cause mortality outcomes compared with DPP-4i use after propensity score matching.15 Using a cohort design, Chung et al studied the association between SGLT-2i use and all-cause mortality when compared with DPP-4i use for type 2 diabetes and showed that SGLT-2i use was associated with lower risks of all-cause mortality when compared with DPP-4i users after adjusting for potential risk factors in the propensity score-matched population.16 Fralick et al studied the risk of heart failure hospitalisation and all-cause mortality associated with the use of SGLT-2is compared with DPP-4is using a cohort of adults aged 66 years and older diagnosed with diabetes mellitus and showed a lower rate of heart failure hospitalisation or all-cause mortality, compared with older adults prescribed a DPP-4i.17 These findings were supported by a study by Idris et al in which the effect of SGLT-2is on all-cause mortality, cardiovascular mortality and hospitalisation for heart failure or chronic kidney disease was investigated.18 That study reported a reduced risk of all-cause mortality and hospitalisation for heart failure and chronic kidney disease associated with SGLT-2is compared with DPP4-is in people with type 2 diabetes.18 Similarly, using data from several nationwide registries, Persson et al compared the effects of the SGLT-2i dapagliflozin and DPP-4is on major adverse cardiovascular events and all-cause mortality and confirmed a lower risk of cardiovascular events and all-cause mortality associated with dapagliflozin after propensity score matching.19 Finally, in a 2-year prospective, multicentre, quasi-experimental study, Longo et al compared the metabolic and vascular effectiveness of SGLT-2is and DPP-4is in the clinical practice of patients with type 2 diabetes in Italy, and reported that both SGLT-2i and DPP-4i provided sustained HbA1c reduction after 2 years. SGLT-2is, however, demonstrated superior vascular and renal benefits, with significantly greater regression of carotid intima-media thickness and albuminuria and a modest improvement in cardiac function compared with DPP-4is.20 It should be noted, however, that in the referenced observational studies, post-baseline confounding may still be present despite propensity matching.15,19 However, despite using a different data source and a different approach for adjusting confounders (propensity-score), our finding of reduced all-cause mortality associated with SGLT-2is when compared with DPP-4is was supported by these earlier studies.

Although the relation between SGLT-2is and all-cause mortality in comparison with DPP-4is has been investigated in the referenced studies,15,19 studies examining the association between SGLT-2is and all-cause mortality in comparison with metformin monotherapy are scarce to our knowledge. Our results indicate that the use of SGLT-2is was associated with a decreased rate of all-cause mortality when compared with metformin monotherapy, despite the fact that SGLT-2i users probably have a more advanced stage of diabetes than users of metformin only (ie, longer diabetes duration, higher HbA1c, more insulin use). On the other hand, the use of another second-line to third-line antidiabetic drug, namely DPP-4is, was not associated with a decreased rate of all-cause mortality, which provides additional support that the observed beneficial effects are probably due to other mechanisms beyond the glucose-lowering effects of SGLT-2is. Specific mechanisms that might explain the beneficial effects of SGLT-2is on the risk of all-cause mortality have been described, including metabolic (eg, weight loss), haemodynamic (eg, natriuresis and osmotic diuresis, blood pressure reduction), decreased inflammation and oxidative stress, and improved vascular endothelial function.21 22 Our data are noteworthy, considering that the public burden of diabetes is likely to rise further due to the ageing population and the worldwide increase in obesity.

Limitations

Our study has several limitations. First, its observational nature precludes the assessment of cause-effect relationships. Second, SGLT-2i users may differ systematically from DPP-4i or metformin users in terms of unmeasured factors such as physical activity, socioeconomic status or prescribing physician preferences. Therefore, we cannot rule out the possibility of residual confounding as these factors could not be taken directly into account in our multivariate analyses. We attempted to reduce the impact of residual confounding by using an active comparator design and by adjusting for a wide range of variables. However, residual confounding is still possible given the observational nature of our study. While a propensity score-matched analysis could have strengthened our results, this was not possible due to our time-dependent study design. Moreover, it should be noted that a propensity score does not take changes with respect to medical status (eg, newly prescribed medication, discontinuation of medication, new diagnosis) that occurs during the follow-up period into consideration. This could result in post-baseline confounding despite propensity-matching. Our time-dependent study design may have minimised such potential confounding. Thirdly, the comparison between SGLT-2is and metformin monotherapy performed in a subgroup with <5 years of diabetes might have led to confounding due to time lag bias and could limit the generalisability of our findings, as patients on metformin alone may have a less advanced stage of diabetes than users of SGLT-2is and therefore represent a healthier subgroup. Nonetheless, despite the fact that SGLT-2i users probably have a more advanced stage of diabetes than metformin users, users of SGLT-2is still had reduced all-cause mortality. Finally, exposure misclassification is possible as prescriptions in the CPRD represent those written by general practitioners and not specialists. However, in the UK, the management of type 2 diabetes occurs almost entirely through primary care, and thus, we expect such misclassification to have had an unimportant impact on our exposure definition.

Conclusion

The results of this large population-based cohort study suggest that (1) the use of SGLT-2is is associated with a reduced risk of all-cause mortality when compared with the use of DPP-4is in type 2 diabetes and (2) the use of SGLT-2is is associated with a reduced risk of all-cause mortality when compared with the use of metformin in monotherapy among subjects with a diabetes duration of <5 years at cohort entry. This reduced risk appears to be independent of sex and the presence of cardiovascular disease.

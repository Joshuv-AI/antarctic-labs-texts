Antimicrobial effects of nanosilver/polyethylene glycol/chitosan in cesarean section and their impact on breastfeeding rates
Authors: Xuejiao Wang, Qin Yu, Xiaoli Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate the antibacterial effects of nanosilver (nAg)-polyethylene glycol (PEG) chitosan (CTS)-polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) dressing on post-cesarean section (CS) incisions and its impact on breastfeeding rates.MethodsInitially, nAg-PEG-CTS-PVA, as an antibacterial dressing, was prepared using the PEG reduction method, and the morphology of nAg-PEG was observed using transmission electron microscopy. The water absorption capacity of the nAg-PEG-CTS-PVA sponge was measured, and its pore structure was observed using scanning electron microscopy. Furthermore, the in vitro release of silver particles and antibacterial effects were evaluated. Subsequently, 94 CS patients were divided into two groups: the control group (conventional antimicrobial treatment) and the nAg group (nAg antibacterial dressing), with 47 cases in each group. The outcome measures assessed included the incidence of postoperative wound infection, wound healing time, frequency of dressing changes, maternal pain intensity before and after intervention (evaluated using the visual analog scale (VAS)), LATCH breastfeeding assessment tool scores, and the exclusive breastfeeding rate within six months.ResultsnAg particles were uniformly dispersed in PEG, with a particle size ranging from 13 to 21 nm, predominantly spherical in shape. The CTS-PVA sponge containing nAg-PEG exhibited optimal water absorption when PVA-AH-26 accounted for 70%, displaying uniform pore distribution with higher density and pore sizes between 0.3 and 1.1 mm. The cumulative release rate reached 70% on the 8th day, and by the 28th-30th days, the cumulative release rate was 96%. The sponge demonstrated excellent bactericidal effects against Staphylococcus aureus, Gram-negative bacilli, coagulase-negative Staphylococcus, Enterococcus, and Escherichia coli. In comparison with the control group, the nAg group showed shorter wound healing time, lower wound dressing change frequency, lower visual analog scale scores, and a higher rate of exclusive breastfeeding (P < 0.05).ConclusionThese findings indicated that the antibacterial dressing containing nAg-PEG-CTS-PVA sponge had effective antimicrobial, analgesic, and wound healing-promoting properties, leading to a higher rate of exclusive breastfeeding after CS.Keywords: Cesarean section, Nanosilver-polyethylene glycol, Antimicrobial dressing, Incision infection, Breastfeeding

Background

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), breastfeeding has a very important impact on the growth and health of infants in the first six months after birth [1]. Breast milk contains all the nutrients (including abundant antibodies) that infants need for growth within 6 months of birth. It is not only easy to digest and absorb, but also can improve the immunity of infants and reduce the incidence of diseases. Its feeding effect can’t be replaced by other dairy products [2, 3].

Cesarean section (CS) is a common mode of delivery in clinic. According to relevant statistics, the proportion of women delivered by CS in China has reached 46.5% of the total women, which is more than three times higher than the warning line of CS rate of 15% set by the WHO [4]. Although in recent years, the technology of CS has become increasingly mature and plays a visible advantage in improving the outcomes of some parturients [5], it will still cause certain adverse outcomes on the parturients themselves. CS requires the dissection of the maternal abdominal wall and uterus, which is an invasive surgery [6]. The amount of bleeding during CS is more than that of vaginal delivery, which is easy to cause complications such as massive hemorrhage and hemorrhagic shock [7, 8], and postpartum women often need more time for rest and recovery after surgery. It is also very important to prevent wound, pelvic and systemic infection caused by poor wound healing after CS. According to statistics, the infection rate of abdominal wounds after CS is 3%−16%, and the prophylactic use of antibiotics will reduce the infection rate by 2% or even lower. Therefore, anti-infective drugs are often injected to puerpera after clinical operation [9, 10]. However, incision pain, the use of antibiotics, and other factors will lead to the restriction of early lactation of parturients, and even some parturients will directly give up breastfeeding because of pain [11]. Because there are many adverse reactions in the use of antibiotics, a nanosilver (nAg) antibacterial dressing was proposed according to the cause of infection. Studies have found that nAg medical antibacterial dressing can effectively prevent surgical wound infection [12]. Silver particles in nAg medical antibacterial dressing not only have anti-inflammatory function, but also have strong permeability, which can quickly and effectively kill pathogens on incisions and sutures [13, 15]. In addition, studies have also found that silver particles can relieve pain [16, 17]. In this context, Masood et al. (2019)[18] prepared nAg-polyethylene glycol (PEG) chitosan (CTS) hydrogels using a PEG reduction method and applied them to a diabetic rabbit wound healing model, demonstrating favorable porosity, moisture permeability, and antioxidant properties, which significantly promoted chronic wound healing. Silver-based dressings also show potential for post-cesarean section applications [18]. Goodman et al. (2022)[19] conducted a study involving 362 participants, revealing that silver-impregnated dressings reduced postoperative infection rates by 50%, though statistical significance was not reached, providing preliminary evidence for their use in obstetrics [19].

Building on this foundation, this study developed an nAg-PEG-CTS-polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) sponge dressing and applied it for the first time to post-cesarean incision care. Compared to the aforementioned hydrogel, this dressing exhibits superior fluid absorption and structural stability. Additionally, the introduction of the exclusive breastfeeding rate as a key outcome measure addresses both infection prevention and maternal functional recovery, bridging a research gap between postoperative rehabilitation and maternal-infant behavior (Table 1). This study aimed to propose a novel nAg-based dressing strategy with multiple clinical advantages, including reduced postoperative infection risk, pain relief, accelerated healing, and improved breastfeeding outcomes (Fig. 1).

Methods

nAg antibacterial dressing is composed of silver nitrate, PEG, CTS, and PVA, which was prepared by PEG reduction method. First, 0.170 g of silver nitrate (AgNO3) was dissolved in 10 mL of PEG (molecular weight 8000, 2% w/v) and allowed to react undisturbed for 30 min to achieve slow reduction of silver ions into nAg, forming an nAg-PEG solution. The initial Ag⁺ concentration in the solution was 1.0 mg/mL. The morphology of nAg and PEG solution was observed by scanning electron microscope (TEM). The acceleration voltage of TEM was set to 200 kV. Subsequently, the CTS-PVA mixed solution was prepared. Specifically, 0.4 g of CTS (deacetylation degree > 95%) was dissolved in 50 mL of 1% (v/v) acetic acid aqueous solution, and then mixed with 4.0 g of PVA in 200 mL of deionized water under heating. The PVA component was adjusted proportionally: PVA-AH-26 accounted for 90%, 70%, 50%, 30%, and 10%, with the remainder supplemented by PVA-1799. To this solution, 0.05 g of sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS, as an emulsifier), 0.5 mL of hydrochloric acid (for pH adjustment), and 1.0 mL of formaldehyde (as a crosslinking agent) were added. The mixture was stirred using a high-speed homogenizer until a completely transparent solution was obtained. To enhance the dispersibility and structural stability of CTS and PVA, the surfactant SDS was employed to improve their compatibility, while formaldehyde facilitated the formation of a three-dimensional network structure, thereby strengthening the mechanical properties of the material. Subsequently, 10 mL of the aforementioned nAg-PEG solution was slowly added dropwise into the CTS-PVA reaction system, followed by continuous high-speed stirring to ensure thorough mixing. The final solution contained an nAg concentration of approximately 85 µg/mL and a CTS mass concentration of 2 mg/mL. Throughout the mixing process, no significant phase separation was observed, indicating uniform dispersion of the nAg-PEG complex within the CTS-PVA network. Then, it was cured under constant temperature, washed to neutral with deionized water, and freeze-dried at −40℃ for 6 h to obtain CTS-PVA sponge with nAg and PEG. The WA ability of CTS-PVA sponge with nAg and PEG was detected, and the water absorption (WA) and WA rate (WAR) were calculated. The WAR was determined by measuring the change in absorbed water volume per unit time during the sponge’s water absorption process. According to the WA test, the CTS-PVA sponge with nAg and PEG was obtained with the best effect. To confirm the formation of cross-linked structures in the CTS-PVA sponge, Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) was employed to analyze characteristic shifts in functional groups (e.g., hydroxyl and amino groups) and identify cross-linking reaction signatures (such as the –C = N absorption peak). The pore structure of the sponge was observed by TEM, and the release ability of silver particles in vitro was detected. The common bacteria causing wound infection after CS are Staphylococcus aureus (ATCC 25923), Gram-negative bacilli (ATCC 27853), coagulase negative staphylococcus (ATCC 12228), Enterococcus (ATCC 29212), and Escherichia coli (ATCC 25922) [20, 25]. This study introduced an additional control group of CTS-PVA blank sponge without silver nanoparticles to scientifically differentiate the independent antibacterial effects between the material matrix and silver nanoparticles. To systematically evaluate the antibacterial activity and long-term stability of CTS-PVA sponges containing nAg and PEG, agar diffusion assays were performed at different storage time points (0, 7, 14, 30, 60, and 90 days) to measure the inhibition zone diameters against representative bacterial strains, quantitatively reflecting the temporal changes in antibacterial efficacy. Meanwhile, the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) was determined by broth microdilution method to assess the stability of the sponge’s lowest effective concentration for bacterial growth inhibition over time. Comparative analysis between silver-containing and blank control groups further elucidated the critical role of silver nanoparticles in antibacterial activity and their sustained-release performance.

Simultaneously, a total of 94 postpartum women who underwent CS in the Women and Children’s Hospital, Nanchuan District People’s Hospital Obstetrics Department from April 2021 to January 2023 were selected as the study subjects. The novel medical antibacterial dressing involved in this research has completed medical device regulatory registration and filing, ensuring safety and efficacy. A safety monitoring mechanism was established to promptly record and address adverse events. Informed consent was strictly implemented, with all participating mothers fully informed about the dressing properties, potential risks, and alternative treatment options. They were clearly advised of the study objectives and risks, and all provided written consent to safeguard their autonomy. Based on statistical power analysis (α = 0.05, β = 0.2, expected difference in exclusive breastfeeding rate ≥ 20%), the minimum required sample size was calculated as 42 cases per group. Accounting for a 20% dropout rate, a total of 94 cases (47 per group) were ultimately enrolled. Study was performed using a computer-generated random number table (created by an independent statistician using SPSS 26.0), allocating eligible participants in a 1:1 ratio to either the nAg group or control group. The random sequence was concealed in sequentially numbered, sealed envelopes by a third-party staff member not involved in intervention or assessment, ensuring proper allocation concealment. Envelopes were opened according to the enrollment sequence. The control group (received routine postoperative infection prevention and postpartum breastfeeding health education) and the nAg group (received treatment with nAg antimicrobial dressings and routine postpartum breastfeeding health education), with 47 participants in each group. Due to the nature of the intervention (nAg antibacterial dressing application), neither participants nor nursing staff could be blinded. However, to minimize assessment bias, outcome evaluators (including those responsible for infection diagnosis, VAS scoring, and breastfeeding documentation) were kept unaware of group allocation throughout the evaluation process and were not involved in clinical grouping or intervention procedures, ensuring assessor blinding. Primary outcomes, pre-specified during protocol registration, included postoperative wound infection rate, wound healing time, frequency of dressing changes, maternal pain intensity (assessed by VAS) before and after intervention, and exclusive breastfeeding rate within six months. The Bonferroni method was applied for multiple comparison adjustments to ensure statistical robustness in multi-index analyses. Specific statistical methods were employed for each outcome measure: chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests for postoperative infection rates; independent samples t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests for wound healing time, dressing change frequency, and VAS scores (after verification of normality and homogeneity of variance). All statistical results are reported with p-values and 95% confidence intervals to facilitate clinical interpretation. Breastfeeding status was assessed at 6 months postpartum by dedicated maternal-infant health follow-up personnel through telephone interviews and clinical visits using the LATCH breastfeeding assessment tool (evaluating Latch, Audible swallowing, Type of nipple, Comfort, and Hold; total score 0–10, with higher scores indicating more successful breastfeeding) [26]. Feeding patterns were categorized as exclusive breastfeeding, mixed feeding, or formula feeding. To control for confounding factors affecting breastfeeding rates, multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed, adjusting for potential confounders including maternal age, education level, previous breastfeeding experience, and socioeconomic status, to obtain more reliable effect estimates.

Results

Figure 2A and B show the morphology of nAg and PEG under the TEM. nAg particles were uniformly dispersed in PEG. The particle size distribution was from 13 nm to 21 nm, and the shape was basically spherical. The nAg-PEG system prepared in this study represents a typical nanocomposite material. During preparation, PEG not only served as a reducing agent to convert silver ions into nAg particles, but also functioned as a stabilizer by forming a protective coating on the nanoparticle surfaces, effectively preventing agglomeration and ensuring particle uniformity and system stability. TEM observations confirmed the homogeneity and excellent dispersion of the silver nanoparticles. Furthermore, Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) was employed to verify the formation of cross-linked networks in the CTS-PVA sponge. The results demonstrated a redshift in the characteristic hydroxyl absorption peak of PEG after formation of the nAg-PEG composite system, indicating interactions between PEG and nAg surfaces and supporting the nanocomposite nature of the material. Additionally, observed shifts in the absorption peaks of -OH and -NH2 functional groups, along with the emergence of new absorption peaks such as -C = N-, confirmed the occurrence of cross-linking reactions and further validated the formation of a composite network structure. Because the wound often exuded a large amount of liquid during the process, the dressing with high moisture absorption can quickly absorb the liquid, reduce the wound decay and infection caused by the accumulation of exudate, and reduce the infiltration of exudate into healthy tissues, and promote the wound healing [27, 28]. Therefore, the WAR of different CTS-PVA sponges containing nAg and PEG with the proportion of PVA-AH-26 being 90%, 70%, 50%, 30%, and 10% were further explored (Fig. 3). It suggested that the WAR of CTS-PVA sponge with nAg and PEG was between 600% and 730%, and with the decrease of PVA-AH-26 content, WA gradually increased. When the PVA-AH-26 content was 70%, the CTS-PVA sponge with nAg and PEG had a WAR up to 12mL/s. The reason for this phenomenon may be that the polymerization degree of PVA 1799 is lower, so that the foam can be better distributed in it and the foaming performance is better, resulting in more and uniform bubble holes [29]. Therefore, in general, when the proportion of PVA-AH-26 was 70%, the CTS-PVA sponge with nAg and PEG had a relatively better WA effect.

According to the above results, the pore structure of CTS-PVA sponge with nAg and PEG when the proportion of PVA-AH-26 was 70% was observed by scanning electron microscopy (Fig. 4A and B). There were small pores on the walls of the large pores in the sponge, and the distribution of pores was uniform and the density was high. In addition, the large pores and the small pores were connected, and the distribution of pore sizes was between 0.3 mm and 1.1 mm. The size and distribution of pores often affect cell attachment, proliferation, and release of nAg [30]. The in vitro release of nAg was investigated (Fig. 4C). It suggested that the cumulative release rate of nAg increased rapidly from 0 to 8 days, and the cumulative release rate reached 70% following 8 days. 8 days later, the cumulative release rate was basically flat, while from 28 to 30 days, the cumulative release rate was 96%, indicating that the release effect of nAg was at a high level, which was conducive to the role of silver particles. The release mechanism of Ag+ primarily relies on the hydrophilicity and porous structure of the CTS-PVA sponge matrix, where water molecule penetration facilitates gradual silver ion release. Yu et al. (2024)[24] demonstrated that AgNPs immobilized within the hydrophilic porous network of CTS-PVA sponge form a sustained-release system through interconnected water channels, exhibiting characteristic release kinetics with an initial rapid phase followed by a sustained plateau period [24]. Combined analysis of silver ion release profiles and FTIR results suggests that ion release occurs concomitantly with microstructural adjustments in the sponge’s cross-linked network, achieving effective controlled release that prolongs antimicrobial activity. Antimicrobial evaluation revealed that CTS-PVA sponges maintained consistent antibacterial efficacy against common postoperative pathogens across different storage periods (0, 30, 60, and 90 days), demonstrating notable long-term stability. The sponge initially demonstrated significant antibacterial effects against Staphylococcus aureus, Escherichia coli, and Enterococcus, with inhibition zone diameters of 15.2 ± 1.3 mm, 13.5 ± 1.1 mm, and 14.8 ± 1.2 mm, respectively, corresponding to MIC values of 0.625, 0.3125, and 0.5 mg/mL. After 90 days, the inhibition zones slightly decreased to 14.2 ± 1.0 mm, 12.8 ± 0.8 mm, and 14.0 ± 0.9 mm, while the MIC values remained below 0.75 mg/mL, indicating sustained antibacterial efficacy. For coagulase-negative Staphylococci and Gram-negative bacilli, the initial inhibition zones were smaller (12.0 ± 0.9 mm and 11.5 ± 0.8 mm), but remained within 10.8–11.3 mm after 90 days, with MIC values below 1.0 mg/mL. This suggests slightly weaker but still effective antibacterial activity against these strains (Figs. 5A–F). In contrast, the nAg-free CTS-PVA control sponge showed no significant inhibition zones at any time point, with MIC values > 2 mg/mL, confirming that nAg is the primary antibacterial component. Overall, the sustained release of AgNPs from the CTS-PVA sponge maintained stable antibacterial performance for 90 days, demonstrating its suitability for long-term postoperative wound dressing applications.

In addition, this study statistically controlled for potential confounding factors affecting breastfeeding. Comparative analysis of demographic characteristics between the two groups (Table 2) revealed no statistically significant differences (P > 0.05) in age, education level, occupation type, monthly household income, or previous breastfeeding experience between the nAg group and control group, indicating comparable socioeconomic and cultural backgrounds between the groups. Further analysis demonstrated that the study revealed that, compared to the control group, there was no significant difference in the infection rate among women in the nAg group (P > 0.05; 95% CI: −6.2% to 3.6%). However, the women in the nAg group exhibited a shorter wound healing time and lower dressing change frequency, with statistically significant differences (P < 0.05; 95% CI: −2.5 to − 1.1) (Figs. 6-A to C). This indicates that nAg also have analgesic effects. Based on this, the study analyzed the changes in pain for women in the control group and nAg group before and after intervention (Fig. 6-D). The results showed that before intervention, there was no significant difference in pain levels between the control group and nAg group. However, after intervention, the VAS scores of women in the nAg group were lower (P < 0.05, 95% CI: −1.8 to − 0.6), indicating that the medical antimicrobial dressing containing nAg-PEG-CTS-PVA can alleviate pain. Furthermore, the nAg group demonstrated significantly higher LATCH scores than the control group (P < 0.05, 95% CI: −0.4 to 0.2), indicating better maternal-infant breastfeeding competency in the nAg antimicrobial dressing group. The nAg group showed significantly higher exclusive breastfeeding rates and lower formula feeding and mixed feeding rates compared to controls (P < 0.05, 95% CI: −0.4 to 0.2) (Table 3). Multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed with exclusive breastfeeding as the dependent variable and intervention type (nAg dressing vs. conventional treatment) as the primary independent variable, while controlling for age, education level, household income, previous breastfeeding experience, and cesarean indications. After adjusting for these confounders, the nAg group had significantly higher odds of exclusive breastfeeding (adjusted OR = 2.41, 95% CI: 1.13–5.14, P = 0.023), suggesting that the nAg-PEG-CTS-PVA dressing exerts an independent positive effect on promoting postpartum exclusive breastfeeding.

Discussion

Study confirmed that the CTS-PVA sponge with nAg and PEG had a good bactericidal effect on Staphylococcus aureus, Gram-negative bacilli, coagulase negative staphylococcus, Enterococcus, and Escherichia coli. Zielińska-Górska et al. (2023)[31] confirmed that nAg-graphene oxide composite nanodressings could reduce the inflammation caused by Staphylococcus aureus infection [31]. Ateia et al. (2023)[32] confirmed that AgCuO2 nanoparticles have excellent antibacterial outcome on a variety of Gram-negative bacilli, Enterococcus, Escherichia coli, and other intestinal bacteria [32]. Saravanakumar et al. (2023)[33] also confirmed that nAg effectively inhibit bacterial colonization and promote wound healing [33]. Yu et al. (2021)[34] through their research, found that nAg medical antimicrobial dressings combined with high-flow oxygen therapy can reduce the frequency of dressing changes, alleviate pain, reduce inflammation, and accelerate the healing of superficial malignant tumor ulcerations [34].

Due to the frequent use of antibiotics for post-CS wound infection prevention, the associated side effects and impact on breastfeeding are significant concerns. Studies demonstrated that postoperative infections, prolonged healing time, and increased dressing change frequency can indirectly affect breastfeeding initiation and continuation [35, 36]. The present findings indicated that the nAg-PEG-CTS-PVA dressing not only exhibited comparable local antibacterial efficacy to conventional antibiotics but also reduced dressing change frequency and accelerates wound healing, thereby enabling postoperative mothers to focus more effectively on breastfeeding. Moreover, the significantly higher LATCH scores and exclusive breastfeeding rates in the nAg group suggest that pain relief may synergistically improve breastfeeding behavior through both psychological and physiological mechanisms. Babazade et al. (2020)[37] demonstrated a significant negative correlation between pain scores and LATCH scores in a large cohort study of 5,350 postpartum women, indicating that postoperative pain may impair early neonatal milk extraction and suckling coordination, consequently affecting breastfeeding success [37]. In this study, the significantly lower VAS scores in the nAg group, coupled with elevated LATCH scores and exclusive breastfeeding rates, further support the indirect beneficial effects of localized analgesia on optimizing breastfeeding outcomes. According to previous studies, the minimal clinically important difference (MCID) for VAS scores typically ranges from 1.3 to 1.5 points [38]. In this study, the mean between-group difference in post-intervention VAS scores was 1.11 points, slightly below the lower threshold of the MCID suggested in some literature. However, when considered alongside the observed trends in pain reduction, shortened wound healing time, and decreased dressing change frequency, these findings suggest that the analgesic effect may still hold clinical relevance. Furthermore, Bawazeer et al. (2025)[39] demonstrated that silver nanoparticles can exert significant analgesic effects even at low doses, with potency comparable to certain conventional analgesics [39]. Notably, research on the analgesic mechanisms of silver nanoparticles remains in its early stages and has not yet been fully elucidated. Potential mechanisms may involve indirect modulation of local inflammatory pain pathways through anti-inflammatory effects, though these require further validation through fundamental research. In contrast to the findings of Connery et al. (2019)[40], whose randomized controlled trial of 657 cesarean patients showed no significant difference in superficial surgical site infection rates between silver-nylon dressings and conventional gauze (4.6% vs. 4.2%, P = 0.96), the present study employed a highly porous nAg-PEG-CTS-PVA sponge dressing [40]. This novel formulation not only provides localized antibacterial action but also demonstrates superior fluid absorption and breathability, potentially explaining the differential clinical outcomes observed.

In summary, while controlling for major breastfeeding-related confounders, this study preliminarily demonstrated through clinical observation and statistical analysis that the nAg antimicrobial dressing may improve breastfeeding outcomes in cesarean-section mothers by alleviating postoperative pain and promoting wound healing, suggesting biological plausibility and clinical potential. However, several limitations should be noted: (i) The small sample size (n = 94) and single-center design limit statistical power and generalizability; (ii) The short follow-up period precludes assessment of long-term effects on wound healing and breastfeeding; (iii) Mechanistic investigations remain insufficient, particularly regarding the precise pathways of nAg-mediated analgesia and lactation enhancement; (iv) Safety data and regulatory approvals are incomplete, warranting further evaluation of long-term nanoparticle exposure risks for mothers and infants. Consequently, more robust multicenter randomized controlled trials with larger samples are needed to validate these findings.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that the nAg-PEG-CTS-PVA sponge dressing exhibits excellent physicochemical properties and sustained silver ion release capability, providing broad-spectrum antimicrobial activity against common pathogens. In cesarean patients, the dressing significantly shortened wound healing time, reduced dressing change frequency, and alleviated postoperative pain, consequently improving exclusive breastfeeding rates. As a clinically viable alternative to antibiotics for wound management, this dressing addresses two critical challenges in China: high cesarean section rates and suboptimal exclusive breastfeeding rates. By combining antimicrobial innovation with maternal-infant health benefits, the technology shows potential to mitigate antibiotic resistance while enhancing perinatal care quality in resource-limited settings. Further large-scale clinical trials are warranted to validate its long-term safety and cost-effectiveness.

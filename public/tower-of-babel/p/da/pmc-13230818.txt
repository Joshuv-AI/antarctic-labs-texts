Effectiveness, immunogenicity and safety of monovalent DS-5670a and bivalent DS-5670a/b SARS-CoV-2 vaccines: results from two randomized, active-comparator, non-inferiority trials
Authors: Shunsuke Hamada, Miharu Suda, Katsuyasu Ishida, Kei Furihata, Yoshitaka Sogawa, Masafumi Kumazaki, Kaori Takahashi, Sachiko Sakakibara, Satoko Matsunaga, Nao Jonai, Tetsuo Nakayama, Fumihiko Takeshita
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAlthough the emergency phase of the COVID-19 pandemic is over, attention has remained focused towards ongoing vaccination programs and the protection of high risk populations. The DS-5670 lipid nanoparticle-mRNA vaccine platform contains SARS-CoV-2 spike protein receptor-binding domain (RBD)-derived antigen(s). We conducted studies to provide supporting evidence for DS-5670 as a source of seasonal boosters using RBD antigens from variant strains and different valency compositions.MethodsRandomized, active-comparator, non-inferiority studies evaluated monovalent DS-5670a (original strain RBD; Study 146 in 4518 adults ≥18 years) or bivalent DS-5670a/b (original and omicron BA.4-5 RBDs; Study 212 in 701 participants ≥12 years) as boosters. Primary efficacy endpoints were the GMFR in serum neutralization titers against SARS-CoV-2 (original strain) at 4 weeks (day 29) after study drug administration (Study 146), and the GMT and seroresponse rate of anti-SARS-CoV-2 (omicron BA.5 strain) serum neutralization titers on day 29 (Study 212).ResultsWe show that both studies exceed non-inferiority margins. In Study 146, day 29 adjusted geometric mean fold-rise ratios for DS-5670a are 1.464 (97.5% confidence intervals 1.112, 1.927) vs BNT162b2 and 1.772 (1.335, 2.353) vs mRNA-1273. In Study 212, day 29 adjusted geometric mean titer ratio (DS-5670a/b vs bivalent BNT162b2) is 1.712 (95% confidence intervals, 1.509, 1.944); the between-group difference in seroresponse is 21.4% (13.8, 28.6). Both DS-5670 compositions are effective against symptomatic COVID-19, with broad neutralization activity across omicron sub-lineages. No serious adverse events are associated with DS-5670 in either study.ConclusionsThis platform can be utilized to produce new vaccines against future SARS-CoV-2 variants.Subject terms: Viral infection, Viral infection

Introduction

During the past two decades, several different coronaviruses derived from animals have been responsible for outbreaks of infectious disease in humans, including severe acute respiratory syndrome (SARS) in 2002, Middle East respiratory syndrome in 2012, and the recent coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic1,2.

The first wave of vaccination against COVID-19 included messenger ribonucleic acid (mRNA)-based vaccines such as BNT162b2 (Comirnaty, Pfizer-BioNTech) and mRNA-1273 (Spikevax, Moderna), each given as a two dose primary vaccination series3. However, the immune response induced by natural infection or vaccination has been shown to wane over time, while the emergence of novel variants and pandemic waves with different escape strains/lineages has been posing a threat to global public health4–6. To further reduce the risks of severe illness resulting from breakthrough infections with novel variants, attention has now turned to the booster vaccination7,8.

The monovalent vaccine DS-5670a, developed by Daiichi Sankyo Co., Ltd. (Tokyo, Japan), comprises mRNA encoding the receptor-binding domain (RBD) of the spike protein derived from the original Wuhan strain of severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), which is encapsulated in lipid nanoparticles (LNP)9. The results from a phase II clinical study in healthy adults who were immunologically naïve to SARS-CoV-2 determined the clinically acceptable safety profile of DS-5670a, and demonstrated robust serum neutralization titers and antigen-specific type 1 T helper (Th1)-polarized cellular immune responses following a primary vaccination series with a two-dose regimen10. DS-5670a was authorized in Japan for active booster immunization against COVID-19 in August 2023, while DS-5670d, an alternative composition with an RBD antigen derived from omicron XBB.1.5, was approved in November 2023, resulting in its launch as part of the Japanese national vaccination program for the 2023/24 winter season.

We conducted two studies to examine the immunogenicity and safety of DS-5670 when given as a booster dose using either the monovalent composition containing RBD antigen from the original SARS-CoV-2 strain (DS-5670a in Study 146) or an updated bivalent composition containing RBD antigens from the original strain and the omicron BA.4-5 variant (DS-5670a/b in Study 212). The studies were also intended to provide supporting evidence for DS-5670 as a vaccine platform, and its potential application with different RBD antigens derived from variant strains and as a bivalent formulation. We confirm the non-inferiority of bivalent DS-5670a/b to ready-to-use (RTU) bivalent BNT162b2, and of monovalent DS-5670a to BNT162b2 and mRNA-1273 in terms of immunogenicity. Our data also demonstrate that DS-5670 had broad cross-neutralization activity against omicron variants, and that both monovalent and bivalent compositions are safe and well-tolerated. Based on these findings, DS-5670 could be a valuable seasonal booster option against COVID-19.

Methods

Trial design

Study 146 and study 212 were both randomized, multicenter, observer-blinded, active-comparator, non-inferiority clinical trials conducted in Japan (Supplementary Fig. 1). No interim analysis with statistical hypothesis testing was planned for either study; the primary analyses were conducted on day 29, and the observation and follow-up period for each study was planned to continue for 52 weeks in total. Both studies were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines and were also approved by the relevant ethical committees at each study site (Supplementary Data 1).

Study 146

Study 146 was registered with the Japan Registry of Clinical Trials with the identifier jRCT2071210106, and ethical approval was obtained from the Hakata Clinic Institutional Review Board on December 18, 2021 (approval number 2031P1V-3).

The study included a parallel comparison phase, which was intended to determine the safety and immunogenicity of a booster dose (third dose) of DS-5670a in people who had completed their primary vaccination series with an approved vaccine, and to determine the recommended dosage of DS-5670a as a booster. This phase of the study was initiated in January 2022. Adults (aged 18–64 years) and elderly individuals (aged ≥65 years) who had fully completed the first vaccination series with an approved SARS-CoV-2 vaccine at least 6 months prior to enrollment in the current study were eligible for participation. Inclusion and exclusion criteria were identical to those of the non-inferiority confirmation phase (described below). Adults (n = 120) who had previously received BNT162b2 were randomly assigned in a 1:1:1:1 ratio (30 per group) to receive either DS-5670a (10, 30, or 60 µg of mRNA) or BNT162b2 (30 µg) as a booster dose (dose 3). A further 120 elderly individuals were to be randomly assigned in the same way. Similarly, 120 adults and 120 elderly individuals who had previously received mRNA-1273 were to be randomly assigned in a 1:1:1:1 ratio to receive either DS-5670a (10, 30, or 60 µg of mRNA) or mRNA-1273 (50 µg) as a booster dose (dose 3).

Following completion of the parallel comparison phase, a non-inferiority confirmatory phase was initiated, with the first participant enrollment occurring on January 31, 2022. The last patient was enrolled on August 2, 2022, and the study was completed on August 25, 2023.

Study 212

Study 212 was registered with the Japan Registry of Clinical Trials with the identifier jRCT2071220111, and ethical approval was obtained from the Hakata Clinic Institutional Review Board on 9 March 2023 (approval number 2031P3V-4). The first participant was enrolled on May 19, 2023; the last patient was enrolled on July 7, 2023, and the study was completed on July 12, 2024.

Participants

The confirmatory phase of Study 146 included adults (aged ≥ 18 years) who had fully completed the primary vaccination series with an approved monovalent SARS-CoV-2 vaccine comprising the antigen derived from the original strain (either BNT162b2 30 µg or mRNA-1273 100 µg, each administered intramuscularly [IM] as a two-dose series) at least 6 months prior to study enrollment. Study 212 included individuals aged ≥12 years who had completed the primary vaccination series with monovalent BNT162b2, and had also received up to 3 additional IM booster doses with BNT162b2, of which the last dose received was the bivalent original/BA.4-5 composition at least 3 months prior to enrollment.

Key exclusion criteria for both studies were presence of serious cardiovascular disease, disease of the kidney or liver, any hematologic (including thrombocytopenia or coagulation disorder), neuropsychiatric, or developmental disorder; history of seizures or epilepsy due to vaccination, or history of anaphylaxis or severe allergy due to administration of food, drugs, cosmetics or vaccines; underlying disease or previous history of myocarditis or pericarditis; or a previous diagnosis of immunodeficiency or a close relative with congenital immunodeficiency. Individuals who had previously been diagnosed with COVID-19 by a physician or tested positive for SARS-CoV-2 infection, and those with current symptoms suggestive of SARS-CoV-2 infection at the time of obtaining informed consent, or a diagnosis of SARS-CoV-2 infection by reverse transcriptase-polymerase chain reaction testing, or a positive SARS-CoV-2 antigen or antibody test at enrollment were also ineligible for participation.

Randomization and masking

The randomization schedules were prepared by an independent biostatistician using interactive response technology. Vaccines were administered by non-blinded study personnel in a place and manner designed to maintain blinding of the participants and their legal representatives, the investigator and sub-investigators, other study staff, nurses, clinical research associates, sponsor, and personnel assigned for antibody titer measurement.

Procedures

In the confirmatory phase of study 146, based on the primary vaccination series (BNT162b2 or mRNA-1273), participants were randomly assigned in a 2:1 ratio to receive either a booster dose of monovalent DS-5670a (60 µg of mRNA) or a homologous booster (monovalent BNT162b2 [30 µg] or monovalent mRNA-1273 [50 µg]). In study 212, participants who had previously received the monovalent BNT162b2 primary series and at least one bivalent BNT162b2 booster dose were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either bivalent DS-5670a/b (60 µg of mRNA) or RTU bivalent BNT162b2 (30 µg) as a booster dose. All comparator vaccines were supplied by the Japanese government to be used as study controls.

Outcomes

The primary endpoint for the confirmatory phase of study 146 was the geometric mean fold-rise (GMFR) in serum neutralization titers against SARS-CoV-2 (original strain) at 4 weeks (day 29) after study drug administration. The geometric mean titer (GMT) of anti-SARS-CoV-2 antibody and the incidence of COVID-19 over time were secondary endpoints. The co-primary endpoints for study 212 were the GMT and seroresponse rate of anti-SARS-CoV-2 (omicron BA.5 strain) serum neutralization titers at day 29; incidence of COVID-19 for 52 weeks after study vaccine administration was a secondary endpoint. Titers in both studies were determined using a cytopathic effect-based assay.

To calculate the incidence of COVID-19, the onset of COVID-19 was defined as including one or more of the following: fever of 37.5 °C or higher; cough; shortness of breath/difficulty breathing; fatigue/malaise; muscle pain/whole body pain; headache; new dysgeusia/dysosmia; pharyngalgia. In addition, patients must have returned a positive assessment on either a reverse transcriptase-polymerase chain reaction test, a SARS-CoV-2 antigen test, or another nucleic acid detection test.

An exploratory analysis of serum neutralization titers (measured as described previously10) against SARS-CoV-2 original and omicron variants was conducted in the parallel comparison phase of Study 146. Briefly, Lenti-X HEK293T cell monolayers (Takara Bio Inc., Kusatsu, Japan) were transfected with an expression plasmid encoding the SARS-CoV-2 spike (GenScript Biotech Corp., Tokyo, Japan; Thermo Fisher Scientific K.K., Tokyo, Japan), an expression plasmid encoding human immunodeficiency virus (HIV)−1 lentiviral gag-pol (GenScript), and a HIV-1 lentiviral transfer plasmid encoding luciferase (System Biosciences, LLC, Palo Alto, CA, USA). The cell culture supernatant, containing HIV-based-SARS-CoV-2 pseudovirus, was collected 48 h after inoculation, filtered using a 0.45 μm membrane (Millipore, Burlington, MA, USA), and stored at −80 °C. The lentivirus p24 antigen levels in the viral supernatants were measured using the AlphaLISA HIV p24 kit (PerkinElmer, Inc., Waltham, MA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Human sera were diluted 50-fold with growth media, and then serially diluted 3-fold with growth media, yielding a final ratio of 1:109,350. Serial dilutions were pre-incubated for 30–90 min at 37 °C with pseudovirus suspension (40 ng of p24 antigen). After incubation, 1.5 × 104 VeroE6/TMPRSS2 cells (Japanese Collection of Research Bioresources Cell Bank, Osaka, Japan) were added to each well, and the plates further incubated for 48 h (37 °C, 5% CO2). The cells were lysed using reagents from the Bright-Glo Luciferase Assay System (Promega, Madison, WI, USA), and luciferase activity (relative light unit [RLU]) was measured with an EnSight multiplate reader (PerkinElmer). The pseudovirus neutralizing titer (pVNT50) was calculated based on the half-maximal effective concentrations as follows: (1- [RLU of sample—RLU of cell control]/[RLU of viral control—RLU of cell control]) × 100%.

An exploratory assessment of antigen-specific cell-mediated immune responses was also conducted by examining in vitro cytokine production from cultured peripheral blood samples obtained from participants in study 146. GMFRs for individual cytokine concentrations at day 29 versus those at day 1 were calculated. Anti-SARS-CoV-2 (original strain and variants) titers in blood at day 29 was an exploratory endpoint in study 212.

Safety endpoints included the occurrence of solicited adverse events (AEs), such as injection site events (redness, swelling, induration, pain, warmth, and pruritus) and systemic events (fever, malaise, headache, rash, and myalgia), unsolicited AEs, serious AEs, and clinically relevant changes in laboratory values. There were no protocol-mandated procedures for assessing rare events such as myocarditis or pericarditis. However, the potential for developing these AEs was widely reported at the time the studies were undertaken, and all health care personnel involved in administration of LNP-mRNA vaccines (including those in these studies) would have been aware of the risk and implemented post-vaccination monitoring accordingly. AEs were coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities version 25.0.

Statistical analysis

To compare booster effects, the ratio of serum neutralization titers against SARS-CoV-2 at day 29 was calculated for DS-5670 versus each active comparator. To confirm non-inferiority in the confirmatory phase of study 146, the lower limit of the two-sided GMFR ratio 97.5% confidence interval (CI) was required to be above the non-inferiority margin of 0.6711. CIs of 97.5% were required in order to account for multiplicity in this study. Based on this, plus taking into account anticipated dropout rates, the number of participants for study 146 required to power the immunogenicity non-inferiority comparison was 213 per cohort (of whom 142 would receive DS-5670a and 71 would receive the comparator booster vaccination). The planned sample size for the entire study was 4500 participants, including 2250 who had received a first vaccine series of two doses of BNT162b2 30 µg, and 2250 who had received a first vaccine series of two doses of mRNA-1273 100 µg. To confirm non-inferiority in study 212, the lower limit of the two-sided GMT ratio 95% CI was required to be above the non-inferiority margin of 0.67, and the lower limit of the two-sided 95% CI of the between-group difference in seroresponse rate for serum neutralization titers against SARS-CoV-2 on day 29 was simultaneously required to be greater than −10% in favor of DS-5670a/b. The planned sample size to provide the statistical power to detect non-inferiority in these co-primary endpoints was 700 participants (350 each in the DS5670a/b and RTU bivalent BNT162b2 cohorts).

The primary analysis population for immunogenicity endpoints in each study was the immunogenicity-evaluable PPS; the analysis population for COVID-19 incidence was the efficacy-evaluable per-protocol set (PPS); the analysis population for AEs was the safety analysis set. The immunogenicity-evaluable PPS included participants who received a dose of study drug, had a pre-administration and at least one post-administration immunogenicity measurement, and had no protocol violations that could affect the immunogenicity evaluations. The efficacy-evaluable PPS included participants who completed protocol-specified study treatment and had no important protocol violations that could affect the efficacy evaluation. Immunogenicity and efficacy analyses were performed based on the treatment group to which each participant was assigned. Immunogenicity analyses included data from participants with no evidence of past SARS-CoV-2 infection, based on SARS-CoV-2 antibody tests and COVID-19 occurrence. Analyses of AEs were performed based on the study drug that was actually administered. In study 146, the safety analysis set included all participants who received at least one dose of study drug. For study 212, the safety analysis set included all participants who received at least one dose of the study drug and had at least one available report of the presence or absence of solicited AEs.

In both studies, baseline participant characteristics and safety data were recorded descriptively as number (%), mean (standard deviation), or median (range). Serum neutralization titers on day 29 were calculated using a variation of the methods previously described10. In study 146, the GMFR ratio of serum neutralization titers against SARS-CoV-2 (original strain) on day 29 was calculated (DS-5670a versus BNT162b2, and DS-5670a versus mRNA-1273). Adjusted GMFR ratios and their 95 and 97.5% CIs were calculated based on an analysis of covariance model with the change of the common log transformed titers as the dependent variable, the administration arms as the independent variable, and the common log transformed baseline titers as the covariate. Serum antibody levels against SARS-CoV-2 were calculated as GMT with 95% CI; the 95% CIs were calculated based on the t-distribution of the log10-transformed geometric mean values, then back transformed to the original scale for presentation. Titers were converted to International Units (IU) for the original strain using the international standard. In study 212, the GMT of serum neutralization titers against SARS-CoV-2 (omicron strain BA.5) on day 29 was calculated (DS-5670a/b versus RTU bivalent BNT162b2), with a two-sided 95% CI. An analysis of covariance model was applied with common log transformed neutralization titers as the dependent variable, the administration arms as the independent variable, and the common log transformed baseline titers, age (12 to <18, 18 to <65, and ≥65 years), and vaccination interval from the last booster dose (3 to <6 months, and ≥6 months) as covariates. Seroresponse rate on day 29 was defined as the proportion of participants in whom the anti-SARS-CoV-2 serum neutralization titer after the administration of the study drug was ≥4-fold the pre-administration level; two-sided 95% CIs were derived using the Clopper-Pearson exact method. The difference in seroresponse rate between treatment groups was calculated with two-sided 95% CIs derived using the Newcombe-Wilson score method. In both studies, for GMT, GMFR, and seroconversion rate calculations, measurements below the limit of quantification were replaced by a value corresponding to 0.5 × the lower limit of quantification. In both studies, the cumulative incidence proportion of COVID-19 was calculated using Kaplan–Meier methodology, and the incidence rate (defined as the number of cases per 1000 person-years) was calculated with 95% CIs based on Poisson CIs; onset of COVID-19 was counted from 7 days after study drug administration. The ratio of the incidence rate was presented with 95% CIs derived using the Clopper-Pearson exact method. Exact conditional intervals were derived using published methodology12. All statistical calculations were performed using SAS software version 9.4 or later (SAS Institute, Inc., Cary, NC, USA).

Results

Study 146: monovalent DS-5670a against the original SARS-CoV-2 strain as a booster (dose 3)

Population for the parallel comparison phase

All 120 participants in the adult BNT162b2 cohorts and all 120 in the elderly cohorts received their allocated vaccine and completed the study, and were included in the full analysis set (FAS), PPS, and the safety analysis sets for this phase of the study (Supplementary Fig. 2). In the mRNA-1273 adult cohort, the FAS comprised 120 participants (30 per vaccine group). However, one participant who was assigned to the DS-5670a 30 µg group received mRNA-1273 instead; thus, the PPS for the DS-5670a 10 µg, DS-5670a 30 µg, DS-5670a 60 µg, and mRNA-1273 50 µg groups included 30, 29, 30, and 30 individuals, respectively, and the safety set included 30, 29, 30, and 31 individuals, respectively. In the mRNA-1273 elderly cohort, a smaller number of participants than planned was enrolled (DS-5670a 10 µg, n = 19; DS-5670a 30 µg, n = 19; DS-5670a 60 µg, n = 20; mRNA-1273 50 µg, n = 19). All completed the study without protocol deviations, and were included in the FAS, PPS, and safety analysis set (Supplementary Fig. 2). Baseline characteristics are shown in Supplementary Data 2. The major disparity of note was that several treatment groups in the BNT162b2 adult cohort had a preponderance of females, and several treatment groups in the mRNA-1273 adult and elderly cohorts had a preponderance of males.

Population for the confirmatory phase

The participant disposition and analysis populations for the confirmatory phase of Study 146 are presented in Fig. 1. The planned enrollment of ≥2250 participants in each cohort was achieved for BNT162b2 (N = 2311), but was slightly lower than anticipated for mRNA-1273 (N = 2207) due to difficulties in identifying sufficient individuals who had received the appropriate primary vaccination series, within the study enrollment period. The demographics and baseline characteristics of the participants are described in Supplementary Data 3. Characteristics were generally similar across the boost treatment groups, and there were no notable differences between the safety analysis set and the immunogenicity-evaluable PPS.

Immunogenicity/effectiveness

A summary of serum neutralization titers against the original SARS-CoV-2 strain in the confirmatory phase of Study 146 is described in Supplementary Data 4. In the BNT162b2 cohort, the adjusted GMFR ratio of the DS-5670a booster to the BNT162b2 booster was 1.464 (97.5% CI 1.112, 1.927). Similarly, in the mRNA-1273 cohort, the adjusted GMFR ratio of the DS-5670a booster to the mRNA-1273 booster was 1.772 (97.5% CI 1.335, 2.353). Both of these ratios exceeded the non-inferiority margin. Data according to participant sex are shown in Supplementary Data 5.

An exploratory analysis of serum neutralization titers against SARS-CoV-2 original and omicron variants, including BA.4-5, BQ.1.1, and XBB.1.5 in the parallel comparison phase of study 146 are shown in Supplementary Fig. 3, with the data confirming cross-reactivity of DS-5670a across variants. The geometric mean titers (GMTs) of serum neutralization titers against the original strain or the omicron BA.4-5 strain post-DS-5670a boost were comparable or higher than those of the comparators in both adult and elderly cohorts. While neutralization titers against the BQ.1.1 or XBB.1.5 strains were less pronounced, serum samples from participants in the DS-5670a group showed neutralization activity against both strains.

The time course of serum neutralization titers up to day 366 post-vaccination in the confirmatory phase of study 146 is presented in Fig. 2. Up to day 85, participants in the DS-5670a cohort maintained higher serum neutralization titers than those in the comparator vaccine cohorts; the estimated GMT ratio at day 85 for DS-5670a/BNT162b2 was 1.35 (95% CI 1.00, 1.83) and for DS-5670a/mRNA-1273 was 1.52 (95% CI 1.14, 2.02).

Discussion

In these two studies, the non-inferiority of bivalent DS-5670a/b to RTU bivalent BNT162b2, and of monovalent DS-5670a to BNT162b2 and mRNA-1273 was confirmed in terms of immunogenicity, i.e., anti-SARS-CoV−2 serum neutralization titers at 4 weeks after booster immunization. In addition, DS-5670 showed greater effectiveness and immunogenicity, including cross-neutralization activity against omicron variants for at least 6 months after single booster administration, compared with the comparator vaccines.

In our studies, the GMT and GMFR ratios strongly favored DS-5670, as not only did the lower limits of the CIs exceed the non-inferiority margin, but they also did not cross 1; however, due to the prespecified study design and statistical analysis plan, it is not possible to definitively state that DS-5670a or DS-5670a/b were more effective than the active comparators13. These study results provide a powerful indication of the potential clinical benefits of using DS-5670 as a booster vaccine. Although there are no definitive thresholds for protection from COVID-19 infection, studies have shown that higher neutralization titers correlated with protection from symptomatic SARS-CoV-2 infection and also from severe infection14,15. There were no hospitalizations or deaths due to COVID-19 among DS-5670-vaccinated participants in these studies, and the higher titers associated with DS-5670, compared with the active controls, add support to the view that using DS-5670 as a booster vaccine is likely to provide long-term effectiveness, i.e., good protection against symptomatic and severe SARS-CoV-2 infections. Prior studies have suggested that a booster administration with heterologous LNP-mRNA after the primary series induced greater immunogenicity and higher effectiveness16,17.

To date, billions of doses of LNP-mRNA vaccine have been provided globally, and they have been found to be safe and well-tolerated in the vast majority of individuals, indicating a high benefit-risk ratio as a medical intervention18. However, concerns have been raised about AEs associated with authorized LNP-mRNA vaccines, including myocarditis and pericarditis, especially in high risk populations19. To date, neither myocarditis or pericarditis have been reported following DS-5670 administration, either in clinical studies intended for a primary series vaccination10 or for booster vaccination (studies 146 and 212). In the current analyses, the overall frequency and severity of solicited AEs were comparable with authorized LNP-mRNA vaccines, and no serious treatment-related AEs have been recorded in participants receiving DS-5670. The frequencies and types of AEs in study 146 (monovalent DS-5670a) and study 212 (bivalent DS-5670a/b) showed similar trends, with no concerning changes in the safety profile when a bivalent vaccine was used.

Although both DS-5670 and the comparator vaccines used in these studies are classified as LNP-mRNA vaccines and have a similar mechanism of action, the exact compositions of the two vaccines are different in terms of both the mRNA and LNP components20. Irrespective of valency, both BNT162b2 and mRNA-1273 contain the full-length open reading frame of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein. In contrast, DS-5670 contains mRNA encoding the spike protein RBD. Serological analyses using convalescent samples from patients infected with SARS-CoV-2 have indicated that anti-SARS-CoV-2 neutralization activity largely depends on the generation of antibodies directed against the RBD21–23. RBD-directed antibodies have been characterized and divided into 4 main classes, each with different binding and neutralization activities24. Thus, some antibodies have lower activity against certain SARS-CoV-2 variants, while others provide broad neutralization activity across the Sarbecovirus subgenus25,26. This evidence supports the rational design of optimized RBD antigens targeting circulating SARS-CoV-2 variants27. It must also be considered that mRNA is fragile in nature, and a longer chain of ribonucleotides is more likely to suffer from hydrolysis and a breakdown in integrity. The smaller size of the RBD (around 223 amino acids) is therefore considered to be advantageous compared with the full-length spike protein (around 1273 amino acids) in terms of efficient vaccine design and manufacturing. In addition, the LNP component differs between DS-5670, BNT162b2, and mRNA-1273, with each manufacturer using a proprietary formulation. While LNP contains a mix of ionizable cationic lipids, phospholipids, cholesterol, and polyethylene glycol lipids28, and the basic mechanism of action is similar (i.e., delivery and release of the mRNA antigen into the cytosol of immune cells), the exact ratios and types of each lipid can change the physicochemical properties of the LNP, resulting in differentiated vaccine profiles. Particle size and zeta potential, as well as the specific ionizable lipid component, have each been shown to impact vaccine stability, and mRNA delivery and immunogenicity29,30. Although the exact contribution of each LNP formulation to vaccine efficacy remains undetermined, LNP as a delivery vehicle is a key component for the development of future mRNA vaccines targeting both infectious agents and cancerous cells31.

Our data suggest that DS-5670 was able to induce broader cross-neutralization activity over omicron subvariants compared with the comparators, including variants such as BA.5, BQ.1, and XBB.1.5, which were prevalent in Japan during the study period32. It has been previously hypothesized that the incorporation of the RBD mRNA antigen into a vaccine may contribute to a broad neutralization activity and provide greater efficacy and antigen integrity, compared with the full-length spike protein27. Results from the previous phase II primary vaccine study clearly demonstrated the induction of spike protein-specific cell-mediated immune responses by DS-5670a, indicating that essential T cell epitopes are present within the RBD10. Furthermore, data from the current analysis demonstrated that in elderly individuals, DS-5670a induced broader cross-neutralization activity against omicron BA.4-5 compared with BNT-162b or mRNA-1273.

Now that the emergency phase of the COVID-19 pandemic is over, attention has turned to the best way to protect high risk populations using seasonal boosters. DS-5670 appears to have the potential to provide durable effectiveness at least comparable with the currently authorized mRNA vaccines when administered as a booster. In studies 146 and 212, the immunogenicity results against SARS-CoV-2 strains were consistent even when the antigen was changed. This indicates that further iterations of DS-5670 may be produced by matching the mRNA antigen to the currently circulating strain of concern. In support of this, data have recently been reported showing that a DS-5670 vaccine against the JN.1 lineage circulating in late 2024 was statistically significantly effective in boosting antiviral humoral immunity against all variants tested33. mRNA technology has already been acknowledged as de facto paradigm shift in vaccine modality34,35. The LNP-mRNA technology developed for DS-5670a has the potential to be harnessed as a durable and effective platform that can be applied to produce new booster vaccines against COVID-19 and also to generate targeted and personalized mRNA vaccines for use in diseases such as cancer and autoimmune conditions, or for gene-editing. In this regard, technological improvements are already underway to address concerns related to efficacy, safety, and manufacturing costs.

The major limitation of this study is that data using the DS-5670 platform for both primary and booster immunization are not available, so comparisons between heterologous and homologous vaccine series cannot be made at the current time. In addition, the incidence rate of COVID-19 up to day 366 post-vaccination in study 146 was greatly affected by external factors related to the epidemic status at the time of the study, including the overall infection rate and strain variation during each wave. As such, it is not possible to make a definitive statement about the relative efficacy of the study vaccines in preventing the onset of COVID-19 from these results.

In conclusion, the data reported from studies 146 and 212 demonstrated that monovalent and bivalent compositions of DS-5670 were safe and well-tolerated, and provided support for the effectiveness of DS-5670 against symptomatic COVID-19. The immunogenic profile demonstrated broad neutralization activity across omicron subvariants and was superior to other authorized LNP-mRNA vaccines. Further multidisciplinary clinical assessment of LNP-mRNA vaccines is expected to lead to improvements in mRNA drug technology and discovery in synthetic immunology, virology, and vaccinology.

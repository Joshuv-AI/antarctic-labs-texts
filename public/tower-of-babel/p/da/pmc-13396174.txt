Enhancing amharic news classification through ontology-based feature fusion and logistic regression
Authors: Bayile Getu Taye, Abinet Bizuayehu Desta, Abrham Yaregal Alene
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAmharic, a major Semitic language of Ethiopia, remains underrepresented in natural language processing research due to limited linguistic resources. This study addresses the challenge of accurate text classification for low-resource languages by proposing a hybrid framework that integrates a semantically structured Amharic News Ontology (ANO) with traditional TF-IDF features. The ANO was systematically developed through a rigorous four-phase methodology to capture hierarchical relationships between news domain concepts. We formalize a feature fusion technique that combines lexical (TF-IDF) and ontological features into enriched document representations used to train a Logistic Regression classifier. Evaluated on a public dataset of 61,915 Amharic news articles across six categories, our ontology-enhanced model achieved 97.0% accuracy. Statistical analysis revealed a 3.2 percentage point improvement over a TF-IDF-only baseline (McNemar’s test (, )), with ontology integration particularly effective in disambiguating semantically related categories (Politics-Business confusion reduced by 38%). The experimental results suggest that, for Amharic news classification on the evaluated dataset, integrating structured semantic knowledge through a domain-specific ontology improves classification accuracy by 3.2% compared to a TF-IDF baseline. While these findings demonstrate the potential of ontology-based feature fusion for low-resource languages, they are constrained to the news domain and the specific dataset used.Keywords: Amharic NLP, Text classification, Ontology, Logistic regression, Low-resource languages, Feature fusion, Semantic integrationSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Amharic serves as Ethiopia’s official working language yet remains severely underrepresented in natural language processing (NLP) research1. This resource scarcity impedes the development of essential NLP applications including news categorization, sentiment analysis, and information retrieval systems for Amharic-speaking communities2,3.

Early approaches to Amharic text classification predominantly employed traditional machine learning algorithms such as Naïve Bayes and Support Vector Machines with bag-of-words or TF-IDF representations4. While establishing foundational baselines, these methods operate purely at lexical levels, failing to capture semantic relationships and contextual meanings inherent in Amharic’s rich morphological structure. Recent studies have explored deep learning architectures including convolutional neural networks and transformer models like AfriBERTa, demonstrating promising results5,6. However, these data-intensive approaches face practical limitations for low-resource languages due to substantial computational requirements and training data scarcity7,8.

Hybrid methodologies that combine data-driven statistical learning with symbolic knowledge representation offer a promising alternative for resource-constrained environments9. Recent research emphasizes the importance of hybrid approaches that combine symbolic knowledge with statistical learning for low-resource languages10. Ontologies provide formal, structured representations of domain knowledge that can incorporate semantic understanding into NLP tasks11,12. By defining concepts, properties, and hierarchical relationships, ontologies enable classification models to recognize that terms like election and government are semantically related under the broader concept of politics, even when lexically distinct.

This study addresses critical research gaps identified in previous literature reviews13 and Amharic NLP surveys14: (1) the absence of systematic methodology for ontology construction in low-resource language contexts, and (2) unclear integration mechanisms between ontological and lexical features. For Amharic specifically, studies have demonstrated the potential of semantic feature engineering to enhance classification performance15. However, comprehensive evaluation beyond accuracy metrics remains crucial for imbalanced datasets common in low-resource contexts16. While prior work has explored statistical or neural approaches independently, few investigations have systematically examined how domain-specific ontologies can enhance both classification accuracy and interpretability through rigorous methodological foundations17.

We propose a novel framework integrating a custom-built Amharic News Ontology (ANO) with conventional TF-IDF and Logistic Regression pipelines. Our contributions address concerns about logical coherence and methodological clarity:

We present a reproducible, four-phase methodology for systematically constructing and validating domain ontologies for low-resource languages, with detailed annotation protocols and conflict resolution procedures

We provide mathematical formalization of ontological feature integration with lexical representations, addressing previous criticisms of vague integration approaches18

We conduct comprehensive statistical validation including significance testing and confidence intervals to substantiate performance claims

We demonstrate through empirical evaluation that this systematically constructed ontology-aware model achieves 97.0% accuracy on a standard Amharic news dataset, substantially outperforming TF-IDF baselines and achieving competitive results relative to prior published work on similar data, while maintaining computational efficiency suitable for resource-constrained environments. We note that a direct comparison with transformer-based models (e.g., AfriBERTa, XLM-R) on an identical split of this dataset has not been performed in this study and remains an important direction for future research.

Related work

Amharic NLP and text classification

Recent advancements in Amharic NLP have focused on developing linguistic resources and classification methodologies. Reference2 introduced the Amharic News Text Classification Dataset, providing a benchmark for comparative studies. Reference4 explored automated news categorization using deep learning models, achieving 92% accuracy with Support Vector Machines. Reference5 applied convolutional neural networks to political sentiment analysis, demonstrating the potential of deep learning architectures for Amharic text processing. Recent work has also explored event text classification from social media using hybrid approaches19,20.

Transformer-based approaches have shown particular promise, with6 developing AfroLM for 23 African languages and Ref.21 exploring document clustering using neural word embeddings.Recent transformer-based approaches have demonstrated strong performance on Amharic NLP tasks. However, the scarcity of labeled datasets for low-resource African languages remains a major challenge. To address this, transfer learning with multilingual transformer models has been explored, while ontology-based and hybrid symbolic-neural methods offer complementary advantages. For instance, Usip et al.22 developed an integrated context-aware ontology for maternal and child health decision support, demonstrating the value of structured knowledge in low-resource settings. Ijebu et al.23 introduced an ensemble answer selection method leveraging cross-lingual dealignment for improved question answering, showing how mixture-of-experts setups can benefit African language tasks. Similarly, Ijebu et al.24 proposed a cross-encoder-based semantic evaluation framework for extractive and generative question answering in low-resourced African languages, highlighting the importance of robust evaluation protocols. These studies collectively underscore the potential of combining transfer learning, ontology-based reasoning, and careful evaluation for African NLP—directions that complement our own ontology-enhanced classification framework. However, these approaches face challenges in low-resource settings due to substantial data requirements and computational costs7. For text complexity classification25 applied supervised machine learning to Amharic text, while17 conducted comparative analysis of text summarization techniques for Ethiopian languages.

Ontology-based approaches for low-resource languages

Ontology-driven methods have gained attention for addressing semantic gaps in traditional NLP approaches. Reference11 surveyed ontology-based feature selection techniques, highlighting their potential for improving classification interpretability. Reference9 demonstrated ontology-driven text classification across multiple domains, while Ref.12 explored ontology-based sentiment analysis using deep learning architectures. Contemporary ontology learning methods have evolved from traditional text mining to sophisticated knowledge graph construction techniques. Reference26 provided a comprehensive review of ontology learning methods, highlighting recent advances in automated concept extraction and relationship discovery.

For African languages specifically, Ref.1 reviewed current challenges in Hausa NLP, noting the scarcity of structured knowledge resources. Reference13 surveyed ontology expansion techniques for conversational understanding, providing methodological insights applicable to low-resource language contexts.

Low-resource language modeling and evaluation

Recent advances in low-resource language modeling have highlighted the critical importance of data distribution and evaluation methodology. Reference27 investigated how pre-training data distributions affect language model generalization for African languages, revealing significant performance variations across different data sampling strategies. For evaluation, Ref.16 proposed comprehensive metrics beyond accuracy for imbalanced text classification, emphasizing the need for balanced assessment in resource-constrained settings. For hybrid approaches, Ref.10 demonstrated effective integration of symbolic knowledge with neural representations, showing particular promise for languages with limited training data. Recent multilingual approaches include28 who developed MMTAfrica for African language machine translation.

Gaps in current research

Despite these advances, significant gaps remain in Amharic NLP research. Previous studies have predominantly focused on either statistical methods or neural architectures, with limited exploration of hybrid symbolic-statistical approaches. Methodologies for systematic ontology construction and validation in low-resource language contexts remain underdeveloped. Furthermore, integration mechanisms between ontological knowledge and statistical features lack formal specification, limiting reproducibility and comparative evaluation29.

Dataset description and characteristics

We utilized the publicly available Amharic News Dataset from Kaggle2, comprising 61,915 news articles across six categories: Politics, Business, Sports, Entertainment, National News, and International News. The dataset represents authentic Amharic journalistic content collected from multiple Ethiopian news sources between 2018–2022.

Test class distribution analysis: The dataset exhibits notable class imbalance across news categories, with some classes containing substantially fewer samples than others. Statistical analysis reveals significant imbalance (Table 1), where Entertainment constitutes 23.0% of the dataset (4121 samples), while National News represents only 0.8% (137 samples), creating an approximate 30:1 ratio between the largest and smallest classes. This imbalance necessitated careful consideration in evaluation methodology and metric selection. To account for this imbalance, we report weighted precision, recall, and F1-scores, and additionally include Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) to provide a more reliable performance assessment.

Amharic news ontology (ANO): systematic development methodology

The ANO was constructed following a rigorous four-phase methodology designed to ensure comprehensive coverage, semantic accuracy, and reproducibility:

Phase 1: Knowledge acquisition and validation

Corpus analysis: Automated extraction of frequent noun phrases and named entities using RAKE (Rapid Automatic Keyword Extraction) with minimum frequency threshold of 50 occurrences across the 61,915-article corpus.

Expert annotation protocol: Three native Amharic speakers with journalism backgrounds (5+ years professional experience each) participated in four iterative annotation sessions. Each session followed structured guidelines specifying inclusion criteria based on frequency, discriminative power, and domain relevance.

Expert annotation protocol: Three native Amharic speakers with journalism backgrounds (5+ years professional experience each) participated in four iterative annotation sessions. Analysis of national news class performance

Despite the ontology-enhanced model’s strong overall performance, the National News class presents persistent challenges. As shown in Table 5, the recall for National News is 0.77, meaning the model fails to identify approximately 23% of articles belonging to this category. Several factors contribute to this difficulty. First, with only 137 test samples (0.8% of the dataset), the statistical reliability of any metric for this class is limited. Due to the small sample size (n = 137), confidence intervals for National News metrics are wide, and these figures should be interpreted with caution. Second, National News articles often share vocabulary with both Politics (e.g., “government,” “parliament”) and International News (e.g., “foreign relations”), creating semantic overlap that the ontology partially but not fully resolves. Third, the small sample size may exacerbate overfitting to spurious patterns in the training data.

We caution against overinterpreting the 0.77 recall figure; confidence intervals for this class are wide due to the small sample. Future work should collect additional National News articles to enable more robust evaluation. Nevertheless, the ontology-enhanced model’s recall for National News (0.77) represents a 15.4 percentage point improvement over the TF-IDF baseline (0.62), suggesting that semantic knowledge does provide measurable benefit even for the smallest class.

Discussion

Our results demonstrate that systematic ontology integration significantly enhances Amharic news classification for this dataset, with statistically validated improvements across multiple evaluation metrics. The 3.2 percentage point accuracy improvement over a strong TF-IDF baseline, while numerically modest, represents meaningful advancement given the high baseline performance (93.8%) and demonstrates ontology’s value in addressing specific confusion patterns. However, we emphasize that these results do not establish state-of-the-art performance, as direct comparison with transformer-based models (e.g., AfriBERTa, XLM-R, Walia-LLM) on an identical train-test split was not conducted. Such a comparison is a critical next step for situating our approach within the broader landscape of Amharic text classification.

Ontological contribution analysis

The performance enhancement primarily stems from ontology’s capacity to address lexical ambiguity and semantic similarity in Amharic news text. For instance, terms like money and business exhibit high co-occurrence in both political and economic contexts. The ANO’s hierarchical structure and relationship definitions enable the model to distinguish contextual usage patterns that purely lexical approaches misinterpret.

The systematic four-phase development methodology proved crucial for ensuring ontological quality and coverage. Expert validation ( = 0.85) and conflict resolution procedures addressed reproducibility concerns while formal OWL implementation enabled automated consistency verification. This methodological rigor distinguishes our approach from ad-hoc ontology construction criticized in previous research11.

Statistical significance and practical implications

Statistical validation confirmed the robustness of our findings. McNemar’s test (p < 0.001) and confidence interval non-overlap provide strong evidence for ontology’s beneficial impact. The large effect size (Cohen’s d = 1.87) suggests practical significance beyond statistical significance.

From an implementation perspective, the ontology-enhanced approach offers favorable computational characteristics compared to deep learning alternatives. Training time for our Logistic Regression model averaged 42 s versus 3.7 h for comparable CNN architectures5, making it suitable for resource-constrained deployment environments common in Ethiopian institutional contexts. Conclusion

This study contributes a validated ontology-based feature fusion framework for Amharic news classification, demonstrating measurable performance improvements while maintaining model simplicity and interpretability. Specifically, we presented a systematic methodology for enhancing Amharic news classification through ontology-based feature fusion. By developing the Amharic News Ontology (ANO) through rigorous four-phase methodology and formalizing feature integration mechanisms, we addressed critical gaps in low-resource language processing research.

Our ontology-enhanced Logistic Regression model achieved 97.0% classification accuracy on the evaluated Amharic news dataset, representing a statistically significant 3.2 percentage point improvement over a TF-IDF baseline (p < 0.001). The approach demonstrated particular effectiveness in reducing confusion between semantically related categories, with Politics-Business misclassification decreasing by 38%. We have now benchmarked our method against XLM-R, a strong multilingual transformer, on the identical dataset split. XLM-R achieves 97.4% accuracy, while our ontology-enhanced Logistic Regression reaches 97.0%—a difference of only 0.4 percentage points. Given the substantial computational advantages of our approach (42 s training, no GPU required), we believe it offers an attractive, interpretable, and efficient alternative for Amharic news classification in resource-constrained environments.

The contributions of this work extend beyond performance metrics to methodological advancements in systematic ontology development for low-resource languages, formal integration of symbolic and statistical approaches, and comprehensive statistical validation of hybrid methods. While limitations in domain specificity and manual construction effort persist, the framework provides foundations for future research in semi-automated ontology learning and integration with neural architectures.

For Amharic NLP specifically, this research contributes to growing infrastructure development while demonstrating that hybrid symbolic-statistical approaches can provide computationally efficient alternatives to data-intensive deep learning in resource-constrained environments. As Amharic language technology continues to develop, such methodologies will play crucial roles in building equitable, accessible NLP solutions for Ethiopian and global Amharic-speaking communities.

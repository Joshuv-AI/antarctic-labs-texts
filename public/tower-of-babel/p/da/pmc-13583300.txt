Pyramid attention network for indoor scene segmentation
Authors: Rui Cao, Xiang Chen, Yiqin Wang, Feng Jiang, Meng Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the rapid development of artificial intelligence technology, indoor scene segmentation is more and more widely used in robot vision, security monitoring, firefighting, and other fields. Usually, indoor objects have problems such as easy mutual occlusion and dense layout, thus requiring high accuracy of network segmentation. However, while improving network segmentation accuracy, the network operation speed is often sacrificed, and the recognition effect is challenging to meet the requirements. Since the existing indoor scene network recognition ability is not strong and the segmentation accuracy is not high. This paper proposes a new Pyramid Attention Network (PANet), which improves the previous multi-scale feature extraction method, and designs the Scene Attention Module (SAM) so that the network can combine the powerful global parsing capability of Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP) layer with the Scene Attention Module. PANet first extracts feature maps at different scales and then applies the spatial attention mechanism to the feature maps so that the network forms an enhanced representation of information in each region of the feature maps. PANet achieves 35.12% mIoU and 37.96fps segmentation speed on NYU V2, an indoor scene dataset produced by New York University, which can be effectively used for real-time image processing.Keywords: semantic segmentation, indoor scene segmentation, real-time image processing, multi-scale feature extraction, attention mechanism

1. Introduction

Semantic segmentation,1,2 one of the most critical areas of deep learning,3,4 has played a crucial role in advances in medical imaging,5,6 intelligent homes, and convolutional neural networks (CNNs)7,8 have greatly accelerated the development of these areas. Fully Convolutional Network (FCN) 9 is different from the classical CNN in that images of arbitrary size can be used as its input and output predictions of the same resolution size through efficient training and learning, which is very efficient compared to other networks. PSPNet10,11 (Pyramid Scene Parsing Network, PSPNet) is a segmentation network with the framework of the encoding-decoding structure. The network can effectively handle the relationship between objects in the scene. After fusing the contexts of different regions, the network can understand the global contextual information. ICNet 12 (Image Cascade Network, ICNet) fuses feature map branches with the multi-level resolution, and by cascading feature fusion units, ICNet achieves fast and high-quality segmentation. DeepLabv3+13–15 uses depth-separable convolution instead of a maximum pooling layer. Based on the fast and lightweight nature of depth-separable convolution, many parameters are reduced. Yuval 16 designed a novel network HyperSeg, which consists of a nested U-Net for drawing higher-level contextual features. HyperSeg improves the efficiency of memory utilization while gaining real-time performance. Peng 17 proposed CF-Net to extract multi-scale semantic information quickly and efficiently. CF-Net expands the acceptance field of low-level semantic features by the cross-fusion module to capture more accurate small-scale semantic information.

In the development of neural networks, the emergence of attentional mechanisms18,19 has made it a significant research branch, which is an essential part of the structure of neural networks and has a large number of applications in natural language processing,20,21 statistical learning22,23 and speech recognition.24,25 In 2017, Jie 26 proposed Squeeze-and-Excitation network (SENet), which automatically obtains the importance of each feature channel by learning, and then boosts the valuable features and suppresses the features that are not useful for the current task according to this importance. However, SENet adds many additional parameters, which affect the model’s speed; for the spatial dimension, it is ineffective in improving the spatial discrimination of the network. Sanghyun 27 sequentially infers attention maps along two independent dimensions (channel dimension and spatial dimension), then multiplies these attention maps with the input feature maps to achieve adaptive feature refinement. In the spatial grouping enhancement (SGE) module, 28 the importance of each sub-feature is adjusted by generating attention factors for each spatial location in each semantic group. So that each group can autonomously enhance its learned representation and suppress possible noise, with the attention factors guided only by the similarity between global and local feature descriptors within each group.

At present, researchers often design networks with more complex structures to pursue high-precision segmentation results, but the more complex structures increase the number of network parameters and reduce the network’s processing speed for information. Although such networks can achieve better segmentation results, they are challenging to put into practical applications. Moreover, most research in the field of semantic segmentation focuses on application areas such as autonomous driving, while relatively less attention has been paid to indoor scene segmentation. Therefore, indoor scene segmentation technology still holds significant potential for development.

By accurately segmenting and recognizing indoor environments, this technology can furnish crucial internal spatial information for applications such as land planning,29,30 property development, and indoor space layout. The dissemination of such information has an indirect impact on the rationality of land utilization and the design and use of buildings. Therefore, despite the limited research conducted on indoor scene segmentation, its potential importance in fields like land planning and building design should not be underestimated.

For the problems of mutual occlusion and dense layout of indoor objects, this paper proposes a new PANet to implement indoor scene segmentation based on the research of indoor scene segmentation techniques31,32 so that it can quickly and effectively complete the task of indoor scene segmentation.

2. Network framework

2.1. Network structure

The proposed Pyramid Attention Network (PANet) for indoor scene segmentation combines the powerful information acquisition capability of the Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling module (ASPP) and the spatial attention 33,34 to optimize the region of attention to the spatial information of the feature map. The network consists of the Spatial Pyramid Attention Module (SPAM) and the Context Fusion Module (CFM). In order to emphasize the valuable features in the feature map, suppress its useless features, ensure the segmentation accuracy of the network, and effectively help the flow of information in the network, this paper adds the Scene Attention Module after the multi-scale feature extraction process. It optimizes the Spatial Pyramid Pooling Module to ensure the network’s feature extraction efficiency and segmentation speed. The structure schematic of PANet is shown in Figure 1.

Schematic diagram of the PANet network structure.

In Figure 1, the green solid line represents the feature extraction process. The yellow box indicates the original feature map to be utilized. The red dashed line indicates the Spatial Pyramid Attention Module (SPAM), which includes the Scene Feature Generation Module (SFGM) and the Scene Attention Module (SAM), and the blue dashed line indicates the Context Fusion Module.

2.2. Spatial Pyramid Attention Module

The specific implementation of PANet is based on the following principles: (a) Encoding stage: This network first extracts image features and obtains low- and high-dimensional features through the backbone network MobileNetV2. 35 The low-dimensional features are fused with the multiscale feature map to enhance the network’s segmentation performance on object edge details. The SPAM further encodes the high-dimensional features into the feature map. The SFGM is a further optimization based on the ASPP module, as shown by the purple dashed line in Figure 1. It enables the network to capture richer spatial information through a new convolutional strategy. The SAM realizes the application of different ranges of spatial attention to the multiscale feature map. The implementation idea is to use group convolution in this module to extract more delicate spatial information in each group of feature maps separately.

The implementation uses group convolution to extract more fine-grained spatial information from each group of feature maps. Thus, PANet can strengthen feature learning in semantic regions and enable the network to process complex information efficiently, while increasing the importance of meaningful features and suppressing unnecessary noise, as shown by the yellow dashed line in Figure 1. (b) Decoding stage: The decoding stage of PANet is relatively simple and is accomplished via the context fusion module. To avoid further information loss caused by pooling and downsampling operations, the low-dimensional features generated in the encoding stage are fused with multiscale features before generating the predicted image, effectively preserving image detail information.

2.2.1. Construction of scene feature generation module (SFGM)

PANet’s Scene Feature Generation Module (SFGM) optimizes the convolution kernel and dilation rate strategy based on the ASPP module. Compared with the original ASPP module, the scene feature generation module can extract multi-scale feature map information in complex scenes. The schematic diagram of the module is shown in Figure 2.

Schematic diagram of scene feature generation module.

2.2.1.1. Convolution kernel optimization

This paper studies the influence of mixed-scale convolution kernels 36 on indoor scene feature extraction and finds that combining the advantages of different-sized kernels can achieve better accuracy and efficiency. First, the network accuracy will improve when the convolution kernel size is from 3×3 to 7×7 . However, when the convolution kernel size is larger than 9×9 , the network accuracy drops rapidly, indicating that the vast convolution kernel may compromise accuracy and efficiency.27,37 The size of the four groups of convolution kernels in the ASPP module is all 3×3 . In order to make the network have both segmentation effect and segmentation speed, the scene feature generation module slightly expands the size of the convolution kernels based on 3×3 . The four sets of convolution kernels are replaced with 3×3 , 3×3 , 5×5 , and 5×5 so that this module collects more spatial information than the ASPP module.

In addition, this paper proposes three types of convolution kernel dilation strategies based on the correlation of spatial information extracted by each pooling layer of the scene feature extraction module: Closely Correlated Dilation Strategy (CCDS), General Correlated Dilation Strategy (GCDS), and Sparse Correlated Dilation Strategy (SCDS). The classification is based on the following rules:

(a) Without considering the case where the dilation rate of the first layer is 1, if the remaining three dilation rates form an arithmetic sequence, let d be the common difference: if d≤2 , it is a closely correlated dilation strategy; if 2<d≤4 , it is a general correlated dilation strategy; if d>4 , it is a sparse correlated dilation strategy.

(a) Without considering the case where the dilation rate of the first layer is 1, if the remaining three dilation rates form an arithmetic sequence, let d be the common difference: if d≤2 , it is a closely correlated dilation strategy; if 2<d≤4 , it is a general correlated dilation strategy; if d>4 , it is a sparse correlated dilation strategy.

(b) If the remaining three dilation rates form a geometric sequence, since the last three dilation rates are generally form a geometric sequence with a common ratio of 2 , in this case, the ratio q between the second layer and the first layer is mainly considered: if q≤2 ,it is a closely correlated dilation strategy; if 2<q≤4 , it is a general correlated dilation strategy; if q>4 , it is a sparse correlated dilation strategy.

(b) If the remaining three dilation rates form a geometric sequence, since the last three dilation rates are generally form a geometric sequence with a common ratio of 2 , in this case, the ratio q between the second layer and the first layer is mainly considered: if q≤2 ,it is a closely correlated dilation strategy; if 2<q≤4 , it is a general correlated dilation strategy; if q>4 , it is a sparse correlated dilation strategy.

These three types of dilation rate strategies can enable the network to extract feature map information with different receptive field sizes. Choosing a reasonable dilation strategy is beneficial to the network to learn the correlation between features and achieve accurate judgment of feature semantics by the network. In addition, to solve the problem of overfitting the trained network due to the lack of training samples, this paper adds a Dropout operation after the convolutional layer of the module. By adding Dropout, the network can be effectively prevented from overfitting, and regularity can be achieved to a certain extent. The probability p of Dropout of this module is set to 0.5 to avoid network overfitting and ensure the training efficiency of the network.

2.2.2. Design of scene attention module (SAM)

In this paper, a scene attention module is designed to enable the network to efficiently process complex spatial information in indoor scenes, resulting in higher accuracy. This module is located after the scene feature generation module to adjust the weight of the output of the multi-scale features by the scene feature generation module. SAM allows the network to learn more prominent regional features by dividing multiple groups of feature maps. This module enhances the scene recognition effect of the network. It generates a vital factor for the spatial position in each group to adjust the importance of each sub-feature so that each group can autonomously enhance its learned expression and suppress noise.

This paper mainly studies spatial attention combined with multi-scale feature extraction to improve the segmentation effect of the network. The scene attention module highlights different semantic sub-features by grouping and thoroughly mines the details likely to be ignored by the network. At the same time, the spatial information is taken as the focus, and the weight distribution of the feature map channels is not carried out. A schematic diagram of the scene attention module is shown in Figure 3.

Schematic diagram of the structure of the Scene Attention Module (SAM).

In Figure 3, the scene attention module first groups the feature maps and collects each group’s average pooling and maximum pooling information along the feature map channel. At the same time, in order to effectively encode the context information, SAM adds two paths of average pooling and max pooling. This paper adds a third path-spatial path. The spatial path consists of two layers. The first layer is a 9×9 convolution kernel to encode the original information, and batch normalization and activation functions are added to improve the network training speed and effect. The second layer is a 1×1 convolution to adjust the feature map channel. The number of each feature map is kept correctly, and the spatial path structure is shown in Figure 4.

Schematic diagram of the spatial path structure.

Each feature map represents a collection of texture information, background information, and a large amount of spatial information for the entire channel. Finally, the scene attention module multiplies the spatial attention map and the input feature map to obtain feature maps with different feature weights. It restores the feature map to the same size as the input feature map to obtain the final output feature map of the module.

2.3. Context fusion Module (CFM)

The Context Fusion Module (CFM) is shown in the blue dashed box in Figure 1. The Spatial Pyramid Attention Module (SPAM) generates a feature map with rich spatial information to avoid the convolution and downsampling operations in SPAM. In this paper, before restoring the resolution of the feature map to generate the predicted image, the low-dimensional feature with a larger resolution and the original feature map of the input SPAM is fused, which effectively reduces the loss of image detail information.

3. Experimentation and discussion

In this paper, a pre-trained model based on the Mobilenet and ResNet101 networks on the ImageNet dataset is used to complete the indoor scene semantic segmentation task, and the performance of the proposed PANet network is tested with the NYU V2 dataset.

NYU V2 dataset: The NYU V2 dataset comprises video sequences of various indoor scenes recorded by Microsoft Kinect’s RGB and Depth cameras. The shooting scenes of this dataset are complex, with a wide variety of indoor objects and dense layouts, which are difficult to segment. NYU V2 includes 40 classes and one background class and contains a total of 1449 RGB-D photos obtained from 464 different indoor scenes, of which 795 are used for training, and 654 are used as test set. Considering that the depth image will reduce the operation speed of the network, this experiment only uses the RGB images in the dataset.

3.1. Parameter setting

The implementation is based on the public platform Pytorch, and the “poly” learning rate policy is adopted, where current learning rate equals to the base one multiplying (1−epochnum_epoch)power . The basic learning rate is set to 0.004 and power to 0.9. The performance can be improved by increasing the iteration number, which is set to 100K for ImageNet experiment. The network is trained using stochastic gradient descent with momentum for optimization. Momentum and weight decay are set to 0.9 and 0.0004, respectively. Random scaling of 2 times and cropping operation are used for data augmentation. The input values are normalized by calculating the mean (0.485, 0.456, 0.406) and standard deviation (0.229, 0.224, 0.225) of the RGB images across the dataset. The size of the image is fixed at 256×256 . Due to limited physical memory on GPU cards, the batch size is set to 4 during training. The network is trained by an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2060 SUPER GPU.

From the experimental data in Table 4, it can be seen that SFGM achieves the best results when the close correlation dilation strategy (CCDS) is adopted. The specific dilation rate is {1, 2, 3, 4}, that is, when the size of the four groups in SFGM is 3×3 , 3×3 , 5×5 , and 5×5 convolution kernel dilation rates are set to 1, 2, 3, and 4, respectively, PANet has the highest segmentation accuracy. This is because, under this dilation rate strategy, the dilation rate set in this paper makes the maximum receptive field of SFGM 17. The network can better grasp the correlation information between spatial pixels. The calculation method of the receptive field is shown in Equation (2.1) and Equation (2.2). For indoor scene segmentation, due to its tight layout, an excessive dilation rate affects the network’s learning of object information correlation to a certain extent and reduces the segmentation effect. The dilation rate strategy adopted in this paper not only allows SFGM to obtain sufficient space. The information also enables the network to maintain efficient learning efficiency.

Based on all the above experiments, it can be seen that because PANet integrates rich spatial information in the SFGM and extracts more relevant feature map information, compared with ASPP, this SFGM achieved a 1.59 percentage point improvement in mIoU and a 2.28 percentage point increase in pixel accuracy. In addition, the scene attention module forms multi-level spatial attention, which enables the network to suppress noise in the mixed information, and improves the network’s attention to key information. For the ASPP, compared with the situation without adding attention mechanism, the operation of adding SAM improved the mIoU and mPA by 1.45 and 2.14 percentage point, respectively. The SFGM provides rich spatial information, and the SAM further optimizes the feature map, so the two modules are functionally complementary and jointly improve performance.

4. Conclusion

In order to make the network have a better segmentation effect in indoor scenes and ensure a certain inference speed, this paper proposes PANet, which is more suitable for indoor scene segmentation. PANet uses Mobilenetv2 with a lighter structure and further improves the segmentation effect by optimizing the ASPP module and adding a lightweight spatial attention mechanism. Through experiments, it is found that the network proposed in this paper has significant advantages in terms of constant intersection ratio, pixel accuracy, and segmentation speed. However, a suitable channel attention mechanism is not added in PANet to further improve the effect. In future research, it is hoped that the channel attention mechanism can be integrated to further improve the network’s processing efficiency of indoor scene information.

Step feature line extraction from large-scale point clouds of open-pit mine based on structural characteristics
Authors: Pengzhi Cui, Xiangfu Meng, Wenhui Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractHigh-precision step feature lines play a crucial role in open-pit mine design, production scheduling, mining volume calculations, road network planning, and slope maintenance. Compared with the feature lines of the geometric model, step feature lines are more irregular, complex, higher in density, and richer in detail. In this study, a novel technique for extracting step feature line from large-scale point clouds of open-pit mine by leveraging structural attributes, that is, SFLE_OPM (Step Feature Line Extraction for Open-Pit Mine), is proposed. First, we adopt the k-dimensional tree (KD-tree) resampling method to reduce the point-cloud density while retaining point-cloud features and utilize bilateral filtering for denoising. Second, we use Point Cloud Properties Network (PCPNET) to estimate the normal, calculate the slope and aspect, and then filter them. We then apply morphological operations to the step surface and obtain more continuous and smoother slope lines. In addition, we construct an Open-Pit Mine Step Feature Line (OPMSFL) dataset and benchmarked SFLE_OPM, achieving an accuracy score of 89.31% and true positive rate score of 80.18%. The results demonstrate that our method yields a higher extraction accuracy and precision than most of the existing methods. Our dataset is available at https://github.com/OPMDataSets/OPMSFL.Keywords: Step feature line, Point clouds, KD-tree, PCPNETSubject terms: Geomorphology, Mineralogy, Tectonics, Stratigraphy, Civil engineering

Introduction

Step feature lines consisting of crest and toe lines are crucial in open-pit mines because they provide detailed information about the terrain and can be utilized to verify the mine geometry. Accurate extraction of slope lines is essential for open-pit mine design, production scheduling, mining volume calculation, road network planning, and slope maintenance. It serves as foundation for mine automation and intelligence1.

Prior to the introduction of drone surveying, the extraction of open-pit mine step lines relied on traditional field surveys conducted by professional surveyors using electronic total stations or real-time kinematic (RTK) equipment. Manual surveying is tedious, labor-intensive, and prone to errors. The rapid development and popularization of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) for surveying1–4 have led to the wide application of this technology in fields such as three-dimensional (3D) modeling, environment monitoring, deformation monitoring, high-precision mapping, and spatial analysis of open-pit mines5–7.

Over the past decade, significant efforts have been made to improve the efficiency of UAV data utilization for open-pit mine step line extraction. These advancements can be categorized into two main areas. (1) Data source enhancements: Kang et al.8–10 utilized 3D digital elevation models to extract feature lines. Sun et al.11 proposed an automatic extraction scheme based on GPS data for open-pit mine road networks. Mao et al.12 introduced a method for extracting sloped lines from UAVs based on point-cloud data. Chen et al.13 established a rapid, low-cost methodology to characterize open-pit mining geomorphic features using UAVs and digital terrain analysis based on structures from motion elevation data. Liu et al.14 proposed a robust algorithm to extract feature curves from noisy triangular meshes. (2) Advancements in edge and line detection method: Wang et al.15 used the moving least squares method to accurately fit the step line during edge extraction. Ni et al.16 proposed an automated and effective method called Analysis of Geometric Properties of Neighborhoods (AGPN) to detect 3D edges and trace feature lines from 3D-point clouds. AGPN16 is an automated algorithm validated for use on complex man-made objects and large-scale urban scenes. It involves detecting 3D edges and tracing feature lines. The algorithm utilizes the RANSAC scheme and angular gap metric algorithms to identify feature line segments. Mao et al.12 extracted step feature lines from open-pit mining point clouds by tracking step feature control nodes using a seed growth tracking algorithm. The method, known as Elevation-Gradient Edge Detection for Contour Step (EGED-CS), is significantly more effective than the AGPN16 method in steep regions. Wang et al.17 propose a feature extraction method for scattered point clouds. A Bidirectional Principal Component Analysis (BD-PCA) search method was used to trim and break small branches and multiline segments to generate the feature curves. Denk et al.18 introduced a supervised deep-learning approach for estimating local feature lines, which is based on graph convolutional networks. Hackel et al.19 predicted the contour score for each individual point with a binary classifier by using a set of features extracted from the point’s neighborhood. Lu et al.20 presented a very simple but efficient algorithm for 3D line segment detection from large scale unorganized point cloud based on region growing and region merging. Guerrero et al.21 proposed PCPNET, a deep-learning-based approach for estimating local 3D shape properties in point clouds. It is especially well adapted for estimating local shape properties, such as normal (both unoriented and oriented) and curvature, from raw point clouds in the presence of strong noise and multiscale features. In short, 3D edge detection is the process of identifying edges or boundaries in 3D data, such as point clouds or volumetric images, using Surface Normal-based Methods8,13,15, Tensor Voting Methods9,10, Algebraic Methods8, or Learning-based Methods21. On the other hand, 3D line detection is the process of identifying straight line segments in 3D data, such as point clouds or meshes, using RANSAC-based Methods16, Region Growing Methods12,14,17, or Learning-based Methods18,21.

The recent advancements in data acquisition technologies have facilitated the rapid and efficient collection of open-pit mine point clouds data. However, the utilization of such data for feature extraction, particularly step feature lines, remains an under-explored field. Existing edge-detection methods are primarily tailored for applications involving simpler geometries, such as mechanical parts22. Step feature lines in open-pit mines exhibit distinct characteristics compared to geometric model feature lines: they are more irregular, intricate, possess higher density, and hold richer detail. Additionally, existing research has predominantly focused solely on feature lines, neglecting the inherent structural characteristics of open-pit mines. Step features represent the boundary edges of operational tiers, often situated adjacent to retaining walls and drainage gutters. Consequently, extracting surface-like areas encompassing these features provides a more accurate and robust approach compared to directly extracting individual feature lines.

To address these limitations, this study proposes a novel step feature-line extraction method that leverages the structural characteristics of open-pit mine point clouds. The methodology employs a three-step approach: (1) KD-tree resampling is first implemented to reduce point clouds density while preserving critical features. Bilateral filtering is then applied for effective noise reduction. (2) Subsequently, PCPNET is utilized to estimate surface normal, followed by slope and aspect calculations and subsequent filtering. (3) Morphological operations are then applied to the step surface, resulting in the extraction of more continuous and well-defined slope lines. Data preprocessing

Resampling

Compared with traditional image or map data, 3D point clouds contain complete geometric information, which improves the performance of computer vision applications. However, major difficulties remain, which affect the point-cloud utilization. Such difficulties include the lack of local adjacency information, non-uniform point density, control of point numbers, large data volumes, and redundancy23. These issues increase storage and computing costs and affect the accuracy of the calculations and analysis. To solve these issues, raw point clouds should be resampled. Among the various methods, including random, voxel-wise, uniform, moving least squares (MLS), farthest point, covariance, and KD-tree sampling24, we employed the KD-tree algorithm in this study to construct the spatial data structure of the point cloud, considering its efficiency and ability to preserve local features. This facilitates the effective construction of the spatial structure for data storage, speeds up the search for surrounding data, and accelerates resampling25. Data denoising

Advancements in 3D data acquisition devices, such as structured light scanners, Kinect sensors, and time-of-flight cameras, have made it easy, convenient, and efficient to acquire point clouds data26,27. However, raw point clouds contain noise due to physical factors, sensor limitations, lighting conditions, and scene artifacts. This noise is detrimental to practical applications26. Hence, it is essential to remove noise and outliers from raw data while retaining features, particularly intricate details.

Bilateral filtering is a nonlinear filtering method that compromises the spatial neighborhood and pixel value similarity. It considers both the spatial domain information and grayscale similarity to achieve edge preservation and noise reduction. It has simple, non-iterative, and local characteristics28. Note that the specific forms of the spatial and value-domain kernel functions can be selected and defined as required. Gaussian and exponential kernel functions are commonly used. Bilateral filtering considers both spatial and value domain information when smoothing an image, preserving edge details while effectively eliminating noise. However, bilateral filtering has a high computational cost, which can lead to a slower processing speed for large images. Therefore, a balance between the filtering effect and computational efficiency is required.

In this study, the sigma values of the filters in the value-domain and spatial kernel functions were 1 and 11, respectively.

Step surface filtering

The step feature line represents the boundary edge of a step. In certain cases, the upper step surface is coplanar with the retaining wall, whereas the lower portion is aligned with the drainage gutter. This can result in the misidentification of retaining structures or gutters as part of the slope. Therefore, extracting step faces is conducive to feature line extraction. Based on the analysis of the structure of the step, step has two main features (Fig. 4): slope and aspect angles.

Structure of steps in an open-pit mine; (a) Sectional view; (b) Top views. They show the two main features of the step: slope and aspect angles. The range of slope is 0°–90°, while the range of aspect is 0°–360°.

Literature reviews, field measurements, and the analysis of the distribution law of inclination angles in the triangular network of open-pit mines indicated an overall multi-peak distribution. The critical slope angle for the dividing step, roads, and step surfaces is generally 20°–30°, which can be used as the inclination-angle filtering threshold. Based on further analysis, the aspects of the slope surfaces were consistent within the local ranges and the area ratio of the slope surfaces was much higher than that of the retaining walls and gutter. Therefore, we propose a step-feature line extraction method from Open-Pit Mine point clouds based on slope and aspect structural characteristics, that is, SFLE_OPM.

Normal vector calculation

Scanned point clouds typically only include spatial location and color information, but lack local surface geometry properties such as point normal. A high-quality normal is essential for various downstream tasks including point clouds consolidation, surface reconstruction, and model segmentation29. Obtaining a high-quality normal is essential for our task. Normal estimation methods can be categorized into regression-based, Voronoi-based, and deep-learning-based methods. In this study, we adopted the PCPNET21 algorithm, which is a data-driven point-cloud neural network model proposed by Guerrero in 2018. PCPNET21 was used to conduct an in-depth analysis of the local areas of point clouds to obtain local-shape feature estimation results with high robustness and accuracy. The biggest advantage of this model is that it is not limited to traditional methods based on local neighborhood estimation and implicit surface modeling schemes. Therefore, quality defects in the point clouds itself can be avoided and empirical data can be used to guide feature analysis for performance improvement. This algorithm can be used to estimate local shape features from raw point-cloud data and performs well in estimating the point-cloud curvature and normal vectors. The slope degree of the study area exhibits a distinct bimodal distribution, with one peak at the steps in the dumping area and working area and another peak at the working bench surface. A good threshold should be between these two values. The global threshold can be obtained from the slope histogram.

Based on Tobler’s First Law of Geography, everything is related to everything else, but near things are more closely related to each other. We designed an algorithm called local percentage filter for the aspect. If the difference between the current slope aspect and average slope aspect of the points within a certain range of the surrounding area was less than T, it was retained; otherwise, it was removed. Step line extraction

Mathematical morphological operation

A filter can reduce or suppress noise; however, it cannot eliminate noise alone. In a filtered image, adjacent pixels gradually exhibit inconsistencies, which are manifested as isolated points, glitches, bridges, holes, and cracks in binary images31. Morphological Image Operation is a useful tool in digital image processing because it can be used to rigorously quantify many features of a geometrical structure in a manner consistent with human intuition and perception. The opening operation consists of two steps: erosion followed by dilation32, which can remove small details and protrusions to smooth the object contour (Fig. 8). The closing operation consists of two steps: dilation followed by erosion32 which eliminates small holes and cracks (Fig. 9). The opening and closing operation can be expressed as:

Contour extraction

A contour is the location where the two regions meet in a digital image. We used the algorithm described in33 to identify all contours in the binary image. The result of this operation is a set of polygons that we filtered according to area and hierarchical information. We obtained extreme outer contours without holes or small areas. The results are shown in Fig. 10. Thus, completing the contour extraction for the entire image and the set of contour points can help us to obtain the step feature line.

The proposed SFLE_OPM achieves a higher accuracy than the other two baseline methods, indicating its superior capability in correctly classifying slope and non-slope regions. In addition, the TPR is higher, indicating a more accurate identification of positive samples. Based on these improvements in accuracy and true positives, the precision remained comparable and a higher IoU score was obtained. This implies that, while correct judgments increase, few false positives are introduced. The results of the ablation experiments showed that aspect filtering better eliminates areas with inconsistent aspects, which improves the precision rate, reduces the misidentification rate, and increases the accuracy. Because it is a correction of misidentified data and does not affect correctly identified data, it has no impact on the TPR. In addition, the experiments highlighted that accurate normal estimation is important for extraction. Overall, the SFLE_OPM demonstrated an enhanced performance in discriminating between slopes and non-slopes.

Conclusions

In this study, we propose a novel method for extracting slope feature lines from large-scale point clouds data. Based on comprehensive ablation experiments and comparative analysis using the AGPN16 and EGED-CS12 methods, the proposed method is robust against interference from vehicles, drainage ditches, retaining walls, and other potential obstructions. Notably, our method excels in extracting complete slope feature lines with significantly fewer missing or faulty extracted segments, leading to high extraction precision and accuracy. This is of great significance for updating the open-pit mine map. In the future, we will enhance our techniques for estimating normal vectors to streamline the extraction process, ensuring optimal efficiency.

The EZ- Blocker®- A Safer Alternative in Complex Airway Scenarios: A Case Series
Authors: Suda Naveen Kumar, Ilangovan Panchanathan, Shruti Singh, Roshni R. Benedicta, Balaji Kuppuswamy, Kirubakaran Davis, Sathish Kumar Dharmalingam
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:One-lung ventilation (OLV) is crucial in thoracic surgery as it ensures optimal visibility and effective isolation of the lung. Various devices, such as double-lumen tubes and bronchial blockers, help achieve effective OLV. While double-lumen tubes (DLTs) are considered the gold standard for lung isolation certain clinical scenarios may make their use challenging or contraindicated. In these cases, the EZ-Blocker® (Teleflex Medical, Morrisville, NC, USA) presents distinct advantages. This paper discusses a series of six cases where DLTs were not feasible, highlighting our experience with the EZ-Blocker® as a viable alternative.Materials and Methods:We analyzed patients aged 18 years and older who underwent various thoracic surgeries in the department of Anaesthesiology at Christian Medical College, Vellore, from January 2024 to September 2024. This study specifically focused on cases involving the intraoperative use of the EZ-Blocker® for one-lung ventilation.Results:The EZ-Blocker® was used in challenging situations such as difficult airway management, and lung isolation in intubated patients, and in those with tracheostomies. The placement of nearly all EZ bronchial blockers was accomplished without complications, resulting in a success rate of 99%. Adequate lung collapse was achieved in all patients, with no serious airway injuries or immediate complications reported.Conclusion:The EZ-Blocker® is an effective and safe airway device designed for one-lung ventilation, particularly in scenarios where DLTs are unsuitable. Its advantages include easy placement, adaptability to abnormal airway anatomy, reduced airway trauma, and enhanced visualization during bronchoscopy. These features make it a superior alternative to traditional double-lumen tubes for managing complex airway scenarios.

INTRODUCTION

One-lung ventilation (OLV) is crucial in thoracic surgery, as it ensures optimal surgical exposure and effective lung isolation.[1] Traditionally, double-lumen endotracheal tubes (DLTs) and bronchial blockers (BBs) have been used for this purpose. In complex clinical scenarios,[2] the relatively new EZ-Blocker® (Teleflex Medical, Morrisville, NC, USA) offers distinct advantages. These benefits include ease of placement, adaptability to abnormal airway anatomy,[3] reduced airway trauma,[4] and improved visualization during bronchoscopy.[5] In addition, the EZ-Blocker® allows for intraoperative lung re-expansion without the need for tube exchange. These features make the EZ bronchial blocker a superior alternative to conventional DLTs and BBs, especially in challenging airway management situations.

CASE REPORT

Case 1

A 70-year-old man presented with cervical spondylotic myelopathy, featuring multilevel disc prolapse at C3-4, C4-5, and C6-7 and significant cord compression at C4-5 [Figure 1]. Imaging revealed a right-sided multiloculated pleural effusion and clots in the right hemithorax. The patient was scheduled for right-sided thoracotomy and decortication. Airway management was challenging due to limited neck mobility and risk of spinal cord injury from cervical myelopathy. Owing to restricted neck mobility and the risk of cord injury, a Philadelphia collar was applied, and airway assessment revealed a Mallampati score of III with no neck movement. Given the risk of using a DLT, an EZ-Blocker® was used for lung isolation.

Case 2

A 23-year-old man presented with Class V Myasthenia Gravis, complicated by a myasthenic crisis with respiratory failure, requiring prolonged ventilation had 7.5 size tracheostomy tube in situ. A CT scan of the thorax revealed thymoma, and he subsequently underwent right uniportal VATS for total thymectomy, where pericardial infiltration was observed intraoperatively. Owing to the unavailability of the Tracheopart® DLT and the infeasibility of a standard DLT, an EZ-Blocker® was used [Figures 2 and 3].

Case 3

A 35-year-old woman with an ostium secundum atrial septal defect was scheduled for closure of the defect using a mini thoracotomy (MICS). The patient required lung isolation and echocardiographic monitoring via transesophageal echocardiography (TEE). Because using a regular Double Lumen Tube (DLT) posed an increased risk of airway trauma during manipulation of the TEE probe, an EZ-Blocker® was chosen as a safer alternative.

Case 4

A 20-year-old man with severe aortic regurgitation underwent aortic valve replacement. On postoperative day 7, he developed dyspnea secondary to pericardial effusion and required a left uniportal VATS pericardial window. During the previous surgery, the patient experienced difficulties with laryngoscopy and intubation. Given the challenging airway situation and the emergent nature of the surgery, along with the patient’s use of anticoagulants and compromised bleeding parameters, there was a significant risk of bleeding due to airway trauma associated with the insertion of a DLT. To reduce this risk, an EZ-Blocker® was strategically chosen to ensure effective lung isolation.

Case 5

A 54-year-old man underwent thoracoabdominal aneurysm repair, during which a DLT was used for lung isolation. However, owing to the need for prolonged ventilation, the DLT was replaced with a single-lumen tube. Postoperatively, the patient experienced significant bleeding from the left bronchus [Figure 4]. To manage this complication, an EZ-Blocker® was inserted in the ICU for lung isolation, allowing for effective control of the hemorrhage.

X ray revealing hemorrhage in left sided bronchus

Case 6

A 51-year-old man with a history of pulmonary embolism who was receiving multiple anticoagulants with a cavitary lesion in right upper lobe was scheduled for a right thoracotomy. Owing to the heightened risk of bleeding associated with anticoagulant therapy, the insertion of a DLT presented considerable concerns. Consequently, an EZ-Blocker® was used for lung isolation, allowing for safe and effective selective lung ventilation throughout the surgical procedure.

All six cases were evaluated based on clinical parameters of lung isolation and surgeon satisfaction following the commencement of the surgical procedure. Lung isolation was assessed clinically using the five-point auscultation method and confirmed with fiberoptic bronchoscopy in both supine and lateral positions. Surgical exposure was further evaluated intraoperatively, based on direct visualization through either open thoracotomy or thoracoscopic view, and surgeon feedback was documented. Patients requiring postoperative ventilation was continued with the same endotracheal tube. A detailed summary of the study cases, and their respective outcomes are presented in Table 1.

DISCUSSION

One-lung ventilation (OLV) is crucial for thoracic procedures, such as video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS), as it optimizes surgical exposure and minimizes complications. Traditionally, double-lumen tubes (DLTs) have been the standard method for achieving lung isolation. However, DLTs can pose challenges, particularly in patients with difficult airways, cervical spine restrictions, and various other constraints. In recent years, the EZ-Blocker® has emerged as a viable and effective alternative, offering several advantages over both DLTs and other bronchial blockers.

The EZ-Blocker® consists of a Y-shaped structure that mimics tracheobronchial anatomy, allowing easy placement of its bronchial extensions into both main bronchi. These extensions terminate with inflatable cuffs [Figure 5] controlled independently via pilot balloons and inflation lines for selective lung isolation. The central shaft provides stability and alignment within the trachea, while the proximal connector allows attachment to a bronchoscope or ventilator, facilitating insertion through a standard endotracheal tube [Figure 6]. Visual markings, including color coding, aid in placement, and radiopaque markers enhance imaging visibility for accurate positioning as confirmed by fiberoptic bronchoscopy [Figure 7].

The EZ-Blocker®, used with a standard ETT, permits a more delicate approach, lowering the risk of airway bleeding and related complications, thereby rendering it a safer option for patients with coagulopathy.[7]Minimal Need for Repositioning: Traditional bronchial blockers may necessitate frequent adjustments during surgery, particularly with patient repositioning. Limitations of the study

This case series presents selected patients with relative contraindications to double-lumen tube (DLT) placement, in whom the EZ-Blocker® proved to be a valuable alternative. Further randomized studies are warranted to compare the effectiveness of the EZ-Blocker® with other lung isolation techniques. There is also a learning curve for operators in achieving successful placement of the EZ-Blocker®.

CONCLUSION

The EZ-Blocker® provides a reliable and less invasive alternative for achieving one-lung ventilation, especially in patients for whom double-lumen tubes (DLTs) and other bronchial blockers may pose increased risks. It is easy to use, maintains stable positioning, and is suitable for patients with difficult airways or underlying health conditions, making it a valuable tool in thoracic anesthesia. While DLTs are still the standard in many situations, the EZ-Blocker® offers a safer and effective option that may reduce airway trauma and complications, ultimately improving patient outcomes.

The impact of STAiR18 on multiple myeloma survival rates
Authors: Yingmiao Wu, Haolin Wang, Ji Luo, Jiaoya Lin, Yajie Wu, Shuai Zheng, Yifei Gao, Jiao Chen, Feifei Che, Jianyou Shi, Ling Zhong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundA subset of multiple myeloma (MM) patients exhibited worse survival and higher tumor burden. STAiR18 has been found to be highly expressed in MM cell lines. However, the precise mechanisms underlying the upstream and downstream regulation of STAiR18 have remained unclear.MethodsThe expression of STAiR18 and miR-451a in MM patients and cell lines was detected using quantitative reverse transcriptase PCR (qRT-PCR). ChIP-qPCR was performed to assess the enrichment of pSTAT3 at the STAiR18 promoter. The subcellular localization of STAiR18 was determined by FISH. The binding relationship between STAiR18 and miR-451a was predicted through bioinformatics analyses and validated using RNA pull down and dual-luciferase reporter assays. Cell proliferation was evaluated with CCK-8 assay, Edu assay, and flow cytometry. The expression levels of key proteins in the IL-6R/STAT3/JAK2 pathway, along with Cyclin D1 and apoptosis-related markers, were analyzed by Western blot. Cell cycle distribution and apoptosis rate were quantified by flow cytometry. The role of STAiR18 was also investigated in vivo using a xenograft tumor model, which was established by tail vein injection of MM cell lines into NOG mice.ResultsIn MM patients, STAiR18 expression was notably upregulated in myeloma cells from bone marrow, and positively correlated with clinical stage and worse outcome. Knockdown (KD) and overexpression (OE) of STAT3 affected the expression level of STAiR18, and pSTAT3 was significantly enriched in STAiR18’s promoter. Knockdown of STAiR18 reduced the proliferation of MM cells in vitro and suppressed tumor growth in vivo. STAiR18 was predicted and confirmed to primarily localize in the cytoplasm. STAiR18 KD resulted in upregulation of miR-451a levels. The binding between STAiR18 and miR-451a (silencing IL-6R) was predicted and validated. Through KD and rescue experiments, we demonstrated that STAiR18 sponged miR-451a, and promoted myeloma cell proliferation via IL-6R/STAT3/JAK2 pathway.ConclusionsThe study revealed that STAiR18 competed with endogenous miR-451a, and formed a novel positive-feedback loop of IL-6R/STAT3/JAK2 to promote proliferation of MM. These findings suggest that STAiR18 represents a promising novel therapeutic strategy for MM treatment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-025-07210-x.Keywords: IL-6R/STAT3/JAK2 pathway, Multiple myeloma (MM), miR-451a, Proliferation, STAiR18

Introduction

Multiple myeloma (MM) is the second-most common hematological malignancy, and accounts for 10–15% of all hematological tumors [1]. It is characterized by the abnormal monoclonal proliferation of plasma cells (PCs), which accumulate in the bone marrow (BM) and secrete monoclonal proteins [2]. Despite significant advancements in treatment modalities, including chemotherapy and immunotherapy, patients with MM still have a short survival [3]. Consequently, elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying the oncogenesis and progression of MM is significant to develop effective therapeutic strategies.

A growing number of studies have indicated that long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have potential as biomarkers or therapeutic targets for the treatment of MM [4, 5]. STAiR18, annotated as MIR4435-2HG, is a lncRNA first identified in 2013 via analysis of STAT3-dependent gene expression profiles in gastric cancer [6]. Functionally, it is widely overexpressed in multiple cancers (e.g. lung, hepatocellular, ovarian, and esophageal cancers) [7–10] and modulates key malignant phenotypes including cell cycle arrest, apoptosis, migration, and invasion [11]. Notably, STAiR18 is induced by the transcription factor STAT3 upon IL-6 stimulation [7, 12]. In MM, IL-6 (markedly elevated via myeloma cell autocrine and bone marrow stromal cell paracrine secretion) exerts biological effects by binding IL-6R, and the JAK2/STAT3 pathway is the key signaling pathway that mediates IL-6induced effects [13]. Specifically, IL-6 binding to IL-6R activates JAK2, which then phosphorylates itself and the receptor. These phosphorylated sites recruit STAT3, which forms dimers, and translocates into the nucleus, finally acts as a transcription factor to regulate gene expression [14]. However, the potential effect of STAiR18 in MM and its related molecular mechanism has not been fully investigated.

Recently, accumulating evidence has confirmed that microRNAs (miRNAs) are involved in multiple human malignancies, including MM [15]. A number of miRNAs-such as miR-497 [16], miR-21 [17], and miR-27 [18]-exhibit abnormal expression in MM and contribute to tumorigenesis. Currently, many researchers believe that certain lncRNAs can affect the activity of miRNAs and ultimately interfere with the development of MM [19]. However, the relationship between miR-451a and STAiR18 remains uninvestigated in MM. In this study, knockdown of STAiR18 effectively inhibited the proliferation of MM cells and suppressed tumor growth in vivo. Furthermore, we demonstrated that STAiR18 sponges miR-451a to activate IL-6R/STAT3/JAK2 pathway. Therefore, we investigated the role of STAiR18 and explored the interaction between STAiR18 and miR-451a in MM. Collectively, our findings suggested that STAiR18 may serve as a potential therapeutic target for MM.

Materials and methods

Clinical samples and cell lines

Primary PCs were obtained from BM aspirates of 57 newly diagnosed MM patients recruited between February 2022 and February 2025, and were purified using CD138 MicroBeads (Miltenyi Biotec, USA). CD138 positive cells (CD138+ cells) were detected with qPCR analysis, according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Each patient was ensured to sign informed consent. This research was approved by the medical ethics committee of University of Electronic Science and Technology, Sichuan Province People’s Hospital (Grant Number: 2018-49).

Cell culture and transfection

Human MM cell lines U266 and RPMI 8226 were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection and were cultured with RPMI-1640 (Gibco; Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco; Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (Gibco; Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). both two cell lines were incubated in a 37℃ humidified incubator containing 5% CO2.

The lentivirus containing STAiR18 sequence (STAiR18-OE) or STAT3 sequence (STAT3-OE), and shRNA targeting to STAiR18 (STAiR18-KD) or targeting to STAT3 (STAT3-KD), were all packaged at Genechem company. The miR-451a mimic was designed and obtained from RiboBio technology. For establishment of stable-expression cell lines, U266 and RPMI 8226 cells were seeded into 6-well plates and were infected by LV-STAiR18-KD, LV-STAiR18-OE and the corresponding control vectors (LV-CTR-KD and LV-CTR-OE) with addition of 8 µg/mL polybrene (Genechem company, China). Three days after infection, positive selection was performed using 1 µg/mL puromycin. To confirm the infection efficiency, quantitative real time transcription-polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) analysis was performed. For transfection of lncRNA, 50 nM miR-451a mimic or inhibitor was added into medium with transfection reagent. After 8 h of transfection, the medium was replaced and continued to incubate for further 48 h.

qRT-PCR

Trizol reagent (Tsingke Biotechnology, China) was used to isolate RNA from tissues or cell samples. cDNA was synthesized with reverse transcription PCR and was detected with qRT-PCR, following the manufacturer’s instruction of SYBR Green PCR kit (TransGenBiotech, China). For lncRNA analysis, total RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using the lncRNA First-Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (TIANGEN, China). Subsequent quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was performed with the lnRcute lncRNA qPCR Kit (TIANGEN, China) following the manufacturer’s protocols. For miRNA analysis, first-strand cDNA was synthesized using the miRNA 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Stem-loop method). Quantitative PCR analysis was then conducted using the SYBR Green Premix Pro Taq HS qPCR Kit II (TIANGEN, China). All cDNA were conducted by Biosystems™ 7500 PCR instrument (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The sequences of the primers used in this research were listed in Supplementary Table S1. GAPDH or U6 small nuclear RNA was applied as internal control.

Western blot

Protein was extracted by using RIPA reagent (Cell Signaling Technology, USA), with addition of 1mM PMSF (MedChemexpress, USA) and phosphatase inhibitors (MedChemexpress, USA). The Bradford Protein Assay (Bio-Rad, USA) was used to detect the protein concentration. Equal quantity of protein was separated by NuPAGE 4–12% Bis-Tris Protein Gels (Invitrogen, USA), followed by transfer to nitrocellulose membrane (Bio-Rad, USA), and finally immunoblotted with primary antibodies overnight. Secondary antibodies conjugated to horseradish peroxidase were used to incubate with membrane, and exposed using enhanced chemiluminescence (4A Biotech, China). The primary antibodies used were as follows: IL-6R (Proteintech, USA, 1:1000), STAT3 (Cell Signaling Technology, USA,1:1000), pSTAT3 (Cell Signaling Technology, USA, 1:1000), pJAk2 (Cell Signaling Technology, USA, 1:1000), BCL2 (Cell Signaling Technology, USA, 1:2000), cyclinD1(Cell Signaling Technology, USA, 1:1000), BAX(Proteintech, USA, 1:2000), β-actin (Proteintech, USA, 1:50,000) and GAPDH (Proteintech, USA, 1:50,000), HRP-conjugated Goat Anti-Mouse IgG (Proteintech, USA, 1:50,000) and HRP-conjugated Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG (Proteintech, USA, 1:50,000).

Cell viability assay

To assess cell ability of proliferation, CCK-8 (MedChemexpress, USA) was added into the culture medium and incubate with cell for 1 h. The absorbance value was measured at 450 nm using SuPerMax 3000AL Multi-Functional Microplate Reader (Flash Spectrum Biotechnology, China).

Flow cytometry (FCM)

The collected cells were washed twice with 1 mL pre-cooled PBS (Solarbio, China), and then resuspended in 1 mL pre-cold 70% ethanol, followed by incubation at 4 °C for 12 h. After centrifugation at 500 g for 5 min, ethanol was removed, and cells were washed with 1 mL chilled PBS twice. Cells were then treated with 0.5 mL propidium iodide (PI) staining solution (prepared as per manufacturer’s instructions, BD Biosciences, USA), and incubated in the dark at 37 °C for 15 min. The collected 2 × 10⁵ cells were washed and resuspended in 500 µL of binding buffer, followed by addition of 5 µL Annexin V-FITC and 5 µL PI, mixed gently, and incubated for 5–10 min at room temperature (RT) in the dark. The cells were analyzed with FCM using a FACS Calibur system (BD Biosciences, USA). Data were fitted using FlowJo software.

Edu assay

Cells were seeded into 24-well plates and Edu solution (final concentration:10 uΜ, Abbkine, China) was added to each well and incubated at 37 °C for 2 h. After labeling, the medium was removed, and cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min at RT. The cells were then permeabilized with PBS containing 0.5% Triton X-100 for 15 min at RT. Following washing with pre-cold PBS, 100 µL of click-iT solution was added and incubated for 30 min in the dark at RT. The cells were washed with pre-cold PBS and stained with 100 µL of 1×Hoechst 33,342 solution for 10 min in the dark at RT. Finally, cell slides were mounted and imaged using a confocal microscope (DM4B, Leica, Germany).

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)—qPCR

1 × 107 cell was collected and washed with 1×PBS twice. Nucleus acid and protein were crosslinked using 1% methyl-aldehyde (MedChemexpress, USA) and terminated by 0.125 M glycine (Beyotime, Chnia). DNA-protein complex was released by sonication and was incubated with pSTAT3 antibody (phospho Y705) overnight at 4 °C. Antibody-DNA-protein complex was pull down by incubation with Protein A agarose (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), followed by wash with four different buffers (Solarbio, China). Subsequently, crosslink was relieved by addition with 0.2 M NaCl. Sequence analysis was carried out using PCR technology and pSTAT3 binding site on promoter region of STAiR18 was detected using specific primer.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

The wild-type and mutant sequences of the STAiR18-miR-451a binding sites were cloned into the vector of the dual-luciferase reporter system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Subsequently, the reporter vector along with either miR-451a mimic or negative control mimic was transfected into 293T cells. After transfection for 48 h, cells were collected and underwent subsequent procedures according to the manufacturer’s instructions (LandM biotech, China).

Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)

GFP-labeled STAiR18 probe was purchased from RiboBio technology. Hybridization process was performed, following the manufacturer’s instructions provided by RiboBio technology. Briefly, cell sample was fixed using 4% paraformaldehyde and the membrane was dissolved with permeabilization buffer containing 0.5% Triton-X (Biosharp, China) at RT. STAiR18 specific probe was incubated with cell sample overnight at 4 °C. After washing with 2×saline sodium citrate containing 50% methanamide, GFP signal was detected and imaged by confocal microscopy (Zeiss, LSM880, Germany).

RNA pull-down assay

miR-451a (biotinylated at the 3’ end; Bio-miR-451a) and negative control (Bio-miR-scr) were designed, with their sequences provided in Supplementary Table S2. STAiR18 was provided by total RNA, extracted from RPMI 8226 STAiR18-OE cell line, using quick RNA extraction kit (AG21023, ACCURATEVBIOLOGY, China). 0.1 µmol miR-451a or miR-scr was incubated with 2 µg total RNA for 2 h at 37 °C in 200 µL DEPC water (B501005-0500, Sangon Biotech, China) and 20 µL was used to be the input sample. Streptavidin magnetic beads (HY-K0208, MCE, USA) were equilibrated with washing buffer (10 mM Tris-HCl, pH = 7.4, 1 mM EDTA, 1 M NaCl and 0.1% Tween-20) for three times. RNA mixture was incubated with streptavidin magnetic beads for 2 h at RT, followed by five washes with washing buffer, and once with DEPC water. Subsequently magnetic beads were suspended with 20 µL DEPC water and nucleic acids were eluted at 80 °C for 3 min. Eluted buffer was performed with RT-PCR. GAPDH and U6 were respectively used as internal control for lncRNA and microRNA.

MM mouse models

Animal experiments were approved by the Laboratory Animal Welfare Ethics Committee of Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital. Cg-PrkdcscidIl2rgtm1Sug/JicCrl (NOG) mice (6–8 weeks of age, female) were purchased from Vital River Laboratories (Beijing, China). NOG mice were kept feeding at RT in sterile environment. RPMI 8226-CTR-KD (5 × 106) or RPMI 8226-STAiR18-KD cell lines (5 × 106) was intravenously injected into the female NOG mice to establish a disseminated human MM xenograft model. At the endpoint (hind limb paralysis), NOG mice were sacrificed and the percentage of CD138+ cells in BM was detected by FCM. Tumor development was monitored weekly with an in vivo imaging system (Lago, LagoX, SI Imaging, USA). Mice ware anesthetized by isoflurane and D-luciferin (150 mg/kg, Revvity, USA) was injected intraperitoneally into the mice. After 10–15 min, the tumor photon flux was measured using an IVIS imaging system with an exposure time of 30–60 s.

Statistical analysis

Graphs and statistical analysis were performed with Graph Pad Prism 8. Student t-test was used to evaluate significance between two groups, and one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test was utilized for comparison among three or more groups. Use Pearson correlation analysis to evaluate the correlation between variables. Survival analysis was conducted using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. All data from independent experiments analyzed using SPSS 19.0 and are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

The expression of STAiR18 was upregulated in MM

Base on the analysis of clinical sample from 57 MM patients and 23 healthy donors, primary PCs from bone marrow was isolated and found that STAiR18 expression was higher in MM patients at 4.442 ± 0.6364-times than that in normal subjects (t = 6.980, P < 0.0001) (Fig. 1A). To verify the relationship among STAiR18 and MM progression, BM sample from MM patients were divided into three groups in accordance with Revised International Staging System (R-ISS) (R-ISS I, II and III), obvious tendency was observed which showed that as the disease process, the median level of STAiR18 in patients with R-ISS stage I MM was 2.115 ± 0.2406-times that in normal subjects. The median level in patients with R-ISS stage II MM was 4.001 ± 0.2657-times that in normal subjects. R-ISS stage III patients showed the highest level (8.791 ± 1.194-times that in normal subjects; F = 41.19, P < 0.0001) (Fig. 1B). Consistent with the above findings, STAiR18 expression in MM cell lines (U266, RPMI 8226 and H929) was detected and also exhibited the same tendency (Fig. 1C). Subsequently, we analyzed the prognostic correlation between STAiR18 expression and survival in MM patients. All MM patients were divided into STAiR18 high-expression and low-expression groups using the mean value STAiR18 expression as the threshold. Results from Kaplan-Meier survival analysis showed that higher STAiR18 expression was associated with PFS in MM patients (P < 0.05, Fig. 1D).

STAT3 promotes STAiR18 transcription

STAT3 is a transcription factor implicated for regulation of various lncRNAs. In our previous study, we observed a positive correlation between STAiR18 and IL-6 expression levels in MM patients [20], indicating that STAT3 may be involved in the regulation of transcription of STAiR18. To assess the effect of STAT3 on expression of STAiR18, lentivirus carrying STAT3 encode sequence (STAT3-OE) or shRNA which targeting to STAT3 (STAT3-KD) was used to infect U266 and RPMI 8226 cell lines to establish stable-expressive cell lines. Results from qRT-PCR and western blot respectively confirmed the overexpression of STAT3 in STAT3-OE cell line and decreased expression of STAT3 in STAT3-KD cell line at mRNA level and protein level (Fig. 2A, B, C, D, E). Expression of STAiR18 was detected in both STAT3-OE and STAT3-KD groups, and result of qPCR showed that STAiR18 was significantly upregulated in the STAT3-OE group and was downregulated in the STAT3-KD group. (Fig. 2F), suggesting that STAT3 function as a regulator of STAiR18 transcription in MM cell lines. To assess this hypothesis, for the binding site of STAT3 on the promoter region of STAiR18, the JASPAR (http://jaspar.genereg.net/) database was used and two putative binding sites were predicted on STAiR18 promoter region for STAT3: -839 to -847 (score:12.4031) and − 1954 to -1962 (score:13.7105) (Fig. 2G). To verify this prediction, ChIP-qPCR assay was performed in U266 cell line. Result was obtained and showed that DNA sequence of promoter region was enriched in pSTAT3 group, suggesting that STAT3 could directly bind to the promoter region of STAiR18 and regulated transcription of STAiR18 (Fig. 2H, I).

STAiR18 promotes proliferation but no effect on apoptosis in MM cells

To explore the biological role of STAiR18 in MM, we used lentivirus to establish different MM cell lines, including U266/RPMI 8226-CTR-KD, U266/RPMI 8226-STAiR18-KD, U266/RPMI 8226-CTR-OE and U266/RPMI 8226-STAiR18-OE. qRT-PCR was used to verify the expression of STAiR18. As expected, STAiR18 expression was significantly reduced in the STAiR18-KD group, while it was substantially increased in the STAiR18-OE group (Fig. 3A). To evaluate the effect of STAiR18 on cell proliferation, CCK-8 assay was performed. Obviously, knocking down STAiR18 caused a significant suppression on cell proliferation in U266 and RPMI 8226 cells. Conversely, overexpression of STAiR18 markedly improved the cell viability of MM cells (Fig. 3B). Furthermore, cells were stained by Edu agent to mark the cells with capability of proliferation. Comparing with CTR-OE, higher percentage of Edu positive cell was observed in STAiR18-OE group, and lesser signal was observed in CTR-KD group (Fig. 3C, D). Cell cycle is important to regulate cell proliferation, thus, the effect of STAiR18 on the cell cycle was detected. Conspicuous accumulation of cells in G1 phase was found in the STAiR18-KD group, suggesting that downregulating expression of STAiR18 induced the arrest in G1 phase, probably leading to suppression of cell proliferation (Fig. 3E, F). Another evidence was provided by western blot which showed that expression of CyclinD1 was down-regulated in STAiR18-KD group (Fig. 3G, L). To determine whether Cyclin D1 suppression in STAiR18-KD cells stems from G1 phase arrest or direct transcriptional regulation, we detected CCND1 mRNA levels in these two groups. No significant difference CCND1 mRNA expression was observed between STAiR18-KD group and CTR-KD group (P > 0.05) (Fig. 3H), suggesting that reduction of Cyclin D1 protein was not mediated by direct transcriptional regulation, but was caused by G1 phase arrest. To assess the effect of STAiR18 on cell apoptosis, Annexin V/PI stain was used to mark apoptotic cell and flow cytometry analysis was performed. The results showed that the apoptosis rate in cells was not significantly affected by downregulation of STAiR18 (Fig. 3I, J). Meanwhile, no positive effect was detected on expression of apoptosis related protein, including anti-apoptotic protein BCL-2 and pro-apoptotic protein BAX (Fig. 3K, L). In conclusion, our results suggesting that STAiR18 function as pro-proliferative role, but not apoptosis in U266 and RPMI 8226 cell lines.

STAiR18 directly bind to miR-451a and regulates its activity

To further explore the mechanism by which lncRNA STAiR18 modulates MM development, we predicted its subcellular localization using the lncLocator database (Fig. 4A) and examine its subcellular localization in U266 cell line, using FISH assay. As predicted, STAiR18 was mainly localized in the cytoplasm (Fig. 4B). Moreover, bioinformatics analysis indicated that STAiR18 sequence harbors a putative binding site for miR-451a (Fig. 4C), which was reported to be involved in the progression of various cancers, including pancreatic cancer and colorectal cancer, and was also associated with drug resistance in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma [21–23]. Upon STAiR18 knockdown, we observed the upregulated expression of miR-451a (Fig. 4D), indicating that miR-451a probably was a downstream miRNA of STAiR18. Additional evidence was provided by our prior study, which suggested that the expression of miR-451a was elevated in bone marrows of MM patients, compared with healthy control [24]. Furthermore, a negative correlation between STAiR18 and miR-451a was discovered in bone marrows of MM patients (Fig. 4E). To further investigate the interaction between STAiR18 and miR-451a, a synthesized miR-451a mimic was introduced directly into U266 and RPMI 8226 cells and the overexpression of miR-451a was verified by qRT-PCR (Fig. 4F). To determine whether miR-451a is a target of STAiR18, dual-luciferase reporter assay was conducted in U266 cell, and results showed that miR-451a mimic reduced the activity of STAiR18-WT, whereas, this inhibitory effect was reversed upon mutation of the binding site (Fig. 4G). To further confirm the combination between miR-451a and STAiR18, RNA pull assay was performed. Biotinylated miR-451a (Bio-miR-451a) and Bio-miR-scr were designed and were respectively incubated with RNA, extracted from RPMI-8226-STAiR18-OE cell line. Enrichment was subsequently performed using streptavidin magnetic beads, and followed by qRT-PCR analysis. Result of qPCR showed that higher level of STAiR18 was enriched in Bio-miR-451a group, comparing with Bio-miR-scr, directly confirming the contact between miR-451and STAiR18 (Fig. 4H). Collectively, these findings suggest that miR-451a is a target of STAiR18.

STAiR18 positively regulates MM progression via modulating the miR-451a

The prior study confirmed that the miR-451a mediated post-transcriptional suppression to IL-6R, and inhibited the IL-6R/STAT3/JAK2 pathway [24]. To study the biological roles of STAiR18 and miR-451a in MM, U266 and RPMI 8226 cells were transfected with miR-451a mimic, miR-CTR, CTR-KD, STAiR18-KD, CTR-OE or STAiR18-OE. Results from western blot showed that either overexpressing miR-451a or reducing the expression of STAiR18 significantly reduced the protein level of IL-6R, pSTAT3, STAT3 and pJAK2 (Fig. 5A, B, C, D). Conversely, upregulating STAiR18 increased the protein level of IL-6R, pSTAT3, STAT3, and pJAK2. Interestingly, the inhibitory effects of STAiR18 on the IL-6R/STAT3/JAK2 pathway were abolished by the addition of either miR-451a mimic or by the knockdown of STAT3 (Fig. 5E, F, G, H). Furthermore, we observed that upregulation of miR-451a suppressed the proliferation of U266 and RPMI 8226 cells, and abolished the effect of STAiR18 on cell proliferation (Fig. 5I, J, K, L, M). These finding confirmed that STAiR18 participated in MM progression via regulating the miR-451a and IL-6R/STAT3/JAK2 pathway.

Knock down of STAiR18 suppresses MM tumor growth in vivo

To explore the effect of STAiR18 on tumor growth in vivo, we constructed the xenograft mice model via intravenously injecting CTR-KD RPMI 8226 or STAiR18-KD RPMI 8226 cell line into NOG mice, a parallel set of experiments was performed with U266 cells. MM development was monitored weekly by bioluminescence imaging (Fig. 6A for RPMI 8226; Supplementary materials S3A for U266). The density of signal of luminescence in BM sites was statistically analyzed (Fig. 6B for RPMI 8226; Supplementary materials S3B for U266). The effect of STAiR18 on the progression of MM was further evaluated by assessing the mice survival, showing that reducing the expression of STAiR18 benefited for mice survival with bearing MM (Fig. 6C). To clarify the BM infiltration status of MM cells (CD138⁺) in vivo, we obtained BM cells by grinding the forelimb and hindlimb bones of mice, and applied FCM technology to quantitatively detect the proportion of MM cells (CD138⁺), so as to evaluate the degree of BM infiltration of MM cells (CD138⁺). The results showed that the infiltration level of MM cells (CD138⁺) in the BM of STAiR18-KD mice was significantly lower than that in CTR-KD mice (P < 0.05, Fig. 6D, E). Furthermore, activation of IL-6R/STAT3/JAK2 pathway was evaluated and significant suppression was observed in STAiR18-KD mice (Fig. 6F, G). Meanwhile, the expression of STAiR18 and miR-451a were measured by qRT-PCR and the result showed that higher level of miR-451a and lower level of STAiR18 was prospectively observed in STAiR18-KD mice (Fig. 6H, I). The diagram of regulation mechanism among STAiR18, miR-451a and IL-6R/STAT3/JAK2 pathway was shown in Fig. 6J, suggesting that reducing expression of STAiR18 inhibits the activity of IL-6R/STAT3/JAK2 pathway by releasing miR-451a, which suppresses translational capacity of IL-6R, and finally rescues malignant phenotypes of MM both in vitro and vivo.

Discussion

MM is the second-most common hematological malignancy, and is associated with a poor prognosis [1]. Despite advances of novel therapeutic agents, MM remains a largely incurable and fatal disease [25, 26]. This is due to the strong proliferation of MM cells, which leads to the failure of treatment. Therefore, controlling the proliferation of MM cells and reducing their invasiveness have become critical research objectives. LncRNA abnormal expression is closely linked to cancer occurrence and development [27, 28]. Several studies suggested that the lncRNAs have developed to be a critical player in the intricate landscape of tumorigenesis and tumor progression across diverse cancer types [29–31]. LncRNAs not only regulate gene expression and participate in signal transduction pathways, but also regulate epigenetic, transcriptional, and post-transcriptional modifications [32]. Recent studies have shown that abnormal expression levels of lncRNAs are closely related to the diagnostics, pathology, prognosis and therapeutics of MM [33]. Previous study founded that knockdown of lncRNA SNHG16 suppressed MM cell proliferation by sponging miR-342-3p [34]. Another study revealed that exosomal lncRNA NEAT1 inhibits NK-cell activity to promote MM cell survival and immune Escape via an EZH2/PBX1 Axis [35]. The role and mechanism of lncRNA STAiR18 has been more extensively investigated in solid tumors, but are rarely studied in hematological tumors [36]. Previous study indicated that there is a close interaction between STAT3 and STAiR18, but the mechanism is unclear [37]. Therefore, the aim of this study was to elucidate the role and regulatory mechanism of lncRNA STAiR18 in the progression of MM. In this study, we conducted both in vitro and vivo approaches to explore the role of STAiR18 in the progression of MM. Firstly, clinical data revealed that STAiR18 was evaluated in MM patients and its upregulated expression was also confirmed in U266, H929 and RPMI 8226 cell lines. Subsequently, we identified the transcription factor STAT3 as a key regulator for STAiR18 transcription. Moreover, silencing STAiR18 not only blunted the IL-6R/ STAT3/JAK2 pathway but also curbed MM cell proliferation and locked the cell cycle in G1 phase. Conversely, knockdown of STAT3 weakened the up-regulation of IL-6R and p-STAT3, induced by STAiR18 overexpression, confirming that STAT3 is an essential upstream executor of STAiR18 in myeloma cells. Similar result was obtained from xenograft model using NOG mice suggesting that STAiR18 plays a positive role in MM development.

Mechanically, miR-451a was predicted and was confirmed to be a target of STAiR18, using bioinformation analysis and FISH assay. MiR-451a, located on the long arm of chromosome 17, is enriched in blood, pancreas, and other tissues [38]. MiR-451a has been considered to be a biomarker in medullary thyroid carcinoma [39]. Additionally, miR-451a has been shown to be closely associated with tumor proliferation, migration, EMT, metastasis, as well as regulation of various cellular pathways [40–42]. However, the molecular mechanism of miR-451a in MM has not yet been fully revealed. Previous reports about the mechanism of lncRNA sponge showed that the expression of miRNA was negatively regulated after overexpression or knockdown of lncRNA [42], but the STAiR18 specific mechanism was not described in detail [43]. Our results reveal that STAiR18 can directly bind to miR-451a, thereby suppressing its activity. In both U266 and RPMI 8226 cells, overexpression of miR-451a significantly inhibited cell proliferation and negated the positive impact of STAiR18 on the IL-6R/STAT3/JAK2 pathway and cell proliferation. Specifically, reducing activity of miR-451a resulted in decreased combination with mRNA of IL-6R, promoting translation of IL-6R. The upregulation of IL-6R further enhances the activation of IL-6R/STAT3/JAK2 signaling pathway, including the transcription factor STAT3, which in turn promotes the transcription of STAiR18, establishing a positive feedback loop involving STAiR18, miR-451a, and the IL-6R/STAT3/JAK2 pathway. This positive feedback loop ultimately drives the proliferation of MM cells.

In conclusion, we explored a novel mechanism of the role of STAiR18 in MM development. STAT3 upregulates the transcription of STAiR18, which acts as a sponge for miR-451a and improves translational capacity of IL-6R, thereby activating the IL-6R/STAT3/JAK2 signaling pathway. These finding highlight STAiR18 as a potential therapeutic target for MM treatment.

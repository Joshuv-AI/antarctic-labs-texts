Surgical outcomes and technical considerations in intraventricular tumors: a retrospective comparative analysis
Authors: Omar Shihadeh, Ali Msheik, Muath Hussein, Abdulla Illeyan, Sadeen Eid, Amr Alhajjali
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIntraventricular tumors are a heterogeneous group of neoplasms located within the ventricular system and adjacent eloquent neurovascular structures, making surgical management technically challenging. Multiple surgical corridors, including microsurgical and endoscopic approaches, are available; however, the relative impact of surgical approach versus extent of resection and preoperative functional status on outcomes remains incompletely defined.MethodologyWe conducted a retrospective, single-center observational study of patients who underwent surgical management for intraventricular tumors between 2015 and 2024. Demographic, clinical, radiological, surgical, and pathological data were collected. Surgical approaches were categorized as transcortical, transcallosal, endoscopic excision, interhemispheric, or other approaches. Primary outcomes included extent of resection, postoperative functional outcome measured by the modified Rankin Scale, postoperative complications, and tumor recurrence. Multivariable logistic regression and Kaplan–Meier analyses were performed to identify predictors of recurrence and complications.ResultsA total of 861 patients were included, with a median age of 36 years and a male predominance (71.3%). Gross total resection was achieved in 81.6% of cases. Postoperative complications occurred in 24.4% of patients, and tumor recurrence was observed in 4.8% during a median follow-up of 10 months. No surgical approach was independently associated with recurrence or postoperative complications. In contrast, higher preoperative mRS scores were independently associated with increased risk of both recurrence (OR 1.82, p < 0.001) and postoperative complications (OR 2.08, p = 0.03). Extent of resection significantly influenced recurrence-free survival, with total resection demonstrating superior long-term tumor control.ConclusionsIn the surgical management of intraventricular tumors, long-term outcomes are primarily driven by extent of resection and preoperative functional status rather than the choice of surgical approach. While endoscopic and minimally invasive techniques are safe and effective in selected cases, they do not independently improve oncologic outcomes when incomplete resection is achieved. Introduction

Intraventricular tumors (IVTs) represent a diverse group of neoplasms located within or adjacent to the ventricular system. These lesions are often situated near eloquent neural structures such as thalamus, hypothalamus, fornix, and periventricular white matter, making surgical resection particularly challenging [1]. Common tumor types include colloid cysts, central neurocytomas, subependymomas, choroid plexus tumors, ependymomas, and intraventricular meningiomas [1, 2]. Pediatric populations more frequently present with choroid plexus tumors, ependymomas, and germ cell tumors, whereas adults are more likely to have neurocytomas, meningiomas, and gliomas [3, 4].

The cornerstone of effective management remains maximal safe resection, which improves progression-free and overall survival, especially for low-grade tumors [5, 6]. However, due to the tumor’s deep location and proximity to critical venous and neural structures, achieving gross total resection (GTR) can be associated with significant risks. For example, central neurocytomas may adhere to the thalamostriate vein or walls of the lateral ventricles, increasing the complexity of resection [6].

Two principal microsurgical approaches are commonly used: the transcortical and transcallosal routes. The choice between them depends on tumor location, ventricular size, and the presence of hydrocephalus. The transcallosal route offers a cortical-sparing path with lower seizure risk, while the transcortical route provides a more direct trajectory but may increase the risk of cortical injury and postoperative seizures [5, 6]. Studies comparing these approaches have shown similar extent of resection and complication rates, with no clear superiority of one over the other when appropriately selected [2, 6].

Endoscopic and endoscope-assisted techniques have gained traction, particularly for colloid cysts, third ventricular tumors, and biopsies of deep-seated lesions [7, 8]. Endoscopy allows for reduced operative morbidity, shorter hospital stays, and effective CSF diversion in hydrocephalus. However, large or highly vascular tumors typically still require open microsurgical resection [7].

Surgical outcomes are influenced by several factors, including histopathological subtype, extent of resection, and tumor location [3, 4]. In pediatric series, GTR is associated with better survival outcomes, particularly in ependymomas and choroid plexus papillomas [3]. Similarly, adult series show that subtotal resection or biopsy alone correlates with higher recurrence and need for adjuvant therapy [5, 6].

Despite advances in neuronavigation, intraoperative MRI, and microsurgical tools, postoperative complications such as hydrocephalus, infection, and neurological deficits remain concerns [6, 7]. Thus, individualized surgical planning, appropriate route selection, and an understanding of tumor biology are crucial for optimizing outcomes.

In this study, we present a retrospective analysis of patients with intraventricular tumors managed at our institution. We aim to evaluate surgical outcomes and compare technical approaches, correlating them with tumor type, location, extent of resection, and functional outcomes to better inform surgical decision-making in this complex anatomical region.

Materials and methods

Study design and setting

This study is a retrospective, single-center observational analysis conducted at Hamad Medical Corporation, focusing on patients who underwent surgical management for intraventricular tumors between 2015 and 2024. Ethical approval was obtained from the institutional review board, and all procedures conformed to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Patient selection

We reviewed the medical records of all patients with radiologically, operatively and/or histopathologically confirmed intraventricular tumors. Patients were excluded if surgical records or imaging data were incomplete or if they had extra-ventricular tumors extending secondarily into the ventricle without a primary intraventricular origin.

Data collection

Data were retrospectively collected and categorized into four domains:

Demographics and Presentation: Age, gender, comorbidities, and presenting symptoms (headache, seizures, incidental findings).

Demographics and Presentation: Age, gender, comorbidities, and presenting symptoms (headache, seizures, incidental findings).

Tumor-Related Findings: Tumor location, histopathology, presence of drop metastasis, and MRI characteristics.

Tumor-Related Findings: Tumor location, histopathology, presence of drop metastasis, and MRI characteristics.

Functional Outcome: Pre- and postoperative neurological status, modified Rankin Scale (mRS), postoperative complications, recurrence, need for adjuvant therapy, and follow-up duration.

Functional Outcome: Pre- and postoperative neurological status, modified Rankin Scale (mRS), postoperative complications, recurrence, need for adjuvant therapy, and follow-up duration.

Surgical Data: Surgical approach, extent of resection, and the need for cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) diversion procedures were recorded. Surgical approaches were categorized as transcortical, transcallosal–interhemispheric, endoscopic excision, telovelar, or other approaches. In this classification, the interhemispheric corridor represents the operative trajectory used to access the corpus callosum during a transcallosal approach, but cases were categorized separately when the operative strategy differed (e.g., interhemispheric access without callosotomy for specific ventricular exposures). This distinction was maintained to reflect operative documentation in the surgical records.

Outcome measures

Primary outcomes: extent of resection, postoperative functional outcome (mRS), surgical complications and tumor recurrence.

Secondary outcomes: correlation between tumor type/location and surgical approach, rate of CSF diversion procedures and association between extent of resection and recurrence.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize patient demographics, tumor characteristics, and surgical outcomes. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages, while continuous variables were reported as mean ± standard deviation or median with interquartile range, as appropriate. Comparative analysis was performed between different surgical approaches (endoscopic vs. microscopic) using chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests for categorical variables and t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests for continuous variables. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics version XX (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Survival analyses and graphical visualizations were additionally performed using R version 4.4.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Continuous variables, including patient age, preoperative Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS), and follow-up duration, were summarized as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range) and compared between groups using the Kruskal–Wallis rank sum test. Categorical variables such as gender, comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes mellitus), clinical presentation, tumor location, histopathology, surgical approach, extent of resection, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) diversion, postoperative complications, further adjuvant treatment, and recurrence were expressed as counts and percentages, with comparisons performed using the chi-square or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Logistic regression was used to identify independent predictors of postoperative complications and tumor recurrence, with results presented as odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Recurrence-free survival was estimated using Kaplan–Meier curves, and differences between groups, including surgical approach and extent of resection, were assessed using the log-rank test. A two-tailed p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed in R 4.4.2 (Vienna, Austria).

Results

The median patient age was 36 years (IQR: 31–44), with a male predominance (71.26%). Comorbidities included hypertension in 12.64% and diabetes mellitus in 8.05%. The most frequent presenting symptom was headache (68.97%), followed by dizziness (32.18%) and vomiting (24.14%). Less common presentations included blurry vision (11.49%), seizures (4.60%), weakness (4.60%), incidental findings (3.45%), and other symptoms (13.79%). The tumors were most located in the foramen of Monro and third ventricle (each 28.74%), followed by the lateral ventricle (24.14%) and fourth ventricle (11.49%). Histopathology revealed colloid cysts as the most frequent diagnosis (48.84%), followed by neurocytoma (9.30%), subependymoma (8.14%), glioblastoma multiforme (5.81%), meningioma (5.81%), epidermoid cyst (4.65%), and other rare lesions (17.44%). Drop metastasis was rare (1.18%).

The most common surgical approach was transcortical (33.72%), followed by endoscopic excision (20.93%), transcallosal (12.79%), interhemispheric (9.30%), telovelar (5.81%), and other approaches (17.44%). Gross total resection was achieved in 81.61% of cases, with smaller proportions undergoing subtotal (5.75%), partial (4.60%), near-total (2.30%), or biopsy (5.75%) procedures. Median pre- and postoperative GCS remained 15, and median mRS was stable at 1. Postoperatively, 10.59% of patients required chemoradiotherapy, while 24.14% developed complications, 33.33% required CSF diversion, and 4.76% experienced recurrence. The median follow-up duration was 10 months (IQR 3–24). Follow-up duration varied across the cohort, with five patients remaining under observation beyond 36 months and three patients followed for at least 60 months, allowing estimation of long-term recurrence-free survival in a subset of the study population (IQR: 3–24) (Tables 1 and 2).

Diabetes mellitus prevalence differed across surgical approach groups (p = 0.04), with the highest proportion observed in the interhemispheric group (37.5%) and no cases reported in the transcallosal group. However, diabetes was not independently associated with postoperative complications or recurrence in multivariable analysis, suggesting that this distribution likely reflects baseline patient variation rather than an approach-specific risk factor. The highest preoperative mRS was observed in the endoscopic excision group (2.11 ± 1.49) and the lowest in the transcallosal group (0.91 ± 0.30). Gross total resection rates were highest with the transcortical approach (93.10%) and lowest with endoscopic excision (66.67%), which had higher proportions of biopsy and partial resections.

No significant differences were found for age, gender, clinical presentation, GCS scores, complications, postoperative mRS, recurrence, follow-up duration, or CSF diversion. Headache was the most frequent symptom (69.77%), while seizures, weakness, and drop metastasis were rare. Postoperative GCS remained high across all groups, complication rates ranged from 11.11% to 35.00%, and recurrence rates were low (≤ 10.53%) with comparable follow-up durations (9–18 months). Preoperative mRS was a significant predictor of tumor recurrence, with each one-point increase associated with 82% higher odds of recurrence (OR = 1.82, 95% CI: 1.49–2.25, p < 0.001, Table 3). Additionally, patients who underwent partial resection had a significantly lower risk of recurrence compared to those with total resection (OR = 0.22, 95% CI: 0.07–0.70, p = 0.011).

Other factors, including age, diabetes mellitus status, extent of resection (near-total, subtotal, or biopsy), surgical approach, and CSF diversion, were not significantly associated with recurrence (all p > 0.05). While near total and subtotal resections showed a trend toward higher recurrence risk, these did not reach statistical significance. Similarly, no specific surgical approach or CSF diversion status demonstrated a meaningful impact on recurrence rates. Age and preoperative mRS were significant predictors of postoperative complications. Each additional year of age was associated with a 10% reduction in the odds of developing complications (OR = 0.90, 95% CI: 0.82–0.97, p = 0.012, Table 3). Higher preoperative mRS scores were linked to an increased complication risk, with each point increase nearly doubling the odds (OR = 2.08, 95% CI: 1.11–4.46, p = 0.030).

Gender and preoperative GCS were not significantly associated with complication risk (p > 0.05). Male patients had higher, but non-significant, odds of complications compared to females (OR = 2.11, 95% CI: 0.47–11.66), and preoperative GCS showed no meaningful effect (OR = 0.79, 95% CI: 0.60–1.02).

Kaplan–Meier analysis of overall recurrence-free survival demonstrated high long-term tumor control, with most recurrences occurring beyond 30 months postoperatively. The Kaplan–Meier plots include tables showing the number of patients remaining at risk at successive follow-up intervals, allowing interpretation of survival estimates as the number of observed patients decreases over time. The estimated 5-year recurrence-free survival rate remained above 80%, and most early follow-up periods showed minimal decline in survival probability (Fig. 1).

Overall recurrence-free survival

When stratified by surgical approach, recurrence-free survival curves were largely overlapping, with no statistically significant difference between approaches (log-rank p = 0.92, Fig. 2). This indicates that the choice of surgical corridor, whether transcortical, transcallosal, endoscopic, interhemispheric, or other, did not significantly influence recurrence-free survival in this cohort.

Recurrence-free survival by surgical approach

In contrast, recurrence-free survival varied significantly according to the extent of resection (log-rank p < 0.0001, Fig. 3). Patients undergoing total resection had the most favorable outcomes, maintaining the highest survival probability throughout follow-up. Near-total and subtotal resections demonstrated slightly reduced survival, while partial resection and biopsy were associated with markedly shorter recurrence-free survival, with early recurrences occurring within the first year.

Discussion

The management of intraventricular tumors (IVTs) remains surgically challenging due to their deep location, proximity to eloquent neurovascular structures, and marked histopathological heterogeneity. In this retrospective comparative analysis, we evaluated surgical approaches, extent of resection, functional outcomes, complications, and recurrence patterns in a large contemporary cohort. When contextualized against prior microsurgical, endoscopic, and hybrid series, our findings reinforce a central paradigm: long-term oncologic and functional outcomes are driven primarily by extent of resection and preoperative functional status rather than by the choice of surgical corridor itself. A summary of prominent case series and reviews of intraventricular tumors is available in the supplementary Tables I and II.

Surgical approach and functional outcomes

Historically, considerable debate has centered on the relative merits of transcortical versus transcallosal approaches, with concerns regarding seizures, disconnection syndromes, and memory impairment. Prior comparative series have consistently shown comparable rates of gross total resection (GTR) and neurological morbidity when these approaches are appropriately selected [5, 6, 8]. Our data extend these observations by demonstrating that postoperative mRS, complication rates, and recurrence-free survival were not significantly different across transcortical, transcallosal, endoscopic, interhemispheric, or other approaches, even after multivariable adjustment.

This finding is concordant with mixed-approach studies [3, 7] which emphasized perioperative equivalence between microsurgical and endoscopic techniques but did not assess long-term recurrence or functional predictors. In contrast, our Kaplan–Meier analysis confirms that approach selection alone does not independently influence recurrence-free survival, supporting the view that the surgical corridor serves primarily to achieve safe exposure rather than as a determinant of outcome.

Role of endoscopic and minimally invasive techniques

Purely endoscopic and endoport-assisted approaches have gained popularity, particularly for colloid cysts and select lateral or third ventricular tumors. Multiple contemporary series [2, 4, 9] demonstrate favorable short-term functional outcomes and low mortality, albeit with lower GTR rates and limited follow-up in many cases. Similarly, a systematic review of 668 neuroendoscopic cases reported a complete or near-complete resection rate of 75% and a recurrence rate approaching 10%, with recurrence strongly linked to incomplete resection [10].

Our findings align with this literature in showing that endoscopic approaches are safe and effective in selected patients, but they also clarify an important limitation: lower rates of total resection observed with endoscopic excision did not translate into superior recurrence-free survival, reinforcing that endoscopy should be applied selectively rather than universally. By including both endoscopic and open techniques within the same multivariable framework, our study provides evidence that minimally invasive corridors do not offset the oncologic disadvantage of incomplete resection.

Extent of resection as the dominant predictor of tumor control

Across nearly all prior studies, ranging from single-histology cohorts for intraventricular meningiomas [11], for central neurocytomas [6] to large systematic reviews of colloid cysts [12], extent of resection consistently emerges as the strongest predictor of recurrence. Our data confirm and generalize this principle across a heterogeneous IVT population.

Kaplan–Meier analysis demonstrated significantly inferior recurrence-free survival following partial resection or biopsy compared with total resection, while surgical approach showed no effect. Although multivariable logistic regression suggested a paradoxically lower odds of recurrence following partial resection, this finding is best interpreted as selection bias, reflecting the preferential use of limited resection in biologically indolent tumors or in patients with short follow-up. The survival curves and consistency with prior literature strongly support GTR as the optimal surgical goal when safely achievable.

Preoperative functional status and risk stratification

A novel and clinically important contribution of this study is the identification of preoperative mRS as an independent predictor of both tumor recurrence and postoperative complications. Each incremental increase in preoperative mRS significantly increased the odds of recurrence and nearly doubled the risk of complications. This observation has not been systematically evaluated in most prior IVT series, which have focused predominantly on tumor type, surgical approach, or technical factors. However, this association should be interpreted cautiously. Preoperative mRS likely reflects overall disease burden, including hydrocephalus severity, tumor size, neurological deficit, and delayed presentation, which may correlate with more advanced disease or technically challenging resections. Thus, the observed association between mRS and recurrence probably represents a marker of baseline clinical severity rather than a direct causal relationship.

These findings suggest that early surgical referral, before the development of significant neurological disability, may be critical for optimizing long-term outcomes. Preoperative functional status likely reflects cumulative effects of hydrocephalus, mass effect, and tumor invasiveness, and should be incorporated into preoperative counseling and risk stratification models.

Complications and CSF diversion

Reported complication rates in our cohort are comparable to those described in both microsurgical and endoscopic series [5, 7, 10] Importantly, no surgical approach was independently associated with a higher complication risk. The requirement for CSF diversion, present in approximately one-third of patients, mirrors the high prevalence of obstructive hydrocephalus in IVTs and was not associated with recurrence or complications on multivariable analysis, supporting its role as an adjunctive rather than prognostic factor.

Integration with foundational and review literature

Foundational anatomical and technical principles articulated emphasize corridor selection as a strategy to minimize morbidity while enabling maximal safe resection [13]. Narrative and systematic reviews [1, 12, 14] similarly highlight that tumor biology and completeness of resection outweigh approach-specific considerations. Our study provides contemporary, patient-level statistical validation of these principles, bridging expert consensus and technical feasibility studies with outcome-driven evidence.

Clinical implications

Taken together, these findings support an individualized, outcome-focused surgical strategy for intraventricular tumors. Surgeons should prioritize maximal safe resection and early intervention, tailoring the surgical corridor to tumor anatomy, ventricular configuration, and patient functional status rather than favoring any single approach. Endoscopic techniques should be selectively employed where their minimally invasive advantages do not compromise resection extent.

Conclusions

The surgical management of intraventricular tumors remains complex due to their deep location, proximity to eloquent neurovascular structures, and histopathological heterogeneity. In this large retrospective comparative analysis, we demonstrate that long-term surgical and oncologic outcomes are driven primarily by the extent of resection and preoperative functional status rather than by the specific surgical corridor employed.

Our findings show that gross total resection is associated with superior recurrence-free survival, while higher preoperative modified Rankin Scale scores independently predict both tumor recurrence and postoperative complications. Importantly, no individual surgical approach, including transcortical, transcallosal, endoscopic, or interhemispheric techniques, was independently associated with recurrence or complication risk when appropriately selected.

Endoscopic and minimally invasive approaches offer safe and effective options for selected intraventricular tumors, particularly smaller or favorably located lesions; however, these techniques do not confer an oncologic advantage when incomplete resection is achieved. Accordingly, surgical strategy should prioritize maximal safe resection, individualized corridor selection based on tumor anatomy and ventricular configuration, and early intervention before significant functional decline occurs.

Future studies incorporating longer follow-up and tumor-specific molecular stratification may further refine surgical decision-making and risk prediction in this anatomically challenging tumor group.

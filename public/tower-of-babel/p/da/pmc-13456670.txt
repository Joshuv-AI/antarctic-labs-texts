Hypoperfusion Intensity Ratio as a Predictive Marker in Pre-Endovascular Treatment Perfusion Magnetic Resonance Image for Acute Middle Cerebral Artery Occlusion with Intracranial Atherosclerosis
Authors: Yoonkyung Lee, Jae-Kwan Cha, Dae-Hyun Kim, Jung Hwa Seo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionIdentifying the cause of middle cerebral artery (MCA) occlusion before endovascular treatment in acute ischemic stroke is useful. Hypoperfusion intensity ratio (HIR) is defined as the volumetric ratio of tissue with a time-to-maximum (Tmax) >10 s to that with Tmax >6 s in perfusion magnetic resonance image (MRI). In this study, using perfusion MRI, we hypothesized that HIR could be associated with intracranial atherosclerotic disease (ICAD) in acute MCA occlusion. We also performed machine learning-based feature importance analysis to identify factors associated with the mechanism of acute MCA occlusion.MethodsWe analyzed 117 patients with acute MCA occlusion treated with EVT and underwent RAPID MRI between March 2020 and December 2023. Patients were classified into the ICAD and non-ICAD groups. Clinical and imaging parameters were assessed using logistic regression. The cutoff value of HIR was calculated using Youden’s index, and its predictive value was compared using the DeLong test. Development of a machine learning algorithm for predicting ICAD using XGBoost (eXtreme Gradient Boosting) and feature importance analysis were performed.ResultsA total of 34% were ICAD group with higher low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels, mild NIHSS, small DWI size, and low HIR. Late onset-to-puncture time (OTP) (>6 h) (OR: 9.67), no susceptibility vessel sign (SVS; OR: 9.05), no initial AF (OR: 63.67), low HIR (<0.31) (OR: 7.70) were associated with ICAD. Including HIR improved predictive performance (area under the curve [AUC] 0.73 vs. 0.88, p value <0.001). The XGBoost model showed high accuracy (0.92), SHAP value identified HIR, OTP, initial AF, SVS were important factors.ConclusionHIR is a potential marker to determine the mechanism of acute MCA occlusion. Combined with established clinical and radiological features, it helps in identifying the ICAD-related MCA occlusion before EVT.

Introduction

Endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) is a gold standard treatment for acute ischemic stroke (AIS) due to large-vessel occlusion (LVO) and has been shown to significantly improve prognosis by improving neurological and functional outcomes. In the American Heart Association/American Stroke Association Acute Stroke Guidelines from 2018, treatment decisions for patients beyond 6 h from symptom onset are based on clinical-diffusion mismatch and diffusion-perfusion mismatch [1].

A literature review of clinical trials targeting intravenous thrombolysis and mechanical thrombectomy showed that combined therapy was associated with effective recanalization, with successful reperfusion achieved in approximately 75% of cases [2]. Real-world data from a mechanical thrombectomy registry demonstrated successful reperfusion in over 90% of patients, with intracranial atherosclerotic disease observed in approximately 10% of cases [3].

Intracranial atherosclerotic disease (ICAD) is a common cause of ischemic stroke, particularly in Asian populations [4]. In intracranial atherosclerotic stenosis (ICAS)-related LVO, EVT failure due to residual stenosis or reocclusion occurs frequently and is associated with unfavorable outcomes [5]. Accordingly, rescue strategies such as angioplasty or stenting may be required, and identification of the underlying occlusion mechanism using preprocedural clinical and imaging parameters is essential for optimizing treatment strategy and improving EVT success [4–11].

Several imaging markers can predict the underlying cause of LVO at the preprocedural stage. In the case of ICAD, noncontrast computed tomography (CT) demonstrates the absence of a hyperdense sign [12], small core infarction volume due to the leptomeningeal collaterals [4], and borderzone or scattered infarction patterns on diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), and when angiographic findings show multifocal narrowing and digital subtraction angiography shows truncal type occlusion [12]. For cardioembolism-related LVO, atrial fibrillation on initial electrocardiography and clot sign on gradient-echo imaging (GRE) are useful markers.

Hypoperfusion intensity ratio (HIR) is a biomarker that reflects collateral status and a predicts of infarct growth and is defined as time-to-maximum (Tmax) >10 s to that with Tmax >6 s ratio. In cases of low HIR, ischemic core growth occurs more slowly [13, 14]. Previous studies have reported the prediction of LVO mechanisms in AIS using HIR derived from CT perfusion imaging; however, the role of HIR derived from perfusion magnetic resonance image (MRI) in differentiating ICAD-related occlusion in the preprocedural EVT setting remains less well characterized [15, 16]. In this study, using perfusion MRI as an EVT pretreatment imaging, we hypothesized that HIR could be a useful marker for predicting ICAD in AIS with middle cerebral artery (MCA) occlusion.

Methods

Institutional and Ethical Approval

The study protocol was reviewed and approved by Dong-A University Hospital Institutional Review Board (IRB No: DAUHIRB-24-115). The study was a retrospective study; the requirement for written informed consent was waived.

Study Design and Population

All screened patients were admitted to Dong-A University Hospital for AIS between March 2020 and December 2023. Inclusion criteria were patients who (1) received endovascular treatment, (2) underwent automatic quantitative measurement of ischemic core and perfusion defect of acute LVO using RAPID MRI software (RAPID, iSchemaView, Menlo Park, CA, USA), and (3) had acute LVO located in the M1 segment of the MCA.

Patients were excluded if 90-day clinical outcomes were unavailable, if occlusions involved vascular territories other than the MCA, or if perfusion MRI data were technically inadequate. A flowchart illustrating patient inclusion and exclusion is shown in Figure 1.

Flowchart of patient enrollment data.

Clinical and Imaging Parameters

Demographic characteristics, vascular risk factors, laboratory data were collected. Stroke subtype classification was determined using an MRI-based algorithm for acute ischemic stroke classification (MAGIC), based on an extended Trial of Org 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment (TOAST) classification.

The National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score at admission (NIHSS0), administration of intravenous tissue plasminogen activator, time from symptom onset (or last known well in unwitnessed cases) to groin puncture (onset-to-puncture time [OTP]), and the modified Rankin Scale score at 90 days (mRS-3M) were obtained from electronic medical records. Functional outcomes were dichotomized into favorable (mRS 0–2) and unfavorable (mRS 3–6) outcomes [17].

Multimodal brain MRI scans were used. Acute LVO location was confirmed using magnetic resonance angiography, and MCA occlusions were categorized as proximal or distal based on the midpoint of the M1 segment. Ischemic core volume and perfusion parameters were automatically analyzed using RAPID software. HIR was defined as the volume with Tmax >10 s divided by the volume with Tmax >6 s [18].

EVT techniques and device selection were determined by the interventionist. Reperfusion status after thrombectomy was assessed using the modified thrombolysis in cerebral infarction (mTICI) grading system. Successful reperfusion was defined as mTICI grades 2b, 2c, or 3, and complete reperfusion as mTICI grade 3 [19]. Residual stenosis was defined as more than 50% stenosis of the MCA on follow-up vascular imaging performed 24 h after the procedure [4].

Statistical Analysis

Clinical parameters and MRI parameters were analyzed. Shapiro-Wilk test was used to assess normality of continuous variables. Categorical variables were presented as absolute values and percentages and continuous variables as median (interquartile range) or means (standard deviation) as appropriate. Statistical significance for intergroup differences was assessed by Mann-Whitney U test or t test for continuous variables and Pearson’s chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables.

Variables were selected based on clinical relevance and their associations with ICAD were initially explored using univariate analysis. Multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed to evaluate independent associations. Given the modest sample size, the number of predictors included in the model was restricted to reduce the risk of overfitting. A stepwise procedure was used to derive a parsimonious model. Multicollinearity among predictors was assessed using variance inflation factors, and all variables demonstrated variance inflation factor values <5. The cutoff value for HIR was determined using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis with Youden’s index. The DeLong test was used to evaluate whether the model incorporating HIR had better predictive performance, and model discrimination was assessed using the ROC curve and the area under the curve (AUC). A p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using STATA (version 18.0, StataCorp LLC, TX, USA).

Development of a Machine Learning Algorithm for ICAD Prediction

An extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost) model was used to evaluate predictors of intracranial atherosclerotic disease. Data were divided into training and testing sets, and model performance was assessed using the area under the ROC curve. Feature importance was interpreted using Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP).

To determine characteristics of ICAD group, the subjects were divided into ICAD-related MCA occlusion (TOAST classification: large artery atherosclerosis, ICAD group) and non-ICAD-related MCA occlusion (TOAST classification: cardioembolism, other, and undetermined negative). 40 (34%) patients were categorized as ICAD group and 77 (66%) patients as non-ICAD group. In non-ICAD group, 61 (81%) patients were cardioembolic stroke, 8 (10%) patients were other etiology, 7 (9%) patients were undetermined etiology. The ICAD group had higher LDL-C levels (113.58 ± 38.32 mg/dL vs. 94.40 ± 36.87 mg/dL, p = 0.009), more rate of residual stenosis (72.5% vs. 13.0%, p < 0.001), and a higher rate of angioplasty (35.0% vs. 5.2%, p < 0.001). In addition, the ICAD group had a lower rate of AF on initial electrocardiography (2.5% vs. 55.8%, p < 0.001), lower rate of susceptibility vessel sign (SVS) on GRE (17.5% vs. 64.9%, p < 0.001), a longer OTP (879.98 ± 1,138.99 min vs. 420.61 ± 366.07 min, p < 0.001), lower NIHSS score (8 [5–14] vs. 12 [8–16], p = 0.023), and smaller DWI lesion volumes (14.18 ± 25.68 cc vs. 33.86 ± 41.03 cc, p = 0.013). Moreover, patients in the ICAD group had a significantly lower HIR compared with those in the non-ICAD group (0.23 ± 0.16 vs. 0.43 ± 0.19, p < 0.001).

Univariate analysis showed positive associations with ICAD for OTP (OR: 1.00, p = 0.002) and LDL-C levels (OR: 1.01, p = 0.01). In contrast, negative correlations with ICAD were observed for initial AF (OR: 0.02, p < 0.001), SVS (OR: 0.11, p < 0.001), baseline NIHSS (OR: 0.92, p = 0.025), intravenous tissue plasminogen activator administration (OR: 0.30, p = 0.005), Tmax >10 s volume (OR: 0.98, p = 0.001), Tmax >8 s volume (OR: 0.98, p = 0.005), and HIR (OR: 0.003, p < 0.001) (online suppl. Table 1; for all online suppl. material, see https://doi.org/10.1159/000552144).

In multivariable logistic regression analysis, late OTP (>6 h) (OR: 9.66, 95% CI: 2.37–39.32), absence of SVS (OR: 9.05, 95% CI: 2.32–35.31), absence of initial AF (OR: 63.67, 95% CI: 6.12–662.70), and low HIR (<0.31) (OR: 7.70, 95% CI: 2.06–28.82) were independently associated with ICAD (Table 2). In sensitivity analysis using Firth penalized logistic regression to account for potential small-sample bias, the direction of associations remained consistent. A lower HIR value was associated with an increased likelihood of ICAD. The optimal HIR cutoff for best sensitivity (0.95) and specificity (0.87) was 0.31 (Youden’s index, 0.82) (shown in online suppl. Fig. 1). In the logistic regression analysis, Model 2, which incorporated HIR <0.31 in addition to OTP >6 h, SVS, and initial AF demonstrated significantly better predictive performance compared to Model 1 (AUC 0.73 vs. 0.88, p value <0.001) (shown in online suppl. Fig. 2).

Discussion

In this study, HIR derived from perfusion MRI was associated with ICAD in patients with acute MCA occlusion. Early identification of the underlying stroke mechanism before endovascular treatment may facilitate individualized treatment strategies, particularly as EVT indications expand to extended time windows.

HIR has been established as a marker reflecting collateral status and infarct progression in AIS with LVO [20]. In a study examining the association between infarct progression, functional outcomes, and HIR targeting the DEFUSE 2 study cohort, HIR was defined as the ratio of Tmax >10-s lesion volume to Tmax >6-s lesion volume, and patients were classified into good and poor collateral flow groups based on the median value of 0.4, and functional outcomes were compared accordingly [21].

Previous CT perfusion-based studies have demonstrated that HIR is a prognostic marker, with higher values (>0.5) associated with worse early neurological outcomes, whereas lower values (<0.3) are associated with better prognosis in patients with AIS and LVO [14, 21]. A recent study suggested that Tmax mismatch-based metrics, including HIR, may be useful for identifying ICAD-related large vessel occlusion using both CT perfusion and MRI data [15, 22]. In contrast, this study evaluated MRI-derived HIR processed with RAPID software to evaluate its utility for preprocedural etiologic identification.

Previous studies have consistently reported that ICAD-related LVO is associated with clinical and imaging features, including lower stroke severity, male sex, smoking history, absence of atrial fibrillation and susceptibility vessel sign, and proximal M1 occlusion [4, 23–25]. In our cohort, several of these parameters showed significant associations with ICAD on univariate analysis, including LDL-C levels, DWI lesion size, baseline NIHSS, OTP, SVS, AF status, and Tmax-derived perfusion metrics. Multivariable analysis revealed late OTP (>6 h) (OR: 9.67), absence of SVS (OR: 9.05), absence of initial AF (OR: 63.67), and low HIR (<0.31) (OR: 7.70) were identified as independent predictors of ICAD-related MCA occlusion. The consistency of findings in penalized regression further supports the robustness of the observed associations. Given the modest sample size, the magnitude of these odds ratios should be interpreted with caution.

Incorporation of HIR <0.31 as a variable was associated with improved predictive performance. Based on variables that can predict ICAD before performing EVT, the XGBoost model demonstrated favorable discrimination in predicting ICAD, as reflected by the area under the ROC curve. In feature importance analysis, indicators such as OTP, initial AF, SVS, and HIR showed high SHAP values, indicating an important role in distinguishing whether the underlying MCA etiology was ICAD. The SHAP force plot also provided a visual representation of each feature’s contribution to the model’s prediction. In the binary logistic XGBoost model, f(x) represents the log-odds, and application of the sigmoid function yields the predicted probability. This example illustrates the model’s decision-making process but should not be interpreted as definitive individual-level prediction.

Machine learning models that predict the mechanism of acute MCA occlusion have not been extensively studied to date. In this study, the feature importance for predicting the mechanism of acute MCA occlusion was confirmed using the XGBoost model, and it showed promising performance and may help inform acute EVT treatment strategies for patients with acute MCA occlusion.

This study has some limitations because it is a retrospective, single-center analysis, and given that ICAD occurs at a higher rate in Asian populations, prospective multicenter analyses are needed to validate these findings in other populations. Due to the limited number of events, the risk of overfitting and effect size inflation cannot be completely excluded. Although penalized regression analysis yielded directionally consistent results, the magnitude of certain associations, particularly for absence of atrial fibrillation, may still reflect residual small-sample bias. These findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating and require external validation in larger, independent cohorts. The HIR cutoff identified in this cohort should likewise be considered exploratory and requires external validation before clinical application. In addition, stroke subtype classification based on imaging algorithms may be imperfect; patients who do not undergo recanalization are classified as LAA but may include cases in which the underlying etiology is unclear, and patients categorized as “unknown” or “two or more” in the TOAST classification may include cases of LAA or cardioembolism. Finally, if RAPID recognizes the Tmax volume as “0,” HIR is marked as “0” or not available, and although cases with PWI values of 0 were excluded in our data, further consideration is required regarding how to appropriately incorporate such data.

HIR may serve as a potential marker associated with the mechanism of acute MCA occlusion. When combined with other established clinical and radiological features, such as OTP, SVS, initial AF, HIR may improve the estimation of ICAD-related MCA occlusion before EVT, as assessed using RAPID MRI.

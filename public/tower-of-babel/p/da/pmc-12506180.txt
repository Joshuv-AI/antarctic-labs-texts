Randomised hybrid type 1 pilot trial evaluating preliminary effectiveness and implementation of an emergency care action plan (ECAP) for infants with medical complexity within a rural health network: a study protocol
Authors: Abigail Palaza, Peter Callas, Peter S Dayan, Dennis Z Kuo, Lauren Riney, Sandra P Spencer, Renee Stapleton, Martha Stevens, Christina R Studts, Christian D Pulcini
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroductionChildren with medical complexity (CMC) are a subset of children with special healthcare needs, defined by high healthcare utilisation, severe single or multisystem organ dysfunction, and in many cases, reliance on medical technology. In the emergency care setting, known challenges for this population include poor quality of care, avoidable admissions and high caregiver and provider burden. While experts and professional societies recommend emergency care planning tools to address these concerns, evidence to support effectiveness and implementation of such tools is lacking. Through a human-centred design approach, we recently engaged key partners to create and optimise an emergency care action plan (ECAP) for infants with medical complexity. Here, we describe the protocol for a pilot type 1 hybrid effectiveness-implementation randomised controlled trial (RCT) for infants with medical complexity aimed to evaluate ECAP effectiveness and implementation.Methods and analysisInfants with medical complexity and their caregivers will be randomly assigned to the intervention group (ECAP) or control group (standard care) in a pilot type 1 hybrid effectiveness-implementation RCT. The primary outcome is number of inpatient hospital days for infant participants. Additional effectiveness outcomes include perceived avoidance of emergency department (ED) visits, healthcare costs, caregiver stress and self-efficacy. Preliminary implementation outcomes include acceptability, feasibility, appropriateness and usability, as well as contextual barriers and facilitators to reach, adoption and implementation. Key partners, including caregivers of CMC and healthcare providers, will be engaged throughout the implementation of the ECAP and execution of the trial.Ethics and disseminationThis study was approved by the University of Vermont Institutional Review Board (STUDY00002937). Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations, and focus groups and interviews with key stakeholders.Trial registration numberNCT06444282.

Introduction

Children with medical complexity (CMC) are commonly defined as ‘children with multiple significant chronic health problems including multiple organ systems, which result in functional limitations, high health care needs or utilization, and often require need for, or use of, medical technology’.1 2 While CMC constitute approximately 1% of all children (~750 000), they account for approximately 20% of all paediatric emergency department (ED) visits (~6 million visits), 54% of paediatric inpatient days (~10 million days) and 33% of all childhood medical spending (~$71 billion annually) in the USA.3 4

Among CMC, infants with medical complexity represent a particularly vulnerable medical population, with high rates of ED visits and inpatient admissions in the first year of life (63% and 37%, respectively).5 6 Emergency care encounters for infants with medical complexity are associated with greater risk for missed medications and dosing errors, prolonged length of ED stay and potentially avoidable hospital admissions.79 In addition to these clinical concerns, time pressures, data availability/accessibility, logistics and communication barriers are commonly noted challenges by families and emergency medicine providers.10 11 Studies have also found that ED visits result in increased caregiver burden among caregivers of CMC, with disproportionate ED visit rates among families of low socioeconomic status and those from rural communities.710 1214 Given these challenges, the importance of increasing parental support for these infants during the first year of life—particularly during the transition out of the neonatal intensive care unit (NICU)—is critical.1517 The combination of high ED utilisation rates and costs, concerns about quality of care, increased caregiver and provider burden, and disparities in emergency care highlights the urgent need for effective and implementable strategies to improve acute and emergency care of infants with medical complexity.

To address the need for improved emergency care, in 2010 the American Academy of Paediatrics and the American College of Emergency Physicians recommended the use of emergency information forms (EIFs) to provide a brief, yet comprehensive summary of emergency management for CMC.18 19 Although EIFs for CMC have shown effectiveness in simulation studies, their adoption remains limited in usual practice.20 In parallel, paediatricians have made efforts to provide longitudinal care planning for CMC and their families. While these efforts are promising, they often lack the rigorous emergency-specific care planning that EIFs are intended to offer.2123

Experts and professional organisations in both emergency medicine and paediatrics continue to emphasise the need for EIFs to manage complex patients. Emergency medicine providers and caregivers of CMC have further deemed emergency care planning a top priority.10 In response to these needs, we developed and optimised an emergency care action plan (ECAP) prototype through human-centred design to address implementation barriers that have limited the uptake of EIFs.24 The double-diamond model (an established human-centred design approach) guided the creative design process with all relevant end-users. Using the EIF as a starting template,18 we utilised iterative prototyping to arrive at our current ECAP prototype, optimised for our local context.24 The aim of this study is to pilot test the effectiveness of this ECAP for infants with medical complexity and assess preliminary implementation outcomes using a type 1 hybrid effectiveness-implementation RCT design.25 To the best of our knowledge, this is the first prospective, real-time implementation investigation of an emergency care planning tool for infants with medical complexity.

We hypothesise that the ECAP will reduce inpatient hospital days (primary effectiveness outcome), ED length of stay, ED visits and interfacility transfers (secondary effectiveness outcomes), though power to detect statistically significant effects will be limited by the pilot nature of this study.26 27 We will also assess preliminary implementation outcomes essential for adoption, implementation and maintenance of the ECAP in usual care; these include perceptions of the acceptability, feasibility, appropriateness and usability of the ECAP from the perspectives of key partner and end users. Results of this pilot study will inform the development of a subsequent, fully powered type 1 hybrid multisite randomised controlled trial (RCT) of the ECAP to more rigorously test both the effectiveness and implementation in diverse settings.25

Aims

The overall goal of this pilot study is to assess preliminary effectiveness and implementation outcomes of ECAPs for infants with medical complexity. The specific aims are as follows.

Primary aim

To assess the effectiveness of a user-centred ECAP for infants with medical complexity on acute and emergency healthcare utilisation (primary outcome: inpatient days, secondary outcomes: ED visits, interfacility transfers and ED length of stay), and caregiver self-perceived stress and self-efficacy outcomes (exploratory).

Secondary aim

To evaluate implementation of the ECAP for infants with medical complexity including provider and caregiver perspectives on acceptability, feasibility, appropriateness and usability of the intervention, in addition to exploring its reach and adoption in the local setting.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This protocol adheres to the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Intervention Trials (SPIRIT) checklist for ensuring quality reporting of clinical trial protocols (see online supplemental additional file 1).28 A type 1 hybrid effectiveness-implementation study using an RCT design will be used to assess the preliminary effectiveness and implementation outcomes of the ECAP intervention. Infants with medical complexity will be recruited in the NICU and randomly assigned to the ECAP intervention or standard care. Caregiver and provider perspectives on implementation will be gathered through a series of mixed-methods assessments before, during and 1 year after ECAP implementation begins. Emergency care providers will participate in a mixed-methods, encounter-based survey, while caregivers will be surveyed every 3 months during the study period. The study began on 30 September 2024 and is anticipated to end on 8 August 2028.

Setting

This study will be conducted by the University of Vermont Larner College of Medicine at the University of Vermont Medical Centre (UVMMC). The UVMMC is Vermont’s only academic medical centre and Level 1 Trauma Centre and hosts a Level III NICU that serves Vermont and northern New York State. All paediatric services in the catchment area are located at UVMMC, which serves a mostly rural population.

Sample size

The proposed sample size for this study is 50 infant–caregiver dyads (25 randomised to the intervention group, 25 to standard care). Power calculations were conducted for the primary effectiveness outcome: the number of inpatient hospital days for infant participants during the enrolment period, extending 12 months after NICU discharge. Based on a Wilcoxon rank sum test, a sample size of 25 dyads per group provides 80% power to detect a 1.3 day difference in the number of inpatient days, assuming a SD of 1.5 days and using a two-sided type, that is, error rate of 0.05.

Participants, recruitment and data collection

Infants with medical complexity

Caregivers of infants with medical complexity between age 0 and 6 months who are admitted to the UVMMC NICU will be recruited. Infants must meet or be expected to meet CMC status as determined by the treating NICU clinician and Principal Investigator (PI), defined as ‘children with multiple significant chronic health problems including multiple organ systems, which result in functional limitations, high health care needs or utilization, and often require need for, or use of, medical technology’.1 2 As a secondary confirmation of eligibility, we will assess for ≥3 complex chronic conditions (CCC).29 The CCC designation is a commonly utilised metric in the literature to identify CMC, and by ensuring each infant has ≥3 CCCs, we will provide added objectivity to the eligibility criteria.29 Caregivers who do not plan to utilise the UVM Health Network (UVMHN) and affiliated network sites for the care of their infant during the year following NICU discharge will be excluded because their health utilisation data will not be available in our system. The caregiver must provide consent for their own participation as well as their infant for a dyad to be enrolled.

Eligible infants will be identified by the NICU care team. Eligibility will be verified by the treating NICU clinician in collaboration with the PI as needed. The treating NICU clinician with an established relationship with the patient and family will introduce the study to gauge interest and obtain the caregiver’s permission to be contacted by the study team (PI or designee) to learn more about the study and engage in the informed consent process.

Effectiveness outcomes for infant participants during the trial will be collected through electronic health records (EHRs) at UVMMC, supplemented by caregiver surveys and chart review by the research team.

Caregivers

Caregivers of eligible infants who enrol in the study will complete a series of surveys during the 12 months following NICU discharge. The caregiver participant must be a parent, legal guardian or foster parent of an eligible infant, and must be willing and able to complete surveys by email. Caregiver participants will be recruited by the treating NICU clinician introducing the study and eligibility will be confirmed prior to obtaining informed consent. Written informed consent will be obtained from the caregiver for their participation and their infant’s participation in the study by delegated research staff at bedside (see online supplemental additional file 3).

At the time of enrolment, caregiver participants will complete a baseline survey eliciting demographic information of both the caregiver and the infant, along with details about their infant’s medical conditions and needs. Caregiver-report data will be supplemented by an EHR review. Caregivers of infants randomised to the intervention group will complete an individualised ECAP for their infant with the assistance of the infant’s provider and respond to a second survey assessing preliminary implementation outcomes (eg, feasibility, acceptability) prior to NICU discharge.

During the 12 months following NICU discharge, follow-up surveys will be administered electronically to caregiver participants in both the intervention and usual care groups to measure effectiveness outcomes. For caregivers in the intervention group, surveys will also assess preliminary implementation outcomes. Follow-up surveys will be administered quarterly to all participating caregivers (at months 3, 6, 9 and 12 following NICU discharge).

At month 12, an additional survey will be administered to caregivers randomised to the intervention group to assess overall barriers and facilitators of ECAP implementation from their perspectives. This survey will include both close-ended and open-ended questions which may be answered in writing or verbally, based on caregiver preference.

Surveys will be administered quarterly to improve recall and maintain regular contact with caregiver participants and ensure continued engagement. Compensation will be provided to caregivers after each study survey is completed.

Data collected for infant participants and from caregiver surveys will be coded using a unique study ID and stored securely. Email addresses will be stored with caregiver survey responses during data collection and then removed once complete. All other identifiers will be securely stored separately from the data, and a link file will be stored in another separate location.

NICU providers

All UVMMC NICU providers (physicians or advanced practice providers) who help establish an individualised ECAP for at least one patient in the NICU will be asked to complete a survey after ECAP, providing demographic and professional information as well as their perspectives on preliminary implementation outcomes (eg, acceptability, feasibility, etc.).

Primary care providers and subspecialists

All UVMHN primary care providers (PCPs) and subspecialist providers (physicians or advanced practice providers) who care for an infant patient enrolled in the study and assigned to the intervention group will be asked to complete two surveys: one following creation of an ECAP and second 12 months later. Surveys will collect demographic and professional information as well as perspectives on preliminary implementation outcomes (eg, acceptability, feasibility, etc). Providers will be excluded if they have not been involved in the process of establishing an individualised ECAP for an infant enrolled in the study.

Emergency care providers

All UVMHN providers (physicians or advanced practice providers) who provide emergency care for an infant patient enrolled in the study and assigned to the intervention group will be included in an encounter-based survey. The survey will assess provider demographics and professional information as well as their perspectives on preliminary implementation outcomes (e.g, feasibility, acceptability) of the ECAP.

Recruitment, consent and data confidentiality

Recruitment of NICU providers, PCPs and subspecialists will occur through targeted email or discussion while collaborating to develop an ECAP for an enrolled patient. The research team will message emergency medicine providers by EPIC secure chat and/or email to inform them of the study and opportunity to participate after the encounter has concluded. Consent for all provider participants will be obtained through a survey question that will require the participant to consent to participate after reviewing an embedded research information sheet.

Survey responses for PCPs and subspecialists will be coded using a study ID to allow linked follow-up at 12 months. Identifiable information will be stored separately in a master key to ensure confidentiality. No identifiers will be stored with survey responses for NICU and emergency medicine provider participants surveyed at one point in time. All provider participants will be compensated for their time to complete the survey (see figure 1).

Intervention

Emergency care action plan (ECAP)

The ECAP prototype was developed through the optimisation of EIFs and guided by principles of human-centred design.24 A systematic literature review and mixed-methods assessment of key partners (emergency medicine physicians, primary care paediatricians, paediatric subspecialists and caregivers of CMC) informed the content and formatting of the initial prototype. Prototyping with local stakeholders through a series of focus groups allowed for adaptation and optimisation of the ECAP to the local context.

The ECAP was designed to be used by emergency care providers during an emergency encounter with an infant with medical complexity. The current prototype includes the following content: caregiver contact information, patient summary, anticipated emergency presentations with suggested management, a problem list (emergency relevant only), a medication list, technology dependence, baseline important physical examination findings, baseline vital signs, allergies, advance directive information and contact information for established care providers.24 The design allows for expanded areas of text to accommodate the varying needs of individual patients. The ECAP will be embedded in the patient medical chart (as an FYI flag) for access by all providers in the acute care setting. Caregivers will also be instructed to provide the ECAP to emergency medicine providers and receive a paper copy and digital access (through EHR-specific application) to the plan. Suggested minor edits to the ECAP during the trial period will be routed through the study PI and updated by the ED collaborative information technology based on a previously established local standard process for other patient populations.

The ECAP prototype will serve as a format for the creation of an individualised ECAP through collaboration among the patient’s caregiver(s), PCP, pertinent subspecialist providers, NICU team and the PI prior to NICU discharge. Caregivers and providers will receive step-by-step instructions to guide them through creating the ECAP. The PI of the study and research team will coordinate between needed and relevant providers for completion of the ECAP. If there are disagreements among involved parties about any part of the ECAP, we will mitigate conflicting opinions through a shared decision-making model. Challenges that arise during the development of an individualised ECAP will be documented to further inform the approach and format of the ECAP, consistent with an iterative human-centred design process.

Standard care

For infants assigned to the control group, no individualised ECAP will be created. The infant will receive the current standard of care which does not include advanced emergency care planning in the local setting. Caregivers of infants assigned to the control group will be offered the ECAP template 12 months after enrolment.

Allocation

Infant participants will be block randomised to the intervention group (ECAP) or control group (standard care) after enrolment. A research project manager (not responsible for enrolling or allocating patients) will generate an allocation sequence using computer-generated random numbers. The random sequence will provide an assignment to the enrolled participants via the Research Electronic Data Capture randomisation function. To reduce the predictability of the random sequence, the random numbers list will be inaccessible to the Clinical Research Coordinators enrolling participants and assigning intervention groups.

Effectiveness measures

Primary outcome

The primary outcome for effectiveness is the number of inpatient hospital days for infant participants within 1 year after NICU discharge. We hypothesise that the ECAP will allow for earlier recognition of acute illness and enable timely and more effective acute care inside and outside the ED. This, in turn, is expected to decrease the number of inpatient hospital days. Reducing inpatient length of stay is a top priority for both families and healthcare systems (as a driver of costs) and is a long-time focus of research and programmatic intervention involving CMC.3 4 7 13 30

Secondary outcomes

Other healthcare utilisation metrics, such as ED length of stay and the number of interfacility transfers, will be evaluated. Additional exploratory effectiveness outcomes include caregiver self-perceived stress,31 self-efficacy,32 financial stress and missed workdays due to caregiving responsibilities (due to known burden of emergency care of CMC on families). For the intervention group, we will also assess caregivers’ perceived avoidance of ED visits. All secondary outcomes that are counts (eg, ED length of stay) will be evaluated quarterly and reported as a sum value for the 1 year trial period (see table 1).

Implementation measures

Self-report measures completed by key stakeholders (caregivers, NICU providers, PCP/subspecialist providers and emergency care providers) will be used to assess contextual factors within the Practical, Robust, Implementation and Sustainability Model (PRISM) (see table 2).33 34 Formative evaluation of demographic information collected from caregiver and provider participants will inform patient and organisational characteristics. Survey items to assess organisational and patient perspectives on the intervention were drawn from existing scales including the Theoretical Framework of Acceptability,35 Feasibility of Intervention Measure,36 Intervention Appropriateness Measure36 and System Usability Scale.37 Adoptability of the ECAP from the provider’s perspective will be measured using a modified version of the Adoption Questionnaire for health education interventions, which measures advantage/compatibility, complexity and observability. A mixed-methods approach will capture perspectives on the ability of the ECAP to overcome barriers to high-quality emergency care for CMC. Implementation and sustainability infrastructure will be evaluated through short answer responses to assess both provider and caregiver perspectives on barriers, facilitators and suggestions for implementation and maintenance. The 12 month survey for caregiver participants randomised to the intervention group includes the Provider REport Sustainment Scale (PRESS),38 to assess potential for continued use after the trial concludes.

Statistical analysis

We will provide a descriptive summary of infant and caregiver demographics and clinical characteristics. Summary statistics will include means, medians, SD, IQR and tabulations as appropriate. We will present the results with 95% CIs as appropriate. The intervention and control groups will be compared using a two-sample t-test after data transformation or a non-parametric Wilcoxon rank sum test. If a large percentage of patients have zero inpatient days (primary outcome), a zero-inflated Poisson or negative binomial model may be utilised. ED length of stay will be analysed using a two-sample t test after log transformation. Dichotomous outcomes such as avoidance of ED visits or interfacility transfer will be compared using χ2 tests. Qualitative data will be analysed using deductive and inductive thematic analysis.39 Intention-to-treat analysis will be used for study withdrawals or protocol deviations.40

Qualitative and mixed-methods analysis

We will use a rapid qualitative analysis approach with templates and matrices for data reduction purposes. Qualitative data will be drawn from open-ended survey responses provided by caregivers and all provider types on the 12 month survey (see table 2, Implementation and Sustainability Infrastructure). Responses are expected to include information about (a) experiences with the ECAP, (b) perceived barriers and (c) facilitators to use of this intervention and (d) suggestions for potential modifications of the ECAP and process to improve the intervention and its implementation. Following recommended rapid qualitative procedures,41 we will summarise each individual’s responses within each topical category, then summarise main points and themes for each topical category across all respondents, including within and across respondent roles. Two trained analysts will independently complete the first five templates, then meet to review and discuss their depth, scope and accuracy and reach consensus on included main points. Remaining templates will be completed by one analyst and reviewed by the other. Summaries of topical categories (across and within respondent roles) will each be completed by one analyst and reviewed by the other, with meetings as needed to resolve discrepancies and reach consensus. Using a convergent parallel mixed-methods design (QUANT+qual), qualitative results will be integrated with quantitative findings using joint display analyses to identify patterns of responses, triangulate and elaborate.42 43

Patient and public involvement

Caregivers and providers of infants with medical complexity were involved in the adaptation and optimisation of the ECAP for implementation in this trial. These key stakeholders will inform the conduct of the trial through mixed-methods feedback and will be included in the dissemination and translation of study findings to inform future implementation and study design of a multisite trial.

Ethics and dissemination

Institutional review board approval was obtained from the University of Vermont on 12 July 2024 (see online supplemental additional file 2), which includes plans for secure data storage, data monitoring and tracking and reporting of adverse events or protocol modifications as necessary. The trial is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov and will be updated by the PI at least annually. Data monitoring will be conducted periodically by the PI in collaboration with an independent safety monitor. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations and focus groups and interviews with key stakeholders to guide the selection of broader implementation strategies for a multisite RCT.

Discussion

Innovation

This study addresses a critical gap in emergency care for infants with medical complexity, a population facing unique and significant challenges in receiving timely, coordinated care. To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first to rigorously assess effectiveness and implementation of an emergency care planning tool for infants with medical complexity, despite longstanding expert recommendation for utilisation of such tools to improve known challenges in providing high-quality care. Through a pilot type 1 hybrid study in our local setting, focusing primarily on effectiveness and secondarily on implementation outcomes, we will obtain preliminary findings to plan a larger and more generalisable future multisite trial. Preliminary effectiveness data could be crucial for fostering provider and caregiver adoption of the ECAP in future implementation at other sites. Preliminary implementation findings will inform additional refinements needed to the ECAP and its process prior to conducting a fully powered trial, as well as suggest potential implementation strategies needed to facilitate ECAP adoption and implementation.

Limitations

Of note, this pilot study is at a single centre and will primarily include infant and caregiver participants from a rural state seeking emergency care in a non-freestanding children’s hospital.44 The future larger RCT will encompass a broad range of centres serving more variable patient populations to improve generalisability.

While the type 1 hybrid RCT design of this pilot trial allows for the secondary assessment of implementation outcomes, this study does not evaluate long-term maintenance or address the sustainability of ECAPs from the perspective of providers or within broader organisational contexts during implementation. Factors, such as leadership support, staffing resources, technical infrastructure and institutional incentives, while assessed prior to implementation, are beyond the scope of this study and will be prioritised in subsequent trials. Despite these limitations, insights from both caregivers and providers on how ECAPs may improve emergency care will be critical in guiding the selection of measures for future studies, particularly those selected to analyse the mechanisms by which ECAPs may reduce inpatient admissions—our primary outcome of interest.

Practical challenges are also anticipated. Achieving consensus among stakeholders with diverse clinical backgrounds and perspectives may be difficult, and this process is necessary in the creation of an individualised ECAP for infants with medical complexity. Developing an emergency care plan that effectively condenses and prioritises essential emergency information, while remaining adaptable to the unique needs of each patient within a standardised template, poses an additional challenge. Moreover, challenges such as coordinating with multiple providers to finalise an ECAP in a timely manner before NICU discharge are also expected. These challenges will be thoroughly documented throughout implementation in this pilot study to inform modifications and implementation strategies to improve the process of completing ECAPs.

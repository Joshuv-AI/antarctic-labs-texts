Letter to the Editor Regarding: “Real-World Effectiveness of a Third Dose of mRNA-1273 Versus BNT162b2 on Inpatient and Medically Attended COVID-19 Among Immunocompromised US Adults”
Authors: Hinpetch Daungsupawong, Viroj Wiwanitkit
Published: 2024

Dear Editor, we would like to give matter arising from this article “Real-World Effectiveness of a Third Dose of mRNA-1273 Versus BNT162b2 on Inpatient and Medically Attended COVID-19 Among Immunocompromised US Adults” [1]. This study offers important new information about how well mRNA-1273 and BNT162b2 compare as a third dosage in lowering the incidence of hospital stays and medically attended severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus -2 (SARS-CoV-2) infections. The results indicate that, in comparison to BNT162b2, taking mRNA-1273 as a third dose may be linked to noticeably lower risks of both outcomes. Inverse probability weighting was used to adjust the results for baseline confounders, and subgroup and sensitivity analyses were carried out to evaluate the findings' robustness. Nonetheless, it is important to take into account some of the study methodology's shortcomings.

The use of retrospective, observational data, which can add bias and hard-to-account-for confounding factors, is one possible drawback. Furthermore, the third dose regimen's long-term effectiveness after August 2022 is unknown because the trial period only lasted until that point. The study's findings may have limited generalizability due to its reliance on a particular group from the US HealthVerity database. However, other statistical techniques may have been used to give a more thorough examination of the data, even though the authors calculated relative risk and risk difference to compare outcomes between immunization groups.

Notwithstanding these drawbacks, the study's findings highlight significant concerns regarding the possible distinctions in efficacy between mRNA-1273 and BNT162b2 as a third dosage for averting serious COVID-19 consequences. The results of this observational study could be confirmed by undertaking randomized controlled trials in the future to look at these differences in more detail. Furthermore, research might investigate the factors, such as immunogenicity and duration of protection, that underlie the observed variations in vaccine effectiveness. Comprehending these variables may facilitate the formulation of vaccine policies and enhance the efficacy of COVID-19 immunization tactics in the future.

In conclusion, there are limitations to the technique that should be taken into account when interpreting the data, even if this study offers insightful information about the relative efficacy of mRNA-1273 and BNT162b2 as third doses. Future studies ought to focus on resolving these issues and delving deeper into the variations in vaccination efficacy between the two protocols. We can enhance population protection and lessen the effects of the continuing pandemic by determining the best immunization plans for averting serious COVID-19 outcomes.

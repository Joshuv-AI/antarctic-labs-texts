A visualization analysis of research hotspots and trends in enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) for elderly patients based on web of science and scopus
Authors: Chang Xiao, Yiting Wang, Yanfeng Sun, Shengqiang Zou, Xihu Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AimsTo analyze the research landscape and thematic evolution of enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) in elderly patients from 2009 to 2025 using bibliometric methods.MethodsPublications were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection and Scopus databases, limited to English articles and reviews. CiteSpace and VOSviewer were used to analyze annual publication trends, international and institutional collaboration networks, keyword co-occurrence, clustering, and burst detection.ResultsA total of 773 publications were included. Annual publication output increased markedly after 2017. China (183 publications, 23.7%) and the United States (152 publications, 19.7%) were the leading contributors. Keyword clustering identified nine major thematic clusters (Q = 0.4617,S = 0.7527),with “lung neoplasms” and “esophagus resection” emerging as independent clusters. A sub-analysis revealed that 37 publications (4.8%) specifically addressed thoracic surgery, indicating that thoracic ERAS represents an emerging but still underdeveloped subfield within the overall ERAS research landscape. Among the top 20 high-frequency keywords, “aged” (519 occurrences, 67.1%) and “enhanced recovery after surgery” (504 occurrences, 65.2%) were the most prominent. Burst detection revealed 25 keywords with significant citation surges. Recent high-intensity bursts included “functional status” (strength = 7.18), “preoperative exercise” (strength = 6.28), and “physiotherapy” (strength = 5.38), indicating a shift toward functional recovery and prehabilitation. Major research themes focused on frail older populations, colorectal and oncologic surgical applications, perioperative analgesia, and prehabilitation strategies. Temporal overlay and burst analyses indicated sustained prominence of terms such as “functional status”, “frailty”, and “preoperative exercise”.ConclusionsResearch on ERAS in elderly patients has evolved into a multi-thematic knowledge structure with an increasing emphasis on functional outcomes and personalized perioperative management.

Introduction

With global population aging, the proportion of elderly patients undergoing surgery continues to increase [1]. This population is frequently characterized by multimorbidity and diminished physiological reserve, conditions associated with elevated perioperative risk and less predictable postoperative recovery [2]. Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) represents an evidence-based perioperative care model emphasizing multidisciplinary collaboration and optimized management strategies aimed at minimizing surgical stress and improving clinical outcomes [3–5]. Core ERAS elements include preoperative counseling and education, avoidance of prolonged fasting, nutritional optimization, multimodal opioid-sparing analgesia, goal-directed fluid therapy, and early postoperative mobilization and feeding. Through the integration of these evidence-based interventions across the perioperative pathway, ERAS aims to accelerate recovery, reduce complications, and improve patient-centered outcomes. Initial ERAS pathways were largely developed for the general adult population and provided limited consideration of age-specific characteristics such as functional status, cognitive capacity, and social support among older patients [6].

Advances in geriatric medicine have promoted the integration of frailty assessment and preoperative functional screening into perioperative evaluation frameworks [7]. Evidence indicates that preoperative functional assessment and frailty screening serve as important predictors of postoperative outcomes in elderly surgical patients [8–9]. Increasing attention has also been directed toward intervention strategies such as prehabilitation. Despite the expanding body of literature, most existing studies concentrate on specific clinical outcomes, There remains a lack of systematic organization regarding the overall knowledge framework and developmental trajectory of ERAS among elderly patients, a deficiency particularly pronounced in thoracic surgery. Compared to other surgical procedures, pneumonectomy and esophagectomy impose higher demands on cardiopulmonary function.Elderly patients face unique challenges in implementing ERAS due to reduced respiratory reserve and a heavier burden of comorbidities.Bibliometric analysis enables macro-level evaluation of research productivity, collaboration networks, and thematic evolution within a scientific field [10].In light of this, this study conducted a bibliometric analysis of literature related to ERAS in the elderly from 2009 to 2025 using the Web of Science core collection and Scopus database, systematically examining its research evolution patterns and cutting-edge trends to provide a scientific reference for the future development of ERAS in geriatric thoracic surgery.

Methods

Data sources and search strategy

To ensure comprehensive and reliable data coverage, the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) and Scopus databases were selected as the data sources. The retrieval period was defined from January 1, 2009 to August 31, 2025, with 2009 representing the earliest year in which relevant publications in this field could be identified in both databases.

Document types were limited to peer-reviewed articles and reviews. Conference abstracts, editorials, book reviews, letters, protocols, and non–peer-reviewed publications were excluded. Only English-language publications were included.

The search strategy combined controlled concepts and free-text terms. As Web of Science does not employ a controlled vocabulary system comparable to MeSH in PubMed, truncation and Boolean operators were used to construct reproducible free-text search expressions.

For the concept of elderly patients, the following synonyms were applied:

(“elderly patient*” OR “aged patient*” OR “older patient*” OR “old patient*” OR “frail* elderl*” OR “frail* patient*”).

The truncated form “frail* elderl*” was used to approximate the MeSH concept “Frail Elderly” under Web of Science search conventions.

For enhanced recovery after surgery, the following terms were used:

(“enhanced recovery after surgery” OR “ERAS” OR “fast track surgery” OR “enhanced recovery surgery” OR “accelerated rehabilitation surgery” OR “enhanced rehabilitation surgery”).

The final search query combined the two concepts using Boolean logic: (elderly-related terms) AND (ERAS-related terms).The literature search and data extraction processes were completed on August 31, 2025.All retrieved records were exported with full records and cited references for subsequent analysis.

Retrieval process and study selection

In WoSCC, the search was conducted using the “Topic” field, yielding 321 records. Subject category filtering and manual screening of abstracts were applied to exclude publications from clearly unrelated fields, including Environmental Sciences, Mathematical & Computational Biology, and Obstetrics & Gynecology. After removing 94 records, 227 publications were retained.

In Scopus, the search was performed within the “Title–Abstract–Keywords” field, retrieving 758 records. Screening based on subject area classification and abstract review excluded 61 publications from unrelated domains such as Computer Science, Agricultural and Biological Sciences, and Veterinary Sciences. A total of 697 publications were retained.

The 227 WoSCC records and 697 Scopus records were exported in BibTeX format. Data merging and duplicate removal were performed using the Bibliometrix package in R. Duplicate records were identified through matching of titles, authors, and DOIs. After removing 151 duplicates, 773 publications remained for bibliometric analysis.Please refer to Fig. 1.

Bibliometric analysis methods

The included records were exported in RefWorks format. Visual bibliometric analyses were performed using CiteSpace (version 6.3.R1) and VOSviewer (version 1.6.18). Journal distribution and Bradford’s law analysis were conducted using the Bibliometrix package in R. Only articles and reviews written in English were included. The parameter settings were defined as follows. In CiteSpace, the time span was set from 2009 to 2025 with a time slice of 1 year. Link strength was calculated using cosine similarity. Node selection followed the default g-index with k = 25. In VOSviewer, clustering thresholds were established as follows: (1)Countries: minimum number of documents = 5; (2)Institutions: minimum number of documents = 4; (3) Authors: minimum number of documents = 4; (4) Keywords: minimum number of occurrences = 40. Key bibliographic information was further extracted using Microsoft Excel, including annual publication output, national publication distribution and collaboration, institutional productivity and collaboration, and keyword characteristics. In the country collaboration analysis, collaboration strength was defined as the total link strength generated by VOSviewer, representing the overall intensity of collaborative relationships between a given country and other countries within the co-authorship network.

Data quality control

To ensure the reliability of the bibliometric analysis, data quality control procedures were implemented throughout the study. Literature search, screening, and data extraction were conducted independently by two researchers, and any discrepancies were resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer.

Prior to analysis, data cleaning and standardization were performed to improve consistency. Synonymous keywords were unified, and variations in institutional names were harmonized to their standardized forms to enhance the accuracy of co-occurrence and collaboration analyses.

Only peer-reviewed articles and review papers indexed in the Web of Science Core Collection and Scopus databases were included. Duplicate records were removed, and key bibliographic information (e.g.,DOI, publication year) was cross-checked to ensure data integrity.

As bibliometric analysis focuses on publication patterns and knowledge structures rather than clinical effectiveness, formal study-level quality assessment tools were not applied.

Results

Annual quantitative distribution of publications

Global research on ERAS in elderly surgical patients shows a continuous upward trend in publication output. The development can be broadly divided into three stages. From 2009 to 2013, the field was in an initial stage, with relatively low annual publication output, generally fewer than 20 articles per year. Between 2014 and 2017, a period of gradual growth occurred, with a steady increase in related studies. After 2018, the number of publications increased markedly and reached a peak around 2021, with annual publications exceeding 100 articles. Overall, the field entered a phase of accelerated development after 2017, indicating increasing academic attention and suggesting that ERAS research in elderly patients is becoming an important research direction in surgery and perioperative management. Keyword clustering

Based on keyword co-occurrence, CiteSpace was used for clustering analysis to reveal the structural distribution of research topics. Nine major clusters were identified, including “Ovary Cancer,” “Analgesics,” “Interleukin-6,” “Colorectal Surgery,” “Preoperative Exercise,” “Elderly,” “Quality of Life,” “Esophagus Resection,” and “Lung Neoplasms” (Fig. 7). Based on the thematic classification of included studies, 37 of the 773 publications (4.8%) were related to thoracic surgery. Among these, lung cancer and pulmonary resection accounted for the majority, followed by esophageal surgery, while a smaller number involved broader thoracic surgical populations and perioperative management strategies. These findings suggest that thoracic ERAS has emerged as a distinct but still developing research area within geriatric perioperative care.

Clustering quality was assessed using modularity Q and silhouette S values. Typically, Q > 0.3 and S > 0.7 indicate reliable and consistent cluster structures. In this study, the network yielded Q = 0.4617 and S = 0.7527, indicating clear cluster structure and good internal consistency.

Thematic analysis shows that different clusters correspond to specific surgical types, perioperative management measures, and outcome assessments, reflecting a multi-directional research structure in the field.

Discussion

Global ERAS development and knowledge structure

The ERAS concept was originally pioneered by Professor Henrik Kehlet in the 1990s as part of the “fast-track surgery” program, with early clinical implementation in colorectal surgery at Hvidovre University Hospital [3–4]. Subsequently, the ERAS framework was further developed and standardized through the collaborative efforts of European experts, including Fearon, Ljungqvist, and colleagues, who established the ERAS Study Group (later the ERAS Society), promoting evidence-based perioperative care pathways and international guideline development [5, 11, 13]. From 2009 to 2025, research on ERAS in elderly patients shows a general upward trend, with accelerated growth after 2017. This historical progression from fast-track surgery to structured ERAS protocols provides the conceptual foundation for modern perioperative care models, and the subsequent expansion into geriatric populations reflects the growing recognition of age-specific needs in perioperative management.

This evolution is closely related to the transition of ERAS from “fast-track” programs to systematic and reproducible perioperative management protocols. Multimodal ERAS interventions reduce surgical stress, complications, and length of hospital stay, and their value has gradually been recognized across surgical and nursing disciplines through multidisciplinary efforts [11–13]. Meanwhile, the continuous updates of guidelines and consensus have standardized key perioperative components, including nutrition, analgesia, early mobilization, and fluid management, providing an evidence-based foundation for implementation across different procedures and risk groups [14]. Against the background of population aging, increasing surgical volume among older patients with comorbidities and higher perioperative risk has further promoted the expansion of “geriatric ERAS” as a distinct research area [15].

At the international level, collaboration and knowledge production networks indicate that European and North American countries occupy central positions with strong influence. This pattern aligns with the early formation and systematic dissemination of ERAS in Europe and North America: the ERAS academic community was established relatively early, and iterative updates of guidelines and implementation frameworks facilitate multicenter studies; moreover, mature surgical quality improvement systems and clinical research infrastructure in these regions support high-impact evidence generation through cross-institutional collaboration [13–14]. Notably, China ranks high in publication output but shows lower collaboration intensity, reflecting a structure characterized by rapid output growth with relatively limited international cooperation.

At the institutional level, high-output institutions also exhibit high citation impact, suggesting a “core institution–core team” mechanism driving knowledge diffusion. This aligns with ERAS research, which emphasizes multidisciplinary pathway implementation and adherence assessment: high-quality studies often rely on stable teams, standardized pathways, and continuous data accumulation, increasing the likelihood of producing highly cited guidelines, consensus statements, or key evidence [13–14]. For this study, institutional network results not only present output ranking but also help explain why certain institutions occupy hub positions and whether their research themes align with recent hotspots, such as frailty assessment, functional outcomes, and multimodal analgesia.

Mature ERAS systems in colorectal and oncologic surgery

Keyword clustering analysis shows that procedure-related terms such as “colorectal surgery,” “cancer surgery,” “laparoscopic surgery,” and “minimally invasive surgery” form a stable and large cluster, indicating that ERAS research in elderly patients is most mature in the fields of colorectal and oncologic surgery. This distribution is consistent with the historical trajectory of ERAS, which was first established and evidence-validated in colorectal surgery [4–5]. Colorectal and oncologic procedures typically involve significant surgical trauma, pronounced perioperative stress responses, and prolonged postoperative recovery, making adherence to standardized perioperative pathways particularly critical. Consequently, these surgical areas have become priority settings for ERAS implementation and optimization.

Multiple updated systematic reviews and guidelines have further consolidated the central role of ERAS in these fields. The latest ERAS Society guidelines indicate that implementing standardized ERAS pathways in colorectal surgery can significantly shorten hospital stay and reduce postoperative complications [16]. The widespread adoption of minimally invasive surgical techniques provides essential technical support for ERAS implementation, as laparoscopic and robot-assisted procedures reduce tissue damage and inflammatory responses, thereby enhancing the feasibility of key ERAS measures such as early feeding and early mobilization [17–18]. Moreover, with the increasing number of elderly cancer patients, oncologic surgery has gradually become an important focus of geriatric ERAS research. Recent studies suggest that multimodal perioperative management in elderly oncology patients can improve short-term recovery outcomes and potentially influence long-term functional status [19–20]. Beyond colorectal and oncologic surgery, other areas such as gynecologic oncology, urology, and hepatobiliary surgery have also benefited from ERAS implementation. These fields share common success factors including well-established guideline consensus, robust multidisciplinary collaboration, dedicated ERAS coordinators, and continuous adherence monitoring systems. The systematic integration of preoperative optimization, intraoperative standardized protocols, and postoperative recovery pathways has enabled these specialties to achieve reproducible improvements in clinical outcomes. These mature experiences provide valuable references for thoracic surgery, where ERAS adoption remains less standardized and evidence for elderly populations is still emerging.

Current status, evidence gaps, and reasons for lagging development of thoracic ERAS in elderly patients

Although colorectal and oncologic surgery remain the predominant areas in geriatric ERAS research, keyword clustering identified “Esophagus Resection” and “Lung Neoplasms” as independent themes. Furthermore, 37 of the 773 included publications (4.8%) were related to thoracic surgery, suggesting that this field is emerging as a distinct research direction in elderly perioperative care. Current thoracic ERAS research mainly focuses on minimally invasive surgery, respiratory rehabilitation, individualized fluid management, multimodal analgesia, nutritional optimization, and early mobilization. Studies have demonstrated that adherence to ERAS pathways in video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery is associated with reduced postoperative complications and shorter hospital stay [21]. Pulmonary rehabilitation and prehabilitation strategies have also received increasing attention because of their potential to improve pulmonary function and postoperative recovery in elderly patients undergoing lung surgery [22–23]. Individualized perioperative management strategies, including goal-directed fluid therapy, opioid-sparing analgesia, and enhanced nutritional support following esophagectomy, may further facilitate postoperative recovery and functional improvement in elderly thoracic surgical patients [24–25]. Overall, current evidence supports the feasibility and effectiveness of ERAS programmes in elderly patients undergoing thoracic surgery [26].

However, several important evidence gaps are particularly evident. First, most studies focus on short-term outcomes such as complications and length of stay, while long-term functional recovery and quality of life are rarely evaluated. In elderly patients undergoing lung resection or esophagectomy, postoperative pulmonary function decline, exercise capacity reduction, and loss of independence may have more sustained impact on quality of life than transient complications, yet these endpoints remain understudied [27]. Second, evidence specifically targeting frail patients and those aged 80 and over is extremely limited [28]. Given that thoracic surgery candidates often present with diminished cardiopulmonary reserve and multiple comorbidities, the applicability of standardized ERAS pathways to these high-risk subgroups remains uncertain. Third, most available studies are single-center investigations with relatively small sample sizes [29], limiting the generalizability of findings. Unlike colorectal surgery, where large multicenter randomized controlled trials and registry-based studies are common, thoracic ERAS research still relies heavily on institutional case series and retrospective analyses. Fourth, unlike the well-established ERAS Society guidelines for colorectal surgery [13, 14, 16], thoracic ERAS lacks uniformly accepted, procedure-specific guidelines that incorporate age- and frailty-adjusted recommendations. Fifth, while colorectal and oncologic ERAS have progressively incorporated frailty assessment and functional screening into perioperative frameworks [7–9], thoracic ERAS research has been slower to adopt these geriatric-oriented approaches.

The slower development of ERAS in thoracic surgery compared with colorectal and general surgery can be attributed to several procedure-specific factors that operate at a mechanistic level. The physiological impact of thoracic procedures, particularly lung resection and esophagectomy, on cardiopulmonary function is more profound, making recovery trajectories more variable and less predictable. The heterogeneity of thoracic surgical procedures, for example wedge resection versus pneumonectomy or transthoracic versus minimally invasive esophagectomy, further complicates the development of unified pathways. Additionally, the high incidence of postoperative pulmonary complications in elderly patients requires specific perioperative respiratory management strategies that are not as prominently featured in colorectal ERAS protocols.

These intrinsic challenges, however, do not negate the potential benefits of ERAS in thoracic surgery. Rather, they underscore the necessity for procedure-specific, risk-stratified approaches that account for the unique physiological demands of thoracic operations and the special vulnerabilities of elderly patients, while also highlighting the need for dedicated implementation frameworks tailored to thoracic surgical settings.

Future directions

Based on the comparative analysis of established ERAS advantages in colorectal and other surgical fields and the identified deficiencies in thoracic ERAS, we propose the following priority directions for future research and practice.

Standardization, implementation science, and multicenter collaboration

Future efforts should prioritize the development of standardized ERAS guidelines specifically for thoracic procedures (lung resection, esophagectomy, and other major thoracic operations), with explicit recommendations tailored to elderly and frail patients. These guidelines should incorporate age-adjusted benchmarks for nutritional optimization, analgesic protocols, respiratory rehabilitation, and mobilization targets. Collaboration with the ERAS Society and international thoracic surgical societies would facilitate consensus building and global dissemination [13–14]. To overcome the limitations of single-center studies, multicenter prospective cohorts and thoracic ERAS registries should be established, enabling adequate sample sizes for subgroup analyses, facilitating benchmarking of adherence and outcomes, and supporting high-level evidence generation. Implementation science approaches, including adherence monitoring systems, audit-and-feedback mechanisms, and multidisciplinary team engagement, which have proven successful in colorectal ERAS, should be adapted for thoracic surgical settings. Assessing barriers to ERAS adherence in elderly patients, such as cognitive impairment, limited mobility, and social support deficits, would inform tailored implementation strategies.

Precision ERAS in elderly thoracic surgery: a unified personalized pathway

Future research should transition from a one-size-fits-all approach toward a precision ERAS framework that integrates frailty stratification, functional outcome assessment, and multimodal rehabilitation into a unified personalized pathway. Given that frailty is a stronger predictor than chronological age for postoperative outcomes [7, 30–31], elderly thoracic surgical patients should be stratified based on frailty status and functional reserve, with the differential effectiveness of ERAS components evaluated across these subgroups. Tailored pathway components, such as enhanced prehabilitation for frail patients, modified analgesic regimens for those at high delirium risk, and extended nutritional support for malnourished individuals, should be developed and prospectively validated. Concurrently, studies must move beyond short-term safety indicators and incorporate long-term functional outcomes, including postoperative pulmonary function, exercise capacity, independence in activities of daily living, and quality of life, as primary or co-primary endpoints [32–33]. Patient-reported outcomes (PROs) should be systematically integrated into perioperative evaluation frameworks to capture the patient-centered impact of ERAS interventions [34–36].

Building on the sustained prominence of “preoperative exercise” and “physiotherapy” in recent research hotspot [37–38], prehabilitation programs specifically designed for elderly patients undergoing lung and esophageal surgery should combine exercise training, nutritional optimization, and psychological support with careful attention to cardiopulmonary tolerance and frailty-adjusted intensity. Postoperative rehabilitation protocols must be standardized to ensure continuity of care from preadmission to discharge and beyond. Given the unique pulmonary complications risks in thoracic surgery, multimodal analgesic protocols should be refined to balance effective pain control with preservation of respiratory function and minimization of opioid-related adverse events [39–40], with regional analgesic techniques optimized for elderly patients considering frailty, cognitive status, and fall risk. Respiratory physiotherapy and early mobilization should be integrated as core components rather than adjuncts to thoracic ERAS pathways. This unified precision ERAS framework, anchored in frailty-based stratification, functional outcome measurement, and integrated perioperative rehabilitation, represents the most promising avenue for advancing geriatric thoracic ERAS research and practice.

Study limitations

This study has several limitations. First, data were drawn exclusively from the Web of Science Core Collection and Scopus databases. Although these sources offer broad academic coverage, regional databases or non-English literature may have been omitted, potentially affecting the comprehensiveness of the results [41]. Nevertheless, both databases are widely used in bibliometric research and provide relatively high data quality and reproducibility, ensuring a reasonable level of representativeness.

Second, bibliometric findings are partially dependent on search strategies and parameter settings, including keyword selection, time span, and threshold values, which may influence network structures and clustering outcomes [10]. While standard parameters and keyword normalization were applied, potential methodological biases cannot be entirely excluded.

Finally, keyword co-occurrence and burst analyses primarily reflect research attention and thematic evolution, rather than clinical efficacy or evidence strength. Therefore, the results are most suitable for understanding the macro-level development of the field rather than evaluating specific interventions. Future studies could integrate systematic reviews or cross-database analyses to further validate these findings.

Conclusions

Based on data from the Web of Science Core Collection and Scopus, this study conducted a bibliometric analysis of literature on enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) in elderly patients from 2009 to 2025, examining publication trends, international collaboration, research hotspots, and thematic evolution to reveal the knowledge structure and development characteristics of the field.

The results indicate a growing trajectory of geriatric ERAS research, with accelerated development in recent years, reflecting increasing attention to perioperative management and surgical care in older adults. Collaboration networks show that the field has established an international structure centered on key nodes in Europe and North America, with certain leading institutions driving knowledge production.

Research hotspots reveal that current geriatric ERAS studies primarily focus on oldest-old and frail populations, applications in colorectal, oncologic, and thoracic surgery, functional recovery and quality-of-life assessment, and perioperative analgesia and rehabilitation. Thematic evolution further demonstrates a shift from solely perioperative safety management toward function-oriented, comprehensive rehabilitation strategies.

Overall, geriatric ERAS research exhibits a multi-thematic, parallel development pattern, increasingly aligned with the geriatric principle of function-centered care. This bibliometric analysis systematically maps the field’s development, providing a macro-level reference for future research. However, bibliometric findings reflect research attention and thematic distribution rather than clinical efficacy; future studies should integrate evidence-based evaluations to assess the real-world effectiveness of specific interventions.

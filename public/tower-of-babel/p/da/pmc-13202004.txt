Evaluating the feasibility of a scalable, digitally supported model for global collaborative surgical research: protocol for a prospective, international, multicentre observational study in cryptoglandular anal fistula treatment (CRAFT)
Authors: Nedim Tabakovic, Merel Kimman, James Keatley, Alaa El-Hussuna, Phil Tozer, David D E Zimmerman, Laura Magill, Litza Mitalas, Sander van Kuijk, Tom Pinkney, Stephanie Breukink
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionManagement of cryptoglandular anal fistula is characterised by wide variation in diagnostic strategies, surgical techniques and outcome reporting, limiting comparison between studies and hindering evidence-based guideline development. This study aims to implement a standardised core outcome measurement set within a large international observational framework and to evaluate the feasibility of a scalable digitally supported model for global collaborative surgical research.Methods and analysisCryptoglandular anal fistula treatment is a prospective, international, multicentre observational study comprising two components: a short-term audit capturing clinician-reported outcomes at 3 months and a long-term cohort capturing clinician- and patient-reported outcomes over twelve months. Adults undergoing surgery for primary or recurrent cryptoglandular anal fistula are eligible, excluding non-cryptoglandular aetiologies. Data are collected using secure electronic case report forms and digitally administered patient-reported outcome measures, with paper alternatives available where required. Outcomes are defined according to the Anal Fistula Core Outcome Measurement Set and include clinical and radiological healing, recurrence, complications, reintervention, development of additional fistulas, symptoms, psychological impact of treatment, continence, quality of life and additionally work productivity. The study was designed around a predefined nine-step framework, including multidisciplinary coordination, central ethical approval to support local submissions, artificial intelligence-assisted translation of study materials with native review and implementation of secure digital data capture systems. Based on previous European Society of Coloproctology studies and expected centre volumes, the audit arm aims to include approximately 1000 patients and the cohort arm 500 to 750 patients.Ethics and disseminationCentral ethical approval has been obtained from the Medical Ethics Review Committee of the Maastricht University Medical Centre+ under METC 2024-0374 (audit arm) and METC 2024-0361 (cohort arm) with local approvals or waivers secured in participating countries according to national regulations. Written informed consent is obtained for cohort participation. Results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and international conferences, with the aim of informing future guideline development and supporting patient-centred care in cryptoglandular anal fistula management.Keywords: SURGERY, Colorectal surgery, HEALTH ECONOMICS

Introduction

Problem and impact

Cryptoglandular anal fistulas are abnormal tracts connecting the anal canal to the perianal skin, arising from the anal glands in the intersphincteric space at the level of the dentate line1,3 and affect approximately one in 5000 individuals annually.4 Surgical management aims to eradicate the fistula while preserving sphincter function.5 Current evidence shows substantial heterogeneity in outcomes and measurement methods used in clinical studies, complicating data synthesis and clinical comparison.6 7 As a result, treatment decisions are often guided by local expertise rather than comparative evidence and many patients continue to experience persistent symptoms and recurrence, significantly impairing quality of life.8 9

Gap in evidence

The most recent European Society of Coloproctology (ESCP) guideline includes forty-two recommendations for anal fistula management, of which only four are supported by moderate-quality evidence, while the remainder rely on lower certainty.7 Evidence remains inconsistent due to heterogeneity in outcome definitions, measurement instruments and follow-up intervals, limiting data synthesis and guideline development.6 The Anal Fistula Core Outcome Set (AF-COS)10 and the subsequent Anal Fistula Core Outcome Measurement Set (AF-COMS)11 were developed to standardise outcome measures, definitions and assessment intervals, enabling more consistent and comparable research.

Objectives

Building on previous ESCP collaborative studies,12 13 the cryptoglandular anal fistula treatment (CRAFT) study applies the AF-COS and AF-COMS in a prospective, international, multicentre design to evaluate whether patient characteristics, preoperative diagnostic modalities and surgical techniques are associated with clinician- and patient-reported outcomes in the surgical management of cryptoglandular anal fistula. The objectives are formulated as follows:

Primary objective

To evaluate whether patient characteristics, demographics, preoperative diagnostics and surgical techniques are associated with clinician- and patient-reported outcomes in the management of cryptoglandular anal fistula, using standardised AF-COMS definitions across real-world clinical settings.

Methods and analysis

Study design and setting

The CRAFT study is a prospective, international, multicentre, observational study with two arms: an audit with short-term follow-up and a cohort with long-term follow-up. Both capture real-world clinical practice in the surgical management of cryptoglandular anal fistulas using standardised AF-COMS outcome definitions. The audit arm collects clinician-reported outcomes at 3 months postoperatively, whereas the cohort arm extends follow-up to twelve months and includes both clinician- and patient-reported outcomes.

Participating centres, including academic and non-academic hospitals across high-, middle- and low-income settings, contribute prospectively collected data. Patient care follows each centre’s routine clinical protocols, with no imposed standardisation or interventions.

Audit arm recruitment was open from 1 January 2025, with sites activating as they received local ethical approval until 30 June 2025, and 3-month follow-up extending until 30 September 2025. Cohort arm recruitment opened on 1 April 2025, with sites activating as they received local ethical approval until 30 September 2025, and a twelve-month follow-up extending until 30 September 2026, marking the expected completion of the study.

Participants

Adults aged 18 years or older undergoing surgery for a primary or recurrent cryptoglandular anal fistula are eligible for inclusion. Patients with Crohn’s disease or other non-cryptoglandular aetiologies (eg, tuberculosis, malignancy, obstetric trauma, ulcerative colitis, pelvic radiation or actinomycosis) are excluded. The diagnosis of aetiology is determined by the treating surgeon based on clinical, radiological and intraoperative findings.

Sampling procedure and sample size

All eligible patients undergoing surgery during the study period are included consecutively. If a patient has more than one procedure during this time, only the first (index) surgery is recorded at baseline. Centres may take part in one or both study arms, and patients can be included in both if their surgery falls within the window of each arm.

This is a descriptive observational study without formal hypothesis testing. Based on previous ESCP studies and expected case volumes, the audit arm aims to include approximately 1000 patients and the cohort arm 500 to 750 patients, reflecting feasibility rather than formal power calculations.

Follow-up

In the audit arm, follow-up is limited to 3 months and captures only clinician-reported outcomes, recorded from routine postoperative visits and/or clinical records. Retrospective data entry is permitted if data were collected prospectively and documented in local records, provided entry occurs within 4 months after closure of the inclusion period.

In the cohort arm, patients are followed for twelve months with assessments at 30 days and at 3, 6 and 12 months. Both clinician- and patient-reported outcomes are collected.

Outcomes and variables

Core variables include baseline demographics (age, sex, BMI, comorbidities such as diabetes, immunosuppressive therapy and smoking), fistula characteristics (type and complexity), prior surgical history and preoperative imaging. Intraoperative variables include antibiotic use, seton drainage and surgical technique.

Data collection forms

Patient registration and follow-up are coordinated through Ldot, a Research Data and Workflow Management platform developed and maintained by Maastricht University. Ldot is used for patient enrolment and for the automated distribution of electronic case report forms via Castor Electronic Data Capture (EDC), a secure, cloud-based system for clinical research data management.

Patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) are administered digitally through Castor EDC via Ldot, which also sends automated reminders at predefined intervals to ensure timely completion. If a digital response is not received, local staff contact patients by telephone, in person or during clinic visits. Paper-based PROMs are available at participating sites when digital completion is not feasible.

Statistical analysis plan

Descriptive statistics will be used to summarise patient characteristics, diagnostics, treatments and outcomes. Associations between predictors and outcomes will be assessed using multivariable mixed-effects models to account for clustering at the hospital and country level. Logistic regression will be used for binary outcomes, linear regression for continuous outcomes and frailty models for time-to-event outcomes. Confounding variables will be pre-specified and adjusted for in all models.

Missing data will be addressed using multiple imputation where appropriate, based on the assumption of data missing at random. The number of imputations will be set to the percentage of incomplete records and predictive mean matching will be used to draw values for continuous variables. All analyses will be conducted using validated statistical software. A two-sided p value <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

Cryptoglandular anal fistula treatment study framework

Build a multidisciplinary team

A core team was assembled, including colorectal surgeons, clinical epidemiologists and health technology assessment experts from Maastricht University, the ESCP and the University of Birmingham. Regular meetings coordinated study design, logistics and quality oversight.

Align with a professional association

The study was conducted under the guidance of the ESCP, enabling alignment with its research network, endorsement processes and national coordinators to facilitate centre recruitment and monitor progress at the national level.

Define the clinical problem and research question

Knowledge gaps identified through recent guidelines were used to formulate a research question addressing diagnostic and surgical variability in cryptoglandular anal fistula management.

Develop a flexible study protocol

A protocol and data management plan were drafted for local Dutch approval. The protocol was intentionally designed with flexibility to accommodate diverse regulatory and infrastructural settings.

Obtain central ethics approval

Central approval from the Maastricht University Medical Centre+ ethics committee was obtained. This Dutch approval served as a template for submission packages to local or national regulatory bodies and facilitated parallel approvals across different healthcare systems.

Outreach, recruitment and engagement

Recruitment materials and registration tools were distributed through ESCP channels, mailing lists and social media, followed by webinars to guide site activation and data entry.

Translate materials for local approvals

PROMs were translated using forward–backward methodology and reviewed by native-speaking coordinators. Other study materials were translated using AI-assisted drafts, which were subsequently verified and adapted by local teams.

Implement a secure data capture system

Data collection used two digital platforms, both compliant with General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and institutional data protection standards.

Maintain regular study communication

Ongoing communication was maintained through bi-weekly newsletters during the inclusion period and direct support channels for participating centres.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and members of the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination planning of this study protocol. The research question and outcome measures are based on previously developed consensus frameworks (AF-COS and AF-COMS), with input from patient representatives.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical approval

This study is classified as non-WMO research under Dutch law and has been approved by the Central Committee on Research Involving Human Subjects, under METC 2024-0374 (audit approval) and METC 2024-0361 (cohort approval) and registered at the University of Maastricht under study number GDPR-3343. Local ethical approval or waivers are obtained in all participating countries in accordance with national legislation and site-specific requirements. National coordinators initiate and oversee submissions at the institutional or national level, with the audit and cohort arms reviewed separately when required.

Informed consent forms

Centrally provided consent templates are adapted to national legal and ethical requirements and describe the study purpose, procedures, data handling and withdrawal rights. Consent is obtained either before or on the day of surgery, prior to discharge, by the treating clinician or a delegated member of the research team.

To facilitate multinational participation, Patient Information Sheets and consent forms are first translated using artificial intelligence tools and subsequently reviewed and approved by the native-speaking national coordinator to ensure linguistic accuracy before local ethics submission.

Consent procedures

In the audit arm, data are collected anonymously from routine clinical records and, in most jurisdictions, do not require individual consent. Patient notification or written consent is obtained where mandated locally. In the cohort arm, written consent is required for participation and follow-up, which is documented in Ldot.

Participant risk and burden

This is a non-interventional study that involves no additional procedures beyond routine care. The only burden in the cohort arm is the completion of questionnaires (forty-four items). There are no physical risks associated with participation, and participation does not influence treatment decisions.

Withdrawal

Participants may withdraw from the study at any time without providing a reason, and this does not affect their clinical care. Upon withdrawal, no further data will be collected or contact made. Data collected prior to withdrawal will be retained for analysis unless the participant specifically requests deletion. Incomplete follow-up will be recorded, and missing data will be handled using appropriate statistical techniques.

In the audit arm, where data are collected anonymously from routine clinical records, individual withdrawal is not applicable unless local regulations require consent. In those settings, withdrawal procedures follow local ethical requirements.

Protocol development and amendments

The CRAFT protocol was developed by the Study Management Group in collaboration with national coordinators, methodologists and members of the ESCP Cohort Studies Committee. Contributions included study design, outcome selection, statistical analysis planning and feasibility assessment across international centres. Authorship for future publications will follow ICMJE criteria and the ESCP cohort studies authorship policy, based on substantial contributions to study design, coordination, patient inclusion, data completeness, data analysis or manuscript writing.

Substantial changes to the study design, eligibility criteria, data collection procedures or analysis will be reviewed by the Study Management Group, submitted to relevant ethics committees and communicated to all sites. Minor changes, such as administrative updates or clarifications, will be documented internally and archived.

Bias and quality assurance

Potential sources of bias include centre-level variation in diagnostic and treatment pathways (given the high proportion of complex and recurrent fistulas among participating sites), variability in surgeon experience and selective participation in the cohort arm. Standardised definitions and validated outcome measures are intended to minimise measurement bias.

Central data checks ensure consistency and completeness, with local teams responsible for accurate data entry and query resolution. No on-site monitoring is planned. PROMs are translated using forward–backward methodology, and all data entry platforms comply with the EU GDPR.19

Data sharing policy

Following publication of the primary results, de-identified data may be shared upon reasonable request, pending approval by the Study Management Group, and contingent on signing a GDPR-compliant data-sharing agreement. Only pseudonymised data will be shared, and no identifiable information will be released. All EU data will be stored and processed exclusively within EU boundaries in accordance with GDPR.

Dissemination

Results will be disseminated through presentations at national and international conferences and publication in peer-reviewed journals. The ESCP audit and cohort studies network will also serve as a dissemination platform. Reporting of the study results will adhere to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guidelines.

Reporting of early stopping

Although early termination is not anticipated, any premature closure of study components will be reported to all relevant stakeholders and ethics bodies. If objectives cannot be met, an interim analysis will be conducted and reported.

Discussion

The CRAFT study is designed to address key gaps in the management of cryptoglandular anal fistulas. It is the first international, prospective study to apply the AF-COS and AF-COMS frameworks across a broad range of hospitals, collecting both clinician- and patient-reported outcomes over a 12-month period.10 11 By systematically capturing diagnostic, treatment and outcome data, the study aims to clarify how these fistulas are managed in routine care and how different strategies relate to patient outcomes.

Previous research in this field has been limited by small sample sizes, substantial heterogeneity in outcome definitions and poor methodological quality, restricting evidence-based decision-making. The most recent ESCP guidelines reflect this, with many based primarily on low-level evidence.7 CRAFT addresses these limitations by applying standardised outcome definitions and validated measurement tools, enabling more reliable comparisons across centres and over time.6 20

Designed to be practical and inclusive, CRAFT builds on the model of previous ESCP-led studies. Participating centres can enrol patients during routine care, with the option of including retrospectively collected data in the audit arm, allowing for wide participation including hospitals with limited research infrastructure.12 13 In parallel with the clinical protocol, a structured study framework was developed to provide practical guidance on team formation, regulatory processes, AI-assisted translation and secure digital data capture, supporting scalable, inclusive and technology-driven international collaboration.

Digitalisation in healthcare has made it possible to routinely collect and share data across countries. Still, practical barriers such as limited digital access and language differences remain.21 22 In CRAFT, these challenges are addressed through AI–assisted translations. Drafts are reviewed by national coordinators who are native speakers of the target language, enabling faster and more uniform approval processes across countries. This approach demonstrates the potential of AI-supported translation for future multilingual healthcare research.23

Patient-reported outcomes are a key component of the CRAFT study. While traditional endpoints like healing and recurrence are routinely collected, other relevant aspects, including pain, incontinence, fatigue, treatment burden and impact on work, are often underreported.8 CRAFT ensures that both disease-specific and general health are captured consistently, by using validated tools including the AF-QoL, EQ-5D-5L and St. Mark’s (Vaizey) incontinence scale.

There are several limitations to consider. As an observational study, CRAFT cannot establish causal relationships. Although confounding will be adjusted for statistically, heterogeneity in healthcare systems and experience may introduce bias. Furthermore, some centres in the cohort arm may be more experienced, better resourced or see a greater proportion of recurrent or complex fistulae, introducing selection bias. PROM collection may also be hindered in settings where patients lack digital access or prefer traditional data collection. To minimise missing data, paper-based PROMs and telephone follow-up are supported.

Despite these challenges, CRAFT is expected to generate comprehensive real-world data sets on the surgical treatment of cryptoglandular anal fistulas. While primarily hypothesis-generating, its findings will inform future trial design and contribute directly to guideline development. More broadly, CRAFT presents a scalable, replicable model for global research in a modern digital era. The nine-step study framework demonstrates how international collaboration can work through digital innovation and real-world feasibility, offering a blueprint for similar international studies.

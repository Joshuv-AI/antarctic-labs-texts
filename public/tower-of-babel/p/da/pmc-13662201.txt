Multisensor Condition Monitoring Dataset from a Sulzer Industrial Centrifuge at Arab Potash Company: A One-Year Time Series for Predictive Maintenance Research
Authors: Doaa Ahmad Alqaraleh, Sami Salama Hussen Hajjaj, Mohd Zafri Baharuddin, Mohd Radzi Aridi, Usha Moorthy, Sathishkumar Veerappampalayam Easwaramoorthy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This paper presents a publicly accessible industrial condition monitoring dataset collected from a Sulzer centrifuge at the Arab Potash Company (APC) in Jordan. The twelve-month dataset (September 2022–August 2023) was acquired using an ABB 800xA distributed control system (DCS) sampling four sensor channels: torque (% of nominal load), vibration velocity (mm/s RMS), and dual bearing temperatures (°C, solid and liquid ends) at one-minute intervals (≈0.0167 Hz), yielding approximately 525,600 observations per channel. The preprocessing pipeline included timestamp synchronisation, duplicate removal, and linear interpolation for short gaps; records exceeding DCS alarm thresholds (vibration ≥12 mm/s, torque ≥85%, temperature ≥85 °C) were retained and labelled as candidate anomalies rather than deleted, to preserve event realism. The dataset is internally consistent, plant-specific, and suitable for exploratory benchmarking in industrial AI, digital twin development, and prognostics and health management (PHM) research.

Background & Summary

Industrial centrifuges represent critical assets in process industries due to the strong interconnection that exists among separation quality, processing rate, and energy. Unplanned shutdowns cascade into economic production losses as well as risks to personnel and equipment safety. As such, condition-based maintenance solutions and prognostics & health management applications have become integral parts of industrial plants’ ongoing reliability initiatives1,2. Current industry surveys on rotating equipment indicate an emerging trend from rule-driven troubleshooting towards data-driven and artificial intelligence-augmented analysis pathways for hot spot detection, remaining useful life estimation, and decision-making assistance based on exposure to high-quality sensor data and long-term production system logging1,3.

The dataset introduced in this paper captures one full year of multisensor monitoring from a Sulzer centrifuge operating at the Arab Potash Company (APC) in Jordan as shown in Fig. 1. Data acquisition was performed by an ABB 800xA DCS, synchronising heterogeneous sensor channels at one-minute intervals. The installed sensor suite comprises thermocouples at the main bearings (solid and liquid ends), an accelerometer near the rotor housing, a torque transducer at the gear output shaft, a lubricant sensor in the oil circuit, and an infrared overheating sensor at the hydraulic clutch. Of these, four variables are included in the publicly released dataset (torque, vibration, Temp_Solid, Temp_Liquid); the lubricant and clutch-overheating channels were logged by the DCS but are excluded from the current release due to data quality and proprietary constraints. This architecture reflects a pragmatic PHM stack for rotating assets, spanning thermal, vibrational, and load pathways that commonly precede functional degradation3. From a research standpoint, the dataset’s temporal depth (≈12 months) and sampling resolution (1-minute cadence) enable analyses at multiple time scales: (i) short-window dynamics for transient behaviours and early-warning indicators, (ii) daily/weekly cycles for operational regimes and control-policy effects, and (iii) seasonal drifts that may confound model stability. Seasonal analysis of the CFG-42 dataset revealed that all four sensor channels are systematically higher in winter (Dec–Feb) than summer (Jun–Aug): Vibration 7.76 vs 5.22 mm/s (−32.7%), Temp_Solid 66.8 vs 63.7 °C (−4.7%), Temp_Liquid 64.2 vs 59.3 °C (−7.7%), and Torque 68.8 vs 66.3% (−3.5%). This counter-intuitive pattern (higher values in winter) reflects elevated production loads during winter months at APC, and is a key feature researchers should account for when building season-aware PHM models. Long-horizon, high-granularity logs are precisely what modern PHM literature calls for when benchmarking deep learning models (e.g., sequence models, autoencoders) and hybrid physics–ML approaches for rotating equipment2,3.Fig. 1Image of the Sulzer CFG-42 industrial centrifuge at Arab Potash Company (APC), Jordan, showing the main rotor assembly, hydraulic clutch, and drive train.

Image of the Sulzer CFG-42 industrial centrifuge at Arab Potash Company (APC), Jordan, showing the main rotor assembly, hydraulic clutch, and drive train.

To support technical validation, the APC/ABB setup incorporates alarm thresholds within the DCS logic e.g., vibration at 12 mm/s, torque at 85% of rated load, and oil temperature at 85 °C which we use as guardrails for sanity checks, range validation, and optional weak labels for anomaly studies. While community standards (ISO 10816/20816 series) provide general guidance for evaluating vibration severity in rotating machinery, exact limits are machine-class dependent; thus, we treat the thresholds embedded in the APC control system as plant-specific criteria and report exceedances transparently4.

The Data Descriptor aims to make this resource broadly reusable beyond the potash domain. First, it offers a clean, unified schema with consistent timestamping and units across torque, vibration, and bearing temperatures, easing ingestion into analytics workflows. Second, it provides metadata about sensor placement and alarm logic, lowering the ramp-up cost for researchers who wish to replicate CBM baselines or to develop bespoke pipelines (e.g., threshold-guided weak supervision, cost-aware alarms). Third, it facilitates comparative studies—traditional signal processing vs. learned representations; supervised vs. self-supervised anomaly detection; model-based vs. data-driven RUL—reflecting active directions in PHM and predictive maintenance1,3. Finally, the dataset aligns with the broader Industrial AI and digital-twin agenda: high-fidelity, plant-scale time series are pre-requisites for twins that couple physics with data to anticipate degradation and optimize maintenance windows. Recent reviews underscore digital twins’ value for predictive maintenance—closing the loop between monitoring, diagnostics, and control—yet they also note the scarcity of open, long-duration datasets from real production environments5. By releasing a full-year log with sensor topology and control thresholds, this contribution helps bridge that gap for rotating machinery.

Method

Data source and collection architecture

The dataset has been collected from a continuously running Sulzer industrial centrifuge during the dehydration process of the Arab Potash Company (APC) in the southern part of the Dead Sea, Jordan. The process is controlled by the 800xA Distributed Control System by ABB. The 800xA system acts as both the data acquisition component and the supervisory level. The DCS aggregates inputs from multiple sensor modules distributed across the centrifuge’s mechanical and hydraulic subsystems. It synchronizes analog (e.g., temperature, vibration, torque) and digital (e.g., alarm triggers, valve states) signals into a unified log stored in a plant historian database. The system thus provides synchronization in the time dimension, redundant-free data transmission streams, and minimal latency of less than 1 s. The data were automatically generated as timestamped CSV files through the historian interface of the company ABB and were later gathered into a database.

As detailed in Table 1, sensor placement followed standard rotating-machine condition monitoring practice. The two thermocouples were mounted directly on the bearing housing surfaces: one at the solid-end (drive-shaft coupling side) and one at the liquid-end (hydraulic clutch side), both positioned close to the bearing outer race to ensure accurate thermal response. The accelerometer was bolted to the rotor housing at the 12 o’clock position with its measurement axis aligned radially to the shaft centreline, in accordance with ISO 10816-3 mounting recommendations. The torque transducer was integrated into the gear output shaft between the gearbox and the drive coupling. All sensors were wired directly to the ABB 800xA DCS I/O modules, with signal conditioning performed internally by the DCS prior to historian storage at one-minute intervals.

The sensors were calibrated according to the manufacturer’s calibration procedures. The orientation of the accelerometer on the rotor housing emphasized the axis sensitive to radial excitations, whereas the thermocouples were integrated into bearing housing sleeves made of stainless steel.

Sampling frequency and duration

All channels were sampled every 60 seconds (sampling frequency fs ≈ 0.0167 Hz; Nyquist limit fn ≈ 0.0083 Hz) over a continuous period of 12 months (September 2022 – August 2023), yielding approximately 525,600 records per variable. This rate is appropriate for the intended analysis targets: slow degradation trends, thermal cycling, production-regime changes, and long-horizon drift—phenomena that evolve on timescales of minutes to months. Per the Nyquist criterion, phenomena faster than approximately 2 minutes (above 0.0083 Hz) cannot be resolved; rotor-dynamic signatures (bearing defect frequencies, gear-mesh harmonics) in the Hz–kHz range require dedicated high-frequency acquisition outside the scope of this dataset. The one-minute cadence was chosen to balance long-term storage feasibility with DCS historian throughput, consistent with common industrial PHM practice.

Data preprocessing and quality assurance

A reproducible data preprocessing pipeline in Python (pandas) and R programming languages is developed to ensure the integrity and consistency of data obtained directly from the operational logs of a distributed control system (DCS) in its raw form. All timestamp entries have been processed for parsing and converted to a standard datetime format with synchronization to the local time zone (UTC + 3) and accuracy to one minute.

Records that were duplicates due to the historian buffering mechanism have been eliminated to counteract redundant sampling. This data is then sorted according to time. Incomplete reading data were handled by a combined strategy with forward and backward propagation followed by linear time interpolation (pandas interpolate(method = ‘time’), limit = 10 min; gaps >10 min left as NaN). Post-hoc impact analysis confirmed only 33 records were interpolated (0.007% of 482,506 observations), with negligible distributional effect on Temp_Solid (mean difference 0.11 °C, 0.17%). Torque and Vibration showed locally higher differences at the 33 interpolated points, but these represent a statistically insignificant fraction of the full dataset. This method was selected for its physical appropriateness for slowly-evolving thermal and mechanical signals and allowed for short-term data consistency with overall awareness of the longer data intervals that were retained and marked as missing, as illustrated in Fig. 3.Fig. 3Interpolation impact analysis. Cross-variable checks for consistency were conducted through pairwise correlation analyses between torque data, vibration data, and bearing temperatures. These analyses enable the detection of potential inconsistencies in sensor data. After the completion of the above quality checks, the cleaned and validated data was then exported in both xlsx and csv formats with the name CFG_42_EDS_Data_clean. The entire data preprocessing pipeline with associated metadata is included with the data to facilitate reproducibility and adherence to the FAIR data principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable).

System reliability and data integrity

To promote data integrity in the collected datasets, a variety of data integrity strategies were exercised throughout the data acquisition process. For instance, the ABB 800xA DCS uses checksum checks as well as double buffering of each data packet that is transmitted. This strategy prevents any incomplete data from being recorded due to transient communication errors (ABB System 800xA Documentation). Each sensor data point had cables rated to shield against any effects of EMI in the high-demand industrial setting. This strategy is well-supported by recent studies, which found that maintaining signal quality in industrial monitoring systems is a significant challenge posed by high EMI intensity6,7. A record of periodic calibrations had been documented by the maintenance personnel of the Arab Potash Company (APC). This ensured that the results of the data collected in this project had no bias in the form of data drift, in accordance with recent practices in maintaining data quality in industrial sensor systems8. Lastly, manual checks conducted jointly with automated error-detection software predicted that data error rates of approximately 0.05% had been experienced in the data collected in this project, in accordance with recent studies that emphasized the importance of detecting data bias, data drift, as well as missing values in industrial sensor data systems9,10.

Environmental and operational context

It is a machine that functions in a temperature range of 35–55 °C, which is a typical feature of the industrial zones of the Dead Sea, known for harsh climatic and chemical environments. This operational environment is characterized by large temperature variations between day and night, as well as varying mechanical cycles of load, as well as a high saline humidity that causes faster corrosion, which in turn affects the sensor performance. This makes this machine experience a true operational stress, representing a challenge that is a unique ecologically realistic testbed for the evaluation of BPE techniques.

These dynamic factors not only interact with bearing friction, lubrication viscosities, as well as the imbalances in rotors, but also introduce non-stationarity in vibration signals and temperature profiles, factors that are not always considered in experimental results11,12. As a result, this experimental data incorporates both the randomness of failures as well as the effects of environment-related drift factors, presenting a unique challenge in validating the robustness of algorithms used in fault identification, prognosis, as well as Remaining Useful Life estimation13.

In addition, the long-term dependencies arising from thermal and load interactions make this dataset particularly valuable for domain adaptation research, which addresses the challenge of transferring fault-diagnosis models trained on one machine or site to another where labelled data are scarce. A recent study (doi.org/10.1016/j.prostr.2026.02.039) highlights data scarcity in rotating machinery as a primary bottleneck for domain adaptation; the one-year CFG-42 log can serve as a source domain for such investigations. More broadly, the dataset supports transfer learning, cross-site generalisation, and industrial AI tasks14–16. It is therefore fair to say that this dataset, with a combination of natural field stresses, high-frequency sensor channels, as well as maintenance annotations, is a benchmark resource in formulating adaptive digital twin solutions17–19.

Data continuity was audited by comparing expected timestamps against actual logged entries. Statistical consistency and cross-correlation

To evaluate the internal consistency of the multi-sensor dataset, correlations between torque, vibration amplitude, and bearing temperatures (solid-end and liquid-end) were analyzed using both Pearson (linear) and Spearman (rank-based) methods.

The Pearson results indicate a moderate positive relationship between torque and solid-end bearing temperature (r = 0.37), while vibration shows a slightly stronger correlation with the same temperature (r = 0.45). In contrast, the relationship between torque and vibration is weak (r = 0.22), suggesting that load variations are only partially reflected in vibration behavior. Correlations involving the liquid-end bearing temperature are generally weaker, with torque showing minimal association (r = 0.10) and vibration exhibiting a modest relationship (r = 0.27). The two bearing temperatures themselves display a mild positive correlation (r = 0.33), indicating consistent but slightly delayed heat transfer along the shaft and lubrication system.

Spearman correlation analysis supports these findings, confirming similar monotonic relationships across variables, with the strongest associations observed between vibration and bearing temperatures (ρ ≈ 0.33–0.42).

From a physical perspective, these relationships are consistent with expected machine behavior. The torque–temperature coupling reflects frictional heat generation under increased load, while the stronger vibration–temperature link highlights the sensitivity of dynamic response to thermal effects on lubricant properties and bearing stiffness. The weak torque–vibration relationship aligns with the behavior of a well-balanced machine, where vibration is influenced more by mechanical condition than by instantaneous load. Meanwhile, the moderate coupling between bearing temperatures suggests gradual heat propagation with a slight spatial lag.

Overall, the results demonstrate coherent and physically meaningful interdependencies among the measured variables, consistent with established rotating machinery dynamics, as shown in (7).

External comparative validation against industrial standards

To further validate the reliability of the centrifuge dataset, the measured vibration levels were benchmarked against internationally recognized ISO standards (ISO 10816/20816) for rotating machinery condition assessment. These standards classify vibration severity (RMS velocity, mm/s) into operational zones ranging from Good to Unacceptable, depending on machine type and installation. Industrial centrifuges of this class typically fall within Class II–III, where acceptable vibration levels range between approximately 2.8 mm/s and 4.5 mm/s.

The recorded vibration values in this study (0.31–0.94 mm/s) remain significantly below these thresholds, consistently within the good operating region. Furthermore, only a negligible fraction of observations (0.08%) approached alarm-level limits defined by the control system (12 mm/s). This confirms that the machine operated under stable and well-balanced conditions throughout the monitoring period, with no indication of mechanical instability or sensor anomalies.

Beyond standards-based validation, the dataset was compared with widely used benchmark datasets in rotating machinery research, including NASA IMS, PRONOSTIA, FEMTO-ST, and XJTU-SY. A key distinction lies in data duration: while most public datasets span minutes to days, the present dataset covers a full year of operation. This extended temporal scope enables observation of slow degradation mechanisms and long-term operational variability that are typically absent in laboratory-generated data.

Another important difference concerns sensor diversity. Benchmark datasets predominantly rely on high-frequency vibration signals, whereas the present dataset integrates vibration, temperature, and torque measurements. This multi-domain structure allows for the analysis of coupled thermo-mechanical effects and more realistic system behavior under varying operational loads.

In addition, unlike laboratory datasets that rely on artificially induced faults, this dataset reflects natural variations in real industrial conditions. This makes it particularly valuable for applications such as anomaly detection, domain adaptation, and remaining useful life (RUL) estimation under realistic constraints. Collectively, these characteristics position the dataset as a bridge between controlled experimental benchmarks and real-world industrial requirements.

A seasonal analysis was also conducted to examine internal consistency under varying environmental conditions. Physical coupling validation across mechanical load, heat generation, and vibrational behaviour

In order to further authenticate the physical correctness of this data, an analysis of the intrinsic relationships between loading, temperature, and vibration, as variables which interact dynamically in rotatry systems, has been undertaken. In an industrial centrifuge, increased loading (torque) tends to create higher friction in the bearings, which generates increased heat as indicated by higher bearing temperatures, whereas higher vibration is indicative of better mechanical stability as a function of loading intensity, as opposed to loading itself. The presence or absence of such relationships is, of course, a critical verification of data integrity, to assure that what is apparently machine data is in fact genuine, as opposed to noise, i.e., machine malfunction, data artifacts, etc.

The data set shows the correct thermal-mechanical coupling, in which higher values of torque correspond to higher bearing temperatures, as observed in bearings containing solids, which experienced more stress. The strong trend of having r values of approximately 0.37 for temperature in bearings containing solids supports correct thermal diffusion models, as it is clear that temperature sensors measured accurately changes in temperatures caused by loading. The low value for r in the relation between torque values and vibration level, which appeared to be approximately 0.22, is also accurate, as it is more dependent on balance, rather than loading levels, which vary by time.

Moreover, a moderate correlation exists between the values of vibration and bearing temperatures (r values approximately 0.45 for the solid-end sensor), which is indicative of realistic, temperature-mediated stiffness variations in the bearing set. An increased temperature level leads to small variations in viscous fluidity and dynamic bearing stiffness, which give rise to predicted changes in vibration level, as has been abundantly observed in published studies of rotationally connected machinery subjected to varying temperature conditions. The coherence of such inter-domain relationships ensures that this data set is an appropriate representation of inter-linked physical processes, which essentially constitute an industrial centrifuge process. The degree of conformation of observed phenomena to known principles of mechanics is an adequate indication of data validity and appropriateness for further applications, including machine learning, hybrid modeling, and stress-strength analysis of reliability.

Noise characterization and sensor stability in relation to industrial dcs specifications

To examine the sensitivity of data from the sensor to noise, as well as the stability of data yielded by the sensor over an entire year, a noise characterization test analysis was conducted to determine how this correlated to what is to be expected from an ABB 800xA DCS installation with industry-grade sensors, which is capable of functioning in heavy environments that include high electromagnetic interference, temperature, as well as heavy industry-related vibration.

Power spectral density (PSD) analysis of vibration and torque signals revealed dominant energy at approximately 1.6 × 10−5 Hz (period ≈17 h). At the 1-minute sampling rate (Nyquist limit ≈0.0083 Hz), this peak characterises long-horizon operational drift—thermal loading cycles, production-rate variations (dominant PSD peak at 1.63 × 10−5 Hz, period ≈17.1 h, consistent across all four sensor channels)—rather than rotor-dynamic phenomena. High-frequency rotor dynamics (Hz–kHz range) are outside the Nyquist limit and were not the intended measurement target of this DCS-level dataset. This characterisation suits systems in an industrial environment, as is suitable for accelerometer as well as torque sensors under constant loading conditions. High freq values in noise analysis showed more than 40 dB below signal level values, suggesting a strong EMI protection, which is a requisite in verifying the acquisition system of DCS, as it follows double-buffering along with checksum verification of all received data packets.

Long-term stability was also confirmed by analysis of variance using a moving window of 200 points, which showed little drift throughout the year, ranging from 0.33 for vibration variance to 112 for torque variance, which is to be expected for heavy machinery subjected to intermittent loading conditions. The fact that there is little to no inflation in variance values indicates that aging of the sensor, which could lead to degradation of measurement quality, did not occur. Moreover, the box plot distribution of torque, vibration, and bearing temperatures indicated small inter-quartile ranges, which behaved normally in terms of spread distribution. The data distribution continued to remain entirely below the safety limits of DCS, as indicated in previous industry-based monitoring studies, which presented similar trends as those indicated by noise behavior in this study. This is enough indication that this data is of high fidelity, fit for analysis of spectral signals, detection, as well as advanced modeling of anomalies

Temporal continuity and completeness

A review of the timestamp data confirmed perfect sampling regularity and temporal continuity across all sensor channels. The mean sampling interval is exactly 60.0 s (nominal cadence), with no gaps exceeding 5 minutes detected across the full 12-month monitoring period, yielding 100% temporal completeness (zero missing rows, zero duplicate timestamps). Cross-correlation between the torque and vibration channels confirmed zero-lag synchronisation, consistent with ABB 800xA DCS clock tolerances.

Comparative interpretability, validity, and full validation summary

Altogether, the set of comparisons undertaken using external standards, public benchmarks, internal operational conditions, physical coupling dynamics, as well as noise versus stability analysis, ensures a full validation of the Sulzer centrifuge data set as a whole. The magnitudes of the data set’s vibrational values remain well within internationally accepted ISO 10816/20816 limits, thus verifying that the observed mechanics during the yearlong period amount to safe, stable, and acceptable industry standards of operation. Comparisons undertaken using standard laboratory data sets, including NASA IMS, PRONOSTIA, XJTU-SY, and FEMTO-ST, accentuate the benefit of this data set, bearing in mind that it is a long-term, multi-modal, real-world data set, which does not include environmental conditions observed in a laboratory-controlled scenario.

Comparisons within the data set itself, for different seasons, loading, and maintenance cycles, have uncovered consistent patterns of data, which correlate well with known machine dynamics, indicating that this data set is anything but randomly variable. The inter-relationships between torque, vibration, and bearing temperature, as established in known machine dynamics studies, also go a long way in ascertaining that this data set is indeed a representation of real machine dynamics in this plant’s turbines. Last but not least, noise analysis in this data set indicates stability in signal fidelity, as would be expected of ABB 800xA DCS in a real-world environment. Taken together, these analyses demonstrate that the dataset is internally consistent, physically coherent, and suitable for exploratory benchmarking in condition monitoring, predictive maintenance, and hybrid physics–ML research. The validation is plant-specific; researchers applying this dataset to other contexts should account for APC-specific operating conditions (Dead Sea industrial environment, APC production cycles) and the DCS-level sampling resolution (1 min), which precludes rotor-dynamic frequency analysis.

Usage Note

The Sulzer Centrifuge Condition Monitoring Dataset is a unique, openly available benchmarking resource with the potential to drive future innovations in predictive maintenance (PdM) and industrial AI. This dataset is obtained from a regularly functioning centrifuge process at the Arab Potash Company (APC) plant. This real-world dataset provides insight into the production process during steady-state operations as well as during realistic production or environmental variations. This real-world insight applies to the development of machine learning models with predictive potential21 at the analytical or prognosis level. It is also particularly applicable for analyzing machine reliability metrics like stress-strength modeling and mean time to degrade or failure22 through the association of torques (stress) with corresponding bearing temperatures (strength)23.

The dataset is also suitable for creating digital twins or carrying out hybrid simulations with analytical models combined with real-world models to simulate failure modes or operational dynamics24. Its multimodal nature makes it suitable for investigations involving sensor fusion modeling, transfer learning, or Explainable Artificial Intelligence with the focus on the paradigm of PHM 4.025,26. By offering the dataset in both Excel and CSV file formats with consistent metadata and with a focus on the principles of FAIR data, this particular dataset provides a reproducible body of truth that is applicable to both academic and industrial benchmarking purposes.

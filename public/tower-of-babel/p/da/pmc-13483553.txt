Rel+ macrophages disturbing cardiac niche contributes to cardiac dysfunction following sepsis
Authors: Yuanqun Zhou, Yu Zhu, Yue Wu, Shunxin Yang, Xinming Xiang, Xingnan Ouyang, Qinghui Li, Xiaodan Wang, Li Wang, Liangming Liu, Tao Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCardiac dysfunction is a major cause of high mortality in sepsis. A well-organized cardiac microenvironment, consisting of cardiomyocytes and non-cardiomyocytes, is essential for maintaining heart function. The mechanism is still unclear.MethodsSingle-cell and single-nuclei RNA sequencing of the left ventricular tissues from cecal ligation and puncture-induced septic shock mice models were used to detect the change of cardiac cells. Immunofluorescence of myocardial tissue was conducted to validate key cell subpopulations, and PKM2 siRNA-loaded targeting nanomaterials were used to observe the role of Mac2.ResultsThe noncontractile phenotype of cardiomyocytes was major type following sepsis. Rel+ resident macrophages (Rel+ Mac), sepsis-specific cardiac resident macrophages subpopulation, presented metabolic reprogramming with high glycolysis signatures, established a pro-inflammatory niche in septic heart. Mechanistically, Rel+ Mac contributed to cardiomyocyte contractile phenotype switching after sepsis by ITGB1, ITGA9, LAMA2, ITGA4, IL6, TNF, VCAM1 and MMP13 signal axis. Furthermore, Rel+ Mac orchestrated broader microenvironment disruption by interacting with vascular leakage-associated venous endothelial cells and pericytes, vascular hypo-responsiveness associated smooth muscle cells, and cardiac matrix remodeling fibroblasts subpopulation FB3. Targeting Rel+ Mac metabolic reprogramming by PKM2 siRNA-loaded with nanomaterials protected cardiac function after sepsis.ConclusionsRel+ Mac, along with associated microcirculation and stromal cells, disrupt the cardiac niche and synergistically contribute to the occurrence of sepsis-induced cardiac dysfunction. Among which, Rel⁺ macrophages act as key contributors within a broader network (mitochondrial dysfunction, Ca²⁺ mishandling, oxidative stress). These results may provide targets and strategies for the treatment of sepsis-induced myocardial injury in a multifaceted and integrated manner.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-026-08294-9.

Introduction

Cardiac dysfunction was involved in high mortality after sepsis. Impairments of left ventricular systolic and diastolic function were the main feature of sepsis-induced cardiac dysfunction [1]. The excessive inflammatory, mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, disorders of calcium homeostasis and endothelial dysfunction were major mechanisms up to now [1, 2]. While there is still a lack of specific intervention strategies for myocardial damage after sepsis in clinic.

As a multicellular organ, the heart’s performance is not only modulated by cardiomyocytes (CM), but also by the non-cardiomyocytes including fibroblasts (FB), endothelial cells (EC), vascular smooth muscle cells (SMC) and immune cells and so on [3]. Cardiac niche determined by non-cardiomyocytes participated in the cardiac development, homeostasis, and disease [4–7]. As a major immunocyte in heart, macrophages played an important role in aging and myocardial infarction via coupling to cardiomyocytes electrically [8–10]. A comprehensive understanding of the dynamic communication of cells in cardiac niche is important for the development of new therapeutic strategies for cardiac dysfunction after sepsis.

Here, with cecal ligation and puncture-induced septic shock mice models, heart tissue was applied to single-cell and single-nuclei RNA sequencing, the changes of cardiac cells and their contribution in the myocardial microenvironment and cardiac dysfunction following sepsis were comprehensively characterized.

Methods

A detailed description of the materials and methods is provided in the Supplemental Material. Data supporting the findings of this study are available from the corresponding authors on request.

Animal procedures

C57BL/6 WT mice were obtained from Daping Hospital Laboratory Animal Center. PKM2flox/flox mice, possessing 2 loxP sites flanking exon 4–6 of the Pkm gene (constructed by Cyagen Biosciences, Jiangsu, China), were maintained in a C57BL/6 genetic background. PKM2flox/flox mice were crossed with Cx3cr1creER mice to generate PKM2flox/flox × Cx3cr1creER (PKM2−/−) mice. Fluorescent protein expression in tissue-resident macrophages was induced by crossing Cx3cr1Cre with Rosa26-tdTomato (Cx3cr1-Cre; R26-tdTomato mice) (constructed by Cyagen Biosciences, Jiangsu, China). The work has been reported in line with the ARRIVE criteria [11]. Details for generating septic mice model were supplied in the Supplemental Material Methods.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using Prism v.9.0 (GraphPad Software). For animal experiments, data were presented as means ± standard deviation (SD). Differences between two groups were analyzed using unpaired Student’s t-test. Experiments with more than two groups were analyzed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by Tukeys's post hoc analysis and (SNK/LSD) comparison. Statistical significance was considered at P < 0.05.

Results

Non-contractile cardiomyocytes were identified as the main myocytes subpopulations in heart after sepsis

Impairments of left ventricular systolic and diastolic function were the main features of myocardial dysfunction after sepsis. To elucidate the underlying mechanism of myocardial contractile dysfunction after sepsis, left ventricular tissues from septic and normal mice were measured by single-cell nuclear transcriptome. A total number of 33,956 nuclei were obtained for following analysis (18,210 from sepsis and 15,746 from normal). Of these, 14,373 nuclei were defined as cardiomyocytes (Ttn, Myh6, Tnnt2, Myl2, Fhl2), and were identified to five cardiomyocyte subclusters (CM1-CM5) (Fig. 1A, B). CM1 presented high-level expressions of contractile force-generating related genes. CM2 were associated with intracellular immunoregulatory responses. CM3 were related with mechanosensing complex formation in the sarcomere. CM4 were high levels of stress-response genes which was mainly involved in the acute inflammatory response. CM5 enriched nuclear-encoded mitochondrial genes and displayed functional enrichment in mitochondrial respiration and energy metabolism (Fig. 1C and Fig S1).

According to the expression profiles of contraction-related genes, CM1 exhibited a highest contractile score, CM2, CM3 and CM4 showed lower contractile scores, CM5 indicated a moderate level of contractility (Fig. 1D). And then, the cardiomyocytes were re-defined into 2 clusters, contractile cardiomyocytes (Tnnt2+ CM, including CM1 and CM5) and non-contractile cardiomyocytes (Ankrd1+ CM, including CM2, CM3 and CM4) (Fig. 1E). There were 87.5% of contractile CM but only 12.5% of non-contractile CM in normal heart tissues. Following sepsis, the non-contractile CM increased to 89.14%, and the contractile CM only 10.86%. The results were testified by immunostaining which presented that the fluorescence intensity of contractile CM marker Tnnt2 was significantly reduced after sepsis (Fig. 1F). Furthermore, we isolated cardiomyocytes from normal and septic mice by Langendorff perfusion and collagenase digestion, then the contractility and calcium transients of individual cardiomyocytes were measured using the IonOptix Myocyte Calcium and Contractility System. The results showed that the sarcomere contraction amplitude, maximum sarcomere shortening velocity and sarcomere relaxation velocity of individual cardiomyocytes were significantly decreased after sepsis (Fig. 1G-J). The calcium transient amplitude of cardiomyocytes also showed a significantly decrease in the sepsis group compared with the normal group (Fig. 1K-L). Together, these findings indicate that sepsis alters the contractile phenotype of cardiomyocytes and impairs their contractility. Further analysis was conducted to explore the altered contractile phenotype of cardiomyocytes after sepsis by the differentiation trajectory (Fig. 2A and B). Contractile CM1 was located at the initial stage and non-contractile CM2/3/4 were located at a relatively later stage along the pseudotime trajectory (Fig. 2C). After sepsis, the gene expressions involving in cardiomyocyte contraction were decreased, while the gene expressions (Ankrd1 and Nppb) relating to heart failure were significantly increased along the pseudotime, indicating that the phenotype of cardiomyocytes was changed from contractile to non-contractile after sepsis (Fig. 2D and E).

Ion channels determine the formation of action potentials in cardiomyocytes, and the changes of ion channels-related genes following sepsis were further analyzed (Fig S2A and B). The results showed that Cacna1c encoding L-type calcium channel α-subunit (Cav1.2) was upregulated in non-contractile CM after sepsis. which led to intracellular calcium overload, and then resulted in arrhythmias and contractile dysfunction [12]. Potassium channel currents contributed to membrane repolarization [13]. Several potassium channels (Kcna4, Kcnd3, Kcnq1 and Kcnh2) were downregulated in both contractile and non-contractile CM after sepsis, which indicated that the action potential duration was prolonged and the conductivity was damaged, and disrupted the coordination of excitatory contractile coupling. Moreover, the Scn5a (encodes Nav1.5) was up-regulated in all cardiomyocytes after sepsis, which led to the prolong of action potential duration and prone to ventricular arrhythmias [14] (Fig S2C and D).

To explore the key factors that convert cardiomyocytes from contractile to non-contractile in sepsis, the GeneSwitch algorithm was applied to analyze the switch genes and transcription factors along the pseudotime trajectory (Fig. 2F, G and H). The results showed that Ankrd1, Rrad, Pdk4 and Lcn2 were earlier activated genes along the pseudotime which stimulated cardiomyocyte phenotypic switching. The involving transcription factors included Stat3, Hand2, Egr1, Nr4a1, Bhle40 and Atf4 along the pseudotime. Network analysis revealed that Egr1 was a key transcription factor targeting Rrad and Lcn2 (Fig. 2I). The expression of Egr1 was testified which presented in non-contractile CM in normal heart tissue, and was significantly increased in contractile CM after sepsis (Fig. 2J). Above results indicate that cardiomyocyte phenotype is converted from contractile to non-contractile after sepsis and Egr1 may be a key transcription factor.

To further identify the role of Egr1 in the cardiomyocyte phenotype switching, we conducted a small interfering RNA targeting Egr1 (si-Egr1) and Egr1 overexpression plasmid (Egr1 OE) (Fig. 2K). The results showed that knockdown of Egr1 with si-Egr1 transfection significantly attenuated the LPS-induced upregulation of Ankrd1 and the downregulation of TNNI3 in HL-1 cardiomyocytes (Fig. 2L). Similar with LPS stimulation, overexpression of Egr1 significantly increased the expression of Ankrd1 while reduced the expression of TNNI3 (Fig. 2M). These results confirm that Egr1 plays an important role in regulating phenotypic switching in cardiomyocytes.

Rel+resident macrophage (Rel+Mac) played a key role in the cardiomyocyte contractile phenotype switching after sepsis by metabolic reprogramming

Cardiac niche played critical role in the cardiac function. Monocytes/macrophages (Mrc1, C1qb, Cd163, Lyz2, Ly6c2, Ccr2) are the largest population of immune cells in hearts (Fig S3) which were further subdivided into monocyte (Mo1) and six macrophage (Mac1-6) subclusters (Fig. 3A). Mac1 was major enriched in normal hearts and involved in highly expression of antigen presentation-related genes (H2-DMa, H2-Ab1, and H2-Eb1). Mac2 only presented in the septic heart with high levels of Fpr1, IL1rn and inflammatory chemokine (Ccl5, Cxcl2). Mac3 ~ Mac6 were presented both in normal and septic heart, associated with macrophage differentiation, angiogenesis and anti-inflammatory, the regulation of lipid transport and cholesterol homeostasis, and glycogen metabolism, respectively (Fig. 3B, C and D).

Based on the signature Ccr2+, Mac3 was identified as monocyte-derived macrophages and with a small increase in its proportion after sepsis. The other macrophage subclusters (Mac1, Mac2, Mac4, Mac5, and Mac6) were identified as cardiac tissue-resident macrophages with the signature Ccr2- (Fig. 3B). Among them, Mac2 was major subcluster which comprised 54.7% of macrophages in sepsis and was specifically expressed after sepsis which was testified via immuno-fluorescence staining (Fig. 3D and E). Rel was identified to specifically expressed in Mac2 by DoRothEA algorithm, which was known to involv in glucose metabolism and macrophage polarisation [15] (Fig. 3F). Further analysis showed that Mac2 denoted high glycolysis activity with high expression of glycolysis related enzyme such as Pkm2, Gapdh, Hk2 (Fig. 3G and H). These results indicate that Mac2 (highly expressed Rel), as a sepsis-specific cardiac resident macrophage subset, play key role in the transforming to pro-inflammatory in macrophages by metabolic reprogramming with glycolysis via PKM2.

Previous studies showed that macrophages were involved in regulation of myocardial function via participating in myocardial electrical conduction [9]. So, the interaction of major subcluster Rel+ Mac (Mac2) and cardiomyocytes after sepsis was further investigated. The Cell-cell interaction analysis showed that the intercellular communication between Rel+ Mac and cardiomyocytes was involved in ITGB1, ITGA9, LAMA2, ITGA4, IL6, TNF, VCAM1 and MMP13 signal axis(Fig. 3I). The target genes of these interaction axis were contribute to calcium signaling, actin cytoskeleton, and cardiac muscle contraction(Fig. 3J). These results suggest that Rel+ Mac contribute to cardiomyocyte contractile dysfunction in sepsis by metabolic reprogramming.

To further confirm the role of Rel+ Mac with high glycolysis in cardiomyocyte contractile dysfunction caused by sepsis, cardiac macrophage-specific PKM2 knockout mice (PKM2-/-) were generated by crossing PKM2flox/flox mice with Cx3cr1CreER mice (Fig. 3K). Echocardiographic results showed that under baseline conditions, PKM2-/- mice exhibited no significant differences in cardiac function compared with PKM2flox/flox controls (Fig. 3L-M). After sepsis, PKM2-/- mice exhibited improved cardiac function indicated by higher ejection fraction (EF) compared to PKM2flox/flox mice (95% confidence interval (CI) of PKM2flox/flox:69%-83%, 95% CI of PKM2flox/flox+Sepsis:45%-65%, 95% CI of PKM2-/-:71%-80%, 95% CI of PKM2-/-+Sepsis:58%-80%) (Fig. 3L-M). We also assessed the contractility of single cardiomyocyte from septic controls and PKM2-/- mice. The results showed that the sarcomere contraction amplitude, maximum sarcomere shortening velocity and sarcomere relaxation velocity were significantly restored in individual cardiomyocytes from PKM2-/- mice after sepsis (Fig. 3N-Q). The calcium transient amplitude of cardiomyocytes also showed a significantly increase in septic PKM2-/- mice compared with PKM2flox/flox controls (Fig. 3R-S). These findings further confirmed that Rel+ Mac with high PKM2 expression play an important role in cardiomyocyte contractile dysfunction caused by sepsis.

Venous ECs and pericytes (PC) played key role in the occurrence of vascular leakage, feedback loop between Rel+Mac and EC, SMC contributed to disturbance of microcirculation following sepsis

Vascular unit determined tissue perfution and barrier. To explore the alteration of vascular unit cells, 11 distinct populations of EC, SMC and PC were reclustered and identified. ECs was identified by pan-EC markers Pecam1, Cdh5 and Vwf, comprised 6 populations including capillary EC (Car4, Rgcc), arterial EC (Dll4, Efnb22, Sma3g), venous EC(Ackr1, Nr2f2), endocardium (Npr3, Smoc1), lymphatic EC (Pdpn, Prox1), and prolifetive capillary-like EC(Car4, Rgcc, Mki67)(Fig. 4A and Fig S4A-C). Capillary EC represent more than 60% of all ECs, similar to the recently published single-cell data [3]. Compared with normal samples, sepsis induced the increase of venous EC proportion and the decrease of capillary EC and arterial EC proportion(Fig. 4B).

Vascular leakage and hyporesponsibility were key mechanims of microenvironment contributing to organ dysfucntion following sepsis. To evaluate cardiac vascular endothelial barrier function following sepsis, we detected the extravasation of intravenously administered Evans blue dye (EBD). Compared with the normal group, the septic mice showed increasd extravasation of EBD in the heart, suggesting higher vascular permeability in their cardiac tissue (Fig S4D). Our previous study has confirmed that sepsis-induecd vascular leakage primarily occurs in venule and capillary network [16]. To identify the specific cell subpopulations underlying increased cardiac vascular permeability during sepsis, we observed the expression of vascular permeability markers across different EC subtypes. The results showed vascular permeability markers Plvap and Aqp1 in venous ECs showed robust expression following sepsis(Fig. 4C). At same time, the genes of vessel integrity and stability (Klf2, S1pr2 and Robo4), and endothelial barrier related genes including tight junction genes Tjp1, Tjp2, Jam3, Jam2 and adherens junction genes Ctnnb1, Cdh5, Ctnnd1 were down-regulation in venous ECs after sepsis(Fig. 4D). These results demonstraated that venous ECs were the major location of the occurrence of vascular leakage after sepsis. PC, as mural cells covering microvascular capillaries, played an essential role in vascular permeability (Fig. 4E). Our previous study has found that sepsis induecd PC loss, and exogenous PC infusion can improve vascular barrier function after sepsis [16–18]. To explore how PC exert this protection, we analyzed the interactions between PCs and ECs, we analyzed the interactions between PCs and ECs. The results showed that compared with normal heart, the interaction of PC2 with capillary EC, venous EC, and arterial EC, were significantly reduced after sepsis, as well as the decrease of cell-cell junctions(Fig. 4F and G). The Jam2/Jam3 and Gas6/Axl axis, which were crucial for cell adhesion, were predominant for endothelial-pericyte interactions in healthy individuals, while these interactions were significantly disturbed after sepsis, indicating that the cability of pericytes supporting endothelial cells was compromised in sepsis(Fig. 4H). These findings suggst that PC2 and venous ECs together contribute to the increase in vascular permeability following sepsis.

SMCs determined perfusion of tissue and modulating myocardial microcirculation. Three SMC populations were acquired (Fig S5A and B). SMC1 express Rgs5 and Fabp4, showed contraction-related genes and cyclin gene expression and scores. SMC2 highly expressed contraction-related genes Cnn1 and Actg2, defined as contractile SMC. SMC3 expressed stress response genes such as Fos, Junb, Jun and Klf2, and also expressed high levels of proteinase genes (Fig S5C, D and E). Overall DEGs of SMC analysis showed that the pathways related with contraction and energy metabolism such as OXPHOS were significantly downregulated after sepsis, which may lead to local blood flow hypoperfusion following sepsis (Fig S5F). Moreover, sepsis evoked signals to inflammatory response, extracellular matrix assembly, collagen degradation, TNF and NFκb pathways, which were invoved in SMC phenotypic switching (Fig S5G). The contractile SMC markers Myl9, Acta2 and Myh11 were significantly down-regulated after sepsis while synthetic SMC markers Spp1, Nmt1, Col1a1 and Col3a1 were significantly elevated (Fig S5H). The results indicated that SMC were converted to synthetic phenotype from contractile after sepsis, which is consistent with our previous finding that sepsis induces reduced contractility in SMC [16]. In our previous study, the reduce of contractility of vascular smooth muscle cells after sepsis was closely related to endothelial cells. By EC-SMC interactions analysis, Dll4-Notch3 and Edn1-Ednra axises between arterial ECs and SMC were identified, which were significantly decreased in sepsis (Fig. 4I). These results suggested that sepsis-induced vascular hyporeactivity was related to SMC and arterial ECs.

The phelotype of immune cells was closely related with microcirculation, above results showed that Rel+ Mac was sepsis-specific subcluster, and then the relationship of Rel+ Mac and vascular unit cells after sepsis was observed. Cell-cell interaction analyses showed that interaction of Rel+ Mac with capillary ECs, venous ECs, arterial ECs, SMC2 was enforced (Fig. 5A). After sepsis, several signaling pathways including MIF, CCL, ANNEXIN, CSF, SELE and CSF3 were activated. Multiple chemokines (Mif, Ccl7, Ccl4, Ccl3, C3, Rarres2) and adhesion factors (Sele) secreting by venous ECs and SMC2 acted on specific receptors in Mac2 and mediated its aggregation and pheylotype transformation(Fig. 5B and C). At same time, the interaction between capillary ECs and Rel+ Mac triggered macrophage polarization from a reparative to a pro-inflammatory phenotype by Col4a1-Sdc4, Col4a2-Sdc4, Col4a1-Cd44 and Col4a2-Cd44, suggeting that capillary ECs provided a trophic niche for pro-inflammatory phenotype of Rel+ Mac after sepsis(Fig. 5D and E). Meanwhile, Rel+ Mac exacerbated endothelial cells related inflammation and constructed a pathological positive feedback loop by secreting high levels of TNF and VCAM1 and then interacting with relevant receptors in endothelial cells (Fig. 5B).

Rel+Mac triggering pro-inflammatory transformation of fibroblasts FB3 played an important role in matrix remodelling and cardiacl dysfunction in sepsis

Cardiac fibroblasts (FBs) are a distinct mesenchymal cell type that are responsbile for construction of extracellular matrix (ECM). Four fibroblast clusters were identified in septic heart (Fig. 6A). FB1(Hsd11b1, Apoe, Angptl2) involved in ECM organization, cellular stress response and viral defence. FB2(Ugdh, Cd248, Tmeme100, Gfpt2, Ly6c1) belonged to vascular epicardial fibroblasts, involving in angiogenesis and wound healing, similar to fibroblast-Sca1-high (FB-SH) reported by Farbehi et al. [19]. FB3(Lcn2, Cxcl1, Ccl2) involved in ribosome biogenesis and the regulation of translation. FB4 (Postn, Dkk3, Col8a1, Ctgf) was identified as activated fibroblasts, and participated in ECM assembly and wound healing (Fig. 6B and Fig S6A). Ratio analysis revealed that FB1 was the predominant subtype in the normal heart and significantly reduced after sepsis, whereas FB3 was significantly expanded after sepsis (Fig. 6C), suggesting that FB3 subpopulation maybe participate in the progression of sepsis-induced cardiac dysfunction. Further analysis revealed that FB3 were linked to IL-17 and TNF signaling pathway and had higher expression of inflammatory-related genes and lower expression of ECM-related genes (Fig. 6D, E and Fig S6B, C, D). In addtion, FB3 expressed with high levels of Lcn2 specifically in the septic heart (Fig. 6F and Fig S6E), which involved in lipid transport, and the onset of cardiometabolic disease via its pro-inflammatory effects. Co-expression network analysis revealed that Lcn2/matrix metalloproteinase 2 (Mmp2) pathway played a critical role in FB3 phenotype after sepsis, which were testified with immunofluorescence staining (Fig. 6G, H and I). The results suggest that FB3 is pro-inflammatory fibroblasts, contribute to matrix remodelling after myocardial injury via the Lcn2/Mmp2 axis in septic heart. To further validate the regulatory role of Lcn2 in FB3, we performed a simulated Lcn2 knockout using the scTenifoldKnk algorithm and assess its perturbation effects under sepsis conditions. Lcn2 knockout resulted in significant dysfunction of pro-inflammatory-related genes and MMP genes (including Mmp2) (Fig S7A), indicating its central role in matrix remodeling and inflammatory regulation within FB3 subpopulation. We further constructed a targeted AAV9-TCF21-EGFP-mir30-shRNA(mLCN2) (shLcn2) adeno-associated viral vector to achieve cardiac fibroblast-specific knockout of Lcn2 (Fig S7B). The results showed that shLcn2 infection significantly reduced the proportion of Lcn2-positive fibroblasts in cardiac tissues of septic mice (Fig S7C and D). Following viral-mediated Lcn2 knockdown, septic mice showed restored cardiac EF (Fig S7E and F), significantly attenuated myocardial injury (Fig S7G), and markedly reduced myocardial inflammation (Fig S7H and I). These results further underscore the critical role of the Lcn2+ FB3 subpopulation in sepsis-induced cardiac dysfunction. NicheNet analysis between FB3 and cardiomyocytes showed that FB3 mediated the transition of cardiomyocytes from contractile to non-contractile phenotypes via transcription factors or genes which led to the phenotypic transition of cardiomyocytes by CCL2, SERPINA3, SOCS3, FOS, JUNB, HAND2, EGR1 and STAT3 (Fig S8A and B). To further explore the role of FB3 in cardiomyocyte phenotype switching, we isolated cardiac fibroblasts from normal and septic mice, and the condtioned medium from these cells was used to culture HL-1 cardiomyoctes (Fig S8C). The results showed that in HL-1 cardiomyocytes cultured with conditioned medium from septic fibroblasts, the expression of TNNI3 was significantly reduced, while the expression of Ankrd1 was significantly increased (Fig S8D and E). In addition, cellular energy metabolism analysis showed that the conditioned medium from septic fibroblasts significantly decreased ATP production, maximal respiratory capacity and reserve respiratory capacity in HL-1 cardiomyocytes (Fig S8F). These results suggest that pro-inflammatory fibroblasts FB3 played an important role in sepsis-induced myocardiac contractile dysfunction.

Previous studies found that the interaction between immune cells and cardiac fibroblasts was closely related with cardiac function [20]. Cell-cell interactions analysis showed that sepsis-sepcific Rel+ Mac constituted a large amount of communication network with FB3, FB2 and FB1 after sepsis, and FB3 was the key point of communication with the most strong input and output signals (Fig S8 G and H). TNF, Vcam1 signalling between Rel+ Mac and FB3 were activated (Fig. 6J, K and L), which contributed to activation of pro-inflammatory FB3 and ECM re-construction, and the resulted in myocardial remodelling. In addition, C3-C3RA, C3-ITGAM or ITGB2, APP-CD74, Anxa1-Fpr1 or Fpr2 pathways from FB3 to Rel+ Mac led to activation of complement system and immune response which was related with macrophage metabolic reprogramming (Fig. 6M and N). To further explore the role of FB3 in Rel+ Mac activation, we isolated cardiac fibroblasts from normal and septic mice, and the condtioned medium from these cells was used to culture RAW264.7 cells (Fig. 6O). The results showed that the expression of iNOS and TNFα in RAW264.7 cells cultured with conditioned medium from septic fibroblasts was significantly reduced (Fig. 6P), suggesting the activation of macrophages caused by pro-inflammatory fibroblasts.

Nanoparticles targeting Rel+ Mac metabolic reprogramming antagonized sepsis-induced cardiac dysfunction

Above results highlighted Rel+ Mac metabolic reprogramming (characterised by Pkm2 related with glycolysis) played an important role in the occurrence of sepsis-induced cardiac dysfunction. In order to observe whether targeting Rel+ Mac metabolic reprogramming was to improve cardiac function after sepsis, nanomaterials (PKMsi@NPs) with PKM2 siRNA-loaded targeting cardiac-resident macrophages were designed and synthesized (Fig. 7A). Size distribution (123.16 ± 3.42 nm) and zeta potential (40.02 ± 3.13 mV) of PKMsi@NPs were measured by dynamic light scattering and cryo-transmission electron micrographs(Fig. 7B and C). In vitro experiments demonstrated that PKMsi@NPs was successfully up-taken by macrophages (Fig. 7D) without affecting cell viability (Fig. 7E). Further assessment of the in vivo targeting capability revealed that PKMsi@NPs successfully targeted cardiac-resident macrophages in mice (Fig. 7F). The effects of PKMsi@NPs on sepsis-induced dysfunction were observed. Echocardiography revealed that PKMsi@NPs treatment significantly enhanced cardiac function of septic mice (95% CI of the sepsis group: 52%-70%; 95%CI of the PKMsi@NPs group: 71%-85%) (Fig. 7G). Consistently, PKMsi@NPs treatment reduced the level of cTNI (Fig. 7H), and restored myocardial fibre arrangement with HE staining of cardiac pathology (Fig. 7I-J). In addtion, compared with the sepsis group, the PKMsi@NPs group exhibited significantly reduced levels of TNFα, IL-6 and IL-1β in both blood and cardiac tissue, as well as decreased bacterial colony-forming units (CFUs) in the blood and peritoneal cavity(Fig S9). PKMsi@NPs extended the survival rate from 37.5% to 68.75% (Hazard ratio (logrank) = 0.3625, 95% CI: 0.1300–1.011, p = 0.0335)(Fig. 7K). We also investigated the effect of the glycolysis inhibitor 2-Deoxy-D-glucose (2-DG) and Oxamate on sepsis-induced cardiac dysfunction and found that they also can improve myocardial function in septic mice (Fig S10 ), similar to the effect of PKM2 siRNA nanoparticles. This results suggest that targeting Rel+ Mac-glycolysis pathway may provide a novel therapy for sepsis-induced cardiac dysfuction.

Discussions

The changes in the myocardial microenvironment during sepsis directly affect patient’s cardiac function and prognosis. It is still unclear about the changes of different cardiac cells and their roles after sepsis. In present study, with cecal ligation and puncture-induced septic shock models, we comprehensively characterised the cell phenotype changes in the heart with sepsis and the contribution in the myocardial microenvironment and cardiac dysfunction. In parallel with the phenotypic of cardiomyocytes changed to noncontractile from contractile following sepsis, a novel sepsis-specific cardiac resident macrophages subpopulation Rel+ Mac showed metabolic reprogramming with high glycolysis signatures, and established a pro-inflammatory niche in septic heart. Mechanistically, Rel+ Mac contributed to cardiomyocyte contractile phenotype switching after sepsis by ITGB1, ITGA9, LAMA2, ITGA4, IL6, TNF, VCAM1 and MMP13 signal axis. Mechanistically, Rel+ Mac orchestrated broader microenvironment disruption by interacting with vascular leakage-associated venous endothelial cells and pericytes, vascular hypo-responsiveness associated smooth muscle cells, and cardiac matrix remodeling fibroblasts subpopulation FB3. Targeting Rel+ Mac metabolic reprogramming by PKM2 siRNA-loaded with nanomaterials protected cardiac function after sepsis. Our study revealed the disturbance of cardiac niche and the cell-to-cell dynamic communication in cardiac cells during sepsis, and these results may provide targets and strategies for the prevent and treatment of sepsis-induced myocardial injury in a multifaceted and integrated manner (Fig. 8).

Macrophages, the most abundant immune cell population in the heart, are considered the sentinels for damage and circulating infectious agents (such as bacteria). Previous studies suggested that the main biological functions of macrophages were involved in phagocytosis and bacteriostasis, inflammatory responses, antigen processing and presentation, as well as immune regulation [21, 22]. Recent studies reported their new functions in cardiac development, homeostasis, and diseases [10, 23]. To maintain cardiac homeostasis, macrophages perform typical housekeeping tasks, such as participating in the metabolism of cardiomyocytes and the matrix, removing locally shed cellular material, contributing the physiological remodelling of cardiac tissue, and possibly regulating cardiac autonomic nerve control [9, 24, 25]. Macrophages also can interact with cardiomyocytes dirctly via gap junctions containing connexin 43 (CX43) and promte electrical conduction [8, 26]. In the development of cardiac dysfunction, macrophages play a dual role in promoting inflammation and repair. In the early stages of myocardial infarction, CCR2+ cardiac macrophages were recruited to infarcted area of heart and were critical drivers of cardiac inflammatory responses and injury [27]. Cardiac resident macrophages expand in heart failure induced by pressure overload, then promte cardiac repair by stimulating angiogenesis and inhibiting fibrosispromoting [28]. The plasticity of macrophages allowed them to respond to various changes in the environment, fulfilling the physiological needs at a given time. However, the role of macrophages in sepsis-induced myocardial injury is still not fully recognized. Our research found a unique subset of cardiac resident macrophages Rel+ Mac polarised from a reparative to a pro-inflammatory phenotype via metabolic reprgramming after sepsis and contributed to cardiomyocyte contractile phenotype switching after sepsis by ITGB1, ITGA9, LAMA2, ITGA4, IL6, TNF, VCAM1 and MMP13 signal axis. Rel+ Mac also interacted with vascular leakage-associated venous endothelial cells and pericytes, vascular hyporesponsiveness-associated smooth muscle cells and arterial endothelial cells, and cardiac matrix remodeling fibroblasts subpopulation FB3, together participating in sepsis-induced cardiac dysfunction. In normal conditions, cardiac resident macrophages are dominated by fatty acid oxidation, Rel+ Mac showed high activity glycolysis and have the highest expression of genes coding glycolysis-related enzymes including PKM, GAPDH, Aldoa and LDHA. Glycolysis was responsible for maintaining the pro-inflammatory phenotype of macrophages and promting the formation of cardiac inflammatory niche after sepsis [29]. Rel, which is known to be involved in glucose metabolism and macrophage polarisation, showed a high level of expression in Rel+ Mac, suggesting that Rel triggered the transcriptional activation of genes related to glycolysis especially PKM, thereby promoting Rel+ Mac metabolic reprogramming and transforming to pro-inflammatory macrophages. This may be an important mechanism of cardiomyocyte contractile phenotype switching and cardiac dysfunction following sepsis. Furthermore, we found the levels of REL and the de-convolution-calculated Rel+ Mac signatures were significantly increased in patients with sepsis (GSE79962 and GSE237861) (Fig. S11A-B). Analysis of data of human cardiac myeloid cells sorted from failing human hearts (GSE112630) also revealed higher REL expression in CCR2- macrophages or CCR+ monocytes than in CCR2+ macrophages(Fig S11C). In addition, analysis of single-cell RNA sequencing data from sepsis patient blood samples revealed elevated REL expression and Rel⁺ macrophage signatures in monocytes post-sepsis (GSE167363), and compared with survivors, non-survivors exhibited higher REL expression and Rel⁺ macrophage signatures (Fig S11D-E). Overall, our data obtained from humans were consistent with the trends in the animal experiments, suggesting that Rel⁺ macrophage signatures may serve as potential biomarkers for sepsis-induced myocardial dysfunction.

Cardiomyocytes contractile function impaired is the main feature of myocaridal dysfuction in sepsis. Our study showed that 87.50% of cardiomyocytes lost their contractile performance after sepsis. Consistent with this, the transcriptional profiles of ion channel genes associated with action potential generation in cardiomyocytes also were significantly disrupted. Moreover, the zinc-finger Egr1 was identified as key transcription factors to promote the transition from a contractile to a non-contractile phenotype in cardiomyocytes with sepsis. Previous studies have shown that Egr1 plays an important role in a variety of biological processes including tissue injury and immune response [30, 31]. Recent studies highlighted the key role of Egr1 in cell fate transitions. Egr1 could bind at distal enhancers correlates with both transcriptional activation and repression, and promote monocytic/macrophagic differentiation by down-regulateing enhancer activity and gene expression [32]. Egr1 overexpression in cardiac fibroblast directly induced fibroblast-to-myofibroblast transformation, and regulated several fibrosis-related genes, thereby promoting pathological cardiac fibrosis [33]. Our study showed that Egr1 was up-regulated in non-contractive cardiomyocyte and regulated the earlier activated genes that convert cardiomyocytes from contractile to non-contractile in sepsis, such as Rrad and Lcn2. Egr1 can be activated by a wide variety of different stimuli. We revealed the extensive interactions between cardiomyocytes and Rel+ Mac after sepsis and servral pathways were involved including cGMP-PKG signaling, calcium signaling, actin cytoskeleton and cardiac muscle contraction, but whether the increased Egr1 in cardiomyocyte after sepsis is directly related to Rel+ Mac and its specific mechanism requires further sutdy.

Vascular leakage is a major pathophysiological feature of sepsis as well as an important mechanism leading to organ dysfunction [34]. Our data provided sufficient scientific evidence that venous ECs were the major site of vascular leakage caused by sepsis. Compared with the normal group, the number of venous ECs was significantly increased after sepsis. Moreover, Vegfa, vascular endothelial growth factor a, which binds to Vegfr2 and mediate high permeability and transient opening of cell-cell junctions [35], showed upregulation in venous ECs with sepsis. Consistant with this, the cell-cell junction score of venous ECs was decreased after sepsis. At the same time, pericyte coverage of endothelial cells was reduced in sepsis, which also mediate vascular leakage caused by sepsis [16]. Consistent with our proivous study, arterial ECs and SMC together participated in the reduced vasoconstriction and lower perfusion in sepsis. Studies showed that the vascular unit cells also affect the composition of immune microenvironment by controlling the entry and exit of immune cells [19], our study found that venous ECs and SMC2 interacted with Rel+ Mac via chemokine singalling and mediated the aggregation of pro-inflammatory macrophages, while collagen singalling released by capillary ECs provided a trophic niche for Rel+ Mac, promoting its pro-inflammatory phenotype after sepsis, which promoted vascular niche disorder and myocarial dysfunction. These results were correlative observations requiring further validation.

Cardiac fibroblasts play a key role in maintaining the basic structure and function of the heart, as well as regulating cardiac remodelling under pathological conditions [36]. In the early stages of cardiac injury, fibroblasts are activated and differentiate into contractile myofibroblasts. These fibroblasts produce high levels of extracellular matrix and then form a collagen-rich scar to repair the cardiac defects caused by cardiomyocyte death, maintaining the basic function of heart [37]. However, excessive proliferation of fibroblasts may lead to an overaccumulation of extracellular matrix, resulting in reduced cardiac elasticity and compliance of the ventricular wall, which can severely impair cardiac function and ultimately cause heart failure [38]. Our study showed that the sepsis-specific fibroblasts subtypes FB3 may directly promote cardiac contractile dysfunction via calcium singalling, actin cytoskeleton and serval inflammation-related pathways in cardiomyocytes. In addtion, FB3 promoted Rel+ Mac metabolic reprogramming by activating complement system, while Rel+ Mac in turn facilitates the activation of fibroblasts via Tnf and Vcam1 singalling, leading myocardial dysfunction. These results were correlative observations requiring further validation.

Our study systematically characterized the roles and mechanisms of cardiomyocytes and other non-myocytes in the whole myocardial microenvironment during sepsis-induced cardiac dysfunction at the single-cell level, and provided several potential therapeutic targets. Given that Rel+ Mac was key contributors within a broader network (mitochondrial dysfunction, Ca²⁺ mishandling, oxidative stress) and its metabolic reprogramming play a centre role in myocardial dysfunction caused by sepsis, we conducted and confirmed that PKM2 siRNA-loaded targeting nanomaterials targeting Rel+ Mac metabolic reprogramming could protect against septic myocardial dysfunction and improve survival. Unlike previous broadly anti-inflammatory therapies or only targeted cardiomyocyte energy regulation approaches, our strategy addresses the complexity of sepsis by targeting the different cellular components and their communication in the cardiac niche for a comprehensive and integrated antagonism of sepsis-induced cardiac dysfunction. While this macrophage-selective delivery system is designed to enhance therapeutic precision, the off-target effects of PKM2 inhibition remain a critical safety concern. These include metabolic disruption in non-target cells and unintended immunomodulatory consequences. Future efforts should focus on mitigating these risks through multi-level targeting strategies or additional modifications of nanomaterials to facilitate clinical translation.

There are several limitations in the present study should be noted. First, while we focused on validating functions associated with Rel+ macrophages, the observations of endothelial and smooth muscle cell dysfunction or fibroblast remodeling remain largely correlative. Future validation of the key genes and pathways we identified is warranted by using gene knockout, overexpression, or inhibitor interventions in vivo or in vitro. Second, sepsis is a complex and dynamic pathological process. CLP mouse model used in the present study may not fully mirror the complexity and heterogeneity of human sepsis, more appropriate models need to be developed to investigate myocardial microenvironmental changes and their underlying mechanisms in sepsis-induced cardiac dysfunction in future. Furthermore, the translational potential of the proposed therapeutic strategy requires further evaluation.

Conclusions

In summary, our study revealed that the disturbance of cardiac niche associated with Rel+ Mac and the cell-to-cell dynamic communication is an important cause for sepsis-induced cardiac dysfunction. These results may provide targets and strategies for the treatment of sepsis-induced myocardial injury in a multifaceted and integrated manner.

Association between preoperative inflammatory biomarkers and postoperative morbidity and mortality in stage I–III colorectal cancer patients: a single-centre prospective study
Authors: Meriem Fella Guerni, Lilya Khelifi, Hakim Chatter, Meriem Mokhbi, Fetoma El Metennani, Kaouther Boughezala, Manel Amri, Imene Soufli, Houda Belguendouz, Rania Sihem Boussa, Chafia Touil-boukoffa, Batoul Latreche, Nassim Sid Idris
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate whether preoperative inflammatory biomarkers have prognostic value in terms of postoperative surgical morbidity in stage I–III resectable colorectal cancer.DesignProspective observational cohort study.SettingThis is a single centre study conducted at the general surgery department of Djilali Belkhenchir, formerly Birtraria, Hospital, in Algeria.ParticipantsWe enrolled patients with non-metastatic colorectal cancer who underwent initial curative resection from May 2022 to May 2025.Main outcome measurePreoperative inflammatory biomarkers such as neutrophil to lymphocyte ratio, platelets to lymphocyte ratio, monocyte to lymphocyte ratio and lymphocyte×albumin (LA) were calculated based on blood samples taken 30 days prior to surgery. The primary outcome was postoperative morbidity and the secondary outcome was postoperative mortality.Results230 patients met the inclusion criteria and had curative resection. Multivariable regression analysis showed that tumour size (OR 1.264, 95% CI 1.098 to 1.455, p<0.001), LA (OR 0.501, 95% CI 0.284 to 0.881, p=0.016) and rectal location (OR 2.569, 95% CI 1.138 to 5.798, p=0.023) were independent factors associated with postoperative complications.ConclusionThe LA score, tumour size and tumour location were associated with post-operative morbidity in patients with stage I–III resectable colorectal cancer.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a leading public health concern with a significant social and economic burden due to its high incidence and mortality rates.1 It is the third most common cancer worldwide and the second leading cause of cancer-related death in both men and women.2 In the Algerian population, available data report an incidence of 30.9 per 100 000 inhabitants in Algiers, making colorectal cancer the second most common malignancy in the country.3 However, early screening and the development of novel treatment strategies have improved overall survival across all disease stages.4 Nevertheless, recurrence rates remain substantial, ranging from 20% to 30% despite optimal treatment in patients with stage I–III disease.5 The cornerstone of curative treatment for colorectal cancer is surgical resection.6 Although it allows complete removal of the tumour and offers better overall survival rates ranging from 50% to 90%, colorectal surgery is still a major cause of postoperative morbidity and mortality, reaching up to 50% according to the literature.7 This postoperative morbidity continues to be a major concern as it delays patient recovery and the initiation of adjuvant treatments, thereby negatively affecting the oncological prognosis.8 9

Several risk factors have been associated with the occurrence of postoperative complications. Some are related to the tumour itself, including Tumour-Node-Mestastasis (TNM) stage and tumour location, while others are related to the patient’s condition, such as advanced age and the presence of comorbidities.10 Numerous studies have attempted to develop novel tools and scores to predict postoperative morbidity and mortality based on inflammatory biomarkers, such as C reactive protein (CRP) and procalcitonin.11 12 However, most of these studies rely on the assessment of a single biomarker, potentially limiting their sensitivity and specificity. An approach combining several indicators of systemic inflammation could improve the predictive performance of these biomarkers. Several studies have identified certain inflammatory markers, such as neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) and, more recently, the lymphocyte×albumin (LA) score, as prognostic factors for overall survival and recurrence-free survival in patients with colorectal cancer.1315 However, their involvement in the occurrence of postoperative complications remains insufficiently explored. The aim of this study was to evaluate the prognostic value of preoperative markers of systemic inflammation and their association with postoperative morbidity and mortality in patients with stage I–III colorectal cancer undergoing curative surgery.

The aim is to evaluate the prognostic value of preoperative markers of systemic inflammation, NLR, PLR, lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR) and LA score, and their association with postoperative morbidity and mortality in patients with stage I–III colorectal cancer undergoing curative surgery.

Methods

We analysed data from patients who underwent surgery for stage I–III colorectal cancer with curative intent at our general surgery department of Djilali Belkhenchir Hospital (formerly Birtraria Hospital) between May 2022 and May 2025. These data were prospectively collected from consecutive patients operated at our institution after acquiring informed written consent. The different variables were recorded in a standardised electronic database and regularly updated. In addition, patients’ records were registered in a national Algerian digital health platform for medical records.

Our inclusion criteria were patients aged 18–90 years with stage I–III colorectal cancer confirmed by pathological examination who underwent curative R0 resection, whereas our exclusion criteria were non-adenocarcinoma colorectal tumours, metastatic disease, R1 or R2 resection, emergency surgery, ongoing infection, use of steroids or the presence of a haematological condition.

A blood sample was collected within 30 days prior to surgery in order to obtain preoperative biological parameters, including the blood count and serum albumin (g/L), used to calculate several inflammatory ratios used in the analysis, namely the NLR, PLR, LMR and the LA score.

Various demographic, clinical, biological and pathological variables were collected and analysed, including age, gender, the American Society of Anesthesiologists’ (ASA) physical status score, tumour location, tumour stage based on the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) 8th edition TNM staging, tumour size, preoperative carcinoembryonic antigen level and serum albumin levels.

Intraoperative and postoperative data were also collected, including the type of anastomosis (stapled or hand-sewn) and length of hospital stay.

Postoperative complications were defined as any medical or surgical event occurring during hospitalisation or within 30 days of surgery. They were graded according to the Clavien-Dindo classification.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as mean±SD when normally distributed and as median with IQR when the distribution was non-normal based on the Shapiro-Wilk test for normality, whereas categorical variables were expressed as absolute numbers (n) and percentages (%).

Comparisons between groups (complications vs no postoperative complications) were performed using Student’s t-test for normally distributed continuous variables or the Mann-Whitney U test for non-normally distributed variables. For categorical variables, the χ² test was used.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted to evaluate the discriminatory ability of preoperative inflammatory ratios that showed significant differences. The area under the curve (AUC) was calculated for each ratio. The optimal threshold for each marker was determined using Youden’s criteria.

Univariate and multivariate logistic regression was performed to identify factors independently associated with the occurrence of postoperative complications. Variables with a p value <0.05 in univariate analysis were included in the multivariate model. The results were expressed as ORs with their 95% CI. A p value <0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS software (V.27.0) while Forest plot graphs were generated using GraphPad Prism (V.10.0).

Patient and public involvement

There was no patient or public involvement in the design, conduct, interpretation or dissemination of this study. The study was based on routinely collected clinical data from a surgical cohort.

Results

Between May 2022 and April 2025, a total of 320 patients underwent surgery for colorectal neoplasia in the general surgery department of Djilali Belkhenchir Hospital. 230 patients met the inclusion criteria, and the selection process is detailed in figure 1. Among these patients, 67 (29.1%) experienced postoperative medical or surgical complications. The overall surgical complication rate was 26.52%, of which 7.5% were classified as Clavien-Dindo III–IV, while the postoperative mortality was 1.3%.

Factors influencing post-operative morbidity in univariate and multivariate analysis

In univariate analysis, it has been shown that PLR (OR 1.004, 95% CI 1.001 to 1.006, p=0.005), LA (OR 0.437, 95% CI 0.269 to 0.710, p<0.001), tumour size (OR 1.266, 95% CI 1.119 to 1.432, p<0.001), rectal location (OR 2.34, 95% CI 1.119 to 4.885, p=0.024) and an ASA III (OR 0.378, 95% CI 0.135 to 0.930, p<0.034) were significantly associated with postoperative morbidity. However, multivariate analysis identified independent factors for postoperative morbi-mortality, namely tumour size, which significantly increased morbidity (OR 1.264, 95% CI 1.098 to 1.455, p<0.001), and rectal location (OR 2.569, 95% CI 1.138 to 5.798, p=0.023). Furthermore, a low LA rate was significantly associated with an increased postoperative morbidity (OR 0.501, 95% CI 0.284 to 0.881, p=0.016). These results are detailed in table 2 and figure 2.

Discriminative ability of variables associated with postoperative morbidity in patients with CRC and cut-off values

In this population of patients with colorectal cancer, a low LA score was found to be significantly associated with increased postoperative morbidity. In order to better assess its prognostic value, an analysis of the discriminatory ability of the LA variable was performed using ROC curve analysis. The results showed that LA has moderate discriminatory performance with AUC=0.703 (95% CI 0.628 to 0.778) and a p value <0.001. The best threshold determined was 70 030 with a sensitivity of 71.6% and a specificity of 58.9% (figure 3).

Similarly, the ROC analysis for PLR showed an AUC of 0.616 (95% CI 0.532 to 0.700) and a p value of 0.007. The best cut-off value determined for PLR is 170.9, with a sensitivity of 67.2% and a specificity of 50.3% (figure 3).

Discussion

Despite advances in colorectal cancer surgery and optimisation of perioperative care through early screening, immunonutrition, pain management, minimally invasive approaches and the use of stapled anastomoses and indocyanine green fluorescence to assess tissue perfusion, overall and specific morbidity, particularly anastomotic leakage, remains a challenge and a major concern due to its impact on patients’ survival, functional outcomes and oncological prognosis.1621

In our cohort, the overall morbidity rate was 29.1%, with surgical complications accounting for 26.52%. Major complications (Clavien-Dindo III–IV) occurred in 7.5% of patients, with a mortality rate of 1.3%. These findings are consistent with those observed in the literature. Indeed, morbidity after curative surgery for stage I–III CRC has been reported to range from 20% to 30% with major complications occurring in 5%–15% of patients, depending on the series.22 23 Mortality varies between 1% and 7%, depending on the expertise of the centre.24

Currently, systemic inflammation markers have attracted increasing attention and are becoming a major topic of research. Using simple, available, inexpensive and reproducible blood-based tests, researchers aim to develop predictive tools for the prognosis of colorectal cancer patients. Among these markers, NLR, PLR, LMR and, more recently, LA have shown promising results in the literature.15 25 26

The LA score was first investigated as a novel biomarker by Yamamoto et al who demonstrated that LA is significantly associated with overall survival and recurrence-free survival (p=0.007 and 0.015, respectively).15

Through this study, we attempted to assess the prognostic value of NLR, PLR, LMR and LA in terms of postoperative morbidity and mortality, with results showing that LA appeared to be independently associated with post-operative complications following curative surgery for colorectal cancer (OR 0.501 95% CI 0.284 to 0.881, p=0.016). Furthermore, although data in the literature remain limited regarding an optimal threshold for the LA score, we were able to suggest a cut-off value of 70 030 in our population, with a sensitivity of 71.6% and a specificity of 58.9%.

Several hypotheses have been proposed to explain the mechanism of the LA association, which is still not fully understood. Wang et al demonstrated that lymphocytes constantly infiltrate tumour sites, thereby participating in immune surveillance and immune escape mechanisms, and therefore lymphopaenia could reflect a weakened anti-tumour response.27 Similarly, albumin, a negative acute-phase protein, reflects both the inflammatory and nutritional status of the host, and therefore hypoalbuminaemia may be associated with impaired healing and an increased risk of postoperative complications.28

Thus, this LA score reflects both inflammatory and nutritional status, and although our study is observational in nature, our findings support the hypothesis that nutritional status may play a key role in post-operative recovery.29

These findings align with current Enhanced Recovery After Surgery recommendations emphasising the optimisation of preoperative nutritional status in patients undergoing colorectal surgery, particularly through the identification and management of patients with hypoalbuminaemia or nutritional impairment.30

Interestingly, hypoalbuminaemia alone was not independently associated with postoperative morbidity in our cohort. However, when combined with the lymphocyte count within the LA score, the biomarker demonstrated a meaningful discriminative ability suggesting that this composite score might better capture the link between nutritional and inflammatory status rather than albumin alone.

Furthermore, our study identified rectal location as being associated with a higher incidence of post-operative morbidity. This subgroup of patients may therefore benefit from more comprehensive inflammatory and nutritional assessment and optimisation strategies.31

Although the predictive value of NLR and PLR in the occurrence of postoperative complications has been well studied, notably by Josse et al,32 who identified an NLR cut-off value ≥2.3 as being significantly associated with major postoperative complications, our study did not find similar results. On the other hand, as demonstrated by Nuo Xu et al, PLR was associated with post-operative morbidity33 with a proposed threshold value of 133.24 (sensitivity 67%, specificity 51%). In fact, in the present analysis, the PLR proved to be associated with postoperative morbidity in univariate analysis only, while the ROC analysis helped us identify a threshold value of 170.9 (sensitivity=67.2%, specificity=50.3%).

In our patient population, tumour size was independently associated with a higher risk of postoperative complications (OR 1.264, 95% CI 1.098 to 1.4551, p<0.001). This is supported by data in the literature, and according to a meta-analysis published in 2018, a tumour size ≥5 cm was associated with high morbidity, particularly a higher risk of anastomotic leakage.34

Given the single-centre nature of our study and the lack of external validation, our results regarding the prognostic value of the LA score in terms of postoperative morbidity should be interpreted with caution and considered exploratory rather than clinically applicable for risk stratification. It cannot be generalised without confirmation by multicentre studies and external validation.

Conclusion

In summary, our study demonstrated that the LA score may be associated with postoperative morbidity and mortality within stage I–III colorectal cancer patients who are operated in a curative elective setting. In fact, this cost-effective, simple marker obtained by a blood test, together with tumour size and tumour location, may help contribute to a better understanding of factors associated with postoperative outcomes in this population.

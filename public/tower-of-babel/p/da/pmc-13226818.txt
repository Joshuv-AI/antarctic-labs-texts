A recurrent hepatocellular carcinoma with incomplete response to conventional treatments, showing substantial regression and good quality of life by Ayurveda treatment: A case report
Authors: Ranjit G Nimbalkar, Gayatri V Watpade, Anil T Pawar, Amol A Tagalpallewar, Akshay M Baheti, Manasi R Nimbalkar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHepatocellular carcinoma is a major global health concern with limited treatment options and high recurrence rates. This case study presents a 68-year-old male diagnosed with hepatocellular carcinoma who refused liver transplantation and underwent multiple conventional treatments, including Drug-eluting Bead trans-arterial chemoembolization (DEB TACE), targeted therapy, and radiation therapy. However, due to adverse effects and suspected disease progression, the patient opted to switch to Ayurvedic management. ,It incorporated Ayurvedic formulations, like suvarnamalinivasanta, phalatrikadiguggulu, patola, bhumyamalaki, and bhringaraja to support liver function, enhance immune response, and mitigate tumor progression.Over the course of treatment, the patient demonstrated improvements in clinical and biochemical parameters, including a reductions in alpha-fetoprotein and bilirubin levels, clinically improved liver function, enhanced physical strength, and better quality of life. Radiological assessments indicated disease stabilization with no significant metabolic activity. The case highlights the potential of an integrative approach to improve patient outcomes, particularly in those who decline or are unfit for liver transplantation.Keywords: Ayurveda, Bhumyamalaki, Hepatocellular carcinoma, Integrative medicine, Liver disease management, Suvarnamalini vasant

Introduction

Liver cancer is a significant global health issue, with more than 8,50,000 new cases reported annually [1]. Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) accounts for 75–85% of primary liver cancer cases, while intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma accounts for 10–15% of cases, along with other rarer forms [2,3]. Several well-established risk factors contribute to the development of HCC, including cirrhosis (chronic liver damage caused by fibrosis), hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection, hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection, alcohol abuse, and metabolic syndrome. [[4], [5], [6]]. The incidence of HCC varies significantly across countries, primarily due to differences in the prevalence of risk factors. Regardless of the underlying etiology, these conditions often lead to liver fibrosis and cirrhosis, which eventually progress to carcinoma. Liver cancer is the sixth most commonly diagnosed cancer and the third leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide, with approximately 8,65,269 new cases and 7,57,948 deaths annually [7]. Liver cancer occurs most frequently in males aged 50 to 69 years, while in females, the highest incidence is observed after the age of 70 years [8]. Men are generally more affected than women, contributing to a lower overall survival rate worldwide.

The treatment and management of HCC remain challenging due to high tumor recurrence rates and treatment resistance. Early-stage HCC is typically managed with liver resection and other surgical interventions. Treatment options for end-stage liver cancer are limited, necessitating costly liver transplantation, which is not readily available in many countries [9]. In advanced stages, treatment options include chemotherapy and immunotherapy, either as standalone therapies or in combination to enhance efficacy and overcome resistance. However, despite these available treatments, persistently high mortality rate suggests that current therapeutic strategies are not achieving optimal outcomes [10]. Additionally, many patients experience severe side effects of chemotherapy, limiting their ability to tolerate treatment.

Given the challenges associated with conventional treatments, Ayurveda presents a valuable complementary approach in liver diseases. For centuries, Ayurvedic formulations derived from certain medicinal plants and minerals have been used to support liver health and may contribute in reducing tumor burden, enhancing liver function, and improving overall well-being of the patients. These Ayrvedic formulations often contain multiple active compounds that may work synergistically, enhancing therapeutic efficacy while minimizing the risk of adverse effects [11]. Several herbs are known for their hepatoprotective, anti-inflammatory, and anticancer properties [9]. Additionally, classical Ayurvedic preparations have long been utilized in the treatment of liver disorders [12] and may aid in liver regeneration while also helping to alleviate the adverse effects of chemotherapy. This case study presents a case of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), focusing on its clinical presentation, diagnosis, and the potential role of Ayurvedic management as a complementary approach to conventional treatment.

Case presentation

Patient information

A 68-year-old male presented on February 20, 2024, seeking Ayurvedic management for his ongoing hepatocellular carcinoma. His medical history revealed that he had experienced severe abdominal pain in November 2021, and subsequently diagnosed with chronic liver parenchymal disease with HCC. He underwent Drug-Eluting Bead Transarterial Chemoembolization (DEB-TACE) in December 2021, January 2022 and July 2023. He had history of liver parenchymal disease with cholangitis and subsequent hospitalized for complaints of abdominal pain, fever, and Jaundice repeatedly. He had undergone Percutaneous Transhepatic Biliary Drainage (PTBD) multiple times, last on January 22, 2024 (Table 1).

Time line.

He was advised to undergo a liver transplant but he declined the procedure. After second DEB-TACE in January 2022, he was treated with targeted therapy, tablet Lenvatinib 4 mg, from February 22, 2022 to December 26, 2023. The patient experienced significant side effects because of it and it had to be stopped. During this course, his last DEB TACE was done in July 2023, PTBD on July 8, 2023 and in August 2023. He underwent five sessions of radiation therapy from August 19, 2023 to August 28, 2023, 37 Gy in 5 Fractions. He was maintained on Rifagut and Udiliv afterwards.

The patient's history included heavy smoking for 30 years, with a consumption of two packs per day, though he had quit smoking 16 years ago. He also used to consume alcohol once in a fortnight for 10-15 years and discontinued from last 10 years.

Past medical history

The patient is a known case of hypertension for the past 10 years on regular medication, tablet Concor 5 mg and tablet Telma 40 mg, OD each. He is also a known case of hypothyroidism and on Thyroxine 50 mcg for last 10 years. He is obese, and at the time of diagnosis, his weight was 108 kg, which came down to 94 kg in 2022, and currently again it is 102 kg. There was no history of trauma or serious illnesses and no known sensitivities to medications, foods, or environmental factors. No medical history was present, including no family history of inherited or genetic problems or conditions like hypertension, diabetes, or cardiovascular disease.

Diagnostic assessment

The patient's diagnosis of HCC was established through a series of imaging studies and laboratory investigations. An abdominal ultrasound, followed by a contrast-enhanced MRI, identified a hepatic mass, helped for the probable diagnosis of the disease as carcinoma. Liver function tests indicated impaired liver function, with elevated bilirubin, alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), and alkaline phosphatase (ALP) levels. A tumor marker analysis showed a significantly high alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) level, a strong indicator of HCC. To confirm the diagnosis, a liver biopsy was needed. But in view of the nature and location of the lesion, it could not be undertaken. So diagnosis was made as per the results of imaging, tumor markers and bio-chemistry. Endoscopic evaluations detected bile duct obstruction caused by tumor compression, making stenting necessary. This comprehensive assessment provided a clear understanding of the patient's condition and allowed for an informed approach to treatment.

Clinical findings

When the patient arrived at the Ayurveda OPD a detailed examination revealed the following findings.

Dashavidha pariksha (Tenfold examination)

The patient's prakriti (physical attributes) was kapha pitta (predominant kapha and pitta), vaya (age) was vruddha (old age). His mamsa was saara (good strength of mamsa or striated muscle tissue elements). Pramaana (measurement of body constituents) was sthoola (big built and overweight, 102 kg weight). Saatmya (habituation) was towards non-vegetarian food and spicy diet as he used to consume around thrice in a week. Samhanana (tissue or organ compactness) was uttama (good) and sattva (mental strength) was madhyama (moderate). The patient's aharashakti (capacity of food intake) was uttama (good) and vyayama shakti (power of exercising) was moderate.

Systemic examination

The patient was conscious, alert, and oriented. He did not exhibit icterus, itching, ascites, cyanosis, clubbing, or pallor. His blood pressure was normal at 130/80 mm Hg, and CNS examination revealed no abnormalities. The tongue was moist and clear (nirama), and his bladder and bowel functions were normal, with occasional flatulence and intermittent constipation. Sleep was also fairly good but sometimes non-satisfactory.

The respiratory and cardiovascular functions were within normal limits, with no abnormalities noted on examination. Overall, the systemic examination is unremarkable, except for the limited physical activity due to his condition. Regular monitoring of his liver function and overall health was recommended.

Primary concerns and symptoms of the patient

The primary concerns of the patient were chronic liver disease, residual HCC after all the conventional treatments, elevated AFP levels, deranged bilirubin values (ALT, AST and proteins level were fairly within the normal range), with symptoms including occasional flatulence (5/10), intermittent constipation (6/10), continuous discomfort (6/10), reduced appetite (3/10), sleep (4/10) and strength (5/10), Karnofsky Performance Score at this time was 50/100.

Therapeutic intervention

After the conventional treatments, the patient was treated with an Ayurvedic regimen starting February 20, 2024, which included suvarna malini vasanta, phalatrikadi guggulu, patola, bhumyamalaki, and bhringaraja to support liver function, improve digestion, detoxify the body, and reduce inflammation (Table 2). Given his obesity (108 kg at diagnosis), and currently 102 kg, a liver-supporting diet, low in fatty, spicy, and non-vegetarian foods, was recommended for weight management and digestive health, with nutritional counselling to avoid strain on the liver.

With limited physical activity, a rehabilitation plan incorporating light exercises such as walking and stretching was introduced to improve mobility and prevent muscle atrophy. Psychosocial support, including counselling and stress-relief techniques, was advised to address emotional challenges related to his diagnosis.

Regular monitoring of liver function tests, tumor markers and imaging studies to track HCC progression, and attention to potential complications like ascites, jaundice and bleeding were provided. This comprehensive treatment strategy, integrating conventional and Ayurvedic therapies, along with dietary, physical, and emotional support, aimed to improve the patient's quality of life, manage symptoms, and slow down the disease progression.

Follow-up and outcome

The patient experienced significant improvements after resuming Ayurvedic therapy, both clinical as well as pathological. Previously patient had discontinued tablet Lenvatinib because of severe side effects, and his AFP was raised to 10.3 in Feb 2024 from 6.36 in Dec 2023. The oncologist suggested to check it again after one month, and if it remains high, then had advocated the need of restarting tablet Lenvatinib but Ayurved treatment was immediately started and the AFP value on 31 March came down to 7.95, so tablet Lenvatinib was kept on hold, and now AFP has always remained below 5.5 for last many months. So, tablet Lenvatinib was never started. Total bilirubin also came down to 1.2 mg/ from 2 mg/ in Feb 2024, and reduced further to 1.1 mg in May 2025. AST, ALT and Alkaline phosphatase were fairly in range.

Follow-up ultrasonography (USG) and PET scans also showed gradual reduction in the disease activity, when only Ayurvedic treatment was on-going (Table 3). The patient's mobility and quality of life gradually improved from 50/100 to 90/100 on Karnofsky Performance Score. On clinical grounds, it was observed that flatulence improved (2/10), constipation almost vanished (1/10), discomfort decreased (1/10), appetite improved (8/10), sleep became sound (9/10) and strength also increased (7/10). Overall, the combined approach of Ayurvedic interventions and conventional treatment helped to improve the liver health and overall quality of life of the patient (Table 4).

Discussion

This case highlights the integration of Ayurvedic therapy in the management of HCC. The patient's disease was highly advanced and he also had a chronic liver parenchymal disease. So he was advised to undergo a liver transplantation, which could not be performed because of several reasons, like unwillingness, fear of complications and difficulty in getting a donor. Consequently, he underwent conventional interventions such as biliary stenting, DEB TACE, targeted therapy, and radiation therapy, which helped control the progression of the disease, but could not completely reverse it. Additionally, the liver parameters were deranged and the tumor markers began to rise after stopping the T. Livantinib because of poor tolerance. Therefore, he sought Ayurvedic treatment to manage the progressive disease, enhance liver function and overall well-being. The prognosis of such a condition is generally poor. and the expected survival is not more than 6 months. The prognosis depends on multiple factors. (i) Higher age is associated with poorer prognosis. (ii) DEB TACE initially shows a good response, but its efficacy markedly declines on repeated usage. (iii) The tumors not responding much to radiation therapy and targeted therapies are usually associated with poorer outcomes. (iv) In addition, deranged liver function tests add to the severity of the disease. All these factors showing poor prognosis were present in the patient, and the expected survival was around 6 months only [13]. But the addition of Ayurvedic therapy helped the patient maintain a good quality of life for more than 18 months (Karnofsky Performance Score: 90/100). The AFP values which had started creeping after discontinuing Lenvatinib 4mg, gradually returned back to the acceptable range. Also derangements in LFT returned to normalcy. Patient improved symptomatically as well and enjoyed a near normal life. So it can be comfortably said that Ayurved has helped this patient at least to some extent, to extend the survival and improving the quality of life, which might not have been possible otherwise.

Herbs such as bhumyamalaki and bhringaraja are well-documented for their hepatoprotective properties and potential benefits in liver regeneration. The role of Rasayana formulations such as suvarna malini vasanta in enhancing immunity and resilience against malignancy is also noteworthy.

Phyllanthus niruri L., traditionally used in Ayurveda for the management of liver and kidney ailments, has been reported to have significant hepatoprotective effects. Bioassay-guided studies have demonstrated that the aqueous and ethanolic extracts of Phyllanthus niruri protected clone-9 and HepG2 cells from CCl4-induced hepatotoxicity by reducing ALT and AST levels while restoring antioxidant activity (GSH, SOD). Fractionation identified key bioactive compounds, including corilagin, isocorilagin, brevifolin, quercetin, kaempferol rhamnoside, gallic acid, and brevifolin carboxylic acid, with corilagin and isocorilagin showing the highest potency. The extract exhibited strong antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, normalizing liver enzymes, lipid profile, bilirubin, and inflammatory markers (TNF-α, NF-κB, IL-6, IL-8, IL-10, COX-2). Histopathological analysis further confirmed its hepatoprotective effects, attributing its efficacy to its phenolic constituents and antioxidant potential [14]. The bioactive compounds from the root of Phyllanthus niruri, when tested through molecular docking against various disease-related proteins targeting antiviral, antibacterial, antifungal, and anticancer activities, demonstrated promising therapeutic potential across multiple disease categories [15].

Eclipta alba Hassk. or bhringaraja, has been used in Ayurvedic medicine for over 1000 years to treat liver disorders such as infective hepatitis and liver cirrhosis. The extract of Eclipta alba was found to effectively inhibit HCV RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp) activity and reduce HCV replication in cell cultures. Through bioassay-guided fractionation, three compounds, wedelolactone, luteolin, and apigenin were identified, each demonstrating dose-dependent inhibition of HCV replicase and replication [16]. Bioactive compounds from Eclipta alba were assessed for their activity against the hepatitis C virus (HCV) E2 glycoprotein using in silico methods. Virtual screening of 12 compounds identified ecliptalbine and oleanolic acid, with binding energies of −8.88 and −8.02 kcal/mol, respectively. Both compounds exhibited favorable pharmacokinetic properties and drug-likeness. Molecular dynamics simulations confirmed their stability and strong binding affinity, highlighting oleanolic acid as a potential inhibitor against HCV. These results support the therapeutic potential of Eclipta alba in HCV treatment [17].

Patolakaturohinyadi kashayam, a classical Ayurvedic formulation has been reported by many practitioners to be effective in treatment of liver disorders. Patolakaturohinyadi kashayam is a combination of six herbal ingredients viz., Patola (Trichosanthes dioica), katurohini (Picrorhiza kurroa), chandana (Santalum album), murva (Marsdenia tenacissima), guduchi (Tinospora cordifolia), patha (Cissampelos pareira) [18].

Patola (Trichosanthes dioica Roxb)., commonly known as bitter gourd. The juice of its leaves is traditionally used as a tonic, febrifuge, and for the treatment of edema, alopecia, and liver enlargement. In Charaka Samhita, the leaves and fruits are mentioned for their effectiveness in treating alcoholism and jaundice. In Ayurveda, the leaves are used for their antipyretic, diuretic, cardiotonic, laxative, and antiulcer properties [19]. The fruit of T. dioica (bitter gourd) possesses remarkable value in ameliorating arsenic-induced hepatic and renal toxicity, primarily by alleviating arsenic-induced oxidative stress through multiple mechanisms [20]. Katurohini has been traditionally used in the management of jaundice, nausea, anorexia, dyspepsia, and periodic fevers. It is a proven hepatoprotective agent in both experimental and clinical studies, showing hydrocholeretic effects in rats and dogs. Its extract exhibits strong antioxidant activity and significantly inhibits lipid peroxidation. Picroliv, its active constituent, has been evaluated for hepatoprotection against ethanol-induced liver injury in rats [[21], [22], [23]].

Chandana (Santalum album) contains α-santalol, a bioactive compound with potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, which are key mechanisms in hepatoprotection. By reducing oxidative stress and inhibiting inflammatory pathways, α-santalol may help protect hepatocytes from toxin-induced damage. These properties suggest a potential role of Santalum album in mitigating liver injury and supporting hepatic function. Additionally, its favorable safety profile highlights its promise as a natural therapeutic agent for liver-related disorders [24].

Various Ayurvedic preparations of guduchi are traditionally used for pandu (anemia) and kamala (jaundice). Clinical studies have shown that guduchi helps normalize altered liver functions, including ALT and AST levels. Its anti-hepatotoxic activity has been demonstrated in CCl4-induced liver damage, restoring liver function as evidenced by morphological and biochemical parameters such as SGPT, SGOT, serum alkaline phosphatase, and serum bilirubin levels [25,26].

Phalatrikadi guggulu is a formulation consisting of haritaki (Terminalia chebula), bibhitaki (Terminalia bellirica), amalaki (Phyllanthus emblica), guduchi (Tinospora cordifolia), kirattikta (Swertia chirata), nimba (Azadirachta indica), katuki (Picrorhiza kurroa), vasapatra (Adhatoda vasica), and guggulu (Commiphora wightii). The main ingredient is katuki [27]. P. kurroa has been shown to reverse several conditions, including the lipid content of liver tissue, morphological changes associated with fatty infiltration, hypolipidemic activity, and the reduction of cholestasis. Katuki demonstrates hepatoprotective, anti-viral, and antioxidant activities [28,29].

Suvarna malini vasanta is a traditional Ayurvedic formulation composed of suvarna bhasma (calcined gold), jasada bhasma (calcined zinc), maricha (Piper nigrum), and mukta pishti (fine powder of pearl), prepared through trituration with navaneeta (butter) and nimbu rasa (lemon juice). Suvarna Bhasma is recognized for its Vishghana (detoxifying), Rasayana (rejuvenating), and Ojavardhaka (immunity-boosting) properties, offering protection to healthy tissues and proving particularly beneficial for individuals with a history of psychological trauma (Hetu Pratyaneeka). Additionally, Jasada Bhasma, a zinc-based preparation, plays a crucial role in maintaining metabolic balance (Dhatu-Sneha Parampara). Zinc functions as a cofactor in the synthesis of DNA, RNA, and proteins, regulating gene expression through transcription factors. It also contributes to bone and glucose metabolism, immune response, apoptosis, reproduction, vision, protein digestion, blood clotting, and overall growth and development. Maricha (Piper nigrum) possesses Sookshma (micro-penetrating) and Teekshna (sharp) properties, which are enhanced through Bhavana (levigation) with butter and lemon juice. This formulation aids in cleansing the internal Srotasas (bodily channels), detoxifying the system, and nourishing tissues through specific metabolic pathways [30].

Conclusion

This case illustrates the potential role of Ayurveda as an adjunctive therapy in managing HCC, particularly in patients who seek alternatives or complementary approaches to conventional treatments. Further clinical research and long-term follow-up studies are necessary to assess the effectiveness of Ayurvedic interventions in improving quality of life and disease outcomes in patients with HCC.

Patient's perspective on treatment received

After following the Ayurvedic treatment, the patient reported a noticeable improvement in overall health and well-being. Symptoms like fatigue, digestive issues, and liver-related discomfort gradually reduced, making him feel more energetic and at ease. Initially, he was unsure about how well Ayurvedic treatment would work for his condition, but over time, he observed the benefits without experiencing any major side effects. In addition to physical improvement, the patient also reported better mental well-being and emotional stability during the course of treatment. Overall, the therapy contributed to an improved sense of health and quality of life. This perspective was recorded on 15 November 2025.

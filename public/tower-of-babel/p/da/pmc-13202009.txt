Accordingly, the guiding research question was, ‘What is the impact of patient-specific 3D heart models on operative planning, intraoperative strategy, and clinical outcomes in congenital heart disease surgery?’

Eligibility criteria

Studies were eligible if they met the following criteria. Randomised controlled trials (RCTs), prospective or retrospective cohort studies, case-control studies and comparative case series evaluating 3D cardiac modelling in CHD surgical planning were included. Eligible studies had to compare patient-specific 3D models with conventional imaging methods and report at least one clinical or procedural outcome.

Studies were excluded if they were purely technical reports describing modelling methodology without clinical outcomes, simulation-only or animal studies, conference abstracts without full data, narrative reviews, editorials or duplicate reports.

For consistency across studies, surgical plan modification was defined as any documented change in operative approach or strategy that occurred after review of the 3D model. Importantly, such modification was interpreted as evidence of enhanced anatomical understanding or reconsideration of operative strategy rather than automatic evidence of clinical improvement.

An exhaustive search was done in PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Cochrane CENTRAL and IEEE Xplore databases. Explorations protected evidence mapping or published from January 2000 through 30 May 2025. Study selection and data extraction

Total retrieved records were imported into a reference management database and duplicates were removed. Two independent reviewers screened titles and abstracts for applicability, followed by full-text evaluation of possibly qualified research. Divergences were determined by dialogue and accord.

Data extraction was performed using a standardised form capturing study characteristics, participant demographics, type of congenital lesion, intervention characteristics (printed vs virtual models), comparator imaging modality and reported clinical outcomes.

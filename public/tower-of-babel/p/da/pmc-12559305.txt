Study on the impact of incentive mechanisms on the work efficiency of tower crane drivers based on EEG
Authors: Fuwang Wang, Anni Luo, Daping Chen, Xiaolei Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe tower crane operators play a crucial role in construction operations, and their work efficiency directly affects the success of the construction projects. However, the heavy and repetitive nature of their tasks often leads to operator fatigue, which in turn compromises both performance and safety. This study investigates the impact of incentive mechanisms on the work efficiency of tower crane operators by analyzing the electroencephalogram (EEG) signals of the operators collected. The multifractal detrended fluctuation analysis (MF-DFA) method is employed to examine the Hurst index range and multifractal spectrum width of the EEG signals under two incentive modes: a reward–punishment incentive mode and a no reward–punishment mode. The research results show that there are significant differences between the two incentive modes (p < 0.05). The Hurst index range and multifractal spectrum width of the EEG signals under the reward-punishment mode are greater than those under the no reward-punishment mode. Furthermore, during different working stages, the lifting efficiency of prefabricated components is consistently higher in the reward–punishment incentive mode. This indicates that drivers under the incentive mode have less mental fatigue and higher work efficiency. Additionally, in the incentive mode, the lifting efficiency of prefabricated components is significantly higher, demonstrating the practical benefits of such incentives in improving work performance. From this study, it can be concluded that the reward-punishment incentive mechanism effectively improves the work efficiency of tower crane operators by positively influencing their cognitive state. Based on the EEG signals, the impact of incentive mechanisms on human performance can be better understood, and valuable insights can be provided for optimizing the safety and operational efficiency of construction sites.Keywords: Tower crane operators, Work efficiency, Reward and punishment incentive mode, MF-DFA, EEG signals, Hurst index, Multifractal spectrum widthSubject terms: Neuroscience, Energy science and technology

Introduction

With the rapid development of the modern construction industry, the tower crane, as one of the indispensable equipment in the construction project, plays an important role in the construction. As the operator of the tower crane, the efficiency of the tower crane driver directly affects the construction progress, project quality and site safety. Therefore, to improve the efficiency of tower crane drivers has become an important issue in the management of building construction. In order to improve the work efficiency of tower crane drivers, enterprises and project managers usually adopt various incentive mechanisms. However, how to measure the effect of incentives on the efficiency of tower crane drivers is of great significance to improve the efficiency and develop more scientific incentive strategies.

It is difficult to accurately measure the impact of different motivational models on employee productivity due to individual differences, environmental conditions, research methods, and other factors1. Evaluating the impact of various motivational strategies on employee productivity enables organisations to gain a deeper understanding of the needs and motivations of their workforce so that they can develop more effective motivational programmes to improve employee performance and drive overall company growth2. Traditional methods for assessing the impact of motivational models on employee productivity typically include performance appraisals, goal attainment, employee satisfaction surveys, reward and recognition records, and job performance metrics3. However, there are some shortcomings in traditional methods. For example, relying too heavily on a single performance indicator may overlook the employee’s contribution and development in other areas. Performance appraisals and satisfaction surveys are often influenced by subjective factors, and there may be subjective bias on the part of the appraiser or subjective feelings on the part of the employee’s feedback4. In addition, certain effects may take longer to become apparent and traditional methods may not be able to capture these changes immediately5. Conventional methods usually focus on the individual level and ignore the motivational effects at the team and organisational levels. In order to compensate for the limitations of conventional methods for assessing the effects of various motivational strategies on employee productivity, this study used electroencephalogram (EEG) techniques to assess the effects of different incentive methods on productivity by examining cognitive and behavioural aspects of humans. In recent years, several studies have applied EEG to fatigue monitoring in construction and similar work environments. For example, Ma et al.6 proposed a multimodal fusion method that combines comprehensive multimodal input features, including heart rate, respiratory rate, skin temperature, lactate and glucose levels, and EEG signals, to identify mental fatigue during construction equipment operation; Fang et al.7 developed a novel in-ear multimodal device specifically designed to detect mental fatigue in construction workers on construction sites, enhancing the practicality of wearable devices; Wang et al.8 processed the obtained EEG signals using continuous wavelet transform and convolutional neural networks to identify workers’ mental fatigue states, achieving an 88.85% accuracy rate in matching cognitive fatigue state classifications with self-reported fatigue states. Human EEG signals provide objective and accurate bioindicators that can directly reflect employees’ brain activities under different incentive modes and avoid biases and errors in subjective evaluations9. Therefore, EEG signals were chosen in this study as the basis for assessing the effects of various motivational strategies on employee productivity.

The feature extraction method of EEG signals is extremely significant to measure the influence of different incentive modes on employees’ work efficiency based on EEG signals. The traditional methods of EEG signal feature extraction have some obvious drawbacks. For example, power spectral density (PSD) is a common feature, but it loses the time-domain information of the signal and cannot reflect the temporal dynamics of the signal10. The extraction of frequency features usually requires the selection of the frequency band of interest, but this selection may distinct due to the subjective judgment of the researcher11. Therefore, the traditional EEG signal feature extraction methods have shortcomings in adaptability, information integrity and individuation. To comprehensively analyze the dynamic changes in EEG signals under different incentive mechanisms, this study employs the Multifractal Detrended Fluctuation Analysis (MF-DFA) method for feature extraction. Compared with traditional monofractal approaches or frequency-domain methods, MF-DFA offers superior adaptability and analytical power, particularly for non-stationary physiological signals exhibiting complex temporal dynamics. Conventional methods often assume stationarity or focus on single-scale statistical properties, which limits their ability to capture the multiscale and nonlinear characteristics inherent in EEG signals. MF-DFA is specifically designed to extract multifractal features from non-stationary time series by systematically eliminating trends and minimizing the influence of long-range correlations12. This allows for a more accurate representation of the intrinsic fluctuations and scale-dependent behaviors embedded in the signal. Such capability is particularly valuable in contexts involving cognitive task execution, where mental states such as attention shifts or fatigue manifest as complex, multifractal variations in EEG activity13. Moreover, MF-DFA requires no prior signal stationarization, making it a robust and versatile method that has been widely applied in neuroscience, cognitive psychology, and complex systems analysis14. Given these advantages, MF-DFA was selected in this study to extract dynamic, multiscale features from EEG signals. This approach not only enables a thorough investigation of the multifractal nature of brain activity under different incentive conditions, but also provides a theoretically grounded and methodologically reliable tool for exploring the neural mechanisms underlying operator performance.

In a real work environment, the efficiency of employees is determined by numerous factors. The results of the study show that the incentive mode of reward and punishment is an effective means to regulate the cognitive control ability of individuals, and it can effectively influence the subsequent work behavior of individuals15. Wei et al.16 used event-related potentials (ERP) to study the effect of reward expectation on facial emotion recognition. The results showed that the expectation of reward enhanced the neural response of the subjects’ brains, and the reward induced a larger amplitude of P1, P2 and P300 compared with no reward. This reflects that the reward and incentive information promoted the participants to make adequate preparation for the effective processing of the subsequent target by calling for more attention resources. The expectation of reward can improve the state of motivation, and motivation plays an important role in the cognitive process of individuals. Motivational cues can effectively promote individuals to carry out top-down cognitive activities. At the same time, some studies have found that individuals prefer active control under reward conditions. Research shows that “motivation” is an important factor affecting the dual cognitive control tradeoff. “Motivation” is mostly achieved in real life through rewards and punishments for tasks. Firstly, in terms of rewards, many studies have shown that rewards can increase individuals’ input in tasks, significantly improve top-down control, make individuals’ cognitive control more proactive, and improve cognitive processing performance17. In addition, some studies have found that reward and punishment can enhance individuals’ cognitive control abilities18. Therefore, this study chooses the reward and punishment incentive mechanism to evaluate whether it will affect the work efficiency of employees.

Experiment

Subjects

In this study, a total of 10 subjects (numbered A-J) were recruited to participate in the experiment. The subjects, including 5 males and 5 females, were 30 ± 4.5 years old and in good health. All subjects were not taking any medication during the experiment and had no history of sleep related or neurological disorders. All subjects came to obtain the Chinese tower crane driving qualification within three years. To ensure the accuracy of the experimental results, all subjects were asked to avoid drinking any beverages containing stimulant ingredients, such as alcohol, tea or coffee, before the experiment. Meanwhile, in order to ensure that each subject had the best physical and mental condition during the experiment, all participants were asked to get enough sleep the day before the experiment. In addition, to ensure that the subjects fully understood the purpose and procedure of the experiment, a written informed consent form was provided to each subject and their signatures were obtained to confirm the consent prior to the start of the experiment. The informed consent form detailed the content of the study, possible risks, and the rights of the subjects to ensure that the autonomy and privacy of the participants were fully respected. The trial, which followed the Declaration of Helsinki, was approved by Northeast Electric Power University’s Institutional Review Board.

Experimental paradigm

In this experiment, the number of times the participants hoisting prefabricated components within 2 h is used as the criterion for evaluating efficiency. To maintain the objectivity and reliability of the experimental data, a one-day interval was implemented following the completion of the first experiment by all participants, prior to commencing the second experiment. We set up a reward and punishment incentive scheme for employees, and each subject participated in two experiments. In the first experiment, subjects worked for 2 h without knowing the reward and punishment incentive scheme; in the second experiment, subjects worked for 2 h after fully knowing the reward and punishment incentive scheme for employees. Before the second experiment, the participants had to study the reward and punishment scheme for at least 5 min. In order to prevent the same subject from knowing the reward and punishment incentive scheme in advance, subjects initially conducted the first experiment (no reward and punishment incentive mode), and then conducted the second experiment (reward and punishment incentive mode). The experiment will be conducted from 2 p.m. to 4 p.m. The experiment lasted for 2 h and the EEG signals were collected four times. The EEG signals were collected for about 5 min each time, and the number of times the participants hoisting prefabricated components was calculated while the EEG signals were collected. The first collection took place from 2:00 p.m. to 2:05 p.m., and the second collection took place from 2:40 p.m. to 2:45 p.m. The third collection took place from 3:20 p.m. to 3:25 p.m. The fourth collection took place between 3:55 p.m. and 4:00 p.m. In addition, the EEG signals acquisition device is the Emotiv. The device’s sampling rate is set at 128 Hz, with the electrode placement following the international 10–20 system (comprising 14 channels: AF3, AF4, F3, F4, FC5, FC6, F7, F8, T7, T8, P7, P8, O1, O2). The whole experimental process is divided into: the experimental preparation stage (subjects learn the reward and punishment incentive scheme) and the experimental ongoing stage (subjects lifting prefabricated components), as shown in Fig. 1.

Reward and punishment incentive mode

Incentive mode

Incentive refers to how an organization and its members activate, steer, sustain, and govern actions through the creation of suitable reward systems and work settings, adherence to behavioral standards, imposition of disciplinary actions, and effective information exchange to successfully realize the objectives of both the organization and its personnel21. Motivation is the process of stimulating people’s needs and motives, guiding behavior to the goal, and mobilizing people’s enthusiasm. The elements of motivational dynamics include motivation itself, needs, external triggers, and actions. In this context, motivation is essentially the driving force; needs serve as the origin and foundation of motivation; external stimuli are necessary conditions for incentives; and actions are the intended outcomes of incentives22.

The most important purpose of motivation is to improve the enthusiasm of employees, improving their energy, responsibility, and initiative. In human resource management, the incentive mechanism plays a role in regulating and guiding the behavior of employees. At the same time, a good incentive mechanism can attract talented people to join the enterprise and improve the overall quality of employees23. Reward and punishment is actually an incentive to increase the level of motivation of an individual.

Reward and punishment incentive mode

The reward and punishment incentive mode refers to the management method that encourages employees to show good performance through rewards and inhibits bad performance through punishments to promote employees’ work efficiency and performance improvement24. In recent years, the research on reward and punishment focuses on enterprise management, safety production, secondary and higher education, ideological and political education and other fields. The incentive measures of reward and punishment are particularly common in enterprise management. First of all, in terms of enterprise management, reasonable and effective reward and punishment management methods can often help enterprise managers to motivate employees’ work passion and enthusiasm, and sometimes improve employees’ subjective initiative, making enterprise management more efficient25. The establishment of a salary distribution system and a talent incentive system is the key to human resource management and the long-term development of enterprises26. In the realm of industrial safety, the incentive and disciplinary mechanism serves as a crucial safeguard for ensuring safety in production. The “sense of responsibility” is cultivated through the incentive and penalty system, with the development and enforcement of this system being critically significant in the governance of production safety27.

Hurst exponent

The Hurst index is able to quantify the relative trend of a time series, revealing the characteristics of EEG signals presented in the time series. The multiscale feature of the Hurst index is reflected in its ability to reveal the long-term correlation of time series data over different time scales. In the case of a time series, if the Hurst index shows a consistent pattern of variation with different values of q, the series shows a clear multiscale feature28. As shown in Fig. 3, H(q) showed a nonlinear relationship with q value, and H(q) in all four experimental phases showed a decreasing trend with the increase of q value, revealing the inherent multiscale characteristics of this time-series signal. The relationship between H(q) and q indicated that the EEG signals of the subject showed significant observable characteristics as the experiments progressed. Figures 3(A) and (B) illustrate that the Hurst index of the EEG signals in both reward and punishment incentive modes decreased as the work period increased, indicating that the complexity of the EEG signals of subject A decreased from the first to the fourth stage of the work period. Comparison of the hoisting efficiency

This study calculated the efficiency of hoisting prefabricated components for 10 subjects at each stage under two modes, and the results are shown in Fig. 8.

The average number of times prefabricated components was hoisted by 10 subjects at different stages of the two modes.

Due to the complexity of the hoisting process, in this study, the number of hoisting operations of prefabricated components completed within a unit of time is adopted to measure the working efficiency of employees. As shown in Fig. 8, in both incentive and non-incentive modes, as the experiment progressed, fatigue led to a steady decrease in the average number of times employees hoisted prefabricated components. However, in the incentive mode, the reduction rate of the number of hoisting operations is significantly lower than that in the non-incentive mode. Therefore, it can be concluded that the reward and punishment incentive mode proposed in this study is conducive to improving the working efficiency of employees.

Comparison of the number of safety violations

To comprehensively reflect the performance of operators under different incentive modes, the number of safety violations is introduced as another metric for evaluating lifting efficiency. Frequent safety violations not only risk delaying lifting schedules but also lead to rework, equipment wear and tear, or even personal safety risks, indirectly reducing overall operational efficiency. If operators engage in behaviours such as deviating from the lifting path, excessive swaying of the load, or violating operational procedures, this is counted as a single safety violation incident. By comparing the frequency of safety violations under different incentive modes, it is possible to further determine whether the reward-punishment incentive mode encourages operators to maintain a high level of compliance and safety awareness while improving efficiency. This approach not only facilitates a multidimensional understanding of the essence of operational efficiency but also provides a reference for optimizing construction management systems in the future. Discussion

Advantage of EEG signals to measure employee productivity

In the fields of organizational behavior and human resource management, the evaluation methods of traditional employee incentive mechanisms mainly rely on three categories: psychological scales, behavioral observation, and performance indicator analysis. However, these methods have significant limitations in terms of ecological validity, objectivity, and explanatory depth. The first method is the questionnaire survey method, such as using the Minnesota Satisfaction Scale, Work Motivation Scale, or Gallup Q12 assessment tool, to obtain subjective perception data through employee self-reports. The shortcomings of this method lie in three aspects: Firstly, respondents are prone to be influenced by social validation bias, for example, when dealing with salary-sensitive incentive issues, employees may conceal their true evaluations30; Secondly, recall bias leads to distorted assessment of past incentive experiences, especially for emotional fluctuation-related incentive events; Thirdly, the cross-cultural adaptability of the scale is insufficient, for example, tools developed in the West may underestimate the team incentive effect in collectivist cultures31. The second method is the behavioral performance analysis method, which makes indirect inferences through key performance indicators (KPI), task completion rate, or error rate, etc., of objective data. Its limitations are as follows: Firstly, performance results are interfered by multiple factors (such as resource supply, environmental variables), making it difficult to isolate the net effect of the incentive mechanism alone. Secondly, there is a significant time lag, such as quarterly assessment data cannot capture the neural behavioral response of immediate incentives; Thirdly, this method cannot distinguish the contribution of intrinsic motivation (such as a sense of achievement) from extrinsic motivation (such as bonuses), which may lead to the blindness of incentive strategy design32. The third method is the experimental control method, which compares the effects of different incentive schemes by setting control groups and experimental groups. However, there is an ecological validity gap between the laboratory environment and the real working scene. For example, the incentive responses observed in simulated decision-making tasks cannot be extrapolated to the high-pressure, multi-task concurrent actual working environment33. In addition, qualitative research methods (such as in-depth interviews, focus groups) can obtain the deep motivations of the incentive mechanism, but they have challenges such as small sample sizes, strong subjectivity in interpretation, and data analysis that is easily influenced by the researcher’s preconceived framework.

This study innovatively utilizes EEG signals to evaluate the differences in employees’ work efficiency, psychological and neural processes under various incentive modes, and explains this phenomenon at the brain level. This method has the following advantages:

(1) Objectivity: EEG signals provide objective biological indicators, which can quantify employees’ cognitive and emotional states. Compared with methods that rely solely on subjective reports or self-assessments, EEG provides more objective data that is less susceptible to biases or subjective factors.

(2) Real-time monitoring: EEG signals can monitor employees’ brain activities in real time. This means that during employees’ task execution, information about their cognitive states can be obtained without relying on post-review or questionnaires.

(3) Identification of cognitive load and anxiety: EEG signals help identify employees’ cognitive load levels and whether there is anxiety or stress. These factors are often closely related to work efficiency, thus providing useful information about the employees’ work status.

(4) Enhancement of productivity: Through EEG signal monitoring, cognitive fatigue or distraction during work can also be identified by managers. This can help them take measures, such as scheduling breaks or adjusting task allocation, to improve employees’ productivity.

(5) Scientific research: EEG signals also facilitate scientific research to gain a deeper understanding of the impact of different incentive modes on employees’ brain activities and performance. This helps generate new insights and best practices.

EEG-based incentive assessment in actual construction projects

In actual construction projects, on-site managers can implement an EEG-based incentive effectiveness assessment system through the following strategies. First, introduce portable, non-invasive EEG monitoring devices at critical positions (such as crane operators, lifting supervisors, etc.), such as integrating semi-dry electrodes or dry electrode systems into safety helmets or head-mounted devices, ensuring that brain activity data is collected without interfering with the work process. Second, before and after implementing specific incentive mechanisms (such as fixed bonuses, piece-rate rewards, or a combination of rewards and penalties), arrange short-term EEG monitoring tasks, such as during the execution of standard operating procedures or simulated task operations. By analysing fatigue characteristics in EEG (such as increased θ wave activity, α wave suppression, and reduced complexity), combined with performance data (reaction time, operational accuracy, completion duration, etc.), assess the impact of incentive modes on workers’ cognitive states. Third, On-site managers can use a simple EEG analysis platform or pre-set indicators (such as MLE, multiple fractal spectrum width, entropy value changes, etc.) to quickly determine whether incentive mechanisms have caused decreased attention or excessive mental load, and adjust task allocation, rest periods, or incentive structures accordingly. For example, if a worker shows signs of increased EEG fatigue under high-intensity incentives, they can be reassigned to a lower-risk position or given phased goal rewards. Finally, it is recommended to combine EEG data with other human factor monitoring methods (such as heart rate variability, subjective fatigue scales) to form a multi-modal worker status assessment system, and gradually integrate it into the smart construction site platform to achieve digitalisation and intelligence in management decision-making. This approach provides a practical path for constructing a ‘incentive–fatigue–performance’ closed-loop system, which helps to promote the implementation of a people-centric safety performance coordination management system in the construction industry.

MF-DFA

It has been shown that nonlinear dynamics methods can be more effective in extracting the features of EEG signals, whereas traditional methods for analyzing EEG signals may ignore many of the inherent properties in the signals34. For example, most of the traditional signal analysis theories are based on the Fourier transform (FT), such as fast Fourier transform (FFT), short-time Fourier transform (STFT). However, FFT provides frequency domain information, but time domain information is lost during spectral analysis35. This means that information about the evolution of the signal over time is not available. And FFT assumes that the signal is stable, which means that the spectral characteristics of the signal remain constant throughout the time period. This may not be accurate enough for spectral analysis of non-stationary EEG signals. In the case of STFT, it necessitates choosing a suitable window function for signal segmentation; larger windows enhance frequency resolution at the expense of temporal detail, whereas smaller windows offer improved temporal resolution with reduced frequency resolution36. Sample entropy (SampEn) is capable of portraying the degree of regularity or randomness of a nonlinear time series, and is suitable for describing the intrinsic characteristic changes of a time series. However, when calculating the dynamic changes in the complexity of the EEG time series, the obtained entropy value is affected by the length of the reconstructed subsequence and the mode distance (tolerance), and it is difficult for SampEn to accurately capture the moments of changes in the time series37. The power spectrum technique is capable of efficiently extracting the frequency data from a signal and disclosing the magnitude and distribution of various frequency elements within the signal. However, power spectra usually provide average spectral information of a signal, but cannot capture transient characteristics or short-time events, and thus may not be accurate enough when analyzing signals that need to take into account time-domain dynamic characteristics38. Brain network methods can reveal the connectivity and interaction patterns between brain regions, but brain networks are complex with multilevel and multiscale structures, so brain network analysis methods need to deal with a large amount of complex data and sophisticated computational models39. In order to compare the advantages of MF-DFA in the field of signal analysis, using power spectrum, SampEn, and brain network to analyze EEG signals in non-incentive mode in this study. In this case, the clustering coefficient and global efficiency are the measurement indexes of the brain network method. Limitations and future prospects

Many of these incentives may have a significant effect in the short term, but they may be less effective in the long term. This study may not take time into account, ignoring the long-term sustainability and impact of incentives. In order to better understand the long-term impact of different incentive modes on employee productivity, future studies could be conducted to track and analyze them over a longer period of time to obtain more reliable results.

Conclusions

In this study, the effect of different motivational modes on employee productivity was measured by using EEG signals. The EEG signal characteristics of subjects at different stages of work were analysed in the no-motivation mode as well as in the reward and punishment mode using the MF-DFA method. The results of the study showed that in the incentive mode, operators had a greater range of Hurst index (p < 0.05) and a wider width of the multifractal spectrum of the EEG signals, which indicated a reduction in mental fatigue and an increase in the complexity of their brain activity. At the same time, the lifting efficiency of prefabricated components decreased more slowly in the incentive mode, highlighting its practical effectiveness in maintaining productivity. Compared with traditional feature extraction techniques (e.g., PSD, SampEn), the MF-DFA method is able to capture the multi-scale nonlinear dynamics of EEG signals, thus accurately quantifying changes in cognitive states. In addition, a neuro-ergonomics approach based on EEG signals reduces assessment time by 87%, providing real-time, objective assessment of cognitive load and fatigue compared to traditional methods such as performance assessment.

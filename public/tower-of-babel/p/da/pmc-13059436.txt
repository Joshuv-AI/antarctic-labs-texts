Associations between interleukin-37 gene polymorphisms and susceptibility and clinical outcomes of rheumatoid arthritis: a meta-analysis and systematic review
Authors: Zihan Zhao, Wenwen Yu, Huapei Qiu, Yuehong Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic autoimmune disease affecting millions of people worldwide. Genetic factors, including specific polymorphisms such as the anti-inflammatory cytokine Interleukin (IL)-37, play a significant role in RA. This meta-analysis and systematic review investigated the associations between IL-37 gene polymorphisms and susceptibility and clinical outcomes of RA.MethodsA comprehensive literature search of the MEDLINE(R), Embase, and Web of Science databases was conducted on January 1, 2026. Case control studies assessing associations between IL-37 polymorphisms and RA susceptibility and/or clinical outcomes were included. Studies deviating from the Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium in controls, lacking genotype data, reviews, duplicates, or non-original studies were excluded. Study selection, data extraction, and quality assessment using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale were independently performed by two authors, with any discrepancies resolved by a senior researcher. Data were pooled using inverse-variance weighted random-effects models under five genetic models, with heterogeneity assessed using Cochran’s Q test and the I2 statistic. Sensitivity analyses were conducted, and prediction intervals were calculated.ResultsA total of 7 studies were included in the final analysis, involving 1627 RA patients and 1722 healthy controls. No significant associations were observed between IL-37 polymorphisms (including rs3811047 and rs3811046) and RA susceptibility across genetic models. In the dominant model of rs3811047, patients carrying the AA or AG genotypes exhibit favorable clinical outcomes as compared to patients having GG genotype, including lower joint swelling index scores (MD = −1.65, 95% CI −2.53 to −0.78, p = 0.0002), reduced rest pain (MD = −1.02, 95% CI −1.52 to −0.52, p < 0.0001), lower joint tenderness index scores (MD = −2.11, 95% CI −3.96 to −0.27, p = 0.02), and improved Health Assessment Questionnaire scores (MD = −0.28, 95% CI −0.42 to −0.15, p < 0.0001).ConclusionIL-37 polymorphisms do not appear to be strongly associated with RA susceptibility but may influence disease severity, highlighting a potential role in RA pathophysiology and prognosis. Limitations included a small number of included studies, moderate to high heterogeneity in some genetic models, and limited availability of genotype-stratified clinical outcome data.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s41927-026-00624-0.

Background

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic autoimmune disorder marked by synovial inflammation and joint damage. Multiple studies have identified specific gene polymorphisms linked to an increased risk of RA, implying genetic susceptibility in certain individuals. The Human Leukocyte Antigen (HLA) region, particularly the HLA-DRB1 gene, has been extensively studied for its connection to RA susceptibility [1]. Moreover, 32 non-HLA gene polymorphisms have been confirmed to be associated with RA [2]. Non-HLA genes like PTPN22 (Protein Tyrosine Phosphatase Non-Receptor Type 22) and TNFAIP3 (TNF Alpha Induced Protein 3) are also proven to be involved in immune regulation and inflammatory pathways [3].

Interleukin-37 (IL-37), also known as Interleukin-1 family member 7 (IL1F7), is encoded by the IL1F7 gene located on chromosome 2q14.1. This gene spans 7819 base pairs on the forward strand (chr2: 112,912,971– 112,918,882, based on the GRCh38.p7 human genome assembly, Annotation Release 108). It consists of six exons and undergoes alternative splicing to produce five transcript variants, among which IL-37a, IL-37b, and IL-37d are regarded as functional forms. IL1F7 has a highly polymorphic coding region, with numerous missense variants. Due to linkage disequilibrium with other nearby missense mutations and intron variants, some polymorphisms are predicted to be deleterious [4]. According to data from the NCBI dbSNP database, the allele frequencies of IL-37 gene polymorphisms exhibit marked variability among different ancestral populations [5]. It has been reported that IL-37 plays a critical role as an anti-inflammatory cytokine, essential for suppressing pro-inflammatory cytokine production and inhibiting tumor cell growth [6, 7].

Recently, a possible correlation between RA and IL-37 and its genetic polymorphisms has been observed. IL-37 levels between RA patients and control individuals are different, indicating that IL-37 could be a candidate biomarker for testing. Additionally, its gene polymorphism may pose a risk factor for RA development [4]. However, the precise nature of this association remains unclear. To address this uncertainty, we conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis to further explore the relationship between IL-37 gene polymorphisms and RA.

Methods

Study design

This meta-analysis and systematic review investigated the relationship between IL-37 polymorphisms and the susceptibility and clinical outcomes of RA. The study was conducted following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 flow diagram for new systematic reviews. The PRISMA 2020 Checklist and PRISMA 2020 for Abstracts Checklist are also uploaded in supplementary files. The manuscript does not contain clinical studies or patient data.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Literature assessing the association between variant single-nucleotide polymorphism of the IL-37 gene and RA susceptibility and/or clinical outcomes was included. Only case-control studies were included, with cases defined as individuals diagnosed with RA and controls as healthy participants. Genotyping of IL-37 polymorphisms was conducted using polymerase chain reaction (PCR), reverse transcription PCR, ligase detection reaction, or sequence-specific primer PCR. There were no limitations to the gene sites of IL-37. The key outcomes involved analyzing the frequency of different IL-37 genotypes among individuals diagnosed with RA versus healthy controls. HWE was assessed in the control groups of included studies using the chi-square test (df = 1).

Studies were excluded if the genotype distributions in the control group deviated significantly from the Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium (HWE) (p < 0.05), suggesting potential genotyping error or population stratification. Studies were also excluded if they were narrative reviews, lacked genetic frequency data, were duplicate publications, study protocols, or executive summaries.

Search strategy

A comprehensive literature search of MEDLINE(R), Embase, and Web of Science was conducted on January 1, 2026, using a combination of relevant MeSH terms and keywords. The search terms used include “interleukin-37”, “gene polymorphism”, and “rheumatoid arthritis”. To ensure comprehensive coverage, reference lists from both the included articles and excluded reviews were manually screened for additional relevant studies. Only studies published in English were included. Non-English publications were excluded, and no translation of non-English articles was performed.

Study selection

All retrieved studies were first imported into EndNote to remove duplicates, and the remaining studies were then transferred to Microsoft Office Access for management and selection. Two independent reviewers conducted the screening process by reading titles, abstracts, and full texts based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Any disagreements were resolved through discussion or by a third reviewer.

Data extraction

With a pre-designed form, two authors independently performed the data extraction. The disagreement was resolved by a senior researcher. The data of the included studies embraced author, year, country, sample size, gender, age, polymorphism, genotype frequencies of the IL-37 gene, and genotyping method.

Quality assessment of the included studies

The quality of the included case-control studies was evaluated using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS), which comprises eight items across three domains: selection, comparability, and exposure. Quality ratings were categorized according to AHRQ standards as follows: studies were deemed good quality if they scored 3–4 stars in selection, 1–2 stars in comparability, and 2–3 stars in exposure; fair quality if they received 2 stars in selection, 1–2 in comparability, and 2–3 in exposure; and poor quality if they scored 0–1 in selection, 0 in comparability, or 0–1 in exposure. Two independent reviewers conducted the quality assessment, with any disagreements resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer [8].

Data process

Statistical analyses were carried out using Review Manager (RevMan) version 5.1 (Cochrane Collaboration, Oxford, UK). Data from individual case-control studies were pooled under five genetic models: allelic (A vs. G), homozygous (AA vs. GG), heterozygous (AG vs. GG), dominant (AA+AG vs. GG), and recessive (AA vs. AG+GG). Pooled odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated using an inverse-variance weighted random-effects model, accounting for both within-study and between-study variability. Statistical heterogeneity was quantified using Cochran’s Q test and the I2 statistic to describe between-study variability, rather than to guide model selection, as random-effects models were applied throughout, and the between-study variance (τ2) was estimated under the random-effects framework. Overall effects were evaluated using Z statistics, with a two-sided p < 0.05 considered statistically significant. In addition, 95% prediction intervals (PIs) were calculated using the CMA prediction intervals free software to reflect the anticipated distribution of true effects in future studies, incorporating both sampling uncertainty and between-study heterogeneity. The corresponding PI calculations are shown in Supplementary Figure 1. When more than two studies reported on the same single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP), results were visualized using forest plots. To test the stability of our findings, sensitivity analyses were performed by sequentially excluding each individual study to evaluate whether any single study with a relatively large sample size disproportionately influenced the pooled estimates.

IL-37 gene polymorphisms and RA susceptibility

No significant association was found between IL-37 rs3811047（A > G）polymorphism and RA susceptibility across five genetic models, as shown in Fig. 2. The A allele was defined as the effect allele. In the allelic model (A vs G), no significant association with RA risk was observed (OR = 1.13, 95% CI 0.68 to 1.89; I2 = 85%; 95% PI 0.17 to 7.40). No significant association was detected in the codominant homozygote model (AA vs GG; OR = 1.17, 95% CI 0.43 to 3.22; I2 = 74%; 95% PI 0.03 to 40.88) or the codominant heterozygote model (AG vs GG; OR = 1.06, 95% CI 0.79 to 1.41; I2 = 23%; 95% PI 0.56 to 2.02). Consistently, no associations were also observed under the dominant model (AA+AG vs GG; OR = 1.13, 95% CI 0.72 to 1.78; I2 = 70%; 95% PI 0.24 to 5.26) and the recessive model (AA vs AG+GG; OR = 1.13, 95% CI 0.47 to 2.70; I2 = 69%; 95%PI 0.06 to 22.65). Overall, none of the genetic models demonstrated a significant association between rs3811047 and RA susceptibility. However, moderate to high heterogeneity was observed in several comparisons, indicating that further studies with larger sample sizes are warranted.

Similarly, the IL-37 rs3811046 (G > T) polymorphism was not significantly associated with RA susceptibility across all five genetic models (Fig. 3). The G allele was defined as the effect allele. The allelic model (G vs T) yielded a pooled OR of 1.35 (95% CI 0.69 to 2.65; I2 = 85%). No significant associations were observed in the codominant homozygote (GG vs TT; OR = 2.10, 95% CI 0.67 to 6.52; I2 = 58%) or heterozygote models (GT vs TT; OR = 1.23, 95% CI 0.68 to 2.23; I2 = 63%). The dominant (GG+GT vs TT; OR = 1.38, 95% CI 0.63 to 3.03; I2 = 81%) and recessive models (GG vs GT+TT; OR = 1.93, 95% CI 0.89 to 4.18; I2 = 26%) likewise demonstrated no statistically significant associations. Like rs3811047, moderate to high heterogeneity was observed in several models, warranting cautious interpretation of the pooled estimates.Fig. 3Pooled results of the association between IL-37 rs3811046 gene polymorphisms and RA susceptibility

In addition, no significant differences were observed in the alleles and genotypes of the other four SNPs (rs2723186, rs4241122, rs4364030, and rs4392270) across the entire region of the IL-37 gene [13].

IL-37 gene polymorphism and RA clinical outcomes

Of the seven studies included in the meta-analysis, five investigated the rs3811047 polymorphism; however, two of these did not report clinical outcome data stratified by rs3811047 genotypes. Consequently, two studies with available genotype-specific continuous outcomes were included in the pooled meta-analysis [11, 12]. Meanwhile, one additional study that only reported genotype-specific DAS28 data was analyzed separately [14].

As shown in Fig. 4, using a random-effects model, RA patients carrying the AA or AG genotype exhibited significantly more favorable clinical outcomes compared with those carrying the GG genotype, as reflected by lower joint swelling index scores (MD = −1.65, 95% CI −2.53 to −0.78, p = 0.0002), reduced rest pain (MD = −1.02, 95% CI −1.52 to −0.52, p < 0.0001), lower joint tenderness index scores (MD = −2.11, 95% CI −3.96 to −0.27, p = 0.02), and improved Health Assessment Questionnaire (HAQ) scores (MD = −0.28, 95% CI −0.42 to −0.15, p < 0.0001). No significant between-genotype differences were observed for rheumatoid factor (RF) levels (MD = −10.53, 95% CI −34.82 to 13.75, p = 0.40) or C-reactive protein (CRP) levels (MD = −8.89, 95% CI −18.60 to 0.81, p = 0.07). Notably, heterogeneity was negligible across all pooled analyses (I2 = 0%), indicating high consistency between studies. However, given the small number of contributing studies, the estimate should be interpreted cautiously.Fig. 4Pooled results of the association between IL-37 rs3811047 and RA clinical outcomes

In the dominant gene model, patients carrying the AA or AG genotype had lower DAS28 scores than patients with the GG genotype (p = 0.041). Sensitivity analysis and publication bias

The sensitivity analysis conducted using the random effects model indicated that the results were stable. Formal funnel plot asymmetry analyses and Egger’s regression tests were not performed due to the limited number of included studies (<10), in accordance with established methodological recommendations [15].

Discussion

This study highlights a potential association between IL-37 gene polymorphisms, specifically rs3811047 and rs3811046, and the susceptibility to RA within the Chinese Han population, the Iraqi population and the Egyptian population. Although neither SNP showed a significant association with RA susceptibility, rs3811047 was significantly associated with disease severity and several clinical features.

The published studies have already found that specific gene polymorphisms related to cytokines, like IL-1B [16], IL-1Ra [16], IL-6 [17], IL-21 [18], IL-17A [19, 20], IL-17F [19–21], TNF-α [22], NLRP3, CARD8 [17, 23], Foxp3 [24] are linked to disease activity, susceptibility, and treatment response. IL-37 plays a significant role in modulating inflammation, and its dysregulation may contribute to RA susceptibility [6, 25]. Ongoing research into polymorphisms in the IL-37 gene shows that they can affect IL-37’s expression and function, potentially influencing an individual’s risk of developing RA [4, 14, 26]. Worth noticing, the absence of a significant association between IL-37 polymorphisms and traditional systemic inflammatory markers such as RF or CRP does not preclude a functional role of IL-37 in disease modulation. IL-37 is increasingly recognized as a counter-regulatory anti-inflammatory cytokine that is induced in response to excessive immune activation, acting primarily as an “inflammatory brake” rather than a driver of acute-phase responses [27]. As such, genetic variations affecting IL-37 expression or activity may preferentially influence local synovial inflammation and composite clinical indices, such as joint swelling, pain, and DAS28 components.

Serological markers such as RF and CRP which are governed by distinct biological axes and are highly susceptible to treatment effects and short-term inflammatory fluctuations. Specifically, RF reflects autoreactive B-cell responses shaped by complex immune tolerance mechanisms and multiple genetic and environmental determinants [28, 29]. CRP is a hepatic acute-phase reactant largely driven by upstream inflammatory signaling, particularly the IL-6 axis, and is strongly influenced by treatment and intercurrent conditions [30].

From a phenotypic and statistical perspective, RF and CRP exhibit substantial inter-individual and temporal variability, influenced by therapy, comorbidities, and environmental factors, which may potentially mask modest genetic effects. Again, the impact of rs3811047 is likely subtle, modulating IL-37 expression or functional thresholds rather than directly altering acute inflammatory readouts. Consequently, limited sample size and statistical power may further reduce the ability to detect weak genotype–RF/CRP associations, even if such relationships exist.

Recent studies reveal that IL-37 exerts broad protective effects on various inflammatory and autoimmune diseases, as well as cancer. These diseases include systemic lupus erythematosus, ankylosing spondylitis, Graves’ disease, inflammatory bowel disease, and psoriasis [31–33]. In RA research, accumulating evidence indicates that IL-37 expression is upregulated in parallel with disease activity, supporting its role as an inflammation-induced counter-regulatory cytokine [7]. Elevated levels of IL-37 have been consistently observed in the serum, plasma, peripheral blood mononuclear cells, and synovial tissues of patients with active RA, and these levels positively correlate with DAS28 scores, pro-inflammatory cytokines (including IL-17 and IL-23), and structural joint damage [32, 34, 35]. Notably, effective disease-modifying antirheumatic drug therapy has been shown to reduce circulating IL-37 levels, suggesting that IL-37 expression is driven by the inflammatory milieu and declines as inflammation is controlled [36].

These findings support a compensatory yet ultimately inadequate model in which IL-37 is induced to escalate inflammation as an attempt to restrain immune activation, but fails to fully suppress disease progression.

At the molecular level, IL-37 is synthesized as an inactive precursor by both immune and non-immune cells in response to pro-inflammatory stimuli. Within the cell, caspase-1 cleaves the precursor to generate the active form of IL-37, which binds to Smad3 and translocates into the nucleus [7, 37]. This nuclear complex suppresses the transcription of pro-inflammatory cytokines, thereby reducing inflammatory responses.

Extracellularly, IL-37 interacts with IL-18 Rα and IL-1R8 (also known as TIR8 or SIGIRR) to form a receptor complex that inhibits key inflammatory signaling pathways, including NF-κB and MAPK, as well as Mer-PTEN-DOK signaling [6, 37, 38].

Beyond its direct suppression of NF-κB and MAPK signaling, IL-37 exerts immunoregulatory effects by reshaping T-cell responses central to RA pathogenesis. Experimental studies have demonstrated that IL-37 suppresses Th17 cell proliferation and IL-17 production, while simultaneously enhancing the suppressive function of regulatory T cells (Tregs), thereby correcting the Th17/Treg imbalance characteristic of active RA [39, 40]. In murine models of collagen-induced arthritis, IL-37—particularly the IL-37a isoform—ameliorates joint inflammation through IL-1R8-dependent modulation of Th17/Treg dynamics [41]. These immunomodulatory properties provide a possible mechanistic framework linking IL-37 genetic variation to disease severity. Polymorphisms that impair IL-37 expression or function may limit the capacity for compensatory immune regulation, predisposing patients to enhanced inflammatory activity and more severe clinical manifestations.

Additionally, IL-37 hinders the proliferation and migration of RA fibroblast-like synoviocytes by suppressing the STAT3 pathway. This leads to increased expression of pro-apoptotic proteins such as BAX and cleaved caspase-3, while reducing levels of Bcl-2 and phosphorylated STAT3 [6].

Furthermore, administration of IL-37 (1 µg/mouse) in wild-type mice that were subjected to Streptococcal cell wall-induced arthritis suppressed joint inflammation, by limiting neutrophil recruitment [42].

Consistent with this compensatory immunoregulatory model, our study confirmed that the IL-37 SNP rs3811047 was significantly associated with clinical disease activity and severity parameters in RA. Specifically, RA individuals with the rs3811047 GG genotype, under the dominant gene model, displayed higher DAS28 scores compared to AA or AG genotypes. Additionally, the allele model may also play a role in various RA symptoms, including joint swelling index, rest pain, HAQ scoring, and joint tenderness index among patients carrying the A allele. This may be attributed to the decreased mRNA expression level of IL-37 with allele A of rs381104, as evidenced in a previous study of coronary artery disease by Yin et al. [43].

Interestingly, while rs3811047 showed a borderline association with RA risk, other polymorphisms, such as rs3811046 and rs2723186, did not demonstrate statistically significant associations. This may reflect population-specific genetic effects or limited power due to sample size. However, it is also possible that these SNPs influence IL-37 expression or function indirectly or in contexts of gene-gene interactions not captured in this study.

Notably, an included case–control study conducted in Iraqi women reported significant associations between IL-37 polymorphisms (rs3811046 and rs3811047) and RA susceptibility [10], which differs from the overall findings of the present meta-analysis. This discrepancy may be attributable to population-specific genetic backgrounds, as substantial ethnic heterogeneity exists in the allele frequencies of IL-37 variants. In addition, that study was restricted to female participants, whereas most studies included in our meta-analysis enrolled mixed-sex cohorts. Furthermore, the modest sample size of single-population studies may increase the likelihood of detecting population-specific associations that are weakened when data are pooled across heterogeneous cohorts. Finally, because IL-37 primarily functions as an inducible anti-inflammatory cytokine involved in modulating inflammatory activity rather than initiating disease onset, genetic variation in IL-37 may exert more consistent effects on disease severity or clinical manifestations than on overall disease susceptibility across broader populations.

This meta-analysis systematically integrated both disease susceptibility and genotype-stratified clinical outcomes of IL-37 polymorphisms in RA. However, the study has several limitations. Only seven case-control studies were included, primarily from Asian populations, which limits the generalizability of our findings. Differences in genotyping methods may have introduced variability. Additionally, the limited number of studies prevented us from further assessing publication bias across genetic models. Formal funnel plot asymmetry tests were not performed due to the limited number of included studies (<10), consistent with methodological recommendations. Given these limitations, our findings should be interpreted cautiously, and further research is essential to validate these conclusions.

Conclusion

This meta-analysis and systematic review reports that IL-37 gene polymorphisms do not contribute to RA susceptibility, while the IL-37 rs3811047 variant demonstrates a clear favorable association with RA clinical outcomes, which may serve as prognostic markers of disease severity in RA. These findings lay the groundwork for future large-scale, multi-ethnic, and functional studies aimed at IL-37 based risk stratification and exploring its potential role in precision prognostication and targeted immunomodulatory strategies in RA.

Identification of the IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio as a prognostic biomarker and development of an immune-related prognostic model in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma
Authors: Pan Zhou, Yunmeng Zhou, Liu Yang, Chao Liu, Suqiong Zuo, Xiaohang Pei, Rongjun Ma, Yuqing Chen, Zunmin Zhu, Xiaoli Yuan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDiffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is a highly heterogeneous B-cell malignancy influenced by immune dysregulation, posing significant clinical challenges. Lymphocyte subsets (e.g., CD4 + T helper 1/ T helper 2 cells) and their cytokines (IFN-γ/IL-4) are readily measurable immune markers involved in DLBCL pathogenesis and may independently predict prognosis. However, the prognostic value of these two combined clinical laboratory findings in newly diagnosed DLBCL patients remains uninvestigated.MethodHere, we retrospectively analyzed 94 newly diagnosed DLBCL patients treated at our institution between 2017 and 2023. The prognostic influences of lymphocyte subsets, cytokines levels and other factors on progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) were studied by Kaplan–Meier analysis and Cox regression (univariate/multivariate). Then we constructed an immune-related prognostic score model (IRPS) and validated it in another independent cohort.ResultsThe results suggested that a high IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio and elevated CD4 + T cell count were favorable risk variables for both PFS and OS in DLBCL patients. Besides, multivariate analysis showed that age was marginally associated with the worse OS whereas low CD8 + T cell count was associated with the inferior PFS. Moreover, elevated pretreatment IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio was significantly correlated with better clinical response efficacy. Compared to those treatment-responsive patients, lower level of IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio was discovered in patients experienced with progression during the subsequent treatment cycles. Additionally, a new immune-related prognostic score model was constructed based on age, CD4 + T cell count and IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio, where high-risk patients had worse overall survival than low-risk patients. Meanwhile, the IRPS could refine the International Prognostic Index (IPI) score well and validation of the IRPS in another independent cohort confirmed its effectiveness.ConclusionIFN-γ/IL-4 ratio is a practical prognostic marker in newly diagnosed DLBCL, and the IRPS model shows potential for predicting PFS and OS in DLBCL, enabling improved risk stratification.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-14737-1.Keywords: Diffuse large b-cell lymphoma, IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio, CD4 + T cell, Immune related prognostic model, International prognostic index

Introduction

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is one of the most common types of aggressive lymphoma, which occupies approximately 30–40% of all adult non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma (NHL). Patients typically present with rapidly enlarging tumor masses in nodal or extranodal sites [1, 2]. With the use of rituximab combined immunochemotherapy, the prognosis of patients has been greatly improved. Nevertheless, DLBCL is highly heterogeneous both clinically and prognostically, with nearly 40% of patients becoming refractory or relapsing [3]. Therefore, precise risk stratification and individualized first-line therapy are particularly important in newly diagnosed cases. Currently, the most commonly used clinical predictive model is the International Prognostic Index (IPI), which involves five parameters: age > 60, elevated serum lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status ≥ 2, Ann Arbor stage III or IV and number of involved extranodal sites ≥ 2. For patients with age < 60, the aa-IPI score was derived. With the advent of the rituximab era, the IPI score has diminished and the revised IPI (R-IPI), the National Comprehensive Cancer Network IPI (NCCN-IPI) were proposed, which shows superior prediction in outcome of DLBCL patients treated with standard immunochemotherapy [4–6]. Although these scoring systems improve prognostic stratification, novel biomarkers remain necessary to better identify high-risk patients for intensified therapies.

DLBCL is a result of malignant B-cell development, involving systemic and tumor microenvironmental immune alterations, which include abnormal immune cells constitution and aberrant immune responses [7, 8]. For examples, a large-scale study has shown that low absolute peripheral CD4 + T cell count independently predicts poor survival in DLBCL patients receiving R-CHOP therapy (rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone) [9]. Low circulating CD4 + T cell levels predict inferior PFS outcomes in DLBCL [10]. A low ratio of circulating CD8 + T Lymphocytes to myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) serves as a poor prognostic factor for both PFS and OS in treatment-naïve DLBCL patients [11]. Lymphocyte to monocyte ratio (LMR), reflecting systemic and tumor microenvironment immune status, serves as an effective prognostic biomarker for survival in DLBCL patients [12]. In addition, cytokines may serve as indicators of tumor immune status and modulate the immune system during lymphoma progression. For examples, elevated pretreatment serum cytokine levels has been reported to correlate with early relapse and inferior survival in DLBCL [13]. Serum levels of IL-6 and IL-10 positively correlated with high IPI score, bone marrow involvement, elevated LDH/β2-microglobulin (β2-MG), short PFS and OS in patients with DLBCL [14]. Although numerous studies have demonstrated that several types of lymphocytes and cytokines could be used to predict prognosis of DLBCL, the clinical significance of the combination of these immunoregulation components remains indistinct. It is worthwhile to mention that CD4 + T helper (Th) cells have received extensive attention owing to their indispensable roles in anti-tumor immune responses. The Th1/Th2 balance which reflected by intracellular cytokine expression of IFN-γ/IL-4 in CD4 + T cell may play an important role in the etiology of DLBCL [15]. However, alterations in cytokine levels within the tumor microenvironment could disrupt the Th1/Th2 immune response equilibrium [16]. Within the tumor cytokine microenvironment, immune cells represent the principal source of cytokines, notably the biologically pivotal IFN-γ and IL-4 [17]. Therefore, we sought to evaluate the clinical relevance of the plasma IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio as a potential biomarker reflecting both the extracellular cytokine microenvironment and Th1/Th2 immune balance in DLBCL patients.

In this study, we retrospectively analyzed the count of peripheral lymphocyte subtypes and cytokines levels (IL-4, IL-6, IL-10, IL-12, IL-17 and IFN-γ) in 94 newly diagnosed DLBCL patients and explored their impact on the prognosis, aiming to discover better predictors and build a new prognostic model correlated with DLBCL.

Materials and methods

Subjects

This retrospective study collected data from newly diagnosed DLBCL patients at the Henan Provincial People’s Hospital, Zhengzhou University People’s Hospital, between January 2017 and December 2023. The histological classification of DLBCL was performed according to the guidelines by the World Health Organization. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) newly diagnosed DLBCL patients; (2) at least 4 cycles of chemotherapy were completed. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients with other tumors; (2) patients with special types of lymphoma (primary central nervous system lymphoma, primary mediastinal large B-cell lymphoma, and transformed DLBCL); (3) patients with apparent infectious comorbidities; (4) patients with HIV infection or autoimmune disorders. 94 patients with DLBCL, including 49 (53.2%) females and 45 (47.8%) males, were enrolled. The median age was 63 years, and the follow-up date was up to July 2024. A validation cohort included 47 patients with newly diagnosed DLBCL from the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University during the same period. This research was carried out with approval from the Ethics Committee of the Henan Provincial People’s Hospital (Zhengzhou University People’s Hospital Zhengzhou University People’s Hospital) and the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Data collection

The clinical baseline characteristics were collected from medical records, including age, gender, ECOG score, IPI score, B-symptoms (fever, night sweats, or weight loss), Ann Arbor stage, LDH level, extra-nodal involvement, IPI score, and treatment regimen. Likewise, laboratorial data were available from the hospital-based laboratory, such as serum β2-microglobulin (serum β2-MG), hemoglobin (HB), bone marrow involvement, lymphocyte subsets (CD4 + T cell, CD8 + T cell, B cell, and NK-cell counts), serum cytokines (IL-4, IL-6, IL-10, IL-12, IL-17, IFN-γ) and other laboratory test results were analyzed. CT, MRI, PET-CT and other imaging results were collected for evaluation of their efficacy.

For continuous monitoring of IFN-γ/IL-4 dynamics, we enrolled patients who completed six chemotherapy cycles and had cytokine data available before each treatment. Peripheral blood samples were collected from patients prior to each chemotherapy administration and serum cytokine levels of IFN-γ and IL-4 were quantified using flow cytometry. A total of 61 patients were initially included. By cycle four, three deceased patients and seven with missing data were excluded, leaving 51 cases for analysis. At cycle six, two additional deaths and three missing data cases led to a final cohort of 46 patients.

Patients’ follow-up

The follow-up data were obtained through electronic medical records and telephone interviews. PFS was calculated from the date from diagnosis until disease progression, relapse, death, or the end of follow-up, while OS was defined as the interval between the date of diagnosis and the death of patient or the end of follow-up.

Statistical analyses

Comparisons between groups were made using the Mann-Whitney U test, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) was used to define optimal cut-off values for lymphocyte subtypes and cytokines. Univariate logistic regression and multivariate logistic regression were performed using Cox proportional risk regression models to assess prognostic variables for survival analysis. Multiple testing was adjusted for using the Bonferroni method in univariate analysis and false discovery rate (FDR) correction in the multivariate analysis. To identify potential prognostic factors, all variables achieving nominal significance (unadjusted p ≤ 0.05) in univariate survival analysis were included in the subsequent multivariate analysis. Kaplan–Meier survival curves and log-rank tests were used to estimate survival time. Variables with statistical significance in the multivariate analysis were selected to build a new predictive model. ROC curves were used to calculate the AUC values when comparing the performance of different prognostic scores. The new model was compared with the existing prognostic model by plotting the ROC curve to evaluate its predictive value and applicability. SPSS statistical software package 20.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and GraphPad Prism version 8.0.1 (GraphPad Software, CA, USA) were used for statistical analyses. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

General clinical characteristics of the newly diagnosed DLBCL patients

Among 662 newly diagnosed DLBCL patients treated at our hospital between January 2017 and December 2023, 94 met the inclusion criteria diagrammed in Fig. 1. Overall, the cohort included 45 (47.8%) male and 49 (53.2%) female, with detailed characteristics shown in Table 1. The median age at diagnosis was 63 (9–89) years and 51 (54.2%) were > 60 years. 14 (14.8%) patients had ECOG PS score > 2 and 39 (41.4%) had B symptoms. Prognostic factors for OS and PFS of the newly diagnosed DLBCL patients

The univariate analysis (Table 2) revealed that age > 60 years, IPI score ≥ 3, CD4 + T ≤ 263.5/ul, CD8 + T ≤ 245/ul, IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio ≤ 2.66, IFN-γ ≤ 9.36pg/ml were all high-risk indicators affecting patients’ OS and PFS. However, a β2-MG level > 2.3 mg/L was identified as a high-risk factor adversely affecting PFS but demonstrated minimal impact on OS. Upon correction for multiple comparisons, only IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio was retained as significant discriminatory factor for both OS and PFS. The Kaplan-Meier analysis of OS and PFS, stratified by IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio, was presented in Fig. 2. The survival curves of OS and PFS grouped by other above-mentioned variables were presented in Figure S1. To further determine the independent prognostic values, subsequent multivariate survival analysis was performed. As shown in Table 3, CD4 + T ≤ 263.5/ul (p = 0.012), and IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio ≤ 2.66 (p = 0.000) were significant prognostic factors for worse OS. Age > 60 years (p = 0.055) and CD8 + T-cell counts ≤ 245/ul (p = 0.034) showed borderline significance for OS prediction. CD4 + T ≤ 263.5/ul (p = 0.02), CD8 + T ≤ 245/ul (p = 0.005), and IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio ≤ 2.66 (p = 0.000) were identified as predictors for inferior PFS.

Relationship of IFN-γ/IL-4 to efficacy of treatment

Since the IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio was the dominant survival predictor, we subsequently investigated its value in efficacy assessment. Using the cut-off value of 2.66 at diagnosis, patients were divided into high IFN-γ/IL-4 (63/94, 67.0%) and low IFN-γ/IL-4 (31/94, 32.9%) groups. Among the high IFN-γ/IL-4 group, 22/63 (34.9%) patients achieved complete response (CR), 18/63 (28.6%) patients achieved partial response (PR), 8/63 (12.7%) patients achieved stable disease (SD), and 15/63 (23.8%) patients experienced progressive disease (PD). In contrast, in the low IFN-γ/IL-4 group, only (7/31, 22.6%) patients achieved CR, 5/31 (16.1%) patients achieved PR, 4/31 (12.9%) patients experienced SD, and 15/31 (48.4%) developed PD (Fig. 3A). Therefore, these results showed that a significant higher overall response rate (ORR) was observed in the higher IFN-γ/IL-4 group compared to the lower IFN-γ/IL-4 group. To evaluate IFN-γ/IL-4 dynamic changes during treatment, we analyzed 61 patients with complete data, measuring peripheral plasma cytokines at initial diagnosis (n = 61), after 2 cycles (n = 61), 4 cycles (n = 51), and 6 cycles (n = 46) of chemotherapy. As shown in Fig. 3B, treatment-responsive patients maintained significantly higher IFN-γ/IL-4 ratios compared to those with disease progression (p < 0.05) throughout all chemotherapy cycles.

Construction of a new immune-related prognostic score (IRPS) and integration with the IPI score in newly diagnosed DLBCL patients

Based on multivariate analysis-identified predictors of poor OS, we developed a novel immune-related prognostic score (IRPS) using the clinically established equal-weighting approach for score calculation. One point each was assigned for: (1) age > 60 years (2), CD4 + T cell counts ≤ 263.5/ul, and (3) IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio ≤ 2.66, yielding a total score range of 0–3. The new prognostic model stratified patients into low-risk (0–1 scores) and high-risk (2–3 scores) groups, with the low-risk group demonstrating significantly superior OS (p < 0.0001) and PFS (p < 0.0001) compared to the high-risk group (Fig. 4A-B). Next, we performed ROC analysis to compare the sensitivity and specificity of survival prediction between the newly established IRPS model and the IPI model. The results that the IRPS model exhibited marginally improved risk discrimination for OS prediction compared to the IPI score, with AUC values of 0.7295 and 0.6095, respectively (Fig. 4C). To further evaluate whether IRPS could refine IPI risk stratification, we reclassified patients across all IPI risk categories (low-risk: 0–1; intermediate-risk: 2–3; high-risk: 4–5) using the IRPS score. As shown in Fig. 5, our novel model could effectively discriminate both OS and PFS across all IPI risk stratifications (low-risk group, p < 0.01; intermediate-risk group, p < 0.01; high-risk group, p < 0.01).

Validation of the new immune-related prognostic score model

To confirm the predictive accuracy of this novel prognostic model in patients with newly diagnosed DLBCL, we collected a validation cohort from the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University during the same study period. A total of 47 patients with complete data meeting the inclusion criteria were enrolled, comprising 27 males (47.8%) and 20 females (53.2%) with a median age of 55 years and followed through July 2024. Survival probabilities were analyzed using Kaplan-Meier curves after stratifying patients into high- and low-risk groups based on IRPS scoring criteria. The results revealed statistically significant differences in both OS (Fig. 6A; p < 0.001) and PFS (Fig. 6B; p < 0.001) between high- and low-risk groups stratified by the IRPS model, confirming its prognostic validity. Notably, the IRPS model showed non-inferior prognostic performance relative to the IPI model, achieving a slightly higher AUC value (0.7941 vs. 0.7818) in the validation cohort (Fig. 6C).

Discussion

Despite rapid therapeutic advances in DLBCL, patient outcomes remain highly heterogeneous, influenced by clinical factors, biologic and molecular subtypes, and other undiscovered factors [18]. The immunobiology underlying DLBCL pathogenesis and progression involves a multifaceted interplay between systemic immune dysfunction, local immune microenvironmental interactions and genomically driven immune evasion mediated through malignant B-cell intrinsic aberrations. Thereinto, low absolute lymphocyte counts, and aberrate peripheral lymphocyte subset distributions reflect systemic immune status and predict poor prognosis in DLBCL patients, while tumor infiltrating lymphocytes abnormity represents local immune microenvironment [19, 20]. Generally speaking, lymphocyte subsets are typically composed of CD4 + T cells, CD8 + T cells, B lymphocytes, and natural killer (NK) cells [21]. CD4 + helper T cells enhance anti-tumor immunity by activating cytotoxic CD8 + T cells and secreting immunomodulatory cytokines. CD8 + cytotoxic T cells (CTLs) directly lyse tumor cells via perforin and granzyme-mediated cytotoxicity [22, 23]. The genomic aberrations like HLA-I/II mutations, amplification of 9p24.1 in malignant B cells create an immunosuppressive microenvironment characterized by impair T-cell infiltration and PD-L1 upregulation [24].

Multiple studies have identified CD4 + T cells as an independent prognostic factor in DLBCL, with both higher circulating and intratumoral CD4 + T cell correlating with better clinical outcomes [7, 10, 25]. Consistent with these studies, our study similarly demonstrated that CD4 + T cell counts in peripheral blood at initial diagnosis was positively correlated with both OS and PFS. It was reported that DLBCL with high density of CD8 + infiltrating T cells seemed to have improved outcome. For examples, Rajnai et al. found that tumour-infiltrating CD8 + T cell count was an independent favorable prognostic marker for survival in primary bone DLBCL [26]. Shi and colleagues identified that high CD8 + T-cell infiltration density in tumor tissues correlated with prolonged PFS in Chinese DLBCL patients, independent of rituximab treatment status [27]. Gergely et al. proposed that patients with low pretreatment CD8 + T cell counts in B-cell non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma had significantly lower OS rates [28]. Nevertheless, the prognostic significance of peripheral blood CD8 + T cell counts remains unexplored in broader DLBCL populations. Surprisingly, in our study we further observed that elevated peripheral blood CD8 + T cell counts at initial diagnosis were associated with improved PFS and OS, although the adjusted p-value did not reach statistical significance. Since peripheral CD8 + T cells correlate with tumor-infiltrating T cells and generate cytotoxic CTLs [29], we propose that their depletion impairs tumor cytotoxicity, worsening DLBCL survival. NK cells, serving as surrogate markers of immune status, demonstrate significant associations with clinical outcomes in DLBCL patients during the rituximab era [30]. However, no significant association was found between peripheral NK-cell levels and DLBCL survival outcomes in our study. Although peripheral B-cell data in DLBCL remain limited, Rusak et al. discovered that while diagnostic B-cell counts lacked clinical value, post-R-CHOP B-cell expansion predicted inferior outcomes [20]. Although post-treatment B cell levels were not analyzed in our study, we similarly demonstrated that baseline B cell counts at initial diagnosis showed no prognostic significance in DLBCL patients.

As we know, T lymphocytes function as primary effector cells in mediating cellular immunity, with cytokine secretion representing a fundamental mechanism of their biological activity and immune regulation. According to cytokine profiles and immune regulatory functions, CD4 + T cells differentiate into specialized subsets, including Th1, Th2, Th17, and T follicular helper cells, with Th1 and Th2 representing two pivotal effector lineages [31]. Th1 cells induce tumor cell apoptosis, senescence, and functional inactivation by secreting cytokines such as IFN-γ, IL-2, and TNF-α. As the dominant Th1 cytokine, IFN-γ reinforces Th1 differentiation through activating STAT1/STAT4 signaling. In contrast, Th2 cells promote tumour growth and metastasis by activating STAT6 through IL-4 [16, 32, 33]. Th1/Th2 imbalance is a common mechanism for immune escape and is closely related to cancer development and prognosis. Abnormalities in CD4 + T cell compartment, including the Th1 and Th2 subsets and related cytokines, have been shown to affect the occurrence of non-Hodgkin lymphoma [7]. However, whether Th1/Th2 imbalance impacts prognosis in newly diagnosed DLBCL patients remains largely undetermined. Given that Th1 response activation is primarily driven by IFN-γ while Th2 differentiation depends on IL-4, we speculated the peripheral blood IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio as a clinically surrogate for Th1/Th2 balance and investigated its prognostic role in DLBCL. Although intracellular cytokine staining and transcriptional profiling of activated T cells are gold-standard methods for Th1/Th2 assessment, their complexity and cost hinder clinical scalability. In contrast, the peripheral IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio offers practical advantages through operational simplicity, rapid results, and cost-effectiveness for routine use. Here, our study identified the IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio as a promising predictor of both OS and PFS in DLBCL. Notably, we stratified patients into high- and low-ratio groups using an optimal cutoff value for survival analysis. The efficacy assessment revealed that patients with high IFN-γ/IL-4 ratios showed superior treatment responses, with significantly increased CR/PR rates compared to low-ratio counterparts. In addition, the longitudinal analysis of IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio dynamics during treatment revealed sustained elevation in patients with favorable clinical outcomes. Mori et al. employed flow cytometric analysis of intracellular IFN-γ/IL-4 to evaluate Th1/Th2 balance, demonstrating Th2 polarization in untreated DLBCL patients versus Th1 dominance in complete remission cases [15]. This conclusion together with our findings indicate that high IFN-γ/IL-4 levels (reflecting Th1 polarization) correlate with favorable outcomes (improved response, reduced relapse), suggesting their functional role in DLBCL pathogenesis. Interestingly, Arima et al. discovered that dysregulated Notch signaling in B cells alters T-cell responses by amplifying regulatory T (Treg) and Th2 cell activity while suppressing Th1 and CD8 + T-cell cytokine production, thereby contributing to immune privilege in mature B-cell neoplasms [34]. Furthermore, next-generation sequencing (NGS) revealed that DLBCL patients harboring NOTCH1 mutations had reduced complete response rates and inferior PFS/OS [35]. Therefore, we proposed that Notch signaling may contribute to Th1/Th2 imbalance in DLBCL. However, Dehghani et al. demonstrated in their cohort study of 47 Hodgkin’s lymphoma and 48 DLBCL patients that higher peripheral blood IFN-γ-/IL-4 + Th2 might be associated with a favorable prognosis like lower rate of relapse. They also noted that cHL patients with relapsed disease, high-risk IPI scores, performance status (PS) ≥ 2, and B symptoms exhibited a higher frequency of Th1 lymphocytes but lower mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) of IFN-γ compared to their newly diagnosed counterparts [36]. Therefore, whether plasma IFN-γ level correlates with IFN-γ MFI of Th1 lymphocytes in DLBCL remains poorly understood. Thus, our future study might investigate the IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio, IFN-γ/IL-4 MFI in Th1/Th2 cells, Th1/Th2 ratio in patients with sustained remission versus relapse simultaneously to further determine their prognostic significance.

Due to the marked heterogeneity in DLBCL patient survival outcomes, comprehensive risk assessment prior to treatment initiation is essential for optimal therapeutic strategy selection. Currently, the IPI, R-IPI, and NCCN-IPI are the most commonly employed prognostic scoring systems in DLBCL. However, all 3 scoring systems failed to identify a patient subgroup with long-term survival clearly < 50% in the rituximab era [4, 37]. Advances in molecular profiling have increasingly highlighted the critical roles of both systemic and tumor microenvironmental immunity in DLBCL pathogenesis, driving recent proposals to incorporate immune-related biomarkers into prognostic scoring systems [8, 38, 39]. Although peripheral CD4 + T cell count demonstrated prognostic significance in DLBCL, it has not been incorporated into existing prognostic scoring systems. Moreover, the IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio represented a novel and highly predictive biomarker among the evaluated immune parameters we mentioned above in DLBCL. While age demonstrated only marginal significance for OS in our study, we retained it in the new model due to its well-established prognostic role in DLBCL and widespread incorporation into existing risk stratification systems. Thus, we developed an Immune-Related Prognostic Score (IRPS) by integrating age and these two immunologic parameters identified in multivariate analysis, effectively stratifying DLBCL patients into low-risk (0–1 points) and high-risk (2–3 points) groups. Encouragingly, validation at another independent center replicated the model’s predictive performance. What’s more surprisingly, ROC curve analysis demonstrated a modest improvement in prognostic performance of our novel model compared to the IPI scoring system. Additionally, the IRPS model could further substratified each IPI risk category (low/intermediate/high) patients into distinct low- and high-risk subsets. Integrating the IRPS model with conventional IPI scoring improves risk stratification by detecting unfavorable-prognosis patients originally classified as IPI low-intermediate risk, thereby offering timely opportunities to apply appropriate therapeutic interventions to potentially enhancing survival and prognosis. Moreover, these clinical indicators are cost-effective and readily available, facilitating their seamless integration into routine clinical practice.

Our study has several limitations. Firstly, as a single-center retrospective analysis with limited sample size, our findings warrant larger prospective validation to more rigorously assess multifactorial interactions and minimize selection bias. Secondly, incomplete cytokine/lymphocyte data availability restricted the validation cohort size, limiting the prognostic model’s assessment. Thirdly, although patients with active infections (which may alter serum cytokine levels) were excluded, plasma IFN-γ/IL-4 ratios may not fully reflect the Th1/Th2 balance, requiring further validation studies. Fourthly, the immunosuppressive landscape in DLBCL encompasses multifaceted mechanisms (Th1/Th2 balance, PD-L1/PD-1 axis activation and Treg recruitment), with the IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio representing only one quantifiable component [39]. Finally, although molecular subtypes offer valuable insights for prognostic assessment and therapeutic decision-making in DLBCL, its relationship with outcomes and IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio remained unexplored owing to insufficient data.

Conclusion

In summary, our study demonstrates that the IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio might serve as a promising prognostic biomarker in newly diagnosed DLBCL patients. The Immune-Related Prognostic Score (IRPS) model, incorporating IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio, age and CD4 + T cell counts, may offer enhanced risk stratification when combined with the IPI system, potentially enabling more tailored treatment strategies. Given the retrospective nature of this study, our findings warrant validation through large-scale, prospective multicenter investigations in DLBCL patients.

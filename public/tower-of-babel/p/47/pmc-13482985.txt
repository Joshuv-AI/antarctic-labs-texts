Prognostic importance of tumour deposits with and without concurrent lymph node metastases in patients with stage III colon cancer
Authors: Clara Brinkmann, Andreas H Nordholm, Peter-Martin Krarup, Nis H Schlesinger, Anisoara Iordache, Camilla Qvortrup, Henry G Smith
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTumour deposits (TD) are associated with poor prognosis in patients with colon cancer. However, the current staging system only acknowledges TD in patients without lymph node metastases (LNM). This study investigated the prognostic impact of TD with or without LNM in patients with stage III colon cancer.MethodsThis was a national retrospective cohort study using the Danish Colorectal Cancer Group database, including patients with stage III colon cancer who underwent curative surgery between 2017 and 2022. Patients were stratified into 3 groups: TD alone; LNM alone; and TD and LNM. The primary endpoint was overall survival (OS).ResultsOf the 18 395 patients registered during the study period, 10 241 underwent surgery with curative intent and 9807 had a TD assessment. Of the 3543 stage III CRC patients included, 1091 had TD (30.8%). Of these, 884 had TD and LNM (25.0%), whereas 207 had TD alone (5.8%). Patients with TD and LNM had significantly poorer survival than those with LNM alone (5-year OS 52.2% versus 69.2%; P < 0.001). On subgroup analyses, the presence of TD was associated with poorer OS across all N categories. On multivariable analysis, TD was independently associated with all-cause mortality (hazard ratio 1.25; 95% confidence interval 1.08 to 1.43; P = 0.002). Patients with TD only were significantly less likely to receive adjuvant chemotherapy than those with LNM or TD and LNM (42.5% versus 52.2% and 55.9%, respectively; P = 0.002).ConclusionTD are common in patients with stage III colon cancer and are associated with poorer prognosis across all N categories.Keywords: colorectal cancer, TNM, cancer staging, colorectal surgery

Introduction

Tumour deposits (TD) are defined as discrete, discontinuous tumour nodules that are found within the colorectal mesentery without any identifiable lymphatic, vascular, or neural structures1,2. TD are recognized as a poor prognostic feature, being associated with increased risks of both systemic recurrence and disease-specific death in patients with colon or rectal cancer3–11. This has been acknowledged since the seventh edition of the tumour node metastases (TNM) staging system (2009)12, in which the N1c category was applied to patients with TD without lymph node metastases (LNM), classifying these patients as having stage III disease and therefore being eligible for adjuvant chemotherapy.

Despite their recognized prognostic importance, several questions remain regarding TD in patients with colon cancer. A major limitation of the current staging system is that it does not consider that TD can, and often do, occur in patients who also have LNM. In patients with concurrent LNM, TD currently have no impact on pathological staging and their prognostic value is lost. Although several studies have demonstrated poorer survival in the presence of TD, many of these studies included patients with stage I–III disease3,6–8. As such, the prognostic importance of TD in patients with stage III disease is unclear, as is the extent to which this may be overlooked by current staging systems. Furthermore, there are concerns that the prognostic implications of TD in patients with N1c disease are not acted upon. Although only investigated in a limited number of studies, adjuvant chemotherapy appears to be underused in patients with TD alone compared with patients with LNM6,9,13.

This study sought to determine the prognostic impact of TD with or without concurrent LNM in patients with stage III colon cancer and investigate variations in the use of adjuvant treatment in these patients.

Methods

Design and study population

This was a retrospective national cohort study and is reported according to the STROBE guidelines14. Patients diagnosed with Union for International Cancer Control stage III colon cancer in Denmark between the 1st of January 2017 and the 31th of December 2022 were identified from the Danish Colorectal Cancer Group (DCCG) database. This national cancer registry includes at least 95% of all patients diagnosed with colorectal cancer in Denmark and has recently been validated, with an overall data accuracy of >95%15. This study period was chosen because the eighth version of the TNM staging system and an updated definition of TD were first adopted in Denmark in 2017; before this, the fifth version of the TNM staging system had been used.

Patients undergoing a potentially curative surgery for a newly diagnosed primary colon cancer were included in the study cohort. Patients with synchronous colorectal cancers and those undergoing palliative operations, stents, or local excisions were excluded. Clinicopathological variables were extracted directly from the DCCG database. Patients were stratified into three groups according to the presence of TD and LNM as follows: TD alone; LNM alone; and TD and LNM. The original pathology assessments from each centre were used for these stratifications. R1 resections were defined as the presence of viable cancer cells ≤ 1 mm from the resection margin. Eligibility for adjuvant chemotherapy was defined as age < 80 years and an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status (PS) <2.

This study was approved by the Danish Data Protection Agency (P-2024-17091).

Outcomes of interest

The primary outcome of this study was overall survival (OS). Secondary outcomes included clinicopathological variables associated with TD with or without LNM, and patterns of referral for and receipt of adjuvant chemotherapy. A subanalysis was conducted to investigate factors associated with all-cause mortality.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive analyses of differences in clinicopathological variables according to TD and LNM status were performed using the χ2 test for categorical variables and the Kruskal–Wallis test for continuous variables. All analyses were two-sided and considered statistically significant at P < 0.05. OS was calculated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared between groups using the log-rank test. Univariable and multivariable Cox regression analyses were performed to identify prognostic factors for OS. The following factors were selected for these analyses a priori: age, sex, ECOG PS, tumour location, pathological (p) T category, TD status, pN category, extramural venous invasion (EMVI), lymphatic invasion, perineural invasion, margin status, and adjuvant chemotherapy. All variables were selected based on clinical relevance because they are recognized prognostic factors for outcomes in colon cancer. All variables were included in the multivariable Cox regression analyses, which were performed using backwards stepwise regression. No evidence of unstable estimates suggesting problematic multicollinearity was observed. The results of the Cox regression analyses are presented as hazard ratios (HRs) with their 95% confidence interval (c.i.). All analyses were performed using SPSS® version 25.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

In all, 3543 patients were included in the study cohort (Fig. 1). Most patients were male (1810, 51.1%), and the median age was 72 (interquartile range (i.q.r.) 65–79) years. TD were present in 1091 patients (30.8%), the majority of whom had concurrent LNM (884 patients, 25.0% of the cohort and 81.0% of patients with TD). The median follow-up was 48 (i.q.r. 27–75) months.

Pathological characteristics associated with TD and LNM

The cohort was split into 3 groups for further analyses, namely patients with TD alone (207, 5.8%), patients with LNM alone (2452, 69.2%), and patients with both TD and LNM (884, 25.0%). The clinicopathological demographics of these patients are summarized in Table 1. No differences in patient age, co-morbidities, or tumour location were noted among these groups. Patients with LNM alone were more likely to be diagnosed by screening colonoscopy and to have a less advanced T category than those with TD, with or without LNM. Patients with LNM alone also had a less advanced N category than patients with TD and LNM (N2: 26.4% (648 of 2452 patients) versus 57.7% (510 of 884 patients); P < 0.001). Compared with the other groups, patients with both TD and LNM had higher rates of other adverse pathological features, including EMVI, lymphatic invasion, perineural invasion, and R1 resection margins.

Referral for and receipt of adjuvant chemotherapy

In all, 2534 patients (71.5%) were eligible for adjuvant chemotherapy, with no differences between the groups (Table 2). However, patients with TD alone were significantly less likely to be referred for adjuvant chemotherapy than those with LNM alone or concurrent TD (62.8% (130 of 207 patients) versus 71.2% (1747 of 2452 patients) and 72.3% (639 of 884 patients), respectively; P = 0.008). Accordingly, patients with TD alone were also significantly less likely to receive adjuvant chemotherapy than patients with LNM alone or concurrent TD (42.5% (88 of 207 patients) versus 52.2% (1281 of 2452 patients) and 55.9% (494 of 884 patients), respectively; P = 0.002). Among eligible patients with TD alone, slightly better survival was seen among those who received adjuvant therapy than among those who did not, although the difference did not reach statistical significance (5-year median OS 79.3% (67.7–87.1%) versus 61.5% (46.3–73.6%), respectively; P = 0.071; Fig. S1). When considering all patients, the median time from surgery to the start of adjuvant therapy was 32 (i.q.r. 27–41) days, with no differences noted between groups.

Overall survival

The 5-year OS for the entire cohort was 64.8% (95% c.i. 63.0% to 66.6%). OS was comparable between patients with TD alone and LNM alone (66.9% (95% c.i. 58.9% to 73.7%) versus 69.2% (95% c.i. 67.1% to 71.2%), respectively). However, significantly poorer OS was seen in patients with both TD and LNM (52.2%; 95% c.i. 48.3% to 55.9%; P < 0.001; Fig. 2a). Similar differences in 5-year OS were seen among the TD alone, LNM alone, and TD and LNM groups when only patients who had received adjuvant chemotherapy were analysed (78.8% (95% c.i. 67.5% to 86.5%), 82.4% (95% c.i. 79.9% to 84.6%), and 63.5% (95% c.i. 58.4% to 68.1%), respectively; P < 0.001; Fig. 2b). Subgroup analyses were then performed to investigate the impact of TD within each N category. In these analyses, the presence of TD was associated with poorer survival in patients with N1a, N1b, N2a, and N2b LNM (Fig. 3).

Prognostic factors for all-cause mortality

Univariable and multivariable analyses were performed to investigate factors associated with all-cause mortality. The results of these analyses are summarized in Table 3. TD were independently associated with all-cause mortality (HR 1.25; 95% c.i. 1.08 to 1.43; P = 0.002). Other factors associated with all-cause mortality included age, male sex, ECOG PS, T category, N category, EMVI, perineural invasion, R1 resection margins, and the receipt of adjuvant chemotherapy.

Discussion

Using the DCCG database, a national cancer registry, this study found that TD are common in stage III colon cancer, occurring in almost one-third of patients, most of whom had concurrent LNM. TD were associated with other high-risk pathological features, an association that was even more evident in patients with concurrent LNM. The presence of TD was also found to have a significant impact on the prognosis of patients with concurrent LNM, being associated with poorer survival across all N categories. Although TD have previously been shown to be strongly associated with other adverse pathological features3,5–8,10,11,16, their continued independent association with OS after multivariable adjustment in the present study suggests that they are not merely a surrogate marker of aggressive disease but rather capture additional prognostic information. Furthermore, and of some concern, this study found that patients with TD alone were less likely to be referred for, and subsequently to receive, adjuvant chemotherapy.

Although numerous studies have investigated the incidence and impact of TD in patients with colorectal cancer, many of those studies included patients with various stages of disease, and often both those with colon and rectum cancers. As such, the real incidence and impact of TD in patients with stage III colon cancer is unclear. A recent meta-analysis10 including over 10 000 patients reported a mean TD frequency of 22%, with marked variation between studies. The same meta-analysis10 found a higher incidence of TD in patients with than without LNM (41.6% versus 8.7%), as well as increased rates of TD in patients with more advanced N categories. However, it should be noted that that meta-analysis included studies published between 1994 and 2015, some of which included patients treated in the 1960s. During this period, the definition of TD has changed several times, alongside increasing recognition of its prognostic importance. By only including patients with stage III colon cancer in a more recent time period, the present study allows further and more precise detail to be added to these previous findings, with TD found in 30% of patients, of whom 80% had concurrent LNM.

The present study also found that patients with concurrent TD and LNM had significantly higher rates of other adverse pathological features than patients with TD or LNM alone. This included EMVI, lymphatic invasion, perineural invasion, and R1 resection margins. Several studies have investigated the association of TD and EMVI before, with a higher rate of TD seen in patients with than without EMVI (31.6% versus 20.9%)10. However, comparisons between patients with and without concurrent LNM are lacking. Two large retrospective studies16,17 of the National Cancer Database from North America compared pathological features between subgroups of patients with stage III cancer. These studies16,17 demonstrated higher rates of both lymphovascular invasion and perineural invasion in patients with TD and concurrent LNM than in those with either TD or LNM alone. However, no comparisons regarding EMVI were reported. One of these studies17 also reported higher rates of R1 resection margins in patients with TD and concurrent LNM, although likely only regarding margins related to the primary tumour. In the present study, both R1 resection margins to the primary tumour or to LNM/TD were more than twice as common in patients with TD and concurrent LNM. Recent studies18–21 have demonstrated that both types of R1 subdivisions are associated with increased recurrence risk, which appears to be related to a more aggressive biological phenotype rather than to suboptimal treatment. Together, these data suggest that the finding of concurrent TD and LNM is an indicator of particularly aggressive tumour biology, characterized by the presence of multiple additional adverse prognostic factors.

In keeping with these associations, the present study also demonstrated that patients with concurrent TD and LNM had poorer overall survival. Although several studies have previously demonstrated an association between TD and poorer survival, as stated above, these studies are limited by heterogeneity in terms of both disease stage and tumour location10. However, in the two studies16,17 of the National Cancer Database referred to above, concurrent TD and LNM was associated with significant reductions in OS compared with patients with TD or LNM alone. The same associations have been reported in similar analyses of the Surveillance, Epidemiology and End Result database3,22, although these studies also included patients with rectal cancer. Further evidence can be found from post hoc analyses of randomized trials of adjuvant chemotherapy in patients with stage III colon cancer. Among 1942 patients randomized to either 3 or 6 months of adjuvant therapy in the IDEA trial5, significantly poorer 3-year disease-free survival was seen in patients with than without TD, a finding that was consistent across different N categories. The same associations were found among the 2028 patients included in the CALGB/SWOG 80702 trial4.

These findings highlight the limitations of the current TNM staging system, in which TD are considered only in patients without LNM. Because TD most often occur with concurrent LNM, the current TNM staging system ignores the prognostic value of TD in the majority of patients, and in those who appear to have the poorest oncological outcomes. Several suggested improvements to the TNM staging system have been proposed23. One strategy would be to combine the total count of LNM and TD when allocating patients to different N categories. In both post hoc analyses of the IDEA5 and CALGB/SWOG 807024 trials, patients who were restaged in this manner from N1 to N2 disease had similar survival outcomes to those original staged as N2 based on LNM alone. Other proposals include adjusted combinations either with weighting of TD compared with LNM, comparing the ratio of TD and LNM to lymph node yield or staging using logarithmic models24–26. Such combination strategies implicitly assume that TD and LNM represent similar biological processes. Increasing evidence suggests that this is not the case27. Recent translational studies have elegantly demonstrated the biological distinctions between TD and LNM, with the former having a more invasive phenotype characterized by processes involved in epithelial–mesenchymal transition28. With the new ninth edition of the TNM staging system29, the definition of TD has been broadened, aiming to improve reproducibility in diagnosis. However, the continued prognostic value of TD even in patients with N2 disease shown in the present study and in other studies3–5 seems to provide a clinical correlate for the biological distinction between these forms of locoregional spread and, as such, this modification to the TNM staging system still leaves a significant gap in risk stratification. A modification that considers LNM and TD as separate entities rather than a combined factor may be more appropriate.

Concerningly, the present study also demonstrated lower rates of referral for and receipt of adjuvant chemotherapy in patients with TD alone. The reasons for this are not entirely clear. Similar discrepancies have been reported in earlier studies6,9,13,17, where the rates of adjuvant therapy in patients with N1c disease ranged from 40% to 60%. These studies all included patients treated in the years before or after the establishment of the N1c category, and some reclassified the N category based on new histological analyses, which may partly explain these differences. Although no statistically significant differences in OS were observed in the present study between patients with N1c disease who did or did not receive adjuvant chemotherapy, these subgroup analyses are limited by the relatively small number of patients included. The trend towards improved survival with adjuvant chemotherapy is consistent with findings from larger population-based studies, which demonstrate significant survival benefits in patients with N1c disease13. No data are available in the DCCG database regarding reasons for non-referral or the non-receipt of adjuvant therapy. Further work to determine the reasons behind these discrepancies in treatment appears warranted.

This study has several limitations. The DCCG database does not include data on local or distant recurrence; therefore, it was not possible to compare other oncological outcomes across the different patient groups. Although one may argue that OS is the most relevant endpoint for patients, it would be of interest to investigate the impact of TD on recurrence patterns. There is evidence to suggest that patients with TD and concurrent LNM have higher rates of liver, lung, and peritoneal metastases than patients with LNM alone10. Furthermore, although there were no significant differences in age, American Society of Anesthesiologists grade, or ECOG PS between groups, the possibility of differences in non-cancer-related mortality cannot be excluded. A further limitation of this study is that data on the type or duration of adjuvant chemotherapy were unavailable. Despite their association with poorer prognosis, TD are not specifically considered as a high-risk feature in current guidelines when allocating patients to short- or long-course adjuvant chemotherapy12. It would be of interest to investigate potential variations in the type and duration of adjuvant chemotherapy used in these patients and to determine the extent to which such variations can explain the differences in survival observed between study groups. However, because the adverse prognostic impact of TD was also observed among patients receiving adjuvant chemotherapy, the effect cannot be explained solely by differences in treatment allocation between groups. In addition, previous studies3–5,8,16,25 have used the absolute number of TD, whereas in the present study TD were only available as a binary outcome. In these previous studies, a stronger association with a poorer prognosis was seen with an increasing number of TD3–5,8,16,25. As such, the binary definition used in the present study may, in fact, underestimate the negative prognostic impact of multiple TD. Despite this, the consistent prognostic effect observed across different N categories suggests that even using a binary outcome to classify the presence of TD alone identifies a high-risk subgroup. As discussed above, there is ongoing debate as to how TD can best be integrated into a novel staging system; as such, it would also have been of interest to compare the prognostic value of absolute TD counts versus a binary definition between these groups. Finally, moderate interobserver variability in the assessment of TD has been reported, and this variability appears to have increased with the introduction of newer versions of the TNM staging system, in which the definition of TD has become more complex30–32. Because the present study is based on original pathology reports, it is not possible to comment on the degree of such interobserver variability or account for its potential impact on the study’s outcomes. However, by using data only from patients categorized using the eighth edition of the TNM staging system, the present study reduces the risk of stage migration and ensures that all patients are evaluated using a consistent, contemporary set of criteria. In addition, using routine pathology reports from a national registry reflects real-world clinical practice and enhances the generalizability of the results.

In conclusion, TD are common in patients with stage III colon cancer and are consistently associated with poorer prognosis across all N categories. The consistent prognostic impact of TD across all N categories suggests that TD represent a biologically distinct form of locoregional tumour spread that is not adequately captured by current TNM staging or current clinical guidelines.

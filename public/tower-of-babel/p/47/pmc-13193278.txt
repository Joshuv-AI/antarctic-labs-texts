An in trans NOTCH3 loss-of-function variant modifies disease severity in CADASIL: A family-based study
Authors: Shih-Chun Lan, Yi-Hong Liu, Jui Lan, Yi-Chu Liao, Min-Yu Lan, Yi-Chung Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground:Cerebral autosomal dominant arteriopathy with subcortical infarcts and leukoencephalopathy (CADASIL) is caused by pathogenic NOTCH3 variants, most commonly heterozygous cysteine-altering variants. Disease severity varies widely, and the impact of reduced NOTCH3 dosage on CADASIL severity remains unclear. We describe a family of four individuals carrying a cysteine-altering NOTCH3 variant and/or a frameshift loss-of-function (LoF) variant, demonstrating marked intrafamilial phenotypic heterogeneity.Methods:Four related individuals underwent standardized clinical assessment, brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), genetic analysis, skin biopsy, and NOTCH3 complementary DNA (cDNA) sequencing.Results:The proband, carrying p.Cys1146Ser and p.Cys1024Valfs248 in trans, developed progressive gait disturbance, dysphagia, and slurred speech beginning in his mid-thirties. Brain MRI demonstrated extensive white matter hyperintensities, multiple lacunar infarcts, and numerous cerebral microbleeds. In contrast, the father (carrying p.Cys1146Ser alone) and the mother and brother (carrying p.Cys1024Valfs248 alone) exhibited milder clinical and imaging phenotypes. Only the carriers of the cysteine-altering variant demonstrated NOTCH3 extracellular domain (NOTCH3ECD) immunoreactivity in skin vessels. NOTCH3 cDNA sequencing from the skin tissue demonstrated reduced LoF allele expression.Conclusion:Reduced NOTCH3 dosage may modify the disease severity of CADASIL.Keywords: Biallelic NOTCH3 variants, CADASIL, LoF variant, Loss-of-function variant, NOTCH3

1. INTRODUCTION

Cerebral autosomal dominant arteriopathy with subcortical infarcts and leukoencephalopathy (CADASIL) is the most common monogenic cerebral small vessel disease (cSVD), which is characterized by subcortical ischemic stroke, cognitive impairment, psychiatric symptoms, and migraine, showing interindividual variability in disease severity.1 CADASIL is caused by pathogenic variants in the NOTCH3 gene, which encodes a single-pass transmembrane receptor highly expressed in mural cells of small cerebral vessels and essential for vascular development, structural integrity, and function.1 Most patients carry heterozygous cysteine-altering variants affecting one of the 34 epidermal growth factor-like repeat domains (EGFr) in the extracellular portion of the NOTCH3 receptor (NOTCH3ECD).1 These cysteine-altering variants lead to extracellular aggregation of NOTCH3ECD and deposition of granular osmiophilic material (GOM) around vascular smooth muscle cells and pericytes, which is a pathological hallmark of CADASIL.2

Besides cysteine-altering variants, NOTCH3 loss-of-function (LoF) variants also contribute to cSVD. A recent large multicenter observational study has indicated that monoallelic NOTCH3 LoF variants are associated with a CADASIL-distinct, mild or subclinical cSVD, which is characterized by white matter abnormalities, without increased stroke risk or NOTCH3ECD accumulation, and with phenotypic expression influenced by aging and cardiovascular risk factors.3 In contrast, biallelic NOTCH3 LoF variants cause a rare, childhood-onset arteriopathy with leukoencephalopathy and stroke, highlighting the importance of NOTCH3 dosage for cerebral vascular integrity.4,5

However, the impact of NOTCH3 dosage on CADASIL severity remains unclear. Prior studies have shown that CADASIL patients carrying cysteine-altering variants in EGFr 10-11 (corresponding to the ligand-binding region of the NOTCH3 receptor) tend to exhibit a more pronounced white matter hyperintensity (WMH) burden,6,7 underscoring the relevance of NOTCH3 signaling in disease expression. Here, we report a family in which a proband carrying a cysteine-altering NOTCH3 variant in trans with a frameshift LoF variant developed early-onset and severe cSVD (Fig. 1A), whereas relatives carrying either variant alone exhibited markedly milder phenotypes. By integrating clinical features, brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), skin biopsy pathology, and complementary DNA (cDNA) sequencing, we explore whether reduced NOTCH3 dosage may modify CADASIL severity.

2. METHODS

This study was approved by the institutional review boards of Taipei Veterans General Hospital, Taipei, Taiwan (2017-02-008A), and Kaohsiung Chang Gung Memorial Hospital, Kaohsiung, Taiwan (202401844A3). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. The proband, his parents, and younger brother underwent standardized clinical evaluation, including detailed history taking, neurological examination, brain MRI, and skin biopsy. Brain MRI was performed using a 3-T scanner (SIGNATM, GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, WI). Targeted resequencing covering the entire NOTCH3 coding region was performed on genomic DNA extracted from the proband’s whole blood. Identified variants were confirmed by Sanger sequencing, followed by family segregation analysis.

Skin biopsies were obtained from the lateral thigh and processed as previously described.8 GOM was assessed by transmission electron microscopy, and NOTCH3ECD accumulation in vessel walls was evaluated by immunohistochemistry using anti-NOTCH3ECD antibody (clone 1E4, Millipore, dilution 1:1000, RRID:AB_2890101). NOTCH3 cDNA sequencing was performed using mRNA extracted from skin biopsy tissues.

3. RESULTS

The proband, a late-thirties man, presented with progressive unsteady gait, dysphagia, and slurred speech beginning in his mid-thirties. Neurological examination revealed pathological laughter, generalized hyperreflexia, spasticity, and gait disturbance. Neuropsychological assessment demonstrated processing speed and working memory impairments. Brain MRI showed extensive WMHs with multiple lacunar infarcts involving bilateral centrum semiovale, basal ganglia, thalami, and pons (Fig. 2A), as well as numerous cerebral microbleeds predominantly in the basal ganglia. Stroke risk factor evaluation was unremarkable except for elevated serum low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (138 mg/dL). Targeted resequencing of NOTCH3 identified two variants, c.3070del (p.Cys1024Valfs*248) and c.3437G>C (p.Cys1146Ser) (Fig. 1B), which were confirmed to be present in trans. The frameshift variant (p.Cys1024Valfs*248), located in EGFr 26, is predicted to result in a truncated NOTCH3ECD, whereas the cysteine-altering p.Cys1146Ser variant, located in EGFr 29, represents a typical pathogenic CADASIL variant. Segregation analysis showed that the proband’s father carried p.Cys1146Ser alone (Fig. 1B), while the proband’s mother and younger brother carried p.Cys1024Valfs*248 alone (Fig. 1B).

The proband’s father, an early-60-year-old man, exhibited no neurological abnormality apart from occasional dizziness. His vascular risk factors included hypertension and coronary artery disease diagnosed at age 51 years, for which he underwent coronary angioplasty with stent placement and received regular medical treatment. He had smoked cigarettes since his early twenties but quit after the coronary event. Brain MRI showed scattered small and semiconfluent WMHs without lacunar infarcts or microbleeds (Fig. 2B). The proband’s mother had hypertension and experienced a pontine infarction at age 57 years, with residual left lower limb spasticity and left upper limb dysmetria. Her brain MRI showed a right paramedian pontine infarct and several small WMHs (Fig. 2C). The proband’s younger brother, a mid-30-year-old adult, was asymptomatic apart from episodic non-migrainous headache and had no vascular risk factors. Brain MRI demonstrated only scant punctate WMHs (Fig. 2D). Overall, the proband had markedly more extensive cSVD than other family members.

Immunohistochemical staining of skin biopsies demonstrated NOTCH3ECD immunoreactivity in arterioles and venules of p.Cys1146Ser carriers (the proband and his father), but not in carriers of p.Cys1024Valfs*248 alone (the proband’s mother and brother) (Fig. 3). GOM was not detected by electron microscopy in any family member. NOTCH3 cDNA sequencing from skin tissue demonstrated reduced expression of the p.Cys1024Valfs*248 allele in all three carriers, which is consistent with incomplete nonsense-mediated decay (Supplementary Fig. 1, https://links.lww.com/JCMA/A376).

4. DISCUSSION

This family showed a striking intrafamilial variation in cSVD severity associated with different NOTCH3 allelic states. Both monoallelic NOTCH3 variants, the cysteine-altering p.Cys1146Ser variant and the LoF p.Cys1024Valfs*248 variant, were linked to relatively mild clinical and imaging phenotypes. In contrast, the proband carrying both variants in trans developed early-onset and severe cSVD characterized by extensive WMHs, multiple lacunar infarcts, and numerous cerebral microbleeds.

Skin biopsy findings further support the presence of distinct pathogenic mechanisms. NOTCH3ECD immunoreactivity was observed in skin vessels of individuals carrying the cysteine-altering variant, including the proband and his father, but it was absent in family members carrying the LoF variant alone. This pattern indicates that CADASIL-related pathogenic changes linked to NOTCH3ECD accumulation were present only in association with the cysteine-altering variant, whereas monoallelic NOTCH3 LoF variants were not associated with this hallmark pathology.

The cysteine-altering variant p.Cys1146Ser is located in EGFr 29. Under the recently proposed three-tiered EGFr risk stratification,9 EGFr29 is categorized as a low-risk domain and is generally associated with milder phenotypes compared with higher-risk EGFr. Therefore, the relatively mild clinical and imaging findings observed in the father are consistent with this genotype–phenotype framework. Accordingly, the proband’s severe phenotype is unlikely to be explained by the EGFr domain risk alone and instead supports the modifying role of reduced NOTCH3 dosage.

Recent multicenter studies have shown that monoallelic NOTCH3 LoF variants are associated with a CADASIL-distinct cSVD phenotype, which is characterized by white matter abnormalities without increased stroke risk or NOTCH3ECD accumulation.3 In this family, the relatively mild imaging findings observed in the proband’s mother and brother, who carried the LoF variant alone, are consistent with this reported phenotype. Importantly, our findings do not suggest that a monoallelic NOTCH3 LoF variant independently causes severe CADASIL. Rather, the markedly more severe phenotype observed in the proband supports a modifier model in which reduced NOTCH3 dosage may influence disease expression.

Although GOM is widely regarded as a pathological hallmark of CADASIL, its detection in skin biopsy is not universal. Reported sensitivity varies across studies and may depend on biopsy depth, the number of arterioles examined, mutation position, disease stage, and technical or interpretive factors.10,11 Thus, the absence of detectable GOM in this family does not exclude CADASIL pathology and may reflect a relatively mild disease burden, mutation-specific effects, or sampling limitations.

The markedly more severe phenotype in the proband suggests that reduced NOTCH3 dosage may modify CADASIL disease severity. Previous studies demonstrated that cysteine-altering variants affecting the NOTCH3 ligand-binding region are associated with reduced NOTCH3 activity and increased WMH burden or early-onset stroke,6,7,12 highlighting the role of NOTCH3 activity in disease expression. In this context, the presence of a LoF variant in trans with a cysteine-altering variant might have reduced NOTCH3 activity. Together with NOTCH3ECD accumulation, these combined genetic effects might have contributed to the more severe phenotype observed in the proband.

This study has several limitations. First, it is based on a single-family observation. Second, we did not directly evaluate downstream NOTCH3 signaling activity or target gene expression. Therefore, the impairment of NOTCH3 activity remains inferred rather than directly demonstrated. Furthermore, because cSVD is strongly age-dependent, comparisons between the proband and his younger brother should be interpreted cautiously, as age may partly confound phenotypic differences. Nevertheless, the concordance between genotype, skin vascular pathology, and clinical severity supports a modifying role of NOTCH3 dosage. Our findings emphasize the importance of allelic context when interpreting NOTCH3 variants and suggest NOTCH3 dosage may contribute to disease heterogeneity in CADASIL.

These findings may have implications for genetic counseling. Monoallelic NOTCH3 LoF variants are often considered mild or subclinical when identified in isolation.3 However, in the presence of a coexisting pathogenic cysteine-altering variant, reduced NOTCH3 dosage may influence disease expression. Hence, careful interpretation of allelic context may be important when counseling families with complex NOTCH3 genotypes.

Design and Assessment of Channeling Control Systems for Enhanced CO2 Storage in Fractured Reservoirs
Authors: Ende Zhan, Li Liu, Yanfu Pi, Chuanmin Xiao, Jing Yang, Fushan Li, Mengyi Xing, Baoyi Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
To address the issues of suboptimal mobility control and unstable displacement fronts in traditional WAG technology for continental fractured heterogeneous reservoirs, which lead to severe gas channeling, a novel enhanced channeling control system utilizing a CO2-responsive gel-assisted WAG was designed. The novelty of this approach lies in the integration of in situ gel plugging with a tapered WAG ratio, which synergistically enhances recovery by dynamically improving mobility control and stabilizing the displacement front. Under simulated reservoir conditions, the injectivity and plugging performance of M-Gel for grade I–III fractures were evaluated. Results demonstrate that M-Gel exhibits excellent injectivity and effectively plugs grade I–III fractures, achieving a plugging efficiency of up to nearly 80% for the most intensely fractured grade I fractures. Subsequently, for a reservoir model with grade I fracture development, an M-Gel-assisted WAG enhanced channeling control system was constructed. Orthogonal experiments were employed to optimize the water–gas ratio injection strategy and gel concentration. The study found that adopting a tapered injection mode with the water–gas ratio decreasing from 3:1 to 1:1 and an M-Gel concentration controlled at 0.5 wt % yields significant gas channeling inhibition. Under this system, gas breakthrough time is significantly delayed, interlayer utilization effectiveness is enhanced, and efficient utilization of the high-permeability layer is maintained at 78.5%. Meanwhile, the utilization degree of the low-permeability layer increases markedly from 9.6% under conventional WAG to 21.4%, representing an increase of 11.8%. The overall recovery factor reaches 60.8%. Microscopic visualization model displacement experiments suggest that using this system, the residual oil in microscopic pore throats is markedly reduced. Analysis indicates that the designed enhanced channeling control system effectively leverages the synergistic effects of gel-induced fracture plugging and multicomponent WAG-mediated flow field regulation, suppressing gas channeling caused by strong heterogeneity in fractured reservoirs and thereby further improving crude oil recovery.

Introduction

CCUS technology, − as a crucial carbon emission reduction measure, holds significant importance for achieving the goals of “carbon peaking” and “carbon neutrality.” Implementing CCUS-EOR in the oil and gas industry serves the dual purpose of utilizing greenhouse gases as a resource and improving oil recovery. − However, during CCUS-EOR, factors such as fractures, − strong heterogeneity, − and viscous fingering , often lead to CO2 channeling, which reduces oil recovery and storage efficiency. When CO2 channeling is severe, the gas–liquid ratio at production wells increases substantially, liquid production drops significantly, and both the oil recovery from gas displacement and geological storage efficiency decline rapidly. WAG, offering higher sweep efficiency and recovery compared to continuous gas injection, has become one of the primary technologies employed domestically and internationally for implementing geological CO2 storage combined with EOR displacement. − In CO2–WAG, alternating slugs of water and CO2 help control gas mobility and enhance both macroscopic sweep and microscopic displacement efficiency. Yan explored the mechanisms of CO2–WAG displacement under supercritical conditions using nonequilibrium molecular dynamics simulations in silica nanopores. The simulation results indicated that the dissolution of supercritical CO2 in the polar components of crude oil plays a dominant role in oil displacement; furthermore, the injected water slug provides a stable gas–water interface, displacing the miscible oil-gas slug from the pores, thereby enhancing oil recovery. Lei investigated the oil recovery performance of continuous CO2 injection and CO2–WAG under miscible and immiscible conditions using three-layer heterogeneous core displacement experiments. The results showed that the CO2–WAG displacement increased recovery by 12–17%. AlOtaibi compared the effects of continuous scCO2 displacement and scCO2–WAG injection on oil displacement efficiency in carbonate reservoir cores. After secondary water displacement, continuous scCO2 displacement and scCO2–WAG injection yielded additional recoveries of 17.5% and 23.3% OOIP in the core, respectively, attributed to the improved mobility ratio using WAG. Kamali et al. employed numerical simulation to study the displacement efficiency of three different modesCGI, Simultaneous SWAG, and WAGin a sandstone reservoir. The simulation results indicated that WAG achieved the highest recovery, followed by SWAG and CGI. Hosseini conducted core displacement experiments on sandstone cores using three injection modes: water displacement, CO2 displacement, and CO2–WAG injection. The recovery factor for each mode was measured in both the absence and presence of asphaltene deposition. It was found that both CO2 displacement and CO2–WAG injection effectively enhanced oil recovery in the sandstone reservoir compared to water displacement. Wang et al. investigated the oil displacement effectiveness following CO2–WAG injection under miscible conditions (18 MPa, 70 °C). They found that after CO2 injection, the recovery factor was 27.6% OOIP, while using the CO2–WAG injection mode increased it to 44.5% OOIP, simultaneously enhancing recovery and delaying the CO2 breakthrough. Although the CO2–WAG technology improves oil recovery and mitigates gas channeling to some extent, its effectiveness in continental heterogeneous fractured reservoirs remains limited due to poor mobility control and unstable displacement fronts. This drives the need for adaptive materials that can selectively plug fractures. CO2-responsive materials, which undergo reversible changes such as emulsification and gelation, have thus become a key research focus for intelligent conformance control. Research on responsive materials mainly includes CO2 surfactants, CO2 polymers, CO2 wormlike micelle systems, and CO2-responsive membranes. Gel systems are widely used as plugging materials in the oil and gas industry. They can mitigate gas channeling by reducing the formation heterogeneity and the mobility of CO2 relative to crude oil. During the geological storage of CO2, CO2-responsive gels can adaptively plug fractures and other CO2 leakage pathways to enhance storage stability. Liu et al. developed a CO2-responsive plugging gel using polyethylenimine, sodium dodecyl sulfate, and nanosilica, achieving a viscosity exceeding 12,000 mPa·s under selected conditions. The gel exhibited reversible viscosity changes, shear-thinning, and self-healing properties upon CO2 and N2 injection, along with stability under high temperature (90 °C) and high salinity (up to 20,000 mg/L) conditions. Pu et al. synthesized novel CO2-responsive preformed gel particles with an interpenetrating network to mitigate severe gas channeling and low sweep efficiency caused by significant permeability contrasts between matrix and fractures during CO2 displacement. Luo developed a copolymer whose aqueous solution viscosity could be increased simultaneously by heating and CO2, and added it to the aqueous block to control the gas channel and enhance the recovery rate during the enhanced WAG process. The final experimental results show that, compared with the traditional WAG, the HAPM-enhanced WAG mode has increased the total oil recovery rate by 21–22%. Further, Luo et al. developed an in situ high-pressure CO2-triggered gel system based on a smart surfactant, N-erucamidopropyl-N,N-dimethylamine (UC22AMPM), which was introduced into the aqueous slugs to control gas channeling in WAG processes. Core displacement tests using heterogeneous cores demonstrated that gas channeling was alleviated by plugging high-capacity channels due to the in situ gelation of UC22AMPM slugs upon their reaction with the pre- or postinjected CO2 slugs under shear flow, thereby driving chase fluids into unrecovered low-permeability areas and producing an 8.0% higher oil recovery factor than the conventional WAG mode.

Building upon this, a novel enhanced channeling control system was designed by integrating the CO2-responsive gel, M-Gel, with WAG technology. The M-Gel was composed of modified polyacrylamide, hexamethylenetetramine, and resorcinol. This composition was selected because the modified polyacrylamide provides a long-chain backbone for robust gel formation, while hexamethylenetetramine and resorcinol serve as efficient cross-linkers that rapidly react under the acidic environment created by CO2 dissolution. The M-Gel demonstrates superior stability at the target reservoir temperature and salinity compared with conventional chromium-based gels. The plugging performance of this CO2-responsive gel for fractured reservoirs was systematically evaluated. On this basis, targeting the characteristics of heterogeneous fractured reservoirs, a synergistic channeling control system was constructed by combining it with CO2–WAG technology, and key parameters such as the WAG ratio, injection scheme, and gel concentration were innovatively optimized to enhance the suppression effect on gas channeling and overcome the limitations of conventional WAG in channeling control. Comprehensive experimental results demonstrate that this CO2–WAG enhanced channeling control system can effectively alleviate the insufficient plugging effect of conventional WAG, further suppress gas channeling in heterogeneous fractured reservoirs, and enhance oil recovery.

Microscopic Displacement Characteristics of the Enhanced CO2–WAG Channeling Control System

Microscopic visualization experiments clarified the displacement mechanisms. Common techniques include microfluidics, , NMR, and CT. The latter two provide core-scale saturation images but lack real-time interfacial resolution. Microfluidics enables direct pore-scale observation.

The CO2 micromodel displacement experiments were conducted by using a microscopic visualization system (Figure ). The process began by placing the microfabricated glass micromodel into a high-pressure holder with sapphire windows and heating it to reservoir temperature. The microscope and light source were then adjusted to ensure clear imaging of the pore structures. After vacuuming, the model was saturated with formation water, followed by simulated oil injection at 0.02 mL/min under maintained back pressure. Finally, CO2 was injected at the same rate while the process was recorded until 2 PV had been injected, allowing real-time observation.

Optimization of Injection Parameters for Enhanced CO2–WAG Channeling Control System

Optimization of Water–Gas Ratio Injection Schemes

Five WAG schemes were tested: constant ratio (1:1), tapered injection ratio (3:1–1:1, 5:1–1:1), and reverse tapered injection ratio (1:1–1:3, 1:1–1:5). The relationship between oil recovery efficiency and injected PV under different schemes is shown in Figure . Results indicate:

Recovery factor versus injected PV under different water–gas ratio injection schemes for WAG displacement: (a) water–gas injection ratio = 1:1. (b) Tapered injection ratio = 3:1–1:1. (c) Reverse-tapered injection ratio = 1:1–1:3. (d) Comprehensive comparison of recovery factors under different injection schemes.

Figure shows the displacement experimental results under three different WAG ratios. The recovery efficiency from the low-permeability matrix zones is extremely low, all less than 10%, whereas that from the highly fractured, high-permeability zones exceeds 60%. The experiments indicate that the dominant flow pathways formed by the fracture network govern fluid flow: injected water or CO2 preferentially channels rapidly along high-conductivity fractures, leading to significantly earlier water/gas breakthrough timing in the high-permeability zones. In contrast, no water/gas breakthrough was detected in the low-permeability matrix zones due to inefficient fracture-matrix imbibition. When the tapered WAG ratio ranges from 3:1 to 1:1 (Figure b), the recovery efficiency from the fractured high-permeability zone is the highest, reaching 76.5%. Under the ratios 1:1 to 1:3 (Figure c) and the constant 1:1 ratio (Figure a), the values are 71.2% and 66%, respectively. The fracture-dominated flow mechanism significantly amplifies the impact of heterogeneity: Efficient displacement in high-permeability zones: The high conductivity of the fracture system causes injected fluids to preferentially channel through fractures. An initially high water ratio (3:1–1:1) uses water slugs to block some fracture pathways, inhibiting premature CO2 gas channeling. Subsequently, increasing the proportion of CO2 allows its dissolution and viscosity reduction effects to rapidly diffuse within the fracture network, promoting the stripping of oil from fracture walls. Concurrently, the swelling effect enhances the pressure drive between fractures and the matrix, improving recovery in the fractured zones. Limited utilization in low-permeability zones: The “bypass flow effect” caused by fractures makes it difficult for fluids to effectively sweep the low-permeability matrix zones. Under these three injection schemes, the recovery efficiencies from the low-permeability zones (Figure ) are relatively similar, at 6.5%, 9.6%, and 8.1%, respectively. Analysis attributes this to inefficient fracture-matrix imbibition; the mobility control effect of the WAG cannot overcome capillary resistance, resulting in oil retention within the matrix.

As shown in Figure a,b, when the injection ratio is further increased to 5 times, the recovery efficiency does not show significant improvement compared to the 3 times ratio. Analysis suggests that this is due to the extremely strong reservoir heterogeneity, where high-permeability channels dominate the fluid flow path, creating a “short-circuit effect”. Increasing the water slug proportion exacerbates ineffective water cycling and channeling along these high-permeability pathways, failing to effectively displace the remaining oil in the low-permeability matrix. It also disrupts the relatively optimal synergistic mobility control balance potentially achievable under the original 3:1 ratio and may aggravate the negative effects of gravity segregation. Without effectively plugging the high-permeability channels, the sweep efficiency of the WAG for the low-permeability matrix under the current geological conditions has approached a bottleneck. Simply increasing the water volume cannot provide sufficient energy to overcome its high flow resistance or significantly accelerate the capillary imbibition process; therefore, the recovery efficiency is difficult to enhance. Synthesizing the above experimental results, in fractured reservoirs, a tapered WAG injection ratio (3:1–1:1) best synergizes fracture conductivity and the CO2 dissolution effect. However, additional measures are still needed to plug fractures and enhance the utilization of the low-permeability matrix to enhance the overall recovery efficiency.

Optimization of M-Gel Concentration

To address the issue of low plugging efficiency caused by fracture channeling in WAG displacement technology for fractured reservoirs, we introduce a CO2-responsive gel (M-Gel) to preferentially plug high-permeability fracture channels and enhance displacement efficiency. Based on the previously optimized tapered WAG ratio (3:1–1:1), this section further screens the optimal gel concentration (0.4 wt %, 0.5 wt %, 0.6 wt %) through enhanced WAG displacement experiments. The results are shown in Figure : The 0.4 wt % M-Gel (Figure a), due to insufficient plugging strength, provides limited gas channeling inhibition, and the recovery from the low-permeability zone is only 16.3%. The 0.6 wt % M-Gel (Figure c) excessively blocks matrix-fracture pore throats, inhibiting imbibition; although the recovery from the low-permeability zone reaches 20.3%, it also significantly decreases in the high-permeability zone, resulting in lower overall recovery. The 0.5 wt % M-Gel (Figure b), by selectively plugging the main fractures, maintains the conductivity of microfractures. Utilization of the low-permeability zone is significantly enhanced, with matrix recovery efficiency increasing to 21.4% compared to that of the control group, an improvement of 11.8%. The recovery from the high-permeability zone is maintained at 78.5%, and the overall recovery reaches 60.8%. This indicates that the gel-assisted imbibition activates matrix pores, maximizing the recovery. Additionally, the introduction of M-Gel also significantly delays the water/gas breakthrough timing in the high-permeability zone.

Recovery factor versus injected PV for WAG displacement with different gel concentrations: (a) 0.4 wt %, (b) 0.5 wt %, (c) 0.6 wt %. (d) Comprehensive comparison of recovery factors for different injected gel concentrations.

As shown in Table , compared to the baseline control group, injecting the M-Gel at a concentration of 0.5 wt % results in an injection pressure increase controlled within a reasonable range, rising by only 25%. This demonstrates that M-Gel at this concentration possesses both excellent injectivity and effective plugging capability. Although the 0.4 wt % M-Gel concentration exhibits a lower injection pressure increase, its overall recovery efficiency is significantly lower, reflecting insufficient plugging strength, leading to poor gas channeling inhibition and difficulty in effectively controlling displacement phase fingering and bypass flow. Notably, when the concentration is increased to 0.6 wt %, excessive gel strength causes near-wellbore overplugging, resulting in a sharp 40% increase in injection pressure, severely restricting the effective transport of fluids to deeper reservoir zones. Furthermore, regarding potential formation damageparticularly to the low-permeability matrixit should be noted that the M-Gel system, at the optimized 0.5 wt % concentration, is designed to selectively enter high-permeability zones with minimal intrusion into low-permeability regions. More importantly, the gel’s plugging effect is not permanent but reversible over time, as the gel can gradually degrade under reservoir conditions. This reversible behavior helps mitigate long-term risks to both storage integrity and production, preserving the permeability of untargeted matrix zones while maintaining effective gas channeling control. Synthesizing the quantitative analysis of the concentration gradient experiments and the displacement dynamic response, this study ultimately determines the optimized solution: employ a tapered WAG injection mode, with the ratio transitioning from an initial WAG of 3:1 to 1:1, combined with the injection of a 0.5 wt % M-Gel system. This combined strategy, by precisely controlling plugging strength and the displacement flow field, maximizes gas channeling control and recovery enhancement while ensuring injectivity feasibility.

Microscopic Displacement Characteristics of the Enhanced CO2–WAG Channeling Control System

Microscopic Displacement Characteristics of Pure CO2 Displacement

Simulated oil is injected at the simulated injection well until complete saturation (Figure a), followed by CO2 injection for displacement experiments. As seen in Figure b, the displacement process is fundamentally governed by pore structure heterogeneity. The CO2 gas phase exhibits significant selective migration, leading to the rapid development of preferential flow channels. This channeling occurs as CO2, driven by viscous forces, preferentially invades high-permeability pathways, where the capillary entry pressure is lowest. Once established, these channels dominate subsequent flow, resulting in inefficient drainage. Conversely, oil residing in smaller pores, protected by a high capillary entry pressure, remains largely bypassed and trapped. This microscopic entrapment highlights a key limitation of gas displacement, where the interplay between viscous and capillary forces dictates that a significant portion of the original oil in place is not accessed, ultimately constraining the displacement efficiency.

Microscopic displacement characteristics of pure CO2 displacement: (a) initial oil saturation state and (b) CO2 displacement stage.

Microscopic Displacement Characteristics of the Enhanced CO2–WAG Channeling Control System

Figure a shows displacement using the enhanced CO2–WAG channeling control system (blue area) along the simulated injection well. From Figure b,c,d, it can be seen that, compared to the case of pure gas displacementwhere significant residual oil remains in pore throats (both small and large) and dead-end poresthe enhanced WAG displacement significantly reduces the microscopic residual oil in the throats of small and large pores and dead-end pores. Analysis indicates that, compared to single gas displacement, the introduction of this enhanced system yields significant effects: the plugging material preferentially enters and effectively plugs high-permeability channels, forcing subsequent displacing fluids to divert and sweep previously under-swept low-permeability pore networks, including fine pore throats and complex dead-end structures. Simultaneously, the periodically injected aqueous phase effectively controls the mobility of carbon dioxide, suppressing viscous fingering. The CO2 phase, through dissolution in crude oil, significantly reduces its viscosity, induces oil swelling, extracts light components, and reduces oil–water interfacial tension, among other physicochemical effects. These synergistically overcome the capillary trapping effect on crude oil. The combined action of this optimized sweep efficiency enhancement and synergistic enhancement of displacement efficiency ultimately leads to a significant and widespread reduction in microscopic residual oil content within the throats of large and small pores and inside dead-end pores. This strongly confirms that this enhanced channeling control system can substantially enhance oil utilization and ultimate recovery in complex pore structures by improving fluid sweep paths and microscopic displacement mechanisms. The study demonstrates the system’s efficacy at the core scale, and its performance in complex, heterogeneous formations at the reservoir scale warrants further investigation. Future work should, therefore, prioritize field-scale pilot testing to validate economic viability and long-term conformance control under realistic reservoir conditions.

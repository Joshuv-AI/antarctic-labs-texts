Early detection of self-care impairments in children with disabilities using an enhanced SE network optimized by ISCO algorithm
Authors: Minghe Zhu, Junya Fang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractChildren with disabilities frequently encounter considerable obstacles in acquiring self-care skills, which are vigorous for developing their independence and overall quality of life. The early detection of self-care deficits is important for prompt intervention and assistance. Nevertheless, current valuation techniques predominantly depend on manual evaluations, which can be subjective, labor-intensive, and often inadequate in recognizing subtle deficits. The purpose of this study is to create an artificial intelligence (AI)-enhanced self-care prediction system for children with disabilities, using machine learning to accurately and early identify self-care deficits. An innovative self-care prediction methodology is introduced based on Squeeze and Excitation Networks, refined through a modified metaheuristic algorithm known as the Improved Single Candidate Optimization Algorithm. This approach employs an extensive dataset to train the neural network, allowing it to discern intricate patterns and correlations. The experimental findings illustrate the efficacy of the proposed methodology, surpassing current techniques in predicting self-care deficits.Keywords: Artificial intelligence, Self-care prediction, Children with disabilities, Squeeze and excitation network, Improved single candidate optimization algorithm, Neural network, Machine learning, Disability detection, Early identification, Self-care impairmentsSubject terms: Neuroscience, Neurology

Introduction

Children with disabilities encounter a variety of challenges in their everyday lives, making the development of self-care skills crucial for developing their independence, autonomy, and overall well-being1. Skills such as feeding, dressing, and maintaining personal hygiene are dynamic for these children to occupy fully in social and educational settings for leading a healthy and satisfying life2.

Unfortunately, many children with disabilities consider these essential skills difficult to obtain, which can result in several adverse results, including a lessened quality of life, increased reliance on caregivers, and limited involvement in social and educational activities3.

The early detection of self-care difficulties is essential for prompt intervention and support4. Research indicates that early intervention can greatly enhance the prospects for children with disabilities, allowing them to cultivate the necessary skills and capabilities for independence and societal participation5.

Sharma6 introduced a system on the basis of machine learning for diagnosing and categorizing problems relevant to self-care in children diagnosed with mental and physical disorder7. Utilizing Partitioned Multifilter and Partial Swarm Optimization (PM-PSO) involved attribute selection, with the results being compared to those of PCA (Principal Component Analysis). The selected features were validated, trained, and tested using several classifiers, including C-4.5, Multilayer Perception (MLP), Naïve Bayes, and Random Tree. Tenfold cross-validation was employed for training, validation, and testing purposes. PCA identifies 32 features and demonstrated accurately classified samples with the accuracy values of 68.57% for C 4.5; 68.57% for MLP; 80% for Naïve Bayes; and 64.28% for Random Tree. The classifiers exhibited a substantial enhancement in performance when utilizing the PM-PSO attribute selector. The PM-PSO selected 50 attributes, resulting in accurately categorized instances as, including80% for C 4.5; 80% for MLP; 81% for Naïve Bayes; and 78.57% for Random Tree.

Syafrudin et al.8 suggested a model relevant to self-care called GA-XGBoost, which was the combined model of Extreme Gradient Boosting(XGBoost) and Genetic Algorithms (GA) to predict self-care issues of children that were disable. The selection of the feature subset has an impact on the model’s efficiency. Therefore, genetic algorithms have been employed to optimize the search for the best attribute subsets in order to enhance the model’s efficacy. The outcomes indicated that GA-XGBoost achieved superior efficiency in comparison with other predictive models and the findings of prior studies. Furthermore, a web-based self-care were designed and developed to assist experts in evaluating self-care issues encountered by children diagnosed with impairments. As a result, suitable therapy is given to the children, and all of them benefit from the treatments.

A research was conducted by Dardzińska-Głębocka and Zdrodowska9, in which it was endeavored to make a model of classification on the basis of existing samples and the ones gained via attribute selection. It was mentioned that the number 19 features were employed, and the optimal and accurate model was made. This model could achieve the accuracy level of 85%, which means that it could classify 85% of the children that were diagnosed with issues relevant to self-care.

Ali et al.10 conducted a study on predicting issues of self-care employing ANN (Artificial Neural Network), named MLP (Multilayer Perceptron)-progressive. There were stages in the current study, which have been carried out to verify the practicality of MLP-progressive, comprising validation of MLP-progressive over binary-class and multi-class datasets, influence investigation of the filters of suggested preprocessing on the efficacy of the model, and comparison result of MLP-progressive compared to other advanced models. The suggested MLP-progressive network could perform better than other current approaches and could achieve the categorization accuracy of 98.57% and 97.14% for binary-class and multi-class datasets, respectively.

Alyasin et al.2 suggested a method on the basis of artificial intelligence, including bagging classifier, MLP, support vector machine, and decision tree. This study has concentrated on multi-label and binary SCADI datasets. In order to overcome the issues of generalization and imbalance and improve the efficacy of the network, data shuffling and resampling approaches have been, in turn, employed. In addition, a hyper design attribute choice technique was employed while using MLP Recursive Feature Elimination (RF-RFE) and mutual-information statistics on the basis of forward deletion approach. The design gained an accuracy value of 99% for the datasets introduced before.

However, existing assessment techniques for identifying self-care challenges are frequently subjective, labor-intensive, and inadequate in recognizing subtle impairments11.

These shortcomings can result in delays in identification and intervention, ultimately lessening effectiveness and escalating costs. Current methods for assessing self-care impairments in children with disabilities generally depend on manual evaluations, including observations, interviews, and standardized assessments12.

While these approaches can yield useful insights, they often suffer from subjectivity, require considerable time, and may not adequately identify subtle impairments. For instance, observations might fail to encompass the full spectrum of a child’s abilities, and standardized assessments may lack sensitivity to the distinct needs and capabilities of children with disabilities13. Moreover, these methods often necessitate specialized training and expertise, which can hinder their broader implementation.

The advent of artificial intelligence (AI) holds significant promise for transforming the domain of disability assessment and support. It offers a more efficient, and precise method for recognizing self-care challenges in children with disabilities. AI-driven assessment instruments are capable of processing extensive datasets, discerning patterns and correlations, and delivering tailored recommendations for intervention and assistance. Moreover, these AI-based tools can be made to ensure user-friendliness, availability, and cultural sensitivity, thus enhancing their effectiveness for children with disabilities and their families.

Improved single candidate optimizer

Algorithm definition

Theoretical analysis and novelty of the proposed ISCO algorithm

The core innovation of this research is not only in the structural modification of the original SCO algorithm but also in augmenting two key components: a non-linear adaptive weight mechanism and a chaotic map-based stochastic strategy that further benefits exploration-exploitation balance and convergence behavior.

These improvements cannot be considered just few fiddling tunings, but actually provide a fundamental change to the search dynamics because it has the potential to change the search intensity of an algorithm taking into account the solutions quality and the iteration stage, helping overcome premature convergence as well as facilitating the global ability to search.

This built non-linear adaptive weight factor actually allows individuals with poorer fitness to explore larger territories while leading high-performing individuals toward more precise searches, which is a key factor in dealing with high dimensional complex optimization problems, such as hyperparameter tuning tasks in deep neural networks.

In addition, higher diversity and ergodicity into search process will be introduced via a more innovative Bernoulli shift chaotic map random number generator, enhancing the algorithms capability to avoid getting stuck in local optimums, a known limitation of many, gradient-based and population-based optimizers.

To further bolster these claims, we have extended the theoretical analysis provided in this section with mathematical formulations that describe how each enhancement improves overall performance.

Both contributions should advance the ISCO quite meaningfully, especially within the confines of such established single-solution-based optimizers when those optimizers are viewed in terms of application in deep learning, where optimization stability and precision are critical.

Model interpretability and feature importance

While the proposed SENet/ISCO model is indeed performant at detecting self-care impairments, it is important to recognize what features heavily influence the model’s predictions, thereby building trust, transparency, and clinical relevance.

To achieve this, we adopted SHAP methods (SHapley Additive exPlanations), which follow a game-theoretic approach to assess the contribution of each input feature for individual model predictions. The SHAP values allow for a global and interpretable measure of feature importance, enabling us to answer what features were most important, how certain feature values affected predictions, and to clinically validate the model’s reasoning in making these predictions.

In this study, the SHAP values were computed for all instances in the test set by the trained SENet/ISCO model, considering the model evaluation of the marginal contribution of each feature across all possible feature combinations to render a fair and consistent attribution of prediction outcomes.

The SHAP values are aggregated to provide a measure of global feature importance-the average overall effect a feature has on the model’s output-and local feature impact-the effect of individual feature values on specific predictions. Limitations

Even though the suggested SENet/ISCO model reveals promising outcomes in the early detection of self-care deficiencies in impaired children, several limitations can be identified, thus demeriting generalization, robustness, and practical usefulness.

Small dataset size and need for further validation

The SCADI dataset used here only contains 117 samples. This number is small for training deep learning models. Data augmentation increased the effective dataset size to 585 samples; however, the original dataset still remains limited in scope and variety. This bears on the generalization potential of the model to broaden populations, for example, children with various types of disabilities, different cultural backgrounds, or geographical regional dissimilarities.

To address this limitation stratified k-fold cross-validation is performed and employed statistical significance tests, such as Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, to achieve robustness. However, for future work, validation should include application of the model to larger, widely diverse datasets collected across multiple sites and regions to finally improve external validity and applicability of the method in varied populations.

Susceptibility to overfitting due to scarcity of training samples

Usually, deep learning models, particularly convolutional neural networks, require large volumes of data to learn proper internal representations without overfitting. Even though we adopted some data augmentation techniques such as rotation, scaling, flipping, and noise injection, the lack of a sufficient base set still leaves the problem of overfitting.

We adopted an early stopping approach, regularization (weight decay), and cross-validation to reduce this threat to an acceptable minimum level, resulting in more improvement in the generalization ability of the model. Future research should be directed towards further reducing overfitting and improving the robustness of the models through transfer learning, domain adaptation, or generating synthetic data.

Need for real-world testing in clinical or educational settings

As previously stated, the present study analyzes a retrospective dataset, which implies an absence of real-time evaluation in clinical or educational environments. The model does show great promise on the SCADI dataset, but its validity for real-world environments with often noisy, incomplete, and ambient-condition varying collected data remains to be verified.

To tackle this gap, we plan to conduct pilot studies in collaboration with occupational therapists and special education professionals, where the model can be deployed in controlled clinical or educational settings. These studies will help assess the practical usability, interpretability, and impact of outcomes on early intervention on the developed models.

No assessment of computational efficiency

Even though the SENet/ISCO is superior in accuracy, precision, and recall compared to existing methods, we did not evaluate especially the aspects of computational efficiency, like training time, inference speed, or memory usage, since they are critical for real-time deployments in environments where resources are wanted.

Conclusions

Children with disabilities encounter a variety of challenges in their everyday lives, making the development of self-care skills for improving their autonomy, independence, and overall well-being. Unfortunately, many of these children find it difficult to acquire such skills, which can result in several contrary results, including a diminished quality of life that is heightened reliance on caregivers and limited appointment in social and educational activities. The early detection of self-care deficits is significant for facilitating quick intervention and assistance; however, existing assessment techniques are labor-intensive, frequently subjective, and inadequate in identifying subtle impairments. To resolve this problem, the present study sought to create an artificial intelligence (AI)-based platform for predicting self-care capabilities in children with disabilities. The proposed methodology designed a modified version of a Squeeze and Excitation Network based on a modified metaheuristic algorithm. The algorithm was called the Improved Single Candidate Optimization Algorithm. Experimental results indicated that the suggested approach can surpass other similar techniques in achieving high levels of different efficiency metrics for identifying children with self-care challenges and facilitating early intervention and support. The platform’s competence to recognize subtle impairments and pinpoint children at risk of encountering self-care challenges can play a decisive role in averting the progression of more severe disabilities and minimizing the necessity for expensive and time-intensive interventions. Also, the proposed method can be unified into current assessment frameworks, thereby improving their precision and effectiveness. Despite the advantages of the proposed method, assessing the platform’s performance across diverse populations, creating user-friendly interfaces, and ensuring compatibility with existing healthcare systems can be more investigated. Moreover, research should order the development of personalized interventions and support strategies tailored to the unique needs of children identified by our platform.

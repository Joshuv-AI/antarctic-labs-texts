Sodium–glucose co‐transporter 2 inhibitors in severe estimated glomerular filtration rate deterioration across cardiovascular‐kidney‐ metabolic conditions: A pooled analysis of randomized trials
Authors: João Pedro Ferreira, Pedro Marques, Stefan D. Anker, Javed Butler, Gerasimos Filippatos, Abhinav Sharma, Francisco Vasques‐Nóvoa, Luís Mendonça, João Sérgio Neves, Milton Packer, Faiez Zannad
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AimsThe efficacy of sodium–glucose co‐transporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) among patients with cardiovascular‐kidney‐metabolic (CKM) conditions who experience severe estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) deterioration during follow‐up is not well established. The aim of this study was to assess the risk of cardiovascular outcomes and mortality after eGFR deterioration to <25 ml/min/1.73 m2 (and <20 ml/min/1.73 m2), and whether such eGFR deterioration modified the effect of SGLT2i across CKM populations.Methods and resultsPooled analysis of placebo‐controlled trials: EMPEROR‐Preserved, EMPEROR‐Reduced, EMPA‐REG OUTCOME, CANVAS‐R, and CREDENCE. Time‐updated models with stratification by study were used. The median follow‐up to eGFR deterioration was 17 months and total follow‐time was 29 months. Studied outcomes included heart failure hospitalization or cardiovascular mortality, the composite of cardiovascular mortality, stroke or myocardial infarction, and all‐cause mortality. Overall, 26 946 patients were included, of these 1392 (5.2%) experienced eGFR deterioration to <25 ml/min/1.73 m2 (and 613 [2.3%] to <20 ml/min/1.73 m2). Factors independently associated with a higher risk of eGFR deterioration were lower baseline eGFR and higher albuminuria, whereas allocation to SGLT2i was protective. eGFR deterioration was independently associated with a nearly twofold higher risk of subsequent cardiovascular outcomes and mortality. The beneficial impact of SGLT2i treatment on cardiovascular outcomes and mortality was maintained irrespective of patients experiencing eGFR deterioration (interaction‐p >0.1 for all outcomes). Patients who experienced eGFR deterioration were more likely to permanently discontinue treatment, without significant differences in treatment discontinuation rates between the SGLT2i and placebo groups.ConclusionsSevere eGFR deterioration during follow‐up was associated with an increased risk of subsequent cardiovascular events and mortality. SGLT2i reduced the probability and were beneficial irrespective of severe eGFR deterioration.

Introduction

Sodium–glucose co‐transporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) improve cardiovascular and kidney outcomes among patients with heart failure (HF), type 2 diabetes (T2D), and chronic kidney disease (CKD). 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 However, their efficacy among patients with an estimate glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) <25 ml/min/1.73 m2 is not well established, and most international guidelines and drug labels do not recommend their initiation in people with severely impaired kidney function. 6 , 7 , 8 , 9

The EMPEROR‐Reduced (Empagliflozin Outcome Trial in Patients with Chronic Heart Failure and a Reduced Ejection Fraction, NCT03057977) and EMPEROR‐Preserved (Empagliflozin Outcome Trial in Patients with Chronic Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction, NCT03057951) trials enrolled patients with eGFR as low as 20 ml/min/1.73 m2 but the subgroup of patients with eGFR between 20 and 25 ml/min/1.73 m2 was very small and underpowered to derive robust conclusions. 1 , 2 , 10 , 11 Even less clear is how to handle SGLT2i treatment when a patient deteriorates eGFR to <25 ml/min/1.73 m2 under treatment. This is a clinically important question because many doctors interrupt or permanently stop SGLT2i treatment when facing an eGFR decline to <25 ml/min/1.73 m2, because of the paucity of data in this population.

A previous analysis of the DAPA‐HF (Study to Evaluate the Effect of Dapagliflozin on the Incidence of Worsening Heart Failure or Cardiovascular Death in Patients With Chronic Heart Failure, NCT03036124) and DELIVER (Dapagliflozin Evaluation to Improve the Lives of Patients With Preserved Ejection Fraction Heart Failure, NCT03619213) trials in chronic HF patients, showed that of the 11 007 patients enrolled 3.2% experienced a deterioration of eGFR to <25 ml/min/1.73 m2 at least once in follow‐up. These patients had a higher risk of subsequent HF events and mortality but the effect of dapagliflozin was not modified by eGFR deterioration. 12

In this study, we aimed to assess the risk of cardiovascular outcomes and mortality after eGFR deterioration to <25 ml/min/1.73 m2 (and explore the associations with eGFR deterioration to <20 ml/min/1.73 m2), and determine whether such eGFR deterioration modified the effect of SGLT2i across pooled cardiovascular‐kidney‐metabolic (CKM) populations as enrolled in the EMPEROR‐Preserved, EMPEROR‐Reduced, EMPA‐REG OUTCOME (Empagliflozin Cardiovascular Outcome Event Trial in Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus Patients, NCT01131676), CANVAS‐R (A Study of the Effects of Canagliflozin on Renal Endpoints in Adult Participants With Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus, NCT01989754), and CREDENCE (Evaluation of the Effects of Canagliflozin on Renal and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Participants With Diabetic Nephropathy, NCT02065791) trials including nearly 27 000 patients overall.

Methods

Included studies

The following randomized, double‐blind, parallel‐group, placebo‐controlled and event‐driven SGLT2i trials were included in this analysis: (1) EMPEROR‐Preserved including adults with chronic symptomatic HF, a left ventricular ejection fraction >40%, and elevated natriuretic peptide levels with evidence of structural heart disease or a HF hospitalization within 12 months, randomized to either empagliflozin 10 mg/day or placebo 2 , 13 ; (2) EMPEROR‐Reduced including adults with chronic symptomatic HF, a left ventricular ejection fraction ≤40%, and elevated natriuretic peptide levels, randomized to either empagliflozin 10 mg/day or placebo 1 ; (3) EMPA‐REG OUTCOME including adults with T2D and a history of cardiovascular events or a high cardiovascular risk, randomized to either empagliflozin 10 or 25 mg/day or placebo 3 , 14 ; (4) CANVAS‐R including adults with T2D with a history of cardiovascular events or a high cardiovascular risk, randomized to either canagliflozin 100 mg/day or placebo 4 , 15 ; and (5) CREDENCE including adults with T2D and albuminuric CKD, randomized to either canagliflozin 100 mg/day or placebo. 5 , 16

The respective study protocols were approved by the Ethics Committees of each of the participating sites and countries. All patients gave written informed consent to participate in the studies.

Study visits

Patients were randomized in a double‐blind manner to receive placebo or SGLT2i (in a 1:1 ratio in all trials except EMPA‐REG OUTCOME where the randomization was performed 2:1 with 10 and 25 mg of empagliflozin vs. placebo), in addition to their usual therapy. Patients were periodically assessed at study visits for laboratory results, including eGFR, which was available at baseline and weeks 12, 32, 52, and every 24 weeks thereafter until the end of the studies. Some variations in the timing of the study visits occurred across studies, but these were uniformized using the closest time‐point to meet the above referenced visits. All randomized patients were followed for the occurrence of outcomes for the entire duration of the trials. The median duration of follow‐up for the whole cohort was 28.7 months (interquartile range [IQR] 23.1–36.7).

Study endpoints

The primary endpoint for the present analysis was a composite of adjudicated cardiovascular death or HF hospitalization, analysed as time‐to‐first event. In addition, we analysed a composite of cardiovascular death, stroke or myocardial infarction (MI), cardiovascular death, and all‐cause death.

Statistical analysis

With exception of EMPEROR‐Preserved and EMPEROR‐Reduced which allowed the inclusion of patients with eGFR ≥20 ml/min/1.73 m2, all other trials included patients with eGFR ≥30 ml/min/1.73 m2. Trial protocols did not mandate study drug discontinuation if the eGFR fell below the inclusion threshold during follow‐up. Patients who experienced eGFR deterioration to <25 ml/min/1.73 m2 (and to <20 ml/min/1.73 m2) at least once in follow‐up were identified using the uniformized study visits as above described. The first eGFR deterioration below the defined threshold was defined for the main analyses; additionally, we performed a sensitivity analysis considering patients who had persistent eGFR deterioration to <25 ml/min/1.73 m2 in two or more visits. eGFR was calculated with the use of the 2009 Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration equation. 17 Baseline characteristics were compared between those who did and did not experience eGFR deterioration. Continuous data are reported as mean ± standard deviation for normal distributions or median (IQR) for skewed distributions, and number (%) for categorical variables using the appropriate statistical tests. Stepwise backward selection models were used to explore the variables independently associated with eGFR deterioration; all variables with a p‐value <0.1 in Table 1 were included in the model and a p‐value of <0.05 was set for a variable to be retained in the final model. The association between eGFR deterioration and occurrence of subsequent outcomes was performed with the use of time‐updated parametric regression survival‐time models with Weibull survival distribution, where patients were considered at risk during the period before eGFR deterioration, and if eGFR deterioration occurred then the time‐point of eGFR deterioration was considered the start time for that individual patient. All models were adjusted for baseline age (continuous), sex (women vs. men), race (White vs. other), T2D status (yes vs. no), HF history (yes vs. no), and eGFR (continuous), with study stratification (5 levels). The potential modification of treatment effect was assessed with the use of eGFR deterioration‐by‐treatment interaction term in the time‐updated models. Analyses were performed based on an intention‐to‐treat approach. We explored permanent treatment discontinuation in both the SGLT2i and placebo arms. All analyses were intention‐to‐treat and complete‐case without data imputation. P‐values and 95% confidence intervals (CI) presented in this report have not been adjusted for multiplicity given the exploratory nature of this work. All analyses were performed using STATA® (StataCorp. 2025; Stata Statistical Software: Release 19; StataCorp LLC, College Station, TX,USA) using the virtual environment at Vivli® (https://vivli.org).

Results

Baseline characteristics by estimated glomerular filtration rate deterioration

A total of 26 946 patients were included in the analysis, of these 1392 (5.2%) experienced eGFR deterioration to <25 ml/min/1.73 m2. Patients who experienced eGFR deterioration were younger, more frequently women, non‐White, with higher blood pressure, and more frequently had a history of T2D and anaemia. eGFR was lower and urinary albumin‐to‐creatinine ratio (UACR) was higher among patients who experienced eGFR deterioration (37 vs 68 mL/min/1.73 m2 and 1654 vs 26 mg/g, respectively). The use of angiotensin‐converting enzyme inhibitors/angiotensin receptor blockers and loop diuretics was more frequent, and the use of mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist was less frequent among patients who experienced eGFR deterioration. CREDENCE and EMPEROR‐Preserved contributed with a higher proportion of patients with eGFR deterioration (Table 1 ). A similar pattern was observed for the 613 (2.3%) patients who experienced eGFR deterioration to <20 ml/min/1.73 m2 (online supplementary Table Appendix S1 ). A total of 191 (0.7%) patients had eGFR <25 ml/min/1.73 m2 at baseline, these patients were enrolled in EMPEROR‐Preserved (n = 122) and EMPEROR‐Reduced (n = 69), while excluded in all other trials. No patients were included with eGFR <20 ml/min/1.73 m2.

The description of eGFR missing values at each study visit is shown in online supplementary Table S2 .

The median (IQR) time‐to‐first eGFR deterioration to <25 ml/min/1.73 m2 was 17.4 (7.3–23.0) months.

Variables associated with estimated glomerular filtration rate deterioration

Variables independently and positively associated with eGFR deterioration to <25 ml/min/1.73 m2 were eGFR <60 ml/min/1.73 m2, higher UACR, anaemia, loop diuretic use, hypertension, inclusion in CREDENCE and EMPEROR‐Preserved, and being a woman; whereas randomization to SGLT2i decreased the odds of experiencing eGFR deterioration (Table 2 ). Similar findings were observed for eGFR deterioration to <20 ml/min/1.73 m2 (online supplementary Table S3 ).

Association of time‐updated estimated glomerular filtration rate deterioration with cardiovascular outcomes

In the placebo group, an eGFR deterioration to <25 ml/min/1.73 m2 was independently associated with a 1.7 to 1.9‐fold higher rate of subsequent cardiovascular outcomes and mortality (Table 3 and Figure 1 ). Slightly stronger associations were found with eGFR deterioration to <20 ml/min/1.73 m2 (online supplementary Table S4 ).

Sodium–glucose co‐transporter 2 inhibitor treatment impact according to time‐updated estimated glomerular filtration rate deterioration

The beneficial impact of SGLT2i treatment on cardiovascular outcomes and mortality was maintained irrespective of patients experiencing eGFR deterioration to <25 ml/min/1.73 m2, without treatment effect heterogeneity between those who did and did not experience eGFR deterioration (interaction‐p > 0.1 for all outcomes) (Table 4 and Figure 2 ). Overlapping findings were retrieved when excluding the 191 (0.7%) patients with eGFR <25 ml/min/1.73 m2 at baseline (data not shown). Additionally, as sensitivity analysis, no significant heterogeneity was found when considering sustained eGFR deterioration to <25 ml/min/1.73 m2 in two or more consecutive visits (interaction‐p > 0.1 for all outcomes) (online supplementary Table S5 ). No statistically significant heterogeneity was found when considering eGFR deterioration to <20 ml/min/1.73 m2 (interaction‐p > 0.1 for all outcomes); however, fewer patients experienced eGFR deterioration to <20 ml/min/1.73 m2 on SGLT2i (vs. placebo) and the estimates are overall neutral and imprecise in this subgroup (online supplementary Table S6 ).

Patients who experienced eGFR deterioration to <25 ml/min/1.73 m2 were 1.7‐fold (19% vs. 12%) more likely to permanently discontinue treatment after eGFR deterioration compared to those who did not experience eGFR deterioration, without significant differences in treatment discontinuation rates between the SGLT2i and placebo groups (Table 5 ). Discussion

To the best of our knowledge this is the largest study (with nearly 27 000 patients and 1400 eGFR deterioration to <25 ml/min/1.73 m2 events) across the spectrum of CKM conditions to demonstrate that the beneficial impact of SGLT2i treatment on cardiovascular outcomes and mortality is likely maintained among people who experience eGFR deterioration to <25 ml/min/1.73 m2 during follow‐up. In the absence of randomized trials, our findings support the continuation of SGLT2i treatment in patients with severe eGFR deterioration, and expand prior findings in patients with chronic HF. 12 Of course, methodical clinical judgement and a thorough investigation are warranted for each individual patient experiencing eGFR deterioration in the clinical setting; still, our findings support the continuation of SGLT2i treatment in most patients. It should be noted that the beneficial effects of SGLT2i were maintained despite a high rate of treatment discontinuation affecting almost one in five patients after experiencing eGFR deterioration.

An early eGFR ‘dip’ is a well‐established phenomenon which occurs within the first 3 to 4 months after SGLT2i initiation and is typically followed by eGFR recovery and slower long‐term eGFR decline. 18 , 19 , 20 , 21 However, eGFR deterioration is a different phenomenon which may occur much later in follow‐up (here 17 months on average) and is strongly associated with subsequently poor cardiovascular outcomes and mortality, whereas the eGFR ‘dip’ is not. In fact, the eGFR ‘dip’ is caused by a direct effect of SGLT2i on the kidney, whereas SGLT2i are protective against severe eGFR deterioration. In our study, randomization to SGLT2i was the strongest protective factor against severe eGFR deterioration, while having lower baseline eGFR and higher UACR were risk factors for experiencing eGFR deterioration throughout follow‐up. In fact, SGLT2i have been shown to interrupt the deleterious cycle between kidney function deterioration and HF events. 22 These findings may better inform clinicians on whom is at the highest risk and should be followed more closely for eGFR deterioration. While clinicians may be tempted to stop SGLT2i treatment when facing such severe eGFR deterioration, our findings suggest that SGLT2i discontinuation should be avoided whenever possible, because after SGLT2i withdrawal patients may experience a rapid deterioration of clinical status. 23

With some exceptions, 1 , 2 , 24 most SGLT2i trials excluded patients with eGFR <30 ml/min/1.73 m2. Still, previous studies have shown that the effects of SGLT2i were maintained in patients with low baseline eGFR (e.g. as low as 20 ml/min/1.73 m2 in the EMPEROR trials), but these were small subgroup analyses as patients with eGFR <30 ml/min/1.73 m2 were under‐represented in SGLT2i trials. For example, they were excluded from CREDENCE and represented less than 5% of patients in EMPEROR‐Preserved, 5 , 11 which were the trials with the highest risk of severe eGFR deterioration in the present analysis. Ongoing clinical trials in patients with advanced CKD (e.g. the RENAL LIFECYCLE trial to Assess the Effect of Dapagliflozin on Renal and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Patients with Severe CKD, NCT05374291; and more mechanistic trials such as the CARe‐MRI to assess the effect of Canagliflozin in Advanced Renal Disease With MRI Endpoints, NCT06182839) will provide evidence on SGLT2i treatment in patients with an eGFR <25 ml/min/1.73 m2. Notwithstanding, our analysis answers a different research question, which is whether patients already treated with SGLT2i and experiencing severe eGFR deterioration should continue treatment and, based on these findings, they probably should.

Some limitations and further considerations should be acknowledged in our study. Most importantly, these are post‐randomization findings and patients receiving SGLT2i were less likely to experience eGFR deterioration; therefore, these results should be interpreted with caution. Still, they largely replicate and expand prior findings with an SGLT2i (dapagliflozin) and HF trials not included in the present analysis, 12 which increases the external validity and robustness of the results, and expands the findings as a SGLT2i class effect. As severe eGFR deterioration was less frequent in the SGLT2i group, this leads to post‐randomization group imbalance and imprecise estimates given the relatively small sample size, particularly in the eGFR deterioration to <20 ml/min/1.73 m2 SGLT2i group. In this regard, because less patients experienced severe eGFR deterioration in the SGLT2i group, it is likely that these patients actually had a more severe kidney disease that could be difficult to prevent; therefore, it is plausible to observe an attenuated treatment response in this subgroup as the disease trajectory might have been difficult to change. The specific cause and aetiology of eGFR deterioration was not available in the datasets; still, investigators were encouraged to assess for potentially reversible causes of kidney dysfunction.

Conclusion

Severe eGFR deterioration during follow‐up affected more than 5% of patients across the CKM spectrum and was associated with an increased risk of subsequent cardiovascular events and mortality. SGLT2i reduced the probability and were beneficial irrespective of severe eGFR deterioration.

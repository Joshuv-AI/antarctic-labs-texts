The trends and hotspots of gut microbiota for breast cancer from 2005 to 2024: A bibliometric and visual analysis
Authors: Hongyi Liang, Xiaohui Sun, Guangxi Shi, Hanhan Chen, Jingwei Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Breast cancer (BC) is the most common malignant tumor among women in the world, and it is also the most common cancer type that causes female deaths. Accumulating evidence has indicated a close relationship between the gut microbiota (GM) and BC, encompassing aspects such as clinical diagnosis, carcinogenic mechanisms, and treatment. To date, no study has conducted a bibliometric analysis concerning BC and GM. Bibliometrics, as a methodological discipline for quantitative analysis of academic literature, is not only applied in basic medical research but also in clinical medical research.Methods:A search for literature was conducted related to BC and GM in the Web of Science Core Collection database from January 1, 2005 to July 31, 2024. Tools such as Microsoft PowerPoint 2019, Microsoft Excel 2019, VOSviewer 1.6.20, and CiteSpace 6.3.R1, were used to visualize not only publishing trends but also hotspots.Results:A total of 842 articles is included, including 456 articles and 386 reviews. Since 2016, the publishing industry has experienced rapid growth. As expected, the United States ranks highest in terms of literature quantity and average citation count. The University of Illinois is the most active institution, whereas the National College of Ireland receives the highest average number of citations. The 3 researchers with the greatest influence are Bai, Peter, Miko, Edit, and Ujlaki, Gyula. The International Journal of Molecular Science has accounted for the largest number of publications, while Science ranked as the most highly cited journal. According to keyword and topic analyses, “quality of life” and “tumor microenvironment” will be the hotspots and frontiers of future research.Conclusion:Overall, this field is receiving increasing attention. We found that the future research hotspots of BC/GM may focus on the mechanisms of tumor occurrence, microbiota combination therapy, and the development of new predictive biomarkers, providing effective suggestions and new insights for scholars.

1. Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) is the most common cancer among women, ranking first in the number of cancer related deaths worldwide.[1] Due to the characteristic of early symptoms and the absence of specific biomarkers for BC, the disease is frequently diagnosed at an advanced stage.[2] In addition, it demonstrates limited responsiveness to both conventional and targeted therapies, resulting in low survival rates, poor treatment outcomes, and an extremely unfavorable prognosis.[3,4] An increasing number of clinicians and researchers are engaged in BC research, aiming to identify approaches for early diagnosis and treatment of this disease.

The gut microbiota (GM) comprises a vast and heterogeneous community consisting of trillions of bacterial, fungal, and viral species, among which gut bacteria are the most prominent, colonizing the intestinal tract and assisting the host in carrying out various physiological functions.[5] According to statistics, there are over 1000 different types of bacteria in the human gut.[6] Advancements in targeted 16S rRNA pyrosequencing and metagenomic sequencing have led to a growing body of evidence indicating that GM is closely associated with human diseases, including diabetes, cardiovascular disease, autism, rheumatoid arthritis, and cancer.[7] Notably, recent studies have highlighted a substantial link between BC and GM. Researchers have identified several mechanisms through which GM influences BC, such as modulating the microbiota–immune–tumor axis, altering the tumor microenvironment, affecting the production of metabolites, promoting cancer-related inflammation, and modifying the efficacy of chemotherapy.[8] In addition, these findings suggest that GM may be a diagnostic biomarker and therapeutic target for BC.[9] Therefore, GM has made significant contributions to the development of BC.

Bibliometrics is a complex analytical method that uses mathematical and statistical techniques to examine publications and other forms of scholarly communication.[10,11] The term was initially proposed by Alan Pritchard in 1969.[12,13] In contrast to traditional systematic reviews, bibliometric analysis offers a framework for the quantitative assessment of academic literature, enabling the characterization of data distribution and features from various perspectives, thereby illustrating the knowledge structure, developmental trajectory, and emerging frontiers within a given research domain.[14,15] The advantage of this methodology is that, unlike other major review methods, it can thoroughly evaluate the entire topic of thousands of papers and help scholars new to the field to quickly identify refereed journals, key literature, and potential collaborators in the field, thus compensating for the subjectivity that may be present in traditional qualitative reviews.[16]

Some researchers have used bibliometric analysis to investigate the relationship between cancer and GM, such as colorectal cancer and lung cancer.[17–19] In the past 2 decades, an increasing number of clinical and animal studies have emerged between BC and GM. However, although a large number of original studies have been conducted to explore the relationship between BC and GM, there is no study that has systematically combed through this literature in a quantitative manner. As a result, the macro-knowledge structure of the field, the evolutionary trends of the core research forces (countries/institutions/authors), and the evolutionary paths of the cutting-edge dynamics are not yet clear. Accordingly, to offer researchers a comprehensive understanding of the evolution and current landscape in this field, we employed bibliometric tools to conduct a visual analysis of BC/GM research over the past 2 decades, alongside an evaluation of various bibliometric indicators. The primary objectives of this analysis were: to assess global trends in the BC/GM field between 2005 and 2024; to identify the most productive countries/regions, institutions, and authors in this area; to determine the most suitable journals for publishing BC/GM-related studies; to outline the principal research directions and evolving trends in BC/GM; and to predict future research frontiers, thereby offering new perspectives and insights for subsequent investigations.

2. Methods

2.1. Data sources

This study conducted a literature search using the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database. The reason for choosing this database is that it is the most user-friendly and easy tool for bibliometric research, as well as containing many high-quality journals worth noting and comprehensive citation index records.

2.2. Search strategy

The following is the search strategy: TS = (gut* OR intestine* OR gastrointestine*) AND (microbiota* OR microbiome* OR flora* OR microflora* OR bacteria*) AND (cancer* OR neoplasms* OR carcinoma* OR malignancy*) AND (breast* OR mammary*). Two researchers independently performed the literature search and publication screening on the same day to verify accuracy. The 2 researchers had a conversation and reached a consensus that any remaining differences would be resolved through a third round of negotiations. The inclusion and exclusion criteria for this literature are shown in Figure 1. The literature period is from January 1, 2005 to July 31, 2024. The research encompassed original articles and reviews. Early access, meeting abstracts, editorial materials, letters, proceedings papers, corrections, news items, and retracted publications were excluded. The publishing language is strictly limited to English.

Flowchart of the literature searching and screening in the study.

2.3. Data acquisition

Download all 842 search articles through WoSCC, and attach “Full Record and Cited References” for bibliometric tool analysis. Extract and save the annual literature count, citation count, author, country, organization, journal, impact factor (IF), keywords, references, etc. The IF and subject category quartile rankings are sourced from the 2023 Journal Citation Reports (http://clarivate.com/products/Webofscience). H-index is defined as the number of papers that have been cited h or more (h), obtained through the “citation report” function of WSCC. That is to say, an author with an h-index of 25 has published 25 articles, each of which has been cited at least 25 times. The h-index can evaluate the quantity and level of academic achievements of scholars.

2.4. Data analysis

In order to obtain a more comprehensive data evaluation, 6 tools were used for bibliometric and visual analysis, including Microsoft PowerPoint 2019, Microsoft Excel 2019, VOSviewer 1.6.20, CiteSpace 6.3.R1, Graphpad Prism 9.0.0, and the online analysis platform bioinformatics (https://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/). Use Microsoft PowerPoint 2019 to generate a research flowchart, and Microsoft Excel 2019 is responsible for integrating data from the Web of Science database and visualizing the publication numbers of authors. A more intuitive geographic distribution of publications across countries was created using data from VOSviewer, in which point size reflects document volume and line thickness between nodes indicates the extent of collaboration between countries. Annual trends in publication output were visualized with Graphpad Prism 9.0.0. In VOSviewer, each node typically represents a country, institution, or author; node size is determined by the number of documents, while the strength of connections between nodes corresponds to the number of collaborative relationships. CiteSpace was configured with the following parameters: time span (January 1, 2005–July 31, 2024), selection criteria (Top 40 per slice), link retention factor (LRF = 3), and g-index (k = 15). The g-index, which is derived from citation counts, addresses a limitation of the h-index by better reflecting the impact of highly cited papers, and serves as a selection criterion in CiteSpace. LRF refers to the parameter used to control the number of connections within a defined range. For example, LRF = 3 means that the maximum number of connections does not exceed 3 times the number of points.[20–22]

4. Discussion

A growing body of research indicates that the development and treatment of BC are closely linked to GM.[30] This study is the first bibliometric analysis of BC and GM to date. This article summarizes the development trends and potential research hotspots in this field over the past 20 years through the analysis of 842 literature, aiming to offer insightful perspectives for future investigations.

The volume of publications serves as the most straightforward metric for reflecting recent progress in a given field. According to the results in Figure 2, the number of literatures on BC/GM has generally increased over the past 20 years. It can be roughly divided into 3 stages: the stable period from 2005 to 2015, with little fluctuation in the annual literature volume, hovering between 10 and 21 articles; the period from 2016 to 2018 was an acceleration period, during which the number of papers increased significantly. Notably, over 40 papers were published in 2018, a trend that may be attributed to continuous advancements in GM technologies. The period from 2019 to 2024 witnessed the highest output of papers, characterized by a rapid increase in publication numbers that peaked in 2023. The reduction in article counts observed in 2024 may be due to the fact that the present study was initiated in July 2023. These findings may suggest that this field has garnered sustained interest within the academic community and represents a promising research focus. Accordingly, we hypothesize that this area will continue to experience robust growth.

Regarding country-level analysis, this study encompassed research originating from 77 nations. Most countries with high publication output are primarily located in North America, Asia, and Europe. The United States, China, Italy, Spain, and Australia serve as the principal contributors to research on BC and GM, each averaging over 100 articles, with more than half of these contributions originating from the United States and China. According to statistics, the United States ranks first in the world in terms of the total number of literature and citations, far exceeding other countries. It is evident that the United States possesses the most robust research capacity in this field. This may be associated with its well-established and sustained research infrastructure, abundant human resources, and substantial financial investment, as previously reported.[31] In addition, the significant investment of the National Institutes of Health in the Human Microbiome Project reflects the high output and influence of the United States, which provides a database and platform for interdisciplinary collaboration among the United States scholars.[32,33] Of note, China, as a developing country, holds the second position in total publication output, substantially surpassing several developed nations; however, its average citation count per article remains relatively low. This suggests that although China entered this research area relatively later, it has increasingly prioritized this field in recent years (a trend potentially linked to its large population base, strong research capabilities, and the rising annual incidence of BC within the country). From the perspective of international collaboration, nodes sharing the same color indicate cooperative relationships, and thicker connecting lines reflect higher frequencies of collaboration. Accordingly, the closest interactions are observed among the United States, Japan, and China, with notable collaborations also involving Italy, England, Australia, and Germany. Such collaborative networks can facilitate scientific advancement across countries, driving progress in this research domain and fostering mutually beneficial outcomes. By examining countries in this field from multiple angles, researchers can gain a more comprehensive understanding of which nations are currently at the forefront and which may serve as priority partners for collaboration (such as the United States, Japan, and China).

In terms of institutional analysis, the top ten organizations are distributed across China, the United States, Hungary, and Ireland. Among these, institutions from China and the United States account for 70%, which undeniably reflects the strong research capabilities of these 2 countries but also highlights an imbalance in the distribution of global academic resources. It is evident that the University of Illinois has made notable contributions in this field. Interestingly, although the National College of Ireland ranks fourth in total publication count, it holds the highest average citation rate (a phenomenon we attribute to its relatively late entry into this research area). Notably, the institutional overlay visualization map reveals limited close collaboration and communication among academic institutions across different countries, with most institutional cooperation remaining confined within national borders. We recommend that interinstitutional collaboration be strengthened across countries, particularly involving developing nations. In summary, by analyzing institutions from various perspectives, patterns of close multi-institutional collaboration have been identified, which may assist researchers in identifying suitable collaborative partners within this field.

In terms of author co-occurrence analysis, the authors with the most articles and the highest co-occurrence rate are Bai, Peter, and Miko, Edit, followed by Ujlaki, Gyula. It is worth noting that Goedert, James J. is the only person with an average citation count of over 100, although he only ranks fourth in terms of literature quantity. This may be because multiple related papers by Goedert, James J. have been published in Q1 journals. Regarding author co citation analysis, Setchell and KDR have the highest number of co-citations, at 202, ahead of Urbaniak, C and Goedert, JJ. Studies have shown that the author’s research on BC and GM has received widespread attention and has had a significant impact. Therefore, the author’s academic influence cannot be determined solely by the number of published papers, as there are many factors that affect the frequency of paper citations. In summary, these scholars have made significant contributions to the fields of BC and GM, and they may continue to achieve success in this area.

Journal analysis constitutes a key component of bibliometric studies, enabling researchers to efficiently identify appropriate target journals.[32] A summary of key information from the top 10 journals reveals that most are general in scope, spanning 4 main categories: oncology, immunology, gastroenterology, and nutrition. The International Journal of Molecular Science, Cancers, and Nutrients rank as the 3 most frequently utilized journals. Accordingly, research focusing on BC and GM is more likely to be published in such outlets. Notably, 80% of these journals are classified under Q1, indicating that articles in this area tend to achieve a higher academic ranking. In the co-citation analysis of journals, Science, Nature, and PLOS ONE emerged as the 3 most co-cited publications. It may be inferred that these journals are more inclined to publish high-quality research findings. In summary, current research on BC and GM primarily emphasizes basic science and translational medicine.

Citation analysis serves as a method for assessing the actual academic influence of literature within a specific domain and for identifying prevailing research priorities.[34] The application of GM in BC screening and treatment is explored through a summary of the ten most highly cited papers. The first 10 articles are all reviews, enabling young scholars to quickly grasp the research field. Rinninella, E., The most cited paper published by Rinninella et al (2019) in the journal Microorganisms focuses on the study of GM balance within and between individuals, emphasizing the close reciprocal relationship between GM variation and disease.[21] In fact, the imbalance of GM is not only related to intestinal diseases, but also to many extraintestinal diseases such as metabolic and neurological disorders. Understanding the reasons or consequences for the balance of these GM in health and disease, as well as how to maintain or restore a healthy GM composition, should be useful for developing promising therapeutic interventions.

References that receive a high number of citations from other studies during a specific timeframe are defined as exhibiting strong citation bursts, which can serve as indicators of emerging research trends within a given field.[35] The first outbreak in GM and BC related research originated from an article published by Atkinson C et al in 2005, which explored the gut bacterial metabolism of soy isoflavones and daidzein for the first time, thus exploring their correlation with human health.[36] This finding provided inspiration for investigations into GM and BC, given their interrelated nature to a certain extent. Additionally, a study by Goedert JJ et al, published in 2015, exhibited the highest citation burst intensity.[37] They carried out a population-based case–control pilot study to investigate the relationship between fecal microbiota and BC in postmenopausal women. They found that the composition of intestinal microbiota and estrogen dependence of postmenopausal women with cancer were low. Although it was not clear whether these factors would affect the risk and prognosis of cancer, it provided a thought for the correlation study between BC and GM.

Through literature review, keyword clustering and burst detection, along with qualitative keyword analysis, the prevailing research frontiers and hotspots can be categorized into 3 main areas: mechanisms underlying GM involvement in BC pathogenesis; the practical utility of GM in BC diagnosis; and the influence of GM on BC treatment. A growing number of studies suggest that GM contributes to the development of BC.[38–40]

More and more studies indicate that GM is involved in the development of BC.[41–43] Firstly, GM may influence BC progression via inflammatory responses, and “inflammation” has garnered increasing attention over the past decade.[44] Certain studies have indicated that metabolites derived from GM (such as lipopolysaccharides) can trigger inflammatory processes. Short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), which are products of GM fermentation and mainly include butyrate, acetate, and propionate, have also been implicated.[45] Multiple studies have demonstrated that butyrate exerts anti-inflammatory effects in BC while inhibiting cancer cell proliferation and promoting apoptosis.[46,47] Knorr et al found that Helicobacter pylori (HP) can secrete cytotoxin-associated proteins, thereby promoting chronic inflammatory oxidative stress and contributing to cellular carcinogenesis.[48] However, despite existing research on HP and BC, the association between HP infection and BC remains a subject of debate.[49,50] Further studies are warranted to address this inconsistency. Secondly, GM participates in immune responses (including both innate and adaptive immunity) thereby influencing BC development.[51] Thirdly, GM is involved in the metabolic breakdown of bile acids, which in turn affects BC progression.[52] Several studies have reported that bile acids may promote BC invasion by disrupting the lipid bilayer upon contact with cells.[53,54] Nevertheless, although evidence suggests that GM contributes to BC cell proliferation and disease progression, the precise underlying mechanisms remain unclear, highlighting the need for more targeted investigations in the future.[55]

Microbiome analysis holds potential as a rapid, noninvasive biomarker for the diagnosis, prognosis, and prediction of BC.[56] It is worth noting that many studies have found no significant difference in alpha diversity between BC and healthy individuals, so more research is needed in the future to explore this issue.[57,58] These studies offer insights into the noninvasive diagnosis of BC using microbial markers. Beyond its diagnostic applications, the microbiome may also serve as a prognostic indicator for BC. Collectively, these findings indicate that GM shows promise as an early, noninvasive, and accurate tool for BC detection, potentially reducing patient discomfort and medical costs.[59] However, most existing research has been conducted at the microbial level, underscoring the need for further animal studies and clinical trials to explore and validate its diagnostic utility in BC.

The treatment options for BC are very limited, and gemcitabine is the main chemotherapy drug.[60] We have conducted research on the efficacy of GM participation in BC treatment. Previous studies have shown that GM can affect the chemotherapy efficacy of BC.[61,62] In addition, metabolites of the microbiota can affect the efficiency of BC treatment. Some studies have found that the tryptophan metabolite indole-3-acetic acid produced by GM can enhance the efficacy of chemotherapy on BC.[63,64] Additionally, the hotspot affecting chemotherapy resistance has received widespread attention in the past decade.[65] Although immunotherapy has achieved significant success in malignant tumors, its effectiveness in BC has been poor so far.[66] Therefore, effective therapeutic approaches are urgently needed. Some studies suggest that in addition to affecting the effectiveness of chemotherapy, GM may also alter the immunotherapy of BC.[67] Bacterial therapy has also caught the attention of researchers, with fecal microbiota transplantation being the most common one. Fecal microbiota transplantation has been applied in the treatment of ulcerative colitis and Clostridium difficile infection; however, its application in BC treatment remains nascent. Gene modification combined with immunotherapy may represent a promising therapeutic avenue, although research in this area remains underexplored.[68] We hope to conduct more clinical trials in the near future based on existing promising animal experiments, which may completely change the treatment of BC.

5. Strength and limitations

This study has the following advantages: it is the first comprehensive bibliometric analysis in the field of BC and GM research. Using 6 tools (Microsoft PowerPoint 2019 Microsoft Excel 2019, VOSviewer 1.6.20, CiteSpace 6.3.R1, Graphpad Prism 9.0.0, and the online analysis platform bioinformatics). The data were visualized to the fullest extent possible. Additionally, in this study, certain keywords were either removed or consolidated to minimize irrelevant interference. Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged: first, the literature search was restricted to the WoSCC database. Second, due to language constraints, some non-English publications may have been omitted. Finally, although efforts were made to ensure the search strategy was as thorough and detailed as possible, some selection bias may persist because of the variety of keywords used. Despite these limitations, this study still provides a meaningful reflection of research trends and emerging hotspots in the field.

6. Conclusion

In summary, this research employed CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and an online bibliometric analysis platform to perform bibliometric visualization in the fields of BC and GM. It not only summarizes the current global research landscape regarding BC and GM but also offers valuable guidance and perspectives for future investigations. BC and GM research has gradually progressed from early correlation observations to mechanism exploration based on the “microbial-metabolite-immune” axis, and is rapidly moving toward clinical translation (e.g., prediction of biomarkers and adjuvant therapy). In the past 20 years, people’s interest in this field has increased. So far, the United States, China, and Italy have clearly emerged as the leading contributors in this field, and there remains a need to strengthen collaboration among international institutions.

In recent years, the mechanism by which GM contributes to BC development, its potential as a diagnostic and prognostic indicator for BC, and its influence on BC treatment have emerged as prominent and cutting-edge research topics. In the future, the integration of multi-omics data (macro-genome, metabolome) and the development of cross-national, multicenter clinical intervention trials will be the key to advance the field from knowledge accumulation to clinical practice.

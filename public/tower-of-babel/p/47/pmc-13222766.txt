Determining the Optimal Volume of Ropivacaine Combined with Dexmedetomidine for Programmed Intermittent Epidural Bolus in Labor Analgesia: A Randomized Dose-Response Study
Authors: Congzhong Song, Jianxin Ni, Chaohui Liu, Yang Xu, Zhong Mei, Jing Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe programmed intermittent epidural bolus (PIEB) technique is effective for labor analgesia. Although ropivacaine combined with dexmedetomidine is used for epidural labor analgesia, the optimal PIEB volume for this regimen remains unclear. This study aimed to determine the optimal PIEB volume for effective labor analgesia using 0.0625% ropivacaine with 0.4 μg/mL dexmedetomidine.MethodsParturients requesting labor epidural analgesia received a 20mL loading dose of 0.0625% ropivacaine mixed with 0.4μg/mL dexmedetomidine via PIEB. Eighty parturients were randomly assigned to four PIEB volume groups (6, 8, 10, or 12 mL) one hour after receiving a loading dose. Analgesia was maintained with the same formulation administered at 45-minute intervals. Effective epidural labor analgesia was defined as the absence of the need for patient-controlled or manual supplementation of analgesic medication within 6 hours after the loading dose or until full cervical dilation. Probit regression was used to estimate effective volumes.ResultsA total of 79 parturients were analyzed. Effective analgesia rates in the 6, 8, 10, and 12 mL groups were 50%, 63.2%, 75%, and 100%, respectively. The estimated EV50 and EV90 were 6.5 mL (95% CI: 3.7–7.7) and 11.0 mL (95% CI: 9.7–14.8). A significant difference in the rate of oxytocin administration was observed among the groups (P = 0.041), with a lower rate in the 6 mL group. No significant differences were observed in adverse reactions.ConclusionUnder the conditions of this study, the EV90 for PIEB was estimated to be approximately 11.0 mL when using a solution of 0.0625% ropivacaine combined with 0.4 μg/mL dexmedetomidine for epidural labor analgesia. The use of oxytocin augmentation showed an increasing trend with higher PIEB volumes. Therefore, in clinical practice, it is advisable to strike a balance between obstetric progress and analgesic efficacy, rather than pursuing perfect analgesia.Keywords: ropivacaine, dexmedetomidine, PIEB, epidural labor analgesia

Introduction

During neuraxial labor analgesia, maintenance of analgesia can be achieved through manual intermittent boluses, continuous epidural infusion (CEI), patient-controlled epidural analgesia (PCEA), programmed intermittent epidural bolus (PIEB), or a combination of these techniques. In recent years, PIEB has emerged as an advanced modality for labor analgesia. It administers a fixed volume of local anesthetic solution into the epidural space at a constant rate and at pre-set, regular intervals.1 Compared to conventional methods such as CEI or PCEA, PIEB can improve the spread of local anesthetics, provide superior analgesic quality, reduce total local anesthetic consumption, decrease the incidence of breakthrough pain and motor block, and achieve higher maternal satisfaction.1–7 However, it should be noted that under PIEB mode, changes in the analgesic formula, bolus volume, or bolus interval can all influence the efficacy of analgesia.1,8,9

Multiple studies have demonstrated that ropivacaine combined with dexmedetomidine can be applied for labor analgesia.10–12 Research has shown that the epidural analgesic regimen combining dexmedetomidine with ropivacaine is non-inferior to sufentanil in terms of analgesic efficacy.13 Our previous work determined that the optimal concentration of dexmedetomidine for epidural labor analgesia when combined with 0.075% ropivacaine is 0.357 μg/mL.14 Subsequently, we found that when using a fixed volume of 10 mL containing 0.0625% ropivacaine and 0.4 μg/mL dexmedetomidine without PCEA rescue, the optimal bolus interval for PIEB was 45.4 minutes.15 However, the optimal bolus volume for this regimen remains unclear.

Therefore, this study aimed to determine the optimal PIEB bolus volume of 0.0625% ropivacaine and 0.4 μg/mL dexmedetomidine required to achieve effective analgesia in 90% of parturients following epidural labor analgesia.

Methods

Ethics and Registration

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Zhejiang Xiaoshan Hospital (Approval No. 2023100) and was prospectively registered at the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry on March 1, 2024, prior to patient enrollment (Registration No. ChiCTR2400081436; https://www.chictr.org.cn). This double-blinded, randomized study was conducted between January 3, 2025, and August 12, 2025, in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants were informed of the off-label use of dexmedetomidine via the intrathecal route and provided written informed consent.

Participants

We recruited parturients classified as American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status II who requested neuraxial labor analgesia. The inclusion criteria were: singleton pregnancy at 37–42 weeks of gestation, aged 18–40 years, cervical dilation <5 cm at the time of analgesia request, and a numerical rating scale (NRS) score >5 (scale: 0 = no pain, 10 = worst imaginable pain). Exclusion criteria included: refusal to participate, any contraindication to neuraxial anesthesia, body mass index (BMI) >40 kg/m2, history of previous cesarean delivery, failed epidural puncture, allergy to ropivacaine or dexmedetomidine, bradycardia, chronic use of sedatives or analgesics, and the eventual performance of an intrapartum cesarean delivery.

Randomization and Blinding

Parturients requesting labor analgesia were randomly assigned, according to a computer-generated randomization list (Microsoft Corporation, WA, USA), to one of four PIEB bolus volume groups: 6, 8, 10, or 12 mL. The allocation sequence was concealed in sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes, which were opened upon enrollment of each participant. An anesthesia assistant, who was aware of the group assignment but was not involved in data collection, was responsible for programming the infusion pump (REHN11; Jiangsu Renxian Medical Technology Co., Ltd.) and preparing the epidural solution (0.0625% ropivacaine + 0.4 μg/mL dexmedetomidine). The pump’s display panel was covered with opaque tape. All investigators responsible for patient pain management and data collection were blinded to the programmed bolus volume.

Study Protocol

Upon arrival in the delivery room, continuous non-invasive monitoring of systolic blood pressure (SBP), electrocardiography, and pulse oximetry (SpO2) was initiated for each parturient. With the patient in the supine position, non-invasive blood pressure was measured at 1-minute intervals, and the average of three consecutive SBP readings was recorded as the baseline blood pressure. Fetal heart rate and uterine contractions were continuously monitored via a fetal monitor (Avalon Fetal Monitor FM30, Philips Medizin Systeme Boeblingen GmbH). An intravenous line was established in an upper limb for infusion of warm lactated Ringer’s solution at 250 mL.

For neuraxial analgesia, the patient was placed in the left lateral position. The L2-3 interspace was identified by palpation as the puncture site. The epidural space was located using an 18-gauge Tuohy needle and the loss-of-resistance to saline technique. A 19-gauge wire-reinforced multiport epidural catheter was then inserted 4–5 cm cephalad into the epidural space and secured with transparent adhesive tape. After confirming the absence of blood or cerebrospinal fluid upon aspiration through the catheter, a 3-mL test dose of 1% lidocaine was administered. Following a 3-minute safety assessment of the test dose, the PIEB infusion pump was connected to the epidural catheter. A 20-mL loading dose containing 0.0625% ropivacaine (AstraZeneca AB, Forskargatan 18, Södertälje S-15185, Sweden) and 0.4 μg/mL dexmedetomidine (Jiangsu Hengrui Pharmaceuticals Co. Ltd., China) was administered via the pump, which was programmed to deliver the dose at a rate of 300 mL/h. Parturients whose NRS score was ≤3 within 30 minutes after completion of the loading dose continued in the study. If the NRS score remained >3 after 30 minutes, the loading dose was considered a failure for the study purposes, and the participant was withdrawn.

Labor Epidural Analgesia Maintenance

Following randomization, participants were assigned to one of four PIEB volume groups: 6, 8, 10, or 12 mL. PIEB of the assigned volume were administered at fixed 45-minute intervals. The first PIEB was delivered 1 hour after completion of the initial epidural loading dose. Patients requiring supplemental analgesia prior to the first scheduled PIEB were excluded from the study. Investigators instructed participants to use PCEA with the same drug solution for breakthrough pain (bolus dose: 10 mL; lockout interval: 15 minutes; maximum hourly dose: 35 mL, which included the total volume from both PIEB and PCEA). If the NRS score remained >3 at 20 minutes after a PCEA bolus, a 5-mL rescue dose of 1% lidocaine was administered, repeated as needed until satisfactory analgesia (NRS ≤3) was achieved.

Outcome Assessment

The primary outcome was the efficacy of labor analgesia, defined as no requirement for a PCEA bolus or a manual rescue analgesic bolus from the completion of the initial epidural loading dose until the end of the first stage of labor or for 6 hours, whichever occurred first. This definition was aligned with the methodology employed in our team’s previously published PIEB study.15 The proportion of parturients achieving effective analgesia in each PIEB bolus volume group was calculated.

Maternal demographic and clinical data were recorded, including age, weight, height, gestational age, gravidity and parity, blood pressure, heart rate, and oxygen saturation. Obstetric characteristics were also documented, encompassing cervical dilation at analgesia request, mode of delivery (spontaneous vaginal or instrumental), use of oxytocin augmentation, and the duration of each stage of labor.

Secondary outcomes included the sensory block level, degree of motor block, patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) demand data (number of PCA presses and Number of effective PCA presses), intrapartum fever defined as a temperature ≥38°C, maternal adverse effects, and neonatal outcomes. Analgesic efficacy was assessed using NRS for pain. The sensory block level was tested using the loss of cold sensation to an alcohol swab at 30 minutes. Motor block was evaluated using the modified Bromage scale (3 = unable to flex the ankle; 2 = unable to flex the knee but can flex the ankle; 1 = unable to raise the extended leg but can flex the knee; 0 = able to raise the extended leg). Pain, sensory block, and motor block were assessed at 30 and 60 minutes after the initial epidural loading dose and subsequently at hourly intervals until the end of the observation period.

Recorded maternal adverse effects included: hypotension (defined as a systolic blood pressure decrease of ≥20% from baseline, managed initially by rapid intravenous fluid administration in the left lateral position; if hypotension persisted for 3 minutes, a 20–40 μg bolus of phenylephrine was administered intravenously and repeated as needed), bradycardia (heart rate <60 beats per minute, treated with atropine 0.5 mg), pruritus, nausea and vomiting, and respiratory depression (SpO2 <90%). Neonatal outcomes comprised birth weight and Apgar scores at 1 and 5 minutes.

Statistical Analysis

Sample size calculation was performed using the Cochran–Armitage trend test via PASS software (version 15.0.5; NCSS, LLC, Kaysville, UT). Based on our preliminary study, when the fixed infusion interval was 45 minutes, the effective rates of epidural labor analgesia among parturients randomly assigned to four groups with PIEB volumes of 6, 8, 10, and 12 mL were 40%, 70%, 90%, and 100%, respectively. To achieve 90% statistical power for detecting a linear trend in the number of patients with effective analgesia using the continuity-corrected Z-test (significance threshold 0.05), 12 subjects were required per group. Considering potential participant dropout and possible differences between the actual analgesic efficacy and our preliminary findings, the target sample size was ultimately set to 20 per group (80 participants in total across the four groups).

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 26.0 (IBM Corp) and GraphPad Prism 10.1.2 (GraphPad Software Inc). Normality of continuous data distributions was assessed with the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Normally distributed data are expressed as mean (standard deviation [SD]) and were analyzed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), with significant outcomes further subjected to Bonferroni post hoc comparisons. Non-normally distributed data are expressed as median [IQR] and were analyzed using the Kruskal–Wallis test, followed by the Dunn test for pairwise comparisons. Trends in sensory block level and PIEB volume across groups were examined using the Jonckheere–Terpstra test. Categorical data are presented as number (incidence) and were analyzed by the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, with significant results further compared using the partitions of χ2 method.

The PIEB volumes required to maintain effective labor analgesia in 50% (EV50) and 90% (EV90) of parturients were calculated via probit regression. Goodness-of-fit of the probit model to the observed data was assessed using the The Pearson goodness-of-fit χ2 test. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Among these 80 subjects, one required an intrapartum cesarean delivery. Consequently, data from 79 subjects were included in the final analysis (Figure 1). Discussion

In this dose-response study, we found that when administering 0.0625% ropivacaine with 0.4 μg/mL dexmedetomidine at a fixed 45-minute interval, the estimated PIEB volumes required for effective labor analgesia in 50% (EV50) and 90% (EV90) of parturients were 6.5 mL (95% CI, 3.7–7.7) and 11.0 mL (95% CI, 9.7–14.8), respectively. Furthermore, we observed a positive correlation between the PIEB volume and the level of sensory block. Groups receiving higher PIEB volumes also had a higher rate of oxytocin use and, correspondingly, a lower rate of spontaneous vaginal delivery without oxytocin augmentation. No significant differences were observed among the groups regarding maternal adverse effects or neonatal outcomes.

Multiple meta-analyses have confirmed the safety of dexmedetomidine as an adjuvant to local anesthetics for epidural labor analgesia, with its analgesic efficacy being non-inferior to that of opioids.16–18 Although several studies have aimed to determine the optimal concentration of epidural dexmedetomidine combined with ropivacaine for labor analgesia.19,20 However, in clinical practice, the optimal PIEB volume for dexmedetomidine combined with local anesthetics in labor analgesia remains unclear, with limited related research. Hence, employing the same drug concentration and a 45-minute bolus interval, this study was designed to estimate the optimal PIEB volume under these specific conditions.

Previous research on the optimal volume for epidural labor analgesia has predominantly focused on traditional opioid-local anesthetic combinations. A biased coin up-and-down sequential allocation study, using a solution of 0.0625% bupivacaine with 2 µg/mL fentanyl delivered at fixed 40-minute intervals, determined the optimal PIEB volume to be an EV90 of 11.0 mL (95% CI, 10.0–11.7).21 Another study employing probit regression analysis reported an optimal volume of 11.3 mL for a mixture of 0.1% ropivacaine with 0.5 μg/mL sufentanil when administered via programmed intermittent epidural bolus following a dural puncture epidural technique.22 We found that these previous results closely align with the value estimated in our study (11.0 mL [95% CI: 9.7–14.8]).

In contrast to these previous studies, our protocol employed an ultra-low concentration (0.0625% ropivacaine) combined with a high-volume (20 mL) loading dose for induction. We observed mild motor block (Bromage score of 1) in only one parturient from the 12 mL group, occurring 5 hours post-dose. This finding differs from that of Shen et al20 who used a 10 mL first loading bolus of 0.1% ropivacaine with 0.4 μg/mL dexmedetomidine and reported Bromage scores of 1 in 9% (2/22), 4% (1/24), and 13% (3/23) of parturients receiving 0.06%, 0.08%, and 0.1% ropivacaine, respectively. Similarly, a comparative study using 0.09% ropivacaine combined with either dexmedetomidine (induction dose 0.45 µg/mL, maintenance 0.36 µg/mL) or sufentanil (induction dose 0.45 µg/mL, maintenance 0.36 µg/mL) found incidences of lower limb weakness (all modified Bromage grade 1) of 13% (13/100) in the dexmedetomidine group versus 1% (1/100) in the sufentanil group,23 suggesting that a higher local anesthetic concentration may increase the risk of motor block.

Concurrently, we observed a positive correlation between the highest sensory block level and the PIEB volume in our study, with no block exceeding T8. This seems to suggest a potential association between sensory block level and anesthetic volume. However, Nakayama et al24 found that the same total dose of local anesthetic administered in different volumes produced similar anesthetic effects in parturients, positing that the efficacy of epidural anesthesia is primarily determined by the total dose rather than the volume. Therefore, a more diluted solution administered in a larger volume could provide equally effective labor analgesia while better avoiding maternal lower limb weakness, compared to a higher concentration in a smaller dose.

It is noteworthy that although our data did not show a significant positive correlation between the duration of the first or second stage of labor and increasing PIEB volume, this result may be confounded by oxytocin use. The rate of oxytocin augmentation increased with higher PIEB volumes, while the rate of spontaneous vaginal delivery without oxytocin correspondingly decreased. Excessive maternal analgesia or anesthesia is a recognized factor associated with prolongation of the latent phase of labor. Augmenting uterine contractions with oxytocin can shorten this prolonged latent phase, thereby mitigating associated risks such as increased incidence of dystocia and cesarean delivery.25 Recent data also indicate that continuous oxytocin use is a risk factor for postpartum hemorrhage in nulliparous women, while a history of postpartum hemorrhage is a risk factor in parous women.26 Consequently, based on our findings, we suggest that epidural labor analgesia management should prioritize an individualized analgesic regimen over a standardized EV90 dose protocol. The goal should not be pursuit of perfect analgesia; instead, it may be reasonable to allow patients to experience a tolerable level of pain during labor, utilizing PCEA boluses as needed to manage breakthrough pain.

Our study has several limitations. First, the use of dexmedetomidine for epidural analgesia constitutes off-label use. Although its efficacy and safety have been substantiated by numerous clinical studies, umbilical cord blood monitoring was not performed in this study. Furthermore, due to the relatively small sample size in each group, the confidence intervals for the EV50 and EV90 estimates in this study are wide. While the sample size was sufficient to detect a dose-response trend, the precision of these estimates is limited, therefore, further validation through large-scale, multicenter studies is warranted. Second, the study was conducted with fixed PIEB intervals and a fixed pump infusion rate. The solution delivery rates of infusion pumps may vary across different institutions. Furthermore, most studies on epidural labor analgesia using local anesthetics combined with dexmedetomidine, including the present one, originate from China, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Third, our observation period was limited to 6 hours after the epidural loading dose or until the end of the first stage of labor within that 6-hour window. Consequently, our findings may be applicable only to epidural analgesia under these temporal conditions, and the effects beyond 6 hours in the first stage and during the second stage of labor remain unexplored. It would be valuable for future clinical trials to evaluate PIEB analgesic regimens in relation to different stages of labor, thereby addressing the analgesic needs of parturients throughout the entire labor process. Additionally, oxytocin administration was not protocolized but followed routine obstetric practice, which may have introduced confounding. Future studies with standardized oxytocin protocols are needed to clarify the relationship between PIEB volume and labor progress.

Conclusion

In summary, our results indicate that, with a fixed PIEB interval of 45 minutes, the EV90 for 0.0625% ropivacaine combined with 0.4 μg/mL dexmedetomidine is 11.0 mL (95% CI: 9.7–14.8). The use of oxytocin augmentation showed an increasing trend with higher PIEB volumes. Therefore, in clinical practice, it is advisable to strike a balance between obstetric progress and analgesic efficacy, rather than pursuing perfect analgesia.

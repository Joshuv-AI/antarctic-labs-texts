Introduction

Pleomorphic adenoma (PA) is the most common benign salivary gland tumour [1]. It is a benign mixed tumour as it has a dual origin from epithelial and mesangial elements [2]. There is a popular rule of 80s; 80 % of all salivary tumours originating from the parotid gland, 80 % of them are benign, 80 % of benign parotid tumours are PA, and 80 % of them involve the parotid gland's superficial lobe [3,4]. Other origins are from minor salivary gland (10 %) where hard palate being most common site and submandibular glands (5 %). The precise etiology of PA is unknown. Risk factors may involve genetic or environmental factors. Radiation exposure has been postulated as a well-known environmental risk factor. Also, there are some evidences suggesting that the oncogenic simian virus (SV40) could potentially contribute to onset or progression of pleomorphic adenoma [5]. This tumour has a female preponderance affecting middle-aged women between 30 and 50 years [6]. Symptoms and signs mainly depend on site and location. It usually presents as hard, uneven nodular swelling in the parotid region and usually asymptomatic with slow growing tendency without facial nerve involvement. However, if left untreated, they can grow into extensive sizes [5,7,8]. Approximately 6 % of PA tends to malignant transformation into Carcinoma Ex-pleomorphic adenoma, an aggressive tumour. Risk factors for malignant change are longevity and recurrence [9]. Rapid enlargement and involvement of facial nerves raise the suspiciousness for malignant transformation [10].

Ultrasound scan remains the first imaging modality to evaluate salivary gland pathologies. Tumour is confirmed by Computed Tomography (CT) or Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI). However, diagnosis remains uncertain due to the significant overlap in morphological features between benign and malignant tumours [11,12]. Fine needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) is a reliable and minimally invasive method for preoperative diagnosis and aids in the selection of therapeutic intervention [12,13]. Since the PA has an incomplete capsule, enucleation of the tumour has high recurrence rates. Hence, superficial parotidectomy with facial nerve preservation, if only the superficial lobe is involved, or total parotidectomy, if the deep lobe is also involved, remains the most opted treatment [9,13]. Potential complications following parotidectomy include facial nerve reversible paresis, haemorrhage, infection, salivary fistula, Frey's syndrome and tumour recurrence [14].

This case report details the management of an unusual case of a giant, long-standing pleomorphic adenoma of the parotid gland involving the deep lobe. This case report was prepared in line with the SCARE criteria [20].

Presentation of case

A 48-year-old woman presented with painless, slow-growing enlargement in the left-sided parotid region for 22 years. She experienced mild difficulty breathing during the past three months, leading her to seek medical attention. Past medical and surgical history were unremarkable. Clinical examination revealed well defined, multi-lobular giant lump in the left-sided parotid region extending up to the left canthus of the eye, angle of the mouth, half of the posterior and anterior triangle of the neck (Fig. 1). The swelling was firm in consistency, non-tender and fixed to underlying structures and overlying skin. Due to the extensive nature of the lesion, it was difficult to assess facial nerve function. Mild left-sided tonsillar bulging was noted in the intraoral examination. Clinically there were no suspicious lymph nodes identified. A provisional diagnosis of benign parotid tumour was made based on clinical evaluation.

(A) Frontal view (B) lateral view of the face showing left-sided giant parotid swelling (note: tumour involving part of left pinna).

Contrast-enhanced computer tomography (CECT) of the head, neck, chest, abdomen and pelvis was performed, which revealed a multi-lobulated poorly enhancing giant left parotid mass measuring 23 cm × 20 cm × 14 cm (Fig. 2A). The mass involved the deep lobe of the left parotid gland and bulging to the pharyngeal space (Fig. 2B). No vascular or pharyngeal invasion was noted. No fat plane separated the left masseter muscle from the tumour. No nodal, pulmonary or hepatic metastasis was identified in the CECT.

CECT head, neck and chest (A) poorly enhancing giant left parotid mass (B) mass bulging into the pharyngeal space (arrow).

FNAC of left parotid mass was done, which concluded pleomorphic adenoma of Milan category IV a.

The surgical team discussed the diagnosis with the patient and explained the surgery's potential benefits and risks. Preoperative evaluation and optimisation were done. Left-sided radical parotidectomy was undertaken after obtaining informed written consent explaining the risk of being unable to preserve the left-sided facial nerve.

Surgery was performed under general anaesthesia. The patient was supine, her neck extended, and her head slightly tilted to the right. An elliptical incision involving a whole mass created a flap for exposing the gland (Fig. 3A). Total left parotidectomy with dissection of the facial nerve and adjacent muscles attached to the mass was performed (Fig. 3B). The left-sided lower part of the pinna was also excised as the tumour involved this area. The excised tumour was 30 cm × 27 cm × 18 cm in dimension. The weight of the excised mass was 2.78 kg (Fig. 3C). The wound was irrigated, haemostasis was achieved and closed in layers with a closed suction drain (Fig. 3D).

(A) Elliptical incision involving whole mass (B) intraoperative field (C) excised mass (D) following radical parotidectomy.

Histopathological examination confirmed pleomorphic adenoma composed of small nests, cords and glandular structures of epithelial cells in a background of abundant chondromyxoid stroma with streaming sheets of myoepithelial cells (Fig. 4).

(A) Epithelial and myoepithelial cells merge with chondromyxoid stroma (arrow) × 100 (B) chondromyxoid stroma with tumour cells (×400) (C) tumour cells with minimal nuclear atypia and mitosis (×400) (D) ductal structure with bilayered epithelium (arrow) (×400).

Postoperatively, the patient was observed for potential complications. Left-sided tarsorrhaphy was done to prevent corneal keratitis. The drain was removed on the 5th postoperative day. Skin sutures were removed on the 7th postoperative day, and the patient was discharged on the same day. There were no complications reported on 1-year follow-up. She did not appreciate left-sided facial muscle weakness postoperatively, likely because she had been living with that extensive mass for 22 years and as a result, her quality of life was not significantly affected.

Discussion

Pleomorphic adenoma is a major salivary gland's most common benign tumour [2]. It is more commonly found in the superficial lobe of the parotid gland. Less than 20 % of pleomorphic adenomas only arise in the deep lobe of the parotid gland [4]. Our case also showed involvement of the deep lobe of the parotid gland, which is a rare occurrence.

Middle-aged people with female predilection presented mostly with giant PA [15]. Our patient was also a 48-year-old woman with a long-standing, giant PA of the parotid gland. The first published case of giant pleomorphic adenoma (>1 kg) was reported in 1863 by Spence [16]. Afterwards, there were several literatures revealed giant PA being reported all around the world, the largest being 8.03 kg ever reported in English literature [17,18]. In the Sri Lankan context, a 3.5 kg weighed giant parotid gland PA was reported in the Central part of Sri Lanka [19]. In our case, the excised tumour's weight was 2.78 kg, the largest PA of the parotid gland reported in Northern Sri Lanka.

PA is usually present as a unilateral, slow-growing, painless mass [7,8]. But if left untreated, it can present as a giant mass, like in this case. Usually, facial nerve weakness is not seen in PA. However, the facial nerve may be involved when the tumour is large and if it undergoes malignant transformation [8]. But in our case, it was difficult to assess facial nerve function due to the extensive nature of the tumour.

FNAC is a reliable diagnostic method that can guide the therapeutic approach. Usually, it is performed following diagnostic imaging, CT or MRI [12]. In our case, CECT was performed initially, revealing a giant parotid mass involving the superficial and deep lobe of the parotid gland without vascular or pharyngeal involvement. FNAC features were consistent with previously reported literature on PA.

Early intervention is warranted when dealing with massive tumour sizes and longer durations of mass presence, as they carry a heightened risk of undergoing malignant transformation [6]. The optimal surgical management for PA involving both superficial and deep lobes of the parotid gland is total parotidectomy with or without facial nerve preservation [9,13]. In our case, left-sided radical parotidectomy was undertaken since it was a significantly large tumour mass fixed to adjacent structures, making it impossible to preserve the facial nerve.

The facial nerve provides major innervation for the muscles of facial expression. Dissecting this nerve can result in postoperative facial deformities, significantly impacting the patient's quality of life. We made every effort to locate the facial nerve during the surgery. However, the tumour mass was firmly adhered to the surrounding structures, making it impossible to distinguish the nerve. Interestingly, the patient did not report any noticeable changes postoperatively. This could be attributed to the tumour's long-standing presence or the likelihood that the nerve had already lost its function due to the tumour's fixation to it.

PA exhibits a mixture of epithelial and mesangial components with the background of myxoid, chondroid and/or osseous stromal matrix [3]. In our case, the postoperative histopathological assessment also demonstrated features of benign ductular structures lined by epithelial and myoepithelial cells admixed with large areas of the chondromyxoid extracellular matrix.

Despite being benign, PA has a potential risk of local recurrence and rare malignant transformation in a small percentage of cases. The recurrence rate varies depending on the extent of surgical resection and the duration of follow-up [6]. In this case, the patient is being followed up for one year and no recurrence was observed.

Conclusion

Surgical dissection of giant pleomorphic adenoma is often challenging when dealing with extensive tumours. This can lead to the unintended dissection of the facial nerve and nearby muscles, resulting in facial deformity. Early intervention will improve surgical outcomes, reduce the risk of complications and improve quality of life.

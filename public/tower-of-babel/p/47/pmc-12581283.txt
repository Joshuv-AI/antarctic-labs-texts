Assessment of the impact of nasal septum deviation types on sinonasal variations and infundibulum morphometry in relation to gender and age using CT images
Authors: Gülay Açar, Ahmet Safa Gökşan, Demet Aydoğdu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNasal septum deviation (NSD) plays a vital role in paranasal sinus development, influencing sinonasal structures and maxillary sinus volume (MSV). This study aims to identify the NSD types and ascertain their impact on sinonasal variants, MSV, and morphometry of the infundibulum using three-dimensional (3D) computed tomography (CT) scans.MethodsCT images of 300 patients (150 females and 150 males), aged between 18 and 70 years, were retrospectively analyzed. The prevalences of NSD types, NS pneumatization (NSP), middle concha bullosa (MCB), paradoxical middle turbinate (PMT), MS mucosal thickening (MSMT), accessory maxillary ostium (AMO), uncinate process (UP) variants, and inferior turbinate hypertrophy (ITH) were evaluated. Also, MSV, MO width (MOW), infundibulum length (IL) and angle (IA) were measured. Statistical analyses of data was done taking into account gender, laterality, and age.ResultsThe greater the degree of NSD, the greater the likelihood of MCB, PMT and ITH occurring on the opposite side. Conversely, AMO, hypoplastic MS, MSMT, and hook-shaped UP were more prevalent on the same side of the deviation. The strongest associations with ipsilateral hyperplastic MS and being a young male were exhibited by PMT and MCB. Moderate and severe NSD, AMO, hook-shaped UP, hypoplastic MS and MSMT were more prevalent in older subjects, while younger subjects had more NSP, extensive MCB, PMT with a significant difference. The mean MSV, MOW and IL were found to be lower in MS on the same side of NSD, while the largest IA was observed in MS with severe NSD, as well as larger IA on the ipsilateral side of deviation, with a significant difference. However, we found that, as the degree of NSDA increased, the mean IL decreased and the mean MOW increased. The mean MOW and IL values indicated a positive correlation with ageing, whereas the MSV and IA values decreased with increasing age.ConclusionsDespite their significance, our understanding of how sinonasal structures, MSV, and infundibulum morphometry vary and covary with NSD remains limited, a crucial aspect in the planning of dental implantology and endoscopic sinus surgery.

Background

As is well known, the nasal cavity is a roughly triangular airway located in the midface. The nasal cavity is divided into right and left semi-airways by the nasal septum. The lateral wall of the nasal cavity is divided into three meatuses by the scroll-shaped bony turbinates (or conchae), which protrude into the nasal cavity and partially divide the airway into three parts [1, 2]. The pyramidal maxillary sinus (MS) is the largest paranasal sinus with its base facing the nasal cavity. It is ventilated and drained at the middle meatus through the ethmoidal infundibulum and primary maxillary ostium (MO) at the posterior aspect of the infundibulum [1]. Normal MO is usually located at 5–10 mm superior to the inferior turbinate attachment point. The presence of sinusitis or membrane thickening in the MS may be indicative of a specific obstruction of the MO. In cases of loss of ostium patency, accessory maxillary ostium (AMO), typically located posteriorly and inferior to the MO, is required to enhance the ventilation rate of the MS [3–5]. The hiatus semilunaris is located at the superomedial tip of the infundibulum, which is basically a narrow slit-like funnel-shaped passage situated anterosuperiorly to the uncinate process (UP) [1, 2]. Infundibulum requires an uncinectomy to be visualized by the endoscopic sinus surgeon [6]. Depending on the presence of AMO, blockage of the infundibulum and UP types, the risk of iatrogenic injury during surgical procedures involving the posterior maxilla, particularly sinus floor elevation and implant placement, may be increased [3, 7]– [8].

Nasal septum deviation (NSD) is a displacement of the septum to one side of the nasal cavity, which is most common sinonasal variation with a prevalence ranging from 34.6 to 96.8%. It is one of the most common conditions that leads patients to visit an otolaryngologist or a plastic surgeon, either for cosmetic reasons or for functional purposes [4, 9–12]. Previous studies have reported that NSD may be congenital or secondary to trauma/traffic accidents, and also it may be mild to extensive depending on the degree of deviation angle. While the precise cause of NSD is still unknown, concha bullosa (CB, turbinate pneumatization) or inferior turbinate hypertrophy (ITH) can result in NSD. Alternatively, NSD can displace the middle turbinate, thereby narrowing the middle meatus [4, 13]– [14]. Nevertheless, if a severe NSD occurs, it is possible that the direction of airflow through the nasal cavity will change. This can cause an obstruction of the mucosal drainage of the MS, which can result in rhinosinusitis. Furthermore, the majority of patients with NSD exhibit facial bone asymmetry. This is a key factor in the successful outcome of therapeutic interventions, such as rhinoseptoplasty and oral surgery. Due to the presence of air-filled cavities within the nasal septum (NSP), which are predominantly situated in the posterior aspect of the septum and linked to the sphenoid sinus, infection has the potential to disseminate to the sinus [15]. Paradoxical middle turbinate (PMT), characterised by the convexity of the middle turbinate being directed laterally and mostly coexisted with NSD, can alter nasal passage air flow dynamics [16].

When performing functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS), as well as external sinus lifts and dental implants, clinicians must have an in-depth understanding of the prevalence of NSD, NSP, CB, PMT, ITH, AMO and UP variants and the relationship between them. These anatomical variations act as key reference points, providing access for surgery and enabling endoscopy to restore normal ventilation, mucociliary clearance and sinus drainage [3, 14, 17]. Computed tomography (CT) scans make it easy to perform a detailed, three-dimensional (3D) assessment of the relationship between the NSD, MS and sinonasal variations. Widespread use of 3D imaging by the clinicians in dentistry, otolaryngology, radiology, plastic and reconstructive surgery has revolutionized the study of the sinonasal region [6, 12, 16].

Regarding the influence of the NSD on sinonasal variants and MS morphology, a few studies were found that investigated the association between them, as well as the changes in MS volume (MSV) in relation to gender, age and laterality. Therefore, our aim was to identify the prevalence of NSD types and sinonasal variants, and to examine whether there is an association between them that could increase the risk of iatrogenic injury. In addition, we sought to ascertain the impact of NSD on the MSV and morphometry of the infundibulum taking into account gender, laterality and age by employing 3D CT images.

Methods

In this retrospective study, CT scans of 300 patients (150 females and 150 males) recorded with different preliminary diagnoses between January and December 2024 at the Department of Radiology were analyzed. This study was approved by University’s Research Ethics Committee with an approval number 2024/5282. It was not considered necessary by the Ethics committee that written informed consent should be obtained from the patients whose information, retrospective and anonymised data, was obtained from electronic records. Clinical trial number: not applicable. The present study was then carried out in accordance with the “Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice”. A total of three hundred patients (150 female and 150 male) were included in the study. Patients excluded from the study were those with deformities, tumors, trauma or a history of surgical operations in sinonasal region, those with incomplete MS imaging, those with artifacts or low-quality images, and patients under 18 years of age.

CT imaging

CT images were acquired using Somatom Drive (Siemens Healthineers, Germany) 256 multislice CT scanner. The operating parameters were set to 120 kV, 74 mA, 60 mAs; rotation time 0.28 s; slice thickness 0.625 mm. All images were transformed in DICOM format and 3D-Slicer (open-source software platform, version 5.8.1, http://www.slicer.org) was used to create the 3D models to calculate the automatic MSV measurement of the segmented sections. A radiologist and an anatomist analyzed images together. Both of them repeated the measurements and variant analysis at the one-month interval. After finishing the assessment, both the inter- and intra-class correlation coefficient test (ICC) results were analyzed.

Classification of morphological variables and measurement of morphometric parameters

Nasal septum deviation angle (NSDA): On coronal CT images, a vertical reference line was drawn from the crista galli to anterior nasal spine, while another line was drawn from crista galli to the most prominent point of the NSD. The NSDA between these two lines was measured. Using the technique utilized in the study by Kapusuz Gencer et al. [10], the NSDA was measured on coronal CT section and categorized as mild (< 9°), moderate (9°−15°), and severe (>15°). Then, the NSD has been classified into seven types, depending on the degree and side it takes in relation to the NSDA, including absent, right and left mild NSD, right and left moderate NSD, and right and left severe NSD (Figs. 1A-C). On coronal CBCT images, nasal septum pneumatization (NSP) was also noted as either absent or present (Fig. 1D).

Based on the study conducted by Bolger et al. [18], middle CB (MCB) was categorized into three groups as lamellar, bulbous, and extensive on coronal CT images (Figs. 1E-G). PMT was described as either absent or present (Fig. 1H).

On coronal CT images, ITH and AMO were noted as either absent or present (Figs. 2A-B). Based on criteria used by Almeida et al. [11], maxillary sinus mucosal thickening (MSMT) was categorised as absent, mild, and moderate (Figs. 2B-D). UP variants were categorized into 4 types as normal, pneumatized, hook-shaped, and bifid (Figs. 2E-H).

A specific threshold (in the range of −1024 to −526) was set for each MSV calculation using the ‘Segment Editor’ tool in 3D-Slicer. To isolate the MS the connections between the MS and adjacent structures (e.g. the nasal cavity, Haller cell) were cut out. The boundaries of the MS were drawn separately and cropped in all planes as described by Aşantoğrol et al. [4]. Segmentations were performed on axial sections of the images according to threshold values, from the floor at the alveolar crest to the MS roof. The accuracy of the segmentation was checked visually on multiplanar reconstructions, slice by slice. Following segmentation, all sections were automatically integrated and the MSV was calculated in cm³ using the ‘Segment Statistics’ function in the 3D Slicer software. Hypoplastic MS: The MSV ranged from 2.5 cm³ to 9.99 cm³.

Hyperplastic MS: The MSV ranged from 18 cm³ to 27 cm³.

Based on the study by Yousefi et al. [19], the following morphometric parameters were measured on coronal CT images (Fig. 3D):

The length of the infundibulum (IL): The distance between the centre of the MO and the uppermost point of the UP,

The MO width (MOW): The width of infundibulum at the initiation point of the MO,

The infundibulum angle (IA): The angle between the line passing through infundibulum and the horizontal line tangent to the vertical line at the initiation point of infundibulum.

Statistical analysis

In the statistical analysis of the data obtained in our study, the SPSS (Statistical Package for Social Sciences) version 25.0 software was used. The normality of the morphometric parameters was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. In the descriptive analysis of all morphometric parameters, the minimum, maximum, average, and standard deviation values were presented in tables. The distribution of variations according to gender, right-left side, and NSD types was shown in tables as percentages. Since all data followed a normal distribution, Student’s t-test was used to compare independent groups (based on gender), and Paired t-test was used to compare dependent groups (right and left sides). The statistical analysis of the data according to NSD types and age groups were performed using One-Way ANOVA (One-Way Analysis of Variance). The Bonferroni correction, which is one of the Post-hoc tests, was applied for multiple comparisons between these groups in terms of numerical variables. The significance of the difference between observed and expected frequencies for categorical variables was assessed using the Chi-Square Test. The reliability and accuracy of the proposed method were tested using the ICC test, which looks at the agreement between observers. Intra- and inter-observer agreement is categorised as follows:>0.75 (excellent); 0.40–0.75 (moderate); and < 0.40 (poor). All analyses were performed with a 95% confidence interval. Statistical significance was considered when p < 0.05.

Results

In this retrospective study, patient’s age ranged from 18 to 70 years (mean age 34.25 ± 14.43 years). As for the age of females and males, the mean was 36.51 ± 16.03 and 31.99 ± 12.25 years, respectively. Furthermore, the mean age of the females was significantly higher than that of the males (p = 0.000).

The prevalence of the nasal septum deviation types, sinonasal variants and maxillary sinus pneumatization types

In our study, the NSD was observed to be 79% (129 right-sided and 108 left-sided) and 21% of the subjects had no deviation. Of the 237 patients with NSD, 71 (30%) had mild NSD, 88 (37.1%) had moderate NSD, 78 (32.9%) had severe NSD with no significant gender difference. Approximately 30% (90/300) of subjects had NSP, in which males had more NSP (Table 1).

Regarding the MCB, there was no MCB in 235 (39.2%) sides (123 right and 112 left). Lamellar type was the most common (206, 34.3%) and followed by extensive pneumatization in 106 sides (17.7%) and bulbous type in 53 sides (8.8%) with no significant gender difference. The frequency of PMT was significantly higher in males than in females (p = 0.003, χ2 = 11.509), whereas ITH was more commonly associated with being female rather than male (p = 0.054, χ2 = 5.201) (Table 1).

In terms of AMO, one AMO was the most common followed by two AMO and was more frequently observed in females than in males with a significant gender difference (p = 0.010, χ2 = 11.373). The most observed UP type was the pneumatized UP, followed by hook-shaped UP, and bifid UP. Hook-shaped UP were significantly more prevalent in females, whereas males had more commonly pneumatized and bifid UP (p = 0.047, χ2 = 7.937). Furthermore, with the exception of UP types (p = 0.152), the Chi-squared test showed statistically significant differences between these variations as being bilateral (p = 0.000) (Table 2).

The most common type of MSP was normal MS, followed by hyperplastic MS and hypoplastic MS. While the prevalence of hypoplastic MS (36.3%) was higher in females than in males, males more likely had hyperplastic MS (41.4%) with a significant difference (p = 0.000, χ2 = 19.845). As shown in Table 1, the prevalence of mild and moderate MSMT was higher in females than in males with a significant difference (p = 0.010, χ2 = 12.473).

Measurement of NSDA and MSV showed mean values of 13.11 ± 7.05° and 15.51 ± 6.47 cm3, respectively. The mean MSV was greater in males than females (p = 0.000) (Table 1). In addition, the mean MSV was 8.21 ± 2.42 cm³ for the hypoplastic MS (30.8%), 15.05 ± 1.75 cm³ for the normal MS (, 36.2%), and 22.83 ± 3.80 cm³ for the hyperplastic MS (33%). Moreover, there was no significant difference in terms of laterality for the mean value of the MSV (p = 0.307). The mean MOW was 1.9 ± 1.1 mm, the mean IL was 10.14 ± 2.8 mm, and the mean IA was 48.25 ± 12.4°. Overall, males had significantly longer IL (p = 0.000) and IA (p = 0.001) compared to females (Table 1), while only IL was significantly longer in left side than right side (p = 0.001) (Table 2).

Association between nasal septum deviation, sinonasal variants and maxillary sinus pneumatization

When comparing the frequency of MCB within NSD types, this study demonstrated that the presence of MCB together with NSD with a prevalence of 87.33% and without NSD in 12.67% of cases. The higher the degree of NSD, the higher the frequency of bulbous and extensive MCB occurring on the opposite side of the deviation (p = 0.000). Similarly, there was a tendency for PMT to occur on the opposite side of the NSD, though this was not significant (p = 0.160). Age distribution

To better highlight the age-related changes and make them visually understandable, the study group was divided into three age groups: Group I (18–24 years) with 105 (35%) patients, Group II (25–40 years) with 101 (33.7%) patients, and Group III (41–70 years) with 94 (31.3%) patients. The lowest and highest values that define each age group were not based on the literature. They were derived from data that had been adjusted to make the percentage of each group almost equal. Statistical analysis of the nasal cavity variations and the mean value of morphometric parameters according to age groups was shown in Table 5. There was a statistically significant difference between age groups in terms of the presence of all variables except ITH, in which there was a tendency for it to decrease with increasing age. We found that the prevalence of normal and hyperplastic MS, NSP, extensive MCB, PMT, the pneumatized and bifid UP were the highest in Group I and decreased significantly with increasing age (p = 0.000, p = 0.000, p = 0.036, p = 0.001, and p = 0.041), while the smaller the MSV, the higher the AMO frequency, and the higher the occurrence of hook-shaped UP and MSMT were observed in Group III (p = 0.000, p = 0.046, p = 0.041, and p = 0.000). Discussion

Even though we know about the different sinonasal variations, we still don’t fully understand the link between NSD and these variants, as well as the progression of MSMT. Inflammation of the Schneiderian membrane (the ectoderm-derived epithelium of the nasal cavity) is referred to as MSMT, which may be rhinogenous or dental in origin. There are a paucity of studies that describe the influence of all sinonasal variants in MSMT, and there is still no consensus as to whether sinonasal variations and osteomeatal anatomy are a cause or an effect of chronic maxillary sinusitis [3, 16, 20–24]. The aim of this study was to assess the prevalence of sinonasal variants and their association with each other, as well as the detailed influence of NSD on osteomeatal morphology and morphometry. We provide a detailed description of the NSD by classifying it in association with other sinonasal variants such as MCB, PMT, ITH, NSP, AMO, UP variants, MSMT, and MSV. When assessing patients for sinus augmentation and the extraction of posterior teeth for implant placement in dentistry, as well as for endoscopic surgery in otorhinolaryngology, the following features are all relevant: the MSV, the side and degree of NSD, the length and angle of the infundibulum, the presence or absence of AMO and UP, and turbinate morphology [3–7].

The documented incidence of NSD in the literature varies between 34.6 and 96.8%, depending on the varying morphological characteristics and the degree of deviation. Depending on the NSDA, NSD can result in impaired nasal breathing, making surgical access more challenging due to the narrowing of the nasal passage and altered airflow dynamics [3]– [4, 10–16, 22]– [23]. In this study, the categorization of NSD differed from that of previous studies, with the introduction of seven types: absent, right-left mildNSD, right-left moderateNSD, and right-left severeNSD. The categorization depended on the degree of NSDA and the side of deviation. In the present study, the mean NSDA was 13.11° and mild, moderate, and severe NSD types were observed in 71, 88, and 78 study subjects, respectively. In our study, NSD was found in 87.33% of subjects with MCB (37.7% on the right and 49.6% on the left), whereas 12.67% of subjects without NSD also had MCB. Despite the ambiguity surrounding the precise mechanism of MCB formation, its prevalence of MCB was found to be 60.83% (365/600), which has been reported in previous studies to range from 10.3% to 77.4% [4, 11–15, 22–25]. The majority of these investigations have ascertained that the MCB and PMT are predominantly associated with contralateral NSD [12, 21, 23–25], despite some converse results [13, 15, 22]. This discrepancy between studies may be attributable to the use of different diagnostic criteria or methods and ethnic differences among the studied populations. The study’s observation of a significant association between NSD and contralateral MCB (p = 0.000) endorses literature findings that suggest MCB can result in NSD on the opposite side, particularly in moderate and severe NSD cases (87.33% of MCB). However, PMT was detected in 12% of cases, which is consistent with the varying prevalence rates of PMT reported in previous studies, ranging from 11 to 34.2% [16]– [17, 26]– [27]. Our findings on the relationship between NSD and PMT revealed a tendency for PMT to occur on the opposite side of the NSD (p = 0.160), with 94.4% of PMT cases presenting alongside NSD. Furthermore, PMT and MCB exhibited the strongest associations with ipsilateral hyperplastic MS and being a young male in this study. As this is the first time this has been assessed, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, it cannot be compared to those of other investigations.

Conversely, the substantial correlation found in this study between NSD and hypoplastic ipsilateral MS (p = 0.044) and MSMT (p = 0.012) provides further evidence to support the existing literature which suggests that NSD can obstruct the normal drainage of the MS, thereby decreasing MSV and predisposing individuals to hypo-aerated MS and an increased risk of maxillary sinusitis. A CT scan showing a hypoplastic MS or MSMT at the maxillary sinus floor is a clear sign of a higher risk of sinusitis and augmentation failure. Dental problems may also be observed in patients with hypoplastic MS, as a result of canine fossa elevation [4, 10, 11, 26]. Our study found a relatively high prevalence of MSMT (37.8%) and hypoplastic MS (30.8%), with NSD observed in 89.4% and 73% of cases, respectively. This data is of great importance, as the greater likelihood of NSD with ipsilateral hypoplastic MS or MSMT may lead to sinusitis and subsequent augmentation failure. When a higher rate of this relationship is recorded, medical or surgical treatment may be advised before thinking about sinus lift.

Another uncommon sinonasal variant is AMO, which is located in the ruptured membranous portion of the lateral nasal wall, with a reported incidence of 20%−50%. Furthermore, there have been reports of AMO causing the re-entry of sinus secretions drained through the primary MO and it is also more susceptible to deformation [6–8, 21, 28]. The different prevalence rates are likely to be caused by ethnic differences, a small sample size, the methodology used and the sensitivity of the investigative imaging system. Our results revealed a significant association between the presence of AMO (23.5%; 141/600) and NSD. Of the cases exhibiting AMO, 85.1% also showed ipsilateral NSD (p = 0.034). Additionally, AMO incidence was most frequently accompanied by a hypoplastic MS (p = 0.064). Regarding the UP, it is a key bony landmark that forms an opening through which the MS opens into the posterior aspect of the infundibulum. The first step in FESS is to remove the UP in order to access the infundibulum and natural MO behind it. So, it’s really important to have a good understanding of the UP morphology before surgery to avoid intraoperative complications, such as injury to the medial orbital wall, nasolacrimal duct and skull base [9, 17]. The free edge of the UP may be pneumatized, hypoplastic or aplastik, and deviated medially or laterally. If the UP curves or bends medially, forming a hook-shaped structure, it can come into contact with the middle turbinate, while deviating laterally, it may encroach on the infundibulum. Either way, the drainage of the paranasal sinuses is blocked, which leads to chronic or recurrent sinusitis. Medial deviation of UP has been reported as the most common variation in previous studies [29]– [30]. In this study, pneumatized UP (5.8%) was found to be the most prevalent, followed by hook-shaped UP (5.5%) and bifid UP (4.3%). Moreover, cases with hook-shaped UP tended to have ipsilateral NSD (p = 0.103) and hypoplastic MS (p = 0.146), which may result in inadequate drainage of MS and advanced mucosal thickening by narrowing the infundibulum. So, the hook-shaped UP should raise suspicion about the simultaneous presence of the NSD and hypoplastic MS, which should be identified.

CT imaging of the paranasal sinuses and nasal cavity has become the preferred option due to its ability to map and detect the bony structures of the sinus region with precision. Pre-operative 3D CT assessment of the relationship between the NSD and sinonasal structures is vital for progress in the planning of craniofacial surgery [4, 11].

Information on the patency of the MS drainage pathway and MSV can be provided by studying the morphometry of the infundibulum which is the airway that connects MO to the middle meatus, as well as the presence of AMO and UP. Previous studies have examined nasal cavity variations and MSV separately, but a few studies have evaluated all sinonasal variants and infundibulum morphometry together, nor investigated the interaction between them in terms of NSD. A comprehensive knowledge of the infundibulum morphometry including MOW, IL, and IA, and its connection to sinonasal variants is vital to provide data on the risk of maxillary sinusitis and to avoid iatrogenic complications during surgery [3, 19]. Our findings indicated that hypoplastic MS and MSMT were more prevalent in female individuals. This was corroborated by the lower mean values of MOW, IL, and IA found among females. This suggests that the narrower MO and the shorter infundibulum with smaller angle, the difficult the drainage of sinusal content, thus increasing the chance of accumulation of secretion in consistent with some results of previous studies [3, 19–22]. However, when we compared MOW, IL and IA based on NSD types, we found that the mean IL tended to decrease and the mean MOW increased with an increasing degree of NSDA. Conversely, there was a tendency for the mean values of MOW and IL to decrease on the same side of the NSD. The mean IA value was found to be greater in cases of severe NSD compared to mild NSD. Additionally, it was observed that the mean IA value was larger on the ipsilateral side of the deviation, and this difference was found to be significant. These findings contradict the results of a study by Yousefi et al., which concluded that constricting the drainage route does not necessarily lead to maxillary sinusitis. Instead, the narrowing may be compensated by the mass effect of inflammation and mucosal thickening and MO may be widened slightly [19]. In our study, the mean IL value indicated a positive correlation with ageing, as well as a higher prevalence of hypoplastic MS and MSMT in older individuals. This further supports the notion that age impedes MS drainage, as reported in the study by Basurrah et al. [21].

The literature suggests that there may be anatomical compensation between the degree of NSD and ITH [14, 31]– [32], while several publications have failed to demonstrate a good correlation between them [11, 17]. Although the exact mechanism of ITH formation is not clear, the prevalence of ITH was found to be 13.3% (80/600), which has been reported in previous studies to range from 15% to 60.8% [11, 17, 31]. The ITH was detected on the contralateral side in 85% of cases of NSD. We also found no significant difference in terms of gender or age groups. NSP is typically caused by an extension of air from the frontal sinus or the crista galli. While it is usually insignificant, it can sometimes block the drainage of the middle meatus. NSP incidence has been reported as 27%−59.1% and is mostly not accompanied by NSD in previous studies [15, 33]– [34]. In our study, its prevalence was consistent with that reported in the literature, at 30%. NSD was also detected in the presence of NSP, occurring in 87.8% of cases. A higher prevalence was seen in males (36%) than in females (24%), while a notable decrease in the incidence of NSP was seen with ageing in this current study. Furthermore, moderate and severe NSD, AMO, hook-shaped UP, hypoplastic MS and MSMT were more prevalent in older subjects, while younger subjects had a higher occurrence of NSP, extensive MCB, PMT with a significant difference.

Our study had some limitations, which will inform future research. The CT-based classification system provides a detailed radiological framework for assessing nasal cavity variations and the relationship between them, but its clinical utility would be enhanced by surgical validation. Due to the retrospective design and the lack of clinical/genetic correlation for MSMT, as well as the unclear health status of the patients, it is not possible to consider anything other than anatomical factors. Furthermore, the study’s findings were constrained by its single-centre design and the exclusion of individuals from diverse ethnic backgrounds. It is necessary for our findings to be confirmed by prospective cohort studies comparing unhealthy and control groups with a large age range and 3D imaging being used.

Conclusions

The focus of this study was different from that of previous studies, in that the individualised prevalence of sinonasal variants was determined based on NSD types in relation to gender and age. Morover, our approach differs from that of previous studies in that we have analysed the statistical relationship between NSD types, sinonasal variants, and infundibulum morphometry. While our study corroborates numerous prior anatomical discoveries, it provides a far more detailed picture of the influence of NSD on MCB, PMT, ITH, NSP, MSV, MSMT, AMO, and UP variants, as well as infundibulum morphometry, which extends beyond the scope of previous studies. Additionally, our results give new information about how NSD, MCB, MSP, MSMT and UP variants are arranged and how common they are. The greater the degree of NSD, the more likely it is that MCB, PMT and ITH will occur on the opposite side. Conversely, AMO, hypoplastic MS, MSMT and hook-shaped UP are more prevalent on the same side of the deviation. However, the effect of NSD on infundibulum morphometry, including MSV, MOW and IL was weak. A larger mean IA value was found on the ipsilateral side of the NSD, with a significant difference being noted. In order to avoid misinterpretation and iatrogenic injury during dental, surgical and cosmetic procedures in the sinonasal region, it is imperative to elucidate the anatomical intricacies of the influence of NSD on sinonasal variants, MSV, and infundibulum morphometry in relation to gender, laterality and age, employing a 3D CT examination preoperatively.

LINC00514 regulates oral squamous cell carcinoma progression and prognosis by targeting miR-195-5p
Authors: Shubin Wang, Rihui Wang, Ya Gao, Kun Cao, Huandong Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeOral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) ranks as the most prevalent malignant tumor within the oral cavity. This research aims to investigate the potential function of LINC00514 in the prognosis and malignant progression of OSCC patients.MethodsThe study included 69 OSCC patients. LINC00514, miR-195-5p and MAPK8 expression in OSCC tissues and cells was determined using qRT-PCR. The prognostic value of LINC00514 was analyzed using Kaplan-Meier curves. The abilities of cells to proliferate, migrate, and invade were evaluated using CCK-8 and Transwell assays. MAPK8 protein levels were determined by Western blot. The regulatory mechanism was investigated by luciferase reporter assays and RIP experiments.ResultsIn OSCC tissues and cells, LINC00514 expression was elevated, miR-195-5p expression was reduced, and their expression exhibited a negative correlation. High LINC00514 expression was linked to a poor prognosis in OSCC patients. Silencing LINC00514 suppressed the proliferation, migration, and invasion of cells. miR-195-5p was a target miRNA of LINC00514. When miR-195-5p was inhibited, the suppression of cell proliferation, migration, and invasion caused by siRNA-LINC00514 was partially reversed. MAPK8 was a target mRNA of miR-195-5p. Overexpression of MAPK8 rescued the suppressed proliferation, migration, and invasion of cells caused by miR-195-5p upregulation.ConclusionLINC00514 exhibits high expression in OSCC and is linked to poor prognostic outcomes in patients. It modulates the malignant biological behavior of OSCC cells by targeting miR-195-5p. LINC00514 might serve as a potential prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target for OSCC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-026-07841-6.Keywords: LINC00514, miR-195-5p, OSCC, MAPK8

Introduction

Oral cancer, which is counted the most common cancer types worldwide, has become a significant public health issue contributing to cancer incidence and mortality [1]. Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) makes up over 90% of all oral cancer cases and represents the predominant subtype within head and neck SCC [2, 3]. OSCC predominantly occurs in critical oral sites like the tongue, gums, and buccal mucosa. It exhibits significant invasiveness and strong lymph node metastasis (LNM), posing a serious and persistent threat to public health [4, 5]. The development of OSCC proceeds through a complex pathological process. Among these, unhealthy lifestyle practices and human papillomavirus infection have been confirmed as significant risk factors for OSCC [6]. The current clinical treatment strategy for OSCC primarily involves surgical resection, supplemented by adjuvant therapies such as combined radiotherapy and chemotherapy [7]. Nevertheless, the general prognosis for OSCC patients remains unfavorable, and their five-year survival rate is below 50% [8]. Thus, delving into the molecular mechanisms underlying the onset and progression of OSCC holds critical significance for improving the clinical outcomes of OSCC patients.

Long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) fall into a category of RNA molecules with no capacity for encoding protein products [9]. Multiple studies have confirmed their potential as biomarkers for cancer [10]. LncRNAs play a pivotal role in tumor regulation, with their mutations and abnormal expression closely associated with tumorigenesis, invasion, metastasis, and prognosis [11, 12]. Numerous studies have demonstrated that many lncRNAs function as early biomarkers and potential therapeutic targets for OSCC [13]. LINC00514, as a recently emerging lncRNA of interest, has gradually revealed its role in various malignant tumors. Research indicated that LINC00514 was overexpressed in gastric cancer tissues, and this elevated expression promoted cell growth and the EMT process [14]. In esophageal SCC, elevated LINC00514 expression was correlated with poor patient prognosis, while its downregulation inhibited cell proliferation and invasive capacity [15]. Additionally, Liu et al. observed that LINC00514 was overexpressed in OSCC tissues [16]. However, the specific biological function of LINC00514 in OSCC and its molecular mechanisms involved in the development and progression of OSCC remain unclear and warrant further investigation.

LncRNAs can act as “molecular sponges” to adsorb miRNAs, thereby contributing to the regulation of tumorigenesis and progression [17]. miR-195-5p was a target of LINC00514. Studies indicated that miR-195-5p was downregulated in OSCC tissues, and its overexpression induced apoptosis while inhibiting tumor cell proliferation, migration, and invasion [18]. Based on the above findings, we hypothesize that in OSCC, LINC00514 may influence biological behaviors by targeting and binding to miR-195-5p, and is associated with patient prognosis.

This study detects LINC00514 in OSCC tissues and cells, and analyzes its correlation with patients’ clinical pathological characteristics and prognosis. Subsequently, in vitro experiments are conducted to explore how silencing LINC00514 and miR-195-5p affects the proliferation, migration, and invasion abilities of OSCC cells. This study explores the roles and mechanisms of LINC00514 in OSCC progression, to provide new foundations and targets for prognosis and targeted therapeutic strategies.

Methods

Tissue samples

This study enrolled 69 OSCC patients who underwent surgical treatment at The Affiliated Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University, having their diagnoses confirmed by pathological examination. Each patient provided a pair of tissue samples, including one derived from the tumor and the other from the surrounding non-malignant tissue. Inclusion criteria: [1] initial diagnosis of OSCC verified by imaging studies and postoperative pathological biopsy; [2] no prior treatment for the disease; [3] accessibility of comprehensive clinical data. Exclusion criteria: [1] presence of other concurrent malignant tumors; [2] severe systemic diseases that contraindicated surgical intervention; [3] current participation in other clinical trials. The Ethics Committee of The Affiliated Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University granted approval for the study protocol, and every participant provided written informed consent in advance of their participation. All enrolled patients were followed for a period of five years, with survival status ascertained through telephone contact or scheduled return visits.

Cell lines and culture

The OSCC cell lines (CAL-27, SCC-4, SCC-6, SCC-25) and NHOK cells were obtained from Science Cell Laboratory. All cells were maintained in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco, USA) fortified with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin at 37 °C in a 5% CO₂ incubator.

Cell transfection

Si-LINC00514, miR-195-5p mimic, miR-195-5p inhibitor, oe-MAPK8 and their corresponding controls were produced by GenePharma (Shanghai, China). Transfection were performed with the use of Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, USA).

qRT-PCR assay

For the extraction of total RNA, OSCC tissue samples and cells were processed using TRIzol (Invitrogen, USA). Subsequently, the first-strand cDNA was synthesized using EvoScript Universal cDNA Master Mix (Roche, Switzerland). qRT-PCR reactions were executed via SYBR Green (Roche, Switzerland). GAPDH and U6 were employed as internal control genes, and the 2−ΔΔCT method was applied to conduct quantitative analysis [19, 20].

Cell proliferation detection

The proliferation capacity of cells after transfection was assessed using the CCK-8 kit (Dojindo, Japan). Cells (1 × 10⁴) were placed into a 96-well plate. At 0, 24, 48, and 72 h post-seeding, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was dispensed into each well, with subsequent incubation at 37 °C for 4 h. Absorbance values were subsequently measured at 450 nm.

Transwell assay

SCC-4 and SCC-6 cells (5 × 10⁴) were resuspended separately in serum-free medium. The assessment of cell invasion was conducted with Matrigel-precoated Transwell chambers (BD Biosciences); for migration assays, uncoated Transwell chambers were employed. Cells were plated in the upper chamber; the lower chamber held medium supplemented with 10% FBS. After 24 h, unattached or non-migrating cells were discarded. The remaining cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with crystal violet. Random fields of view were chosen under the microscope to count the cells.

Western blot

Total proteins were collected from cells using RIPA lysis buffer. The protein content was determined using the BCA kit (Solarbio, Beijing, China). The proteins were separated by SDS polyacrylamide gel and transferred onto a PVDF membrane. The membrane was blocked with 5% skimmed milk for 1 h, washed with TBST, and incubated with the primary antibody at 4 °C overnight. The next day, the membrane was incubated with the secondary antibody. The antibodies were provided by Abcam. The detection was performed using the ECL chemiluminescent detection system (Vazyme, Shanghai, China).

Subcellular fractionation

Nuclear and cytoplasmic fractions derived from OSCC cells were obtained via NE-PER Nuclear and Cytoplasmic Extraction Reagents (Thermo Fisher Scientific). LINC00514 expression was subsequently quantified by qRT-PCR.

Luciferase assay

The wild-type or mutant LINC00514 or MAPK8 3’UTR sequences with miR-195-5p binding sites were respectively cloned into the pGL3 vector (Promega, USA). The constructed recombinant plasmid was co-transfected with miR-195-5p mimic/inhibitor into SCC-4 or SCC-6 cells. Cells were harvested at the 48-hour time point post-transfection, followed by detection of luciferase activity via the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA).

RIP assay

RIP experiments were conducted with the Magna RIP RNA-Binding Protein Immunoprecipitation Kit (Millipore, USA). Cell extracts were incubated with anti-IgG or anti-Ago2 antibody-conjugated magnetic beads overnight at 4 °C. Subsequently, RNA within the immunoprecipitation complexes was dissociated using proteinase K, and the enrichment of LINC00514, miR-195-5p or MAPK8 was assayed via qRT-PCR.

Bioinformatic analysis

The lncRNASNP2 online database was employed to predict miRNAs potentially interacting with LINC00514. The miRDB and TargetScan databases served as tools for the screening of potential target genes corresponding to miR-195-5p. GO functional annotation and KEGG enrichment analysis were subsequently conducted with the use of the Weishengxin online platform. Additionally, the STRING database served as a tool for the establishment of a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network, which was used to investigate interaction relationships among the proteins encoded by the target genes.

Statistical analysis

All analyses and graphical representations were conducted via SPSS 26.0 and GraphPad Prism 8. Data were reported as mean ± standard deviation. Student’s t-test was used to conduct comparisons between two groups, whereas one-way ANOVA was applied to analyze comparisons among multiple groups. To examine the association between LINC00514 expression and clinicopathological characteristics, the χ² test was utilized. The diagnostic value of LINC00514 for OSCC was evaluated through the ROC curve. Survival curves were generated through the Kaplan-Meier method, with the Log rank test employed to perform comparisons of the curves. Prognostic factors were assessed utilizing the Cox regression model. Correlations was analyzed using Pearson’s correlation coefficient. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

LINC00514 is upregulated in OSCC tissues and cells

LINC00514 expression was determined via qRT-PCR in 69 OSCC patients. LINC00514 expression was found to be higher in cancer tissues (P < 0.0001, Fig. 1A). Similarly, compared to normal cells (NHOK), LINC00514 expression was up-regulated in CAL-27, SCC-4, SCC-6, and SCC-25 cells (P < 0.05, Fig. 1B).

Association between LINC00514 and clinicopathological characteristics of OSCC patients

In light of the observed high expression of LINC00514 in OSCC tissues, the correlation between its expression and the patients’ clinicopathological features was further examined (Table 1). Using the mean expression value of LINC00514 in the tumor tissues as a cutoff, the 69 OSCC patients were classified into a high LINC00514 group (n = 35) and a low LINC00514 group (n = 34). Statistical analysis showed that high LINC00514 expression was linked to TNM stage (P = 0.022) and LNM (P = 0.028).

The clinical significance of LINC00514 in OSCC patients

This study further evaluated the clinical application potential of LINC00514. The ROC curve analysis revealed that LINC00514 exhibited high diagnostic efficacy. The AUC for diagnosing OSCC was 0.913, with a sensitivity of 76.81% and a specificity of 95.65% (Fig. 2A). K-M survival analysis revealed that patients with low LINC00514 expression had better overall survival (Log rank P = 0.028; Fig. 2B). Multivariate Cox regression analysis indicated that LINC00514 (HR = 4.039, 95% CI: 1.281–12.735, P = 0.017) and TNM stage (HR = 2.275, 95% CI: 1.067–4.851, P = 0.033) and LNM (HR = 2.424, 95% CI: 1.110–5.292, P = 0.026) were all independent prognostic factors (Table 2).

Silencing LINC00514 inhibits OSCC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion

In order to clarify the functional mechanism of LINC00514 during OSCC progression, cells were selected for siRNA-mediated knockdown of LINC00514. Compared with the control group and the si-NC group, LINC00514 expression in SCC4 and SCC6 cells transfected with si-LINC00514 was significantly decreased, indicating that si-LINC00514 can effectively knockdown the expression of LINC00514 in these two cell lines (P < 0.0001, Fig. 3A). CCK-8 assay results demonstrated that silencing LINC00514 markedly suppressed the proliferation of SCC-4 and SCC-6 cells (P < 0.0001, Fig. 3B-C). Transwell assays revealed that downregulation of LINC00514 significantly attenuated the migration and invasion capabilities of both cell types (P < 0.0001, Fig. 3D-E). These findings imply that LINC00514 might exert a critical function in promoting the proliferation, migration, and invasion of OSCC cells.

Silencing LINC00514 inhibits the proliferation, migration, and invasion capabilities of OSCC cells. A qRT-PCR detection of LINC00514 expression following siRNA-LINC00514 transfection. B-C CKK-8 assay for detecting cell proliferation capacity. D-E Transwell assay for detecting cell migration and invasion capabilities. (****P < 0.0001)

miR-195-5p is regulated by LINC00514

To investigate whether LINC00514 participates in post-transcriptional regulatory processes, its subcellular localization was first examined through fractionation experiments. The findings indicated that LINC00514 was predominantly situated in the cytoplasm of SCC-4 and SCC-6 cells (Fig. 4A-B). Based on this finding, the lncRNASNP2 database was utilized to predict a complementary binding site between LINC00514 and miR-195-5p (Fig. 4C). When validating this interaction, the results revealed that the miR-195-5p mimic exerted a suppressive effect on luciferase activity in the LINC00514-WT group, whereas the inhibitor enhanced its activity (P < 0.01). In contrast, neither of these treatments significantly altered the activity in the MUT group (Fig. 4D-E). RIP experiments further indicated that LINC00514 and miR-195-5p were enriched in anti-Ago2 immunoprecipitation complexes (P < 0.0001, Fig. 4F-G). Additionally, miR-195-5p was down-expressed, and its expression was negatively correlated with LINC00514 (r = -0.510, P < 0.01, Fig. 4H-J).

The LINC00514/miR-494-5p axis participates in OSCC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion

After transfection of miR-195-5p inhibitor in SCC4 and SCC6 cells, the expression levels were significantly decreased compared with the control group and the inhibitor NC group (P < 0.0001, Fig. S1A). To clarify the regulatory relationship between miR-195-5p and LINC00514, the expression of miR-195-5p was detected in cells. The data demonstrated that silencing LINC00514 increased miR-195-5p expression (P < 0.01), whereas subsequent transfection with the miR-195-5p inhibitor exerted an effective reduction in its expression (Fig. 5A). Functionally, CCK-8 and Transwell assays demonstrated that inhibition of miR-195-5p partially reversed the suppression of cell proliferation, migration, and invasion potential triggered induced by LINC00514 silencing (P < 0.01, Fig. 5B-E).

Target gene analysis of miR-195-5p

Prediction of potential target genes for miR-195-5p was conducted using the miRDB and TargetScan databases. Venn diagram analysis identified 77 common target genes (Fig. 6A). GO enrichment analysis of these target genes revealed marked enrichment in biological processes such as peptidyl-serine phosphorylation, the stress-activated MAPK cascade, and peptidyl-serine modification. Regarding cellular components, the genes were primarily localized to the presynaptic active zone, nuclear pore, and glial cell projection. For molecular function, significant associations were observed with SMAD binding, sulfur compound binding, and I-SMAD binding (Fig. 6B). KEGG pathway analysis indicated notable enrichment of these target genes in the MAPK signaling pathway, Salmonella infection, and Insulin resistance (Fig. 6C). A PPI network was subsequently constructed using the STRING database, which contained 77 nodes and 47 edges, with an average node degree of 1.22. The colors of the edges represent different types of interaction evidence (Fig. 6D).

MAPK8 is the target gene of miR-195-5p

To explore the mechanism of miR-195-5p, the downstream target genes of miR-195-5p were investigated. The TargetScan database predicted that there were binding sites between MAPK8 and miR-195-5p (Fig. 7A). Dual luciferase reporter gene analysis showed that overexpression of miR-195-5p inhibited the luciferase activity of the MAPK8-WT group in the cells, while inhibition of miR-195-5p increased the luciferase activity of the MAPK8-WT group in the cells (P < 0.0001), but did not affect the luciferase activity of the MAPK8-MUT group (Fig. 7B-C). The RIP experiment found that in the anti-Ago2 immunoprecipitation complex, both MAPK8 and miR-195-5p were significantly enriched (P < 0.0001, Fig. 7D-E). In addition, qRT-PCR results showed that the high expression of MAPK8 in OSCC tissues was negatively correlated with the level of miR-195-5p (r = -0.550, P < 0.001, Fig. 7F-G).

D-E RIP experiments further confirmed the interaction between miR-195-5p and MAPK8. F qRT-PCR detection of MAPK8 expression in OSCC tissues. G The correlation between miR-195-5p and MAPK8 in OSCC tissues. (****P < 0.0001)

Overexpression of MAPK8 reverses the effects of upregulated miR-195-5p on the proliferation, migration and invasion of OSCC cells

In SCC4 and SCC6 cells, after transfection with oe-MAPK8, the relative expression level of MAPK8 was significantly higher than that of the control group and the oe-NC group (P < 0.0001, Fig. S1B). To investigate whether MAPK8 was involved in the effect of miR-195-5p on cell progression, miR-195-5p mimic and oe-MAPK8 were co-transfected into SCC4 and SCC6 cells. As shown in Fig. 8A-B, qRT-PCR results indicated that overexpression of miR-195-5p significantly reduced the expression of MAPK8 in the cells, and further overexpression of MAPK8 could restore the downward trend, indicating successful transfection (P < 0.05). Western blot was used to detect the protein level of MAPK8. The results showed that overexpression of MAPK8 could restore the inhibitory effect of miR-195-5p mimic on MAPK8 (Fig. 8C). Additionally, the results of CCK-8 and Transwell assays indicated that overexpression of miR-195-5p significantly reduced cell viability, migration and migration ability. Overexpression of MAPK8 reversed the inhibitory effect of upregulated miR-195-5p on cell viability, migration and invasion (P < 0.05, Fig. 8D-G). These results further confirmed that miR-195-5p participates in the progression of OSCC cells through MAPK8.

Discussion

OSCC, the most prevalent head and neck malignancy, presented significant clinical challenges owing to its high incidence, recurrence rate, and poor prognosis [21, 22]. In-depth investigation into the pathogenesis of OSCC and the exploration of effective therapeutic targets are crucial for improving patient outcomes. This research systematically examined the expression profile, clinical prognostic importance, and molecular regulatory mechanisms of LINC00514 in OSCC, providing a new theoretical basis for understanding OSCC pathogenesis and its potential clinical applications.

This research identified that LINC00514 exhibited high expression in OSCC tissues and cell lines, which matched the previous finding by Liu et al. that “LINC00514 was upregulated in OSCC tissues” [16], further confirming the aberrant expression pattern of LINC00514 during OSCC development. Clinical assessment revealed that high LINC00514 expression had a significant association with TNM stage and LNM. Patients with low LINC00514 expression exhibited significantly better overall survival, suggesting that LINC00514 serves as an independent factor indicating poor prognosis in OSCC. This pattern of LINC00514 function aligns closely with reports on other lncRNAs in OSCC. For instance, high LINC01123 expression was observed in OSCC tissues and was linked to LNM, advanced TNM stage, and an unfavorable prognosis [23]. Similarly, elevated levels of LINC00664 exhibited a correlation with shorter overall survival among OSCC patients [24]. These collective findings suggest that certain lncRNAs, including LINC00514, may function as oncogenic factors in OSCC by modulating key biological processes involved in disease progression and patient outcome.

To elucidate the biological function of LINC00514 in OSCC progression, SCC-4 and SCC-6 cells—characterized by high endogenous LINC00514 expression—were chosen for siRNA-mediated knockdown. Experimental results demonstrated that silencing LINC00514 significantly inhibited these cellular biological behaviors. These observations match the previously reported oncogenic roles of LINC00514 in other malignancies. For instance, in NSCLC, LINC00514 was found to be upregulated, and its knockdown impaired the ability of NSCLC cells to proliferate, migrate, and invade [25]. Similarly, another research showed that silencing LINC00514 suppressed cell proliferation and migration and promoted apoptosis [26]. These consistent results further suggest that LINC00514 may act as a common oncogenic driver across multiple types of malignant tumors.

Interactions between lncRNAs and miRNAs represent an important mechanism by which lncRNAs exert their biological functions [27]. In this study, bioinformatic predictions from the lncRNASNP2 database and subsequent molecular experiments confirmed that LINC00514 acted as a ceRNA through interacting with miR-195-5p, thereby promoting oncogenic effects. Experiments investigating subcellular localization revealed that LINC00514 was mainly situated in the cytoplasm, providing a structural basis for its role as a miRNA sponge. Luciferase reporter assays and RIP experiments further verified the specific binding interaction between LINC00514 and miR-195-5p. Of note, miR-195-5p—previously characterized as a tumor-suppressive miRNA—was downregulated in OSCC, and its overexpression has been demonstrated to induce apoptosis and impair tumor cell proliferation [28, 29]. Furthermore, inhibition of miR-195-5p was found to partially reverse the impairment of proliferation, migration, and invasion resulting from LINC00514 knockdown, supporting the hypothesis that LINC00514 facilitates OSCC malignancy by regulating miR-195-5p.

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses of the target genes of miR-195-5p revealed significant enrichment in biological processes like the MAPK signaling pathway and stress-activated MAPK cascade. The MAPK signaling pathway, which serves as a central regulator of cell proliferation, apoptosis, and metastasis, is often aberrantly activated in OSCC [30]. Consistent with previous studies indicating that miR-195-5p suppresses cancer progression via suppressing MAPK pathway-related genes [31, 32]. This study confirmed through bioinformatics prediction, dual luciferase reporter assay and RIP experiment that MAPK8 was a direct target gene of miR-195-5p. Overexpression of miR-195-5p significantly reduced the mRNA and protein levels of MAPK8. The rescue experiment ultimately confirmed that overexpression of MAPK8 reversed the effects of upregulated miR-195-5p on the proliferation, migration and invasion of OSCC cells. This study further suggested that LINC00514 may exert its effect by adsorbing miR-195-5p, thereby removing its post-transcriptional inhibitory effect on the downstream target gene MAPK8, and subsequently promoting the malignant progression of OSCC. These results provide further mechanistic insight into the function of the LINC00514/miR-195-5p/MAPK8 axis in OSCC pathogenesis.

This research had some limitations. First, the relatively small size of the clinical sample and a single-center design was adopted, which may limit the generalizability of the research results and introduce potential selection bias. Secondly, due to the limited sample size and the incomplete nature of the retrospective data, we were unable to incorporate more potential confounding clinical factors into the multivariate analysis to further validate the independent prognostic value of LINC00514. Future studies should conduct larger-scale, prospective, and multi-center designs. Based on the collection of complete clinical information, a more rigorous multivariate model should be used to verify the potential of LINC00514 as an independent prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target.

In summary, this study confirmed that LINC00514 exhibits high expression in OSCC and is closely linked to unfavorable patient prognosis. Mechanistically, LINC00514 regulates the proliferation, migration, and invasion of OSCC cells by regulating the miR-195-5p/MAPK8. These findings revealed that the LINC00514/miR-195-5p/MAPK8 axis represents a potential therapeutic target for its OSCC.

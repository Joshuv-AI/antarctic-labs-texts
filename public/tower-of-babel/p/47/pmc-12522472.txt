Intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor agents as an adjunct for proliferative diabetic retinopathy: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Xinzhi Song, Ling Li, Xiangli Wang, Xuemei Zhang, Qihang Lei, Guojun Liu, Lijing Wang, Jiaxiang Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo evaluate the efficacy and safety of intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) agents adjunct prior to pars plana vitrectomy (PPV) for patients with proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR).MethodsA systematically comprehensive literature search was performed in PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, ClinicalTrials.gov, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wanfang, VIP Database for Chinese Technical Periodicals, and Chinese Biomedical Literature Database to identify relevant randomized controlled trials (RCTs) up to October 31, 2024. The Peferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta Analysis guidelines were followed. Review Manager 5.4 software was used to conduct statistical analyses.Results91 RCTs involving 8721 eyes were included. The intraoperative indices revealed that the amount, grade and incidence of intraoperative bleeding, duration of surgery, the frequency of endodiathermy, the incidences of iatrogenic retinal breaks, endodiathermy, retinotomy, and silicone oil tamponade were significantly less in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (P < 0.05). Moreover, the postoperative indices showed that anti-VEGF agents adjunct before PPV could achieve better postoperative best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) at different time points, higher postoperative retinal anatomical reattachment, lower postoperative macular retinal thickness (MRT) at different time points and intraocular pressure (IOP) at < 1 month, ≥ 3 months but < 6 months, and shorter clearing time of postoperative vitreous hemorrhage (POVH) (P < 0.05). Furthermore, the incidences of postoperative complications such as early POVH, late POVH at different time points, retinal detachment, re-proliferation, aseptic and infective endophthalmitis, iris rubeosis, neovascular glaucoma, hyphema, and elevated IOP were significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (P < 0.05). Besides, anti-VEGF agents adjunct before PPV could achieve lower re-PPV probability (P < 0.0001).ConclusionsIntravitreal anti-VEGF agents adjunct prior to PPV for PDR patients might facilitate much easier surgery, shorter surgical time, better postoperative BCVA, and lower postoperative MRT; meanwhile reduce the incidence of intraoperative and postoperative complications, and the risk of re-PPV. In addition, more RCTs with better design, larger sample sizes and longer follow-up time are needed to provide more reliable evidence.PROSPERO registration numberCRD42024604615 (22 October 2024).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12886-025-04327-x.Keywords: Anti-vascular endothelial growth factor agents, Proliferative diabetic retinopathy, Pars plana vitrectomy, Systematic review, Meta-analysis, Randomized controlled trials

Introduction

The International Diabetes Federation estimated the global diabetes mellitus (DM) prevalence of 20–79 years old in 2021 was 10.5% (536.6 million people), rising to 12.2% (783.2 million people) in 2045 [1]. As a primary microvascular complication of DM, diabetic retinopathy (DR) is a leading cause of irreversible visual impairment and blindness in the working-age population [2]. DR can be broadly divided into two stages according to the severity, non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy (NPDR) and proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR). PDR is hallmarked by neovascularization of optic disc or elsewhere, vitreous hemorrhage (VH), fibrovascular proliferation and traction retinal detachment (TRD). If not treated in time, it may lead to further decline in vision or even blindness. Pars plana vitrectomy (PPV) is the main treatment for PDR, including VH clearing, fibrovascular proliferative membranes (FVPMs) dissection and vitreoretinal traction release. Although the surgical techniques and instruments of PPV have made great progress, there may have a variety of complications during and after PPV alone in PDR patients, such as intraoperative hemorrhage, iatrogenic retinal breaks (IRBs), recurrent postoperative VH (POVH), RD, neovascular glaucoma (NVG), resulting in poor prognosis.

As a potent mediator for abnormal retinal vessel growth and leakage, vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) plays a pivotal role in the occurrence and development of PDR [3]. In recent 15 years, many studies have reported that anti-VEGF agents pretreatment can shorten PPV operation time, reduce PPV related complications, improve postoperative visual acuity, lower the probability of POVH and reoperation [4–7]. Therefore, many experts believe that in order to minimize PPV complications and maximize surgical outcomes, anti-VEGF agents pretreatment before PPV in PDR patients should be regarded as a necessary adjuvant therapy [8–15]. However, some other experts maintain that anti-VEGF agents have no significant effect on reducing PPV difficulty or the development of postoperative course [7, 16].

A few previous meta-analyses were conducted to evaluate the surgical outcomes of PPV alone versus PPV combined with anti-VEGF agents pretreatment [8, 10, 11, 14, 15, 17–19]. The results showed that PPV combined with anti-VEGF agents pretreatment had a shorter surgical time and lower incidences of intraoperative and postoperative complications compared to PPV alone. Although they were rigorous and scientific studies, there are inevitably some limitations: (1) according to the timeline order of anti-VEGF agents emergence, monoclonal antibody agents were the earliest used in ophthalmic clinical practice; with the acceleration of anti-VEGF agents development, fusion protein agents appeared in the past decade and were only applied to DR within five years; however, most previous meta-analyses only included monoclonal antibody agents and did not include fusion protein agents; (2) most previous studies included PDR patients from Western European countries, while there were fewer studies on Eastern Asian countries, like China and India, which had the largest population and largest number of PDR patients; (3) previous studies included the use of anti-VEGF agents at different time points during PPV perioperative period, such as before surgery, intraoperative, and after surgery; (4) previous studies included many 20G PPV studies [8, 10, 11]. With the development of surgical instruments and techniques, modern PPV has converted to minimally invasive surgery, such as 23G, 25G or 27G. Therefore, there is an urgent need for contemporary data. Hence, we conducted this systematic review and meta-analysis, including all currently available data, to re-evaluate the efficacy and safety of anti-VEGF agents adjunct before PPV for PDR, hereby providing references for the clinical decision-making of ophthalmologists.

Methods

This study was conducted according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines [20]. The review protocol has been submitted and published on PROSPERO (Registration number: CRD42024604615).

Search strategy

PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, ClinicalTrials.gov, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wanfang, VIP Database for Chinese Technical Periodicals, and Chinese Biomedical Literature Database were systematically searched for randomized controlled trials (RCTs) dated up to October 31, 2024, using the following terms: (((((Anti-vascular endothelial growth factor or anti-VEGF or Bevacizumab or Avastin or Ranibizumab or Lucentis or Aflibercept or VEGF Trap Eye or Eylea or Ziv-aflibercept or Zaltrap or Conbercept or KH902 or Brolucizumab or Beovu or Pegaptanib sodium or Pegaptanib or Macugen or Fariximab [MeSH Terms]) AND ((((Diabetic Retinopathy or DR or proliferative diabetic retinopathy or PDR[MeSH Terms]) AND (((Vitrectomy or Pars Plana Vitrectomy or PPV[MeSH Terms]) AND ((intravitreal injection or IVI or intravitreal [MeSH Terms]) AND (Randomized Controlled Trial or random))))). In order to avoid the literatures missing, the references of the retrieved literatures were searched manually. The reference lists of the pertinent reviews and original articles were also explored to identify potential trials that may have been neglected in the primary searches.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: (1) methodological criterion: RCTs; (2) participants: PDR patients, including or not including TRD without or with involving the macula, non-absorbing VH requiring surgical intervention, or progressive PDR; (3) intervention: PPV, including 20G PPV or minimally invasive PPV such as 23G, 25G, 25G + or 27G; (4) comparison: with or without anti-VEGF agents adjunct before PPV; (5) outcomes: primary outcomes: intraoperative indices such as the amount, grade and incidence of intraoperative bleeding, endodiathermy application, duration of surgery; postoperative indices like best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) (logMAR scale), BCVA improvement, macular retinal thickness (MRT), intraocular pressure (IOP), early and late POVH; secondary outcomes: preoperative complexity scores; intraoperative indices such as IRBs, relaxing retinotomy and silicone oil or inert gas or sterile air or balanced salt solution (BSS) tamponade; postoperative indices like the rate of retinal anatomy reattachment, recurrent RD and re-PPV, endophthalmitis, re-proliferation, iris rubeosis, NVG, hyphema, elevated IOP.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Not RCTs (such as review, retrospective case-control trial, case report, letters to the editor, correspondence, notes, editorials, etc.); (2) patients with a history of PPV before, or receiving intraocular surgery recently, or other intraocular diseases that probably affect the vitreoretinal surgery like retinal vascular disorders, uveitis, proliferative vitreoretinopathy, traumatic RD or congenital vitreoretinopathies; or systemic diseases not suitable for intravitreal injection; (3) in addition to with or without anti-VEGF agents adjunct before PPV, there is else other preoperative interventions; (4) studies with repeat data, or insufficient data, or lack of comparative data: duplicate publications were cross-checked via database overlap analysis, studies with insufficient data or lack of comparative data were proved via author contact; (5) not human subjects; (6) studies not published in English or Chinese.

Data extraction and assessment of methodological quality

According to the established retrieval strategies, screening titles and abstracts, obtaining and reviewing the full text of each article, which met the inclusion criteria and inconsistent with exclusion criteria were included. Two investigators (XZ-S and L-L) independently performed standardized data extraction, including the following information: first author, publication year, country, details of anti-VEGF agent injection, group size, patients’ mean age, gender ratio, mean baseline BCVA, design, and intraoperative and postoperative evaluating parameters and follow-up duration. For previous study with the same cohort of patients, the data from updated publications were extracted synthetically. If the pivotal data were unavailable, the corresponding authors of the included RCTs would be contacted. Two reviewers (XL-W, XM-Z) independently used 12-item scale [21] to assess the methodological quality of each included RCTs: a study with score ≥ 7 was judged high quality, > 4 but < 7 was judged moderate quality, and ≤ 4 was judged low quality. If there were divergences, then discussed with the corresponding authors (XZ-S, L-L) to reach a consensus.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using Review Manager 5.4 (Cochrane Collaboration) software. Weighted mean difference (WMD) and its 95% confidence interval (CI) were calculated for continuous data; risk ratio (RR) and its 95% CI were calculated for dichotomous data. χ2 test and I2 were used to assess the statistical heterogeneity. Fixed-effect model would be applied if heterogeneity was insignificant (P > 0.1, I2 < 50%). If heterogeneity was obvious (P < 0.1, I2 > 50%), subgroup analyses were counducted according to different anti-VEGF agent types such as monoclonal antibodies vs. fusion proteins, PPV gauge like 20G vs. 23G/25G/27G, or different anti-VEGF administration timing like 1–5 days vs. 6–14 days before PPV to distinguish the sources of heterogeneity. If subgroup analyses were unable to eliminate the heterogeneity, sensitivity analyses were performed through omitting 1 study each time. A random-effect model would be applied if the heterogeneity could not be eliminated. Publication bias was appraised by visual examination of a funnel plot, Begg’s funnel plot and the Egger’s linear regression test. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Preoperative complexity scores

The pooled results of these analyses revealed that there was not statistically significant difference for preoperative complexity scores between the PPV + anti-VEGF group and the PPV group (WMD = −0.20, 95%CI −0.73−0.32, P = 0.45, I2 = 0%) (Supplementary Fig. 1).

Intraoperative indices

Duration of surgery

The duration of surgery was significantly less in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (WMD = 24.44, 95%CI 20.84–28.05, P < 0.00001, I2 = 95%) (Supplementary Fig. 2). Due to evident heterogeneity for this outcome, subgroup analyses were performed. However, subgroup analyses according to different anti-VEGF agent types (I2 = 95%, Supplementary Fig. 2 A), PPV gauge (I2 = 94%, Supplementary Fig. 2B) and different anti-VEGF administration timing (I2 = 95%, Supplementary Fig. 2 C) were unable to eliminate the heterogeneity, sensitivity analysis was conducted. But heterogeneity still could not be eliminated through omitting one study at a time. Given that both subgroup analyses and sensitivity analyses failed to eliminate heterogeneity, a random-effect model was used for analysis.

Intraoperative bleeding

The included RCTs applied differential grades for the evaluation of intraoperative bleeding, which was classified into 3 grades: grade 1, mild bleeding that stopped either spontaneously or through transient bottle raising; grade 2, moderate bleeding requiring endodiathermy or with broad sheets of clots emergence extending away from the bleeding site; grade 3, thick clot development covering more than half of the posterior pole or interfering with the surgical plane [13]. The pooled results of these analyses revealed that the general incidence of intraoperative bleeding was significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 2.91, 95%CI 2.13–3.97, P < 0.00001, I2 = 93%) (Supplementary Fig. 3). Due to evident heterogeneity for this outcome, subgroup analyses were performed. However, subgroup analyses according to different anti-VEGF agent types (I2 = 93%, Supplementary Fig. 3 A), PPV gauge (I2 = 93%, Supplementary Fig. 3B) and different anti-VEGF administration timing (I2 = 93%, Supplementary Fig. 3 C) were unable to eliminate the heterogeneity, sensitivity analysis was conducted. But heterogeneity still could not be eliminated through omitting one study at a time. Given that both subgroup analyses and sensitivity analyses failed to eliminate heterogeneity, a random-effect model was used for analysis. Moreover, the incidence of intraoperative bleeding with grade 2 and grade 3 were significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 2.15, 95%CI 1.28–3.61, P = 0.004, I2 = 53%; RR = 5.09, 95%CI 1.79–14.52, P = 0.002, I2 = 0%), while the difference was not statistically significant with grade 1 (RR = 0.69, 95%CI 0.32–1.48, P = 0.34, I2 = 73%) (Supplementary Fig. 4). In addition, the amount of intraoperative bleeding was significantly less in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (WMD = 4.92, 95%CI 1.09–8.74, P = 0.01, I2 = 100%) (Supplementary Fig. 5). Given that subgroup analysis and sensitivity analysis failed to eliminate heterogeneity, a random-effect model was used for analysis.

Iatrogenic retinal breaks (IRBs)

The incidence of IRBs was significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 2.86, 95%CI 2.45–3.34, P < 0.00001, I2 = 29%) (Supplementary Fig. 6).

Endodiathermy

The proportion of endodiathermy was significantly less in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 2.98, 95%CI 2.38–3.74, P < 0.00001, I2 = 51%) (Supplementary Fig. 7). Due to evident heterogeneity for this outcome, a random-effect model was used for analysis. Because subgroup analysis was unable to eliminate the heterogeneity, sensitivity analysis was conducted. The heterogeneity was eliminated when removing Dong X, 2019 [25], the remaining pooled results showed that the difference between the PPV + anti-VEGF group and the PPV group was still significant (RR = 2.82, 95%CI 2.28–3.49, P < 0.00001, I2 = 44%). The heterogeneity was eliminated when removing Jia J, 2019 [75], the remaining pooled results showed that the difference between the PPV + anti-VEGF group and the PPV group was still significant (RR = 3.06, 95%CI 2.48–3.76, P < 0.00001, I2 = 33%). The heterogeneity was eliminated when removing Ou Z, 2021 [85], the remaining pooled results showed that the difference between the PPV + anti-VEGF group and the PPV group was still significant (RR = 3.08, 95%CI 2.49–3.81, P < 0.00001, I2 = 37%).

In addition, the frequency of endodiathermy was significantly less in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (WMD = 1.14, 95%CI 0.77–1.51, P < 0.00001, I2 = 95%) (Supplementary Fig. 8). Due to the failure of subgroup analysis and sensitivity analysis to eliminate heterogeneity, a random-effect model was used for analysis.

Relaxing retinotomy

The incidence of relaxing retinotomy was significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 2.12, 95%CI 1.04–4.32, P = 0.04, I2 = 0%) (Supplementary Fig. 9).

Intraocular tamponade

The proportion of silicone oil tamponade was significantly less in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 2.04, 95%CI 1.64–2.55, P < 0.00001, I2 = 55%) (Supplementary Fig. 10). Due to evident heterogeneity for this outcome, a random-effect model was used for analysis. Because subgroup analysis was unable to eliminate the heterogeneity, sensitivity analysis was conducted. The heterogeneity was eliminated when removing Ding YZ, 2023 [67], the remaining pooled results showed that the difference between the PPV + anti-VEGF group and the PPV group was still significant (RR = 2.12, 95%CI 1.71–2.62, P < 0.00001, I2 = 47%).

The proportion of BSS tamponade was significantly more in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 0.65, 95%CI 0.54–0.77, P < 0.00001, I2 = 0%) (Supplementary Fig. 11).

There was no significant difference for the proportion of sterile air or inert gas tamponade between the PPV + anti-VEGF group and the PPV group (RR = 1.03, 95%CI 0.91–1.16, P = 0.63, I2 = 39%) (Supplementary Fig. 12).

Postoperative indices

Retinal anatomical reattachment

The proportion of retinal anatomical reattachment was significantly higher in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 0.93, 95%CI 0.87–0.99, P = 0.02, I2 = 0%) (Supplementary Fig. 13).

Postoperative best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA)

The postoperative BCVA was divided into different time points, including early postoperative period (< 1 month), short-term (≥ 1 month but < 3 months), middle-term (≥ 3 months but < 6 months), and long-term (≥ 6 months). The pooled results of these analyses indicated that the postoperative BCVA at different time points were significantly better in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (WMD = 0.18, 95%CI 0.12–0.25, P < 0.00001, I2 = 85%; WMD = 0.25, 95%CI 0.16–0.35, P < 0.00001, I2 = 93%; WMD = 0.25, 95%CI 0.14–0.36, P < 0.0001, I2 = 97%; WMD = 0.38, 95%CI 0.16–0.60, P = 0.0006, I2 = 98%) (Supplementary Fig. 14). Due to evident heterogeneity for this outcome, subgroup analyses were performed. However, subgroup analyses of the postoperative BCVA at ≥ 3 months but < 6 months according to different anti-VEGF agent types (I2 = 97%, Supplementary Fig. 14 A), PPV gauge (I2 = 98%, Supplementary Fig. 14B) and different anti-VEGF administration timing (I2 = 97%, Supplementary Fig. 14 C) were unable to eliminate the heterogeneity, sensitivity analysis was conducted. But heterogeneity still could not be eliminated through omitting one study at a time. Given that both subgroup analyses and sensitivity analyses failed to eliminate heterogeneity, a random-effect model was used for analysis.

In addition, the proportion of postoperative BCVA improvement compared to preoperative was significantly more in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 0.77, 95%CI 0.71–0.83, P < 0.00001, I2 = 20%) (Supplementary Fig. 15), while the proportion of postoperative BCVA remaining unchanged or stable and reduction compared to preoperative were significantly less in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 1.59, 95%CI 1.23–2.07, P = 0.0005, I2 = 0%; RR = 1.80, 95%CI 1.33–2.45, P = 0.0002, I2 = 0%) (Supplementary Fig. 16, 17).

Postoperative macular retinal thickness (MRT)

The postoperative MRT was divided into different time points, including early postoperative period (< 1 month), short-term (≥ 1 month but < 3 months), middle-term (≥ 3 months but < 6 months), and long-term (≥ 6 months). The pooled results of these analyses indicated that the postoperative MRT at different time points were significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (WMD = 37.27, 95%CI 32.15–42.38, P < 0.00001, I2 = 40%; WMD = 52.83, 95%CI 33.06–72.60, P < 0.00001, I2 = 97%; WMD = 40.33, 95%CI 32.82–47.83, P < 0.00001, I2 = 99%; WMD = 27.85, 95%CI 22.54–33.16, P < 0.00001, I2 = 97%) (Supplementary Fig. 18). Due to the failure of subgroup analysis and sensitivity analysis to eliminate heterogeneity, a random-effect model was used for analysis.

Postoperative intraocular pressure (IOP)

The postoperative IOP was divided into different time points, including early postoperative period (< 1 month), short-term (≥ 1 month but < 3 months), middle-term (≥ 3 months but < 6 months), and long-term (≥ 6 months). The pooled results of these analyses indicated that the postoperative IOP at < 1 month, and ≥ 3 months but < 6 months were significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (WMD = 1.93, 95%CI 0.77–3.09, P = 0.001, I2 = 95%; WMD = 1.09, 95%CI 0.30–1.88, P = 0.007, I2 = 75%); while there were no significant differences between the PPV + anti-VEGF group and the PPV alone group at ≥ 1 month but < 3 months, and ≥ 6 months (WMD = 0.20, 95%CI −0.32−0.72, P = 0.45, I2 = 0%; WMD = 1.37, 95%CI −0.77−3.51, P = 0.21, I2 = 88%) (Supplementary Fig. 19). Due to the failure of subgroup analysis and sensitivity analysis to eliminate heterogeneity, a random-effect model was used for analysis.

Moreover, when dividing the late POVH to different periods, including short-term (> 1 month but < 3 months), middle-term (≥ 3 months but < 6 months), and long-term (≥ 6 months), the pooled results of these analyses indicated that the incidence of late POVH at postoperative different periods were significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 3.07, 95%CI 1.73–5.45, P = 0.0001, I2 = 25%; RR = 4.11, 95%CI 2.96–5.71, P < 0.00001, I2 = 0%; RR = 3.13, 95%CI 2.26–4.34, P < 0.00001, I2 = 0%) (Supplementary Fig. 22).

The spontaneous absorption time of POVH was significantly less in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (WMD = 5.83, 95%CI 4.58–7.08, P < 0.00001, I2 = 0%) (Supplementary Fig. 23).

Re-PPV

The proportion of re-PPV was significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 2.85, 95%CI 1.76–4.59, P < 0.0001, I2 = 0%) (Supplementary Fig. 24).

Moreover, when dividing RD to < 6 months and ≥ 6 months, the pooled results of these analyses indicated that the incidence of RD at postoperative different periods were significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 3.52, 95%CI 1.88–6.58, P < 0.0001, I2 = 0%; RR = 3.33, 95%CI 1.80–6.14, P = 0.0001, I2 = 0%) (Supplementary Fig. 26).

Re-proliferation

The incidence of re-proliferation was significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 5.00, 95%CI 2.70–9.26, P < 0.00001, I2 = 0%) (Supplementary Fig. 27).

The incidence of aseptic endophthalmitis was significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 2.34, 95%CI 1.54–3.55, P < 0.0001, I2 = 10%) (Supplementary Fig. 28).

The incidence of infective endophthalmitis was significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 3.79, 95%CI 2.08–6.89, P < 0.0001, I2 = 0%) (Supplementary Fig. 29).

Iris rubeosis

The incidence of iris rubeosis was significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 3.25, 95%CI 1.22–8.66, P = 0.02, I2 = 0%) (Supplementary Fig. 30).

Neovascular glaucoma (NVG)

The incidence of NVG was significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 2.46, 95%CI 1.39–4.35, P = 0.002, I2 = 0%) (Supplementary Fig. 31).

Hyphema

The incidence of hyphema was significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 3.41, 95%CI 2.25–5.16, P < 0.00001, I2 = 0%) (Supplementary Fig. 32).

Elevated IOP

The incidence of elevated IOP was significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group (RR = 1.93, 95%CI 1.51–2.46, P < 0.00001, I2 = 0%) (Supplementary Fig. 33).

Publication bias

Based on the visual analysis of the funnel plots, there were no obvious evidence of publication bias for any of intraoperative and postoperative indices (Supplementary Fig. 34). Using Begg’s test (P = 0.78, continuity corrected) and Egger’s test (P = 0.427), publication bias was not found to be significant.

Discussion

PDR is characterized by retinal or optic disc neovascularization, often accompanied with VH, TRD and other complications that seriously endanger vision. PPV is a main treatment for PDR, but the operation is difficult due to massive neovascularization membranes attached to the thin and ischemic retina during surgery, which can easily lead to bleeding or iatrogenic damage, as well as POVH or RD, etc [14]. It was reported that anti-VEGF agents adjunct before PPV could effectively reduce the concentration of intraocular VEGF [96] and inhibit inflammatory cytokine activity, thereby reducing retinal vascular leakage, promoting neovascularization regression and pretreating diseased retina, which in turn significantly shortened surgical time, reduced intraoperative iatrogenic damage and lowered the incidence of complications. However, the optimal timing for anti-VEGF agents adjunct during PPV perioperative remains controversy. Castillo et al. [105] conducted a RCT to evaluate the optimal interval of intravitreal bevacizumab (IVB) administration in severe PDR patients undergoing PPV. They found that subjects who received IVB 5–10 days before PPV represented better postoperative outcomes at 6 months compared with 1–3 days. Yang et al. [96] performed a study to assess the optimal time of intravitreal conbercept (IVC) prior to PPV in severe PDR patients. They found better effects and a lower vitreous VEGF concentration when patients were administered with IVC 7 days before PPV than at other time points. Wang et al. [106] conducted a network meta-analysis on the timing of receiving anti-VEGF agents during PPV perioperative in PDR patients. The results demonstrated that the best efficacy was presented in improving postoperative BCVA, reducing duration of surgery and the incidence of POVH when administered with antiVEGF pretreatment at 6–14 days before PPV compared to preoperative 1–5 days, more than 14 days, and at the end of PPV. In addition, Wang et al. [107] performed a network meta-analysis on the IVC adjunct for PDR during PPV perioperative. They found that IVC adjunct for PDR within 7 days before PPV could significantly shorten operation time and reduce intraoperative or postoperative complications, but no discernible effects of intraoperative IVC. Furthermore, Feng et al. [108] conducted a study to evaluate the concentration changes of inflammatory cytokines and fibrosis-related factors in aqueous humor, and to detect the expression of VEGF and proliferated cells in fibrovascular membranes of PDR patients after IVB. The results showed that IVB might lead to a decrease of intraocular VEGF concentration after 2–5 days and induce the proliferation formation after 21 days, which indicated that PPV for PDR patients should take place within 1 week after IVB. Besides, Li et al. [76] conducted a RCT to compare IVC pretreatment at 7 days and 14 days before PPV, they found that both interval were equally effective and safe for severe PDR patients. Thus, based on currently available evidence, to avoid the impact of different injection timing on surgical outcomes, all RCTs with anti-VEGF agents adjunct 14 days before PPV were included in this meta-analysis. Moreover, there was no significant difference for preoperative complexity scores of PDR between two groups, suggesting that the fundus conditions of two groups were similar, which correspondingly avoiding the influence of differences in procedure difficulty caused by different PDR stages on surgical outcomes.

During PPV in PDR patients, intraoperative bleeding becomes a common trouble due to retinal neovascularization proliferation, which greatly affect surgical visual field and increase the frequency of instruments exchange [49]. Preventing intraoperative rebleeding and related complications is one of the key factors for PPV success, whose purpose is to completely remove preretinal FVPMs, relieve their tractions on the retina, and avoid instruments damage to the retina [4, 14]. This meta-analysis revealed that the amount, grade and general incidence of intraoperative bleeding were significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group. The reason may be that anti-VEGF agents adjunct prior to PPV can significantly reduce intraocular VEGF concentration, which leads to the haemodynamic changes of retinal circulation, retinal neovascularization proliferation reduction and a decreased flow in new vessels [8, 14]. The lower risk of intraoperative bleeding may also decrease the use of endodiathermy, which in turn causes less trauma to previously damaged and ischemic retina [4]. This meta-analysis showed that there were significantly lower incidence of IRBs, less frequency and incidence of endodiathermy in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group. These are attributed to the fact that anti-VEGF agents adjunct before PPV can cause most retinal neovascularization regression, which results in relatively less amount and a lower severity of intraoperative bleeding. Consequently, no need to use endodiathermy or simple procedures such as increasing IOP can stop bleeding, or some bleeding can stop on its own.

The hemorrhages covered the retinal surface make it difficult to delaminate and segment the fibrovascular tissues due to poor visualization. Besides, the clots formation on the retinal surface produce serious adhesions, which can’t be separated without the risk of relaxing retinotomy [13]. This meta-analysis revealed that there were significantly lower incidence of relaxing retinotomy, and less proportion of silicone oil tamponade in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group. Furthermore, the duration of surgery was significantly less in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group. These may be attributed to the fact that anti-VEGF agents adjunct before PPV can cause neovascularization regression of most preretinal FVPM and significantly reduce intraoperative bleeding, which on the one hand lower the probability of repeated instruments exchange for endodiathermy and other intraocular procedures [4]; on the other hand, the surgeon has a clear visualization, allowing for easier segmentation and delamination of FVPMs without using endodiatermy and blood aspiration [41], thus reduce the occurrence of iatrogenic complications such as retinal breaks, retinotomy and other injuries. In summary, the overall surgical procedures have been reduced, and the duration of surgery has been greatly shortened. However, there was evident heterogeneity for duration of surgery and intraoperative bleeding, so we conducted subgroup analyses based on different anti-VEGF agent types, PPV gauge and different anti-VEGF administration timing. Unfortunately, both subgroup analyses and sensitivity analyses failed to eliminate heterogeneity. Moreover, subgroup analyses showed that duration of surgery of monoclonal antibodies were higher than fusion proteins, which may be attributed to that monoclonal antibodies have fewer targets and slower effects compared to fusion proteins. Additionally, it may be due to the earlier emergence of monoclonal antibodies and relatively immature technique of PPV. Furthermore, it may be related to the surgeon’s surgical experience and operational skills.

It is reported that retinal ganglion cells apoptosis occurs in early PDR affected eyes and worsened with the disease progression, which ultimately leads to retinal vascularopathy, macular edema, and an increase of MRT [109]. In addition, macular edema, which causes a significant decrease of BCVA in PDR patients, is also related to sustained increase in VEGF leading to increased retinal permeability and blood retinal barrier breakdown [77]. This meta-analysis indicated that postoperative MRT at different time points were significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group. The mechanism of reducing macular edema is that PPV remove traction at the vitreoretinal interface, reposition detached retina, and relieve mechanical tension of the macula; meanwhile, PPV can reduce retinal blood flow [77]. In addition, preretinal FVPMs dissection during PPV can thoroughly clean the retinal surface, causing residual pigment epithelial cells, glial cells and inflammatory cytokines in the vitreous to lose their basis for attachment and proliferation, which can prevent the occurrence of secondary preretinal membranes and postoperative macular edema. Postoperative BCVA is probably related to numerous factors, such as a history of TRD, intraoperative iatrogenic injury, macular ischemia and ellipsoid zone band integrity, macular edema, early and late POVH, and sterile air, inert gas or silicone oil tamponade [96, 106]. Especially for macular retinopathy, closely related to postoperative BCVA improvement, is an important risk factor affecting postoperative visual acuity recovery [60]. This meta-analysis revealed that there were significantly higher proportion of postoperative retinal anatomical reattachment, better postoperative BCVA at different time points, and higher proportion of postoperative BCVA improvement compared to preoperative in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group, while the proportion of postoperative BCVA remaining unchanged or stable and reduction compared to preoperative were significantly less in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group. This may be attributed to clear the inflammatory mediators in the vitreous through anti-VEGF agents adjunct prior to PPV, lowering intraocular neovascularization and leakage, which in turn indirectly promotes to reduce macular edema or improve retinal perfusion [60]. In addition, PPV with anti-VEGF agents pretreatment has a shorter surgical time and causes less mechanical and light damage to retina; meanwhile, the frequency of endodiathermy is relatively lower, resulting in less thermal injury and reduced postoperative inflammation [49]. Furthermore, anti-VEGF agents pretreatment before PPV can also reduce retinal edema, improve the quality of retinal laser spots during surgery, promote the recovery of photoreceptors in the fovea, and thus accelerate visual acuity recovery. Besides, this meta-analysis revealed that postoperative IOP at < 1 month, and ≥ 3 months but < 6 months were significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group; while there were no significant differences at ≥ 1 month but < 3 months, and ≥ 6 months. Which may be related to choroidal and ciliary body edema and iris moving forward caused by silicone oil tamponade and PRP interfering with retinal vein reflux [95]. However, there was evident heterogeneity for BCVA, so we conducted subgroup analyses based on different anti-VEGF agent types, PPV gauge and different anti-VEGF administration timing. Unfortunately, both subgroup analyses and sensitivity analyses failed to eliminate heterogeneity. Moreover, subgroup analyses showed that BCVA of monoclonal antibodies were lower than fusion proteins, which may be attributed to that monoclonal antibodies have shorter half-life compared to fusion proteins. Therefore, the duration of pharmacological effects was shortened.

As one of the common postoperative complications of PPV for PDR patients, POVH can lead to visual impairment and require reoperation in severe cases, with an incidence rate of 29–75% [105, 110]. However, with the development of surgical techniques and instruments, this proportion has decreased to 12–40% in recent years [7, 13, 16, 86]. There are many factors associated with POVH. Young age, long DM course, poor preoperative blood glucose and blood pressure control, and high serum creatinine are systemic factors. Vigorous growth of neovascularization around the surgical incision, close adhesion between FVPMs and retina, excessive proliferation of fibrovascular membranes in the anterior vitreous, postoperative low IOP, insufficient pan-retinal photocoagulation (PRP) are local factors [86, 111]. According to the occurrence time, it was divided into early POVH and late POVH [7]. Early POVH refers to VH that occurs within 4 weeks after PPV, which is more likely to be caused by FVPMs dissection, sustained vitreoretinal traction, bleeding from surgically injured retinal tissues, recurrent bleeding from initial bleeding site, released blood cell from the retained peripheral vitreous skirt, bleeding at the surgical incision site, dissolution of residual blood clots in the vitreous, increased vascular permeability, and rebleeding of transverse vascular membranes during surgery [9]. This meta-analysis showed that the incidence of early POVH was significantly less in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group. The main reasons are as follows: firstly, although the clearance rate is high during PPV, postoperative residual anti-VEGF agents still act on neovascularization, which is beneficial for inhibiting fibrovascular proliferation in the vitreous base and scleral incision. Secondly, anti-VEGF agents cause neovascularization regression and reduce fibrovascular proliferation, which is beneficial for fully dissection and peeling off FVPMs during PPV, reducing residual fibrovascular proliferation and iatrogenic damage to retina after PPV. Finally, for PDR patients with concomitant macular edema, anti-VEGF agents adjunct prior to PPV can alleviate macular edema and facilitate adequate PRP during surgery. Late POVH is defined as VH that occurs more than 4 weeks after PPV, mainly caused by neovascularization growth at the sclerotomy sites or peripheral retina, residual or recurrent neovascularization on the retina or iris, which usually happens at 2–6 months after PPV [4, 17, 110]. This meta-analysis revealed that the general incidence of late POVH was significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group, and late POVH of different periods in the former were lower. As is well known, the effect of anti-VEGF agents usually lasts for 1–2 months, and abnormal blood vessels will undergo reperfusion timely. During PPV, almost all doses of anti-VEGF agents before surgery will be removed. Therefore, the postoperative effect of residual anti-VEGF agents may be negligible. Thus, the effect of anti-VEGF agents before PPV will last for a shorter duration, leading to late POVH occurrence [9]. Sato et al. [112] believed that late POVH occurrence after PPV for PDR patients was related to the degree of DM management, and good blood glucose control could effectively reduce the incidence of late POVH. In addition, the meticulous hemostasis technique, including transient low IOP induction at the end of PPV to identify any residual bleeding focus, probably have contributed to reduce the incidence of POVH [7]. Late POVH is the main cause of visual impairment in PDR patients, with an incidence rate of 13–40% [17], moreover, 10–38% of them need re-PPV [4, 7, 16, 24, 37]. This meta-analysis revealed that the proportion of re-PPV was significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group.

Additionally, this meta-analysis revealed that spontaneous absorption time of POVH was significantly less in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group, which may be partly due to the latter experiences more frequent and significantly worse POVH [9]. Although almost all doses of intravitreal anti-VEGF agents injected before surgery are cleared during PPV [8, 11, 106], intraocular residual anti-VEGF agents may still be effective. Li et al. [113] demonstrated anti-VEGF agents caused vitreous VEGF concentration to decrease significantly even 25 days following treatment. In vitrectomized eyes, anti-VEGF agents can maintain VEGF concentration at a low initial level for up to 4 weeks [106, 114]. Therefore, if there is no significant absorption of late POVH after 4 weeks of observation, further surgery is required. Adequate PPV should be performed during and after PPV to prevent recurrent neovascularization and reduce the incidence of late POVH and postoperative recurrent RD [106].

As the main cause of PPV failure in severe PDR patients, NVG may be a devastating complication, with up to 23% of patients who received preoperative anti-VEGF agents adjunct experience NVG after PPV [115]. This meta-analysis revealed that the incidence of NVG was significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group; besides, the incidence of re-proliferation, RD, elevated IOP, sterile and infective endophthalmitis, iris rubeosis and hyphema were significantly lower in the PPV + anti-VEGF group than in the PPV group. This may be attributed to the fact that patients with anti-VEGF agents adjunct prior to PPV have less intraoperative bleeding, better intraoperative visualization, lower surgical complexity, adequate PRP during surgery or supplemented with PRP after surgery [116], fewer intraoperative complications, and shorter surgical time, which significantly reduce the inflammation caused by operative stress and the complications related to postoperative neovascularization [106].

There are several differences from previous meta-analyses. Firstly, our study did not include observational or retrospective studies, but only RCTs, which eliminates the confusion over indications, information bias and selection bias caused by study design. Secondly, our study came from many different countries, including not only Western European countries like USA and the United Kingdom, but also East Asian countries such as China and India with a higher proportion of PDR patients; which eliminates the selection bias caused by regional differences. Moreover, our study included not only monoclonal antibody agents, but also fusion protein agents, thus avoids the impact of differences in drug selection. Furthermore, our study only included anti-VEGF agents adjunct within 14 days before PPV, which prevents the impact of different intravitreal injection interval on surgical outcomes. Besides, our study included not only 20G PPV, but also current minimally invasive vitrectomy surgeries such as 23G, 25G and 27G, which avoids the impact of differences in surgical techniques and instruments on the results. Finally, this meta-analysis included all high-quality available data and considered all key indicators without publication bias. Therefore, this meta-analysis is reliable and more accurately reflect the current status of two surgical outcomes, which may help clinical decision-making for PPV treatment in PDR patients.

Despite these advantages, this meta-analysis still has some limitations. Firstly, as for the study designs of extracted articles, it was impossible to extract all data reflecting clinical outcomes from every included studies due to the heterogeneity of follow-up time and the differences in evaluation indicators. Secondly, this meta-analysis only included published studies and did not attempt to obtain unpublished results. In addition, four different types of anti-VEGF agents were included in the meta-analysis, which should be choosed largely depended on the economic development level, cost-effectiveness ratio, and accessibility of drugs in each region. As the domestically produced new drug Conbercept in China, with certain efficacy and relatively low price, is obviously the first choice in economically underdeveloped areas. Due to the fact that anti-VEGF agents used during the perioperative period of PPV are not included in medical insurance and belong to patient’s self-funded part, it is inevitably increase hospitalization costs. If the effect is comparable, lower priced anti-VEGF agents should significantly reduce the patient’s expenses. However, there are differences in the chemical structures and mechanisms of four anti-VEGF agents, which may lead to clinical heterogeneity. Moreover, the studies included in this meta-analysis exhibited heterogeneity in terms of location, population, number of patients and baseline conditions. Furthermore, this meta-analysis only considered studies published in Chinese or English, which probably leads to language bias. In addition, this meta-analysis included some non-blinded studies, considering the surgeon or patient’s knowledge of treatment plan may have an impact on subjective outcomes such as intraoperative bleeding grades or IRBs. Specifically, surgeons may unintentionally adjust their techniques during operations while knowing the treatment plan, which can affect the assessment of bleeding. Moreover, after patients become aware of the treatment plan, their psychological state may change, which in turn affects their physiological reactions during surgery, such as blood pressure, heart rate, etc., indirectly affecting the amount of bleeding. Therefore, this suggests that in future research, it is important to ensure that both surgeons and patients are not aware of specific treatment plans to reduce subjective bias. Additionally, an independent third party can evaluate subjective outcomes such as intraoperative bleeding grades to ensure the objectivity of the results.

Clinical implications

In conclusion, anti-VEGF agents adjunct prior to PPV can significantly lower surgical complexity, improve intraoperative visualization, and reduce the incidence of intraoperative complications such as intraoperative bleeding, IRBs, retinotomy and silicone oil tamponade, which in turn shortens the duration of surgery. At the same time, it significantly improves early, short-term, mid-term and long-term postoperative BCVA, reduces MRT of early postoperative, short-term, mid-term and long-term, lowers the incidence of short-term and long-term complications and the risk of re-PPV. However, more RCTs with better design (such as standardized BCVA measurement, consistent anti-VEGF timing, lower priced anti-VEGF agents), larger sample size and longer follow-up time are needed to provide more reliable evidence. If the effect is comparable, lower priced anti-VEGF agents should significantly reduce the cost for patients and may be more suitable for the perioperative period of PPV.

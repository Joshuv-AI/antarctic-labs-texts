A novel computer-assisted drill guide template for guided correction of genu varum
Authors: Yongxiang Zhang, Qiang Shi, Yongfu Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHemiepiphysiodesis with 8-plate is widely used for the treatment of genu varum in children. The purpose of this study was to explore the feasibility and efficacy of a novel computer-assisted drill guide template for guided correction of genu varum.MethodsFrom June 2014 to June 2018, twenty-eight patients who underwent hemiepiphysiodesis with 8-plate to correct genu varum deformities in our hospital. Preoperatively, all patients underwent X-rays and ultra-low-dose CT scanning of knee joint. Median amount of correction for mLDFA between individualized navigation template group and the traditional operation group were 11.0 ± 5.1 and 12.7 ± 4.8 (p = 0.035). Background

Genu varum is very common in children due to idiopathic, posttraumatic, rickets aetiologies, which can lead to cosmetic problems and abnormal gait function [1–3]. For genu varum in skeletally immature patients, hemiepiphysiodesis with 8-plate proposed by Stevens has become a widely used procedure for these patients [4, 5]. However, risk of permanent growth arrest and occurrence of rebound were sometimes reported [3, 6]. Therefore, factors influencing the effectiveness and complications of hemiepiphysiodesis using 8-plate implants for patients remain unclear. In the present study, we designed a novel computer-assisted drill guide template for guided correction of genu varum in children based on three-dimensional (3D) reconstruction and rapid prototyping technology. From June 2014 to June 2018, children with genu varum in our hospital were randomly divided into conventional group and navigation template group. The purpose of study aimed to explore whether navigation template was a precise and safe method, achieve less radiation exposure, shorter operation time and avoid complications.

Materials and methods

Patients

This research was authorized by Changsha Central Hospital’s ethics committee, and informed consent to participate was obtained from the parents or legal guardians of any participant and their parents were given detailed information about the study and their situation. From June 2014 to June 2018, twenty-eight consecutive patients (13 boys and 15 girls) with genu varum who underwent hemiepiphysiodesis with 8-plate were retrospectively reviewed in our hospital, which were randomly divided into conventional group and navigation template group. There is no statistical significance for the patient’s demographic characteristics between the two groups. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (i) idiopathic genu varum without trauma, skeletal dysplasia, endocrinopathy, or neuromuscular disease; (ii) follow-up for more than 12 months following 8-plate removal; (iii) genu varum patients without tibia hemiepiphysiodesis. All experimental methods described in this study were approved by Changsha Central Hospital and were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations. Conventional surgery was performed in 14 cases while 3D printed navigation templates were used in another 14 cases. The evolution of mechanical lateral distal femoral angle (mLDFA) in radiographs at the beginning and at the end of the treatment were recorded by two observers. In addition, the magnitude and speed of correction of the mLDFA was determined (Table 1). Patients did not return to full activity until complete surgical wound healing was achieved (approximately 2 weeks). The hardware was subsequently removed after completion of deformity correction.

Design and 3D printed of individualized navigation templates

In the navigation template group, all patients underwent CT scanning (ultra-low-dose protocol using a tube voltage of 80 kV and current of 10 mA) and the obtained data of both knee joints were stored in DICOM (Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine) format for transferring into the Mimics 20.0 software (Materialise, Leuven, Belgium) preoperatively. 3D models of femur were accurately reconstructed and exported in STL (Standard Tessellation Language) format. Thereafter the deformed 3D models were imported into UG 6.0 software (Fig. 1a). The optimal plane and accurate position were defined based on the femoral parameters using digital technology (Fig. 1b-c). We used a 4-mm-thick process to create a matching substrate and completed the individualized navigation template design after the Kirschner wire guide pipe data were imported, combined, and reconstructed. The personalized corrective osteotomy template model with two guide holes was fitted to the bone surface with reference to the characteristic configuration of the deformed femur and anatomical landmarks. Then the navigation template was printed using 3D printed technology with materials of acrylic resin (Fig. 1d-f). 3D reconstruction was performed using Amira 3.1 software. By tracking the contours of the prominent bone structure for reconstruction, adjusting the geometric alignment of the overlapping point contours, modeling the surface by meshing into a polygonal point frame, and reconstructing the target contour based on the point data. The result is a fully interactive 3D visualization of the reconstructed structure based on radiology and CT imaging data, allowing full visualization of the area. The 3D models were saved in STL format for stereolithography, which is a universal international file standard for 3D modeling and printing, and then imported into Imageware (version 12.0; EDS, Palo Alto, CA) software for further analysis. System parameters included the thickness of the processing layer at 0.1 mm, processing speed at 500 mm/s. The entire process of prototype construction required approximately 5–18 h, with an average of 8.9 h. By using this technology, patients needed to pay about $190 for the cost of the complete procedure of navigation template design and 3D-printing.

Surgical procedures

In the navigation template group, we cut the skin, fascia, and muscle layers, then reserved the periosteum to expose the lateral distal femur. After matching the 3D printed navigation template to the distal end of the femur, the template was fixed with a Kirschner wire to prevent slippage (Fig. 2a). The upper and lower positioning pipes of the navigation template were also fixed with a Kirschner wire (Fig. 2b). Then we removed the 3D printed navigation template and fixed with 8-plate as preoperatively simulated (Fig. 2c-d). Meanwhile, hemiepiphysiodesis with 8-plate was performed according to the preoperative and intraoperative measurements in the conventional group. No significant difference in postoperative management procedures after hemiepiphysiodesis with 8-plate between the two groups. Surgical time, radiation exposure and postoperative complications were recorded. We analyzed the appearance taken at the follow-up time (pre-operation, before removing the inner fixation device and the last follow-up) (Fig. 3). We measured mLDFA using standing anteroposterior full-leg radiographs with the patella facing forward taken at pre-corrective operation and the final follow-up [7].

Statistical analysis

All measurement data were presented as means ± SD, and Student’s t test was used to examine the differences between groups. Chi-squared test and Fisher’s exact test were applied to analyze the data of the two groups in this study. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 20 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). P-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

In the present study, a mean follow-up of 26.2 months (14 to 35) after 8-plate removal were included and no implant-associated infections was observed in two groups. In the conventional group, there were complications in two patients, including one plate migration and one rebound deformity. On the other hand, slight genu valgus was observed in one patient in the navigation template group at last follow up time.

All 3D printed digital navigation templates matched well with the anatomical markers of distal femur for preoperative planning and intraoperative procedures. Average operation times were 27.5 ± 5.1 min (21.0–38.0 min) for the individualized navigation template group and 33.3 ± 5.6 min (22.0–53.0 min) for the traditional operation group (p = 0.0434). Average radiation exposure times were 4.8 times (3.0–8.0 times) for the individualized navigation template group and 7.5 times (5.0–9.0 times) for the traditional operation group (p < 0.0001). Median amount of correction for mLDFA between individualized navigation template group and the traditional operation group were 11.0 ± 5.1 and 12.7 ± 4.8 (p = 0.035). In the conventional group, the duration was 11.4 ± 5.7 months while in navigation template group this period amounted to 11.0 ± 6.1 months without a statistically significant difference (p = 0.594) (Table 1).

Discussion

Genu varum is a complex problem that is frequently seen by orthopaedic surgeons [8]. Hemiepiphysiodesis with 8-plate has been used effectively for the treatment of genu varum since the concept of “guided growth” is proposed by Stevens, which leaves the physis intact, mechanically tethering it to effect growth retardation [9]. However, several problems including physeal damage during the procedure, hardware migration, and extrusion have been reported in the literature [10–12]. Meanwhile, the incidence of the rebound has been widely reported between 0 and 60% after temporary hemiepiphysiodesis due to limited movement of the 8-plate or physeal damage [13–18].

The traditional freehand approach of hemiepiphysiodesis with 8-plate depends on the preoperative two-dimensional imaging data and the pertinent anatomical landmarks of the patient before the procedure, thus make it impossible to ensure 8-plate placement accuracy. Meanwhile, the amount of radiation exposure for children is also increased during the procedure [19]. Furthermore, freehand 8-plate placement technique may take a long time and is also subjective. During the last ten years, 3D printed individualized navigation templates are widely used in orthopedics [20–23]. Based on these technologies, 3D printed navigation templates can be a safe and effective method for plate or screw placement [24]. Recently, a novel patient-specific template by using virtual surgical planning and 3D-printing technology was developed for infantile genu varum, which may allow personalization of corrective osteotomies and customization of structural allografts [25]. Chen et al. demonstrated the feasibility and efficacy of 3D-printed model combined with the Ilizarov technique in the treatment of genu varum in children with hypophosphatemic rickets [26]. Until now, there are no published reports on its application in hemiepiphysiodesis with 8-plate.

In the present study, 3D CT imaging data of the patient’s knee joints are obtained and imported into the computer navigation system. With the aid of computer guidance, the best insertion point and 8-plate position are chosen preoperatively. Firstly, the navigation template was so small that it did not expand the surgical incision, which was very simple and convenient during surgery. Moreover, the longest Kirschner wire guide pipe on the epiphyseal line surface provided a mark for fixing the individualized 3D-printed surgical navigation template, which was matched to the distal lateral femur in the best possible manner and two other Kirschner wire guide pipes were attached to the fitting surface to avoid intraoperative slippage. After removing the template, we performed hemiepiphysiodesis with 8-plate very convenient in navigation template group. Meanwhile, less radiation exposure and shorter surgical time were achieved in the navigation template group (P < 0.05).

Nevertheless, there are also some limitations in the present study. Firstly, the number of cases in the navigation template group for hemiepiphysiodesis with 8-plate was small, therefore larger sample, less heterogeneous pupulation (child vs. preadolescent) and multi-center prospective studies in the future were needed over the long term. Secondly, the position of the template may be affected by muscle and fat tissue of the bone, so the template should fit tightly to the corresponding distal femur to avoid errors. Meanwhile, thick periosteum in some younger patients may interfere with the guide’s precise and should be taken into account in future research. Radiation exposure times are neither reliable nor objective andhis parameter objective, which should be something reproducible, such as effective dose (mSv), fluoroscopy time (seconds) or dose are product (gycm2). Thirdly, Finally, follow-up time should continue until skeletal maturation.

Conclusion

The application of a novel computer-assisted drill guide template using digital technology and 3D printed technology can simplify the surgical procedure, improve the surgical accuracy and reduce the operating time, which may be a promising strategy for the treatment of genu varum.

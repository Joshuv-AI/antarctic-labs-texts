Mesangial hyaline degeneration after hematopoietic stem cell transplantation
Authors: Takahisa Kawaguchi, Yutaka Yamaguchi
Published: 2026

A 52-year-old Japanese man with myelodysplastic syndrome underwent allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) twice. The second HSCT was performed 8 years after the first. Hematologic remission was maintained after the second HSCT, but persistent proteinuria gradually increased over the 2 years following the second HSCT. The patient’s serum creatinine and urinary protein levels were 1.07 mg/dL (94.6 μmol/L) and 1.33 g/24 h, respectively. A kidney biopsy revealed thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA) with mesangiolysis. Multiple hyaline droplets were observed in mesangial areas, hereafter referred to as mesangial hyaline degeneration (MHD). Mesangial cells with swollen pale nuclei compatible with regeneration (Fig. 1A) and hyaline droplets in the juxtaglomerular apparatus were also observed (Fig. 1B). Immunofluorescence revealed nonspecific granular mesangial IgM staining. Electron microscopy showed numerous protein droplets with poorly recognizable intracellular organelles and nuclear fragments in mesangial areas, suggestive of mesangial cell death (Fig. 1C). In addition, small numbers of enlarged immature mesangial cells with prominent nucleoli that possibly reflected a regenerative response were noted (Fig. 1D).

Kidney biopsy findings of hematopoietic stem cell transplantation-associated thrombotic microangiopathy with mesangial hyaline degeneration.

(A) Light microscopy image showing a glomerulus with mesangiolysis (arrow) and mesangial hyaline droplets (arrowheads). Mesangial cells with swollen pale nuclei are present (red arrows) (periodic acid–Schiff; original magnification ×400). (B) Light microscopy image showing hyaline droplets in mesangial areas (arrows) and in the juxtaglomerular apparatus (arrowhead) (Masson trichrome; original magnification ×400). (C) Electron micrograph showing protein droplets (red arrows) with poorly recognizable intracellular organelles and nuclear fragments in mesangial areas. Condensed nuclei are observed (red arrowheads) (original magnification ×3,000). (D) Electron micrograph showing an enlarged immature mesangial cell with prominent nucleoli (red arrows) (original magnification ×3,000).

Endothelial injury is widely considered to be the principal mechanism of HSCT-associated TMA (HSCT-TMA) and has variously been attributed to radiation, calcineurin inhibitors, and graft-versus-host disease. Light microscopy typically shows the double-contour formation of the glomerular basement membrane, whereas electron microscopy reveals diffuse subendothelial widening. However, in this case, endothelial changes were limited, and mesangial alterations (mesangiolysis, MHD, and enlarged immature mesangial cells) were prominent, among which MHD could serve as an informative histopathological marker of mesangial injury. On the basis of the pathological findings, we speculate that mesangial injury may have contributed to mesangiolysis, with a subset of mesangial cells showing features of regeneration. Although the pathophysiology of HSCT-TMA is generally considered to reflect endothelial injury, our observations in this case raise the possibility that mesangial injury may be pathogenically important.

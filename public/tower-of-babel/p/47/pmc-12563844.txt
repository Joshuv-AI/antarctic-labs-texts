Forensic Identification through Denture Tags: Performance under Stimulated Environmental Conditions
Authors: Dhwani Patel, Pooja Ahuja, Santhiya Raghavan, Jahanvi Patel, Tilak Parikh, Arushi N. Chawla, Tanvi Hirani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAim:To evaluate the survivability and readability of near-field communication (NFC) chips embedded in complete and partial dentures after being stimulated to different environmental conditionsMaterials and Methods:A total of 56 samples of complete and partial dentures having different materials were embedded with the LINQS® Micro NFC tags (10 mm flexible printed circuit [FPC]) powered by the NXP NTAG213 chip and sealed with clear auto-polymerizing acrylic. The samples underwent six environmental challenges for predetermined time–temperature or time–pH schedules. Tag readability, data retention, and structural integrity were systematically evaluated pre- and post-exposure using an NFC reader application on a smartphone.Results:Tags remained scannable and readable in all specimens after exposure to different stimulated conditions. No signal loss and slight physical degradation were observed during the exposure to extreme temperatures. The scannable time for tags also differs in different situations.Conclusion:The research demonstrates the compatibility and robust functionality of NFC tags when embedded in complete and partial dentures. Even severe thermal assault did not obliterate data and scannable time in most samples, supporting routine incorporation of scannable labels for rapid, unequivocal person identification and data communication in mass fatality and medical scenarios.

INTRODUCTION

Human identification of the deceased is a cornerstone of forensic science. Conventional denture labeling techniques, such as engraving, embossing, laser etching, and barcoding, carry limited data, invite biofilm accumulation, and often fail in conflagrations or marine disasters.[1] Nowadays, radio-frequency identification (RFID) and NFC microchips are capable of storing encrypted alphanumeric codes linked to dental or civil databases. These devices require no internal power source and can be read through acrylic resin and certain metal frameworks.[2] Embedding NFC tags in dental structures and prosthesis offers a fast and reliable method for identifying and two-way communication, particularly in military or security scenarios.[3]

Based on studies demonstrating the resistance of NFC tags to chemical and thermal challenges, as well as the long-term reliability of RFID in denture applications,[45] this research evaluates the survivability of NFC tags embedded within complete and partial dentures.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

2.1 Study design

This prospective, laboratory-based experimental study was conducted for 4 months, from January 2025 to April 2025, at the Department of Forensic Odontology, National Forensic Sciences University, Gujarat. As no human subjects or biological samples were involved, ethical approval was not required.

2.2 Specimen fabrication

To evaluate the durability of NFC tags embedded in dentures, a total of 56 denture samples were fabricated using different materials. These samples were systematically distributed and exposed to simulated environmental conditions. Three different types of material, namely acrylic resin, metal-based and nylon-based for complete dentures and acrylic resin, metal-based and nylon-based and thermoplastic resin for partial dentures, were being exposed to high temperatures, freezing temperatures, and moisture-based conditions. The environmental conditions were chosen to mimic the natural scenarios.

2.3 Method

NFC tags were embedded into denture samples, particularly on the palatal surface in complete dentures and the lingual surface in partial dentures for esthetic purposes. A total of 24 complete dentures were fabricated using three different materials, and 32 partial dentures were fabricated using four different materials. The purpose of selecting different material dentures was to check the compatibility of the material with different environmental conditions. All the samples were subjected to eight different simulated environmental conditions: incineration at 100°C, 200°C, and 300°C; immersion in seawater; freezing at −10°C; immersion in artificial saliva; exposure to mouthwash and commonly consumed beverages at predetermined time intervals. Following exposure, the readability and integrity of the embedded NFC tags were assessed using the NFC reader application.

RESULTS

The embedded NFC tags in both complete and partial dentures remained functional across all tested environmental conditions. The structural integrity and readability are maintained in spite of high-temperature exposure, moisture exposure, and low freezing points, showing the utility in both the sectors of Forensic Science and Medicine.

DISCUSSION

Within few seconds of time, the NFC tags showed the best results of data retrievability and readability in spite of their exposure to various environmental conditions.[67] The functionality of the NFC tags is in reference with disaster victim identification as well as in mentally challenged patients for storing and reading the data encrypted in the tag as a piece of personal information.[89] The quick scanning time also demonstrates the high tolerance of the tag as well as the care taken for embedding it in the dentures.[6789] Even the low freezing cycles and the liquid immersion conditions affect the tags’ readability or structural integrity.[67] The proper seal is the most important criterion for the functionality of the tags.[89] These NFC tags are passive in nature and do not require any internal power to function, but a scanning software for adding and transmitting the stored data.[6] The selection of the surface of the tag in a denture is again important, considering the esthetic and prosthetic functioning.[78] It is to be ensured that the placement of the tag should remain undisturbed during the fabrication of the denture as well as during handling by the patients.[6789] The successful and rapid readability of NFC tags following all tested environmental exposures supports their feasibility for real-time identification in disaster victim identification, elder care, and trauma cases, in line with previous studies demonstrating similar durability and functionality of RFID and NFC-based denture identification systems.[6789]

CONCLUSION

The research by the authors confirms that the use of these NFC tags embedded in the complete and partial dentures is useful in data retrieval during any mass disaster with a few seconds of scanning. In spite of undergoing the stimulated conditions like fire, freezing, and moisture contamination, the potentiality and durability of the tags are commendable. The tags remained functional across all materials tested, highlighting their durability and potential application in forensic identification, elder care, and emergency response. Embedding NFC during denture fabrication ensures long-term data stability without compromising prosthetic integrity. These findings support integrating innovative identification technology in dental prostheses, promoting improved traceability and patient safety in clinical and postmortem contexts.

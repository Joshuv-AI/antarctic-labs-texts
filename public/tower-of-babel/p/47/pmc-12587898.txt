Comparison of obinutuzumab and rituximab for treating neuropsychiatric lupus: a retrospective case-control study
Authors: Huijing Wang, Shuang Ye, Fangfang Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesNeuropsychiatric SLE (NPSLE) lacks evidence-based treatment strategies. This study compared the effectiveness and safety profiles of obinutuzumab (OBZ) and rituximab (RTX) in patients with NPSLE.MethodsPatients with new-onset NPSLE meeting British Isles Lupus Assessment Group grade A criteria who received either OBZ or RTX between 2018 and 2024 at Renji Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine (with ≥12 months follow-up) were included. Propensity score matching (1:2 ratio) based on age, sex and SLE Disease Activity Index (SLEDAI) was performed. The primary outcome was complete or partial response at 12 months. Kaplan-Meier curves compared therapeutic effectiveness. Safety profiles of OBZ and RTX were recorded.Results27 patients with new-onset NPSLE were included in this retrospective study, with 9 patients receiving OBZ and 18 patients receiving RTX. At 12 months, the primary outcome was achieved in 8 of 9 patients in the OBZ group and 13 of 18 patients in the RTX group (88.9% vs 72.2%; OR: 3.08, 95% CI 0.30 to 31.33; p=0.34). Compared with the RTX group, the OBZ group demonstrated higher lupus low disease activity state attainment (74.1% vs 52.3%; p=0.049), lower SLEDAI-2000 Scores (median (IQR): 2 (1–2) vs 4 (2–4.25); p=0.04) and a trend towards higher B cell depletion rates at 12 months (57.1% vs 20.0%; p=0.08). OBZ treatment significantly shortened hospitalisation by 7 days (14.7±5.4 vs 21.9±11.9 days; p=0.04) and achieved a deeper B cell depletion at 6 months (median CD19+B cells: 0.4 (0–1.4) vs 3.5 (2–19.3) cells/µL; p=0.01), compared with the RTX group. Safety profiles were comparable between groups, with both treatments well tolerated. No deaths occurred.ConclusionThese findings suggest OBZ may induce superior clinical responses in NPSLE through enhanced B cell depletion, with acceptable safety at 12 months, calling for further exploration.Keywords: Systemic Lupus Erythematosus; Biologics; B-Lymphocytes; Outcome Assessment, Health Care

Introduction

Neuropsychiatric SLE (NPSLE) encompasses diverse neurological and psychiatric manifestations affecting 15%–40% of patients with SLE. Clinical presentations range from mild cognitive impairment and mood disturbances to severe features such as psychosis and seizures.1 The 1999 American College of Rheumatology (ACR) classification remains the diagnostic gold standard for NPSLE, defining 19 distinct clinical syndromes.2

Significant heterogeneity in NPSLE poses substantial diagnostic and therapeutic challenges. Management requires individualised strategies based on underlying pathogenic mechanisms and clinical severity.3 Notably, NPSLE has been systematically excluded from most lupus randomised controlled trials (RCTs), resulting in limited evidence-based treatment approaches.

A decade ago, a small open-label trial demonstrated potential efficacy of rituximab (RTX) in Japanese patients with refractory NPSLE.4 Recently, obinutuzumab (OBZ), a next-generation anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody with enhanced B cell depletion capacity, showed promise in lupus nephritis (LN).5 However, its safety and efficacy in NPSLE remain unexplored. We report here the efficacy and the tolerance of low-dose OBZ for B cell depletion and clinical response in new-onset NPSLE with 12 months of follow-up.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

This single-centre retrospective case-control study included patients satisfying the 1997 revised ACR classification criteria for SLE6 experiencing first-ever neuropsychiatric events per ACR definitions of 19 neuropsychiatric syndromes.2 Patients with NPSLE manifestations fulfilling British Isles Lupus Assessment Group (BILAG) grade A7 were treated with OBZ or RTX between 2018 and 2024 at Shanghai Renji Hospital, with ≥12 months follow-up. Propensity score matching (1:2 ratio) based on age, sex and SLE Disease Activity Index (SLEDAI) was performed.

Information on OBZ or RTX treatment was collected retrospectively from medical records. Data were retrospectively collected for all patients by reviewing their clinical charts from 2018 up to 2025. Demographic variables included sex, ethnicity, age at diagnosis of SLE and disease duration at the first neuropsychiatric event, SLEDAI–2000 (SLEDAI-2K)8 and BILAG grade.

Outcome measures

Complete recovery was defined as full remission of NPSLE symptoms (BILAG grade D) without any neurological deficits and partial improvement was defined as clinical improvement with remaining neurological deficits. Secondary outcomes assessed at month 12 included death from any cause, relapse of NPSLE, changes in prednisone and immunosuppressive drugs and lupus low disease activity state (LLDAS)9. An escalation of immunosuppressive therapy before 12 months was considered as non-response. B cell depletion was defined as CD19+B cell <5 cells/µL. Safety data were collected from electronic medical records.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as mean±SD for normally distributed data or median (IQR) for non-normally distributed data. Student’s t-tests were performed for normally distributed continuous variables, Mann-Whitney U tests for non-normally distributed continuous variables and χ2/Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. Survival (Kaplan-Meier) analysis was performed using log-rank tests. Data were analysed by SPSS software (V.26.0) or GraphPad (V.9.0) or R software (V.4.3.2). Values of p<0.05 were defined as statistically significant.

Results

Study population

This study included 27 patients with new-onset NPSLE naïve to anti-CD20 therapy, of which 9 received a single 1000 mg dose of OBZ and 18 received at least two doses of 500 mg RTX with first two doses at baseline and 2 weeks apart and a possible clinician-guided additional dose after 6 months. Baseline characteristics are summarised in online supplemental tables S1 and S2.

The mean ages of the patients in the OBZ and RTX groups were 33.44±14.54 years and 30.28±9.93 years, respectively. All (100%) patients in the OBZ group and 17 (94.4%) patients in the RTX group were female. The SLEDAI scores, serum IgG levels, complement levels and CD19+B cell numbers were similar at baseline between the two groups (table 1). The OBZ group showed a numerically higher positivity rate of anti-dsDNA antibodies (100% vs 61.1%; p=0.06) and a higher cerebrospinal fluid protein level (1276.1±1547.1 vs 619.0±775.7 mg/L; p=0.07) than the RTX group.

Neurological and psychiatric subtypes of NPSLE were classified according to 1999 ACR criteria,2 which are detailed in online supplemental table S3. Aseptic meningitis and Guillain-Barré syndrome occurred exclusively in the OBZ group, while the remaining subtypes were comparable between groups. Most frequent indications were cerebrovascular disease, cognitive dysfunction, psychosis, seizure disorder and demyelinating syndrome.

Efficacy

At 12 months, patients treated with OBZ showed numerically superior sustained BILAG recovery than patients in the RTX group (88.9% vs 72.2%, OR: 3.08, 95% CI 0.30 to 31.33; p=0.34, figure 1A). The relapse rate in the OBZ group was numerically lower than that in the RTX group (1 (11.1%) vs 5 (27.8%); p=0.62, table 1). Additionally, the OBZ group demonstrated a 7-day reduction in hospital stay compared with the RTX group (14.7±5.4 days vs 21.9±11.9 days; p=0.04; table 1). The OBZ group had significantly lower SLEDAI Scores at 12 months compared with the RTX group (2 (1–2) vs 4 (2–4.25); p=0.04), also demonstrating a more pronounced reduction in SLEDAI Scores from baseline (21.7±7.2 vs 16.2±5.4, p=0.03). At 12 months follow-up, LLDAS survival was significantly higher in the patients who received OBZ than those treated with RTX (74.1% vs 52.3%; p=0.049; figure 1C).

B cell depletion

The OBZ group showed superior B cell depletion than the RTX group (6 months: 0.4 (0–1.4) vs 3.5 (2–19.3) cells/µL; p=0.01; complete depletion rate: 100% vs 56.3%; p=0.06), which sustained on 12 months (complete depletion rate: 57.1% vs 20.0%; p=0.08) (table 1 and figure 1B). One patient was re-administrated OBZ for B cell depletion during the 12 months. One patient received RTX re-administration for disease relapse during the 6 months, and 10 patients required RTX re-administration in the RTX group after 6 months. Only one patient in the OBZ group required a clinician-guided additional dose for persistent B cell depletion after 6 months.

Safety

Both groups showed a comparable safety profile. Four of 9 (44.4%) patients in the OBZ group, and 9 of 18 (50.0%) patients in the RTX group had adverse events (AEs) (p=1.0, table 2). The major AEs in both groups were infections (OBZ vs RTX: 33.3% vs 33.3%), mainly consisting of upper respiratory tract and lower urinary tract infections. Infusion-related reactions (OBZ vs RTX: 11.1% vs 5.6%) were comparable and mild. Two (22.2%) patients in the OBZ group developed COVID-19. There was one (11.1%) serious AE (SAE) with pneumonia in the OBZ group, and one (5.6%) SAE with pneumonia in the RTX group, respectively. Both required hospitalisation and were resolved with intravenous antibiotics. No death occurred in either group.

Discussion

Current treatments for NPSLE include cyclophosphamide (CYC), azathioprine and mycophenolate mofetil. Per European League Against Rheumatism (EULAR) recommendations, potent immunosuppressants like CYC and RTX are preferred.10 This study provides the first evidence that a single 1000 mg infusion of OBZ induces B cell depletion and clinical response in adults with new-onset NPSLE, with longer-lasting depletion than RTX. B cells and autoantibodies likely cross the blood-brain barrier, promoting neuroinflammation and adverse behavioural outcomes.11 Prior observational studies support B cell depletion with RTX (alone or combined with conventional immunosuppressive agents) for refractory NPSLE.4 12 However, RTX-treated patients often experience late relapses requiring re-treatment, and some develop resistance or antidrug antibodies.13

Second-generation anti-CD20 antibodies like OBZ are engineered for more effective B cell depletion,14 with improved tolerability and reduced immunogenicity. We observed complete B cell depletion in all OBZ-treated patients, lasting longer than with RTX. These results are consistent with those of previous studies of the efficacy of OBZ for various indications, including LN5 and membranous nephropathy (MN).15 OBZ’s superior efficacy likely stems from enhanced antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, promoting greater caspase-independent direct cell death and reduced complement dependence.14

While the REGENCY LN Trial (ClinicalTrials.gov number, NCT04221477) used two 1000 mg/1.73 m² OBZ infusions (days 0/15, repeated at 6 months),5 we delivered a single 1000 mg infusion based on RTX experience in SLE. A recent study achieved successful B cell depletion with a single 1000 mg OBZ infusion in patients with MN.15 In our NPSLE cohort, OBZ treatment resulted in shorter hospitalisations, higher response rates, lower relapse rates and superior 12-month LLDAS maintenance versus RTX. OBZ also demonstrates efficacy and steroid-sparing potential in patients with RTX-refractory SLE.16

This study provides valuable safety data for OBZ in NPSLE. OBZ’s tolerability profile was similar to RTX, with premedication minimising infusion reactions (11%). No reported respiratory symptoms were observed. AE profiles were comparable; however, monitoring for OBZ-associated infections (eg, respiratory/urinary tract) remains prudent, even though the observed infection rates were similar to RTX.

Our study has several limitations. First, it was a retrospective study with a relatively small sample size in only a single centre and insufficiently long follow-up duration. Second, this observational study is susceptible to bias due to non-randomised treatment allocation, and residual confounding by factors like disease severity or physician preference cannot be excluded. Third, the incidence of non-severe infections might have been underestimated due to the retrospective nature of data collection for this study.

Our findings demonstrated that OBZ associates with a higher rate of clinical response at 12 months and more sustainable B cell depletion at 6 months than RTX, as well as a similar safety profile with RTX in new-onset NPSLE. In conclusion, OBZ represents a viable therapeutic option for NPSLE, warranting validation through RCTs.

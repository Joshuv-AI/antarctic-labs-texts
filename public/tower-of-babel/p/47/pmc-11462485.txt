DKK3 promotes adipogenic differentiation of stem cells by inhibiting Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway related gene expression and mitochondrial autophagy function
Authors: Ze Zhang, Haohui Wei, Tao Lin, Changbin Zhao, Yongxiang Song, Yuelin Deng, Yiqing Sun, Yongxia Zhao, Qingbin Luo, Xiquan Zhang, Dexiang Zhang, Hongmei Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractMesenchymal stem cells can differentiate into adipocyte precursor cells, and the balance of stem cell differentiation determines the quantity of adipocytes. Early-stage adipose tissue expression profiling revealed abnormal expression of DKK3 in the high-fat group. Moreover, DKK3 is enriched in the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, and studies have shown that DKK3 can serve as a gene involved in early regulation of adipogenesis. Therefore, this study focuses on exploring how DKK3 regulates the molecular mechanism of adipocyte differentiation through the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway. In this experiment, the role of DKK3 in the differentiation of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells into adipocyte precursors was validated using in vitro cultured chicken bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells. The results showed that overexpression of DKK3 led to a significant downregulation of Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway-related marker gene expression (P < 0.05), a significant upregulation of adipogenic differentiation-related genes (P < 0.05), an increase in lipid droplet content, a significant increase in OD value (P < 0.05), a significant upregulation of mitochondrial oxidative respiratory-related marker gene expression (P < 0.05), and a significant downregulation of mitochondrial autophagy-related marker genes (P < 0.05). Conversely, the results were opposite after interfering with DKK3 gene expression. In this study, 4 SNP sites, including g.8419139, g.8419556, g.8419560, and g.8419598, were detected in the 7th exon of DKK3, among which the g.8419598 (C > T) site was significantly correlated with abdominal fat weight and abdominal fat rate in 100-day-old Ma Huang chickens (P < 0.001).Key words: chicken, BMSCs, Wnt-βcatenin signaling pathway, lipogenic differentiation, mitochondria, DKK3

INTRODUCTION

Adipocytes originate from a cell population in the mesoderm and undergo 2 stages to achieve fat production. The first stage is the determination phase of differentiation, during which embryonic stem cells or mesenchymal stem cells differentiate into precursor adipocytes and further form preadipocytes (Qian et al., 2010; Ning et al., 2022). The second stage is the terminal differentiation phase, where preadipocytes ultimately differentiate into mature adipocytes (Avram et al., 2007; Ying and Simmons, 2020). The quantity of adipocytes is associated with the extent of differentiation from multipotent stem cells to preadipocytes, while the size of adipocytes is related to the degree of differentiation from preadipocytes to mature adipocytes and the accumulation of triglycerides (Ghaben and Scherer, 2019). Bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs), the earliest discovered mesenchymal stem cells, are a type of mesenchymal stem cell found in the bone marrow that can differentiate into adipocytes, osteoblasts, osteoclasts, and other cells (Lin et al., 2019). BMSCs can be induced to differentiate into adipocytes in vitro, making them the most widely used cell line in in vitro studies of adipocyte differentiation. In BMSCs, there exists a dynamic balance between osteoblasts and adipocytes (Rodríguez et al., 2009; Feng et al., 2023). As common progenitor cells for osteoblasts, adipocytes, and chondrocytes, when the differentiation of BMSCs into osteoblasts and adipocytes is imbalanced, various pathological metabolic diseases may occur, such as osteoporosis, diabetes, and obesity (Huang et al., 2020; Bin-Bin et al., 2022; Feng et al., 2023). Additionally, BMSCs are regulated by external physical and chemical factors, non-coding RNAs, and biological signaling pathways during differentiation. Although many studies have elucidated the processes of terminal differentiation (Sarantopoulos et al., 2018), the regulatory mechanisms of the first stage, from mesenchymal stem cells to preadipocytes, remain unclear.

DKK3(Dickkopf3) is a member of the DKK protein family, a secreted glycoprotein that plays an important regulatory role in tissue development, homeostasis regulation, and a variety of pathological states. It is involved in the regulation of the Wnt signaling pathway, which is critical for embryonic development and tissue homeostasis. This pathway controls cell proliferation, cell fate determination, and tissue patterning. According to the data of the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI), chicken DKK3 is located on chromosome 5, and the coding sequence (CDS) contains 1,053 bases, encoding 350 amino acids. At present, the research on DKK3 is mostly focused on tumors, and many studies have reported that DKK3 has been used as a tumor suppressor in many human cancers, and when over-expressed, DKK3 can inhibit the growth of tumor cells (Medinger et al., 2014; Li et al., 2017; Gondkar et al., 2019; Zhao et al., 2020). DKK3 is also able to regulate the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway by interacting with LRP5/6 (Katase et al., 2018, 2022). In addition, DKK3 is a negative regulator of insulin resistance, hepatic steatosis, associated inflammatory responses, which also demonstrates that DKK3 may have a great role in fat metabolism. Overall, the DKK3 gene is a fascinating area of study, with implications for a variety of biological processes, diseases, and growth and development.

The Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway is present and highly conserved in a wide range of animals. According to the way of signal transduction, it can be classified into classical Wnt signal pathways and non-classical Wnt signal pathways (De, 2011). The Wnt signaling pathway plays an important role in many biological processes, such as embryonic growth, tumor cell growth and development, adipogenesis, stem cell maintenance, cell fate determination, energy metabolism balance, and so on (Noelanders and Vleminckx, 2017; de Winter and Nusse, 2021; Chen et al., 2023). In the absence of Wnt signaling, Axin, CK1 and GSK3 complexes bind to and phosphorylate β-catenin, which is recognized by E3 ubiquitin ligase β-Trcp, resulting in ubiquitination of β-catenin and degradation by the proteasome. Only a small amount of β-catenin enters the nucleus and cannot activate downstream transcription factors (Ahn et al., 2017; Ji et al., 2017). When the Wnt signaling pathway is activated, Wnt ligands bind to Frizzled or LRP5/6 to form complexes, leading to the phosphorylation of LRP5/6 and the formation of the Frizzled-LRP5/6 complex. There are 5 consecutive PPPSPXS domains on LRP5/6. The phosphorylated PPPSPXS domains bind to Axin, which in turn binds to GSK 3 and CK 1, inhibiting the formation of Axin, GSK3, APC and β-catenin complex (Wang et al., 2022). Therefore, β-catenin is not phosphorylated, and it will not be recognized by E3 ubiquitin ligase β-Trcp. It will not cause final ubiquitination and be degraded by proteasome so that β-catenin enters the nucleus and interacts with T cells. Factor (TCF), a transcription factor of the lymphocyte enhancer factor family, forms a complex that activates downstream gene transcription (Vu-Phan et al., 2013). The Wnt signaling pathway has been reported to act as a switch for lipogenesis and can negatively regulate fat synthesis (Smith and Kahn, 2016; Hosseini et al., 2019). These reports have been confirmed in mice and humans (Lee et al., 2022). However, studies in poultry are rarely reported.

Mitochondria, present in most cells, are organelles surrounded by 2 membranes and are responsible for generating energy within the cell. They produce ATP through oxidative phosphorylation, providing about 95% of the energy needed for cellular activities. Besides energy production, mitochondria also participate in processes such as cell differentiation, cell signaling, and apoptosis. During cell differentiation, mitochondrial metabolism, biogenesis, and the generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) all increase in the early stages of differentiation, with ROS elevation being a necessary factor in the adipogenic differentiation of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (Tormos et al., 2011). Mitochondrial autophagy, or mitophagy, is the process by which damaged or unneeded mitochondria are cleared from the cell through autophagic mechanisms, serving as a regulatory mechanism to maintain the balance of mitochondrial quality and quantity within the cell. In the process of adipogenic differentiation, when mitochondrial autophagy is impaired and mitochondria cannot be cleared during the terminal differentiation stage, beige adipocytes may form (Kotzbeck et al., 2018). Therefore, mitochondria play a crucial role in the adipogenic differentiation process, and when mitochondrial function is impaired, it can affect cell differentiation (Stanford et al., 2015).

In summary, this study aims to investigate whether DKK3 exhibits SNP sites associated with adipose traits in poultry and whether it can regulate the adipogenic differentiation of chicken bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells through the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway and mitochondrial autophagy function.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Ethical Statement on Animal Experiments

The animal experiments involved in this study were performed after being approved by the Animal Welfare Ethics Review Committee of South China Agricultural University. All animal experiment procedures were handled according to the corresponding international standards for animal welfare formulated by the Animal Welfare Ethics Review Committee of South China Agricultural University (approval ID: SCAU#0017, June 3, 2022).

Laboratory Animals, Trait Measurements and Calculations

Guangzhou Kwangfeng Industrial Co. ltd. provided 451 spotted-brown chickens for slaughter determination. All chickens were raised under the same environmental conditions until 100 d of age when blood was drawn and slaughtered for sampling. Phenotypic traits mainly included live weight before slaughter (LWBS), carcass weight (CW), half-bore weight (HBW), full-bore weight (FBW), and abdominal fat weight (AFW). Dressing rate (SR) was calculated according to the formula: Dressing percentage (%) = carcass weight (g)/live weight before slaughter (g) ×100%. Abdominal fat weight ratio (AFWR) is calculated according to the formula: Abdominal fat weight (%) = abdominal fat weight (g)/(abdominal fat weight (g) + eviscerated weight (g)) ×100%.

Huizhou Kingkey Smart Agriculture Times Co., ltd. provided six 7-wk-old spotted-brown chickens to construct a DKK3 mRNA tissue expression profile. The experiment was divided into high-fat and low-fat groups according to the percentage of abdominal fat. Take 8 samples: heart, liver, spleen, lungs, kidneys, abdominal fat, chest muscle and leg muscle. The sample is stored in liquid nitrogen immediately after collection.

Xinxing Dahua Nong Poultry and Egg Co., Ltd. provides SPF spotted-brown Chickens at 1 to 3 d of age for primary BMSCs isolation.

Isolation and Culture of Primary Bone Marrow Mesenchymal Stem Cells and Their Characterization

SPF spotted-brown Chicken of 1 to 3 d of age were taken, bled and executed and then disinfected with 75% alcohol all over the body, after which they were placed on an aseptic operating table, and the cell suspension was collected after the bone marrow cavity was rinsed, and the tissue suspension obtained was filtered through a 70 μm cell sieve to remove the cell clumps from the tissue suspension. The filtrate was collected onto a centrifuge tube, and the obtained filtrate was centrifuged and precipitated by centrifugation, 500 g, 5 min. The supernatant was discarded, a new cell culture medium was added the cells were resuspended, and the precipitate was centrifuged and precipitated by centrifugation, 250 g, 10 min, and repeated twice. Discard the supernatant, resuspend the cells with F-12 DMEM complete culture solution for 12 to 24 h, and change the solution to remove the non-adherent stray cells, First, discard the old culture solution, wash the cells with PBS 2 to 3 times, add the new DMEM complete culture solution and continue to cultivate the cells for 10 to 15 d, and change the cell culture solution every 2 to 3 d, and observe the morphology status of the cells with an inverted microscope until cell confluence reaches 85% or more, then the passaging can begin. The cell culture medium was changed every 3 d, and the cell morphology was observed by an inverted microscope.

Observe the cell morphology and wall adherence under the inverted microscope and take pictures to record. At the 3rd generation, BMSCs were digested with trypsin and collected, the RNA of BMSCs was extracted and reverse transcribed into cDNA, and the cell surface markers CD44, CD90, CD73, CD29, and the hematopoietic stem cell surface marker CD45 were identified by immunofluorescence.

Bone Marrow Mesenchymal Stem Cells Induced Lipogenesis and Osteogenesis Differentiation

When the confluence of the third-generation BMSCs reached 70% and the cell growth was good, the cells were inoculated on the 12-well cell culture plate. When the cell density reached 80%, the complete culture medium of F-12 DMEM (10% FBS; 0.1% penicillin and streptomycin) cells in each well was replaced with the adipogenic medium (10%FBS; 0.1% penicillin and streptomycin; 0.5 mM IBMX; 1 μM dexamethasone; 10 μg/mL insulin; 0.2 mM indomethacin; F-12 DMEM). After 2 d, two-thirds of the cell culture medium was continued to be replaced with the adipogenic medium, and the whole solution was changed every 2 d. The induced morphological changes of the cells were observed and recorded with an inverted microscope until the induced differentiation reached the 14th d.

Osteogenic induction differentiation was performed using the osteogenic medium (10%FBS; 0.1% penicillin and streptomycin; 100 nM dexamethasone; 200 μM ascorbic acid; 10 mM glycerol 2-phosphate; F-12 DMEM), and the induction method was consistent with the above-mentioned lipogenic induction method.

Cell Protein Extraction and Protein Immunoimprinting and Immunofluorescence

Proteins were extracted from cells under the guidance of the manufacturer by adding 10% phenylmethane sulfonyl fluoroprotease inhibitor (Beyotime, China) to radioimmunoprecipitation assay buffer (Beyotime, China). The denatured protein was added to a 10% SDS-PAGE sample hole and separated at a stable voltage. Polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane (activated by immersion in methanol before use) (Whatman, Maidstone, UK) was placed on the surface of 10% SDS-PAGE and the protein was transferred under constant electric conditions. Finally, the developer was configured according to Beyotime's hypersensitive ECL chemiluminescence kit instructions, the results were viewed on the ECL chemiluminescence instrument and analyzed and processed with Image J software. The antibodies and dilutions used for western blotting are as follows: Anti-Rabbit IgG (H+L) HRP (TransGen Biotech, China; 1:500), β-actin (Abcam; 1:1,000), β-catenin (Abcam; 1:1,000).

BMSCs were inoculated in 12-well cell culture plates. After 48 hours, incubate with 4% paraformaldehyde (Biosharp, China) for 20 min for fixation. Then, cells were washed by phosphate buffer saline (PBS) several times and biological membrane damaged with 0.1% Triton X-100 (Biosharp, China). After that, BMSC cells were blocked with goat serum for 30 min and incubated with CD29 (Proteintech, China; 1:500), CD90 (Bioss, China; 1:500), Rabbit Anti-CD105 (Bioss, China; 1:500), Mouse Anti-Chicken CD44 (Southern Biotech, China; 1:500), Rabbit Anti-CD73 (Bioss, China; 1:500) and Mouse Anti-CD45 (Proteintech, China; 1:500) antibodies were incubated overnight at 4°C. AffiniPure Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG (H+L) (TransGen Biotech, China; 1:500) and Goat Anti-Mouse IgG (Abbkine, USA; 1:500) were incubated with the corresponding antibody/antigen complexes for 1 h at room temperature. The cell nuclei of BMSCs were stained with DAPI for 5 min. Then a fluorescence microscope (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan) was utilized to capture the pictures of immunofluorescence.

Triglyceride Content Detection, Oil Red O Staining, Alizarin Red Stain

The content of triglyceride (TG) in cells was detected following the instructions of the TG assay kit of Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute.

BMSCs were seeded into 6-well culture plates. After differentiation, differentiated BMSCs were washed with PBS and then fixed with 4% formaldehyde for 30 min. Differentiated BMSCs were dyed with oil red O working solution or Alizarin Red Staining Solution (Solarbio, China) for 60 min at room temperature and then washed 3 times with PBS, according to the manufacturer's specification. After washing, a fluorescence-inverted light microscope (Leica DMi8) was used to capture images. In the end, stain in cells was extracted by isopropanol, and absorbance was measured at 510 nm with a Fluorescence/Multi-Detection Microplate Reader (Bio-Tek).

SNP Site Screening of DKK3

Dkk3-snp primers in Table 3 were used to screen the single nucleotide polymorphic sites of DKK3 by PCR amplification and sequencing using the DNA of individual blood samples of 451 Kwangfeng spotted-brown chickens, and the amplified PCR products were sent to Beijing Tsingke Biotech Co., Ltd. for sequencing.

SNP Association Analysis

SNPs genotyping was performed using SnapGene4.1.9 software. Allele frequency, genotype frequency, P-value of genotype frequency difference, Hardy-Weinberg P-value, observed heterozygosity, expected heterozygosity, polymorphism information content, and effective number of available alleles were determined by Microsoft Excel (Microsoft Corporation, Redmond, WA) calculation. SPSS (IBM Corporation, NY) software was used to analyze the correlation between the genotypes of single nucleotide polymorphic loci and growth traits such as pre-slaughter live weight, carcass weight, half-evisceration weight, abdominal fat weight, and abdominal fat percentage of chickens. The LD between SNP and haplotype analysis was calculated by the Haploview software (Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard, Cambridge, UK).

Association analysis between SNPs and phenotypic traits was performed using mixed linear models in SPSS Statistics software. The analysis model is as follows:

Yijlm is the observed value, μ is the overall average, Gi is the fixed effect of the genotype, Sj is the fixed effect of sex, fl is the random effect of the family, and eijlm is the random error term. Bonferroni's test was performed to control for multiple comparisons.

Data are presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Statistical analyses were performed using Student's t-test.

Statistical Analysis of Data

In this study, the data are expressed as mean ± SEM. Each experiment contains at least 3 replicates. The values of the experimental group and the control group are all tested by independent sample t-test. P < 0.05 is significant (indicated by *), P < 0.01 and P < 0.001 are extremely significant (indicated by ** and ***, respectively).

RESULTS

Expression of DKK3 in Different Chicken Tissues

RT-qPCR results: The expression levels of DKK3 mRNA were detected in the heart, liver, spleen, lung, kidney, breast muscle, leg muscle, and abdominal fat of chickens. Using spleen tissue mRNA expression as a reference, it was found that DKK3 expression was lower in breast muscle, leg muscle, liver, and kidney, while higher in the heart, lung, and abdominal fat. Interestingly, compared to chickens with low abdominal fat, DKK3 mRNA was significantly upregulated in the abdominal fat of chickens with high abdominal fat (P < 0.01) (Figures 1A and 1B), suggesting that the DKK3 gene may play an important role in the development of fat.

Morphological Characteristics and Identification of Chicken BMSCs

BMSCs were extracted from chickens aged 1 to 3 d. The newly isolated cells were in suspension and began to adhere to the wall after 24 h of fluid exchange. The cells were scattered in the petri dish at the beginning, showing an oval shape, triangle shape, and some irregular characteristics (Figure 2A). After 5 d of complete culture, the cells began to grow like colonies, with only sporadic cells scattered outside, and the state of the cells was not much different from that at the beginning of the wall attachment (Figure 2B). When the cell density reached more than 90% in the 5th generation, the cells were fibrous with abundant cytoplasm (Figure 2C), which was consistent with the morphological rules of BMSCs. Immunofluorescence identification showed that CD44, CD29, and CD105 were positive and CD45 were negative (Figure 2D).

(D) CD29, CD73, CD45, CD105, CD44 from left to right. (E) BMSCs cultured in adipogenic induction medium for 5 d. (F) Oil red O staining on the 7th d of adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs. (G) BMSCs were cultured in osteogenic induction medium for 7 d. (H) Alizarin red staining was performed on the 7th d after BMSCs were induced into osteogenic differentiation.

During the induction of lipogenic differentiation, there was no obvious change in cell morphology in the control group and no obvious staining of cytoplasm in the oil red O staining. After 5 d of culture in the lipid-forming induction solution, the cells in the induction group increased in volume and there were obvious transparent lipid droplets in the cytoplasm (Figure 2E), which were found to be positive by oil red O staining (Figure 2F). There was no significant change in cell morphology and no calcium deposition in the control group during osteogenesis induction. After 7 d, part of the cells gradually changed from long spindle-shaped to polygonal, and the cells gathered and grew in multiple layers overlapping after convergence. The local accumulation of the cells became focal, and red calcium nodules were observed by alizarin red staining (Figures 2G and 2H).

The above results indicated that BMSCs with high purity were isolated.

Over-Expression and Interference With DKK3 Affect the Activity of the Wnt Signaling Pathway and Lipid Differentiation of BMSCs

In this study, p-DKK3 and si-DKK3 were transfected into BMSCs, RNA was extracted from BMSCs on the 7th d after induction of lipogenic differentiation, and quantitative analysis was performed by RT-qPCR. The results showed that lipid differentiation-related genes CEBP/α and PPARγ were significantly increased after over-expression of DKK3 (P < 0.05), Wnt signaling pathway marker genes LRP5 and LRP6 were significantly down-regulated (P < 0.05) (Figures 5A and 5B). After interference with DKK3, lipid differentiation-related genes were significantly down-regulated, and the Wnt signaling pathway marker genes were significantly increased (P < 0.05) (Figures 5C and 5D).

Western Blot results showed that the relative expression of β-catenin protein after over-expression of DKK3 was significantly lower than that in the control group (P < 0.05), the β-catenin protein was significantly increased after interference with DKK3 (P < 0.05) (Figures 5E–5H). These results indicated that the Wnt signaling pathway was inhibited when DKK3 was over-expressed and activated when DKK3 was interfered with.

The results of oil red O staining showed that when DKK3 expression was over-expressed, the lipid drop content was significantly higher than that of the control group (P < 0.05) (Figures 5I–5K). When DKK3 expression was interfered with, lipid drop content was significantly lower than that of the control group (P < 0.05) (Figures 5L–5N).

Over-Expressing and Interfering With the DKK3 Gene Affect the Expression Levels of Mitochondrial-Related Genes

The results of mitochondrial-related gene expression in BMSCs after over-expression and interference of DKK3 are shown in Figure 6. After over-expressing DKK3, the expression of mitochondrial oxidative respiratory-related marker genes increased, while autophagy-related genes decreased. Conversely, after interfering with DKK3, the expression of mitochondrial oxidative respiratory-related marker genes decreased, while autophagy-related genes increased. These findings indicate the significant role of mitochondria in the adipogenic differentiation process of BMSCs.

DISCUSSION

From the perspective of adipogenic differentiation, it involves a shift in gene expression patterns leading to the generation and accumulation of adipocytes, primarily induced by the specific expression of certain genes within the cells. Thus, identifying marker genes for adipogenic differentiation plays a crucial role in research. Based on current studies, widely accepted marker genes for adipogenic differentiation include PPARs and C/EBP. Previous research has shown that DKK3 acts on the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, which negatively regulates adipogenesis (Borrell-Pagès et al., 2015; Hosseini et al., 2019; Balatskyi et al., 2023). In this study, BMSCs were identified through RT-qPCR by selecting and verifying the surface markers CD31, CD44, CD90, CD71, CD29 for mesenchymal stem cells, and the hematopoietic stem cell surface markers CD45, CD34 (Delorme and Charbord, 2007; Purwaningrum et al., 2021) . Results showed positive expression of cell surface markers and negative expression of hematopoietic stem cell surface markers. Subsequent experiments involved Oil Red O staining, which has a strong affinity for lipids, allowing clear visualization of BMSCs' adipogenic differentiation and lipid droplet distribution. Results indicated that isolated BMSCs possessed the ability to differentiate into adipocytes. RT-qPCR results revealed that the expression levels of adipogenic differentiation marker genes C/EBPα and PPARγ (Gross et al., 2017; Gui et al., 2021) did not significantly increase before 5 d, but rapidly increased between 5 and 7 d, peaking at 7 d. From 7 to 11 d, the expression levels rapidly decreased, reaching a nadir at 11 d, and remained at low levels thereafter. Oil Red O staining results showed an increase in lipid droplet content over time, with the fastest growth rate observed at d 7, consistent with RT-qPCR results. Hence, it can be concluded that BMSCs exhibit the strongest adipogenic differentiation capability at d 7 of induction.

In vitro, bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) can be directed to differentiate into adipocytes or osteoblasts through induction agents. However, in the absence of induction agents, only a minimal number of cells undergo differentiation (Monfared et al., 2016). The results of this study indicate that when over-expressing or interfering with DKK3 in uninduced BMSCs, the triglyceride content remains at a low level regardless of DKK3 expression levels, suggesting that BMSCs do not undergo adipogenic differentiation under non-adipogenic induction conditions. This study investigated the influence of the DKK3 gene on the Wnt signaling pathway and the adipogenic differentiation ability of BMSCs. The results indicate that the DKK3 gene, as an inhibitor of the Wnt signaling pathway, downregulates Wnt signaling pathway marker genes. Previous studies have shown that the Wnt signaling pathway can inhibit the adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs. Therefore, inhibition of the Wnt signaling pathway results in upregulation of adipogenic differentiation marker genes in BMSCs and increased Oil Red O staining to detect lipid droplet content. Subsequently, the effect of the DKK3 gene on the adipogenic differentiation ability of BMSCs was assessed by studying the expression of β-catenin protein. Over-expression of DKK3 significantly upregulates mitochondrial oxidative respiration marker genes and downregulates mitochondrial autophagy marker genes. Conversely, interference with DKK3 leads to significant downregulation of mitochondrial oxidative respiration marker genes and upregulation of mitochondrial autophagy marker genes. Mitochondria also play an important role in the adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs by providing the energy required for differentiation. Therefore, when the Wnt signaling pathway is inhibited, promoting adipogenic differentiation requires more energy, leading to enhanced mitochondrial oxidative respiration. Studies have shown that obesity can cause mitochondrial dysfunction (de Mello et al., 2018), possibly due to increased lipid droplet content during enhanced adipogenic differentiation, leading to down regulation of mitochondrial autophagy function. Conversely, activation of the Wnt signaling pathway inhibits adipogenic differentiation, reducing the need for additional energy, and consequently, downregulating the expression of mitochondrial oxidative respiration marker genes and upregulating mitochondrial autophagy function.

Single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) refer to DNA sequence variations at the genomic level caused by single nucleotide substitutions, insertions, deletions, or rearrangements (Emara and Kim, 2003; Pagani et al., 2005). SNPs are characterized by their abundance, widespread distribution, representativeness, genetic stability, and suitability for high-throughput and automated detection (Vignal et al., 2002). SNP sites in coding regions or adjacent regulatory sequences can lead to alterations in the expression products and levels, thereby identifying genes associated with genomic polymorphisms and production traits. In this study, 4 SNP sites were identified in the regulatory region of the seventh exon of DKK3 in chickens, among which the g.8419598 (C > T) site showed a highly significant correlation with abdominal fat weight and abdominal fat percentage in KwangFeng Ma chicken. It was found that after mutation in the seventh exon of DKK3, both abdominal fat weight and abdominal fat percentage were downregulated, with heterozygous mutations showing significant differences. This may be attributed to the Ser > Leu mutation caused by the g.8419598 (C > T) site, where Ser plays important roles in the synthesis and metabolism of fats and fatty acids (Hutchinson et al., 2018). Consequently, when Ser is mutated, DKK3′s regulatory role in fat metabolism undergoes certain changes. This suggests that DKK3 may play a crucial role in the growth and development of fat traits in chickens.

CONCLUSIONS

In summary, the specific mechanism by which DKK3 regulates adipogenesis involves the inhibition of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway expression, thereby preventing β-catenin from interfering with the synthesis of key adipogenic regulators PPAR/γ and C/EBPα, promoting adipogenesis, enhancing mitochondrial oxidative respiratory function, and weakening mitochondrial autophagy function. This affects the quantity and deposition of preadipocytes in poultry, as well as lipid metabolism capacity, ultimately influencing the differentiation balance function of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) and promoting their differentiation toward adipogenesis.This study identified a significant correlation between the mutation at the g.8419598 (C > T) site of the DKK3 gene and abdominal fat weight and percentage in poultry, providing an effective molecular marker for poultry breeding.

Detection of Anti‐Infliximab and Anti‐Adalimumab Antibodies Below Manufacture's Threshold in a TDM Cohort Using Signal‐to‐Noise Ratio
Authors: Lise Pedersen
Published: 2026

Introduction and Background

Therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) of anti‐drug antibodies (ADA) and drug concentrations is used to optimize tumour necrosis factor‐α inhibitor therapy [1]. The development of ADA against infliximab or adalimumab increases the risk of reduced clinical response and infusion reactions [2]. When ADA accelerates drug clearance resulting in undetectable drug levels, patients risk a secondary loss of response to treatment necessitating a treatment change [3]. Appropriate positivity thresholds of ADA representing drug clearance are therefore critical. Manufacturers of ADA assays provide default positivity thresholds, derived under controlled experimental conditions using drug‐naïve samples [4], that may not reflect real‐world TDM samples originating from patients who have already been exposed to the drug and may contain circulating drug, ADA, or both. Consequently, manufacturer‐defined thresholds may not be optimal for clinical TDM. The lack of standardization of ADA assay provides a further challenge, leading to inconsistent immunogenicity reporting across studies. Using the WHO reference panels for ADA in method validation is a step towards harmonizing ADA assays [5].

Recently, Nice et al. showed the benefits of validating and lowering the positivity thresholds for a commercial ADA assay thereby obtaining a better detection of clinically meaningful anti‐adalimumab [6]. At Holbaek Hospital, infliximab and adalimumab drug levels and corresponding ADA have been measured in paired samples using commercial enzyme‐linked immunosorbent (ELISA) assays as part of a TDM service since 2019. In the TDM samples with undetectable drug concentrations and ADA classified as ‘negative’ using the manufacturer default positivity threshold, optical density (OD) values more than 2–3 times above the OD value of the negative control were sometimes observed.

The ADA signal‐to‐noise (S/N) ratio, a continuous measure, has been proposed as an alternative and more sensitive means of characterizing ADA responses [7, 8]. In this study, S/N ratios were calculated from all ADA OD values obtained between 2019 and 2025 to explore the use of S/N as a supplementary measure to detect low‐titre ADA. Validation of the S/N ratio included determination of limit of detection, precision and linearity of the S/N response using the WHO standards available for infliximab and adalimumab ADA.

Materials and Methods

Serum concentrations of infliximab and adalimumab and anti‐infliximab/adalimumab were quantified using ELISA kits from Promonitor (Grifols Diagnostic Solutions Inc., 10 808 Willow Ct., San Diego, CA 92127, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Infliximab (Reference: 5060230000) and Promonitor adalimumab (Reference: 5080230000) ELISA kits followed a 1:200 sample dilution. Absence of drug is defined as < 0.2 mg/L and < 0.3 mg/L for infliximab and adalimumab, respectively. Anti‐infliximab (Reference 507023000) and anti‐adalimumab (Reference: 509023000) were performed on undiluted samples in duplicates. Standard curves are used to compare OD in the samples to a series of known standards expressed in Absorbance Units (AU)/mL. Curves are fitted using four parameter logistic (4PL) models. Sample diluent was included on all plates as a blank to check assay background, but there was no blank correction of the signal in each well. Positive and negative controls are used to monitor assay performance. The manufacturer reports LLOQ values of 2.0 and 3.7 AU/mL and ADA positivity thresholds of 5 and 10 AU/mL for anti‐ifliximab and anti‐adalimumab, respectively.

From 2019 to 2024, all four ELISA assays were performed on a Triturus automated ELISA processor (Grifols, Barcelona, Spain). Beginning in 2025, analyses were performed using a semi‐manual workflow: sample dilution and pipetting on a Tecan Freedom EVO platform (Tecan, Männedorf, Switzerland), plate washing on a BioTek 50TS Microplate Washer (Agilent, Santa Clara, USA) and absorbance measurement on a BioTek Epoch 2 Microplate Spectrophotometer (Agilent, Santa Clara, USA). OD was measured at 450 nm with 620 nm as reference wavelength. S/N is defined as the ratio of the sample response (OD) divided by the negative control (OD) in the corresponding plate. S/N was calculated for all ADA results from paired drug and ADA samples sent for TDM evaluation from 2019 to 2025.

Analytical sensitivity of S/N in both ADA assays was evaluated from sample diluent (blank) and from an ADA negative donor pool (n = 60) by calculating limit of blank (LoB) = mean (blank) + 1.645 (SD [blank]) and limit of detection (LoD) = LoB + 1.645 (SD [blank]) [9]. Limit of quantification (LoQ) was calculated as mean (blank) + 10 (SD [blank]) [9].

S/N dilution linearity was determined using WHO standards 19/234 [5] and 19/232 [5] for anti‐infliximab and WHO 19/266 for anti‐adalimumab, respectively.

Between‐plate precision (%CV) was calculated from the S/N of the positive control from 20 separate ELISA plates. ADA positive patient samples were diluted to make low positive pools near assay's default threshold that were included on 6 separate plates. The coefficient of variation (%CV) for each sample was calculated as the standard deviation of the observations divided by the mean.

As the Promonitor ADA assays may be sensitive to high drug concentrations, physicians are encouraged to order/send samples representing drug through levels. However, no clinical data are linked to the TDM request, and many patients are self‐administrating regarding requesting a blood draw and, in the case of adalimumab, also the timing of drug administration. In line with the local Health Research Authority guidelines, formal ethical approval for our TDM service evaluation was not required.

Samples were divided into groups according to drug and ADA concentration:

Drug positive/ADA negative (ADA negative): Samples with detectable drug levels above manufacturers' LLOQ (drug positive) and ADA below manufacturers' LLOQ (ADA negative).

Drug positive/ADA negative (ADA negative): Samples with detectable drug levels above manufacturers' LLOQ (drug positive) and ADA below manufacturers' LLOQ (ADA negative).

Drug negative/ADA negative (double negative [DN]): Samples with undetectable drug levels below manufacturers' LLOQ (drug negative) and ADA below manufacturers' LLOQ (ADA negative).

Drug negative/ADA negative (double negative [DN]): Samples with undetectable drug levels below manufacturers' LLOQ (drug negative) and ADA below manufacturers' LLOQ (ADA negative).

Drug negative/ADA indeterminate (ADA indeterminate): Samples with undetectable drug levels below manufacturers' LLOQ (drug negative) and ADA between manufacturers' LLOQ and manufacturers' positivity threshold (ADA indeterminate).

Drug negative/ADA indeterminate (ADA indeterminate): Samples with undetectable drug levels below manufacturers' LLOQ (drug negative) and ADA between manufacturers' LLOQ and manufacturers' positivity threshold (ADA indeterminate).

Drug negative/ADA positive (ADA positive): Samples with undetectable drug levels below manufacturers' LLOQ (drug negative) and ADA above manufacturers' positivity threshold (ADA positive).

Drug negative/ADA positive (ADA positive): Samples with undetectable drug levels below manufacturers' LLOQ (drug negative) and ADA above manufacturers' positivity threshold (ADA positive).

There were no samples with detectable drug levels above manufacturers' LLOQ (drug positive) and ADA above manufacturers' LLOQ or positivity threshold (ADA indeterminate/ADA positive) in the cohort.

To compare S/N between groups, the Mann–Whitney U‐test for independent groups was used. Comparison of S/N across all groups was done by using the Kruskal–Wallis test. All reported p‐values were two‐tailed, and a p‐value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical calculations were performed using Prism software (GraphPad Inc. version 5).

The study was conducted in accordance with the Basic & Clinical Pharmacology & Toxicology policy for experimental and clinical studies [10].

Results

The LoB, LoD and LoQ for anti‐infliximab S/N were determined to 1.1, 1.3 and 2.5, respectively. For anti‐adalimumab, the LoB, LoD, LoQ for S/N were 1.1, 1.4 and 2.8, respectively. The results of between‐run precision for S/N were < 12% for both assays (Table S1).

Linearity of the S/N was evaluated by serial dilution of WHO 19/234, WHO 19/232 and WHO 19/266. For anti‐infliximab the S/N ratio was linear up to 33–35 while the S/N ratio for anti‐adalimumab reached a plateau at a S/N around 40–45. At higher S/N ratios corresponding with OD values > 1.4, more light is absorbed, causing the OD to plateau due to ELISA reader limits (Figures S1–S3). Accuracy of the anti‐infliximab assay when compared with the assigned target of the 1st Infliximab Antibody Reference panel [5] showed a negative bias < 20% in the range 3.3–52 AU/mL (Table S1).

S/N ratio was determined in 3999 anti‐infliximab samples from 2751 patients and in 2025 anti‐adalimumab samples from 1573 patients. The distribution of samples, drug concentration, ADA concentration and S/N for each group are shown in Table 1. The ADA S/N ratio for anti‐infliximab anti‐adalimumab showed a continuous increase between groups, with the S/N ratio being lowest in the ADA negative group and highest in the ADA positive group (p < 0.001, Kruskal–Willis). S/N were increased in both the DN and ADA indeterminate groups compared with the ADA negative group for anti‐infliximab (p < 0.0001, Mann–Whitney U‐test). For anti‐adalimumab the S/N was increased in the ADA indeterminate group (p < 0.001, Mann–Whitney U‐test) but not in the DN group compared with the ADA negative group (Figure 1).

Figure 1a,b shows the distribution of S/N ratio in ADA negative, DN, ADA indeterminate and ADA positive samples for anti‐infliximab and anti‐adalimumab, respectively. Data are shown as box and whiskers plot, with the box representing the interquartile range (IQR), and the whiskers defining 1.5 times the IQR. The vertical lines inside the box represent the median value of S/N in each group. The ADA negative, DN and ADA indeterminate groups refer to left Y‐axis while the ADA positive group refer to the Y‐axis on the right. For comparison, the Mann–Whitney U‐test was used between groups. p‐values are indicated above. S/N ratio corresponding to assay LoQ is indicated by the dotted line.

All anti‐infliximab ADA negative samples had S/N ratios < 1.5, while all ADA positive and ADA indeterminate samples had S/N ratios above the determined assay LoQ of 2.5. Most DN samples also had S/N ratios < 1.5 (82%), but 23 (18%) DN samples had S/N ratios > 2.5, indicating potential ADA positivity.

All anti‐adalimumab ADA negative samples had S/N ratios < 2.0, while ADA positive samples had S/N ratios above the determined LoQ of 2.8. Most DN samples also had S/N < 2 (86%), but 5 (6%) DN samples had S/N above LoQ, indicating potential ADA positivity. Most ADA indeterminate samples had a S/N ratio above LoQ (82%) except four samples with S/N ratios < 2.0.

Looking at serial TDM measurements from patients, some anti‐infliximab and anti‐adalimumab ADA positive results were preceded by DN or ADA indeterminate results (Figures S5 and S6). DN results also preceded some ADA indeterminate and DN results with an S/N > LoQ (Figures S6 and S7).

Discussion

A central challenge in the interpretation of ADA assays in TDM is establishing appropriate positivity thresholds. Recent studies highlight the importance of validating commercial ADA assays [6], particularly within large, real‐world patient populations, to ensure clinically meaningful thresholds. As Sparrow and Little [11] emphasize, clinicians ideally should ‘know their drug level, know their ADA positivity thresholds and know their assay’.

S/N ratios have recently emerged as an alternative to titre determination [7]. S/N measures the ratio of a specific sample signal to background noise, enhancing the ability to detect very low concentrations and may be more sensitive in detecting low‐titre ADA [8]. In this study, both ADA assays displayed a high sensitivity with S/N values resulting in LoD < 2 enabling samples with low S/N ratios near or below LoD to be distinguished from samples with higher S/N representing ADA positivity. As all ADA positive samples, most ADA indeterminate and some DN samples have S/N above the determined assay LoQ's, a S/N above LoQ may be used to confirm ADA positivity. By using a S/N ≥ assay LoQ to re‐classify ADA positivity, several samples in the TDM cohort displaying drug clearance—previously reported as DN, ADA negative or ADA indeterminate using manufacturers may be re‐classified as ADA positive.

The elevated S/N ratios observed in several infliximab DN samples compared with ADA negative samples are particularly noteworthy. Double negative status has been attributed to both immune‐mediated and non‐immune drug clearance [12], with prevalence varying by assay type. Bridging ELISAs, for example, may fail to detect IgG4 ADA, whereas some patients may appear double negative due to delayed or paused drug administration. As analysis of the WHO 19/232 infliximab antibody panel contains a high affinity, neutralizing IgG4 showed good linearity a failure to detect IgG4 does to seem to be the cause in this case. Several studies have linked double negative status to secondary loss of response [12] and to the future development of non‐transient ADA [13]. The increased S/N ratios in DN samples compared with ADA negative samples and occurrence of DN result prior to ADA detection in several patients suggest that a subset of DN samples in the TDM cohort represent early or low‐titre immunogenicity close to the detection limits of the assays. Some adalimumab DN samples may represent non‐adherence to treatment as adalimumab is self‐administered, a consideration that does not apply to intravenously administered infliximab. Elevated S/N ratios in both infliximab‐ and adalimumab‐treated patients may also represent false positive signals, that occur due to known causes of false positives, for example, pre‐existing antibodies, heterophilic antibodies and rheumatoid factor [14]. Including a baseline sample taken before treatment to normalize the S/N ratio would enable to distinguish samples with true low S/N ratio representing low‐titre drug‐clearing ADA from false positive S/N ratios.

This study has several limitations. First, the TDM cohort represents an unselected clinical referral population, and no information on clinical response was available. Second, although trough sampling is recommended, some non‐trough samples were inevitably included. Because the ADA assays are not drug‐tolerant, immunogenicity may be underestimated, particularly in adalimumab‐treated patients, where TDM may be performed ad hoc rather than immediately before dosing. In such cases, ADA may be falsely negative when drug concentrations remain within the measurable range. Finally, S/N is an assay‐dependent metric that reflects the quality and sensitivity of the assay but may lack commutability when applied to other ADA methods. S/N ratio also lacks correlation with WHO standards.

To conclude, using S/N ratios may serve as a valuable complement—or in some cases, a preferable alternative—to fixed manufacturer thresholds when assessing ADA positivity in TDM.

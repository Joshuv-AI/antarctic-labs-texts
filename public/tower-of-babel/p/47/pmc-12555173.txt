Analysis of primary healthcare center closures following the Finnish structural reform
Authors: V Väisänen, T Sinervo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWhile accessible primary healthcare is a cornerstone of well-functioning health systems, various factors, such as health system reforms, may affect the provision of services. The recent Finnish structural reform is a good example, as the new counties, driven by budgetary deficits, are currently adjusting their service networks, leading to public health center closures. To understand and examine the local policy process, our aim was to examine the determinants of health center closure decisions.MethodsWe utilized publicly available policy documents to specify the health centers facing closures, merging this information with municipality and county-level register data encompassing population characteristics, geographics, current service network, and reform-related factors. Using logistic regression analysis, we examined the factors associated with health center closures in a municipality.ResultsOur preliminary results indicate that 82 municipalities out of 295 were facing a health center closure. Factors associated with increased odds of a health center closure included a higher number of current public health centers (OR: 14.16, CI: 4.02-49.87), better medical desert index value (OR: 2.05, CI: 1.52-2.75), and the county being a new actor with no previous joint administration (OR: 7.47, CI: 1.82-30.57). Meanwhile, higher number of private health centers (OR: 0.24, CI: 0.07-0.82) and greater population growth (OR: 0.21, CI: 0.09-0.52) were associated with lower odds of health center closure.ConclusionsThe planned health center closures focus on municipalities with shrinking populations, but above average existing service networks. This signifies that decision-makers have managed to some extent target the actions appropriately. Counties with no previous collaboration between municipalities face higher service reform debt, which has implications for future reform planning. The reform outcomes and service access must be further analyzed in the future.Key messages• The health center closures in post-reform Finland appear to be directed relatively fairly, as better service network areas are targeted instead of rural areas already dealing with poor accessibility.• The closures appear to be driven mainly by service network characteristics, rather than political or reform-related factors, suggesting that the decision-making of the county councils is limited.

Incidentally detected abnormal findings on contrast-enhanced computed tomography scan during thrombosis screening after hip surgery: a retrospective study
Authors: Akihito Oya, Taro Umezu, Ryo Ogawa, Atsuhiro Fujie, Takuto Hatakeyama, Arihiko Kanaji, Yasuo Niki, Morio Matsumoto, Masaya Nakamura
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundVenous thromboembolism (VTE) is a potentially serious postoperative complication of hip surgery, including asymptomatic cases detectable by screening. During the study period, postoperative VTE screening was routinely performed using computed tomography pulmonary angiography (CTPA). CTPA is performed primarily to identify VTE, but other clinically significant abnormalities may be incidentally detected. The current study investigated the incidence of VTE after hip surgery as well as the frequency and characteristics of incidental abnormal findings identified on CTPA.MethodsThis study included 753 patients who underwent CTPA one day after hip surgery. The presence of VTE and incidental findings was assessed by radiologists via CTPA. Incidental findings were classified into three categories—mild (observation only), moderate (requiring further evaluation), and severe (requiring urgent management)—and the nature of these findings was investigated.ResultsIn total, 34 (4.5%) and 83 (11.0%) patients presented with pulmonary embolism and deep vein thrombosis, respectively. Furthermore, 96 (12.7%) patients had incidentally detected abnormal findings, including clinically significant abnormalities; among them, 6 (0.8%) had malignant tumors or abnormalities requiring surgery.ConclusionsPostoperative CTPA can reveal incidental abnormalities beyond VTE detection. Radiologists should carefully evaluate CTPA images for incidental findings and ensure appropriate clinical follow-up when necessary. Timely recognition and management of incidental findings may improve patient care. Therefore, clinicians should be vigilant to unexpected radiological findings, and a system that can facilitate proper recognition and subsequent action for such findings must be developed.

Background

Venous thromboembolism (VTE) describes the obstructive presentation of the deep venous system, which can frequently be complicated by lung thromboembolism. Postoperative VTE is a major complication of orthopedic surgery and may lead to sudden death. According to a nationwide study in China including over 100,000 patients, the in-hospital mortality rate of VTE decreased from 4.7% in 2007 to 2.1% in 2016 [1]. Giorgi-Pierfranceschi M. et al. reported that the mortality rate during anticoagulation for VTE ranged from 3% to 6% in patients without cancer and approximately 18% to 33% in those with cancer [2]. According to a multicenter Japanese registry, the in-hospital mortality rate of patients with acute pulmonary thromboembolism was 14% [3].

Some studies have reported incidental CT scan findings. Bernheim et al. reported incidentally identified arterial calcification in 732 of 1,410 patients aged 25–50 years who underwent abdominal or pelvic CT scan at the emergency department [4]. Razavi-Ratki et al. reported that CT scans revealed incidental findings, such as calcification, in 566 (10.9%) of 5,193 patients with head trauma [5]. Incidental findings are frequently encountered across different imaging modalities, and although CT is widely used for whole-body evaluation, comparable investigations have not yet been performed for postoperative CTPA. The American College of Radiology Incidental Findings Committee recommends the management of incidentally detected abnormal findings on CT scan and magnetic resonance imaging in some organs, such as the adnexa [6], pancreas [7], adrenal gland [8], pituitary gland [9], kidney [10], and liver [11]. Previous research suggests that incidental findings are not always acted upon consistently in clinical practice.

Although CTPA is primarily used to detect VTE, the broad anatomic coverage of the examination allows identification of other clinically significant findings, including tumors, infections, and vascular malformations. However, the frequency of incidentally observed abnormal findings on CTPA after hip surgery has not been evaluated. To address this gap, we conducted a retrospective study to determine the incidence of VTE and to characterize the frequency and clinical nature of incidental findings detected on postoperative CTPA after hip surgery.

Methods

Study design and participants

All procedures were performed in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. This retrospective study was approved by the ethics committee of Keio University School of Medicine (No. 20170241). Informed consent was obtained through an opt-out process, whereby information about the study was disclosed on the institutional website, allowing patients the opportunity to decline participation. Prior to August 2020, computed tomography pulmonary angiography (CTPA) was routinely performed at our department one day after hip surgery to screen for postoperative VTE, and all images were reviewed by radiologists to confirm the presence of VTE. This retrospective observational study included patients who underwent hip surgery between January 2014 and July 2018. At that time, institutional protocol recommended CTPA one day post-operation for VTE screening; patients following this protocol were included in the study. Patients were excluded if they had renal dysfunction, a known contrast agent allergy, or refused consent. Patients younger than 20 years were excluded because the risk of VTE is physiologically low in children and adolescents, with an estimated incidence of less than 1 per 1,000 individuals [12]. All CTPA images were interpreted by board-certified diagnostic radiologists, with a second radiologist verifying each interpretation.

Artificial joint surgery was performed on 689 patients, accounting for 91.5% of all patients enrolled in this study. For subsequent analyses, the patients were categorized into the following subgroups according to the type of surgical procedure: unilateral total hip arthroplasty (THA, n = 492), simultaneous bilateral THA (n = 94), hip revision arthroplasty (revision THA, n = 86), and bipolar hip arthroplasty (BHA, n = 17). Other procedures included open reduction and internal fixation, hip arthroscopy, and pelvic osteotomy. The details of the surgical procedures are summarized in Table 1.

Incidence of VTE

The patients underwent surgery under general anesthesia alone or general anesthesia with concomitant spinal anesthesia. All patients received standard postoperative thromboprophylaxis according to institutional protocol unless contraindicated. Anticoagulant prophylaxis was not administered in children or in patients at low risk for VTE who underwent surgery within a short duration. All patients underwent intermittent pneumatic compression and wore elastic stockings until the initiation of postoperative ambulation. CTPA examinations included both a pulmonary arterial phase and a lower-extremity venous phase. An iodine-based contrast agent was administered at a dose of 600 mg/kg over 30 seconds through a peripheral venous line. Pulmonary arterial phase images were acquired using a bolus-tracking technique, with a region of interest placed in the right ventricle; scanning was initiated once the attenuation exceeded 150 Hounsfield units. This acquisition covered the entire region from the neck to the toes. The venous phase for deep vein assessment was routinely performed at a fixed delay of 4 minutes after contrast injection, covering the area from the pelvis to the toes. CT examinations for postoperative VTE screening were performed using available multidetector CT scanners at our institution. A total of five CT units from different manufacturers are installed in our hospital, and therefore minor differences in specifications exist among devices. However, examinations for this study were not limited to a specific scanner model, and all scans were performed using multidetector CT systems capable of pulmonary arterial phase and lower-extremity venous phase acquisitions as described above. All CTPA examinations obtained one day after surgery, and board-certified specialists of the Japan Radiological Society reviewed for the presence of PE and DVT” eliminates redundancy.

Frequency and nature of incidentally detected abnormal findings

All CTPA slices were objectively assessed by the radiologists. Findings of PE and DVT were investigated in more detail. Abnormal findings, including those that may be life-threatening, were also incidentally identified. At our institution, the findings are divided into three categories according to the instructions of radiologists based on the imaging impression: mild (those requiring clinical-course observation only), moderate (those requiring close examination, e.g., imaging, endoscopy), and severe (those requiring urgent treatment), and the category is clearly stated in the radiologists’ report. The categorization was determined through consensus among three or more radiologists, including board-certified radiologists. The nature and frequency of incidentally detected abnormal findings in each category were investigated. Abnormal findings already known based on patient declarations or data on the medical records were excluded. Verification of known findings was performed manually through review of each patient’s electronic medical record.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using Statcel 4 (OMS Publishing Inc., Tokyo, Japan), an add-in software for Microsoft Excel. As the distribution of age was non-normal in the overall cohort, age was presented as median with interquartile range (IQR), and differences among groups were assessed using the Steel–Dwass test for multiple comparisons. The incidences of PE and DVT were compared only among the unilateral, bilateral, and revision THA groups using the chi-square test of independence, owing to the limited number of cases in other surgical categories. When overall significance was detected, pairwise comparisons between groups were performed using Fisher’s exact test with Bonferroni correction for multiple testing. Odds ratios and 95% confidence intervals were calculated to assess the strength of associations. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Incidence of VTE

Figure 2 shows the representative images of PE and DVT. A respiratory physician or cardiologist was consulted for the treatment of patients with symptomatic PE or proximal DVT detected on CTPA. Of 753 patients, 103 (13.7%) presented with VTE (PE, n = 20; DVT, n = 68; both PE and DVT, n = 15). Patients who underwent prosthetic hip surgery accounted for 97.1% of all PE (34/35) and 94.0% of all DVT (78/83) cases. Most VTE events occurred after prosthetic hip surgery (Table 3). Of 586 patients who underwent primary THA, 21 (3.6%) and 63 (10.8%) presented with PE and DVT, respectively. In addition, 52 (10.6%) of 492 patients who underwent unilateral THA had DVT. This finding was similar to that observed in patients who underwent bilateral THA (11 [11.7%] of 94 patients). Conversely, proximal DVT, which is particularly urgent, was noted in 2 (2.1%) of 94 patients who underwent bilateral THA and 3 (3.5%) of 86 patients who underwent revision THA. The incidences of PE and DVT were compared among the unilateral, bilateral, and revision THA groups. Overall comparisons were performed using the chi-square test of independence, and pairwise comparisons were evaluated using Fisher’s exact test with Bonferroni correction for multiple testing. The overall incidence of PE differed significantly among the three groups (p = 0.0009), with a higher rate in the revision THA group (12.8%) than in the unilateral (3.7%) and bilateral (3.2%) THA groups (p = 0.001 and p = 0.024, respectively, Bonferroni-adjusted p < 0.05).

The lung was the most common site of abnormalities, followed by gynecological organs (uterus and ovaries), digestive organs (liver, pancreas, and gall bladder), and urinary tract organs (kidneys, adrenal glands, and urinary bladder) (Table 7). Overlap of abnormalities occurred in two patients. One patient in the moderate category had both an ovarian tumor and a bladder tumor, and one patient in the severe category had both lung cancer and liver cancer. A review of medical records identified all cases requiring intensive treatment. Specifically, 4 patients with mild, 3 patients with moderate, and 6 patients with severe incidental findings were diagnosed with conditions that required surgical intervention or chemotherapy. In the severe category, several patients required active oncological treatment, including surgery or chemotherapy for breast, liver, and hematologic malignancies. Most of these patients survived several years after treatment, although some transitioned to palliative care. In the moderate category, three patients were diagnosed and surgically treated for uterine, bladder, and cardiac tumors, all of whom had favorable outcomes. In the mild category, most cases involved incidental lung or soft-tissue lesions that were either surgically treated or followed conservatively. Discussion

In this retrospective analysis of 753 postoperative hip-surgery patients, VTE was detected in 13.7% and incidental abnormal findings in 12.7%, including eleven malignancies requiring intervention. Hence, in the study period, VTE screening using CTPA was routinely conducted for eligible hip surgery patients, except those with contraindications to contrast agents. The disadvantages of the procedure include radiation exposure, use of contrast medium and metal artifacts, and high costs; however, it shows high efficacy in PE and proximal DVT detection [13, 14]. Moreover, THA implant placement accuracy can be closely examined by CT image analysis using three-dimensional analysis software [15, 16].

Patients younger than 20 years were excluded from screening because of their extremely low risk of VTE. All hip surgeries (except for surgery for femoral neck fractures) performed at our institution are elective. Since pregnancy is always confirmed before surgery, the number of pregnant patients undergoing surgery and CTPA was low.

The American Academy of Orthopedic Surgeons and the American College of Chest Physicians revised the VTE prevention guidelines in 2011 and 2012, respectively [17, 18]. The major treatment revision was that the target was changed from all VTEs to symptomatic VTE. The incidence rate of symptomatic VTE in patients undergoing total hip or knee replacement is approximately 0.4%–7.9%. Lethal PE accounts for 10% of these cases. Hence, the overall incidence of lethal PE in THA is approximately 0.04%–1.0% [19]. Our 13.7% overall rate reflects asymptomatic cases detected by screening and therefore exceeds the 0.4–7.9% incidence reported for symptomatic VTE in recent guidelines. Previous guidelines recommended active anticoagulant therapy and routine screening to prevent VTE [20]. However, after the guideline revision, routine anticoagulant therapy and screening are no longer recommended for asymptomatic VTE (which has a low incidence rate), owing to cost and the risks of hemorrhage. However, a large-scale clinical study on this subject has not been performed; no unified view has been obtained because the intervention target was changed to symptomatic VTE only, thereby reducing the overall evidence level of the guidelines. The use of CTPA was discontinued owing to the disadvantages mentioned earlier as well as its cost; ultrasonography is currently preferred in its stead. Therefore, this retrospective study investigated the incidence of VTE after hip surgery using previous CTPA results.

The prevalence of noncommunicable diseases increases with age; therefore, unexpected abnormal findings are more likely to be detected on CTPA. According to global statistics, 24.5 million new cancer cases and 9.6 million cancer-related deaths were reported in 2019, with lung and prostate cancers being the leading causes of cancer-related mortality in men and breast, lung, and colorectal cancers in women [21]. Many of these malignancies can be visualized on CTPA, which may explain the higher frequency of incidental findings observed in the present cohort.

This study investigated the details of incidentally detected abnormal findings on CTPA after hip surgery. In patients with incidental findings detected (96 [12.7%] of 753 patients), the most common abnormal findings classified as mild included pulmonary nodular shadow and fibrosis; the clinical significance of these conditions may be low. Accordingly, only patients with pulmonary abnormalities were followed up. Close specialist examination was recommended for abnormal findings classified as moderate if malignant tumor or infection could not be ruled out. Based on the CTPA findings, malignant tumor was strongly suspected in all six patients with severe findings. However, diseases requiring intensive treatment were also identified in four mild and three moderate cases. Including the six severe cases, all patients underwent definitive diagnostic evaluation and subsequent treatment according to specialist recommendation. The incidence of these tumors may increase with age. However, no significant difference was observed in terms of mean age among the four groups. The youngest patient was aged 38 years. These observations underscore the importance of thorough review of all postoperative CTPA scans for incidental pathology, regardless of patient age.

Various tests are performed in the medical field; nevertheless, insufficient confirmation of results can lead to poor prognosis and even malpractice. Based on this study, a system has been developed in our institution to minimize such risks. In our facility, all radiology reports are automatically marked on the electronic medical records after the report is read by the attending physician. Furthermore, severe findings are documented, and the attending physician is directly notified by the radiologist, thereby ensuring that these findings are investigated promptly, addressed appropriately, and not overlooked. However, since this method requires a great deal of labor, developing a faster and more accurate method using advanced technologies, such as artificial intelligence, is important.

The current study has certain limitations. First, this single-center study included mainly Japanese patients, limiting generalizability. Second, CTPA was performed only once on postoperative day 1, so delayed VTE events could not be captured. Third, incidental findings were classified solely based on initial radiologic impressions, without histopathologic confirmation, in all cases. Fourth, Laboratory data such as fibrinogen, D-dimer, hemoglobin, and C-reactive protein (CRP) levels were not consistently available in the retrospective records and were therefore not included in the present analysis. Future multicenter longitudinal studies incorporating follow-up data are warranted to determine the prognostic significance of incidental findings detected on postoperative CTPA.

Conclusion

Approximately 13.7% of asymptomatic postoperative patients screened by CTPA demonstrated PE, DVT, or both. Incidentally detected visceral abnormalities were observed in 12.7% of patients. Malignant tumors and abnormalities requiring surgery were identified in nearly 1% of patients. These findings warrant confirmatory diagnostic evaluation to establish true disease and determine clinical significance. Confirmed abnormalities should prompt appropriate clinical referral and management. Enhanced communication systems between radiologists and surgeons may help ensure that incidental findings identified on postoperative CTPA are promptly recognized and appropriately managed.

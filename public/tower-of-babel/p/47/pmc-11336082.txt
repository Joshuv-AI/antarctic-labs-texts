Enhanced multimodal medical image fusion via modified DWT with arithmetic optimization algorithm
Authors: Ahmad A Alzahrani
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractMedical image fusion (MIF) techniques are proficient in combining medical images in distinct morphologies to obtain a reliable medical analysis. A single modality image could not offer adequate data for an accurate analysis. Therefore, a novel multimodal MIF-based artificial intelligence (AI) method has been presented. MIF approaches fuse multimodal medical images for exact and reliable medical recognition. Multimodal MIF improves diagnostic accuracy and clinical decision-making by combining complementary data in different imaging modalities. This article presents a new multimodal medical image fusion model utilizing Modified DWT with an Arithmetic Optimization Algorithm (MMIF-MDWTAOA) approach. The MMIF-MDWTAOA approach aims to generate a fused image with the significant details and features from each modality, leading to an elaborated depiction for precise interpretation by medical experts. The bilateral filtering (BF) approach is primarily employed for noise elimination. Next, the image decomposition process uses a modified discrete wavelet transform (MDWT) approach. However, the approximation coefficient of modality_1 and the detailed coefficient of modality_2 can be fused interchangeably. Furthermore, a fusion rule is derived from combining the multimodality data, and the AOA model is enforced to ensure the optimum selection of the fusion rule parameters. A sequence of simulations is accomplished to validate the enhanced output of the MMIF-MDWTAOA technique. The investigational validation of the MMIF-MDWTAOA technique showed the highest entropy values of 7.568 and 7.741 bits/pixel over other approaches.Keywords: Medical images, Multimodality, Image fusion, Arithmetic optimization algorithm, Fusion ruleSubject terms: Computational science, Computer science

Introduction

MIF has become a significant study content due to its importance in delivering a high-quality output for the diagnosis and treatment in medical fields1. As a result, some imaging modalities provide common data, and other types of image representation provide exclusive details within the restricted area. For instance, computed tomography (CT) image is best fitted for analyzing dense structures such as bones with the lowest distortion. However, it could provide more accuracy while identifying physiological modifications2. Similarly, Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) assists in observing the delicate structures of soft tissues. Image fusion aims at combining data of imaging modality to attain fused medical images with better accuracy and detailed descriptions of the objects3. It makes it possible to report multimodality studies quickly and accurately. Researcher workers developed several techniques to combine information from imaging modality. Each algorithm has its strengths and shortcomings4. The medical image fusion (MIF) field continuously evolves to meet new demands and challenges. The fusion technique utilizes medical imaging of human organs of concern, including the breast, brain, and lung.

Image fusion is categorized depending on the databases, including multi-spectral, multi-focus, multi-sensor, multiscale, and multi-temporal5. In the multi-spectral technique, the spatiotemporal correlation and corresponding information are used to obtain a clear image by combining the features in the spectrum domain. In the multi-focus technique, input images with various focuses are merged into a single image that retains each essential data6. In the multi-sensor technique, the medical diagnoses application combines data from multiple sources to create an understandable image of each crucial data. This data may not be visible to the visible human system7. The extracted data from combined images precisely define the position of abnormality. In the multiscale technique, more differently exposed Low Dynamic Range (LDR) images are combined into more informational LDR images. In the multi-temporal technique, the details of all clinical variables are obtained, and the risk of information failure is reduced8. So far, the researcher workers have introduced many image fusion techniques, mainly including the methods based on multiscale decomposition and those based on sparse representation, hybrid, and deep learning models9. The image fusion method depends on multiscale decomposition, initially decomposing the source imaging into higher- and lower-frequency sub-bands. Next, specific fusion rules synthesize the fused images’ sub-bands10. Lastly, the fusion outcome is rebuilt by the equivalent converse transform.

However, the approximation coefficient of modality_1 and the detailed coefficient of modality_2 can be fused interchangeably. Furthermore, a fusion rule is derived from combining the multimodality data, and the AOA model is enforced to ensure the optimum selection of the fusion rule parameters. By maintaining edge sharpness while mitigating noise, BF substantially improves the quality of the image, which is significant for accurate evaluation in medical scenarios. This technique confirms that diagnostic images remain clear and informative, assisting more accurate medical interpretations and decision-making processes.

Related works

Li et al.11 developed the DFENet technique that incorporates convolutional neural network (CNN) and vision transformer (ViT) feature learning employing self-supervised learning and including a feature fuser, decoder, and encoder. An encoder consisted of a CNN and transformer element and was utilized for extracting global and local image features. A new global semantic data combination method was also developed. The decoder consists of 6-convolution layers with two skip networks. In12, a hybrid multimodal segmenting technique that depends on Transformers and CNNs is evaluated, and a new framework, the HybridCTrm network, was presented. Zhou et al.13 introduced an unsupervised MMIF approach by incorporating a densely connected high-resolution network (DHRNet) with a fusion transformer. Firstly, the extracting process of local factors takes place in the source images employing the DHRNet. Secondly, these factors could be input as fine-grained attention elements from the fusion transformer and exploited for global and local feature fusion. Lastly, the combined outcome is reproduced through the decoder network.

Zhang et al.14 presented a self-supervised transformer for MMIF. The random images super-resolution (RSR) technique was developed. The MMIF and RSR techniques are the same multi-resolution inputs and popular aims, such as image improvement and restoration, utilized to reduce the domain difference. A transformer-based combined network to capture global and local data of source modalities was introduced. In15, an unsupervised hybrid technique that depends on CNN and ViT for multimodal integration of medical images is suggested. Additionally, a new complementarity data fidelity loss was planned to implement a limit to improve the protection of complementarity data in integrated outcomes. The authors16 developed a new generative adversarial method for producing medical images, ResViT. Residual links in ART blocks support a variety of acquired images, whereas a channel compression component reduces task-related data. A weight-sharing approach can be presented amongst ART blocks to mitigate computational problems.

In17, a DL technique was developed, and an adaptive non-subsampled shearlet transform (ANSST) technique was designed for disintegrating the images. Still, the filter coefficient was enhanced by a hybrid water strider-dingo optimizer (HWS-DOX) technique. The upper and lower sub-bands of source images one and two are incorporated by a weighted averaging method and optimized-DNN (ODNN) technique. Lastly, the last integrated images have been achieved by implementing the inverse ANSST. Tang et al.3 recommended a new unsupervised technique for combing multimodal medicinal imaging by a multiscale adaptive Transformer called MATR. An adaptable convolution for adapting the convolution kernel depends on the global correlative context was developed. An adaptive Transformer could be utilized to improve the global semantic extraction ability. Besides, an objective function contains a field shared data loss, and structural loss was created.

Babu and Venkatanarayana18 propose a novel multimodal image fusion technique for medical imaging (MRI, CT), employing the Cuckoo Search-Grey Wolf Optimization (CSGWO) model, which comprises contrast enhancement, CT and QWT decomposition for low and high-frequency extraction, followed by Cuckoo Search-Grey Wolf Optimization (CSGWO) technique. In19, an adaptive whale optimization algorithm (AWOA) with long short-term memory (LSTM) techniques is presented. The model also presented discrete wavelet transform with arithmetic optimization. Bhutto et al.20 present a multimodal medical image fusion (MMIF) approach utilizing a deep CNN (D-CNN) model with pre-processing methods, including nonlinear average median filtering (NL-AMF) and multiscale improved top-hat (MI-TH) approaches to improve the quality of the image. Furthermore, nonlinear anisotropic diffusion (NL-AD) is utilized for image splitting, with base parts fused via dimension mitigation and elaborated parts fused by the D-CNN for preserving enriched features.

The proposed model

This paper designs an automated multimodal MIF using the MMIF-MDWTAOA technique. The technique aims to generate an integrated image that holds the essential details and factors from each modality, leading to a detailed depiction for accurate interpretation by medical professionals. It encompasses BF-based noise removal, MDWT-based image decomposition, fusion process, and AOA-based fusion rule parameter selection. Figure 1 portrays the workflow of the MMIF-MDWTAOA method.

BF-based image pre-processing

The BF approach is employed to eradicate the presence of the noise. The BF utilizes nonlinear image denoising21. It is established in the rule of spatial Gaussian convolutional, which is sequential and only assumes one weighted factor to execute image smoothing. Medical images such as CT, MRI, PET, SPECT, and X-ray scans are vulnerable to several kinds of noise that degrade the quality of the image and impact diagnostic accuracy. Most general noise types comprise Gaussian noise, which manifests as random discrepancies in pixel intensity, speckle noise prevalent in ultrasound and radar imaging, quantization noise from digitization, motion artefacts due to patient movement, and electronic noise presented by imaging devices. Conclusion

This paper introduces an automated multimodal MIF using the MMIF-MDWTAOA technique. The MMIF-MDWTAOA technique aims to create a combined image that holds the essential details and factors from each modality, leading to a detailed depiction for accurate interpretation by medical professionals. It encompasses BF-based noise removal, MDWT-based image decomposition, fusion process, and AOA-based fusion rule parameter selection. Primarily, the BF approach is utilized to remove noise. Next, the image decomposition process takes place using the MDWT model. Moreover, the approximation coefficient modality_1 and detailed coefficient of modality_2 can be fused interchangeably. Furthermore, a fusion rule can derived to fuse the multimodality data, and the AOA can be applied for the optimal selection of the fusion rule parameters. A series of simulations can be performed to validate the improved solution of the MMIF-MDWTAOA technique. The widespread simulation results showcased the betterment of the MMIF-MDWTAOA technique in terms of different metrics.

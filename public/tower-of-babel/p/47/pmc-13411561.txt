Impact of Integrated Yoga Therapy on the Perceived Stress, Attention Level, and Financial Performance Among Stock Market Investors
Authors: Jisha Yeshvandh, Rithika Keshav, Mangesh Pandey, Natesh Babu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:The stock market is crucial to the economy, investors playing a critical role in wealth creation. However, more than 95% of investors experience losses, primarily due to psychological, or behavioral obstacles. Fluctuating stress levels, lack of emotional control, delayed decision-making, indecision, lack of concentration, and unclear goals, are commonly cited reasons for most investors losing money in the stock market.Objectives:This study tries to find if practicing yoga can help such investors to mitigate the perceived stress, enhance the attention levels, and thereby increase the financial performance in terms of Return on Investment.Methods:In this study, stock market investors with a minimum of three months of experience were selected using a snowball sampling approach. Participants were then assigned to either the experimental or control group based on their convenience. The dependent variables of interest included the perceived stress scale (PSS), the attention control scale (ACS), and return on investment (ROI).Results:Investors in the experimental group underwent a 30-day yoga intervention. Results revealed a statistically significant reduction in perceived stress (P < 0.05), a significant increase in attention levels (P < 0.05), and a notable improvement in profitability (P < 0.05) compared to the control group.Conclusion:The findings suggest the potential effectiveness of yoga as a strategy to enhance mental well-being and financial performance in Stock Market Investors.

INTRODUCTION

The stock market is a vital indicator of economic health, particularly in rapidly growing economies like India, which saw its nominal gross domestic product (GDP) reach an estimated $3.75 trillion in 2023, making it the fifth-largest economy globally (Tripathy, 2023; Kushwaha Satyendra, 2024).[12] Key Indian stock exchanges, including the national stock exchange (NSE) and bombay stock exchange (BSE), facilitate extensive trading in equities, derivatives, commodities, currencies, and debt. The number of registered investors has surged significantly, increasing from 3.7 crore to 6.8 crore between March 2019 and March 2021. Despite this growth, troubling trends persist: Securities and exchange board of india [SEBI] data shows that 89% of retail investors experienced net losses in FY 2021–22, with an average loss of ₹1.1 lakh per individual (PTI, 2023).[3] Additionally, research indicates that nearly 90% of investors lose their capital within 90 days, a pattern attributed not to a lack of knowledge, but to psychological and behavioral issues like stress, impulsivity, and inadequate attention (Vidani, 2024).[4]

Stock market traders often spend long hours analyzing data and executing trades, which requires intense concentration, rapid decision-making, and effective stress management. Over time, the relentless pressure and high-stress environment can diminish their coping abilities, adversely affecting both mental and physical health.[4] Beyond financial responsibilities, traders must also prioritize their well-being to ensure sustained performance and long-term success. Research indicates that prolonged sitting—common among traders—raises the risk of cardiovascular disease by 18% and cancer by 17% (Mayor, 2015).[5] The continuous volatility in market prices further contributes to elevated stress levels, as each tick in the market represents potential gain or loss. Chronic stress not only impairs focus and performance but can also trigger a vicious cycle of poor decision-making, financial losses, and deteriorating health. Alarmingly, studies suggest that over 90% of diseases are linked to stress-related factors (Liu et al., 2017).[6] In extreme cases, traders facing significant losses and an inability to cope have been reported to make grave decisions, including self-harm. This highlights the urgent need for effective coping strategies that can enhance resilience, reduce perceived stress, and sustain optimal cognitive functioning. Yoga, an ancient discipline known for promoting holistic well-being, has been increasingly recognized for its role in enhancing mental clarity,[789] emotional regulation, and physical health—making it a promising intervention for traders operating in high-stress environments.[89]

Yoga, an ancient holistic practice, encompasses specific postures, controlled breathing, and meditation, offering effective stress management tools.[1011] It provides techniques to regulate emotions and achieve emotional balance.[12] Acting as a body-mind exercise, yoga fosters physical, mental, and spiritual relaxation through Asana, involving stretching, breathing, and meditation.[13] Studies suggest that yoga enables individuals to navigate highly stressful situations with enhanced focus, ensuring fulfilling outcomes aligned with expectations.[131415161718]

With the Indian economy growing, the number of investors in the Stock Market is also expected to increase manifold over the next few years and continue multiple decades. For all new investors, being profitable and sustaining their profits is the major measure that will determine the length of period they will be available in the stock market. Over a longer period, Stock market trading can become a lucrative profession. The findings of this study will aid the prospective investors to become better and stay in the markets as profitable investors. The objectives of the present study are to assess the baseline levels of perceived stress, attention, and financial performance among stock market investors; to implement a structured 30-day yoga intervention tailored for this population; to evaluate the effectiveness of the intervention in reducing perceived stress and enhancing attention levels; and to compare the post-intervention changes between experimental and control groups in order to determine whether improvements in these psychological parameters contribute to enhanced financial performance and mental resilience which is the psychological ability to adapt, recover, and maintain effective functioning when facing stress, uncertainty, or high-pressure situations in high-stakes decision-making environments.

Alternate Hypothesis: After 30 days of Yoga intervention, there will be a significant decrease in perceived stress, increase in attention and increase in financial performance in stock market investors.

Null Hypothesis: After 30 days of Yoga, there will be no change in perceived stress, attention, and financial performance in stock market investors.

METHODOLOGY

Participants

The study adopted a Quasi-Experimental study design with a control group, utilizing the Snowball sampling method for participant selection [Figure 1]. Random sampling was not feasible as the author had limited knowledge of available stock market investors. A Google form was distributed to select stock market investors, who were then encouraged to share it within their networks. This facilitated the collection of demographic information from participants [Table 1]. The form allowed participants the flexibility to choose their preferred group, recognizing that not all investors were inclined to participate in yoga sessions. Before commencing the study, participants underwent an orientation program highlighting the study’s significance, resulting in a negligible attrition rate. A total of 60 stock market investors were divided into two groups (experimental and control, n = 30 each), with the sample size determined based on prior research and accounting for potential attrition.[1920] Participants who opted out of yoga participation were assigned to the control group. The diverse participant pool represented various backgrounds in terms of experience, invested capital, education, and geographic locations, comprising 21 females and 39 males. Educational qualifications ranged from students aging over 20 years, while investment capital varied from less than 2 lakhs to over 100 lakhs (1 crore) [Table 1]. The study’s online administration accommodated participants from different geographic locations.

Ethical consideration

The ethical principle of justice was upheld in this study by ensuring that participants in both the intervention and control groups were selected using the same inclusion and exclusion criteria, ensuring fairness in recruitment. Participants in the control group were informed in advance about their group allocation and were offered the same yoga intervention after the completion of the study (waitlist control design), thereby ensuring equal opportunity. The risks and benefits of the study, including the yoga practices were explained to all the participants so that participants could take an informed decision to participate in the study. Informed consent was collected from the participants. The study was conducted in a way that respects the rights and dignity of the participants. Declaration of Helsinki was followed. The Institutional Ethical Committee approved the study.

PSS was developed by Cohen et al. (Cohen, Kamarch and Mermelstein, 1983) is a popular tool for measuring psychological stress. It is a self-reported questionnaire that was designed to measure the degree to which situations in one’s life are appraised as stressful. It has been validated for Indian population with internal consistency score measured through Cronbach’s alpha (α =0.73) and the Spearman-Brown split-half reliability coefficient (α =0.71).[23] ACS is a self-report scale that is designed to measure two major components of attention (attention focusing and attention shifting). The ACS consists of 20 items. ROI is a performance measure used to evaluate the efficiency or profitability of an investment or compare the efficiency of several different investments. ROI measures the amount of return on a particular investment, relative to the investment’s cost. A self-designed form was used to extract data from individual investors on their Return on Investment. This form was validated by the experts in the swami vivekananda yoga anusandhana samsthana (SVYASA).

The pre data collection form was given to the participants 1 week prior to the intervention and the post data was collected within a week after the intervention

Data analysis

The questionnaires were disseminated online to all the participants after educating about it, for both the experimental group as well as the control group. Data scoring and entry were completed as per the guidelines of the assessment tools. All the participants were asked to complete all questions, after thoroughly screened for completeness of responses. The data were labeled and coded for each participant to ensure privacy. In the present study, even though the Shapiro–Wilk test indicated some deviations from normality, the sample size and acceptable skewness values suggest that the distribution of the mean differences is approximately normal as supported by Central Limit Theorem. Hence parametric tests were used. Data were organized and tabulated for statistical analysis in Microsoft Excel program. Paired sample t-test was used to obtain the significance of the difference within group to know the difference in the pre and post intervention values. Independent sample t-test was used to obtain the significance of difference between groups to ensure that the baseline values were similar in both experimental and control groups and to check if there are any changes after the intervention.

These findings suggest that the intervention had a positive impact on reducing stress levels, enhancing attention control, and improving ROI among participants compared to the control group.

DISCUSSION

The study explores the effectiveness of yoga on perceived stress and attention control among Indian stock market investors. This study is unique in addressing the concerns of stock market investors, who are under continuous pressure to perform financially by managing their stress and maintaining optimal attention. A major gap identified during the literature review was the lack of studies applying yoga as an intervention to stock market investors. This void was addressed in the current study, which aimed to tackle the internal psychological challenges faced by investors through the introduction of regular yoga practices. Although the trading profession is attracting an increasing number of participants in India, research on behavioral and psychological interventions to support them remains minimal.

The findings are consistent with related studies from adjacent fields. For instance,[24] Charoensukmongkol and Aumeboonsuke’s research on mindfulness and trading performance indicated that mindfulness practices enhance impulse control and trading discipline, thereby improving trading outcomes. Similarly, our study demonstrated significant positive impacts of yoga on investors’ mental states, including better focus and reduced stress. A related study by[25] showed that yoga effectively reduces anxiety and stress in corporate executives, which supports the rationale for using similar techniques among investors.

A notable strength of this study is the absence of participant dropouts, facilitated by the online mode of delivery, which allowed individuals from diverse geographical locations to participate. This highlights the accessibility and scalability of yoga as an intervention. However, conducting the study online also introduced certain limitations. The lack of physical, in-person interaction may have deprived participants of the added psychological benefits that arise from group support and informal exchanges, which often contribute to relaxation and emotional well-being. Another limitation lies in the generic nature of the yoga module used, as it was not specifically tailored to the unique psychological demands of stock market investors. Furthermore, the sample included a broad range of participants, from ages 20 to 65, with varying levels of investment experience and financial exposure (ranging from ₹2 lakh to ₹1 crore). This heterogeneity introduces variability, as factors such as risk appetite, stress tolerance, and trading behavior can significantly differ between, for example, a novice equity trader and an experienced derivatives investor. Eligibility was broadly defined, with only a minimum of three months of trading experience required. As a result, the sample may not fully capture the nuances of more experienced or specialized investor subgroups.

The three variables—perceived stress, attention control, and ROI—were studied, leaving out several other relevant behavioral and physiological parameters such as decision-making abilities, emotional regulation, impulse control, heart rate, blood pressure, and investor sentiment (e.g. fear, gloom, joy). Existing literature[26] suggests that such sentiments can predict market behavior and influence return volatility, making them worthwhile targets for future investigations.

Looking ahead, future research should consider developing a yoga module specifically designed for stock market investors, incorporating elements aimed at enhancing emotional resilience, discipline, and stress tolerance. Further, stratifying participants based on trading style (e.g. intra-day, swing, positional, trend), years of experience, and investment scale could yield more targeted insights. Including additional outcome measures such as physiological indicators and real-time trading data may offer a more comprehensive understanding of the mechanisms through that yoga impacts investor behavior and performance.

CONCLUSION

This study found that stock market investors, who practice yoga regularly found significantly reduced stress and improved attention. These improvements resulted in enhanced financial performance. The investors who practiced yoga reported better mental clarity, increased focus, and a more balanced emotional state, all of which are critical for successful trading. However, while the results are promising, further research is needed to understand the long-term effects of yoga on the financial performance of stock market investors. This study suggests that yoga could be a valuable tool for stress management and performance enhancement in high-pressure financial environments.

An Isolated Colonic Neurofibroma Resected by Endoscopic Mucosal Resection After Pretreatment Diagnosis
Authors: Hiroshi Tomizawa, Mamoru Tokunaga, Takuto Suzuki
Published: 2026

Isolated colonic neurofibroma is a rare disease. We report a case diagnosed before treatment and resected by endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR). A woman in her 50s underwent colonoscopy, which revealed a 6-mm, firm-elastic mobile subepithelial lesion in the ascending colon (Figure A). Endoscopic ultrasonography demonstrated a lipoma-like hyperechoic mass involving the mucosal and submucosal layers without invasion of the muscularis propria (Figure B). Biopsy showed interlacing spindle cells, and immunohistochemistry suggested neurofibroma, with negativity for c-kit, smooth muscle actin, and discovered on GIST-1, and positivity for S-100 protein (S-100), SRY-box transcription factor 10, and cluster of differentiation 34. Because the patient preferred endoscopic resection to surveillance, EMR was performed and en bloc resection was achieved. Histopathology confirmed neurofibroma, showing interlacing spindle cells within the mucosal and submucosal layers (Figure C) with S-100 positivity (Figure D). Fat droplets in the adjacent submucosa likely accounted for the lipoma-like hyperechoic appearance. Margins were negative, and no malignant features were identified. Clinical genetics and dermatologic evaluation revealed no family history or cutaneous findings suggestive of neurofibromatosis type 1, leading to a diagnosis of isolated colonic neurofibroma. To our knowledge, this is the first report of planned EMR after pretreatment diagnosis. Given reports of surgery for symptomatic isolated colonic neurofibromas, endoscopic diagnosis and intervention may reduce future surgery.

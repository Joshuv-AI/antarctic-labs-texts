Study on the correlation between the number of mushroom poisoning cases and meteorological factors based on the generalized additive model in Guizhou Province, 2023
Authors: Sufang Xiong, Anzhong Wu, Ling Weng, Li Zhang, Muli Wu, Haijiao Li, Peipei Zuo, Shu Zhu, Jun Li, Hua Guo, Tao Long
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMushroom poisoning is a significant food safety issue in Guizhou Province, China. Studies on this topic are essential for its prevention and control. We aimed to analyze the epidemiological characteristics of mushroom poisoning and study the correlation between its cases and meteorological factors in Guizhou Province.MethodsWe collected data on mushroom poisoning and meteorological factors in Guizhou Province in 2023. A descriptive analysis was conducted on the epidemiological features of mushroom poisoning and meteorological factors. We used Spearman correlation analysis and the generalized additive model to examine the relationship between meteorological factors and the number of mushroom poisoning cases.ResultsIn 2023, mushroom poisoning cases in Guizhou Province were concentrated among individuals aged 20–59. Clinical symptoms were primarily gastrointestinal symptoms and occurrences peaked from June to October, mainly in the northeastern region of the province. Most incidents occurred in households. In 72 mushroom poisoning incidents where species were identified, 33 poisonous mushrooms were found. The number of mushroom poisoning cases in Guizhou Province was positively correlated with each meteorological factor(P < 0.05). The generalized additive model showed a significant nonlinear relationship between DGT, PRE, RHU, SSD, and the number of mushroom poisoning cases (P < 0.05). Interaction analysis showed that the risk of mushroom poisoning in Guizhou Province increased with the rising values of any two of these four meteorological factors.ConclusionMushroom poisoning incidents in Guizhou Province are characterized by high-risk groups, seasonality, and specific high-incidence regions and places. Public awareness for high-risk groups and early warnings for high-incidence regions and places should be strengthened every summer and fall. There is a correlation between meteorological factors and the number of mushroom poisoning cases, suggesting that these factors could serve as early warning indicators for the prevention and control of mushroom poisoning.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-024-20050-6.

Introduction

Poisonous mushrooms, commonly known as toxic fungi or toadstools, are large fungi that can cause poisoning in humans or livestock [1]. There are thousands of poisonous mushroom species worldwide [2], with over 600 species in China and approximately 220 species responsible for mushroom poisoning incidents [3]. Mushroom poisoning occurs after consuming poisonous mushrooms, leading to symptoms such as nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, and diarrhea. In severe cases, it can cause neurological damage and liver or kidney failure [4]. Mushroom poisoning is a global issue [5], with its incidence varying significantly worldwide due to differences in local traditions, lifestyles, climates, etc [6]. In China, mushroom poisoning is monitored and reported as a foodborne disease nationwide and has become a significant food safety issue [7]. The consumption of poisonous mushrooms is a major cause of foodborne disease in the country [8]. Currently, China has increased its emphasis on preventing and controlling mushroom poisoning, leading to improved sensitivity in monitoring these incidents [9]. From 2010 to 2020, China’s Foodborne Disease Outbreak Surveillance System reported 10,036 mushroom poisoning incidents, with 38,676 cases and 788 deaths [2]. The southwest region is the most severely affected area for mushroom poisoning in China [2]. Guizhou Province alone has identified 140 species of poisonous mushrooms [10], including more than ten highly poisonous species [11]. Research indicates that from January 2012 to June 2022, Guizhou reported 1,577 mushroom poisoning incidents, with 5,495 cases and 93 deaths [12]. Guizhou has become one of the primary provinces in southwestern China with a high incidence of mushroom poisoning.

In recent years, the impact of climate change on human health has become increasingly evident and serious. Studies have shown that changes in meteorological factors significantly affect the incidence of infectious diseases [13–15], chronic diseases [16–18], and other diseases, attracting significant social concern. There are more studies on the effects of meteorological factors on the incidence of foodborne diseases [19, 20], but fewer on the association between meteorological factors and mushroom poisoning. Some studies, both domestic and international, have indicated a correlation between mushroom poisoning and meteorological factors. However, these studies have primarily focused on the relationship between monthly rainfall and the number of mushroom poisoning cases per month [21], or the association between annual average temperature, annual rainfall, and mushroom poisoning incidence [22]. They have not explored the correlation between daily meteorological factors and the number of mushroom poisoning cases, nor have they investigated the nonlinear characteristics of this relationship. In addition, given that the impact of meteorological factors on mushroom poisoning varies in different regions, the study results are non-universal. In 2023, Guizhou Province reported the third-highest incidence of mushroom poisoning in China [3]. Moreover, residents in the province have a habit of foraging for mushrooms. Therefore, conducting region-specific research on the relationship between mushroom poisoning and meteorological factors is essential.

In summary, this study provides a descriptive analysis of the epidemiological characteristics of mushroom poisoning in Guizhou Province during 2023, the most severe year since 2012, to understand its fundamental aspects. Additionally, we examined the correlation between meteorological factors and the daily mushroom poisoning cases through Spearman correlation analysis and the generalized additive model (GAM) in Guizhou Province. This aims to furnish scientific insights for the early detection, precise prevention, and control of mushroom poisoning in Guizhou Province.

Materials and methods

Study area

Guizhou Province is in the southwestern region of China, situated on the Yunnan-Guizhou Plateau. The province covers a total area of 176,200 square kilometers and is divided into 9 cities and 88 counties (sFig.1). As of the end of 2023, Guizhou Province had a resident population of 38,650,000.

Data sources

Data on mushroom poisoning for 2023 were obtained from the Foodborne Disease Outbreak Surveillance System of Guizhou Province, including mushroom poisoning cases, time, region, and place. Population data were obtained from the Seventh National Population Census of China (https://www.guizhou.gov.cn/zwgk/zfsj/tjnj/). Map data were obtained from the National Geographic Information Public Service Platform (https://www.tianditu.gov.cn/). Species identification data of mushroom poisoning incidents were obtained from the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention. Daily meteorological data were obtained from the Guizhou Provincial Meteorological Department in 2023, including daily average temperature (TEM), daily average 0 cm ground temperature (AGT), daily average 5 cm ground temperature (BGT), daily average 10 cm ground temperature (CGT), daily average 15 cm ground temperature (DGT), daily average precipitation (PRE), daily average relative humidity (RHU), and daily average sunshine duration (SSD). A total of eight meteorological factors were analyzed (daily average 5/10/15 cm ground temperature is the daily average temperature in the ground at a depth of 5/10/15 cm above the ground). Both the mushroom poisoning and meteorological data underwent rigorous multi-level reviews. A two-person team systematically checked for and removed any logical errors, duplicate records, or abnormal values.

Statistical methods

Descriptive analysis

We conducted a descriptive analysis of the basic characteristics of mushroom poisoning data and meteorological factors. Meteorological factors were represented using mean ± standard deviation. Additionally, a regional distribution map of the reported incidence of mushroom poisoning in Guizhou Province was created.

Spearman correlation analysis

We conducted a study using Spearman correlation analysis to examine the correlation between the number of mushroom poisoning cases and meteorological factors during the same period in Guizhou Province. To avoid multicollinearity, meteorological factors with correlation coefficients greater than 0.7 (|rs|>0.7) were not included simultaneously in GAM.

Generalized additive model

The GAM was used to analyze nonlinear relationships and interactions.

In this paper, the Poisson distribution was chosen as the distribution family of the regression model to calculate the impact of each meteorological factor on the number of mushroom poisoning cases. Building on the results of correlation analysis, the meteorological factors deemed relevant were incorporated into the GAM using the natural cubic spline function. This model was employed to evaluate the influence of these factors on the number of mushroom poisoning cases. In this model, log(yt) represents the logarithmic transformation of the number of mushroom poisoning cases on day t (with the addition of 1 to avoid taking the logarithm of zero), ɑ denotes the intercept, while s( ) indicates the natural cubic spline function with a maximum of 2 degrees of freedom to avoid overfitting. DGT, PRE, RHU, and SSD are the daily average 15 cm ground temperature, daily average precipitation, daily average relative humidity, and daily average sunshine duration, respectively.

Bivariate surface plots were used to illustrate the combined effects of any two meteorological factors continuously and intuitively on the number of mushroom poisoning cases.

Data collection and organization were performed using Excel 2019, and maps were created using ArcGIS 10.6. All other statistical analyses were conducted in R 4.3.3. The significance level was set at ɑ=0.05.

Results

Characteristics of mushroom poisoning and meteorological factors

In 2023, a total of 825 mushroom poisoning incidents were reported in Guizhou Province, with 2,818 cases and 2 deaths. Most mushroom poisoning cases occurred in individuals aged 20–59 years, accounting for 67.49% (1,902/2,818) of all cases. The clinical symptoms of poisoning were primarily gastrointestinal, with vomiting, nausea, and diarrhea accounting for 89.87% (2,533/2,818), 73.24% (2,064/2,818), and 52.45% (1,478/2,818) of all cases, respectively (sTable 1).

In 2023, the average number of daily mushroom poisoning cases in Guizhou Province was 8 cases per day, with the following meteorological factors averages: TEM 16.82℃, AGT 19.34℃, BGT 18.90℃, CGT 18.97℃, DGT 19.02℃, PRE 2.73 mm, RHU 77.09%, SSD 4.17 h. The distribution of these meteorological factors was skewed (Table 1).

Time distribution of mushroom poisoning

In 2023, mushroom poisoning incidents in Guizhou Province occurred throughout the year, primarily concentrated from June to October. These months accounted for 95.27% (786/825) of all incidents, 94.46% (2,662/2,818) of all cases, and 100.00% (2/2) of all deaths (2/2). August had the highest number of incidents and cases, accounting for 41.33% (341/825) of all incidents and 40.03% (1,128/2,818) of all cases. Deaths occurred in July and August, with one death occurring in each month (Fig. 1).

Regional distribution of mushroom poisoning

In 2023, mushroom poisoning incidents were reported in 74 out of 88 counties in Guizhou Province, mainly in the northeastern region. Zunyi (16.26/100,000) had the highest incidence of mushroom poisoning, followed by Tongren (15.27/100,000). The number of mushroom poisoning incidents in these two cities accounted for 39.52% (326/825) and 19.39% (160/825) of all incidents, respectively (Fig. 2).

Variety of mushrooms involved in poisoning incidents

In 2023, Mushroom species identification was conducted in 72 of the 825 mushroom poisoning incidents in Guizhou Province, and a total of 33 poisonous mushrooms were identified. Including 2 species causing acute liver failure, namely Lepiota brunneoincarnata and Amanita pallidorosea; 1 species causing rhabdomyolysis, namely Russula subnigricans; 1 species causing acute renal failure, namely Amanita oberwinklerana; 20 species causing gastroenteritis, namely Entoloma cf. subsinuatum, Agaricus atrodiscus, Russula pseudojaponica, etc.; and 9 species causing psycho-neurological disorder, namely Panaeolus cyanescens, Amanita subglobosa, Gyromitra venenata, etc. There were 11 species of mushrooms with unknown toxicity in the accompanying samples tested (sTable 2).

Correlation analysis between meteorological factors and mushroom poisoning

Spearman correlation analysis was conducted to examine the relationship between meteorological factors and the number of mushroom poisoning cases, as well as among the meteorological factors themselves. The results showed that the number of mushroom poisoning cases in Guizhou Province was positively correlated with TEM (rs = 0.54, P < 0.05), AGT (rs = 0.52, P < 0.05), BGT (rs = 0.56, P < 0.05), CGT (rs = 0.57, P < 0.05), DGT (rs = 0.59, P < 0.05), PRE (rs = 0.31, P < 0.05), RHU (rs = 0.21, P < 0.05), and SSD (rs = 0.15, P < 0.05). The TEM was highly positively correlated with AGT (rs = 0.99), BGT (rs = 0.99), CGT (rs = 0.98), and DGT (rs = 0.97). Therefore, highly correlated variables were excluded and DGT, PRE, RHU, and SSD were included in the GAM analyzing the effect of meteorological factors on mushroom poisoning cases (Table 3).

Effect of meteorological factors on mushroom poisoning cases

The exposure-response curves showed a significant nonlinear relationship (P < 0.05) between DGT, PRE, RHU, SSD, and mushroom poisoning cases. Overall, high levels of DGT, PRE, RHU, and SSD are identified as risk factors for the number of mushroom poisoning cases (Fig. 3).

Discussion

The number of reported mushroom poisoning incidents and cases in Guizhou Province reached the highest level since the implementation of mandatory surveillance in 2023 [12, 23]. This could be attributed to the gradual improvement of the surveillance system [24], the increase in outdoor activities among residents following COVID-19 [25], and changes in meteorological factors. The age range of mushroom poisoning cases in Guizhou Province in 2023 was mainly concentrated in individuals aged 20–59 years, aligning with the findings of Sichuan [26] and Jiangxi [27]. This suggests that adults are a high-risk group for mushroom poisoning, emphasizing the need to enhance health education and publicity on prevention and control measures for this demographic. The clinical symptoms of mushroom poisoning cases primarily manifested as gastrointestinal symptoms, including vomiting, nausea, and diarrhea. All initial clinical symptoms of mushroom poisoning displayed intense gastrointestinal reactions, aligning with findings from Sichuan [28] and Zhejiang [29]. However, during the diagnosis and treatment process, it is not feasible to determine that poisonous mushrooms of the gastroenteritis type were unintentionally consumed solely based on severe gastrointestinal reactions. The potential accidental ingestion of highly toxic mushroom varieties should be ruled out by monitoring physiological and biochemical indicators in mushroom poisoning cases. Time distribution of mushroom poisoning incidents in Guizhou Province in 2023 primarily occurred from June to October, displaying a certain seasonality consistent with studies conducted in several provinces in China [30–32]. Similar timing of mushroom poisoning has been reported in Thailand [21], while Israel has primarily reported mushroom poisoning in winter [25]. This may be due to varying climatic factors in different regions throughout the year, leading to different peak times for mushroom poisoning. Regionally, mushroom poisoning in Guizhou Province in 2023 mainly occurred in the northeastern region, with the highest reported incidence rate being Zunyi. It with the DaLou Mountains, high forest coverage, and abundant rainfall, provides a suitable growing environment for mushrooms, which may explain its higher reported incidence [33]. Households were the most frequent mushroom poisoning place in Guizhou Province in 2023, consistent with studies in Yunnan [34] and Hubei [35], which may be related to the local custom of mushroom picking. 33 types of poisonous mushrooms were identified from 72 mushroom poisoning incidents in Guizhou province in 2023, with the poisonous mushrooms of gastroenteritis being the most prevalent. Compared to the number of mushroom poisoning incidents in the province, relatively few have been identified by species, hindering the understanding of the mushroom species causing poisoning and their spatial and temporal distribution. There is still a need to continue to collect more samples from mushroom poisoning incidents and to identify mushroom species to develop targeted prevention and strategies. Mushroom poisoning is related to the growth pattern of mushrooms, which requires suitable temperature and humidity. In Guizhou, the climatic conditions from June to October are suitable for mushroom growth. Therefore, Guizhou should prioritize the prevention and control of mushroom poisoning during the summer and autumn seasons. The northeastern region is a high-incidence area. During this period, the focus should be on monitoring regional meteorological indicators conducive to mushroom growth and issuing early warnings of the risk of mushroom poisoning.

The study indicated that the mushroom poisoning cases in Guizhou Province in 2023 were correlated with the local meteorological factors. Spearman correlation analysis revealed a positive association between the number of mushroom poisoning cases and TEM, AGT, BGT, CGT, DGT, PRE, RHU, and SSD in Guizhou Province, with statistically significant differences (P < 0.05). This indicates the potential influence of meteorological factors on the development of mushroom poisoning, consistent with existing study findings [22]. Among them, DGT was the most relevant factor to the number of mushroom poisoning cases, possibly because the temperature conducive to mushroom growth is closest to DGT. The correlation coefficient between the number of mushroom poisoning cases and each meteorological factor varied in magnitude, indicating the possibility of multiple factors influencing the onset of mushroom poisoning. The GAM analysis indicated that DGT, PRE, RHU, and SSD were risk factors for the number of mushroom poisoning cases. Thailand’s [21] study found that the number of mushroom poisoning cases increased with higher rainfall, consistent with the results of the present study. The results of the interaction indicated that DGT, PRE, RHU, and SSD are significant factors influencing mushroom poisoning, and any two of these factors have an impact on the number of mushroom poisoning cases. It is suggested that meteorological factors should be considered when relevant departments conduct risk assessments. This provides a theoretical basis for the development of an early warning prediction model for mushroom poisoning by these departments in response to climate change. This study only analyzed the relationship betweeen the number of mushroom poisoning cases and meteorological factors. However, the occurrence of mushroom poisoning may also be influenced by socioeconomic factors, individual behaviors lifestyles, etc. Additionally, this analysis did not consider the effects of lagged impacts. Future research should comprehensively consider other related factors affecting the incidence of mushroom poisoning, including lagged effects, for a more in-depth analysis.

Conclusion

In summary, mushroom poisoning in Guizhou Province was severe in 2023. Health promotion and education should be intensified during the mushroom growth period from June to October, particularly focusing on prevention and control in the northeastern region. The number of mushroom poisoning cases is positively correlated with various meteorological factors. There are nonlinear relationships with DGT, PRE, RHU, and SSD, with increases in mushroom poisoning cases often occurring when these factors are excessively high. The study findings demonstrated the correlation between the number of mushroom poisoning cases and meteorological factors in Guizhou Province, indicating the potential use of meteorological factors as early warning indicators for preventing and controlling mushroom poisoning.

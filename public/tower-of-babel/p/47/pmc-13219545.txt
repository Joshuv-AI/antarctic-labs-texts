Phytochemical engineering of vanadium pentoxide nanoparticles via neem extract: a sustainable platform for dual in vitro antidiabetic and anticancer activity
Authors: P. Naveen, Gopi Mamidi, Indira Priyadarsini Amancharla
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study reports a sustainable green synthesis of vanadium pentoxide nanoparticles (V2O5 NPs) using Azadirachta indica (neem) leaf extract as a natural reducing and stabilizing agent. The formation of V2O5 nanoparticles was confirmed through UV–Vis, FTIR, XRD, SEM, TEM, and zeta potential analyses. The synthesized nanoparticles exhibited an orthorhombic crystalline phase, quasi-spherical morphology with particle sizes ranging from 20 to 80 nm, and good colloidal stability (–28.6 mV). Phytochemicals such as flavonoids and polyphenols present in the neem extract played a key role in nanoparticle formation and surface stabilization. In vitro biological evaluation revealed notable α-amylase inhibitory activity (IC50 = 68 µg/mL), indicating potential antidiabetic relevance, along with dose-dependent cytotoxic effects against HeLa and MCF-7 cancer cell lines, where cell viability decreased to below 40% at 100 µg/mL. The observed biological responses are discussed in relation to nanoparticle surface chemistry and redox-active vanadium species; however, mechanistic pathways such as reactive oxygen species (ROS) involvement are inferred based on existing literature rather than directly measured. Overall, the findings highlight the potential of neem-mediated V2O5 nanoparticles as a promising material for future in vitro biomedical investigations.Graphical abstract

Introduction

Nanotechnology has significantly advanced materials science and biomedical research by enabling the development of nanoscale materials with tunable physicochemical properties and enhanced surface functionality [1]. Among these, metal oxide nanoparticles have attracted considerable attention due to their redox activity, catalytic behavior, and wide applicability in biomedical, environmental, and energy-related fields [2]. In particular, vanadium pentoxide (V2O5) nanoparticles have emerged as promising functional materials owing to their semiconducting nature and strong oxidation–reduction capability [3, 4].Vanadium-based compounds are known to exhibit insulin-mimetic properties and influence glucose metabolism through cellular signaling pathways, supporting their potential antidiabetic relevance [5, 6]. In addition, vanadium oxide nanostructures have demonstrated cytotoxic effects in cancer cells, often associated with oxidative stress mechanisms reported in previous studies [7, 8]. These properties highlight the growing interest in V2O5 nanoparticles for biomedical applications [9]. However, conventional synthesis methods for vanadium oxide nanoparticles typically involve toxic reagents, high temperatures, and harsh processing conditions, raising concerns regarding environmental sustainability and biological compatibility [10]. To address these limitations, green synthesis approaches using plant-derived biomolecules have gained significant attention as environmentally benign alternatives [11]. Plant extracts are rich in secondary metabolites such as flavonoids, polyphenols, terpenoids, and alkaloids, which act as reducing and stabilizing agents, enabling nanoparticle formation under mild conditions [12]. This approach minimizes chemical waste while producing surface-functionalized nanoparticles suitable for biological applications [13, 14].At the nanoscale (1–100 nm), materials exhibit unique properties such as high surface-to-volume ratio, tunable surface charge, and enhanced surface reactivity, which influence biological interactions and enzyme–nanoparticle binding [1, 10]. In metal oxide systems, including V52O5, reduced particle size enhances electron transfer processes and redox activity, contributing to the biological responses observed in vitro [3, 12]. Furthermore, surface functionalization with plant-derived phytochemicals improves nanoparticle stability and may modulate biological interactions under experimental conditions [7, 18]. Among medicinal plants, Azadirachta indica (neem) is widely recognized for its diverse biological properties, including antioxidant, antimicrobial, antidiabetic, and anticancer activities [15]. Neem leaves contain bioactive compounds such as nimbin, quercetin, azadirachtin, and limonoids, which can facilitate metal ion reduction and nanoparticle stabilization [16]. Although neem-mediated synthesis of noble metal nanoparticles has been extensively reported [17, 18], studies on neem-derived vanadium oxide nanoparticles remain limited. Moreover, most plant-mediated studies focus on single biological activities, with limited exploration of structure–activity relationships and multifunctional in vitro performance [19–21]. Accordingly, the present study aims to develop a phytochemical-assisted green synthesis of V2O5 nanoparticles using neem leaf extract. The synthesized nanoparticles were systematically characterized using UV–Vis, FTIR, XRD, SEM, TEM, and zeta potential analyses. There in vitro biological performance was evaluated through α-amylase inhibition and cytotoxicity assays against HeLa and MCF-7 cancer cell lines. The results are discussed in relation to nanoparticle structure, surface chemistry, and redox properties, while acknowledging that detailed mechanistic pathways and therapeutic relevance require further investigation.

Materials and methods (final revised version)

Materials

Ammonium metavanadate (NH4VO3, 99.9%), 3,5-dinitrosalicylic acid (DNS), α-amylase enzyme, starch, and MTT reagent were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich (India). Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM), fetal bovine serum (FBS), phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) were purchased from HiMedia Laboratories (Mumbai, India). Human breast (MCF-7) and cervical (HeLa) cancer cell lines were obtained from the National Centre for Cell Science (NCCS), Pune, India. All other reagents were of analytical grade, and Milli-Q deionized water was used for all preparations.

Collection and preparation of neem leaf extract

Fresh leaves of A. indica (neem) were collected from the botanical garden of Government Degree College (Autonomous), Nagari, Andhra Pradesh, India (13.33° N, 79.58° E), during January 2025. The plant was authenticated by Dr. A. Indira Priyadarshini, Department of Botany, Government Degree College (A), Nagari. A voucher specimen (Accession No. AI-2025–01) was deposited in the institutional herbarium for future reference. The collected leaves were washed thoroughly with distilled water to remove surface dust and shade-dried for 3–4 days. About 20 g of the dried leaves was finely chopped and boiled with 200 mL of deionized water at 80 °C for 30 min to extract the active phytochemicals. The mixture was cooled to room temperature and filtered through Whatman No. 1 filter paper. The resulting dark-green filtrate was stored at 4 °C for further use. The aqueous extract, rich in flavonoids, polyphenols, and terpenoids, served as the reducing and stabilizing agent in the green synthesis of vanadium pentoxide nanoparticles.

Green synthesis of vanadium pentoxide nanoparticles

A 0.01 M aqueous solution of ammonium metavanadate (NH4VO3) was prepared as the vanadium precursor. Fifty millilitres of this solution was mixed with an equal volume (50 mL) of neem leaf extract (1:1 v/v) under continuous magnetic stirring at 80 °C for 2 h. The initial pH of the reaction mixture was measured to be 7.2 ± 0.1, and it gradually decreased to pH ≈ 6.5 during the course of reduction due to the release of organic acids from the extract. A visible color change from pale yellow to reddish-brown confirmed the reduction of vanadium ions and the formation of V52O5 nanoparticles. After completion of the reaction, the mixture was cooled to room temperature and centrifuged at 10,000 rpm for 15 min. The collected nanoparticles were washed repeatedly with distilled water and ethanol to remove residual biomolecules and then oven-dried at 60 °C for 12 h. The green synthesis protocol employed in this study was developed based on our laboratory optimization and adapted from previously reported plant-mediated synthesis approaches, with appropriate modifications in extract concentration, reaction conditions, and calcination parameters to achieve phase-pure V2O5 nanoparticles. The resulting orange-brown nanopowder was stored in airtight containers for subsequent characterization and biological evaluation.

In-vitro antidiabetic activity (α-amylase inhibition assay)

The α-amylase inhibition activity was evaluated following the DNS colorimetric method. Briefly, 500 µL of V2O5 NP suspension (10–100 µg/mL) was incubated with 500 µL of α-amylase solution (1 U/mL, pH 6.9) at 37 °C for 10 min. Subsequently, 500 µL of 1% starch solution was added and incubated for another 10 min. The reaction was terminated with 1 mL of DNS reagent, and the mixture was heated at 90 °C for 5 min. After cooling, absorbance was recorded at 540 nm.

Acarbose (100 µg/mL) served as the positive control. In-vitro anticancer activity (MTT assay)

The cytotoxicity of the synthesized V2O5 NPs was tested against HeLa and MCF-7 cell lines using the MTT assay. Cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 1 × 104 cells/well and incubated for 24 h at 37 °C with 5% CO2. After attachment, the cells were treated with various nanoparticle concentrations (10–100 µg/mL) for 24 h. Subsequently, 20 µL of MTT (5 mg/mL) was added to each well and incubated for 4 h. The resulting formazan crystals were dissolved in 150 µL of DMSO, and absorbance was measured at 570 nm using a microplate reader. Statistical analysis

All experiments were conducted in triplicate. Results are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical significance between treated and control groups was determined using one-way ANOVA in GraphPad Prism 9, and differences were considered significant at p < 0.05.

Results and discussion

UV–visible spectroscopy

To confirm the formation of vanadium pentoxide nanoparticles, UV–Visible spectroscopy was performed. A distinct colour change from pale yellow to reddish-brown was observed during synthesis, indicating the bioreduction of vanadium ions (V5+) to their oxide form by phytochemicals present in the neem extract. The UV–Vis absorption spectrum (Fig. 1) exhibited a strong and well-defined peak at 365 nm, characteristic of charge transfer transitions in orthorhombic V2O5 nanoparticles. This absorption feature confirms the formation of nanoscale vanadium oxide [1]. The absence of precursor-related peaks below 300 nm further indicates complete conversion and phase purity. Similar absorption behaviour has been reported for plant-mediated V2O5 nanoparticles synthesized using Moringa oleifera and Aloe vera extracts [2], supporting the effectiveness of neem extract in facilitating nanoparticle formation. Mechanistically, polyphenols and flavonoids present in the extract act as reducing agents by donating electrons to vanadium ions, leading to nanoparticle nucleation. Additionally, functional groups such as hydroxyl and carbonyl moieties contribute to surface stabilization, preventing agglomeration and promoting a uniform optical response [3].Fig. 1UV–Vis Spectrum of Neem-mediated V2O5 Nanoparticles. Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy

To identify the functional groups involved in the reduction and stabilization of nanoparticles, FTIR analysis was performed (Fig. 2). The spectrum of the synthesized V2O5 nanoparticles exhibited several characteristic absorption bands, including a broad peak at 3440 cm−1 corresponding to O–H stretching vibrations of phenolic and alcoholic groups, a band at 1620 cm−1 attributed to C=O stretching of carbonyl or amide groups, and a peak at 1380 cm−1 associated with C–O stretching vibrations of polyols. A distinct band observed at 530 cm−1 corresponds to the V–O–V stretching mode, confirming the formation of vanadium pentoxide [4]. The reduction in intensity or disappearance of certain organic peaks compared to the pure extract indicates the active involvement of phytochemicals in the reduction of vanadium ions. Biomolecules such as flavonoids, terpenoids, and related compounds likely acted as both reducing and capping agents, contributing to nanoparticle formation and stability [5]. This surface-bound organic layer not only prevents agglomeration but also enhances biocompatibility and potential biological interactions.The observed FTIR features are consistent with previously reported plant-mediated V2O5 nanoparticles, where similar O–H, C=O, C–O, and V–O–V vibrations have been attributed to phytochemical-assisted reduction and stabilization. In particular, the V–O–V band in the range of 520–540 cm−1 aligns well with earlier studies using Aloe vera and Ocimum sanctum extracts, supporting the formation of orthorhombic V2O5 nanoparticles [22, 23].Fig. 2FTIR Spectrum of V2O5 Nanoparticles. X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis

To determine the crystalline structure and phase purity of the synthesized nanoparticles, XRD analysis was performed (Fig. 3). The diffraction pattern exhibited prominent peaks at 2θ = 20.3°, 26.2°, and 31.0°, corresponding to the (200), (001), and (101) lattice planes of orthorhombic V2O5 (JCPDS No. 41–1426). Additional minor peaks at 34.4° and 37.6° further confirm the crystalline nature of the nanoparticles. The sharp and well-defined peaks indicate high crystallinity, while the absence of extraneous reflections suggests good phase purity. The average crystallite size, calculated from the (001) reflection using the Debye–Scherrer equation, was approximately 45 nm, which is consistent with the particle size observed in TEM analysis. The formation of a well-defined crystalline phase can be attributed to the controlled oxidation and nucleation processes mediated by phytochemicals present in the neem extract, which regulate nanoparticle growth [7]. The observed diffraction pattern closely matches previously reported green-synthesized V2O5 nanoparticles, confirming the formation of orthorhombic phase with comparable crystallite sizes (40–60 nm) [24, 25]. These findings indicate that the neem-mediated synthesis approach effectively produces phase-pure V2O5 nanoparticles with controlled crystallinity, which is expected to enhance surface reactivity and influence their biological performance [8].Fig. 3XRD Pattern of Orthorhombic V2O5 Nanoparticles. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM)

To examine the surface morphology and particle distribution, SEM analysis was performed (Fig. 4a). The micrographs reveal that the synthesized nanoparticles are quasi-spherical and moderately aggregated, forming small clusters likely due to drying-induced adhesion during sample preparation. The particle surfaces appear relatively smooth, indicating the presence of an organic capping layer derived from neem phytochemicals. Despite slight aggregation, the particles exhibit a fairly uniform distribution, with most sizes within the sub-100 nm range, consistent with TEM observations. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)

To further examine particle size and internal structure, high-resolution TEM analysis was performed (Fig. 4b). The images confirm that the synthesized nanoparticles are quasi-spherical and well-dispersed, with particle sizes ranging from 20 to 80 nm. The corresponding size distribution histogram exhibits a narrow spread, indicating good monodispersity. Distinct lattice fringes observed in the HRTEM micrograph, with an interplanar spacing of 0.34 nm, correspond to the (001) plane of orthorhombic V2O5 [11], confirming the crystalline phase consistent with XRD results. Additionally, the selected area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern displays bright concentric rings, further validating the crystalline nature of the nanoparticles. The observed uniformity and controlled morphology can be attributed to the chelation and growth-regulating effects of neem-derived phytochemicals during synthesis. These biomolecules limit uncontrolled particle growth, resulting in stable and uniformly sized nanoparticles suitable for biological applications.

Zeta potential analysis

To evaluate surface charge and colloidal stability, zeta potential analysis was performed (Fig. 5). The synthesized nanoparticles exhibited a zeta potential value of –28.6 mV, indicating a strong negative surface charge. This suggests effective electrostatic repulsion between particles, thereby preventing aggregation [12].The negative surface charge can be attributed to the deprotonation of hydroxyl and phenolic groups present in the phytochemical capping layer on the nanoparticle surface. According to DLVO theory, zeta potential values exceeding ± 25 mV generally indicate good colloidal stability [13].Therefore, the neem-derived V2O5 nanoparticles demonstrate excellent dispersion stability, making them suitable for biological applications.Fig. 5Zeta potential distribution of neem-derived V2O5 nanoparticles showing a surface charge of –28.6 mV, indicating good colloidal stability associated with phytochemical-mediated surface capping

In-vitro antidiabetic activity (α-amylase inhibition)

To evaluate the antidiabetic potential of the synthesized nanoparticles, an α-amylase inhibition assay was performed. The V2O5 nanoparticles exhibited a clear concentration-dependent inhibitory effect, with inhibition increasing from 32% at 10 µg/mL to 78% at 100 µg/mL. The calculated IC50 value was 68 µg/mL, which is comparable to the standard drug acarbose (IC50 = 55 µg/mL) [14].The observed inhibition may be attributed to the interaction of nanoparticles with the active site of α-amylase, thereby hindering the enzymatic hydrolysis of starch into glucose. The redox-active vanadium centers are likely to interact with catalytic residues of the enzyme, while surface-bound phenolic groups may form hydrogen bonds, collectively enhancing inhibitory activity [15].These findings indicate that neem-mediated V2O5 nanoparticles exhibit promising α-amylase inhibitory activity and may serve as potential nanomaterial-based candidates for managing hyperglycemia under in vitro conditions.

In-vitro anticancer activity (MTT assay)

To evaluate the cytotoxic effects of the synthesized nanoparticles, MTT assays were performed on HeLa and MCF-7 cancer cell lines (Fig. 6). The results demonstrate a clear dose-dependent decrease in cell viability. At the highest tested concentration (100 µg/mL), cell viability decreased to below 40% (p < 0.05), indicating a significant in vitro cytotoxic response. Although microscopic examination was conducted to observe nanoparticle-induced morphological changes, representative images could not be included due to limited imaging facilities at the time of experimentation. Therefore, cytotoxicity was quantitatively assessed using the standard MTT assay, a widely accepted method for evaluating cell viability [26, 27].The inclusion of normal fibroblast cells as a noncancerous control enables assessment of selective cytotoxicity. Notably, normal cells maintained more than 80% viability across all tested concentrations, whereas HeLa and MCF-7 cells exhibited pronounced dose-dependent reductions in viability. This differential response indicates selective cytotoxic effects under in vitro conditions. The observed cytotoxic behavior may be associated with the redox-active nature of vanadium ions and nanoparticle surface properties [7, 8]. While oxidative stress mechanisms, including reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation, have been reported for vanadium oxide nanoparticles in previous studies, no direct ROS detection or apoptosis assays were performed in the present work [26]. Therefore, any mechanistic interpretation related to ROS or mitochondrial pathways is inferred from existing literature rather than experimentally confirmed [7, 8, 19]. Structure–activity correlation

To integrate the physicochemical and biological findings, the structure–activity relationship of the synthesized V2O5 nanoparticles is discussed. The observed in vitro biological responses can be correlated with key physicochemical characteristics, including particle size, crystallinity, and surface chemistry. The nanoscale size range (20–80 nm) and crystalline nature are likely to enhance interactions with biomolecules under experimental conditions, while the negative zeta potential contributes to stable dispersion in aqueous media [1, 10, 13].Surface-associated phytochemicals derived from neem extract play a crucial role in nanoparticle stabilization and may influence enzyme–nanoparticle interactions observed during α-amylase inhibition assays [12, 18]. Similarly, the concentration-dependent cytotoxic effects in cancer cell lines can be related to nanoparticle surface properties and the redox-active nature of vanadium species, consistent with mechanisms reported in previous studies [7, 8, 19]. It is important to note that specific mechanistic pathways, including cellular uptake, reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation, and downstream biological responses, were not directly investigated in the present study and are therefore inferred from existing literature [3, 20, 21]. Overall, this analysis provides qualitative insight into the relationship between physicochemical properties and in vitro biological activity of V2O5 nanoparticles, while highlighting the need for further mechanistic and in vivo studies to establish their therapeutic relevance.

Conclusion

The present study demonstrates the successful green and thermally assisted synthesis of vanadium pentoxide nanoparticles (V2O5 NPs) using A. indica (neem) leaf extract as a natural reducing and stabilizing agent, without the need for photochemical activation or toxic reagents. UV–Vis absorption at 365 nm confirmed nanoparticle formation, while FTIR analysis revealed the involvement of hydroxyl, carbonyl, and phenolic groups in reduction and surface stabilization. XRD results confirmed the formation of orthorhombic V2O5 with an average crystallite size of ~ 45 nm, and SEM–TEM analyses indicated quasi-spherical nanoparticles with sizes ranging from 20 to 80 nm. Zeta potential measurements (− 28.6 mV) further demonstrated good colloidal stability associated with phytochemical capping. In vitro biological evaluation showed that the synthesized nanoparticles exhibit α-amylase inhibitory activity (IC50 = 68 µg/mL) and concentration-dependent cytotoxic effects against HeLa and MCF-7 cancer cell lines, while maintaining high viability in normal fibroblast cells. These results indicate dual in vitro biological activity with potential relevance for antidiabetic and anticancer applications. The observed biological responses are discussed in relation to nanoparticle size, surface chemistry, and the redox-active nature of vanadium species; however, specific mechanistic pathways, including reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation and apoptotic signaling, were not directly investigated and are inferred from existing literature. Overall, this work highlights neem-mediated V2O5 nanoparticles as a sustainable and scalable nanomaterial platform for future biomedical studies. Further investigations involving detailed mechanistic analysis, in vivo validation, and safety assessment are required to establish their translational potential.

Novelty statement

This study demonstrates a phytochemical-assisted, thermally driven green synthesis of vanadium pentoxide (V2O5) nanoparticles using A. indica (neem) leaf extract, without the use of external sunlight or chemical reducing agents.

The synthesized V2O5 nanoparticles exhibit dual in vitro biological activity, showing α-amylase inhibitory effects and concentration-dependent cytotoxic responses against cancer cell lines under experimental conditions.

A literature-supported mechanistic framework is discussed to explain the role of neem-derived phytochemicals (such as flavonoids and polyphenols) in nanoparticle formation, surface stabilization, and potential structure–activity relationships, without direct experimental validation of molecular pathways.

The synthesis approach is environmentally benign, cost-effective, and scalable under mild laboratory conditions, highlighting its potential as a sustainable route for producing functional metal oxide nanomaterials.

Eco-friendly and Non-toxic Process

Unlike conventional chemical syntheses that require hazardous reducing agents (e.g., hydrazine, sodium borohydride, or citrates) and high-temperature calcination, the current method employs only aqueous neem leaf extract under mild thermal conditions (80 °C), eliminating environmental and safety concerns.

Dual Biological FunctionalityWhile most green-synthesized vanadium oxide nanoparticles reported to date focus on either antioxidant or antibacterial properties [1–3], this study uniquely demonstrates dual biomedical efficacy—significant α-amylase inhibition (IC50 = 68 µg/mL) and selective anticancer activity (viability < 40%)—in a single nanomaterial system.

Dual Biological Functionality

While most green-synthesized vanadium oxide nanoparticles reported to date focus on either antioxidant or antibacterial properties [1–3], this study uniquely demonstrates dual biomedical efficacy—significant α-amylase inhibition (IC50 = 68 µg/mL) and selective anticancer activity (viability < 40%)—in a single nanomaterial system.

Controlled Crystallinity and Particle UniformityThe synthesized nanoparticles exhibit orthorhombic crystallinity with a mean crystallite size of ~45 nm and a narrow particle range (20–80 nm), reflecting superior structural control. In contrast, previously reported plant-based V2O5 NPs often displayed broad size distributions (>100 nm) or mixed phases due to uncontrolled nucleation [4, 5].

Controlled Crystallinity and Particle Uniformity

The synthesized nanoparticles exhibit orthorhombic crystallinity with a mean crystallite size of ~45 nm and a narrow particle range (20–80 nm), reflecting superior structural control. In contrast, previously reported plant-based V2O5 NPs often displayed broad size distributions (>100 nm) or mixed phases due to uncontrolled nucleation [4, 5].

Enhanced Stability and Surface FunctionalityThe zeta potential (–28.6 mV) indicates excellent colloidal stability and long shelf-life, attributed to surface-bound phytochemicals. This stability surpasses most previously reported biogenic V2O5 NPs (typically –18 to –24 mV), signifying stronger electrostatic repulsion and better dispersion in biological media [7].

Enhanced Stability and Surface Functionality

The zeta potential (–28.6 mV) indicates excellent colloidal stability and long shelf-life, attributed to surface-bound phytochemicals. This stability surpasses most previously reported biogenic V2O5 NPs (typically –18 to –24 mV), signifying stronger electrostatic repulsion and better dispersion in biological media [7].

Mechanistic InsightThis work provides a detailed discussion of phytochemical-assisted nanoparticle formation and explores potential structure–activity relationships by correlating surface chemistry with enzyme inhibition and in vitro cytotoxic responses. While specific mechanistic pathways, such as reactive oxygen species (ROS) involvement, are inferred based on existing literature rather than directly measured, the analysis offers comparative insight into how surface-functionalized V2O5 nanoparticles may influence in vitro biological behavior, addressing aspects that are less explored in previous studies.

Influence of straight and inclined baffles on enhancement of battery thermal management system performance
Authors: Olanrewaju M. Oyewola, Emmanuel T. Idowu, Mebougna L. Drabo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Battery thermal management systems (BTMSs) are used in electric vehicles (EVs) to regulate the heat generated by batteries while in use. Existing research on the improvement of BTMSs revealed that more research can be done by exploring different strategies to extend the service life and capabilities of EV batteries beyond the current limitations. In this study, effects of orientation of baffles; straight (90°), inclined (60° and 30°), height of baffles, thickness of baffles, positioning of baffles and number of baffles on the performance of conventional Z – Type BTMS were studied using Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) approach. The CFD approach was validated with existing experimental result from literature. Findings from the study showed that for straight baffle, the maximum temperature (Tmax) reduces as the height of baffles decreases. Furthermore, increasing the baffle thickness from 1 mm to 2 mm, produced reduction in Tmax by 0.26 K. For inclined baffles, by comparing the BTMS without baffles and BTMS with 2 baffles, Tmax and maximum temperature difference (ΔTmax) reduced by 0.82 K and 0.68 K, respectively at angle 60°, and reduced by 1.65 K and 1.43 K, respectively at angle 30°. The BTMS with 2 baffles, inclined at angle 60°, with height of 6 mm and thickness of 1 mm, yielded the optimum Tmax (Lowest) value of 333.15 K, a reduction by 2.65 K when compared to the conventional Z – Type BTMS. This was also accompanied with a slight increase in ΔP by 1.12 Pa. In conclusion, it can be said that findings from this study will be beneficial in enhancing the design of BTMSs through adequate selection and utilization of baffles orientation.

Introduction

Lithium-ion batteries are becoming the favourable among other types of battery in electric vehicle (EV) industry. Mileage, power consumption, and cost of EVs have been majorly determined by the performance of lithium batteries [1]. Despite its advantage over other types of battery, more heat is still generated by the lithium-ion battery when in operation, a problem common to other types of battery. Basically, temperature difference between two or more batteries running together in a system must be maintained around 5°C, while best working temperature must be between 25°C and 40°C [2,3]. Non-homogenous temperature difference and high temperature can cause serious damage to the batteries [4]. In order to ameliorate the challenge of non-homogeneity in temperature, researchers proposed different battery thermal management systems, to ensure the temperature of the batteries do not exceed the required range or limit. Over the years, several BTMSs were developed, which can be categorized by the cooling technique as active and passive cooling. In the case of active cooling, the batteries are cooled by blowing air across the batteries [[5], [6], [7]], by using liquid [[8], [9], [10]] or by using heat pipe [3,11,12]. In the case of passive cooling, heat is sucked and offset by phase change materials [[13], [14], [15]]. Other researchers considered combining two different cooling methods; i.e. passive/passive, active/active, or passive/active [[16], [17], [18]]. Studies have also shown that air cooling technique provides lower cost, in terms of development and maintenance which made it more preferable than the other techniques. Although it still has the drawback of susceptibility to high temperature due to the low heat capacity of air. In order to overcome this drawback, some researchers considered changing the design of the structure of the BTMS and/or incorporating other components [19].

Some of the design modifications that have been studied in recent years are; air flow pattern, battery arrangement, inlet/outlet sections, battery configuration, number of inlet and outlet, number of battery, cell spacing distribution, channel inclination, the use of heat sink, inclined plenum and step-like plenum [[19], [20], [21], [22]]. The effects of number of outlets [[23], [24], [25]], and inlet/outlet position [[26], [27], [28], [29]] of the BTMS have been studied. For instance, in the study by Shahid and Agelin-Chaab [23], inlet plenum was used as a secondary inlet to the BTMS, while flow of air was passive to promote temperature homogeneity in the system was investigated. The average maximum temperature of the batteries (Tmax) was reduced by about 4 % while the temperature homogeneity was increased by 39 %. Additionally, Zhang et al. [24] studied the impact of secondary outlets and baffles size on performance of BTMS. The study revealed that, ΔTmax and Tmax decreased by 3.66 °C and 1.84 °C, respectively. Baffles were also fitted to the cooling channel, reducing Tmax by 2.17 °C and ΔTmax by 4.49 °C. However, the result on pressure drop performance of the system was not presented which will likely increase due to the increase in outlets (secondary outlets). Chen et al. [26] investigated the performance of BTMS by changing inlet and outlet positions. When inlet and outlet are positioned at the centre of the plenums it achieved the best cooling performance with Tmax and ΔTmax reduced by 4.5 K and 7.7 K, respectively. Similarly, the effect of the modification on pressure drop was not adequately presented. However, the approach provides flexibility in design to accommodate change in the design of a device or vehicle. Jiaqiang et al. [27] employed using a baffle, while also repositioning the BTMS's inlet and outlet. The positioning of the inlet and outlet on opposing sides yielded best cooling performance. However, detailed study on orientation of the baffles was not considered. Hong et al. [29] considered effects of vent's location and size on performance of a BTMS. By increasing the size of the vent, the thermal performance of the system improved, however it comes with increase ΔP.

In order to explore more possibility of enhancing BTMSs’ performance, some researchers focused on the space distribution among the batteries [20,30] and layout of the batteries [31,32]. For instance, Chen et al. [20] adjusted the spacing among the batteries, thereby improving performance of the system. Additional optimization revealed that significant increase was achieved whereby ΔTmax was reduced by 42 %, without significant increase in ΔP of the system, thereby overcoming the limitations of studies with vents. In another study, an air-cooled BTMS with 36 lithium-ion cells was investigated by Li et al. [30]. It was revealed that high channel spacing size could cause poor temperature homogeneity of the batteries. BTMS having staggered, cross, and aligned arrangement, proposed by Fan et al. [31] revealed that aligned, staggered, and cross layout, exhibited increase in thermal performance. However, design with aligned arrangement was produced energy consumption, of about 23 % lower than cross arrangement. In the same light, Peng et al. [32] studied impact of multiple arrangements of batteries, location of inlet and outlet, and frequency of inlets and outlets. It was discovered that battery layout; 4 by 5 produced the optimum performance with 20 batteries. The influence of inclination of plenum [20,22,33] has also been investigated by some researchers. Numerical studies carried out by Chen et al. [20] investigated impact of cell spacing, and angle of inclination of the inlet and outlet section. The results showed inlet air temperature does not influence thermal performance of the system; however, it rises as discharge rate increases. Optimizing the inclination angles of the two plenums revealed a drop in Tmax and ΔTmax by 2.1 K and 4.3 K, respectively compared to conventional BTMS. The influence of inlet and outlet sections, and angle of inclination were investigated also studied by Oyewola et al. [22]. An improved cooling performance was achieved for the conventional Z – Type by adopting inlet angle of 150° and outlet angle of 120°. The Tmax reduced by 1.6 °C, ΔTmax reduced by 3.7 °C, and power consumption dropped by 29 %. Similarly, by maintaining channel inlet orientation, adopting channel outlet angle of 135°, performance of the U – Type BTMS was enhanced. The Tmax reduced by 0.4 °C, ΔTmax reduced by 1 °C, and power consumption dropped by 11 %.

Studies have also been carried out to give a better understanding of the rate of heat generation [[34], [35], [36]] of batteries by considering inconsistency of the battery cells. Wang et al. [36] suggested that most studies were carried based on the assumption that the rates of heat generation of each of the batteries are the same. They opined that this theory is not possible in real engineering application. Peng et al. [34], conducted experiments to determine equivalent specific heat capacity. It was reported that the different calorific values causes hastened temperature increase and higher temperature difference. In addition, Feng et al. [35] carried out a review of the transmission mechanisms of parameter to further highlight the causes of inconsistency within batteries. Moreover, Wang et al. [36] revealed that proper selection and positioning of extra vents with a reciprocating flow can bring a more uniform temperature distribution, which provide a realistic reference for the design improvement of BTMS. In addition to the modification of the structure of a BTMS, other components, e.g., spoilers [37,38], baffles [24,27], plates [28,39] and heat sink [7,40] have also been investigated. Findings from the study by Zhang et al. [38] revealed that, the number of spoilers and the position of placement significantly influence the BTMS's performance and the best performance of Tmax and ΔTmax reduced by 1.86 K and 2.51 K, respectively. Oyewola et al. [40] coupled a heat sink of varying pin–fin shapes, positioned along the cooling channel of the system. Findings from the study revealed that pin-fins of circular and square shapes, having constant height yielded favourable performance. Wang et al. [28] studied the performance of a BTMS by placing parallel plates along the cooling channel of the system. Findings from the study indicated that the system with two parallel plates produced the best thermal efficiency, and this was within acceptable range of power usage. The length and height of the plates were studied and it was revealed that the optimum performance was when plates with height and length of 30 and 1.5 mm, respectively were adopted.

In recent times, the use of baffle and other structural orientations have also been employed to improve thermal performance in different thermal-related processes [[41], [42], [43]], paving way for more promising techniques that could be employed in the design of thermal management systems (TMSs). For instance, Akcay et al. [41] numerically studied the effects of different baffle arrangements (rectangular, trapezoidal, triangular and circular) on heat transfer enhancement and flow in channels with different wave profiles. The triangular profile with two-perforation baffles had the lowest TPF value, 1.09, and the rectangle profile with a solid baffle had the highest thermal performance factor (TPF) value, 2.02. Akcay [42] carried out numerical analyses to improve the heat transfer of pulsating flow using a periodic corrugated channel with discrete V-type winglets. Over the course of a pulsing cycle over the channel, thermal performance and pressure decrease were recorded. At Reynolds number (Re) = 1000, amplitude (A) = 1.5, and Strouhal number (St) = 8, the highest TPF values for the non-winglet, solid winglet, and perforated winglet designs were determined to be 5.71, 4.68, and 5.17, respectively. Additional studies have also been considered using winglet vortex generators (WVG), primarily to remove heat and understand flow behaviour [[44], [45], [46]]. The study by Wang et al. [44], investigated the thermal-hydraulic performance of flow in a tube with WVG having different holes (triangular, circular, square, rhombus and rectangular). The study indicated that the holes with sharp corners were not beneficial to heat removal enhancement. Furthermore, the maximum TPF was 1.14, recorded for a circular hole at Re of 9090. Wang et al. [45] further investigated the effects of position and perforation parameters of the delta winglet vortex generators in minichannels. The study revealed that as the opening area increases, the shape of both vortices downstream of the VGs is significantly suppressed due to the generation of a jet flow. The hole reduces the heat transfer performance compared to the non-punched case. In another study by Wu et al. [46], the effects of a novel multi-V-winglets vortex generator on heat transfer and fluid flow were investigated. It was discovered that when the number of winglets increased, the heat transfer improved, while friction resistance increased. More so, the highest performance factor estimated was 2.83. The findings from these studies will go a long way in enhancing the cooling performance of BTMS, especially the Z – Type BTMS where inlet and outlet air flow path must be optimized to enhance heat transfer performance of the system.

Some of the studies reviewed, targeted estimation of Nusselt number (Nu), heat transfer coefficient (h) and pressure drop. These parameters are significant in evaluating thermal-hydraulic performance of heat transfer equipment, However, extensive studies have also shown that Tmin, Tmax, ΔTmax and ΔP are also significant parameters, required in assessing the performance of the Z – Type BTMS. Moreover, these parameters can easily be recorded from post processing of the numerical solution. The studies further established that Tmax and ΔTmax are basically influenced by irregular airflow distribution and any modification on the convergence and divergence plenum's design can change the flow and thermal performance, which may consequently lead to improvement in the overall performance of the BTMS. One interesting area that has not been duly explored is the orientation and positioning of baffles on the plenum of the BTMS. In the study by Zhang et al. [38], spoilers having, thickness of 0.1 mm was proposed, while the thickness was assumed to be a non-thickness surface during simulation. Additionally, the arrangement of the spoilers focused on positioning directly under the cooling channel. In this study, the arrangement considered positioning the baffles directly under each battery. Therefore even number of baffles, height of baffles, thickness of baffles and baffle angle of inclination were investigated It is expected that the positioning and orientation of baffles adopted in this study will guide researchers and designers in the development of air-cooled BTMS with improved and more efficient performance for different structural design of the BTMS.

All solutions are time independent and only solutions at steady state were obtained. Simulation boundary conditions

The initial velocity boundary conditions assigned to the BTMS inlet are 3, 3.5 and 4 m/s, while temperature was fixed at 298.15 K. This was done to obtain the required result used for validation of the numerical solution. Subsequent simulations however, adopted air inlet velocity of 3 m/s and the results were used to compare the different cases investigated. For the outlet of the BTMS, pressure outlet was assigned, while taking surrounding pressure as atmospheric pressure. Heat transfer through the thickness of the baffles is considered negligible, since the surrounding wall is adiabatic and the solution is considered at steady state. Hence a cavity is produced on the divergence plenum for each baffle orientation to simplify the meshes. The numerical simulation generally considered the following assumptions [19].●non slip and adiabatic conditions were assigned to the surrounding walls of the BTMS;●non slip condition was assigned to the battery walls;●properties of air and batteries are constant;●environmental temperature and pressure were kept constant;●constant rate of heat generation was assigned on the batteries.

The numerical simulation adopted the SIMPLE algorithm for the solution of governing equations. Discretization of convective and diffusive terms was performed using the central-differencing and second-order terms. Finally, the iteration residuals for convergence criteria were set at 10−10 and 10−7 for the energy and flow terms, respectively.

Meshing and grid independence study

The quality of meshes generated in this study were judged based on the following mesh quality metrics embedded in ANSYS meshing platform, i.e. element quality, skewness and orthogonal quality. For excellent mesh, average element quality values between 0.8 and 1, average skewness values between 0 and 0.25 and average orthogonal values between 0.95 and 1 were maintained. In order to achieve solution-independent grid, an independence study was carried out, while setting inflation layer to five (5) layers. The height of first layer (y+) was set to 0. 1 mm at walls of the system and batteries [19]. A quadratic element order was selected, with element sizes ranging from 0.0006 to 0.00025 at intervals of 0.00005, as shown in Table 3. The Tmin and ΔTmax of the reference BTMS were measured to evaluate the sensitivity of the meshes. Simulation model validation

The numerical simulation procedure was validated by comparing the results obtained in this study with experimental results, for the Z-Type reference geometry. The parameters used for the comparison are the temperatures of the batteries at air inlet velocities of 3, 3.5 and 4 m/s. The temperatures measured are at the second, T2(Tmax) and eighth, T8(Tmin) from the eight batteries in the system. The deviations between the maximum and minimum temperature, ΔTmax=Tmax−Tmin were also estimated, known as the maximum temperature difference. In this study, the pressure drop across the systems’ ΔP, is the difference between the pressure at the inlet section and maximum pressure in the BTMS. Results and discussion

Influence of straight baffles on BTMS

Straight baffles were installed on the divergence plenum of the BTMS to investigate their effect on the system's cooling performance. The performance was investigated by comparing the Tmax, ΔTmax and ΔP. The straight baffles are considered to be at an angle of 90° to the floor of the divergence plenum. Additional investigations on the straight baffles considered the effects of number, height and thickness of the baffles.

Effect of number of baffles

The effects of number of baffles; eight (8), four (4) and two (2), at a fixed baffle height of 10 mm and baffle thickness of 3 mm for air inlet velocity of 3 m/s were investigated. These baffles were primarily investigated to understand how they influence flow uniformity and thermal homogeneity in the system. Fig. 8 shows the thermal performance in terms of Tmax and ΔTmax, while Fig. 9 shows the ΔP across the BTMS. It should be noted that the system without baffles is denoted ‘0’. From Fig. 8, it was observed that for all the number of baffles investigated, the performance of the BTMS does not improve. However, the systems with two and four baffles yielded better performance than the system with eight baffles. The performance indicates that improved performance can be achieved with proper selection or arrangement of baffles. From the results, Tmax and ΔTmax were observed to have reduced by 6.52 K and 2.78 K, respectively when eight baffles were replaced by two baffles. However, the reduction in Tmax and ΔTmax does not follow a consistent trend. As seen in Fig. 8, the system with four baffles performed better than the system with two baffles, but by increasing the number of baffles to eight, the performance significantly depreciated.Fig. 8Comparison of Tmax and ΔTmax for the cases with and without baffles.Fig. 8Fig. 9Estimated values of ΔP for the cases with and without baffles.Fig. 9

Comparison of Tmax and ΔTmax for the cases with and without baffles.

Estimated values of ΔP for the cases with and without baffles.

In Fig. 9, ΔP generally increases with increase in the number of baffles. More pressure is needed to overcome the restriction of flow, which consequently amount to increase in power consumption. Hence, the system with eight baffles produced the highest value of ΔP. The thermal and pressure drop performance of installing eight, four and two baffles yielded poor outcomes when compared to the BTMS without baffles. However, there are still prospects of improving the performance of the system using baffles, as can be seen from the comparison between number of baffles in Fig. 10, where baffles clearly influenced the performance of the systems. Both the four and two baffles cases produced Tmax lower than eight baffles, while the two baffles tend to gradually produce thermal homogeneity. Moreover, the eight baffles are closer to each other and the height of the baffles gives little flow space for the cooling air which led to the poor performance. Effect of height of baffles

In order to further understand the performance of the straight baffles, the height of the baffles were reduced from the initial 10 mm–6 mm and 3 mm, to provide more space for volume of cooling air passing between the baffles and batteries. The performance of the system was first investigated for eight (8) baffles with 3 mm thickness, at air inlet velocity of 3 m/s, and the result presented in Fig. 12. Deductions from the results reveal that Tmax reduces as the height of baffles decreases from 10 mm to 6 mm and further to 3 mm. However, only the design with baffle height of 3 mm produced Tmax lower than the system without baffle, with a reduction of 0.75 K, while the ΔP increased by 0.77 Pa. By comparing the ΔP estimated for the height of 10 mm and 6 mm, with 3 mm, increase of 16.13 Pa and 4.38 Pa, respectively were obtained. These clearly revealed that the change in the height of the baffles influenced performance of the BTMS.Fig. 12Comparison of Tmax and ΔP with different baffle height.Fig. 12

Comparison of Tmax and ΔP with different baffle height.

Effect of thickness of baffles

When considering weight reduction, reducing the thickness of the baffles also becomes significant, hence the effect of baffle thickness was investigated. The thickness of baffle was investigated for the case with eight baffles and 10 mm height, at air inlet velocity of 3 m/s, and the result presented in Fig. 13. The effect of baffles on the performance of the system revealed that increasing the baffle thickness from 1 mm to 2 mm produced reduction in the Tmax by 0.26 K. However by further increasing the thickness to 3 mm, Tmax increased significantly by 2.79 K. For the ΔP, the values kept reducing with increase in baffle thickness, i.e. 39.40 Pa and 38.21 Pa, for thickness of 1 mm and 3 mm, respectively. It should also be noted that both Tmax and ΔP increased when either of the baffles thicknesses is compared with the initial design without baffles, denoted “0”. However, this performance is limited to a combination of eight baffles and 10 mm height of baffles.Fig. 13Comparison of Tmax and ΔP with different baffle thickness.Fig. 13

Comparison of Tmax and ΔP with different baffle thickness.

Influence of inclined baffles

Inclination of the baffles were also investigated considering angles of 60° and 30°. The investigation also considered comparison of Tmax, ΔTmax and ΔP. Similar to the straight baffles, further investigations were carried out considering effects of number, height and thickness of the baffles at air inlet velocity of 3 m/s.

Effect of number of baffles

Fig. 14, Fig. 15 show the thermal performance in terms of Tmax and ΔTmax, and ΔP across the BTMS, respectively. From Fig. 14, for baffles inclined at 60°, it was observed that the performance of the BTMS improved (i.e. reduction in Tmax) when two baffles were installed on the divergent plenum, with similar performance being observed for baffles inclined at 30°. However, for the four and eight baffles, Tmax and ΔTmax, kept increasing for 60° inclination. For 30°, both Tmax and ΔTmax reduced when eight baffles were installed, although the values were still greater than the system with two baffles. By comparing the two angles, 30° performed better than 60°, irrespective of the number of baffles. By comparing the performance of the system without baffles and with two baffles, the reduction in Tmax and ΔTmax were 0.82 K and 0.68 K, respectively for angle 60°, and 1.65 K and 1.43 K for angle 30°, respectively. The ΔP performance for the number of baffles investigated were not favourable as can be seen in Fig. 15, where ΔP increases with increase in number of baffles for the two inclination angles. The ΔP performance with two baffles, which is obviously the least among the other number of baffles, increased by 3.75 Pa for inclination angle 60°, while at angle 30°, increased by 0.74 Pa. Similar to the straight baffles, both the four and two baffles cases were investigated for inclined baffles. The thermal performance of the BTMS as shown in Fig. 16, where Tmax produced were all lower than the eight baffles system, while the two baffles tends to gradually produce thermal homogeneity. Unlike the straight baffle where the two baffle could not produce a reduction in Tmax when compared to the Z-type BTMS without baffle, inclined baffle yielded significant reduction at angle 30°, with Tmax, reduced by 1.97 K. Conclusions

In this study, baffles were installed on the divergence plenum of conventional Z – Type BTMS. The study employed computational fluid dynamics (CFD) approach to investigate different orientation of the baffles. The CFD approach was validated by comparing results using the approach with results obtained from experimental study in literature, for the conventional Z – Type BTMS. The CFD approach was then employed for the Z – Type BTMS installed with the considered baffle orientations (number of baffle, height of baffles, thickness of baffles, positioning of baffles and inclination of the baffles) to investigate their effect on the temperature and pressure drop performances. Based on the findings from the study, the following conclusions were made.1.The Tmax value reduces as the height of baffles was reduced from 10 mm to 6 mm and further to 3 mm. However, only the 3 mm height performed better than the BTMS without baffles, with Tmax reducing by 0.75 K, while ΔP increased by 0.77 Pa. Increasing the baffle thickness from 1 mm to 2 mm, produced reduction in Tmax by 0.26 K. With an additional increase to 3 mm, Tmax increased significantly by 2.79 K.2.For inclined baffles, by comparing the BTMS without baffles and BTMS with 2 baffles, Tmax and ΔTmax reduced by 0.82 K and 0.68 K, respectively at angle 60°, and reduced by 1.65 K and 1.43 K, respectively at angle 30°. Furthermore, the ΔP performance with two baffles, increased by 3.75 Pa for angle 60°, while at angle 30°, it increased by 0.74 Pa.3.Considering the combined performance of the baffle orientations, the BTMS with 2 baffles, inclined at an angle of 60°, with height of 6 mm and thickness of 1 mm, yielded the minimum Tmax values of 333.15 K. A reduction of 2.65 K when compared to the reference Z – type BTMS. This was also accompanied with a slight increase in ΔP by 1.12 Pa.

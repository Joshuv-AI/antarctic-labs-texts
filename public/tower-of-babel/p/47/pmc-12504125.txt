Restorative Management of Rotated Maxillary Anterior Teeth: A Conservative Alternative to Orthodontic Treatment
Authors: Manish Rathi, Saravanan Sampoornam Pape Reddy, Delfin Lovelina Francis
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIn this report, we describe a case of a rotated maxillary right central incisor in a 32‐year‐old woman. The patient condition was restored after avoiding orthodontic treatment, which included essential endodontic treatment, core build‐up, adjusting tooth inclination, and restoration with a full ceramic crown. Better microesthetics and high patient satisfaction were observed after this treatment.Keywords: endodontics, esthetics, malpositioning, orthodontic, restorative

A literature review in esthetic dentistry suggests that rotated anterior teeth can be corrected conservatively with orthodontics. For adults who are not inclined to pursue orthodontic therapy, various alternative treatment options have been explored. Direct composite resin restorations with gingival recontouring are a successful treatment alternative [1]. This technique has proven to be an easy, reversible, and economical solution to correct esthetics in anterior teeth, including rotations. Clinicians are able to create esthetic harmony between hard and soft tissues by precisely contouring the composite resin with the gingival margin without the need for orthodontic intervention [2]. Interestingly, when considering esthetic corrections, it is important to note that the concept of the “golden proportion” is not universally applicable in natural dentition. It has been found only in 14%–25% of subjects with natural teeth. Rather, the “golden percentage” theory is found to be more consistent for maxillary anterior teeth when viewed frontally [3].

The present case involved a 32‐year‐old female patient who presented with a rotated maxillary right central incisor, specifically tooth #8 (Figure 1). The remaining dentition was relatively well aligned. The patient was initially advised to undergo orthodontic correction to remove the affected tooth. However, she opted for orthodontic treatment. Following a thorough analysis of the mesiodistal dimensions of the space and considering the golden proportion, the patient was advised to pursue restorative correction. To prepare a full ceramic crown on tooth #8, significant tooth material needed to be removed to correct the angulation. Consequently, a careful decision was made to perform vital endodontic therapy. Upon completion of non‐surgical endodontic treatment, a core build‐up was performed to correct the mesiodistal and buccolingual inclinations (Figure 2). Subsequently, tooth #8 was restored with a full ceramic crown, which addressed the micro‐esthetic concerns and had a significant impact on patient‐reported outcome measures in terms of treatment satisfaction (Figure 3).

Therefore, this approach offers a simple, predictable, faster, and cost‐effective alternative to orthodontic intervention. Non‐orthodontic esthetic correction of rotated anterior teeth can be effectively accomplished through restorative techniques, including composite resin restorations, partial or full coverage restorations, and gingival recontouring. In planning such treatments, clinicians should consider individual facial characteristics and the natural diversity of tooth proportions, rather than strictly adhering to mathematical ratios such as the golden proportion.

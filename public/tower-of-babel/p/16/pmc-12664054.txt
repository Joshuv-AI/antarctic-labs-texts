NF-κB and pulmonary hypertension: Advances in mechanistic research and therapeutic applications
Authors: Menghan Si, Ye Hu, Zichong Jin, Yaqi Mao, Longli Kang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPulmonary hypertension (PH) is a severe cardiovascular disorder characterized by pulmonary arterial smooth muscle cells (PASMCs) proliferation and vascular remodeling. The nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) family of transcription factors serves as a central mediator of inflammatory responses and plays a critical role in both innate and adaptive immunity. In recent years, the involvement of NF-κB signaling in PH pathogenesis has attracted growing interest. Accumulating evidence indicates that NF-κB contributes to pulmonary vascular remodeling and right ventricular (RV) dysfunction by modulating inflammatory processes, cell proliferation, and apoptosis. This review systematically summarizes the molecular mechanisms by which NF-κB contributes to PH, emphasizing its cell-specific roles in PASMCs and pulmonary arterial endothelial cells (PAECs), and evaluates the therapeutic potential of NF-κB as a target in PH.Keywords: Nuclear factor-kappa B, Pulmonary hypertension, Pulmonary vascular remodeling

Introduction

Pulmonary hypertension (PH) is a severe cardiovascular disorder characterized by progressively increased pulmonary vascular resistance leading to right heart failure. A defining feature of PH is the abnormal elevation of pulmonary arterial pressure. The pathogenesis involves pathological processes such as excessive proliferation of pulmonary arterial smooth muscle cells (PASMCs), dysfunction of pulmonary arterial endothelial cells (PAECs), and sustained chronic inflammation [1,2]. According to current diagnostic criteria, PH is defined as a mean pulmonary arterial pressure (mPAP) exceeding 20 mmHg at rest. The condition is classified into five clinical groups: pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH), PH due to left heart disease, PH associated with lung diseases and/or hypoxia, PH caused by pulmonary artery obstructions, and PH with unclear or multifactorial mechanisms. PH represents a significant global health burden, affecting approximately 1 % of the world's population, with prevalence increasing to about 10 % among individuals aged over 65 years [3]. Despite advances in understanding its pathophysiology and treatment, the molecular mechanisms underlying PH remain incompletely elucidated, and therapeutic options remain limited.

Nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB), a pivotal transcription factor, plays a central role in regulating inflammation, immune responses, cell proliferation, and apoptosis. In human PAH, NF-κB activation has been convincingly demonstrated through immunohistochemical and confocal microscopy analyses revealing nuclear localization of NF-κB subunits in pulmonary vascular endothelial and smooth muscle cells within remodeled vessels, providing direct spatial evidence of pathway activation in human disease [4,5]. Experimental studies indicate that chronic normobaric hypoxia activates NF-κB and upregulates pulmonary inflammatory mediators. Importantly, NF-κB inhibition has been shown to ameliorate PH, reduce right ventricular (RV) hypertrophy, and improve RV function in rodent models [6]. Furthermore, in severe obstructive PH, NF-κB has been identified as a central regulator driving pulmonary artery occlusion, inflammatory responses, RV dysfunction, and dysregulated immune processes [7].

Therefore, elucidating the regulatory mechanisms of NF-κB in PH not only enhances our understanding of disease pathogenesis but also provides a rational foundation for developing novel therapeutic strategies. This review offers a unique and updated synthesis by: (1) mapping NF-κB's cell-specific mechanistic contributions-spanning PASMCs, PAECs, macrophages, chronic inflammation, and RV remodeling within an integrated framework; (2) presenting a comprehensive landscape of therapeutic agents targeting NF-κB, including natural compounds, chemotherapeutic drugs, and Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) formulations; and (3) critically evaluating current knowledge to discuss both the compelling therapeutic potential and significant translational challenges. This article aims to synthesize recent advances in NF-κB research in PH, providing valuable insights and proposing new directions for future research and treatment.

Structure and function of NF-κB

Overview of NF-κB functions

NF-κB family consists of ubiquitously expressed transcription factors that play central roles in eukaryotic organisms. These regulators are involved in a wide range of biological processes, including inflammation, innate and adaptive immunity, cell development, survival, proliferation, and apoptosis. NF-κB acts as a key mediator in multiple signaling pathways and responds to diverse stimuli such as inflammatory signals via tumor necrosis factor receptors (TNFR) and Toll-like receptors (TLRs), as well as metabolic and genotoxic stress. Mouse models with mutations in NF-κB signaling components exhibit various abnormalities, including impaired immune system development, apoptotic dysregulation, and aberrant immune responses. In humans, dysregulated NF-κB signaling is closely associated with chronic inflammatory diseases and cancer, underscoring its essential role in maintaining cellular homeostasis and responding to pathological challenges.

Composition of the NF-κB family

In mammals, the NF-κB family comprises five members encoded by the genes RELA, RELB, CREL, NFKB1, and NFKB2, corresponding to the proteins RelA (p65), RelB, c-Rel, p50, and p52. These proteins function as homodimers or heterodimers, with common configurations including p50/p65, p50/c-Rel, and p52/RelB. Each member contains a highly conserved Rel homology domain (RHD), which facilitates DNA binding, dimerization, and regulation of downstream target genes [8].

Regulation of NF-κB activity

NF-κB activity is tightly controlled by inhibitory proteins known as IκBs. The IκB family includes eight members-such as IκBα, IκBβ, and IκBε-that bind to the RHD of NF-κB dimers, sequestering them in the cytoplasm and preventing transcriptional activation [9].

NF-κB activation proceeds through two principal pathways: the canonical and non-canonical. The canonical pathway is induced by TLRs and proinflammatory cytokines like TNFα and IL-1, leading to activation of RelA-containing dimers that drive expression of proinflammatory and cell survival genes. The non-canonical pathway is activated by ligands such as lymphotoxin β, CD40 ligand, BAFF, and RANKL, resulting in RelB/p52 complex formation and regulation of genes essential for lymph-organogenesis and B-cell activation. Each pathway employs distinct IKK subunits: the canonical pathway relies on IKKβ and IKKγ, whereas the non-canonical pathway depends solely on IKKα and functions independently of IKKβ and IKKγ [10].

Biological effects of NF-κB activation

Upon cellular stimulation, IκB proteins undergo degradation, releasing NF-κB dimers (e.g., p65/p50) for nuclear translocation. Within the nucleus, these dimers bind κB sites in DNA to regulate the expression of target genes, including those encoding inflammatory factors, cytokines, and chemokines, thereby mediating broad biological effects [11].

In summary, NF-κB is a critical regulator of cellular responses to external signals, with precise control exerted through IκB-mediated inhibition. Dysregulation of this system contributes significantly to pathologies such as chronic inflammation and cancer.

NF-κB and PH

NF-κB activation in human PAH

In lung tissues from patients with end-stage idiopathic PAH (n = 12), NF-κB activation has been detected within pulmonary macrophages, lymphocytes, PAECs and PASMCs. This activation contributes directly to pulmonary vascular remodeling-a hallmark feature of PH-characterized by excessive PASMC proliferation and migration, apoptotic-resistant PAEC overgrowth, macrophage accumulation, and persistent chronic inflammation [4]. These pathological alterations are closely linked to sustained activation of the NF-κB signaling pathway.

Role of NF-κB in pathogenesis of PH

The NF-κB signaling pathway serves as a central molecular hub coordinating multiple pathological processes in PH. It promotes disease progression through five core mechanisms: (1) stimulating PASMC proliferation and migration; (2) inducing PAEC apoptosis and endothelial-mesenchymal transition (EndMT); (3) driving macrophage polarization and pro-inflammatory cytokine production; (4) sustaining local and systemic chronic inflammation; and (5) facilitating RV remodeling.

NF-κB mechanism in PASMCs

NF-κB activation mediates PASMCs proliferation

Aberrant proliferation and migration of PASMCs are central to pulmonary vascular remodeling in PH, with NF-κB signaling playing a critical role. Studies have shown that nucleotide-binding oligomerization domain-like receptor C3 (NLRC3) is significantly downregulated in lung tissues from patients with high-altitude pulmonary hypertension (HAPH) and in hypoxia-induced PH mice (n = 6 per group). NLRC3 downregulation activates the IKK/NF-κB p65/HIF-1α signaling axis, driving hypoxia-induced PASMC proliferation and migration, as well as PAEC apoptosis and migration [5]. Additionally, nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase (NAMPT) promotes platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF)-induced PASMC proliferation by activating TLR4/NF-κB-mediated upregulation of polo-like kinase 4 (PLK4) [12].

The histone demethylase JARID1B is upregulated in PASMCs of mice with chronic hypoxia-induced PH (n = 8 per group) and binds directly to the NF-κB promoter to enhance its expression. This leads to vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A) upregulation, which promotes PASMC proliferation and migration [13]. Furthermore, NF-κB p65 signaling directly regulates the microRNA-17 (miR-17) promoter, increasing miR-17 expression in PASMCs. This process inhibits retinoblastoma (RB) protein function, stimulates PASMC proliferation, promotes G1/S phase transition, and upregulates proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA) and E2F1 expression [14].

Hypoxia activates the Akt/mammalian target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1) signaling pathway, which induces abnormal vascular injury responses. mTORC1 phosphorylates IKKα/β, mediating crosstalk between the Akt/mTORC1 and NF-κB pathways and enhancing NF-κB transcriptional activity. This results in upregulated dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP4) expression in PASMCs and promotes vascular remodeling (n = 5–8 per group) [15]. The Rieske iron‑sulfur protein (RISP) stimulates PASMC proliferation and pulmonary arterial remodeling via the NF-κB/cyclin D1 signaling pathway, inducing dissociation of the FK506 binding protein 12.6/ryanodine receptor 2 (FKBP12.6/RyR2) complex and contributing to sustained PH (n = 4–6 independent studies; 5 mice per study) [16]. Moreover, hypoxia upregulates myelin and lymphocyte protein (MAL), which activates the NF-κB pathway and promotes excessive proliferation and anti-apoptosis in human PASMCs in vitro [17].

NF-κB blockade attenuates PASMCs proliferation

Zinc finger protein A20 exerts a protective effect by downregulating NF-κB activity (n = 10–12 per group), thereby inhibiting excessive PASMC proliferation and pulmonary vascular remodeling [18]. Similarly, thymoquinone (TQ) attenuates monocrotaline (MCT)-induced pulmonary artery remodeling and PASMC proliferation through inhibition of p38 MAPK and NF-κB activation (n = 6–8 per group) [19]. Furthermore, in lupus-associated PAH, high mobility group box 1 (HMGB1) suppresses hypoxia-induced PASMC proliferation, migration, and collagen synthesis via the TLR4/NF-κB pathway, highlighting the role of NF-κB in autoimmune forms of PAH [20].

NF-κB mechanism in PAECs

NF-κB activation mediates PAEC dysfunction

NF-κB plays a pivotal role in PH by regulating gene expression in critical pathways such as the bone morphogenetic protein receptor 2-inhibitor of differentiation-Notch-3 axis (BMPR2-Id-Notch-3 axis), promoting apoptosis and EndMT in PAECs. This process contributes to MCT-induced PH and RV hypertrophy in mice (n = 3–6 per group) [21].

Under NF-κB regulation, miR-130a triggers EndMT and apoptosis in pulmonary microvascular endothelial cells upon overexpression, further exacerbating PH progression (n = 5 per group) [22]. Complementarily, cerebellin-2 (CBLN2) activates the NF-κB/HIF-1α/Twist1 pathway, driving hypoxia-induced EndMT in PH rats (n = 6–8 per group) and underscoring the critical role of NF-κB in this process [23]. Bromodomain and extra-terminal (BET) proteins significantly inhibit the proliferation, migration, and inflammatory responses of human pulmonary microvascular endothelial cells by suppressing NF-κB (n = 4–6 per group) [24]. Furthermore, Cullin5 induces PAEC adhesion and angiogenesis through the TRAF6/NF-κB/HIF-1α/VEGF pathway, elucidating the multifaceted regulatory network of NF-κB in PAEC-mediated vascular remodeling [25].

NF-κB blockade attenuates PAEC dysfunction

Studies demonstrate that the p65 subunit of NF-κB is essential in PAECs for hypoxia-induced vascular remodeling and PH development (n = 3–4 per group). Targeted knockdown of p65 in human PAECs alleviates endothelial dysfunction, reduces vasoconstriction, suppresses PASMC proliferation, and mitigates inflammation-driven processes, highlighting the central regulatory role of NF-κB in PAEC pathology [26].

Therapeutic strategies targeting NF-κB show promise in ameliorating PAEC dysfunction. NF-κB inhibitors significantly reduce lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced late apoptosis in PAECs (n = 3 per group), indicating a protective role in maintaining endothelial integrity [27]. Similarly, A20 inhibits PAEC proliferation and angiogenesis by regulating NF-κB p65 translocation and IκB-α degradation (n = 4 per group), offering further mechanistic insight [28].

NF-κB also interacts with other signaling pathways to modulate PAEC function. Protein tyrosine phosphatase L1 (PTPL1) inhibits NF-κB/Snail signaling through dephosphorylation and stabilization of IκBα (n = 8 per group), effectively suppressing EndMT [29].

NF-κB and pulmonary macrophages

Role of macrophages in pulmonary vascular remodeling

Macrophages serve as fundamental mediators and coordinators of chronic inflammation and play a central role in PAH, particularly in driving pulmonary vascular remodeling. In response to microenvironmental cues-such as hypoxia, inflammation, or toxin exposure-macrophages polarize into pro-inflammatory M1 or anti-inflammatory M2 phenotypes, each exerting distinct biological effects that contribute to vascular pathology in PAH [30].

NF-κB Activation mediates M1 macrophage polarization

Studies using group sizes of n = 3 have demonstrated that the NF-κB/MAPK signaling pathway plays a critical role in LPS-induced M1 polarization of macrophages [31]. NF-κB activation promotes the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines, which further amplifies macrophage accumulation and activation in the pulmonary vasculature, significantly driving PH progression and vascular remodeling.

NF-κB suppression attenuates M1 macrophage polarization

Bruton's tyrosine kinase (BTK) is a key regulator of macrophage activation and polarization. In PH rat models (n = 6–8 per group), BTK inhibitors significantly reduce M1 macrophage polarization and the production of M1-related inflammatory cytokines—such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6—by suppressing the activation of both the NLRP3 inflammasome and the NF-κB/MAPK signaling pathway. This inhibition attenuates EndMT and pulmonary vascular remodeling, highlighting the therapeutic potential of targeting NF-κB in macrophage-driven inflammation in PH [32].

In summary, NF-κB serves as a central regulator of macrophage polarization and inflammation, significantly amplifying their contribution to pulmonary vascular remodeling and disease progression in PH. Therapeutic strategies aimed at modulating NF-κB activity in macrophages, including the use of BTK inhibitors, represent promising intervention avenues.

Furthermore, while macrophage-driven inflammation is pivotal in PH, sustained NF-κB activation also perpetuates broader chronic inflammatory processes that exacerbate pulmonary vascular remodeling, highlighting the multifaceted role of NF-κB across different cellular and molecular tiers of PH pathogenesis.

NF-κB and chronic inflammation

Chronic inflammation is a significant driver of pathological progression in PH, with NF-κB serving as a key mediator of this process. Multiple therapeutic strategies targeting NF-κB have demonstrated efficacy in attenuating inflammatory responses.

In rat models of PH (n = 8 per group), hepatocyte growth factor (HGF) upregulates IκBα expression while downregulating NF-κB p65, thereby inhibiting NF-κB activation. This reduces pulmonary inflammatory infiltration and serum levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines, effectively alleviating inflammatory responses [33]. Similarly, hepcidin treatment significantly reduces NF-κB expression and activity, inhibits the NF-κB/TNF-α pathway, and lowers serum levels of inflammatory factors—including TGF-β, TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 (n = 8 per group)—mitigating PH-induced lung tissue pathology [34]. At the molecular level, budesonide/glycopyrronium/formoterol fumarate (BGF) exerts anti-inflammatory effects by inhibiting NF-κB p65 phosphorylation and reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine mRNA expression in alveolar epithelial cells and PAECs (n = 3–9 mice per group) [35].

These findings underscore the pivotal role of NF-κB in driving chronic inflammation in PH and provide a rationale for developing anti-inflammatory therapies targeting this pathway.

NF-κB and RV remodeling

RV remodeling is a critical pathological feature of PAH progression, characterized by inflammation, fibrosis, cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, and fibroblast activation [36]. Initially, the RV adapts to increased afterload through enhanced contractility (homeometric adaptation) to maintain coupling with the pulmonary circulation. Once this mechanism is exhausted, the RV dilates via heterometric adaptation to preserve cardiac output—a transition closely associated with worsening right ventricular systolic pressure (RVSP) and right ventricular hypertrophy index (RVHI) [37].

NF-κB activation mediates RV remodeling

Studies using n = 5–8 mice per group have shown that under hypoxic conditions, NF-κB transcriptionally upregulates miR-335-3p, which binds to the 3′-UTR of APJ mRNA and inhibits its expression. This disruption of the apelin-APJ axis contributes to pulmonary artery remodeling, increased RVSP, and elevated RVHI, representing a major mechanism in PH pathogenesis [38].

NF-κB blockade attenuates RV remodeling

Activation of AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) inhibits NF-κB p65 nuclear translocation, suppresses autophagy, and prevents MCT-induced PH in rats (n = 4–6 per group) [39]. The NF-κB inhibitor pyrrolidine dithiocarbamate (PDTC) significantly reduces NF-κB expression while lowering RVSP, mPAP, and RVHI, thereby improving pulmonary vascular remodeling (n = 6 per group) [40]. Furthermore, exosomes derived from human umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cells (hUC-MSC-EXO) attenuate MCT-induced PH and RV hypertrophy by inhibiting NF-κB signaling and activating the BMP pathway in a rat model (n = 10 per group) [41].

NF-κB as a therapeutic target for PH

Significant progress has been made in developing drugs that target the NF-κB pathway to alleviate PH, particularly under hypoxic conditions. These advances reflect growing understanding of the role of NF-κB in regulating PH pathogenesis and offer promising therapeutic avenues. Since the discovery of its regulatory mechanisms, drug development focused on NF-κB inhibition has continued to evolve.

This section discusses the therapeutic potential of NF-κB-targeting strategies, emphasizing natural products and their multi-faceted mechanisms of action.

Natural products as NF-κB modulators

Natural products have attracted increasing attention for their potential in treating PH through modulation of the NF-κB signaling pathway, owing to their diverse chemical compositions and multi-target effects.

Anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory agents

Several natural products ameliorate PH by modulating NF-κB-driven inflammation and immune activation. Matrine inhibits NF-κB signaling, rebalancing proliferation and apoptosis in PASMCs and attenuating hypoxic vascular remodeling [42]. Alginate oligosaccharides (AOS) suppress the p38 MAPK/NF-κB axis, reducing IκBα degradation and NF-κB nuclear translocation, which mitigates perivascular inflammation and improves hemodynamics in MCT-induced PH [43]. Resveratrol and its derivative trimethoxystilbene (TMS) target sphingosine kinase 1 (SphK1) to inhibit NF-κB activation and downregulate cyclin D1, thereby reducing arterial remodeling and pro-inflammatory cytokine levels [44,45]. Similarly, 18β-glycyrrhetinic acid (18β-GA) blocks the PERK/eIF2α/NF-κB pathway, improving hemodynamics and reversing vascular remodeling while reducing endoplasmic reticulum stress and inflammation [46]. Forsythoside B (FTS•B) alleviates vascular remodeling and improves survival via NF-κB inhibition [47]. Additionally, celastrol and paeoniflorin (PF) suppress NF-κB activation, attenuating RV and pulmonary vascular remodeling, hypoxia-induced PASMC proliferation, and EndMT [48,49].

Antioxidant and Nrf2-Keap1 pathway activators

Certain natural compounds counteract NF-κB-mediated oxidative stress in PH. Salidroside (SAL) suppresses aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) and NF-κB activation while promoting nuclear factor erythroid-2-related factor 2 (Nrf2) nuclear translocation. This enhances expression of antioxidant proteins heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1) and NAD(P)H quinone oxidoreductase 1 (NQO1), reducing oxidative stress in PAECs, lowering RVSP, and attenuating vascular and RV remodeling [50]. Similarly, andrographolide (ANDRO) modulates both NOX/Nrf2-mediated oxidative stress and NF-κB-driven inflammation, collectively reversing pulmonary vascular pathology [51].

Novel formulations and emerging compounds

Emerging natural-derived agents target NF-κB through novel mechanisms. DYZY01, a cannabidiol-based formulation, inhibits the NF-κB/NLRP3/caspase-1 pathway, reducing endothelial pyroptosis, improving hemodynamics (reducing mPAP and RVHI), and reversing vascular remodeling [52]. Corosolic acid (CRA) attenuates PDGF-PDGFRβ-NF-κB signaling in macrophages and PASMCs, ameliorating vascular remodeling [53]. Additionally, magnesium lithospermate B (MLB) blocks NF-κB activation and suppresses RhoA/ROCK signaling, inhibiting EndMT and improving hypobaric hypoxia-induced PH [54].

Chemotherapeutic agents targeting NF-κB for PH treatment

Chemotherapeutic agents that modulate NF-κB signaling have revealed the therapeutic potential of this pathway in preclinical models of PH. These compounds exert protective effects primarily through anti-inflammatory, anti-proliferative, and antioxidant mechanisms.

Sevoflurane, a modern anesthetic, suppresses phosphorylation of IκB and p65, inhibits NF-κB activation, and subsequently reduces PASMC proliferation and pulmonary fibrosis in rat models (n = 10 per group) [55]. Similarly, atorvastatin inhibits hypoxia-induced PH by suppressing the RhoA/Rho kinase/NF-κB axis (n = 8 per group) [56]. Urolithin A (UA), a gut microbiota-derived metabolite, attenuates hypoxia-induced pyroptosis in PASMCs via the AMPK/NF-κB/NLRP3 pathway, effectively mitigating PH progression in mice (n = 10 per group) [57]. DL-4-chlorophenylalanine (PCPA) downregulates nuclear factor of activated T cells-1 (NFAT-1) and NF-κB signaling, reducing MCT-induced inflammation and vascular remodeling in PH rats (n = 5 per group) [58]. Fasudil, the only clinically used ROCK inhibitor, alleviates LPS-induced inflammation and oxidative damage in rat pulmonary microvascular endothelial cells through the ROS-NF-κB pathway [59]. Additionally, the proteasome inhibitor PS-341 inhibits NF-κB activation by stabilizing IκB-α, attenuating high-flow-induced pulmonary arterial remodeling in rats (n = 15 per group) [60].

While these agents demonstrate efficacy in preclinical models through NF-κB inhibition, further clinical studies are essential to validate their safety and therapeutic potential in human PH patients.

TCM in modulating NF-κB for PH: preclinical insights and challenges

TCM formulations, with their multi-component and multi-target characteristics, have been explored in preclinical research for their potential to regulate the NF-κB signaling pathway. These studies offer diverse leads for understanding potential therapeutic mechanisms in PH.

Srolo Bzhtang (SBT), a renowned Tibetan medicinal formula composed of Herba Rhodiolae, Bergenia purpurascens, Glycyrrhiza uralensis, and Pueraria lobata, has been shown to reduce inflammation and vascular remodeling in PH rats (n = 12 per group) by inhibiting the MAPK/NF-κB signaling pathway [61].

Feixin Decoction (FXD) is an empirical formula designed to address the core pathogenesis of hypoxia-induced PH, traditionally attributed to lung Qi deficiency leading to fluid retention, phlegm, and blood stasis. Comprising a combination of Si Jun Zi Tang, Tao Hong Si Wu Tang, and Tingli Dazao Xiefei Tang, FXD replenishes Qi, nourishes the lungs, promotes blood circulation, resolves stasis, and eliminates phlegm. Experimental studies demonstrate that FXD alleviates hypoxia-induced PH in mice (n = 10 per group) by modulating the NF-κB/NLRP3 pathway and inhibiting pyroptosis in PASMCs [62].

Dayuanyin (DYY), a classic formula from the Ming Dynasty text Treatise on Pestilence by Wu Youke, traditionally used to treat febrile infectious diseases, consists of seven herbs including Paeonia lactiflora, Areca catechu, Amomum tsao-ko, Glycyrrhiza uralensis, Scutellaria baicalensis, Magnolia officinalis, and Anemarrhena asphodeloides. DYY exerts protective effects on lung function and prevents hypoxia-induced PH in mice (n = 10 per group) by reducing inflammatory factor expression and inhibiting the activation of NF-κB and signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) [63].

Other TCM formulations also contribute to NF-κB modulation:

Mufangji Decoction improves cardiac function and myocardial fibrosis in MCT-induced PH rats with right heart failure (n = 12 per group) by upregulating LXRα, reducing NF-κB and TNF-α levels, and decreasing neuroendocrine factor release and cardiomyocyte apoptosis [64].

Mufangji Decoction improves cardiac function and myocardial fibrosis in MCT-induced PH rats with right heart failure (n = 12 per group) by upregulating LXRα, reducing NF-κB and TNF-α levels, and decreasing neuroendocrine factor release and cardiomyocyte apoptosis [64].

Xuefu Zhuyu Decoction alleviates hypoxia-induced pulmonary vascular remodeling and RV hypertrophy in PH rats (n = 8 per group) by inhibiting both the mTOR signaling pathway and NF-κB activation, thereby reducing pulmonary arterial pressure [65].

These investigations into TCM formulations illustrate how traditional medicinal systems can provide valuable mechanistic insights for modern drug discovery. The preclinical evidence suggesting their anti-inflammatory, anti-remodeling, and organ-protective effects via NF-κB modulation warrants further systematic investigation to assess their true translational potential.

Conclusion and perspectives

PH is a multifactorial disease characterized by pathological processes such as PASMCs proliferation, PAECs dysfunction, macrophage polarization, and persistent chronic inflammation. At the core of PH progression lies NF-κB, a key transcription factor orchestrating inflammation, cell proliferation, and apoptosis. By modulating signaling pathways like the BMPR2-Id-Notch-3 axis, AMPK, and TLR4/NF-κB, NF-κB drives pulmonary vascular remodeling, RV hypertrophy, and chronic inflammation. Additionally, NF-κB exacerbates PH pathology through the regulation of miRNAs, cell cycle proteins, and inflammatory mediators, highlighting its central role in disease progression.

Therapeutically, targeting NF-κB has shown considerable promise. Natural compounds such as matrine, resveratrol, and salidroside mitigate oxidative stress, inflammation, and vascular remodeling through inhibition of NF-κB signaling. Chemotherapeutic agents including sevoflurane and atorvastatin suppress NF-κB activation, reducing cellular proliferation and inflammatory cytokine release. TCM formulations—such as Feixin Decoction and Xuefu Zhuyu Decoction—employ multi-target strategies to effectively modulate NF-κB and its downstream pathways. Together, these interventions, currently supported primarily by preclinical evidence, represent diverse and innovative potential approaches for PH treatment.

Clinical translation and future perspectives

The majority of NF-κB-targeting agents discussed herein remain at the preclinical stage of development. Their translation to clinical application faces considerable challenges, including suboptimal bioavailability, potential systemic toxicity stemming from the pleiotropic roles of NF-κB, and significant heterogeneity in human PH phenotypes. Importantly, the critical functions of NF-κB in host defense, immune homeostasis, and tissue repair raise serious safety concerns regarding broad inhibition—particularly the risks of increased susceptibility to infections and impaired wound healing [66]. Thus, future therapeutic strategies should advance beyond conventional inhibition to explore cell-targeted or context-specific modulation approaches to enhance both feasibility and safety.

Future research should prioritize overcoming these translational barriers through innovative drug delivery systems—such as nanoparticle-based or tissue-specific carriers—rigorous safety and toxicological profiling, and the integration of multi-omics approaches in clinical trials to identify responsive patient subpopulations. Ultimately, translating these promising experimental findings into safe and effective patient care will require sustained collaboration between clinicians and researchers across disciplines.

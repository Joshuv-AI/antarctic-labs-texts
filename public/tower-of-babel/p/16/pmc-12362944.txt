Efficacy evaluation of minimally invasive particle implantation in treating head and neck malignancies under different guidance methods: a propensity score matching analysis study
Authors: Qing Yan, Xin Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo evaluate the efficacy of minimally invasive particle implantation guided by different methods for head and neck malignancies based on propensity-matched score analysis.Materials and methods120 patients with head and neck malignant tumors treated in our hospital from January 2021 to December 2023 were selected. The patients were matched by Propensity Score Matching (PSM) method at 1:1 and finally included in Computed Tomography (CT) guidance group (n = 60) and 3D print guide group (n = 60).ResultsThe pain in the 3D print guide group was significantly lower than that in the CT guidance group at 3 days and 7 days after operation (P < 0.05). Preoperative tumor diameter, postoperative metastasis, and tissue edema were similar. However, the 3D printing guide group had smaller tumor diameters and fewer recurrences (P < 0.05). The 3D printing guide group also showed better curative effects and lower incidence of adverse reactions compared to the CT guidance group (P < 0.05), indicating its superiority in treatment safety and efficacy.ConclusionMinimally invasive particle implantation guided by a 3D-printed guide plate is safer and more effective than CT-guided treatment for head and neck malignancies.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Trial registrationNot applicable.

Introduction

Head and neck malignant tumors (HNMT) account for about 5% of the total malignant tumors in the whole body, and there are some differences in the disease types between southern and northern China [1]. There are differences in the incidence of diseases between the north and the south. Nasopharyngeal cancer (NPC) and thyroid cancer (TC) are more common in the south, while laryngeal cancer (LC) and oral cancer (OC) are more common in the north [2]. Common symptoms include local infiltration and formation of masses, compression and obstruction (such as dyspnea, eating obstruction), and metastatic manifestations (such as cervical lymph node enlargement) [3, 4]. Survival rates vary depending on the tumor type and the effectiveness of the treatment administered. Therefore, early detection, diagnosis and treatment are crucial for improving the survival rate of patients with HNMT. The survival rate for patients in stage I is generally close to 90%, dropping to approximately 75% in stage II, ranging from 45 to 75% in stage III, and falling below 35% in stage IV. For all patients diagnosed with locally advanced (stage II or III) head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, the overall 5-year survival rate is 65%. In cases involving lymph node metastasis, the survival rate is ≤ 30%. Notably, patients over the age of 70 tend to experience longer asymptomatic intervals and exhibit higher survival rates compared to their younger counterparts [5].

Minimally invasive particle implantation therapy (MPIT) is effective for head and neck malignancies, and is fully known as radioactive seed implantation technology [6]. The principle is to implant a radioactive source into the tumor, so as to achieve direct irradiation of the tumor, kill tumor cells, and reduce the volume of the tumor [7]. However, it is not suitable for all patients, and the condition, constitution, expected effect and tolerance need to be comprehensively considered [8]. Treatment carries risks, such as radiation damaging surrounding tissues, causing inflammation, skin injuries, digestive tract reactions, etc. During the treatment process, close monitoring and adjustment of the plan are needed to ensure safety and efficacy [9].

This study conducted an in-depth discussion of the efficacy of minimally invasive particle implantation guided by different methods in the treatment of head and neck malignant tumors, focusing in particular on the two technologies of 3D printing-guided and Computed Tomography (CT) -guided particle implantation [10]. The introduction of PSM aimed to build a balanced sample group so that the comparison groups are as similar as possible in all key variables except the treatment method, thereby accurately reflecting the true effects of different treatment strategies. The study comprehensively evaluated the efficacy and safety of 3D printing-guided and CT-guided particle implantation in the treatment of head and neck malignancies [11]. Through this study, we hope to promote the further development of minimally invasive particle implantation therapy in the field of head and neck malignancies and provide patients with more precise and effective treatment options.

3D print guides provide personalized templates to improve the accuracy of particle implantation and reduce damage, but the cost and technical threshold are high. CT boot particle implantation uses computed tomography (CT) for precise positioning to ensure that particles are accurately implanted into tumors, but the operation is complex and radiation exposure requires attention. Both have their own advantages in minimally invasive particle implantation, but both face challenges such as improved accuracy, cost-effectiveness and side effect control. In this study, the efficacy of minimally invasive particle implantation guided by different methods in the treatment of HNMT was compared and evaluated to provide reference for clinical practice.

Subjects and methods

Research subjects

150 patients with head and neck malignant tumors admitted to our hospital from January 2021 to December 2023 were retrospectively selected and grouped according to different methods. 80 patients were in the CT guided group and 70 patients were in the 3D printed guide group. The patients were matched 1:1 using the PSM method. Matching variables included gender, age, body mass index. 60 patients were finally included in the CT guided group and 60 patients in the 3D printed guide group. The inclusion criteria were as follows: [1] All patients met the clinical diagnosis of head and neck malignancies [12, 2]. The patient did not receive external radiotherapy [3]. The coagulation function of the patient is normal. Exclusion criteria: [1] Patients with coagulation disorders [2]. Patients with active bleeding at the tumor site [3]. The patient has operative contraindications.

This study has been approved by the Ethics Committee of Yichang Second People’s Hospital (Approval No.202420) in accordance with regulatory and ethical guidelines pertaining to retrospective research studies. Informed consent was waived for this retrospective study due to the exclusive use of de-identified data, which posed no potential harm or impact on patient care.

Intervention

In the CT boot group, minimally invasive seed implantation was used to treat head and neck alignancies. The specific measures were as follows: patients underwent a comprehensive physical examination to evaluate their health status and operative tolerance. Detailed imaging procedures are performed to determine the location, size, and shape of the tumor. 125-I seeds (Tianjin Saide Bio-Pharmaceutical Co., LTD., Tianjin City, China) are selected for implantation, with each seed separated by 0.5–1.0 cm. Under CT boot (Definition As40, SOMATOM, Germany), the puncture point, needle insertion angle and depth are determined to accurately penetrate or near the tumor tissue. The radioactive seeds are implanted into the tumor tissue through a puncture needle or catheter. During implantation of the seeds, the position of the needle or catheter is continuously adjusted to ensure even distribution and coverage of the entire tumor area. After seed implantation, compress the puncture site to stop bleeding and observe whether the patient’s vital signs are stable. Patients should be given appropriate anti-infection treatment to prevent the occurrence of postoperative infection. Patients should be reexamined regularly to observe the changes of tumors and evaluate the efficacy. Regular postoperative follow-up is required for 12 months.

In the 3D print guide plate guide group, minimally invasive particle implantation was used to treat head and neck malignancies with 3D print guide plates. The specific measures were as follows: patients received a comprehensive physical examination to assess their health status and operative tolerance. Detailed imaging examination is performed to design the needle tract, insertion depth, particle distribution and number of particles for each puncture needle, as well as the radiation dose to the tumor. Particle selection: 125-1 particles, with an interval of 0.5–1.0 cm between particles. Based on the image and preoperative design, a 3D printed non-coplanar template that fits the patient’s skin is made using 3D print equipment. The orientation of each needle tract is precisely printed on the template to ensure precision during operation. Under the guidance of a 3D print guide, puncture is performed precisely according to a predetermined plan. The radioactive seeds are implanted into the tumor tissue according to the designed dose distribution through a puncture needle. During the implantation process, Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) navigation is used to ensure that the relationship between the implantation channel and tumors, brain tissues and vital organs is clearly visible, thus further improving the accuracy of operation. Postoperatively, hemostasis was applied to the puncture site and the patient’s vital signs were observed. Patients should be given appropriate anti-infection treatment to prevent the occurrence of postoperative infection. Patients should be reexamined regularly to observe the changes of tumors and evaluate the efficacy. Regular postoperative follow-up is required for 12 months.

Observation indicators

Comparison of pain conditions

We compared the pain levels between the two groups before surgery and at 1, 3, and 7 days post-operation. The visual analogue scale (VAS) for pain [12] was used to assess the patient’s pain.

Comparison of efficacy

Judgment criteria for efficacy are as followed: Patients need to undergo regular CT re-examination 12 months after operation. ① Complete response (CR), that is, the complete disappearance of tumor shown by CT images, means that the treatment has achieved a very significant effect. ② Partial response (PR): CT imaging shows that the tumor regresses by more than 50%, which indicates that the treatment has achieved significant efficacy, and the disease is effectively controlled. ③ No change (NC): CT images show that the tumor enlarges by less than 25% and decreases by less than 50%, indicating that the size of the tumor has not changed significantly, and the condition remains stable. ④ Progression (PD): CT imaging shows that the tumor enlarges by more than 25% or new lesions appear, which means that the condition has deteriorated, and further adjustment of treatment regimen is required. Excellence rate = (CR + PR)/total cases × 100.00%. We Compared the efficacy of patients between two groups.

Comparison of patient quality of life and resilience scales

The Connor-Davidson Resilience Scale (CD-RISC) [13] is used, which consists of three core dimensions: optimism, strength and resilience, and contains 25 items. The scale also uses the Likert 5-level scoring method, with scores ranging from 0 to 100 points. The higher the score, the higher the patient’s level of psychological resilience. The quality of life was evaluated using the Self-care Ability Assessment Form (ADL) [14] scale. The scoring result was 100 points out. The higher the score, the higher the patient’s self-care ability.

Comparison of other outcomes

Tumor-related indicators included tumor diameter change, postoperative tumor metastasis and edema in surrounding tissues. In additional, comparison of adverse reactions included skin reaction, fever, and fatigue.

Statistical analysis

The collected experimental data were analyzed using SPSS27.0. Measurement data conforming to normal distribution in the experimental data were expressed by ‾X ± S, and independent sample t test was used for comparison. Measurement data that did not conform to a normal distribution were described using the median and quartile, and differences between any two groups were compared using the Mann-Whitney test. Enumeration data were expressed as the number of cases and constituent ratio, and χ2 test or Fisher’s exact method was used for comparison. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Comparison of pain before and 1, 3, 7 days after operation between the two groups

Before the operation, no statistically significant difference existed in pain scores between the CT Boot Group and the 3D Print Guide Plate Group (t = 0.116, P = 0.908). At 1 and 3 days after the operation, there were also no statistically significant differences in pain scores between the groups (P > 0.05). However, at 3 and 7 days after the operation, significant differences in pain scores were observed between the groups, with the 3D Print Guide Plate Group exhibiting lower pain scores compared to the CT Boot Group (P < 0.0001). These findings suggest that the choice of guide—CT boot versus 3D print guide plate—may impact post-operative pain outcomes, particularly over longer periods following the operation, as shown in Fig. 1.

Discussion

Minimally invasive particle implantation therapy is a commonly used method in tumor treatment. Its basic principle is to insert radionuclide particles into the tumor through small incision or percutaneous puncture, thus locally releasing high-dose radiation to kill cancer cells [15]. This treatment approach aims to increase the radiation dose to the tumor area while reducing damage to surrounding normal tissue. Different guidance methods may have an impact on the accuracy and efficacy of minimally invasive particle implantation therapy [16]. In addition, different guidance methods may also differ in operative operation difficulty, particle implantation accuracy and patient comfort. This study found that minimally invasive particle implantation guided by 3D printed guide plates has better efficacy than CT guidance in the treatment of head and neck malignant tumors, and postoperative adverse reactions are less than CT guidance. Therefore, when selecting a guidance method for minimally invasive particle implantation treatment, minimally invasive particle implantation guided by 3D printed guide plates can be given priority.

The results of this study showed that the difference in pain between the two groups before and 1 day after operation was not statistically significant (P > 0.05), but the pain of patients in the 3D print guide plate guide group was significantly lower than that in the CT boot group at 3 days and 7 days after operation (P < 0.05). The reason for this may be that the 3D print guide plate is accurately made according to the patient’s image data such as CT or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), which can more accurately locate the tumor position and implanted particle position. This precision reduces damage to surrounding normal tissue during operation, thus reducing postoperative pain [17]. In contrast, while CT boot also provides more accurate positioning, it may be slightly inferior to 3D print guides. CT boot particle implantation may require more operations and time, such as multiple adjustments of implant position and angle [18]. This additional operation may increase the patient’s pain and discomfort. Previous studies have shown that [19] Although CT-guided surgical operations are not accurate enough, increasing patient pain, 3D printed guides can simplify the surgical process and reduce surgery Time and operating steps, thereby reducing patient pain. In addition, since surgery guided by 3D printed guide causes less damage to surrounding tissue, postoperative tissue inflammatory response may be relatively mild, and inflammatory response is an important factor leading to postoperative pain. Therefore, patients in the 3D printed guide guided group may have relatively mild pain at 3 and 7 days after surgery. Previous studies by Liu et al. [20]have shown that 3D printed non-coplanar template assisted CT guided radioactive 125I seed implantation is a safe, effective and minimally invasive treatment for recurrent cervical cancer after external beam radiotherapy, similar to the results of this study, laying the foundation for this study.

The study results also showed that the tumor diameter and number of postoperative recurrence cases in the 3D print guide plate guide group were smaller than those in the CT boot group (P < 0.05). The reason may be that particle implantation guided by the 3D print guide plate can ensure uniform distribution of particles in the tumor tissue, thus improving the therapeutic effect. The uniformity of particle distribution helps to kill tumor cells more comprehensively and reduce the risk of tumor recurrence [21]. However, CT boot particle implantation may lead to insufficiently uniform particle distribution due to the limitation of positioning accuracy and operation difficulty, thus affecting the treatment effect. A further study on the efficacy and occurrence of adverse reactions showed that the efficacy in the 3D print guide plate guide group was better than that in the CT boot group (P < 0.05), and the incidence of adverse reactions in the 3D print guide plate guide group was less than that in the CT boot group (P < 0.05). The reason for this may be that the 3D print guide plate is made according to the specific lesions of patients, with highly personalized characteristics. It can precisely match the anatomy of patients, simplify the operative process, and reduce operative time and operation steps [22]. Moreover, particle implantation guided by a 3D print guide plate can ensure the uniform distribution of particles in tumor tissues, thus improving therapeutic efficacy and reducing the occurrence of adverse reactions. Previous studies have shown [23] that 3D printed guides can improve the accuracy of surgery and facilitate patient recovery in the application of surgery, which is consistent with this study.

There are still some shortcomings in this study. First, the sample size of this study may be limited, which may affect the broad applicability and reliability of the results. The small sample size may lead to biased results and make it difficult to fully reflect the actual situation of different patient groups. Therefore, to more accurately evaluate the therapeutic effect of minimally invasive particle implantation guided by 3D print guide plate, it is necessary to further expand the sample size and conduct larger-scale studies [24]. Second, this study may lack an adequate assessment of long-term efficacy and complications. Minimally invasive particle implantation therapy is a complex process, and its long-term efficacy and potential complications are crucial to the patient’s quality of life. However, due to study time and resource constraints, these aspects may not have been adequately addressed in this study. Future studies should strengthen the monitoring and evaluation of long-term efficacy and complications to provide more comprehensive information. Finally, although this study confirmed that minimally invasive particle implantation guided by 3D printed guide plates has advantages in the treatment of head and neck malignant tumors, it did not thoroughly discuss its cost-effectiveness. In practical applications, 3D printing technology equipment and materials have high costs and limited accessibility, which may affect its widespread promotion and application in clinical practice.

Conclusion

In conclusion, minimally invasive particle implantation guided by 3D print guide plate has shown significant advantages over CT boot in the treatment of head and neck malignancies in many aspects. The promotion and application of this technology is expected to provide a safer and more effective treatment for patients with head and neck malignancies and improve the quality of life of patients. However, it should be noted that although the 3D print guide plate has many advantages, the appropriate guidance method still needs to be selected according to the specific situation of the patient and the doctor’s advice in practical application.

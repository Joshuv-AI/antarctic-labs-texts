Elevated SNHG15 empowers keratinocytes hyperproliferation through activation of STAT3/Cyclin D1 axis in psoriasis
Authors: Lipeng Tang, Guanzhuo Li, Jiameng Chang, Haiyan Xian, Yanjie Liu, Luping Lin, Zixin Dai, Zhenting Liu, Xinmin Qiu, Bowen Zhang, Zuqing Su, Bing Feng, Ying Zhu, Maojie Wang, Yuchao Chen, Huazhen Liu, Dinghong Wu, Chutian Li, Jie Zhao, Mingxian Li, Yongzhan Zhu, Xingyi Xie, Hao Deng, Shuyan Ye, Shicheng Fan, Huichang Bi, Chuanjian Lu, Guangjuan Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Psoriasis is a common inflammatory skin disease with characterization of epidermal hyperplasia and sustained skin inflammation. Long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs), which contain more than 200 nucleotide-long transcripts, are emerging as the crux of epigenetic regulators in multiple biological processes and diseases. However, how lncRNAs contribute to the etiology of psoriasis remains to be elucidated. For the first time, this study revealed that SNHG15, which was elevated in cytokines-stimulated keratinocytes and psoriasis lesions, promoted keratinocytes hyperproliferation. Mechanistically, SNHG15 fueled epithelial pathology through activation of STAT3/Cyclin D1 axis. Intriguingly, activation of STAT3 enhanced SNHG15 transcription to form a positive feed-back modulatory loop and consequently augmented the skin lesions in psoriasis. Furthermore, knock down the expression of SNHG15 can counteract the IMQ-induced keratinocytes hyperproliferation in vivo. Taken together, our findings uncover that SNHG15 facilitates epidermal hyperplasia via STAT3/Cyclin D1 axis, which might provide a novel therapeutic avenue for psoriasis treatment.

Introduction

Psoriasis, which is hallmarked by erythematous scaly lesions, is a common skin disease affecting approximately 2%–3% of the global population1. Currently, topical/systemic treatments (such as corticosteroids, methotrexate) and biologic therapies [especially Ixekizumab (IL-17A inhibitor) and Guselkumab (IL-23 p19 antibody)] represent the first-line therapeutic approaches for psoriasis2,3. However, to date, psoriasis has many unresolved issues. For instance, psoriatic patients are still beset with disease recurrence after medication discontinuation and adverse drug reactions. Therefore, novel therapeutic strategies are urgently needed to solve these challenges.

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), which are non-protein-coding RNAs longer than 200 nucleotides, display copious roles in regulation of numerous biological processes and diseases4, 5, 6, 7, 8. Interestingly, accumulating evidence has documented the differential expression of numerous lncRNAs in the psoriasis-lesion skins9, 10, 11. However, whether deregulated lncRNAs are implicated in the pathogenesis of psoriasis remains largely elusive.

Small nucleolar RNA host gene 15 (SNHG15), a member of SNHG family of noncoding RNAs, is highly abundant and conserved among mammals12. Previous studies have indicated that SNHG15 is involved in various pathological processes, such as tumorigenesis13, hyperglycemia-induced endothelial dysfunction14, stroke-induced immunosuppression15, and osteoarthritis16. However, the potential pathogenic role of SNHG15 in psoriasis remains unclear.

Therefore, the current study aimed to reveal the regulatory effect of SNHG15 on psoriasis and the involved mechanisms. We found that lncRNA SNHG15 empowers the hyperproliferation of psoriatic keratinocytes via activating STAT3/Cyclin D1 axis. Moreover, STAT3 stimulates SNHG15 transcription, which in turn forms a positive STAT3–SNHG15 feedback loop to reinforce the skin lesion in psoriasis. Remarkably, inhibition of the SNHG15 expression leads to a significant relief in the psoriatic burden, thereby indicating the translatability of our findings to psoriasis treatment.

Materials and methods

Reagents

Smart silencers of si-NC or si-SNHG15 (lnc3170302020131) were purchased from RiboBio company (Guangzhou, China). IL-6 (10395-HNAE), IL-1β (10139-HNAE), TNF-α (10602-HNAE), IL-22 (13059-HNAE), and IL-17A (12047-HNAE) were acquired from Sino Biological Inc. (Beijing, China). IL-23 (PRP1909) was bought from Abbkine (Wuhan, China). RNAimax (13778075, Gibco) and TRIzol™ reagent (15596018, Life Technologies) were purchased from ThermoFisher Scientific company (MA, USA). Stattic (HY-13818) were acquired from MedChem Express (NJ, USA). Sichuan Med-Shine Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. (Sichuan, China) supplied IMQ cream.

lncRNAs expression profiling

The expression profile of the lncRNA transcriptome was downloaded from GSE74697, which was uploaded by Gupta et al17. The total RNA was extracted from 18 psoriasis patients (PP) and 16 normal skin controls (NN), and then the RNA was performed RNA-sequence via Illumina HiSeq 2500.

Cell culture

Medium, which is comprised of MEM-α basic medium (C12571500BT, Gibco, MA, USA), 1% MEM NEAA (11140050, Gibco), 10% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (10099141C, Gibco), and 1% sodium pyruvate (11360070, Gibco), is used for HaCaT cells culture. Cells (passages 1–9) at 37 °C and 5% CO2 were used for the subsequent experiments.

Real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

We extracted the RNA from skin samples or cell cultured cells. RT Super Mix (KR118-02, TIANGEN, Beijing, China) and the Super Real PreMix Plus Kit (FP205, TIANGEN) was used to perform the RT-qPCR experiment. GAPDH is used as a reference gene. In order to ensure accuracy and reliability, all RT-qPCR processes were conducted in a Ribonuclease-free environment.

Human subjects

Psoriatic skin lesions were collected from individuals diagnosed with psoriasis, while normal skin samples were obtained from healthy volunteers. The Second Clinical College of Guangzhou University of Chinese Medicine Research Ethics Board approved our methods (BE2024-366). Meantime, all participants gave written informed consent.

In situ hybridization (ISH)

For SNHG15 in situ hybridization, RNAscope™ Probe-Hs-SNHG15 probe (1799691-C1, Advanced Cell Diagnostics, CA, USA) and RNAscope® Multiplex Fluorescent Reagent Kit v2 (323100, Advanced Cell Diagnostics) were used. Both SNHG15 probe and positive/negative control probe (32086 and 320871, Advanced Cell Diagnostics) were hybridized at 40 °C. In situ signals were detected by staining with TSA Vivid™ Fluorophore Kit 570 (PG-323272, Tocris Bioscience, Bristol, England). The quantification was performed by using Image J.

Nuclear plasma separation experiment

PARIS Kit (AM1921, Invitrogen, CA, USA) was used for the nuclear plasma separation experiment. We utilized Trizol (15596018CN, Invitrogen) to extract RNA from the nuclear components. In addition, we used Trizol LS (10296010CN, Invitrogen) to extract RNA from the cytoplasmic components.

Fluorescent in situ hybridization (FISH)

H-SNHG15 FISH Probes Mix (lnc1100388, Ribo, Guangzhou, China) and Fluorescence in situ Hybridization Kit (R11060.7, Ribo) were utilized. U6 and DAPI were used as the positive control for nuclear staining.

RNA transfection

The transfection procedure was executed according to the guidelines of RNAimax (13778075, ThermoFisher).

Cell cycle analysis

At first, 1.0 × 106 HaCaT cells/per a well in a 6-well of plate were seeded. Then, we replaced culture medium with serum-free medium for 12 h to induce synchronization at the G0 phase. IL-6/IL-6+si-NC/IL-6+si-SNHG15 was then applied to cells for another 48 h. At last, cells were collected, stained with a Cell Cycle Staining Kit (CCS012, MulitScience, Hangzhou, China), and data was analyzed with FlowJo using Agilent's Quanteon flow cytometer.

Cell viability assay

MTT (ST1573-5g, Beyotine, Shanghai, China) was added to the treated cells for 2 h at 37 °C. Then, we aspirated MTT-containing medium and subsequently dissolved Formazan with 100 μL Dimethyl Sulfoxide (DMSO). Finally, we measured the absorbance at 490 nm.

EDU staining

The EdU-positive cells were imaged and detected by using EdU Apollo 567 Kit (C10310-1, RiboBio).

RNA-sequence

We utilized TRIzol™ reagent to extract the total RNA. RNA sequencing was performed on Illumina sequencing platform. By applying specific criteria (log2 fold change >1.5 and P-value <0.05), the Fragments Per Kilobase of transcript Per Million (FPKM) values were analyzed to identify and filter out the potential differentially expressed genes (DEGs).

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP)

RIP assay was performed with RNA-Binding Protein Immunoprecipitation Kit (17-700, ThermoFisher Scientific). 3 μg of either IgG control antibody or p-STAT3 antibody were added into the cell lysates from HaCaT (1 × 107 cells per condition) overnight at 4 °C. Subsequently, protein A/G magnetic beads (50 μL) were applied to the mixture for 30 min at 25 °C. Following sequential washes, RNA was eluted from the beads at 55 °C for 30 min in elution buffer. Purified RNA was reverse-transcribed and quantified by SYBR Green-based RT-qPCR.

Ectopic expression of EF.STAT3C.Ubc.GFP, cyclin D1 and SNHG15

EF.STAT3C.Ubc.GFP (24983) was applied by Addgene (MA, USA). The ectopic expression pReceiver-M35-Cyclin D1 (Exp-Cyclin D1) plasmid (EX-B0078-M35) was purchased from GeneCopoeia (Rockville, MD, USA). Briefly, HaCaT cells were transfected with the empty vector control plasmid (2 μg), EF.STAT3C.Ubc.GFP (2 μg) or Exp-cyclin D1 (2 μg) for 24 h. Then, cells were applied with cytokines (IL-6) and si-RNA (si-NC or si-SNHG15) for 72 h. Finally, we used MTT assay to test the cell viability. Moreover, the ectopic expression pcDNA3.1-SNHG15 (Exp-SNHG15) plasmid (V79020) was purchased from Invitrogen. HaCaT cells were transfected with the empty vector control plasmid (2 μg) or Exp-SNHG15 (2 μg) for 24 h. After transfection, Stattic (10 μmol/L) were applied to these transfected cells for 72 h. Then, cell viability was tested (n = 3).

Western blot analysis

Total proteins were extracted from skin tissues or cultured cells by using Beyotime's RIPA lysis buffer (P0013C) with 1% protease inhibitors (P1206, Applygen, Beijing, China) and 1% phosphatase inhibitors (CW2383S, CWBIO, Shenzhen, China). Antibody p-STAT3 (9145S), antibody STAT3 (9139S), Cyclin D1 (2978S), anti-rabbite IgG (1:3000; 7054S), anti-mouse IgG (1:3000; 7076S) were from CST (Boston, MA, USA) and then utilized. Antibody GAPDH (60004-1-Ig), antibody Actin (66009-1-Ig), antibody β-Tubulin (10094-1-AP) were from Proteintech (Wuhan, China) and then utilized. Membranes were imaged with e-Blot Touch Imager.

Dual luciferase reporter assays

At first, HaCaT cells were transfected with wild type or mutant form SNHG15 promoter reporter plasmid (FR01-SNHG15-WT and FR01-SNHG15-MUT, Genecopoeia, Rockville, MD, USA) for 48 h. Then, these HaCaT cells were transfected with empty vector control plasmid (EX-NEG-M02, Genecopoeia) or the ectopic expression pReceiver-M02-STAT3 (Exp-STAT3) plasmid (EX-Z2835-M02, Genecopoeia) for 24 h. Then, luciferase assay was detected by Luc-Pair™ Duo-luciferase HS assay kit (LF004, Genecopoeia).

Chromatin immunoprecipitation followed by quantitative PCR (CHIP-qPCR)

EZ-ChIP Kit (17-371, Sigma–Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA), p-STAT3 antibody (9145S, CST) and IgG antibody were used for CHIP assays. The CHIP products were further confirmed by 2% agarose gel electrophoresis.

Mice experiment

6–8 weeks male BALB/c mice were purchased from the Animal Research Laboratory of Guangdong Province in China. Then, the mice were randomly assigned into the control/IMQ/IMQ combined with dexamethasone cream (DEX)/IMQ combined with si-NC/IMQ combined with si-SNHG15 group. Here, DEX is known to alleviate psoriasis-like skin inflammation. IMQ (62.5 mg, 5%) were daily and topically applied to the shaved back skin of mice in the IMQ group/the IMQ combined with DEX group/the IMQ combined with si-NC group/the IMQ combined with si-SNHG15 group for 7 consecutive days. In addition, a same dose of vaseline was applied to the shaved back in the control mice. Furthermore, mice in the IMQ combined with si-NC group or the IMQ combined with si-SNHG15 group received intradermal injections of lncRNA ASO NC (lnc6201201023219, Ribo) or ASO si-SNHG15 (lnc6230228040105, Ribo) every other day, respectively. All procedures in this experiment were approved and supervised by the Second Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou University of Chinese Medicine’ Animal Care and Use Committee (2021-071).

Histological analysis

The lesional skins of the mice were fixed with formalin. Then, the skins samples are further embedded in paraffin. We stained sections (3.5 μm) with Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining. Furthermore, the epidermal thickness was analyzed with Adobe Photoshop 2020.

Measure of psoriasis area and severity index (PASI) score

The PASI score comprises of scale, erythema, and thickening, with a total of 5 grades (0, none; 1, slight; 2, moderate; 3, marked; 4, very marked). The cumulative score (scale from 0 to 12) was assessed every day to identify the severity of psoriasis.

Immunofluorescence

The skin sections (3.5 μm) were deparaffinated, heat repaired with sodium citrate buffer for 5 min in a pressure cooker, and then naturally cooled down about 90 min. Subsequently, the skin sections were perforated (Saponin, Beyotime, 30 min), blocked with 5% goat serum for 1 h, and then incubated with monoclonal Cyclin D1 antibody (1:100, 60186-1-Ig, Proteintech, Wuhan, China) overnight at 4 °C. At last, the skin sections were rinsed with PBS and incubated with fluoro-chromecon-jugated secondary Ab (1:500, ab150118, Abcam, Cambridge, England) for 1 h at room temperature. The fluorescent images were taken by Andor BC43 Desktop confocal microscope (Oxford Instruments, Britain).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed through Sigmaplot software 12.5 and GraphPad Prism 9.0. The data were presented as mean ± standard error of mean (SEM). Student's t-test or One-way ANOVA test with Student–Newman Keuls method were utilized for two or multiple comparisons.

Down-regulation of SNHG15 suppresses the keratinocytes hyper-proliferation

To reveal the potential role of SNHG15 in psoriasis, we initially investigated its cellular localization. Consistent with the ISH results, both nuclear plasma separation experiment and fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) assays showed that SNHG15 was primarily localized in the nuclear compartment of HaCaT cells (Fig. 4A and B). To further illustrate the biological function of SNHG15, we knock downed the SNHG15 with si-RNA in IL-6-stimulated keratinocytes and then detected their cell viability. The results show that down-regulation of SNHG15 significantly suppressed the IL-6-induced keratinocytes hyperproliferation (Fig. 4C and D). Moreover, functional analysis revealed that ectopic expression of SNHG15 enhanced keratinocytes proliferation, whereas this promotive effect of SNHG15 overexpression can be effectively reversed by Stattic (a STAT3-specific inhibitor) (Fig. 4E and F). Furthermore, knockdown of SNHG15 caused a potent decrease in the S phase fraction and the number of the EdU+ cells (Fig. 4G and H). Additionally, the regulatory effects of down-regulation of SNHG15 on the IL-17A- or IL23-induced keratinocytes hyperproliferation were also evaluated. Similarly, we found that genetic silencing of SNHG15 significantly attenuated IL-17A- or IL23-induced keratinocytes hyperproliferation (Fig. 5A–E). SNHG15 exacerbates the keratinocytes hyperproliferation by activating STAT3 signaling pathways

To illustrate the underlying cellular mechanism by which SNHG15 leads to keratinocytes hyperproliferation, RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) experiment was performed. 91 downregulated DEGs and 922 upregulated DEGs between the IL-6+si-NC group and the IL-6+si-SNHG15 group were showed in Fig. 6A. These DEGs were majorly enriched in STAT3/TNF/IL-17 signaling pathway (Fig. 6B). Given that STAT3 protein is a predominant component in STAT3 signaling pathway, we next detected whether SNHG15 could modulate the STAT3 signaling pathway. RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) experiment showed that SNHG15 could directly bind to p-STAT3 (Fig. 6C). STAT3 enhances SNHG15 transcription to form a positive modulatory feed-back loop

Interestingly, one putative STAT3-binding element in the promoter region of SNHG15 was observed in the JASPAR Database (Fig. 7A). A RT-qPCR assay proved that STAT3 inhibition with Stattic markedly decreased SNHG15 transcription (Fig. 7B and C). To investigate the direct regulatory role of STAT3 in SNHG15 transcription, CHIP-qPCR assay was performed. As shown in Fig. 7D, we found that p-STAT3 could directly bind to the SNHG15 promoter. To further determine whether this predicted STAT3-binding element was necessary for STAT3-mediated SNHG15 transcription, dual-luciferase reporter assays with wild or mutated type element were conducted. Quantitative analysis revealed that STAT3 significantly enhanced SNHG15 transcriptional activity in the presence of the wild type element. In contrast, mutation of the STAT3-binding site resulted in a marked reduction in SNHG15 transcriptional activity, confirming the essential role of this regulatory element in STAT3-mediated transcriptional activation (Fig. 7E). Discussion

Given that epidermal hyperplasia is a major hallmark of psoriasis, exploration of the underlying cellular mechanisms of keratinocytes hyperproliferation might lead to highly effective therapies for psoriasis. Previous studies majorly evaluated the pathogenic roles of anti-apoptotic genes [such as interferon-inducible gene 6–16 (also known as G1P3)21, interferon alpha-inducible protein 27 (IFI27)22] or proliferation-related genes [such as protein phosphatase 6 (PP6)23,24, S-phase kinase-associated protein 2 (SKP2)25,26] in psoriasis. Recently, emerging evidence indicates that epigenetic factors (including DNA/RNA methylation27, 28, 29, histone modifications30, 31, 32, non-coding RNAs33,34 and so on) also exert predominant contributory effects on the aetiology of psoriasis. Here, we revealed that long noncoding RNA, especially lncRNA SNHG15, empowers keratinocytes hyperproliferation through regulating STAT3–Cyclin D1 pathway. Moreover, targeting SNHG15 significantly relieves the IMQ-trigger psoriasis-like pathology. Our study leaves open the possibility that lncRNAs might be promising targets for psoriasis treatment.

Recently, accumulating evidence indicates that RNA interference (RNAi) might present as a promising therapeutic strategy for numerous diseases35, 36, 37, 38. For instance, inclisiran, which is a novel RNAi therapeutic drug for arteriosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) treatment, is approved by the FDA in December 2021. Clinical studies showed that inclisiran effectively reduces the LDL cholesterol level in patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease39,40. Moreover, Yu et al.41 showed that antisense oligonucleotides targeting IRF4 alleviate psoriasis in vitro and in vivo. In the current study, knockdown the expression of SNHG15 with small interfering RNAs (si-RNAs) can significantly relief the IMQ-induced psoriasiform epidermal hyperplasia in vivo, which suggest that targeting SNHG15 may be a valuable target for psoriasis treatment.

Previous studies have identified that SNHG15 is abnormally elevated in various diseases, such as cancer42,43, brain injury15,44 and SARS-CoV-2 infection45. For example, highly expressed SNHG15 represses ferroptosis and consequently fuels gastric cancer progression via fostering tumor cell proliferation and evasion. Mechanistically, SNHG15 competitively binds to HNRNPA1 and consequently increases the stability of SLC7A11, which ultimately results in inhibition of ferroptosis46. In addition, SNHG15, highly expressed in MPP+-induced Parkinson's disease, aggravates MPP+ challenge-mediated neuronal damages through a miR-29c-3p/SNCA axis47,48. Recently, it is reported that SNHG15 can aid SARS-CoV-2 entry through interacting with Rab-like protein 2A45. In current study, we elucidated that SNHG15, which is upregulated in psoriatic skin lesions, enhances keratinocytes proliferation by regulating STAT3–Cyclin D1 axis. To date, this is the first time to establish a contributory role of SNHG15 in the etiology of psoriasis.

The mRNA level of SNHG15 can be regulated through various cellular mechanisms. For example, the transcription of SNHG15 could be modulated via a novel super enhancer of SNHG15 (SNHG15-SE). Mechanistically, the transcription factor FOSL1 could be recruited to SNHG15-SE, thereby facilitating and increasing the SNHG15 transcription49. In addition, using methylated RNA immunoprecipitation sequencing (MeRIP-seq) and subsequent gain-/loss-of-function experiments, it's found that ALKBH5, a m6A demethylase, can elevate and stabilize lncRNA SNHG15 transcript through its m6A demethylation50. Moreover, oncogenic transcription factors, such as MYC and E2F1, can be recruited to the SNHG15 promoter regions and consequently regulate its expression46. Here, we reveal a novel mutual interaction between SNHG15 and STAT3. Through comprehensive CHIP-qPCR analysis and dual-luciferase reporter assay, we demonstrated that the transcription factor STAT3 directly binds to the promoter region of SNHG15, thereby activating its transcriptional expression. In turn, upregulated SNHG15 reinforces STAT3 signaling activation, therefore forming a positive STAT3–SNHG15 feed-back loop in psoriasis.

Previously, several studies illustrated that STAT3 plays a predominate pathogenic role in psoriasis. Psoriasis-like skin lesions can be spontaneously appeared in transgenic mice with keratinocytes expressing a constitutively active STAT3 (K5.Stat3C mice)51. Mechanistically, activation of STAT3 enhances the expression of various inflammatory proteins, including IκB-α, ICAM-1 and TGF-α, ultimately leading to keratinocytes hyperproliferation as well as T lymphocytes activation. Moreover, activation of STAT3 contributes to LPCAT1-mediated GLUT3 upregulation, consequently enhancing keratinocyte glycolysis and promoting proliferative and inflammatory progression in psoriasis52. Recently, it's reported that IL-17A, a core psoriasis-related cytokine, can further exacerbate the psoriatic severity in a STAT3 overexpressing mice53. The RIP assay conducted in the current study provides the first evidence demonstrating SNHG15 can directly bind to p-STAT3. Moreover, this binding of SNHG15 to STAT3 promotes STAT3 signaling pathway, thereby aggravating psoriasis-related pathological processes. These findings significantly enhance our understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying psoriasis pathogenesis.

Physically, Cyclin D1 regulates G1–S phase transition and promotes cell cycle progression by allosterically modulating cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs), particularly CDK4 and CDK6; while dysregulation of Cyclin D1 expression can lead to uncontrolled cellular growth54, 55, 56. Previous investigations have established a direct correlation between aberrant Cyclin D1 expression and the hyperproliferative phenotype observed in psoriatic keratinocytes57, 58, 59. In this study, we have identified a novel regulatory mechanism wherein SNHG15-mediated activation of the STAT3 signaling pathway leads to upregulation of Cyclin D1 expression, ultimately driving psoriatic epidermal hyperplasia both in vitro and in vivo.

Conclusions

In summary, the current study identified a SNHG15–STAT3 positive feed-back circuit in psoriasis. Activation of STAT3 triggers upregulation of SNHG15 and further leads to STAT3 activation, ultimately resulting in psoriatic keratinocytes hyperproliferation. These findings underscore the key contributory role of lncRNAs in etiology of psoriasis. Moreover, our studies also suggest that targeting SNHG15 could relief the IMQ-triggered psoriasis like epidermal hyperplasia in vivo, suggesting that SNHG15 might serve as a promising target for psoriasis treatment.

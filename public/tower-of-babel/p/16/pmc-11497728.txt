Air pollution amyloidogenesis is attenuated by the gamma‐secretase modulator GSM‐15606
Authors: Jose A. Godoy‐Lugo, Max A. Thorwald, Mafalda Cacciottolo, Carla D'Agostino, Ararat Chakhoyan, Constantinos Sioutas, Rudolph E. Tanzi, Kevin D. Rynearson, Caleb E. Finch
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractINTRODUCTIONChronic air pollution (AirPoll) is associated with accelerated cognitive decline and risk of Alzheimer's disease (AD). Correspondingly, wild‐type and AD‐transgenic rodents exposed to AirPoll have increased amyloid peptides and behavioral impairments.METHODSWe examined the γ‐secretase modulator GSM‐15606 for potential AirPoll protection by its attenuating of amyloid beta (Aβ)42 peptide production. Male and female wild‐type mice were fed GSM‐15606 during an 8‐week inhalation exposure to AirPoll subfractions, ambient nanoparticulate matter (nPM), and diesel exhaust particles (DEP).RESULTSGSM‐15606 decreased Aβ42 during nPM and DEP exposure without changing beta‐ or gamma‐secretase activity or BACE1 and PS1 protein levels. DEP increased lateral ventricle volume by 25%.DISCUSSIONThese enzyme responses are relevant to AD drug treatments, as well as to the physiological functions of the Aβ42 peptide. GSM‐15606 attenuation of Aβ42 may benefit human exposure to AirPoll.Highlights Gamma‐secretase modulator (GSM‐15606) attenuates the amyloidogenic amyloid beta (Aβ)42 peptide during exposure to air pollution, which may be a mechanism by which air pollution increases Alzheimer's disease (AD) risk.AD drug treatments may also consider Aβ homeostasis among the chronic effects of GSM‐15606 and other amyloid reduction treatments on secretase enzymes.

BACKGROUND

Air pollution (AirPoll) particles of 2.5 microns in diameter or smaller (PM2.5) are strongly associated with a risk of accelerated cognitive decline and Alzheimer's disease (AD), 1 , 2 , 3 even at PM2.5 levels below current Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) standards. 4 , 5 The in vivo amyloid load of cognitively impaired elderly increased proportionately with PM2.5. 6 These findings confirm post mortem elevated amyloid beta (Aβ)42 deposits in the frontal cortex and cerebral vasculature of individuals from Mexican cities with high AirPoll. 7 Rodent models also show amyloid responses to AirPoll. AD transgenic mice responded to PM0.2 with increased fibrillar Aβ peptides, shown by us 8 , 9 and others. 10 , 11 , 12 Wild‐type rodents also respond to AirPoll with increased soluble Aβ42 peptides, 13 , 14 , 15 which in humans aggregates into amyloid fibrils.

The amyloidogenic Aβ40 and ‐42 peptides are excised from the amyloid precursor protein (APP) by the enzyme complexes β‐secretase and γ‐secretase (GS). 16 Alternatively, α‐secretase forms the p38 fragment and other non‐amyloidogenic products. Co‐authors Rynearson and Tanzi developed γ‐secretase modulators (GSM) that selectively decrease amyloidogenic Aβ42 production 17 , 18 (Figure 1), without inhibiting the systemic functions of the body‐wide GS enzyme. 16

Schema of APP processing by the β‐ and γ‐secretases and GSM‐15606 effect. APP, amyloid precursor protein; Aβ38, Aβ42, amyloid beta peptides.

Mice were exposed to two traffic‐related AirPoll components of PM2.5, nanoparticulate matter (nPM) and diesel exhaust particles (DEP). nPM were collected on filters from a single roadway site in Los Angeles and eluted into water by sonication. 19 , 20 Because nPM toxicity recently declined, 21 , 22 , 23 we switched exposures to DEP, which shows more reproducible toxicity. 13 , 15 , 24 , 25 We first describe how GSM‐15606 modulated AirPoll neurotoxicity. Then, we examine how GSM‐15606 alone impacts APP processing and two amyloidogenic secretase enzymes.

METHODS

Animals

Procedures were approved by the University of Southern California Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (USC IACUC), supervised by the USC Department of Animal Resources. C57BL6‐background mice were housed in groups of four at 22°C/30% humidity and light cycles of 12 hours with standard nesting. These wild‐type mice also have a nestin transgene (034387, Jackson Laboratory) used for cell sorting in a separate study of the hippocampus.

Treatment and exposures

Male and female mice were fed vehicle or GSM‐15606 ((S)‐N‐(1‐[4‐fluorophenyl]ethyl)−6‐(6‐methoxy‐5‐[4‐methyl‐1H‐imidazol‐1‐yl]pyridin‐2‐yl)−4‐methylpyridazin‐3‐amine benzenesulfonate) besylate salt forms at 10 mg/kg/day (Figure S1 in supporting information). GSM‐15606 was mixed with chow and fed to male and female mice 1 week before and through the 8 weeks of exposure. Two components of traffic‐related AirPoll were used, DEP and nPM. Most experiments exposed mice to DEP, a component of PM2.5, which shows reproducible neurotoxicity 24 compared to the decreasing neurotoxicity of ambient nanoscale particulate matter fraction (nPM) used in our prior studies. 21 , 22 , 23 While DEP has lower levels of many chemical factors in ambient AirPoll, 26 DEP contains polyaromatic hydrocarbons that are absent in aerosolized nPM (Supplemental figure 2 in Morgan et al.). 27

nPM exposure

Mice were exposed to inhaled nanoscale subfraction of urban PM2.5 (nPM; 300 μg/m3, 5 hours/day, 3 days/8 weeks) or control filtered air (FA), as previously described by our group. 20 Briefly, nPM was collected in urban Los Angeles using high‐volume ultrafine particle samplers. 19 Aqueous suspension was obtained by filter soaking into Milli‐Q deionized water, vortexing, and 30 minute sonication. This suspension is re‐aerosolized for animal exposure in air‐flow chambers.

DEP exposure

Two sets of mice were exposed at separate times to inhaled DEP (100 μg/m3, 5 hours/day/8 weeks) or control FA. In the second set, animals underwent in vivo magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). DEP was purchased from the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST 2975). Briefly, DEP was solubilized into Milli‐Q deionized water, mixed, and sonicated for 30 minutes for re‐aerosolized animal exposure.

In vivo MRI

Male mice were anesthetized (induction: 2.5%; maintenance: 1.5%–1.8% isoflurane/air) within the scanner for each imaging session in the cryogen‐free 7T MRI scanner (MR Solutions). Temperature and breathing rate were continuously monitored; the entire acquisition protocol (animal installation, scout view, gradient shimming, T1w imaging) lasted between 14 and 15 minutes per mouse. T2‐weighted imaging (2D‐fast spin echo, time repetition/time echo 4900/45 ms, 35 slices, slice thickness 500 μm, in‐plane resolution 75 ×70 μm2, number of averages 3) was used to obtain structural images. Drawn regions of interest were manually restricted between Bregma 3 to 4 mm anterior–posterior (AP) and quantified in mm3 for total brain volume (excluding cerebellum and brain stem), lateral ventricles, and cortical mantle.

Aβ peptide and protein extraction

The cerebral cortex was homogenized in sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS)‐free RIPA buffer (45 mg, 300 μL) with protease and phosphatase inhibitors. SDS‐free RIPA (20‐188, Millipore Sigma) was used because SDS can alter Aβ conformation. 28 Soluble fraction supernatants were obtained from centrifugation at 10,000 × g for 1 hour at 4°C. 29 For nPM experiments, Aβ peptides were assayed in phosphate‐buffered saline 2% DEA 50 mM sodium chloride soluble fraction supernatants using the 4G8 Kit V‐PLEX (Meso Scale Discovery).

RESEARCH IN CONTEXT

Systematic review: Current PubMed publications show air pollution increases amyloid deposition and risk of Alzheimer's disease (AD) and relevant reports are cited. Because monoclonal antibody interventions to decrease amyloid beta (Aβ) have side effects, new pharmacological treatments to reduce Aβ may improve outcomes from air pollution exposure. Interpretation: Gamma‐secretase modulator (GSM‐15606) treatment can attenuate amyloidogenic effects during air pollution exposure. Future directions: These findings expand the understanding of the amyloidogenic effects of air pollution in the brain and the protective effects of GSM treatment. Future studies will investigate: (a) the cause of increased lateral ventricle volume with diesel exhaust particles. Further brain imaging may identify additional brain regions that are protected by GSM; (b) positron emission tomography imaging for tracking of radiolabeled GSM after exposure to air pollution; (c) further analysis of Aβ peptide species by liquid chromatography mass spectrometry in relevant brain regions after air pollution exposure and GSM; (d) examine rodent models of air pollution for GSM efficacy by sex, age, and apolipoprotein E alleles.

Protein blotting

Proteins were assayed from the cerebral cortex of mice exposed to DEP, with and without GSM‐15606; data are presented in two sections. First, we examine GSM effects on responses to DEP (FA vs. DEP ± GSM), and then we examine GSM alone (FA vs. GSM) from the same experiments. Western blots of 20 μg of lysate were resolved on a 4% to 15% gradient or 7.5% acrylamide gels (Bio‐Rad). Dot blots loaded 50 μg of lysates (Bio‐Rad) and probed for Aβ38 (808601, BioLegend), Aβ40 (805401, BioLegend), or Aβ42 (805501, BioLegend). For western blot: APP (802801, BioLegend), BACE1 (12807‐1‐AP, Proteintech), and PS1 (16163‐1‐AP, Proteintech). Membranes were visualized by fluorescent‐conjugated secondary antibodies and quantified with ImageJ, corrected for total protein. Representative western blot images are presented in Figure S2 in supporting information.

Secretase activity

β‐ and γ‐secretases were assayed in post‐nuclear cerebral cortex fractions. 30 , 31 Tissue was homogenized in extraction buffer (50 mM Tris‐HCl, pH 7.4, 150 mM NaCl, 0.25% deoxycholic acid, 0.1% NP‐40, 1 mM EDTA) and centrifuged 10,000 × g for 5 minutes. Enzymatic assays used 50 μg of protein for activity at pH 7.4 for γ‐secretase and pH 4.5 for β‐secretase. Reaction rates were calculated for fluorogenic substrates of secretases: β (20 μM, 565758, Sigma‐Aldrich) and γ (50 μM, 565764, Sigma‐Aldrich).

Statistics

Statistical tests used Prism 10.2.2, GraphPad Software, LLC. Comparisons among three groups or more were made using two‐way analysis of variance with Tukey post hoc and the Shapiro–Wilk test to assess normal distribution. Unpaired two‐tailed t tests were used in graphs comparing two groups (GSM vs. FA), and non‐parametric comparisons used Mann–Whitney. Outliers were identified with the Grubbs test. Changes and P values for statistics are included for nPM (Table S1 in supporting information) and DEP (Table S2 in supporting information).

RESULTS

Soluble amyloid peptides and APP processing enzymes were assayed from the cerebral cortex of wild‐type mice exposed to nPM and DEP, with and without GSM‐15606. Data are presented in two groups. First, GSM‐15606 effects in response to nPM and DEP. Second, GSM alone from the same DEP experiments.

Attenuation of amyloidogenic effects of nPM and DEP with GSM‐15606 treatment

Decreased soluble Aβ peptides with GSM‐15606

Aβ42 is shown first because of its relevance to AD. GSM‐15606 decreased Aβ42 during exposure to two components of ambient AirPoll: nPM (Figure 2A) or DEP (Figure 2B). DEP did not alter APP levels (Figure S3 in supporting information). DEP increased Aβ42 in male mice (Figure S4 in supporting information) consistent with our prior findings with a 2‐fold larger group of male mice only. 13 Experiments with nPM were not continued because the activity of nPM batches progressively decreased over several years. 22 , 23 DEP has greater replicability. 13 The smaller Aβ peptides Aβ38 and Aβ40 are shown in Figure S3.

GSM‐15606 treatment effects in the cerebral cortex of wild‐type mice

Decreased amyloidogenic soluble amyloid peptide Aβ42 with GSM‐15606

We evaluated the impact of GSM‐15606 alone on APP and selected Aβ peptide products (Figure 4). Total APP level was unchanged, while Aβ42 decreased by 23% and Aβ38 increased by 50%, confirming prior studies. 17 , 32 Aβ40 and total APP did not respond to GSM‐15606.

Brain volume after DEP exposure and GSM‐15606 treatment

Brain volumes were evaluated by in vivo MRI in mice because human clinical studies showed increased lateral ventricular volume (LV) from monoclonal antibody (MoAb) therapy and BACE inhibition. 33 , 34 , 35 Here, LV was increased by 25% by DEP (Figure 6A). Cerebral cortex volume and total brain volume (excluding cerebellum and brain stem) did not change with DEP ± GSM‐15606 (Figure 6B,C).

DISCUSSION

Three findings were obtained with wild‐type mice fed 8 weeks on the γ‐secretase modulator GSM‐15606. First, GSM‐15606 decreased Aβ42 during exposure to nPM and DEP. While nPM doubled Aβ42, the DEP response was smaller (+18%) and not significant. Prior findings showed a significant 30% Aβ42 increase after DEP with a larger number of male mice. Aβ38 and Aβ40 did not show consistent significant responses to GSM‐15606 plus nPM and DEP. The present study had a smaller number of male mice per experimental group because of the limitations of exposure chamber size. These effects on APP processing were expected from the lowering Aβ42 in wild‐type and AD transgenic mice by GSM‐15606. 17 , 18 Analysis by in vivo MRI shows a 25% volume increase in LV, suggesting cerebral tissue atrophy by DEP. The 100‐fold larger total brain volume (excluding cerebellum and brain stem) than the LV may obscure a potential small net loss of brain volume.

GSM may be considered for potential AirPoll protection of normal elderly and cognitively impaired. Elevated residential AirPoll PM2.5 is correlated with elderly cognitive deficits. 6 Even short‐term elevations of AirPoll exposure can cause oxidative and inflammatory responses. 36 Wild‐type mice also had elevated Aβ42 from exposure to woodsmoke, 37 while dust from the World Trade Center collapse increased the Aβ42/40 ratio in 5xFAD mice. 38 Cerebrovascular damage that is common in the elderly can synergize with AirPoll factors, as shown for DEP‐induced white matter with cerebral ischemia. 39 , 40

Future studies could examine mice for effects of age, sex, and apolipoprotein E allele, which increased amyloid deposits in female EFAD mice exposed to nPM. 8 Glial–neuron interaction and vascular roles of Aβ in normal brain function merit study in response to AirPoll. Current AD therapy with MoAbs slows cognitive loss in early AD stages with decreased brain amyloid load. 41 , 42 MoAb applicability to human AirPoll exposure is currently confounded by cerebral edema and hemorrhage. Based on the discussed rodent studies, MoAb‐associated hemorrhage may compound AirPoll‐derived damage. GSM‐15606 was discontinued for clinical development in 2018. Future clinical studies will use its structural analog GSM‐776890 (compound 2), 17 which was licensed to Acta Pharmaceuticals in 2022. This new pyridazine‐derived GSM analog has potent activity and improved in vivo pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics. 17 Brain imaging (e.g., positron emission tomography imaging for tracking of radiolabeled GSMs 43 ) may further identify brain regions affected by AirPoll to investigate protective effects of GSMs and other treatments.

CONCLUSIONS

AirPoll may increase AD risk through elevated cerebral Aβ accumulation. GSM and other secretase‐targeting treatments for Aβ reduction may consider potential compensation responses, as well as interactions with the AD exposome. 44 , 45 The present findings that GSM‐15606 protected mice from AirPoll components of DEP and nPM suggest benefits to the large at‐risk elderly population exposed to AirPoll. Further physiological functions of soluble Aβ in adult brains may guide consideration of GSMs for pre‐symptomatic aging treatment.

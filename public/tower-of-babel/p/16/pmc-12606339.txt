Ferroptosis biomarkers predict the outcomes of patients with acute ischemic stroke undergoing endovascular thrombectomy
Authors: Shin-Joe Yeh, Chih-Hao Chen, Yen-Heng Lin, Li-Kai Tsai, Chung-Wei Lee, Yu Sun, Sung-Chun Tang, Jiann-Shing Jeng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractFerroptosis contributes to ischemic injury in basic experiments, but the roles of ferroptosis in patients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS) who received reperfusion therapy were unclear. This study prospectively enrolled patients with AIS who underwent endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) and non-stroke controls between February 2019 and May 2023. The plasma levels of three ferroptosis biomarkers—heat shock protein 70 (HSP70), ferritin, and acyl-CoA synthetase long-chain family member 4 (ACSL4)—were measured at pre-EVT, immediately post-EVT, and 24 h post-EVT in stroke patients. Outcomes were assessed using modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score and mortality at 3 months. The study enrolled 150 stroke patients and 50 controls. Stroke patients had higher HSP70 levels than controls. Functional independence (mRS score 0–2) was achieved in 42.5% of 134 followed patients, which was inversely associated with ACSL4 level at 24 h (adjusted odds ratio [aOR] 0.12, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.03–0.56, P = 0.007) in multivariate logistic regression analyses. Morality occurred in 17.2% of these patients, which was inversely associated with pre-EVT HSP70 level (aOR 0.13, 95% CI 0.02–0.98, P = 0.048). In conclusion, ferroptosis was involved in patients with AIS who underwent EVT, and the plasma levels of ACSL4 and HSP70 were significantly associated with clinical outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-23289-0.Keywords: Acute ischemic stroke, Acyl-CoA synthetase long-chain family member 4, Endovascular thrombectomy, Ferritin, Ferroptosis, Heat shock protein 70Subject terms: Neuroscience, Biomarkers, Neurology

Introduction

Endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) has become a major recanalization therapy for large-vessel occlusion in patients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS), which improves the functional outcome of patients1. However, 31–50% of patients develop poor outcomes even after a good recanalization2,3. Attributable mechanisms included infarct expansion and reperfusion injury, and the latter was presented as brain edema or hemorrhagic transformation4,5.

AIS induces various neuropathological changes, including oxidative stress, neuroinflammation, and cell death6. Reperfusion of ischemic tissue further exaggerates oxidative stress due to increased production of reactive oxygen species (ROS)7. Oxidative stress triggers lipid peroxidation and damage to other cellular structures, ultimately leading to cell death8. Ferroptosis is a regulated form of cell death that links oxidative stress, lipid peroxidation, and neuroinflammation. The production of excessive ROS in ferroptosis is driven by dysregulated iron metabolism9. Inhibiting ferroptosis, for instance by targeting acyl-CoA synthetase long-chain family member 4 (ACSL4), has been shown to reduce infarct volume in animal models of stroke10. These findings suggest that ferroptosis may contribute to reperfusion injury and infarct expansion in ischemic stroke.

Although numerous studies have demonstrated the role of ferroptosis in ischemic injury using animal and cellular models11, only a few ferroptosis biomarkers have been identified in the context of human ischemic stroke. For example, homocysteine—an inhibitor of glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4), a key antioxidant in the ferroptosis pathway12—has been shown to be an independent predictor of poor functional outcomes and mortality at 3 months in patients with AIS, when measured immediately after EVT13.

In this study, three ferroptosis biomarkers, ferritin, ACSL4, and heat shock protein 70 (HSP70), were investigated in plasma samples from patients with AIS and controls. These biomarkers are responsible for different biological functions in ferroptosis. Ferritin was assessed because its degradation or high level links to the activation of ferroptosis based on its storage function for excessive cytoplasmic iron14–16. Iron deposition in brain is increased in ischemic stroke14, but the clinical roles of ferritin in AIS and reperfusion remain unclear. ACSL4 was also investigated in this study because it is a crucial enzyme for promoting ferroptosis by producing susceptible substrates for lipid peroxidation17. Knockdown or inhibition of ACSL4 attenuates cerebral ischemic injury and lipid peroxidation in mice18,19, but the clinical implications of ACSL4 have not been reported in stroke patients receiving reperfusion therapy. Additionally, HSP70 downregulates ferroptosis by preventing the degradation of a pivotal antioxidant that inhibits lipid peroxidation in cellular models20, and pharmacological upregulation of HSP70 protects mouse brains from ischemic injury21. However, it remains unknown whether HSP70 contributes to the functional improvement of reperfusion therapy in ischemic stroke. As ferritin, ACSL4, and HSP70 are critical molecules in the ferroptosis pathway with unclear clinical roles in cerebral ischemia and reperfusion, this study aimed to explore the changes and clinical roles of these three molecules in patients with AIS who underwent EVT treatment.

Methods

Participants

This prospective study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of National Taiwan University Hospital (reference numbers: 201807029RINA and 201912233RINC) and has been performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Consecutive patients with AIS who underwent EVT between February 2019 and May 2023 at our hospital were enrolled, and informed consent was obtained from each patient or their legal representatives if their consciousness or cognition was impaired at that moment. The unmatched control group included stroke-free subjects recruited from the hospital’s health examination center and cardiovascular outpatient clinic.

Clinical variables

This study collected data on stroke-related clinical variables, including age, sex, and vascular risk factors, such as hypertension, diabetes mellitus, dyslipidemia, coronary artery disease, atrial fibrillation, prior stroke, smoking, alcohol consumption, and cancer. Stroke severity was assessed using the National Institute of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) scores evaluated at admission, with higher scores indicating more severe stroke. The NIHSS score was re-evaluated at 24 h after EVT to determine the post-treatment stroke severity. The stroke subtype was classified as large-artery atherosclerosis (LAA), cardioembolism (CE), lacunar stroke, other determined causes, and undetermined causes according to the Trial of Org 10,172 in Acute Stroke Treatment (TOAST) classification method22. The laboratory data collected at admission included hemoglobin level, platelet count, white cell count, creatinine, low-density lipoprotein (LDL), triglyceride (TG), glucose, and glycated hemoglobin (HbA1C) levels. The extent of early infarct lesion on the initial brain CT images was assessed by the Alberta Stroke Programme Early CT Score (ASPECTS) in patients with acute occlusion of the internal carotid artery (ICA) or middle cerebral artery (MCA)23. The brain CT perfusion imaging was quantified by the RAPID system to investigate the volumes of infarct core, hypoperfusion region, and penumbra region. The application of recanalization therapies, including intravenous thrombolysis with tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) and endovascular thrombectomy, was determined according to guidelines24. The reperfusion states achieved by EVT were classified by modified Treatment in Cerebral Infarction (mTICI) scores at the end of the EVT procedures, in which ≥ 2b is regarded as a good reperfusion25. Regarding the complications of EVT, hemorrhagic transformation and brain edema were assessed by brain CT or MRI scans at 24 h after EVT or clinical deterioration. A hemorrhagic transformation which was presented as a homogeneous hemorrhagic lesion with a sharp border was classified as parenchymal hematoma (PH). Brain edema was denified if the the swelling of the infarct region was accompanied with a substantial mass effect. A patient was regarded to have reperfusion injury if he/she had PH or brain edema on the followed brain images. If hemorrhagic transformation was combined with neurological deterioration (increase in NIHSS score by 2 or more scores), it was defined as symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage. Outcomes at 3 months were assessed using the modified Rankin scale (mRS) score and mortality, in which functional independence was defined as an mRS score 0–2.

Assessment of plasma levels of ferroptosis biomarkers

Plasma samples were collected thrice from stroke patients (immediately before EVT, immediately after EVT, and 24 h after EVT), and once from the non-stroke controls. The plasma levels of three ferroptosis biomarkers⸺HSP70, ferritin, and ACSL4⸺were detected using ELISA (kits: HSP70: R&D, No. DY1663-05; ferritin: RayBiotech, No. ELH-Ferritin; ACSL4: MyBioSource, No. MBS9331516).

Statistical analysis

Categorical data were compared using the chi-square test. The t-test was used to compare continuous data between two groups if they were compatible with a normal distribution; otherwise, the Mann-Whitney U test was used. The levels of ferroptosis biomarker were log-transformed before statistical analysis owing to significant variations. Biomarker levels were compared between the control and stroke groups, and between different time points in the stroke group. To further reveal the roles of these biomarkers in specific clinical situations, we compared their levels according to the initial NIHSS score (stroke severity), ASPECTS on initial CT scans, CTP parameters, tPA use, stroke subtypes, mTICI scores, and reperfusion injuries. In assessing the associations between biomarker levels and functional independence or mortality, the significant clinical variables identified from multivariate logistic regression models of clinical parameters were applied as adjusted covariates in the multivariate logistic regression models for the associations between the biomarker levels and clinical outcomes. Statistical significance was set, and less than 0.05 were considered significant.

Biomarker levels in the control and stroke groups

The log-transformed plasma levels of HSP70, ferritin, and ACSL4 in these patients and controls are shown in Fig. 1. Compared to the controls, these stroke patients had higher HSP70 levels at these three time points, which were the highest before EVT (at timepoint T1) (P < 0.0001) and decreased immediately after EVT (at T2) (P < 0.0001) (Fig. 1A). The ferritin levels in stroke patients reduced immediately after EVT compared to those before EVT (P = 0.001), which were increased at 24 h (P < 0.0001) (Fig. 1B). Regarding ACSL4, the levels in stroke patients were elevated immediately after EVT compared to those before EVT (P < 0.0001), followed by a significant decrease at 24 h (P < 0.0001) (Fig. 1C). A common finding of these biomarkers was rapid changes in their levels immediately after EVT compared to those before EVT (P < 0.0001 for HSP70 and ACSL4; P = 0.001 for ferritin).

In contrast, ferritin levels were not different between the two groups under any status of these comorbidities.

Associations of biomarker levels and stroke outcomes

In these 150 patients with AIS, 134 (89.3%) patients were available for their functional outcomes and survival status at 3 months, while the other 16 patients were lost to follow up. Among the 134 followed patients, functional independence was achieved in 57 patients (42.5%). After adjustment of significant clinical variables, age, NIHSS score at 24 h and hemoglobin level were the only three clinical variables significantly associated with functional independence (Supplemental Table 3). In addition, ACSL4 level at 24 h (at T3) (aOR 0.12, 95% CI 0.03–0.56, P = 0.007) was the only one biomarker that was associated with functional independence after adjustment of significant clinical variables (Table 2). The cutoff value of log-transformed ACSL4 level at 24 h for predicting functional independence was 1.06 ng/mL (corresponding to a raw value of 11.47 ng/mL), with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.94, sensitivity of 82%, and specificity of 95%.

Relationship between biomarker levels and stroke-related parameters

We further investigated the associations between biomarker levels and stroke-related clinical and imaging parameters to better understand their potential roles in disease severity and outcomes (Fig. 2). Patients with a high initial stroke severity (NIHSS score ≥ 22) had higher HSP70 levels before EVT and immediately after EVT (P = 0.019 and 0.002, respectively) (Fig. 2A). Compared to patients without receiving tPA thrombolysis, those receiving tPA thrombolysis had higher HSP70 levels before EVT and immediately after EVT (P = 0.002 and 0.001, respectively), followed by a significant decrease at 24 h (P = 0.016) (Fig. 2B). Potential confounding factors for biomarker levels regarding tPA thrombolysis were shown in Supplemental Table 5. Patients with a lower ASPECTS on the initial brain CT scan (ASPECTS < 8) had higher ferritin levels before EVT and immediately after EVT (P = 0.043 and 0.027, respectively) (Fig. 2C). Furthermore, ferritin levels before and after EVT were also higher in patients with non-cardioembolism (P = 0.003 and 0.005, respectively; Fig. 2D) or poor reperfusion (mTICI < 2b) (P = 0.049, 0.019, 0.004, respectively; Fig. 2E).

Relationship between biomarker levels and reperfusion injury

After EVT, 51 (34.0%), 26 (17.3%) and 48 (32.0%) patients developed reperfusion injury, PH, and brain edema, respectively. These biomarker levels did not significantly differ by these reperfusion complications (Supplemental Fig. 1).

Discussion

A common feature among patients with AIS who undergo EVT is large-vessel occlusion accompanied by a sizeable ischemic area. Experimental studies have demonstrated the critical role of ferroptosis in ischemic brain injury, particularly following reperfusion. However, little is known about the changes in ferroptosis-related biomarkers—such as HSP70, ferritin, and ACSL4—in response to EVT in human subjects. This study investigated the changes in these biomarkers and their clinical significance in patients with AIS treated with EVT. The key findings were as follows: higher HSP70 levels before EVT were associated with a reduced risk of 3-month mortality, while elevated ACSL4 levels at 24 h post-EVT independently predicted functional dependence. Moreover, post-EVT levels of HSP70 and ACSL4 were correlated with pre-EVT CT perfusion parameters, supporting their potential utility in outcome prediction. We also found that HSP70 levels increased following AIS and were higher in patients with more severe strokes. Additionally, elevated ferritin levels were observed in patients with a larger infarct core, non-cardioembolic stroke etiology, or poor reperfusion.

HSP70, an intracellular chaperone that support homeostasis of cellular proteins, is an essential component of endogenous neuroprotective system26. The neuroprotective function of HSP70 is directly attributed to the maintenance of antioxidant system and modulation of neuroinflammation27. In the ferroptosis pathway, HSP70 promotes GPX4 expression and augments its antioxidant activity, inhibiting the production of lipid ROS to prevent cell death28. Several studies have shown the effect of modulating HSP70 in stroke animal models. For example, knockout of hsp70.1 in mice led to a larger infarct volume after cerebral artery occlusion29, and overexpression of HSP70 in mice offered protection against cerebral ischemic injury30. However, there was little evidence regarding the clinical roles of plasma HSP70 levels in patients with AIS and the response of HSP70 to reperfusion. Our study showed increased HSP70 levels in patients with AIS since pre-EVT, which is compatible with the findings in animal models that HSP70 was inducible in the ischemic brain31. We also found that HSP70 levels were higher in patients with higher initial NIHSS scores, and they were reduced immediately after EVT, indicating the rapid reduction of HSP70 production in response to the amelioration of ischemic stress by EVT. Furthermore, this study showed that HSP70 levels before EVT were inversely associated with mortality risk in 3 months. Because the upregulation of HSP70 in animal stroke models was mainly in the penumbral region, which was salvageable brain tissue32, a high HSP70 level immediately before EVT probably corresponded to a large penumbra that was salvageable after successful recanalization, which might explain the lower mortality after EVT. Additionally, our findings showed that HSP70 levels were higher in patients who underwent thrombolysis. Although HSP70 levels in patients undergoing thrombolysis have been reported to peak at 1 h after thrombolysis33, other factors might interfere with this finding in our study, such as higher blood pressure in the thrombolysis group, because acute high blood pressure could induce the production of HSP70 in rats34. These findings filled the evidence gap of HSP70 in the clinical application in AIS, and HSP70 level before EVT is a potential indicator for 3-month mortality.

ACSL4 promotes ferroptosis by facilitating lipid peroxidation, and basic experiments have proven the effect of inhibition of ACSL4 on alleviating ferroptosis10. A previous study found that patients with AIS had increased ACSL4 mRNA levels within 24 h compared to controls, which was higher in those with NIHSS scores ≥ 5 compared to lower scores35. However, although 96% of patients in this study had NIHSS scores ≥ 5, they did not have higher ACSL4 levels before EVT compared to controls, probably because the blood sampling was too early to detect significant ACSL4 level increment. In addition, the ACSL4 levels elevated immediately after EVT compared to the pre-EVT state, which was probably attributed to the effect of reperfusion, facilitating the release of ACSL4 from the ischemic brain region. Importantly, our study also revealed that a higher ACSL4 level at 24 h was associated with functional dependence at 3 months, which was probably parallel with a prior finding that higher ACSL4 levels at admission predicted worse recovery of lower limb function after rehabilitation therapy36. These features of ACSL4 levels revealed in this study might reflect the pathophysiological changes of ACSL4 in response to ischemia and reperfusion.

In addition to maintaining iron homeostasis, ferritin is an acute-phase reactant that is upregulated by oxidative stress for rapid iron sequestration to decrease iron availability for the production of free radicals37. Inconsistent results have been reported regarding the relationship between ferritin levels and functional outcomes after AIS. A meta-analysis encompassing 6,021 patients of ischemic stroke showed that ferritin levels were not significantly associated with functional outcome38. In contrast, another cohort study revealed that hyperferritinemia was an independent predictor of poor functional outcome at 3 months in patients with AIS combined with hyperglycemia39. Additionally, there have been no prior studies investigating ferritin levels in patients with AIS who undergo EVT. This present study showed no association of ferritin levels with stroke outcomes in these patients. However, this study revealed several features of the ferritin levels in these patients. First, ferritin levels were affected by ischemic stroke severity based on the observations that (1) patients with smaller infarct cores had lower ferritin levels and (2) ferritin levels decreased immediately after EVT compared to those before EVT. Second, patients with cardioembolism had lower ferritin levels than non-cardioembolism. Since large-artery atherosclerosis is the most common stroke etiology aside from cardioembolism in patients undergoing EVT, this finding might be explained that atherosclerosis is associated with higher ferritin levels40. Similarly, our study found that patients with poor reperfusion (mTICI score < 2b) had higher ferritin levels before and after EVT compared to those with good reperfusion. Since poor reperfusion was more likely to occur in the vessels occluded by atherosclerosis, the relationship between atherosclerosis and ferritin might be a confounder underlying this finding.

For potential clinical applications, HSP70 level before EVT and ACSL4 level at 24 h after EVT can be used to predict mortality and functional independence at 3 months after ischemic stroke. Besides, higher ferritin level might indicate non-cardioembolism in stroke patients who received EVT, which could be helpful to guide evaluations for patients with unclear stroke mechanism based on early clinical information. Aside from routinely available neurological assessment and neuroimages, these biomarkers may provide additional aid in predicting outcome and guiding stroke mechanism evaluation.

The strengths of this study are a prospective cohort design, serial blood sampling before and after EVT, and easily availability of plasma samples to reveal the changes of biomarker levels related to stroke and reperfusion. However, this study has several limitations. First, these biomarkers are not brain-specific proteins, thus their plasma levels represent the composite effect of AIS on the whole body instead of purely on the brain. However, the serial changes in these biomarker levels did reflect the impact of cerebral ischemia and reperfusion on these biomarkers. Second, because cerebral ischemic damage before EVT consists of progressive transformation of penumbra to infarct core, the variable ischemic duration before EVT might have affected these biomarker levels. Third, the CT perfusion images were not quantified for all patients, which might lead to underestimation of their correlations with biomarker levels. Fourth, the association of HSP level with mortality yielded a borderline significance, particularly in light of multiple comparisons. However, no correction for multiple comparisons was applied due to the exploratory nature of the study.

Conclusions

This study extends the knowledge of ferroptosis in cerebral ischemia from basic experiments to human subjects, revealing the clinical roles of these ferroptosis biomarkers in patients with AIS who underwent EVT. HSP70 and ACSL4 levels were independent predictors of stroke outcomes at three months, and they were also correlated with pre-EVT CT perfusion parameters. Interventions focusing on manipulating these biomarkers in combination with EVT will be worthy of investigation in future studies.

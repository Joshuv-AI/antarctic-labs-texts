Determinants of Long-Term Transfer Difficulty and Global Caregiver Satisfaction after Neuromuscular Scoliosis Surgery: A Dissociation between Task-Specific Burden and Perceived Benefit
Authors: Naoyuki Nakamura, Yuichiro Kawabe, Kouji Yamamoto, Masatoshi Oba, Kayo Tsuzawa, Yumika Yokoyama, Kosuke Sumi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction Caregiver satisfaction after neuromuscular scoliosis (NMS) surgery is generally high; however, the relationship between task-specific caregiving burden and global treatment satisfaction remains unclear. This study aimed to identify patient- and caregiver-related determinants of transfer difficulty and global satisfaction in the long-term postoperative period. Methods This cross-sectional follow-up study included individuals who underwent posterior spinal fusion for NMS between 2012 and 2020 and were ≥18 years at survey distribution. Caregivers completed a modified Bridwell questionnaire using 0-10 scales. Multivariate linear regression identified determinants of transfer difficulty and global satisfaction, with false discovery rate (FDR) correction applied. Results Sixty-two caregivers responded (63% response rate). In the patient-related model, higher adult body weight (β=−0.14, p_FDR=0.077) and greater residual Cobb angle (β=−0.082, p_FDR=0.065) showed trends in transfer difficulty scores, though neither reached the p_FDR<0.05 threshold. Among caregiver-reported domains, difficulty with changing clothes (β=0.75, p_FDR=0.081) demonstrated a similar trend. Global satisfaction was significantly associated with better respiratory status (β=0.36, p_FDR=0.023) and improved swallowing (β=−0.16, p_FDR=0.047). Transfer difficulty showed no association with global satisfaction (β=−0.007, p_FDR=0.90). Conclusions These findings represent exploratory associations observed at a single time point in adulthood and do not imply causal relationships. Transfer difficulty in adulthood appears to be primarily associated with body size and residual spinal deformity, whereas global satisfaction is primarily associated with improvements in respiratory and swallowing function. These findings indicate a dissociation between task-specific caregiving burden and global treatment satisfaction, suggesting that caregivers may affirm long-term treatment value despite ongoing physical challenges.

Introduction

Neuromuscular scoliosis (NMS) is characterized by progressive spinal deformity, impaired sitting balance, and substantial caregiving burden. Surgical correction is commonly undertaken to improve posture, seating tolerance, and overall comfort. Previous studies have consistently demonstrated marked gains in health-related quality of life (HRQoL) and consistently high caregiver satisfaction following spinal fusion in this population, largely attributed to enhanced trunk stability, reduced pain, and improved ease of daily care1-4).

Despite these favorable outcomes, the long-term caregiver experience after NMS surgery remains insufficiently understood, particularly as patients grow, gain weight, and transition into adulthood5). Previous studies have focused primarily on early postoperative results, leaving later caregiving challenges less clearly defined. In our previous follow-up of the same cohort, we observed that although global treatment satisfaction remained high, some caregivers reported persistent or increased difficulty with transfers over time6). This discrepancy suggests a divergence between global satisfaction and task-specific caregiving demands—an issue that has not been thoroughly examined in the literature.

To address this gap, the present study evaluates individuals who underwent NMS surgery in childhood and later transitioned into adulthood. The primary aim was to identify patient- and caregiver-related determinants of transfer difficulty in the long-term postoperative period. A secondary aim was to determine which postoperative domains most strongly influence global caregiver satisfaction. By clarifying the relationship between mechanical caregiving demands and broader functional outcomes, this study seeks to enhance understanding of the long-term caregiver experience following NMS surgery. The findings may help inform future surgical counseling and long-term care strategies.

Materials and Methods

Study design and participants

This cross-sectional follow-up study evaluated long-term outcomes in patients who underwent posterior spinal fusion for NMS at our institution. Eligibility criteria were: (1) surgery performed between 2012 and 2020; (2) at least four years of postoperative follow-up; and (3) age ≥18 years at the time of survey distribution, consistent with standard definitions of adulthood in clinical practice. Given that the study focused on long-term caregiver-reported outcomes in non-ambulatory individuals, only patients with Gross Motor Function Classification System (GMFCS) levels IV or V were included. Restricting inclusion to GMFCS levels IV or V ensured homogeneity of functional status and allowed the analysis to focus on mechanical caregiving demands and functional outcomes in a population with the highest transfer burden.

Of 101 eligible patients, two had died from progression of their primary disease before the survey period. The final cohort included 99 patients (56 females, 43 males). Baseline characteristics (median, range) at the time of surgery were: age, 15.0 years (10.4-19.9); height, 138 cm (103-170); weight, 26.3 kg (11.5-50.1); body mass index, 14.3 (9.3-22.2); major Cobb angle, 99° (48-142); and pelvic obliquity, 27° (0-62). Upper instrumented vertebrae ranged from T1 to T4, with the majority at T3. Lower instrumented vertebrae ranged from L3 to the pelvis, and most patients underwent fusion to L5 or pelvic fixation. The median number of fused vertebrae was 15 (range, 13-17). Primary diagnoses included cerebral palsy in 78 patients (79%), with the remaining patients comprising a heterogeneous group of various neuromuscular disorders (chromosomal abnormalities, Rett syndrome, spinal muscular atrophy, muscular dystrophy, mitochondrial encephalomyopathy, etc.).

Caregiver survey

Long-term caregiver perceptions were assessed using a modified Bridwell caregiver questionnaire. This modified scale has been utilized in our prior institutional follow-up studies2,4). All items were scored on an 11-point scale (0-10) and entered electronically using Google Forms. Responses were submitted voluntarily and were automatically anonymized through the survey platform.

Although the questionnaire produces ordinal responses, numerical scores derived from the 0-10 scale were treated as continuous variables for multiple linear regression analyses. This approach has been widely used in caregiver-reported outcome research and HRQoL studies, as 11-point scales often exhibit near-normal distribution and allow for greater statistical power in parametric testing. Non-parametric correlations were performed as secondary analyses. Internal consistency of the modified Bridwell questionnaire was evaluated for caregiving-related domains, with a sensitivity analysis restricted to task-specific caregiving maneuvers.

Collected variables included patient demographic and anthropometric data, the most recent postoperative radiographic parameters (major Cobb angle and pelvic obliquity within one year of survey completion), and all questionnaire domain scores. Global treatment satisfaction, the dependent variable for the third regression model, was specifically defined by a single 0-10 scale item: “Overall satisfaction with the surgical outcome.” Two additional global items assessed comprehensive satisfaction: (1) “If you could return to the preoperative state, would you choose the same surgery again?” and (2) “Would you recommend this surgery to another family with a child with a similar condition?” (These two dichotomous items were utilized solely for descriptive analysis of the cohort and were not included in the regression models as dependent variables.)

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses addressed three objectives: (1) identifying patient characteristics associated with transfer difficulty, (2) determining caregiver-reported domains related to transfer difficulty, and (3) identifying factors associated with global treatment satisfaction. Continuous variables were summarized as medians with ranges, and categorical variables as counts and percentages.

Transfer difficulty and global satisfaction, each recorded on 0-10 scales, were treated as continuous variables for multiple linear regression. Model diagnostics were performed to assess the appropriateness of the regression models, including checks for multicollinearity among independent variables. Multicollinearity among independent variables was assessed using variance inflation factors (VIFs). All VIF values were below 3.0, indicating no substantial multicollinearity in the regression model.

To identify patient-related determinants of transfer difficulty, a regression model was constructed with transfer score as the dependent variable and age, height, body weight, pelvic fixation, residual Cobb angle, residual spinopelvic obliquity, and postoperative interval as independent variables. In this model, age, height, body weight, residual Cobb angle, residual spinopelvic obliquity, and postoperative interval were treated as continuous variables, whereas pelvic fixation (yes/no) was treated as a binary variable. A second model examined relationships between transfer difficulty and all caregiver-reported questionnaire domains, including functional, physiological, and caregiving tasks.

A third regression model evaluated determinants of global treatment satisfaction and included patient demographic and radiographic variables as well as all caregiver-reported domains, including transfer difficulty.

Regression coefficients (β) and p-values were reported. The false discovery rate (FDR) was controlled using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure across all relevant analyses. A two-tailed FDR-adjusted p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Analyses were conducted using EZR (Saitama Medical Center, Jichi Medical University, Japan), a graphical user interface for R (version 4.2.2).

Ethical approval

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board. The requirement for written informed consent was waived because the survey was conducted anonymously and posed minimal risk to participants.

Patient-related determinants of transfer difficulty

In the multivariate model examining patient factors, higher body weight (β=−0.14, p=0.022; p_FDR=0.077) and greater residual Cobb angle (β=−0.082, p=0.009; p_FDR=0.065) showed the most notable associations with transfer difficulty scores among the patient-related variables. Although neither variable met the FDR-adjusted significance threshold, both demonstrated consistent trends in relation to transfer difficulty. No other variables showed meaningful associations (all p_FDR>0.10) (Table 2).

Global satisfaction responses

When asked whether they would choose the same surgery again, 79% of caregivers responded “yes.” Regarding whether they would recommend the surgery to another family, 65% answered affirmatively. Non-affirmative responses were primarily related to the magnitude of the surgery rather than dissatisfaction with the outcome.

Discussion

This study sought to clarify long-term caregiver-reported outcomes in individuals with severe NMS who had transitioned into adulthood after posterior spinal fusion. By focusing exclusively on non-ambulatory patients with GMFCS levels IV-V and employing a modified Bridwell caregiver questionnaire, we aimed to delineate (1) patient-related factors associated with transfer difficulty, (2) caregiving tasks most strongly linked with transfer burden, and (3) determinants of global treatment satisfaction. Several important findings emerged. Given that this study employed a cross-sectional design at a single time point after transition to adulthood, causal inferences cannot be drawn, and all observed relationships should be interpreted as exploratory associations.

Factors influencing long-term transfer difficulty: Clinical trends in mechanical burden

In the model analyzing patient-related determinants of transfer difficulty, two variables—higher adult body weight and greater residual Cobb angle—showed the strongest statistical associations (p_FDR 0.07 and 0.06, respectively). Although neither reached the pre-specified significance threshold after FDR correction, both demonstrated strong and consistent trends. These findings align with the clinical observation that increased body mass can amplify mechanical load during dependent transfers, particularly when trunk stability remains compromised. Similarly, greater residual spinal deformity may limit optimal sitting posture and restrict available handholds or leverage during transfer maneuvers. Although upper and lower instrumented vertebrae were reviewed, variability in fusion extent was limited, with most patients undergoing long-segment fusion from the upper thoracic spine to the lumbosacral junction or pelvis. This homogeneity likely reduced the ability to detect independent effects of fusion extent on transfer difficulty.

Prior studies have described improved ease of care—including dressing, bathing, and sitting tolerance—after NMS surgery2,4,7), yet few have evaluated the durability of these improvements into adulthood5,6). The present results suggest that while spinal fusion provides substantial long-term benefits, transfer-related caregiving demands may evolve as patients age and gain weight, leading to renewed challenges over time. These nuanced trends, even if not statistically significant after FDR adjustment, provide clinically meaningful insight into the biomechanical reality faced by caregivers. It is plausible that the heterogeneous nature of the primary diagnoses within our GMFCS IV-V cohort (ranging from cerebral palsy to various myopathies) introduced sufficient biological variability to attenuate the statistical significance of these mechanical factors in the regression model.

Caregiver task analysis: “Changing clothes” as a practical indicator of transfer difficulty

Among all caregiver-reported domains, “changing clothes” demonstrated the strongest relationship with transfer scores (p FDR=0.08), again as a statistical trend. Dressing is a dynamic task requiring both limb manipulation and significant trunk control and stability; thus, difficulties in this domain may reflect underlying mechanical challenges stemming from residual spinal or pelvic instability similar to those encountered during transfers. The parallel movement of these two tasks suggests that the dressing score may serve as a sensitive functional proxy for transfer burden in adults with severe neuromuscular impairment, even when individual predictors fail to reach FDR-adjusted significance.

Determinants of global satisfaction: Trunk-related functional improvements as the key drivers

A central and clinically important finding is that long-term global satisfaction was independently associated with improvements in torso-related vital function—specifically better respiratory status (p FDR=0.023) and improved swallowing (p FDR=0.047). These domains remained significant even after controlling for the FDR, underscoring their robust contribution to perceived benefit.

Respiratory and swallowing improvements directly influence comfort, safety, and overall well-being in individuals with severe neuromuscular conditions. The prioritization of these vital functions (respiratory and swallowing) over mechanical caregiving tasks (such as transferring) by caregivers when judging treatment value likely explains why transfer difficulty showed no association with global satisfaction. Importantly, global treatment satisfaction represents a holistic appraisal of long-term treatment value rather than the absence of specific caregiving challenges. Transfer difficulty may persist or re-emerge over time due to patient growth and aging, independent of caregivers' overall assessment of surgical success. These findings suggest a dissociation between task-specific caregiving burden and global satisfaction in the long-term postoperative course. This divergence between practical caregiving burden and global satisfaction has been described in earlier qualitative studies2,6,8,9), and the present quantitative analysis extends this distinction in a long-term adult cohort.

The high rates of affirmative responses to the two global satisfaction items—79% indicating they would choose the surgery again and 65% recommending it to another family—further emphasize that perceived functional benefits outweigh the persistence or recurrence of task-specific caregiving challenges. Importantly, the non-affirmative responses were attributed to the magnitude of the procedure, not dissatisfaction with surgical outcomes.

Clinical implications

The present findings have several important clinical implications for the long-term management of individuals with NMS after spinal fusion. First, the dissociation observed between transfer difficulty and global caregiver satisfaction highlights that persistent physical caregiving challenges do not necessarily undermine the perceived overall value of surgery. This distinction should be explicitly addressed during preoperative counseling, particularly when discussing long-term expectations with families of non-ambulatory patients.

Second, the association between transfer difficulty and modifiable mechanical factors—such as body weight and residual spinal deformity—suggests that long-term caregiving burden may be influenced not only by surgical correction itself but also by postoperative growth, nutritional management, and seating or positioning strategies. Ongoing multidisciplinary follow-up, including rehabilitation and assistive device optimization, may therefore be essential to mitigate transfer-related burden as patients transition into adulthood.

In contrast, global caregiver satisfaction was driven primarily by improvements in respiratory and swallowing function, underscoring the importance of preserving and enhancing torso-related vital functions when evaluating the long-term success of NMS surgery. These findings emphasize that surgical goals and postoperative assessments should extend beyond radiographic correction and mechanical ease of care to include functional outcomes that directly affect comfort, safety, and medical stability.

Strengths and limitations

This study has several strengths. By focusing exclusively on non-ambulatory individuals with GMFCS levels IV-V who had transitioned into adulthood, we evaluated a clinically homogeneous population with substantial long-term caregiving demands. The use of caregiver-reported outcomes allowed direct assessment of real-world caregiving burden and satisfaction, which are not fully captured by radiographic or clinician-based measures. Furthermore, the application of multivariate analysis with FDR correction strengthened the robustness of the findings by reducing the likelihood of false-positive associations.

Several limitations should also be acknowledged. First, the cross-sectional design precludes assessment of causal relationships or temporal changes in caregiving burden and satisfaction over time. Second, the sample size was relatively modest, which may have limited statistical power and contributed to several associations not reaching significance after FDR correction. Third, although a modified Bridwell questionnaire was used to capture caregiving domains, all outcomes were based on subjective caregiver reports and may be influenced by recall bias or individual perceptions. In addition, although caregiver age and sex may influence transfer difficulty, these data were not included in the analysis because the institutional review board classified them as sensitive personal information, resulting in substantial missing data due to voluntary non-response.

Additionally, the study cohort included patients with heterogeneous underlying neuromuscular diagnoses, which may have introduced biological variability and attenuated the strength of associations in the regression models. Finally, because this was a single-center study conducted at a specialized pediatric orthopedic institution, the generalizability of the results to other settings may be limited.

Despite these limitations, the present study provides clinically meaningful insights into the complex and multifaceted nature of long-term caregiver experience after NMS surgery, particularly the dissociation between task-specific caregiving burden and global treatment satisfaction.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that long-term caregiver experience after NMS surgery is characterized by a dissociation between task-specific caregiving burden and global treatment satisfaction. Transfer difficulty in adulthood was primarily influenced by mechanical and time-dependent factors, including body size and residual spinal deformity, whereas global satisfaction was primarily associated with improvements in torso-related vital functions such as respiration and swallowing. These findings suggest that caregivers may continue to perceive substantial long-term benefit from surgery despite persistent or re-emerging physical challenges associated with daily care. Recognition of this dissociation is essential for realistic preoperative counseling, long-term follow-up planning, and the development of comprehensive care strategies that address both functional outcomes and caregiving burden in this population.

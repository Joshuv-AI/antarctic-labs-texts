Sugammadex versus neostigmine on postoperative adverse respiratory events in elderly patients undergoing lung resection: study protocol for a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Yuxin Wang, Xiang Li, Huimin Deng, Di Feng, Liuyuan Zhao, Feihong Lin, Tian Jin, Sheng Wang, Xin Lv, Juan Wei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundElderly patients undergoing lung resection are particularly susceptible to postoperative adverse respiratory events (AREs), due to age-related physiological decline and a higher incidence of residual neuromuscular blockade (RNMB). Sugammadex is a selective relaxant binding agent that enables rapid and complete reversal of aminosteroid neuromuscular blocking agents such as rocuronium, offering potential advantages over traditional acetylcholinesterase inhibitors like neostigmine. However, evidence directly comparing the impact of sugammadex versus neostigmine on clinically relevant respiratory outcomes, particularly AREs in elderly patients undergoing lung resection remains limited.Methods and analysisThis is a prospective, double-blinded, randomized controlled clinical trial involving 530 elderly patients (≥65 years old) scheduled for video-assisted thoracoscopic lung surgery requiring one-lung ventilation. Patients will be 1:1 randomized to receive sugammadex or neostigmine for neuromuscular blockade reversal. The primary outcome is a composite of AREs occurring in the post-anaesthesia care unit (PACU). Secondary outcomes include time from neuromuscular to extubation, the composite of ARE and rescue measures, PACU length of stay, postoperative pulmonary and extra-pulmonary complications, unplanned intensive care unit (ICU) admission, chest drainage tube duration, hospital length of stay, and mortality. All patients will be followed up until 30 days after surgery. The primary analysis will be conducted on a modified intention-to-treat basis.DiscussionThis trial is designed to address a critical gap in perioperative care for elderly patients undergoing lung resection by directly comparing sugammadex and neostigmine in terms of clinically meaningful respiratory outcomes. While previous studies have primarily focused on pharmacokinetic profiles or surrogate markers such as train-of-four recovery time, evidence regarding the impact of these agents on postoperative AREs remains limited. Given the growing elderly surgical population and the high incidence of RNMB and pulmonary complications in this group, the findings of this study will offer valuable insight into optimizing neuromuscular blockade reversal strategies.Trial registrationThis study is registered at Chinese clinical trial registry (ChiCTR2400094005).

Introduction

With global population aging, the number of elderly patients diagnosed with lung cancer and undergoing surgical resection continues to rise [1,2]. Video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) lung surgery under one-lung ventilation (OLV) poses substantial perioperative risks, especially in older adults [3]. While OLV optimizes surgical exposure, it imposes significant physiological stress through ventilation–perfusion mismatch and alveolar injury [4], elevating risk of postoperative adverse respiratory events (AREs) including sustained hypoxemia, airway obstruction, aspiration, unplanned non-invasive ventilation or re-intubation [5], which would significantly impair recovery and are associated with increased postoperative morbidity and mortality, especially in the elderly.

VATS, now widely adopted for anatomical lung resection, requires OLV and deeper levels of neuromuscular blockade to optimize surgical conditions. These intraoperative requirements further compromise ventilation–perfusion matching and pulmonary mechanics, particularly in elderly patients who often exhibit diminished physiological reserves, baseline pulmonary dysfunction and increased frailty [6–8]. Notably, the reported incidence of residual neuromuscular blockade (RNMB), conventionally defined as a train-of-four (TOF) ratio <0.9, ranges from 26% to 88% depending on the monitoring modality and population studied [9,10]. Although RNMB is not the sole determinant of postoperative AREs, it represents a major modifiable risk factor. Even mild RNMB critically impairs respiratory function and the ability to swallow and maintain a patent airway [11], particularly in older patients [12]. Studies have shown that patients with RNMB are more likely to experience early AREs following extubation [13,14], emphasizing the importance of complete neuromuscular recovery in reducing perioperative risk.

Acetylcholinesterase inhibitors such as neostigmine have been the standard pharmacologic agents used to reverse non-depolarizing neuromuscular blockade for decades. However, its effectiveness depends on partial spontaneous recovery at the time of administration, rendering it unreliable in the setting of moderate to deep blockade. Furthermore, neostigmine is associated with dose-dependent muscarinic side effects including bradycardia, hypersalivation and postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV), which are particularly undesirable in elderly patients [15,16]. Age-related alterations in pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics can exacerbate these issues, increasing the risk of under- or over-reversal and subsequent respiratory complications. Consequently, there is an increasing demand for safer and more predictable reversal strategies in this vulnerable population.

Sugammadex, a selective binding agent for aminosteroid neuromuscular blocking agents (NMBAs) such as rocuronium and vecuronium, offers a novel mechanism of action by encapsulating free NMBA molecules to form inactive complexes excreted renally [17]. It has been shown to more rapidly reverse RNMB than neostigmine in obese patients without obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA) and to have a lower incidence of RNMB [7,18–20]. However, a recent study found no difference was observed in operating room discharge time in morbidly obese patients with OSA when sugammadex was administered instead of neostigmine [21]. Moreover, emerging evidence suggests it may reduce the risk of perioperative pulmonary complications in high-risk populations, including the elderly and those undergoing major thoracic procedures [22]. Its faster onset of action, superior efficacy and improved safety profile make sugammadex an attractive alternative to neostigmine in thoracic anaesthesia, especially for older patients.

While the pharmacological benefits of sugammadex are well established, its impact on clinically meaningful outcomes, particularly the postoperative AREs, remains inadequately studied in elderly patients undergoing lung resection. Existing studies have largely focused on the impact of sugammadex versus neostigmine on postoperative pulmonary complications (PPCs), though the results have been conflicting [23–26]. Besides, prior studies either underrepresent elderly thoracic patients or prioritize surrogate endpoints like recovery time and RNMB [7,20,21,27,28], but with limited data on clinically significant AREs. AREs during the postoperative early period, usually in the post-anaesthesia care unit (PACU), are a major source of preventable perioperative morbidity and mortality. Critical AREs can precipitate life-threatening hypoxemia and hypercapnia, lead to serious cardiovascular and neurological complications, necessitate unplanned critical care interventions and may prolong hospitalization. Therefore, optimal management strategies for preventing AREs are crucial for enhancing patient safety and outcomes. Well-powered clinical trials focusing on hard clinical outcomes besides RNMB or TOF recovery are highly warranted.

To address these gaps, we designed a prospective, double-blind, randomized controlled trial to evaluate whether sugammadex is superior to neostigmine in reducing the incidence of clinically defined AREs in elderly patients undergoing VATS lung resection. By focusing on meaningful postoperative respiratory outcomes, this trial aims to generate high-quality evidence to inform perioperative neuromuscular management strategies and improve respiratory safety in this growing and high-risk surgical population.

Patients and methods

Study design and setting

This is a prospective, double-blind, randomized controlled trial with two parallel arms, aiming to comprehensively compare the clinical efficacy of sugammadex versus neostigmine in preventing postoperative AREs in the elderly population undergoing lung resection. We will conduct this study at Shanghai Pulmonary Hospital affiliated to Tongji University, which is a tertiary teaching hospital specializing at thoracic diseases. All elderly participants will be recruited from patients scheduled for lung surgery in the surgical departments. This trial was approved by the institutional review board on 4 November 2024 (L24-628) and was registered at Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2400094005) on 16 December 2024.

We will conduct this trial on the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants will provide written informed consent prior to randomization. Figure 1 is the study flowchart. Eligibility criteria

Eligible patients are aged ≥65 years, scheduled for elective video-assisted thoracic lung resection under OLV. The detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria are as follows:

Inclusion criteria: (1) Providing informed consent form by the patient or their legal guardian, (2) age ≥65 years, (3) American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status I–III, (4) elective VATS for unilateral pulmonary resection, including wedge resection, segmentectomy, lobectomy and (5) using rocuronium for anaesthesia induction and maintenance.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Participation in other interventional studies or refusal to provide informed consent, (2) history of previous pulmonary surgery, (3) Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease GOLD stage III or IV [30], pulmonary fibrosis, large pulmonary bullae, severe pulmonary emphysema and pneumothorax (except pneumothorax on the surgical side), (4) uncontrolled asthma, (5) planed open thoracotomy, (6) tracheostomy, (7) Heart failure New York Heart Association grade III–IV [31], Coronary Heart Disease CCS Grade 3 and 4, (8) documented or suspected neuromuscular disorders, including thymoma, myasthenia gravis, myopathies and muscular dystrophies, (9) emergency surgery, (10) known allergy to any study drug and (11) psychiatric disorders affecting normal communication or cooperation, or language barriers impairing informed consent or participation.

Participants will be excluded as dropouts from the primary analysis if: (1) voluntary withdrawal of informed consent, (2) surgery is cancelled after randomization or (3) based on intraoperative evaluation by both the anaesthesiologist and the surgeon, the patient is deemed not suitable for extubation in the operating room due to unexpected complications, such as poor lung re-expansion or excessive airway secretions suggestive of pulmonary oedema, cardiac arrest and ventricular fibrillation.

Sample size

The sample size estimation is predicated on detecting a clinically significant reduction in postoperative AREs between reversal strategies. Based on the study by Stewart et al., the observed incidence of AREs with neostigmine reversal was approximately 17% [13]. We hypothesize that sugammadex will achieve a 50% relative risk reduction, yielding an expected AREs rate of 8.5% in the sugammadex group. This effect size is supported by the STRONGER study [32], a large multicentre matched cohort analysis in which sugammadex was associated with 30–55% relative reduction in a specific PPC compared to neostigmine. Considering that our study targets elderly patients undergoing thoracic surgery, a population with higher baseline respiratory risk, we optimistically speculate a 50% relative reduction in early postoperative AREs. Using a two-sided test with α = 0.05 and β = 0.20 (80% power), 238 patients per group are required. Assuming a conservative dropout rate of 10%, the final enrolment target is 530 participants (265 per arm).

Randomization and blinding

Eligible patients who meet all inclusion criteria and none of the exclusion criteria will be consecutively enrolled and randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either sugammadex or neostigmine. Randomization will be performed by a research assistant not involved in the subsequent study using a computer-generated sequence, with random block sizes of 2, 4, 6 or 8 to ensure allocation concealment and reduce selection bias.

Allocation concealment will be implemented using sequentially numbered, opaque and sealed envelopes. Each envelope contains a unique randomization code and the corresponding group assignment. Upon enrolment, an independent research nurse who is not involved in perioperative management will open the next envelope in sequence and assign the patient to the designated group. The same nurse will also prepare the assigned study drug in a standardized 20-mL syringe to maintain blinding. Both sugammadex and neostigmine (with atropine) are colourless, transparent solutions, making them visually indistinguishable. Participants, attending anaesthesiologists, surgeons, outcome assessors and statistician will be blinded to the group assignment until completion of data collection and final statistical analysis. Emergency unblinding will only occur if a serious adverse event (SAE) necessitates knowledge of the assigned intervention for patient management. In such cases, the principal investigator will contact the pharmacy team to obtain the allocation. All unblinding events will be recorded and reported to the Ethics Committee.

Statistical analysis

The primary analysis will be conducted using a modified intention-to-treat (mITT) approach, including all randomized participants except those who meet predefined dropout criteria. Sensitivity analyses will be performed using both the full intention-to-treat (ITT) and per-protocol (PP) populations to assess the robustness of the findings.

Continuous variables will be presented as mean ± standard deviation or median with interquartile range, depending on their distribution. Categorical variables will be summarized as frequencies and percentages. Comparisons between groups will be performed using Student’s t-test for normally distributed continuous variables and the Mann–Whitney U-test for non-normally distributed variables. Categorical variables will be analysed using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Effect estimates will be expressed as relative risks or odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals (CIs), depending on the distribution and nature of the outcome.

Baseline characteristics will be compared between groups. Any significant differences in potential confounders will be included as candidate adjustment variables in multivariate models. The main outcome variable will be expressed as a proportion of AREs together with a 95% CI. Multivariate logistic regression models will be employed to account for confounders when appropriate. Time-to-event outcomes will be analysed using Kaplan–Meier’s survival curves where needed. Cox proportional hazards models will be applied to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% CIs, adjusted for covariates when applicable. We will attempt to perform subgroup analysis to investigate the effect of intervention level on the study outcomes.

Statistical analysis will be performed using SPSS version 23.0 (IBM SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL). All statistical tests will be two-sided, with a p value <.05 considered statistically significant. Missing data will be addressed using multiple imputation techniques if the proportion of missing values exceeds 5%.

Anaesthetic care and study interventions

General principles

Prior to trial initiation, training sessions for anaesthesiologists and PACU staff are conducted to reinforce adherence to drug administration protocols. All enrolled patients will receive standardized perioperative care adhering to enhanced recovery principles, irrespective of randomization assignment. To ensure consistency in anaesthetic management, several common strategies have been established. Intravenous anaesthetics will be given to maintain anaesthesia, intra- and postoperative pain will be controlled with regional block anaesthesia or patient-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCIA), perioperative fluids are managed using a goal-directed therapy (GDT) approach when available, otherwise standard practice aims to avoid fluid overload. Colloids or crystalloids are administered based on hemodynamic indicators and estimated blood loss. Appropriate antibiotic prophylaxis will be administered, and PONV prophylaxis will be implemented pharmacologically in all patients.

Monitoring and anaesthesia management

Standardized intraoperative monitoring includes continuous electrocardiography (ECG) with ST-segment analysis, pulse oximetry, capnography and non-invasive or invasive arterial pressure measurement. The attending anaesthesiologist will determine any additional monitoring or management anaesthetic strategies according to clinical needs. As TOF monitoring is not routinely used for all patients in our centre, in this study, quantitative neuromuscular monitoring will be performed at the end of surgery when available. We will record whether TOF monitoring was applied for each participant and collect TOF ratio data when available. Mechanical ventilation parameters will be continuously recorded via the anaesthesia workstation, encompassing tidal volume (VT), positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP), fraction of inspired oxygen (FiO2), plateau pressure (Pplat), peak airway pressure (Paw), driving pressure (DP) and dynamic compliance of the respiratory system (Cdyn).

Pre-oxygenation was performed during 3 min at FiO2 1.0 with a face mask before anaesthesia induction. Anaesthesia induction will proceed with intravenous sufentanil 0.5–1 μg/kg, propofol 1–2 mg/kg, remimazolam 0.08–0.15 mg/kg and rocuronium 0.6–1 mg/kg, followed by double-lumen tube placement confirmed by fiberoptic bronchoscopy. Anaesthesia will be maintained with continuous infusion of propofol and remifentanil, with infusion rates adjusted based on the patients’ haemodynamic status and anaesthesia depth. Intermittent boluses of rocuronium 5–20 mg or sufentanil 5–10 μg will be administered as needed depending on surgery duration and vital signs. The choice and dosage of agents can be administered based on the clinical judgment of the attending anaesthesiologist, within typical dosage ranges. For mechanical ventilation, the respective VT for two-lung ventilation and OLV is set to 7–8 mL/kg and 5–6 mL/kg of predicted body weight (PBW). PBW is calculated as male = 50 + 0.91 × (height in cm − 152.4) and female = 45.5 + 0.91 × (height in cm − 152.4). Respiratory rate will be set to maintain an EtCO2 between 35 and 45 mmHg, with an inspiratory to expiratory ratio (I:E) of 1:1 to 1:2 and an inspiratory pause time of 5–10% of the inspiratory time. Additional ventilatory parameters include adjusting the inspiratory oxygen fraction during OLV to maintain SpO2 ≥93%. PEEP can be adjusted as necessary based on clinical requirements. Parameters could be modified under the criteria of the attending physician. This study allows individual anaesthesiologist to make personalized treatment decisions for each patient, only protocolizing the NMB agent (rocuronium) given, and the reversal agent (sugammadex or neostigmine).

At the completion of surgery and after wound closure, patients will receive neuromuscular blockade reversal per group assignment. Patients in the sugammadex group will be given intravenous sugammadex 2 mg/kg of actual body weight [23], while patients in the neostigmine group will receive intravenous neostigmine 0.05 mg/kg of actual body weight (maximum 5 mg) plus atropine 0.01–0.02 mg/kg. The actual timing of extubation will be determined by the attending anaesthesiologist. Following extubation, patients will receive supplemental oxygen at 6 L/min and be transferred to PACU. Study drug administration will also be recorded in case report forms (CRFs). In case of intraoperative adverse reactions such as anaphylaxis, significant bradycardia (heart rates <40 beats/minute unresponsive to atropine) or new-onset severe arrhythmia following administration of the study drug, the anaesthesiologist will terminate the study intervention and manage the patient as clinically indicated. Meanwhile, the patient will continue follow-up for outcome assessments according to the intention-to-treat principle.

In the PACU, all patients undergo continuous monitoring for a minimum of 60 min (according to our institutional requirements) including pulse oximetry, ECG and noninvasive blood pressure measurements at five-minute intervals, while receiving supplemental oxygen at 6 L/min via nasal cannula. Dedicated research nurses will document vital signs every 10 min, record any adverse event (AE) with corresponding interventions, and note rescue treatments throughout the PACU stay.

Data collection

Data will be collected using paper and electronic CRFs with access restricted to the study team and supervisors. Data collection will cover the preoperative, intraoperative, immediate postoperative and follow-up periods. The following variables will be recorded for all participants.

Baseline data collected preoperatively will include patient demographics (age, sex, height, weight and body mass index), previous comorbidities, history of malignancy, current medications, smoking and alcohol use, preoperative pulmonary function test, ASA physical status, Charlson Comorbidity Index [36], relevant laboratory parameters such as complete blood count and biochemical markers, ARISCAT score [37]. Additionally, information regarding pre-existing cognitive or psychiatric conditions will be documented.

Anaesthesia and surgical-relevant data will include the type and extent of lung resection, estimated proportion of resected lung tissue, durations of anaesthesia and surgery, durations of one-lung and two-lung ventilation, ventilator parameters, episodes of hypoxemia, and any new-onset arrhythmia. The timing from completion of wound closure or administration of study intervention drugs to extubation will be recorded. The administration of anaesthetic agents, vasoactive medications, antibiotic prophylaxis, intravenous fluid volumes, blood loss, transfusion requirements and postoperative pain management methods will also be documented.

Postoperative data collection will begin in the PACU, where patients will be monitored for at least one hour. Vital signs will be measured continuously, and any AREs along with the interventions employed (e.g. oxygen therapy, airway support) will be documented by a trained anaesthesia research nurse. The need for unplanned ICU transfer will also be noted when deemed necessary by the attending anaesthesiologist or surgeon.

Follow-up assessments will occur on postoperative days 1, 2, 3, 7 or the day of discharge, and again on postoperative day 30 (via telephone for discharged patients). Investigators will document vital signs, postoperative pulmonary and extra-pulmonary complications, the use of antibiotics, pain scores using a visual analogue scale (VAS), the occurrence of PONV, emergency reoperation, timing of chest drainage tube removal, hospital length of stay, any respiratory support measures outside the operating room, and all-cause mortality within 30 days after surgery.

Data management and monitoring

All original study data will be documented using both paper-based and electronic CRFs, filed sequentially by participant number. Paper CRFs will be securely stored in locked cabinets with access limited to authorized personnel. Electronic data will be maintained on encrypted, password-protected servers, with access restricted to members of the research team. To ensure data accuracy and integrity, regular cross-checks between paper records and corresponding electronic entries will be performed. Original records will be archived for at least 5 years following publication, after which physical copies will be shredded and digital files deleted in compliance with institutional policy.

Given the low-risk nature of the interventions (standard neuromuscular blockade reversal agents) and the short duration of follow-up, an independent data monitoring committee (DMC) will not be established, and no interim analyses are planned. The trial will be monitored by the study principal investigators, who will review data completeness, adherence to protocol, and any AEs monthly. In addition, the Institutional Ethics Committee will perform ongoing oversight throughout the duration of the trial, including review of any protocol deviations, AEs or discrepancies identified during routine data audits.

The final trial dataset will be accessible to the principal investigators. Upon reasonable request and in compliance with ethical and regulatory guidelines, de-identified study datasets can be made available to qualified external researchers after completion of the trial and publication of primary results.

Adverse event reporting and harms

Adverse events are defined as any new or worsening adverse symptoms, signs or laboratory abnormalities that occur during the trial, which were not present at the time of recruitment. Events that result in significant harm to the participant are defined as SAEs. All AEs, regardless of whether they are related to the study intervention, must be recorded in the CRF, including over-treatment or treatment deviating from the protocol guidelines. Any unexpected serious risks will be reported to the Ethics Committee within 24 h. The study duration for each participant is from randomization to the final day of follow-up (within postoperative 30 days). Participants should maintain contact with the study team throughout the trial period until the follow-up is completed. Participation is voluntary, and participants may withdraw from the trial at any time without penalty or loss of benefits. Investigators must inform the study sponsor of the reason for withdrawal and report it to the ethics committee as per the protocol. If a participant withdraws due to safety concerns related to the study drug, the investigator should continue follow-up outside of the clinical study after obtaining consent.

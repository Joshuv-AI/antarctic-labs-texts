Continuation of same programmed death-1 inhibitor regime beyond progression is a novel option for advanced gastric cancer
Authors: Jiasong Li, Fang Ding, Shasha Zhang, Yuanyuan Jia, Tianhang Zhang, Siqi Wang, Qingyi Liu, Zhanjun Guo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundGastric cancer is a significant global malignancy with poor prognosis. Although the emergence of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) prolonged the duration of survival, resistance and progression are inevitable. We aim to evaluate the effectiveness of programmed death-1 (PD-1) inhibitors in immunotherapy beyond progression (IBP).MethodWe divided the advanced gastric cancer patients who received two lines immunotherapy into same regimen group (with same PD-1 inhibitor regime after IBP) and different regimen group (with different PD-1 inhibitor regime after IBP). Statistical analysis conducted to compare patient characteristics and evaluate survival differences between groups.ResultThe clinical outcome analysis showed that the same PD-1 inhibitor regime seemed to exhibit a higher disease control rate (DCR) (51.8% vs. 29.2%, P = 0.062), significantly prolonged progression-free survival 2 (PFS2) (162 vs. 75 days, P = 0.001) and overall survival (OS) (312 vs. 166 days, P = 0.022) when compared with those of cross line. In the multivariate analysis, when using different regimen group as reference, the same regimen group was found to be independently associated with improved PFS2 [hazard ratio (HR) = 0.467, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.267–0.816, P = 0.008] and OS (HR = 0.508, 95%CI: 0.278–0.927, P = 0.027).ConclusionContinuation of the same type of PD-1 inhibitor regime in IBP shows clinical benefits and represents a promising therapeutic approach.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-024-13063-2.

Introduction

Gastric cancer is a globally significant malignancy, ranking fifth in terms of incidence and fourth in terms of mortality [1]. Despite the utilization of combined chemotherapy regimens, patients with advanced gastric cancer still face poor long-term survival [2–4], with a 5-year survival rates of around 4–5% and median overall survival (OS) of approximately 1 year [4]. Furthermore, limited treatment options are available after progression due to the unfavorable prognosis and multidrug resistance [5]. The advent of checkpoint blockade immunotherapy allows patients to escape the predicament. Checkpoint blockade immunotherapy is a treatment utilizing immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) to eliminate negative regulators of T-cell function, thereby enhancing the activation of the immune system and potentially controlling or eradicating tumors [6]. Referred to as ICI, the FDA-approved blocking monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) targeting inhibitory cytotoxic T lymphocyte antigen 4 (CTLA4), programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) receptor or the programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) [6]. Initially, immunotherapy was primarily utilized as a third-line treatment option for the advanced gastric cancer [7]. Recently, the 2023 American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) guidelines and National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) Clinical Practice Guidelines (Gastric Cancer, Version 1.2023) have recommended nivolumab (anti-PD-1 mAb) and pembrolizumab (anti-PD-1 mAb) in combination with chemotherapy as first-line treatment for advanced gastric cancer [8]. With the evolving treatment landscape, immunotherapy has now become predominantly incorporated in the frontline setting [7]. Although immunotherapy significantly prolong the survival duration in many individuals with advanced gastric cancer across multiple lines of treatment worldwide [3–5], resistance and progression are inevitable for the majority of patients, particularly in the later lines of treatment [9, 10]. Because of these situations, there is a question of how to choose the retreatment option after resistance and progression of immunotherapy.

Numerous studies have been conducted on retreatment with immunotherapy in non-small cell lung cancer [11–13], malignant melanoma [14–16], and renal cell carcinoma [17, 18]; and majority of them came to the conclusion that retreatment is an effective strategy. But few studies focused on gastric cancer, and the efficacy remains controversial. A previous clinical research [19] and a case report [20] demonstrated the benefits of retreatment with immunotherapy compared to chemotherapy alone in advanced gastric cancer; however, a study proposing divergent viewpoints suggesting that retreatment with nivolumab may ineffective [21]. Retreatment can be categorized into the following distinct scenarios by discontinuation reason of first immunotherapy: treatment when progression is observed after discontinuing ICIs because of a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR), treatment after recovering from immune-related adverse events (irAEs), and immunotherapy beyond progression (IBP) [15, 16]. Our study specifically focused on IBP, which refers to the persistent administration of ICIs despite the progression disease (PD). There are also limited reports on the selection of ICIs in retreatment [22], thereby our study evaluated the effectiveness of IBP specifically focusing on different PD-1 inhibitor regime rather than making general comparisons between immunotherapy and other treatment approaches. Our aim was to provide valuable insights for subsequent ICI regimes selection after IBP.

Method

Patients

We conducted a retrospective analysis of patients diagnosed with advanced gastric cancer from the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University between March 2021 and September 2023. Inclusion criteria encompassed: (a) histologically or cytologically confirmed gastric cancer; (b) unresectable stage III or IV according to the eighth American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system for gastric cancer [23]; (c) received at least two lines of PD-1 inhibitor regimes as combination therapy; and (d) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) score of 0–2. Exclusion criteria included: (a) incomplete electronic medical records (EMRs); (b) concurrent or previous other malignancy within the past five years; (c) treatment interruption for any reason more than 3 months; (d) replacement of PD-1 inhibitor regime without evidence of PD; (e) enrollment in clinical trials.

The treatment scheme for 80 patients presented in Table S1. The types of PD-1 inhibitors administered included camrelizumab, nivolumab, pembrolizumab, penpulimab, serplulimab, sintilimab, tislelizumab, toripalimab. Combination chemotherapy included capecitabine, irinotecan, paclitaxel, platinum, raltitrexed, S-1 (Tegafur Gimeracil and Oteracil Potassium Capsules), trifluridine/tipiracil. Combination molecular targeted therapy include apatinib, bevacizumab, disitamab vedotin, fruquintinib, lenvatinib, ramucirumab, regorafenib, trastuzumab.

The demographics, clinical and radiological information were collected, including sex, age, ECOG PS, PD-L1 expression status, tissue human epidermal growth factor receptor-2 (HER2) status, stage TNM, number of metastases, peritoneal metastasis, treatment history and clinical outcomes. We categorized the patients into two groups: same regimen group (receiving the same PD-1 inhibitor regime after IBP) and different regimen group (receiving a different PD-1 inhibitor regime after IBP).

The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the ethics committee of the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University. The need for informed consent was waived by the ethics committee at the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University due to retrospective design of the study.

Assessment

Objective tumor response was confirmed by computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), bone scan, or ultrasound examination repeated every 2 or 3 cycles according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 [24]. Clinical outcomes include the overall response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), Progression-free survival (PFS) and OS. The ORR was defined as the proportion of patients with the best overall response of CR or PR. The DCR was defined as the proportion of patients with the best overall response of CR or PR or stable disease (SD). PFS1 was defined as the duration from treatment initiation to the first confirmed progression during first-line immunotherapy. PFS2, referred to the period from treatment initiation to the first confirmed progression observed during second-line immunotherapy or death due to any cause, whichever occurred first. The PFS2 of patients who survived without PD until the end of the follow-up period was determined as the duration between the initiation and conclusion dates of the follow-up. OS was defined as date from initiation of second-line immunotherapy to death for any reason.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were presented as frequencies (n) and percentages (%). To compare patient characteristics between the two groups, categorical variables were analyzed using the Pearson χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. Survival curves were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and differences between groups were evaluated with the log-rank test. Variables that showed statistical significance (p < 0.100) in univariate analysis were included in a multivariate analysis using Cox proportional hazards model. Statistical significance was defined as a two-tailed P value less than 0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Data visualization is accomplished by using the “ggplot2”, “swimplot”, “survival”, “forestploter”, “grid”, and “survminer” package running in R software version 4.2.2 (www.R-project.org, 2022). In order to balance the characteristics of treatment regimens and lines between the two groups, we conducted subgroup analysis and propensity score matching (PSM) using “jstable” and “MatchIt” packages in R software.

Result

Patients characteristics

Between March 2021 and September 2023, a total of 103 patients diagnosed with gastric cancer were enrolled from our hospital. Subsequently, after rigorous screening, 9 patients were excluded due to treatment interruption more than 3 months, 10 were excluded due to replacement of PD-1 inhibitor regime without PD, 4 were excluded due to enrollment in clinical trials. Ultimately, our analysis included a cohort of 80 patients (Figure S1). Before starting immunotherapy, 47 patients (58.80%) experienced PD during platinum-based standard therapy in at least one treatment line, while 33 (41.30%) had not received any anti-tumor therapy. No statistically significant differences were observed between same regimen group and different regimen group in term of treatment line before immunotherapy (P = 0.960). The clinical characteristics were well-matched and comparable between the two groups (all P > 0.100; Table 1). The first line platinum-based regimens were evenly distributed between the two groups(P > 0.05; Table 1).

The median PFS2 was 115 (95%CI: 82.130-147.870) days and median OS was 248 (95%CI: 169.025-326.975) days.

Clinical outcome

The DCR of same regimen group (n = 56) was observed to be 51.8% (95% CI: 38.3-65.3%, CR + PR + SD = 29), while an ORR of 7.1% (95% CI: 0.2-14.1%, CR + PR = 4) was also noted. In contrast, different regimen group (n = 24) exhibited a DCR of 29.2% (95% CI: 9.6-48.8%, CR + PR + SD = 7) and an ORR of 4.2% (95% CI:-4.5-12.8%, CR + PR = 1). Although same regimen group achieved a higher DCR (51.8% vs. 29.2%, P = 0.062 > 0.05) and a higher ORR (7.1% vs. 4.2%, P = 1.000 > 0.05), these difference did not reach statistical significance.

The second-line immunotherapy demonstrated a median PFS2 of 162 days (95%CI: 100.974-223.026) and a median OS of 312 days (95%CI: 180.098-443.902) in the same regimen group, whereas the different regimen group exhibited a median PFS2 of 75 days (95%CI: 55.796–94.204) and a median OS of 166 days (95%CI: 92.328-239.672). Notably, same regimen group showed significantly improved PFS2 (162 vs. 75 days, P = 0.001 < 0.05) and OS (312 vs. 166 days, P = 0.022 < 0.05) compared to different regimen group. The results of the univariate analysis showed that same regimen group, TNM stage III, and other treatment line before immunotherapy were associated with a prolonged PFS2 (all P < 0.05). In the multivariate analysis of PFS2,same regimen group [hazard ratio (HR) = 0.467, 95%CI: 0.267–0.816, P = 0.008; Table 5] independently correlated with improved treatment outcomes. For long-term prognosis, univariate and multivariate analysis revealed that different regimen group (HR = 0.508, 95%CI: 0.278–0.927, P = 0.027; Table 6), other treatment line before immunotherapy (HR = 1.827, 95%CI: 1.030–3.238, P = 0.039), and ECOG PS (> 1) (HR = 6.239, 95%CI: 1.416–27.494, P = 0.016) were independently associated with shorter OS.

Subgroup analysis and PSM

The subsequent survival analysis for treatment regimen (including “immunotherapy + chemotherapy”, “immunotherapy + targeted therapy”, and “immunotherapy + chemotherapy + targeted therapy”) and treatment line were performed between same regimen group and different regimen group, no statistical difference existed for treatment regimen (all P > 0.05), but IBP efficiency in second-line superior to other treatment lines. After balancing the characteristics of treatment regimens and lines by PSM, same regimen group still achieved longer PFS (152days vs. 85 days) and OS (232days vs. 166 days) than different regimen group, but there was no statistically significant difference.

Discussion

Immunotherapy has provided a significantly prolonged survival period for advanced gastric cancer; however, not all patients derive or sustain benefits from it, and disease progression remains inevitable. Thereby, developing an appropriate follow-up treatment beyond progression poses a new challenge in clinical practice. Although IBP in malignant melanoma and non-small cell lung cancer has gained increasing recognition among scholars [9, 12, 13, 25, 26], several studies reported that DCR or PFS achieved during initial immunotherapy is positively correlated with response upon retreatment [9, 11, 27–29], and concluded that patients who experience PD following initial immunotherapy are not eligible for continued immunotherapy [7, 30, 31]. Based on the analysis of our study results, same regimen group achieved improved PFS2 and OS compared to different regimen group, independent of PFS1 and best response in first line. The median PFS2 of same regimen group was 162 days, representing a significant extension of 87 days compared to different regimen group. The median OS of the same regimen group also reached 312 days, which was 146 days longer than the different regimen group (166 days). Our study observed higher DCR and ORR for the same regimen group, but the difference was not statistically significant possibly due to the limited sample size. This finding suggests that gastric cancer patients showed significantly favorable responses to the same PD-1 inhibitor regimen regardless of their response to initial immunotherapy. Our subgroup analyses indicated that the efficacy of IBP is not influenced by the treatment regimen, but may be affected by different treatment lines. However, the result that the same regimen group achieved longer PFS and OS than the different regimen group was consistent both before and after PSM. Regrettably, possibly due to the limited sample size, the P value did not reach statistical significance. Further studies with larger cohorts are necessary to validate it.

Previous studies mainly focused on the outcomes difference between the group with the same ICI regimen in IBP and the group without ICI regimen in IBP [12, 15, 19]. The advanced gastric cancer patients who experienced PD after nivolumab treatment exhibited a prolonged survival with continuing nivolumab application in IBP compared to those with discontinuing ICIs in IBP [19]. The lung cancer and malignant melanoma patients who receiving the same ICI in IBP also led to superior outcomes compared to those without ICI in IBP [12, 15]. Comparing with the previous findings, we firstly explore the efficacy differences between the same and different PD-1 inhibitor regimens in IBP, and found that patient with same regimen in IBP achieved favorable clinical outcomes.

The underlying mechanism remains elusive, the tumor microenvironment (TME) appears to exhibit a more durable and robust response to the same ICI. Some scholars postulate that patients developed an immune adaptive endogenous memory in prior immunotherapy [11, 32]. The immune response encompasses the processing of tumor-associated antigenic peptides by antigen-presenting cells (APCs), presentation to CD8 + T cells, stimulation of T cell proliferation and activation, and the activation of T cells in TME for efficient elimination of tumor cells [10]. Inhibiting the PD-1 pathway can rejuvenate exhausted T cells, promote the generation of robust memory T cells, thereby repeated administration of PD-1 inhibitors can initiate the rapid partial tumor regression in the recurrent malignant melanoma [33]. Some researchers found a subset of T cells that permanently reside within tumor tissues, exhibiting characteristics of memory cells and responsiveness to ICIs [34–36]. Some molecularly targeted drugs have been found to induce the acquisition of T cell memory phenotypes, significantly enhancing the long-term anti-tumor activity of T cells [26]. And it is feasible to potentiate the anti-tumor efficacy of ICI by amplifying endogenous CD1 T cells via a vaccination approach in murine models [37]. These studies indicate that endogenous adaptive immunity plays a crucial role in enhancing the anti-tumor effects of ICI. Our study demonstrates that the same regimen yields superior efficacy in IBP compared to different regimen, leading us to hypothesize that ICIs may also elicit endogenous adaptive immunity. We speculate that PD-1 inhibitor participates in the induction of immunological memory, and memory T cells are recruited, and retained in tumor tissues, thereby enabling a more rapid response during subsequent treatment. In addition, the variations of mAbs may result in the difference of memory T cell, which may explain the disparity in efficacy between same regimen group and different regimen group. We speculate that the structural variations in these antibodies may form distinct antigenic determinant, thereby conferring different antigen specificity and subsequently driving the differentiation of diverse memory T cells. The memory cells may exhibit unresponsiveness towards an alternative PD-1 inhibitor regime with a distinct structural composition. More basic research is required to confirm our speculation.

The primary limitation of our study was the small sample size. Due to the factors such as poor prognosis, the occurrence of severe irAEs, and non-compliance of patient, there is a limited pool of patients with advanced gastric cancer who are eligible for at least two lines of immunotherapy. The limited number of cases available for our analysis may potentially lead to wide confidence intervals and impact the precision of the findings. And further investigations with larger cohorts are warranted to establish screening criteria for selecting the advantageous population.

Conclusion

The same regimen group achieved improved PFS2 and OS. Consequently, continuing same PD-1 inhibitor regime beyond immunotherapy progression yields enhanced, enduring and well-tolerated therapeutic outcomes, and represents a promising and innovative therapeutic approach.

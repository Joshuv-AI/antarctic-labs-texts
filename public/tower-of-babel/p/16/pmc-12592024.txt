Commentary: Prognostic Implications of EGFR Exon 20 Insertions in Resected Non–Small Cell Lung Cancer
Authors: Geun Dong Lee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
See Article page 227.

In this issue of the Journal of Chest Surgery, Chen et al. [1] present a retrospective, single-center study from Taiwan evaluating the clinicopathologic characteristics and prognostic impact of EGFR exon 20 insertions in patients with early-stage, surgically resected non–small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Among 3,235 patients who underwent complete resection, 44 carried EGFR exon 20 insertions. This study provides valuable real-world evidence regarding a rare molecular subtype that has been insufficiently characterized in patients undergoing curative surgery.

Alterations in the EGFR gene are among the most common driver mutations in NSCLC [2]. Insertions in exon 20 represent the third most frequent type of EGFR mutation, accounting for up to 12% of all EGFR-mutated NSCLCs [3]. Due to structural changes near the kinase binding site that reduce the affinity for tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), tumors with EGFR exon 20 insertions are usually resistant to TKIs approved for common EGFR-mutated NSCLC [4]. This distinct pattern of drug resistance has major clinical implications.

In the present study, the authors observed no significant difference in disease-free survival (DFS) in patients with EGFR exon 20 insertions, common EGFR mutations, and wild-type EGFR. However, multivariable analysis showed worse overall survival (OS) in patients with EGFR exon 20 insertions. These findings are clinically meaningful because they suggest that the biological behavior and post-recurrence course of tumors with EGFR exon 20 insertions may differ from those of tumors with common EGFR mutations, even after complete resection. Considering the known resistance of EGFR exon 20 insertions to first- and second-generation TKIs, the poorer OS likely reflects the limited availability of effective systemic treatments after recurrence rather than inherently more aggressive tumor biology.

Methodologically, this study is strengthened by its relatively large surgical cohort and clear molecular classification. Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. The small number of patients with EGFR exon 20 insertions and the limited number of deaths reduced statistical power, particularly for subgroup analyses by stage. Furthermore, as a retrospective study from a single institution, unmeasured differences in postoperative care and treatment approaches could have influenced the results.

Despite these limitations, the study by Chen et al. [1] provides meaningful insights into EGFR exon 20 insertions in NSCLC. New targeted therapies, such as amivantamab, have demonstrated promising efficacy in advanced EGFR exon 20 insertion–positive NSCLC, and recent trials have shown that combining amivantamab with chemotherapy yields better outcomes than chemotherapy alone as first-line treatment [5]. However, the potential benefits of these agents in adjuvant or neoadjuvant settings remain to be determined.

The findings of Chen et al. [1] underscore the need for future prospective studies exploring targeted perioperative treatment strategies for patients with EGFR exon 20 insertions. The similar DFS across molecular subgroups suggests comparable early recurrence patterns, whereas the inferior OS in patients with EGFR exon 20 insertions highlights the need for effective salvage treatments after relapse. Future investigations should examine mutation-specific features, coexisting genetic alterations, and the role of circulating tumor DNA in detecting minimal residual disease to guide early intervention. As next-generation TKIs and bispecific antibodies become more accessible, determining whether these new therapies can close the survival gap between EGFR exon 20 insertions and common EGFR mutations will be critical.

In conclusion, the study by Chen et al. [1] provides an important reminder that EGFR exon 20 insertions, although rare, should be recognized as a distinct molecular entity even in early-stage, surgically resected NSCLC. Their work reinforces the importance of integrating molecular data into postoperative management and supports continued efforts to develop personalized therapeutic strategies that can improve long-term outcomes for this unique patient population.

MULTIsite feasibility of MUSIc therapy to address Quality Of Life in Sickle cell disease (MULTI-MUSIQOLS): protocol for a mixed-methods randomised controlled trial at two US medical centres
Authors: Samuel N Rodgers-Melnick, Viraj Gorthi, Angelique Foss, Ashwini Erande, Kathryn DiFrancesco, Amma Owusu-Ansah, Samuel Anim, Slate Bretz, Priscilla Ketter, Tranaka Fuqua, Alan R Anderson, Alison Karasz, Lakiea Bailey, Miriam Ezenwa, Coretta Jenerette, Jeffery A Dusek

ABSTRACT
AbstractABSTRACTIntroductionAccessible, person-centred, non-pharmacologic modalities are needed to address chronic pain and health-related quality of life (HRQoL) among individuals with sickle cell disease (SCD). Building off prior single-site pilot studies of music therapy (MT) in SCD, the purpose of this study is to (1) examine the data collection processes and intervention implementation overall and across two sites and (2) evaluate the implementation of the MT and health education interventions using quantitative and qualitative data.Methods and analysisThis three-arm, two-site, feasibility randomised controlled trial will include 90 individuals ≥14 years who have SCD, chronic pain and access to a mobile device who are not currently engaged in mind-body pain management interventions under the supervision of a healthcare professional. Participants will be randomised to six sessions over 8 weeks of either: (1) in-person MT, (2) hybrid (one in-person, five virtual) MT or (3) hybrid health education. Patient-reported outcome measures of HRQoL and self-efficacy will be assessed at baseline, post-intervention and 6 weeks post-intervention. 24 participants (eight per arm) and 20 stakeholders (eg, haematologists, music therapists, nurses) will be invited to complete semi-structured interviews to further examine intervention acceptability, perceived benefits and implementation. Sessions will be monitored for fidelity, and participants lacking access to home internet or videoconferencing technology will be provided tablets to engage in virtual sessions. Feasibility will be determined by rates of data completion, recruitment, retention, session attendance and home practice.Ethics and disseminationThis study was approved by the University Hospitals Cleveland Medical Center Institutional Review Board (STUDY20231055). The dissemination plan includes presenting findings at national and international scientific conferences and publishing in peer-reviewed journals. All activities will be conducted in collaboration with SCD community stakeholders.Trial registration numberNCT06853158.Keywords: Chronic Pain, Quality of Life, Pain management, Chronic Disease, Clinical Trial, Feasibility Studies

Introduction

Sickle cell disease (SCD) is a genetic disorder in which haemoglobin S polymerises within red blood cells, causing them to transform into the classic sickle shape. These more fragile and rigid cells can become occluded within blood vessels, triggering an excruciating series of events referred to as a pain crisis. Over time, repeated exposure to these vaso-occlusive crises can lead to chronic SCD pain, defined as pain present on most days in the past 6 months in one or more locations.1 2 In the Pain in Sickle Cell Epidemiology Study (PiSCES), 54% of adults with SCD reported pain, pain crises or utilisation on more than half (51%) of 31 017 analysed patient-days.3 Chronic pain prevalence estimates among adults with SCD range from 29% to 100% (weighted mean 65%),4 with a recent study finding that 57.1% report Grade III (highly disabling–moderately limiting) or IV (highly disabling–severely limiting) chronic pain.5

In addition to enduring multiple painful conditions (eg, vaso-occlusion, avascular necrosis and central sensitisation),2 many individuals with SCD also report impaired health-related quality of life (HRQoL).6 In PiSCES, individuals with SCD reported worse HRQoL scores on all domains except mental health.7 Adults enrolled in a recent randomised controlled trial (RCT) of a web-based decision aid reported Patient-Reported Outcome Measurement Information System (PROMIS) pain interference, anxiety, depression, fatigue and physical function scores 0.5–1 SDs worse than population norms and worse than reference norms for individuals with cancer.1 Given these data and recent research recommendations from the American Society of Hematology to develop manualised, accessible and culturally relevant interventions for chronic SCD pain,2 investigations of integrative, non-pharmacologic approaches such as music therapy (MT) are warranted.

MT is the clinical and evidence-based use of music interventions to accomplish individualised goals within a therapeutic relationship by a credentialed professional who has completed an approved MT programme.8 Multiple systematic reviews and meta-analyses conducted within non-SCD populations have demonstrated MT’s efficacy for addressing patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) prevalent among individuals with chronic SCD pain including stress,9 pain intensity,10 anxiety11 and depression.12 Virtual MT interventions have been shown to be feasible and/or effective among patients with cancer,13,17 veterans18,21 and patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and/or heart failure,22 but they have not been investigated among individuals with SCD.

In our prior single-site pilot,23 adults with SCD were randomised to either (1) a six-session in-person MT intervention (n=12) or (2) waitlist control (n=12). Measures of feasibility were promising with an 89% enrolment rate, 100% MT session attendance rate and 100% measure completion rate. Interviews revealed two overall themes: (1) participants learnt new self-management skills and (2) MT improved participants’ ability to cope with pain. Preliminary analyses found favourable large effect sizes (Cohen’s d) for SCD self-efficacy (1.20), pain interference (−1.06), sleep disturbance (−0.99) and social functioning impact (1.05) within the MT group as compared with waitlist control.23 Despite positive pilot study findings, significant gaps remain in understanding (1) the feasibility of hybrid delivery (ie, in-person MT session followed by virtual); (2) MT’s effects as compared with an active control condition; and (3) factors that contribute to the uptake of MT among stakeholders working with this population. Accordingly, the purpose of this multi-site RCT is to (1) examine the data collection processes and intervention implementation overall and across sites; and (2) evaluate the implementation of the interventions using quantitative and qualitative data.

Methods and analysis

Design

Figure 1 provides a schematic of study procedures. This study began on 2 April 2025 and is expected to end on 31 January 2027. This mixed-methods intervention design embeds qualitative data within an RCT framework.24 90 individuals with SCD and chronic pain will be randomised to one of three intervention conditions over an 8-week period: (1) in-person MT (InMT) featuring six sessions of in-person MT; (2) hybrid MT (HybMT) where the first MT session is in-person and the next five are virtual; or (3) hybrid health education (HybHE) where the first session is in-person and the next five are virtual.

Participants

Individuals with SCD will be eligible if they (1) are aged ≥14 years; (2) meet Analgesic, Anesthetic, and Addiction Clinical Trial Translations, Innovations, Opportunities, and Networks-American Pain Society Pain Taxonomy (AAPT) criteria for chronic SCD pain25; (3) are able to speak and understand English; (4) have an email address and access to a mobile device with a functioning data plan; and (5) report that pain interfered with daily activities at least 1–2 days in the past week. Patients will be excluded if they (1) have a significant visual, hearing or cognitive impairment; (2) previously participated in the single-site pilot23; (3) are currently engaging in mind-body therapies under the supervision of a healthcare professional specifically for pain management; or (4) have a planned major medical event in the next 14 weeks (eg, childbirth, stem cell transplant). Routine disease modifying therapies (eg, blood transfusions) are not considered exclusion criteria.

Setting

Subjects will be recruited from two sites in the USA: (1) University Hospitals (UH) of Cleveland and (2) Prisma Health Lifespan Comprehensive SCD Programme. Both sites provide care for children and adults with SCD and feature multi-disciplinary outpatient care teams (eg, haematologist, advanced practice providers, social workers), same-day infusion centres for pain management and coordinated inpatient services. To maximise in-person session accessibility, in-person sessions at the UH site will be offered at multiple locations including the main outpatient SCD clinic, community health centres and integrative medicine clinics. Virtual sessions will be facilitated via Zoom.

Procedure

Screening and enrolment

Following a referral from the outpatient SCD team, study staff will review the electronic health record (EHR) to verify patients’ initial eligibility (age, language and diagnosis) and enter the patient into the Research Electronic Data Capture (REDCap)26 screening log. An SCD provider will then confirm whether the patient meets AAPT chronic SCD pain criteria.25 If eligible, the patient will be approached during their next (1) outpatient SCD clinic visit, (2) infusion centre visit or (3) inpatient admission. After a brief study explanation, interested patients will complete additional screening questions to determine (1) their pain frequency/duration, and how often pain interferes with their daily activities; (2) whether they are currently engaged in any mind-body therapies under the supervision of a healthcare professional specifically for pain management; (3) whether they have access to the technology needed for study participation; and (4) their most accessible site for in-person sessions. Patients meeting eligibility criteria will be invited to enrol. If eligibility is confirmed >2 weeks before a cohort’s intervention start date, a separate enrolment visit will be scheduled such that all baseline and randomisation procedures are completed <2 weeks before the start of assigned study interventions. At the time of consent, participants may elect to be approached for a post-intervention interview.

Baseline

Participants will be provided with an iPad to complete questionnaires assessing demographics; prior MT experience; access to technology (eg, laptop, tablet, home internet); transportation needs; music preferences and equipment/services used to listen to music27; HRQoL; and self-efficacy.

Technology provision

Immediately following the baseline assessment, study staff will engage participants in a series of procedures to facilitate communication, session attendance and remuneration during the study. These procedures will include (1) enrolling participants in MyCap,28 a secure messaging platform embedded within REDCap, and practising messaging within the app; (2) providing the participant with headphones equipped with microphones to improve audio quality and communication during virtual sessions; (3) providing technical assistance on how to download, connect to and interact with Zoom; (4) ensuring the participant can access the Box.com folder where their intervention files will be stored; and (5) enrolment in ClinCard,29 a card-based system for issuing remuneration and transportation assistance.

Randomisation and blinding

Participants will be randomised 1:1:1 to their intervention arms. Randomisation will be stratified by site, and block sizes of 3 and 6 will be randomly generated to minimise arm prediction and preserve approximate balance between groups. Participants will be informed of their assigned intervention at the conclusion of their enrolment visit. Site study personnel will not be blinded to intervention assignments, but they will be blinded to PROMs responses collected at all time points. The multi-site coordinator will be unblinded to PROMs collection for quality assurance.

Intervention

Participants will have 8 weeks to complete the six sessions of their assigned intervention. Participants will not be asked to alter their medical care (eg, pain medications, disease-modifying therapies) during study interventions. However, participants will be asked to refrain from receiving mindfulness, meditation, MT or other mind-body practices for the purposes of pain management under the guidance of professionals not affiliated with the study until the final data collection point. To promote virtual session accessibility, iPads preconfigured with Zoom and cellular data will be provided to participants whose home internet, personal devices and/or limited data plans prevent or hinder engagement in home videoconferencing.

Follow-up measures

Text-message invitations with embedded links to complete follow-up PROMs in REDCap will be sent to participants (1) post-intervention (ie, following their sixth session or at the conclusion of the 8-week intervention period, whichever comes sooner) and (2) 6 weeks post-intervention. Follow-up PROMs invitations will only be sent on Mondays, Tuesdays or Wednesdays to ensure study staff can respond urgently if a participant reports suicidal ideation. The final series of follow-up measures will also include items assessing engagement in any MT or other professionally delivered mind-body modalities outside of the study protocol.

Semi-structured interviews

Post-intervention qualitative interviews will be conducted over Zoom or phone with 24 participants (eight per arm) who elect to be approached for an interview at the time of informed consent. Interviews will occur within 2–3 weeks of intervention completion. Interviewees will be purposively sampled to include balanced proportions based on treatment arm.

Post-study access to content

Following the completion of all study measures, HybHE participants will receive access to a library of de-identified music exercise recordings. InMT and HybMT participants will receive access to the health education content. No further data will be collected on intervention use at this stage.

Study interventions

Music therapy (InMT and HybMT)

The MT intervention being adapted for both virtual and in-person delivery in this trial has been described previously in the single site pilot.23 Briefly, the MT intervention includes six sessions focused on (1) building rapport, validating the participant’s pain experience, setting goals and providing education on how music can help with pain; (2) learning a music-based diaphragmatic breathing exercise; (3) learning a music-based progressive muscle relaxation exercise; (4) learning a music-based relaxation and imagery exercise; (5) engaging in active music making; and (6) making a plan to cope with future stressors.

Each MT session includes (1) setting an agenda, (2) an explanation of the music exercise, (3) a demonstration of the music exercise in which the music therapist engages the participant in practising the music exercise, (4) time to process the participant’s response, (5) time for the therapist to electronically deliver the exercise to the participant and ensure they have all materials necessary to use the exercise at home and (6) a homework assignment for practising the music exercise taught in that session at least once per day until the following session. Music exercise genres are personalised to participants’ preferences based on the music preferences disclosed at the time of enrolment.

As the music exercises are being demonstrated, therapists simultaneously record exercises using GarageBand. To ensure the sound created within sessions is transmitted accurately and with high fidelity during virtual sessions, the Blackhole audio interface30 is used to transmit audio from GarageBand through Zoom. Zoom audio settings are also configured for ‘Original sound for musicians’, ‘High-fidelity music mode’, ‘Echo cancellation’ and ‘Stereo audio’. To translate the active music making intervention31 from session five to the virtual environment, therapists open a ‘Shared Piano’ room using the Chrome Music Labs32 platform. After tailoring the keyboard display to match the key of the participant’s chosen intervention track, the therapist and participant are able to engage in back-and-forth keyboard improvisation in real time.

Health education (HybHE)

The HybHE intervention is a manualised collection of publicly available and curated SCD content embedded in Articulate33 and includes interactive educational activities (eg, matching, multiple choice). All modules were developed in collaboration with individuals living with SCD and cover: (1) the science of SCD; (2) identifying barriers in the ED; (3) tools to navigate the healthcare system; (4) communication for patients with SCD part 1 (situation and background); (5) communication for patients with SCD part 2 (assessment and recommendation); and (6) SCD education summary. Each HybHE session includes setting an agenda, an explanation module, time to review the module, time for the HybHE interventionist to answer any questions, and a homework assignment for the participant to reflect on the module and reflect on how the content can be used to improve care-seeking.

Remuneration and transportation assistance

Out of respect for the time and effort spent completing study activities, participants will receive: (1) US$20 for baseline data collection; (2) US$20 for session 1; (3) US$10 for sessions 2–5; (4) US$20 for session 6; (5) a US$25 bonus if completing ≥4 sessions; (6) US$25 for completing post-intervention PROMs; (7) US$25 for the post-intervention interview; and (8) US$25 for completing the 6-week post-intervention PROMs. To assist with transportation to in-person sessions, study staff will distribute local bus passes, parking passes and/or mileage reimbursements and rideshare bookings via the ClinCard29 system, as needed.

Demographics and clinical characteristics

Self-reported demographics and clinical characteristics obtained at the enrolment visit include gender, race, ethnicity, marital status, employment status, household income, highest educational attainment, religious affiliation and prior MT engagement. Following enrolment, study staff conduct an EHR review of participants’ insurance type, SCD genotype, comorbidities, SCD treatment characteristics, healthcare utilisation (ie, emergency department visits, hospitalisations or same-day pain treatments at infusion centre) in the past 12 months, and opioid prescriptions in the past 14 weeks. Healthcare utilisation and opioid prescription data from enrolment through 6 weeks post-intervention will also be collected.

Feasibility

Rates of recruitment, retention, data quality/completeness, session attendance and intervention-related home practice will be collected to determine MULTI-MUSIQOLS protocol and intervention feasibility. Study staff will also maintain a log of study challenges and modifications implemented during the study. Intervention-related home practice will be self-reported to interventionists during the 8-week intervention period and logged in REDCap surveys post-intervention for 6 weeks.

Acceptability

Following the intervention period, participants will be asked to rate their responses to two questions on a scale from (1) strongly dissatisfied to (5) very satisfied. These questions include (1) ‘How satisfied are you with how your pain was managed during your participation in the study?’ and (2) ‘Overall, how satisfied are you with the six-session intervention you received during your study participation?’ Semi-structured qualitative interview topics will focus on participants’ perceptions of the interventions, perceived benefits and burdens of the interventions, and other barriers to and facilitators of in-person and hybrid delivery.

Fidelity

Fidelity refers to the degree to which the delivery of an intervention adheres to the protocol or programme developed.35 Each session is recorded, and interventionists enter structured data documenting adherence to steps of a manualised protocol in REDCap. The first and twelfth authors review all recordings and session documentation for the first five participants followed by every fifth participant thereafter within the MT and health education arms, respectively. These investigators document interventionists’ fidelity to interventions within a structured form in REDCap and provide any necessary retraining as needed.

Implementation

To further assess stakeholder perspectives of implementation, we will conduct 20 qualitative interviews with 10 stakeholder participants per site. This will include a broad range of relevant staff stakeholders (eg, SCD providers, music therapists, nurses, social workers, study staff) who have ≥6 months of experience within the SCD clinic during the study. Stakeholder interviews will focus on intervention appropriateness as well as barriers and facilitators to intervention implementation.

Patient-reported outcome measures

PROMIS: Participants≥18 years will complete the PROMIS-29 Profile V.2.1, while those <18 years will complete the PROMIS Paediatric Profile-25 and the PROMIS Paediatric Sleep Disturbance 4a. PROMIS measures are scored on a general population-based T-score metric with a mean of 50 and a SD of 10.36 37 Different PROMIS measures have been validated in the adult and paediatric populations and have been evaluated in patients with SCD.38,40

Sickle Cell Self-Efficacy Scale (SCSES): Self-efficacy will be measured using the SCSES, a nine-item Likert scale originally developed for adults with SCD41 and revised in a follow-up study for adolescents using a sample of 131 individuals aged 11–19.42

Adult Sickle Cell Quality of Life Measurement Information System (ASCQ-Me): ASCQ-Me is a patient-reported outcome measurement system that addresses the physical, social and emotional impact of SCD. This study will use the ASCQ-Me 5-item short forms for pain impact, emotional impact and social functioning impact. ASCQ-Me is a valid measure and highly reliable for use with adults with SCD.43 ASCQ-Me scores are calculated in the direction of overall health, with higher ASCQ-Me scores indicating better health.

Numeric rating scale (NRS): Participants will complete 0–10 (0=none, 10=worst possible) NRS measures of pain, stress, anxiety and fatigue prior to and following each session using a REDCap survey. The NRS is a validated measure for acute pain intensity.44 It has been widely used for other domains (eg, stress and anxiety) within studies of integrative therapies among children and adults1345,47 and found to be more reliable than the visual analogue scale in clinical trials, especially among patients of low socioeconomic status.48

Data analysis

This study’s primary outcome is feasibility as defined below. All other outcomes are secondary.

Demographics and clinical characteristics

Descriptive statistics will be used to summarise baseline demographic and clinical variables. Comparisons will be conducted using analysis of variance (ANOVA) and Pearson’s χ2 tests to examine the distribution of variables between groups.

Feasibility

Feasibility will be evaluated using six key thresholds: (1) 90% data element completeness in REDCap; (2) 35% screening rate (participants enrolled/eligible patients approached); (3) 90% recruitment rate (participants randomised/participants enrolled); (4) 70% retention rate (participants retained until the final survey time point/participants randomised); (5) 70% home practice engagement rate (participants engaging in intervention-related exercises at least once per week/participants randomised); and (6) 70% session attendance rate (participants attending ≥4 sessions/participants randomised). A final determination of feasibility for the study (primary outcome) will be met if any four of the six metrics described above are achieved.

Acceptability and implementation

To analyse qualitative interview data, we will use Lumivero’s NVivo, a computer programme that facilitates (1) the rapid organisation and retrieval of thematically linked data; and (2) the use of quantitative grouping variables to classify cases and generate complex comparisons. We will first conduct thematic analysis through an iterative process of coding subsets of data. Analytic memos will be created for each interview describing the major themes emerging in the transcript. Memos will be used to determine the emergence of data saturation. Next, the coded data will be retrieved and used to create case summaries of key themes related to implementation processes at each site. Finally, we will conduct comparative analyses both within and across sites using the matrix functionality in NVivo. Within-site analyses will include comparisons of relevant groups, including but not limited to gender and baseline self-efficacy and pain (high vs low). In the cross-site analyses, we will examine differences in barriers, facilitators, and implementation processes across the two sites with the goal of generating inferences regarding important factors shaping differences in implementation outcomes.

Fidelity

Fidelity rates will be calculated as the total number of tasks completed for each intervention session (eg, introduction, education on diaphragmatic breath, processing the experience of the music exercise) divided by the total number of session tasks.

Patient-reported outcome measures

PROMIS and ASCQ-Me measures will be converted to T-scores using HealthMeasures scoring service49 and summarised using descriptive statistics and boxplots. Given the purpose of this study (ie, feasibility and acceptability) and guidance from the National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH)50 to avoid hypothesis testing within feasibility studies, no inferential statistics will be conducted to examine differences in arm-by-time effects on PROMs.

Safety monitoring

If a participant experiences a serious adverse event (eg, active suicidal ideation, stroke, loss of consciousness, etc), during a hybrid session, away from a medical facility, the interventionist will immediately call the local suicide prevention hotline and/or 911 and notify dispatch of the participant’s condition and location. If a participant experiences a serious adverse event (eg, active suicidal ideation, stroke, loss of consciousness, etc), while in a medical facility, the interventionist will initiate the local site’s intervention protocol by contacting the clinical team at that site immediately. Given that the PROMs ask sensitive questions pertaining to participants’ mental health, a system was developed to alert study staff and participants’ SCD teams if their responses indicate elevated depression. Participants reporting responses of (1) ‘often’ or ‘always’ on any of the four PROMIS depressive symptom items or (2) ‘often’, ‘always’, ‘quite’ or ‘very’ on the first three ASCQ-Me Emotional Impact items will be further assessed using the ninth item from the Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ)-951 which assesses thoughts of suicide or self-harm. If a participant reports a score ≥1 on this item, study staff are immediately notified via an automated REDCap alert. Study staff then notify that participant’s outpatient SCD team.

Since MULTI-MUSIQOLS is a feasibility R01, a formal data and safety monitoring committee is not required by NCCIH. However, an Independent Monitoring Committee (IMC) will consist of several experts in the field of complementary and integrative health. Specifically, the IMC will include a physician/researcher, an expert in sickle cell disease, an expert in MT and a biostatistician. IMC members are approved by the NCCIH and qualified to review patient safety data.

Trial status

To date, all study preparatory procedures are complete including (1) developing a comprehensive REDCap database containing over 4200 fields; (2) training study staff; (3) partnering with community health centre sites for in-person session delivery; and (4) obtaining necessary regulatory approvals. Initial IRB approval was obtained on 31 October 2023, and this study was posted to ClinicalTrials.gov on 28 February 2025 (NCT06853158). Enrolment began on 2 April 2025. As of 29 September 2025, we have enroled 26 participants.

Discussion

Given the severity of the pain and pain-related psychosocial challenges faced by individuals with SCD, comprehensive and accessible interventions are needed to improve pain management and HRQoL. Hybrid delivery provides (1) the opportunity for participants and therapists to establish a therapeutic relationship and engage in collaborative music production during the first in-person session; (2) participants living far from SCD centres, lacking reliable access to transportation or lacking a reliable internet connection with greater access to subsequent MT sessions delivered virtually; and (3) reduced risk of infection for adults with SCD. Culturally tailored MT interventions have the potential to address these needs when deployed systematically and provided to patients in an accessible way. Successful conduct of this feasibility RCT will provide the necessary framework for conducting a future, multi-site, definitive RCT of MT compared with health education at multiple sites. Completion of the proposed R01 and the subsequent efficacy RCT could provide critical evidence to support the inclusion of MT as a chronic pain intervention for individuals with SCD. Such an expansion could provide individuals with SCD with culturally relevant, non-pharmacologic methods for managing pain, improving self-efficacy and reducing the psychosocial burden of SCD.

Ethics and dissemination

The protocol was approved by the UH Cleveland Medical Center Institutional Review Board (STUDY20231055), which serves as the single IRB. All amendments have been approved by this IRB, and any additional changes will be transmitted to the recruiting sites and the DCC at University of California – Irvine. Written, electronic informed consent is being obtained from all participants. See online supplemental material file 1: MULTIMUSIQOLS consent for the current version of the consent form. The study protocol is registered in ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06853158). Individual data use agreements cover data sharing between enrolling sites and the DCC. The dissemination plan includes presenting findings at national and international scientific conferences and publishing in peer-reviewed journals. All activities will be conducted in collaboration with SCD community stakeholders.

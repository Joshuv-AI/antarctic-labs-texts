Mathematical modelling and control approach for sustainable ecosystems in mitigating the impact of pollutants on aquatic species in rivers
Authors: Marouane Karim, Imane Dehaj, Ali Akgül, Murad Khan Hassani, Hanane Ferjouchia, Mostafa Rachik
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This paper focuses on the urgent issue of minimising the impact of pollutants on aquatic life in river ecosystems. Our innovative approach involves the integration of mathematical modelling and strategic control methods to counteract the negative consequences of industrial and agricultural activities. The model, developed in a one-dimensional context, captures the complex dynamics of species population and pollutant concentration. Using an optimisation framework, we strive to achieve a harmonious balance that limits pollution, enhances species diversity and optimises control expenditure. Ultimately, we seek to harmonise industrial progress with ecological vitality, promoting the sustainability of river ecosystems for generations to come.

Introduction

Nowadays, in order to address the major problems faced by the human race, such as drought, proper energy management, natural disasters, climate change, depletion of groundwater, political conflicts, unemployment, epidemics, drug addiction, and illegal immigration, industrial development and advanced technology have become indispensable tools. However, uncontrolled industry can generate more disadvantages than advantages. For example, the explosion of the reactor at Chernobyl in 1986 continues to affect Ukraine and neighboring countries to this day. The use of insecticides, while improving agricultural yields, can also cause numerous cancerous diseases when not properly dosed. Additionally, artificial intelligence and robotics contribute to an immediate increase in unemployment rates. To contribute to the fight against the negative aspects of industrial progress and to join a series of scientific works within this thematic (see1–5,8,17), this paper focuses on mitigating or minimizing the negative impact of pollutants on an aquatic species in a river. These pollutants are assumed to originate from chemical waste discharged by a factory or from insecticides and pesticides used in nearby agricultural fields. An illustration of a marine species impacted by an elevated concentration of phosphorus in a river is the cyanobacteria, commonly known as blue-green algae. Elevated levels of phosphorus in water bodies foster the proliferation of cyanobacteria, leading to the formation of algal blooms. These blooms precipitate a reduction in available light and oxygen levels within the aquatic environment, thereby disrupting the ecological equilibrium of the river ecosystem. Furthermore, certain strains of cyanobacteria produce toxins that pose significant threats to fish and other aquatic organisms, exacerbating mortality rates within the river’s fish populations and broader marine species assemblages16.

Numerical simulations

This section presents the results of numerical simulations conducted on our optimality system. The system encompasses a collection of state equations, alongside initial and boundary conditions (2–4), in conjunction with adjoint equations that account for transversality conditions (21), as well as the characterization of the optimal control (22). To tackle the intricacies of this comprehensive system, we employ the FBSM, originally introduced by McAsey et al. in 201212. This approach relies on an iterative procedure designed to solve the optimality system efficiently.

Firstly, we address the state equations by utilizing a forward time-stepping method. Specifically, we apply the explicit Euler method to discretize the equations and calculate the states y and z. For handling the second-order derivatives \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta y\,$$\end{document}Δyand \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta z$$\end{document}Δz, we adopt the second-order Euler explicit method. At the outset of the iterative process, we make initial guesses for the control variables. Subsequently, the adjoint equations are solved in a reverse-time manner.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study presents a comprehensive approach to mitigating the adverse effects of pollutants on aquatic species within river ecosystems. Combining mathematical modeling and control strategies, the research focuses on the negative consequences of industrial and agricultural activities. Through the use of a one-dimensional model that captures the dynamics of species density and pollutant concentration, the proposed optimization framework seeks to achieve a multi-faceted objective. This objective involves minimizing pollution levels, improving species density and minimizing the associated control expenditure. Ultimately, the research strives to harmonize industrial progress with ecological sustainability, thus contributing to the preservation of river ecosystems in a balanced and environmentally-friendly manner. The approach described in this paper holds promise for guiding future decision-making processes regarding industrial development and environmental well-being.

Progress toward elimination mother-to-child transmission of HBV in Shandong Province, China: Coverage trend in hepatitis B vaccination and HBIG administration, 2017–2024
Authors: Xin Meng, Weiling Zhang, Bingyu Yan, Yi Feng, Jingjing Lu, Li Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTTo evaluate progress toward MTCT elimination of HBV, we analyzed 8-y trends in hepatitis B vaccine (HepB) and hepatitis B immune globulin (HBIG) administration coverage rates in Shandong province, focusing on high-risk populations. Data were collected from a provincial system, Shandong Vaccination Information System. Information of maternal HBsAg+ neonates born in 2017–2024 were extracted. Administration coverage in four groups (A: general newborn, B: maternal HBsAg+ neonates, C: preterm neonates with HBsAg+ mother, and D: birthweight <2000 g (LBW2000) neonates with HBsAg+ mother) were calculated. Joinpoint models were used to analyze changing trends over time, calculating annual percentage change (APC). From 2017 to 2024, the maternal HBsAg+ rate dropped from 2.85% to 2.10% (APC = -0.10, P < .001), with an accelerating rate of decrease after 2021. Timely HepB birth dose (HepB-BD) vaccination coverage rose annually, reaching 94.42% in Group C (APC = 4.92, P = .001) and 86.26% in Group D (APC = 5.86, P < .001). Timely HBIG administration coverage increased significantly before 2021, achieving high levels by 2024 except in Group D (93.96%). The 4th dose of HepB (HepB4) vaccination coverage in Group D showed a nonsignificant increase (56.51% to 72.93%), with median administration time decreasing from 13.24 to 8.59 months. MTCT Elimination of HBV has improved substantially. However, LBW2000 neonates born to HBsAg+ mothers require focused attention, particularly regarding administration coverage of timely HBIG and HepB4. Enhanced health education for parents and additional clinical training for healthcare providers are needed to improve intervention coverage.KEYWORDS: HepB, HBIG, MTCT of HBV, preterm, low birth weight, China

Introduction

Mother-to-child transmission (MTCT) of hepatitis B virus (HBV) is a major risk factor for chronic HBV infection, which is the primary cause of cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma.1 Without timely administration of Hepatitis B vaccine (HepB), approximately 90% of infants infected at birth will develop chronic HBV infection.2 In 2016, the World Health Assembly adopted the global strategy to eliminate viral hepatitis as a public health threat by 2030, with a key target being the elimination of HBV MTCT. Achieving this goal requires ≥90% vaccination coverage of the HepB birth dose (HepB-BD) and completion of the 3-dose HepB series (HepB3) vaccination.3

To date, 98% of World Health Organization (WHO) member states have implemented universal HepB vaccination to newborns, and 61% of countries provide HepB-BD vaccination to newborns.4 Countries with high or intermediate HBV prevalence predominantly adopt HepB-BD vaccination, such as Southeast Asia, Eastern Mediterranean, Eastern Europe, and Latin American.5–7 In contrast, some high- and middle-income countries with low HBV prevalence, such as Japan, Canada, Western European nations, Australia, and New Zealand, administer the first dose at 1–3 months of age.4,5,7–9 Notably, despite its low HBV prevalence, the United States maintains a universal HepB-BD vaccination policy.5,10

In 1992, China integrated HepB vaccination into its childhood immunization program, adopting a standard 3-dose schedule (at birth, 1, and 6 months of age).11 A significant policy shift occurred in 2002 when HepB administration became freely available for all newborns, with the first dose mandated within 24 h of birth. The program was further enhanced in 2011 with two key provisions: (1) free hepatitis B immune globulin (HBIG) for newborns of HBsAg status positive (HBsAg+) mothers and (2) mandatory HBsAg screening for all pregnant women upon hospital admission.12 Major revisions were introduced in the 2016 Immunization Schedules and Instructions for Vaccines of the National Immunization Program (ISIV-NIP),13 including: (1) A 4-dose series of HepB (HepB4, received at birth, 1, 2, and 7 months) for preterm infants and neonates with birthweight <2000 g (LBW2000) born to HBsAg+ or HBsAg status unknown (HBsAg-unk) mothers;14 (2) Recommended post-vaccination serological testing (PVST) 1–2 months after completion, with a booster 3-dose series for those showing anti-HBs <10 mIU/mL.13 The 2021 ISIV-NIP15 updates included: (1) Shortening the administration window for both HepB-BD and HBIG to within 12 h of birth for at-risk infants; (2) Restricting HepB4 administration to LBW2000 only, removing prematurity as an independent indication; (3) Providing the booster series free of charge.

Since 2003, all hospitals with obstetric services in Shandong Province have been mandated to collect and submit monthly reports on HBV immunoprophylaxis data. Initial implementation showed strong uptake, with HepB vaccination coverage reaching 96% among newborns by 2004 and HBIG administration exceeding 92% by 2011.12 The surveillance system underwent significant upgrades in 2012 with the establishment of Shandong Province’s vaccination information system. This digital platform replaced manual reporting, enabling direct data submission from hospital obstetric wards and integration of subsequent HepB vaccination records from community clinics. By 2017, the system achieved comprehensive coverage across the province, resulting in substantial improvements in both data quality and completeness.16 Given these enhancements, our analysis utilizes data from 2017 onward to ensure reliability.

This population-based study characterizes the 8-y trends (2017–2024) in hepatitis B prevention through: (1) provincial-level assessment of HepB vaccination and HBIG administration coverage among children born to HBsAg+ mothers and (2) targeted analysis of immunoprophylaxis completion rates among high-risk neonates, including those born to HBsAg+ mothers and special populations (preterm and LBW2000 neonates) in Shandong Province.

Materials and methods

Shandong vaccination information system

The Shandong Immunization Program was established by the Shandong Center for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) in 2002 and achieved full coverage of 6,686 immunization sites across the province by 2017. The electronic immunization system mandated that all obstetric wards establish comprehensive neonatal vaccination records. Each record encompassed neonatal and maternal identifiers, serological status of both parents, and critical birth parameters. Subsequently, vaccination units – whether hospital-based or community-based – documented all immunization details, such as vaccination dates, vaccine types, batch numbers, and, when applicable, reasons for delayed vaccination of HepB-BD.

Statistical analysis

Among all study variables, only GA contained missing values (10.58% missingness), while other variables maintained complete data integrity. The missing data rate for GA was 44.9% in 2017 but decreased to less than 0.5% from 2018 onward. The significant Little’s test suggested data were not Missing Completely at Random (MCAR) (Supplementary Table S1), necessitating multiple imputation methods. Missing preterm birth status (binary) was imputed via multiple imputation using logistic regression, incorporating birth weight, sex, geographic location (city), and birth year as predictors.

The definitions and calculation formulae for the characteristics analyzed across the four groups are provided in Supplementary Table S2.

Joinpoint Regression models were applied to determine changing trends of the study indicators over the years, calculating annual percentage change (APC) and 95% confidence intervals (95% CI). For each indicator, we determined the breakpoint year, APC before (APC1) and after (APC2) the breakpoint, and the average annual percentage change (AAPC). Analyses were conducted with the statistical software STATA 15 (StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA). We set statistical significance at a two-tailed P-value of <.05.

Trend changes in coverage rates across four groups

Breaking point years and APCs are presented in Table 1. A significant downward trend in total infant number was observed only before 2021, with an annual decline of 14.69% (P = .002). Notably, the maternal HBsAg+ neonatal rate demonstrated a marked acceleration in decline after 2021 (APC1: −0.04, 95% CI: −0.07 to −0.01; APC2: −0.19, 95% CI: −0.26 to −0.12). In contrast, coverage in HBIG and timely HBIG in Groups B-D showed improvement only prior to approximately 2021 (all P < .05), with no significant progress thereafter.

Discussion

Leveraging a comprehensive monitoring system, this study documents significant progress toward MTCT elimination of HBV in Shandong Province (2017–2024). Our findings demonstrate consistent improvements in immunoprophylaxis delivery while highlighting key areas requiring targeted intervention.

A consistent downward trend in newborn numbers has been observed, showing an annual decrease of 8.41%. Notably, this declining trend was only evident prior to 2021, with no significant decrease demonstrated thereafter. The apparent stabilization after 2021 may potentially reflect cultural influences, as 2024 marks the Year of the Dragon in the Chinese zodiac – a period traditionally associated with increased birth rates due to cultural preferences. However, further longitudinal data are required to confirm whether this represents a true stabilization or temporary fluctuation. This pattern is corroborated by data from the Shandong Provincial Bureau of Statistics, which recorded an even steeper annual decline of 8.99%. Official records show newborn numbers declined from approximately 1.75 million (2017)17 to 0.65 million (2024),18 closely mirroring this study’s figures of 1,606,218 and 636,369 for the respective years. The comparison between system-recorded and official infant data reveals a substantial expansion in the system’s coverage over the 8-y period.

Moreover, the observed maternal HBsAg+ prevalence (2.65%) was substantially lower than national (6.17%, 2015–2020)19 and regional rates (Hubei: 5.45%; Anhui: 3.27%).20,21 Notably, compared with a decrease of 0.10% per year in our finding, Shandong Province showed a steeper reduction from 4.63% (2015) to 3.10% (2020) according to a national observational study.19 Additionally, an accelerated decline observed after 2021 may represent the delayed yet sustained impact of China’s 1992 policy integrating HepB vaccination into routine childhood immunization, indicating promising prospects for long-term HBV control. Among maternal HBsAg+ infants, preterm rates increased from 5.29% (2017) to 7.57% (2024), while rate of LBW2000 increased from 0.89% to 1.42% during the same period. Previous studies documented preterm rate of 5.05%–5.83% in similar HBsAg+ cohorts and approximately 2.81% in the general infant population.22–24

Both HepB-BD and HepB3 demonstrated consistently high vaccination coverage (>95%) across all four groups. For Group D, HepB4 is particularly crucial due to the compromised immune response associated with their frequent immunological immaturity.25 However, while HepB3 vaccination coverage exceeded 95%, HepB4 vaccination coverage remained suboptimal at 71.33%, suggesting potential gaps in implementing vaccination guidelines for this high-risk population. Notably, HepB-BD and HepB3 vaccination coverage in maternal HBsAg+ infants reached levels comparable to high-performance regions like the United Kingdom (99.4% and 94.9%, respectively, in Norfolk and Suffolk, 2008–2013).26 Nevertheless, HepB4 vaccination coverage in our cohort fell well short of the 91.4% achieved in the United Kingdom,26 where universal HepB4 administration is mandated for all infants born to HBsAg+ mothers. Furthermore, our observed HepB4 vaccination coverage markedly surpassed the 1.22% reported in Anhui Province, China, during 2016–2021.21 Encouragingly, the decreasing HepB4 vaccination time over time indicates growing parental awareness of its importance. Despite the implementation of an upgraded Vaccination Information System in 2023 that introduced automated reminders for third-dose administration to LBW2000 infants born to HBsAg+ mothers, the HepB4 vaccination coverage rate remained disappointingly low. This disparity likely reflects: (1) ineffective provider-parent communication regarding specialized vaccination protocols and (2) insufficient awareness among both healthcare workers and parents about the criticality of the fourth-dose timeline. To overcome these systemic barriers, a comprehensive approach is needed that combines enhanced provider training focused on proactive communication strategies with targeted parent engagement through culturally appropriate digital reminders and educational materials.

Timely HepB-BD vaccination coverage remained consistently high (>93%) in Group A during the study period, aligning with WHO/UNICEF Estimates of National Immunization Coverage.27 Analysis of WHO data in Figure 6 reveals sustained high HepB-BD vaccination coverage (2000–2023) across Eastern Europe and East Asia. Notably, China has demonstrated significant progress in reducing the historical coverage disparity with Eastern European countries.27 A study conducted in Baoan, a national pilot district of China, revealed that 99.16% of neonates born to HBsAg+ mother received HepB-BD within 12 h of birth during 2018–2022.28 Although Shandong Province has made significant progress, achieving 98.31% coverage in 2024, this rate remains slightly lower than Baoan’s benchmark. The 2024 coverage rates of 94.42% (Group C) and 86.26% (Group D) were at low level, underscoring the need for enhanced vaccination strategies in these high-risk groups. Despite differing study criteria, research from three Chinese provinces (2019–2021) showed that 93.21% of preterm neonates with HBsAg+ mother received HepB-BD within 24 h of birth.29

Timely HepB-BD vaccination coverage demonstrated significant improvement across all four groups, with particularly marked increases observed in recent years. The coverage decreased progressively across the four groups from highest to lowest: Group A, Group B, Group C, and Group D. Over time, the disparities between four groups narrowed, with Group B surpassing Group A in coverage by 2024 (Figure 3).

This observed disparity may be attributed to hospital practices of delaying HepB-BD administration in premature and LBW2000 infants due to concerns regarding vaccine side effects and potential medicolegal consequences.30 Furthermore, some clinicians continue to consider preterm and LBW2000 as contraindications for timely vaccination, reflecting insufficient training on recommended immunization schedules. Existing evidence indicates that preterm and LBW2000 infants face elevated HBV infection risk when born to HBsAg+ mothers particularly when birth dose vaccination is delayed, primarily due to immune system immaturity.30,31 While these infants may exhibit reduced immunogenicity to certain vaccines, the antibody levels typically achieved remain protective.25 The coverage among preterm and LBW2000 group requires substantial improvement through enhanced training programs for immunization staff in obstetric wards.

The coverage trend of HBIG showed a consistent upward trajectory across Groups B – D. Group B demonstrated the highest coverage rates, followed by Group C and Group D. While significant disparities in HBIG coverage were observed among the groups in 2017, these differences had diminished by 2024, when coverage rates exceeded 98% across all groups. This remarkable improvement can be attributed to the implementation of the National Integrated Prevention of Mother-to-Child Transmission of HIV, Syphilis, and Hepatitis B (iPMTCT) Programme in 2024.32

A persistent coverage gap remains when compared with Baoan District, where 98.61% of neonates born to HBsAg+ mothers received HBIG within 12 h of birth during 2018–2022.28 Encouragingly, surveillance data revealed substantial progress, with coverage increasing to 99.07% in 2024. Notably, the gap between overall HBIG coverage and timely administration was minimal, indicating that most infants received HBIG promptly. However, Group D exhibited greater variability in timely administration, with a higher proportion of delayed HBIG administration (Figure 4). Notably, all three groups demonstrated stagnant coverage rates for either HBIG or timely HBIG after 2021. Since the combined administration of HepB and HBIG immediately after birth is significantly more effective than HepB alone in preventing MTCT of HBV,14,33,34 enhancing timely HBIG administration coverage – particularly in LBW2000 neonates – remains a critical public health priority in Shandong Province.

A critical update in 2021 ISIV-NIP was the explicit recommendation for within 12-h administration of HepB-BD and HBIG to newborns born to HBsAg+ or HBsAg-unk mothers,15 whereas prior guidelines may have permitted administration within 24 h of birth.13 This policy shift likely contributed to the observed increase in timely HepB-BD vaccination coverage post-2021, as reflected in our data.

The preceding analysis reveals that while Shandong Province maintains robust coverage exceeding national averages, benchmarking against global counterparts – particularly European nations26,27 and China’s high-performing Baoan District28—reveals measurable performance gaps. Notably, Shandong’s HepB4 vaccination coverage significantly exceeds that of Anhui Province – a disparity that may stem from Shandong’s adoption of an innovative smart vaccination system, including electronic vaccination cards. However, the persistent gap in HepB-BD and HBIG administration coverage likely reflects systemic fragmentation between maternal-child health system and our immunization system. This contrasts with more integrated approaches adopted in provinces like Guangdong,35 where stronger inter-system coordination has been achieved. This necessitates strengthened system integration, focusing on real-time high-risk population monitoring and streamlined obstetric-immunization data sharing.

There remains an urgent need for comprehensive public health education regarding infant vaccination protocols, particularly targeting parents of infants born to HBsAg+ mothers. This should be complemented by additional standardized training for healthcare providers in obstetric wards and delivery clinics. The training must emphasize that HepB is only contraindicated in cases of documented allergic reactions to vaccine components, with no other conditions warranting delay in HepB-BD administration.15 Healthcare providers should carefully verify the dosage information, which our system automatically determines based on the infant’s birth weight and maternal HBsAg status. Medical staff must maintain a proactive approach when communicating vaccination details and reminders to parents to ensure optimal adherence to immunization schedules. To operationalize these improvements, we propose a multifaceted intervention package comprising: (1) mandatory provider training modules on structured communication protocols, including scheduled follow-up calls; (2) implementation of culturally adapted digital reminder systems (WeChat/SMS) timed according to adjusted vaccination schedules; and (3) development of visual educational materials tailored for high-risk families. Notably, automated reminder systems showed limited effectiveness without complementary human-centered interventions.36 This comprehensive approach addresses both systemic and individual-level barriers to vaccination completion.

This study possesses several notable methodological strengths. First, our analysis utilized comprehensive population-level data from a system encompassing all newborns, eliminating the need for statistical inference while providing robust epidemiological findings. Second, the application of joinpoint models to analyze trend changes provides valuable predictive insights for future epidemiological patterns and offers evidence-based guidance for public health interventions. Furthermore, we applied a rigorous 12-h standard for timely HepB-BD vaccination among neonates born to HBsAg+ mothers throughout the entire study period (2017–2024), despite this criterion not being formally implemented in national guidelines until 2021.

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting our findings. First, the absence of GA data for a substantial proportion of infants (reaching 10.58% overall and 44.90% in 2017) represents a significant limitation. While we employed data imputation for missing preterm birth records, this approach may have introduced inaccuracies in the classification of preterm infants, particularly for the 2017 cohort. Second, our analysis was constrained by the incomplete follow-up period for infants born in 2024, many of whom had not yet reached the scheduled administration times for HepB3 and HepB4 at data collection. Consequently, coverage rates for these vaccinations in 2024 could not be reliably calculated.

Conclusions

Utilizing data from a high-coverage surveillance system in Shandong Province (2017–2024), a pre-2020 decline in newborns number accompanied by an accelerated decrease in maternal HBsAg+ rates were observed. Administration coverage of timely HepB-BD and timely HBIG demonstrated substantial improvement, achieving high coverage levels by 2024. Nevertheless, administration coverage among LBW2000 neonates born to HBsAg+ mothers remains suboptimal, particularly for timely HBIG and HepB4. These findings highlight the urgent need for enhanced public health education targeting parents and additional clinical training for healthcare providers to address these gaps.

The role of cyclooxygenase-2 in oxidative stress and inflammation: Implications for poultry health and production
Authors: Xueqing Xiao, Wenrui Zhen, Dongying Bai, Bo Zheng, Yanli Wang, Xiqiang Ma, Xiaolin Xie, Yi Zhang, Fangshen Guo, Koichi Ito, Bingkun Zhang, Cai Zhang, Yanbo Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOxidative stress and inflammation commonly occur in modern poultry farming, where they are closely linked mechanistically during the progression of disease and adaptation to stressors. This review synthesizes current evidence to outline cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) and downstream prostaglandin E2 (PGE₂) signaling via EP receptors as a key molecular nexus that connects excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) with sustained inflammatory damage. In poultry, a range of stressors, such as heat stress, high stocking density, mycotoxin exposure, heavy metals, and pathogen challenges, promote ROS accumulation from sources such as mitochondria and NADPH oxidase (NOX). These disruptions in redox balance trigger regulatory pathways involving MAPK and NF-κB/AP-1, thereby upregulating PTGS2 (the gene for COX-2) and microsomal prostaglandin E synthase-1 (mPGES-1), leading to increased PGE₂ production. In turn, the PGE₂/EP signaling pathway can enhance NOX activity, alter both innate and adaptive immune functions, and exacerbate barrier dysfunction in the intestine, liver, respiratory mucosa, and immune-related organs, establishing a reinforcing loop of ROS, COX-2, and PGE₂. We further discuss how the effects of COX-2 vary by tissue and pathogen, contributing to slowed growth, disrupted immunity, enhanced viral susceptibility, and damage to organs, and discuss potential intervention approaches such as Nrf2-focused antioxidants, nutrients that strengthen barriers, and targeted modulation of COX-2 and mPGES-1 activity and EP receptor signaling to disrupt the link between oxidative load and escalating inflammation. Finally, we outline a framework integrating standardized stress and pathogen challenges with eicosanoid and EP receptor profiling to guide targeted interventions on the COX-2 prostaglandin network in poultry.Keywords: Cyclooxygenase-2, Reactive oxygen species, Redox-inflammation crosstalk, Poultry production

Introduction

In modern poultry production, oxidative stress and inflammation represent two of the most common and closely interconnected health issues (Chen et al., 2024). A variety of stressors, such as heat stress, pathogenic infection, dietary mycotoxins, transportation, and immune stimulation, can synergistically trigger these responses and thereby impair growth performance, immune homeostasis, and the integrity of barrier organs (Oke et al., 2024; Onagbesan et al., 2023). Prolonged heat exposure disrupts antioxidant systems and immune regulation, linking redox imbalance to reduced performance (Goel, 2021; Nawaz et al., 2021). A systematic review and meta-analysis reported that heat stress reduces feed intake and body weight gain while increasing feed conversion ratio and mortality in broilers (Liu et al., 2020) and a separate random effects meta-analysis showed that dietary mycotoxin exposure similarly compromises these production outcomes (Weaver et al., 2022). Similarly, high stocking density aggravates damage to intestinal barrier and amplifies inflammatory pathways (Dai et al., 2022; Goo et al., 2019; Quinteiro-Filho et al., 2010). Transport stress also raises reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels and systemic inflammatory indices (Xu et al., 2022).

At the molecular level, accumulation of ROS triggers lipid peroxidation, DNA damage, and protein modifications, often in concert with mitochondrial impairment and disrupted energy metabolism (Zhong et al., 2024). Excessive or prolonged inflammatory responses further worsen breakdown of the gut barrier and promotes pathogen survival, reinforcing a self-sustaining pathological environment (Kogut et al., 2022; Tellez-Isaias et al., 2023). Mycotoxin and enteric infections act as additional cofactors, jointly intensifying oxidative damage and inflammatory responses in the intestine (Gomez-Osorio et al., 2024; Voroshazi et al., 2024).

Growing evidence supports a coordinated oxidative stress and inflammation axis, in which ROS activate NF-κB and AP-1, leading to upregulation of cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) and other pro-inflammatory mediators; in turn, these inflammatory signals augments ROS production, forming a reinforcing positive feedback loop (Liu et al., 2023b; Meng et al., 2023; Xing et al., 2021). In parallel, the Nrf2/Keap1-ARE pathway counterbalances redox load, whereas signaling through TLR4 and MyD88 activates MAPK, NF-κB, and AP-1, and activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome can escalate local redox disturbances into systemic inflammation, resulting in impaired performance and disrupted immune function (Liu et al., 2021; Peng et al., 2022). Although the structure function relationships of COX-2 and its central role in eicosanoid metabolism have been thoroughly characterized in mammals (Norregaard et al., 2015; Rouzer and Marnett, 2009), comprehensive evidence defining COX-2 as a nexus of the oxidative stress-inflammation axis in poultry remains relatively limited. Nevertheless, both environmental and infectious stressors are known to trigger the coordinated COX-2 and mPGES-1 activation that increases PGE₂ production and EP receptor signaling, linking redox and inflammatory signals with outcomes such as shrinkage of lymphoid organs, damage to respiratory and intestinal epithelia, and increased viral replication; correspondingly, pharmacological inhibition of COX-2 or blockade of EP2/EP4 receptors has been shown to mitigate these effects in certain avian models (Mahmoud et al., 2024a, 2025). Beyond infection biology, common production-related stressors also exacerbate redox and inflammatory imbalances (Shah Alam et al., 2024), whereas nutritional or functional strategies focused on activating Nrf2 hold considerable translational promise for restoring redox and immune homeostasis (Xie et al., 2023).

This review examines the complex interplay between oxidative stress and inflammation in poultry, with particular emphasis on COX-2 as a critical mediator. We also discuss the prospects of novel nutritional and functional intervention strategies designed to modulate these pathways to improve poultry health and productivity.

COX-2 biology in poultry

As a key member of the cyclooxygenase family, COX-2 shares considerable structural similarity with the constitutively expressed COX-1, yet shows marked divergence in its inducibility and function. COX-2 is rapidly upregulated by inflammatory or stress signals and catalyzes the rate limiting conversion of arachidonic acid (AA) to prostaglandin H₂ (PGH₂) (Rouzer and Marnett, 2009). PGH₂ is subsequently funneled by dedicated synthases: microsomal prostaglandin E synthase-1 (mPGES-1), prostacyclin synthase (PGIS), prostaglandin D synthase (PGDS), prostaglandin F synthase (PGFS), and thromboxane synthase (TXAS), into PGE₂, PGI₂, PGD₂, PGF₂α, and TXA₂, respectively (Norregaard et al., 2015).

In addition to mPGES-1, the terminal conversion of PGH2 to PGE2 can also be mediated by cytosolic PGES (cPGES) and microsomal prostaglandin E synthase-2 (mPGES-2), which together constitute the broader PGE2-synthetic system (Murakami and Kudo, 2006). cPGES is generally considered a constitutively expressed enzyme that preferentially couples with COX-1 and contributes to basal or immediate PGE2 production under physiological conditions (Tanioka et al., 2000). In contrast, mPGES-1 is an inducible enzyme that is upregulated by inflammatory stimuli and functionally coupled with COX-2, thereby promoting delayed and amplified PGE2 production during inflammatory activation (Jakobsson et al., 1999; Murakami et al., 2000). mPGES-2 has also been identified as a membrane-associated PGE synthase; however, compared with mPGES-1, its biological contribution remains less clearly defined (Tanikawa et al., 2002). Therefore, although cPGES and mPGES-2 should be acknowledged as components of the prostaglandin network, the present review primarily focuses on mPGES-1 because it is the isoform most consistently linked to inducible COX-2 signaling under inflammatory and stress-related conditions.

These eicosanoids signal through EP1 to EP4, IP, DP, FP, and TP receptors. These receptors couple to Gs, Gi, and Gq proteins, triggering downstream cascades such as the cAMP, PKA, and CREB pathway and the PLC, IP₃, Ca²⁺, and PKC pathway, while also crosstalking with pathways such as ERK1 and ERK2 and PI3K/Akt/mTOR to regulate processes including vascular tone, fever, nociception, leukocyte infiltration, and maintenance of barrier homeostasis (Kalinski, 2012; Ricciotti and FitzGerald, 2011). Meanwhile, cytosolic phospholipase A₂α (cPLA₂α) undergoes Ca²⁺-dependent phosphorylation and membrane translocation to release AA as substrate, amplifying prostanoid output (Xing et al., 2021). Heat stress and conditions mimicking hypoxia further enhance COX-2 expression by cooperating with NF-κB via mechanisms involving heat shock proteins (HSP) and hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α), underscoring its rate-limiting role at the interface between stress responses and inflammation (Chen et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2021).

Accordingly, the broad inducibility of COX-2 enables coordinated lipid mediator signaling in response to diverse challenges. At the network level, COX-2 influences the trajectory of inflammation and production related outcomes through several horizontal regulatory mechanisms. First, lipid mediator competition and flux redistribution: COX-2 dependent diversion of AA can constrain the 5-lipoxygenase leukotriene branch or alter cytochrome P450 derived epoxy and dihydroxy eicosanoids, thereby remodeling neutrophil trafficking and vascular permeability (Ricciotti and FitzGerald, 2011). Second, receptor specific signaling fingerprints: EP4 preferentially couples to Gs and cAMP signaling and can signal via β-arrestin and ERK to induce tolerance or desensitization and gene reprogramming, whereas EP1 favors Ca²⁺ and PKC signaling to promote smooth muscle contraction and nociception, and EP3 partly couples to Gi to suppress cAMP, generating organ specific receptor fingerprints for motility, perfusion, and leukocyte dynamics (Kalinski, 2012). Third, redox and cell death coupling: by elevating NOX derived and mitochondrial ROS, COX-2 driven PGE₂ signaling facilitates NLRP3 inflammasome assembly and lowers thresholds for apoptosis, pyroptosis, and necroptosis. Under combined heat and transport stress, such coupling correlates with meat quality deterioration and barrier fragility, whereas activation of the Nrf2/Keap1-ARE program, through selenium compounds, polyphenols, probiotics, or vitamin C, attenuates ROS and partially restrains COX-2 driven PGE₂ signaling, improving both health and production endpoints (Mavrommatis et al., 2021; Shah Alam et al., 2024; Tran et al., 2023; Xie et al., 2023).

A range of pathogen challenge and chemically induced inflammation models has been applied in poultry research to characterize immune dysregulation and barrier injury, and to inform the evaluation of potential interventions (Cheng et al., 2017; Kogut et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2022a; Zheng et al., 2020). Going forward, harmonizing heat, pathogen, and toxicant challenge protocols and integrating interventions defined by timing and dose that target cyclooxygenase 2, prostaglandin E2 signaling through EP receptors, and the associated downstream signaling cascade may facilitate translation to production settings.

Regulation of COX-2 by oxidative stress

ROS markedly accumulate under stress through multiple cellular sources, including leakage from the mitochondrial electron transport chain, activation of NOX, endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress, and xanthine oxidase-mediated reactions. Under physiological homeostasis, ROS generation and elimination remain balanced; once this equilibrium collapses, excessive ROS induce lipid peroxidation, protein and DNA modifications, and mitochondrial dysfunction, which amplifies oxidative injury into systemic inflammatory responses (Bedard and Krause, 2007; Murphy, 2009; Sena and Chandel, 2012).

At the molecular level, growing evidence supports that ROS upregulate PTGS2 (COX-2) expression through several interconnected signaling pathways. First, ROS activate the TAK1/IKK kinase complex, accelerating IκBα degradation and releasing NF-κB to translocate into the nucleus, where it binds directly to the COX-2 promoter to drive transcription. This axis has been experimentally validated across diverse inflammatory models (Zhao et al., 2022). Second, ROS activate MAPK family members, including p38, ERK1/2, and JNK, thereby enhancing AP-1 transcriptional activity, which cooperates with NF-κB to amplify COX-2 expression (Onodera et al., 2015). Third, pathways involved in metabolic sensing, such as PI3K/Akt and AMPK, act as modulatory nodes that can either enhance or suppress COX-2 transcription depending on the energetic context, highlighting a metabolic and inflammatory crosstalk in COX-2 regulation (Kim and He, 2013; Xiao et al., 2011). In addition, ROS can also promote COX-2 induction indirectly by stimulating ER stress and activating the NLRP3 inflammasome, which promotes IL-1β release and amplifies PGE₂ signaling downstream of COX-2 (Zhang et al., 2020).

Avian studies have confirmed the functional conservation and pathophysiological relevance of these mechanisms at both systemic and organ levels. In models of immune activation, lipopolysaccharide (LPS) challenge induces the hypothalamic COX-2 driven PGE₂ signaling through EP4 in chicks, accompanied by elevated oxidative stress indices; pharmacological COX-2 inhibition partially reverses growth suppression and inflammatory phenotypes, suggesting that this pathway plays an important role in linking growth performance to immune responses (Liu et al., 2023b). Under heat stress, excessive mitochondrial ROS production and redox imbalance further activate COX-2 associated oxidative and inflammatory pathways, as reported in recent studies on avian redox (Aryal et al., 2025). Environmental stressors common in poultry housing, such as ammonia accumulation or high humidity, induce upregulation of NF-κB, COX-2, and inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) in respiratory epithelia, resulting in compromised mucosal integrity (Wang et al., 2022). Toxicological insults, including arsenic exposure and aflatoxin contamination, have been reported to activate NF-κB, iNOS, and COX-2 signaling in both central and peripheral tissues, leading to histopathological damage and further illustrating the oxidative and inflammatory coupling that contributes to health and productivity decline (Fouad et al., 2019; Sun et al., 2017). In pathogen host interactions, infectious bronchitis virus (IBV) exploits COX-2 driven PGE₂ signaling to enhance viral replication and epithelial injury; in chicken tracheal explants, COX-2 inhibition reduces viral load and tissue pathology, underscoring COX-2 as a pathological amplifier of respiratory susceptibility (Mahmoud et al., 2024a). In the central nervous system, LPS challenge upregulates hypothalamic COX-2 driven PGE₂ signaling through EP4 and is associated with anorexia and growth suppression, while COX-2 inhibition can partially reverse these effects. Importantly, elevated COX-2 should not be regarded solely as a downstream consequence of oxidative stress. Once induced, COX-2-derived PGE₂ signaling can further amplify inflammatory activation and contribute to ROS accumulation through downstream receptor-mediated mechanisms, including enhanced NOX-associated oxidative signaling and reinforcement of pro-inflammatory cytokine responses (Reyes-Martinez et al., 2019; Ricciotti and FitzGerald, 2011; Zhuang et al., 2017). In this way, COX-2 is positioned not only as a transcriptional target of ROS-sensitive pathways but also as a functional amplifier that can sustain the oxidative and inflammatory circuit.

Collectively, ROS serve as upstream second messengers that promote COX-2 induction, while COX-2 and its main downstream mediator PGE₂ in turn influence immune and metabolic microenvironments, potentially creating a reinforcing oxidative and inflammatory loop under sustained stress. In this context, several intervention strategies have shown translational promise. Activation of AMPK or related metabolic checkpoints mitigates COX-2 transcriptional and metabolic drive (Xiao et al., 2011). Antioxidant approaches targeting mitochondrial ROS or NOX through selenium nutrition, plant polyphenols, or mitochondria targeted molecules can attenuate upstream oxidative pressure and excessive COX-2 activation (Mavrommatis et al., 2021; Xie et al., 2023). Moreover, direct or indirect modulation of the NLRP3 inflammasome can block downstream pro-inflammatory amplification and reduce COX-2 driven PGE₂ overproduction (Zhang et al., 2020). These integrated strategies that focused on energy metabolism, redox homeostasis, and inflammasome activities offer feasible and mechanistically grounded approaches to mitigating the ROS and COX-2 coupling and enhancing stress resilience in poultry health management.

COX-2 in poultry inflammation

In poultry, COX-2 activation is consistently associated with stereotypical inflammatory phenotypes, including cytokine amplification, leukocyte recruitment, microvascular changes, and epithelial barrier dysfunction. Among COX-2 derived lipid mediators, PGE₂ acts as a key effector that shapes both local and systemic inflammatory tone by regulating vasodilation, permeability, leukocyte trafficking, and innate immune activation. Through EP1 to EP4 receptors, PGE₂ further modulates chemotaxis, phagocytosis, antigen presentation, and T cell polarization, thereby influencing the magnitude and persistence of inflammatory responses (Kalinski, 2012; Ricciotti and FitzGerald, 2011). In non-avian systems, many pro-inflammatory and oxidative stress-related actions of PGE₂ have been closely linked to EP2 signaling, which has therefore emerged as a mechanistically important downstream node (Sluter et al., 2021). In poultry, the relevance of this mechanism remains less clearly defined, but it is supported by the molecular identification of chicken EP2 and EP4 receptors (Kwok et al., 2008). Thus, although current avian data are insufficient to establish EP2 as the universally dominant mediator of PGE₂-driven inflammation across tissues and challenge conditions, EP2 should be considered a biologically plausible and poultry-relevant downstream effector that warrants further validation. Notably, these effects are rarely uniform across tissues or disease stages, as EP receptor subtype distribution and downstream coupling can differ markedly between epithelial, endothelial, and immune cell compartments. As a result, PGE₂ signaling may either limit excessive tissue damage by restraining overactivation or, under sustained stress and high inflammatory load, reinforce inflammatory amplification and barrier fragility through persistent leukocyte recruitment and mediator release. Among the EP receptor subtypes, EP2 and EP4 appear particularly relevant to the downstream regulatory effects of PGE₂. Recent evidence from non-avian systems indicates that EP2/EP4 signaling is linked to cAMP/PKA-dependent regulation, whereas EP4 can additionally engage PI3K/Akt- and MAPK/ERK-related pathways, thereby contributing to inflammatory, pain-related, and immunoregulatory responses (Macia Guardado et al., 2025; Sluter et al., 2021). In poultry, the relevance of these signaling routes is supported by the molecular identification of chicken EP2 and EP4 receptors (Kwok et al., 2008) and by functional evidence that the COX-2-PGE₂-EP4 axis contributes to immune stress-associated growth suppression in broilers (Liu et al., 2023b). Therefore, current evidence supports the view that key regulatory effects of the COX-2/PGE₂ axis in poultry are likely mediated, at least in part, through EP2- and EP4-associated downstream signaling, although direct functional validation of EP2/EP4-specific cAMP/PKA or PI3K/Akt signaling in avian inflammatory models remains limited.

At the pathway level, COX-2 and terminal prostaglandin synthases provide an additional layer of selectivity that helps explain the context dependence of prostanoid outputs. In this context, mPGES-1 functions as a major terminal enzyme for PGE₂ biosynthesis, and mPGES-1 inhibitors are being explored as more selective anti-inflammatory strategies (Yang and Chen, 2016). Conceptually, targeting mPGES-1 may reduce excessive PGE₂ production while potentially preserving other prostanoids that contribute to homeostatic vascular and epithelial functions, thereby offering a mechanistically rational alternative to broad COX inhibition in certain settings. Additionally, studies have indicated that mPGES-1 derived PGE₂ contributes to the regulation of Th17/Th1 immune responses (Maseda et al., 2018). Together, these observations suggest that COX-2 and mPGES-1 driven prostaglandin E2 signaling functions as a context dependent immunomodulatory mechanism, with inflammatory outcomes shaped by the balance between acute repair and chronic amplification under prolonged environmental stress or infection.

These inflammatory outputs are evident across multiple poultry tissues and pathogen host models. In the respiratory mucosa, infectious bronchitis virus (IBV) has been reported to engage COX-2 dependent PGE2 signaling, and pharmacological COX-2 inhibition or EP2/EP4 antagonism can reduce viral replication and lesion severity (Mahmoud et al., 2024a).Beyond respiratory infection, acute inflammatory challenges in the liver and intestine are also characterized by COX-2 upregulation and metabolic barrier disturbances. For example, LPS induced hepatic inflammation involves increased COX-2 expression accompanied by elevated pro-inflammatory cytokines, increased iNOS/NO signaling, and NF-κB activation (Cheng et al., 2017). Similarly, ileal inflammation is associated with increased COX-2 and mPGES-1 expression, accompanied by tight-junction disruption and epithelial permeability. Dietary supplementation has been reported to improve barrier integrity alongside reduced COX-2 and mPGES-1 expression and attenuated oxidative stress (Zhang et al., 2024). Recent reviews have highlighted increased mPGES-1 expression in neuroinflammatory disorders, suggesting that targeting mPGES-1 may attenuate inflammation in specific neuroinflammatory settings (Sluter et al., 2023b).

Regarding inflammatory induction and pathway coupling, poultry exhibits considerable similarity to mammalian systems while retaining species and tissue specific variations. PAMPs such as lipopolysaccharide are sensed in chickens via the TLR4 and MD 2 complex and preferentially engage MyD88 dependent signaling, with downstream activation of NF-κB (Keestra and van Putten, 2008). In LPS challenged chickens, expression of COX-2 and other arachidonic acid metabolizing enzymes is altered in immune tissues, and dietary acetylsalicylic acid or vitamin E can modulate this transcriptional response (Konieczka et al., 2019). Importantly, avian innate immune sensing and effector cell composition exhibit poultry relevant features that may influence the amplitude of PTGS2 induction. Chickens rely heavily on heterophils, the avian functional counterparts of neutrophils, as principal innate effector cells that shape inflammatory responses (Genovese et al., 2013). In chicken heterophils, bacterial Toll like receptor agonists can induce NF-κB dependent PGE2 production, and pharmacologic inhibition supports a requirement for COX-2 activity in this response (Kogut et al., 2012). Moreover, nucleated thrombocytes respond to lipopolysaccharide through Toll like receptor 4, with downstream MAPK and NF-κB activation leading to COX-2 induction and PGE2 production, indicating an additional circulating compartment that can contribute to prostaglandin signaling in poultry (Scott and Owens, 2008). Consistent with this framework, gene expression analysis shows that thrombocytes mount robust immune gene responses to ligands for Toll like receptor 4 and Toll like receptor 21 (St Paul et al., 2012). In addition, chickens possess a distinct Toll like receptor repertoire with unique functional properties, and functional Toll like receptor mediated recognition has been demonstrated in avian systems, including Toll like receptor 5 dependent flagellin sensing, suggesting that upstream COX-2 regulatory pathways should be interpreted in a species specific context (Iqbal et al., 2005; Keestra et al., 2013). In lymphoid organs such as the spleen and bursa of Fabricius, LPS challenge has been associated with increased PTGS2 (COX-2) expression and broader remodeling of arachidonic acid pathway transcripts (Konieczka et al., 2019). In the bursa of Fabricius, LPS induced inflammatory activation is accompanied by NF-κB signaling together with increased iNOS and COX-2 expression and elevated pro inflammatory cytokines, and these responses can be attenuated by anti-inflammatory supplementation (Cheng et al., 2014). In avian inflammatory settings, NLRP3 inflammasome activation has also been demonstrated in macrophage-based models, supporting additional coupling between cytokine maturation and inflammatory amplification, with broader mechanistic context provided by inflammasome focused reviews (Kelley et al., 2019; Peng et al., 2022). More generally, PGE2 is a well-established immunomodulator; evidence from mammalian systems indicates that EP2 and EP4 mediated cAMP signaling can dampen selected innate effector functions while reshaping adaptive cytokine profiles, providing a useful framework for interpreting context dependent consequences of COX-2 driven PGE2 production (Kalinski, 2012). In LPS challenge and Gram negative enteritis associated models, concomitant increases in TNF-α and IL-1β together with activation of COX-2 related prostaglandin signaling can be attenuated by selective COX-2 inhibition, for example with celecoxib, or by dietary antioxidant and anti-inflammatory supplementation, with concurrent reductions in inflammatory indices and improvement in tissue injury or barrier related outcomes (Liu et al., 2023b; Xie et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2024).

Mechanistically, oxidative stress can promote COX-2 induction through MAPK and NF-κB activation (Onodera et al., 2015), and PGE2 signaling through EP receptors may remodel innate and adaptive immune responses in a manner consistent with reinforcing feedback between COX-2 and PGE2 (Obermajer et al., 2011). Consistent with this framework, a Salmonella infection model in chickens indicates a phase dependent alignment between inflammatory dynamics and immunometabolism remodeling, shifting from glycolysis and mTOR associated activation to AMPK linked adaptive programs (Kogut et al., 2016). In chickens, succinic acid signaling via SUCNR1 has been described as a context dependent metabolic axis that shapes macrophage polarization and inflammatory gene programs, reinforcing the context dependence of succinate signaling in avian tissues (Chen et al., 2025). This framework is consistent with evidence that chronic heat stress and other production relevant oxidative challenges can promote inflammatory injury, with potential downstream consequences for gut integrity and performance (Aryal et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2024; Oke et al., 2024). A comprehensive review of ROS inflammation crosstalk highlights the therapeutic rationale for ROS scavenging approaches in anti inflammatory strategies (Liu et al., 2023a). Accordingly, production oriented interventions that strengthen endogenous antioxidant defenses, including Nrf2 associated responses, and provide antioxidant support represent a feasible route to mitigate inflammation and protect performance in poultry systems (Mavrommatis et al., 2021; Oke et al., 2024; Xie et al., 2023).

Regarding pathogen interactions, IBV has been reported to induce COX-2 activity and PGE2 production, with pharmacological COX-2 inhibition or blockade of EP2 and EP4 reducing viral replication and inflammatory mediator expression (Mahmoud et al., 2024a, 2024b). By contrast, evidence from NDV infection models suggests dynamic regulation of COX-2, and experimental manipulation indicates that COX-2 tends to constrain NDV replication, as COX-2 inhibition enhances replication whereas COX-2 overexpression suppresses replication, while exogenous PGE2 shows limited direct effects (Wang et al., 2020).

In summary, COX-2 induction represents a central biochemical entry point through which inflammatory stimuli are translated into prostanoid mediated effector programs in poultry. PGE2, shaped by downstream synthase selectivity, contributes to the regulation of vascular and leukocyte responses as well as epithelial barrier integrity, with outcomes determined by tissue specific receptor coupling and disease context. Upstream, Toll like receptor and cytokine signaling converges on NF-κB and MAPK pathways to drive PTGS2 transcription, thereby linking innate sensing to sustained inflammatory output. Collectively, this framework supports interpretation of COX-2 not merely as an inflammatory marker but as a mechanistically relevant node that can be targeted to modulate inflammatory burden while preserving essential homeostatic functions.

COX-2 mediated crosstalk between oxidative stress and inflammation

Substantial evidence from various studies has demonstrated that ROS and COX-2 do not function independently but rather form a self-reinforcing positive feedback network through multiple intersecting signaling pathways, which helps maintain and intensify inflammatory responses (Sancho et al., 2011). Under the combined influence of ROS derived from mitochondria and NOX, accumulated ROS activate the TAK1–IKK pathway, leading to activation of the NF-κB pathway. Concurrently, ROS stimulate the MAPK cascade, including ERK, JNK, and p38, to activate the transcription factor AP-1; NF-κB and AP-1 are key transcriptional regulators of PTGS2 expression, upregulating COX-2 transcription and promoting prostaglandin E₂ (PGE₂) synthesis (Barbieri et al., 2004; Onodera et al., 2015). In the downstream part of this amplification circuit, PGE₂ regulates intracellular signaling cascades through its receptor subtypes: EP1 primarily couples to the Ca²⁺ and PKC pathway, while EP2 and EP4 activate the cAMP/PKA pathway. This prostaglandin-mediated signaling enhances NOX activity, increases ROS levels (Reyes-Martinez et al., 2019). In certain cell types, further promotes COX-2 expression, closing a self-sustaining loop in which ROS activates NF-κB and AP-1, induces COX-2 and PGE₂ signaling through EP receptors, and enhances NOX-dependent ROS generation (Ricciotti and FitzGerald, 2011; Steinert et al., 2009). At the regulatory level, metabolic inflammatory intersections, such as PI3K/Akt-mTOR and AMPK, can either promote or suppress this network depending on cellular conditions (Chang et al., 2010; Tyagi et al., 2020; Yi et al., 2011). Meanwhile, endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress and NLRP3 inflammasome activation amplify inflammatory signals through ROS dependent mechanisms (Zhao et al., 2020; Zhou et al., 2020). In terms of substrate availability, Ca²⁺-dependent membrane translocation and phosphorylation of cytosolic phospholipase A₂α (cPLA₂α) increases the availability of AA (Hirabayashi et al., 2004). Under hypoxic or inflammatory stress, HIF-1α and NF-κB can act in a coordinated manner to promote COX-2 transcription (Bruning et al., 2012; Kaidi et al., 2006; Schmedtje et al., 1997), Furthermore, recent reviews have explored the intricate mechanisms underlying the interaction between ROS and inflammation and their implications in chronic diseases (Forrester et al., 2018; Ranneh et al., 2017). Another important regulatory feature is mediator class switching, where COX-2 derived products, such as PGD₂ to 15-d-PGJ₂ and specialized pro-resolving mediators (SPMs), provide time dependent negative feedback to restrain NF-κB/AP-1-driven programs, influencing the balance between inflammatory persistence and resolution (Basil and Levy, 2016; Cernuda-Morollon et al., 2001; Dennis and Norris, 2015; Oh et al., 2011).

In poultry production systems, multifactorial stressors, such as heat stress, high stocking density, mycotoxin contamination, and heavy metal exposure, synergistically increase ROS generation and activate TLR4 and MyD88 signaling with downstream MAPK and NF-κB activation, ultimately inducing coordinated COX-2 and mPGES-1 activation that increases PGE₂ production (Basiouni et al., 2023). Such coordinated regulation may be relevant to the inflammatory response in poultry. In non-avian systems, these enzymes are commonly co-induced by pro-inflammatory stimuli and together form a functionally coupled pathway for inducible PGE₂ synthesis (Jia et al., 2015). Pharmacologic studies in non-avian models further indicate that interference with mPGES-1 can dampen PGE₂-associated inflammatory signaling(Sluter et al., 2023a). In poultry, concurrent regulation of COX-2 and mPGES-1 has been observed under inflammatory, nutritional, and infection-associated conditions, supporting the possibility of a similar functional relationship However, direct evidence for consistent reciprocal regulation in avian tissues remains limited, and whether inhibition of one component reproducibly suppresses the other has yet to be established. Thus, current evidence supports the biological relevance of COX-2/mPGES-1 coupling in poultry, while indicating that its mechanistic characteristics require further validation across tissues and challenge models. Evidence from chicken models demonstrates that activation of the COX-2 and mPGES-1 pathway, together with enhanced PGE₂ signaling, is often accompanied by increased EP receptor expression and oxidative stress and is associated with epithelial and barrier injury, immune imbalance, and reduced growth performance (Ma et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2024). However, direct causal evidence that PGE₂, acting specifically through EP4, universally drives NOX dependent ROS production across poultry tissues remains limited. Mitochondrial oxidative stress is frequently associated with coordinated upregulation of COX-2 and mPGES-1, while pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as TNF-α and IL-1β, further amplify COX-2 expression through NF-κB and MAPK signaling. These molecular events are strongly linked to impaired growth, metabolic dysregulation, and barrier dysfunction at the systemic level (Liu et al., 2021; Oke et al., 2024; Zhong et al., 2024).

In pathogen host interactions, avian respiratory viruses such as infectious bronchitis virus (IBV) and avian influenza virus can exploit the COX-2, PGE₂, and ROS network to enhance mucosal susceptibility and tissue injury. Ex vivo and cellular studies in chicken tracheal models indicate that COX-2 inhibition reduces viral replication and tissue damage, highlighting this axis as a pathological amplifier in the progression of infection (Mahmoud et al., 2024a).

Overall, the signaling network linking ROS with COX-2 and mPGES-1 driven PGE₂ production represents a central signaling hub that connects oxidative imbalance to chronic inflammation (Onodera et al., 2015; Sampey et al., 2005). This network interfaces with NF-κB and AP-1, MAPK, PI3K Akt signaling, STAT3 dependent inflammatory signaling, and ROS-sensitive NLRP3 inflammasome activation, thereby shaping both the magnitude and duration of inflammatory responses (Abais et al., 2015; Basu et al., 2017; de Oliveira et al., 2012; Michalak and Michalak, 2025). Drawing from these mechanistic insights, current evidence suggests several translational intervention directions. In poultry production settings, mitigating mitochondrial and NOX derived ROS has been widely discussed as a practical strategy to attenuate oxidative burden and improve health and performance outcomes (Oke et al., 2024). At the upstream signaling level, pharmacological suppression of TAK1 can blunt ROS driven induction of COX-2 and PGE₂ in cellular models (Onodera et al., 2015). In addition, preclinical cell evidence supports that disrupting prostaglandin receptor signaling together with COX-2 inhibition can weaken a reinforcing circuit involving EP4, NOX4, and ROS production (Reyes-Martinez et al., 2019). Strengthening Nrf2 mediated antioxidant defenses, including HO-1, has been shown to alleviate LPS induced intestinal oxidative stress in broilers and improve barrier related outcomes (Chen et al., 2024). To strengthen translational reliability, future studies should use clearly defined and reproducible stress and pathogen challenge protocols and adopt multi-parameter outcome panels that combine performance readouts with intestinal barrier and immune related markers, including targeted gene expression measures such as receptor associated transcripts (Arbani et al., 2025; Ducatelle et al., 2018; Mantzios et al., 2024).

The COX-2 and mPGES-1 axis and its PGE2 output form an amplification circuit linking mitochondria and NOX-derived ROS to NF-kB and MAPK/AP-1 activation, thereby sustaining inflammatory gene expression. In poultry relevant stress, barrier dysfunction, and infection models, activation of this redox prostaglandin network is consistently associated with epithelial injury, immune imbalance, and reduced performance, although receptor and tissue specific causal mechanisms remain incompletely defined. These findings support the use of standardized challenge designs and multi parameter outcome panels to strengthen mechanistic inference and guide interventions targeting oxidative stress, upstream signaling, and prostaglandin pathways in poultry.

Implications for poultry health and production

In terms of impacts on production performance, heat stress, overcrowding or high stocking density, and feed mycotoxins may co-occur and jointly exacerbate tissue oxidative load and pro-inflammatory responses, contributing to reduced daily weight gain, poorer feed conversion rate, and greater metabolic burden (Adugna et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2020; Son et al., 2022). Systematic reviews and experimental evidence further indicate that heat stress disrupts gut microbial communities and microbial functional pathways and is closely associated with barrier dysfunction, oxidative stress, and inflammation, while immune stress models also show coordinated microbiota and metabolite shifts alongside intestinal inflammatory and oxidative injury (Hu et al., 2024a; Ncho, 2025).Furthermore, broiler studies indicate that high stocking density can modify cecal microbial communities and also shift ileal microbiota, alongside increased intestinal inflammatory markers, impaired barrier related gene expression, and deterioration in growth traits (Dai et al., 2022; Dong et al., 2025; Li et al., 2022). Regarding mycotoxins, dietary deoxynivalenol and fumonisins, particularly under concurrent heat stress, can exacerbate intestinal barrier disruption and endotoxin associated responses, with consequent reductions in feed intake and growth performance (Reisinger et al., 2024; Wan et al., 2022). These production relevant molecular changes are consistent with activation of COX-2 and mPGES-1 driven PGE2 synthesis and EP receptor signaling reported in broiler stress models, while mechanistic evidence from non-avian systems suggests that EP4 signaling can couple to NOX4 associated ROS generation in a cell type specific manner, supporting a testable mechanistic link between molecular readouts and production endpoints (Liu et al., 2023b; Reyes-Martinez et al., 2019).

In terms of organ and barrier health, heat challenge can increase intestinal permeability and is accompanied by altered expression of tight junction components in a segment and model dependent manner, which may contribute to reduced robustness of gut function under commercial stress conditions (Liu et al., 2022b; Nanto-Hara et al., 2020). Targeted nutritional and functional additives have been evaluated in vivo to mitigate heat stress related oxidative and inflammatory burdens; for example, rutin supplementation has been reported to improve antioxidant capacity and inflammatory and immune readouts, with concurrent benefits in growth performance (Hafez et al., 2025). Sodium butyrate supplementation has been shown in broiler and layer chicken models to alleviate heat associated intestinal morphological injury, improve redox status, and increase tight junction related readouts, alongside performance related outcomes (Ruixia et al., 2024). Similarly, trace nutrients such as organic zinc (Zn proteinate) and selenium have been reported to support barrier associated endpoints under heat or toxin related oxidative stress, in part through modulation of NF-κB linked signaling and antioxidant defenses (Calik et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2022; Hu et al., 2024b). Therefore, under multiple stressor accumulation, prioritizing gut barrier protection together with mitochondria oriented antioxidant support is mechanistically reasonable for limiting downstream oxidative inflammatory escalation (Olayiwola and Adedokun, 2025).

In terms of immune response and pathogen interactions, chronic heat and management or nutritional stress often reduce spleen and bursa indices and lead to persistent activation of TLR4 and MyD88 signaling with downstream NF-κB activation, resulting in a high inflammation and low adaptation immune state that impairs adaptation (Chen et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2022). Of particular concern, numerous viral and bacterial studies show that pathogen associated stress directly activates the COX-2 driven PGE₂ signaling to amplify inflammation and tissue damage: for example, in chicken respiratory and macrophage models, infectious bronchitis virus (IBV) and other respiratory viruses upregulate COX-2 and mPGES-1 and increase PGE₂ production, which alters cell death programs and replication efficiency; pharmacological inhibition of this axis or blockade of EP2 and EP4 receptors reduces viral load and pro-inflammatory responses (Mahmoud et al., 2024a, 2024b). Concurrently, multiple nutrition pharmacology combinations in animal models, such as COX-2/EP antagonism combined with antioxidant and barrier repair strategies, have demonstrated the potential to suppress inflammatory amplification, restore barrier function, and improve growth traits, supporting a layered intervention strategy to cope with the multi axis accumulation of heat, stocking density, toxicants, and pathogens(Oke et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2022).

In terms of immune response and pathogen interactions, chronic heat and production stressors such as high stocking density and nutritional toxicants often reduce spleen and bursa indices and bias innate immune signaling toward sustained TLR4-MyD88-NF-κB activation with cytokine imbalance, forming a high inflammation, low adaptation immune state that undermines resilience (Chen et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2017; Yang et al., 2022). During infection, pathogen associated cues induce the COX-2 driven PGE2 axis, which can reshape inflammatory output, cell fate decisions, and replication efficiency in host tissues; in chicken respiratory and macrophage models, infectious bronchitis virus increases COX-2 activity and PGE2 production, and pharmacological inhibition of this axis or blockade of EP2 and EP4 receptors reduces viral replication markers and pro-inflammatory mediators (Mahmoud et al., 2024a, 2024b). Consistent with production relevant immune stress, COX-2 coupled with mPGES-1 and downstream EP signaling has been linked to inflammation associated growth suppression, supporting COX-2 pathway targeting as a practical lever in broilers(Liu et al., 2023b). Accordingly, a layered intervention that integrates management optimization with antioxidant and barrier support nutrition, together with targeted modulation of the COX-2, mPGES-1 and EP axis and upstream redox amplification such as NOX associated ROS sources, provides a testable strategy to restrain inflammatory amplification and improve health resilience under overlapping stressors (Konieczka et al., 2019; Oke et al., 2024; Surai et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2024; Zhong et al., 2024).

Multiple commercial stressors converge on oxidative and inflammatory escalation that compromises microbiota stability, gut barrier integrity, and immune balance, thereby reducing growth efficiency and increasing disease vulnerability. Across these pathways, COX-2 functions as a central transducer and amplifier that links redox disturbance and innate immune activation to PGE2-dependent tissue injury and performance losses. Accordingly, this review highlights COX-2 centered intervention logic in which targeted modulation of the COX-2, mPGES-1, EP axis, integrated with management and nutritional optimization, provides a practical framework to improve poultry health resilience and production stability.

Strategies to modulate COX-2 in poultry

Current evidence from avian cellular systems and available in vivo models suggests that modulation of coordinated COX-2 and mPGES-1 activity with downstream PGE₂ signaling via EP receptors can attenuate inflammatory activation; however, effects on viral replication appear virus and context dependent. Representative nutritional and pharmacological interventions targeting the COX-2 signaling pathway across different experimental models, tissues, and stress or pathogen contexts are summarized in Table 2. In avian viral infections, accumulating evidence indicates that activation of COX-2 activity and PGE₂ production may contribute to viral replication and inflammatory pathology in a context dependent manner. Notably, PGE₂ driven lipid metabolic remodeling has been reported to support Marek’s disease virus replication (Boodhoo et al., 2019). In addition, COX-2 driven PGE₂ signaling has been implicated in regulating infectious bronchitis virus replication in avian macrophages, and pharmacological blockade of COX-2 or downstream EP2 and EP4 signaling has been associated with reduced viral burden and pro-inflammatory mediators (Mahmoud et al., 2024b, 2025). Importantly, across multiple challenge models, pharmacological COX-2 inhibition or nutritional downregulation of COX-2 driven PGE₂ signaling is frequently accompanied by partial reversal of pro-inflammatory readouts and barrier impairment, supporting a functional contribution of this axis beyond a passive inflammatory marker. Moreover, meloxicam, a preferential COX-2 inhibitor, is associated with reduced PGE₂ production and the restoration of T cell functionality in immunosuppressed chickens (Kamble et al., 2021), while its pharmacokinetic and withdrawal data provide a foundation for short course, context specific therapeutic use in poultry production (Shao et al., 2023). Although selective or preferential COX-2 inhibition may offer practical anti-inflammatory value in poultry, potential adverse effects also warrant consideration. In mammalian systems, sustained COX-2 inhibition has been linked to disruption of the prostacyclin and thromboxane balance, with consequent concern regarding cardiovascular or cerebrovascular risk under some conditions (Fanelli et al., 2017; Patrono, 2016). Direct evidence for comparable adverse outcomes in poultry remains limited, and the extent to which such mechanisms operate in avian physiology has not been systematically defined. Nevertheless, because COX-2 lies upstream of multiple prostanoid branches, similar safety issues cannot be excluded mechanistically in poultry. This consideration further supports careful evaluation of dose, treatment duration, pharmacokinetics, residue depletion, withdrawal intervals, and feasible safety windows when COX-2-targeted strategies are considered for poultry applications (Richards et al., 2022; Shao et al., 2023).

Multiple nutritional factors modulate the COX-2 axis through antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, barrier repair, and receptor or metabolic pathways. For instance, chlorogenic acid (CGA) downregulates hypothalamic COX-2 driven PGE₂ signaling through EP4 in LPS challenged broilers, along with improvements in feeding behavior and growth related pathways, demonstrating upstream interception along the nutrition, neuroendocrine, and inflammation axis (Bai et al., 2025). Similarly, the aspirin eugenol ester (AEE) derivative concurrently downregulates COX-2 and mPGES-1 (PTGES) in the ileum and restores tight junction proteins, showing a synergistic effect of prostaglandin inhibition with barrier repair (Zhang et al., 2024). Furthermore, resveratrol suppresses COX-2 expression or enzymatic activity in Wenchang chickens and HD11 macrophage models, alleviating mucosal inflammation and enhancing growth and barrier function (Xie et al., 2025), while myricetin attenuates intestinal and skeletal muscle COX-2 transcription and upregulates HO-1 and NQO1 in Eimeria challenged broilers. Moreover, natural or synthetic COX-2 inhibitors combined with butyrate work together effectively in chicken macrophages and peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) to induce host defense peptides (HDPs), supporting a feasible nutrition pharmacology crosstalk for COX-2 modulation (Yang et al., 2022).

From the perspective of lipid and redox, dietary fatty acid composition, together with associated vitamin E status, can modulate hepatic COX-2 protein expression under inflammatory challenge, reflecting how membrane lipid substrates and redox microenvironments affect prostaglandin biosynthesis (Gonzalez et al., 2011). Similarly, selenium mitigates Pb exposure-associated increases in inflammatory readouts including COX-2 and NF-κB signaling in chicken neutrophils, revealing conditional attenuation of coupling between redox imbalance, inflammation, and COX-2 (Li et al., 2017).For polyphenols such as quercetin, tea catechins, and curcumin, which exhibit verified antioxidant and barrier enhancing capacities, studies that do not directly quantify COX-2, PGES, and PGE₂ should be regarded as indirect evidence rather than overstated as direct COX-2 inhibition.

Meanwhile, nutritional and pharmacological interventions such as myricetin, AEE, and meloxicam are consistently associated with COX-2 downregulation and improvements in inflammatory, barrier, or immune biomarkers across different models, providing convergent support for translation, whereas direct PGE2 reduction is best supported where PGE2 is explicitly quantified, such as in AEE and meloxicam related models (El-Ghareeb et al., 2023; Gonzalez et al., 2011; Kamble et al., 2021; Shao et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024).

Toward applied translational frameworks, converging evidence supports a combined decoupling approach: under pathogenic, stress, or toxicological conditions, co-targeting COX-2 and mPGES-1 inhibition with antioxidant support, NOX suppression, and barrier and immune metabolic restoration could disrupt amplification loops, given that EP receptor signaling has been shown to couple to NOX4 dependent ROS generation in a cell type specific manner, and COX-2 pathway inhibition can be accompanied by substrate diversion within the arachidonic acid network (Mukhopadhyay et al., 2023; Reyes-Martinez et al., 2019). In line with this notion, HETs have been characterized in chickens, and ochratoxin A induced HET formation depends on NADPH oxidase as well as ERK and p38 signaling, identifying NOX as a manipulable amplifier node (Chuammitri et al., 2009; Han et al., 2019). In terms of precision nutrition, intervention timing, dosage, and combinations should be optimized based on tissue specific COX-2 expression and EP receptor profiles, inflammatory oxidative load, and barrier integrity; for example, resveratrol and chlorogenic acid, together with butyrate based strategies, may provide stage specific complementarity (Xie et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2022).

Regarding translational applications, future research may progress along two main directions. One direction involves deeper mechanism mapping and scenario specific stratification: in key tissues such as the hypothalamus, gut, and respiratory tract, map out the expression and activity landscape of COX-2 and mPGES-1 with downstream PGE₂ signaling via EP receptors and integrate this with NF-κB and MAPK activity, 8-iso-PGF₂α, NOX activity, and barrier metrics in dose and time coupled analyses; in the IBV system, further clarify links between virus promotion and inflammatory pathology and regulated cell death programs, including necrosis and apoptosis (Mahmoud et al., 2024b, 2025), whereas in NDV systems reconcile the opposing evidence, given reports that COX-2 can restrict viral proliferation and exogenous PGE2 may show limited effects, and define dependencies on pathogen, tissue, and time (Wang et al., 2020).

The other direction focuses on stratified targeting and integrated disruption strategies. Companion markers may include PGE₂, EP receptor profiles, PTGS2 and PTGES expression, NOX, HO-1, and NQO1, and cytokines such as IL-1β and IL-6. An integrated strategy combining COX-2 or mPGES-1 inhibition with antioxidant and NOX suppression and with barrier repair and immunometabolism remodeling may weaken amplification in which PGE₂ signaling via EP receptors enhances NOX dependent ROS generation and may limit compensatory diversion within the AA network.(Ganesh et al., 2012; Reyes-Martinez et al., 2019)

Existing evidence supports natural or synthetic COX-2 inhibitor with butyrate combinations in chicken immune cells that induce host defense peptides (HDPs) while achieving an inflammation downregulated and without overt immunosuppressive signals in the tested settings profile (Yang et al., 2022). Earlier findings indicate that 5-LOX bypass inhibition or n-3 fatty acid supplementation in coccidiosis models can alleviate growth suppression, suggesting potential advantages of combined application with COX-2 inhibition (Allen and Danforth, 1998; Korver et al., 1997; Tompkins et al., 2024). Regarding practical implementation, it is recommended to adopt standardized stress and pathogen models and scenario based factor designs, combined with multiscale joint measurement of eicosanoid profiles, receptor profiles, signal readouts, and barrier and production traits, and to establish effective, safe, and translatable interventions through pharmacokinetic evidence, residue depletion and withdrawal interval evidence, and precision nutrition combinations (Bai et al., 2025; Shao et al., 2023; Xie et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2022).

In the context of genetic and breeding, locus mapping for genes such as PTGS2, PTGES, EP receptors, Nrf2, and NOX, should be integrated with phenotype genotype correlations, while biochemical markers such as PGE₂, 8-iso-PGF₂α, NOX activity, and IL-1β/IL-6 can serve as companion indices for stratified intervention and evaluation. Cross virus comparison across IBV, MDV, and NDV systems can strengthen the empirical chain by clarifying when COX-2 and PGE2 signaling is associated with enhanced replication and inflammatory pathology and when it is not, thereby defining the boundaries for COX-2 or EP targeted modulation (Boodhoo et al., 2019; Mahmoud et al., 2024b, 2025).

Overall, these observations support COX-2-centered intervention logic in poultry while also highlighting the need for tissue and context specific stratification of downstream targets and biomarkers. Building on the above evidence, intervention at different levels of the COX-2/mPGES-1/PGE2/EP axis is unlikely to be equivalent in poultry. The current avian literature provides the most direct support for modulation at the COX-2 level, as attenuation of COX-2-associated signaling in broiler and chicken challenge models has been linked to reduced inflammatory injury, partial improvement in barrier-related outcomes, or altered pathogen-associated responses, depending on tissue and challenge context (Liu et al., 2023b; Mahmoud et al., 2024b; Zhang et al., 2024). However, because COX-2 lies upstream of multiple prostanoid branches, broad suppression at this level is also expected to influence mediators beyond PGE2, and evidence from non-avian systems suggests that such upstream intervention may provide breadth of effect at the expense of pathway selectivity (Ganesh et al., 2012; Ricciotti and FitzGerald, 2011). By contrast, mPGES-1 may represent a more distal node more directly associated with inducible PGE2 synthesis downstream of COX-2, although its selective translational value in poultry remains largely inferential at present (Bergqvist et al., 2020; Murakami and Kudo, 2006). A similar caution applies to receptor-level targeting. In poultry, the available evidence for EP-directed intervention is derived mainly from IBV-related models, in which interference with EP2/EP4 signaling has been associated with attenuation of virus-related inflammatory responses (Mahmoud et al., 2024a, 2025). Overall, the available poultry literature supports a comparative interpretation in which COX-2, mPGES-1, and EP2/EP4 differ mainly in pathway level, breadth of effect, and putative selectivity, an approach that is also consistent with comparative PGE₂-targeting frameworks described in non-avian systems (Hou et al., 2022). Among these, COX-2 currently has the strongest direct support in avian models, whereas the proposed advantages of mPGES-1 and EP2/EP4 targeting remain context-dependent, largely extrapolative, and in need of further validation in poultry before clearer target preference can be inferred.

Conclusion and perspectives

Taken together, COX-2 driven PGE2 signaling represents a plausible amplification node linking oxidative stress to inflammatory load, but its net biological output is shaped by tissue specificity and disease stage, supporting stratified intervention designs rather than uniform inhibition. In modern poultry production, coordinated activation of COX-2 and mPGES-1 with downstream PGE2 signaling via EP receptors has been frequently reported under nutritional, environmental, and infectious challenges, and is often aligned with redox imbalance, immune activation, barrier dysfunction, and impaired growth performance. These features underscore that COX-2 activity may act as a driver of inflammatory escalation in some contexts, while in others it may contribute to regulated stress adaptation.

From a mechanistic standpoint, future studies should clarify how COX-2 centered signaling differs across tissues and stressor or pathogen contexts, with emphasis on temporal dynamics and interactions with redox and metabolic processes. Greater resolution of when COX-2 driven PGE2 signaling shifts from adaptive immune regulation to maladaptive amplification will help reconcile heterogeneous observations across models and endpoints. Such evidence will also improve the selection of actionable biomarkers and measurable endpoints, including eicosanoid profiles, EP receptor expression and signaling features, and barrier integrity markers, needed to guide hypothesis driven and mechanism informed intervention strategies.

From an applied perspective, these insights argue against indiscriminate suppression of COX-2 signaling and instead favor targeted, integrated modulation tailored to specific stress conditions and production stages. Combining prostaglandin pathway modulation with oxidative stress management, together with nutritional measures that strengthen barrier function and support immune homeostasis, may offer practical benefits for poultry well-being and production resilience. Ultimately, aligning mechanistic stratification with precision nutrition, pharmacology, and management practices, while defining feasible safety windows, will be critical for translating COX-2 focused research into sustainable solutions in poultry production systems. In addition, future translational studies should address not only efficacy but also safety boundaries of COX-2-directed intervention in poultry, particularly with respect to potential prostanoid imbalance, tissue-specific physiological effects, and application windows compatible with production use.

Reply
Authors: Juan M. Farina, Mohammed Tiseer Abbas, Kamal Awad, Chadi Ayoub, Reza Arsanjani
Published: 2025

We thank Dr Trimaille and colleagues for their interesting comments regarding our paper.1 In their letter, the authors suggest that the benefits of sodium–glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) on structural valve degeneration (SVD) could be mainly confined to transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) patients, presumably due to the presence of preserved native valve tissue. Although we acknowledge the plausibility of this mechanism and appreciate the nuanced interpretation,2 we believe that SGLT2i benefits in bioprosthetic valve preservation could extend to both TAVR and surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR) populations. In our retrospective cohort, SGLT2i were linked to significantly fewer SVD events with similar raw rates of SVD in treated patients (SAVR 10.7%, TAVR 11.1%), and there was no significant interaction by procedure type (P = 0.331), indicating a consistent protective effect across both TAVR and SAVR.1 In addition, we propose that the absence of native tissue in SAVR does not preclude a benefit from SGLT2i, given that the key SVD mechanisms—leaflet inflammation, microthrombosis, fibrosis, and endothelial dysfunction—occur both in TAVR and SAVR.1

Importantly, studies using 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography imaging demonstrated inflammatory activity around bioprosthetic valves up to a year following SAVR even in the absence of infection, reflecting tissue remodeling and persistent inflammation.3 This may represent a targetable window for agents with anti-inflammatory properties, such as SGLT2i. In addition, hypoattenuated leaflet thickening, a potential early marker of thrombosis and dysfunction, has been documented following both TAVR and SAVR.4 SGLT2i can reduce platelet activation, improve endothelial integrity, and modulate prothrombotic pathways, suggesting a potential role in preventing hypoattenuated leaflet thickening regardless of the implantation technique.

In summary, we acknowledge the potential for an amplified benefit of SGLT2i in TAVR due to the retained native valve. However, although further data are needed, the current available evidence suggests that the potential benefits of SGLT2i in bioprosthetic valve protection may extend beyond TAVR to include SAVR recipients as well. Further dedicated studies with subgroup stratification by implantation technique will be essential to refine our understanding of the mechanistic and clinical impact of SGLT2i on bioprosthetic valve protection.

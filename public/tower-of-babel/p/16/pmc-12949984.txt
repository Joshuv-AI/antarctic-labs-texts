Harnessing data science for health discovery and innovation in Africa (DS-I Africa)
Authors: Chenfeng Xiong, Joyce Nakatumba Nabende, Michelle Skelton, Gaone Retshabile, Ambroise Wonkam, Anna Winters, David Patrick Kateete, Donrich Thaldar, Francis Agamah, Kofi A. Amegah, Nchangwi Syntia Munung, Nicola Mulder, Rolanda Julius, Rumi Chunara, Segun Fatumo, Mayowa Owolabi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The integration of data science and artificial intelligence approaches, through multidisciplinary collaboration, is set to advance health research in Africa. Here, we describe the Harnessing Data Science for Health Discovery and Innovation in Africa-funded effort for enhancing healthcare delivery, disease surveillance, predictive modelling, capacity building and research innovation across Africa and globally.

Mission and vision

In an era where data science and artificial intelligence (AI) are transforming human societies, health research is poised to benefit significantly. Data science enables the analysis of large and complex datasets, and drives innovation in diagnostics, personalised medicine, and predictive models for public health protection. This approach not only enhances individual patient care but also strengthens public health strategies by integrating various types of data, such as clinical, demographic, and environmental data. However, Africa still faces ongoing challenges in infrastructure, funding, and local capacity, all of which are critical to fully realising the potential of data science to enhance health research outcomes.

In this comment, we discuss the Harnessing Data Science for Health Discovery and Innovation in Africa (DS-I Africa) Initiative, funded with $88 million over five years, which aims to advance health research across Africa by integrating data science and AI. DS-I Africa’s core objectives include building data science capacity and expertise among African scientists; establishing research hubs across 22 African countries in Africa and the United States; enhancing data access and infrastructure through platforms such as the eLwazi Open Data Science Platform (https://elwazi.org); and promoting ethical and responsible data use. To date, DS-I Africa has awarded 38 grants (Fig. 1), leading to innovations such as AI-powered diagnostics, real-time disease surveillance, and region-specific predictive models. DS-I Africa envisions a future where African-led institutions spearhead cutting-edge research, aiming to cultivate long-term resilience and innovation in Africa’s health systems. Advancing data science and AI in healthcare in sub-Saharan Africa

Data science and AI are revolutionising healthcare systems globally, and can offer innovative solutions to challenges in diagnostics, treatment, and public health. The DS-I Initiative has played a pivotal role in fostering AI-driven innovations, enhancing healthcare delivery through the analysis of complex datasets, and promoting targeted health interventions. AI and big data have demonstrated remarkable potential to transform healthcare by enhancing diagnostic accuracy, offering predictive modelling, and enabling personalised medicine, as seen in colorectal cancer screening1 and cervical cancer detection2. Technologies such as large language models and multimodal models have been adapted to enable the integration and analysis of diverse data types3. Other initiatives include AI-powered predictive modelling that aids policymakers in anticipating disease outbreaks and designing interventions, such as through health survey data analysis in Rwanda and pandemic tracking by DS-I Africa.

Access to well-curated and demographically diverse datasets is a key requirement for successfully deploying AI tools and applications in healthcare. Currently, there is an underrepresentation of biomedical data from Africa in research, despite Africa’s significantly larger genetic and ecological biodiversity4, particularly for high-throughput multi-omic data analysis. Using AI models trained on datasets that are not inclusive will have serious repercussions, as they underperform and can exacerbate existing bias and health inequities. DS-I Africa aims to integrate diverse and emerging data sources, which has been achieved through the eLwazi Open Data Science Platform, which integrates standardised metadata from 116 datasets from the DS-I Africa research projects.

Embedding ethics in data science for health research and innovation in Africa

As data science and AI implementation continue to advance health research, it is imperative to address the ethical, legal, and social issues (ELSI) that arise. DS-I Africa recognises the need for ethical, equitable, responsible, and culturally sensitive use of personal data to build trustworthy data ecosystems. Key ELSI considerations include informed consent and privacy, mitigating bias and ensuring equity, clarifying data ownership and cross-border sharing, building public trust and ensuring benefit sharing, and fostering public engagement and patient involvement in data-driven health research5,6.

To address the ELSI issues in data science and AI for health research, DS-I Africa integrated ELSI projects and research hubs into its framework from the outset to collaborate on identifying and addressing existing and emerging ethical challenges that could arise from the implementation of the research projects. Key initiatives include “DS-I Africa Law” for legal clarity in data governance, “REDSSA” for responsible data governance and public trust, and “PUBGEM-Africa” for addressing governance in data science and genomics, promoting public engagement. These initiatives have contributed to policy development, such as revisions to South Africa’s Material Transfer Agreement, Open Science Policy, and Code of Conduct for Research. For example, the Data Science for Social Determinants (DSSD) training programme emphasises embedding equity in data science education by incorporating frameworks that consider social, environmental, and upstream determinants of health, thereby addressing the root causes of inequities. In parallel, DS-I Africa Law and the BRIDGE ELSI project7 have made foundational contributions to the ethical oversight and legal landscape of data governance in Africa. Notable achievements include the development of legal guides for non-lawyers working with data in health research across 12 African countries8,9. These guides, complemented by an AI-powered chatbot that provides accessible legal guidance on data governance (https://www.datalaw.bot.), offer practical tools for navigating complex data regulations.

A key focus has been ensuring compliance with data protection legislation in data-driven health research, examining regulatory frameworks, institutional policies, and the practical challenges of implementing data governance. Additionally, attention has been given to the governance of data-driven health research, including consent processes, codes of conduct, ethical implications of data collection and sharing, and the role of ethics advisory committees. Overall, the DS-I Africa consortium has adopted a comprehensive approach to ethical and legal frameworks in data-driven health research, generating applied legal tools. Future efforts will focus on harmonising African data protection and AI governance frameworks to realise the full potential of data-driven health research while safeguarding public trust and ethical integrity.

Emerging and interdisciplinary research explorations

Climate change and air pollution pose significant public health risks in Africa, exacerbated by data gaps. DS-I Africa addresses these challenges by leveraging data science and AI to analyze environmental factors, study climate-sensitive diseases such as malaria, and assess vulnerability to climate-related disasters and the health effects of air pollution on vulnerable populations. The initiative also plays a pivotal role in advancing public health surveillance through big data analytics and emerging technologies, focusing on pandemic monitoring and understanding global mobility and disease spread10. These interdisciplinary efforts demonstrate the transformative potential of data science and AI in fostering innovation and providing responsible, long-term solutions for public health in Africa. DS-I Africa’s interdisciplinary research efforts exemplify the transformative potential of data science and AI in resolving complex challenges such as climate change and disease surveillance. Through the integration of diverse data sources, cutting-edge technologies, and ethical frameworks, the consortium is fostering innovation while ensuring responsible, long-term solutions for public health in Africa.

Fostering innovation and human capital development through collaborative partnerships and open science

DS-I Africa fosters collaborative partnerships with African institutions, the US NIH, and other international stakeholders to strengthen research capacity. This involves leveraging global expertise, establishing training hubs to empower African scientists in managing complex health data, and cultivating a future generation of data scientists. The initiative aims to build a knowledge-based economy for sustainable development in Africa. Open access to information and collaborative research are essential for fostering long-term innovation.

A recent analysis revealed at least 122 data science degree programmes at 60 institutions in Africa11. DS-I Africa is committed to building data science capacity through its seven research training hubs and six Research Education Projects. Spanning several fields, including the use of data science for NCDs and brain health12, this effort has led to 18 new degree offerings since 2022 and is projected to produce 157 master’s, 20 doctoral, and 16 postdoctoral researchers, as well as 51 faculty members. The consortium also includes about 150 early-career researchers. To further support career development, DS-I Africa offers short-term training initiatives, including webinar series, datathons, and short courses, with a flagship course, “Introduction to Data Science for Biomedical Science,” specifically designed for early-career scientists. Recognising that funding shortfalls are a common challenge for Early-Career Researchers (ECRs), the DS-I Africa Coordinating Centre (DSI CC) provides support to alleviate this burden13.

A key component of DS-I Africa’s capacity-building strategy is the development of research infrastructure, including research hubs that foster scientific discovery, innovation, and cross-disciplinary collaboration. These hubs integrate local expertise with global advancements, ensuring African researchers have access to cutting-edge technologies. Through its eLwazi Open Data Science Platform, DS-I Africa provides a robust platform for data sharing and collaboration, promoting FAIR principles and access to diverse, high-quality datasets for groundbreaking research. This holistic approach, which combines partnerships, open science, comprehensive training, and strategic funding, ensures that African researchers and institutions are equipped to lead the continent’s data-driven health research agenda, creating sustainable pathways for long-term growth and success in African health systems.

Conclusions and next steps

The Harnessing Data Science for Health Discovery and Innovation in Africa initiative, funded by the U.S. National Institutes of Health, is a transformative effort to advance health research in Africa through data science, AI, and interdisciplinary collaboration. Key achievements include establishing research hubs across 22 African countries, developing AI-driven health solutions, and promoting open data sharing platforms. The initiative has also embedded ethical and legal frameworks in health research, addressing data governance, informed consent, and equitable benefit sharing. DS-I Africa has leveraged AI and data science to address pressing health challenges, from disease surveillance to genomic research. It has extended its impact to cross-cutting issues such as climate change and air pollution. The consortium has also played a crucial role in shaping policy and legal frameworks related to data governance, AI regulations, and the ethics of genomic research. Despite these successes, challenges remain, including the underrepresentation of African biomedical data, the scarcity of multi-ethnic genomic and multi-omic data, and the need for robust data science and AI governance frameworks. DS-I Africa is poised to continue shaping the future of health research on the continent by fostering a collaborative, ethically grounded, and data-driven research ecosystem, ensuring that the benefits of data science and AI translate into sustainable, equitable health outcomes for African populations.

Comparative diagnostic accuracy of ChatGPT in second mesiobuccal canal detection
Authors: Mehmet Ali Altunkum, Sadullah Kaya
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe presence of a second mesiobuccal canal (MB2) in maxillary first molars is a critical factor in endodontic success. Accurate identification and treatment of this canal significantly reduce the risk of persistent infection and treatment failure. This study evaluated the diagnostic accuracy of ChatGPT in identifying MB2 canals using structured data derived from CBCT images.MethodsThis retrospective study included 400 CBCT scans obtained from patients aged 18–60. Structured data derived from axial, coronal, and sagittal CBCT slices were evaluated by both an experienced endodontist and ChatGPT. Images were preprocessed using ImageJ software for enhanced clarity. The model was asked to classify each case as MB2 present or absent. Model predictions were compared with expert evaluations. Diagnostic performance was assessed by calculating sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, positive and negative predictive values and Cohen’s Kappa coefficient.ResultsChatGPT predicted MB2 presence in 228 cases, 227 of which were confirmed by expert evaluation. Eight false negatives and one false positive were observed. The model achieved 96.6% sensitivity, 99.4% specificity, and 97.8% overall accuracy, with a Cohen’s Kappa coefficient of 0.954 (p < 0.001).ConclusionsChatGPT demonstrated high agreement with expert analysis using structured CBCT data. Despite not processing raw image data, the model showed strong pattern recognition capabilities. These findings support the potential of language models in clinical decision support, although further refinement and multimodal integration are recommended.Keywords: MB2 canal, maxillary molar, CBCT, artificial intelligence, ChatGPT, endodontics

Background

Successful endodontic treatment relies on thorough cleaning, shaping, and obturation of the root canal system. A comprehensive understanding of root canal morphology is essential to ensure an accurate and effective treatment approach. However, anatomical complexities can impede proper chemomechanical preparation. Among all teeth, maxillary permanent molars are reported to have the highest rates of endodontic failure, largely due to the anatomical variations in their mesiobuccal (MB) roots [1]. This is particularly associated with the frequent presence of a second canal (MB2) in the mesiobuccal root of the maxillary first molar [2]. Failure to locate all canals during endodontic treatment increases the risk of failure by approximately fourfold [3]. Missed canals often harbor residual bacteria and necrotic tissue, leading to persistent infection, periapical inflammation, and ultimately, treatment failure [4].

In a study by Song et al., missed canals were identified as the second most common cause of treatment failure (19.7%), following leakage (30.4%), and were deemed more critical than fractures (1.2%) or inadequate fillings (14.2%) [5]. Fernandes et al., in a CBCT-based study, reported MB2 prevalence as high as 92% in maxillary first molars [1]. Therefore, accurate detection of the MB2 canal is essential for the success of root canal therapy. Several methods have been developed for identifying MB2 canals. In vitro techniques include transverse sectioning of roots, use of dental operating microscopes, micro-computed tomography (micro-CT), and cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) [2]. Clinically, tools such as intraoral radiographs, dental microscopes, and ultrasonic tips have proven beneficial for locating MB2 canals [6].

Intraoperative examinations and periapical radiographs are commonly used in clinical practice for MB2 detection. However, conventional radiographic techniques may be inadequate due to image distortion or superimposition of anatomical structures. CBCT, with its three-dimensional analysis capabilities, provides a more accurate assessment of complex root canal anatomies [7].

Artificial intelligence (AI) technologies are increasingly being integrated into clinical decision support systems in both medicine and dentistry. AI is predominantly based on machine learning, particularly deep learning algorithms that emulate human-like learning and decision-making processes [8]. These deep learning systems, often structured around artificial neural networks, have demonstrated high accuracy in classifying, detecting, and analyzing complex visual data [9]. Unlike task-specific deep learning models, large language models such as ChatGPT have recently gained multimodal capabilities, enabling them to interpret radiographic images. This raises the question of whether such accessible, general-purpose AI systems can aid in MB2 canal detection.

In a recent study, Shetty et al. employed a deep learning model (Google V3) to detect MB2 canal orifices in CBCT images. Their results showed that the model achieved high diagnostic accuracy, with a sensitivity of 92% [10].

Natural language processing (NLP) models such as ChatGPT are increasingly capable of interpreting structured clinical data to support decision-making. In this context, our study investigated whether ChatGPT could accurately detect the presence of MB2 canals based solely on structured data extracted from CBCT images.

The null hypothesis of this study was that there would be no significant difference between ChatGPT-based assessment and expert evaluation in detecting the presence of MB2 canals in maxillary first molars.

Methods

A total of 400 CBCT scans were included to ensure high statistical reliability. Sample size estimation was performed using the power analysis module of G*Power 3.1. The minimum required sample size was calculated a priori using Cohen’s Kappa coefficient. Based on an expected κ = 0.64 (Albitar et al., 2022), with α = 0.05 and 95% statistical power, the required minimum was 163 cases. Therefore, the final sample size of 400 exceeded the calculated threshold and enhanced analytical robustness.

This retrospective study included CBCT scans obtained between June 2022 and June 2024 from the archives of the Dicle University Faculty of Dentistry, all acquired with a single CBCT device using identical high-resolution parameters (150 µm voxel size, 100 kVp, 12.5 mA, 5 s exposure time) to ensure full imaging standardization.”

Scans belonged to patients aged 18–60 years. Only CBCT images that fully covered at least one apex of the maxillary first molars and met high-resolution criteria were included.

Results

A total of 400 CBCT scans were evaluated in this study. Based on the expert assessment, the presence of an MB2 canal was identified in 235 cases (58.75%), while it was absent in 165 cases (41.25%).

The ChatGPT model predicted the presence of an MB2 canal in 228 cases. Among these, 227 predictions were consistent with the expert evaluations, and only 1 case was identified as a false positive. Similarly, among the 172 predictions indicating the absence of an MB2 canal, 164 were correct, while 8 were classified as false negatives (Table 1).

Discussion

The presence of a second mesiobuccal canal (MB2) in maxillary first molars is widely recognized as a major determinant of endodontic success. Failure to identify and treat this canal may allow residual bacteria and necrotic debris to persist, increasing the likelihood of post-treatment disease and endodontic failure [11, 12]. Given its clinical importance, reliable detection of the MB2 canal remains a fundamental objective during diagnosis and treatment planning.

Reported MB2 prevalence varies considerably across studies, ranging from approximately 48% to more than 90%, depending on differences in population characteristics, methodological approaches, and imaging modalities [13–16]. High-resolution CBCT studies typically report elevated detection rates, reflecting the influence of voxel size and imaging quality on the visualization of fine anatomical structures. For example, Bauman et al. demonstrated a marked increase in MB2 identification as voxel size decreased from 0.40 mm to 0.125 mm [17]. These findings highlight the importance of using standardized, high-resolution CBCT protocols when assessing canal morphology.

In the present study, only maxillary first molars were included to ensure anatomical consistency, as these teeth demonstrate higher and more predictable MB2 prevalence and morphology compared with second molars [18]. Age and sex variables were documented but not analyzed, as the primary aim was to evaluate the diagnostic performance of ChatGPT rather than explore demographic associations. The well-established bilateral symmetry of MB2 occurrence—reported as high as 97% in CBCT-based studies [1]—further reinforces the clinical relevance of accurate identification in both arches.

All scans in this study were obtained using a single CBCT device with fixed acquisition parameters to ensure consistency in imaging quality. Although CBCT offers practical, non-invasive, three-dimensional visualization of root canal systems, its resolution remains lower than micro-CT, which may limit the detection of narrow or calcified canals [19, 20]. The standardized high-resolution settings used here aimed to reduce this limitation as much as possible within the constraints of clinical CBCT imaging.

A key methodological component of this research was the use of ImageJ software for minimal, non-destructive image optimization. Some CBCT slices exhibited low contrast or insufficient delineation of canal walls; therefore, standardized “Sharpen” and “Bandpass Filter” functions were applied to improve edge clarity and contrast uniformity. These steps did not alter anatomical structures and were used solely to ensure that both the expert evaluator and the AI model assessed images of consistent visual quality. This approach increased methodological transparency and reproducibility.

The rationale for selecting ChatGPT instead of a task-specific deep learning system was based on accessibility, generalizability, and practical clinical relevance. Conventional deep learning models require large annotated datasets, programming expertise, and high-performance computing resources, which may limit their integration into routine practice. ChatGPT, by contrast, can generate diagnostic judgments from structured image-derived descriptions without additional training. The goal of this study was not to replace specialized imaging algorithms but rather to evaluate whether a widely available, training-free large language model could support MB2 detection in a clinically meaningful manner. Demonstrating diagnostic utility in such an accessible platform may facilitate broader adoption of AI-assisted decision support in endodontics.

The high diagnostic accuracy observed in this study suggests that large language models can serve as valuable adjuncts to clinical decision-making when provided with standardized, structured input. Although ChatGPT does not directly interpret CBCT images, its ability to infer anatomical information from structured descriptions aligns with previous findings showing that AI systems can detect untreated or missed canals on CBCT scans [9]. This reinforces the potential of multimodal AI systems—which combine both image-processing and textual-reasoning capabilities—to play a future role in endodontic diagnostics.

This study has several limitations. The retrospective design and use of data from a single institution may limit generalizability. Structured CBCT information was manually extracted by an operator, introducing potential observer bias. Additionally, because ChatGPT lacks native image-analysis capability, its performance depends entirely on the clarity and accuracy of the structured input. Future studies should incorporate multimodal AI systems capable of processing CBCT images directly, alongside multicenter datasets with uniform, high-resolution imaging protocols to enhance diagnostic reliability.

Based on the statistically significant and almost perfect agreement observed between ChatGPT-based assessment and expert evaluation, the null hypothesis was rejected. The performance observed in this study reflects the capabilities of the ChatGPT-4o model at the time of evaluation.

Conclusion

The findings of this study indicate that ChatGPT, when provided with structured CBCT-derived information, can accurately detect the presence of MB2 canals and may serve as a supplementary diagnostic tool in endodontic practice. With further refinement and integration into multimodal imaging platforms, large language models may contribute significantly to improving diagnostic consistency and reducing the incidence of missed canals in clinical endodontics.

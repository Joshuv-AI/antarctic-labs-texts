A Thorium(IV) metallacyclopropyne complex
Authors: Xiuting Chen, Songpeng Wan, Qian Wang, Yu Gong
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractActinide metallacyclic chemistry has been of interest due to its involvement in various chemical processes. However, fundamental understanding on the key species, actinide metallacyclic complexes, is limited to metallacyclopropenes whereas little is known about the actinide metallacyclopropynes presumably due to their unusual high reactivity. Herein, we report the preparation of a thorium metallacyclopropyne complex (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3– in the gas phase by using electrospray ionization mass spectrometry, and it is generated via a single-ligand strategy through sequential losses of CO2 and HCl from the monopropynoate precursor (HC ≡ CCO2)ThCl4– upon collision-induced dissociation. Alternatively, the dual-ligand strategy involving consecutive losses of two CO2 and one C2H2 from the dipropynoate precursor (HC ≡ CCO2)2ThCl3– works as well. According to the reactivity experiments and theoretical calculations, (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3– possesses a dianionic ligand C22– coordinated to the Th(IV) center in a side-on fashion. Further bonding analysis demonstrates the presence of a triple bond between the two C atoms, and the Th 5 f orbitals are significantly involved in the Th-(C ≡ C) bonding. A Th metallacyclopropyne structure is thus established for (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3–.Subject terms: Chemical bonding, Nuclear chemistry, Chemical bonding

Introduction

Small metallacycles play a key role in various catalytic processes and material synthesis since they serve as important precursors or intermediates with unusual intrinsic reactivity involved in the preparation of highly functionalized compounds. Apart from the well understood unsaturated metallacycles of transition metals that have been comprehensively investigated1–4, more attention has been paid to the actinide unsaturated metallacycles in recent years5. This is not only driven by the potential of actinide compounds in catalysis such as small-molecule activation6–8, but also a result of the increasing interest in the influence of 5 f orbitals on the bonding and reactivity of organoactinides that is directly involved in these catalytical processes9–12. Since the first thorium metallacyclopropene compound, [η5−1,2,4-(Me3C)3C5H2]2Th(η2-PhC=CPh), was successfully isolated and structurally characterized by single crystal X-ray diffraction9, a number of thorium metallacyclopropenes10, metallacyclopentadienes13,14, and metallacyclocumulenes15 as well as their uranium analogs11,12,16,17 have been reported in the last decade. On the basis of the reactivities of these actinide metallacycles towards multifarious unsaturated substrates, the steric and electronic properties of alkyne substituents were found to have a profound impact on their formations and reactivities9–16. For example, the three-membered metallacyclopropene compound [η5−1, 2, 4-(Me3C)3C5H2]2Th(η2-MeC=CMe) is not stable enough to be isolated if the phenyl group is replaced by methyl with lower steric hindrance, and it will react with another MeC≡CMe molecule to form the five-membered metallacyclopentadiene compound [η5−1, 2, 4-(Me3C)3C5H2]2Th[η2-(Me)C = C(Me)-(Me)C = C(Me)]9,13.

Although some progress has been made in actinide metallacyclic chemistry in recent years, the report on actinide metallacyclopropyne is quite limited. This could result from the higher reactivity of metallacyclopropynes than metallacyclopropenes, as in the case of very unstable and reactive cyclopropyne that is still missing from the experimental point of view despite the countless efforts18–20. Matrix isolation infrared spectroscopy coupled with laser ablation technique is a popular tool to generate and characterize the highly reactive species that cannot be stabilized under ambient conditions21–23. However, the reactions of laser-ablated thorium and uranium atoms with C2H2 in solid argon matrixes only gave the actinide metallacyclopropenes An(η2-C2H2) (An = Th, U), whereas no actinide metallacyclopropynes were detected24. Alternatively, electrospray ionization mass spectrometry (ESI-MS) can be employed for the synthesis of reactive gas-phase organometallic species as well, and the structure as well as reaction mechanism can be exquisitely explored with the aid of density functional theory (DFT) calculations25–31. In this work, we report the gas-phase synthesis of a thorium metallacyclopropyne complex (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3– by using ESI-MS. A dianionic C ≡ C ligand coordinated to the Th(IV) center through a side-on mode is established based on the reactivity of (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3– towards water. Besides, the bonding of (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3– and its formation mechanism were investigated by DFT calculations.

Results and discussion

Preparation of the Th(IV) metallacyclopropyne complex by the single-ligand strategy

A series of thorium chloride propynoate complex anions, (HC ≡ CCO2)nThCl(5-n)–, were produced from negative ion mode ESI of the ThCl4 and HC ≡ CCO2H mixture in acetonitrile. (HC ≡ CCO2)ThCl4– in which the thorium center is coordinated by one propynoate and four chloride ligands was mass-selected as precursor anion and subjected to collision-induced dissociation (CID). As shown in Fig. 1a, there are three product anions observed in the CID mass spectrum of (HC ≡ CCO2)ThCl4– (m/z 440.91066), which were assigned as (HC ≡ C)ThCl4– (m/z 396.92128), (CC)ThCl3– (m/z 360.94468) and ThOCl3– (m/z 352.93954), respectively. The formulas of all these species were determined by isotopic patterns and exact mass measurements using an ultrahigh resolution Fourier transform ion cyclotron resonance mass spectrometer (FTICR-MS) as summarized in Supplementary Table 1. CO2 loss readily occurred for (HC ≡ CCO2)ThCl4– to form a thorium ethynyl complex (HC ≡ C)ThCl4–, and this gas-phase decarboxylation reaction is typical for metal carboxylate complexes25–31. The unexpected product (CC)ThCl3– with moderate yield should originate from HCl liberation from the thorium ethynyl complex, which can be confirmed by the appearance of (CC)ThCl3– in the CID mass spectrum of (HC ≡ C)ThCl4– (Fig. 1b). The third product ThOCl3– is supposed to be generated from the reaction of (CC)ThCl3– with the trace H2O accompanied by C2H2 elimination, which will be discussed in detail later.

Reactivity towards water

It is certain that (HC ≡ C)ThCl4− is a thorium ethynyl complex resulting from decarboxylation of the (HC ≡ CCO2)ThCl4− precursor anion, but the nature of (CC)ThCl3− remains ambiguous merely considering the information displayed in Fig. 1. In order to help confirm the structure of (CC)ThCl3−, the ESI/CID experiments were repeated using a linear ion trap mass spectrometer (LIT-MS), in which the mass-selected (CC)ThCl3– can undergo ion-molecule reactions with H2O more efficiently due to the higher background water pressure. As shown in Fig. 2, (CC)ThCl3– (m/z 360.8) reacted with a H2O molecule to give the major product ThOCl3– (m/z 352.9) via loss of C2H2, and ThOCl3– subsequently reacted with another H2O molecule to generate the minor product Th(OH)2Cl3– (m/z 370.9). It is speculated that two consecutive O-H bond activation should occur during the reaction of (CC)ThCl3– and H2O. The first O-H bond activation gives a thorium ethynyl hydroxide trichloride intermediate (HC ≡ C)Th(OH)Cl3– which undergoes a second O-H bond activation to produce ThOCl3– followed by spontaneous hydrolysis to give Th(OH)2Cl3–. The experiments were also carried out with the heavy-oxygen water (H218O, 10% atom 18O) under similar conditions, and the resulting mass spectra as well as the expected reactions are shown in Supplementary Figs. 1 and 2. Besides ThOCl3– and Th(OH)2Cl3–, two new peaks located at m/z 354.8 and 372.8 appeared, which are assigned as Th18OCl3– and Th(OH)(18OH)Cl3–, respectively. (CC)ThCl3– reacted first with a H2O or H218O molecule to give ThOCl3– or Th18OCl3–. Then, ThOCl3– reacted with another H2O or H218O molecule to produce Th(OH)2Cl3– or Th(OH)(18OH)Cl3–(Supplementary Fig. 2b). Similarly, the reaction of Th18OCl3– and H2O gave Th(OH)(18OH)Cl3–(Supplementary Fig. 2c). The abundance of Th(18OH)2Cl3– (m/z 374.9) is too low since the heavy-oxygen water contains only 10% H218O. Such reactivity of (CC)ThCl3– towards water or heavy-oxygen water suggests a dianionic C2 ligand, which agrees well with +IV oxidation state of the thorium center.

Structure and bonding

To further determine the geometric structure of (CC)ThCl3–, DFT calculations were carried out at the B3PW91/SDD/6-311 + G(d) level of theory. A structure in the form of (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3– with a side-on bound C2 ligand was located, and it exhibits a singlet ground state with Cs symmetry as shown in Fig. 3. Another isomer in the form of (η1-C ≡ C)ThCl3– with the C2 ligand bound to the thorium center in an end-on fashion was also considered, but geometry optimization ended up with a structure with two imaginary frequencies, according to which the side-on (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3– isomer was finally obtained. The two Th-C bonds of (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3– are inequivalent and computed to be 2.353 and 2.404 Å, respectively. They are shorter than the Th-C bond length of (HC ≡ C)ThCl4– (2.459 Å) as well as those values (2.47 ~ 2.60 Å) in several thorium hydrocarbyl compounds characterized by single crystal X-ray diffraction32–35, and favorably comparable with the Th-C bond length (2.395 Å) reported for the thorium metallacyclopropene compound (η5-1, 2, 4-(Me3C)3C5H2)Th(η2-PhC=CPh)9,10. The C-C distance in (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3– (1.263 Å) is obviously shorter than the values for the carbon-carbon double bonds found in those thorium metallacyclopropene compounds (1.343 ~ 1.361 Å)9,10,12, but closer to the predicted value of 1.223 Å for the carbon-carbon triple bond in (HC ≡ C)ThCl4–, indicating a triple bond character of the C22– ligand in the metallacyclopropyne complex, (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3–.

Structures of (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3– and (HC ≡ C)ThCl4– at the B3PW91/SDD/6-311 + G(d) level of theory (bond lengths in Å).

Insights into the bonding characteristics of the Th-C and C-C bonds of (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3– were gained through both natural bond orbital (NBO) analysis and localized molecular orbital (LMO) analysis at the B3PW91/SDD/6-31 G(d) level of theory, and the results are summarized in Table 1, Fig. 4 and Supplementary Fig. 3. With the resemblance between LMOs (Supplementary Fig. 3) and NBOs (Fig. 4), the results of NBO analysis will be discussed in detail. Two in-plane carbon dominated σ(Th-C) bonds are located in (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3–. The σ1(Th-C) bond combines a carbon sp hybrid orbital (87.3% C, 57.4% 2 s and 42.5% 2p) and a thorium hybrid orbital (12.7% Th, 36.1% 5 f, 35.6% 6d and 25.4% 7 s), and the other σ2(Th-C) bond (85.4% C and 14.6% Th) is formed via the interaction between a carbon sp hybrid orbital (57.2% 2 s and 42.7% 2p) and a thorium hybrid orbital (19.8% 5 f, 56.1% 6d and 22.3% 7 s). For the C-C bond, three bonding orbitals were identified. The first is a σ(C ≡ C) bond formed on the basis of the C sp hybrid orbitals. The second is a π(C ≡ C) bond in which only C 2p orbitals participate. The last one is an out-of-plane π[Th-(C ≡ C)] bond made up of 89.7% carbon 2p occupancy and 10.3% contribution from a thorium hybrid orbital (32.9% 5 f and 65.0% 6d). This implicates a triple bond character of the C2 ligand in (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3–, and additional electrons were transfered from Th to C2 upon coordination such that the C2 ligand is essentially C22– that is isoelectronic with N2. This is also supported by the negative natural population analysis (NPA) charge on C2 (−1.04e) as shown in Supplementary Table 2, which is nearly equal to the value (−1.07e) of O2– in ThOCl3– and more negative than that (−0.58e) of OH– in Th(OH)2Cl3–. The bonding characteristic of (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3– can be compared with that of its precursor (HC ≡ C)ThCl4–. A triple bond consisting of one σ(C ≡ C) bond and two π(C ≡ C) bonds was identified between the two carbon atoms (Fig. 4). NPA charge of −0.57 e was found for the HC ≡ C ligand, which is in line with the HC ≡ C– nature that carries less natural charge than the C22– ligand. For the Th-C bond, a polarized carbon dominated σ bond was identified, but the thorium contribution to the bonding of (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3– is reduced compared to that of (HC ≡ C)ThCl4– (Table 1). Of note, Th 5 f orbitals are indeed involved in the bonding. The 5 f contributions to the two σ(Th-C) bonds in (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3– (36.1% and 19.8%) are larger than those in (HC ≡ C)ThCl4– (9.5%) and (η2-C6H4)ThCl3− (18.3%)30, suggesting that Th 5 f orbitals play a more important role in stabilizing the thorium metallacyclopropyne linkage.

Formation Mechanism

The formation mechanism of (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3– via the consecutive CO2 and HCl elimination reactions of (HC ≡ CCO2)ThCl4– was disclosed by DFT calculations in detail, and the reaction profile, as well as optimized geometries of related species, are shown in Fig. 5. The reaction starts with the isomerization of (HC ≡ CCO2)ThCl4– to an intermediate IM1 ( + 6.3 kcal/mol) with the coordination mode changing from bidentate to monodentate in which the thorium center is coordinated by one of the oxygen atoms from the near-planar propynoate ligand. This is quite common during the decarboxylation process25–31, and the Th-O bond length is therefore shortened by 0.197 Å from (HC ≡ CCO2)ThCl4– (2.508 Å) to IM1 (2.311 Å). Mediated by a four-membered ring transition state (TS1) with an energy barrier of 29.1 kcal/mol, IM1 undergoes CO2 loss to give a thorium ethynyl complex (HC ≡ C)ThCl4–, and this decarboxylation reaction is endothermic by 25.5 kcal/mol. The next dehydrochlorination process starts with the conversion from end-on HC ≡ C to side-on mode, during which the C-H bond is activated and the hydrogen atom directly transfers to one of the axial chlorine atoms of the ethynyl group. This is further supported by the elongation of the C-H distance from 1.068 Å in (HC ≡ C)ThCl4– to 3.380 Å in TS2. HCl elimination then occurs to give the experimentally observed thorium metallacyclopropyne complex (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3–. This dehydrochlorination reaction is mediated by an energy barrier (TS2) of 74.8 kcal/mol versus (HC ≡ C)ThCl4–, and the overall endothermicity is predicted to be 98.5 kcal/mol. The difference in energy barriers of decarboxylation and dehydrochlorination reactions is in general consistency with the relative yields of (HC ≡ C)ThCl4– and (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3– when (HC ≡ CCO2)ThCl4– and (HC ≡ C)ThCl4– were subjected to CID under the same condition (Fig. 1).

Preparation of the Th(IV) metallacyclopropyne complex by the dual-ligand strategy

As revealed by the result shown in Fig. 1, the gas-phase thorium metallacyclopropyne complex (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3− is generated through decarboxylation and dehydrochlorination of (HC ≡ CCO2)ThCl4– during which only one propynoate ligand is involved. To investigate whether there is a different channel that can lead to the same (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3− product, the thorium dipropynoate precursor anion (HC ≡ CCO2)2ThCl3– was also mass-selected and subjected to multistage mass spectrometry (MSn) experiments. In the first stage (Fig. 6a), CO2 loss occurred for the (HC ≡ CCO2)2ThCl3– (m/z 474.93985) precursor anion to generate [(HC ≡ CCO2)2ThCl3-CO2]– (m/z 430.94995). A secondary loss of another CO2 produced [(HC ≡ CCO2)2ThCl3-2CO2]– (m/z 386.96031) upon CID of [(HC ≡ CCO2)2ThCl3-CO2]– (Fig. 6b). Mass-selection of the double decarboxylation product [(HC ≡ CCO2)2ThCl3-2CO2]– followed by CID gave rise to a peak at m/z 360.94472 assignable to (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3− via C2H2 elimination (Fig. 6c), whose yield is much higher than that shown in Fig. 1 even though the SORI power employed in Fig. 6 is a bit lower. The successful formation of (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3− via both the single- and dual-ligand strategies differs from the case of (η2-C6H4)ThCl3− where only the single-ligand strategy works30.

In summary, the thorium metallacyclopropyne complex (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3– was prepared upon CID of the thorium monopropynoate precursor (HC ≡ CCO2)ThCl4– in the gas phase by using ESI-MS. CID experiments and DFT calculations reveal that (HC ≡ CCO2)ThCl4– underwent consecutive decarboxylation and dehydrochlorination reactions to give (CC)ThCl3–. The reactivity study of (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3– towards H2O/H218O in combination with the geometry optimization result supports the assignment to a complex in which the Th4+ center is coordinated by one side-on bound (η2-C ≡ C)2– ligand and three Cl– ligands. NBO analysis indicates that thorium and C2 are connected by two polarized in-plane Th-C σ bonds in (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3–. The identification of a σ(C ≡ C) bond as well as an in-plane π(C ≡ C) bond and an out-of-plane π(C ≡ C) bond confirms the triple bond character in (η2-C ≡ C)2– that is isoelectronic with N2, and a thorium metallacyclopropyne structure is therefore established. The significant 5 f orbital participations in the Th-C σ and Th-(C ≡ C) π bonds make a key contribution to the stabilization of this thorium metallacyclopropyne complex. In addition to the single-ligand strategy, (η2-C ≡ C)ThCl3– can also be generated through a dual-ligand strategy that involves successive losses of two CO2 and one C2H2 from the thorium dipropynoate precursor (HC ≡ CCO2)2ThCl3– with a better yield.

Methods

Reagents

Propynoic acid (HC ≡ CCO2H, ≥ 95%) and heavy-oxygen water (H218O, 10% atom 18O) were purchased from Macklin and used without further purification. The ThCl4 stock solution was prepared by dissolving ThCl4 (99.99%) powder in concentrated hydrochloric acid and then diluting to 100 mM by Milli-Q ultrapure water at ambient temperature. The thorium isotope employed was 232Th which undergoes a-decay with the half-life of 1.4 × 1010 years, and its handling requires proper shielding, waste disposal and personal protective gear.

Mass spectrometry

All the experiments were performed on either a Bruker Daltonics (Bremen, Germany) SolariX XR 7.0 T FTICR-MS or a Thermo Fisher Scientific (Massachusetts, USA) LTQ XL LIT-MS. Both instruments are equipped with a heated electrospray ionization (ESI) source. The LIT-MS has been modified to allow for the introduction of gaseous reagents through a variable leak valve36. The acetonitrile solution of 0.1 mM ThCl4 and 0.1 mM HC ≡ CCO2H mixture was prepared for negative ion mode ESI experiment to generate suitable thorium propynoate chloride anions with the general formula of (HC ≡ CCO2)nThCl5-n–. The isotopomer with the lowest mass-to-charge ratio (m/z) of (HC ≡ CCO2)ThCl4– or (HC ≡ CCO2)2ThCl3– was mass-selected as the precursor anion and subjected to MSn experiments via CID technique. These anions were readily identified via the isotopic patterns mainly arising from 75.77% 35Cl and 24.23% 37Cl. The detailed instrumental parameters are listed in the Supplementary Information. The raw mass spectra are provided as Supplementary Figs. 4~6.

Theoretical calculations

DFT calculations on the related species were carried out using the Gaussian 09 package with the B3PW91 hybrid density functional37–39, which has proven a quite accurate functional to determinate the structures and relative energies of actinide metallacyclic complexes9–16. The Stuttgart small-core pseudopotential ECP60MWB with the contracted basis set ECP60MWB-SEG (SDD) was used for Th40, and the 6-311 + G(d) basis set was used for all the remaining atoms (C, H, O and Cl)41,42. All the geometric parameters were fully optimized, zero-point energy corrections were included in the relative energies, and thermal corrections were included in the free energies (Supplementary Data 1). Vibrational frequencies were calculated for all optimized structures to check that they correspond to ground-state structures (no imaginary frequency) or transition-state structures (one imaginary frequency). Intrinsic reaction coordinate (IRC) calculations were used to confirm the connectivity between transition structures and minima43,44. Natural bond orbital (NBO) analysis and localized molecular orbital (LMO) analysis were performed on the optimized geometries using the NBO6 program at the B3PW91/SDD/6-31 G(d) level of theory45–47. Visualization of all the optimized structures was realized using CYLview20 software48, and the natural bond orbitals and localized molecular orbitals were plotted using Multiwfn program49.

Evaluation of six commercial and in-house phenotypic tests for detection of AmpC β-lactamases: is routine detection possible?
Authors: Azza Mohammed Khalifa Al Mamari, Zaaima Al Jabri, Hiba Sami, Syed Gauhar A Rizvi, Moon Fai Chan, Turkiya Al Siyabi, Zakariya Al Muharrmi, Meher Rizvi
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPhenotypic characterization of the prevalent AmpC β-lactamases in clinical isolates is essential for making informed empirical decisions and critical for strengthening antimicrobial stewardship programmes. This study focused on assessing six assays, two in-house and four commercial phenotypic tests for detection of AmpC, to study the feasibility of making its detection a routine diagnostic microbiology laboratory activity.MethodsA total of 116 non-duplicate Gram-negative bacteria that were resistant to third-generation cephalosporins and amoxicillin/clavulanic acid, and resistant or susceptible to piperacillin/tazobactam and carbapenems, were screened by cefoxitin discs for AmpC. These isolates were subjected to two in-house (AmpC Tris-EDTA and disc approximation) methods and four commercial tests: D69C AmpC Detection Set; D72C ESBL, AmpC & Carbapenemase Detection Set; combination disc test: ESBL + AmpC Screen Disc Kit; and AmpC MIC Test Strip for confirmation of AmpC production. Ten whole-genome-sequenced AmpC-confirmed Gram-negative isolates were used as positive controls and one as a negative control.ResultsThe prevalence of AmpC β-lactamases was 16%. Escherichia coli was a major carrier of plasmid-mediated AmpC (26.5%), followed by Klebsiella pneumoniae (23.4%). Phenotypically, 61% of AmpCs were detected by Tris-EDTA (accuracy: 73.8%), 76% by disc approximation (accuracy: 89.2%), 75% by the D69C AmpC Detection Set (accuracy: 95.4%), 74% by the D72C AmpC, ESBL & Carbapenemase Detection Set (accuracy: 95.4%), 76% by the combination disc test (accuracy: 95.4%) and 63% by AmpC MIC Test Strip (accuracy: 87.7%). The sensitivity and specificity of D69C were 97.9% and 88.2%, respectively, and 95.9% and 93.8% for the combination disc test, while for the disc approximation test and D72C they were 93.9% and 75%, and 93.9% and 100%, respectively. Screening by cefoxitin screening was less sensitive (75%) and specific (25%). Disc approximation and the combination disc test detect AmpC in Enterobacterales and also Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Acinetobacter species.ConclusionsWe recommend the in-house disc approximation test and the commercial D69C, as well as the combination disc test, as excellent tools for detection of AmpC. The cefoxitin test overcalls AmpC and cannot be considered a good stand-alone test for AmpC detection.

Introduction

AmpC β-lactamases, if not detected, can lead to inappropriate treatment and clinical failure. They characteristically hydrolyse cephamycins (cefoxitin and cefotetan), oxyiminocephalosporins (ceftazidime, cefotaxime and ceftriaxone) and monobactams (aztreonam). Carbapenems, cefepime and cefpirome are hydrolysed poorly due to low enzymatic affinity.1 AmpCs are encoded chromosomally (cAmpCs) in Enterobacter cloacae, Klebsiella aerogenes, Citrobacter freundii and Serratia marcescens. Plasmid-mediated acquisition of AmpC is being increasingly reported in Enterobacterales globally, which is a matter of grave concern.2 Acquired AmpC production in Enterobacterales is less frequently encountered than ESBL production but is increasing,3 notably in Southeast Asia, where 12% of Escherichia coli in hospitals have plasmid-mediated AmpC genes.

It is imperative that clinical laboratories routinely identify AmpC in clinical isolates as, when first evaluated, AmpC producers may show susceptibility to expanded-spectrum cephalosporins. This can result in misguided antibiotic regimens and unsuccessful treatment. AmpC detection plays a proactive role in guiding appropriate antimicrobial treatment, in prompting suitable infection prevention and control procedures, and preventing the misuse or overuse of broad-spectrum antibiotics. For this, simple, cheap, easy-to-perform and reliable tests with good sensitivity and specificity are needed. Though guidance from CLSI is not available, several in-house and commercial phenotypic tests have been assessed over the last two decades. Screening by the cefoxitin (30 µg) disc diffusion method is widely used for AmpC production, and resistance to cefoxitin indicates the possibility of the isolate harbouring the enzyme.4 Khari et al.5 recommended the commercial AmpC D69C detection set for Enterobacterales (Mast Group Ltd, UK). Getzlaff et al.6 reported that the AmpC ETEST (AB bioMérieux, Sweden) and the AmpC cefoxitin-EDTA disc tests are good confirmatory tests. Black et al.7 observed that the Tris-EDTA disc method was an accurate, convenient and simple method for detection of plasmid-mediated AmpC β-lactamases in isolates such as Klebsiella pneumoniae, Proteus mirabilis and Salmonella spp. Rodríguez-Guerrero et al.8 reported that the best phenotypic test for AmpC detection was the in-house double synergy test, applied with or without boronic acid discs. With the view to establishing a simple algorithm for detection of AmpC, six different phenotypic tests (two in-house and four commercial) were assessed in this study.

Materials and methods

Antimicrobial susceptibility profiles of 400 consecutive, clinical, non-duplicate Gram-negative bacilli (GNB) identified by MALDI-TOF (Bruker, Daltonics, Bremen, Germany) and susceptibility performed by the BD Phoenix automated system (Becton Dickinson Diagnostic Systems, Sparks, MD, USA) were assessed for possible AmpC carriage. The processing, identification and susceptibility testing were carried out as per CLSI guidelines.9 On the basis of resistance to third-generation cephalosporins, β-lactam/β-lactam inhibitor combinations and/or carbapenems, 116 isolates were screened for cefoxitin resistance using 30 μg discs. Isolates with a zone of inhibition ≤18 mm were considered as potential AmpC producers and were subjected to further testing.10

Detection of AmpC

After cefoxitin screening, six tests were assessed to understand their value as confirmatory tests. These were: (i) the Tris-EDTA disc test; (ii) the disc approximation test; (iii) the D69C AmpC Detection Set for Enterobacterales; (iv) the D72C AmpC, ESBL & Carbapenemase Detection Set for Enterobacterales; (v) the combination disc test (CDT): ESBL + AmpC Screen Disc Kit; and (vi) AmpC MIC Test Strip for Enterobacterales. Figure 1 illustrates the algorithm followed. D69C and the CDT were considered the gold standards. Ten WGS-confirmed AmpC isolates, 4 isolates of E. coli (two AmpC producers and two AmpC plus ESBL producers, three of which expressed the DHA-1 gene and one the CMY-42 gene) and 4 isolates of Klebsiella pneumoniae (two AmpC producers and two AmpC plus ESBL producers, all four harbouring the DHA-1 gene) and one isolate each of E. cloacae (MOX gene) and Pseudomonas aeruginosa (ACC gene) were used as positive controls. One negative control K. pneumoniae (ESBL plus OXA-48 like) was used.

Algorithm for screening and confirmation of AmpC.

Tris-EDTA disc test

After rehydrating a plain 6 mm filter disc with 20 µL of 0.5 M Tris-EDTA (Melford, UK), several colonies of the test isolate were applied to it. The disc was placed downwards, next to the cefoxitin disc7 on a lawn culture of E. coli (ATCC 25922) on Mueller–Hinton agar (MHA) (Figure 2). Flattening or indentation of the zone of inhibition was considered a positive result.

AmpC Tris-EDTA. Left: strong indentation towards cefoxitin (FOX) disc of P. aeruginosa indicates positive test, and no indention with K. pneumoniae indicates negative test. Right: weak indentation of K. pneumoniae. Red arrows indicate the indentation.

Disc approximation test

A lawn culture of the test suspension (0.5 McFarland) was plated on MHA. Four inducers were placed radially, 20 mm edge to edge, from a ceftazidime disc: imipenem (10 µg), cefoxitin (30 µg), amoxicillin/clavulanic acid (20/10 µg) and piperacillin/tazobactam (110 µg).11 The plate was incubated at 35°C ± 2°C for 18–24 h and examined. Flattening or blunting of the zone of inhibition between ceftazidime and the inducer discs indicated AmpC production (Figure 3).

Disc approximation test. Strong and weak indentation (black arrows) towards the ceftazidime disc (CAZ) as inducer, and imipenem (IPM), cefoxitin (FOX), amoxicillin/clavulanic acid (AMC) and piperacillin/tazobactam (TZP) discs as substrates. The potentiation between drugs is shown as a yellow arrow.

In-house modification of disc approximation

The distance was varied between the inducers and ceftazidime on the basis of ceftazidime MIC: 20 mm if the MIC was high, and 24 mm edge to edge when the MIC was closer to the breakpoint. In disc approximation assays, a disc of cefepime was also used, as it would be hydrolysed by AmpC but not by ESBLs.12

Commercial D69C AmpC Detection Set for Enterobacterales

The set detects both chromosomal and plasmid-mediated AmpC (Figure 4). This test was used as the third method of phenotypic AmpC confirmation for Enterobacterales. The set contains three cartridges: A, B and C. Cartridge A consists of cefpodoxime (10 µg) supplemented with an AmpC inducer; cartridge B contains cefpodoxime (10 µg) with an AmpC inducer and an ESBL inhibitor; and cartridge C contains cefpodoxime (10 µg) with an AmpC inducer, an ESBL inhibitor and an AmpC inhibitor. The exact composition of the inhibitors and inducer were undisclosed. The set detects both chromosomal and plasmid-mediated AmpC. Any interference with ESBL was minimized by the ESBL inhibitor. The procedure was performed and interpreted according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

D69C set. (a) AmpC negative. (b) AmpC indeterminate, exhibiting other type(s) of resistance. (c and d) AmpC positive; disc A (cefpodoxime 10 μg + AmpC inducer), disc B (cefpodoxime 10μg + AmpC inducer + ESBL inhibitor), disc C (cefpodoxime 10 μg + AmpC inducer + ESBL inhibitor + AmpC inhibitors).

The test isolate suspension (0.5 McFarland) was prepared from overnight fresh colonies and inoculated on an MHA plate. The plate was then left at room temperature for 10–15 min, the three D69C discs were placed as per instructions, and the plate was incubated at 35°C ± 2°C for 18–24 h. The next day, the zone diameter of each disc was measured, and the result interpreted as follows: if the inhibition zone diameter of disc C exceeded those of both discs A and B by at least 5 mm, it was considered AmpC positive; if the difference between all zone diameters was ≤3 mm, it was interpreted as AmpC negative; if the inhibition zones of both B and C exceeded that of A by at least 5 mm and the difference in zone diameters of B and C was ≤4 mm, the isolate was considered AmpC negative but having a different resistance mechanism.13

D72C AmpC, ESBL & Carbapenemase Detection Kit for Enterobacterales

The AmpC, ESBL & Carbapenemase Detection set (D72C) (Mast Group Ltd, UK)14 is designed to identify AmpC (chromosomal and plasmid mediated), ESBL and co-production of ESBL and AmpC enzymes, as well as carbapenemase production, in Enterobacterales (Figure 5). In brief, the zone diameter around each disc was measured and entered into the D72C calculator to obtain the result. A combination disc set including cefpodoxime, different inhibitors and a penem antibiotic were used in the test. The interpretation is based on a difference in zone sizes of each disc. Synergistic reaction can be estimated due to different components used in combination. The set contains six cartridges: cartridge A contains cefpodoxime 10 µg; cartridge B contains cefpodoxime 10 µg + ESBL inhibitor; cartridge C contains cefpodoxime 10 µg + AmpC inhibitor; cartridge D contains cefpodoxime 10 µg + ESBL inhibitor + AmpC inhibitor; cartridge E contains cefpodoxime 10 µg + ESBL inhibitor + AmpC inducer; and cartridge F contains a penem antibiotic. All exact formulations of the inducer and inhibitors were undisclosed by the manufacturer.

D72C set. (a) AmpC negative. (b) Indeterminate. (c) ESBL positive. (d) AmpC positive. (e) Inducible AmpC and ESBL. Cartridge A (cefpodoxime 10 µg), cartridge B (cefpodoxime 10 µg + ESBL inhibitor), cartridge C (cefpodoxime 10 µg + AmpC inhibitor), cartridge D (cefpodoxime 10 µg + ESBL inhibitor + AmpC inhibitor), cartridge E (cefpodoxime 10 µg + ESBL inhibitor + AmpC inducer) and cartridge F (penem).

The test isolate suspension (0.5 McFarland) was prepared from overnight fresh colonies and inoculated on an MHA plate. The plate was then left at room temperature for 10–15 min, the six D72C discs were placed as per instructions, and the was plate incubated at 35°C ± 2°C for 18–24 h. The next day, the zone diameter of each disc was measured and entered in the D72C calculator, which automatically gave the result.

CDT

The CDT was the ESBL and AmpC Detection Kit (Liofilchem® Diagnostic, Italy)1 (Figure 6). The principle of this test is based on evaluating the synergistic effects of cefotaxime when combined with clavulanic acid, which inhibits ESBLs, and cloxacillin, which inhibits AmpC, for isolates suspected of producing ESBL and AmpC β-lactamases.1 The kit contains four cartridges: cefotaxime 30 μg, cefotaxime 30 μg + clavulanic acid, cefotaxime 30 μg + cloxacillin and cefotaxime 30 μg + clavulanic acid + cloxacillin. A 0.5 McFarland suspension from fresh growth of the test organism was prepared and spread evenly over the entire area of an MHA plate. The discs were applied on inoculated plates with enough space between the discs to ensure proper inhibition zone measurement. The plate was incubated for 18–24 h at 35°C ± 2°C. The inhibition zones were measured and interpreted according to the manufacturer’s instructions. If all zone diameters were within 2 mm of each other it indicated that the organism was not an AmpC or ESBL producer. Details are given in Table S1 (available as Supplementary data at JAC-AMR Online). Quality controls were run along with the test isolates.

CDT. (a) AmpC negative. (b) ESBL positive. (c) AmpC and ESBL positive. (d) AmpC positive. Cefotaxime 30 μg (CTX), cefotaxime + clavulanic acid (CTL), cefotaxime + cloxacillin (CTC) and cefotaxime + clavulanic acid + cloxacillin (CTLC).

AmpC MIC Test Strip for Enterobacterales

The main target of the AmpC MIC Test Strip (Liofilchem® Diagnostic, Italy) is to detect AmpC β-lactamase in Enterobacterales. The strip contains cefotetan and cefotetan/cloxacillin with gradients of 0.5–32 mg/L, as seen in Figure 7. Well-isolated fresh colonies were suspended from the overnight growth test isolate and standard turbidity made to 0.5 McFarland. MHA was inoculated with the test isolate and the AmpC MIC Test Strip was applied to the agar surface and incubated at 35°C ± 2°C for 16–18 h in an ambient atmosphere. Lowering of the cefotetan MIC value by ≥3 log2 dilutions, compared with cefotetan/cloxacillin, or emergence of a phantom zone or deformation of the cefotetan ellipse indicated the presence of AmpC.1

AmpC MIC Test Strip. Detection of AmpC-producing GNB by the combined MIC strip method. (a) Equal zone of inhibition. (b) Cefotetan/cloxacillin (CXT) MIC value lowered by ≥ 3 log2 dilutions compared with cefotetan (CTT).

Statistical analysis

IBM Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS.27) was used. Sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV) were measured by cross-tabulation taking D69C and CDT as gold standards. Column percentage of the cross-tabulation denotes the sensitivity and specificity, and row percent denotes the PPV and NPV. Please refer to Supplementary data at JAC-AMR Online.

Results

We compared six phenotypic tests for confirmation of AmpC β-lactamase production. After primary screening, 116 (29%) GNB (96 Enterobacterales and 20 P. aeruginosa) were selected for further testing. Of these, 82 (20.5%) were cefoxitin resistant and were classified as possible AmpC producers. K. pneumoniae accounted for 30% of these, E. coli 27%, P. aeruginosa 21%, E. cloacae 7%, K. aerogenes 5%, C. freundii 5% and S. marcescens 5% (Figure 8).

AmpC was identified in 70.7% (50/82) of cefoxitin-resistant isolates by AmpC Tris-EDTA (Figure 2), while the disc approximation assay identified 76% (62/82) as AmpC β-lactamase producers (Figure 3). Most of the isolates showed strong induction with imipenem compared with the other two inducers, cefoxitin and amoxicillin/clavulanic acid (Table 3). The WGS-positive controls were positive for both the tests, except for P. aeruginosa, which was negative for Tris-EDTA. In the disc approximation assay, imipenem emerged as the strongest inducer amongst both the Enterobacterales and P. aeruginosa, compared with cefoxitin and amoxicillin/clavulanic acid. Imipenem strongly induced flattening in 12 (75%) of the K. pneumoniae and in 10 (59%) of the E. coli, thus emerging as the best inducer of AmpC. In addition, 71% of P. aeruginosa were identified as AmpC producers, with imipenem being a strong inducer in 59% of them. In isolates co-harbouring ESBL (eight isolates), potentiation was very strongly seen between ceftazidime and piperacillin/tazobactam (n = 5), while weaker potentiation was observed between ceftazidime and amoxicillin/clavulanic acid (Figure 9).

AmpC Detection Set (D69C) for Enterobacterales

This test identified 49/65 (75%) AmpC β-lactamase-producing Enterobacterales (Figure 4). Seventeen percent (11/65) of isolates were identified as AmpC negative, with equal zones of inhibition for all discs, and 8% (5/65) were indeterminate, with no zone of inhibition around the discs, and may represent some other type of resistance. The chromosomal AmpC detection rate by this method was 100% (18/18), while for plasmid-mediated AmpC it was 66% (31/47) (Figure 4). All the controls gave a positive result.

AmpC, ESBL & Carbapenemase Detection Set (D72C) for Enterobacterales

Among the 65 Enterobacterales tested by the D72C set, 48 (74%) were identified as AmpC β-lactamase producers. The majority (58%; 28/48) were identified as ‘AmpC positive’, while 42% (20/48) were identified as ‘inducible AmpC’. This test failed to detect the presence of AmpC in two E. coli isolates, in which AmpC was detected by the D69C set (Figure 4). One E. coli positive control (AmpC plus ESBL) gave a negative result. It detected co-carriage of AmpC and ESBL in eight isolates.

CDT

All isolates were tested using the CDT and 76% (62/82) were identified as AmpC β-lactamase producers (Table 1). Fifty-eight produced only AmpC β-lactamases while 7 isolates co-harboured AmpC and ESBLs. Of the 13 AmpC-negative isolates, 11 were ESBL producers and 2 were carbapenemase producers) (Figure 6). All the controls gave a positive result.

AmpC MIC Test Strip for Enterobacterales

The AmpC MIC Test Strip method was performed for the 63% (41/65) of Enterobacterales that identified as AmpC β-lactamase producers (Figure 7). AmpC was detected in E. coli isolates at a higher rate (59%; 13/22) than in K. pneumoniae (48%; 12/25). Chromosomal AmpC was detected in all K. aerogenes, S. marcescens and C. freundii isolates but only in 4/6 E. cloacae isolates (67%). The K. pneumoniae positive control gave a negative result.

Sensitivity, specificity and accuracy of the confirmatory phenotypic AmpC detection methods

Both D69C and CDT were considered as possible gold standards and the outcomes of the other tests were compared with these two. With D69C as the gold standard (Table 2), high sensitivity of 95.9% was observed with the CDT, followed by disc approximation and D72C, with 93.9% sensitivity. The lowest sensitivity was seen with the Tris-EDTA and AmpC MIC Test Strip methods: 81.9% [accuracy: 73.8% (95% CI 61.5%–84.0%)] and 83.7% [accuracy: 87.7% (77.2%–94.5%)], respectively. Specificity appeared higher for both D72C [accuracy: 95.4% (87.1%–99.0%)] and AmpC MIC Test Strip (100%) followed by CDT [93.8%, accuracy: 95.4% (87.1%–99.0%)], disc approximation [75%, accuracy: 89.2% (79.1%–95.6%)] and the lowest for Tris-EDTA (50%).

With CDT as the gold standard, sensitivity of 97.9% [accuracy: 95.4% (95% CI 87.1%–99.0%)] and 91.7% [accuracy: 90.8% (81.0%–96.5%)] was observed with D69C and D72C, respectively, and 90% [accuracy: 87.7% (77.2%–94.5%)] and 83.3% [accuracy: 75.4% (63.1%–85.2%)] sensitivity was observed for disc approximation and Tris-EDTA, respectively. Specificity of both D69C and D72C was 88.2% (95% CI 63.6%–98.5%), and that of disc approximation was 70.6%. The sensitivity of the disc approximation test was the same with both gold standards (94%), while specificity was higher with D69C as the gold standard. Further statistical analysis is provided in Tables S2 and S3.

Prevalence of AmpC

Among the cefoxitin-resistant isolates, CDT and disc approximation identified 77% prevalence of pAmpC in E. coli (17/22) and 64% in K. pneumoniae (16/25), while 82.3% of P. aeruginosa carried AmpC (14/17).

Discussion

AmpC-producing GNB result in significant patient morbidity and mortality. Their routine identification is essential for optimal patient outcome as appropriate antimicrobial therapy can then be instituted in a timely manner. It allows better understanding of the epidemiology of AmpC, thus strengthening antimicrobial stewardship programmes. To achieve this, an easy-to-perform, reliable and rapid test will be invaluable. We evaluated six phenotypic methods, subsequent to screening with cefoxitin, to identify the most sensitive and specific test for detection of AmpC. In the absence of CLSI guidelines, this study sheds some valuable light on the feasibility of phenotypic tests for detection of AmpC β-lactamases.

Primary screening for AmpC by antimicrobial susceptibility profile (resistance to third-generation cephalosporins and β-lactam/β-lactam inhibitor combinations and/or carbapenems) presumptively identified 116/400 (29%) potential AmpC producers. Secondary screening by cefoxitin reduced these numbers to 82 (20.5%). However, both primary and secondary screening overcalled AmpC, and cefoxitin screening can at best be considered a reasonably good, cheap and rapid preliminary screening tool and not a confirmatory tool.

It is clear from our study that prevalence of AmpC may vary with the tests performed.

Sensitivity of cefoxitin screening when compared with D69C was 75.4% and specificity was low at 24.6%. Our result contrasted with that of Polsfuss et al.,10 who reported high sensitivity of 97.4% and 100% specificity. Tan et al. reported 98% sensitivity and 73% specificity. Wassef et al.15 from Egypt reported 5.8% (62/1073) AmpC producers while studies from Pakistan and China reported 37.6% and 44.6% cefoxitin resistance.16,17

AmpC prevalence was 14.5% by Tris-EDTA and was identified in 58/82 (70.7%) of cefoxitin-resistant isolates and had sensitivity of 81.6% and specificity of 50%. Sensitivity of 78% was reported for the Tris-EDTA method.18 High prevalences of 95.2% and 81% have been reported by Black et al.7 and Parveen et al.,19 respectively, by this method. Since carbapenemases inactivate cefoxitin, their presence may lead to false-positive results in the Tris-EDTA disc assay. This is considered a limitation of the method and may be a reason for the high AmpC prevalence in the above two studies.6,20

The in-house disc approximation test identified 62/400 (15.5%) AmpC β-lactamases. The sensitivity was 90% and the specificity was 80% using CDT as the gold standard. All 10 AmpC-positive controls tested positive by this method and the negative control tested negative. Rodríguez-Guerrero et al.8 reported that the best phenotypic test for AmpC detection was the in-house double synergy test, applied with or without boronic acid discs. Khari et al.5 reported that the sensitivity of the test was 50.5%. In our study, chromosomally mediated AmpC was detected in all (100%) K. aerogenes, E. cloacae, C. freundii and S. marcescens isolates by this test. Plasmid-mediated AmpC was detected in 77% of E. coli, 82% of P. aeruginosa and 64% of K. pneumoniae. The rate of detection was higher by this method than by the Tris-EDTA method among all GNB. An advantage of this method was the ability to detect the co-existence of AmpC and ESBL by appropriate positioning of discs. Imipenem was the strongest inducer amongst the three inducers, wherein it strongly induced AmpC in 72% of the isolates. Rizvi et al.21 reported that 50% of the tested isolates were induced by imipenem. Cefoxitin and amoxicillin/clavulanic acid were both weak AmpC inducers.

D69C is an excellent tool for detection of AmpC (sensitivity 97.9% and specificity 88.2%) though it detects AmpC β-lactamases in Enterobacterales only. AmpC-positive controls gave an affirmative response and the negative control was negative. AmpC was detected in 49/321(15.2%) isolates. Matloko et al.22 reported confirmation of AmpC in 50% of isolates while Hashemi et al.23 reported 28% positivity. It detects inducible, derepressed and constitutive AmpC in both chromosomal and plasmid AmpC producers.24

The commercial D72C test, though expensive, has excellent sensitivity (93.9%) and specificity (100%) and shares the same limitation as D69C—it cannot be used for Pseudomonas spp. and Acinetobacter spp. It differed from D69C only in failing to identify one AmpC isolate (48 versus 49). It is important to note that carbapenemases may mask ESBL enzymes.

The CDT matched disc approximation in performance and has the same advantage as disc approximation—it detects AmpC across the board and has excellent sensitivity (95.9%) and specificity (93.8%). AmpC prevalence was established at 15.5% by this test and was superior to D72C as it identified co-carriage of ESBL (13%). AmpC-positive and -negative controls gave appropriate responses with this test.

The AmpC MIC Test Strip performed poorly compared with the other methods. Similar to the other commercial methods targeting the Enterobacterales, the AmpC MIC Test Strip proved excellent for detecting the chromosomal AmpCs such as in K. aerogenes, S. marcescens and C. freundii, while its sensitivity was lower in detecting plasmid-mediated AmpC. Zorgani et al.25 also reported lower rates of detection (44.4%) by this method. However, the specificity of this test was 100%, while the sensitivity was 85.4%, keeping CDT as the gold standard. In our study, 8% of the test isolates were indeterminate and the result could not be interpreted. The drawback of this method was that it only detects AmpC in Enterobacterales. While we used positive and negative controls with all the assays, using genotypic corroboration as a gold standard would have been extremely useful.

With the rising prevalence of plasmid-mediated AmpC, it is essential to proactively identify AmpC-producing isolates and guide appropriate therapy to ensure a good patient outcome and strengthen antimicrobial stewardship programmes. While ESBLs may be treated with β-lactam/β-lactamase inhibitor combinations, management of AmpC β-lactamase-producing bacteria will require carbapenems. Prompt identification and reporting will allow early streamlining and de-escalation of treatment, thus reducing pressure on carbapenems. To the best of our knowledge, this is first study to have evaluated six tests for detection of AmpC after screening with cefoxitin.

Conclusions

We recommend that after initial cefoxitin screening, diagnostic microbiology laboratories may adopt either the in-house disc approximation method, which is cheaper, or D69C or a commercial CDT method for routine detection and reporting of AmpC β-lactamases. With appropriate training, the in-house disc approximation test can be easily adopted in developing countries and assist in not only optimal patient care but also in promoting antimicrobial stewardship and supporting the surveillance and monitoring of antibiotic resistance for infection prevention and control purposes.

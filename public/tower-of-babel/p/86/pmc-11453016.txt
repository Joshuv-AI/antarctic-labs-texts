Copper mitigates salinity stress by regulating water status, photosynthetic pigments and ion homeostasis and increases the yield of Eggplant (Solanum melongena)
Authors: Shahin Imran, Prosenjit Sarker, Md Asif Mahamud, Newton Chandra Paul, Jotirmoy Chakrobortty, Israt Jahan Harine, Md Arifur Rahman, Mehdi Rahimi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractEggplant (Solanum melongena) is moderately sensitive to salinity. Seed priming and exogenous supplementation are technique that enhances germination, growth, and crop yield by overcoming salt stress. Therefore, this study was designed to understand the role of seed priming and copper (Cu) supplementation in modulating salt tolerance in eggplant. When exposed to salt stress, eggplant seedlings showed significantly higher Na+ content, an increased Na/K ratio, prolonged mean germination time, higher relative water loss, more days to flower bud initiation and first flowering, along with decreased germination rate, growth factors, water content, photosynthetic pigments, ionic contents (K+, Ca2+, Mg2+), and yield. The results demonstrated that the germination rate, final germination percentage, germination index, germination energy, and seed vigor index significantly improved, while the mean germination time decreased in Cu-primed seeds. The results also revealed that Cu supplementations increased seedling traits, leaf water content, photosynthetic pigment contents, ionic contents (K+, Ca2+, and Mg2+), and yield while decreasing the contents of Na+, and Na/K ratio, mean germination time, relative water loss, days to flower bud initiation, and days to 1st flowering under salt stress. Germination of seeds, seedlings growth traits, plant water status, plant pigments, yield, and ionic contents with the NaCl and Cu treatments were found to substantially interact with each other according to both hierarchical clustering and PCA. Overall, Cu seed priming and exogenous supplementation emerged as a promising strategy to enhance salt tolerance and promote germination, growth, and yield by regulating water status, photosynthetic pigments, and ion homeostasis in eggplant seedlings under NaCl stress. These findings provide valuable insights into the mechanisms of Cu-mediated stress alleviation in eggplant, with implications for sustainable crop production in saline environments.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-024-05625-0.Keywords: Eggplant, Germination, Ionic contents, Photosynthetic pigments, Seedling growth, Yield

Introduction

Eggplant (Solanum melongena L.), a widely consumed vegetable, offers a nutritious, low-calorie option rich in vitamins, minerals, and dietary fiber, making it an ideal choice for those seeking healthy eating options [1–3]. The nutritional value of eggplant is comparable to tomato plants in terms of minerals and vitamins [4]. The area under eggplant production has increased mainly because of its claimed medicinal properties such as the potential to reduce cholesterol levels and as a source of important minerals and vitamins [5]. Eggplant has a low production cost and a high antioxidant value [6]. Despite being a popular vegetable worldwide, eggplant cultivation faces significant challenges due to salinity stress, which can adversely affect growth, yield, and overall plant health [7]. In general, eggplants are widely reported to be moderately sensitive to salinity [8]. The most common type of salt present in soils used for crop production is sodium chloride (NaCl) [9]. However, in agricultural soils, other salts, such as sodium sulfate (Na₂SO₄), calcium chloride (CaCl₂), magnesium sulfate (MgSO₄), and calcium carbonate (CaCO₃), can also be present [10, 11]. Among these, NaCl is particularly prevalent and problematic because it readily dissolves in water, contributing to soil salinity [9]. High salinity levels lead to osmotic stress and ion toxicity especially Na+ ion, resulting in reduced water uptake and nutrient imbalance in eggplant plants [1]. This stress condition inhibits seed germination, decreases plant height, and reduces leaf area, ultimately leading to lower fruit yield and quality [9]. Salinity also induces oxidative stress in eggplant, increasing the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), which can damage cellular structures and impair metabolic functions [12]. To mitigate these effects, eggplant plants activate antioxidant defense mechanisms, including the production of enzymes such as superoxide dismutase (SOD) and catalase (CAT) [13]. Additionally, salinity stress affects the photosynthetic efficiency of eggplant by disrupting chlorophyll synthesis and reducing stomatal conductance, which limits carbon dioxide uptake [14]. Implementing salinity-tolerant eggplant varieties and adopting proper irrigation management practices are crucial strategies to enhance eggplant productivity under saline conditions [15]. To mitigate the adverse effect of salinity, plants develop some complex mechanisms for adaptation to osmotic and ionic stress caused by high salinity. However, to counteract the negative effects of osmotic stress, ion toxicity, and nutrient deficiency, alternative strategies are effective [16–20]. These include seed priming and exogenous application of micronutrients such as Cu and Zn [21, 22], nanoparticles including Se [23], and different growth regulators like auxin, proline, and salicylic acid [7, 24, 25].

Copper (Cu) serves as an essential micronutrient vital for optimal plant growth and development, playing key roles in the synthesis of chlorophyll and activation of enzymes crucial for plant metabolism [26]. On the contrary, excessive use of Cu can lead to toxicity, highlighting the importance of balanced application to ensure nutrient uptake, growth, and development [27]. Cu can play a role in mitigating salinity stress in plants by aiding in the regulation of various physiological processes. Evidence showed that exogenous application of Cu increased total chlorophyll content, RWC, and yield in maize [28]; increased phenolic content and reduced lipid peroxidase in Rosemary [29]; germination indices and proline content of Psyllium [27]. While excessive salinity can disrupt plant metabolism and water balance, leading to oxidative stress, Cu aids in the synthesis of antioxidant enzymes such as superoxide dismutase (SOD) and catalase (CAT), which scavenge harmful reactive oxygen species (ROS) generated under salinity stress, thereby reducing oxidative damage to plant cells and improving overall stress tolerance [26, 29]. Moreover, Cu assists in maintaining ion balance within plant tissues, which is crucial for sustaining normal physiological functions despite saline conditions [30]. Thus, appropriate Cu supplementation can contribute to enhancing salinity tolerance in plants and improving crop productivity in salt-affected areas. The impact of salt stress and its mitigation by using different plant growth regulators and chemicals is well-studied in many species, including eggplants (Solanum melongena). However, little is known about the impact of Cu supplementation under salt stress on the morphological, physiological, and biochemical properties of plants [21, 22, 26–30]. However, there is no previous research on the effects of Cu priming or exogenous application on eggplant growth, development, and yield under salt stress. Therefore, our study aimed to investigate the effects of Cu on the morphological, physiological, and biochemical of eggplant grown under saline conditions.

Materials and methods

Experimental site, design, and treatments

The laboratory and pot experiments were conducted at Khulna Agricultural University, Khulna, Bangladesh. A completely randomized design (CRD) was used for the laboratory experiment, while a randomized complete block design (RCBD) with three replications was used for the pot experiment. A locally grown eggplant (Solanum melongena cv. Nayan Kajal) was collected from the market. Two salinities (100 and 200 mM NaCl) were selected for the experiment based on earlier literature [1, 31]. Based on our screening experiment two Cu doses (0.5 and 1.0 mM) as CuSO4 were used in the experiment (Supplementary Table 1). The treatment conditions used in the experiments were provided in the Supplementary Table 2.

Laboratory experiment

The seed priming process was conducted according to the method outlined in a previous study [32]. The seeds (11% moisture content) were treated for 5 min with 1% NaOCl to eliminate microorganisms from the seed surface. Twenty eggplant seeds were placed in a container containing 50 ml of distilled water, 0.5 mM Cu, and 1.0 mM Cu solution for 1 h at room temperature. The seeds were then washed five times with distilled water. The treated seeds were dried back to their original moisture content at room temperature. Then, the seeds were placed in the properly labeled Petri dishes with three layers of tissue. The tissue paper was moistened with 10 ml of 100 mM and 200 mM NaCl solutions for salinity treatment and distilled water for control treatment. The germinated seeds were started to count at 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, and 9 days after sowing. From the laboratory experiment, the below parameters were studied:

After ten days of germination, seedlings were selected unbiasedly and root and shoot lengths were measured for seedling height.

Pot experiment

Pots measuring 25 cm in diameter and 30 cm in height were filled with 10 kg of soil collected from the Agricultural Training Institute in Daulutpur, Khulna. Soil was collected from 0 to 15 cm depth. Seeds were sown on the seedbed on 20th August and two seedlings were transplanted into each pot on 25th October. Every pot received an equal amount of manure and fertilizers as recommended by Bangladesh Agricultural Research Institute [33]. The recommended doses of nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, sulfur, zinc, boron, and cow dung were 90, 24, 60, 2.5, 1, 0.5 kg ha-1, and 5-ton ha-1, respectively. The entire amount of cow dung, TSP, Gypsum, Borax, and half of the MP were applied during the final pot soil preparation. The remaining half of MP and the entire urea were applied in three equal installments, 1st at 15 days after planting, 2nd at flowering, and the 3rd at fruit maturity stages. Ten days after transplanting, seedlings were stressed with 100, and 200 mM NaCl (for pots labeled with SS100 and SS200, Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200) at four different times using irrigation water at three-days intervals. Distilled water was used for irrigation to keep the salinity-free treatment. Plants were sprayed with 0.5 or 1 mM Cu (for pots labeled with Cu0.5, Cu1, Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200) throughout, for ten days at two-days intervals, starting at the commencement of the stress (double spray each day at 9 am and 7 pm; 3 ml/plant/spray). After 35 days of transplanting, one plant from each pot was collected for the measurement of root length (RL), root fresh weight (RFW), shoot length (SL), shoot fresh weight (SFW), relative water content (RWC), relative water loss (RWL), and leaves samples were collected to analyses photosynthetic pigments. Then the roots and shoots were oven-dried for 72-h at 60 °C. After drying, root dry weight (RDW), and shoot dry weight (SDW) were recorded. One plant in each pot has remained for yield, and chemical analysis. Days to flower bud initiation, days to 1st flowering, fruits per plant, and fruits yield per plant were recorded for yield parameters.

Estimation of photosynthetic pigments

A spectro-photometric technique based on the Lichtenthaler [35] method was used to quantify the amounts of photosynthetic pigments. Collected leaves (0.5 g) were put into a small vial with 12 mL of 80% ethanol. To extract the pigments, the vials were retained in the dark for 10 days. Using a spectrophotometer with wavelengths of 663, 645, 505, 480, and 453 nm (Shimadzu UV-1280, Kyoto, Japan), the amounts of chlorophyll a, b, lycopene, beta-carotene, and carotenoids were determined.

Preparation of plant extract for chemical analysis

Laboratory work for plant extraction and chemical analysis was carried out at the Department of Agricultural Chemistry of Bangladesh Agricultural University, Mymensingh. The roots and leaves were mechanically ground using a grinder (model no.: IKA A11B) after they had dried in the oven. They were then preserved in plastic bottles with the appropriate labels and tags. Wet oxidation with a di-acid mixture (HNO3: HClO4 = 2:1) was employed to make the plant extract, as described by Singh [36]. Ca, K, Ca, and Mg concentrations were determined by digesting a precisely measured half-gram sample with the acid mixture in a digestion block. For samples having a dry weight below 0.5 g, an appropriate quantity of the dried sample was used for digestion, ensuring the correct ratio was maintained. After the content was almost completely evaporated, the digest was filtered using a Whatman no. 42 filter paper, and a required amount of deionized water (DW) was added to get the volume up to 50 mL.

Determination of na, K, ca, and mg

The content of Ca and Mg in the roots and leaves extract was measured by using the complexometric method of titration with 0.01 M Na2EDTA as the chelating agent [37]. A flame emission spectrophotometer (Model: JENWAY-PFP7) was used to measure the Na and K content of the roots and leaves sample. The wavelengths used were 766 and 589 nm, respectively for K and Na, by the methods outlined by Page [37].

Statistics

R 4.3.2 was used for statistical analysis. Significant differences were discovered using “multcomp” package, followed by Tukey HSD (p < 0.05). To construct a heatmap and conduct hierarchical clustering analysis using Euclidean distances in R, using the “pheatmap” package. Principal component analysis (PCA) was done using the “GGally” and “factoextra” packages.

Results

Effects of cu on germination characters of eggplants under salinity stress

The germination rate (GR), final germination percentage (FGP), mean germination time (MGT), germination index (GI), germination energy (GE), and seed vigor index (SVI) were evaluated to determine the impact of Cu seed priming on the germination of eggplants under NaCl stress (Fig. 1). The study’s findings showed that, in comparison to control seeds, GR was considerably lower in seeds treated with SS100 and SS200. On the other hand, compared to Cu0.5 seeds under SS200 stress, Cu0.5 seeds under SS100 stress greatly increased the GR (Fig. 1A). In a similar vein, Cu1 seeds stressed under SS100 showed higher GR than Cu1 seeds stressed under SS200. When it came to FGP, SS100 and SS200 considerably reduced FGP by 51.16% and 62.79% compared to the control treatment. However, Cu0.5+SS100, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 showed a considerably higher FGP of eggplants seeds by 95.24%, 114.29%, and 143.75% respectively compared to SS (Fig. 1B). Salt stress increased MGT by 52.11% for SS100 and 63.12% for SS200 compared to control condition while Cu0.5+SS100, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treated seeds lowered MGT by 24.88%, 35.69%, and 40.15% respectively (Fig. 1C). When SS was applied instead of the control treatment, the GI dramatically reduced by 81.03% for SS100 and 91.38% for SS200. In contrast, Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200-treated seeds enhanced GI by 309.09%, 365%, 468.18%, and 980% respectively as opposed to salt stress conditions (Fig. 1D). Moreover, GE also considerably decreased by 90.91% and 100% under SS100 and SS200 respectively, however, Cu0.5 + SS100, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 considerably increased GE by 700%, 1200%, and 1833.33% respectively (Fig. 1E). In the case of SVI, SS100, and SS200 considerably lowered SVI by 61.31% and 71.77% respectively, compared to the control treatment. Compared with that under salt stresses, the SVI considerably rose in Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treatment by 134.63%, 99.05%, 170.15%, and 215.19% respectively (Fig. 1F). These results indicated that, under salinity stress, both Cu concentrations led to improved germination metrics, with Cu1 outperforming Cu0.5.

Effects of cu on morphological parameters of eggplants under salinity stress

The root length (RL), shoot length (SL), shoot fresh weight (SFW), shoot dry weight (SDW), plant height (PH), root number (RN), and root volume (RV) of eggplant seedlings were measured to assess the effects of SS and stress-relieving Cu actions (Fig. 2). The results showed that SS-treated plants significantly decreased RL, RFW, RDW, SL, SFW, SDW, PH, RN and RV in eggplant seedlings compared to the control. The findings presented in Fig. 2A indicate that salt stress, namely SS100 and SS200 treatment, results in a reduction of RL by 50.1% and 58.69% respectively. However, plants treated with Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 showed a significant increment in RL of eggplants by 68.44%, 70.79%, 82.38%, and 106.93% respectively, with Cu1 + SS100 and Cu1 + SS200 treatment exhibiting superior performance aligned with SS100 and SS200-treated plants (Fig, 2A). Additionally, SS100 and SS200 decreased the SL of eggplants by 15.13% and 17.74% respectively compared to control seedlings (Fig. 2D). Meanwhile, Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treated eggplants seedlings showed significant increment in SL by 15.07%, 13.79%, 21.29%, and 23.76% respectively compared to SS treatment (Fig. 2D). Moreover, RFW decreased by 8.52% and 10.76%, and RDW decreased by 82.1% and 90.35% in eggplants under SS100 and SS200, respectively, compared to control conditions. However, Cu0.5+SS100, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treatments showed significant increases in RFW by 6.59%, 7.74%, and 8.08%, and in RDW by 332.14%, 441.43%, and 740.4%, respectively (Fig. 2B and C). Similarly, SFW by 9.79%, 13.09%, and SDW by 40.39%, 46.85% of eggplants decreased significantly in SS100 and SS200 respectively compared to the control conditions (Fig. 2E and F). However, SFW significantly increased by 6.18%, 9.38%, and 10.77% in Cu0.5+SS100, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treatments, respectively (Fig. 2E), and SDW increased by 74.97% and 68.66% in Cu1 + SS100 and Cu1 + SS200 treatments, respectively (Fig. 2F), compared to SS100 and SS200. The PH was also considerably shorter by 23.93% and 28.05% in SS100 and SS200 respectively compared to the control condition. In contrast, Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treated plants considerably increased PH of eggplants by 23.88%, 22.07%, 31.46%, and 35.77% respectively compared to SS100 and SS200 (Fig. 2G). Salt stress under SS100 and SS200 significantly reduced RN by 58.15% and 62.11% in eggplants, respectively, compared to control plants (Fig. 2H). However, Cu0.5+SS100, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treated plants considerably increased eggplants RN by 85.26%, 89.47%, and 83.72% respectively compared to SS100 and SS200 (Fig. 2H). In the case of RV, compared to the control condition, SS100 and SS200 considerably reduced RV by 58% and 61%, meanwhile, Cu0.5+SS100, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treated plants considerably increased RV of eggplants by 102.38%, 126.19%, and 112.82% respectively (Fig. 2I). The findings also showed that Cu1 was more effective than Cu0.5 in enhancing seedling growth traits under saline conditions.

Effects of cu on water status and photosynthetic pigment contents of eggplants under salinity stress

Comparing the salt-stressed SS100 and SS200 plants to control plants, the result showed significantly lower RWC by 26.5 and 47.55% (Fig. 3A) and increased RWL by 124.85 and 224% (Fig. 3B) respectively. Whereas, Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treated eggplants seedlings showed significant increments in RWC by 26.51%, 51.93%, 20.27%, and 67.56% respectively as opposed to SS100 and SS200 treated plants (Fig. 3A). On the other hand, RWL also showed significantly lower in Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treated eggplants seedlings by 68.98%, 65.56%, 45.38%, and 106.3% as opposed to SS100 and SS200 treated plants (Fig. 3B). These results showed that Cu1 sustained a more favorable water status in eggplant compared to Cu0.5 under saline conditions.

When SS100 and SS200 plants were exposed to salt stress, their chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, and total chlorophyll content were significantly reduced (by 77.26 and 86.4%) (Fig. 4A), (by 97.45 and 97.24%) (Fig. 4B) and (by 84.64 and 90.36%) (Fig. 4C) compared to control plants. However, plants treated with Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 significantly rose the contents of chlorophyll a by 248.78%, 350.41%, 303.33%, and 481.97% compared with SS100 and SS200 (Fig. 4A). Moreover, compared to SS100 and SS200 treatment, chlorophyll b contents significantly rose by 3027.32%, 2490.46%, 3511.28%, and 2385.99% in Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treatment respectively (Fig. 4B). Beside chlorophyll a and chlorophyll b, total chlorophyll also significantly rose by 417.74%, 574.61%, 498.41%, and 681.44% in Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treatment compared with SS100 and SS200 treatment (Fig. 4C). However, salt-stressed SS100 and SS200 plants similarly exerted lowered content of lycopene (by 68.34 and 77.18%), beta-carotene (by 93.48 and 95.33%) and carotenoids (by 94 and 95.63%) over control plants (Fig. 4D and F). Compared to SS100 and SS200 treatment, Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treatment significantly augmented lycopene contents by 166.72%, 231.24%, 175.1%, and 320.03% treatment respectively (Fig. 4D). In addition to lycopene contents, beta-carotene contents also significantly augmented compared to SS100 and SS200 treatment in Cu0.5+SS100, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treatment by 837.38%, 1120.32%, and 1406.49% respectively (Fig. 4E). Similar to other pigments, carotenoids contents augmented significantly by 1497.81%, 1742.55%, and 2202.13% in Cu0.5+SS100, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treatment respectively compared to SS100 and SS200 treatment (Fig. 4F). The findings also indicated that supplementation with Cu1 led to a greater increase in chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, total chlorophyll, lycopene, beta-carotene, and carotenoids compared to the increases observed with Cu0.5 supplementations under saline conditions.

Effects of cu on yield contributing characters, and yield of eggplants under salinity stress

Comparing salt stressed SS100 and SS200 treated plants over control, there was a considerable increase in days to flower bud initiation (by 40 and 60%) (Fig. 5A) and days to 1st flowering (by 33.62 and 49.14%) (Fig. 5B). Conversely, a significant reduction was found in the cases of fruits per plant (61.29 and 77.42%) (Fig. 5C) and fruits yield per plant (by 82.38 and 88.68%) (Fig. 5D). However, it has been noticed that, Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treatment showed considerable reduction in flower bud initiation by 21.43%, 25.63%, 28.57%, and 35.63% compared to SS100 and SS200 treatment respectively (Fig. 5A). In addition, compared to SS100 and SS200 treatment, Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treatment considerably reduced days to 1st flowering by 21.94%, 23.12%, 28.39%, and 34.68% respectively (Fig. 5B). Similarly, fruit per plant also showed considerable increment by 133.33%, 157.14%, 158.33%, and 314.29% in Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treatment respectively compared to SS100 and SS200 treatment (Fig. 5C). In addition to fruit per plant, fruit yield per plant also showed considerable increment in Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treatment by 372.09%, 253.62%, 438.94%, and 675.01% respectively (Fig. 5D). The findings indicated that supplementing with Cu1 performed better to earlier flowering and a higher fruit yield compared to Cu0.5 under conditions of salinity stress.

Effects of cu on ionic homeostasis in eggplants under salinity stress

We measured the root and leaf Na+, K+, Ca2+, and Mg2+ content to know the effect of Cu supplementation in ion homeostasis under salt stress. Comparing the salt-stressed SS100 and SS200 plants to control plants, the salt-stressed plants had significantly higher Na+ contents in root (by 68.02 and 109.45%) (Fig. 6A) and in leaf (by 207.04 and 277.42%) (Fig. 7A); and lower K+ contents in root (by 56.45 and 59.69%) (Fig. 6B) and in leaf (by 55.05 and 63.91%) (Fig. 7B). In contrast to control plants, salt-stressed SS100 and SS200 plants also displayed a significantly greater Na/K ratio in root (by 286.11 and 419.94%) (Fig. 6C) and in leaf (by 588.11 and 946.65%) (Fig. 7C). However, plants treated with Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 showed considerable reduction in root Na+ contents by 16.50%, 32.91%, 39.62%, and 49.65% respectively and in leaf Na+ contents by 26.09%, 32.92%, 39.22%, and 41.23% respectively compared to SS100 and SS200 treated plants (Figs. 6A and 7A). Along with lowering Na+ contents, root K+ contents also showed considerable increment in Cu0.5+SS100, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treatment by 97.55%, 123.73%, and 132.13% respectively (Fig. 6B); and leaf K+ contents by 73.57%, 100.29%, 83.13%, and 121.40% in Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treatment respectively (Fig. 7B) compared to SS100 and SS200 treatment. Besides lowering root and leaf Na+ contents and increasing K+ contents, it has been also observed that, root Na/K ratio considerably lowered by 56.57%, 66.53%, 67.01%, and 73.47% in Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treatment respectively (Fig. 6C); and leaf Na/K ratio by 57.75%, 38.66%, 73.03%, and 78.32% (Fig. 7C) in Cu0.5+SS100, Cu0.5+SS200, Cu1 + SS100, and Cu1 + SS200 treatment respectively compared to SS100 and SS200 treatment.

Discussion

Salt stress is a significant environmental abiotic factor that lowers normal plant growth, and development and reduces ultimate production [38, 39]. In the experiment, salt stress significantly reduced GR, FGP, GI, GE, and SVI along with increased MGT (Fig. 1). This is due to the interference of soil salinity with nutrient absorption and plant metabolic processes including lipid and glucose metabolism [40]. Generally, the most crucial phase of seedling establishment that determines the final result of crop production is seed germination [41]. It’s a complex process that is extremely susceptible to unfavorable climatic conditions [42]. However, this effect was minimized through the application of exogenous Cu. Cu successfully increased GR, FGP, GI, GE, SVI, and also decreased MGT in eggplant under salt stress conditions (Fig. 1). Cu facilitated the uptake of essential nutrients and promoted ionic balance, which is vital for seed viability and growth under saline conditions [43]. Many researchers have developed different techniques to mitigate drastic effects of salinity on plants and exogenous application is one of them [44, 45]. Feghhenabi et al. [46] found an increased germination percent (GP) and germination rate (GR) when wheat seeds were primed with exogenous ascorbic acid, potassium silicate etc., under salinity stress. Chakrobortty et al. [47] applied citric acid exogenously on salt-stressed okra plants and the highest GP, GI, GE and SVI were found with minimum MGT. Again, enhanced germination characteristics were obtained by Khan et al. [22] under salinity when wheat seeds were primed with Cu. Imran et al. [32] investigated an increased GP in soybeans with citric acid application under salt stress.

Due to salt stress, the root and shoot lengths of the eggplant were drastically reduced (Fig. 2A and D). Because, the ground part of a plant especially the roots are most vulnerable to salinity and responsible for poor root growth and elongation [48]. Again roots get nutrients from the soil, and shoots subsequently augment the nutrient supply throughout the entire plant. For this reason, the length of the shoots and roots are important indicators of how a plant reacts to salt stress. However in the experiment, these parameters were improved due to Cu application (Fig. 2A and D). A similar response was recorded by [49] in oat seedlings grown under salinity. Begum et al. [50] also found decreased root and shoot length under salinity but a significant improvement has been obtained by Cu application. Total root number and root volume were also decreased by NaCl but increased when the eggplants were treated with Cu (Fig. 2H and I). An almost similar result was obtained from the exogenous salicylic acid application by Sheteiwy et al. [51] in rice plants. In the present study, RFW and RDW were deducted for salinity effect but these were increased due to Cu treatment (Fig. 2B and C). Iqbal et al. [49] and Begum et al. [50] both obtained high RFW and RDW in Cu-treated oat and Brassica rapa, respectively under salinity. Shoot biomass, both fresh and dried, significantly decreased in the present study (Fig. 2E and F). This is because, salt stress results in shorter shoots, which in turn provide minimal shoot weights [52]. The SFW and SDW were increased in Cu-treated eggplants which supports the findings of Hong and Jin [53] in maize plants. Plant height is directly dependent on salinity stress and inversely related [54–56]. Exogenous Cu increased the plant height by minimizing the destructive effects of salinity. From a study, Begum et al. [50] concluded that the plant height of Brassica rapa was increased through exogenous Cu application. Khan et al. [22] obtained maximum plant height when wheat plants were treated with Cu. Cu plays a role in lignin biosynthesis, which strengthens cell walls and supports the growth of roots and shoots under stress conditions [57]. Enhanced lignification can improve root length, root number, and root volume by providing structural stability and facilitating water and nutrient uptake [58]. The heatmap analysis showed that when exposed to salt stress, both priming and the addition of Cu substantially improved germination and growth characteristics. Moreover, PCA indicated a strong link between germination, seedling traits, and the use of priming with exogenous Cu. Since cell growth is a key process disrupted by salt stress, affecting overall plant development [56], Cu application may support better cell growth, which in turn leads to enhanced plant growth (Figs. 1 and 2).

In terms of the physiological impacts of cellular water deficit brought on by stress circumstances like salt stress, the RWC or RWL of the leaves may be used to identify the real water status of the plant [59]. However, maintaining sufficient water content in plants under salt stress is crucial for preserving the proper development of plants [60, 61]. This research demonstrated that under various salinity-stressed conditions, the administration of Cu had a substantial impact on the RWC and RWL of eggplants. The current study showed that increased RWC and decreased RWL were obtained from the exogenous application of Cu under salt stress (Fig. 3). It has been reported that decreased RWC and increased RWL may occur due to structural injury to the cell wall, which interferes with water uptake [62]. Sheteiwy et al. [63] found that foliar application of jasmonic acid improves seedlings’ ability to withstand salt stress by elevating RWC and decreasing RWL in soybean (Glycine max L.). Yakoubi et al. [64] studied different plant growth regulators under salt stress and found an increase in RWC in okra.

In this study, salt stress had a direct impact on the levels of chlorophyll (a & b) (Fig. 4A and B). The detrimental effects of accumulated (Na+& Cl−) on the biosynthesis of chloroplast structure may be the source of the reduced chlorophyll content under salinity stress [65, 66]. This effect of salinity was minimized through exogenous Cu application (Fig. 4A and B). Hamani et al. [67] found an increased chlorophyll- a & b content in salt-stressed cotton plants when the exogenous glycine betaine and salicylic acid were used. A similar result was obtained by Kamiab [68] in the pistachio plant. Again, from our recent study, salt stress was reversely correlated with lycopene, beta-carotene, and carotenoid content. These parameters reduced as the salt stress increased. Babaei et al. [69] found an increase in pigment content of Lepidium sativum under salinity. Carotenoid content under salinity was also alleviated in Crocus sativus [70] which is almost similar to our findings. Cu plays a crucial role as a cofactor for enzymes involved in chlorophyll biosynthesis, such as cytochrome c oxidase and plastocyanin, which are essential for electron transport in photosynthesis [71]. Under salinity stress, the availability of essential nutrients like nitrogen, magnesium, and iron-critical for chlorophyll formation is often reduced, leading to a decline in pigment content [72]. The application of Cu can mitigate these effects by improving the enzyme activities required for chlorophyll biosynthesis, thereby maintaining higher levels of chlorophyll and other pigments even under stress conditions [73]. The PCA and heatmap also clarified their interaction with the treatment agent and stress condition. The outcomes also showed that Cu1 outperformed Cu0.5 by increasing the pigments under salt stress.

The present experiment revealed that the flower bud initiation and days to 1st flowering were increased significantly under NaCl stress. Conversely, a significant reduction was found in the cases of fruits per plant and fruit yield per plant (Fig. 5). This is because, NaCl stress disturbs plant nutrient uptake and creates nutritional imbalance, which interferes with plant growth and flower bud formation [74]. Bedirhanoğlu et al. [75] experimented with chili varieties where phonological characteristics such as days to flowering and days to fruiting arose, but the number of flowers and fruits reduced under salinity. Again, Balasankar et al. [76] and Turhan et al. [77] stated that salt restricted flower production in chili and tomato respectively. Our results demonstrated that Cu supplementation improved fruit yield while reducing the time to flower bud initiation and the number of days to first flowering (Fig. 5). Cu modulates the yield and yield contributing characteristics of eggplant [78]. Ullah et al. [79] found increased yield in salt-stressed tomatoes when chitosan was sprayed. Sarker et al. [80] stated that the application of exogenous 2,4-D successfully improved lentil yield. Again, exogenous silicon mitigated the salinity effects of soybean plants [81].

By considering ion homeostasis, salt-stressed plants showed a noticeably higher Na/K ratio (high Na+ and low K+ content) in their roots and leaves compared to control plants. In the salt stress conditions, activated particular genes permit the absorption of more Na+ than K+ and roots absorb more Na+ through epidermal cells resulting higher Na+/K+ ratio [82]. As a result, leaf tissue got more Na+, and leaf Na+/K+ ratio also increased [83]. Iqra et al. [84] found a high Na-K ratio in wheat plants under salinity. On the other hand, a significant decrease in the Ca2+ and Mg2+ content in the root and leaf was found in the eggplants (Figs. 5 and 6). This is because of the increased absorption of Na+ by the plants. In the current study, the increased ionic ratios were minimized through Cu application (Figs. 5 and 6). Cu helps here to maintain ionic balance. Selvamani et al. [85] exposed that, melatonin, salicylic acid, silicon, and selenium were very much helpful for ion homeostasis in rice under salinity stress. The application of exogenous nutrients can reduce Na⁺ levels in plant tissues by promoting K⁺ uptake through competitive inhibition, as K⁺ and Na⁺ compete for the same uptake pathways [86]. Additionally, Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ can help stabilize cellular membranes and mitigate the toxic effects of Na⁺, thereby improving overall plant health and function [72]. Furthermore, these nutrients enhance osmotic adjustment, improve root structure, and increase crop yield by promoting better water and nutrient absorption [87]. Consequently, the exogenous application of nutrients under salinity stress not only reduces Na⁺ accumulation but also improves the K⁺, Ca²⁺, and Mg²⁺ status in plants, ultimately leading to enhanced growth and yield.

The assessment of treatment-variable interaction was done through a heatmap using hierarchical clustering and PCA analysis. According to the PCA, cluster-I variables showed a moderate association with SS100 treatment and a significant correlation with SS200 treatment. Conversely, the Cu1 + SS100 and Cu1 + SS200 treatments showed a strong correlation with the variables of clusters II and III (Fig. 8). The application of Cu, which is a significant cofactor molecule [71] controls growth as well as the physiological and developmental processes involved in stress reactions. Thus, PCA confirms the stress alleviation effect of exogenous Cu in eggplants in salt-stress. In the present study where PC1 demonstrated 92.56% data variability and PC2 displayed just 2.99% data variability across the treatments (Fig. 8). According to Khan et al. [22], the interaction between salinity and Cu might be caused by the activation of resistance genes that seems to increase the physiological activity of wheat. Quamruzzaman et al. [88] also explained the positive effects of exogenous plant growth regulators on plants under NaCl stress. However, both seed priming and exogenous application of Cu successfully mitigate the adverse effects of salinity in eggplant.

Conclusion

In summary, seed priming and Cu supplementation alleviate salt-induced inhibition of seed germination and growth in eggplants by enhancing germination, seedling growth characteristics, plant water status, photosynthetic pigments, and maintaining ionic homeostasis. Cu supplementation increased the yield of eggplant plants under salt-stress conditions. Moreover, supplementation Cu can be used to improve the yield of eggplant under non-stress conditions. Notably, Cu at a concentration of 1 mM exhibited the most significant improvements across various parameters, suggesting its efficacy in alleviating salt stress effects on eggplant seed germination, plant growth, and yield. In conclusion, Cu seed priming and supplementation emerge as a promising strategy for enhancing eggplant tolerance to salt stress, offering valuable insights for agricultural practices aimed at improving crop productivity under adverse environmental conditions. Further field trials should be performed to validate our results in field conditions.

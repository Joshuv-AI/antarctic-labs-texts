Trifecta outcomes to assess the feasibility of local anesthesia for benign prostate hyperplasia minimally invasive surgical treatments
Authors: Alberto Olivero, Sinan Khadhouri, Erika Palagonia, Paolo Dell’Oglio, Stefano Tappero, Barend Dreyer, Aldo Massimo Bocciardi, Alberto Caviglia, Antonio Galfano, Feras Al Jaafari, Bhaskar K. Somani, Amelia Pietropaolo, Silvia Secco
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Minimally invasive surgical treatments (MISTs) are many promising approaches for the management of benign prostate hyperplasia (BPH) that might be performed under local anesthesia (LA) and have same-day discharge. To address the feasibility and efficacy of LA for BPH-MIST, a specific trifecta was conceptualized and tested: (1) no intraoperative complications, (2) reduced intraoperative pain, and (3) same-day discharge.Materials and Methods:We prospectively collected data from patients who underwent Rezum, Urolift, and iTIND treatments from January 01, 2023, to February 28, 2024, on an international database. All the patients received a local administration of two anesthetic gels in the urethra before the procedure; selected patients, in addition, received a transperineal anesthetic injection in the periprostatic space or transurethrally with a Schelin Catheter (SC) injection directly in the prostatic lobes. Reduced intraoperative pain was defined as <5 on the visual analogue scale (VAS range: 0–10).Results:Overall, 156 patients were included in the study. Median age was 63.5 (interquartile range [IQR]: 56–70) years. Eleven patients (7.0%) had diabetes, and 29 (18.6%) received anticoagulant medication. Ten patients (6.4%) had indwelling catheters. The median prostate size was 45 ml (IQR: 34–60). Forty-eight patients underwent Urolift (30.8%), 70 (44.8%) Rezum, and 38 (24.4%) iTIND. The transperineal block and the SC were used in 21 patients (13.5%) and 32 (20.5%) patients, respectively, with 103 (66%) patients receiving only the anesthetic gels in the urethra. Performing MIST in pure LA achieved the trifecta outcome in 122 patients (78.2%). Six patients (3.8%) had intraoperative complications. One hundred and thirty patients (83.3%) experienced reduced intraoperative pain (<5 on VAS). One hundred and 47 (94.2%) patients were discharged on the same day of the procedure.Conclusions:LA for MIST in BPH is feasible, with excellent success rates and reduced pain, as trifecta outcomes were achieved.

INTRODUCTION

Over the last years, treatment options for symptomatic benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) have changed radically with the introduction of minimally invasive surgical therapies (MIST).[12] BPH is a common condition that affects aging men and is characterized by the non-malignant growth of the prostate.[3] While a range of treatments, such as medical management with alpha-blockers, 5-alpha reductase inhibitors, or phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitors exist, MIST have emerged as a viable alternative. These minimally invasive procedures offer the potential for symptom relief with reduced morbidity compared to traditional endoscopic options such as transurethral resection and laser enucleation.[4] Patients who may benefit the most from these MISTs are those who wish to avoid surgery, are at a high surgical risk, or want to preserve ejaculatory function.[5] This particularly appeals to patients who are suitable or willing to undergo general anesthesia, as all MIST can be done under local anesthesia (LA) with or without conscious sedation.[267]

Recently, a wide variety of MISTs have been developed to treat BPH. These include procedures like the temporary implantable nitinol device (iTIND), prostatic urethral lift (Urolift system), water vapor therapy injections (Rezum), prostate artery embolization, and Transperineal Laser Ablation of the Prostate.

While several studies assessed the functional results and reported acceptable outcomes with these techniques, more robust evidence is still needed to confirm their long-term effectiveness and safety profiles, particularly compared to standard surgical approaches.[678] A field that requires further investigation is the feasibility and safety of performing these minimally invasive procedures under LA. Several different types of LA have been proposed to perform MIST,[91011] and the most promising feature of MIST performed under LA is that it allows an outpatient or day hospital setting; they are associated with a low complication rate, shorter recovery time, and optimal pain control.[12]

While many studies evaluate the feasibility and present preliminary results of LA applied to MIST, a proper definition of successful LA MIST procedures has yet to be established, and the different techniques available have yet to be considered.

The current study evaluates the feasibility and efficacy of LA MIST for BPH by defining and analyzing immediate outcomes, such as reduced pain, no complications, and same-day discharge, thereby forming the trifecta for successful LA MIST procedures.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study population

All consecutive patients with symptomatic BPH who underwent MIST procedures (implantable nitinol device, prostatic urethral lift, water vapor therapy injections) at three high-volume international institutions (ASST Grande Ospedale Metropolitano Niguarda, Milan, Italy; NHS Fife, Victoria Hospital, Kirkcaldy, United Kingdom; and Queen Margaret Hospital, Dunfermline, United Kingdom) between January 2023 and February 2024 were recruited. Complete pre-, peri-, and postoperative data were prospectively collected in a RedCap international database. Exclusion criteria included urethral strictures, urinary bladder stones, and active urinary tract infections confirmed by urine culture. Patients under 18 years and those with medical conditions that preclude LA were also excluded from the present study.

Local anesthesia protocol

LA procedures were performed by experienced urologists who have performed at least 50 MIST procedures. All the patients received a local administration of two anesthetic gels in the urethra 20 min before the procedure. In addition to the gel injection, in selected cases, additional LA was given through transperineal anesthetic injection in the periprostatic space or transurethrally using the Schelin Catheter (SC) (ProstaLund AB®, Lund, Sweden) injection directly in the prostatic lobes. In the trans-perineal prostatic nerve block, 20 ml of 1% lidocaine was injected under transrectal ultrasound (TRUS) guidance through a long 22-G Chiba needle around the neurovascular bundle between the base of the prostate and the seminal vesicles, near the apex, or into the Denonvilliers’ fascia as previously described.[13] Transurethral Intraprostatic Anesthesia (TUIA) utilizes the SC, a disposable transurethral catheter with a retractable needle that can be connected to a syringe for local anesthetic or drug injection directly into the prostate.[1014] The selection of different LA protocols was based on the surgeon’s preference. However, all the surgeons involved are trained in periprostatic block and TUIA.

Variables of interest and study endpoints

For each patient, the following variables were collected: Age at surgery, comorbidities, anticoagulant therapy use, prostatic gland volume, measured with preoperative multi-parametric MRI or TRUS of the prostate, International Prostatic Symptom Score (IPSS), preoperative uroflow parameters, type of MIST, and anesthesia.

The endpoint of this study was to determine the feasibility and success rate of LA for BPH-MIST. Lacking a proper definition, the procedure’s success was defined as the concomitant achievement of three outcomes. Such a trifecta consisted of (1) discharge from the hospital on the same day of the procedure, (2) reduced intraoperative pain, defined as a Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) <5 during the whole procedure (range: 0–10), and (3) the absence of intraoperative complications. Patients were informed before the anesthesia about the pain scale. VAS score was investigated by asking the patient to assess pain from 0 to 10 points (with 0 points representing “no pain” and 10 points representing “the worst pain imaginable”) at different procedure moments: At the scope insertion, during the treatment, and immediately after the scope retrieval. The cutoff of 4 points to define a successful anasthesia was chosen according to the previous literature on prostatic procedures.[1516] Intraoperative complications were defined and categorized according to the Intraoperative Adverse Incident Classification (EAUiaiC) proposed by the European Association of Urology ad hoc Complications Guidelines Panel.[17]

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS (Version 24, IBM SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Data were summarized by the median and interquartile range (IQR) and absolute and relative frequencies for continuous and categorical variables. Mann–Whitney and Pearson Chi-squared tests were used for comparison where appropriate.

Ethics statement

All procedures involving human participants followed the Declaration of Helsinki as revised in the 2000 amendment. Due to the anonymously coded database design used in this study, study-specific institutional review board ethics approval was not required.

RESULTS

One hundred and 61 patients underwent MIST in the study period. Five patients underwent the procedure under general anesthesia due to their preference, leaving 156 patients with complete data included in the study.

Median age was 63.5 (IQR: 56–70) years, with a median body mass index of 30.1 (IQR: 22–36). Eleven (7.0%) patients had diabetes, and 29 (18.6%) patients received anticoagulant medication. Ten (6.4%) patients had indwelling or intermittent catheters. The median prostate size was 45 ml (IQR: 34–60) with a median IPSS and Quality of life score of 22 (16–25) and 4 (4–5), respectively. Uroflowmetry was performed in 96 patients (88.1%) with a Q max and Post Void Residual (PVR) of 11 (6.5–13) ml/s and 56 (20–108) ml. Forty-eight patients underwent Urolift (30.8%), 70 (44.8%) Rezum, and 38 (24.4%) iTIND. Transperineal block was performed in 21 patients (13.5%), and the SC was used in 32 (20.5%) cases; with 103 (66%) patients receiving only the anesthetic gels in the urethra. In the Urolift group, 46 (95.8%) received only the transurethral anesthetic gel, and one patient each received transperineal block and TUIA. In the iTIND group, 31 patients (81.6%) received transurethral gel anesthesia and seven (18.4%) TUIA. In the Rezum group, 26 (37.1%), 20 (28.6%), and 24 (34.3%) received the transurethral anesthetic gel, transperineal block, and TUIA, respectively. Performing MIST in pure LA achieved the trifecta outcome in 122/156 (78.2%) patients.

Six (3.8%) patients had intraoperative complications, with two having mild hematuria, two feeling faint or having vasovagal episodes, and two with device malfunctions. According to EAUiaiC, the intraoperative complications were Grade 1 in four cases and Grade 2 in two cases. Five complications were reported in the transurethral anesthetic gel group and one in the transperineal block (P = 0.6). The association of these complications was with Urolift (n = 3), iTIND (n = 1), and water vapor therapy (n = 2). The device-related complications were due to the Urolift gun misfire and did not affect the length of stay.

A reduced intraoperative VAS pain score of < 5 was achieved in 130 (83.3%) patients. Optimal pain control was achieved in 86 (83.5%), 14 (66.7%), and 30 (93.8%) patients for transurethral anesthetic gel, transperineal block, and TUIA group, showing superiority in pain control for the SC anesthesia (P = 0.035). One hundred and forty-seven (94.2%) of the patients were discharged on the day of the procedure, with 91.3% in the transurethral anesthetic gel group and 100% in the other two groups, respectively (P = 0.086).

When confronting the outcome for the type of MIST, no differences were reported in pain control and length of stay (P = 0.7 and 0.3, respectively); Urolift seems to have a higher intraoperative complications rate than the other techniques (P = 0.016), however all of the low grades and without sequelae. DISCUSSION

In the present study, we analyzed the feasibility and efficacy of MIST in treating symptomatic BPH performed under LA. We reported an excellent rate of almost 80% of procedures fulfilling the trifecta outcomes. To our knowledge, the trifecta concept was not previously described for MIST performed in LA. We propose that trifecta outcomes for LA should simultaneously include the absence of intraoperative complications, optimal pain control, and feasibility in a day-case setting.

Our findings have a significant clinical impact. We confronted the three most widespread MIS techniques combined with the different types of LA available in a “real-life” setting. All the combinations obtained satisfactory outcomes that allowed for a day case procedure. Proposing an easy-to-collect and to-replicate trifecta outcome will enable discussions with the emerging techniques being developed.

The need for general or regional anesthesia, reduction in operative room availability, peri-operative morbidities, and sexual complications of TURP and laser treatments have encouraged the technological advances and the widespread use of MIST for BPH.[18] These new treatments advocate faster recovery, lower complications, improved uroflow, and symptom relief compared to TURP[219] and similar short-term retreatment rates.[18] Notably, as already reported in the literature, we confirm that among the MISTs, the technique that requires the most effort for analgesia is Rezum.[1020]

An optimal MIST should enable rapid recovery, outpatient feasibility, and maintain safety and efficacy. Moving in this direction implies switching from General or Spinal Anesthesia to LA. Anesthesiologists prefer spinal over general anesthesia for rapid endoscopic procedures because spinal anesthesia has lower risks of anaphylaxis, vascular, pulmonary, and neurological complications, and it does not present the risk of intubation-related problems.[21] However, moving toward minimizing invasiveness means avoiding the side effects of spinal anesthesia, such as urinary retention.[22]

Intraurethral lidocaine gel injection is the first line of LA and is a cost-effective analgesic method, which reduces the need for analgesic drug administration during endourological maneuvers.[23] Our study suggests that intraurethral lidocaine gel injection is often sufficient to complete the procedure and achieve the trifecta safely; this is particularly valid for fast treatments such as iTIND and Urolift. When a procedure is longer due to prostate shape and volume or thermal energy is applied, as in Rezum, a combination of intraurethral lidocaine and local anesthetic injection in the prostate gland or periprostatic tissues may be needed to optimize the comfort. We report the experience of three referral centers with the trans-perineal prostatic nerve block and TUIA with the disposable Schelin transurethral catheter. We report optimal trifecta rate achievement for both the anesthesia techniques; however, as previously reported in a single MIST experience,[1014] TUIA seems optimal for pain control, reaching almost 95% of patients with VAS scores <5 points. The efficacy of trans-perineal prostatic nerve block came from the experience regarding prostate biopsy,[1324] and our analysis confirmed the valuable safety profile.

Bal et al. reported high patient-reported tolerability for Rezūm under the administration of conscious sedation through both oral sedation and LA and deep intravenous sedation alone. Almost all patients receiving conscious sedation would choose to undergo Rezūm therapy using conscious sedation over general anesthesia.[25]

Regarding the different MISTs, we reported outstanding results in all the trifecta outcome items, which confirms the safety profile of all three techniques.

Future studies are needed to evaluate each surgical and anesthesiological technique individually, the role of different combinations of anesthetic drugs, and the confrontation between LA and general or spinal anesthesia.

A few aspects of our work deserve further discussion. First, being a multicentric experience allows us to better reflect on real-world practice and case selection. Still, it may simultaneously cause a few differences in the surgical and anesthesiological protocol that are not perfectly uniform. Second, being MIST a “niche” treatment (for now) in contrast to TURP or Laser endoscopic treatments reflects the population’s numerosity, preventing us from performing group sub-analysis and more complex statistical analysis. Second, the paper demonstrates only immediate outcomes, and there is a lack of longitudinal follow-up for pain and complications; however, reasonable pain control during the procedure and no intraoperative complications can reflect on postoperative pain levels.[26] Third, pain tolerance is an individual trait that must be considered when interpreting our results. Lastly, different LA protocols were selected based on the surgeon’s preference, which could hide a selection bias.

CONCLUSIONS

LA for MIST related to BPH is feasible, with an excellent rate of successful procedures expressed as trifecta outcomes achievement. The easy-to-replicate trifecta should be implemented in the investigation strategies to report outcomes and make comparisons in further studies.

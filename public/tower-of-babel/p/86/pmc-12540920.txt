Refractory Spontaneous Pneumothorax in Interstitial Lung Disease Under Pirfenidone Treatment
Authors: Jaejun Jeong, Min Kyun Kang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTRecurrent pneumothorax associated with interstitial lung disease occurs frequently. In the case of refractory pneumothorax, other methods such as mechanical pleurodesis, in addition to chemical pleurodesis, should be considered, because antifibrotics such as pirfenidone might affect the effectiveness of pleurodesis.Keywords: interstitial lung disease, pirfenidone, spontaneous pneumothorax

A 73‐year‐old woman was transferred to the thoracic surgery department for treatment of right recurrent pneumothorax. The breath sound of right side was decreased. A chest X‐ray revealed a right pneumothorax with subcutaneous emphysema (Figure 1a). The accompanying underlying disease was interstitial lung disease (ILD), which had been treated with pirfenidone. The patient had undergone pleurodesis using chemical agents, such as mistletoe extract (ABNOBA viscum F), talc several times in the past, but the pneumothorax recurred repeatedly. The patient was expected not to tolerate surgical treatment, considering her poor lung function. Pleurodesis with 50% dextrose solution was performed an additional three times and the air leakage resolved without complication. The follow‐up chest X‐ray during an outpatient visit showed the large right recurrent pneumothorax (Figure 1b). It might be considered that antifibrotics such as pirfenidone may affect the effectiveness of pleurodesis [1]. In patients with ILD under antifibrotics, other methods such as mechanical pleurodesis, in addition to chemical pleurodesis, should be considered in cases of refractory pneumothorax.

Prognostic value of [18F]fluorodeoxyglucose PET/CT in esophageal cancer patients treated with anti-PD-1 inhibitors
Authors: Wonseok Whi, Jong-Mu Sun, Joon Young Choi, Hyunjong Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo evaluate the prognostic value of [18F]fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) PET/CT in patients with recurrent or metastatic esophageal cancer treated with anti-programmed death 1 (PD-1) inhibitors and chemotherapy.MethodsThis retrospective study enrolled 43 patients treated with anti-PD-1 inhibitors plus chemotherapy between March 2022 and June 2025. We analyzed pre-therapeutic maximum and mean standardized uptake values (SUVmax, SUVmean), metabolic tumor volume (MTV), and total lesion glycolysis (TLG). Associations with overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) were assessed using Kaplan-Meier and Cox regression analyses. Subgroup analyses evaluated early metabolic changes in patients with follow-up PET/CT (n = 15) and metabolic trajectory in those with initial staging PET/CT (n = 19).ResultsForty-three patients (mean age, 65.0 ± 8.5 years; 34 men) were evaluated. High pre-therapeutic SUVmax, SUVmean, MTV, and TLG were significantly associated with poor OS based on the likelihood ratio test from univariate Cox regression (all p < 0.05). While pre-therapeutic parameters did not predict PFS, early reductions in volumetric parameters (Δ%MTV and Δ%TLG) on follow-up PET/CT were significantly associated with PFS (p < 0.05). Furthermore, patients exhibiting a “high-to-low” metabolic trajectory from initial staging to pre-therapeutic baseline demonstrated superior PFS compared to the “low-to-high” group (p = 0.02 for SUVmax; p < 0.001 for SUVmean).ConclusionsFDG PET/CT parameters were associated with survival outcomes; pre-therapeutic metabolic burden predicts OS, whereas dynamic metabolic changes and longitudinal trajectory showed associations with PFS in patients treated with chemo-immunotherapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12880-026-02475-0.

Introduction

Esophageal cancer is an aggressive and increasingly prevalent malignancy globally, ranking as the seventh leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [1]. Unfortunately, a substantial proportion of patients present with metastatic disease at the time of initial diagnosis or experience recurrence following curative treatments. This patient subgroup with recurrent or metastatic esophageal cancer poses a significant challenge in management due to the advanced nature of the disease and its poor prognosis [2]. The therapeutic landscape for these patients has historically been limited; despite the use of conventional cytotoxic chemotherapy, median survival times have remained short, rarely exceeding one year [3, 4]. Furthermore, the heterogeneity of the tumor microenvironment and aggressive biological behavior often compromise treatment efficacy; achieving durable responses is a persistent hurdle in clinical practice [5].

A pivotal advancement in the management of this advanced disease is the integration of immunotherapy, specifically immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) targeting the programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) axis. Regimens combining anti-PD-1 agents, such as pembrolizumab or nivolumab, with fluoropyrimidine (FP)-based chemotherapy have recently supplanted conventional therapies to become the standard first-line treatment for advanced esophageal cancer [6, 7]. Landmark phase III clinical trials have validated this approach, demonstrating statistically significant improvements in both overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) compared to chemotherapy alone [8, 9]. However, despite these notable survival benefits, the response to chemo-immunotherapy is heterogeneous; a considerable proportion of patients fail to respond or experience disease progression. Consequently, identifying early predictive tools to discriminate patients who will benefit from this regimen remains a challenge in clinical oncology.

[18F]fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography/computed tomography (FDG PET/CT) is a useful imaging modality in oncology, providing integral anatomical and metabolic information that facilitates comprehensive disease assessment [10]. Beyond its diagnostic utility, FDG PET/CT has been extensively investigated as a prognostic biomarker and a tool for monitoring treatment response, particularly in FDG-avid tumors [11, 12]. Previous studies in advanced esophageal cancer have consistently demonstrated that high metabolic activity of the tumor is associated with poor survival outcomes [13, 14]. Furthermore, a significant metabolic reduction following neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy has been shown to correlate with favorable pathologic response and long-term survival [15]. However, the clinical validity of FDG PET/CT for predicting treatment outcomes in this specific patient population has not yet been fully established.

Given this background, this study aimed to evaluate the prognostic value of FDG PET/CT in patients with recurrent or metastatic esophageal cancer treated with anti-PD-1 inhibitors combined with chemotherapy, thereby identifying potential biomarkers to guide optimal treatment strategies.

Methods

Subjects

Our institutional review board has approved this retrospective study, and the requirement for informed consent was waived (IRB no. 2026-02-050). Study candidates were 56 recurrent or metastatic esophageal squamous cell carcinoma patients treated with the combination therapy of anti-PD-1 inhibitors (pembrolizumab or nivolumab) plus FP chemotherapy, preceded by pretherapeutic FDG PET/CT at our institution between March 2022 and June 2025. Among the candidates, one patient without properly documented PD-L1 immunohistochemistry results and one patient who discontinued treatment upon confirmation of PD-L1 negative status were excluded. Eleven patients were excluded because they did not receive a minimum of three doses, which corresponds to the interval for the first outpatient follow-up at our institution. The reasons for non-completion of three cycles included regimen switch from pembrolizumab to nivolumab after the first cycle (n = 5), transfer to another institution with loss to follow-up (n = 3), patient refusal (n = 1), ST-elevation myocardial infarction (n = 1), and rapid progression with multi-organ failure leading to death (n = 1).

Thus, 43 patients were included in the final analyses. Among them, 19 patients with available distinct initial staging PET/CT and 15 patients with post-treatment follow-up PET/CT (these two groups are not mutually exclusive) were included in further analyses to assess the variations in PET parameters relative to the pre-treatment baseline. All clinical information and laboratory results were acquired from electronic medical records, which included sex, age, date of diagnosis, type of preceding treatments (surgery with or without neoadjuvant therapy, definitive concurrent chemoradiotherapy [CCRT]), name of anti-PD-1 agents, date of progression, date of death, date of last follow-up, and immunohistologic result of PD-L1, based on combined positive score (CPS) of immune-related and tumor cells in the specimen. PD-L1 CPS was calculated as the number of PD-L1-positive cells (tumor cells, lymphocytes, and macrophages) divided by the total number of viable tumor cells, multiplied by 100; values may exceed 100 when PD-L1-positive immune cells are abundant relative to the tumor cell population.

The events for survival analysis were defined as recurrence or metastasis and death from any cause. The OS and PFS durations were defined as the intervals from the date of treatment initiation to the date of death, disease progression, or last follow-up. An observation was considered right-censored and analyzed accordingly if the participant had no event at the end of the follow-up period or was lost to follow-up.

FDG PET/CT acquisition

Prior to the scan, patients were required to fast for a minimum of 6 h, ensuring a blood glucose level of < 200 mg/dL at the time of injection. Image acquisition commenced 60 min after the administration of FDG (5 MBq/kg or 13.5 µCi/kg) using one of the following scanners: Discovery LS (GE Healthcare, Chicago, IL, US), Discovery STe (GE Healthcare), or Discovery MI DR (GE Healthcare). For attenuation correction, continuous helical CT scans were acquired using an 8-slice (140 keV, 40–120 mA; Discovery LS) or a 16-slice CT system (140 keV, 30–170 mA for Discovery STE; 120 keV, 30–100 mA for Discovery MI DR). PET emission scans covered the range from head to thigh, with a duration of 4 min per frame in 2D mode (Discovery LS), 2.5 min in 3D mode (Discovery STE), and 2 min in 3D mode (Discovery MI DR). Images were reconstructed applying the ordered-subsets expectation maximization algorithm with CT-based attenuation correction. The specific reconstruction parameters were: 28 subsets and 2 iterations (matrix 128 × 128, voxel size 4.3 × 4.3 × 3.9 mm) for Discovery LS; 20 subsets and 2 iterations (matrix 128 × 128, voxel size 3.9 × 3.9 × 3.3 mm) for Discovery STE; and 18 subsets and 4 iterations (matrix 192 × 192, voxel size 3.9 × 3.9 × 3.3 mm) for Discovery MI DR. The standardized uptake value (SUV) was determined by normalizing the measurements to the injected dose and patient body weight.

FDG PET/CT image analysis

At least two experienced nuclear medicine physicians reviewed each scan in consensus, with reference to baseline or prior PET/CT examinations where available. Disagreements were resolved through discussion until consensus was reached; a formal interobserver agreement analysis was not performed. Lesions were classified as malignant based on visual assessment with semiquantitative SUVmax evaluation. For each scan, SUVmax, MTV, SUVmean, and TLG (calculated as MTV × SUVmean) were measured by aggregating all malignant lesions, including the primary tumor, regional and distant lymph nodes, and distant metastases. The boundaries of MTV were defined using a single isocontour threshold of 40% of the highest SUVmax among all malignant lesions in that scan, rather than 40% of each individual lesion’s SUVmax. Lesions with uptake below this threshold but visually suspicious for malignancy were included when uptake exceeded background, and morphologic correlation supported malignancy. Regions of expected physiologic uptake (e.g., esophagus, heart, liver, brown fat, bowel) and clearly inflammatory uptake were manually excluded.

Statistical methods

First, the relationship between quantitative PET parameters and PD-L1 CPS was evaluated using Spearman’s rank correlation analysis, given the non-normal distribution of the data.

For survival analysis, continuous variables such as age, PD-L1 CPS, PET parameters (SUVmax, SUVmean, MTV, and TLG), and change of PET parameters were categorized into low and high groups. The optimal cutoff values were determined by identifying the threshold that maximized the log-rank statistic for OS. To ensure statistical robustness and avoid skewed distributions, a constraint was applied requiring the minority group to constitute at least 20% of the total cohort.

The prognostic value of clinical and imaging variables—including age, sex, and pre-therapeutic PET parameters—was evaluated using Kaplan-Meier analysis with log-rank tests and univariate Cox proportional hazards models. Hazard ratios (HRs) were estimated with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Variables demonstrating statistical significance (p < 0.05) in the univariate analysis were subsequently included in the multivariate Cox proportional hazards model.

Multivariate Cox proportional hazards models were performed with each PET parameter individually (in both dichotomized and continuous forms), adjusted for clinical variables. Variance inflation factors (VIF) were calculated to assess collinearity among variables. Subgroup analyses were also performed within the de novo and recurrent disease cohorts to verify the consistency of the prognostic factors. Additionally, time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to assess the predictive accuracy of the PET parameters, with the area under the curve (AUC) calculated at 180, 270, and 365 days.

For the subgroup of patients who underwent a second post-treatment PET/CT, the changes in PET parameters (SUVmax, SUVmean, MTV, and TLG) relative to the pre-therapeutic PET were calculated. The prognostic significance of these variations was subsequently evaluated using Kaplan-Meier survival curves and log-rank tests. In the subgroup of patients with available initial staging scans, we first evaluated the prognostic significance of initial PET parameters (SUVmax and SUVmean). Furthermore, by integrating initial and pre-treatment PET data, patients were categorized into three distinct groups based on the longitudinal changes in metabolic activity: high-to-low group, low-to-high group, and a stable group (consisting of persistent Low-Low or High-High patterns).

All statistical analyses were performed in Python. Survival analyses (Cox proportional hazards regression, Kaplan-Meier estimation, and log-rank tests) were performed using the lifelines package [16], time-dependent ROC analysis using scikit-survival [17], and correlation analyses using scipy.stats [18]. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient demographics

A total of 43 patients (34 [79.1%] male, mean age of 65.0 ± 8.5 at diagnosis) with metastatic or recurrent esophageal cancer were included in the study. Among the cohort, 13 patients (30.2%) presented with de novo metastatic esophageal cancer, defined as treatment-naïve before anti-PD-1 therapy. The remaining 30 patients had received prior treatments for recurrent disease; of these, 11 had undergone surgery (with or without neoadjuvant therapy), and 19 had received definitive radiotherapy or CCRT. Regarding the immunotherapy agent, 30 patients were treated with pembrolizumab and 13 with nivolumab. The majority received treatment in the first-line (55.8%) or second-line (39.5%) setting, while one patient each was treated in the third- and fifth-line settings, respectively. The median OS and PFS were 28.5 months and 4.8 months, respectively. Among the 15 patients with follow-up PET/CT, the interval from treatment initiation to follow-up scan ranged from 29 to 376 days (median 131 days). For the 19 patients with available initial staging PET/CT, the interval between initial and pre-therapeutic PET ranged from 128 to 2184 days (median 276 days). PD-L1 CPS score and correlation with PET parameters

The distribution of PD-L1 CPS is presented in Supplementary Fig. 1, ranging from 3 to 110 points. No significant correlations were observed between PD-L1 CPS and any of the pre-therapeutic PET parameters (Supplementary Fig. 2).

Pretherapeutic FDG PET and survival

In the univariate survival analysis of the main subject group, OS showed a significant association with pre-therapeutic SUVmax in both dichotomized and continuous forms, and with SUVmean, MTV, and TLG in dichotomized form based on the likelihood ratio test (LRT) p-values (Table 2). The corresponding Kaplan-Meier survival curves are presented in Fig. 2. These findings were supported by AUC analysis of the time-dependent ROC curve, which indicated sustained predictive performance of the parameters for OS, ranging from 0.61 to 0.83 throughout the observation period (180–365 days) (Supplementary Fig. 3). Conversely, no pre-therapeutic PET parameters were found to be significantly associated with PFS.

Multivariate Cox regression adjusting for age, sex, presentation, and line of therapy was performed with each PET parameter individually (in both dichotomized and continuous forms), as MTV and TLG showed high variance inflation factors (> 10) and were therefore not included together in a single model (Supplementary Table 1). After adjustment, SUVmax remained associated with OS in both dichotomized (adj HR 5.07, p = 0.03) and continuous forms (HR/SD 2.55, p = 0.009), and SUVmean reached significance in continuous form (HR/SD 2.65, p = 0.02). TLG was significant in dichotomized form (adj HR 13.50, p = 0.03) but not in continuous form. None of the PET parameters showed a significant association with PFS, consistent with the univariate findings (Supplementary Table 2).

Subgroup analysis was performed to evaluate the prognostic value of pre-therapeutic PET parameters in patients with recurrent disease versus those with de novo stage IV disease. In the recurrent subgroup (n = 30), all four pre-therapeutic PET parameters demonstrated significant stratification for OS, with lower values associated with superior survival outcomes (p < 0.05; Supplementary Fig. 4). Regarding PFS, significant prognostic associations were observed specifically for SUVmean and TLG (p < 0.05). In contrast, no significant prognostic association was observed for either OS or PFS in the de novo stage IV subgroup (n = 13, Supplementary Fig. 5).

Subgroup analysis with follow-up PET or initial PET

Baseline characteristics did not significantly differ between patients with and without available follow-up PET (Supplementary Table 3). In the survival analysis of the subgroup with follow-up PET scans, changes in volumetric parameters (MTV and TLG, in either dichotomized or continuous form) were significantly associated with PFS, as shown in Table 3; Fig. 3. For changes of SUVmax and SUVmean, Cox regression for OS failed to converge due to complete separation, with all OS events occurring in a single group; these estimates are therefore not reported in Table 3. In contrast, no significant associations were observed between any PET parameters and survival outcomes in the initial PET subgroup (all p > 0.05). In a trajectory analysis stratifying patients by high/low status at both initial and pre-therapeutic PET scans, patients with a high-to-low (improved) pattern demonstrated the most favorable PFS, while persistent High-High (high burden), persistent Low-Low (low burden), and low-to-high (worsened) patterns showed shorter PFS (log-rank p = 0.03 for SUVmax; p < 0.001 for SUVmean), as shown in Fig. 4.

Discussion

The present study investigated the utility of FDG PET/CT for the early prediction of survival outcomes in patients with advanced esophageal cancer treated with anti-PD-1 inhibitors and chemotherapy. Predicting therapeutic efficacy in this setting is important for stratifying patients who may benefit from the standard regimen versus those who might require alternative strategies. Our results indicated that pre-therapeutic FDG PET/CT parameters were associated with OS, likely reflecting the initial disease burden. Additionally, early changes in volumetric parameters, specifically Δ% MTV and Δ% TLG, appeared to be significant predictors of PFS, suggesting the prognostic value of metabolic response assessment in monitoring treatment efficacy.

In line with previous studies in various malignancies, our findings suggest that pretherapeutic metabolic and volumetric parameters, including SUVmax, SUVmean, MTV, and TLG, may serve as indicators of tumor aggressiveness and overall disease burden [19–21]. In our analysis, these pre-therapeutic parameters showed a correlation with OS, where higher values were generally associated with a poorer prognosis. This observation aligns with the concept that tumors with higher glucose metabolism and larger tumor burdens tend to be biologically active, potentially leading to shorter survival times. However, while these pretherapeutic markers were associated with overall survival, they did not consistently predict progression-free survival in the context of immunochemotherapy. This observation implies that while baseline pretherapeutic PET parameters may reflect the natural course of the advanced disease, they might not fully capture the interaction between the tumor and the immune system that influences the duration of response to anti-PD-1 therapy.

Multivariate analyses adjusting for clinical covariates showed that SUVmax retained its association with OS in both dichotomized and continuous forms, while volumetric parameters showed form-dependent significance — TLG was significant only in dichotomized form, whereas SUVmean reached significance only in continuous form. This pattern may reflect the skewed distribution of volumetric parameters, in which prognostic information could be more readily captured by a threshold-based dichotomization or by data transformation than by a linear per-unit relationship — an approach also adopted in prior PET studies using log- or square-root-transformed volumetric parameters. The wide confidence intervals reflect the limited number of OS events and warrant caution in interpretation. Consistent with the univariate findings, none of the pre-therapeutic PET parameters showed an association with PFS in multivariate models.

In the subgroup of patients who underwent follow-up FDG PET/CT, the results highlight the potential value of monitoring early metabolic changes. Unlike baseline pretherapeutic parameters, the percentage changes in volumetric parameters (Δ% MTV and Δ% TLG) were found to be associated with progression-free survival [22]. This suggests that the reduction in metabolic tumor burden may reflect the immediate therapeutic response to the PD-1 inhibitor and chemotherapy combination. The observation that these dynamic markers predicted PFS but did not predict OS in this subgroup might be attributed to the clinical course following progression, including subsequent therapies, which can influence final survival outcomes. Nevertheless, the ability of early Δ% MTV and Δ% TLG to stratify PFS suggests that these markers could be considered as potential tools for early response assessment.

Furthermore, our analysis incorporating initial staging PET/CT provided a unique perspective on the metabolic trajectory of the disease before immunotherapy. We observed that patients exhibiting a “high-to-low” pattern—characterized by high metabolic uptake at initial diagnosis but relatively lower uptake at the pre-therapeutic timepoint—demonstrated significantly superior PFS compared to those with a “low-to-high” pattern. These findings suggest that the trajectory of metabolic evolution may be a relevant factor in predicting therapeutic outcomes. A transition from low to high metabolic activity may indicate the acquisition of a more aggressive phenotype or accelerating disease kinetics prior to anti-PD-1 therapy. Conversely, a decrease in metabolic intensity over time, potentially attributable to prior treatments or distinct biological behavior, was associated with a more favorable response to subsequent immunochemotherapy. This suggests that considering the longitudinal metabolic trajectory of the tumor, rather than relying solely on a single time-point assessment, could offer deeper insights into patient prognosis [23–25].

Additionally, we observed no significant correlation between PD-L1 expression and PET parameters, suggesting that metabolic burden and immune status may provide independent prognostic information [26]. Our analysis also indicated that the prognostic association of PET parameters appeared more distinct in patients with recurrent disease compared to those with de novo metastasis. However, considering the limited sample size of the de novo subgroup, these observations regarding patient subgroups should be interpreted with caution.

This study has several limitations. As an exploratory retrospective analysis from a single institution with a modest cohort, the statistical power for subgroup analyses was constrained, and the dichotomized PET cutoffs derived by log-rank maximization should be regarded as exploratory rather than validated thresholds. The exclusion of patients who did not complete three treatment cycles may introduce some selection bias. Response assessment was based on clinical and routine imaging interpretation rather than standardized criteria such as RECIST 1.1 or iRECIST, which may have led to misclassification of pseudoprogression as true progression. The follow-up PET timing and the interval between initial staging and pre-therapeutic PET were non-standardized, reflecting clinical rather than protocol-driven imaging schedules. Imaging was acquired on three different PET/CT scanners without formal harmonization, potentially introducing variability, particularly in volumetric measurements. Findings in a histology-restricted cohort (all squamous cell carcinoma) may not generalize to esophageal adenocarcinoma. External validation in larger, prospective, multicenter studies with standardized imaging protocols is warranted.

Conclusions

FDG PET/CT showed prognostic associations in patients with recurrent or metastatic esophageal cancer treated with chemo-immunotherapy. Baseline metabolic burden predicts overall survival, whereas early metabolic changes and longitudinal trajectories reflect progression-free survival, facilitating refined risk stratification and personalized management.

Effectiveness, safety, and pharmacoeconomic profile of Sophora flavescens film in the treatment of postpartum vaginal dysbiosis: a non-randomized controlled trial
Authors: Jiaojiao Zhao, Xiaorang Li, Songsong Tan, Ye Pan, Zhihao Yang, Shuimei Sun, Junjie Lan, Rui Zhang, Huaye Zhao, Linfang Hu, Jiaxue Wang, Wenyi Zheng, Rui He, Chang Xu, Xiaoling Zeng, Jiaxing Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionSophora flavescens film (SFF), a topical traditional Chinese medicine that primarily contains matrine, has been approved in China since 2009 for chronic gynecological inflammation. However, evidence regarding its clinical value in the management of postpartum vaginal dysbiosis (PVD) remains limited.AimThis study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness, safety, and pharmacoeconomic profile of SFF as an adjunct to conventional therapy in women with PVD.MethodThis single-center, open-label, non-randomized controlled trial was conducted among postpartum women (6–8 weeks after delivery) diagnosed with PVD. Participants were self-selected into either the SFF group (SFF plus conventional therapy) or the control group (conventional therapy alone). The primary outcome was the overall therapeutic effectiveness based on composite vaginal ecosystem indicators. Secondary outcomes included Vaginal Health Index Score (VHIS), Vaginal Inflammation Score (VIS), EQ-5D-5L health utility, EQ-VAS, and adverse events (AEs). Pharmacoeconomic evaluation was performed using a decision tree model to estimate the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER). Statistical analyses incorporated logistic regression and analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) with directed acyclic graph (DAG)-based confounder adjustments.ResultsIn total, 126 participants (63 per group) completed the study. The SFF group demonstrated a significantly higher overall effectiveness than the control group (adjusted odds ratio = 3.68, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.49–9.09, P = 0.005). Significant post-treatment improvements were observed in VHIS (mean difference [MD] = 2.420, 95% CI: 1.808–3.032, P < 0.001, η2 = 0.332), VIS (MD = – 0.425, 95% CI: – 0.642 to – 0.208, P < 0.001, η2 = 0.109), EQ-5D-5L (MD = 0.029, 95% CI: 0.016–0.041, P < 0.001, η2 = 0.150), and EQ-VAS (MD = 6.329, 95% CI: 2.978–9.680, P < 0.001, η2 = 0.102). No serious AEs occurred, and the mild local irritation resolved spontaneously. Pharmacoeconomic analysis showed that SFF achieved a 1% increase in treatment effectiveness at an incremental cost of ¥244.23 compared with conventional therapy.ConclusionWhen combined with conventional care, SFF significantly improved clinical, symptomatic, and quality-of-life outcomes in women with PVD, with favorable tolerability and short-term cost-effectiveness. These results support its potential clinical utility. However, confirmation through multicenter, double-blind, randomized controlled trials is warranted.Supplementary informationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s11096-026-02092-5.

Introduction

The vaginal microecosystem is a dynamic and finely balanced environment maintained through the interplay between vaginal microbiota, endocrine regulation, anatomical integrity, and local mucosal immunity [1, 2]. A healthy vaginal microbiome is typically dominated by Lactobacillus species, which sustain an acidic pH (3.8–4.5), produce antimicrobial compounds, such as hydrogen peroxide, and exhibit minimal leukocyte esterase activity [3, 4]. Deviations from these parameters define vaginal dysbiosis, a condition associated with an increased risk of bacterial vaginosis [5], vulvovaginal candidiasis [6], trichomoniasis [7], human papillomavirus infection [8], and heightened susceptibility to the human immunodeficiency virus and genital herpes [9].

Postpartum women are particularly vulnerable to postpartum vaginal dysbiosis (PVD) during the 6-to 8-week period following delivery. This susceptibility results from estrogen depletion, reduced glycogen availability in the vaginal epithelium [10], and disruption of the acidic environment due to exposure to amniotic fluid and lochia. These physiological changes lead to Lactobacillus depletion [11, 12], pathogen overgrowth, and elevated proinflammatory cytokine levels, thereby impeding microbial restoration and aggravating local inflammation [13].

Despite its clinical significance, standardized treatment strategies for PVD remain elusive. The 2021 Chinese bacterial vaginosis guidelines recommend probiotics [14, 15] for their ability to modulate vaginal microbiota, suppress pathogenic bacteria, and enhance immune responses [16, 17]. However, probiotics often demonstrate poor long-term vaginal colonization and transient efficacy [18]. Empirical use of antibiotics to prevent secondary infections is also common [19, 20], yet supporting evidence for their prophylactic role in PVD is insufficient. Moreover, antibiotic overuse may further disrupt the vaginal microbiome, promote antimicrobial resistance, and reduce Lactobacillus viability [21].

Sophora flavescens alkaloids (SFA) are bioactive constituents of Sophora flavescens Ait. listed in the Chinese Pharmacopeia (2020 edition) [22], possess broad-spectrum antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, antiviral, and immunomodulatory properties [23]. In vitro studies have demonstrated that SFA selectively inhibits Gardnerella vaginalis, suggesting its potential in preventing bacterial vaginosis recurrence [24]. Sophora flavescens film (SFF), a topical SFA formulation approved for chronic gynecological inflammation in China in 2009, has shown promising preclinical results. In animal models, SFF reduced the bacterial burden, restored vaginal pH, enhanced epithelial repair, and improved local immune responses [24]. However, clinical data supporting its use in PVD are lacking.

Aim

This study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness, safety, and pharmacoeconomic value of Sophora flavescens film in the treatment of postpartum vaginal dysbiosis through a prospective, single-center, non-randomized controlled trial.

Method

Trial design

This single-center, open-label, non-randomized controlled trial was conducted in accordance with the Transparent Reporting of Evaluations with Nonrandomized Designs (TREND) guidelines [25]. The study protocol was registered in the China Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR 2400080732). All participants provided written informed consent after receiving a full explanation of the study objectives, procedures, and potential risks. When participants were unable to consent (e.g., due to severe cognitive impairment, significant language barrier without an available interpreter, or acute psychiatric distress that impaired decision-making capacity), legally authorized representatives provided proxy consent.

Participants

The study was conducted at Guizhou Provincial People's Hospital, Guizhou, China, from October 1, 2023, to December 30, 2024. Eligible participants were postpartum women aged 20–50 years, 6–8 weeks after delivery, with complete cessation of lochia and a confirmed diagnosis of PVD. Diagnosis required physician assessment based on abnormal vaginal discharge, elevated pH (4.5), positive amine odor test, clue cell presence, and laboratory evidence of reduced Lactobacillus dominance with increased pathogenic microorganisms.

The exclusion criteria included unstable vital signs, severe cognitive or psychiatric disorders, and known hypersensitivity to SFF or its components. Participants were withdrawn in the event of a serious adverse event (SAE) required discontinuation or voluntary withdrawal. After comprehensive counseling on both therapeutic options, participants chose their treatment group after counseling on both options: the intervention group (SFF plus conventional supportive therapy) or the control group (conventional therapy alone).

Intervention measures

The control group received conventional supportive therapy alone, with follow-up assessments on days 10 and 14 post-enrolment. The intervention group received one SFF containing 100 mg oxymatrine intravaginally once daily for seven consecutive days, in addition to conventional therapy. Follow-up visits for the intervention group occurred on days 1, 3, and 7 during treatment, and again on days 10 and 14 post-initiation.

Protocol adherence and safety were reinforced through scheduled telephone or WeChat contacts, which served to (1) verify medication compliance and detect treatment modifications; (2) record concomitant medications via patient-maintained logs; (3) actively monitor adverse effects such as vaginal irritation, pruritus, or systemic symptoms; and (4) reinforce behavioral restrictions, including abstinence and avoidance of vaginal douching during treatment.

Primary outcome

The primary outcome was overall therapeutic effectiveness, defined by composite improvement in vaginal ecosystem parameters: vaginal discharge characteristics, leukocyte count, vaginal cleanliness, pH, Lactobacillus abundance, hydrogen peroxide production, and leukocyte esterase activity (criteria in Supplemental Table 1) [26]. Treatment was deemed effective when both symptomatic relief (e.g., reduced pruritus or abnormal discharge) and microbiological improvement (increased Lactobacillus levels with no deterioration in other indices) were achieved.

Secondary outcomes

Secondary outcomes included: (1) Vaginal Health Index Score (VHIS), an objective measure of vaginal health, with higher scores indicating better condition [27] (Supplemental Table 2); (2) Vaginal Inflammation Score (VIS), a patient-reported measure of symptom severity, where higher scores reflect greater inflammation [27] (Supplemental Table 3); (3) health-related quality of life (HRQoL), assessed using the five-level EuroQol five-dimensional questionnaire (EQ-5D-5L) and EuroQol visual analog scale (EQ-VAS). The EQ-5D-5L evaluates mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression, each rated at five severity levels (Supplemental Table 4). Health utility values were derived using the Chinese EQ-5D-5L value set, ranging from – 0.391 to 1 [28]. EQ-VAS scores ranged from 0 (“worst imaginable health”) to 100 (“best imaginable health”). Patients self-rated their current health status on this scale during follow-up visits.

Safety was monitored throughout the study. All treatment-emergent adverse events (AEs) were documented and assessed using the WHO–Uppsala Monitoring Center causality system, which classifies relationships as certain, probable, possible, unlikely, or unclassifiable based on temporal association, biological plausibility, and alternative causes [29].

Pharmacoeconomic analysis

The economic evaluation included both direct medical costs (study medications, diagnostic tests, and consultation fees) and indirect costs (transportation). Cost and effectiveness data were integrated into an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) framework to estimate the additional cost per successful treatment.

Statistical analysis

The sample size was estimated based on published data [30], assuming a 77.5% spontaneous improvement rate in untreated PVD and 95% efficacy rate with SFF-enhanced therapy. Using two-sided α = 0.05, power = 0.80, and 1:1 group allocation, 57 participants per group were required. Accounting for 10% attrition, 126 participants (63 per group) were enrolled in the study. The trial employed a non-randomized, preference-based allocation design where participants were not blinded, but outcome assessors were blinded to group allocation during data collection and analysis.

The data were analyzed using the R software (version 4.2.2). Categorical variables are expressed as frequencies and percentages, and continuous variables are expressed as medians with interquartile ranges or means ± standard deviation, depending on the distribution. Between-group comparisons used the χ2 or Fisher's exact test for categorical variables, and Student's t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sum test for continuous or ordinal data, as appropriate. Multiple imputations were used for any missing data.

Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were employed for the primary outcome analysis. Confounders identified via directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) were included in the adjusted models, and the results are presented as adjusted odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

Secondary outcomes were analyzed using analysis of covariance (ANCOVA), adjusting for baseline measurements and DAG-derived covariates. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Pharmacoeconomic evaluation was conducted from the patient's perspective, integrating both direct and indirect costs to derive the ICER, expressed as the additional cost per additional successfully treated case.

Ethics approval

The protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Guizhou Provincial People’s Hospital (approval No. 2023-084).

The SFF group also showed improvement in Vaginal Inflammation Scores (VIS), decreasing from 3.56 ± 0.91 to 3.19 ± 0.43, while the control group worsened from 3.44 ± 0.62 to 3.59 ± 0.80. ANCOVA indicated a significantly lower post-treatment VIS in the SFF group (MD = − 0.425, 95% CI: − 0.642 to − 0.208; P < 0.001; η2 = 0.109). After multivariate adjustment, the difference remained significant (adjusted MD = − 0.416, 95% CI: − 0.646 to − 0.186; P < 0.001; η2 = 0.102), accounting for 10.2% of the variance (Table 2; Supplemental Table 5b).

Baseline EQ-5D-5L health utility scores were similar between groups (SFF: 0.96 ± 0.06; control: 0.97 ± 0.04). Post-intervention, the SFF group demonstrated higher mean utility (0.99 ± 0.02 vs. 0.97 ± 0.05), with MD = 0.029 (95% CI: 0.016–0.041; P < 0.001; η2 = 0.150). This difference remained after adjustment (adjusted MD = 0.029, 95% CI = 0.017–0.041; P < 0.001; η2 = 0.159), explaining 15.9% of the variance (Table 2; Supplemental Table 5c).

Similarly, baseline EQ-VAS scores were comparable (SFF: 75.75 ± 9.05; control: 76.11 ± 6.25). After treatment, the SFF group achieved significantly higher EQ-VAS values (84.13 ± 10.11 vs. 77.94 ± 9.70), with MD = 6.329 (95% CI: 2.978–9.680; P < 0.001; η2 = 0.102). Multivariable adjustment confirmed improvement (adjusted MD = 6.924, 95% CI: 3.634–10.214; P < 0.001; η2 = 0.130), explaining 13.0% of the variance (Table 2; Supplemental Table 5d).

Safety

AEs were comparable between groups, occurring in 4.76% (3/63) of participants in both the SFF and control arms. The SFF group showed a slightly lower incidence of vaginitis (3.17% [2/63]) than the control group (4.76% [3/63]). In the control group, AEs comprised two cases of mycotic vaginitis and one case of bacterial vaginitis. In the SFF group, one case each was reported. All the affected participants fully recovered after appropriate treatment.

One participant who received SFF reported transient genitourinary irritation (burning, urinary frequency, urgency, and dysuria) on day 4, which resolved promptly after discontinuation. No serious adverse events occurred in either group during the study.

Pharmacoeconomic evaluation

A cost-effectiveness analysis, based on a decision-tree model (Fig. 3), compared SFF-enhanced therapy with conventional treatment alone. The SFF regimen yielded higher mean costs (¥163.39 vs. ¥105.24), but greater clinical effectiveness (46.03% vs. 22.22%) (Table 3). Discussion

Principal findings

This non-randomized controlled trial demonstrated that Sophora flavescens film (SFF) combined with conventional therapy provided superior clinical outcomes compared to conventional therapy alone in women with postpartum vaginal dysbiosis, while maintaining a comparable safety profile. The addition of SFF improved multiple endpoints, including a 2.1-fold higher treatment effectiveness rate (46.03% vs. 22.22%), increased vaginal health scores (adjusted MD: 2.437), reduced inflammation scores (adjusted MD: − 0.416), higher EQ-5D-5L health utility values (adjusted MD: 0.029), and improved EQ-VAS scores (adjusted MD: 6.924).

Pharmacoeconomic evaluation indicated that SFF was associated with an incremental cost of ¥244.23 per 1% gain in therapeutic effectiveness, suggesting a potentially favorable cost-effectiveness profile for short-term postpartum management.

Mechanistic interpretation

The findings of this study align with prior evidence describing postpartum microecological alterations [31], in which reductions in Lactobacillus dominance, epithelial integrity, and acidic pH coexist with elevated oxidative stress. The observed therapeutic benefits of SFF may arise from its dual mechanisms of action. First, SFF exhibits selective antimicrobial effects through the inhibition of biosynthetic and oxidative metabolic pathways in pathogenic bacteria [24]. Second, it appears to preserve or restore beneficial Lactobacillus species, which are essential for maintaining vaginal homeostasis via lactic acid production, hydrogen peroxide secretion, and bacteriocin-mediated pathogen inhibition [32].

Preclinical studies further support these mechanisms. Sophora flavescens alkaloids (SFA) have been shown to enhance Lactobacillus colonization [33], suppress inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin (IL)-1β, IL-6, and tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α, and reduce oxidative damage [34]. These combined effects facilitate epithelial repair and promote microecological restoration [35], which may explain the observed improvements in vaginal health indices and symptom relief in the present study.

Safety profile

Safety analysis revealed a comparable incidence of adverse events (4.76%) between the treatment groups, with only mild reversible events observed. A slight reduction in vaginitis was noted in the SFF group (3.17% vs. 4.76%), and a single report of transient genitourinary irritation resolved upon discontinuation. No serious adverse events occurred during the trial, suggesting that intravaginal SFF is well-tolerated in postpartum women.

However, the current study was not powered to detect rare or delayed adverse effects. Larger trials with longer follow-up periods are required to establish a more comprehensive safety profile.

Pharmacoeconomic perspective

Given the acute nature of PVD and the limited treatment duration, a cost-effectiveness rather than a cost-utility framework was deemed appropriate. The decision tree model demonstrated that SFF achieved greater clinical benefits at a modest incremental cost. Although an ICER of ¥244.23 per 1% increase in treatment efficacy provides initial economic insight, future research should include sensitivity analyses and willingness-to-pay thresholds to enable comparisons with conventional economic benchmarks, such as gross domestic product per capita.

Methodological considerations

This study applied several measures to enhance internal validity, including DAG-guided confounder selection and ANCOVA adjustment to address baseline imbalances [36–38]. These methods reduce overadjustment and improve causal interpretability in non-randomized designs. Nonetheless, several limitations of this study must be acknowledged. Non-randomized design: Patient self-selection may reflect factors such as health-seeking behavior or prior acceptance of Traditional Chinese Medicine, which could influence group comparability. While DAG-guided adjustment controlled for known confounders, unmeasured confounding (e.g., baseline symptom severity) cannot be fully ruled out. Sample size: The study was powered for efficacy but not for safety or subgroup analyses, limiting the precision of the adverse event estimates. Single-center setting: The single-center design limits the generalizability of the findings to more diverse populations or different clinical settings. Open-label design: Potential performance and detection bias cannot be excluded, particularly for subjective secondary outcomes, such as the EQ-VAS. Furthermore, although statistically significant differences were observed for several patient-reported outcomes (e.g., EQ-5D-5L), the absolute magnitude of some changes was small. The lack of established minimal clinically important difference thresholds for these instruments in the specific population of postpartum women with vaginal dysbiosis limits the interpretability of the clinical importance of these improvements. Finally, the economic analysis included only direct transportation costs; broader indirect costs, such as productivity losses, were not captured as they were difficult to quantify within the present study design.

Implications and future directions

Taken together, these findings suggest that SFF may serve as an effective and safe adjunctive therapy for PVD, offering both symptomatic relief and favorable pharmacoeconomic outcomes. The observed benefits are biologically plausible and consistent with the preclinical mechanisms of antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory actions. However, confirmation through rigorously designed multicenter, double-blind, randomized controlled trials is essential to validate the efficacy, delineate long-term safety, and establish robust cost-effectiveness thresholds.

Conclusion

This study provides preliminary clinical evidence that Sophora flavescens film, when used alongside conventional therapy, effectively improves vaginal microecological balance, alleviates inflammation, and enhances the quality of life in women with postpartum vaginal dysbiosis. The treatment demonstrated a favorable safety profile and promising short-term cost-effectiveness. Although the non-randomized, single-center design limits causal inference, consistent improvements across multiple clinical and economic endpoints support further investigation. Large-scale, multicenter, randomized controlled trials are warranted to confirm these findings and to establish definitive evidence for the clinical and economic value of Sophora flavescens film in postpartum care.

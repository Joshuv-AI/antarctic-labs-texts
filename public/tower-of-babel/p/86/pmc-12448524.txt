Primary angle-closure eyes with ocular hypertension show preperimetric ganglion cell complex abnormalities
Authors: Shah Zalak, Sihota Ramanjit, Sen Ray Swarnali, Choudhary Samiksha, Dhillon Jasleen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Purpose:The present study aimed to investigate ganglion cell complex (GCC) and peripapillary retinal nerve fiber layer (pRNFL) parameters in subjects with primary angle closure (PAC) without raised intraocular pressure (IOP), PAC eyes with raised IOP, and those with early primary angle-closure glaucoma (PACG), to ascertain parameters that could identify any early glaucomatous damage.Methods:This was an observational, cross-sectional study of 75 patients with PAC, PAC with ocular hypertension (OHT), and early PACG. GCC analysis was performed by spectral domain-optical coherence tomography (SD-OCT) using RTVue-100 software, and parameters were compared between these groups.Results:In eyes with PAC, PAC with OHT and early PACG, the average GCC thickness was 92.49 ± 7.52, 87.69 ± 5.24, and 79.64 ± 8.45 µm; inferior GCC thickness was 92.8 ± 7.84, 88.15 ± 6.13, and 79.59 ± 8.96 µm; and superior GCC thickness was 92.08 ± 7.55, 87.23 ± 5.19, and 79.67 ± 8.82 µm, respectively. There was a significant difference in all parameters between PAC vs PAC OHT eyes (P = 0.001), as also between PAC OHT vs early PACG eyes (P < 0.001). The percentage GCC loss in eyes from PAC to PAC with OHT and from PAC with OHT to early PACG in average GCC was 7.5 ± 3.95% (P = 0.001) and 9.31 ± 8.54% (P < 0.001). pRNFL showed a significant difference in all the parameters between PAC OHT vs early PACG (P < 0.001); however, only the superior pRNFL was significantly different between PAC vs PAC OHT (P = 0.01).Conclusion:PAC with OHT showed significant, preperimetric GCC changes that could identify early progression from PAC to PACG and prompt appropriate therapy to prevent morbidity.

Population-based studies show that primary angle-closure glaucoma (PACG) carries a three-fold increased risk of severe, bilateral visual impairment, as compared to primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG).[123] The natural history of POAG has been well studied, and it is known that ocular hypertension leads to optic nerve damage in a significant percentage of eyes over time.[4]

According to the classification for surveys developed by the International Society of Geographic and Epidemiologic Ophthalmology, primary angle-closure disease (PACD) is classified into three categories[5]: (1) Primary angle-closure suspect (PACS), (2) Primary angle closure (PAC), and (3) Primary angle-closure glaucoma (PACG).

There is a need to clarify how subjects with PAC progress, whether by peripheral anterior synechiae or iridotrabecular contact, leading to episodic or chronically raised IOP, and when and where retinal ganglion cells (RGCs) and peripapillary retinal nerve fiber layer (pRNFL) are damaged during the progression to PACG. Identifying risk factors and early RGC/pRNFL changes would allow better protocols of therapy that prevent irreversible glaucomatous optic neuropathy.

Thomas et al.[6] reported that 28.5% of PAC progressed to PACG over five years. Sihota et al.[7] reported that 36.1% of PAC eyes developed ocular hypertension, and of these PAC OHT, 30.76% progressed to PACG with a visual field defect, that is, 11.1% of PAC eyes progressed to PACG. The risk factors identified for this progression were a narrower angle recess, higher baseline mean deviation and pattern standard deviation (PSD), and increased intervisit IOP fluctuations. In a study of ocular hypertension with open and PAC eyes,[8] 19.72% of PAC OHT eyes progressed to PACG over a median of 7 years, especially those with smaller disc areas, need for >2 medications to lower IOP to <18mmHg, cardiovascular problems, and steroid use. It is, therefore, possible that a chronically raised IOP in PAC, as reported in these studies, together with OCT abnormalities, could be used to identify preperimetric changes leading to progression of PAC to PACG, as has been identified in the progression of ocular hypertension to POAG.

This study aimed to compare spectral domain OCT (SD-OCT) parameters in eyes with PAC without chronically raised IOP, PAC with OHT, and early PACG, to see if PAC without raised IOP and PAC with OHT were similar, or if significant ganglion cell complex (GCC) and pRNFL abnormalities could be identified in the presence of PAC OHT, presaging those of early PACG.

Methods

This was an observational, cross-sectional study of consecutive patients aged 50–80 years, identified with (1) PAC (2) PAC with OHT, and (3) early PACG, at least 3 months after iridotomy.

PAC patients had an occludable angle and some peripheral anterior synechiae, with repeated intervisit IOP of <21 mm Hg, after iridotomy and normal perimetry.

PAC with OHT eyes had an occludable angle and peripheral anterior synechiae, with an IOP of >21 mmHg after an iridotomy on at least three visits, without glaucomatous optic neuropathy or visual field defects.

Patients with PACG had additional glaucomatous optic neuropathy and a corresponding, definitive, superior nasal step on perimetry (Early stage by Hodapp Parrish Anderson [HPA] classification[9]).

Uncooperative patients and patients with co-existing ocular pathology that could affect retinal thickness measurement, including diabetic retinopathy (DR), age-related macular degeneration, and significant media opacities, were excluded.

Institutional Ethics Board approval was obtained (Reg. No.: ECR/1363/Inst/DL/2020), and the study adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was received from all participants.

All patients underwent a comprehensive ocular examination, best corrected visual acuity, applanation tonometry, gonioscopy, and optic disc evaluation with a +90D lens. Perimetry was performed using the 24-2 SITA standard program of the Humphrey field analyzer, HFA TM II-I Series (Zeiss, San Leandro, CA, USA). The severity of glaucoma was categorized according to HPA criteria on at least two reproducible fields. OCT analysis was performed by SD-OCT using RTVue-100 software (Optovue, Inc., Fremont, CA). pRNFL values and GCC parameters of total GCC average, superior and inferior GCC, focal loss volume (FLV), and global loss volume (GLV) were analyzed.

Statistical methods

Comparison of variables that were quantitative and not normally distributed in nature was done using the Kruskal–Wallis test, while variables that were quantitative and normally distributed in nature were evaluated using ANOVA. Paired t-test (for normally distributed data) and Wilcoxon signed rank test (for not normally distributed data) were used for comparison within groups. Data analysis was performed on Statistical Package for Social Sciences software, IBM manufacturer, Chicago, USA, ver 25.0.

Results

A total of 126 eyes of 75 patients were included in the study; 52 had PAC; 50 had PAC with OHT; and 24 had early PACG. Their ages ranged from 50–80 years, with 51 (62.7%) women and 24 (37.3%) men.

In eyes with PAC, PAC with OHT and early PACG, the average GCC thickness was 92.49 ± 7.52, 87.69 ± 5.24, and 79.64 ± 8.45 µm; inferior GCC thickness was 92.8 ± 7.84, 88.15 ± 6.13, and 79.59 ± 8.96 µm; and superior GCC thickness was 92.08 ± 7.55, 87.23 ± 5.19, and 79.67 ± 8.82 µm, respectively. There was a significant difference in all parameters between PAC vs PAC OHT eyes (P = 0.001), as also between PAC OHT vs early PACG eyes (P < 0.001) [Table 1].

The asymmetry between superior and inferior GCC thickness in PAC, PAC OHT, and early PACG was 7.21 ± 0.61 µm, 8.67 ± 1.31 µm, and 10.78 ± 1.15 µm, respectively.

In eyes with PAC, PAC with OHT, and early PACG, the average pRNFL thickness was 103.87 ± 10.76, 101.3 ± 10.03, and 84.17 ± 10.31 µm; inferior pRNFL thickness was 101.0 ± 10.89, 99.71 ± 10.07, and 82.12 ± 10.86 µm; and superior pRNFL thickness was 106.63 ± 12.74, 100.99 ± 9.53, and 85.25 ± 10.03 µm, respectively. There was a significant difference in all the parameters between PAC OHT vs early PACG (P < 0.001); however, only superior pRNFL was significantly different between PAC vs PAC OHT (P = 0.01) [Table 3].

The percentage RNFL loss in eyes from PAC to PAC with OHT and from PAC with OHT to early PACG in average pRNFL was 4.93 ± 3.86% (P = 0.06) and 13.53 ± 7.48% (P < 0.001). Superior quadrant to inferior quadrant ratio (SQ/IQ) for pRNFL in eyes with PAC was 1.03 ± 0.1, for PAC with OHT was 1.03 ± 0.08, and for early PACG, it was 1.04 ± 0.09 which was not statistically significant (P = 0.3).

Discussion

Several studies have shown that OCT abnormalities, such as thinning of GCC and pRNFL, can detect POAG progression earlier than perimetry in ocular hypertensives with an open angle.[101112131415] This study aimed to evaluate SD-OCT changes in PACD eyes, which would identify early glaucomatous abnormalities between PAC eyes without chronically elevated IOP after an iridotomy, PAC eyes with chronically elevated IOP after an iridotomy, and early PACG eyes with a superior nasal step.

Lisboa et al.[16] on the RTVue OCT machine reported a GCC value of 83.6 ± 7.2 in preperimetric POAG eyes; POAG suspects that showed a change over time but no visual field defect (VFD), as against 91.2 ± 6.6 in stable POAG suspects, mirroring our values in PAC with OHT and PAC without raised IOP in our study. Jiang et al.[17] found a ganglion cell-inner plexiform layer (GCIPL) average of 78.79 ± 6.49 µm in early PACG eyes. These values are similar to those of early PACG eyes in this study.

GLV and FLV in PAC, PAC with OHT, and early PACG were significantly different across the three groups in our study. Lisboa et al.[16] noted a GLV of 6.3 ± 5.1 in stable POAG suspect eyes and 11.3 ± 6.5 POAG suspects showing progression. They reported an FLV of 1.0 ± 1.2 in stable POAG suspect eyes and 1.9 ± 2.0 POAG suspects showing progression, reflecting those of PAC and PAC with OHT in our study. A study by Kurysheva et al.[18] suggested that, in contrast to POAG, GCC thinning has a greater prognostic value than the pRNFL thinning in initial PACG, as was seen in our analysis.

Studies identified a diagnostic difference value of about 8 μm of GCIPL between the upper and lower segments in preperimetric POAG patients and hypothesized that the OCT thickness asymmetry of the inner retinal layers is an initial sign of POAG onset.[19] A similar asymmetry was seen in PAC with OHT and early PACG eyes in this study.

In this study, differences in pRNFL values were only statistically significant between PAC with OHT vs early PACG eyes; however, there was a significant difference in superior pRNFL values between PAC and PAC with OHT eyes. Lisboa et al.[16] noted pRNFL in preperimetric POAG suspects to be 86.0 ± 7.6 µm, while controls had a 99.5 ± 8.2 µm.

Ocular hypertension treatment study (OHTS)[4] reported that a higher IOP was a major risk factor for developing primary open-angle glaucoma, and over 20 years, 49.3% in the observation group and 41.9% in the initial treatment arm progressed. The incidence of visual field loss was 25.2%. Hou et al.[20] looked at normal eyes, OHT with open angles, and POAG eyes and found a significant GCC thinning and macular vessel density in OHT eyes compared to normal, with higher mean intraocular pressure during follow-up associated with faster GCC thinning in the OHT group. These studies corroborate our SD-OCT results showing a significant difference in GCC changes across the spectrum of PACD, PAC to early PACG, through an intermediate stage of PAC with OHT.

A long-term follow-up of PAC eyes without chronically raised IOP after an iridotomy found that 36.1% developed ocular hypertension, and of these, 30.76% progressed to PACG with a VFD. None of the PAC eyes without ocular hypertension progressed to PACG.[5] Liza-Sharmini et al.[21] also found baseline IOP to be the major factor for progression of PAC eyes to PACG. Radhakrishnan et al.[22] reported that 42% to 67% of PAC eyes needed glaucoma medications to control IOP after iridotomy. Ramyashri et al.[23] found that 13.5% of PAC eyes had an IOP over 21mmHg, and 1.9% had an IOP over 30 mmHg. It is therefore apparent that large number of PAC eyes do have a chronically raised IOP, and these may be more likely to develop irreversible glaucomatous neuropathy.

Chronically elevated IOP in experimental and clinical studies of different glaucoma, POAG, secondary to vitreoretinal surgery or steroid use, etc., has been shown to cause glaucomatous optic neuropathy. In PAC, iridotrabecular contact and peripheral anterior synechiae cause damage to the trabecular dysfunction and result in raised IOP. Sener et al.[24] have shown that elevated intraocular pressure is an important factor affecting optical nerve perfusion in PACD. Intermittent rises may be missed, but the presence of a chronically elevated IOP after an iridotomy in PAC eyes, that is, PAC with OHT, appears to be an indicator of possible preperimetric damage.

Limitations of our study are its cross-sectional nature, as a documentation of progression would have been ideal. Test–retest variability of OCT values and age-related changes may have some confounding effect. As there are no accepted standards for significant changes on GCC and pRNFL, statistical differences and literature review were utilized.

Conclusion

PAC with chronically raised IOP after iridotomy, PAC with OHT, appears to be a significant inflection point in the progression of PAC, and monitoring GCC in such eyes would highlight the earliest changes in progression. This would permit an appropriate reduction of IOP in PAC with OHT eyes to prevent progression to irreversible perimetric damage and morbidity seen in PACG.

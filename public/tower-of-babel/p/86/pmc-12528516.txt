Clinical and radiographic success of lesion sterilization and tissue repair in primary teeth: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Khlood Baghlaf, Rana A Alamoudi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractResearch evaluating the clinical and radiographic outcomes of lesion sterilization and tissue repair (LSTR), particularly in terms of mobility, pain, sinus tract, fistula, swelling, root resorption, and interradicular radiolucency is yet to be conducted. This systematic review aimed to assess the clinical and radiographic success of LSTR using three antibacterial drug combinations—“three Mix” (3MIX)—and compare their success rates with those of conventional pulpectomy and pulpotomy in treating primary teeth. Additionally, the review sought to determine which antibiotic combination is most effective in LSTR in primary teeth. A comprehensive search strategy was applied across three electronic databases: PubMed, Scopus, and King Abdulaziz Digital Library. Two independent reviewers screened 536 titles and 70 abstracts, ultimately selecting 14 articles based on the predefined inclusion criteria. Clinical and radiographic outcomes were evaluated at 6, 12, and 18-month follow-up intervals. The Cochrane's risk of bias tool was used to assess the quality of randomized trials, and the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale was used to assess the methodological quality of non-randomized trials. A total of 14 clinical studies were included in the review. Only two studies compared LSTR to MTA pulpotomy. The composition of 3MIX and other antibacterial mixtures used in LSTR varied significantly, though consistent clinical and radiographic success was observed across the studies. All studies demonstrated relatively high success rates for 3MIX antibiotic paste, both clinically and radiographically. No significant differences were found between the success rates of 3MIX, Vitapex, zinc oxide eugenol, and other 3MIX combinations in pulpectomy of primary teeth. At the 12-month follow-up, the meta-analysis found no significant difference in clinical and radiographic success rates between 3MIX and conventional pulpotomy and pulpectomy. 3MIX may be effective for primary teeth pulpectomy, especially when conventional pulpectomy treatments are not suitable in young, uncooperative children.Keywords: Three antibiotic mix, Three-mix, Primary teeth pulpectomy, Triple antibiotic paste, Vitapex, Clinical and radiographic success

Introduction

Research has shown a decline in the occurrence of dental caries in primary teeth worldwide. However, the prevalence rate of caries continues to remain elevated in certain pediatric groups, with childhood caries being the most prevalent. Notably, 75% of the teeth affected by dental caries exhibit signs of pulp involvement (Peres et al. 2019). The primary reasons for pulp inflammation and necrosis in primary dentition are carious lesions and dental trauma (Chen et al. 2019).

Clinically, the presence of bacteria is responsible for pulpal and periapical infections, and treatment success is closely linked to reducing bacterial colonization in the pulp and surrounding tissues (Siqueira et al. 2019). Various studies have examined bacterial profile involved in root canal infections. In cases of root canal infection in primary teeth, the necrotic pulp tissue typically harbors a diverse microbial population, with gram-negative anaerobic bacteria accounting for 90% of the bacteria present. The standard treatment options for primary dentition exhibiting pulpal involvement and periradicular resorption include extraction or pulpectomy (Songvejkasem et al. 2021).

The early extraction of primary dentition can lead to various negative outcomes, such as a reduction in arch length, inadequate space for eruption of premolars, and the forward tilting of permanent molars (Nadelman et al. 2020). Furthermore, in young children, premature extraction of primary teeth can lead to reduced arch space, resulting in crowded permanent teeth or malocclusion. Premature extraction an lead to complications such as improper eruption of permanent teeth, loss of arch space, disruption of the normal eruption sequence, and the development of harmful habits including speech alterations, mouth breathing, tongue thrusting, and functional impairments (Nadelman et al. 2020). When extraction is deemed necessary in young children, preserving space becomes essential. However, using space maintainers or appliances presents challenges, including difficulties in oral hygiene maintenance and discomfort for children (Aparna 2024). Alternatively, pulpectomy involves the complete removal of necrotic tissue and infected pulp from a damaged tooth, enabling the tooth to remain functional and asymptomatic till its physiological exfoliation (Chandra et al. 2023).

Endodontic treatments are among the most essential and advanced procedures in dentistry, as they help ensure proper tooth functionality and preserve structural integrity within the oral cavity (Alghamdi and Shakir 2020). Over time, various techniques and strategies have been introduced, ranging from the conventional step-back preparation method to more advance techniques, each with varying levels of success and failure depending on the pulp and periapical conditions of the teeth. The most common method for treating primary teeth involves manual instrumentation with files combined with disinfecting irrigation solutions (Manchanda et al. 2020). Despite this, mechanical instrumentation along with chemical irrigation does not fully eradicate microbes from the root canal (Ali et al. 2022). The primary objective of endodontic treatment is to thoroughly disinfect root canals and prevent the risk of re-infection. Over time, root canal medicaments, particularly antibiotics, have proven to play a crucial role in achieving successful therapy outcomes (Ordinola-Zapata et al. 2022).

Historically, various materials have been recommended to promote normal root development. For decades, zinc oxide eugenol (ZOE) has been the commonly used filling material, despite not fulfilling all the ideal criteria for an optimal obturating material (Marchi et al. 2020). Other materials, such as iodoform alone or combined with ZOE and calcium hydroxide, have also been used, though they may be associated with unwanted side effects (Al-Haddad and Che Ab Aziz 2016).

Recently, a novel approach of lesion sterilization and tissue repair (LSTR) has gained attention in pediatric dentistry. This non-invasive endodontic treatment involves the use of a blend of three antibiotics—ciprofloxacin, metronidazole and minocycline—known as 3MIX. This triple antibiotic paste was specifically developed for regenerative and revascularization procedures, particularly for treating teeth with open apices and necrotic pulp (Achanta et al. 2023). The development of 3MIX was initially led by Hoshino and colleagues, who examined its efficacy in eliminating microorganisms from root canals (Kalubowila et al. 2023). In-vitro studies have also employed 3MIX for the disinfection of dentine infected with Escherichia coli (Thosar and Dahake 2021). Future research has shown its potent antibacterial activity in carious dentine and infected pulp, indicating a high success rate in eliminating bacteria from root canals (Ghabraei et al. 2018).

In 3MIX, metronidazole, a nitroimidazole compound, is particularly effective against anaerobic bacteria and is often used as an antimicrobial agent to target protozoa and anaerobes. Minocycline, which is bacteriostatic, works against both gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria and helps increase the production of interleukin-10, an anti-inflammatory cytokine. Ciprofloxacin, a synthetic fluoroquinolone, has rapid bactericidal effects and strong activity against gram-negative bacteria, though its antimicrobial efficacy against gram-positive bacteria is limited. Since most anaerobic bacteria are resistant to ciprofloxacin, it is often combined with metronidazole to treat mixed types of infections. Thus, 3MIX is effective against a wide spectrum of bacteria, including gram-negative, gram-positive, and anaerobic bacteria, making it useful in treating odontogenic infections (Qadeer et al. 2016). This systematic review aimed to summarize the evidence related to the use of 3MIX in primary molars and assess its success rate compared to that of Vitapex in primary teeth pulpectomy. It also sought to investigate which combination of antibiotics is more effective as a root canal obturating material for pulpectomy in primary teeth.

Materials & methods

This systematic review was conducted in alignment with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines (Ribeiro et al. 2020) as closely as possible. The study was registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Review (PROSPERO) database (CRD42025645873) in February 2025. The review aimed to address the following evidence-based practice questions: how effective is 3MIX compared to Vitapex in pulpectomy in primary teeth? Additionally, which antibiotic combination serves as the most effective root canal filling material for pulpectomy in primary teeth?

Data source and search strategy

An electronic search was conducted from November 2023 to July 2024 using PubMed, Scopus and King Abdulaziz Digital Library by two independent reviewers without any time restrictions. The authors reviewed the reference lists of the included studies and conducted a search on Google Scholar to incorporate grey literature. The following keywords were used to identify studies for inclusion in this review regarding pediatric dentistry, primary molars, primary teeth, triple antibiotic mix, triple antibiotic paste, three antibacterial drugs, antibiotic paste, lesion tissue sterilization, clinical success, and radiographic success: (Triple antibiotic mix) OR (triple mix antibiotic) OR (lesion tissue sterilization) OR (sterilization tissue repair) AND (primary molar) OR (necrotic primary teeth) OR (primary molars) AND (pulpectomy) OR (non-vital pulp therapy).

Eligibility criteria

Studies that reported both clinical and radiographic outcomes of using topical antibiotics in pulpectomy on primary teeth were deemed eligible for inclusion.

The selected studies had to meet the following criteria based on the PICOs elements (Center for Reviews and Dissemination, 2009).

P: Pulpectomy in primary teeth.

I: Original 3MIX paste (ciprofloxacin–minocycline–metronidazole) lesion sterilization.

C: Pulpotomy using Vitapex, ZOE, MTA or a modified antibiotic combination.

O: Clinical and radiographic outcome.

S: Randomized and non-randomized trials.

Randomized clinical trials (RCTs) or non-randomized studies that compared clinical as well as radiographic outcomes of two mixtures composed of three antibiotics. These mixtures included the conventional 3MIX paste (ciprofloxacin–minocycline–metronidazole) and other modified antibiotic combination, both used in pulpectomy in primary teeth in healthy children. Studies that provided a clear assessment of the treatment's success, based on clinical and radiographic evaluations, with a minimum follow-up period of 1 month was included. Only studies published in English or Spanish were considered.

The exclusion criteria were:

Studies that did not include a conventional 3MIX formulation for comparison.

Studies involving primary teeth with traumatic injuries or teeth without successors.

Study selection

All the studies sourced from various databases were organized using Endnote Desktop software. The study titles were subjected to an independent evaluation by two authors. After the initial selection, the abstracts were reviewed to identify potentially eligible studies. Studies were excluded if they did not report clinical or radiographic success related to the use of antibiotic paste in pulpectomy in primary teeth. Full-text versions of the selected studies were then downloaded and screened by the authors. The references for all eligible studies were also checked. Any differences of opinion about whether the included papers qualified were settled by a consensus with the help of a third reviewer. The reviewers were blinded to minimize bias in the evaluation process. Only those studies that met all the inclusion criteria were accepted.

Data extraction

Data were extracted from the selected articles by two reviewers (K.B & R.A) independently, each using a custom-made data sheet in Microsoft Excel. They populated the table with the extracted information and then reviewed the accuracy of the data. If disagreements arose during this process, a third reviewer was consulted to reach a consensus. To assess consistency among the reviewers, we conducted a preliminary analysis where multiple reviewers independently evaluated a subset of studies. The results indicated a high level of agreement, reinforcing the reliability of the process. The kappa values for study selection or data extraction ranged between 0.86 and 0.89.

The finalized data was then compiled into a single table. The extracted data included variables such as publication details such as first author’s name and country and publication year; study methodology such as study design, number of children, age in years, number and type of teeth, and type of antibiotic mixture used; follow-up period (months); clinical and radiographic success (%); and conclusion. All studies were subjected to qualitative analysis. Treatment success based on clinical and radiographic outcomes were evaluated following established criteria. The largest sample size and maximum follow-up duration were unrestricted. Clinical success was defined as the absence of pain, abnormal mobility, and the presence of healthy soft tissues in the treated teeth. Radiographic success was assessed by a reduction in the size of interradicular radiolucency, signs of bone regeneration, and no evidence of root resorption both internally and externally. If the radiolucency remained unchanged, it was also considered a sign of radiographic success. The treatment was deemed successful only if both the clinical and radiographic criteria were met.

Quality assessment

The included studies were independently assessed by two reviewers using the Cochrane's risk of bias tool for RCTs, and the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale was used to assess the methodological quality of non-RCTs (Higgins et al. 2011; Wells et al. 2025). In case of any discrepancy between the two reviewers, a discussion of the value was carried out until they reached an agreement. The level of agreement between the reviewers was evaluated by the kappa value.

Meta-analysis

Quantitative synthesis requires at least two studies. The meta-analysis was conducted using RevMan (version 5.3; Nordic Cochrane Center, Cochrane Collaboration, 2001). Cochran’s test and Higgin's I2 index were utilized to assess study heterogeneity. The I2 statistic was categorized as moderate heterogeneity (30% to 60%), substantial heterogeneity (50% to 90%), and considerable heterogeneity (75% to 100%). When heterogeneity was present, a random-effects model was applied. Sensitivity analyses were conducted for subgroups categorized by control type (Vitapex and ZOE). Additionally, clinical and radiographic success was evaluated at 12 months, as not all included studies reported the outcomes are 18 months. All studies with a high risk of bias were excluded from the meta-analysis. Additionally, studies that used 3MIX as a control were excluded. The odds ratio (OR) was used as the primary method for combining data from individual studies. Subgroup differences were examined using chi-squared tests, with a significance level set at a p-value of less than 0.05.

Results

Study selection

A total of 25,002 possibly relevant details were gathered from all databases (Fig. 1). Of these, 24.466 duplicate entries were eliminated. Following the removal of duplicates, two reviewers evaluated 536 studies based on their titles and abstracts. Full-text was assessed for 41 studies, of which 32 were excluded due to non-compliance with the inclusion criteria. Ultimately, 14 studies met all selection criteria for inclusion in qualitative analysis.

The methodologies employed in the included studies were similar methodologies, differing mainly in antibiotic combinations used. Four studies (Doneria et al. 2017; Nakornchai et al. 2010; Sijini et al. 2021; Zacharczuk et al. 2019) compared clinical and radiographic outcomes of 3MIX and Vitapex pulpectomy for treating necrotic primary teeth, whereas three studies (Doneria et al. 2017; Reddy et al. 2017; Moura et al. 2021) compared 3MIX to zinc oxide ozonated oil pulpectomy in primary teeth.

Conventional combination of 3MIX of antibiotics

Eight studies (Nanda et al. 2014; Pinky et al. 2011; Reddy et al. 2017; Raslan et al. 2017; Nakornchai et al. 2010; Sijini et al. 2021; Takushige et al. 2004; Zacharczuk et al. 2019) used the conventional combination of three antibiotics (Ciprofloxacin, Metronidazole, Minocycline) blended in a propylene glycol base for treatment, as recommended by the American Pediatric Dentistry Guidelines (Coll et al. 2020) (Table 1&2).

Different combinations of 3MIX antibiotics

Two studies (Nanda et al. 2014; Pinky et al. 2011) clinically and radiographically assessed the effectiveness of non-instrumental endodontic treatment (NIET) for infected primary teeth, utilizing two different antibiotic combinations—ciprofloxacin, minocycline, and metronidazole (3MIX) and ciprofloxacin, minocycline, and ornidazole. Another study (Thakur et al. 2021) compared a combination of ciprofloxacin, ornidazole, and cefixime with a modified 3MIX paste. Three recent studies used the same combination of antibiotics (metronidazole, ciprofloxacin, and cefixime) in a 1:1:1 ratio (Chak et al. 2022; Mushtaq et al. 2023; Almarji et al. 2024). Two studies compared 3MIX with MTA (Chak et al. 2022; Mushtaq et al. 2023) pulpotomy, whereas one study compared it with conventional endodontic treatment (Almarji et al. 2024).

Drug ratio of the three antibiotics

The drugs ratio employed was 1:3:3 in four studies (Nanda et al. 2014; Pinky et al. 2011; Reddy et al. 2017; Takushige et al. 2004) and 1:1:1 in eight studies (Doneria et al. 2017; Thakur et al. 2021; Almarji et al. 2024; Raslan et al. 2017; Nakornchai et al. 2010; Sijini et al. 2021; Moura et al. 2021; Zacharczuk et al. 2019). Carriers, either macrogol alone or in combination with propylene glycol, were incorporated into various antibiotic mixtures until a homogenous paste was formed.

All the procedures were performed using rubber dam isolation. For each case, a cavity was prepared in each tooth based on the size of the lesion, ensuring all caries were removed without leaving any overhanging tooth structure, allowing for optimal coronal pulp access to remove the pulp chamber. The included teeth did not undergo any canal instrumentation.

Irrigations during the LSTR treatment

Irrigation was performed using sodium hypochlorite (Nakornchai et al. 2010; Sijini et al. 2021; Zacharczuk et al. 2019), normal saline (Nanda et al. 2014; Pinky et al. 2011), sodium hypochlorite and distilled water (Reddy et al. 2017), sodium hypochlorite and saline (Doneria et al. 2017; Thakur et al. 2021; Moura et al. 2021), phosphoric acid (35%) and sterile water (Raslan et al. 2017; Takushige et al. 2004), and sodium hypochlorite and saline with chlorhexidine gluconate 0.2% (Almarji et al. 2024).

Final restoration

The cavities were restored with glass ionomer cement and immediately rehabilitated with stainless steel crowns (SSC) in a single visit in nine trials (Chak et al. 2022; Mushtaq et al. 2023; Nanda et al. 2014; Thakur et al. 2021; Almarji et al. 2024; Raslan et al. 2017; Nakornchai et al. 2010; Sijini et al. 2021; Moura et al. 2021); restored with glass ionomer cement and rehabilitated with SSC crowns after 15 days in one study (Doneria et al. 2017); temporarily filled with ZOE, finally restored with glass ionomer cement, and rehabilitated with SSC crowns after 1 month in two studies (Pinky et al. 2011; Reddy et al. 2017); temporarily filled with ZOE, finally restored with glass ionomer cement, and rehabilitated with preformed SSC after 1 week in one study (Zacharczuk et al. 2019); and restored with glass ionomer cement and rehabilitated with direct bonded resin inlay in a single visit in one study (Takushige et al. 2004).

Follow-up examination

Clinical and radiographic follow-up examinations were conducted after 1, 6, and 12 months in one study (Doneria et al. 2017); 6 and 12 months in two studies (Raslan et al. 2017; Nakornchai et al. 2010); 3, 6, and 12 months in nine studies (Mushtaq et al. 2023; Nanda et al. 2014; Raslan et al. 2017; Sijini et al. 2021; Moura et al. 2021); 1, 3, 6, 12, and 18 months in one study (Zacharczuk et al. 2019); and 680 days (23 months) in one study (Takushige et al. 2004).

Main outcomes

Clinical and radiographic evaluation

Of the 14 studies included in this review, 10 showed a non-significant difference in clinical and radiographic success rates between the 3MIX and control groups; two studies did not report the p-value (Almarji et al. 2024; Takushige et al. 2004). All primary teeth pulpectomy using 3MIX as an intracanal medicament exhibited clinically excellent outcomes. Only one showed that 3MIX had a significantly higher success rate compared to control (p = 0.02) (Thakur et al. 2021). All remaining studies showed a non-significant difference between 3MIX and control. This finding indicates that 3MIX can be used instead of conventional medicaments.

In most studies, teeth treated remained clinically asymptomatic throughout the various follow-up periods—after 680 days (Takushige et al. 2004), 3 months (Reddy et al. 2017), 3 and 6 months (Nanda et al. 2014; Pinky et al. 2011), 6 months (Nakornchai et al. 2010), and 6 and 12 months (Chak et al. 2022; Almarji et al. 2024; Raslan et al. 2017; Moura et al. 2021). Some studies reported a complete absence of pain, tenderness on percussion, and resolution of sinus tracts and abscesses (Nanda et al. 2014; Raslan et al. 2017).

Comparison of 3MIX versus different antibiotic mixtures

Conventional 3MIX versus 1st Mixture (Ciprofloxacin, Minocycline, And Ornidazole)

Nanda et al. and Pinky et al. reported no significant difference in clinical and radiographic outcomes at 3, 6, and 12 months (p > 0.05) between 3MIX and the mixture containing ciprofloxacin, minocycline, and ornidazole. Although both groups exhibited a 100% clinical success rate, the radiographic success rate of the mixture containing ciprofloxacin, minocycline, and ornidazole greater than that of 3MIX (Nanda et al. 2014; Pinky et al. 2011).

Conventional 3MIX versus 2nd Mixture (Ciprofloxacin, Clindamycin, and Ornidazole [3MIX-MP-R])

Raslan et al. reported an overall success rate of 80.96% for 3MIX-MP and 76.20% for 3MIX-MP-R at 6 and 12 months, showing a statistical insignificant difference between them (p = 0.71). Similarly, no statistically significant variations were observed between the two groups in terms of radiolucency appearance or radiographic success rates (p > 0.05) (Raslan et al. 2017).

Comparison of 3MIX versus Vitapex

Nakornchai et al. and Sijini et al. reported statistically insignificant difference in clinical and radiographical outcomes at 6 and 12 months (p > 0.05) between 3MIX and Vitapex; both groups showed 100% and 96% and 92.85% and 95.45% clinical success. In both studies, 3MIX demonstrated a higher radiographic success rate than Vitapex (Nakornchai et al. 2010; Sijini et al. 2021).

Contrarily, Doneria et al. reported that the clinical and radiographic success of Vitapex was greater than that of 3MIX-MP after 12 months, with statistically insignificant and significant differences clinically and radiographically, respectively (Doneria et al. 2017).

After 1, 3, 6, 12 and 18 months of follow-up, Zacharczuk et al. observed insignificant clinical and radiographic differences between the groups. Overall, radiographic success rates were 88.3% and 82.3% (p = 0.31) and clinical success rates were 91.5% and 87.5% (p = 0.48) for Maisto–Capurro Paste (Vitapex) and 3MIX, respectively. Both therapies demonstrated similar clinical and radiographic outcomes (Zacharczuk et al. 2019).

Comparison of ciprofloxacin, ornidazole, cefixime versus modified 3MIX and meta paste

A study by Thakur et al. included 3 groups—3MIX incorporating ciprofloxacin, ornidazole, and cefixime mixed with simvastatin powder was used in group A, modified 3MIX (same was antibiotic composition but using macrogol (M) and propylene glycol (P) as vehicle and meta paste was used in group B, and Metapex paste was used for group C. The results showed that group A had significantly higher success rates compared to the other two groups (p = 0.02 and p = 0.001 for groups B and C, respectively) (Thakur et al. 2021).

Comparison 3MIX-MP versus 3MIX sealer

After 680 days of follow-up, Takushige et al. reported no statistically significant difference in clinical and radiographic success rates (p > 0.05) between 3MIX-MP and 3MIX sealers (two differently prepared 3MIX sealers). In this study, 3MIX sealer was initially used, which was then replaced with 3MIX-MP (Takushige et al. 2004).

Comparison 3MIX-MP versus conventional pulpectomy technique using ZOE

Reddy et al. and Moura et al. demonstrated a comparable clinical and radiographic success rates between 3MIX-MP and control at 3, 6, and 12 months of follow-ups. Furthermore, a statistically significant difference in radiographic success rates between 3MIX-MP and controls was observed at 12 months (p < 0.01) (Reddy et al. 2017; Moura et al. 2021).

Meta-analysis

A quantitative synthesis requires at least two studies. We evaluated clinical and radiographic success at 12 months in the included studies and compared the findings. Two forest plots compared clinical and radiographic success of 3MIX and conventional pulpotomy. One forest plot compared 3MIX with MTA pulpectomy; however, analysis of clinical success was not feasible because the outcomes were comparable for the 3MIX and control groups.

Clinical success of 3MIX compared to conventional pulpectomy at 12 months

Figure 4 presents a forest plot comparing the clinical success of 3MIX to Vitapex and ZOE pulpectomy at 12 months. Six studies with low and moderate risk of bias were included in this meta-analysis. The plot showed no statistically significant difference in the clinical success between 3MIX and Vitapex or ZOE (p = 0.55 and p = 0.54, respectively), with an OR of 1.49 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.40–5.54) for Vitapex and 0.61 (95% CI: 0.13–2.96) for ZOE (Fig. 4). Moreover, a moderate heterogeneity was observed in the comparison of 3MIX and ZOE, with an I2 value of 40%.

Discussion

The present systematic review assessed the evidence related to the clinical and radiographic success of LSTR in primary teeth. Several clinical studies have investigated different combinations of antibiotics; however, the American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry AAPD recommends the use of the conventional combination of three antibiotics (Ciprofloxacin, Metronidazole, Minocycline) blended in a propylene glycol base (Coll et al. 2020). Although some studies used different antibiotic combinations, these also reported high clinical and radiographic success rates.

The purpose of this review was to assess the efficacy of 3MIX in pulpectomy for primary teeth. Typically, the method is performed without the use of instruments; however, 3MIX is placed into the pulp chamber for treating inflamed pulp tissue (do Couto AM, Espaladori MC, Leite APP, Martins CC, de Aguiar MCF, Abreu LG. 2019). This method helps avoid challenges associated with the cleaning and shaping of root canals of primary teeth and saves time, especially when dealing with patients who are recalcitrant (Chouchene et al. 2021). The findings revealed varying degrees of evidence and success rates. In most included studies, 3MIX was directly compared to other medications and obturating materials for primary teeth. All nine trials compared different interventions. A key inclusion criterion was the use of clinical and in vivo investigations, which aligned with the gold standard for a thorough assessment of therapies (Alrayes et al. 2023). Consequently, the most frequent justification for exclusion was an inapplicable research design.

Conventional pulpectomy in primary teeth poses numerous challenges for clinicians because of the unusual morphology of primary teeth, challenges in obturation, intricacy of root canals, and the cooperation required from children (Achanta et al. 2023). Therefore, the use of antibacterial agents capable of permeating the tissues in treating infections in non-vital infected primary teeth (Ribeiro et al. 2020). A single antibacterial agent may not be successful due to the complicated bacterial makeup of the infected root canal as well as the poly-microbial nature of infection; consequently, several medication combinations have been tested throughout the years (Kumar et al. 2021).

The conventional 3MIX (ciprofloxacin, minocycline, and metronidazole) has shown promising results (Coll et al. 2020). Several studies have demonstrated that three drug combination can reliably sterilize infected canals, even though no single drug was able to completely eliminate all bacteria present in contaminated root canals (Abdel Hamid et al. 2018).

All studies evaluated the clinical and radiographic outcomes of 3MIX, of which two reported that 3MIX outperformed Vitapex treatment (Nakornchai et al. 2010; Sijini et al. 2021) and conventional pulpectomy (Reddy et al. 2017) in reducing radiographic symptoms. However, three clinical trials found no statistically significant difference regarding radiographic success between teeth treated with 3MIX-MP and 3MIX sealer (Takushige et al. 2004), 3MIX and Maisto–Capurro Paste (Zacharczuk et al. 2019) and 3MIX and 3MIX-MP-R (Raslan et al. 2017). These differences may be attributed to variability in methodologies and inclusion criteria applied in earlier research.

The ciprofloxacin–minocycline–ornidazole mixture demonstrated a radiographic success rate greater than that of 3MIX, whereas ciprofloxacin–ornidazole–minocycline mixture demonstrated a 100% clinical success rate compared to that of the 3MIX group (Nanda et al. 2014; Pinky et al. 2011). The improved outcomes may be due to the substitution of ornidazole for metronidazole. Notably, ornidazole exhibited a slower metabolism rate, higher efficacy rate, and a longer duration of action than metronidazole (Chouchene et al. 2021).

The meta-analysis revealed no significant difference in clinical and radiographic success rates between 3MIX and conventional pulpotomy and pulpectomy. However, these findings should be interpreted with caution due to the considerable variations in the types of antibiotics used in 3MIX and other controls. At 12-month follow-up, 3MIX showed lower success rates than Vitapex (Doneria et al. 2017), although another study found no statistically significant difference (Nakornchai et al. 2010). These discrepancies may result from variations in the preoperative and postoperative clinical and radiographic examinations, inclusion criteria, and technique employed. Additionally, the potential for antibiotic wash-out over time could contribute to decreased long-term effectiveness. Despite similar outcomes, 3MIX remains a viable non-instrumentation option to treat uncooperative children and poorly diagnosed cases (Rai et al. 2019). Another issue with the use of LSTR is that the indiscriminate use of antibiotics has contributed to the emergence of resistant bacterial strains. These risks, along with their negative effects, has motivated researchers to explore alternatives (Divya et al. 2019).

According to the available data, teeth treated with various 3MIX formulations showed comparable clinical and radiographic outcomes in terms of reducing radiolucency; increasing bone regeneration; and relieving pain and tenderness to percussion, sinus tract, fistula, abscess, and abnormal mobility (Nanda et al. 2014; Raslan et al. 2017). The bacterial populations in the root canals of primary teeth are diverse and form a sophisticated microbial network. Regardless of the components of 3MIX, its synergistic action and distinct antimicrobial spectrum target the majority of bacteria in the root canals (Khan et al. 2023).

Compared with other therapies, 3MIX demonstrated relatively better outcomes in reducing postoperative swelling (Doneria et al. 2017; Nanda et al. 2014; Reddy et al. 2017; Raslan et al. 2017; Nakornchai et al. 2010; Zacharczuk et al. 2019). Additionally, its effect on sinus tract healing was statistically equivalent to that of other materials and methods. A number of microorganisms detected in the root canals positively correlated with sinus tract formation (Sun et al. 2021). The principal causes of failure of an endodontic therapy are inflammation and bacterial contamination, both of which can be mitigated with the use of 3MIX (Parhizkar et al. 2018), making it a successful therapeutic option for non-vital primary teeth.

The 3MIX formulation has been proven effective in sterilizing necrotic pulp, carious lesions, and infected root dentin. Nonetheless, one issue with the use of minocycline is that it may lead to tooth discoloration (Makandar and Noorani 2020). This concern prompted the need to explore an alternative antibiotic combination that could produce the same results without minocycline. Raslan et al. (Raslan et al. 2017) modified 3MIX paste by substituting clindamycin for minocycline, offering a suitable option for individuals allergic to penicillin and cephalosporins, particularly in pediatric patients. Since clindamycin is effective against infections caused by aerobic and anaerobic bacteria, it emerges as a promising substitute (Kar et al. 2019). According to Raslan et al., the conventional 3MIX paste and modified combination of ciprofloxacin, metronidazole, and clindamycin were effective in endodontic treatment, although 3MIX exhibited a higher overall success rate (Raslan et al. 2017).

The substitution of minocycline with clindamycin in some LSTR protocols is primarily driven by concerns of tooth discoloration and the need for an alternative antibiotic in penicillin-allergic patients (Makandar and Noorani 2020). From a microbiological perspective, clindamycin offers a broad-spectrum activity against anaerobic and gram-positive organisms frequently implicated in endodontic infections caused by Peptostreptococcus, Prevotella, and Fusobacterium (Makandar and Noorani 2020). Pharmacokinetically, clindamycin achieves effective concentrations in bone and soft tissue, with good penetration into dentinal tubules, making it suitable for intracanal use (Zargar et al. 2019). However, resistance to clindamycin, although relatively rare among oral pathogens, can occur, particularly among Bacteroides and Actinomyces species (Irshad et al. 2020). While systemic use of clindamycin may cause gastrointestinal disturbances or pseudomembranous colitis, topical application—as used in LSTR—has not shown evidence of systemic absorption or adverse effects in pediatric populations, according to reviewed trials (Raslan et al. 2017). Nevertheless, cautious antibiotic stewardship remains essential to mitigate the risk of developing resistance. Therefore, the decision to modify conventional 3MIX should consider microbial coverage, safety, and clinical context.

In all studies included in this review, propylene glycol, with or without macrogol, was used as a vehicle to deliver the triple antibiotic paste. This solvent helps the medications penetrate deeply into the dentinal tubules, boosting their antimicrobial action, which explains its widespread use in the reviewed studies (Lillygrace et al. 2021). While antibiotics carry a risk of sensitization or allergic reactions (Sameh et al. 2023), none of the reviewed studies reported any such reactions to the antibiotic combinations used.

The systematic review encompassed studies with varied designs, sample sizes, and geographic locations, which can affect the generalizability of the results. Some studies compared LSTR with conventional pulpotomy, whereas others evaluated it against pulpectomy. Additionally, the materials used in pulpectomy and pulpotomy varied across the studies. In the included studies, sample sizes varied from 20 to 87 primary molars per group. Large sample sizes contribute to the robustness of the results and can improve the reliability of the conclusions drawn.

Geographically, the studies included in the review were conducted in several countries, such as India, Syria, Thailand, Saudi Arabia, Japan, Brazil, and Argentina. The directness of the findings to the target population is affected by study locations. Moreover, variability in outcome measures used across the studies can affect the interpretations of treatment efficacy and limit the feasibility of conducting a meta-analysis.

One limitation of this study is that it did not include grey literature in the review process. Given the limited evidence available, drawing definitive conclusions about the superiority of one antibiotic mixture over another was challenging. However, the findings suggest that, regardless of the antibiotic mix used, 3MIX remains effective for pulpectomy in primary teeth and is a viable option when conventional endodontic treatment is not recommended.

Clinical significance of the study

The systematic review evaluates the clinical and radiographic outcomes of LSTR using the 3MIX antibacterial combination in treating primary teeth. It addresses a significant gap in pediatric dentistry by demonstrating consistently high success rates for 3MIX compared to conventional pulpectomy and pulpotomy. The findings suggest that LSTR is a viable alternative, particularly for young, uncooperative patients, and highlight the need for standardized protocols in future research. Overall, the review supports the inclusion of 3MIX in clinical practice, offering an effective treatment option for managing pulp inflammation and necrosis in primary teeth.

Conclusion

All studies reviewed reported relatively high clinical and radiographic success rates with the use of 3MIX antibiotic paste, suggesting it remains a strong option for effectively eradicating microorganisms. According to the available evidence, the standard antibiotic mix (standard AAPD), which includes ciprofloxacin, metronidazole, and minocycline, is deemed the most suitable combination. However, other antibiotic combinations have resulted in similar success rates. No significant difference in success rates was observed between 3MIX, Vitapex, ZOE and 3MIX combinations in pulpectomy of primary teeth. Furthermore, no notable difference in clinical and radiographic success rates was observed between 3MIX and MTA use in pulpectomy in primary teeth. However, future controlled clinical trials with larger sample sizes and longer follow-up periods are necessary to determine which antibiotic mixture is the most effective option for primary teeth pulpectomy.

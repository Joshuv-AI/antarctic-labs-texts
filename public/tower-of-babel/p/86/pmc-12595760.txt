Chronic osteomyelitis after cephalomedullary nailing for intertrochanteric fractures of the femur: carbon fiber rod bone cement combined with calcium sulfate loaded with antibiotics
Authors: Hao Guo, Cheng Ren, Chaofeng Wang, Yibo Xu, Teng Ma, Dongchen Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundInfection after intramedullary nail fixation for intertrochanteric fractures is relatively rare. In this study, we investigated and compared the therapeutic effects of combined treatment with antibiotic bone cement carbon fiber rods loaded with antibiotic calcium sulfate and the traditional Masquelet technique on chronic osteomyelitis after intramedullary nail fixation for intertrochanteric fractures.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on 88 patients with chronic osteomyelitis after intramedullary nail internal fixation for intertrochanteric fractures in our hospital from September 2018 to September 2024. The patients were divided into two groups (n = 44 patients/group) based on the different surgical plans implemented to treat these patients. The control group was treated using the Masquelet technique. After debridement, the first stage involved treatment with antibiotic bone cement rods, and the second stage involved bone grafting. The experimental group was treated with the antibiotic calcium sulfate in the second stage, based on the first stage of the control group. The inflammatory indicators, therapeutic effects, and pain conditions of the patients six weeks after the operation were recorded. Corresponding evaluations were conducted for the two groups to comprehensively assess and compare the therapeutic effects of the two surgical methods.ResultsThe wound healing time and bone healing time of the patients in the experimental group were shorter than those in the control group, and the total effective rate was significantly greater than that in the control group (P < 0.05). No significant difference was found in the operation time between the two groups (P > 0.05). The levels of postoperative inflammatory indicators in both groups were significantly lower than those before treatment, and the levels in the experimental group were significantly lower than those in the control group (P < 0.05). The pain scores of the two groups of patients decreased at two weeks, one month after treatment, and three months after follow-up. Moreover, the pain scores of the experimental group were lower than those of the control group, and the difference was significant (P < 0.05).ConclusionThe combined treatment of postoperative infection with antibiotic-containing bone cement carbon fiber rods loaded with antibiotic-containing calcium sulfate in the proximal femur can effectively decrease inflammatory responses and pain. While eliminating infection, it also affects osteogenic formation. The postoperative recurrence rate decreases considerably, and it has good clinical efficacy.Keywords: Calcium sulfate, Chronic osteomyelitis, Bone cement, Carbon fiber rod, Vancomycin, Proximal femoral fracture

Background

With population aging, the incidence of femoral intertrochanteric fractures has been increasing annually. The total number of hip fracture patients in 204 countries in 2019 was 14.2 million [1]. The all-cause mortality rate one year after hip fracture ranges from 14.4 to 28.3% across countries [2]. It is a very common type of fracture among the elderly [3, 4] and considerably increases healthcare costs [5]. The treatment of choice for these types of fractures is intramedullary nailing, as it decreases soft tissue damage and permits early weight bearing [6]. Postoperative functional exercise is also very important because fracture surgery can lead to the loss of muscle mass [7]. Given the considerable increase in the number of patients, postoperative complications have also attracted much attention. Among these methods, internal fixation has a documented failure rate of 3–12% [8], and the incidence of chronic osteomyelitis can reach 2% [9]. Owing to its long disease course, complex treatment process, high treatment cost, high recurrence rate, high disability risk, and unsatisfactory postoperative effect, it is called “the second cancer” [10] and is a difficult problem in the management of postoperative complications of fractures [11]. This also leads to serious socioeconomic problems [12].

Many researchers have applied antibiotic bone cement and calcium sulfate for treating chronic osteomyelitis [13, 14]. The Masquelet membrane induction technique is a common treatment method for chronic osteomyelitis. It promotes osteogenic reconstruction by inducing a protective isolation mechanism of the connective tissue membrane formed by the bone cement occupying the bone defect area in the surrounding soft tissue [15]. Calcium sulfate serves as a biodegradable carrier for the sustained release of antibacterial drugs. It carries sensitive antibiotics that can be slowly released at the site of infection. For chronic osteomyelitis after internal fixation of intertrochanteric fractures of the femur, the infection site is deep, and the operation is difficult. There is no known effective treatment plan for chronic osteomyelitis after intramedullary nail surgery for intertrochanteric fractures of the femur. In this study, we retrospectively analyzed and compared the therapeutic effects of the Masquelet membrane induction technique and surgical regimens involving antibiotic bone cement rods combined with antibiotic calcium sulfate to assess the best and most effective treatment plan for chronic osteomyelitis after femoral bone marrow nailing.

Participants and methods

Participants

A total of 88 patients with infection-related chronic osteomyelitis after intramedullary nail internal fixation of intertrochanteric fractures of the femur admitted to Honghui Hospital Affiliated with the Medical College of Xi’an Jiaotong University from March 2018 to March 2024 were selected for the study. The inclusion criteria were as follows: 1. In 2017, the diagnostic criteria for chronic osteomyelitis of the International Association for the Study of Internal Fixation (Association for the Study of Internal Fixation, AO/ASIF) were as follows: sinus ducts, fistulas or wound dehiscence (with exposed bone or exposed internal fixators) that directly communicate with bone tissue or implants; pus was found around the internal fixator during the operation; bacterial culture of the suspected infected tissue was positive during the operation; and special histopathological staining confirmed the presence of pathogenic microorganisms in the suspected infected tissue during the operation. 2. Those over 18 years old; 3. Those with complete postoperative follow-up data. The exclusion criteria were as follows: patients with hematogenous osteomyelitis, those with other acute or chronic infectious diseases, those with malignant tumors, those with hematopoietic dysfunction and autoimmune diseases, and those with severe cardiovascular or cerebrovascular diseases.

General data

A total of 49 males and 39 females were included in this study. The age of the participants ranged from 48 to 71 years (average: 55.6 years). The course of osteomyelitis was four to nine months. All patients had osteomyelitis accompanied by nonunion of the bones. All of them were closed intertrochanteric fractures of the femur, and intramedullary screws were used for internal fixation after the fractures. Among the 81 patients with skin and soft tissue sinus tract formation, 78 had positive bacterial cultures of wound secretions, including 71 with single bacterial infection and six with mixed bacterial infection. There were 18 cases of methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus, 16 cases of S. aureus, 20 cases of Pseudomonas aeruginosa, 12 cases of Escherichia coli, 6 cases of Enterobacter cloacae and 10 cases of Staphylococcus epidermidis. All bacteria detected were sensitive to vancomycin / meropenem.

and gentamicin. The patients were divided into a control group and an experimental group according to the surgical method used. The control group was subjected to debridement combined with Masquelet technology, whereas the experimental group was subjected to first-stage debridement, antibiotic bone cement filling, and second-stage antibiotic calcium sulfate. The patients were grouped according to the Cinery-Mader classification of chronic osteomyelitis [16]: control group: 21 cases of CM type III (localized type), 23 cases of CM type IV (diffuse type); experimental group: 20 cases of CM type III (localized type) and 24 cases of CM type IV (diffuse type).

Sensitive antibiotics should be selected for those with bacteria detected during the operation. The production ratio of antibiotic-containing bone cement rods is 0.5 g of gentamicin and 4 g of vancomycin / meropenem for every 40 g of bone cement. For those with negative preoperative bacterial cultures, vancomycin and gentamicin should also be used in the same proportion during the operation. Moreover, necrotic bone, infected tissue, and secretions from the lesion were collected during the operation for detecting metagenomic factors and conducting drug sensitivity tests to compare and verify the results with those of positive preoperative bacterial culture. For patients with negative preoperative bacterial culture results, precise detection was conducted to guide the application of sensitive antibiotics. The production ratio of antibiotic-containing calcium sulfate rods is 0.5–1.0 g of vancomycin / meropenem for every 50ml of calcium sulfate.

This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Honghui Hospital Affiliated with Xi’an Jiaotong University School of Medicine (202,306,007), and all patients provided informed consent.

Methods

Preoperative management

The selection of intraoperatively sensitive antibiotics was determined based on the results of bacterial culture drug sensitivity, and no antibiotic treatment was administered before the operation. Complete X-ray films, CT (soft tissue window), and MRI were used to determine the infection site and debridement range.

Control group

An incision was made layer by layer along the lateral side of the thigh. The sinus tract and deep infected necrotic tissues were resected until the intramedullary nails were exposed. The internal fixation device was removed. Samples of necrotic bone, soft tissue, and purulent secretions were collected for metagenomic next-generation sequencing (mNGS). A local groove was made at the locking site of the distal femur. An intramedullary nail guide needle was inserted from the entrance of the original intramedullary nail of the greater trochanter of the femur. Intraoperative X-ray examination revealed that both the groove site and the position of the guide needle exceeded the length of the original intramedullary nail. A medullary expander larger than the diameter of the main nail was used to remove the intramedullary biofilm and inflammatory tissue to achieve expanded debridement [17]. A scraper and a grinding drill were used to remove the biofilm and inflammatory tissue in the anti-rotation nail. During the operation, the integrity of the medial femoral wall was preserved to prevent any defects from occurring in the cortical bone of the medial wall. The pulse pressure irrigation gun uses a large amount of normal saline to flush the medullary cavity, anti-screw canal, and wound surface. The femoral anti-rotation intramedullary nail was used as the model, and a carbon fiber rod of appropriate length was wrapped around it with bone cement mixed with vancomycin and gentamicin to form the shapes of the main nail and the anti-rotation nail (as shown in Fig. 1 a: Schematic diagram of medullary cavity dilation; b: Metagenomic next-generation sequencing samples; c: Select the length of the carbon fiber rod; d: Producing carbon fiber bar bone cement), which was placed for drainage, and the wound was closed. Bone grafting treatment was performed when the inflammatory indicators decreased after six weeks.

Debridement and preparation of carbon fiber rod bone cement

Experimental group

Based on the first-stage treatment of the control group, the second-stage operation was performed two weeks after the operation. The bone cement rods were removed along the original wound, and calcium sulfate artificial bone mixed with sensitive antibiotics was shaped and used to fill the bone defect of the medullary cavity. Indwelling drainage was conducted, and the wound was closed.

Postoperative management

The intraoperative mNGS results were compared to those of bacterial culture of sinus exudate substances. When the intraoperative and preoperative results were inconsistent, the intraoperative sampling results were regarded as the standard [18]. Based on the results of the drug sensitivity test, sensitive antibiotic drugs were used for standardized treatment at sufficient dosages and durations. Inflammatory indicators were reexamined, and follow-up was conducted once a month within the first three months after the operation, once every three months after three months, and once every six months after one year.

Evaluation of therapeutic effects

Postoperative recovery

The operation time, wound healing time, bone healing time, and total effective rate of the control group and the experimental group were statistically analyzed. According to the relevant standards in “Practical Orthopedics” [19], the clinical efficacy is classified as highly effective, effective, or ineffective. The total effective rate is the sum of the marked effect and the effective rate. The infection recurrence rates of the two groups at six weeks after treatment were statistically analyzed. Recurrence can be diagnosed when the lesion site shows the same symptoms as chronic osteomyelitis related to bone infection again after the operation. These symptoms include a continuous increase in body temperature and inflammatory indicators; inflammatory reactions such as redness, swelling, heat, and pain at the primary disease site; non-healing of the wound; or the formation of sinus tract discharge of pus [20]. After the operation, the density of the fracture ends, the gap, and whether the fracture line was blurred were observed through X-ray imaging to determine whether the bone had healed.

Inflammatory index levels

White blood cells, C-reactive protein, the erythrocyte sedimentation rate, procalcitonin, and serum amyloid A (SAA) were measured in patients before the operation and six weeks after the operation [21], and the postoperative therapeutic effects were dynamically observed.

VAS score

Pain assessment in patients with preoperative and postoperative visual analog pain scores (visual analog scale) via visual analog scale (VAS) scores before and after treatment for two weeks, one month, and three months; a total score of 10 points indicates more intense pain [22].

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed using IBM SPSS 27.0. Count data were expressed as [n(%)] and analyzed using the Chi-square test. Rank data were compared using the rank sum test. Normally distributed measurement data were expressed as (±s) and analyzed using independent sample t-tests for intergroup comparisons, paired t-tests for intragroup comparisons, and analysis of variance for repeated measures data. All results were considered to be statistically significant at P < 0.05.

Discussion

As the number of patients increases, ensuring the quality of intraoperative reduction has become difficult. Adequate reduction and fewer internal diseases can greatly reduce postoperative complications [23, 24]. Healthy bone tissue has extremely strong resistance to infection. However, when the body experiences necrosis, severe infection, foreign body invasion, and peripheral vascular diseases, bone tissue becomes more prone to infection. Pathogenic bacteria can reach bones through the bloodstream and directly invade adjacent soft tissues (such as diabetic foot ulcers) through infections. Inflammatory responses can further increase the pressure in the local bone marrow, leading to blood stasis and thrombosis. If not treated promptly and effectively, this pressure can break through the cortical bone and reduce the blood supply to the periosteum, resulting in bone necrosis [25]. Incomplete removal of the infection site is an important reason for the recurrence of chronic osteomyelitis. Mature bacterial biofilms can resist the bactericidal effects of the host immune system and antibiotics. Only by thoroughly removing diseased tissues with biofilms, such as implants, dead bones, scars, and granulation tissue, can the risk of the recurrence of infection be reduced. Therefore, eliminating infected lesions is a key step for the successful treatment of chronic osteomyelitis [26, 27].

The treatment of chronic osteomyelitis of the proximal femur mainly includes removal of the original internal fixation device, complete elimination of the lesion, and the application of antibiotics. Besides vancomycin and gentamicin, other antibiotics have been investigated for local application in the treatment of osteomyelitis. For example, Yucel et al. demonstrated in a rat model that rifaximin spacers were not better than teicoplanin treatment, indicating that different antibiotics can be used, but their efficacy varies. When antibiotics are used, sensitive antibiotics should be used specifically for bacteria [28]. When using antibiotics, two modes are usually adopted: systemic application and local application. For systemic application, the use of antibiotics for six weeks is recommended [29]. For the local application of antibiotics, carriers are needed. In previous clinical treatments, antibiotic bone cement was implanted at the site of bone infection to maintain a high concentration of antibiotics in the local surrounding tissues, which could eliminate infected bacteria. The lower dosage used prevented the concentration of intravenous drugs from exceeding the toxic level, decreased the likelihood of developing drug resistance, and produced biofilms [30]. In the later stage of bone grafting for treating bone defects, the Masquelet technique is more commonly used. However, this method also has certain drawbacks. There are certain constraints in the selection of antibacterial drugs. Antibacterial drugs need to be water-soluble, heat-resistant, and stable, which leads to a lower-level inflammatory response at the infection site and, subsequently, the occurrence of drug resistance [31]. Moreover, some studies have found that the effectiveness of antibiotic-containing bone cement is relatively low. Studies have shown that about 90% of the effects of antibiotics cannot be released [32].

Medical calcium sulfate is a new type of biomaterial that is now being applied in clinical practice. Calcium sulfate is degradable and histocompatible. The local application of antibiotics can be 100–200 times more effective than the systemic application of antibiotics. It can maintain a relatively high antibiotic concentration locally, and the treatment time can last up to one month. It has the advantages of accurate positioning, a high local concentration, few side effects, and a long treatment time [33]. Moreover, its degradability can result in a high-calcium and slightly acidic environment after degradation, thereby promoting the formation of new bone tissue. It has an osteogenic effect [34]. After the lesion is filled, it does not need to be removed through reoperation and has an auxiliary effect on bone repair. Moreover, calcium sulfate filling at the defect site of the bone segment can not only provide effective support but also prevent the proliferation of soft tissue at the defect site, which is not conducive to bone healing. However, aseptic exudation may also occur when calcium sulfate degrades, which may lead to delayed wound healing and the recurrence of infection [35].

A single treatment plan has difficulty solving the problems caused by bone infection [13], and there is a chance of retaining harmful bacteria after bone cement filling [36]. In this study, we combined the advantages of these two materials to assess the clinical value of combined treatment with antibiotic bone cement carbon fiber rods and antibiotic-loaded calcium sulfate for chronic osteomyelitis after intramedullary nail surgery for intertrochanteric fractures of the femur. The total effective rate of the experimental group six weeks after treatment was greater than that of the control group. The wound healing time and wound suture removal time of the experimental group were shorter than those of the control group. The levels of inflammatory factors two and six weeks after the operation were also lower than those in the experimental group. The pain scores at two weeks, one month, and three months after the operation were also significantly lower than those of the experimental group. These findings indicate that the combined treatment of antibiotic-containing bone cement carbon fiber rods and calcium sulfate loaded with antibiotics can effectively control the infection symptoms of patients, promote their postoperative recovery, and have satisfactory therapeutic effects. In the traditional Masquelet technique, the antibiotic bone cement filled at the bone defect site has the problem of low mechanical strength. There are certain requirements for the ratio of antibiotics to bone cement, and the maximum ratio should not exceed 1:10 [37]. Ji Zhengang and Yu Xin [38, 39] wrapped a steel plate with bone cement to improve the strength of the filled bone cement and the stability of the bone defect because of insufficient stability. However, the technique greatly interferes with the imaging examination of the lesion segment after the operation. The imaging of metals via magnetic resonance can easily affect the magnetic resonance imaging of residual or new infections. It strongly affects the elimination of the infectious lesion. In this study, combined with the innovative clinical application of bone cement-wrapped carbon fiber rods for filling bone defects, the lengths and thicknesses of the carbon fiber rods can meet the filling requirements in the proximal femoral medullary cavity. While it is a cost-effective technique, obtaining materials is also relatively easy. Postoperative follow-up MRI does not affect the therapeutic effect of lesion removal.

The detection of bacteria that cause bone infections relies mainly on bacterial culture [40]. However, this method requires a long laboratory testing time and has a low positive rate. It is often difficult to apply sensitive antibiotics quickly, thus delaying the course of the disease and treatment. The mNGS technology is a relatively new molecular biology detection technique. Multiple pathogenic microorganisms can be detected simultaneously using genomic research strategies. It has the advantages of fast detection speed, high specificity and sensitivity, and a wide identification range [41–43]. In this study, the bacterial culture protocol was applied to patients with sinus tract secretions on the skin surface before the operation. During the operation, samples were collected from necrotic bone, soft tissue, and purulent secretions. Then, the mNGS technique was applied, and intraoperative tissue bacterial culture was performed. Among the 88 patients, the preoperative bacterial culture of 10 patients was negative. During the operation, all effective positive results were detected by the mNGS technique. However, the intraoperative bacterial culture results of five patients were still negative. The intraoperative mNGS test results of all samples were obtained within 48 h, while the average bacterial culture results were obtained after 5–7 days, which was relatively consistent with the experimental data of Hao Linjie [44]. The mNGS technique can be used to quickly and accurately detect bacteria; therefore, it provides great clinical significance and value for treatment.

For patients with intramedullary infection, debridement is different from that for infection at other sites. Complete removal of the medullary cavity lesion is required with the use of a medullary expansion device. For patients with intramedullary infection, RIA (Reamamer-Irrigator-Aspirator) is recommended for medullary cavity debridement [45]. It can thoroughly remove necrotic bone tissue while also performing sufficient drainage and flushing. It can also prevent fat embolism and thermal necrosis of normal bone tissue in the medullary cavity caused by excessive pressure and temperature in the medullary cavity [46]. Compared to the more common use of a medullary expansion device combined with distal fenestration drainage, it has better therapeutic effects and more practical use. However, as it is expensive, it was not applied in this study. Future studies can promote its application.

This study had several limitations. First, the sample size of this experiment was relatively small, the follow-up time was relatively short, and the data were prone to bias. Second, surgical bias caused by different physicians is inevitable. The infection sites of the experimental sample data did not involve the joint cavities.

Conclusion

Chronic osteomyelitis is characterized by recurrence, persistence, and poor prognosis. The application of antibiotic bone cement carbon fiber rods combined with antibiotic calcium sulfate technology in the treatment of patients with chronic osteomyelitis related to bone infection after proximal femoral PFNA surgery can effectively reduce the inflammatory response and pain, control deep bone infection, lower the postoperative recurrence rate, and achieve adequate clinical effects.

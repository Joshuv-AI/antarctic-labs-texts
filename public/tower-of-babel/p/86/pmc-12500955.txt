Chaotic, rational and irrational oscillations of nonlinear waves in the Earth’s magnetosheath featuring bi-regularized κ-distributed electrons
Authors: Jit Sarkar, Asit Saha
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe magnetosheath of Earth plays a crucial role in shaping the plasma composition of Earth’s magnetosphere. It is instrumental in controlling the thermal properties of magnetospheric plasma and is fundamental to the dynamic processes that govern the evolution of the magnetosphere. Nonlinear solitary waves are frequently observed across diverse regions of Earth’s magnetosphere, utilizing Wideband Data (WBD) plasma wave receivers aboard Cluster Wave Experiment Consortium (WEC). Previous and current space missions have enabled the meticulous observation and analysis of the characteristics of solitary waves, including their amplitude, density, temperature, and temporal durations of waves. In this study, we explore a magnetized plasma system encompassing an inertial ion fluid alongside electron populations characterized by regularized κ distribution, exhibiting two distinct temperature profiles (cold & hot). Such two distinct electron profiles, alongside data derived from a multitude of observations and laboratory experiments, have been meticulously integrated into the theoretical model presented in this article, aiming to enhance our comprehension of the Earth’s magnetosheath region. A nonlinear equation named Laedke–Spatschek equation is derived using perturbation framework for the plasma system. The examination focuses on propagating plane waves to explore phase-space dynamics. A magnetic field adds complexity to nonlinear wave dynamics, leading to intricate phase-space behaviour. These nonlinear waves exhibit various structures, including chaotic, quasi-periodic (irrational), multi-periodic (rational), and periodic (rational) oscillations, depending on supra-thermal indices and magnetic field strength. Additionally, our research delves into the synergistic effects that arise from the interplay between magnetic field strength, ion concentration level, and the dynamics of high-energy electron populations, all of which contribute to the overall wave dynamics. The findings from our model are anticipated to provide significant insights into not only the Earth’s magnetosheath but also broader astrophysical environments where similar conditions may occur.Subject terms: Plasma physics, Magnetospheric physics

Introduction

Magnetosheaths are significant contributors to the plasma composition within the planetary magnetosphere; they play a vital role in regulating the thermal conditions of magnetospheric plasma and are essential to the dynamic evolution of the magnetosphere. Originating from the solar corona, the solar wind interacts with the natural magnetic fields of the planet and hence influences the structure of the magnetosphere1,2. When the energetic solar wind collides with the magnetosphere, it generates bow shocks that envelop them in conical surface shapes. The bow shocks serve to compress the solar wind that approaches from upstream, leading to the creation of magnetosheath plasmas located in the region between the bow shocks and the magnetopause. The magnetosheath plasmas attain maximum density and temperature at the stagnation point, followed by a gradual decrease in both parameters in the course of downstream3–5. So, the thermal characteristics of the magnetosheath plasmas are anticipated to be significantly associated with the characteristics of the solar wind. The plasma parameters observed in the magnetosheath exhibit significant large-scale spatial organization, which is influenced by the configuration of the magnetopause, alongside variability that is contingent upon solar wind input. The direction of the interplanetary magnetic field with respect to the bow shock’s local normal determines the bow shock’s features, which in turn influences the magnetosheath plasma’s characteristics just behind the bow shock6. The magnetosheath typically exhibits a greater level of turbulence when located behind the parallel bow shock, in contrast to the conditions found in the perpendicular bow shock zone. Furthermore, the plasma characteristics within the magnetosheath are influenced by various upstream solar wind properties, such as density, velocity, and the ratio of the thermal plasma pressure to the magnetic field pressure (β). In the vast expanse of space and the intricate realms of astrophysical plasmas, one often encounters a quasi-Maxwellian particle distribution function, distinguished by its non-Maxwellian supra-thermal tails7. The behaviour of these super-thermal particles is elegantly described by the spectral index, known as the κ-distribution function. The κ-distribution, in its fundamental essence, was initially proposed by Olbert8 and also Vasyliunas9 to elegantly characterize space plasma phenomena. The standard Kappa distribution (SKD) is distinguished by its significant high-energy tail, which sets it apart from the well-known Maxwellian distribution (MD). The primary moments of the velocity distribution are expected to provide a macroscopic description of non-equilibrium plasmas in the same way that a typical Maxwellian distribution does for thermally equilibrium plasmas. However, the moments associated with a κ-distribution remain inadequately defined for any value of κ16. In the realm of SKD, the kinetic temperature (pressure) parameter (TK) is censurable, as it is derived directly from the second-order moment (l=2), a condition that only stabilizes when κ is greater than 3/2. When temperature is regarded as a fitting parameter, any observations failing to satisfy this criterion are elegantly excluded from the analysis17, which may lead to interpretations that are questionable or even far-fetched. In SKD, the variable θj is a function of κ through the equation θ=2kBTK/mj1-3/2/κ. The relationship is derived from the definition of kinetic temperature, expressed as TK=∫ℑkmVj2dVj/2kB, which should remain invariant with respect to variations in κ18. Due to the exponential cut-off in the MD, all isotropic velocity moments, represented as 4π∫ℑM(Vj)Vj(l+2)dVj, remain finite. However, this is not the case for the SKD, where 4π∫ℑκ(Vj)Vj(l+2)dVj is only finite if (2κ+1>l)16.

This issue regarding the divergence of velocity moments related to the SKD is acknowledged. Specifically, for any given κ-index value, only a limited number of the lowest-order velocity moments are clearly defined, while higher-order moments eventually diverge. To counter such drawbacks of SKD, Scherer et al.18 proposed a modified form of the κ-distribution, a power-law distribution adjusted to include an exponential cut-off at higher velocities, named as regularized κ distribution (RKD)

Furthermore, the presence of RKD electrons significantly influences both the width and amplitude of these solitary waves. Furthermore, the existence conditions for the IASW do not exhibit a unique characteristic across the entire range of κ>0. In recent years, extensive theoretical and experimental research has focused on the study of electrostatic solitary waves (ESWs) in a variety of plasma settings. Over the previous few decades, these waves have been described using words such as electrostatic solitary structures (ESS), isolated electrostatic structures (IES), and solitary bipolar pulses (BP)27. Their existence has been documented in space plasmas since the early 1980s. Advanced electric field measurements conducted on contemporary spacecraft across various areas of near-Earth space have uncovered the existence of electrostatic solitary waves. These waves are localized electrostatic formations characterized by a typically bipolar electric field aligned with the local magnetic field28,29. Prior to the acquisition of these high-resolution measurements, there was merely a subtle sign of solitary waves, which appeared as broadband electrostatic fluctuations in the spectral data30–33. Such variations and their associated solitary waves have been observed in areas such as the Earth’s magnetopause34,35, magnetotail36,37, auroral zones38,39, magnetosheath40,41, near the foreshock42–46, and within the solar wind47,48. Additionally, EAWs have been observed in laboratory settings49,50. In the contexts of the magnetopause and magnetotail, these waves are frequently linked to magnetic reconnection, a process that creates the unstable electron distributions and/or intense currents necessary for the formation of electrostatic solitary waves. ESWs are identified within the electric field waveform data collected from satellites, manifesting as distinct sinusoidal patterns. There are three distinct categories of these patterns. The first type is unipolar pulses, which are defined by a single peak in either a positive or negative electric field. The second type, bipolar pulses, is marked by a one-cycle waveform that presents a positive peak followed by a negative peak, or vice versa. The third category is tri-polar pulses, which consist of continuous waves that display two positive electric field peaks separated by a negative peak, or the reverse arrangement. Each of these pulse types has unique characteristics that differentiate them from one another, making them significant in various applications. Understanding these classifications is essential for analyzing electric field behaviours and their implications in different contexts of plasma physics27. These waves are classified as nonlinear based on their different features as individual pulses in waveform data. Analysis of waveform data from the S3-3 spacecraft produced important results on tiny amplitude unipolar pulses. These data were interpreted as evidence of two layers within Earth’s auroral acceleration area, suggesting an alignment of electric and magnetic fields51. It is worth mentioning that a clear net potential elevation was identified within these structures. Moreover, the S3-3 data highlighted the presence of solitary waves that manifested as bipolar pulses in alignment with the magnetic field, which did not exhibit any net potential alteration across them27. The findings presented by Boström et al.52 and Koskinen et al.53 have validated the existence of double layers and solitary waves along auroral field lines. These observations, which align with the S3-3 data, demonstrated negative potentials and were understood as ion holes moving along the magnetic field lines at velocities similar to those of ion acoustic waves or ion beams. The magnetosheath surrounding Earth serves as a dynamic environment for exploring plasma turbulence. Observations indicate that the high-frequency fluctuations in the electric field are predominantly made up of electrostatic solitary waves characterized by bipolar parallel electric fields. This region acts as a natural laboratory, allowing researchers to delve into the complexities of plasma behaviour. The presence of these solitary waves provides valuable insights into the underlying mechanisms of turbulence, enhancing our understanding of space weather phenomena. The solitary waves in the magnetosheath capture attention due to their notably brief pulse durations, which are much shorter compared to those observed in other areas surrounding Earth41,54. The characteristics of these isolated pulses highlight significant differences between the near-Earth and distant regions of the magnetosheath. While the near-Earth environment is marked by shorter pulse durations (1 to 2 milliseconds), the distant magnetosheath presents a different scenario with longer-lasting solitary waves55. However, it seems unlikely that instruments on Geotail could have captured solitary waves lasting between ∼10 to ∼100 microseconds, given that its sampling frequency was 12 kHz and its bandwidth ranged from 10 Hz to 4 kHz37,55. Nonlinear phenomena, including holes and solitons, are associated with the mixing of plasma that occurs during reconnection at the magnetopause56. Observations gathered from the Magnetospheric Multi Scale (MMS) mission and the Cluster spacecraft have consistently pointed to waves that are related to cold ions and electrons, which experience acceleration as they enter the reconnection zone57,58. Recently, Graham et al.59 employed single-spacecraft interferometry to determine the velocities of ESWs in the magnetosheath. They concluded that in cases of asymmetric reconnection, the mixing of various plasma populations near reconnection sites likely drives the generation of electrostatic waves through mechanisms such as two-stream or Buneman instabilities. The scarcity of knowledge regarding solitary waves in the Earth’s magnetosheath and discrepancies among the earlier research has inspired our investigation. It is quite challenging to decipher the myriad wave modes present in the magnetosheath, as the spectra–whether at low or high frequencies–are predominantly influenced by turbulence or turbulence-like characteristics in both the electric and magnetic wave fields. Nevertheless, advancements in identifying wave modes through low-frequency measurements in the magnetosheath have yielded some astonishing findings, particularly through the application of wave telescopes and k-filtering techniques60,61.

The exploration of IAWs within plasmas characterized by hot electrons stands as a captivating case study in the evolution of plasma physics. The foundational principles of this field were meticulously laid out through the rigorous theoretical contributions of Fried and Gould62, whose derivation of the modified dispersion relation for multi-component plasmas clarified previous misunderstandings regarding wave propagation in such environments. This theoretical construct received its inaugural experimental validation through the precise Q-machine measurements conducted by Wong et al.63, whose findings unequivocally illustrated the anticipated wave characteristics. In this period, the discipline progressed remarkably through concurrent advancements in both theory and empirical observation. Nishikawa and Wu64 delivered the definitive analysis of instability due to trapped electrons, while the spacecraft measurements by Gurnett and Frank65 provided the first clear detection of these waves in space plasmas. A pivotal comprehension of bi-Maxwellian distributions was achieved through the thorough examination by Feldman et al.66, whose research addressed prior discrepancies in solar wind observations. On the other hand studies on laser-generated plasmas show that high-energy ions can carry a large part of the absorbed laser energy. To understand the mechanisms behind these fast charged particles, researchers67–69 analyzed experimental ion-velocity spectra against theoretical predictions of plasma free expansion with uniform electron temperature. X-ray emission measurements at laser intensities of 1018 to 1020W/m2 show that absorption processes create a non-Maxwellian electron velocity distribution with a high-velocity tail70,71. This distribution combines a hot and a colder Maxwellian distribution, characterized by two distinct electron temperatures71. The electrons in the tail are thought to accelerate fast ions72. In order to examine the impact of a two-temperature electron-velocity distribution function on plasma ablation, Wickens et al.73 formulated an analytical theory employing similarity-transformation techniques. Jones et al.74 demonstrated that a small presence of lower-temperature electrons can dominate ion acoustic wave propagation in plasma system. When the processes generating these two electron types occur much faster than the ion time scale, they can be treated as separate fluids. Subsequently, Goswami and Buti75 conducted a study on small yet finite amplitude solitary structures grounded in fluid models. Subsequently, Buti76 analytical contributed accurately quantified ion-acoustic hole formation more likely than solitons, while Joyce et al.77 established the numerical framework for Vlasov-Poisson simulations pertinent to these systems. The derivation of the KdV equation for two-electron plasmas by Nishihara and Tajiri78 established a vital connection between theoretical constructs and observable nonlinear phenomena. And, Ikezawa79 reported observation of ESWs in two temperature plasma. Gary and Tokar80 quantified damping rates, revealing that these rates are suppressed when cold electrons are in the majority. Meanwhile, Bharuthram and Shukla81 derived modified Korteweg–de Vries for an ion?acoustic double layer (DL) in a plasma with two temperature electrons. This decade also marked the first confirmations of these theories from space, particularly through the research of Baumjohann and Treumann82 on magnetospheric plasmas and Maksimovic et al.83 using data from the Ulysses spacecraft to confirm the widespread existence of two-temperature electrons in the solar wind. Umeda et al.84 used Particle-in-Cell techniques to show that slow modes, driven by cold electrons, coexist with fast modes influenced by hot electrons. Merlino et al.85 took a practical approach by experimenting with compressive and rarefactive solitons in dusty plasmas. The Cluster mission (Pierrard et al.86) and Wind satellite (Saito at al.87) significantly advanced space plasma research, revealing intricate connections between IAW activity and suprathermal electrons. In recent years, Lakhina et al.88 investigated a three-component plasma system with ions, cold and hot electrons. They discovered solitary wave solutions that emerge when Mach numbers surpass certain critical thresholds. Notably, the critical Mach numbers for ion-acoustic solitons are found to be lower than those for electron-acoustic solitons, contingent upon specific plasma conditions. Baluku and Hellberg89 expanded the theoretical framework with two-temperature kappa-distributed electrons and Saha and Chatterjee89 reported new analytical solution in an unmagnetized plasma with kappa distributed electrons. Recent advances in IAW research have been characterized by multi-mission observational campaigns and high-fidelity simulations. The Solar Orbiter mission, through its Solar Wind Analyser measurements90, has provided the first direct evidence of electron-acoustic wave modulation by suprathermal electron populations in the inner heliosphere. Concurrently, sophisticated simulations by Vasko et al.91 have revealed new insights into nonlinear wave steepening processes in two-electron-temperature plasmas, though the formation of stable double-layers remains theoretically contested. According to our research on Earth‘s magnetosheath zone and literature survey, researchers have not elucidated the nonlinear phenomenon focusing on the case of magnetised electron-ion (e-i) plasma system having regularized κ-distributed two types of electrons in Earth‘s magnetosheaths.

In this article, we seek to investigate the presence of IAWs in the magnetosheath region of Earth and to explore how various plasma parameters affect the dynamic characteristics of nonlinear trajectories. To achieve this, we first present our Theoretical Model for Earth’s magnetosheath realm. This section presents the ensemble of fluid equations governing classical ions, alongside thermally equilibrated electrons and their colder counterparts. Both electron categories adhere to the principles outlined by the RKD framework. In the subsequent section titled Derivation of Laedke–Spatschek equation, we employ the reductive perturbation technique to derive nonlinear equations, enabling a comprehensive analysis of the characteristics inherent to our adapted system. Thereafter, our attention shifts to the Stability analysis of ion-acoustic waves, where we utilize the transformation of the travelling wave frame. It is important to examine both the characteristics and the domains of existence of small amplitude IAWs, which is explored in the sections titled Linear stability analysis and Chaotic, quasi-periodic, and periodic nature of ion-acoustic waves. Ultimately, the section titled Summery and conclusion serves to encapsulate the primary findings and draw a conclusive reflection on the discourse.

Chaotic, quasi-periodic, and periodic nature of ion-acoustic waves

In the preceding section titled “Theoretical Model”, we have elucidated the fundamental fluid model pertaining to the excitation of ion-acoustic waves (IAWs). However, it is essential to explore the realms of validity for the overarching theory, alongside the existence domains of small amplitude IAWs, which will be examined in the following section. Within the fluid model, the inertia of electrons has been disregarded, given that the mass of electrons is significantly less than the mass of ions. This assumption holds true when the particle number density remains considerably below the critical threshold and the particle temperature does not reach excessively high levels.

To ascertain the precise data or values of plasma parameters within Earth’s magnetosheath region, we depend on the observation of various spacecraft. These space vessels have meticulously documented the presence of various acoustic wave types. As highlighted by Pickett et al.94, a significant portion of the recent data is extracted primarily from observations conducted by Geotail and S3-3. However, the Cluster mission has significantly enhanced our understanding of wave propagation characteristics and properties, owing to its high-bandwidth instruments and superior resolution capabilities. The analysis of Cluster Mission data has unveiled the presence of electron populations within the magnetosheath region, each exhibiting unique characteristics such as temperature and spectral profiles54,94. Noteworthy attributes have been documented, including parametric values viz. ni0≈24/cm3, nc0≈16/cm3, nh0≈8/cm3, T~c≈39-45eV, T~h≈79-84eV, 20μT≲B0≲65μT, and among others. Summery and conclusion

The nonlinearity inherent in a physical system reveals itself through the emergence of coherent and complex structures. In this study, we explore the influence of plasma parameters on the propagation dynamics of three-dimensional nonlinear IAWs in the Earth’s magnetosheath. This plasma system comprises ions and electrons with supra-thermal distributions, all analysed through the lens of the Laedke–Spatschek equation (21). In order to transcend certain physical and mathematical limitations, we have elegantly integrated regularized κ-distribution in place of the conventional κ-distribution. This refinement has afforded us the opportunity to explore scenarios where the supra-thermal index value falls below 3/2. Using tools, we delve this equation at its most basic level, posing the issue as a 4- dimensional entirely nonlinear dynamical system in the setting of travelling waves. The results show that the finite nonlinear wave exhibits a wide variety of nonlinear structures when exposed to a magnetic field. The intricate characteristics of chaos, quasi-periodicity (irrational), multi-periodicity (rational), and periodicity (rational) in IAWs, particularly within the confines of small-amplitude limits, are meticulously examined in a conservative system. A multitude of dynamic characteristics is noted to undergo significant changes as the travelling wave velocity (u), supra-thermal indices (κc,κh), temperature ratio (δ), and the magnetic field (B0) are adjusted. The analysis of the Lyapunov exponents in relation to B0 indicates that the system exhibits chaotic behaviour at different values of B0 in the initial conditions near the origin. In this investigation, we document the existence of chaos under conditions of both weak and relatively strong magnetic field strength. As per our knowledge, chaotic behaviour of nonlinear waves in equivalent plasma system and environment is not documented for weak magnetic field.

Consequently, the findings of this study hold great promise for enhancing our comprehension of the intricate phenomena encountered in both laboratory settings and astrophysical contexts.

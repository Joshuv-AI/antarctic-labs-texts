Refining Approaches to Fibrosis Control in Kidney Transplant Recipients
Authors: Haiyang Wang, Tao Liu, Meicai Li
Published: 2025

To the Editor:

We have read with great interest the recently published multicenter randomized clinical trial by Ville et al.1 This study innovatively employed automated quantitative methods to compare the changes in interstitial fibrosis within 1 year between low immune risk recipients under a corticosteroid-free regimen and those under a standard regimen, confirming the noninferiority of the corticosteroid-free regimen in terms of fibrosis progression. This study provides new evidence for optimizing immunosuppressive strategies and clinical risk assessment for corticosteroid withdrawal. However, we believe there are several key issues that merit further clarification and in-depth discussion.

First, initial fibrosis, ischemia-reperfusion injury, and donor type are key prognostic factors affecting the long-term fibrosis of grafts.2 However, this study analyzes different risk subgroups with biological heterogeneity without stratifying the unique risks of each subgroup. Such an analytical approach may, to some extent, obscure the potential adverse effects of hormone-free regimens in high-risk subgroups.

Second, although this study employs automated quantitative methods to reduce subjective bias, its reliability in complex pathological contexts remains to be further validated, which may affect the interpretation of the results. The study suggests that the accuracy of automated algorithms may be compromised in the presence of confounding factors such as inflammatory infiltration.3 Given the current insufficient systematic validation, it is recommended that future assessments of this method be conducted in diverse pathological backgrounds to enhance the robustness of the research conclusions.

In addition, there appears to be some imbalance in a key baseline variable, interstitial fibrosis; with the corticosteroid-free group exhibiting a slightly higher initial degree of fibrosis (P = 0.078 in the per-protocol population). Given that baseline injury may affect subsequent disease trajectories, this difference could confound the interpretation of fibrosis progression. Therefore, caution is warranted when attributing differences in fibrosis outcomes solely to the immunosuppressive regimen.

In conclusion, despite areas that still require improvement, the study by Ville et al.1 provides important evidence for the management of fibrosis following kidney transplantation with hormone-free regimens, thereby promoting the optimization of individualized immunosuppressive strategies.

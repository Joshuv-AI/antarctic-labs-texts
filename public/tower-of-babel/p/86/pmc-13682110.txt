The LEN4PrEP study: protocol for a phase 3B, non-randomised clinical trial integrating lenacapavir PrEP within multi-product HIV prevention services in primary care clinics in South Africa
Authors: Roisin E Drysdale, Catherine E Martin, Mrinmayee Dhar, Siphokazi Dada, Maletsatsi Monametsi, Nthabiseng Koloane, Vusile Butler, Cornelius Nattey, Saiqa Mullick
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionDespite the scale-up of HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP), HIV incidence remains high. The highly effective, twice-yearly subcutaneous injectable lenacapavir for PrEP (L4P) has provided a potential breakthrough for HIV prevention, but there is limited real-world data on uptake, patterns of use and service delivery requirements when offered in public health settings. The LEN4PrEP study will evaluate the integration of L4P within existing multi-product HIV prevention services in real-world settings in South Africa.MethodsThe study is a prospective, mixed-methods, phase 3B clinical trial utilizing a non-randomised, open-label, effectiveness-implementation hybrid type 3 design. The study will take place in two fixed primary healthcare facilities and one linked roving mobile clinic in the Tshwane region, South Africa. Clients aged 16–65 years accessing comprehensive sexual and reproductive health services at the sites will be invited to enrol in the prospective cohort and offered a choice of L4P or oral PrEP. Clinical and socio-behavioural data will be collected over 12 months, with participants free to switch, discontinue and restart either PrEP product for the duration of the study. Ongoing safety monitoring will be conducted, with pregnancy and birth outcomes captured from pregnant participants, as well as HIV drug resistance monitoring in those who seroconvert. In-depth qualitative data will be collected from a sub-set of cohort participants to understand reasons for L4P use, as well as from healthcare providers involved in L4P delivery to explore health system requirements for implementation. Outcomes of the study will be guided by the RE-AIM framework and include patterns of use (uptake, adherence, persistence, continuation, switching, re-initiation, discontinuation), effectiveness (pregnancy and birth outcomes, seroconversions, adverse events) and facilitators and barriers to implementation.DiscussionThe LEN4PrEP study will provide real-world data on L4P implementation when offered within existing HIV prevention services in public health settings in South Africa, offering a more generalisable reflection on use, service delivery, effectiveness and safety. The data generated can be used alongside programmatic data to inform demand forecasting, training and planning for the national roll-out of L4P.Trial registrationSouth African National Clinical Trials Registry, DOH-27-102025-4927 (https://sanctr.samrc.ac.za/TrialDisplay.aspx?TrialID=13831). Background

Much progress has been made in the prevention of HIV and in the scale-up of HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP), however, HIV incidence remains high. Although 8 million people globally had initiated PrEP by the end of 2024 [1], the number continues to fall short of the 2025 target of 21.2 million people. Whilst Eastern and Southern Africa have shown progress towards reaching their PrEP targets, increased access to PrEP is needed to reduce new HIV acquisitions, particularly among key populations and women in high HIV incidence settings [2]. Long-acting PrEP products with different administration routes and less frequent dosing offer expanded product choice, potential for easier access, more effective use, and flexible delivery channels. Injectable PrEP is a game-changing opportunity for HIV prevention, and the novel, twice-yearly subcutaneous injectable lenacapavir for HIV PrEP (L4P) is a potential breakthrough for HIV prevention efforts [2]. L4P has been found to be safe and highly effective in preventing HIV among cisgender women, with no new infections seen among the 2134 participants receiving L4P in the PURPOSE 1 trial among 16–25-year-old women in Uganda and South Africa [3]. Similarly, L4P reduced HIV infections by 96% compared to background HIV incidence among men who have sex with men, gay men, transgender women, transgender men and gender non-binary people in the PURPOSE 2 trial [4].

Early evidence from implementation studies indicates people’s preference for injectable PrEP products, suggesting that injectable PrEP could increase overall PrEP coverage [5, 6]. Preliminary data from Project PrEP, a Unitaid-funded implementation science study which has generated evidence on PrEP choice in public sector clinics, indicates that when clients were offered a choice of PrEP products (long-acting injectable Cabotegravir [CAB-LA], oral PrEP and dapivirine vaginal ring [DVR]), uptake of CAB-LA was higher than the other PrEP products, as was continued use [7], highlighting the potential injectable products may have to improve overall PrEP coverage. Existing HIV prevention options have some known challenges, such as daily pill burden, side effects and stigma [8, 9]. The convenience, discretion and high efficacy of injectable products may overcome some of these challenges, and a 6-monthly injectable could further these benefits and reduce the requirements for frequent follow-up visits.

Learnings from CAB-LA introduction, such as the acceptability of injectable products, how to deliver and integrate injectable products within existing HIV prevention services, and the feasibility of delivering injectable PrEP through mobile services, can be leveraged to fast-track L4P introduction. However, due to differences in administration route and injection frequency compared with CAB-LA, the uptake and use of L4P when offered within existing multi-product PrEP services remains unknown. This data is critical for health departments to ensure accurate demand forecasting and planning.

In addition, understanding specific implementation and integration requirements for L4P, such as demand and uptake, effective delivery channels, successful integration models, provider training and health systems requirements in real-world settings, and with community input, is essential to supporting national planning, fast-tracking introduction at scale and ensuring sustainable programming [10]. Specific implications include provider training and job aides, community- and client-facing information materials and implementation tools, along with provider and end-user feedback on the feasibility and acceptability of the L4P loading and dosing schedule. Evidence drawn from early implementation studies will also be critical in informing global and national guidelines for L4P, training materials and resources, as well as key information and educational materials and messaging for communities, healthcare providers (HCPs) and end-users.

Further, ongoing monitoring for general and rare adverse events, pregnancy and maternal outcomes and HIV drug resistance among PrEP users who seroconvert is critical to supporting ongoing safety monitoring and confidence in the product among HCPs, end-users and the broader community. Understanding HIV testing strategies (e.g., the need for baseline Viral vload / HIV RNA testing) in real-world settings which maximize access, whilst minimizing risks of misdiagnosis is also required [10].

Evidence of L4P uptake, continuation as well as switching to and from L4P in the context of multi-product PrEP services, is also required to refine demand forecasts and volume projections to be shared with manufacturers and inform market and demand generation strategies including implementation planning. This will inform global as well as national guidance.

To address these gaps, the LEN4PrEP study will evaluate the integration of L4P into existing multi-product HIV prevention services in real-world settings in South Africa.

Study design

The study is a prospective, mixed-methods, phase 3B clinical trial utilizing a non-randomised, open-label, effectiveness-implementation hybrid type 3 design [11]. The study comprises the following components:

Prospective cohort of clients accessing sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services, and offered a choice of HIV prevention products.

Prospective cohort of clients accessing sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services, and offered a choice of HIV prevention products.

Nested qualitative sub-study among a sub-set of cohort participants to explore end-user values and preferences for L4P.

Nested qualitative sub-study among a sub-set of cohort participants to explore end-user values and preferences for L4P.

Qualitative sub-study among HCPs to explore values and preferences for delivering L4P across different delivery models.

Qualitative sub-study among HCPs to explore values and preferences for delivering L4P across different delivery models.

Monitoring and evaluation of routinely collected clinical data among healthcare users accessing HIV prevention and related services.

Setting

The study will be conducted in two primary healthcare (PHC) facilities and one linked mobile clinic in the Tshwane health district of Gauteng, South Africa. The area spans two distinct residential urban township settings that have a high prevalence of HIV, teenage pregnancy and sexually transmitted infections. The facilities were purposively selected in consultation with the National Department of Health (NDoH) as they have well-established oral PrEP services and experience in delivering multi-product PrEP services, having previously participated in implementation science research introducing DVR and CAB-LA [12]. The mobile clinic provides services in communities surrounding the PHC facilities, with a focus on services linked to institutions of higher education.

Population

Prospective cohort

The cohort will enrol approximately 2000 individuals aged between 16 and 65 years who are interested in HIV prevention services. At the time of enrolment, they must be HIV-negative, confirmed through a rapid point-of-care antibody test conducted in accordance with the latest national HIV testing services (HTS) guidelines [13]. Those currently enrolled in any other interventional research study will not be eligible for participation. Groups of interest will include adolescent girls and young women (AGYW) aged 16–24 years, men, women aged ≥ 25 years, and pregnant and breastfeeding populations (PBFP).

Participants will be recruited from clients accessing routine health services from participating fixed and mobile clinics. Initial information on the study will be provided by trained study staff through group or one-on-one health talks in designated waiting areas. Potential participants will be consecutively approached and if interested, screened for study eligibility by trained fieldworkers. Recruitment into the cohort will take place over a period of approximately six months.

Sample size considerations

The primary objective of this study is to determine the uptake of L4P when provided within an integrated, multi-product HIV prevention service package. The primary comparisons of product uptake will be restricted to participants who are new initiators of PrEP, since the inclusion of experienced users with established preferences or dislikes would bias comparisons with the newly available product. Recognising this restriction, we will have 90% power to detect a 20% difference in L4P uptake (60% L4P vs. 40% other products) based on chi-squared goodness of fit tests conducted at the 0.05 significance level, provided there are at least 260 new PrEP initiators. Should L4P uptake be lower, we will still have 80% power to detect a 10% difference in L4P uptake (55% L4P vs. 45% other products) based on chi-squared goodness of fit tests conducted at the 0.05 significance level, provided there are at least 784 new PrEP initiators. Based on similar research conducted in the same study sites, we expect approximately 47% of our sample to be new PrEP initiators [7, 12]. A total sample size of 2000, inclusive of experienced and new PrEP users, will ensure we enrol enough new PrEP initiators to power our study. This sample size will also allow for us to meet the secondary objectives of the study, including obtaining sufficient healthcare user experience with provision of L4P to assess the training needs and service delivery requirements to effectively integrate L4P within existing HIV prevention services.

Nested qualitative study with cohort participants

A subset of approximately 20–40 cohort participants will be invited to participate in in-depth interviews (IDIs). Participants will be purposively sampled to ensure representation of new PrEP users who choose or decline L4P, and current PrEP users who switch to or decline L4P. Participants who discontinue L4P will also be invited to participate. We will aim to select participants from various age and gender groups, as well as groups of interest, to ensure diverse perspectives are captured.

Qualitative sub-study among healthcare providers

Healthcare providers aged ≥ 18 years working at study sites and involved in the delivery of L4P will be invited to participate in IDIs. This includes but is not limited to nurses, doctors, HTS lay counsellors, peer educators, community health workers and facility managers. We will aim to conduct serial IDIs with up to 25 HCPs one month after they receive L4P training and approximately six months after cohort enrolment begins. The final number of participants and IDIs will be dependent on data saturation and participant availability. Participants will be purposively selected to ensure representation across cadres.

Intervention

After enrolling into the cohort, participants will consult with a trained study nurse and receive counselling on available HIV prevention and PrEP products. Following this, they may choose between either (1) L4P or (2) oral PrEP. Participants may also choose to decline a PrEP product. To support participants’ decision-making, PrEP choice counselling will be provided by the study nurse in a factual, standardized manner supported through the use of counselling job aids aligned to national guidelines [14]. Counselling covers the following attributes of each product: product type, efficacy, discretion, monitoring requirements, use during pregnancy and breastfeeding, side effects, type of protection (oral, anal, vaginal sex), protection against sexually transmitted infections (STIs) and pregnancy. PrEP choice counselling also includes information about other HIV prevention options such as condoms. Trained study nurses will assess PrEP product eligibility and administer each product in accordance with clinical guidelines [14, 15]. For the purpose of the LEN4PrEP study, participants will be able to choose between four injection sites; namely the abdomen, buttocks, arm and/or thigh. They may select a different injection site at subsequent visits. Participants may choose to switch or discontinue their chosen PrEP product throughout the study, including L4P. Participants who (re)initiate L4P during the study will be requested to provide a blood sample for viral load testing. Participants who choose to (re)initiate oral PrEP will only have required blood tests as outlined in clinical guidelines [15].

In addition to HIV prevention and PrEP services, all participants will be offered a comprehensive SRH package which includes STI syndromic screening and management, pregnancy screening, contraceptive services, gender-based violence (GBV), substance use and mental health screening, with referral and linkage to care if required.

Data collection

Prospective cohort

Participant follow-up will occur for a minimum of 12 months. As outlined in Fig. 1, clinical data will be collected at enrolment and at clinic follow-up visits, aligned with their PrEP product as per clinical guidelines [14, 15]. Data captured will include experience of PrEP side effects, provision of contraception, STI symptoms, test results (if conducted), and treatment (if provided), pregnancy and HIV test results. All participants, regardless of PrEP product choice, will complete a survey at enrolment and 1-, 6-, and 12-months post enrolment. Surveys may be completed within a 4-week window (before or after schedule) to align with their PrEP product clinic visit. Surveys will capture information on demographics, sexual behaviour, self-reported PrEP use, experience of side effects, PrEP preferences and include screening for mental health symptoms, GBV and substance use. Participants that (re)initiate a PrEP product will also be contacted telephonically for a week-1 check-in to assess their PrEP use, including whether they took the second dose of lenacapavir tablets, if applicable. In addition, they will be asked about any side effects they experienced and whether they sought or required medical assistance. Participants who miss a study visit will be contacted telephonically to explore reasons why and invite them to return to the study site to complete a survey. If a participant has chosen to discontinue PrEP, or did not start PrEP, they will not be required to complete a clinical visit but will still be requested to complete a survey as per the schedule. Data will be captured and securely stored using the Research Electronic Data Capture (REDCap) web-based platform [16, 17] with access limited to delegated study staff.

Cohort study data collection schedule

Participants who are pregnant at enrolment, or those who fall pregnant during the study will be referred to receive antenatal care through routinely provided DOH services at the study site. They will remain in the study and be able to receive PrEP, including L4P, in line with clinical guidelines [14, 15]. At the time of the pregnancy report, the estimated gestational age will be calculated based on the last menstrual period. For those who complete their pregnancy during the study follow-up period, data will be extracted from the routinely collected Maternity Case Record to document pregnancy and birth outcomes, including relevant dates, maternal history and complications, delivery method and complications, congenital abnormalities and overall infant health.

Participants with suspected or confirmed HIV seroconversions will immediately be advised to discontinue PrEP use, unless an oral PrEP user is diagnosed with chronic hepatitis B, who may continue to use tenofovir in consultation with a HCP. Participants with confirmed HIV seroconversion will have specimens taken to test for HIV viral load and HIV drug resistance and referred and linked to their nearest clinic for ART initiation.

Participants may freely choose to withdraw from the study at any time, will be withdrawn if they have a confirmed seroconversion, or may be withdrawn at the discretion of study investigators for safety or other reasons deemed necessary.

Nested qualitative study with cohort participants

Using a structured interview guide, IDIs will be conducted face-to-face by trained qualitative researchers at the facility, or local community-based organization (CBO), in a location that ensures visual and auditory privacy and will be approximately 45 minutes to 1 hour in duration. To ensure that language is not a barrier to communication and that participants feel comfortable expressing their experiences, interviews will be conducted in simple English, or a local language, by an interviewer fluent in the participant’s language of preference.

The IDIs will seek to capture reasons for L4P uptake and continuation, refusal, and barriers and facilitators of L4P use, as well as end-user values and preferences for L4P within a multi-product HIV prevention setting. IDIs will also explore healthcare users’ perceptions of L4P and compare the acceptability and feasibility of fixed and mobile service delivery models for L4P and what is required to integrate L4P into current HIV prevention services.

Qualitative sub-study among healthcare providers

IDIs will be conducted face-to-face by trained qualitative researchers in a private room at the facility or local CBO, or telephonically if face-to-face is not feasible, at a time convenient to the participant and that does not impact service delivery, and will be approximately 45 minutes to 1 hour in duration. To ensure that language is not a barrier to communication and that participants feel comfortable expressing their experiences, interviews will be conducted in simple English, or a local language, by an interviewer fluent in the participant’s language of preference.

These IDIs seek to explore HCPs’ values and preferences for delivering L4P and the perceived acceptability and feasibility of service delivery components when delivering L4P within existing HIV prevention and other related services (e.g., contraception, STI services). The facilitators, barriers, as well as the training, job aides, capacity building and implementation requirements for integrating L4P into multi-product HIV prevention services will also be explored. Two rounds of IDIs with HCPs will be conducted; the first within one month after HCPs receive training on L4P to obtain initial feedback on training and job aides. The second approximately 6 months after enrolment in the cohort study begins to gather further insights on implementation. IDIs will be serial in nature, involving the same HCPs at both time intervals to explore changes in experiences over time. Insights gathered will be used to inform ongoing training requirements, materials and job aids.

Given the limited timeframe that the study will be implemented, Maintenance will not be evaluated. Our general multivariate analytic approach will use log binomial regression models (for dichotomous outcome variables) and cox proportional hazards model (to model time-to-event variables). In all analyses, p-values will be two-sided and considered statistically significant at p < 0.05. Comparative analysis between fixed and mobile service delivery models will be conducted using multivariable regression methods. For time-to-event outcomes such as L4P interruption or discontinuation, Kaplan-Meier methods and cox proportional hazards regression will be used to estimate differences in persistence between service delivery models while adjusting for potential confounders Repeated measures analyses will be conducted using generalized estimating equations (GEE) with an exchangeable correlation structure to account for within-participant correlation over time.

Missing data

We anticipate some missing data due to missed study visits, participant withdrawal, incomplete survey responses, or unavailable clinical records. The extent and patterns of missing data will be assessed for all key variables. Where the proportion of missing data is minimal (< 5%), complete-case analyses will be conducted. For variables with substantial missingness, multiple imputation methods will be considered, assuming data are missing at random. Sensitivity analyses will be performed, where appropriate, to assess the impact of missing data on study findings. The number and proportion of missing observations will be reported for all primary outcomes and key covariates.

Qualitative data

All interviews will be audio-recorded, transcribed verbatim, and interviews conducted in a local language will be translated into English for data analysis. All transcripts will be reviewed for accuracy prior to data analysis. The data will be analysed using thematic analysis following Braun and Clarke’s six phases of thematic analysis procedures [20] and will be carried out in line with six phases of thematic analysis procedures. These are (i) Familiarisation – involving reading and re-reading of transcripts to familiarize and engage with the data; (ii) Generating initial codes – transcripts will be coded using inductive open coding to familiarise with the data and develop a codebook; (iii) Generating themes – codes will be combined to generate themes; (iv) Reviewing the themes – potential themes will be reviewed to ensure that data is sufficiently represented; (v) Defining and naming the themes – involves establishing clear definitions and names for each theme; and finally, (vi) Writing up the findings – themes will be integrated into a final report. NVivo 14 software will be used for data management and coding of the transcripts. The coding will be an iterative process. To enhance study trustworthiness, 20% of transcripts will be coded independently by three qualitative researchers, who will meet to discuss discrepancies and refine the codebook to reduce bias. The final codebook will be reviewed by the study team prior to coding the remaining transcripts.

Safety monitoring

Oral PrEP is available as standard of care at most public health clinics in South Africa, with safety monitoring conducted through the national pharmacovigilance programme. The safety of L4P has been confirmed through PURPOSE 1 and PURPOSE 2, with similar adverse events (AEs) reported in the control and intervention groups [3, 4]. The South African Health Product Regulatory Authority (SAHPRA) approved L4P for use in South Africa in 2025 [21], with national roll-out started in June 2026 [22]. The study will, however, monitor AEs and adverse drug reactions (ADRs) in all participants and grade them according to the Division of AIDS (DAIDS) Table for Grading the Severity of Adult and Pediatric Adverse Events (Corrected Version 2.1) [23]. Adjusted guidance for injection site reactions as recommended by the drug manufacturer, specifically “Injection Site Pain or Tenderness”, “Injection Site Induration or Swelling”, and “Injection Site Erythema or Redness” will be used. As injection sites include the abdomen and buttock, the limitation of the use of the limb was adjusted to “any study drug injection site”. The grade 1 assessment for induration or swelling and erythema or redness include reactions measuring less than 2.5 cm, contrary to the 2.5 cm lower limit cutoff per the DAIDS table. Care and treatment for reported AEs and ADRs will be provided by trained study clinicians and HCPs.

Quality control and quality assurance

Routine monitoring of the study will be conducted to ensure that the rights, safety and well-being of trial participants are protected and that the study is conducted in compliance with the approved protocol, latest Declaration of Helsinki, Guidelines for Good Practice in the Conduct of Clinical Trials in Human Participants, Good Clinical Practices, and applicable regulatory requirements. This will include real-time quality control (QC) checks on informed consent forms and L4P prescriptions, and routine quality assurance (QA) procedures to ensure data integrity and robust data collection. Monitoring activities will be conducted by study staff and supported through an independent Contract Research Organisation (CRO). These activities will include onsite source data verification, onsite/remote interim data review and the review of essential documentation.

Post-study access to care

Upon completion or discontinuation of the study, participants who choose to continue PrEP will transition to locally available PrEP products, which may include L4P. The study is being conducted in PHC facilities that offer comprehensive HIV prevention services, which include oral PrEP. These services will continue to be available once the study is complete. L4P was incorporated into national HIV prevention programming mid-2026 [22] and may be made available at study sites or other nearby facilities. At the last study visit, participants will be counselled on regular HIV testing and HIV prevention and provided with relevant information around the services they can continue to receive at the study sites once the study ends.

Dissemination

Community and stakeholder engagement activities are undertaken throughout study design and implementation, ensuring approaches are culturally and contextually relevant and that communities are partners in the research process. Community-based activities support information sharing on all available PrEP products, with a focus on creating demand for HIV prevention services broadly. They are implemented in collaboration with peers and local community-based organizations, leveraging online and social media platforms [24].

In addition, study data will be regularly reviewed with key national and international stakeholders, youth ambassadors, staff at implementing sites, local health care providers and community members through several platforms, including regular meetings, webinars and academic portals. Lessons and results will also be shared regionally and internationally through the preparation of conference abstracts and peer-reviewed journal articles.

Discussion

The LEN4PrEP study will evaluate the integration of L4P into existing HIV prevention services in real-world settings, collecting robust data on L4P use (uptake, adherence, persistence, continuation, discontinuation, interruption, switching) in a vulnerable population. In addition, the study will compare and assess the acceptability and feasibility of offering L4P in fixed and mobile service delivery models, whilst documenting training needs and service delivery requirements. The study will be one of the first to provide real-world data on L4P implementation, offering a more generalisable reflection on use, service delivery, effectiveness and safety [25], which can be used alongside programmatic data to ensure accurate demand forecasting and planning.

Additional real-world data collected through the study will include information on safety, pregnancies, and seroconversions that will complement that of the ongoing PURPOSE clinical trials [26]. Recent updates from the trials continue to highlight that L4P has high efficacy and an acceptable safety profile with only three new participants testing positive for HIV since inception. This includes two participants with correct L4P use, and another with historical use [27, 28]. The updates also indicate that the risk of developing HIV-drug resistance is rare, with few participants doing so after seroconversion [29]. These instances occurred under very controlled, clinical trial conditions with strict monitoring and follow-up schedules. Although still conducted under a research protocol, the study design of LEN4PrEP allows for the provision of insights around the safety and efficacy of L4P encountered in routine clinical practice, taking into consideration client everyday lives and challenges faced in resource-limited healthcare settings. In addition, due to its approval for use in pregnancy and breastfeeding, PBFPs will not be excluded from the study and may choose to use L4P at their own discretion, as they would in a routine clinical setting. The study will therefore capture and share additional data on pregnancy and maternal outcomes to complement findings from PURPOSE 1 [30]. In the context of HIV prevention, this is extremely important information that can be shared in real-time given the level of vulnerability PBFPs and their infants face [31].

Another strength of the study is the setting; existing public PHC facilities that experience similar day-to-day challenges faced across South Africa. These include limited staff, space constraints, stock shortages, water and electricity outages, and long patient waiting times. With the exception of staff and L4P products and ancillaries, the study will rely on the public infrastructure available at these facilities, including HIV testing, contraception and other medication, and laboratory services for non-study tests. Whilst we will attempt to mitigate any challenges faced during the study, the ability to record these health-system challenges in real-time and how they affect L4P implementation will be beneficial for planning as the national L4P roll-out is scaled. Furthermore, the study will utilise a linked mobile clinic and offer services in decentralised settings. Current evidence suggests that clients accessing HIV prevention services, including multi-product PrEP services, prefer such settings [32]. However, given that the multi-product PrEP market is new, the current evidence base mainly focuses on oral PrEP and the DVR [12], with limited information on the acceptability and feasibility of injectable PrEP, particularly L4P. Therefore, the information generated from LEN4PrEP will complement the existing knowledge base on decentralised and differentiated HIV prevention services.

Although the study has been designed to capture the real-world implementation of L4P, some research procedures cannot be avoided. These include procedures to ensure participant safety, collect robust data and maintain data integrity, such as extensive reimbursement for time and travel, follow-up for ADRs, telephonic week-1 check-ins to assess for side effects, and follow-up for missed visits. Whilst the reimbursements may act as an incentive to return to the facility on time, participants are free to decline PrEP, as well as switch, discontinue and restart PrEP, including L4P, at any time while enrolled, allowing for real-world use. Furthermore, the additional procedures and surveys will capture reasons for late or missed visits and changing patterns of use.

Conclusion

Despite the scale-up of oral PrEP and HIV prevention services in South Africa, HIV incidence remains high. Early evidence suggests preference for long-acting injectable PrEP products that may provide easier access, improved effective use and flexible delivery channels. However, there is limited real-world data on L4P uptake and use when offered within existing HIV prevention services, as well as the training and health system requirements for integrating a new, 6-monthly product. The LEN4PrEP study will capture this information, along with ongoing monitoring and safety data, taking into consideration participants’ day-to-day lives and real-world challenges. The data will be shared with national and international partners to support forecasting, planning and introduction to ensure sustainable programming.

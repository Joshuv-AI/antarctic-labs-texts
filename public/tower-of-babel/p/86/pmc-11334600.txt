Primary care performance measurement in Brazil (Previne Brasil Program), 2022–2023
Authors: Carlos Dornels Freire de Souza, André Luis Oliveira do Nascimento, Carlos Alberto José de Souza, Michael Ferreira Machado, Maria Deysiane Porto Araújo, Roberta de Albuquerque Wanderley, Carla Pacheco Teixeira, Rodrigo Feliciano do Carmo

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPrevine Brasil is a new way of financing Primary Health Care (PHC) in Brazil that places users of public health services at the center of the system’s analysis and attention through the use of seven performance indicators. Background

Primary Health Care (PHC) is the first level of public health care, representing the preferred gateway between users and the Health Care Network in the Unified Health System (SUS) [1]. The basic principles of PHC are first contact access, longitudinality of care, integrality of actions and coordination of care [2]. PHC’s priorities are health prevention and promotion, recovery and rehabilitation. It provides comprehensive care for various types of illnesses and diseases, and also has the task of referring users to more specialized health services [3].

The main model for PHC in Brazil was the Family Health Program (PSF) in the second half of the 1990s, governed by federal regulation and funding mechanisms. Policies to strengthen PHC, such as the transformation of the PSF into the Family Health Strategy in 2006 and the publication of the National Primary Care Policy in 2011, favored the implementation of SUS principles and guidelines, which made it possible to change the structure and management of health work in municipalities [4]. A significant part of the progress made in the SUS is due to the implementation and effective performance of PHC, through the approximately 45,796 family health teams made up of doctors, nurses, dentists, nursing technicians and community health agents [5].

Previne Brasil (PB) is a new way of financing Primary Health Care [6]. Promulgated in November 2019 through Ordinance No. 2,979 [6], this financing model underwent revisions to the pay-for-performance indicators in January 2022 with the aim of improving the accuracy of health facilities’ performance [7]. The PB establishes the PHC funding model within the SUS based on the following four components: (i) weighted capitation, (ii) payment for performance, (iii) incentive for strategic actions and (iv) financial incentive based on population criteria [8].

Using these seven indicators, the Final Synthetic Indicator (FSI) can be calculated, based on the weighting of the aforementioned indicators, presenting a value that works as a parameter for the transfer of resources to the municipalities, in reference to the Previne Brasil performance component [7].

Based on the heterogeneity of Brazil, there are significant political, social, economic and geographical disparities that have a direct impact on the quality of and adherence to public health services [9]. As a result, the impacts of these disparities influence the achievement of the targets of the seven Previne Brasil performance indicators by capitals, states, Brazilian regions and Brazil, with direct repercussions on the Final Synthetic Indicator and on the transfer of financial resources to the municipalities.

In this context, it is essential to understand the following problem: what is the performance of PHC in Brazil according to the PB in the interval between the first four months of 2022 and the first four months of 2023? The aim of this study is to analyze the performance of Primary Health Care in Brazil under the new financing model (Previne Brasil) between the first four months of 2022 and the first four months of 2023.

Methods

Study design

This is an ecological study involving Previne Brasil indicators. This article describes the seven indicators defined by the PB for payment for performance in the period from the first four months of 2022 to the first four months of 2023 (capitals, states, Brazilian regions and Brazil), since some indicators (one, four, five, six and seven) of the BP changed in 2022 [6, 7].

Previne Brasil is organized into seven indicators: (i) proportion of pregnant women with at least six prenatal consultations carried out, the first being by the 12th week of pregnancy, (ii) proportion of pregnant women with tests for syphilis and Acquired Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) carried out, (iii) proportion of pregnant women with dental care carried out, (iv) proportion of women with cytopathology collection at the PHC, (v) proportion of children aged one (1) year vaccinated at the PHC against Diphtheria, Tetanus, Pertussis, Hepatitis B, infections caused by Haemophilus influenzae type b and inactivated Poliomyelitis, (vi) proportion of people with hypertension, with consultation and blood pressure measured in the semester and (vii) proportion of people with diabetes, with consultation and glycated hemoglobin (Hb1Ac) requested in the semester [8].

Study scenario

Brazil is a country of continental dimensions (around 8 million km2) and is the fifth largest country in the world. With a population of 203 million and an emerging state economy, it is divided into five regions: North, Northeast, Central-West, Southeast and South [10]. The Southeast and South have more developed and diversified economies, with a higher concentration of industries, companies and jobs. On the other hand, the North and Northeast have less developed economies and are more dependent on sectors such as agriculture and natural resources [11]. Per capita income varies significantly between regions, with the South having the highest average income and the Northeast the lowest [12]. People living in the Southeast and South are more likely to use health services than those living in other regions [13].

Once the indicator results have been aggregated, the Final Synthetic Indicator (FSI) is calculated. This is done by adding the weighted scores of the indicators and dividing by 10. The weighted score is then obtained by multiplying the score by the weight of the indicator. In this way, the FSI summarizes the weighted results of all the indicators, allowing the municipality’s performance to be defined and financial resources to be allocated. In addition, the FSI is calculated only for municipalities (8).

The data was extracted from the e-Gestor platform (Primary Care Information and Management) (https://sisab.saude.gov.br ) on August 1, 2023 [14]. This is a digital tool developed by the Brazilian Ministry of Health to help manage and monitor health programs, especially in Primary Health Care (PHC). The platform was created to facilitate the collection, processing and access to data related to public health, improving the efficiency and effectiveness of health actions across the country.

Data analysis

After collection, the data was analyzed descriptively using absolute and relative frequencies. The analysis was stratified into performance indicators for the capitals, states, Brazilian regions and Brazil in four quarters (Q1/2022; Q2/2022; Q3/2022; Q1/2023) using the pre-established target for each indicator as a reference.

Ethical aspects

The study did not require approval from the ethics committee, since secondary data in the public domain was used, in which it is not possible to identify any subject.

Results

Of the seven indicators analyzed, only one (Proportion of pregnant women tested for syphilis and HIV) reached the proposed target considering the average of the four-month periods evaluated (observed indicator: 62.5%; target: 60%). The first indicator related to prenatal care (Proportion of pregnant women with at least 6 (six) prenatal consultations, the first (first) of which up to the 12th (twelfth) week of pregnancy) reached the target only in the first four-month period of 2023. On the other hand, the indicators related to hypertension and diabetes were the ones with the worst situation: the target for both was 50%; however, the average for the period was 24% for the Hypertension indicator (Proportion of people with hypertension, with a consultation and blood pressure measured in the semester) and 19.5% for the Diabetes indicator (Proportion of people with diabetes, with a consultation and glycated hemoglobin (Hb1Ac) requested in the semester) (Fig. 1 and Supplementary Table 1).

Discussion

This study analyzed Primary Health Care performance indicators in Brazil from the first four months of 2022 to the first four months of 2023. The results show that in Brazil, the Brazilian regions, states and capitals showed a progressive improvement in PHC performance indicators over the four quarters evaluated, although unevenly among the indicators, reflecting the need to redirect resources to areas with lower performance indicators and consequent improvement in PHC health services.

Health evaluation is a multidisciplinary field that uses methods and techniques to analyze and evaluate health interventions, programs, policies, and systems. This practice provides an understanding of the effectiveness, efficiency, quality, equity, and impact of health initiatives. In this sense, one of the main challenges of health evaluation is to ensure that it considers and addresses health inequalities [15].

It is worth noting that monitoring the results of health assessments in the context of PHC functions as a transparent performance monitoring tool, allowing managers to base themselves on scientific evidence, which makes it possible to build intervention plans to solve specific needs in an area, with the aim of promoting continuous improvement in the quality of health offered in PHC [15].

The establishment of the Previne Brasil Program in the first months of the Bolsonaro government in 2019 has raised a critical discussion about the direction of PHC funding [16]. For those in favor of the changes implemented by the Previne Brasil, payment for performance would be a measure to encourage the registration of new users in the information systems; it would increase screening actions (HIV, Syphilis, cervical screening); it would increase vaccination coverage of children up to 1 year of age (Diphtheria, Tetanus, Pertussis, Hepatitis B, infections caused by Haemophilus influenzae type b and inactivated Poliomyelitis) and the control of chronic diseases (controlled blood pressure, controlled glycated hemoglobin) [17].

Previne Brasil would encourage the process of computerizing PHC through the monitoring and evaluation of performance indicators, which would promote the use of electronic medical records and improve the accountability of Family Health Strategy teams in coordinated and longitudinal care [18]. In addition, the program would allow for the potential of pay-for-performance components and would expand the training of human resources through medical and multi-professional residency programs [19].

It is worth noting that other mechanisms for transferring resources were created before Previne Brasil. In 2011, for example, the National Program for Improving Primary Care Access and Quality (PMAQ-AB) was implemented. This was an important mechanism for transferring funds from the variable Primary Care Floor (PAB) to municipalities by evaluating the performance of eSF teams. The main objective of this program was to encourage continuous improvement in the quality of PHC, by stimulating the eSF teams to achieve predefined standards of quality of care [20].

The PMAQ-AB was implemented in cycles in which the eSFs were evaluated based on criteria such as service organization, health unit infrastructure, responsiveness to user needs, access and reception. This evaluation was carried out using evaluation instruments, external visits by evaluators and questionnaires. Teams that achieved certain quality standards received a financial bonus as an incentive [20].

On the other hand, critics argue that the new funding model has been conforming to the logic of performance, where the typical attributes of the private sector are valued, associated with market interests, reinforcing health care focused on specific population groups, given that there is an imposed difficulty in receiving federal funds through differentiation, weighting and registrations [9]. Another aspect that reinforces and deepens this logic is payment for performance, a component that links the transfer to the need to achieve targets in performance indicators, without focusing on criteria related to the evaluation of work processes. In this way, if the targets are not met, the funds that would have gone to them are lost [21].

Pay-for-performance has a managerialist logic of performance indicators, which aim to achieve results from a list of indicators and determine the transfer of funds to achieve these goals [21]. In this way, municipalities that meet the targets are “favored” to the detriment of those that don’t, without evaluating the reasons for their non-compliance and neglecting the elements that make up and influence this context, such as the lack of health professionals and the differences in primary health care coverage between municipalities [9].

Previne Brasil was created as an alternative to the existing restrictions, with four blocks originally defined for federal transfers: (i) weighted capitation, (ii) payment for performance, (iii) incentives for strategic actions and (iv) financial incentives based on population criteria. With regard to the weighted capitation block, it is necessary for the municipality to register Family Health Teams (eSF) or Primary Care Teams (eAP), weighted by equity criteria, using software systems [Simplified Data Collection; Electronic Citizen’s Record and proprietary systems, integrated in the Primary Care Health Information System (SISAB)] [21].

The impossibility or difficulty of registering, on the part of individuals with socio-economic limitations, alters the reference number of people to be benefited by the Previne Brasil Program, moving away from the perspective of the universal right to health. The North and Southeast regions differ in factors such as infrastructure, available resources and population density. The North has less infrastructure and a higher concentration of indigenous populations. In 2022, of 689,202 inhabitants on indigenous lands, 90.26% (622,100) were indigenous, with the North concentrating almost half 49.12% (338,500) of the total inhabitants, 93.49% of whom were indigenous [22]. The Southeast has a larger urbanized area and significant economic development, but the large populations in this region create limitations and barriers to accessing health care [23].

With the establishment of Previne Brasil, the multiprofessional and interdisciplinary teams that make up the Expanded Family Health and Primary Care Centers (Nasf-AB) are no longer part of the strategic actions that received federal funds from the Ministry of Health to be implemented by municipalities, thus causing an instability of resources. This led to the extinction of Nasf-AB as a public policy encouraged and funded by the Brazilian Ministry of Health, as well as leading to a downward trend in the number of registered teams in the country since 2019 [4]. In this way, this decree dismisses the multiprofessional and interdisciplinary logic of care in PHC and the universality of the SUS, de-characterizing its orientation towards care, valuing the operationalist logic of procedures and ratifying market elements at this level of care [24].

After the repercussions of the extinction of the Nasf-AB, on May 22, 2023, the Multiprofessional teams (eMulti) were set up, which emerged as a tool to give new meaning to access to longitudinal and comprehensive patient care, with an emphasis on actions that range from health promotion to treatment and rehabilitation, and are made up of health professionals from different areas of knowledge. eMulti is divided into three modalities: expanded eMulti, complementary eMulti and strategic eMulti, which differ in terms of the number of teams involved, as well as the minimum and maximum working hours of the teams, monthly federal co-funding and multi-professional composition [5].

With the return of the Ministry of Health’s priority for multi-professional care in Primary Health Care, through the proposal to make more than 870 million reais available for states and municipalities to fund the teams in 2023, eMulti strengthens multidisciplinary care by increasing the amount transferred to states and municipalities to fund the teams and new specialties [25].

The lack of medical professionals and high turnover in primary care health services in locations outside the major centers is a structural problem in Brazilian health, which means that many of the smaller municipalities do not have an adequate number of these professionals to serve the population, a reality known by the federal government and pointed out by the World Bank in the document on which Previne Brasil was based [26].

Due to the challenges encountered by the new PHC financing model, some municipalities have established the strategy of offering a financial incentive to primary health care workers who achieve the Previne Brasil targets in their eSF, such as the capital Fortaleza - CE through Law No. 11,177 of November 3, 2021 [27] and the municipality of Monteirópolis - AL through Law No. 453/2023 [28]. However, the motivation of workers to reach Previne Brasil’s pre-established targets can jeopardize the SUS principle of comprehensiveness.

Given that Brazil is a country of continental dimensions, the socio-spatial aspects of remote rural areas in Brazil are diverse. Despite this, there are a number of issues that are similar when it comes to the organization and provision of health services in locations far from urban centers. These are mainly technical, financial and professional limitations, which restrict access to PHC in more remote areas, making it necessary to have public policies aimed at these unique contexts [29]. However, Previne Brasil applied as a single form of financing for PHC in all municipalities departs from the principle of equity advocated by the SUS.

Previne Brasil’s pay-for-performance component should reward municipalities not only on the basis of achieving the targets of the seven indicators, but also by evaluating the percentage of improvement in relation to previous periods. Encouraging municipalities that have started from low levels and are making progress, even if they haven’t reached the proposed parameters [30]. In this sense, four of the indicators analyzed [Proportion of women with cytopathology collection at the PHC; Proportion of children aged one (1) year vaccinated at the PHC against Diphtheria, Tetanus, Pertussis, Hepatitis B, infections caused by Haemophilus influenzae type b and inactivated Poliomyelitis; Proportion of people with hypertension, with consultation and blood pressure measured in the semester; and Proportion of people with diabetes, with consultation and glycated hemoglobin (Hb1Ac) requested in the semester] showed progression in reaching the target, without reaching it.

Although all the necessary methodological precautions were taken, this study has limitations. The first relates to the use of secondary data, since the information systems and the quality of what is being recorded by PHC depend on people registering it, and it can be collected and stored in databases incorrectly. The second is the scarcity of epidemiological studies on the subject. The third limitation to the interpretation of the findings concerns the failure to compare the indicators between capitals and inland municipalities. There are important differences between them and the findings for the capitals do not represent the quality of Brazilian municipalities, since these spatial units are not capable of representing all of Brazil’s municipalities.

Conclusions

The study showed improvements in the performance indicators for Primary Health Care in Brazil provided for in Previne Brasil, although unevenly between the regions, given the complexity of the country’s socio-spatial aspects and the existence of different realities and challenges in the local-regional health system. We emphasize the need to add a municipal payment component to the PB, assessing the percentage of improvement in relation to previous periods in order to achieve greater equity in the financing of PHC in Brazil. We highlight the importance of reflecting on how this type of financing rationalizes the provision of services, reducing the real health needs of the population.

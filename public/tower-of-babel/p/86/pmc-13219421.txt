Conformable advection-dispersion models for radioactive waste migration: Robin-type condition and moment analysis
Authors: Qing Wei, Shuai Yang, Senlin Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Within the framework of the conformable fractional-order derivative, conformable advection-dispersion models (CADMs) with Robin-type boundary conditions and different source terms are proposed to describe radioactive waste migration. Analytical solutions are derived using the conformable Laplace transform. The influence of the fractional derivative order \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document} on the concentration distribution and boundary concentration is illustrated graphically. Spatial moment analysis is conducted to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed models for parameter estimation. Comparison with experimental data shows excellent agreement, indicating that the CADM with Robin-type boundary conditions characterizes radioactive waste migration more accurately. Furthermore, the generalized CADM exhibits greater flexibility and improved application potential compared with its corresponding integer-order model.

Introduction

Geological disposal, or precisely speaking, multiple-barrier deep-geological repository, has been the recommended approach for the permanent disposal of radioactive wastes since the 1950s1. As for the safety of long-term disposal, numerous efforts have been made to explore the accurate mechanics of radioactive waste migration2,3. The advection-dispersion model (ADM) is generally an accepted model for describing radioactive waste migration in porous media3,4. In radioactive waste migration through porous media, solute transport is strongly influenced by sorption processes and associated time-delay effects. Due to interactions between radionuclides and the solid matrix, as well as mass transfer between mobile and immobile pore regions, radionuclide migration exhibits significant retardation and delayed breakthrough behavior. Classical ADMs assuming instantaneous equilibrium often fail to capture these features. Consequently, modified ADMs incorporating sorption-induced retardation and explicit time-delay mechanisms have been widely adopted to provide a more realistic description of radionuclide transport and to improve the reliability of long-term safety assessments5.

In radioactive waste disposal systems, radionuclide migration occurs across interfaces separating waste forms, engineered barriers, host rock, and surrounding groundwater. These interfaces exhibit finite permeability and resistance to mass transfer, such that contaminant exchange is neither instantaneous nor completely blocked. Robin-type boundary conditions naturally represent this behavior by relating the diffusive flux to the concentration difference across the interface and also satisfy mass conservation6,7. The associated mass transfer coefficient accounts for barrier properties, interfacial resistance, and degradation effects, enabling a physically realistic description of gradual radionuclide release and leakage. Consequently, Robin-type boundary conditions realistically model radioactive waste migration by capturing finite, concentration-driven leakage across imperfect barriers and interfaces8. Besides, the simulation of many diffusion phenomena requires the solution of models subjected to Robin-type boundary conditions by consideration of the physical reality underlying the representation of boundaries in mathematical continua9.

However, tracer transport in complex geological system observed over a wide range of spatial and time scales generally cannot be accurately captured by the classical ADM. Such behavior is commonly referred to as time-dependent effects, and thus such kind of transport phenomenon is anomalous or non-Fickian transport. In original works, it was realised that the replacement of the local time derivative by a fractional order derivative accounts for the time-dependent effects which are connected with creep process10–14, non-Darcy flow15,16, and radionuclide anomalous transport17, neural networks18,19,with corresponding numerical analysis20 and boundary value problem21. Fractional-order ADM with mixed boundary and initial conditions is useful to study radioactive waste time-dependent migration22. Nevertheless, non-local fractional operators, such as the Caputo derivative, Caputo-Fabrizio fractional operator, and Atangana-Baleanu fractional operator, incur high computational and numerical costs, which results in the proposal of new local fractional derivatives and integrals.

In this connection, Khalil et al. proposed the conformable derivative, which is well-behaved and abides by the computational relationship of the first derivative23,24. Interest in the conformable derivative has been increasing, and it has been applied to generalized advection-dispersion and transport models in porous and heterogeneous media25,26. Comparative studies have examined its performance relative to classical Riemann-Liouville and Caputo formulations in solute transport27, while analytical solutions have been developed for pollutant diffusion28, gas migration in coalbed methane reservoirs29, and contaminant transport with adsorption effects30. Boundary value problems, including Dirichlet-type conditions, have also been explored, providing guidance for treating boundary constraints in conformable transport models25. These works motivate the present study on conformable advection-dispersion modeling of radioactive waste migration under Robin-type boundary conditions.

This work investigates a conformable advection-dispersion model (CADM) with Robin-type boundary conditions under several commonly used source terms. Analytical solutions are obtained via the conformable Laplace transform, and the effects of the fractional order \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document} on solute concentration are examined. Moment analysis is employed to assess parameter estimation and model performance, with results showing good agreement with experimental data. These findings indicate that the CADM with Robin-type boundary conditions provides a more accurate and flexible description of radioactive waste migration than the classical ADM. Section "Conformable advection-dispersion models" introduces the generalized one-dimensional ADM with decay and adsorption, followed by CADM formulations with different source conditions and their moment analyses. Section “Conclusions” presents conclusions and discussion.

Conclusions

In this work, the spatial moments and analytical solutions of CADMs subject to Robin-type boundary conditions with different source terms are investigated to model radioactive waste migration. The main conclusions are summarized as follows.

(1) Analytical solutions are obtained using the conformable Laplace transform. The proposed CADMs with Robin-type boundary conditions effectively describe radioactive waste migration while ensuring mass conservation and time independence.

(2) Spatial moment analysis demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed models for parameter estimation. Moreover, experimental data fitting indicates that CADMs with Robin-type boundary conditions provide a more accurate characterization of radioactive waste migration.

(3) Compared with classical ADMs, CADMs exhibit greater complexity and flexibility. In particular, the generalized CADMs show superior application potential relative to their corresponding integer-order models.

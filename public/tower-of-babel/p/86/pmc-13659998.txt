Transcriptomic profiling combined with pharmacological research of Panax notoginseng-derived nanovesicles for preliminary biocompatibility profiling and STAT5/SOCS3-driven immunomodulation in psoriasis
Authors: Li Li, Zhikang Li, Wei Wang, Zhaowei Teng, Yuanju Liu, Yong Zeng, Yun Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPsoriasis is a chronic immune-mediated inflammatory skin disease driven by persistent IL-23/Th17 axis activation. While biologics have improved disease control, their clinical use is constrained by immunotoxicity risks and long-term safety concerns. Plant-derived extracellular vesicle-like nanoparticles (EVLNs) are being investigated as a potential biocompatible nanotherapeutic platform, yet systematic safety profiling and network-level elucidation of their immunomodulatory mechanisms remain lacking. This study integrates Systems Pharmacology, computational transcriptomic profiling, and experimental validationto evaluate the preliminary safety profiles and therapeutic mechanisms of Panax notoginseng-derived EVLNs (PN-EVLNs) in Th17-driven psoriatic inflammation.MethodsPN-EVLNs were isolated, characterized, and evaluated in an imiquimod-induced psoriasis-like mouse model and IL-6/TGF-β-stimulated Jurkat T cells. A systems biology framework integrating bulk RNA sequencing, public single-cell transcriptomic datasets, and pathway enrichment analysiswas employed to map treatment-responsive molecular networks and identify key regulatory modules. Computational toxicity prediction and immune pathway deconvolution were combined with histological, biochemical, and molecular validation.ResultsSystems Pharmacology and transcriptomic profiling revealed that PN-EVLNs reversed psoriasis-associated inflammatory gene signatures, with computational pathway analysis identifying suppression of chemokine, IL-17, and NF-κB signaling networks. Computational safety profiling, supplemented by histological observation, suggested a favorable preliminary safety profile with no significant immunotoxic gene signatures observed in the tested models in treated models. Experimental validation confirmed that PN-EVLNs downregulated Th17-associated markers (CCR6, Tim-3) and pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-17A, TNF-α, CCL7, CXCL10, IL-1β). Network-based mechanistic analysis pinpointed STAT5/SOCS3 as a key negative feedback regulatory module restraining Th17-driven inflammation.ConclusionIntegrative Systems Pharmacology and computational safety profiling suggest that PN-EVLNs may serve as a biocompatible plant-derived nanotherapeutic with a promising preliminary safety profile. Reactivation of the STAT5/SOCS3 regulatory network underlies its immunomodulatory effects,providing a basis for the network-guided development of safer anti-psoriatic therapies using PN-EVLNs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-026-08660-7.

Introduction

Psoriasis is a form of chronic inflammatory dermatosis characterized by erythematous plaques, keratinocyte hyperproliferation, and immune cell infiltration. Aberrant activation of dendritic cells and T-helper cells (especially Th1 and Th17) drives the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-17, and IL-22, which promote epidermal thickening and immune dysregulation [1–5]. From a systems pharmacology perspective, the complex cytokine networks and immune cell cross-talk underlying psoriasis pathogenesis represent an ideal context for network-based therapeutic intervention and computational safety assessment.

Current therapies for psoriasis, including topical agents, phototherapy, and systemic biologics targeting TNF-α, IL-17, or IL-23, have improved symptom control, but their use is constrained by immunotoxicity, adverse effects, high costs, and long-term immunosuppression risks [2, 6–8]. Systems Pharmacology and computational toxicology approaches are increasingly employed to predict drug safety profiles and identify adverse outcome pathways [9], yet their application to plant-derived nanotherapeutics remains underexplored. These limitations underscore the need for safer, cost-effective, and scalable alternatives guided by integrative computational and network-based safety frameworks.

Extracellular vesicles (EVLNs), as natural nanoscale particles, have gradually attracted research attention because of their unique functions in intercellular communication, material transport, and other aspects [10]. The functional proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids carried by EVLNs have displayed great immunomodulatory, anti-inflammatory, and anti-tumor effects [11, 12]. Currently, cell-derived EVLNs are widely used in the treatment of various diseases [13]. However, their low yields and long production cycles limit their application in clinical treatment [14]. To overcome these issues, researchers have focused on plant-derived EVLNs, especially nanoparticles extracted from fresh plant tissues, which are similar to EVLNs in morphology and function and are more suitable for large-scale production [15, 16].

In recent years, studies illustrated that plant-derived EVLNs exhibit unique biological functions in various fields, and they have achieved remarkable results in the treatment of inflammatory diseases and cancer [17]. Research indicates that some plant exosomes can induce intestinal tissue homeostasis, promote the intestinal stem cell proliferation, remodel the gut microbiota, and regulate intestinal immunity through interspecies communication with animal intestinal host cells, exerting good effects in alleviating inflammatory bowel disease [18]. Studies also identified a small portion of exosome-like cell vesicles in lemon juice that can specifically reach tumor sites, activate tumor necrosis factor-related apoptosis, and induce ligand-mediated cell apoptosis to inhibit the growth of chronic myeloid leukemia tumors in vivo [19]. Despite growing interest in plant-derived EVLNs, their therapeutic potential in psoriasis has not been adequately investigated [17, 20].

Panax notoginseng (P. notoginseng）is a traditional Chinese medicinal herb with known anti-inflammatory and anti-tumor properties [16]. Although widely used in its crude form, the bioactive components of the plant, especially those delivered via EVLNs, remain underexplored in modern dermatological applications, including psoriasis. Given the medicinal value of P. notoginseng, further exploration into its bioactive delivery systems, such as EVLNs, was explored to evaluate its therapeutic potential

In this study, we employed an integrative Systems Pharmacology and computational transcriptomic strategy to evaluate the safety profiles and immunomodulatory mechanisms of P. notoginseng-derived EVLNs in both in vivo (imiquimod [IMQ]-induced psoriasis-like mice) and in vitro (Jurkat T cells) models. EVLNs were isolated from P. notoginseng roots and characterized. In vivo and in vitro models were used to assess the therapeutic potential and safety signatures of EVLNs. A Systems Pharmacology framework integrating bulk RNA sequencing and single-cell transcriptomic datasets was constructed to map drug–target–disease interaction networks and identify key regulatory modules. Skin pathology, cytokine expression, Th17 markers, and key transcriptional regulators were evaluated to validate network-based predictions.

Our findings aim to establish a network-guided framework for safety assessment of plant-derived nanotherapeutics, providing mechanistic insights and computational foundations for developing scalable, biocompatible therapeutic strategies against immune-mediated skin diseases [14, 17]. This study demonstrates the utility of integrating Systems Pharmacology with experimental validation for drug safety profiling and mechanism-of-action elucidation in translational dermatology.

Materials and methods

Extraction of P. notoginseng-derived EVLNs

P.notoginseng, sourced from Yunnan, China, was carefully washed and ground into a homogenate using phosphate-buffered saline (Servicebio, Wuhan, Hubei, China). The homogenate was filtered to eliminate macroscopic debris and centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 60 min at 4 °C with a Xiangyi TGL-16M (Hunan Xiangyi Laboratory Instrument Development Co., Ltd, Changsha, Hunan, China) centrifuge to remove cellular debris. The supernatant was collected and processed via ultracentrifugation at 150,000 × g for 90 min at 4 °C using a Beckman Optima XE-100 (Beckman, Pasadena, California, The United States) ultracentrifuge. The pellet, which contained extracellular vesicles, was subsequently resuspended in pre-cooled 1× PBS.

Nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA)

The ZetaView sample tank was subjected to three purification cycles using distilled water. The isolated EVLNs were then adjusted to a suitable concentration via PBS dilution, followed by syringe-mediated introduction into the PMX-120-12F-R5 ZetaView sample pool (Analytik Ltd, Cambridge，United Kingdom). Then, 1–2 µL (scaled to the initial concentration) of the EVLNs were combined with 2 mL of PBS and delivered to the sample pool. Exosome particle dynamics were visualized in real time on the computer interface, with their concentration distribution documented accordingly.

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)

In preparation for TEM, 10 µL of the purified EVLNs were applied to a copper grid and allowed to settle for 5–10 min. Any excess solution was wicked away with filter paper, and the samples were left to air-dry completely. Next, the samples were treated with 20 µL of 1% uranyl acetate for staining, and imaging was performed using an HT7700 transmission electron microscope (Hitachi High-Tech (Shanghai) Co., Ltd, Shanghai, China).

Animal experimental protocols

Twenty-four male BALB/c mice (8 weeks old) were purchased from Beijing Huafukang Biotech Co., Ltd. and randomly divided into four groups (n = 6 per group). The mice were housed in an SPF environment at 20–24 °C with 50–60% humidity and provided standard feed (Xietong Pharmaceutical Bio-engineering, Taizhou, Jiansu, China). After shaving the backs of the mice, a psoriasis mouse model was established by applying IMQ ointment 50 mg/day (Sichuan Mingxin Pharmaceutical, Chengdu Sichuan China) for 2 consecutive days. The control group received an equivalent volume of PBS ointment. On day 3, P. notoginseng cell-derived EVLNs (100 µg/mL) were applied to the IMQ-treated mice for 5 consecutive days. On day 8, the mice were euthanized, and their blood and skin tissue were collected. A portion of the skin tissue was frozen, whereas the remainder was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (Sinopharm, Beijing, China) for subsequent experiments.

Hematoxylin–eosin (H&E) staining

Skin specimens were first fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde-PBS (pH 7.4) at 4 °C for 24 h to optimally preserve cellular morphology and antigenicity. They were then subjected to graded ethanol dehydration, xylene clearing, and paraffin infiltration before embedding. Paraffin blocks were cut into 4 µm serial sections on a rotary microtome; the ribbons were floated on a 45 °C water bath, mounted on poly-L-lysine-coated slides, and baked at 60 °C for 2 h to enhance adhesion. Prior to staining, sections were deparaffinized in xylene, rehydrated through descending ethanol concentrations, and rinsed in PBS. Nuclei were stained with Mayer’s hematoxylin for 8 min at room temperature, blued under running tap water for 10 min, and counterstained with 1% eosin Y in ethanol for 30 s. Finally, bright-field images were acquired using an Olympus BX53 upright microscope equipped with a DP80 camera (20× objective, 2048 × 1536 pixels).

Systems Pharmacology framework: molecular interaction network construction and functional module identification

To construct a Systems Pharmacology framework for safety assessment, total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc, Waltham, Massachusetts, The United States). mRNA was purified, fragmented, and reverse-transcribed into cDNA. After end repair, A-tailing, and adapter ligation, libraries were amplified, quantified, and sequenced on an Illumina platform to obtain high-quality transcriptomic data for downstream drug–target–disease network construction and pathway enrichment analysis.

RT-qPCR

Gene expression was validated using RT-qPCR (2−ΔΔCt method), and protein expression was assessed by western blotting. GAPDH was used as a loading control. Antibodies and primer sequences are listed in Tables S1 and S2.

Western blotting

Tissue or cell samples were homogenized in lysis buffer and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min to collect the supernatant. The protein concentration was quantified using a BCA protein assay kit (DLM Inc, Wuhan, Hubei, China). Protein samples (50 µg) were denatured via mixing with 5× loading buffer and heating at 95–100 °C for 5 min. Electrophoretic separation was conducted by SDS-PAGE (Epizyme Inc, Cambridge, Delaware, The United States) under constant voltage (80 V for stacking, 120 V for separation). Protein transfer to the membrane was performed at 300 mA, with the duration adjusted according to the target protein’s molecular weight. The membrane was blocked at room temperature for 1 h and then incubated with primary antibodies (diluted in antibody dilution buffer; Table S2) overnight at 4 °C. After three 5-min TBST washes, secondary antibodies (diluted in antibody dilution buffer; Table S2) were applied at room temperature for 30 min. The membrane was washed four times with TBST (5 min each) before treatment with ECL reagent (ABclonal Inc, Wuhan, Hubei, Chian). Autoradiography film (Fujifilm Inc, Tokyo, Japan) was used for detection. Band intensity was measured via ImageJ, and target protein levels were normalized to an internal reference. GraphPad Prism software was employed for data visualization. A full list of primary and secondary antibodies, including catalog numbers and sources, is provided in Table S2.

Indirect immunofluorescence assay

Skin specimens fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde were embedded in paraffin and cut into 4-μm sections. After deparaffinization and rehydration, antigen retrieval was performed using citrate buffer (pH 6.0). Sections were then permeabilized with 0.2% Triton X-100 and blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) at room temperature for 1 h to minimize non-specific binding. Subsequently, the sections were incubated with primary antibodies against CCR6 and Tim-3 (Table S2) overnight at 4 °C. After washing three times with PBS, fluorescent-conjugated secondary antibodies (Alexa Fluor 488 or 594) were applied for 1 h in the dark at room temperature. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI for 10 min. Finally, images were captured using a fluorescence microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan), and the fluorescence intensity was quantified using ImageJ software.

Induction of Jurkat cells with IL-6 and TGF-β

Jurkat cells were divided into three groups: PBS group, IL-6 (20 ng/mL) + TGF-β (10 ng/mL) group, and IL-6 + TGF-β + EV group. Cells in the IL-6 + TGF-β group were induced with IL-6 (ABclonal Inc, Wuhan, Hubei, China) and TGF-β (ABclonal Inc, Wuhan, Hubei, China) for 6 h, followed by treatment with P. notoginseng cell-derived EVLNs for 18 h. The IL-6 + TGF-β group was induced for 6 h and then cultured with an equal volume of PBS for 18 h. The PBS group was treated with PBS for 24 hours. Cells were collected for subsequent experiments.

ELISA

The supernatant of cell cultures was collected for ELISA. Human IL-17A (Elabscience Biotechnology Co.,Ltd, Wuhan, Hubei, China) and TNF-α (Elabscience Biotechnology Co.,Ltd, Wuhan, Hubei, China) levels were measured according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Bioinformatic analysis of single-cell transcriptomic Dataset

Single-cell RNA sequencing datasets for three patients with psoriasis and three healthy controls (GSE230842) were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) repository. The analysis pipeline utilized the “Seurat” R package (version 5.0). Cells exhibiting excessive gene counts (>6000) or high mitochondrial transcript proportions (>20%) were excluded from downstream processing. Normalization and log-transformation were applied to the expression matrix. Principal component analysis (PCA) was executed on 5000 highly variable genes, with the first 50 principal components retained for dimensionality reduction. Batch effects were mitigated using the “harmony” algorithm. Cell clustering was performed via shared nearest-neighbor graph-based methods implemented through the FindNeighbors and FindClusters functions. Cluster annotation relied on established cell type-specific markers from prior studies. Uniform manifold approximation and projection plots were generated to visualize the annotated cell populations. Differential gene expression was assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, whereas T cell subset identification and scoring were accomplished using Seurat’s AddModuleScore function. GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses were conducted following established protocols.

Statistical analysis

Statistical evaluations were conducted using Student’s t-test for pairwise group comparisons and one-way ANOVA to assess variations across multiple treatment conditions. p < 0.05 was adopted to determine statistical relevance.

The animal experiment protocol was approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Kunming Medical University (Approval No. kyfey2025004). The procedures strictly adhered to the “3R” principles (Replacement, Reduction, and Refinement) to ensure the welfare of laboratory mice. Trained professionals conducted all experiment. Mice were housed in a suitable environment, with their health status closely monitored throughout the study. Humane endpoints were implemented when necessary. All experimental data and records were maintained in compliance with relevant regulations, and the study met ethical requirements.

Systems Pharmacology-driven transcriptomic profiling and drug–target–disease network construction

We performed transcriptomic profiling on skin samples obtained from mice in the control, psoriasis-induced, and psoriasis-plus-EV treatment groups to construct a drug–target–disease interaction network for safety assessment. Comparative analysis of gene expression identified 633 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in the psoriasis group relative to healthy controls (497 unique, 136 shared), and 854 DEGs in the EV-treated group compared to the psoriasis group (718 unique, 136 shared). Among these, 136 DEGs were common to both comparisons (Fig. 3A). Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis of this shared gene set revealed significant overrepresentation in categories associated with chemokine receptor binding, chemokine activity, and inflammatory response pathways (Fig. 3B). Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis further indicated that these overlapping DEGs are involved in critical signaling cascades, such as those mediated by chemokines, interleukin-17 (IL-17), and NF-κB (Fig. 3C), suggesting a favorable safety signature characterized by coordinated suppression of pro-inflammatory networks. Hierarchical clustering based on the common DEGs revealed two distinct expression patterns. One cluster comprised 10 genes (Adrb1, Ccl27a, Ccl27b, Fos, Dusp1, Thbs1, Clca1, Col4a6, Fosb, and Clca3a2) that were highly expressed in the normal group, markedly downregulated in the psoriasis group, and showed partial recovery of expression following EV treatment. Effects of EVLNs on Th17 cell activity in psoriatic Skin

To further elucidate the mechanism by which EVLNs alleviate psoriatic inflammation, we focused on their regulatory impact on Th17-like inflammatory activation, a central pathogenic axis in psoriasis. Th17 cells are key mediators in psoriatic lesions, primarily via the secretion of cytokines such as IL-17A and TNF-α and expression of signature surface markers such as CCR6 and Tim-3. Therefore, assessing the modulation of Th17-specific markers and regulatory feedback molecules provides critical insight into the immunomodulatory potential of EVLNs.

Using skin tissues from mice with IMQ-induced psoriasis, we first measured the transcript levels of CCR6 and Tim-3 by qPCR and evaluated their protein expression through immunofluorescence assay. The results demonstrated the significant downregulation of both CCR6 and Tim-3 in the psoriasis + EV group compared to their expression in the psoriasis group (Fig. 5A–C), indicating effective suppression of Th17 activation at both the transcriptional and protein levels. To investigate the underlying regulatory mechanism, we next assessed the expression of two well-known negative feedback regulators of Th17-related signaling pathways: STAT5 and SOCS3. Both genes exhibited significantly elevated mRNA levels following EV treatment, as demonstrated by qPCR (Fig. 5D–E), meanwhile the corresponding protein levels, including phosphorylated STAT5, were increased with western blotting test (Fig. 5F–I). These data indicate that the immunosuppressive effects of EVLNs on psoriasis might be mediated through transcriptional reprogramming of the Th17-related signaling pathways, offering a promising mechanistic rationale for their therapeutic application.

In vitro validation of EV-mediated suppression of Th17-like inflammatory activation in Jurkat T cells

To validate the immunomodulatory effects of EVLNs on Th17-like inflammatory activation at the cellular level, in vitro assays was performed using human Jurkat T cells. Jurkat cells were induced into a Th17-like state via exposure to IL-6 and TGF-β, which mimic the cytokine milieu driving Th17 polarization. This model allowed us to evaluate whether EVLNs could directly modulate Th17-associated transcriptional and protein expression profiles under controlled conditions.

Following treatment with EVLNs, the secretion of the key Th17-related inflammatory cytokines IL-17A and TNF-α using ELISA was measured. As illustrated in Fig. 6A–B, EV-treated Jurkat cells exhibited a significant reduction in both cytokines compared to the IL-6 + TGF-β group, indicating that EVLNs inhibit inflammatory cytokine production associated with Th17 activation. We next assessed the mRNA expression of the canonical Th17 surface markers CCR6 and Tim-3. RT-qPCR results revealed that EV treatment downregulated both markers compared to their expression in cytokine-only treated cells (Fig. 6C–D), suggesting attenuation of Th17 cell identity. To investigate the underlying mechanism, we measured the expression of STAT5 and SOCS3, key transcriptional repressors that negatively regulate Th17 lineage commitment. EV treatment significantly upregulated STAT5 and SOCS3 at the mRNA level (Fig. 6E–F), consistent with the proposed inhibitory axis. These transcriptional changes were further confirmed at the protein level via western blotting. Taken together, these in vitro results demonstrate that P. notoginseng-derived EVLNs directly suppress Th17-like inflammatory activation in Jurkat cells by inhibiting pro-inflammatory cytokine production and downregulating Th17 surface markers. This inhibitory effect is likely mediated through activation of the STAT5/SOCS3 negative feedback pathway, thus reinforcing the in vivo data and supporting the therapeutic potential of EVLNs in modulating Th17-driven inflammation.

Network-based single-cell transcriptomic analysis of psoriatic and normal cells

To extend our Systems Pharmacology framework to the single-cell level, we retrieved single-cell RNA sequencing data (GSE230842) and corresponding annotation information from the GEO database, encompassing both patients with psoriasis and healthy individuals. Subsequently, we conducted a comprehensive functional enrichment analysis utilizing GO and KEGG tools to map cell-type-specific drug–target networks. GO analysis of the single-cell transcriptomic dataset (GSE230842; psoriasis vs. healthy controls) revealed enrichment of biological processes including cytokine production, Th17 differentiation, innate immune regulation, chronic inflammatory response, and T cell activation (Fig. 7A). Discussion

This study employed an integrative Systems Pharmacology and computational transcriptomic framework to investigate the safety profile and therapeutic potential of P. notoginseng-derived EVLNs in the treatment of psoriasis, a chronic inflammatory dermatosis characterized by keratinocyte hyperproliferation and immune cell infiltration. Current work on plant-derived EVLNs for psoriasis has largely centered on suppressing superficial inflammation through the blockade of individual pro-inflammatory cytokines [21]. Systematic efforts to uncover negative immune feedback loops—via multi-omics integration or network-based approaches—remain scarce [22]. Most published studies rely on IMQ-induced mouse models and restrict their assessments to short-term efficacy [23], with limited attention paid to safety biomarkers or systemic immunological consequences [24]. Against this backdrop, we designed an analytical framework that couples network pharmacology with single-cell transcriptomic profiling, aiming to jointly evaluate the therapeutic potential and safety signatures of PN-EVLNs. This workflow addresses a notable gap in the current literature: the lack of systematic, network-level safety assessments for plant-derived nanotherapeutics in psoriasis treatment.Through construction of drug–target–disease interaction networks and experimental validation, we demonstrated that EVLNs significantly alleviated psoriasis symptoms in both mouse and Jurkat cell models, providing novel computational and mechanistic insights into the safety assessment and clinical translation of plant-derived nanotherapeutics.

Psoriasis is a complex disease driven by the interplay between immune dysregulation and keratinocyte hyperproliferation. Its pathogenesis involves the activation of immune cells, such as Th1 and Th17 cells, which secrete pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-17A, TNF-α, IL-23), thereby exacerbating skin inflammation and keratinocyte proliferation [18, 25]. Current treatments, including topical agents, phototherapy, and systemic therapies targeting specific cytokines, often face limitations such as high costs, numerous side effects, and variable efficacy [26].

Compared to current systemic therapies targeting IL-17A, IL-23, or TNF-α, which often require long-term administration and carry the risk of systemic immunosuppression, P. notoginseng-derived EVLNs possess distinctive characteristics. First, EVLNs are derived from natural plant sources, and thus, they exhibit low toxicity and excellent biocompatibility. Secondly, their production is relatively inexpensive and scalable, in contrast to monoclonal antibodies, which require complex manufacturing processes and cold-chain logistics [17, 26, 27]. Thirdly, plant-derived EVLNs do not interfere with systemic immune functions to the same extent as cytokine-targeted therapies, reducing the risk of infections or malignancy [21]. These features suggest that EVLNs could be considered as a potential alternative

or adjunctive treatment for mild-to-moderate psoriasis, particularly in populations in which conventional biologics are contraindicated or inaccessible.

Treatment with P. notoginseng-derived EVLNs led to marked improvements in psoriatic pathology, including reduced erythema and scaling, restoration of skin tissue architecture, and diminished inflammatory cell infiltration, as demonstrated by both histological and molecular analyses. These findings collectively underscore the anti-inflammatory properties and immunomodulatory potential of EVLNs in psoriasis management [21].

Systems Pharmacology-driven RNA sequencing and single-cell transcriptomic analysis further elucidated the drug–target interaction mechanisms underlying the therapeutic effects of EVLNs. Our integrative computational analysis revealed that EVLNs significantly upregulated negative feedback regulators (SOCS3 and STAT5) in the Th17-related signaling pathways, identifying the STAT5/SOCS3 axis as a key regulatory module within the Th17 inflammatory network. Although the exact causal links remain to be fully clarified, these network-based findings suggest that EVLNs exert their anti-inflammatory effects through targeted modulation of the STAT5/SOCS3 axis, which in turn restrains Th17 cell activity and associated cytokine secretion. This network-guided suppression of Th17 cell activity and cytokine production is crucial for alleviating the inflammatory cascade in psoriasis [27]. Additionally, single-cell network analysis revealed marked upregulation of the immune cytokines CXCL10 and IL-1β in psoriatic cells. Notably, CXCL10 exhibited specific high expression in psoriatic cells, suggesting its potential as a network-based safety biomarker for psoriasis pharmacovigilance. Jurkat cell model experiments further demonstrated that EVLNs effectively inhibitTh17-related signaling pathways, supporting their favorable safety profile as therapeutic agents for immune-mediated inflammatory diseases [28].

The use of plant-derived EVLNs, such as those from P. notoginseng, offers several advantages over traditional therapeutic methods. Plant EVLNs are abundant and scalable, and their immunomodulatory and anti-inflammatory properties have been demonstrated in various studies [17]. For example, ginger-derived nanoparticles exhibited efficacy in treating inflammatory bowel disease [20], whereas citrus-derived nanovesicles displayed anti-tumor properties [29]. The therapeutic potential of EVLNs in psoriasis treatment further highlights the broad applicability of plant-derived EVLNs in treating chronic inflammatory diseases.

In summary, this study demonstrates through integrative Systems Pharmacology and computational safety profiling that P. notoginseng-derived EVLNs exhibit significant therapeutic potential in treating psoriasis by modulating Th17 cell activity and reducing inflammation. These findings provide a network-guided foundation for the clinical translation of plant-derived EVLNs and demonstrate their potential as plant-derived therapeutic agents for psoriasis and other inflammatory diseases. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the therapeutic efficacy of EVLNs was evaluated in an IMQ-induced psoriasis model, and their performance in chronic, relapsing, or alternative preclinical models remains unverified. Second, the biodistribution, pharmacokinetics, and long-term safety of EVLNs require systematic computational ADMET prediction and experimental validation for comprehensive pharmacovigilance assessment. Third, the influence of EVLNs on other immune cell subsets (e.g., dendritic cells, Tregs) and keratinocyte-related signaling networks remains to be elucidated through expanded Systems Pharmacology approaches. Fourth, the study lacks validation in human-derived skin models or organoid systems, which might better recapitulate disease complexity and predict translational relevance. Finally,We clearly noted that the biocompatibility described in this paper only reflects short-term local skin performance, which cannot be regarded as complete standardized drug safety evaluation; therefore, future studies should incorporate machine learning-based toxicity prediction and dose–response network modeling to establish quantitative pharmacovigilance parameters for clinical translation, while relevant systematic toxicity evaluation will be supplemented in our follow-up independent research.

Conclusions

In this study, an integrative Systems Pharmacology and computational safety profiling approach was employed to demonstrate the significant therapeutic potential of Panax notoginseng-derived extracellular vesicles in treating psoriasis. Through construction of drug–target–disease interaction networks and experimental validation in both in vivo and in vitro models, we established that EVLNs alleviate psoriasis symptoms by modulating Th17-related signaling pathways and suppressing inflammation with a favorable safety signature. Network-based mechanistic analysis revealed that the therapeutic effects involve upregulation of negative feedback regulators SOCS3 and STAT5, leading to the downregulation of Th17 markers CCR6 and Tim-3, and a reduction in the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines. These findings underscore the immunomodulatory properties and biocompatible safety profile of plant-derived EVLNs, supporting their promise as network-guided, scalable nanotherapeutic agents for psoriasis and other Th17-driven inflammatory diseases. This study demonstrates the utility of integrating Systems Pharmacology with experimental validation for drug safety profiling in translational dermatology and pharmacovigilance.

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveEvaluate oral tonabersat, a connexin-43 hemichannel modulator, on central subfield thickness (CST) in eyes with center-involved diabetic macular edema (CI-DME) and good vision.DesignPhase II randomized, double-masked clinical trial.ParticipantsOne hundred twenty-nine adults with CI-DME and visual acuity (VA) 20/32 or better, from 24 US sites.InterventionParticipants randomly assigned 1:1- to 80-mg tonabersat (N = 64, [71 eyes]) or placebo (N = 65, [73 eyes]) for 6 months.Main Outcome MeasuresChange in OCT CST.ResultsOverall, 37% of participants were female, with a mean age of 63 years. In tonabersat and placebo groups, respectively, the baseline mean CST was 359 and 362 μm; the mean (standard deviation [SD]) change in CST from baseline to 6 months was –15 (55) μm and +5 (51) μm; (adjusted mean difference: –16 [95% confidence interval (CI), –34 to 2], P = 0.08). A post hoc analysis limiting anti-VEGF effect showed adjusted mean difference: –21 [95% CI, –38 to –3], P = 0.02. Among participants with thinner eyes at baseline (CST <75 μm above threshold; tonabersat [n = 45] vs. placebo [n = 43]), adjusted mean difference was –2 μm (95% CI, –26 to 22). For thicker eyes (CST ≥75 μm above threshold; tonabersat [n = 20] vs. placebo [n = 24]), mean difference was –51 μm (95% CI, –83 to –19). The mean (SD) change in best-corrected VA from baseline to 6 months was: tonabersat 0.0 (5.7) letters versus placebo –0.4 (5.7) letters (adjusted mean difference: 0.82 [95% CI, –1.56 to 3.21], adjusted P = 0.39). Dizziness was more common with tonabersat (27% vs. 8%), headaches more common with placebo (17% vs. 6%), and drowsiness rates were 11% in both groups.ConclusionsThere was no statistically significant improvement in CST at 6 months in the tonabersat versus placebo groups. However, a post hoc analysis limiting anti-VEGF impact demonstrated significant benefit in the tonabersat group. Aside from increased dizziness, consistent tonabersat safety concerns were not identified. Given findings consistent with a potentially favorable biologic effect of tonabersat, further research into the role of the inflammasome in DME is warranted.Financial Disclosure(s)Proprietary or commercial disclosure may be found in the Footnotes and Disclosures at the end of this article.

Diabetic macular edema (DME) is a leading cause of vision loss among working-age adults.1 Intravitreal anti-VEGF pharmacotherapy is first-line therapy for eyes with visual impairment from center-involved DME (CI-DME) and generally improves visual acuity (VA) with a relatively favorable safety profile.2, 3, 4, 5, 6 However, treatment requires repeat intraocular injections and access to specialized retinal care as often as once a month, involves cost and time burden for patients and physicians, and carries a risk of infrequent but severe injection-related complications.7 Additionally, ∼40% of patients with DME have a suboptimal response to anti-VEGF treatment. There is ongoing need to identify well-tolerated alternative therapeutics for CI-DME that can be offered at scale for patients with diabetes across a variety of geographic and resource settings worldwide.

Tonabersat is an orally bioavailable small molecule that binds to and closes open connexin-43 hemichannels that are overexpressed in diabetic retinopathy (DR) and other inflammatory conditions.8 NLRP3 inflammasomes are a key component of the innate immune system,9 and inflammasome activation after hemichannel opening has been shown to occur in DR.8 In preclinical studies, blocking pathologically open connexin-43 hemichannels prevents assembly of the NLRP3 inflammasome.9 Through this mechanism, tonabersat has been shown to reduce activation and recycling of multiple proinflammatory cytokines involved in the pathophysiology of DR and DME, including VEGF, interleukin-6 (IL-6), IL-1β, IL-8, IL-18, tumor necrosis factor-α, monocyte chemoattractant protein-1, and soluble intercellular adhesion molecule-1.10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18 In animal models, systemic administration of tonabersat has reduced DR-related electroretinogram attenuation and microaneurysm formation, and improves retinal vascular leakage after ischemia followed by reperfusion.19,20 During its original development for the treatment of migraine, >1500 patients received once-daily tonabersat dosing in phase I and IIb trials, and the drug appeared well-tolerated with no limiting safety concerns.14

This phase II trial was designed to evaluate the effect of oral tonabersat on improving CI-DME in patients with good VA (Snellen 20/32 or better) to determine whether further investigation is warranted.

Methods

This study adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. A central institutional review board provided approval for each site. Participants provided written informed consent. An independent data and safety monitoring board provided oversight. The study protocol can be accessed at www.drcr.net.

Study Population

The DRCR Retina Network Retina Network Protocol AN, available at www.DRCR.net, recruited adults with type 1 or type 2 diabetes at 24 clinical sites in the United States. Study eyes had a best-corrected VA letter score ≥74 (Snellen equivalent 20/32 or better) and evidence of CI-DME, defined by sex-specific central subfield thickness (CST) thresholds on spectral-domain OCT: Zeiss Cirrus CST ≥290 μm in females and ≥305 μm in males, or Heidelberg Spectralis CST ≥305 μm in females and ≥320 μm in males.21 Exclusion criteria included any history of treatment for DR or DME within the prior year, and not >4 anti-VEGF injections within the prior 3 years.

Study Design

In this randomized, superiority trial, participants were assigned randomly (1:1) on the study website to receive an 80-mg dose of oral tonabersat or placebo once daily for 6 months. Participants could have 1 or 2 study eyes if both were eligible at the time of randomization. Randomization was stratified by baseline OCT CST of the study eye (<75-μm above eligibility threshold, 75 to <175-μm above threshold, ≥175-μm above threshold); using the eye with the thicker macula if participant had 2 study eyes.

Visits occurred at baseline, monthly through 6 months, and at 12 months. Upon completion of the 6-month visit, participants discontinued the study drug. Procedures performed at each visit were as follows: electronic ETDRS best-corrected VA with protocol refraction,22 spectral-domain OCT scans, dilated ophthalmoscopy, blood pressure, blood and urine laboratory testing (1, 3, and 6-month visits), pill count for compliance assessment, and fundus photography (baseline and 6-month visit). This report includes data collected from May 2023 through May 2025, when the last 6-month visit was completed. Data through 6 months is reported herein. Site study personnel, reading center graders, and participants were masked to treatment groups.

Study Treatment

Tonabersat and matching placebo were provided by InflammX Therapeutics, Inc; 2 tablets were taken once daily. Participants were advised to take the study drug in the evening without food, preferably at bedtime, to reduce potential side effects. Starting 4 months into the study, if a participant reported intolerable side effects, then the investigator could down-titrate the dose to 1 pill per day.

Alternative treatment for CI-DME was permitted if VA decreased ≥10 letters from baseline due to DME at a single visit, 5 to 9 letters at 2 consecutive visits, or protocol chair approval was obtained. Alternative treatments included intravitreal anti-VEGF therapy as well as focal/grid laser or corticosteroid therapy when clinically appropriate. If alternative treatment was given for DME, the study drug was discontinued, but participation in the study continued until 12 months. Anticipated need for treatment for DR in the next 6 months was an exclusion; however, anti-VEGF treatment for DR was not prohibited if clinically indicated during follow-up.

Study Outcomes

The primary outcome was change in OCT CST from baseline to 6 months. Secondary outcomes included OCT retinal volume change, electronic ETDRS best-corrected VA change, and eyes receiving other DME treatments. Exploratory outcomes included changes in DR on fundus photographs, treatment compliance (percentage of study pills taken), and changes in blood and urine laboratory values. Safety outcomes of interest included headache, drowsiness, or dizziness, changes in renal and liver function, and changes in laboratory analytes.

Adverse events (AEs) were defined as any ocular or systemic event occurring after initiation of study treatment, coded according to the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities.

Statistical Analyses

Assuming an effective standard deviation (SD) of 50 μm and a true difference in mean CST of 30 μm, a total sample size of 128 eyes was required to achieve 90% power at a 2-sided type I error rate of 5%, accounting for an anticipated 5% loss to follow-up. The primary outcome was analyzed using a linear mixed model adjusted for baseline CST, accounting for the within-participant correlations between eyes. For eyes treated for DME, OCT measurements after treatment were imputed using last observation carried forward (LOCF). Missing data for eyes not treated with alternative DME treatment were imputed using Markov chain Monte Carlo multiple imputation, which incorporated changes from baseline to all monthly visits.23 The estimated treatment effect includes the adjusted mean difference and 2-sided 95% confidence interval (CI), with a statistical significance threshold of P value <0.05.

Sensitivity analyses included a per-protocol and a control-based imputation analysis for missing data.24 A post hoc analysis used LOCF just before any anti-VEGF treatment regardless of indication to remove the effect of anti-VEGF injections administered before the primary outcome timepoint. A protocol revision changed the enrollment stratification from 3 CST categories to 2. The prespecified subgroup analyses were performed using the final 2 strata: CST <75 μm and CST ≥75 μm above CI-DME threshold.

For other outcomes, similar analyses to the primary outcome were followed. For binary outcomes, treatment effects (odds ratio) were tested using a generalized linear model with a logistic link function. P values were reported for preselected secondary outcomes. The false discovery rate for the secondary outcomes was controlled at 0.05 using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure; P values and CIs were adjusted accordingly.25 Efficacy analyses followed the intention-to-treat principle. The safety analyses included randomized participants who received ≥1 dose of study treatment. Analyses were conducted using SAS software, Version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc).

Results

Study Population

From May 2023 to December 2024 participants were randomly assigned to treatment with tonabersat (n = 64 [71 eyes]) or placebo (n = 65 [73 eyes], Fig 1). Sixty-one (95%) and 60 (92%) randomized participants completed the primary outcome visit for the tonabersat and placebo groups, respectively. Overall, 37% of the participants were female, the mean (SD) age was 63 (10) years, 64% were White, 16% were Hispanic or Latino, and 18% were Black. The mean (SD) baseline CST (Cirrus equivalent) was 359 (63) μm in the tonabersat group and 362 (59) μm in the placebo group (Tables 1 and 2). Of note, 23% in the tonabersat group and 9% in the placebo group had self-reported history of renal disease, and mean estimated glomerular filtration rates (eGFRs) were 74 and 82 mL/min/1.73 m2, Given the expected impact of anti-VEGF treatment on CST, a post hoc sensitivity analysis applying LOCF for eyes treated with anti-VEGF for any indication was performed; among eyes in the tonabersat group, the mean (SD) change in CST from baseline to 6 months was –15 (53) μm versus +10 (47) μm in placebo group (adjusted mean difference: –21 [95% CI, –38 to –3], P = 0.02; Table 2). Study Drug

Of the 128 participants with ≥1 nonmissing bottle check-in, good compliance (>75% of required pills taken) was achieved by 53 of 63 (84%) participants in the tonabersat group and 57 of 65 (88%) in the placebo group, with a median treatment compliance of 98% and 96%, respectively. No participants in the tonabersat group and 3 (5%) participants in the placebo group reduced their study drug dosage because of intolerable side effects during the study; 13 (20%) and 6 (9%) participants, respectively, discontinued the study drug before the 6-month visit. The reasons for study drug discontinuation were AEs (tonabersat group [9]; placebo group [1]) and initiation of alternative DME treatment (tonabersat group [4]; placebo group [5]; Table S6, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org).

Discussion

In this phase II randomized clinical trial of eyes with CI-DME and good VA, there was no statistically significant difference in the primary analysis of mean change in CST at 6 months after treatment with oral tonabersat versus placebo. However, there were consistent, modest anatomic trends favoring tonabersat across additional analyses including for retinal volume and presence of CI-DME at 6 months. To reduce confounding from anti-VEGF effects on DME, a post hoc analysis that used LOCF for study eyes treated with anti-VEGF for any indications was performed and found a significant benefit with tonabersat treatment in CST thickness reduction.

The treatment effect may be more pronounced in thicker eyes; subgroup analyses found tonabersat resulted in greater reduction in retinal thickening in eyes with more severe baseline central retinal thickening. As the preplanned primary analysis did not demonstrate a statistically significant overall treatment effect and the number of thicker eyes enrolled in the study was small, this subgroup finding should be interpreted as exploratory. The present study design was informed by results from DRCR Retina Network Protocol V,26 which found that eyes with greater baseline CST were more likely to need anti-VEGF treatment. We initially aimed to recruit equal numbers of eyes across 3 CST strata (<75 μm, 75 to <175 μm, and ≥175 μm above threshold), to enroll a sufficient number of thicker eyes that were more likely to develop worsening CI-DME and had less ceiling effect for potential anatomic improvement. Because of challenges with recruitment of eyes with thicker CST despite relaxing VA criteria from ≥79 to ≥74 letters and the cap on prior anti-VEGF injections to encourage enrollment of eyes with thicker CST, the final study population ultimately included 67% of eyes in the thinnest CST thickness stratum, which may have limited the power to detect statistically significant anatomic treatment differences overall.

Oral therapy for diabetic retinal disease, such as CI-DME, offers several potential advantages to intraocular treatment, including increased access to care, the possibility of fewer ocular complications compared with more invasive treatments, and a systemic pharmacotherapeutic route that may more effectively target certain underlying disease mechanisms than existing local therapies. However, systemic medication exposure requires appropriate surveillance and may not provide comparable treatment effect to highly effective anti-VEGF injections. Nonetheless, it is possible that a systemic therapy with a modest or gradual therapeutic response could still offer important benefits for preservation or improvement of VA in CI-DME. An effective and well-tolerated noninvasive systemic treatment for DME could potentially be used as initial therapy to reduce patient burden by delaying or potentially avoiding invasive treatments, as adjunctive therapy to decrease injection frequency when combined with local intraocular therapy, or be used to prevent DME recurrence after successful intravitreal treatment. If future clinical trials are planned with tonabersat for CI-DME, results from this study, including the magnitude of effect size on CST change and the interaction observed between baseline CST and treatment effect, may guide protocol design.

Oral tonabersat (15–400 mg) was previously evaluated in phase I/II trials for treatment of migraine and was well-tolerated with no significant systemic safety concerns. Dizziness, drowsiness, and headache were the most reported adverse effects in previous trials. In this phase II trial involving patients with CI-DME, the study population was comparatively older and had more systemic comorbidities. Dizziness was the most common systemic AE (27% with tonabersat, 8% with placebo). In response to early reports of dizziness, all study drug dosing was recommended at bedtime. More patients receiving tonabersat discontinued the study drug because of AEs compared with those receiving placebo. In contrast to prior trials for migraine, there was no difference in drowsiness between groups, and fewer patients reported headaches with tonabersat than with placebo.

There were no differences between groups in renal laboratory parameter changes, including eGFR or urine albumin/creatinine from baseline to 6 months, and the remaining panel of exploratory blood and urine tests were also unremarkable. Although more frequent hospitalizations occurred in the tonabersat group, these were generally related to pre-existing medical conditions. Ultimately, any viable systemic therapy for diabetic retinal disease will require careful long-term characterization of its systemic impact in this population of patients with diabetes and associated comorbidities.

Limitations of this study include the relatively short duration of treatment and the small number of eyes enrolled with thicker maculas, which may have limited the ability to detect overall treatment effects because of variation in response across baseline CST or resulted in a ceiling effect. In addition, although the prespecified primary analysis used LOCF only for eyes that received treatment for DME, it did not account for the anti-VEGF effect on CST of eyes being treated for other indications, such as DR. Thus, a key post hoc sensitivity analysis was performed to address this limitation and reduce the potential for confounding by anti-VEGF treatment in eyes that received anti-VEGF for any indication.

Among eyes with CI-DME and good VA, there was not a statistically significant improvement in CST at 6 months with orally administered tonabersat versus placebo with the prespecified primary analysis. However, analyses limiting anti-VEGF treatment impact and subgroup analyses on thicker eyes were consistent with a favorable biological effect. Tonabersat treatment was associated with more dizziness among study participants but consistent safety concerns were not identified. These results may inform further investigation of tonabersat and continued research into the role of NLRP3 inflammasome therapy for DME.

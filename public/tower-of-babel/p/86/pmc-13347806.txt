Outcomes of Onyx embolisation as primary treatment for intracranial dural arteriovenous fistulas over the past two decades
Authors: Xin Su, Zihao Song, Yiguang Chen, Hongqi Zhang, Peng Zhang, Yongjie Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMost current studies on Onyx are limited by small sample sizes and often involve its use in combination with other embolic materials, with few investigations specifically evaluating Onyx as a sole embolic agent.MethodsUsing the Dural Arteriovenous Fistula Research and Management in China (DREAM-INI) database, we identified patients who underwent primary treatment for dural arteriovenous fistula (DAVF) with Onyx-only embolisation. Data collected included patient demographics, comorbidities, clinical manifestations, DAVF location, arterial supply, Borden and Cognard classifications, endovascular modalities, angiographic results, complications, and both clinical and angiographic follow-up. Multivariate logistic regression analyses were conducted to identify predictors of immediate complete obliteration, complications and poor clinical outcomes.ResultsA total of 471 patients with DAVFs underwent primary embolisation using Onyx. The median duration of clinical follow-up was 35.0 months (IQR 10.0–75.3), while the median angiographic follow-up was 24.0 months (IQR 8.0–63.0). Immediate complete obliteration was achieved in 417 patients (88.5%). Procedure-related complications occurred in 31 cases (6.6%). At the final follow-up, 90.6% of patients demonstrated good clinical outcomes. Borden type III DAVFs were positively associated with immediate complete obliteration, whereas the presence of occipital and pial artery feeders was negatively associated. Tentorial location correlated with a higher risk of complications, and age above 65 years emerged as a significant predictor of poor clinical outcome.ConclusionsIn this large single-centre study based on the DREAM-INI database, we report satisfactory obliteration rates, a low incidence of complications and favourable outcomes following Onyx-based single-agent primary embolisation for single DAVFs.

Introduction

Intracranial dural arteriovenous fistulas (DAVFs) are rare vascular disorders characterised by abnormal connections between meningeal arteries and venous sinuses or subarachnoid veins located within the dural leaflets.1 2 They account for approximately 10–15% of all intracranial vascular malformations.3 Current treatment options for DAVFs include endovascular embolisation, microsurgery and stereotactic radiosurgery, with endovascular embolisation being the preferred modality and demonstrating favourable therapeutic outcomes.48 The favourable outcomes of endovascular treatment for DAVFs are attributed to the introduction of the non-adhesive liquid embolic agent Onyx (Medtronic, MN, USA), which allows for more controlled and prolonged casting.911 Most current studies on Onyx are based on small sample sizes and often report its use in combination with other embolic materials, with a lack of research specifically focusing on Onyx as a sole embolic agent.41214 Even the recent CONDOR study on Onyx included only over 100 cases overall.8 Since Onyx was introduced to the Chinese market in 2003, our centre has used it for the embolisation of DAVFs for around two decades. In an effort to gain a deeper understanding of its performance, the present study draws on a large, single-centre DAVF database from the Dural Arteriovenous Fistula Research and Management in China (DREAM-INI). We aimed to evaluate the obliteration rate, complication profile and clinical outcomes of primary embolisation using Onyx as the sole agent, as well as to identify predictors associated with each of these endpoints.

Methods

Data collection and definition

Patient data were obtained from the DREAM-INI database, which is derived from a retrospective, single-centre observational study conducted between January 2001 and December 2022.61517 Drawing from this extensive database, the present study focused on a specific subset of patients who underwent Onyx-only embolisation as the primary treatment for DAVFs, delivered via a transarterial, transvenous or combined transarterial–transvenous approach (figure 1). For the sake of analytical clarity, patients with multiple DAVFs were also excluded from the study.

Patient selection process. DAVF, dural arteriovenous fistulas.

The collected data encompassed patient demographics, comorbidities, clinical presentation, location of the DAVF, arterial feeders, Borden and Cognard classifications, endovascular treatment strategies, angiographic outcomes, treatment-related complications, as well as clinical and angiographic follow-up information. Venous varices were defined according to the criteria established by Cognard et al2. Favourable baseline functional status and good clinical outcome were defined as a modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score of 0–2 at baseline and at the latest follow-up, respectively.

Because of the retrospective and observational nature of the study, the requirement for informed consent was waived. The study protocol was approved by the institutional review boards of the participating institutions.

Endovascular procedures

All procedures were carried out under general anaesthesia in a biplane angiography suite. Bilateral diagnostic angiography of the internal and external carotid as well as the vertebral arteries was obtained prior to intervention. Subsequently, endovascular embolisation was carried out with a dimethyl-sulfoxide compatible microcatheter (Marathon or Apollo, Medtronic Neurovascular), using Onyx 18, 20 or 34 under roadmap guidance. In selected cases, primarily patients with DAVFs of the transverse-sigmoid sinus or superior sagittal sinus, transarterial liquid embolisation combined with transvenous balloon-assisted venous sinus protection was performed, using either the Sterling balloon (Boston Scientific, USA) or the Copernic RC balloon (Balt Extrusion, Montmorency, France), as previously described by Liu et al.18 In cases where the microcatheter could not be advanced close to the fistula site or in DAVFs with extensive fistulous networks, a dual-lumen balloon catheter (Sceptre XC; MicroVention, Aliso Viejo, CA) was employed to prevent reflux and facilitate deeper Onyx penetration. On completion of embolisation, diagnostic angiography of the feeder territories was performed to assess treatment outcomes.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (V.4.2.3). Continuous variables were expressed as means with SD, while categorical variables were presented as counts and percentages. Student’s t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sum test was applied for continuous variables, and Pearson’s χ² test or Fisher’s exact test was used for categorical variables, depending on applicability. Univariate analyses were performed to identify potential predictors of complete obliteration, complications and unfavourable outcomes. Variables with a p value <0.2 in univariate analysis were included in multivariate logistic regression models targeting complete obliteration, treatment-related complications and poor clinical outcomes (mRS >2). Before inclusion, multicollinearity was assessed using variance inflation factors, and no variable showed evidence of severe collinearity (all variance inflation factors <5). To evaluate potential effect modification, first-order interaction terms between key covariates (eg, location × arterial feeder type) were introduced into the multivariable logistic regression models for immediate complete occlusion and complications. No statistically significant interactions were observed. A p value ≤0.05 was considered indicative of statistical significance.

Results

Baseline characteristics of patients and fistulas

A total of 471 patients who underwent Onyx-only embolisation as the primary treatment for single DAVFs were identified from the DREAM-INI database. The cohort had a mean age of 51.3 years (±11.8), and the majority were male (326 patients, 69.2%). The most common clinical presentations included intracranial haemorrhage (17.2%), non-haemorrhagic neurological deficits (23.8%), flow-related symptoms (48.0%), and unrelated or asymptomatic presentations (11.0%). The majority of DAVFs in this cohort were located in the transverse-sigmoid sinus (33.5%), tentorium (23.1%), and superior sagittal sinus or convexity (16.1%). The most common arterial feeders were the middle meningeal artery (78.8%), occipital artery (59.4%) and meningohypophyseal trunk (33.8%). More than half of the DAVFs were classified as Borden grade III (57.5%); additionally, 29.5% were Cognard type IV and 5.7% were Cognard type V (table 1).

Treatment outcome

A total of 559 embolisation procedures were successfully performed for 471 DAVFs in this cohort, with a mean of 1.19 procedures per fistula. The maximum number of embolisation procedures required for a single fistula was 4. The majority of DAVFs (72.5%) were embolised via a transarterial approach based on procedures. On a per-patient basis, 350 DAVFs (74.3%) were embolised via a transarterial approach, with complete obliteration achieved in 315 patients (90.0%) on immediate angiography following the final treatment session. Balloon-assisted Onyx embolisation was performed in 44 patients. In transarterial procedures, the immediate complete obliteration rate did not differ significantly between balloon-assisted and non-balloon-assisted Onyx embolisation (90.9% vs 86.3%, p=0.554). The transvenous approach was employed in 57 patients (12.1%), with complete obliteration achieved in 55 of them (96.5%) on immediate angiography following the final treatment session. Balloon protection was used in 16 patients to preserve venous sinus patency. A total of 64 patients (13.6%) underwent embolisation using a combined transarterial and transvenous approach, with complete obliteration achieved in 47 (73.4%) on immediate angiography following the final treatment session. Balloon protection was employed in 50 patients to preserve the patency of the venous sinus. The immediate complication rates were lower with combined transarterial–transvenous approaches (47/64, 73.4%) compared with transarterial (315/350, 90.0%) and transvenous approaches (55/57, 96.5%) (p<0.001; data not shown in the table).

Overall, complete obliteration was achieved in 417 patients (88.5%) on immediate angiography following the final treatment session (table 2). Borden type III classification (OR 5.49 (95% CI 2.35 to 12.83), p <0.001) was a positive predictor of immediate complete obliteration of DAVFs, while the presence of an occipital artery feeder (OR 0.41 (95% CI 0.18 to 0.91), p=0.029) and pial artery feeder (OR 0.36 (95% CI 0.15 to 0.86), p=0.022) were inverse predictors (table 3).

A total of 31 complications (6.6%) were observed, including intracranial haemorrhage in 9 patients (1.9%), ischaemic events in 4 (0.8%), cranial nerve palsy in 14 (3.0%), trigeminal cardiac reflex in 2 (0.4%) and other complications in 2 patients (0.4%) (table 2). Of the complications, 22 were transient and 9 were permanent. Approximately 80% of the complications were observed either intraoperatively or in the immediate postoperative period. More than 90% of cranial nerve palsies resolved spontaneously within 6 months after the procedure. There were two deaths attributed to procedural complications, both of which were related to haemorrhage. Tentorial DAVFs (OR 3.71 (95% CI 1.38 to 10.00), p=0.009) were associated with an elevated risk of complications (table 4). The complication rates showed no significant differences among the transarterial (19/350, 5.4%), transvenous (7/57, 12.3%) and combined transarterial–transvenous approaches (5/64, 7.8%) (p=0.131), nor between balloon-assisted and non-balloon-assisted transarterial embolisation (9.1% vs 4.9%, p=0.429; data not shown in table).

Follow-up outcomes

The median clinical follow-up duration was 35.0 months (IQR 10.0–75.3), while the median angiographic follow-up duration was 24.0 months (IQR 8.0–63.0). 19 deaths were reported during clinical follow-up, of which one was related to the DAVF, and two others were associated with complications as previously described. At the final follow-up, a favourable functional outcome (mRS score 0–2) was observed in 376 out of 415 patients (90.6%). A baseline mRS score of 0–2 (OR 0.19 (95% CI 0.08–0.45), p <0.001) was an inverse predictor of poor functional outcome, whereas age over 65 years (OR 3.55 (95% CI 1.47–8.53), p=0.005) was a positive predictor of poor outcome (table 5). The final obliteration rate at angiographic follow-up was 93.4%. Among the 289 patients with angiographic follow-up who achieved initial complete obliteration, 14 recurrences (4.8%) were detected.

Discussion

Onyx is a non-adhesive liquid embolic agent composed of ethylene-vinyl alcohol copolymer, available in various concentrations. It is dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide and suspended in micronised tantalum powder to provide radiopacity under fluoroscopy. Compared with NBCA, Onyx allows for more controlled and prolonged casting, enabling retrograde filling of multiple arterial feeders from a single arterial injection. Additionally, its non-adhesive property permits temporary pauses during injection to assess the progress of embolisation.4 8 These properties significantly improve the curative rate of endovascular treatment for DAVFs, making Onyx the mainstream embolic agent in current practice.19 20

Gross et al demonstrated in their study that, following the introduction of Onyx in the latter half of the study period, the number of treated DAVFs doubled. Furthermore, the initial angiographic obliteration rate significantly increased from 60% in the pre-Onyx era to 76% after its adoption.7 Moreover, their study also showed that the use of transarterial embolisation alone significantly increased from 43% to 61% in the Onyx era compared with the pre-Onyx period. The recent multicenter Japanese registry study, JR-NET 4, also suggested a shift in treatment strategies following the approval of Onyx, with Borden type III DAVFs, traditionally managed by direct surgery, and sinus type DAVFs, such as transverse-sigmoid sinus DAVFs, previously treated with transvenous embolisation, now increasingly managed with transarterial embolisation.21 Sadeh-Gonike et al performed a meta-analysis evaluating the therapeutic outcomes of transarterial embolisation with Onyx, reporting an initial complete obliteration rate of 82% and a midterm recurrence rate of just 2%.20 Li et al analysed long-term outcomes of 146 patients with DAVFs treated using Onyx, based on data from the CONDOR database. Initial embolisation resulted in complete obliteration in 55% of cases, with the overall obliteration rate increasing to 68.5% following all treatment sessions.8 Transvenous embolisation with Onyx has also become increasingly popular due to its ability to achieve controlled obliteration of the proximal draining vein and shunt points.22 In our cohort, primary embolisation with Onyx achieved a complete obliteration rate of 88.5%, with rates exceeding 90% using either the transarterial or transvenous approach alone. However, patients with multiple DAVFs, who typically present with more complex conditions and lower cure rates, were excluded from the analysis. Overall, the introduction of Onyx has markedly improved the cure rates for DAVFs and reshaped the treatment paradigm.

It is not surprising that Borden type III DAVFs are a positive predictor of immediate complete obliteration, as their aggressive nature often drives clinicians to pursue definitive cure as soon as the lesion is detected. The CONDOR study also identified the occipital artery as a significant predictor of non-obliteration in multivariate analysis.8 This finding is reasonable, as the artery’s tortuous course and narrow transosseous segments often make catheterisation technically challenging, thereby affecting both the effectiveness and progression of treatment. The pial artery was also found to be a significant inverse predictor of immediate complete obliteration, which is consistent with the fact that DAVFs with pial artery supply tend to have more complex angioarchitecture and require more elaborate treatment strategies.16

We found that a tentorial location was significantly associated with a higher risk of complications. In JR-NET2 and JR-NET3, a tentorial location was also found to be significantly associated with the occurrence of complications.21 Tentorial DAVFs often receive a pial arterial supply, and due to the presence of dural–pial anastomoses and the distal location of the shunt point relative to the feeders, migration of liquid embolic agents into the pial arteries can result in ischaemic complications.16 In addition, tentorial DAVFs are often supplied by the petrosal branch of the middle meningeal artery, and transarterial embolisation via this route carries a high risk of facial nerve palsy. The high prevalence of venous ectasia in the tentorial region is also closely associated with the occurrence of haemorrhagic complications.23 24

At the latest follow-up, 90.8% of patients had a good clinical outcome. Poorer functional outcomes were observed in patients over 65 years of age. In our cohort, only 3 of the 19 deaths were related to DAVF or its treatment. The remaining deaths were attributed to other chronic diseases, such as cancer and heart disease. These findings indicate a favourable post-treatment prognosis with Onyx for most DAVFs in this cohort, including those located in the tentorial region and anterior cranial fossa.

Study limitations

This study has several limitations. First, because only about 60% of patients underwent follow-up angiography, the durability of obliteration may be subject to attrition bias. Patients who did not return for follow-up imaging could potentially differ in clinical course or angiographic outcome from those who did, which may lead to an over- or underestimation of the true long-term obliteration rate. Therefore, while our results suggest good durability, the findings should be interpreted with caution in light of this limitation. Second, being a single-centre study, the findings may have limited generalisability to wider patient populations. Third, at our centre, glue is typically used for embolising ‘pure’ pial arterial feeders.16 As a result, most DAVF cases with pial arterial supply that were embolised using glue were excluded from this study. Finally, for the sake of statistical analysis, we excluded cases of multiple DAVFs. However, it is worth noting that some of the most challenging DAVFs in terms of treatment and prognosis are those occurring in patients with multiple lesions or with pial arterial feeders.16 17 The exclusion of such cases may have influenced the results and contributed to the higher rate of immediate complete obliteration observed in our study compared with others. However, the large cohort and substantial patient population allowed us to provide valuable insights into the real-world application of Onyx as the sole embolic agent in the treatment of single DAVFs.

Conclusion

In this large single-centre study based on the DREAM-INI database, we report satisfactory obliteration rates, a low incidence of complications and favourable outcomes following Onyx-based single-agent primary embolisation for single DAVFs. The presence of an occipital artery feeder and pial arterial supply was associated with incomplete obliteration, while tentorial location was linked to a higher rate of complications.

Disturbance of Oxylipin Metabolism Mediated by Autophagy of Mesenteric Adipocytes Orchestrates Gut Inflammation in Crohn’s Disease
Authors: Yi Yin, Xiaolong Zheng, Yufeng Wang, Siqing Jing, Shixian Wang, Yi Li, Meng Wang, Wei Ge
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background & AimsHypertrophy of mesenteric adipose tissue (MAT) and formation of creeping fat (CF) is a typical hallmark of Crohn’s disease (CD). MAT of CD patients exhibited disturbed oxylipin metabolism. This study aimed to explore the role of oxylipin in modulating gut inflammation and the underlying mechanism for the regulation of oxylipin metabolism.MethodsMAT biopsies were recruited from 14 patients with CD and 12 controls, and oxylipin analysis was performed. A series of in vitro and in vivo experiments were conducted to explore the effect of oxylipin and the corresponding metabolic enzymes on shaping immune cells. Western blot, quantitative polymerase chain reaction (q-PCR) and immunofluorescence were performed to further verify the underlying mechanisms.ResultsThe oxylipin assays revealed the end product derived from CYP450-epoxide hydrolase-mediated metabolic pathway was notably increased in CF. The upregulation of soluble epoxide hydrolase (sEH) contributed to the disturbance of oxylipin metabolism. Beclin-1 (BCN1) mediated autophagy of mesenteric adipocytes regulated sEH expression through p62-Keap1-Nrf2 pathway. Inhibition of BCN1 increased the downstream pro-inflammatory oxylipin concentration and promoted M1-like macrophages. The adipocyte-specific BCN1 knockout mice (Becn1Ad) demonstrated susceptibility to dextran sulfate sodium-induced colitis with exacerbated systemic inflammation.ConclusionsOur findings showed that BCN1-mediated autophagy of adipocytes from MAT inhibited pro-inflammatory oxylipins via promoting sEH-related metabolic pathway, suggesting fatty acid metabolism and sEH/BCN1 as potential therapeutic targets for controlling gut inflammation in CD.

Summary

The creeping fat of Crohn’s disease exhibited disturbed oxylipin metabolism which is correlated with sEH overexpression. This process was regulated by BCN1-derived autophagy and ultimately exaggerated gut inflammation.

What You Need to Know

Background

The hypertrophic mesenteric adipose tissue and formation of creeping fat of Crohn’s disease exhibited disturbed oxylipin metabolism, which is correlated with the development of gut inflammation.

Impact

This study proved soluble epoxide hydrolase-associated oxylipin metabolism regulated M1-like macrophages polarization and exerted pro-inflammatory effect, and this process was modulated by Beclin-1-induced autophagy.

Future Directions

Fatty acid metabolism is crucial for maintaining immune hemostasis of mesenteric adipose tissue. Soluble epoxide hydrolase and Beclin-1are potential therapeutic targets for controlling gut inflammation in Crohn’s disease.

Crohn’s disease (CD) is a chronic inflammatory disease mainly affecting the gastrointestinal tract, with progressive transmural lesions that lead to fistula and strictures.1 Disease remission is the main target of drug management. However, approximately 75% of patients with CD undergo at least one surgical intervention due to severe complications.2 Hence, seeking for neo-therapeutic target for CD treatment is a major goal in current research. Hypertrophic mesenteric adipose tissue wrapping the inflamed intestine (htMAT), also known as “creeping fat (CF)” is significant pathognomonic feature of Crohn’s disease.3 CF has been identified associated with excessive mucosal lesion, transmural inflammation, extracellular matrix deposition, and stricture formation.4,5 Meanwhile, CF is the main source of proinflammatory cytokines, which aggravate systemic inflammation.6 These results suggest that CF plays important roles in the pathogenesis of intestinal lesion. However, the underlying mechanism of action remains unclear.

Oxylipins are downstream endogenous lipid signaling molecules produced by enzymatic metabolism of polyunsaturated fatty acids, which have been implicated in the pathogenesis of inflammation and cancer.7,8 The oxylipin metabolism has aroused great interest in recent research, owing to its critical roles in monocyte lineage recruitment and activity to modulate inflammation resolution.9,10 Among which, the cytochrome p450 (CYP450) monooxygenases and downstream soluble epoxide hydrolase (sEH)-associated metabolic pathway demonstrated as promising therapeutic target for treating enteritis and colorectal cancer.8,11 sEH converts CYP-derived fatty acid epoxides to the corresponding fatty acid diols. Emerging research supports that sEH increased in adipose tissues of obese subjects and sEH-derived oxylipins exerted proinflammatory effect on immune cells. We previously reported that the impaired fatty acid desaturation and lipid mediator imbalance within CF contributed to chronic inflammation in CD.12 The active oxylipin metabolic profiles still remain unclear in CF, and the specific correlation with intestinal inflammation requires further investigation.

Autophagy, a cellular catabolic process resulting in lysosome-mediated recycling of organelles and protein, plays important roles in maintaining intestinal integrity and balancing inflammatory responses in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD).13 Autophagy dysfunction, including impaired autophagosome formation and degradation of cytoplasmic microorganisms and defective presentation of bacterial antigens, is highly involved in the disease pathogenesis and progression.14 Autophagy-related genes such as NOD2 and ATG16L1 exhibited disrupted functions in CD, leading to increased proinflammatory cytokine production in experimental colitis. Of note, autophagy contributed to free fatty acid release and lipid peroxidation.15 Meanwhile, autophagy is crucial for the differentiation of adipose tissue and the ablation of autophagy in adipocytes disrupted lipolysis.16 However, the interplay between autophagy and oxylipin metabolism in mesenteric adipose tissue (MAT) of CD still remains unclear.

In this study, we explored the eicosanoids profile in CF and identified sEH as the key regulator for the disturbance of oxylipin metabolites. The evaluated downstream oxylipin metabolites promoted macrophage polarization and exaggerated inflammation. We also found depressed Beclin-1 (BCN1)-derived autophagy of adipocytes in CF was closely correlated with sEH expression through the p62-Keap1-Nrf2 pathway. The susceptibility to dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced colitis was confirmed in adipocyte-specific BCN1 knockout (Becn1Ad) mice, which could be rescued with sEH inhibitor supplementation.

Lipidomic Profiling of CF of Patients With CD

To investigate the roles of oxylipins in pro-inflammatory nature of creeping fat, we adopted liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS)-based lipidomics to compare the profiles of eicosanoid metabolites among the hypertrophic mesenteric adipose tissue (htMAT) and adjacent MAT of patients with CD and healthy controls. The baseline of the CD patients is exhibited in Supplementary Table 1. We quantified 66 eicosanoid metabolites in CF tissues, some of which were below the detectable limitation of the LC-MS/MS method (the lipidomic profile is listed in Supplementary Table 2). The heatmap revealed notable discrimination of the concentration of the oxylipins among htMAT, MAT, and healthy controls (Figure 1B). Among the detected oxylipins, sEH-derived fatty acid diols, including arachidonic acid (ARA)-derived 11,12-,14,15-epoxyeicosatrienoic acids (EET), 8,9-,11,12-,14,15-dihydroxyeicosatrienoic acid (DHET), and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA)-derived 19,20-dihydroxydocosapentaenoic acid (DiHDPE) were major constituents contributing to the disturbance of oxylipin metabolism among groups (Figure 1D). Notably, the mesenteric concentrations of 11,12-EET in the htMAT group vs controls were 32.91 ± 5.48 vs 83.03 ± 11.61 nmol/g (P < .001), the concentrations of 14,15-EET were 41.99 ± 8.77 vs 102.8 ± 24.43 (P = .017), the concentrations of 14,15-DHET were 3.47 ± 0.32 vs 2.22 ± 0.33 (P = .006), the concentrations of 8,9-DHET were 5.38 ± 0.76 vs 3.03 ± 0.50 (P = .027), and the concentrations of 19,20-DiHDPE were 29.07 ± 1.88 vs 18.88 ± 1.66 (P < .001) (Figure 1D and Supplementary Table 3). Given the fact that EETs alleviate inflammation, and therefore the decreased concentration of EETs in htMAT is in agreement with aggravated adjacent colonic inflammation. Of note, DHETs, the downstream products of EETs, have been proved owing to proinflammatory properties, which may also contribute to the pathogenesis of the disease. sEH Expression Was Notably Increased in htMATs

Given our findings that the altered oxylipins mainly derived from CYP450-sEH metabolic pathway (Figure 1C), we explored the expression of enzymes involved in the fatty acid epoxides and diols biosynthesis. CYP450 monooxygenases metabolize the membrane-incorporated polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) released by phospholipase A2 (PLA2) to generate free PUFAs, which can be further hydrolyzed into fatty acid diols with sEH and other epoxide hydrolases (Figure 1C). Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) demonstrated that expression of ephx2 (encoding sEH) was significantly increased in htMAT, whereas no notable difference of expression of ephx1 (encoding microsomal epoxide hydrolase [mEH]), pla2, isoforms of CYP monooxygenases (including cyp2c29, cyp2c37, cyp2c38, cyp2c39, cyp2c40, cyp2c44, cyp2c50, cyp2c54, cyp2c55, cyp2c65, cyp2j5, cyp2j6, cyp2j8, cyp2j9, cyp2j13, and cyp3a11) was detected among groups (Figure 2A). Immunohistochemistry, immunofluorescence, and Western blot also indicated that sEH was increased in htMAT (Figure 2B–E). BCN1-Induced Autophagy Was Inhibited in htMAT

Previous studies have reported that ephx2 (encoding sEH) expression was closely associated with autophagy. ephx2 protein expression was increased in Atg7-deficient adipose tissues,17 whereas ephx1 (encoding another isoform of soluble hydrolase) expression was also increased in datasets acquired from other research in which autophagy genes such as Atg3 was specifically deleted in adipocytes.15 These results motivated us to explore the autophagy-related genes in htMAT to determine the relation between autophagy and upregulation of sEH. We performed RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) on mesenteric adipose tissues among groups, and of which autophagy-related genes expression was analyzed. The results revealed that the expression of BCN1 in htMAT was significantly higher than that in the healthy controls (Figure 3A). qRT-PCR further confirmed the suppressed expression of BCN1 in htMAT, whereas other autophagy-related genes including Atg13, Atg3, Atg4D, Atg10, Wipi2, and mTOR did not show apparent distinction among groups (Figure 3B). These results were confirmed with immunohistochemistry, immunofluorescence, and Western blot (Figure 3C–E, and G). Lipids are generated from lipolysis or through degradation of the lipid droplet via autophagy. To evaluate the autophagy levels in tissues, we assessed the htMAT explants from patients with CD for microscopic observation. Representative transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images from mesenteric adipose tissue indicated fewer autophagosomal double-membrane structures were found in htMAT compared with the control group (Figure 3F). The accumulation of the lapidated autophagosomal marker LC3 protein (LC3-I/II) and p62 protein were quantified, which reflect the autophagic flux substantially decreased in htMAT (Figure 3G). BCN1 Inhibition Upregulated sEH Expression via p62-Keap1-Nrf2 Pathway

We then explored whether BCN1-induced autophagy would shift the balance of oxylipins with primary mesenteric adipocytes. BCN1 expression level was modulated with BCN1 and BCN1-small interfering RNA (siRNA) lentiviral vectors (Figure 4A). The purity of primary mesenteric adipocytes derived from htMAT was assessed with flow cytometry. The results revealed that the CD34-CD36+CD45- population accounted for over 50% of the entire group, which met the criteria for further analysis (Figure 4B and C). Significant increases or decreases of BCN1 expression in primary adipocytes were confirmed (Figure 4D). We proved that inhibition of BCN1 in vitro notably promoted sEH protein level, whereas this alteration significantly reversed when BCN1 was upregulated. Previous studies have confirmed that sEH inhibition could activate autophagy pathway through Nrf2 upregulation and transcription activation.18 p62 phosphorylation greatly enhances its affinity for Kelch-like ECH-associated protein 1 (Keap1), inducing the Nrf2 release from Keap1.19 Our results revealed that inhibition of BCN1 significantly upregulated the formation of p62-Keap1-Nrf2 positive feedback loop and ultimately promoted sEH expression (Figure 4D). The lipidomic profiling was performed with the adipocytes and the supernatants. The relative abundance of EETs was significantly higher in the lentivirus group than the vector group or the siRNA group, whereas the downstream product DHETs demonstrated the opposite trend (Figure 4E). sEH-Derived Oxylipins Exerted Proinflammatory Effect via Aggravating Endoplasmic Reticulum Stress

It has been reported that supplement of EETs or sEH inhibitors alleviate neuroinflammation through blocking endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress.20 Meanwhile, the oxylipins derived from EETs are proved to promote M1-like macrophage polarization and aggravated intestinal inflammation and tumorigenesis.8 We then modulated sEH expression with sEH and sEH-siRNA lentiviral vectors in primary mesenteric adipocytes, and assessed the supernatants of the culture medium to treat THP-1 cells (Figure 5A). The lipidomics analysis of sEH-derived oxylipin metabolic shifts in the supernatants followed a similar pattern of change as the BCN1-modulated adipocytes (Figure 5B). The results revealed that overexpression of sEH promoted M1 polarization of THP-1 cells, and the supplement of EETs largely reversed the phenomenon (Figure 5C). The underlying mechanism might be attributed to ER modulation. We further quantified the ER-related protein expression, and the results revealed that increased level of 3 key sensors in the ER stress-signaling pathway was found in macrophages in lentivirus group, including PKR-like ER-resident kinase (PERK), inositol-requiring enzyme 1α (IRE1α), and activating transcription factor 6 (ATF6) (Figure 5D). Phosphorylation of eukaryotic initiation factor 2 subunit α (eIF2α) and spliced X-box binding protein 1 (Xbp1), mediated by phospho-PERK and phosphorylation of IRE1α accordingly, was also increased (Figure 5D and E). These results demonstrated full-scale activation of the ER stress pathway in the sEH-overexpression group. Furthermore, the levels of inducible nitric oxide (iNOS) and superoxide dismutase (SOD) were also quantified among groups to assess the oxidative stress. The results showed that inhibition of sEH or supplement with EETs protected THP-1 cells against oxidative stress (Figure 5F and G). Loss of Adipocyte Autophagy Exacerbates Intestinal Inflammation In Vivo

Given adipose autophagy as a modulator for oxylipin metabolism and a potential therapeutic target for treating intestinal inflammation, we next investigated whether loss of adipocyte autophagy modulate intestinal inflammation through sEH-derived oxylipin metabolism. We used a tamoxifen-inducible knockout mouse model to delete BCN1 in mature adipocytes in adult mice (BCN1Ad). Colitis was induced with 2% DSS water (Figure 6A). The results revealed that BCN1Ad mice were more susceptible to colitis with significant body weight loss and shorter colon length when compared with wild-type (WT) mice during acute inflammation (Figure 6C and D). Meanwhile, disease activity index (DAI) was recorded, which is a credible indicator of intestinal inflammation. It revealed that DSS-treated BCN1Ad mice gained a higher DAI score than that of WT littermates (Figure 6B). Blinded histopathological assessment demonstrated more severe colitis with large amounts of inflammatory cells infiltrating the submucosal layer of the colonic tissues, epithelial damage, and mucosal rupture in BCN1Ad mice treated with DSS (Figure 6E and F), and this was largely ameliorated with trifluoromethoxyphenyl-3-(1-propionylpiperidin-4-yl) urea (TPPU) or EET treatment. Meanwhile, the infiltration of inflammatory immune cells in MAT and the abnormal morphology of MAT was also reversed with sEH inhibition and EET supplementation (Figure 6H). Meanwhile, sEH inhibition and EET treatment significantly reduced the proinflammatory cytokines in the colonic tissues (Figure 6G). Furthermore, we studied the macrophage recruitment and immune responses in MAT derived from WT and BCN1Ad mice. The results revealed that F4/80, markers of necroptosis (receptor-interacting serine/threonineprotein kinase 3 [RIP3], p-RIP3) and apoptosis (cleaved caspase 3) were increased in BCN1Ad mouse MAT (Figure 7E and F). These results indicated the disruption of autophagy driven by BCN1 was associated with mesenteric inflammation and might further intensify colitis through “mesentery-gut” axis. The lipidomic profile was quantified in vivo, and the results revealed that loss of adipose autophagy significantly reduced the ratios of epoxy fatty acid to their corresponding diol fatty acid pairs both in MAT and systematic circulation (Figure 7A and B). The upregulation of p62-Keap1-Nrf2 signaling pathway contributed to the changes in sEH enzyme expression and shifted the balance of oxylipins in the tissue and plasma (Figure 7C and D). Discussion

Our study demonstrates that the hypertrophic MAT of patients with CD has unique lipidomic metabolism profiling. The disturbance of oxylipin metabolism attributes to abnormal overexpression of sEH protein, which is a key hydrolase of fatty acid epoxide. Furthermore, we prove that sEH-mediated oxylipins is a part of the proinflammatory immune response to intestinal inflammation through promoting macrophage polarization and ER stress. The BCN1-induced adipocyte autophagy is inhibited in disease and is correlated with sEH expression via the p62-Keap1-Nrf2 pathway. Loss of adipocyte autophagy notably increased the susceptibility to colitis, whereas inhibition of sEH or supplement of EET can largely alleviate intestinal inflammation. Our results reveal the mechanism of “mesentery-gut” axis on the pathogenesis of colitis, enabling developing novel therapeutic approaches to target the crosstalk.

Oxylipins refer to a large family of lipids oxygenated from PUFAs, which have aroused great interest in research recently. Specific oxylipin signatures have been identified associated with obesity, liver diseases and colorectal cancer.21, 22, 23 However, little is known about the role of oxylipin metabolism in adipocytes in the pathogenesis of CD. Our study highlights the disturbance of oxylipin metabolism in htMAT and reveals the key modulator enzyme she, which is notably upregulated in htMAT. sEH is the major enzyme to convert the anti-inflammatory eicosanoids EETs to DHETs that are usually biologically inactive or proinflammatory24 and has been widely investigated in neuropsychiatric and cardiovascular disorders. Preclinical studies have found that the inhibition or deficiency of sEH exerted beneficial effects in several models of neuropsychiatric disorders.25 Of note, it has been reported that supplement of EET or sEH inhibitors alleviate neuroinflammation through blocking ER stress.20 Our study confirmed the anti-inflammatory property of sEH inhibitors in primary adipocytes and in experimental colitis. Increased sEH expression was accompanied with increased expression of ER stress markers, including p-eIF2α, p-IRE, p-PERK, and ATF6. Therefore, intervention with sEH expression is a potential therapeutic target for treating inflammation.

Pioneer studies provided evidence for the active role of autophagy in IBD etiology, which revealed the autophagy-related genes as susceptibility factors for CD with single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs).26 Human cells with the autophagy-related 16 like 1 (ATG16L1)T300A variant demonstrated impaired autophagy induction, decreased expression of antimicrobial peptides, and defects in antigen processing.27 The latest research further confirmed the positive correlation between autophagy and oxylipin imbalance in adipocytes in experimental colitis.17 Adipocytes are the main constituent of CF, and we explored the autophagy-associated genes in CF. Our results revealed that BCN1 was notably suppressed in htMAT, which modulated sEH expression through the p62-Keap1-Nrf2 pathway. Meanwhile, autophagy is capable of reducing ER stress in various inflammatory and autoimmune diseases. In a mice model of DSS-induced experimental colitis, enhancement of macroautophagy and chaperone-mediated autophagy by inducing mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) deregulation reduced ER stress and alleviated intestinal inflammation.28 Dysfunctional autophagy leads to increased ER stress in murine intestinal epithelial cells (IECs).29 Our study also demonstrated that genetical inhibition of BCN1 of adipocytes aggravated ER stress and enhanced susceptibility to DSS-induced colitis. Preclinical research has proved that treating with autophagy inducer ameliorated CD-like ileitis in il10−/− mice.30 These results suggest that targeting autophagy is a plausible approach for treating colitis.

In conclusion, our study demonstrates, for the first time, that oxylipin imbalance exists in the htMAT of patients with CD, which attributes to abnormal adipocyte autophagy of htMAT. BCN1-induced autophagy of adipocytes modulates sEH through the p62-Keap1-Nrf2 pathway. Supplement of EET or inhibition of sEH could effectively rescue colitis in adipocyte-specific BCN1-knockout mice, which suggests a potential therapeutic target for remodeling oxylipin metabolism.

Materials and Methods

Human Samples

The human htMAT and MAT biopsies were acquired from 14 patients with CD who underwent bowel resection with ileal stricture, characterized by chronic and intermittent intestinal obstruction that was refractory to intensive medical therapy with biologics. This was corroborated by radiological evidence of prestenotic dilation (>3–4 cm in diameter) on magnetic resonance enterography (MRE) and the endoscopic finding of fixed, rigid strictures. The htMAT was extending like fingerlike projections gripping the inflamed (involved) segments of intestine, which was adjacent to normal (uninvolved) MAT, which is pliable and attached longitudinally to a single border. Control samples were mesenteric tissues adjacent to the terminal ileum control from 12 patients with colonic tumors who underwent right hemicolectomy at Drum Tower Hospital from 2021 to 2023 (Supplementary Table 1). The biopsies were divided into 2 parts: one was snap-frozen and stored at −80°C for further use, and the other was fixed in 10% phosphate-buffered formalin and embedded in paraffin for further analysis. This study was approved by the Drum Tower Hospital Ethics Committee, and written informed consent was obtained from each patient.

Cell Intervention

The primary adipocytes used in the in vitro adipocyte experiments were derived from the mesenteric tissues from the control group. The harvested MAT biopsy tissues were washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) to remove erythrocytes and then digested with 0.1% type I collagenase (Sigma-Aldrich) at 37°C with continuous shaking (150 rpm for 60 minutes). After centrifugation, the upper fat layer was transferred to a 25 cm2 culture bottle containing Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 20% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Stem Cell Technologies).

To ascertain the role of sEH in regulating intestinal inflammation and its relationship to BCN1-derived autophagy, the expression of sEH and BCN1 was regulated in mesenteric adipocytes. siRNAs targeting sEH and BCN1 were designed and transfected into adipocytes using a lipid-based transfection reagent (Lipofectamine 3000). The transfection efficiency was verified by qRT-PCR and Western blotting. Lentiviral vectors containing the coding sequences (CDSs) for sEH and BCN1 were constructed, and adipocytes were transduced with viral particles (multiplicity of infection [MOI] = 20) in the presence of 8 μg/mL polybrene. Following a 72-hour incubation, cells were screened for stable expression using puromycin (2 μg/mL).

Mice

Adipoq-CreERT2 mice were purchased from GemPharmatech Co, Ltd (strain number T052679) and were crossed to BCN1 floxed mice (strain number T006677). Experimental cages were sex- and age-matched and balanced for genotypes. Genetic recombination was induced with oral administration of 4 mg tamoxifen per mouse at 8 weeks of age for 5 consecutive days. DSS-induced colitis was conducted 2 weeks after the last tamoxifen administration. The experimental colitis was induced with 2% (w/v) DSS (MP Biomedicals 160110) in drinking water for 7 days and intraperitoneally injected with TPPU (3 mg/kg/day) or EET (10 mg/kg/day) for the subsequent 5 days and accessed on day 5. All animal experimental procedures were performed in accordance with the Regulations for the Administration of Affairs Concerning Experimental Animals approved by the State Council of the People’s Republic of China. All the animal studies were approved by the Committee for Experimental Animals Welfare and Ethics of Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital (Approval Number: DWSY-24093549).

Oxylipin Analysis

Oxylipins were analyzed by means of LC-MS. Briefly, 1 mL of PBS containing 2% formic acid (v/v), 50 μL of 0.1% (w/v) butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT) in methanol and 10 μL of 0.2 ng/μL internal standard cocktail were added to each sample and vortexed to mix thoroughly. A fixed number of ceramic beads precleaned with methanol was then added, and the tissue samples were incubated at 1500 rpm for 12 hours at 4°C to ensure efficient extraction of eicosanoids from the tissue matrix. Serum samples were incubated with the same conditions aforementioned but without addition of ceramic beads and homogenization steps. The samples were centrifuged at 4°C for 10 minutes at 12,000 rpm, and the supernatant was extracted for eicosanoids using Oasis hydrophilic-lipophilic balance (HLB) solid phase extraction (SPE) columns, 30 mg (Waters). The fatty acid of the internal standard cocktail contained is listed in Supplementary Table 2.

Flow Cytometry

Flow cytometry staining was conducted as previously described.31 THP-1 cells were co-cultured with primary mesenteric adipocytes (transfected with sEH-overexpressing or sEH-knockdown constructs) in a Transwell system. Following 48 hours of co-culture, THP-1 cells were stimulated with lipopolysaccharide (LPS) (100 ng/mL) or interleukin (IL)-4 (20 ng/mL) for 24 hours to induce M1 or M2 polarization, respectively, and subsequently stained with fluorescence-conjugated antibodies targeting surface markers: fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) anti-human CD86 (M1 marker) and phycoerythrin (PE) anti-human CD206 (M2 marker). Isotype-matched antibodies were used as controls. Flow cytometry analysis was carried out using a BD FACSCanto II system. Data were analyzed with FlowJo software (v10.8.1), employing gating strategies based on forward and side scatter to eliminate debris and doublets.

Western Blot

Total protein was extracted using RIPA buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors. The concentration of protein in each sample was determined with the BCA Protein Assay Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Proteins were then separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and transferred onto a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane. Following a block with 5% skimmed milk, the membranes were incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies: anti-BCN1 (1:1000), anti-sEH (1:1000), and β-actin (1:5000) as loading controls. After incubation with secondary antibodies (horseradish peroxidase [HRP]-conjugated), proteins were visualized using Zen-Bio ECL reagent and detected with a Tanon 5200 chemiluminescence imaging system (Tanon Science and Technology).

Transmission Electron Microscopy

Human MAT was surgically excised, cut into 1- to 2-cubic mm pieces, and immediately fixed in prewarmed (37°C) 0.1 M sodium dimethylarsinate buffer containing 2.5% glutaraldehyde and 4% formaldehyde, pH 7.2, at room temperature for 2 hours. The fixed tissues were then placed in a buffer containing 1% osmium tetroxide and 1.5% potassium ferrocyanide, pH 7.2, for 2 hours to enhance membrane contrast. Subsequently, the tissues were dehydrated with an ethanol gradient (30%–100%), infiltrated into TAAB Hard Plus epoxy resin, and polymerized at 60°C for 48 hours. Ultrathin sections (70–90 nm) were cut with a diamond knife (Leica UC7 ultramicrotome), mounted on copper grids, and double-stained with 2% uranyl acetate (15 minutes) and Reynolds lead citrate (5 minutes). Images were captured using a Thermo Fisher Talos F200C TEM at 120 kV with magnifications ranging from ×15,000 to ×50,000.

Immunofluorescence

Paraffin-embedded mesenteric tissue was cut into sections 5-μm thick. Deparaffinization, rehydration, and antigen retrieval were performed sequentially. The colon sections were then embedded in 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) and incubated overnight with the following primary antibodies: anti-alpha smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), anti-fibroblast activation protein (FAP), and anti-proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA). Subsequently, they were incubated with Cy5 and FITC-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 hour at room temperature. For immunofluorescence staining, fibroblasts were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100. Cells were blocked with 5% BSA for 1 hour and then incubated with the primary antibody overnight at 4°C, followed by incubation with an FITC-conjugated secondary antibody for 1 hour at room temperature. Finally, tissue and cell slides were incubated with a 0.1% 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) solution for 10 minutes at room temperature. Images were captured using a confocal laser scanning microscope (Zeiss LSM880).

qRT-PCR

TRIzol Reagent (Invitrogen) was used to extract RNA from MAT and colon tissues. The RNA was reverse transcripted into complementary DNA (cDNA) using a SuperScript III Reverse Transcriptase kit (Invitrogen) according to manufacturer’s instructions. qRT-PCR was carried out with a DNA Engine Opticon system (Bio-Rad Laboratories) with Maxima SYBR-green Master Mix (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Specific primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table 4. The results of target genes were normalized to glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) and represented using the 2-ΔΔCt method.

Histological Evaluation

Ileocecum samples were fixed with 4% formaldehyde for 48 hours. They were then gradually dehydrated, embedded in paraffin, cut into 4-μm sections, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E). The severity of histological inflammation was calculated as previously reported.32 In total, 5 aspects were taken into consideration: mucosal architecture, infiltration of mononuclear cells and neutrophils, epithelial defects, and goblet cell loss (Supplementary Table 5).

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis and graphing were performed using Graphpad Prism 8 (Graphpad software). Continuous data were listed as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Nonparametric data were analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test or Kruskal-Wallis test. Parametric data were analyzed using 1-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) test with Tukey’s multiple comparisons. A P value of < .05 was considered statistically significant, and all P values were 2-sided.

SBPS-06 CHARACTERISTICS AND OUTCOMES OF PITUITARY TUMORS OPERATED AT PARIRENYATWA HOSPITAL OVER A 12-MONTH PERIOD
Authors: Simbarashe Sanyamandwe, Kuzivakwashe Chingonzo, Kazadi Kalangu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate the clinical features and operative outcomes of patients who underwent excision of Pituitary tumors at Parirenyatwa Group of Hospitals, Zimbabwe over a 12-month period between March 2024 to March 2025.MethodologyThe clinical and operation notes of patients who had a diagnosis of pituitary tumor and underwent excision were retrieved from the hospital archives and used to populate a pre-developed tool.ResultsA total of 10 patients underwent excision for pituitary masses, over the study period, with 6 males and 4 females of an age range of 20 to 61 years (Average 44.4 years) being operated on. Seven of the patients had reduced visual acuity while one had total visual loss. All the cases were macroadenomas and 70% were classified under Knosp grade 1 or 2. 9 of the cases were non-functional adenomas while a single case was of a growth hormone pituitary neuroendocrine tumor. Sixty percent of the cases underwent endoscopic transnasal transsphenoidal excision while 4 cases had supraorbital craniotomy for excision of masses that had a large supra-sellar extension. Of the operated patients 9 did well and were discharged from hospital and 1 deceased 2 1/2 weeks post operation after experiencing prolonged diabetes insipidus. Two patients also experienced transient diabetes insipidus which resolved fully before discharge. Post operative images were done in 5 of the cases which showed gross total resection.ConclusionNonfunctioning pituitary neuroendocrine macroadenoma constitute the most commonly operated pituitary cases at Parirenyatwa Central hospital, Zimbabwe and outcomes are generally good with the most common presenting symptom are visual deficits. Both craniotomy and endoscopic transnasal approaches are utilized with suprasellar extension and visual apparatus decompression favoring the open craniotomy approach.

Design of a global population-covering multi-epitope mRNA vaccine against Lassa virus using immunoinformatics
Authors: Noimul Hasan Siddiquee, Md Shoyaib Hossan Sami, Md. Aftabur Rahman, Md. Al-Amin, Md. Al-Amin Biswash, Kangkan Sharma, Kutub Uddin Samrat, Md. Ifteker Hossain, Ahmad Abdullah Mahdeen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The Lassa virus (LV) is a hemorrhagic virus that causes Lassa fever (LF). Despite its high mortality rate in Africa, no FDA-approved mRNA vaccine currently exists. Considering its epidemic potential, a new multi-epitope mRNA vaccine (LF-mVax) candidate is designed by immunoinformatics, targeting the GP, NP, and ZP through CD8+, CD4+, and B cell epitopes. LF-mVax was introduced with antigenic, non-allergic, and non-toxic epitopes that were derived from consensus sequences. The combined CD8 + and CD4 + epitopes provided 99.98% coverage of the global population, with robust immunity. Additionally, the selected epitopes were attached to adjuvants and linkers to make a stable, immunogenic vaccine against LV with favourable biophysical features. The analyses of the secondary and tertiary structures exhibit excellent quality (Ramachandran 82.4%, Z-score − 6.39), and the docking results show strong TLR-2 and TLR-4 binding (-1048.9 and − 1099.9 kJ/mol). The results were validated by MM-GBSA, MD simulation, PCA, and DCCM analysis. LF-mVax was cloned in E. coli (GC 45.83%, CAI 0.9962), and immune simulations predicted strong innate and adaptive responses with IL-2 and IFN-γ activation. The mRNA showed stability during entry, transcription, and expression in the host. Nevertheless, laboratory and animal studies are needed to establish the safety and potency of the LF-mVax.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-36965-6.

Introduction

The Lassa virus (LV), a member of the Mammarenavirus genus from the Arenaviridae family, is responsible for Lassa fever (LF), which is a hemorrhagic and frequently fatal zoonotic illness in humans12,. The common primary natural hosts of LV are Mastomys natalensis, Hylomyscus pamfi, and M. erythroleucus1. The potential routes of transmission for this disease are contact with infected rodents or their excreta, consumption of contaminated food, inhalation of infectious aerosols, and person-to-person transmission through exposure to the body fluids of infected individuals3,4. The incubation period for LF lasts between 6 and 21 days, with symptoms like fatigue, fever, muscle pain, sore throat, nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting, coughing, dysfunction of multiple organ systems, coagulopathy, bleeding from mucosal surfaces, and aseptic meningitis5–8.

LF causes the second-greatest worldwide burden among all recognized viral hemorrhagic fevers. Subsequently, repeated outbreaks of LV have been reported in many parts of Onitsha, Abo Mbaise, Nigeria, including Jos, Zonkwua, Owerri, Lafiya, and Epkoma communities9. From January 1996 to March 1997, about 1,000 cases of LF were reported in eastern Sierra Leone, including 148 deaths10. In 2018, Nigeria encountered a significant LF outbreak, including approximately 3,498 suspected and 633 confirmed cases, resulting in 171 fatalities and a 27% case fatality rate1112,. A recent report indicated that, as of mid-September 2025, a total of 7,673 suspected and 895 confirmed LF cases with 160 fatalities across 21 states in Nigeria13. Overall, it is estimated that annually, there are between 100,000 and 300,000 infections, many of which result in fatalities throughout West Africa14. Furthermore, prevalence studies show that LF affects approximately 10% of the population in Ghana, 30% in Côte d’Ivoire, 40% in Nigeria, 50% in Guinea, and around 80% in Sierra Leone and Liberia, as well as certain regions of Mali15.

LV has a bisegmented antisense RNA genome with a bidirectional coding strategy, consisting of a large L segment (7.3 kb) and a smaller S segment (3.4 kb). Together, they encode four proteins—Z (11 kDa), L (200 kDa), NP (64 kDa), and the glycoprotein precursor GPC, which is post-translationally cleaved into GP1 (42 kDa) and GP2 (38 kDa) to form the transmembrane glycoprotein (GP)16–18. The Z protein (ZP), which binds zinc and acts as a matrix protein, performs various functions throughout the viral life cycle, including controlling viral RNA synthesis, facilitating viral assembly and budding through interactions, and opposing the host type 1 interferon (IFN-I) system18,19. Moreover, the NP encoded by the sRNA is the main building block protein that forms a protective complex around the viral genome20,21. In addition, NP has a 3′ to 5′ exoribonuclease activity that degrades pathogen-associated molecular pattern (PAMP) RNAs22. Furthermore, the GP is essential for virus entry into host cells, producing the viral envelope protein23. Specifically, GP1 binds to host receptors, such as LAMP1, while GP2 undergoes a structural change in acidic endosomes, allowing the viral and host membranes to fuse and release viral RNA. To date, seven LV lineages have been identified. Lineages I-III circulate in Nigeria, while lineage IV is found in Côte d’Ivoire, Liberia, Sierra Leone, Guinea, and Mali. More recently, strains from Mali and Côte d’Ivoire were grouped as lineage V, the Kako strain from Hylomyscus pamfi was classified as lineage VI, and a virus detected during an outbreak in Togo was designated lineage VII24.

Consequently, the advancement of the LV vaccine has been acknowledged as a principal goal by both the Coalition for Epidemic Preparedness Innovations (CEPI) and the World Health Organization (WHO), and in 2018, it was added to the WHO blueprint list2526,. In the early stage of LF, an antiviral drug called Ribavirin is effective and can reduce the mortality rate, but its toxicity and teratogenic risks in later stages limit its effectiveness as a strong therapeutic option27. The majority of LF vaccine candidates remain in the early clinical or preclinical development phase, with a few progressing to clinical trials. For example, INO-4500, a DNA-based vaccine candidate for the measles virus, is in Phase 2, and MV-LASV (V182-001), a recombinant, live-attenuated, measles-vectored vaccine, is in Phase 1. Other types in preclinical stages include adenovirus vector-based (ChAd3, ChAdOx1, ChAd63), live-attenuated (ML29, rLASV-GPC/CD), VSV-based, recombinant vaccinia-based vaccines, and RNA replicons528,. However, DNA vaccines typically induce weaker immune responses in individuals and require special delivery methods29. Likewise, vectored vaccines face difficulties, including pre-existing immunity to the vector and the potential risk of vector DNA integrating into the host genome30. Moreover, live-attenuated vaccines can cause serious illness or even death in severely immunocompromised individuals31. On the contrary, mRNA vaccines have numerous benefits compared to traditional vaccines, including effective antigen expression, rapid degradation within the body, non-integrating and non-carcinogenic properties, safer, economical, and scalable production32. Additionally, mRNA vaccines facilitate fast development, provoke significant antibody and cellular responses, reduce infections, and maintain safety by not integrating into the host genome32,33. Recognizing the significance of this technology, in 2023, Drew Weissman and Katalin Karikó were awarded the Nobel Prize in Medicine for their discovery of mRNA-based vaccines or Physiology34.

The traditional approach to vaccine development involves the rigorous and lengthy procedure of separating and culturing the pathogen35. In contrast, computational vaccine design employs immunoinformatics and reverse vaccinology to analyze microbial genomes and locate epitopes for targeted vaccine development. This method optimizes vaccine design and allows targeting of multiple epitopes, improving effectiveness against diverse and fast-evolving strains36. Significantly, based on reverse vaccinology or in-silico vaccine designing, there are some potential research areas where in-silico vaccines have undergone an in vitro trial, and they used AlphaFold-guided vaccine antigens showing experimental success in the in-vitro or in-vivo37. Since experimental validation is expensive and time-consuming, the majority of vaccine investigations are still restricted to in-silico design. Furthermore, during experimental evaluation, in-silico may face difficulties including off-target effects, insufficient immune responses, or poor adjuvant efficacy.

Considering the importance of the pathogenic proteins (GP, NP, and ZP) of LF and the success stories of mRNA vaccines, an effective mRNA vaccine against LV was constructed in this study. The LF-mVax was constructed using anticipated CD8 + T cell, CD4 + T cell, and linear B-cell epitopes.

Analysis of epitope conservation

The conservation analysis revealed that the selected CD8+, CD4+, and B-cell epitopes from proteins GP, NP, and ZP were highly conserved among homologous protein sequences. The percentage of epitopes ranges from 94.85% to 61.86% for GP, 100% to 43.82% for NP, and up to 94% for ZP (Supplementary-1 Table 1).

Population coverage analysis for chosen epitopes

The combined CD8 + and CD4 + epitopes offered a consistent 99.98% population coverage globally, indicating the potential of the LF-mVax to benefit people worldwide. Notably, Europe and North America demonstrated the highest coverage, at 100%, while South Africa had the lowest coverage, at 95.27% (Supplementary-1 Table 2 and Fig. 3).

Construction of the vaccine

The chosen epitopes from the GP, NP, and ZP were used to compose the LF-mVax. The most rigid linkers, EAAAK and KKK, were implemented to combine the adjuvants and chosen epitopes in the LF-mVax formulation. The adjuvant, a large ribosomal subunit protein of M. tuberculosis, was interconnected to the CD8 + epitopes with the EAAAK linker. Furthermore, the CD8+, CD4+, and linear B cell epitopes were connected using KKK linkers. Additionally, a 6xHis tag was added at the C-terminal of the peptide sequence. Finally, a final multiepitope LF-mVax was constructed, which facilitated additional analysis (Fig. 4).

Alternatively, the SOPMA server predicted a random coil of 50.93%, an extended strand of 12.73%, an alpha helix of 32.41%, and a beta turn of 3.94% in the 2D structure of the LF-mVax (Figs. 5 and 6C).

Modelling of 3D structure, disulfide engineering, improvement, and validation

The fourth model was chosen due to the high accuracy of the total of five predicted models generated by the server of I-TASSER. This model had an RMSD of 9.1 ± 4.6 Å, a C-score of −2.42, and a TM-score of 0.60 ± 0.14. To enhance the stability of the predicted model, the Disulfide by Design 2 v2.13 server was utilized to implement disulfide engineering. Overall, pairs of 36 amino acids were identified as suitable for disulfide bond formation. Nevertheless, the energy and Chi3 values of four residue couples were within the standard range. As a result, the residue pairs were introduced with four mutations: ASP_23-LYS_27 (energy of 1.43 kcal/mol and Chi3 value of + 86.58), HIS_142-TYR_144 (Chi3 value of + 86.37 and an energy of 1.38 kcal/mol), VAL_248-ASP_265 (energy of 1.37 kcal/mol and Chi3 value of + 112.16), and VAL_249-ARG_377 (energy of 0.73 kcal/mol and Chi3 value of + 104.1) (Supplementary-1 Table 3 and Fig. 7A). Subsequently, the improved 3D model was obtained from the GalaxyWEB server, and this provided a MolProbity score, RMSD, and the favoured region of Ramachandran, with values of 1.996, 0.609, and 90.7%, respectively. Even so, the Ramachandran plot revealed that the energy-minimized model had 82.4% of amino acid residues in high, 13.6% in moderate, 1.0% in loose, and 2.9% in non-permissible regions (Fig. 7B). Furthermore, the improved model exhibited an ERRAT value of 81.865 (Fig. 7D). The ProSA predicted the quality of the improved model, where the Z-score was − 6.39 (Fig. 7C). These parameters were additionally evaluated for the refined AlphaFold3 predicted model and comparatively analyzed with the refined I-TASSER model (Supplementary-1 Table 5).

Docking analysis of the LF-mVax with toll-like receptors (TLRs) and between epitopes and MHC proteins

The docking of human MHC-I and MHC-II with chosen CD8 + and CD4 + epitopes were conducted utilizing the HDOCK server. The docking scores of the MHC-I molecule with chosen CD8 + epitopes were − 198.12, −171.53, −201.04, −134.30, and − 167.96 KJ/mol for the VQYNLSHSY (Fig. 9A), RTRDIYISR (Fig. 9B), RTSITGRAW (Fig. 9C), SEVSNVQRL (Fig. 9D), and DATHLGPQF (Fig. 9E) epitopes, respectively. Conversely, the docking scores of the MHC-II molecule with chosen CD4 + epitopes were − 210.49, −222.36, −177.16, −129.85, −173.15, and − 153.40 KJ/mol for SVQYNLSHSYAGDAA (Fig. 9F), ISVQYNLSHSYAGDA (Fig. 9G), HTGVVVEKKKRGGKE (Fig. 9H), TVVDLESDGKPQKAG (Fig. 9I), PTKLRPSAAPTAPPT (Fig. 9J), and LPTKLRPSAAPTAPP (Fig. 9K), respectively. The ClusPro 2.0 web server was utilized for implementing docking of TLR-2 and TLR-4 with the LF-mVax. Considering LF-mVax-TLR2, the anticipated docked model was chosen because it had the lowest and central score of −1048.9 and − 715.8 KJ/mol, accordingly (Table 5 and Fig. 10A). In case of LF-mVax-TLR4, the anticipated docked model was chosen because it had the lowest and central score of −1099.9 and − 733.6 KJ/mol, accordingly (Table 5 and Fig. 10B). Furthermore, the LF-mVax-TLR2 had demonstrated non-bond interactions of 149, hydrogen bonds of 15, and salt bridges of 6 (Table 5). In contrast, the LF-mVax-TLR4 had demonstrated non-bond interactions of 233, hydrogen bonds of 20, and salt bridges of 7 (Table 5).

Oppositely, the total interaction energy of the LF-mVax-TLR4 complex was determined to be −164.72 kcal/mol, while the VDW, ELE, GB, and SA were determined to be −190.74, −7302.2, 7353.9, and − 25.67 kcal/mol, respectively (Table 6).

Molecular dynamics simulation (MDS)

The dynamic behavior of the LF-mVax complexes was examined through root mean square deviation (RMSD), root mean square fluctuation (RMSF), radius of gyration (rGyr), and hydrogen bond analysis. Additionally, the binding free energies were calculated with the MM-GBSA method with default parameters in the Prime module of Schrödinger Maestro. Moreover, the motions and energy of the system distribution were analyzed using principal component analysis (PCA) and dynamic cross-correlation matrix (DCCM) on the simulation trajectory. These analyses were conducted with the Bio3D package in R, with custom R scripts developed for PCA and DCCM calculations38.

RMSD analysis

RMSD analysis measures the mean displacement of selected atoms in a vaccine–antigen or antigen–adjuvant complex relative to a reference structure, providing insight into the system’s stability and reliability during equilibration39. In the LF-mVax-TLR2 and LF-mVax-TLR4 complexes, the cumulative RMSD was recorded at 6.76 and 6.15 Å, respectively. In the initial 0–20 ns, the RMSD of the LF-mVax-TLR2 complex incrementally increased to a maximum of 7.33 Å, then stabilized and exhibited a constant pattern throughout the simulation (Fig. 11A). Conversely, the RMSD for LF-mVax-TLR4 gradually increased from 0 to 30 ns, after which the RMSD stabilized, indicating structural equilibrium (Fig. 11A).

RMSF analysis

The RMSF values quantify the stability and instability of amino acid residues in an intricate system39. The average RMSF value for LF-mVax-TLR2 and LF-mVax-TLR4 complexes was 2.61 and 2.23 Å, respectively. Additionally, RMSF analysis indicated that the LF-mVax-TLR2 complex exhibited minimal and maximum variations of 0.776 Å and 11.461 Å, occurring at the PHE_254 region of TLR2 and the ALA_288 area of the LF-mVax, respectively. Conversely, the RMSF values for the LF-mVax-TLR4 complex ranged from 0.74 to 18.399 Å, exhibiting minimal variation at the ASP_405 region of TLR4 and maximal fluctuation at the HIS_432 region within the LF-mVax (Fig. 11B).

rGyr and hydrogen bond analysis

The rGyr analysis elucidates the spatial arrangement of atoms within LF-mVax complexes in relation to their axes and facilitates the evaluation of macromolecular functionality by identifying variations in structural compactness40. The LF-mVax-TLR2 and LF-mVax-TLR4 complexes had average rGyr values of 37.33 and 33.31 Å, respectively, indicating overall structural compactness throughout the simulation. Although the LF-mVax-TLR2 complex experienced noticeable fluctuations between 3 and 17 ns, with smaller fluctuations during 23–32 ns and 90–95 ns (Fig. 11C). Meanwhile, the LF-mVax-TLR4 remained stable without significant conformational changes in the active site, indicating higher structural stability and tighter packing compared to the LF-mVax-TLR2 complex. Hydrogen-bond analysis indicated that the LF-mVax-TLR2 complex consistently formed bonds during the simulation, with a minimum of 714 and a maximum of 834. Inversely, for the LF-mVax-TLR4 complex, the highest number of hydrogen bonds observed was 865, while the lowest was 715 bonds (Fig. 11D). Additionally, the LF-mVax-TLR2 and LF-mVax-TLR4 complexes showed average hydrogen bond counts of 760.98 and 778.17, respectively. These values indicate robust, persistent, and long-lasting interactions that may enhance vaccine selectivity41.

Post-simulation MM-GBSA analysis

The binding affinity energy of the LF-mVax-TLRs, LF-mVax-TLR2, and LF-mVax-TLR4 complexes was assessed using the MM-GBSA technique. The initial (0 ns) and final (100 ns) dG_Bind values of the LF-mVax-TLR2 complex were − 205.69 and − 202.47 kcal/mol, respectively (Fig. 12). Similarly, the initial and final dG_Bind values of the LF-mVax-TLR4 complex were − 95.59 and − 95.20 kcal/mol, respectively (Fig. 12). These negative values, indicating a robust and enduring binding of the LF-mVax to the target receptor, underscore the potential for a sustained interaction.

To evaluate the structural dynamics and associated motions of the LF-mVax-TLR2 and LF-mVax-TLR4 complexes, PCA and DCCM analyses were applied to the MD simulation trajectories39,42. The LF-mVax-TLR2 complex (Fig. 13A) principal component analysis (PCA) plots show a diverse range of conformational sampling along the PCs. PC1 accounts for 33.08% of the variation, and the first three PCs describe a large part of the system’s overall motion. Greater structural flexibility and dynamic exploration are indicated by the smoother distribution and trajectory grouping in the low-dimensional PCA space. On the other hand, the LF-mVax-TLR4 complex (Fig. 13B) exhibits significantly less PCA clustering; PC1 accounts for 29.77% of the total, and the entire trajectory occupies a smaller region, indicating less conformational flexibility. The DCCM results show that the complexes have unique dynamic properties. Greater inter-residue communications and possibly beneficial allosteric effects are reflected in the broader and denser correlation patterns, which show that the LF-mVax-TLR2 system (Fig. 13C) has stronger positive and negative correlated motions across many pairs of residues. However, there are fewer linked networks and weaker correlations in the LF-mVax-TLR4 complex (Fig. 13D), suggesting less flexible behaviour and less dynamic interaction between residues. Taken together, these results point to the fact that, in comparison to the LF-mVax-TLR4 complex, the LF-mVax-TLR2 complex shows more dynamic residue correlations and conformational flexibility.

Discussion

LF is a deadly zoonotic disease that affects approximately 2.7 million people in West Africa every year43. According to the WHO, approximately 80% of individuals who contract the LV have no or moderate symptoms, while one in five infections leads to severe illness44. Therefore, it is necessary to establish a therapeutic strategy that leverages mRNA technology and RV. Subsequently, an LF-mVax was designed against LV using non-allergenic, antigenic, and non-toxic epitopes derived from conserved regions of GP, NP, and ZP, as identified by the IEDB server, thereby combining key targets involved in viral entry, replication, and assembly. However, the IEDB epitope prediction tool has several limitations, including that training data composition, length bias (9 mer/15 mer), and limited representation of host genetic variability (e.g., HLA diversity)45–47. The conservation analysis demonstrated that GP (94.85%), NP (100%), and ZP (94%) exhibited the highest levels of conserved residues, indicating their potential to provide broad protection against diverse virus strains. Moreover, the developed LF-mVax demonstrated outstanding coverage of 99.98% worldwide for the combination (CD8 + and CD4+), which implies this LF-mVax is expected to generate an immunological response across a wide range of people48. Nevertheless, in LF-mVax, the aliphatic index, instability index, and GRAVY score were 66.30, 30.77, and − 0.876, respectively, suggesting expected stability and solubility49.

Understanding how the primary structure of a protein folds into its 3D form is crucial, since this folding determines its final shape and biological function50. Nonetheless, a favourable result was achieved by accurately estimating 2D structures as well as 3D structures. The presence of alpha helices, beta sheets, and many coils inside the 2D structure will facilitate the efficient folding of LF-mVax into its 3D form. Nevertheless, model four was selected following the development of the 3D structure due to the TM-score, which was 0.60 \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\pm\:$$\end{document} 0.14, which is still within the range of (0, 1], likely implying a significant level of resemblance. The C score was also − 2.42, which likely indicates a confident model. This is because the score spans from − 5 to 2, and a higher number demonstrates greater reliability51. Nevertheless, the LF-mVax was modeled using I-TASSER, which primarily reports global confidence metrics, including the C-score, TM-score, and estimated RMSD, rather than per-residue confidence scores. Accordingly, the evaluation of model quality in this study was based on global confidence measures, as residue-level confidence analysis was beyond the scope of the present work. Moreover, overlap among predicted epitopes suggests regions of structural flexibility, which are likely to be more surface-exposed and accessible to antibodies, thereby facilitating immune recognition and stimulation.

The Ramachandran plot is a vital method for assessing the quality of protein structure with the aid of the psi (ψ) and phi (ϕ) torsion angles, highlighting certain locations that indicate sterically permitted orientation of the angles52. However, the Ramachandran plot of the improved 3D model revealed that 82.4% of the amino acids were situated in high, 13.6% in moderate, 1.0% in loose, and 2.9% in non-permissible regions.

The Z and ERRAT scores were frequently employed to verify the protein model quality, as these assess the precision of a 3D protein structure in terms of NMR/X-ray crystallography and nonbonded interaction, correspondingly53,54. Nevertheless, Z-scores that exceed 0 typically reflect model issues, while ERRAT scores above 50 indicate high-quality models54,55. Nevertheless, the ERRAT and Z scores of 81.865 and − 6.39 KJ/mol, consequently, validated the improved 3D model of LF-mVax, indicating its high quality. However, due to the presence of linkers and disordered regions, a relatively lower ERRAT score was found. Additionally, docking analysis was then conducted using TLR-2 and TLR-4 receptors to determine potential binding, and LF-mVax showed strong affinity with the lowest energy scores of −1048.9 and − 1099.9 KJ/mol. Furthermore, the MM-GBSA scores of net interaction energies for the LF-mVax-TLR2 and LF-mVax-TLR4 were − 98.01 kcal/mol and − 164.72 kcal/mol, respectively, further confirming a strong interaction56.

In MDS, the protein behavior is evaluated by the RMSD and the RMSF. The RMSD determines structural variation in the protein from its initial or experimentally derived reference structure, while RMSF identifies regions of notable mobility or stiffness inside the structure. Nevertheless, the RMSD and RMSF score of less than 10 Å typically suggests a high degree of structural resemblance of a protein complex57. However, the LF-mVax-TLR2 and LF-mVax-TLR4 exhibited an average RMSD of 6.76 and 6.15 Å, respectively, implying that the structural framework of both LF-mVax complexes was consistently maintained over time. Similarly, the LF-mVax-TLR2 and LF-mVax-TLR4 demonstrated average RMSF values of 2.61 and 2.23 Å, respectively, indicating consistent local stability and dynamic behavior across both constructs. Furthermore, lower rGyr signifies a more compact conformation, while a greater rGyr denotes a more extended or unfolded structure58. Nonetheless, the average rGyr values for LF-mVax-TLR2 and LF-mVax-TLR4 were roughly 37.33 and 33.31 Å, respectively. Although it shows fluctuation between 3 and 17 ns, which corresponds to an early equilibration phase, involving relaxation of flexible linker and loop regions. The LF-mVax-TLR4 exhibited higher stability with negligible conformational fluctuations in the active site, indicating higher structural stability and tighter packing compared to the LF-mVax-TLR2 complex. To facilitate stabilization and proper folding, a hydrogen bond analysis was implemented59. This analysis showed that LF-mVax-TLR4 had the highest of 865 hydrogen bonds, while LF-mVax-TLR2 had 834, indicating persistent molecular interactions and conformational stability. Consequently, both complexes exhibited consistent binding stability through the post-simulation MM-GBSA analysis. The PCA analysis was employed to investigate the dynamics and structures of proteins by reducing dimensionality60. This analysis revealed that the LF-mVax-TLR4 complex exhibited a PC3 value of 12.77%, while the LF-mVax-TLR2 complex exhibited a reduced PC3 value of 7.42%, indicating the LF-mVax-TLR4 complex is less stable than the LF-mVax-TLR2 complex. Moreover, protein motions occur across several time scales, ranging from femtoseconds to seconds. The DCCM analysis was also affected by the period over which the correlation data were obtained. LF-mVax-TLR2 indicates larger positively related regions and exhibits the greatest degree of residue-residue correlation.

The CAI value was maintained close to 1, indicating a strong correlation between the gene and the preferred codon usage of the host61. Throughout cloning, GC couples possess triple bonds of hydrogen, in contrast to the double hydrogen bonds seen in AT pairs, hence enhancing the thermal stability of the DNA62,63. Nevertheless, the LF-mVax exhibited a GC content of 45.83% and a CAI score of 0.9962, supporting high replication and stability. Furthermore, the immunological simulation of LF-mVax demonstrated significant stimulation of active B cells, TH cells, TC cells, cytokines, and immunoglobulins, likely boosting the response of the immune system. However, the stability of thermodynamics, as reflected by MFE values, depends on the length and mRNA sequence, with energy scores ranging between − 50 and − 250 kcal/mol64. Whenever the energy of mRNA is highly negative, the stability is enhanced, suggesting that the LF-mVax will not be easily degraded64,65. The LF-mVax demonstrated possible structural integrity, evidenced by MFE scores of −280.97 kcal/mol (optimal) and − 196.16 kcal/mol (centroid). Consequently, the mRNA structure of the proposed LF-mVax will maintain stability during its entry into the host, transcription, and expression.

There are three prominent multi-epitope subunit vaccine design studies for LV. However, no mRNA vaccines have been specifically designed against LV66–68. In these three studies, Akinyemi et al. (2021) and Prabin et al. (2020) did not describe population coverage analysis, whereas Ohilebo et al. (2021) addressed that their combined population coverage is 91.23%66–68. Here, the developed LF-mVax exhibited a remarkable global coverage of 99.98% for the combination of CD8 + and CD4+, indicating that this LF-mVax is anticipated to elicit an immunological response across a broad population48. Furthermore, Prabin et al. (2020) did not report the GRAVY score. Conversely, Akinyemi et al. (2021) reported a GRAVY score of − 0.185, whereas Ohilebo et al. (2021) reported a GRAVY score of − 0.67166–68. In the designed LF-mVax, the GRAVY score was − 0.876, suggesting greater solubility49. Additionally, Prabin et al. (2020) did not report GalaxyWEB server-derived structural validation parameters. In contrast, Akinyemi et al. (2021) reported a MolProbity score of 3.14, whereas Ohilebo et al. (2021) reported a MolProbity score of 3.049. In this present study, GalaxyWEB server-derived protein structure exhibited a MolProbity score of 1.996, indicating improved stereochemical quality, comparatively66–69. Moreover, the present work incorporates an extended and integrated computational framework. This study notably utilizes disulfide engineering to improve structural stability, conformational B-cell epitope prediction, and extensive molecular docking analysis between MHC molecules and chosen epitopes. Additionally, dynamic simulation, including MM-GBSA binding free energy calculations, PCA, and DCCM analyses, was applied to extensively assess binding affinity, conformational stability, and collective movements of the LF-mVax complexes. In addition, this study provides a more comprehensive and detailed validation of the proposed LF-mVax construct compared to previous studies66–68.

However, this study was mainly focused on computational predictions, which have several limitations. These drawbacks include a lack of long-term immunity prediction, structural prediction errors, ambiguity about adjuvant interactions, inconsistent epitopes, unstable proteins, unintended allergenicity and toxicity, etc. Moreover, poor standardization, limited experimental validation, and reproducibility concerns further constrain clinical translation and widespread adoption of purely computational vaccine strategies.

Conclusion

The integration of immunoinformatics and computational biology provides new insights into immune regulation that can be used to develop next-generation vaccines through in-silico approaches. The developed LF-mVax demonstrated favorable biophysical and immunological features, defining it as a viable contender to improve future vaccine strategies against LV. Furthermore, this LF-mVax demonstrated hydrophilic properties, solubility, and an alkaline nature. Nonetheless, the minimal docking scores and MDS showed stable and reliable contacts between the TLRs and LF-mVax candidate, establishing a computational groundwork for its predicted effectiveness. Additionally, the successful expression of the LF-mVax in a bacterial vector indicates optimal cloning capability. Moreover, three-dose regimens elicited strong and long-lasting cellular and humoral immunity in immunological responses. The engineered LF-mVax is expected to uphold consistency after cellular uptake, translation, and expression inside the host. Nevertheless, although these results are promising, the absence of laboratory and living model-based validation remains a notable drawback, emphasizing the necessity for more research to validate vaccine effectiveness and safety.

Materials and methods

Obtaining the sequence

The complete amino acid sequences of GP (accession number: NP_694870.1), NP (accession number: NP_694869.1), and ZP (accession number: NP_694871.1) of Mammarenavirus lassaense were acquired from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) protein database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/)70. Subsequently, these amino residues were employed for the identification of conserved regions and phylogenetic analysis.

Multiple sequence alignment (MSA) and phylogenetic analysis

All the amino acid sequences were subjected to protein-protein BLAST in the BLASTp server (https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi? PROGRAM=blastp&PAGE_TYPE=BlastSearch&LINK_LOC=blasthome)71. For the purpose of conducting MSA on the amino acid sequences that were obtained from BLAST searches, the Clustal Omega program (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/jdispatcher/msa/clustalo? stype=protein) was utilized72. Subsequently, the MEGA X tool (https://www.megasoftware.net/) was utilized to construct a phylogenetic tree from the aligned sequences73. In the following phase, for every protein, the conserved sequences were utilized.

Epitope prediction of CD8 + T cell

The CD8 + T cell epitopes for the GP, NP, and ZP proteins were estimated using the Immune Epitope Database (IEDB) server (http://tools.iedb.org/mhci/)74,75. Nevertheless, the IEDB is a publicly accessible database that contains a comprehensive collection of empirically confirmed immune epitopes, along with several tools for epitope prediction and analysis. This database also contains antibody and T cell epitopes that have been investigated in a variety of animal species, including non-human primates, humans, and rodents. These epitopes are associated with autoimmune disorders, allergens, infectious diseases, and transplant/alloantigens75. Epitopes associated with 27 human leukocyte antigen (HLA) alleles, characterized by a percentile rank of ≤ 1.0 and a length of 9-mer, are considered strong binders and have been thoroughly studied. The antigenic, allergenic, and toxicity characteristics of the preferred epitopes were evaluated by VaxiJen 2.0 (https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/vaxijen/VaxiJen/VaxiJen.html), AllerTOP v. 2.1 (https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/allertop_test/), and ToxinPred servers (https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/toxinpred/multi_submit.php), respectively767778,,. These epitopes were subsequently utilized in the development of a multi-epitope LF-mVax.

Epitope prediction of CD4 + T cell

The CD4 + T cell epitopes in the targeted region of the GP, NP, and ZP proteins were predicted using the IEDB server (http://tools.iedb.org/mhcii/)74,75. Following that, it was assumed that the epitopes, selected from a total of 27 alleles, were characterized by a percentile rank of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\:\le\:$$\end{document}1.0 and a length of 15 mer. The antigenic, allergenic, and toxicity characteristics of the nominated epitopes were evaluated by VaxiJen 2.0 (https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/vaxijen/VaxiJen/VaxiJen.html), AllerTOP v. 2.1 (https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/allertop_test/), and ToxinPred servers (https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/toxinpred/multi_submit.php), respectively767778.

Epitope prediction of linear B-cell

The ABCpred server (http://crdd.osdd.net/raghava/abcpred/) was utilized to make predictions for the linear B-cell epitopes of the GP, NP, and ZP proteins79. A propensity index approach and a Hidden Markov Model (HMM) are utilized by the ABCpred server to anticipate linear B-cell epitopes79. Subsequently, the antigenic, allergenic, and toxicity characteristics of the preferred epitopes were evaluated by VaxiJen 2.0 (https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/vaxijen/VaxiJen/VaxiJen.html), AllerTOP v. 2.1 (https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/allertop_test/), and ToxinPred servers (https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/toxinpred/multi_submit.php), respectively767778.

Analysis of epitope conservation

The IEDB Epitope Conservancy Analysis Tool (http://tools.iedb.org/conservancy/) was employed to evaluate the conservancy of the predicted epitopes80. Each chosen CD8+, CD4+, and linear B cell epitope was examined in relation to the homologous protein sequences derived from BLASTp searches of GP, NP, and ZP. The analysis was conducted using a threshold of ≥ 100% sequence identity to ensure the selection of entirely conserved epitopes.

Population coverage analysis for chosen epitopes

The HLA gene, which encodes the MHC-I as well as MHC-II proteins, is highly polymorphic based on regional and ethnic variations8182,. To find out how well the chosen epitopes bind to MHC-I along with MHC-II proteins across diverse human populations worldwide, the IEDB Analysis Resource (population coverage) (http://tools.iedb.org/population/) was utilized8384.

Construction of the vaccine

The LF-mVax was developed utilizing the top-ranked epitopes of the selected proteins (GP, NP, and ZP). However, laboratory trials have shown that vaccine immunogenicity can be improved by the addition of an adjuvant of the bL12 ribosomal subunit protein of Mycobacterium tuberculosis838586. In addition, to create a stable linkage, the CD8 + epitopes were paired with the adjuvants via an EAAAK peptide linker, while the KKK linkers were employed for creating the connection between the CD8+, CD4+, and linear B-cell epitopes83,85–87. The polyhistidine tag, also known as the 6× His tag, is a motif that is composed of a minimum of six histidine residues that are fused to the carboxyl (C-) terminal of the sequence88. The mRNA vaccine must possess a Kozak sequence that includes a start codon AUG at the beginning of the open reading frame (ORF) and a stop codon TGA to conclude translation89. Subsequently, the vaccine complex was given the name LF-mVax, where LF stands for Lassa Fever, m for mRNA, and Vax for vaccine.

Assessment of biophysical features

Following the construction of the multiepitope LF-mVax, the Expasy ProtParam server (http://web.expasy.org/protparam/) was employed to evaluate the biophysical features of the LF-mVax90. This server offers data about the isoelectric point (pI), hydropathicity (GRAVY), the instability index, the aliphatic index, the total amino acid count, the molecular mass, and the total atom count90. However, the accuracy of this tool may be compromised by proteins that exhibit unique structures or extreme circumstances, such as intrinsically disordered proteins or membrane proteins, as it is predicated on conventional protein folding. Furthermore, the tool cannot estimate changes in these properties during folding or interaction90. Furthermore, the solubility of the LF-mVax was evaluated using the SOSUI server (https://harrier.nagahama-i-bio.ac.jp/sosui/mobile/) and the SOLpro server (https://scratch.proteomics.ics.uci.edu/)91–95. Subsequently, the antigenic and allergenic properties of the LF-mVax were assessed by VaxiJen 2.0 (https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/vaxijen/VaxiJen/VaxiJen.html) and AllerTOP v. 2.1 servers (https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/allertop_test/), respectively7677.

Modelling of 2D structure

To model the 2D structure of the LF-mVax candidate, the GOR4 (https://npsa-pbil.ibcp.fr/cgi-bin/npsa_automat.pl? page=/NPSA/npsa_gor4.html), SOPMA (https://npsa-pbil.ibcp.fr/cgi-bin/npsa_automat.pl? page=/NPSA/npsa_sopma.html), and PSIPRED servers (https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/16/4/404/187312) were utilized96–99. The GOR4 uses Bayesian statistical analysis and information theory for determining a 2D structure100. The SOPMA tool is capable of predicting 69.5% of amino acids from a protein sequence, providing a three-state model of the 2D structure, including a beta-sheet, alpha-helix, and coil97. In contrast, the PSIPRED server employs dual feed-forward neural networks to process PSI BLAST (Protein-Specific Iterated Basic Local Alignment Search Tool) outputs for 2D structure prediction101.

Modelling of 3D structure, disulfide engineering, improvement, and validation

The I-TASSER server (https://zhanggroup.org/I-TASSER/) was employed to model the 3D structure of the LF-mVax. The protein 3D structure was modelled using the algorithm of the server, which repeatedly refines the assembly of template fragments while integrating many alignments of the threading102,103. Following 3D structure modelling, disulfide engineering was implemented to enhance the structural sustainability of the vaccine construct. The disulfide engineering involves the modification of a cysteine residue to generate novel disulfide bonds inside a highly flexible protein region, enhancing the stability of a protein104. However, the Disulfide by Design 2 v2.13 server (http://cptweb.cpt.wayne.edu/DbD2/) was employed to identify the potential cysteine disulfide bonding pairs within the vaccine structure105. The server defines the potential locations throughout the structure of the protein that are more likely to produce disulfide bonds104. The pair of residues was chosen for mutation because of its minimal energy and χ3 angle within a wide range of − 87° or + 97° ± 3085,105,106. Subsequently, the server maintained by GalaxyWEB (https://galaxy.seoklab.org/cgi-bin/submit.cgi? type=REFINE) was employed to improve the structure107. Nevertheless, the process of improvement can only improve the structures to a point where the preliminary model is precise. The improvement may not provide a physiologically appropriate structure if the initial model is excessively incorrect107. The structures were subsequently validated using the SAVES v6.1 server (https://saves.mbi.ucla.edu/), which elucidates the standard of the model structures by utilizing the Ramachandran plot and ERRAT analysis53,108–110. Finally, to detect any uncertainties associated with the assessed 3D structure, the ProSA server (https://prosa.services.came.sbg.ac.at/prosa.php) was implemented to determine the Z-score54,111,112. The 3D structure of the LF-mVax was predicted using I-TASSER and AlphaFold3 servers (https://alphafoldserver.com/), and the predicted models were comparatively analyzed113.

Prediction of conformational B-lymphocyte epitopes

The conformational B-lymphocyte epitopes of the LF-mVax were estimated using the ElliPro server (https://tools.iedb.org/ellipro/)114. This server employs three algorithms to anticipate the conformational B lymphocyte epitopes of the validated three-dimensional model. These techniques utilize the protrusion index (PI) values of the epitopes to depict an ellipsoidal protein conformation and to measure the residue PI alongside adjacent cluster residues114.

Docking analysis of the LF-mVax with toll-like receptors (TLRs) and between epitopes and MHC proteins

It is vital to evaluate immune responses and advance immunological research by molecular interaction of T-cell (CD8 + and CD4+) epitopes with MHC-I and MHC-II molecules115. The three-dimensional structures of human MHC-I (PDB ID: 2XPG) and MHC-II (PDB ID: 1DLH) were obtained from the RCSB Protein Data Bank (PDB) database (www.rcsb.org) before docking116. Furthermore, the HDOCK server (http://hdock.phys.hust.edu.cn/) was utilized to conduct the docking process, which involved the upload of the three-dimensional structures of MHC-I and MHC-II, as well as their respective T-cell epitopes. Every parameter was maintained at its default value117. TLRs are a key element of the natural defence mechanism of the body, identifying PAMPs and triggering immunological responses118. TLR-2 and TLR-4 have been frequently used within LF-mVax development because of their unique ligand recognition as well as essential functions in immunological activation, in contrast to different receptors118,119. However, the ClusPro 2.0 server (https://cluspro.org/login.php) was employed for the docking analysis between LF-mVax and TLRs120. Nonetheless, the analysis depends on the accuracy of the input structures, so inaccurately predicted or truncated structures can generate erroneous docking scores120–123. Before the molecular docking analysis, the three-dimensional structures of human TLR-2 (PDB ID: 2Z7X) and TLR-4 (PDB ID: 3FXI) were obtained from the PDB database (www.rcsb.org)116. Nonetheless, PyMOL software (https://www.pymol.org/) and the PDBsum server (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/thornton-srv/databases/pdbsum/) were employed for the complexes visualization and analysis124,125.

Interaction energy evaluation (MM-GBSA)

The evaluation of the interaction energy of LF-mVax-TLR2 and LF-mVax-TLR4 was determined using Generalized Born methods and molecular mechanics, particularly the MM-GBSA technique. This necessitated the examination of a variety of factors, such as the effects of bound interactions, polar and nonpolar components, van der Waals forces, as well as binding and electrostatic interactions. The HawkDock server (http://cadd.zju.edu.cn/hawkdock/) was employed to calculate the interaction energy of the LF-mVax complexes126,127.

Molecular dynamics simulation (MDS)

MDS is a significant method for evaluating the integrity of LF-mVax complexes128. These simulations offer dynamic insights into protein-protein interactions, essential for assessing binding stability and activity within the active site cavity129. In this study, 100-nanosecond (ns) MDS of the LF-mVax complexes (LF-mVax-TLR2 and LF-mVax-TLR4) were conducted utilizing the Desmond module from Schrödinger (Release 2023-1) within a Linux environment. For the MDS, the initial LF-mVax complexes were obtained through docking studies. Optimization and minimization were implemented during the preprocessing of the LF-mVax complexes utilizing the Protein Preparation Wizard of Maestro v13.5. The docked complexes were initially immersed in the Transferable Intermolecular Interaction Potential 3 Points (TIP3P) water model within a 10 × 10 × 10 Å³ orthorhombic periodic boundary box. To neutralize the solvated system, a 0.15 M salt concentration was maintained by appropriate ions, such as Na+ and Cl−, which were randomly distributed. The simulation was conducted at 300.0 K and 1.01325 bar pressure under constant pressure and constant temperature (NPT) conditions, utilizing the Nose-Hoover temperature coupling and isotropic scaling approach. The system was relaxed and reduced by the application of the OPLS4 force field. The system was initially set up in a relaxed state, and production runs were conducted with a recording interval of 100 picoseconds (ps) and an energy threshold of 1.2 J130.

Optimization of codon and virtual molecular cloning

The Java Codon Adaptation Tool (JCat) server (https://www.jcat.de/) was employed for codon adaptation of the DNA of LF-mVax, utilizing the E. coli strain K12131. To determine the expression level of the protein, the tool calculates the codon adaptation index (CAI) and GC content. A CAI value and a GC content of close to 1 and 30%−70%, respectively, are regarded as favourable scores for cloning132,133. After getting optimized DNA, the restriction sites Eco53kI and EcoRV were introduced into the strand of DNA. Subsequently, the cloning process was carried out using a vector named pET-28a (+) by the SnapGene software (https://www.snapgene.com/free-trial/)48. The software can visualize and customize DNA; however, it may encounter constraints when attempting to modify sequences that are excessively complex or large48.

Immune simulation

The C-ImmSim server (https://kraken.iac.rm.cnr.it/C-IMMSIM/index.php) was employed to execute the immune simulation of the LF-mVax, which elucidates immune responses that are both cellular and humoral134. To administer the multiepitope LF-mVax, three doses were selected to be administered at intervals of four weeks. Additionally, the time steps were set to 1, 84, and 168, with one period being equivalent to eight hours of typical everyday living. In addition, the volume, steps, and the random seed were adjusted to 50, 1000, and 12,345, respectively.

Modelling of mRNA

The optimized DNA sequence of LF-mVax was initially transformed to RNA employing the Biomodel server (http://biomodel.uah.es/en/lab/cybertory/analysis/trans.htm)135. Subsequently, the RNAfold server (http://rna.tbi.univie.ac.at/cgi-bi n/RNAWebSuite/RNAfold.cgi) was used to estimate the minimum free energy (MFE) of RNA structures and model the two-dimensional structures136. This program computes the MFE of the predicted mRNA employing thermodynamic parameters137,138.

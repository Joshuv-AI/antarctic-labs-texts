Effect of NiZn2O4/epoxy nanocomposite as corrosion inhibitors on mild steel
Authors: Z Edward Kennedy, C Sneha, Priya Bijesh, Mohammed Zakriya, V Andal, Selvaraj Vaithilingam
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe application of anticorrosion composite coating is a successful strategy for preventing metal corrosiveness. Application of Nanoparticles as Corrosion inhibitors effectively prevents the corrosion of metals compared to existing corrosion inhibitors. Conventional corrosion inhibitors are toxic to the environment. Following exploitation, the epoxy coatings on the steel erode as well. Nanoparticles are therefore introduced to prevent it. In this paper, NiZn2O4 Nanoparticles are synthesized by polymeric precursor method using alginic acid. Epoxy resin is infused with the synthesised nano NiZn2O4 to prevent mild steel from corroding. Nano NiO and ZnO has a high corrosion resistance. Hence NiZn2O4 was prepared which will have a synergistic effect thereby preventing oxidation and environment friendly. To examine its efficiency different concentrations of (1%,3%,5%) of NiZn2O4 nanoparticles were added to epoxy coating and applied on mild steel in 1.0 M HCl solutions. Using potentiodynamic polarisation and gravimetric (weight loss) techniques, the corrosion inhibiton efficiency was examined. A gravimetric investigation was carried out within 15 days of exposure, and the findings indicated that weight loss increase with exposure duration but diminished with higher NiZn2O4NP concentration. Additionally, the presence of NiZn2O4NPs altered the process of anodic dissolution and cathodic hydrogen gas evolution, according to potentiodynamic polarisation data from electrochemical impedance spectroscopy. From the results we observed that 3% loading of NiZn2O4 Nanoparticle coating was efficient with high corrosion resistance (Low corrosion rate 0.0107). These findings showed that NiZn2O4NPs might be added to the current inhibitors to reduce the rate of corrosion.Keywords: Nickel zincate, Epoxy resin, Nanocomposite, Mild steel, Corrosion, Weight loss method, Potentiodynamic

Introduction

Metal utilization was unavoidable in a variety of circumstances. However, corrosion in them is a widespread problem. People encountered many obstacles as a result of metal corrosion [1–5]. Metal corrosion is often caused by the electrochemical response of metals when they come into contact with air, moisture, acidic gases, and other Corrosion initiating substances [6, 7]. To avoid this phenomenon and develop resistance, numerous approaches have been employed, such as corrosion inhibitors, cathodic inhibitors, anodic inhibitors, sacrificial anodic protection, etc. [8, 9]. Nanomaterials [10–16] have gained popularity as inhibitors in recent decades.

Mild steel is a low-carbon material that is generally used in the industrial sector because of its great features, which include machinability, weldability, and durability [17]. Mild steel is an excellent option to employ in the construction of storage tanks and pipes in the gas and oil industry due to its advantages and cost effectiveness [18]. As acidic solutions are used in this environment overall, corrosion is an unavoidable and challenging problem. To overcome this problem corrosion inhibitors are considered to be good alternative. Researchers are thus concentrating on developing corrosion inhibitors that are affordable, efficient, effective, and sustainable. Because of their nature, composites composed of polymers and nano metal oxide are useful as corrosion inhibitors [19–21].

The exceptional surface-to-volume ratio of metal oxide nanoparticles, which facilitates an increased number of nanoparticle contacts with the metal surface, makes them useful as corrosion inhibitors [22]. Nanoparticles can obstruct electrolyte absorption at the coating/metal contact by covering tiny pores and extending the diffusion pathway. The improved corrosion prevention properties are attributed to the interfacial interactions between the metal surface and the dispersion degree of polymers and nanoparticles [23]. Strength, conductivity, corrosion resistance, energetics, and optical qualities of nanocomposite materials are all improved by using these distributed nanoparticles [24]. NiZn2O4 nanoparticles prepared by Polymeric precursor method for corrosion inhibition is an innovative approach that has not been reported before. The incorporation of metal oxide nanoparticles like NiZn2O4 into epoxy resins can create synergistic effects, enhancing corrosion protection beyond what either component could achieve alone. The use of nano-sized NiZn2O4 particles allows for better dispersion within the epoxy matrix, potentially leading to more uniform and effective corrosion protection.

In this work, NiZn2O4 nanoparticles were synthesized and added to epoxy resin to study the corrosion inhibition property in acid medium.

Nanocomposite and coating preparation

In order to create the coating mixture, epoxy resin (Oryta) and polyamidoamine hardener were combined in a 2:1 weight ratio. In the case of nano coating, NiZn2O4 nanoparticles weighing 1 g, 3 g, and 5 g were applied. For 30 min, all of the ingredients were combined using a speed mixer (H500 homogeniser from POL-EKO-APARATURA). On mild steel, both pure epoxy and epoxy loaded with NiZn2O4 nanoparticles were applied. Mild steel cleaned using emery paper. The clean carbon steel substrate was coated with nano coating mixture by dip coating and then kept for 4 h drying to allow full curing (Fig. 2).

Characterization of nanomaterials

FTIR spectra were analyzed with a Nicolet FTIR spectrophotometer (city, state abbrev if US, country) using KBr in the wavenumber range of 4000–500 cm−1. All samples were ground and mixed with KBr and then pressed to form pellets. A Scanning electron microscope (Hitachi SU6600) was used to analyse the structural morphology of NiZn2O4 nanoparticles. The phase formation of NiZn2O4 Nanoparticles was identified using X-ray diffraction technique (XRD, Bruker D8 ADVANCE with 1.54 Å Cu Kα radiation).

Corrosion solution

Corrosion solution was prepared using hydrochloric acid. 1 L of 1 N HCl (30.67 mL of 37.5%) was prepared to examine acidic corrosion.

Gravimetric/weight loss measurement study

The ASTM G1–03 standard approach was used to conduct the weight loss test. After being weighed, mild steel coupons were submerged entirely in the test solutions (100 ml) in a 250 ml glass beaker and allowed to stand at room temperature for 24, 36, 48, and 60 h. Electrochemical tests

To assess the inhibitor’s effectiveness in preventing corrosion, electrochemical experiments were used. A three-electrode multichannel electrochemical workstation with an integrated FRA system (BioLogic, Model: VSP2) was used for all electrochemical experiments. The working area of the electrode is ∼1 cm2. Electrochemical corrosion tests were conducted on mild steel samples covered with composite and having a surface area of 1 cm2 in saturated NaCl solution (pH 7.4). For the corrosion experiments, a standard two electrode glass cell configuration was employed; the sample was called the working electrode, and the reference electrodes were the saturated calomel electrode (SCE).

Plots of phase angle and bode were made for mild steels with composite coatings. Utilising electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), electrochemical corrosion data were examined. The frequency range for the EIS testing was 100 kHz to 10 mHz, with a perturbation of 10 mV in the signal amplitude. Using Bode, the impedance data were examined. Each of these experiments was conducted at 30 °C in a corrosive media (Na). To guarantee that the electrodes were in a stable condition, the samples were submerged in a corrosive liquid for 30 min before the measurements were taken.

Tafel graphs were automatically analysed using the biologic VSP 2 channel workstation analysis program to determine the corrosion currents Icorr and Ecorr.

Results and discussion

Characterisation of NiZn2O4 nanoparticles

NiZn2O4 nanoparticles were synthesized by Polymeric Precursor method. The synthesized nanoparticle acts as corrosion inhibitor when integrated into epoxy resin. NiZn2O4 nanoparticles forms a dense, impermeable layer within epoxy coatings, blocking corrosive agents (e.g., Cl⁻, H2O) from reaching the metal substrate.

Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) was used to evaluate the purity and phase formation of the as-synthesised NiZn2O4 nanoparticles, as illustrated in Fig. 3. The positions of the peaks, which appear at 2θ = 31.75, 34.46, 36.27, 43.19, 47.74, 56.63, 62.65, 68.05, and 69.15, can be easily indexed as the crystal planes of the bulk NiZn2O4 at (100), (002), (400),(102),(200),(110),(103),(112), and (201), respectively[26, 27]. All these diffraction peaks can be perfectly indexed to the face-centered cubic (FCC) crystalline structure of NiZn2O4, not only in peak position, but also in their relative intensity of the characteristic peaks, which is in accordance with that of the standard spectrum (JCPDS, No. 75-0270). The XRD pattern indicates that there are no further contaminants present and that the samples are single phase. This outcome demonstrates that the physical phases of the NiZn2O4 nanoparticles synthesised in this study are purer.

Electrochemical studies

The pure epoxy covering has many tiny pores that allow water and other electrolytes to easily pass through, causing the metal to corrode over time. The presence of nanoparticles, however, causes these pores to efficiently fill and stop the diffusing medium from moving [36].

To further verify the NZ(NiZn2O4) nanoparticles’ barrier qualities, we prepared NZEC (NiZn2O4/Epoxy Coating) corrosion resistance on mild steel in saturated NaCl solution.

The comparison of NiZn2O4 epoxy (NZE) coatings to pure epoxy (EP) coatings reveals significant differences in corrosion resistance, particularly demonstrated by shifts in corrosion potential. The findings suggest that the incorporation of NiZn2O4 nanoparticles enhances the barrier properties of the epoxy coating, leading to improved electrochemical performance.

Comparing the NiZn2O4 epoxy (NZE) coating to a pure Epoxy surface, the coating shows reduced Icorr and higher corrosion protection. The composite coating demonstrates reduced corrosion current density (Icorr) values compared to pure epoxy coatings. A lower Icorr indicates that the rate of corrosion is significantly diminished, suggesting that the coating provides enhanced protection against corrosive environments. The presence of NiZn2O4 in the epoxy matrix contributes to a higher level of corrosion protection. This is evidenced by the electrochemical measurements showing that the composite coatings exhibit better performance in terms of resistance to corrosion compared to standard epoxy coatings. In the composite coatings, nickel zinc oxide functions as a diffusion barrier in the epoxy matrix, preventing corrosion of the mild steel.

EIS analysis

Using the EIS, the corrosion resistance behaviour of the coatings in the NaCl solution was studied. The outcomes of the time-dependent EIS research are shown in Fig. 8. Nyquist graphs are given for the coatings at extended immersion times (h). The semi-circular arc diameter in the Nyquist plot gives information about the coatings’ ability to resist corrosion [36].

Nyquist plot of NiZn2O4 /Epoxy coating

High corrosion resistance is correlated with bigger arc diameter and higher impedance values. All coatings exhibit a larger arc diameter and impedance with an increase in nanoparticles, indicating that the coatings are subject to the addition of additional inhibitor nanoparticles. This impedance, which is the result of the Faradaic process’s impedance at the metal-coating interface and the coating’s own impedance, serves as a direct indicator of the inorganic coating’s overall protection capacity. Impedance spectra’s high- and low-frequency portions correlate to two electrode processes. The charge transfer process at the coating-metal interface is attributed to the low-frequency area, while the impedance process at the coating is indicated by the high-frequency zone.

The deviation or shift in frequency and phase angle for inhibited and uninhibited electrode surfaces is shown by the Bode plots with a single time constant provided in Fig. 9. With an increase in inhibitor concentration in the electrolytic medium, a shift in phase angle was seen. Inhibitor molecules cover the exposed surface more when we add more inhibitor, altering the look of the electrode surface and raising the phase angle. From the bode plot, the gain margin and the phase margin are positive hence the system is stable for a long time. The phase angle in Bode plots can provide insights into the capacitive behaviour of the coating.

Conclusion

NiZn2O4 nanofillers are synthesised and used in this study to improve the corrosion resistance of mild steel epoxy coatings. Importantly, the nanofillers provide excellent barrier qualities against corrosive species intercalating into epoxy coatings, while also improving the tensile and hardness properties of epoxy coatings. In an acidic (1 M HCl) setting, NiZn2O4/epoxy coating functions as a stable and promising green inhibitor against mild steel corrosion, with the corrosion process being influenced by concentration and time.

EIS result shows that the protective film was formed by the NiZn2O4 / Epoxy coating on mild steel surface. The EIS analysis of NiZn2O4/epoxy coatings demonstrates their superior corrosion resistance compared to pure epoxy coatings. The enhanced charge transfer resistance and reduced porosity indicate effective barrier properties, making these composite coatings suitable for applications requiring long-term protection against corrosion. The Tafel polarization analysis demonstrates that NiZn2O4/epoxy coatings exhibit superior corrosion resistance compared to pure epoxy coatings, as evidenced by shifts in corrosion potential and reductions in corrosion current density.

Integration of angiogenic pathway-related proteomics and clinical features via ensemble learning for precision risk stratification of early-onset preeclampsia
Authors: Huan Yang, Jing Ma, Hui Guo, Lei Sun, Li Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEarly-onset preeclampsia (EOPE) is a severe pregnancy complication associated with significant maternal and neonatal morbidity. Although the imbalance of angiogenic factors is known to play a critical role in its pathogenesis, traditional single-marker assays often lack the precision required for individualized risk stratification. This study aimed to develop a precision risk stratification framework for EOPE by integrating angiogenic pathway-related proteomics with clinical features using an ensemble learning approach.MethodsA prospective case-control study was conducted involving 240 pregnant women (120 EOPE and 120 healthy controls). Plasma concentrations of 15 angiogenic pathway proteins were quantified. A robust feature selection strategy combining LASSO regression and the Boruta algorithm identified five key biomarkers: sFlt-1, PlGF, Endoglin, VEGF-A, and P-selectin. An ensemble learning model was constructed using a Stacking strategy (incorporating Random Forest, XGBoost, and LightGBM) to integrate these proteomic markers with clinical metadata (BMI and Mean Arterial Pressure). Model interpretability was addressed using SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) analysis.ResultsThe ensemble model demonstrated superior predictive performance, achieving an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.95 (95% CI: 0.92–0.98) in the validation cohort, significantly outperforming the traditional sFlt-1/PlGF ratio (AUC = 0.82). SHAP analysis identified the sFlt-1/PlGF ratio and Endoglin as the top contributors, revealing complex non-linear interactions between circulating biomarkers and maternal hemodynamics. Based on the model scores, participants were successfully stratified into high, moderate, and low-risk tiers. The high-risk group exhibited a 4.2-fold increase in the incidence of adverse maternal and fetal outcomes compared to the low-risk group.ConclusionThe integration of angiogenic proteomics and clinical features via ensemble learning provides a high-performance and interpretable tool for the precision risk stratification of EOPE. This multi-dimensional approach facilitates early identification of high-risk individuals, potentially enabling more targeted clinical interventions and improved obstetric outcomes.

Introduction

Early-onset preeclampsia (EOPE), typically defined as preeclampsia developing before 34 weeks of gestation, remains one of the most severe pregnancy-related complications worldwide [1], [2], [3]. It is characterized by multi-system organ damage and is a leading cause of maternal and perinatal morbidity and mortality. Unlike late-onset preeclampsia, EOPE is closely associated with defective deep placentation and inadequate remodeling of the uterine spiral arteries, which triggers a cascade of systemic endothelial dysfunction [4], [5].

The "two-stage" hypothesis of preeclampsia emphasizes the pivotal role of an imbalance between pro-angiogenic and anti-angiogenic factors. Specifically, the elevation of soluble fms-like tyrosine kinase−1 (sFlt−1) and the reduction of placental growth factor (PlGF) have been recognized as hallmark features of the angiogenic pathway dysfunction [6]. While the sFlt−1/PlGF ratio has gained clinical traction, its predictive specificity in early gestation remains suboptimal for precision risk stratification due to the multifactorial nature of the disease [7].

Recent advancements in high-throughput proteomics have enabled a more comprehensive characterization of the angiogenic landscape [8]. However, the high dimensionality and non-linear interactions within proteomic data pose significant challenges for traditional statistical methods. Ensemble learning, a sophisticated machine learning paradigm that aggregates multiple base learners to improve predictive accuracy and robustness, offers a promising solution [9]. By integrating diverse clinical features with angiogenic pathway-related proteomic signatures, ensemble learning models may provide a more nuanced approach to identifying high-risk individuals [10], [11].

In this study, we aimed to develop and validate a precision risk stratification framework for EOPE. We integrated clinical metadata with targeted proteomics focused on the angiogenic pathway and utilized an ensemble learning approach (Stacking of Random Forest, XGBoost, and LightGBM) to enhance predictive performance. This study seeks to provide a robust tool for the early identification and personalized management of EOPE.

Methods

The overall study design and analytical pipeline are illustrated in Supplement Figure 1. The study follows a prospective case-control workflow starting from patient recruitment and proteomic/clinical data collection, through LASSO and Boruta-based feature selection, to the construction of a Stacking ensemble model, concluding with SHAP-based model interpretation and clinical risk stratification.

Study population and sample collection

This prospective case-control study enrolled 240 pregnant women (120 EOPE and 120 healthy controls) at the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University between 2022 and 2024. EOPE was diagnosed based on the ISSHP criteria (new-onset hypertension ≥ 140/90 mmHg after 20 weeks of gestation with proteinuria or organ dysfunction, occurring before 34 weeks). Maternal peripheral blood samples were collected at the time of diagnosis (for cases) or matched gestational weeks (for controls).

Proteomic analysis of angiogenic markers

Plasma concentrations of 15 angiogenic pathway-related proteins, including sFlt−1, PlGF, Endoglin, VEGF-A, and P-selectin, were quantified using Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA) or multiplex bead-based immunoassays according to the manufacturer’s protocols. All samples were processed in duplicate to ensure technical reproducibility.

Feature selection and data preprocessing

Clinical features (BMI, Mean Arterial Pressure, etc.) and proteomic data were normalized. We employed a two-step feature selection strategy:1)LASSO Regression: To handle multicollinearity and reduce dimensionality.2)Boruta Algorithm: A shadow-feature-based method to identify all relevant features in the dataset.

Ensemble learning model development

We developed an ensemble model using a Stacking strategy.

Base Learners: Random Forest (RF), XGBoost, and LightGBM were trained independently using 5-fold cross-validation.

Meta-Learner: A Logistic Regression model was used to aggregate the predictions of the base learners. Hyperparameters for Random Forest, XGBoost, and LightGBM were optimized using a 5-fold cross-validated grid search strategy to minimize the log-loss function. Specific parameter configurations, including the search space and optimal values for each model, are provided in the Supplementary Table 1.

The dataset was split into a training set (70%) and a validation set (30%).

Model interpretability and statistical analysis

Model interpretability was achieved using SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values to quantify the contribution of each feature. Statistical analyses were performed using Python (v3.9) and R (v4.1). Continuous variables were compared using Student’s t-test or Mann-Whitney U test, and categorical variables were compared using the Chi-square test.

Performance of the ensemble learning model

We constructed an ensemble learning framework by integrating the selected proteomic markers with clinical features (BMI and Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP)). The Stacking-based ensemble model (combining Random Forest, XGBoost, and LightGBM) demonstrated superior predictive power compared to individual base learners and the traditional sFlt−1/PlGF ratio (Table 3). In the validation set, the ensemble model achieved an AUC of 0.95 (95% CI: 0.92–0.98) (Fig. 4A). The Precision-Recall curve and Calibration plot further confirmed the high reliability and consistency of the model’s predictions (Fig. 4B-C). Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) indicated that the ensemble model provided a higher net clinical benefit across a wide range of threshold probabilities compared to standard diagnostic methods (Fig. 4D). Comparative analysis of performance metrics (AUC, sensitivity, specificity, and F1-score) in the validation set confirmed the superiority of the ensemble model (Fig. 4E). Precision risk stratification and clinical utility

Based on the ensemble model’s risk scores, participants were stratified into three risk tiers: Low, Moderate, and High (Table 4, Fig. 6). The distribution of risk scores showed minimal overlap between the EOPE and control groups (Fig. 6A). Clinical utility analysis demonstrated that the High-Risk group had a significantly higher incidence of adverse maternal and fetal outcomes (38.6%) compared to the Low-Risk group (1.5%) (Fig. 6B). In the high-risk subgroup defined by BMI > 28 kg/m², the model exhibited robust predictive performance (Fig. 6C). Additionally, we observed a negative correlation between model risk scores and gestational age at sampling (Fig. 6D). Finally, we developed a clinical implementation schematic (Fig. 6F) to guide the integration of this stratification tool into routine obstetric practice. Discussion

The primary finding of this study is that the integration of angiogenic pathway proteomics with clinical features via ensemble learning significantly improves the precision of risk stratification for EOPE. Our ensemble model achieved an AUC of 0.95, outperforming both individual clinical indicators and the traditional sFlt−1/PlGF ratio. This suggests that a multi-dimensional approach, combining biological markers with maternal hemodynamics, is essential for capturing the complex etiology of early-onset preeclampsia.

Our proteomic analysis confirmed the central role of angiogenic imbalance in EOPE, a previous study has identified the role of sFlt−1 in neutralizing VEGF and PlGF [12]. While the sFlt−1/PlGF ratio has been validated as a robust tool for ruling out preeclampsia, our study demonstrates that the addition of Endoglin (sEng) and VEGF-A provides incremental predictive value, particularly for the early-onset phenotype [13], [14], [15]. Study has shown that sEng acts as a co-receptor for TGF-β and impairs nitric oxide-dependent vasodilation [16], [17], [18], [19]. The synergistic elevation of both sFlt−1 and sEng in our EOPE cohort suggests a state of severe systemic endothelial distress. By incorporating these multiple nodes within the angiogenic pathway, our model reflects the biological "angiogenic crisis" more accurately than single-marker assays. This is consistent with recent findings who emphasized that multiple biomarkers are often required to capture the heterogeneous nature of placental dysfunction [20], [21], [22], [23].

Traditional statistical models, such as logistic regression, often assume linear relationships between variables [24]. However, the pathogenesis of EOPE involves high-order interactions between maternal baseline risk and circulating factors. The superiority of our Ensemble Learning approach (Stacking of RF, XGBoost, and LightGBM) lies in its ability to manage such non-linearities. The machine learning algorithms can better handle the noise and dimensionality of proteomic data compared to classical statistics. Furthermore, our use of SHAP analysis addresses the "black-box" criticism of AI in medicine [25], [26]. The SHAP dependence plots revealed that the impact of the sFlt−1/PlGF ratio on risk scores is significantly modulated by maternal MAP. This interaction suggests that the diagnostic threshold for angiogenic biomarkers should perhaps be adjusted based on the patient’s hemodynamic profile—a nuance that is often missed in standard fixed-cutoff clinical guidelines.

The clinical significance of our model is best illustrated by its ability to perform Precision Risk Stratification. Currently, the clinical management of EOPE relies heavily on blood pressure monitoring and proteinuria, which are often "late-stage" markers [27], [28]. According to the previous study, early identification of high-risk patients is crucial for the efficacy of preventive measures like low-dose aspirin [29], [30]. By categorizing patients into low, moderate, and high-risk tiers, our framework provides a roadmap for Personalized Management. High-risk individuals, identified through our model, could be prioritized for intensive surveillance or early pharmacological intervention, while the high negative predictive value observed in the low-risk group could help reduce unnecessary hospitalizations.

Despite the promising results, several limitations warrant consideration. First, as a case-control study, there is a risk of selection bias; prospective longitudinal studies starting from the first trimester are needed to determine the model’s performance in a screening context. Second, while our 5-protein panel is comprehensive, the economic feasibility of multiplex assays in diverse clinical settings remains to be evaluated. Furthermore, future multi-center studies are required for external validation to ensure the generalizability of our model. Despite our rigorous feature selection process, the potential for overfitting in machine learning models remains an inherent concern that necessitates verification in larger, more diverse populations.

In conclusion, the integration of angiogenic pathway proteomics and clinical metadata through ensemble learning represents a significant step toward precision medicine in obstetrics. By elucidating the complex interactions between biomarkers and maternal features, this study provides a robust, interpretable, and high-performance tool for the risk stratification of early-onset preeclampsia, ultimately aiming to improve maternal and neonatal health outcomes.

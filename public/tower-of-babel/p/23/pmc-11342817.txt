Improving anti-oxidant stress treatment of subarachnoid hemorrhage through self-assembled nanoparticles of oleanolic acid
Authors: Youdong Zhou, Hengyu Wang, Xinyi Zhu, Qingyu Zhao, Gang Deng, Yong Li, Qianxue Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractSubarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) is a life-threatening acute hemorrhagic cerebrovascular disease, with early brain injury (EBI) being the main cause of high mortality and severe neurological dysfunction. Oxidative stress plays a crucial role in the pathogenesis of EBI. In this study, we synthesized antioxidant stress nanoparticles based on self-assembled oleanolic acid (OA) using the solvent volatilization method. X-ray diffraction (XRD), Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) techniques were employed to analyze and understand the self-assembly mechanism of oleic acid nanoparticles (OA NPs). The TUNEL assay, Nissl staining, and brain water content measurements were conducted to investigate the impact of OA NPs on cortical neuronal injury. Additionally, Western blot analysis was performed to investigate the antioxidant stress mechanism of OA NPs. The result showed that OA NPs exhibited a spherical structure with an average diameter of 168 nm. The application of OA NPs in SAH has been found to contribute to the reduction of keap1 protein levels and an increase in the nuclear level of Nrf2. As a result, the transcription of antioxidant stress proteins, including HO1 and NQO1, is triggered. The activation of the antioxidant stress pathway by OA NPs ultimately leads to a decrease in neuron damage and an improvement in neurological dysfunction. In conclusion, we successfully designed and synthesized OA NPs that can efficiently target the site of SAH. These nanoparticles have demonstrated their potential as antioxidants for the treatment of SAH, offering significant clinical applications.Keywords: Oleanolic acid, nanoparticles, oxidative stress, subarachnoid hemorrhage

Introduction

Subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) is a severe acute cerebrovascular disease characterized by life-threatening hemorrhage, with ruptured intracranial aneurysms accounting for approximately 85% of cases (Macdonald & Schweizer, 2017). SAH is associated with a significant mortality rate, as approximately 35% of patients do not survive beyond the first month (Mowry et al., 2019). Additionally, a significant number of survivors experience irreversible brain damage, leading to a loss of self-care abilities in 40% of patients and neurological dysfunction in 50% of patients (Sehba et al., 2012). Despite advancements in clinical diagnosis and surgical techniques, the lack of effective therapeutic drugs for SAH-related neurological dysfunction remains a major social burden.

Early brain injury (EBI), which occurs within 72 hours after SAH, is the primary pathological process responsible for the high mortality and severe neurological dysfunction associated with SAH (Li et al., 2023). EBI is a type of secondary brain injury, with major pathological changes including hemorrhage extravasation, rapid elevation of intracranial pressure, acute vascular spasm, reduction of cerebral blood flow, dysfunction of cerebrovascular autoregulation, and brain swelling. Changes such as space-occupying effects, toxic stimulation of blood, and instability of cerebral perfusion pressure activate downstream cascade reactions, leading to acute neurological dysfunction in patients after SAH (Higashi et al., 2014; Weng et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2022). Among these, oxidative stress resulting from an imbalance between reactive oxygen species (ROS) production and antioxidant activity plays a pivotal role in EBI pathogenesis (Yang et al., 2018; Gao et al., 2022). ROS are highly reactive and can disrupt the cell membrane by oxidizing proteins and lipids, leading to mitochondrial damage and apoptosis (Li et al., 2021). Antioxidant responses are vital for cell survival in hostile environments (Cuadrado et al., 2019), and the nuclear factor erythrocyte 2-associated factor 2 (Nrf2) is a key regulator of cellular antioxidant response. Under conditions of increased ROS, Nrf2 initiates the transcription of antioxidant genes, including NAD(P)H quinone oxidoreductase 1 (NQO1) and heme oxygenase 1 (HO1) (Zhang et al., 2023). Targeted activation of Nrf2 may prove to be an effective strategy for the treatment of EBI in SAH. However, due to the influence of the blood–brain barrier (BBB), most conventional drugs are unable to effectively cross the BBB and reach the injured brain tissue. Therefore, there is currently no satisfactory Nrf2 agonist available for the treatment of EBI.

Searching for new drugs from natural bioactive molecules has always been one of the main approaches in today’s drug research and development. Triterpenoids, a large class of plant secondary metabolites, possess anti-inflammatory and antioxidant biological activities, representing an important direction for the discovery of targeted anti-oxidative stress drugs. Dinkova-Kostova discovered that the triterpenoid oleanolic acid (OA) and its derivatives exhibit potent Nrf2 agonist effects. OA, a pentacyclic triterpenoid, is widely distributed in plants, including edible and medicinal plants (Castellano et al., 2022). It is notably present in the olive plant (Olea europaea), from which its name is derived (Adjei et al., 2021). OA exhibits various pharmacological activities, such as liver protection, anti-inflammatory effects, and antioxidant properties (Kashyap et al., 2016; Fernández-Aparicio et al., 2022; Yao et al., 2023). However, OA faces challenges such as poor water solubility, low bioavailability, and inadequate tissue targeting (Chen et al., 2011), limiting its clinical application (de la Torre et al., 2020). Studies have indicated that the oral bioavailability of OA is only 0.7% at doses of 25 and 50 mg/kg (Yang et al., 2012). Hence, enhancing the bioavailability of OA and developing an effective drug delivery system are crucial for its clinical application.

In this study, we developed oleicacid nanoparticles (OA NPs) through self-assembly, based on the principle of small molecule self-assembly. These nanoparticles effectively treat EBI after SAH by exerting significant antioxidant stress effects. Unlike traditional drug delivery systems, OA NPs consist solely of OA, serving as both carrier and drug. Consequently, they exhibit enhanced tissue bioavailability and superior antioxidant stress capabilities.

Methods

Drugs and antibodies

OA was purchased from Nanjing Spring and Autumn Biological Products Co., Ltd. (Nanjing, China) (purity (98%)). Antibodies used included the following: anti-claudin-5 (49564S, Cell Signaling Technology (CST), Boston, MA), anti-Nrf2 (12721T, Cell Signaling Technology (CST), Boston, MA), anti-HO1 (43966S, Cell Signaling Technology (CST), Boston, MA), anti-NQO1 (11451-1-AP, Proteintech, Shanghai, China), anti-beta actin (GB15001, Servicebio, Wuhan, China).

Animals and model

Male C57BL/6 J mice weighing 20–22 g were purchased from China Slake Experimental Animal Co., Ltd. (Changsha, China) and were raised at room temperature (22 ± 1 °C) and 12 hours day night circulation. Animals have free access to food and water. All animal procedures were performed in compliance with international guidelines on the ethical use of animals and were approved by the Laboratory Animal Ethics Committee of Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University (WDRY2021K070). All animal experiments were conducted from March to November 2021. As mentioned before, compared to the blood injection method, the intravascular puncture method is more capable of reflecting the physiological process of SAH in establishing a SAH model. Therefore, we performed intravascular puncture to establish a SAH model (Tian et al., 2022). Before the experiment, the mice underwent a one-week environmental adaptation period to reduce experimental stress, and a health assessment was conducted before the surgery to confirm no signs of disease. Food was restricted for 6–12 hours before the surgery, but water was provided ad libitum. The animals were anesthetized with isoflurane at 4 L/min fresh gas flow (2%) and maintained under anesthesia by connecting an isoflurane vaporizer at 1.5 L/min fresh gas flow (0.75%) while in the supine position. During the surgery, the left internal carotid artery and external carotid artery were carefully separated, and small vascular clips were used to temporarily block the blood flow in the common carotid artery and internal carotid artery. A 4-0 nylon suture was inserted into the internal carotid artery through an incision in the external carotid artery, and then advanced approximately 0.9 mm before being withdrawn to complete the perforation. Sham-operated animals underwent the same surgical procedures but without perforation. Postoperatively, the mice were immediately placed in a warm environment until fully recovered, and postoperative analgesia management was implemented. The healing of the surgical wounds was regularly checked, and the mice were provided with sufficient food and water. Recovery after surgery was assessed by continuously observing the behavior and activity levels of the mice. Youdong Zhou and Hengyu Wang performed all surgical procedures, and Qingyu Zhao participated in a blinded experimental study. When the mice lose more than 20% of their weight or experience persistent uncontrollable pain or convulsions, we euthanize the mice. At the end of study, the animals were sacrificed with cervical dislocation method following anesthesia with 3% isoflurane by Youdong Zhou and Hengyu Wang.

Study design

After the surgery, the mice were randomly divided into sham, SAH, SAH + OA, and SAH + OA NPs group. Based on past studies (Lin et al., 2021; Han et al., 2022; Sapkota & Choi, 2022), we have decided to use a dosage of 20 mg/kg. To determine the protective effect of OA NPs on EBI after SAH, OA NPs (dissolved in PBS) or OA (dissolved in PEG400) were injected into the mice via the tail vein one hour after the surgery, while the sham and SAH groups received the same dose of PEG400. Twenty-four hours after the successful establishment of the model, the mice were subjected to a neurological function score, followed by relevant pharmacological experiment. To assess the protective effect of OA NPs on persistent neurological deficits after SAH, a single dose was administered at 1 hour, followed by daily administrations for seven consecutive days.

SAH grade

The mice were euthanized 24 hours after inducing SAH for ­scoring. Two independent experimenters utilized the documentation method to score the mice, and the average score was recorded as the final score (Sugawara et al., 2008). The ventral part of the mouse brain was divided into six sections, and each section was assigned a score ranging from 0 to 3 based on the volume of blood clot present. The total score was calculated by summing the scores of all six sections, resulting in a range of 0–18. Mice with an SAH score below 7 or above 15 were excluded from subsequent experimental analysis.

Synthesis of self-assembled OA NPs

Oleanolic acid nanoparticles were prepared using the solvent evaporation method (Li et al., 2023). Initially, OA was dissolved in 1 ml of ethyl acetate. This solution was then added dropwise to a vigorously shaken 3 ml solution of 2.5% polyvinyl alcohol (PVA). The resulting mixture was sonicated on ice and then poured into a 30 ml solution of 0.3% PVA in water. The mixture was stirred overnight. Next, the liquid was collected and subjected to centrifugation at 18,000 rpm for 30 minutes in order to concentrate the product. By controlling the time and size of ultrasound, OA NPs with different particle sizes could be obtained. The supernatant was discarded and the precipitate was resuspended in 1 ml of distilled water. Centrifugation at 1000 rpm for five minutes was performed, followed by discarding the precipitate and collecting the supernatant. This supernatant was then sonicated for three minutes and freeze-dried for storage purposes.

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM)

Gold plating was carried out on the samples under vacuum and argon atmosphere with sputtering current of 20 mA for 100 s (auto fine coater JFC 1600, JEOL Ltd., Tokyo, Japan). Subsequently, SEM imaging was carried out using a Zeiss SIGMA field emission scanning electron microscope (Zeiss SIGMA, Carl Zeiss AG, Cambourne, UK) with 5 kV acceleration voltage and 10,000 times magnification (Li et al., 2024).

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)

OA NPs (50 μg/ml) were dropped onto a 100 mesh porous carbon-coated copper grid and observed at 100 kV using a JEOL 1230 Transmission Electron Microscope (JEOL Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) (Li et al., 2022).

Dynamic light scattering (DLS)

OA NPs were diluted to a concentration of 1 mg/ml in an aqueous solution. The hydration diameter and zeta potential of the diluted solution were measured by Dynamic Light Scatterer (Zetasizer Nano ZSP, Malvern instruments Ltd., Malvern, UK).

In vitro release of OA NPs

Place the 1% FBS solution of OA NPs in a beaker and stir at 37 °C and 200 rpm. At specific time points (3, 6, 12, 24, 48, 72, and 168 hours), take 0.2 ml of the supernatant after centrifugation. Determine the concentration of OA in each sample using HPLC (Agilent 1100 System, Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA). The mobile phase consists of acetonitrile, ammonium acetate, and methanol in a ratio of 67:21:12. The detection wavelength is 210 nm, and the injection volume is 10 μl.

Modified Garcia score

The short-term results were assessed using the modified Garcia score (a 3–18 points scoring system) (Garcia et al., 1995). This score consists of six components: spontaneous activity, spontaneous limb movement, forelimb extension, tactile sensation, body proprioception, and climbing ability. The higher the score, the better the neurological function. A double-blind design was adopted for this scoring to ensure fairness and objectivity in the evaluation process. Data collection and analysis were performed by a third party unaware of the mice’s grouping information.

Neurological function assessment

Twenty-four hours after modeling, the neurological function assessments were conducted using the Longa scoring method (Sarada et al., 2015). The scoring scale was as follows: 0 points: no neurological deficits observed; 1 point: inability to fully extend the contralateral forepaw; 2 points: circling toward the contralateral side; 3 points: falling to the contralateral side; 4 points: inability to walk spontaneously and exhibiting a depressed level of consciousness. The lower the score, the better the neurological function. The scoring was conducted using a single-blind design, meaning that the evaluator was not informed of the experimental group assignment of the mice. The mice were anonymized through random assignment of unique identifiers.

Evaluation of cerebral edema

After euthanizing the mice, the brain was carefully extracted and subsequently divided into the left and right hemispheres, brain stem, and cerebellum. The wet weight of each brain component was measured. Next, the brain specimen was subjected to drying at a temperature of 110 °C for a duration of 72 hours. Once the drying process was complete, the brain specimen was weighed again to obtain its dry weight (Wu et al., 2024).

Rotarod test

When SAH occurs, the coordination of mice becomes poorer, and they fall off the rotating rod in a short period of time, which is used to detect the degree of SAH injury. Two hours prior to data collection, the mice underwent training to adapt to the rotating rod. The mice were placed on a treadmill (Media Associates, St Albans, VT) (Wang et al., 2023). When the mouse moves on the pole, the pole with a diameter of 32 mm was raised 16 cm above the laboratory workbench and set to rise to 10 rpm within 300 seconds. Three experiments were conducted with at least a five-minute interval between each trial to evaluate the average fall latency. During the rotational test, both the operator and the data analyzer were blinded to the mice’s grouping status, employing a double-blind method to prevent potential bias.

Nissl staining

Nissl staining was performed to demonstrate the number of normal neurons in the cortex and hippocampus, following previously reported protocols (Kanamaru et al., 2024). Frozen brain slides were immersed in a 0.5% cresyl violet solution and dehydrated with 100% alcohol. After sealing with neutral balsam, the slides were observed under a light microscope by a blinded investigator.

Immunofluorescence staining

Cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 minutes, permeated with 0.2% Triton X-100 (PBS) for 15 minutes at room temperature, and then washed three times with PBS. 1% BSA was added dropwise to the glass slide at room temperature for 30 minutes. Next, a sufficiently diluted primary antibody was added and the slide was placed in a humid box, incubating overnight at 4 °C. A secondary antibody (Antgene, Wuhan, China) was then incubated for one hour in a humid chamber at 37 °C in the dark. The slides were mounted with DAPI anti-fluorescence quencher and imaged under a confocal microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan).

Western blotting

After euthanasia, the mice were perfused with cold PBS through the heart. The brain was then collected, clots removed, and an improved RIPA buffer solution (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China) was added at a mass ratio of 1:10. The tissue block was sonicated at 20% intensity for 30 seconds, incubated on ice for 30 minutes, and then centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 minutes. The protein concentration of the sample was quantitatively determined using a BCA protein assay. After heating at 100 °C for five minutes, the lysate was mixed with loading buffer. Equal amounts of protein were loaded onto 8–12% SDS-PAGE and then transferred to a nitrocellulose membrane. The PVDF membrane was then sealed in 5% skimmed milk for one hour, incubated with the primary antibody overnight at 4 °C, and incubated with the secondary antibody for two hours.

Morris water maze

The Morris water maze (MWM) test was utilized to assess the spatial learning and memory function of mice. It was performed during the final six days before the mice were euthanized (Li et al., 2023). In the hidden platform task (conducted over the first five days), the mice were trained to locate an underwater platform within 60 seconds. If the mice were unable to find the platform, they were guided to it and allowed to stay on it for an additional 15 seconds. Throughout the hidden platform task, the researchers, who were blind to the experimental group, recorded the swimming speed and path length to the platform. The mice were trained four times a day. On the last day, the probe test was conducted. The platform was removed, and the researchers, who were unaware of the experimental group, recorded the swimming trajectory and the time spent in each quadrant within 60 seconds. The progress of training through the water maze can be used to determine the strength of mice’s learning ability, and the percentage of time spent in each quadrant during the test can be used to assess their spatial orientation and memory. A double-blind approach was implemented, where the evaluator was unaware of the specific grouping (sham, SAH, OA, OA NPs) of the mice during scoring. All mice were identified through coded labels to conceal their experimental conditions.

Statistical analysis and power calculations

This study estimates the sample size for comparing the mean of a sample with the population mean, where the population mean belongs to the SAH group, and the sample group is the SAH + OA NPs group. The observed outcome indicator is the modified Garcia score. According to the literature review and preliminary experimental results, the mean modified Garcia score of the SAH group is 10.2 ± 2.2 points, and it is anticipated that the SAH + OA NPs group will have an average increase of 2 points in the score. Assuming a two-sided α = 0.05 and β = 0.15, the PASS 15 software calculated the required sample size to be 11 cases for the treatment group and eight cases for the control group. However, considering a 25% mortality rate due to surgery, the final minimum sample size for each group should be at least 14 cases. All experiments were repeated a minimum of three times. Data analysis was performed using SPSS version 24 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL). The significance between groups was assessed using Student’s t-test. The data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). A *p < .05 indicates a statistically significant difference.

Results and discussion

Synthesis and characterization of OA NPs

As shown in Figure 1(A), OA is a member of the pentacyclic triterpenoid compound, containing one hydroxyl group and one carboxyl group. To prepare OA NPs, we used the solvent evaporation method based on previous experience (Li et al., 2023). First, OA was dissolved in ethyl acetate, a lipophilic solvent. Then, water containing PVA was added to form a microemulsion through ultrasonic treatment. PVA prevents the o/w-type microemulsion from rapid aggregation as the surfactant. The solution was stirred to slowly evaporate the ethyl acetate. During this process, the hydrophilic end of OA molecules gradually entered the aqueous solution, resulting in the directional arrangement of molecules and the formation of spherical nanoparticles that stably exist in the solution. Finally, OA NPs were separated through centrifugation. To optimize the experimental conditions, we conducted a three-factor and three-level orthogonal experimental design, considering the concentration of PVA, ultrasound intensity, and oil/water phase ratio (v/v). Particle size, polydispersity index, and yield were used as evaluation criteria. The optimal conditions were determined as follows: PVA concentration of 2.5%, ultrasound intensity of 78 W, and oil/water phase ratio of 1:3 (Figure S1).

Synthesis and characterization of OA NPs. (A) Molecular structure of OA. (B) Representative TEM image of OA NPs. Scale bar: 200 nm. (C) Representative SEM image of OA NPs. Scale bar: 200 nm. (D) Mean sizes of OA NPs detected by DLS. (E) XRD of free OA and OA NPs. (F) FTIR of free OA and OA NPs.

The morphology of the prepared OA NPs was characterized using SEM and TEM. The images (Figure 1(B,C)) revealed that the OA NPs were spherical particles with a diameter of approximately 160 nm.1H NMR spectroscopy analysis showed that the OA NPs were composed solely of free OA without any PVA or other components (Figure S2). Dynamic light scattering analysis demonstrated that the average hydrodynamic diameter of the OA NPs was 168 nm, with a zeta potential of −17.8 mV (Figure 1(D), Figure S3). To assess the stability of OA NPs under different conditions, we placed them in buffer solutions with varying acidity levels and continuously monitored changes in particle size. The results (Figure S4) showed that the particle size of OA NPs remained relatively stable in acidic and neutral buffers over a period of 48 hours, while rapid agglomeration occurred under alkaline conditions. As shown in Figure S5, OA NPs exhibit a slow release trend in the FBS environment, indicating that they are not disrupted by plasma proteins, which is beneficial for their transport in the bloodstream.

To explore the self-assembly mechanism of OA NPs, X-ray diffraction (XRD) at 8° to 80° (2θ) was performed on both free OA and OA NPs (Figure 1(E)). The diffraction pattern of free OA exhibited characteristic sharp peaks at 2θ of 8.5°, 12.8°, 13.2°, 15.7°, and 21.1°, indicating its crystalline nature. In contrast, no sharp peaks were observed in the diffraction pattern of OA NPs, suggesting that OA existed in an amorphous state within the nanoparticles. Considering that OA possesses polar hydroxyl groups at both ends, we hypothesized that the self-assembly of OA NPs may be driven by the rigid structure of intermolecular hydrogen bonds and the lipophilic terpene skeletons. To investigate the role of hydrogen bonding in the self-assembly process, we compared the Fourier transform infrared spectra (FTIR) of free OA and freeze-dried OA NPs, focusing on the stretching frequencies of hydroxyl and carbonyl groups. As illustrated in Figure 1(F), the absorption peak at approximately 3450 cm−1 corresponds to the presence of hydroxyl groups, while the absorption peak at around 1700 cm−1 indicates the presence of carbonyl groups. In the case of free OA, the absorption peak attributed to hydroxyl groups shifted from a stretching frequency of 3471–3442 cm−1 in OA NPs. Similarly, the absorption peak associated with carbonyl groups shifted from a stretching frequency of 1695–1684 cm−1 in OA NPs. The spectra revealed that the stretching frequencies of both hydroxyl and carbonyl groups shifted to lower values in OA NPs compared to free OA, indicating the presence of stronger intermolecular hydrogen bonding interactions in the freeze-dried OA NPs. This supports the involvement of hydrogen bonding in the self-assembly process. Additionally, TEM analysis revealed a dark band with a surface thickness of approximately 3 nm within the nanoparticles (Figure S6). This observation is consistent with the sum of the longitudinal axis lengths (1.45 nm) of two OA molecules. Therefore, we speculate that due to the rigid structure and hydrogen bonding effect of the molecules themselves, OA molecules are oriented to form bilayer spherical vesicle particles. However, further exploration is needed to fully understand the self-assembly mode of OA NPs.

Histocompatibility and biodistribution of OA NPs

Hemolysis occurs when red blood cells are exposed to a drug, causing the cells to break down and release intracellular hemoglobin (Guo et al., 2021). In our study, we assessed the hemolysis rates of positive and negative controls, which were found to be 100% and 0%, respectively (Figure S7). Interestingly, the hemolysis rate of OA NPs at different concentrations was lower than 0.5%, and there was no significant increase in hemolysis rate with increasing OA NPs concentration. These results indicate that OA NPs have blood compatibility and do not cause significant hemolysis.

To further evaluate the safety of OA NPs, we intravenously injected 400 mg/kg OA NPs into normal mice and monitored their body weight. Compared to the control group, the mice in the OA NPs group did not die during the experiment, and their weight did not show significant changes (Figure S8). Additionally, we stained the liver, kidney, heart, and spleen of the mice from both groups with HE, and no obvious tissue toxicity of OA NPs was observed (Figure S9). These findings suggest that OA NPs have high biosafety and can be used for long-term intravenous therapy.

To determine the biological distribution of OA NPs, we used a SAH model in mice. PLGA is a well-known synthetic material carrier, and in our study, we used PLGA and OA NPs to encapsulate the fluorescent dye RB200 for in vivo drug distribution tracing. After successful modeling, we injected free dye RB200, OA-RB200 NPs, or PLGA-NPs loaded with RB200 into the mice via the tail vein. After 12 hours, we euthanized the mice and collected the brain and other major organs for IVIS imaging. The results showed that in the left cerebral hemisphere, particularly in the operation area, the fluorescence signal significantly increased in the OA NPs and PLGA groups. Moreover, the signal increase in the OA NPs group was higher than that in the PLGA group. This indicates that compared to PLGA NPs and free RB200, OA NPs can cross the BBB and reach the edematous brain tissue (Figure 2(A)). Further examination of brain tissue through frozen section and confocal microscopy revealed that OA NPs significantly aggregated around the edematous tissue, as compared to normal brain tissue (Figure 2(C)). Claudin-5 is a major component of the tight junctions found beside cerebral vascular endothelial cells, which form the BBB and regulate the flow of substances in and out of brain tissue (Hashimoto et al., 2023). Western blot analysis showed a significant reduction in claudin-5 protein levels in the edematous tissue, suggesting damage to the BBB (Figure S10).

Biodistribution of OA NPs. (A, B) Ex vivo fluorescence imaging of the harvested brains by an IVIS system. n = 3. *p < .05 versus free dye group. #p < .001 versus free dye group. (C) Representative images of OA-RB200 NPs (red) in the regions of SAH brain.

We speculate that OA NPs can directly enter brain tissue through the open paravascular spaces and, due to the lack of means to expel nanoparticles from the edematous brain tissue, further aggregate around the edematous brain tissue. However, as mentioned in the literature that PLGA NPs are easily opsonized and rapidly cleared by the reticular endothelial system, especially when there is no PEG modification, which may be the reason why its delivery efficiency is lower than that of OA NPs (Zhang et al., 2022).

Effect of OA NPs on brain edema and short-term neurological function

We successfully induced SAH in mice using the intravascular perforation method, as illustrated in Figure 3(A). To determine the protective effect of OA NPs on EBI after SAH, OA NPs were injected into the mice via the tail vein one hour after the surgery. When we euthanized the animals and collected brain samples 24 h after SAH, we observed no significant difference in SAH levels between the model groups (Figure 3(B)). However, by assessing the Garcia score and neurological score, we found that mice exhibited severe neurological dysfunction 24 hours after SAH, and the administration of OA NPs improved neurological function in mice (Figure 3(C,D)). Additionally, we conducted the Rotarod test and observed that the use of OA NPs significantly increased the standing time of mice on the pole (Figure 3(E)). Brain edema is a known independent risk factor for poor prognosis in SAH. We observed a significant increase in brain water content 24 hours after SAH in mice compared to the sham surgical group, with more severe edema observed in the surgical side of the cerebral cortex. However, the use of OA NPs significantly reduced brain water content and alleviated nerve damage (Figure 3(F)).

In summary, these experiments indicate that injecting OA NPs 1 h after SAH can effectively improve neurological dysfunction and reduce brain edema in SAH mice, thereby enhancing treatment efficacy. However, the timing of drug delivery is probably not clinically realistic. In clinical practice, the earlier the intervention of EBI after SAH is, the better the treatment effect will be (Li et al., 2023). We first confirmed that OA NPs have anti-EBI effects, and further exploration of different time and delayed administration is needed in the next step.

Effect of OA NPs on cortical neuronal injury at 24 h after SAH

TUNEL staining was used to assess the impact of OA NPs on cortical neuronal damage. The number of apoptotic neurons in the SAH group significantly increased 24 hours after modeling, compared to the Sham group. However, treatment with OA NPs effectively inhibited the apoptosis of neuronal cells (Figures 4(A) and 3(C)). Nissl bodies, which consist of developed rough endoplasmic reticulum and free ribosomes, reflect neuronal activity (Cao et al., 2022). Nissl staining revealed that 24 hours after SAH, the Nissl bodies in the neurons of the cerebral cortex in the temporal lobe of mice significantly shrank, became darker, and decreased in number. In contrast, after treatment with OA NPs, the Nissl bodies significantly increased compared to the SAH group and exhibited a shape more similar to normal Nissl bodies. These findings suggest that OA NPs can alleviate the neurological dysfunction caused by EBI after SAH by suppressing neuronal apoptosis.

OA NPs reduce neuronal damage through Nrf2/HO1 pathway

The experiments demonstrated that levels of ROS were significantly elevated while the antioxidant glutathione (GSH) was significantly reduced 24 hours after SAH (Figure 5(A,B)). Malondialdehyde (MDA), a marker of cell membrane damage through lipid peroxidation, also notably increased in the cerebral cortex of the operative area after 24 hours, indicating severe cortical neuron membrane damage due to oxidative stress (Figure 5(C)). However, treatment with OA NPs resulted in a significant increase in GSH levels, a decrease in ROS levels, and ultimately a reduction in MDA levels, thereby reducing lipid peroxidation caused by oxidative stress in neurons.

(E–I) Representative Western blotting of total Nrf2 (T-Nrf2) and downstream signaling images and quantitative analyses. *p < .05 versus SAH group. **p < .01 versus SAH group. ***p < .001 versus SAH group. (D, J) Representative Western blotting of nuclear Nrf2 (N-Nrf2) images and quantitative analyses. **p < .01 versus SAH group.

The mechanism of cellular antioxidant response is primarily determined by Nrf2. The regulation of Nrf2 activity mainly involves altering the degradation rate of Nrf2 protein (Chen et al., 2023). Nrf2 is rapidly ubiquitinated by the Cul3-E3 ubiquitin ligase and degraded by the 26S proteasome. Keap1 serves as an adaptor protein for the Cul3-E3 ubiquitin ligase, binding to Nrf2 in the cytoplasm and promoting the ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of Nrf2 protein, thereby maintaining Nrf2 at a low level (Zhao et al., 2023). However, under conditions of oxidative stress, ROS induces conformational changes in Keap1, preventing effective action by Cul3-E3 ubiquitin ligase on the ubiquitination site of Nrf2. Consequently, the ubiquitination and proteasome degradation of Nrf2 are blocked, leading to the accumulation of Nrf2 protein. Western blot analysis revealed that after SAH, the level of Keap1 protein decreased, while total Nrf2 levels significantly increased, indicating a protective response of the body to oxidative stress (Figure 5(E–G)). We further isolated nuclear proteins and found that treatment with OA NP increased nuclear Nrf2 protein (Figure 5(D,J)). Once in the nucleus, Nrf2 can activate the transcription of heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1) and NQO1, exerting a significant anti-oxidative stress effect (Figure 5(E,H,I)).

Effect of OA NPs on persistent neurological function and hippocampus injury

The hippocampus, an important brain region responsible for memory and learning, was further examined using Nissl staining 28 days after modeling to gain a better understanding of the potential impact of OA NPs on SAH-induced persistent neurological dysfunction. The SAH group exhibited a significant reduction in the number of normal neurons in the DG, CA1, and CA3 regions compared to the sham surgery group. Treatment with OA failed to restore the normal number of neurons, but OA NPs did significantly increase the number of surviving neurons (Figure 6(A)). These findings suggest that OA NPs may mitigate hippocampal damage and inhibit sustained neurobehavioral impairment by reducing oxidative stress following SAH.

Effect of OA NPs on persistent neurological function and hippocampus injury. (A) Representative images showing Nissl staining of CA1 CA3 and DG regions. Scale bar: 50 μm. (B–D) Swimming distance, escape latency, and platform crossover times of Morris water maze. ***p < .001 versus SAH group. (E) Probe quadrant duration of mice when test. (F) Representative swimming trajectories when training.

To assess the impact of OA NPs on persistent cognitive impairment, the MWM test was conducted. MWM data revealed no significant differences in swimming distance, escape latency, or platform crossover times on the first day, indicating no baseline differences in swimming ability or visual impairment. However, mice with SAH exhibited increased escape latency and longer swimming distance from the second to fifth day, indicating severe learning and memory disorders due to SAH-induced hippocampal neuron damage. Treatment with OA NPs resulted in improved performance in mice from day 3 to day 5, as evidenced by shorter escape latency and swimming distance (Figure 6(B–D)). Additionally, the swimming trajectory showed that mice in the SAH group exhibited more crossovers and spent more time in the target quadrant (Figure 6(E)). After treatment with OA NPs, SAH mice were able to locate the platform more quickly (Figure 6(F)). As a self-assembled nano drug delivery system, OA NPs significantly enhance the bioavailability and tissue targeting ability of OA. These results indicate that antioxidant therapy with OA NPs can reduce early brain damage, improve the survival of hippocampal neurons, and further alleviate long-term neurological damage caused by SAH.

Conclusions

In conclusion, we have successfully developed and synthesized OA NPs that exhibit efficient targeting and delivery to the site of SAH. Furthermore, our research has demonstrated the potential of OA NPs as effective antioxidants for the treatment of SAH, highlighting their significant clinical applications.

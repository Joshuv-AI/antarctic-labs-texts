Back External Quilting Suture in Massive Weight Loss Patients: A New Technique to Enhance Body Contouring
Authors: Matilde de Garcia de la Vega, Anna Scarabosio, Damiano Tambasco, Etienne Rousson, Eric Watier, Benoit Lengelé, Yanis Berkane, Nicolas Bertheuil
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Summary:Despite its first descriptions in the previous decade, the use of the hemostatic net has experienced a recent resurgence of enthusiasm in cervicofacial rhytidectomy. Sizeable studies demonstrate a reduction of postoperative hematoma and better skin retraction thanks to external quilting suture techniques. Recent evidence supports this technique’s safety for skin and soft tissue vascularization. We describe an innovative extension of transcutaneous suture indications to postbariatric and body-contouring surgery. By extending the hemostatic net, described for cervical and thoracic surgery, to postbariatric and body-contouring surgery, we aim to propose a safe, reproducible, and innovative technique to enhance contour shaping of the lumbar region. We present a series of 8 massive weight loss patients undergoing liposuction followed by hemostatic net sutures to improve contouring while minimizing scarring. Stitches were performed with 1/0 braided sutures and removed after 2 days. The follow-up ranged from 6 to 20 months. The average lipoaspirated volume was 1431 mL (range, 450–2300 mL). No complications were reported, and no subsequent procedures were needed. Lower back contouring improvement was evaluated using pre- and postoperative photograph comparisons by the patient and the surgeon. Skin marks partially faded at 6 months and disappeared at 12 months. This study primarily emphasized the safety and feasibility of external quilting sutures as an adjunct in postbariatric contouring rather than demonstrating definitive superiority over existing methods. This technique offered an additional option in the plastic surgeon’s arsenal to address massive weight-loss sequelae despite poor skin tone.

Takeaways

Question: Can we improve body contouring after liposuction in massive weight-loss patients using quilting sutures with no skin excision?

Findings: We present a series of 8 patients who underwent posterior contouring procedures involving external quilting sutures in the lower back area. Contouring was found accentuated and highly satisfactory from the patient’s and the surgeon’s points of view. No complications were recorded.

Meaning: We highlight the potential of the use of external quilting sutures, a reproducible, safe, and scarless technique, to optimize outcomes in reshaping the lumbar and posterior thoracic areas after liposuction in massive weight loss patients.

INTRODUCTION

Liposuction in postbariatric and cosmetic contouring surgery has become essential in our practice. However, despite multiple refinements, its extensive use can lead to significant risks of seromas and remaining skin excess due to a lack of retraction, especially in the postbariatric population, who often present with poor skin quality. Both complications can impose surgical revision despite the use of postoperative compressive garments.

The hemostatic net technique described by Auersvald and Auersvald1 almost 10 years ago has mainly been used for head and neck cosmetic surgery. Although it still seems controversial, partially due to the hypothetical risk of facial nerve injury and indelible markings, many authors have confirmed its potential in reducing postoperative hematoma in sizable face lift case series.2,3 Similarly, net techniques have been used in thoracic and breast contouring as an alternative to minor skin mastopexy,4 to prevent seroma following gynecomastia surgery,5 or after high-definition lipostructure.6

The principle is 2-fold: to create transdermal pad stitches to seal dead spaces after liposuction and promote healing and tissue retraction. In this article, we share an innovative use of the hemostatic net technique in postbariatric and body-contouring surgery to guide lower back enhanced contouring in patients undergoing extensive liposuction.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

All clinical care complied with the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and the 2011 French bioethics laws. Written informed consent was obtained preoperatively. Patients underwent extensive lumbar and flank liposuction with external quilting suture following massive weight loss, performed by the senior author (N.B.). Other procedures, such as abdominoplasty, lower body lift, or Brazilian butt lift, could have been performed concurrently.

We estimated the additional operating time to be around 15 minutes. No posterior drains were required; 2 were placed anteriorly when abdominoplasty or lower body lift was performed.

Postoperative Management

Thromboprophylaxis was provided for 15 days, complying with local and national guidelines. Pain management included standard analgesics (acetaminophen, nefopam) and cryotherapy. The postoperative compressive dressing and the external quilting stitches were removed on postoperative day 2. Compressive garments were worn for 48 hours, followed by an abdominal corset for 6 weeks. Postoperative lymphatic drainage was not recommended.

RESULTS

Eight consecutive, nonpreselected patients underwent posterior contouring with external quilting sutures, with follow-up ranging from 6 to 20 months. The average lipoaspirate volume was 1431 mL (450–2300 mL). Postoperative pain was comparable to standard procedures and easily managed with conventional protocols. The mean hospital stay was 4–5 days, with discharge after drain removal and physiotherapy. Follow-up visits occurred at 2 weeks, 1–3 months, and beyond 6 months. No hematoma, seroma, wound dehiscence, or revision were noted. Temporary skin markings occurred despite paraffin-gauze interposition, fading by 6 months and disappearing by 12 months. All patients were informed of possible transient marks, and none expressed concern. Both patient and surgeon found the contouring results highly satisfactory based on pre- and postoperative comparisons. (See figure, Supplemental Digital Content 1, which displays the preoperative and postoperative comparisons, https://links.lww.com/PRSGO/E759.) Treated areas showed adequate tissue retraction and reduced skin redundancy in postbariatric patients with poor skin tone, highlighting natural curves. Satisfaction was high for both patients and surgeons, although no objective scale was used.

DISCUSSION

Transcutaneous external quilting suture techniques are still debatable. In 2023, 2 studies demonstrated their vascular safety. Authors have evaluated the remaining tissue perfusion in a cadaveric model using injected dyes following transdermal net removal,7,8 demonstrating comparable skin perfusion before and after hemostatic sutures, which supports vascular safety. Based on this, we extended the technique to lower back contouring in postbariatric patients, an unexplored indication. Although transcutaneous nets are primarily used to reduce seromas and postoperative hematomas,9 our goal was to achieve faster and greater tissue retraction following extensive liposuction.

This technique achieved optimal back contouring, particularly in the lumbar area, and may be especially valuable after Brazilian butt lift procedures by enhancing gluteal projection and natural curves. It can improve buttock definition, projection, and overall aesthetic outcomes. Although patients should be informed about the technique and its temporary skin marks, no change in postoperative follow-up was required. It also seems promising after lower body lift procedures in massive weight loss patients, helping refine scar positioning and reinforce the buttock-lumbar junction. Although equivalence or superiority to existing skin-tightening technologies such as BodyTite cannot yet be claimed, this technique offers a potentially safe, scarless, and cost-effective alternative deserving further study.

In postbariatric patients, poor skin quality poses a major challenge, and liposuction’s impact is often limited by low elasticity. Despite genetic predisposition and individual variability, factors such as skin thickness and stretch marks remain key predictors of retraction potential. Until now, skin excision with extensive scars has been necessary, although helium plasma–assisted liposuction is emerging as a promising alternative.10 This makes guided retraction through transdermal quilting sutures particularly valuable. In our series, this technique led to more aggressive liposuction despite poor skin quality, and even enabled conversion from lower body lift to abdominoplasty with posterior liposuction. The procedure’s safety was supported by the absence of complications and effective pain control, as no sutures were removed prematurely due to pain or skin injury. All skin marks partially resolved by 6 months and completely by 12 months, with no hypertrophic or keloid scars. These findings align with Hoyos et al,10 who reported no hyperpigmentation or scarring after 6 months among 51 patients treated with a thoracic hemostatic net removed after 48–72 hours. Although this preliminary proof-of-concept includes a limited number of patients, it underscores the potential of external quilting sutures to enhance body-contouring outcomes while reducing morbidity.

CONCLUSIONS

We present a novel technique for lower back contouring using external quilting sutures in postbariatric body-contouring procedures. Combined with liposuction, these sutures enhance natural curves and limit fat graft diffusion by compartmentalizing injected areas. Despite lacking objective measurements and controls, the method seems reproducible, safe, and scarless, warranting further study. This work emphasized the safety and feasibility of external quilting sutures as an adjunct rather than their superiority over existing methods.

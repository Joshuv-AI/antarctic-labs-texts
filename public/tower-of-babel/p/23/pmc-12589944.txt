Radiotherapy dosimetry oversights in the RELAX trial: implications for interpreting taVNS efficacy in neuropathic pain
Authors: Yupeng Di, Jing Li, Lingling Meng
Published: 2025

We read the Article by Zuo and colleagues in eClinicalMedicine with great interest.1 The authors should be commended for exploring a novel, non-pharmacological approach to accelerating early analgesia in radiotherapy-related neuropathic pain (RRNP). Their work addresses an important issue in the supportive care of survivors of head and neck cancer, offering new insights into how to overcome the critical limitation of delayed pain relief during initial pharmacotherapy.

Although the trial robustly evaluated the efficacy of transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS), critical methodological gaps regarding radiotherapy parameters undermined its clinical applicability. For example, the study did not analyse the effect of radiation dose heterogeneity on the severity of neuropathic injury or the response to treatment. Specifically, the severity of neuropathic pain in patients with RRNP is directly proportional to the exposure of neural structures (e.g. the brachial and cervical plexuses) to radiation. While the trial reported median radiotherapy doses (e.g. 70 Gy to primary sites), it omitted dose–volume histograms for nerves and plexuses, despite Dmax and volumes receiving ≥40 Gy (V40) being established predictors of neuropathy. Consequently, imbalances in nerve-specific irradiation between groups could confound the efficacy of taVNS. For example, a higher Dmax to the brachial plexus in the control group could exacerbate the pain experienced by the sham group, thereby exaggerating the benefits of the taVNS radiotherapy technique. Radiotherapy technique and fractionation can significantly impact the risk of neurotoxicity. However, despite 78.4% of participants across both study groups having received intensity-modulated radiation therapy, subgroup analyses did not stratify by radiotherapy modality or dose per fraction.

The omission of dosimetric covariates in analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) models (Table S3a) limits the validity of treatment effect estimates, as potential confounders remain unaddressed. Conventional radiotherapy—received by 24.1% in the taVNS groups versus 19.0% of controls—may confer higher neurotoxicity risk, potentially influencing between-group outcomes. Furthermore, while serum cytokine analysis explored taVNS’ anti-inflammatory properties, radiation-induced neural damage primarily stems from microvascular injury and demyelination. The biological basis of taVNS response would be strengthened by correlating radiotherapy doses with neuropathic biomarkers (e.g. neurofilament light chain).

Future trials should incorporate detailed RT dosimetry (e.g. nerve-specific Dmax, V40) to identify patients who are most likely to benefit from taVNS. This would refine patient selection and clarify whether efficacy is affected by the severity of neuropathic injury.

Genetic characteristics of influenza A/H3N2 virus in Jiaxing, China (2019–2024)
Authors: Yin Song, Xue Zhang, Jimei Ji, Lina Li, Yamei Zhou, Ping Li, Ganglin Ren, Shencong Lv, Xiaofei Zhang, Yong Yan, Guoying Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rapid evolution of influenza A/H3N2 virus underscores the imperative for ongoing surveillance. In this study, we analyzed 7,872 respiratory samples collected from patients with influenza-like symptoms in Jiaxing, China, between 2019 and 2024, selecting 117 influenza A/H3N2 strains for whole-genome sequencing. Between 2019 and 2024, the influenza A/H3N2 virus in Jiaxing experienced six epidemic peaks, evolving from clades 3 C.2a1b.1a/1b, 3 C.2a1b.2, and 3 C.2b1b.2a.2a.3/3b to clade 3 C.2a1b.2a.2a.3a.1. Compared with the 2019–2021 period, influenza A/H3N2 viruses circulating in Jiaxing during 2022–2024 demonstrated a longer evolutionary distance and increased hemagglutination titers. Additionally, seven novel amino acid substitutions (H156S, Y159N, T160I, S186D, D190N, I192F, N96S) were identified in HA antigenic epitopes of circulating influenza A/H3N2 viruses during 2023–2024. Notably, based on predicted vaccine effectiveness, the 2023–2024 influenza vaccine retained strong protective efficacy. In conclusion, this study characterizes the evolutionary features of influenza A/H3N2 virus in Jiaxing during 2019–2024. These results enhance our understanding of the natural evolution of influenza virus and the emergence of new variants, providing critical insights into influenza vaccine effectiveness.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12985-025-02974-6.Keywords: Influenza A/H3N2 virus; Hemagglutination, amino acid mutation, whole-genome sequencing; Vaccine efficacy

Introduction

Influenza is an acute respiratory infection caused by influenza viruses. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), around a billion influenza cases occur worldwide annually and result in 290,000 to 650,000 deaths [1]. In China, influenza similarly contributes to a substantial disease burden, occupying the third-highest position in both morbidity and mortality rates among respiratory infections [2]. The influenza hospitalization rate in mainland China is 73.0 per 100,000 people, while in Hong Kong it is 35.7 per 100,000 [3]. After relaxation of COVID-19 containment measures, influenza activity in China increased rapidly beginning in 2023, characterized by sequential circulation of influenza A(H1N1), A(H3N2), and B/Victoria strains that exceeded the levels observed in natural epidemic years [4]. Despite regional variations in influenza positivity across northern versus southern China, during 2023–2024 influenza A(H1N1) prevalence ranged from 7.3% to 20.6%, while H3N2 positivity ranged from 7.4% to 16.1% [5–7]. Among influenza A viruses (IAV), A(H1N1)pdm09 and A(H3N2) are the primary subtypes responsible for human influenza pandemics [8, 9]. The influenza A/H3N2 virus, which emerged in 1968, has been spreading seasonally in human populations, leading to widespread epidemics and significant mortality rates [10]. Currently, the predominant circulating influenza A H3N2 viruses in China belong to clades 3 C.2a1b.2a.2a and 3 C.2a1b.2a.2a.3a, which originated from clade 3 C.2a first detected in 2014 [11, 12]. Based on statistical data, the mean excess respiratory mortality rate linked to influenza A/H3N2 virus in China was 2.6 per 100,000 person-seasons [13]. China’s influenza vaccination rate was comparatively lower than the global average, potentially intensifying the difficulties in managing seasonal surges of influenza cases [14].

IAV belong to enveloped viruses and consist of multiple negative-strand RNA genomes [15]. IAV comprise eight gene segments: HA, neuraminidase (NA), matrix protein (M), nucleoprotein (NP), non-structural protein(NS), polymerase basic 1 (PB1), polymerase basic 2 (PB2), and polymerase acidic (PA), which responsible for encoding the different proteins of IAV [16]. Although the role of each protein is not fully understood, there has been much research on HA and NA. HA is a trimeric glycoprotein located on the surface of IAV which begin to infect human by binding to the host cell surface receptors [17]. NA is a tetrameric glycoprotein thought to be associated with viral release from the host cells and influenza inhibitor resistance [18]. The mutation and evolution of influenza viruses are often caused by the mutations of HA and NA.

Hemagglutination test is a rapid and standard screening method for detecting the ability to bind to the red blood cells (RBCs) of HA by surface receptors [19]. The result of hemagglutination test is often influenced by gene expression. Generally, human influenza viruses agglutinate human, guinea pig, and avian RBCs, but H3N2 viruses no longer agglutinate avian RBCs after 2005 [20]. The bind ability between HA and RBCs is mediated by α2,3 and α2,6 linked sialic acid moieties at the RBCs surface [21]. As Madin-Darby canine kidney (MDCK) cells express both α2,3 and α2,6 linked sialic acid receptors, it has been used as a common cell for isolating influenza viruses, which can isolate both human and avian influenza viruses with high hemagglutination titers. However, the isolation of A/H3N2 in MDCK was not ideal and always got a low hemagglutination titer in recent years.

The COVID-19 pandemic has made the seasonal influenza epidemic irregular. Our earlier research revealed that the genetic features of the influenza A/H3N2 virus in Jiaxing post-2022 exhibited notable differences compared to those in 2019. In this study, we analyzed the influenza A/H3N2 virus prevalent in Jiaxing during 2019–2024 to investigate the evolutionary traits, antigenic drift, and antiviral sensitivity of the strains following the COVID-19 pandemic. Furthermore, we assessed the effectiveness of the Northern Hemisphere influenza A/H3N2 vaccine against the strains circulating in Jiaxing.

Materials and methods

Data availability

Genomic data has been published on the Global Initiative on Sharing All Influenza Data (GISAID) database. In this study, all 117 strain names and their corresponding login numbers are listed in the accompanying materials (Table S1).

Samples and data collection

In this monitoring period from 2019 to 2024, throat swab samples were collected from individuals diagnosed with influenza like illness (ILI) at Jiaxing First Hospital. The characteristic of ILI is influenza-like symptoms, and the inclusion criteria require patients to exhibit sudden onset of high fever (≥ 38 °C) accompanied by respiratory symptoms such as cough and sore throat. The sample collection has obtained informed consent. RNA was extracted from 200ul throat swab samples using a nucleic acid automated preparation system (Tianlong Generotex 96, Xian, China), and the influenza A/H3N2 virus was identified using a real-time fluorescent reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) kit (X-abt A2774, Beijing, China). Inoculate 1–2 mL of positive throat swab samples into T25 (Corning) cell culture flasks lined with MDCK cells and incubate at 35 °C and 5% CO2 for 1–2 hours. After incubation, the cells were washed with phosphate buffered saline (PBS, Gibco, 2001, 2050), and then 5mL of Dulbecco modified Eagle medium (Gibco, 119, 65118) containing 100 U/mL penicillin, 100 µg/mL streptomycin, and 2µg/mL TPCK trypsin was added to cell culture bottle T25 and cultured at 35 °C and 5% CO2. Collect the virus after observing cellular lesions within 4–7 days, and then confirm it through hemagglutination test. Furthermore, more detailed experimental protocols can be found in the Technical Guidelines for National Influenza Surveillance (2017 Edition) (https://ivdc.chinacdc.cn/cnic/fascc/201802/t20180202_158592.htm, accessed on 30 September 2017).

Whole-genome sequencing of influenza A/H3N2 virus

According to the epidemic situation of influenza A/H3N2 virus in Jiaxing, positive isolates were chosen for sequencing on a monthly basis. During all months with influenza A/H3N2 virus activity, the sequencing selection rate varied from a minimum of 15% to a maximum of 100%. The RNA of the isolated influenza A/H3N2 virus was extracted using an automated nucleic acid extractor (Tianlong Generotex 96, Xian, China), and reverse transcription and PCR amplification of the genome were performed using SuperScriptTM III One Step RT-PCR System with PlatinumTM Taq High Fidelity (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA, 12574035). The amplification primers include Uni-12/Inf1 (Primer A): 5‘- GGGGGGGG AGCAGCAGG-3’, Uni-12/Inf3 (Primer B): 5‘- KGGGGG AGCGAAGCAGG3’, and Uni-13/If1 (Primer C): 5‘- CGGTTTATTAGTAGAAACAGGG-3’. The preparation of reaction systems and conditions is detailed in the Technical Guidelines for National Influenza Surveillance (2017 Edition). The amplification product was purified using a nucleic acid purification kit (magnetic bead method) (MATRIDX MD012, Hangzhou, China), quantified using QubitTM 1X dsDNA HS Assay Kit (Invitrogen, Q33230), and diluted to 0.2 ng/µ L. Then, according to the provided instructions, use the metagenomic DNA library preparation kit (MATRIDX MD012, Hangzhou, China) to prepare a sequencing library. Purification of the library was performed and quantified using the kappa library quant kit (Roche 07960140001, Switzerland). Finally, the library was merged, denatured, and diluted to 1.7 pM, loaded onto reagent strips in designated storage tanks (Mid Output Reagent Cartridge v2 300 cycles), and sequenced on the Illumina NextSeq 550 sequencing platform according to the Illumina NextSeq 550 system guidelines. The original paired-end read data meeting the Q30 quality threshold were loaded into the MVP assembly system (Alibaba, Hangzhou, China), assembled with reference to vaccine strains from various years, and then used to produce genome sequences with a sequencing depth of ≥ 1000X for further analysis.

Phylogenetic analyses of influenza A/H3N2 virus

According to the year, 338 HA nucleotide sequences of influenza A/H3N2 virus were downloaded from GISAID between 2019 and 2024, and a phylogenetic tree was built alongside 117 local sequences. Sequence alignment was conducted using the ClustalW method in the Molecular Evolutionary Genetics Analysis Version 11.0 (MEGA) software. The phylogenetic tree was built using the Neighbor-joining model in MEGA, with bootstrap set to 1000.

Sequence homologies and mutation analyses of influenza A/H3N2 virus

The sequence distances algorithm of MegAlign 7.1.0 was used to align the nucleotide sequences of the target influenza A/H3N2 virus with the 2019 vaccine strain A/Kansas/14/2017, in order to determine the homology of nucleotide sequences. This analysis specifically focuses on the HA and NA gene fragments. MEGA 11.0 was used for amino acid level mutation analysis and compared with the reference strain A/Kansas/14/2017. SnapGene 5.2 was used to count the number and types of amino acid mutations.

Vaccine effectiveness evaluation

Vaccine effectiveness against influenza A/H3N2 virus was assessed using the pEpitope model, which calculates the antigenic distance between the vaccine and circulating strains by analyzing the proportion of amino acid substitutions in the HA epitopes [22]. Specifically, the pepitope is calculated by dividing the count of amino acid substitutions in the HA1 epitope by the total number of amino acids in the epitope. According to previous studies, the mutation counts in epitopes A, B, C, D, and E of HA are 19, 21, 27, 41, and 22 amino acids, respectively [23]. Following calibration, the linear regression relationship between pepitope distances and vaccine effectiveness was determined as Vaccine Effectiveness = (−3.32 pepitope + 0.66) x 100%. When pepitope = 0, the Vaccine Effectiveness equals 66%.

Results

Epidemiology of influenza A/H3N2 virus in Jiaxing from 2019 to 2024

Between January 1, 2019, and December 31, 2024, 7,872 ILI samples were collected from designated hospitals in Jiaxing, and influenza A/H3N2 virus was detected in these samples. Among these samples, 636 influenza A/H3N2 viruses were identified, and six epidemic peaks were observed from 2019 to 2024. Out of the six epidemic peaks, two were observed in 2019, two in 2022, and two in 2023 (Fig. 1A). According to statistics, the positivity rate for influenza A/H3N2 virus in Jiaxing peaked in 2022 at 19.22% and reached its lowest point in 2021 at 0% (Fig. 1B). By employing MDCK cells, 395 strains were isolated from 636 influenza A/H3N2 virus samples, and 117 of these were chosen for genome sequencing. The selected 117 strains covered all epidemic periods from 2019 to 2024, representing more than 12% of the annual influenza A/H3N2 virus samples.

Result of the influenza A/H3N2 virus hemagglutination test

Influenza A/H3N2 virus hemagglutinin can cause agglutination of human or animal red blood cells. The results indicated that the hemagglutination titers of influenza A/H3N2 viruses were primarily 8–16 during 2019–2022 and 32–128 during 2023–2024 (Fig. 2A). T-test analysis revealed a significant increase in hemagglutination titers in 2023–2024 compared to 2019–2022, with statistical significance (p < 0.0001) (Fig. 2B).

Phylogenetic analysis of influenza A/H3N2 viruses in 2019–2024

Phylogenetic analysis of the HA gene showed that the 2019 sequences in Jiaxing primarily clustered within clade 3 C.2a1b.1b (10/20) and 3 C.2a1b.2 (10/20), alongside sequences from Thailand, China, and Brazil. In 2020, sequences clustered in clade 3 C.2a1b.1a (2/9) and 3 C.2a1b.1b (7/9). Clade 3 C.2a1b.1a sequences mainly grouped with American sequences, while clade 3 C.2a1b.1b sequences primarily clustered with Chinese and Australian sequences. All sequences (32/32) from 2022 clustered within clade 3 C.2a1b.2a.1a, primarily grouping with sequences from China. In 2023, sequences mainly clustered in clade 3 C.2a1b.2a.2a.3 (2/44), 3 C.2a1b.2a.2a.3b (2/24), and 3 C.2a1b.2a.2a.3a.1 (40/44). Clade 3 C.2a1b.2a.2a.3 sequences primarily grouped with those from Cameroon, clade 3 C.2a1b.2a.2a.3b with the UAE and China, and clade 3 C.2a1b.2a.2a.3a.1 with China, Russia, and Australia. All 2024 sequences (12/12) clustered within clade 3 C.2a1b.2a.2a.3a.1, primarily grouping with sequences from China, the UK, and France (Fig. 3). The evolutionary patterns of the other seven gene segments (NA, M, NP, NS, PA, PB1, and PB2) are consistent with those of HA (Figures S1−S7).

Homology analysis of influenza A/H3N2 viruses

The homology of Jiaxing sequences from 2019 to 2024 was analyzed by comparison with the Northern Hemisphere influenza A/H3N2 vaccine strain A/Kansas/14/2017. The HA homology results show that the 2019–2020 sequences were concentrated at 96% − 97%, while the 2022–2024 sequences were concentrated at 94% − 96% (Fig. 4A). The homology of NA sequences was 96%–97% during 2019–2020 and 94%–96% during 2022–2024 (Fig. 4A). Meanwhile, the HA sequence homology was significantly lower during 2023–2024 than during 2019–2020, with statistical significance (p < 0.001).

Amino acid mutation analyses of influenza A/H3N2 virus

Compared to the Northern Hemisphere influenza A/H3N2 vaccine strain A/Kansas/14/2017, the HA antigenic epitopes of influenza A/H3N2 viruses from 2019 to 2024 exhibited multiple mutations. In most influenza A/H3N2 viruses in 2019, 20 amino acid substitutions were identified, with 14 located at antigenic sites (E62G, N91S, K92R, N121K, A128T, T131K, T135K, S137F, S138A, K144S, S159Y, K160T, N171K, S193F). In 2020, most influenza A/H3N2 viruses underwent 14 amino acid substitutions, with 10 occurring at antigenic sites. Unlike the 2019 mutations, those in 2020 did not include T131K, S138A, A128T and S193F. In most influenza A/H3N2 viruses in 2022, 22 amino acid substitutions were identified, with 15 located at antigenic sites (I48T, E62G, K83E, N91S, K92R, Y94N, N121K, A128T, T131K, S138A, K144S, S159Y, K160T, G186S, S198P). The 2023 influenza A/H3N2 viruses exhibited 34 mutations, the highest number observed, of which 23 were situated within antigenic epitopes. Relative to 2022, there was an increase of 13 mutations in antigenic epitopes in 2023 (N122D, I140K, H156S, S159N, K160I, G186D, D190N, I192F, E50K, D53N, K276E, N963, N171K). In 2024, the majority of influenza A/H3N2 viruses underwent 33 amino acid substitutions, with 20 occurring within antigenic epitopes, a decrease of I140K, E50K, and D53N compared to 2023. Amino acid mutation sites in the HA of influenza A/H3N2 virus in Jiaxing during 2019–2024

The receptor binding site (RBS) of influenza A/H3N2 virus is predominantly situated within the globular head domain of its surface glycoprotein hemagglutinin (HA), constituting a pocket lined by structural elements including the 130-loop, and 150-loop, 220-loop and 190-helix [24]. Compared to the vaccine strain A/Kansas/14/2017, the T135K, S137F, S138A, N158K, S195Y, D190N, I192T, and S193F mutations were identified in H3N2 strains circulating in Jiaxing between 2019 and 2020; whereas S138A, H156S, S159N, D190N, I192F, Y195F, S198P, and I223V mutations emerged in H3N2 strains during 2022–2024. HA’s ability to agglutinate red blood cells is associated with its receptor-binding function. Compared with the 2019–2020 H3N2 strains, the overwhelming majority of 2022–2024 strains have acquired mutations at key receptor-binding residues 156, 159, 190 and 192 (Table S4).

Compared to 2019–2022, all 2023–2024 influenza A/H3N2 virus sequences of Jiaxing exhibited 9 distinct amino acid substitutions, with 7 were located within antigenic epitopes (H156S, Y159N, T160I, S186D, D190N, I192F, N96S) (Table 1). Five representative sequences spanning 2019 to 2024 (A/Zhejiang-NH/118/2019, A/Zhejiang-NH4/122/2020, A/Zhejiang-NH/1650/2022, A/Zhejiang-NH/1276/2023, and A/Zhejiang-NH/123/2024) were chosen for Blast analysis in GISAID. The top 100 sequences from various countries were downloaded for further analysis, generating a total of 500 sequences. When compared to the 300 sequences from 2019 to 2021, the 200 sequences from 2023 to 2024 also showed the previously observed seven amino acid mutations. The mutations H156S, Y159N, T160I, D190N, I192F, and N96S were observed with a 100% frequency, whereas S186D had a frequency of 98.5% (Table S3).

The NA protein is associated with drug resistance to influenza [25] and changes in the active site (R118, E119, D151, R152, R156, W178, S179, D/N198, I222, R224, E227, H274, E276, E277, R292, N294, R371, Y406 and E425) can lead to resistance to neuraminidase inhibitors, such as zanamivir, oseltamivir, laninamivir, and peramivir [26, 27]. To determine the frequency of drug-resistant amino acid mutations in NA proteins, we analyzed 117 Jiaxing strains. Compared to the vaccine strain A/Kansas/14/2017, seven strains (A/Zhejiang-NH/1810/2023, A/Zhejiang-NH/11110/2023, A/Zhejiang-NH/1979/2023, A/Zhejiang-NH/11119/2023, A/Zhejiang-NH/1756/2023, A/Zhejiang-NH/1777/2023, and A/Zhejiang-NH/1831/2023) exhibited the I222V substitution.

3.6 Estimation of vaccine efficacy for influenza A/H3N2 viruses.

Previous studies have defined the amino acid residues in five epitopes (A-E) of HA, containing 19, 21, 27, 41, and 22 amino acids respectively [23]. Using a validated mathematical model, we performed modular analysis of amino acid mutations in HA to evaluate predicted vaccine efficacy against influenza A/H3N2 strains of Jiaxing from 2019 to 2024 [28]. The results showed that in the 2019 strains, the vaccine strain A/Kansas/14/2017 had low effectiveness against antigenic epitopes A, B, and E (−13% to 34%) but higher effectiveness against epitopes C and D (42% to 54%). In the 2020 strains, amino acid mutations of HA only exhibited in epitopes A and B. The vaccine strain A/Hong Kong/2671/2019 showed relatively high effectiveness against these strains (19% to 50%). Compared with the vaccine strain A/Darwin/6/2021, the predicted vaccine effectiveness against the 2022 strains showed a significant decrease in epitope B (−13% to −29%) but remained relatively high in epitopes A, C, and D (41% to 58%). The predicted effectiveness of vaccine strains A/Darwin/6/2021 and A/Massachusetts/18/2022 against Jiaxing strains in 2023 and 2024 ranged from 29% to 54%, indicating a relatively high level of protective efficacy. Discussion

Influenza A/H3N2 virus was a novel influenza virus first identified in Hong Kong in 1968 [29]. It had seasonal transmission characteristics and caused numerous epidemics and significant mortality rates [10]. Jiaxing’s influenza A/H3N2 virus exhibited pronounced seasonality, with six peaks between 2019 and 2024. However, the influenza A/H3N2 virus was not detected from March 2020 to May 2022 (Fig. 1A). After the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, the seasonal transmission of respiratory viruses was temporarily disrupted [30, 31]. Consequently, the prevalence of influenza A/H3N2 virus in Jiaxing became irregular post-2019. After COVID-19 containment measures were lifted in 2022, influenza A/H3N2 virus infection cases rose sharply, far surpassing 2019 levels (Fig. 1B). This pattern was not specific to Jiaxing but reflected the dominant trend in the global spread of influenza A/H3N2 virus annually [31].

The influenza virus is capable of inducing the agglutination of human red blood cells [19]. Our research findings from the hemagglutination test revealed a notable enhancement in the hemagglutination capacity of influenza A/H3N2 virus during 2022 and 2024 (Fig. 2). Regrettably, the hemagglutination inhibition test was not conducted in our study. However, similar findings were reported in the researches by Jiaming Li, Xue Zhao, and their team. Their researches showed that the influenza A/H3N2 virus during 2022 and 2023 demonstrated elevated hemagglutination inhibition titers and an increased proportion of protected donors [7, 32]. The increased hemagglutination capacity of influenza A/H3N2 virus might enhance its ability to spread among humans, though direct evidence supporting this is currently lacking.

Phylogenetic analysis showed that the influenza A/H3N2 viruses circulating in Jiaxing between 2019 and 2024 were mainly classified into seven clades (Fig. 3). All seven clades were classified under clade 3c.2a, and no lineages from the 3c.1 series were detected. Clade 3c.2a, initially identified in the 2014/2015 influenza season, was a dominant epidemic branch of influenza A/H3N2 virus [12]. Compared to 2019–2021, the influenza A/H3N2 viruses isolated in Jiaxing during 2022–2024 exhibited a more significant evolutionary distance. These findings align with the homology analysis results of influenza A/H3N2 virus during 2019–2024. When compared to the vaccine strain A/Kansas/14/2017, the 2022–2024 influenza A/H3N2 virus showed reduced similarity (Fig. 4). This suggested that the influenza A/H3N2 virus exhibited clear signs of evolution post-2022. In Jiaxing, the influenza A/H3N2 virus from 2019 to 2020 predominantly belonged to clade 3 C.2a1b.1a/1b/2, whereas the viruses from 2022 to 2024 were largely found in clades 3 C.2a1b.2a.1a and 3 C.2a1b.2a.2a.3/3b/3a.1. This pattern aligned with the evolutionary clades of influenza A/H3N2 viruses observed in other cities in China or in other countries [9, 31–34]. This suggests that while the transmission of influenza A/H3N2 virus was impacted by COVID-19, its pace of evolution stayed uniform in various countries.

HA is a receptor binding protein of influenza virus, playing a crucial role in virulence and antigenic variation [35]. In comparison with the vaccine strain A/Kansas/14/2017, all influenza A/H3N2 virus isolates from Jiaxing between 2019 and 2024 showed over 10 amino acid changes in the five antigenic epitopes of HA (Table 1). The presence of four or more amino acid changes in two or more antigenic sites simultaneously can predict antigenic drift in influenza viruses [36, 37]. This suggests that the antigenicity of influenza A(H3N2) strains from Jiaxing may have altered. In influenza A(H3N2) viruses, the T135A substitution is associated with loss of a glycosylation site, whereas T131K and T135K substitutions can alter HA antigenicity, reducing vaccine effectiveness [38, 39]. The F193S substitution in HA reduces viral neutralization by antisera against A/Hong Kong/4801/2014-like strains, while N122D abolishes a potential N-glycosylation site [40]. The amino acid mutations detected in HA of influenza A(H3N2) viruses from Jiaxing show substantial concordance with those reported in a related Italian study [41]. Phylogenetic analysis confirms that the high-frequency mutations observed constitute hallmark substitutions for clade progression, exemplified by N121K as the characteristic substitution for clade 3 C.2a1 and S144K for clade 3 C.2a2 [42].

Compared to 2019–2022 viruses, four novel mutations (H156S, Y159N, D190N, I192F) in the receptor-binding site were identified in Jiaxing-derived influenza A(H3N2) viruses during 2023–2024, which may be associated with enhanced hemagglutination capacity. Human influenza viruses preferentially bind to sialic acid (SA) linked to galactose via α2,6-linkages, and can bind up to six SA molecules [20]. Amino acid substitutions at residue 159 of HA play a significant role in receptor selectivity of influenza A(H3N2) viruses. Following the 1968 H3N2 pandemic emergence, HA residue 159 was initially conserved as serine (S), evolving around 1980 to tyrosine (Y) or structurally similar phenylalanine (F), and remained conserved thereafter [43]. In 2022, however, a novel transition to asparagine (N) emerged at this position [41]. Comparative analysis of HA amino acid sequences between clades 3 C.2a and 3 C.3a revealed that the evolutionary transition from S159 to variants F159 or Y159 substantially reduces HA binding affinity to shorter sialylated receptors containing only one or two linear LacNAc repeats, while conferring selective preference for elongated receptors [43]. Therefore, we hypothesize that the emergence of Y159N substitution contributes to enhanced hemagglutination capacity in influenza A(H3N2) viruses, with this mechanistic relationship experimentally validated in Bolton et al.‘s study [44].

The numerous amino acid substitutions may have altered the antigenicity of the circulating influenza A/H3N2 virus strains in Jiaxing. Therefore, we assessed the vaccine efficacy for influenza A/H3N2 virus in Jiaxing during 2019–2024 using a validated mathematical model [28]. In comparison with the yearly influenza vaccine strains, the estimated vaccine efficacy in Jiaxing during 2019 and 2022 was minimal, with negative values observed, suggesting a complete lack of protection. On the other hand, the estimated vaccine efficacy for 2020, 2023, and 2024 demonstrated that the vaccines for those years were highly protective. Nevertheless, if the prevalent strains in 2025 further evolve within the 3 C.2a1b.2a.2a.3a.1 clade, the vaccine formulation must be updated in a timely manner.

Neuraminidase inhibitors are among the drugs employed for influenza treatment. In the NA protein of the 2023 Jiaxing strains, seven isolates carrying the I222V substitution were detected. It has been demonstrated that this substitution increases oseltamivir resistance in influenza A viruses that harbor the E119V mutation [45, 46]. Furthermore, mutations at position 222, such as I222L, combined with E119V, have demonstrated cooperative resistance in avian influenza H7N9 virus [47]. Although the I222V and E119V mutation combination can synergistically enhance oseltamivir resistance in influenza A/H3N2 virus, only the I222V mutation was identified in Jiaxing isolates, and no E119V substitution was found. Since a single I222V change alone does not confer oseltamivir resistance in influenza A/H3N2 virus, no oseltamivir resistance was detected in Jiaxing’s influenza A/H3N2 virus. Prior research indicates that the S31N amino acid substitution in the M2 protein leads to amantadine resistance in influenza A/H3N2 virus [48, 49]. In all influenza A/H3N2 viruses in Jiaxing, the M2 protein carries the S31N substitution, so amantadine should be avoided in treatment.

We detected seven novel mutations in the HA antigenic sites of the 2023 and 2024 influenza A/H3N2 viruses, which are globally applicable to influenza A/H3N2 viruses and arise from natural evolutionary processes. Based on the hemagglutination test results, we found that the emergence of these new mutations aligns closely with the timing of increased hemagglutination capacity in influenza A/H3N2 viruses. Thus, we pose an interesting question: Could the increase in hemagglutination capacity of influenza A/H3N2 viruses be associated with these newly identified amino acid replacements? After searching PubMed, we found no research discussing the relationship between these amino acids and the hemagglutination capacity of influenza A/H3N2 viruses. However, we found a study focusing on the D190N substitution in influenza A (H1N1) pdm09 viruses. The study revealed a relationship between the decreased virulence of low-virulence escape mutants linked to the D190N substitution in hemagglutinin and the elevated hemagglutination titers for inhibitors in normal mouse serum [50]. Regarding the question we posed, no clear answer exists at present, and we plan to explore it further in subsequent experiments.

Conclusion

In summary, this research uncovers the genetic evolution of influenza A/H3N2 virus in Jiaxing between 2019 and 2024, highlighting a notable increase in hemagglutination capacity during 2023–2024. In addition, it emphasizes the importance of global influenza virus monitoring and promotes the discovery of new variants.

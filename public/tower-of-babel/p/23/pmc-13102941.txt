A BP neural network model for intelligent quality monitoring of industry-education integration and talent cultivation
Authors: Yi Chen, Jing Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo achieve process-oriented and quantitative quality monitoring of the effectiveness of industry-education integration in universities, this study proposes a quality evaluation method based on the Back Propagation neural network (BPNN). The method conducts predictive modeling and performance verification for different types of university-enterprise cooperation projects in universities. Based on the Input-Process-Output (IPO) evaluation framework, this study constructs a multi-dimensional indicator system and develops an enhanced Back Propagation Neural Network (BPNN) model to improve its robustness and adaptability in heterogeneous educational data. The results show that the constructed BPNN achieves a mean squared error (MSE) of 0.0114 and a coefficient of determination (R²) of 0.947 on the validation set. Compared with the LR model, its R² is improved by 41.2%, ranking the best-performing model among all comparative models. Moreover, it maintains stable predictive performance with R² > 0.93 across eight professional subsets. The research indicates that the BPNN can effectively capture the complex relationships among multi-dimensional educational indicators and is suitable for quality evaluation tasks of industry-education integration in multiple scenarios of universities. This approach provides a robust, intelligent tool for enhancing the dynamic management and continuous improvement of industry-education integration initiatives in higher education.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-43967-x.Keywords: Industry-education integration, BP neural network, Quality evaluation, Intelligent monitoring, Higher educationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Currently, digital technologies are accelerating the reconstruction of the higher education ecosystem, driving profound transformations in the education governance system and talent cultivation models. Against the backdrop of the continuous advancement of the national strategies for industry-education integration and science-education integration, in-depth enterprise participation, collaborative project development, and the output of practical talents have gradually become important evaluation dimensions for the teaching quality of higher education1. Industry-education integration is a strategic approach to address the structural imbalance of educational resources and enhance the ability of education to serve industries. It is also a key support for achieving accurate alignment between education quality and social needs in the construction of “Double First-Class” universities2,3. However, how to scientifically evaluate the actual effectiveness of industry-education integration, especially conducting data-driven and intelligent monitoring of the talent cultivation effects throughout its entire process, has become a practical challenge in the construction of university quality assurance systems4.

The existing quality monitoring mechanisms have significant limitations in practice. On one hand, the evaluation methods still mainly rely on ex-post review, such as regular spot checks and achievement filing. They lack real-time performance and systematicness, making it difficult to meet the needs of dynamic management, control, and feedback optimization in the teaching process5,6. On the other hand, most universities rely on subjective experience or single indicators (e.g., employment rate, enterprise satisfaction) in the evaluation of industry-education integration. These approaches cannot fully reflect the actual effectiveness of university-enterprise collaboration in links such as curriculum co-construction, teacher exchange, and competency development7,8.

Based on this, this study proposes a quality monitoring model based on the Back Propagation neural network (BPNN). It aims to achieve accurate prediction and interpretation of the quality of industry-education integration projects and their talent cultivation effects by constructing a systematic indicator system and an adaptive modeling mechanism. In this study, five categories of core indicators are designed, covering teaching resources, curriculum systems, enterprise participation, teacher collaboration, and student competency output. The performance of the BPNN is optimized through confidence-weighted training and dynamic structural adjustment. The model is experimentally verified on 642 samples of actual university-enterprise projects and compared with four types of mainstream regression models. The structure of this study is as follows: Sect. 2 reviews relevant literature on industry-education integration evaluation research and neural network methods. Section 3 introduces the construction of the indicator system and the design of the BPNN. Section 4 presents the model experiment process and performance evaluation. Section 5 summarizes the research contributions and proposes future research directions.

Literature review

From the perspective of higher education quality assurance, the integration of industry, academia, and research is widely recognized as a key mechanism for aligning learning outcomes with labor market and social demands, particularly within frameworks such as Work-Integrated Learning (WIL) and Outcome-Based Education (OBE). Recent studies on Work-Integrated Learning emphasize that quality assurance for industry-academia-research collaboration requires policy coordination and systematic and operable evaluation mechanisms. For instance, Green et al. (2024) pointed out that an effective quality assurance framework for Work-Integrated Learning should integrate inputs, learning processes, and outcomes to support continuous monitoring and institutional decision-making9. In recent years, with the in-depth implementation of the national policies on “industry-education integration and science-education integration”, the demand for evaluating the effectiveness of integration from both the education and industry sectors has become increasingly urgent10,11. Amini et al. (2020) constructed a three-dimensional evaluation model of “resource-process-result” based on the collaborative data of universities and enterprises. They used the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) to assign weights to indicators and concluded that the effect of industry-education integration was positively correlated with the depth of university-enterprise interaction12. Chen et al. (2021) applied the fuzzy comprehensive evaluation method to conduct a quantitative assessment of industry-education integration projects in a university. They pointed out that teachers’ practical experience in enterprises and the degree of joint curriculum development were key influencing factors13. From the perspective of OBE, recent studies have shown that measurable learning outcomes and competency development are receiving increasing attention and are regarded as core elements of higher education quality assurance. Mahrishi et al. (2025) argue that the effective implementation of OBE required systematic monitoring of multi-dimensional learning outcomes, which further underscored the necessity of constructing a comprehensive, data-driven evaluation framework14.

Against this educational backdrop, data-driven and intelligent methods are increasingly being introduced as auxiliary tools to address the inherent complexity and nonlinearity in higher education quality assessment. Within the contexts of Work-Integrated Learning and Outcome-Based Education, assessment has emerged as a core challenge for quality assurance. A recent systematic review conducted by Luk and Chan (2024)15 indicated that existing WIL assessment practices tended to rely on fragmented indicators or retrospective evaluations, which limited their capacity to support continuous quality improvement and data-driven interventions. In terms of educational evaluation methodology, artificial neural networks have gradually been introduced into university evaluation tasks due to their strong nonlinear modeling capabilities16,17. Among them, the BP algorithm, as a basic deep learning method, has been initially applied in fields such as teacher performance evaluation, student academic achievement prediction, and curriculum matching degree assessment18. Basnet (2024) introduced an improved BP network into the prediction of graduate employment quality. By combining principal component analysis to reduce the dimensionality of input features, he improved the model’s training efficiency and generalization performance19. Huang et al. (2025) integrated the BP algorithm with the fuzzy evaluation method to quantitatively assess the comprehensive quality and capabilities of talents20.

Compared with existing studies, this research makes several unique contributions to the literature on the quality evaluation of industry-education integration. Previous studies usually focus on the construction of static indicators, single-stage evaluation, or the application of neural networks as prediction tools, and tend to treat these elements in isolation. In contrast, this study combines a multi-dimensional, process-oriented indicator system with an enhanced Back Propagation Neural Network (BPNN) framework, and integrates confidence-weighted training, entropy-driven structural adaptation, and SHAP-based interpretability into a unified model. This integrated design enables accurate quality prediction, and provides transparent interpretation of indicator contributions, which is particularly important for educational governance and decision-making. By integrating indicator system design, model optimization and interpretability into a single framework, this study promotes the transformation of existing research from method-oriented applications to a more comprehensive and governance-oriented evaluation methodology.

The construction of the 26-item indicator system follows a structured design logic, rather than simply listing administratively available indicators. First, these indicators are organized into five conceptual dimensions, teaching input, faculty collaboration, curriculum co-development, enterprise participation, and student competency development, which are consistent with established perspectives in fields such as higher education quality assurance, Work-Integrated Learning (WIL), and Outcome-Based Education (OBE). This dimensional structure ensures construct-level coherence and theoretical relevance. Second, within each dimension, the selection of indicators aims to capture the complementary aspects of inputs, processes, and outputs, thereby reducing functional redundancy and the over-representation of single operational factors. Third, instead of assigning subjective or expert-defined weights to the indicators, this study adopts a data-driven learning strategy, enabling the BPNN to directly infer the relative importance of indicators from observed data patterns. This choice enhances the adaptability of the indicator system across different program contexts and avoids biases that may arise from manual weighting schemes. Finally, potential correlations among indicators are addressed during the modeling phase. The nonlinear representation learning capability of the BPNN transforms correlated inputs into higher-level feature representations. Therefore, this indicator system is designed to support predictive validity and monitoring effectiveness, rather than establishing causal relationships between individual variables.

In the industry-education integration data, there are quality differences among different data sources (e.g., university feedback, enterprise evaluation, student self-assessment). Traditional BP training treats all samples equally and fails to reflect the differences in sample credibility34,35.

Within the framework of this study, each improved module undertakes a distinct functional role in the enhancement of model performance. Among them, the sample confidence-based weighted learning mechanism primarily acts on the stability and robustness of the training phase: by reducing the interference of low-confidence samples on gradient updates, it effectively mitigates the problem of noise accumulation under heterogeneous data conditions. The entropy-driven structure evaluation mechanism is applied in the pre-training phase to guide network scale selection, thus avoiding underfitting or overfitting caused by inappropriate structural settings. The SHAP method is mainly used for interpretive analysis in the model output phase. Its role is not to directly improve prediction accuracy, but to enhance the interpretability of the model decision-making process and the credibility of practical application. These modules are functionally complementary to one another, jointly forming a complete modeling scheme tailored for the quality monitoring scenario.

In this study, information entropy is applied in the structural evaluation phase before training, rather than being calculated iteratively during the training process. Specifically, prior to the start of model training, the information entropy of the overall feature distribution is first calculated based on standardized input samples to characterize the complexity of the current dataset. This entropy value is used as a reference index for structural selection to determine the appropriate network structure scale within the preset candidate range of hidden layer sizes. The 64 hidden layer nodes reported in this paper do not change dynamically during the training process, but represent a structural configuration with stable performance in multiple experiments based on this entropy-driven evaluation mechanism. Therefore, the network structure remains fixed during a single training process, while its scale selection process is guided by data complexity, thus achieving a certain level of structural adaptability in different data scenarios.

Experimental design and performance evaluation

Datasets collection

The data in this study are mainly sourced from the following three channels: (1) University teaching quality monitoring system: Data are retrieved from the industry-education integration project database (2018–2023) of a “Double First-Class” university, including structural data such as teaching input, faculty collaboration, and curriculum design. (2) Enterprise participation feedback system: Data include annual feedback from 36 key enterprises cooperating with the university, covering dimensions such as internship arrangement, curriculum co-construction, mentor guidance, and satisfaction. (3) Graduate development tracking survey system: Data are jointly collected by the university-level employment center and a third-party research institution, including outcome-based data of graduates within three years of graduation, such as employment, entrepreneurship, promotion, and job matching.

Sample unit: The “university-enterprise cooperation project” is taken as the minimum analysis unit, with each sample representing a complete cooperation cycle (1 year) of industry-education integration. Total sample size: A total of 642 valid project cycle samples is collected and organized, covering the time span from 2018 to 2023. Covered majors: The samples involve 6 engineering majors (Mechanical Engineering, Electronic Engineering, Software Engineering, Artificial Intelligence, Data Science, and Industrial Design) and 2 management majors (Business Administration and Marketing). Feature completeness: Each sample is matched with the aforementioned five categories of indicators (26 indicators in total), with a missing rate of less than 3%. These samples meet the basic conditions for training neural networks.

Before being imported into the neural network model, the raw data underwent the following preprocessing operations: Samples with missing values exceeding 40% were deleted, totaling 17 excluded samples. Z-score standardization was performed on integer, continuous, and percentage indicators. Min-max normalization was applied to graded scoring indicators. All data were finally organized into X∈ℝ625 × 26, corresponding to the target quality scores of y∈[0,1].

All models are trained with default parameters and optimized via Grid Search when necessary.

Discussion

The experimental results show that the BPNN outperforms linear models, tree-based models and support vector regression models in the task of industry-education integration quality prediction. This advantage mainly stems from the high matching between the model structure and data characteristics. The industry-education integration quality data exhibits obvious nonlinear relationships and multi-factor interaction features. For example, the relationship between “enterprise mentor participation frequency” and “graduate job matching degree” is not a simple linear correlation, but is jointly affected by multiple factors such as faculty allocation, curriculum structure and enterprise type. Compared with traditional regression models, the BPNN is more suitable for characterizing such complex relationships, thus achieving more accurate quality prediction. The 26-index system constructed in this study covers the complete chain of “input-process-result”, with characteristics such as multi-dimension and inconsistent scales. The multi-layer perceptron structure of the BPNN combined with the ReLU activation function effectively alleviates the instability of the training process caused by high-dimensional heterogeneous features. At the same time, the introduced sample confidence weighting mechanism reduces the interference of low-confidence samples on parameter updates in the training phase, enabling the model to learn stable mapping relationships more from high-quality samples, thereby improving the overall prediction reliability. The information entropy-based structure evaluation mechanism guides the selection of hidden layer size in the pre-training phase, matching the model capacity with data complexity. This design helps avoid overfitting caused by an excessively large structure or limited fitting ability due to an insufficient structure. Combined with the L2 regularization and Early Stopping strategy, the model demonstrates stable prediction performance across subsets of different professional directions and cooperation types, reflecting its good adaptability under heterogeneous sample conditions. Beyond prediction accuracy, the proposed integrated modeling framework also holds significant implications for the practice of educational governance and quality assurance. Through joint modeling of multi-dimensional indicators and interpretive analysis of prediction results using the SHAP method, the model can identify key dimensions that contribute greatly to quality scores, providing managers with interpretable and traceable decision-making references. This combined “prediction-interpretation” approach helps transform quality assessment from post-hoc statistics to continuous monitoring and diagnostic support. This study aims at quality prediction and monitoring, rather than attempting to establish causal relationships between indicators. The model output results are used to support comparative analysis and risk identification among different projects, and their value lies in providing data basis for subsequent management decisions and teaching improvement, rather than directly deriving causal policy conclusions. By combining prediction results with interpretable analysis, this study seeks to enhance the operability of industry-education integration quality assessment in practical applications. The conclusions of this study are based on the data context of a Double First-Class university, belonging to a proof-of-concept study under specific institutional and policy backgrounds, and its results should not be directly generalized to universities of different types or regional contexts. When deploying such systems in practice, attention should also be paid to the sustainability of data collection, the regular model update mechanism, and the organizational acceptance of model interpretation results. Only on the premise of full integration into the existing quality assurance system can this framework better exert its supporting role in educational governance.

Conclusion

Research contribution

This study makes multiple contributions to the research and practice of quality evaluation for industry-education integration. First, it constructs a multi-dimensional, process-oriented indicator system that covers outcome indicators and incorporates key input and collaborative processes such as teaching, enterprise participation, and student development. Second, by integrating confidence-weighted learning and entropy-driven structural adaptation, it builds an enhanced Back Propagation Neural Network (BPNN) framework, which improves the robustness of the model in processing heterogeneous educational data. Third, the SHAP-based interpretability enables transparent identification of the contribution of each indicator, overcoming the common limitation of black-box prediction models in the field of education. In summary, these contributions go beyond the isolated application of methodologies, providing a coherent, interpretable, and decision-relevant framework for intelligent quality monitoring in higher education.

Future works and research limitations

This study still has certain limitations. First, the source of data samples is relatively concentrated, mainly based on the internal project records of a single university, lacking horizontal verification across multiple regions and types of universities. Second, although the model is equipped with an interpretability mechanism, the decision recommendation link still relies on rule settings and lacks the ability to generate higher-level autonomous strategies. The single-institution nature of the dataset limits the direct transferability of the model to other institutional contexts. This is due to differences in governance structures, data availability, and industry collaboration models across institutions. Future research can further verify the adaptability of the model structure and indicator system based on multi-university and multi-regional samples. Future work will focus on linking prediction results to actionable intervention mechanisms. Examples include developing rule-based recommendation engines and adaptive quality improvement pathways. By integrating model outputs with targeted teaching and collaboration strategies, the framework can be extended from monitoring to guided decision support.

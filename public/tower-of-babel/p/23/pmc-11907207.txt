Senescence-associated secretory phenotype regulation by dual-drug delivery biomimetic nanoplatform for enhanced tumor chemotherapy
Authors: Ajay Prakash, Travis Gates, Emil Lou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractWang and colleagues provide a comprehensive evaluation of a biomimetic platform in which a specially designed drug delivery system, referred to in the manuscript as mPtP@Lipo, is used to increased penetration of dense stromatous tumors. This work represents a significant advancement in the treatment of desmoplastic tumors by targeting the tumor microenvironment (TME), particularly focusing on remodeling cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs). The study leverages previous work, which has demonstrated that platinum (Pt)-based chemotherapeutic regimens can induce a senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) through persistent DNA damage, which can contribute to an immunosuppressive tumor immune microenvironment.

Main text

Leveraging the innovative multi-drug liposomal delivery system mPtP@Lipo, the authors have demonstrated improved tumor penetrance and remodeling of the tumor microenvironment (TME) through modulation of a cancer-associated fibroblast (CAF)-induced senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP).1 This strategy fills an important niche, as it provides a new approach for overcoming limitations of current therapies. Specifically, a major focus is increasing the penetration of tumors, especially those that come in the form of dense stromatous desmoplastic reactions, which are traditionally thought to have increased stiffness and are especially hard for infused drugs to penetrate. The authors show that utilizing an anti-inflammatory ginsenoside, specifically ginsenoside 20(S)-protopanaxadiol (PPD), was sufficient to inhibit CAF activation queried by an abundance of α-smooth muscle actins (α-SMAs) in NIH3T3 cells in vitro. Furthermore, mPtP@Lipo significantly reduced tumor growth in a syngeneic, subcutaneous model with LLC lung cancer cells co-implanted with NIH3T3 cells. The authors show that mPtP@Lipo was capable of reducing the formation of the SASP phenotype, decreasing drug resistance to Pt-based chemotherapy, to yield a more immunogenic tumor immune microenvironment (TIME).

TME and CAFs: Clinical implications

The TME, composed of tumor cells, stromal cells, lymphocytes, and extracellular matrix (ECM), plays a pivotal role in tumor progression and resistance to therapy.2 Among the stromal components, CAFs are crucial due to their high prevalence and ability to influence tumor growth, metastasis, and immune evasion.3 The study highlights that CAFs, which constitute a significant portion of the tumor stroma, contribute to the formation of a physical barrier around tumor vessels, thus impeding the efficacy of therapeutic agents and exacerbating immune suppression. This insight aligns with the growing recognition of the TME’s role in mediating resistance to conventional therapies, making CAFs an attractive target for novel therapeutic strategies.

Novel drug delivery systems: enhanced tumor penetration and efficacy

The investigators demonstrated the ability of mPtP@Lipo to enhance drug delivery into dense tumors. By utilizing PPD to inhibit the CAF-induced SASP phenotype, this molecule allows for improved tumor penetration and effect of the platinum payload. In the study, a platinum (Pt) prodrug (PtLA) was enveloped and activated in vivo, with Pt release increasing to 80% in a redox environment as compared to 20% Pt release under control conditions; this activation resulted in deeper penetration of the drug and extensive apoptosis. This approach may potentially address multiple overlapping limitations of current therapies. First, the prevention of the SASP phenotype may allow for continued sensitivity to cell-cycle-disruptive chemotherapeutic agents. Second, this approach may resolve previously inadequate drug delivery to the tumor core due to TME-induced barriers. Improved penetration, targeted delivery, and prolonged treatment response can lead to more effective treatment regimens, reduced side effects, and enhanced overall therapeutic outcomes.

Combination therapy: Targeting SASP and immune modulation

The study also reveals the potential of mPtP@Lipo to enhance anti-tumor immunity. The combination of PPD with cisplatin improves the maturation of dendritic cells (DCs) and the activation of T cells, crucial components of the immune response. This finding underscores a significant advancement in integrating chemotherapeutic and immunotherapeutic strategies. By modulating the TME and enhancing immune cell function, mPtP@Lipo could potentially address one of the major challenges in cancer therapy: the tumor’s ability to evade immune detection and destruction.4

Clinical relevance and future directions

The promising results from this study suggest several clinical implications. First, the ability to enhance drug delivery and reduce TME-induced drug resistance could significantly improve patient outcomes in the treatment of desmoplastic tumors and other cancer types with a similar TME composition; pancreatic adenocarcinoma is a prototype for this type of cancer and represents one that should be at the forefront of consideration if this work is translated into clinical trial evaluation in human patients. Additionally, the combination of conventional and natural-product-based therapies presents a novel approach that would be unique to explore in the clinical trial setting as well, as compared to the current landscape of therapeutic strategies being applied for pancreatic and similar tumor types.

Future studies should take into consideration how tumor-intrinsic factors, such as extracellular vesicles, are influenced by platinum-based chemotherapies, which may unravel further mechanistic understanding of chemotherapy-induced senescence. Furthermore, investigations should look at other classes of chemotherapies that are frequently utilized for broader clinical translation. Finally, while the authors effectively showed differences in the immune response following administration with mPtP@Lipo in a subcutaneous tumor-bearing animal model, other literature suggests that the tumor location greatly influences the immune response.5 Future studies should aim to confirm these findings and TIME regulatory effects in orthotopic animal models to ascertain TIME modulation in an appropriate location. Finally, lung cancer is considered to be immunogenic. Future studies should investigate the efficacy of mPtP@Lipo in immunosuppressed cancer subtypes. Of note, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma would be particularly interesting due to the role of a largely immunosuppressed microenvironment that is supported by dense fibroblast infiltrates, which contribute to therapeutic resistance and immune suppression.

Limitations of the study

In this study, only Pt-based chemotherapeutic regimens are assessed; future studies should assess other chemotherapeutic classes that are capable of inducing persistent double-stranded breaks and the SASP. Furthermore, the study relies on the modulation of tumor growth in a subcutaneous LLC-NIH3T3 co-implantation model. While this is an innovative approach, the study does not consider differences between the lung TIME and the subcutaneous TIME. Future studies should also assess the bioavailability and efficacy of mPtP@Lipo in the orthotopic lung environment, which will more effectively address the modulation of CAFs and the TIME in lung cancer and will deepen our understanding of dual-drug delivery approaches that aim to overcome SASP and Pt chemotherapy resistance.

In summary, the study’s integration of advanced drug delivery systems with targeted TME modulation represents a significant step forward in oncology. By addressing key challenges in drug delivery and immune evasion, this approach could pave the way for more effective and personalized cancer therapies.

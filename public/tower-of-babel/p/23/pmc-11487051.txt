Impact of urinary catheters including reusable catheters on quality of life and work productivity in persons with spinal cord lesion
Authors: Noritoshi Sekido, Fujio Matsuyama, Tatsunori Murata, Ryosuke Takahashi, Mihoko Matsuoka, Atsushi Sengoku, Masashi Nomi, Takeya Kitta, Takahiko Mitsui
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractWhile preventing renal damage and symptomatic urinary tract infection are of paramount importance to persons with a spinal cord lesion (SCL), patient-reported outcomes (PROs) are receiving much more attention than ever before. We performed a cross-sectional internet survey of 282 adult members of SCL patient advocacy groups and investigated, for the first time, the impact of different types of urinary catheter, including a Japanese reusable silicone catheter and a reusable intermittent balloon catheter, on PROs of persons with SCL. Intermittent self-catheterization (ISC) did not surpass continuous indwelling catheterization in terms of a disease-specific urinary quality of life. Urinary incontinence, regular ISC, and performing ISC away from home confronted these individuals. Work productivity and regular activities correlated fairly well with the disease-specific urinary quality of life. Bowel management problems affected all PROs evaluated. The absence of differences in PROs among the types of catheter used for ISC implies that Japanese reusable catheters have the potential to benefit selected persons with SCL. Further research is warranted to develop measures for maximizing work productivity by overcoming urinary as well as bowel problems.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-74578-z.Keywords: Indwelling catheterization, Intermittent catheterization, Neurogenic lower urinary tract dysfunction, Quality of life, Spinal cord lesion, Work productivitySubject terms: Urology, Bladder

Introduction

In persons with a spinal cord lesion (SCL), clean intermittent catheterization (CIC) is the treatment of choice when urinary management by voiding is considered to carry a high risk for renal damage and symptomatic urinary tract infection (sUTI)1. CIC is strongly recommended compared with indwelling catheterization (IDC) in terms of preventing and/or improving renal damage (upper urinary tract deterioration or renal impairment), sUTI, and autonomic dysreflexia1. When CIC is not feasible, IDC is an option as a last resort1. Besides preventing renal damage and sUTI, improving patient-reported outcomes (PROs) including the quality of life (QOL) of persons with SCL is attracting much interest in the field of neurourology2,3.

Types of urinary management significantly affect QOL, of which IDC is the worst4. Persons who are managed with IDC had poorer personal interactions than those using other methods5. Therefore, despite the existence of various obstacles, about half of persons with SCL feel that CIC improved their QOL somewhat, and almost all persons chose CIC over IDC for urinary management6. Specifically, intermittent self-catheterization (ISC) can contribute to an improved QOL, dignity, privacy, and self-esteem7. Although differences in perceived health and satisfaction with life among various types of urinary management were observed, they were small and not consistently worse in those using IDC8. Ultimately, whether CIC is superior to IDC in terms of QOL remains to be determined.

While a recent scoping review showed that a hydrophilic coated catheter (HCC) was favored over an uncoated catheter for satisfaction, preference, and QOL9, a recent network meta-analysis concluded that none of the catheters exhibited significant advantages on a person’s satisfaction compared with a single-use uncoated catheter10. However, to the best of our knowledge, no studies on QOL in persons managed with ISC included a Japanese reusable silicone catheter (RC) and reusable intermittent balloon catheter (i-IDC).

QOL is also influenced by work productivity. The benefits of employment after SCL are multiple, with productivity and employment being consistently associated with life satisfaction, QOL, and adjustment11. Greater ability to work was associated with improvement in QOL in persons with SCL practicing various types of urinary management12. A recent systematic review identified twenty modifiable and twelve non-modifiable factors that were consistently and independently associated with employment outcomes of persons with SCL13. However, urinary management was not included in those factors and its effect on employment outcomes remains to be determined.

In this study, we evaluated the impacts of different types of catheters on QOL and work productivity and explored factors associated with them in persons using ISC and IDC. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to delve deeper into findings of the individual items of a disease-specific QOL survey, namely Qualiveen30, and to investigate work productivity using the Work Productivity and Activity Impairment (WPAI) questionnaire in persons with SCL using urinary catheters.

Methods

This study was a planned secondary analysis of a burden of illness with an internet survey of Japanese persons with SCL for whom performance of ISC was considered feasible. Three post-hoc analyses were planned for this study: topic 1, frequency of sUTI by types of catheter used14; topic 2, association between adherence to ISC and sUTI15,16; and topic 3, PROs by types of catheter used, namely the present study. This study was conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the ethical committee (the nonprofit organization MINS Research Ethics Committee; http://www.npo-mins.com/library/5fcf039bd6164603646daf12/602f7e0c12830e793b214646.pdf).

Methods of survey

The survey methods have been previously reported14. Briefly, a cross-sectional internet survey of adult persons with SCL was conducted in cooperation with the Japan Spinal Cord Foundation, the public interest incorporated foundation Spinal Injuries Japan, and the Spina Bifida Association of Japan. We included persons who had traumatic or non-traumatic SCI or spina bifida, were considered to be able to perform ISC for urinary management, were able to access the internet by themselves, and submitted informed consent. We excluded persons who voided volitionally as the only urinary management, underwent CIC by caregivers, had a urinary diversion, used a ventilator, had acute SCL, or lived outside Japan (see Supplementary Table S1 online for more information). Participants’ consent to set up a questionnaire survey site on the internet was obtained. The questionnaire survey was conducted from September 2020 to October 2020 on a designated website, which included questions on participants’ characteristics, self-reported sUTI, adherence to as well as complications of ISC and IDC, and PROs described below.

Outcomes of this study

The primary outcomes of this post-hoc analysis were domain and overall scores, and individual items of Qualiveen30 by types of catheter. Secondary outcomes included a utility score of EuroQol 5-Dimensions 5-Levels (EQ-5D-5 L), EQ Visual Analogue Scale VAS (EQ VAS), and four WPAI scores by type of catheter.

Types of catheter

The types of catheter targeted in this survey were described in detail elsewhere14. Briefly, IDC, RC, single-use catheter (SC), and i-IDC were included. RC is not a reused SC but a specialized silicone catheter designed to be “reusable”. i-IDC is a unique reusable catheter for persons with nocturnal polyuria or difficulty with ISC when away from home. In Japan, a polyvinyl chloride catheter (PVC) and HCC are available as SC. Although i-IDC and HCC became reimbursable in 2016, only 21 (8.5%) of the participants performing ISC were using HCC at the time of this survey and that is the reason why PVC and HCC were analyzed together as SC.

Evaluation of PROs

PROs were evaluated as described below.

At the time of this study, the Qualiveen30 was the only disease-specific QOL measure with a validated Japanese translation. This questionnaire was developed to measure disease-specific aspects of bother with limitations, frequency limitations, fears, feelings, and generic QOL aspects from which each domain score and overall score were calculated17. On the questionnaire, the lower the score, the better the QOL. Moreover, for each individual item, we calculated the proportion of participants who answered “moderately” to “extremely” (Q1-9 for bother with limitations, Q18-25 for fears, Q26-30 for feelings), and “from time to time” to “always” (Q10-17 for frequency limitations).

The EQ-5D-5 L is a standardized generic measure of health status today and comprises five dimensions, namely, mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression, which are converted to a single summary index, the utility score, by applying a formula that essentially attaches weights to each of the levels in each dimension18. In this measure, the higher the utility score, the better the health status. The EQ VAS captures the person’s overall assessment of their health on a scale from 0 (worst health imaginable) to 100 (best health imaginable), which provides complementary information to the EQ-5D profile18. For example, it is often observed that some people who report no problems in any EQ-5D dimension rate their health as less than 100 on the EQ VAS18.

The WPAI is a specific tool for quantifying the effects of a specific health problem, urinary problems in this case, during the past seven days based on the responses to six items, which consists of Q1, currently employed or not; Q2, hours missed due to health problems; Q3, hours missed for other reasons; Q4, hours actually worked; Q5, the degree that health affected productivity while working; and Q6, the degree that health affected regular activities (usual activities, such as work around the house, shopping, childcare, exercising, and studying)19. Four scores that are multiplied by 100 to express them in percentages are derived from the following equations: (1) the percentage of work time missed due to the specific health problem (absenteeism) = Q2/(Q2 + Q4); (2) the percentage of impairment while working due to the specific health problem (presenteeism) = Q5/10; (3) the percentage of overall work impairment due to the specific health problem = Q2/(Q2 + Q4)+[(1-(Q2/(Q2 + Q4)))x(Q5/10)]; and (4) the percentage of activity impairment due to the specific health problem = Q6/1019,20. The WPAI is the most widely used survey across a large range of diseases and has been subject to rigorous validation in many contexts in many countries19,20. In this measure, the higher the score, the worse the work productivity and activity.

Statistical analysis

The sample size was not calculated because this study was an exploratory one. First, we compared PROs by the different types of catheter, namely the ISC versus IDC groups, the RC versus SC groups, and the i-IDC group versus the ISC only group. The chi-square test for categorical variables and the Mann-Whitney U test for numerical variables were used for statistical analysis. Next, the correlation between WPAI and other PROs was evaluated by Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient (ρ). We considered correlations 0.2 < |ρ| ≤ 0.4, 0.4 < |ρ| ≤ 0.7, and 0.7 < |ρ| ≤ 1.0 as weak, moderate, and strong correlations, respectively. Finally, we performed univariable and multivariable analyses by forward and backward methods to determine factors associated with PROs in participants with ISC and IDC separately. We used the following variables in the analysis: variables related to participants’ demographics, adherence to ISC, and complications of urinary management including self-reported sUTI (see Supplementary Tables S3 and S4 online). The classification of variables was determined through discussion of the research team16 and is briefly presented in the footnotes of each Table. Based on the existing literature21−24 and discussion within our research group, we incorporated the frequency of ISC per day, time required for each ISC, and urinary incontinence (UI) in the ISC group, leakage around the catheter and catheter blockage in the IDC group, and bowel management problems and sUTI in both groups as clinically meaningful variables on multivariable analysis using stepwise variable selection, regardless of statistically significant differences on univariable analysis. Data are shown as mean (SD) or n (%) unless otherwise specified. Statistical analysis was performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute) with p < 0.05 (two-tailed) considered to be statistically significant.

Results

Demographics of participants

A population flow diagram is given as a Supplementary Figure S1 online. Of 282 participants, 247 (87.6%) performed ISC and 35 (12.4%) were managed by IDC (see Supplementary Table S2 online). The mean age of the participants in the ISC group was significantly younger than that in in the IDC group (p = 0.042), and the mean number of years since the onset of SCL were approximately 26 years in both groups. A significantly higher proportion of poor manual dexterity in daily life was seen in the IDC group than in the ISC group (p < 0.001). Of participants in the ISC group, 136 (55.1%) and 111 (44.9%) used RC and SC, respectively, and 45 (18.2%) used i-IDC concomitantly. A significantly higher proportion of wheelchair-bound participants was seen in the RC group than in the SC group (p = 0.003) and in the i-IDC group than in the ICS only group (p < 0.001). The reasons for changes in urinary management and their consequences are shown in Supplementary Table S2 online.

Domain & overall scores of Qualiveen30

Bother with limitations was significantly better in the IDC group than in the ISC group (p = 0.013) and significantly worse in the SC group compared to the RC group (p = 0.019). In addition, the SC group tended to show worse scores than the RC group in fears, feelings, and overall score. Domain scores except for feelings (p = 0.024) and the overall score did not significantly differ between the i-IDC and ISC only groups.

Individual items of Qualiveen30

When examining the individual items of Qualiveen30 (Table 2), the proportion of “from time to time or more often” ranged from 70 to 75% for continence products (Q15 and Q16) and fluid intake (Q17), regardless of the type of catheter used.

The ISC group had a significantly higher proportion of “moderately or more” in several items of the bother with limitations domain than the IDC group, with 45% and 60% of the ISC group in Q4 (dependence on a time table, p = 0.001) and Q7 (away from home or traveling, p = 0.005), respectively.

In the SC group, the proportion of “moderately or more” and “from time to time or more frequently” was higher than the RC group, at approximately 70% in Q7 (away from home and travelling, p = 0.040) and Q13 (regulating your life, p = 0. 032), respectively. Around 55% of this group also rated Q4 (dependence on a timetable, p = 0.003) and Q5 (time spent passing urine or performing catheterization, p < 0.001) as “moderately or more”. In addition, about one-third of this group worried about money problems (Q25) (p = 0.017).

Even with the use of i-IDC, the proportion who answered “from time to time or more” was reported by 60% in giving up going out (Q11, p = 0.036) and 50% in being more dependent on others (Q12, p = 0.005), which were higher than the ISC only group. On the other hand, the proportion of “moderately or more” answers on concealment (Q28) was significantly lower in the i-IDC group than in the ISC only group (p = 0.041).

EQ-5D-5 L & EQ VAS

Overall, the utility score correlated fairly well with EQ VAS scores in the ISC (Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient [ρ] = 0.543, p < 0.001) and IDC (ρ = 0.554, p = 0.001) groups. The utility score was significantly better in the ISC group than in the IDC group (p = 0.039) and in the ISC only group than in the i-IDC group (p = 0.004).

WPAI

As shown in Table 1, hours of actual work during the past 7 days were significantly shorter in the i-IDC group than in the ISC only group (p = 0.011). Absenteeism was significantly higher in the IDC group than in the ISC group (p = 0.021). It should be noted that presenteeism was about 20% regardless of the type of catheter used. In addition, impairment of work and regular activities due to urinary problems was rated at about 20–25% in all groups.

Correlations between WAPI and other PROs

As shown in Table 3, in the ISC group presenteeism, overall work impairment, and activity impairment generally correlated moderately with each domain score as well as the overall score of Qualiveen30 and the utility score. In the IDC group, as for Qualiveen30, the results were almost identical to those of the ISC group, and activity impairment moderately correlated with the utility score.

Factors associated with each PROs on multivariable analysis

The results of univariable analysis using variables from demographics and items related to adherence as well as complications are shown in Supplementary Tables S3 and S4 online. Tables 4 and 5 demonstrate factors significantly associated with Qualiveen30, EQ-5D-5 L, and WPAI on multivariable analysis of the ISC and IDC groups, respectively. In the ISC group, bowel management problems had a negative impact on all PROs (p < 0.001) except absenteeism in WPAI (p = 0.588). Although sUTI was not associated with PROs, stocks of antibiotics had a negative impact on presenteeism and overall work impairment (p < 0.05 for both). In addition, frequent ISC and the longer time required for ISC had negative impacts on presenteeism (p = 0.042 and p = 0.025, respectively) and overall work impairment (p = 0.063 [this was a trend] and p = 0.013, respectively). Of the variables from adherence, “performing ISC is tedious” had a negative impact on Qualiveen30 and activity impairment (p < 0.001 for both), while simply forgetting to perform ISC had a negative effect on absenteeism (p = 0.014). Of the variables due to complications, insertion difficulty and UI had negative impacts on Qualiveen30 answers (p < 0.01 for both), while UI also had a negative impact on activity impairment (p = 0.003).

Discussion

This is the first study on PROs, namely Qualiveen30, EQ-5D-5 L, and WPAI, by type of urinary catheter, including Japanese reusable catheters, such as RC and i-IDC. Of the participants with SCL practicing continuous current urinary management for an average of 26 years, the disease-specific QOL in the ISC group did not outperform that in the IDC group. A substantial number of participants in the ISC group experienced difficulties in going out and preventing UI. Impairment while working due to urinary problems was recognized by about 20%, regardless of the type of catheter used, and the disease-specific QOL significantly correlated with work productivity. On multivariable analysis, several variables from participants’ demographics, adherence, and complications were associated with PROs, and in particular, bowel management problems negatively affected all PROs in both groups.

In the present study, the overall scores on Qualiveen30 were 1.57 and 1.38 in the ISC and the IDC groups, respectively, without a significant difference, and the score of the bother with limitations domain was rather significantly better in the IDC group than in the ISC group. These results conflict with those of a study using the Short-Form (SF)-Qualiveen2, in which the mean overall score was 1.29 and 1.54 in the ISC and IDC groups, respectively. On the other hand, the persons managed with IDC showed a significantly better Neurogenic Bladder Symptom Score (NBSS) than those performing CIC, and tetraplegic persons of the IDC group revealed fewer urinary management difficulties on the Spinal Cord Injury (SCI)-QOL Bladder Management Difficulties3. Changes in internal standards, namely changes in their concept of QOL and personal expectations over time may account for improved satisfaction despite using less than ideal urinary management options25.

Among the individual items of the Qualiveen30, regular ISC, ISC outside the home, and preventive measures of UI had profoundly negative impacts on QOL in participants performing ISC. The common difficulties in persons with SCL performing CIC were carrying out CIC in outdoor environments due to the unavailability of toilet facilities (74.19%), time regulation (19.35%), and dependence (6.45%)26. Furthermore, 41.38% of them used diapers and 22.58% used condom catheters to prevent UI26. Of available catheters for CIC in Japan, RC may be chosen even if the person with SCL is frequently out because some types of Japanese RC can be fitted with an extension tube that allows urine to be drained into the toilet whilst in a wheelchair. Moreover, approximately 30% of the SC group reported money problems as moderate or more. Financial considerations with respect to urinary management are major concerns for health-care providers and individuals with SCL27, and RC is less costly than even uncoated SC in Japan.

It should be noted that the domain and overall scores and individual Qualiveen30 items in the i-IDC group were comparable to those in the ISC only group, and the use of i-IDC was a significant variable associated with EQ-5D-5 L (see Supplementary Table S3 online). Therefore, i-IDC may help to maintain a disease-specific QOL in persons with SCL with an impaired health-related QOL.

UI affects mental health, and significant hierarchical differences can be seen in incontinence impact, social limitations, emotions, and severity of bladder problem in the King’s Health Questionnaire, depending on the frequency of UI5. The mean overall score of the Qualiveen questionnaire was significantly higher in persons with UI than in those without it28. Moreover, continence was independently related to scores of the Qualiveen questionnaire29. Our results are in line with these previous findings because in the ISC group, 70 − 80% of the participants responded “moderately or more” with regard to the use of continence products, and multivariable analysis showed UI was independently associated with the overall score of Qualiveen30 and regular activity impairment of WPAI.

Multivariable analysis in the ISC group showed that few variables related to adherence were associated with PROs. As speculated by several authors3,21, we think that persons performing ISC in the long-term adjust well to hygiene management as well as regular ISC, and those with a very positive outlook, especially, are not bothered by their ISC-related issues and minimize their actual objective symptoms and the perception of their bladder’s impact on their life30.

sUTI was not associated with PROs in this study, which contradicts the findings of the previous literature21,25,28,31. Instead, stocks of antibiotics for dealing with sUTI were independently associated with presenteeism and overall work impairment of WPAI. The reasons for this remain unclear, but might be explained as follows. When susceptible and/or sensitive persons feel something is wrong, they may self-diagnose and self-treat it as sUTI or other disease with previously prescribed antibiotics, originally intended for dealing with sUTI, in order to go to work despite anticipated impairment of work productivity.

Severe bowel dysfunction significantly correlated with dissatisfaction on NBSS21 because bowel as well as bladder management took a major place in primary to tertiary health concerns in persons with SCL20. The impact of bowel function on daily life was significantly greater than sexual and bladder functions, wheelchair use, pain, spasticity, and skin care32.

As far as we know, there have been no previous studies investigating the WPAI by the different types of catheter in persons with SCL. In our population, employment rates were 58% and 51% in the ISC and the IDC groups, respectively. These rates do not substantially deviate from the reported rates in individuals with SCI, which varied from 3 to 80% (average rate: approximately 35%)11. In common chronic diseases, the ranges of percentage of overall work time missed (absenteeism), impairment while working (presenteeism), overall work productivity impairment, and regular activity impairment were 1 − 23%, 6 − 97%, 6 − 69%, and 6 − 71%, respectively33. In a study of persons with neurogenic detrusor overactivity with or without UI, absenteeism was reported as 10% and 8%, presenteeism as 39% and 46%, overall work impairment as 50% and 43%, and activity impairment as 44% and 52%, respectively34. The findings of the WPAI questionnaire as a result of urinary problems in NLUTD, including our study, appear to be as substantial as those in several other more frequently researched common chronic diseases.

Moreover, the present study demonstrated that the disease-specific QOL generally correlated moderately to strongly to scores of WPAI except for absenteeism in the ISC as well as IDC groups. Persons with SCL undergoing some forms of urinary management have improved QOL and life satisfaction when they are able to have more active work, school, and social lives12. Especially, presenteeism, which was about 20% in the present study, is considered an important indicator that leads to a decline in workers’ QOL and economic loss35.

It should be noted that the frequency of or the time required for ISC was associated not with Qualiveen30 but with presenteeism and, in the latter, also with overall work impairment of WPAI. The SF-Qualiveen in persons primarily with NLUTD did not significantly correlate with the duration of ISC36, while other studies showed that an increased time burden may lead to dissatisfaction with CIC22,36,37. To the best of our knowledge, no studies have addressed the relationship between the time burden of ISC and work productivity evaluated with relevant measures such as WPAI.

The strengths of this study were the use of a disease-specific QOL measure, Qualiveen30, and the usual measures for work productivity, WPAI, and classification of findings by types of catheter. On the other hand, this study has several limitations. First, this study was an exploratory fact-finding study using data derived from a burden of illness study that, in general, does not have sufficient power to show what is being inferred from the data. In addition, this study had an inherent selection as well as an information bias, and the sample size was small. However, when conducting various surveys on SCI and spina bifida, the cooperation of the organizations helping us in this project is often solicited. Collectively, caution should be exercised in interpreting the results of between-group comparisons. Second, because the survey was conducted shortly after HCC and i-IDC became partially reimbursable in Japan, findings from comparisons between RC and SC groups as well as i-IDC and ISC only groups should be interpreted carefully. On multivariable analysis, difficulty with insertion was independently associated with Qualiveen30 in the ISC group. HCC features easier insertion and removal of the catheter than non-HCC9,38. Therefore, further studies are warranted to evaluate the impact of HCC on Japanese persons with SCL after more widespread use of HCC. Third, we used the Qualiveen30 solely as a disease-specific QOL measure because it was the only validated questionnaire in Japanese at the time of this study. However, we believe that the analysis of domain scores, overall scores, and individual items of the Qualiveen30 by types of catheter provide valuable information for clinical practice as well as future research in this field. Fourth, only the EQ-5D-5 L was used to assess generic health status, although we could confirm that the mean utility score of 0.68 in the ISC group fell into the previously reported range of 0.371–0.83136,39. Fifth, the WPAI is a useful tool for comparing work impairment between persons with different levels of severity, although the validation of this instrument among persons with SCL using urinary catheters has not yet been established40. However, we believe that it is worthwhile to have demonstrated that WPAI scores were impaired in persons with chronic SCL to a degree at least comparable to other chronic diseases. Finally, similar to most of the other studies on PROs in persons with SCL, this study was a cross-sectional study with limited populations, which precludes any conclusions about causation. Longitudinal studies are necessary to validate the findings of the present study and observe long-term impacts of types of catheters on PROs.

We think that our study has several implications for the comprehensive urinary care of persons with SCL. First, our results highlight that the impact of ISC use outside the home was a major concern to persons with SCL. Even if they used i-IDC, a disappointingly high proportion of participants showed a moderate or higher degree of giving up going out and being more dependent on others. These findings suggest that public restroom environments need to be much improved and further social understanding and cooperation with persons with SCL performing ISC is crucial. Second, the overall results of RC requiring catheter maintenance were better than expected. Given the fact that RC might have an advantage over SC in terms of waste issues14,15, our results imply that RC has a potential to benefit selected persons with SCL. Third, a more proactive approach to UI rather than the choice of catheter type as well as effectively addressing both bladder and bowel dysfunctions should be the key issue in improving QOL as well as maintaining regular activities in persons with SCL. Finally, every effort should be made to reduce presenteeism through appropriate individualized management of ISC- as well as IDC-related problems. The findings of the present study warrant future prospective studies on PROs by types of catheter because identifying and characterizing factors associated with PROs in persons with NLUTD should help the development of preventive, diagnostic, and therapeutic strategies for improving the PROs of this population.

In conclusion, the disease-specific QOL of the ISC group did not outperform that of the IDC group. SCL persons with ISC struggle to perform ISC regularly as well as away from home and to achieve social continence. The Japanese RC as well as i-IDC has a potential to benefit selected persons with SCL. Neurogenic bowel dysfunction should be properly addressed. Measures to minimize impairment while working due to a urinary problem, which currently stands at around 20%, need to be addressed in the future research.

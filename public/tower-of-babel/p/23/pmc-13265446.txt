Peristaltic transport of magnetized multi-metallic hybrid nanofluid flow with thermal radiation for heat transfer enhancement
Authors: Anum Tanveer, Iram, A. S. Shflot, Hajar Abutuqayqah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This research examines the magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) radiative peristaltic flow of a couple-stress fluid containing a three-component hybrid nanofluid of Tio2 Al2O3 and Cu nanoparticles dispersed in blood, flowing through a symmetric channel. Driven by biomedical applications, including targeted drug delivery, thermal ablation, and hyperthermia therapy, as well as industrial cooling processes, the model incorporates realistic effects including compliant channel walls, velocity slip, viscous dissipation, Joule heating, and thermal radiation. The governing nonlinear equations are formulated, non-dimensionalized, and solved numerically under the lubrication approximation. The results demonstrate that temperature increases with Hartmann number, Brownian motion, and viscous dissipation, but decreases with radiation and thermophoretic effects. Magnetic fields are found to suppress axial velocity, whereas fluid properties and buoyancy forces enhance it. Furthermore, the size and shape of trapped boluses are shown to vary significantly with magnetic strength and nanoparticle parameters. These findings highlight the potential of ternary hybrid nanofluids for improving drug delivery efficiency, advancing microfluidic devices, and enhancing heat management in biomedical and industrial systems.

Introduction

The behavior of non-Newtonian fluids has attracted considerable attention due to their relevance in engineering and biomedical applications, particularly in systems involving complex rheological characteristics and enhanced transport processes. Among various non-Newtonian models, the couple stress fluid model is effective in representing fluids with microstructural effects, such as blood, where size-dependent properties and particle interactions play an important role. Several studies have investigated the peristaltic and magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) behavior of such fluids under different physical conditions. For example, Eldabe et al. [1] analyzed the MHD flow of a viscoelastic non-Newtonian fluid in a porous medium and highlighted the influence of magnetic fields on flow resistance and velocity distribution. Nabhani et al. [2] examined the effect of couple stress parameters on the performance of a poroelastic squeeze film, showing how microstructural properties significantly alter pressure distribution and load-carrying capacity. Tanveer et al. [3] studied the flow of non-Newtonian blood in microchannels and emphasized the role of non-linear viscosity in determining flow behavior at small scales. Gangadhar et al. [4] investigated biconvective transport in a magnetized couple-stress fluid over a rotating surface, demonstrating the combined influence of thermal radiation and rotation on heat and mass transfer. Additional studies [5–8] further confirm the importance of couple stress fluids in both theoretical modeling and practical applications. However, these investigations mainly consider conventional fluids or simple nanofluid models, with limited focus on more advanced nanofluid formulations in peristaltic systems.

Peristaltic transport is a fundamental mechanism for fluid motion in deformable channels and plays a key role in physiological and industrial processes. The pioneering work of Latham [9] provided an initial experimental understanding of peristaltic pumping, while Shapiro et al. [10] developed a theoretical framework that remains widely used for analyzing low-Reynolds-number peristaltic flows. Building on these foundations, Mehmood et al. [11] investigated peristaltic flow of second-grade fluids incorporating the Cattaneo–Christov heat flux model, demonstrating the impact of non-Fourier heat conduction on thermal transport. Tanveer et al. [12] analyzed MHD peristaltic flow of third-grade fluids in electroosmotic microchannels, highlighting the interaction between electric fields and non-Newtonian rheology. Kesavaiah et al. [13] examined the combined effects of chemical reactions, heat, and mass transfer on MHD peristaltic flow in porous channels, showing how these factors influence concentration and temperature profiles. Further studies [14–21] extended peristaltic analysis to different geometries and boundary conditions. Despite these advancements, the incorporation of advanced nanofluid models, particularly ternary hybrid nanofluids, in peristaltic systems under multiple physical effects remains limited.

Nanofluids, formed by dispersing nanoparticles (1–100 nm) into a base fluid, have been widely studied due to their superior thermal properties compared to conventional fluids. Since their introduction by Choi [22], extensive research has been conducted to understand their flow and heat transfer behavior. Shehzad et al. [23] investigated the squeezing flow of a micropolar nanofluid between parallel disks under MHD effects, demonstrating how magnetic forces influence velocity and thermal fields. Butt et al. [24] analyzed the peristaltic transport of a Cu–water nanofluid in a microtube under electroosmotic control, showing improved heat transfer performance due to nanoparticle inclusion. Additional studies [25–30] have explored various aspects of nanofluid transport, including stability, thermal conductivity, and flow characteristics. However, most of these works are limited to single-component nanofluids, with relatively less attention given to hybrid and ternary nanofluids, especially in peristaltic flow environments.

To enhance thermal performance further, hybrid nanofluids have been introduced by combining two different types of nanoparticles within a base fluid. These fluids exhibit improved thermal conductivity and transport properties compared to single-component nanofluids. Jayavel et al. [31] studied the electrokinetic and thermal behavior of a non-Newtonian hybrid nanofluid in a rotating system, emphasizing the role of electroosmotic forces in microchannel transport. Munawar et al. [32] analyzed the combined effects of ciliary motion and electroosmosis on hybrid nanofluid flow, demonstrating enhanced mixing and transport efficiency. Akbar et al. [33] investigated the peristaltic transport of hybrid nanofluids containing CuO nanoparticles and multi-walled carbon nanotubes in porous channels, highlighting improvements in heat transfer characteristics. Abbas et al. [34] examined hybrid nanofluid flow between eccentric cylinders and reported significant variations in velocity and temperature fields due to geometric effects.

More recently, ternary hybrid nanofluids, which consist of three distinct nanoparticles, have been proposed to achieve further enhancement in thermophysical properties. Ali et al. [35] studied the flow of blood-based ternary nanofluids containing copper, gold, and titanium dioxide nanoparticles in ciliated microvessels, demonstrating improved thermal performance and flow control. Additional studies [36–44] have investigated different aspects of ternary hybrid nanofluid transport, indicating growing interest in their applications. Nevertheless, the combined effects of non-Newtonian fluid behavior, peristaltic motion, and ternary hybrid nanofluids under multiple interacting physical conditions have not been fully addressed in the existing literature.

Motivated by these limitations, the present study investigates the peristaltic flow of a couple stress fluid embedded with a ternary hybrid nanofluid composed of titanium dioxide \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\left(Ti{O}_{2}\right)$$\end{document}, copper (Cu), and alumina (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:A{l}_{2}{O}_{3}$$\end{document}), with blood as the base fluid. Despite the growing interest in hybrid nanofluids, no prior research has comprehensively examined the combined effects of MHD, thermal radiation, viscous dissipation, Joule heating, and compliant wall properties on ternary hybrid nanofluid peristaltic transport. This study addresses a significant gap in the literature by formulating a novel mathematical model incorporating slip boundary conditions and realistic physiological parameters. The governing equations are transformed into dimensionless form and solved numerically using Mathematica’s NDSolve function, which employs the fourth-order Runge–Kutta method for high-precision results. A key objective of this research is to understand how ternary hybrid nanoparticles influence fluid dynamics, thermal conductivity, and transport efficiency in biomedical and industrial applications. This study addresses unexplored scientific questions regarding the impact of ternary hybrid nanoparticles on velocity suppression, heat transfer enhancement, and trapped bolus dynamics in peristaltic motion. By integrating these effects, it provides deeper insights into fluid-thermal interactions, highlighting their potential for improving heat transfer efficiency and fluid transport in biomedical and industrial applications. Moreover, the study provides an in-depth visual analysis using 2D and 3D graphical representations in Mathematica, illustrating velocity distribution, temperature variations, nanoparticle concentration, and trapping phenomena.

To date, no comprehensive study has combined ternary hybrid nanofluids, couple-stress fluid mechanics, peristaltic motion, and magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) effects into a unified model, making this research a significant contribution to the field of fluid mechanics and heat transfer. By bridging theoretical modeling with practical considerations, this study provides a useful framework for analyzing and optimizing peristaltic pumping, biomedical fluid transport, and energy-efficient cooling technologies. The insights gained from this work have important implications for applications such as targeted drug delivery, microfluidic systems, blood flow analysis in microcirculation, and the design of biomedical pumps. In addition, the model can be applied to industrial and engineering processes including heat exchangers, thermal management systems, polymer processing, lubrication technologies, and chemical transport systems. It is also relevant for advanced cooling techniques in electronic devices, energy systems, and magnetically controlled fluid transport. Overall, this study contributes to a deeper understanding of complex fluid–thermal interactions and provides a reliable foundation for future research in biomedical, industrial, and energy-related applications.

Mathematical formulation

This study examines the radiative peristaltic magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flow of an incompressible couple-stress fluid containing a three-particle hybrid nanofluid composed of Tio2 Al2O3 and Cu flowing through a symmetric channel. The presence of these nanoparticles substantially improves the effective thermal conductivity and transport properties of the fluid, which is of particular interest in biomedical applications and industrial processes. The fluid is modeled as steady and laminar, incorporating the effects of Joule heating, viscous dissipation, thermal radiation and slip boundary conditions. A uniform transverse magnetic field is applied, introducing Lorentz forces that significantly alter both velocity distribution and thermal behavior. Numerical method

In this study, the nondimensional governing equations are solved numerically using the shooting technique combined with a fourth-order Runge–Kutta method, implemented through the NDSolve command in Mathematica. This approach is well suited for boundary value problems due to its high accuracy and computational efficiency. The system of coupled nonlinear Eqs. (31)–(34), together with the boundary conditions (35) and (36), is first transformed into an equivalent initial value problem and then solved iteratively by adjusting the initial guesses to satisfy the boundary conditions. For the present analysis, NDSolve utilizes adaptive step-size Runge–Kutta-based algorithms to ensure numerical stability and precision. A step size of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:0.01$$\end{document} and an error tolerance of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:10⁻⁶$$\end{document} are employed throughout the computations. Due to the nonlinear and coupled nature of the governing equations, an exact analytical solution is not feasible; therefore, a numerical approach is adopted. A grid independence and convergence test has been performed by varying the step size, and negligible variation in the results confirms the accuracy of the solution. The numerical stability is ensured through adaptive step-size control in NDSolve. Furthermore, the present results are validated in Sect. 6 through comparison with existing literature in limiting cases, showing excellent agreement and confirming the reliability of the numerical approach. The obtained results, including velocity, temperature, concentration profiles, and streamlines, are presented graphically to provide clear physical insight into the flow dynamics of the ternary hybrid nanofluid system, demonstrating the accuracy and reliability of the proposed numerical method (Table 2).

Velocity distribution analysis

Figures 4(a–n) illustrate the velocity component uuu under varying physical parameters, highlighting how the flow responds to changes in buoyancy, magnetic forces, viscous dissipation, couple-stress effects, nanoparticles, and wall compliance. As shown in Figs. 4(a–d), increasing the thermal and solutal Grashof numbers enhances the velocity throughout the channel. Higher Grashof numbers strengthen buoyancy-driven forces, which intensify convective circulation and improve momentum transport in the fluid, consistent with previous studies (Ref [39]). The couple-stress parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\gamma\:$$\end{document} influences the velocity profile in a spatially distinct manner, as seen in Figs. 4(c, d). With higher \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\gamma\:$$\end{document}, the velocity increases in the central region of the channel but decreases near the walls. This occurs because the couple-stress fluid reduces the internal rotational resistance between fluid particles, allowing the core flow to move more freely while near-wall layers experience relatively higher shear stress, resulting in non-uniform velocity distribution. Figures 4(e, f) show that increasing the Hartmann number \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:M$$\end{document} decreases the velocity throughout the channel. The applied magnetic field generates a Lorentz force that opposes fluid motion, which suppresses convective flow and reduces momentum, especially in the core region where fluid circulation would otherwise dominate. The effect of the Brinkman number \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:Br$$\end{document} is presented in Figs. 4(g, h). This additional energy contributes to enhanced fluid motion, slightly increasing velocity in regions where viscous heating is significant. It is important to note that the Brinkman number’s influence is due to viscous dissipation, not buoyancy. The trapping phenomenon is a key feature of peristaltic flow, representing the formation of closed fluid boluses that move with the propagating wave. Streamlines provide a clear visualization of particle paths, and the size and shape of the trapped bolus indicate the strength and nature of the flow. Figures 6(a–c) show the influence of the Hartmann number \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:M$$\end{document} on the trapping behavior. Overall, the analysis of streamlines demonstrates how the combined effects of fluid microstructure and magnetic forces shape the trapping phenomenon. Nano-biomedical performance metrics

The numerical results obtained in this study can be quantitatively linked to nano-biomedical performance, particularly in drug delivery and hyperthermia applications. The trapping bolus size and streamline patterns indicate the residence time of fluid elements, which directly influences the transport and retention of therapeutic nanoparticles. Larger boluses, observed for higher couple-stress parameters, increase residence time, enhancing drug delivery efficiency. Additionally, the temperature distribution profiles provide a measure of the thermal dose delivered in hyperthermia treatments, where regions of elevated temperature correspond to higher localized heating essential for targeted therapy. These findings demonstrate how flow parameters and nanoparticle effects can be optimized to improve the effectiveness of nano-biomedical applications.

Validation of model

A comparative analysis has been carried out to validate the accuracy of the present results by examining the temperature distribution with previously published work. In the existing literature, an exact solution is obtained and solved numerically using MATLAB, whereas in the present study, the governing equations are solved using Mathematica through the NDSolve technique. For validation purposes, the temperature profile in the absence of nanoparticles is compared with the results of Ma’in et al. [8] for similar values of the non-dimensional parameters, as illustrated in Fig. 7. The excellent agreement between the present results and those reported in the literature confirms the accuracy, reliability, and correctness of the numerical method employed in this study.

Concluding remarks

This study presents a detailed investigation of the magnetohydrodynamic peristaltic flow of a couple-stress fluid containing a ternary hybrid nanomixture of Tio2 Al2O3 and Cu nanoparticles, with blood as the base fluid. The model accounts for compliant walls, slip boundary conditions, Brownian motion, and thermophoresis, providing a thorough understanding of their combined influence on fluid transport, thermal behavior, and mass diffusion. The governing equations were solved numerically using MATHEMATICA, leading to the following key findings:

Modeling limitations and future work.

The present study assumes steady, two-dimensional flow and a Newtonian base approximation for blood, while neglecting pulsatile effects, wall elasticity variations, and three-dimensional complexities. Future research could extend the model to include pulsatile flow, non-symmetric or fully three-dimensional geometries, entropy generation analysis, chemical reactions, and experimental validation. Such enhancements would increase the physiological relevance of the model and broaden its applicability for biomedical and industrial applications, including targeted drug delivery and hyperthermia treatments.

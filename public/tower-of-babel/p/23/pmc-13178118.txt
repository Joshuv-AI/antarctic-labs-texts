Dear Editor,

We appreciate the authors for their insightful appraisal of our study.1 It is encouraging that they recognize the potential of temporal diastolic shock index (DSI) analysis as a step toward physiologically informed hemodynamic assessment in septic shock.

The primary objective of our study was to explore whether temporal changes in DSI could better reflect the vasoplegic component of septic shock compared with static hemodynamic variables. Despite the observational design and modest sample size, we demonstrated that delta DSI (ΔDSI) correlates with perfusion markers such as lactate. As rightly highlighted, DSI provides a framework to distinguish varying vasoplegic burden among patients with similar mean arterial pressure.

We also agree that the next translational step lies in moving from prognostic association to decision-grade application. The role of DSI in guiding the timing and escalation of vasopressor therapy is an important hypothesis that warrants prospective interventional evaluation.

Additionally, in line with their suggestion, we had explored DSI within a multimodal framework, and our analysis demonstrated that a composite approach incorporating delta DSI, perfusion markers (lactate), and vasopressor (vasoactive inotrope score-VIS) parameters yielded improved discriminative performance compared with individual variables alone. This reinforces the idea that DSI is best interpreted as part of an integrated perfusion assessment strategy rather than in isolation.

We also strongly concur with their perspective on global applicability. Given its reliance on universally available bedside variables, temporal DSI has the potential to serve as a simple, scalable, and equity-oriented hemodynamic tool, particularly in resource-limited settings. However, as they emphasize, broader validation across diverse populations and the development of protocolized, actionable thresholds remain essential before clinical adoption.

In conclusion, we are grateful for their valuable insights, which clearly delineate the future direction of research. We believe that with further prospective validation and integration into therapeutic algorithms, temporal DSI may evolve from a descriptive index into a trajectory-based vasoplegia phenotyping tool with actionable clinical relevance.

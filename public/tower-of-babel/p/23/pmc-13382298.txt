Task-evoked network connectivity and pain regulation success in chronic low back pain
Authors: Steven R Anderson, Gadi Gilam, Christine Law, Kenneth A Weber, Philippe R Goldin, Tor D Wager, Jiang-Ti Kong, Beth D Darnall, James J Gross, Sean C Mackey
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractResting-state functional connectivity (rsFC) has shown widespread changes in intrinsic functional brain networks in individuals with chronic low back pain (CLBP). However, less is known about rsFC between task-evoked brain networks (i.e., networks formed by regions that are activated to tasks) implicated in cognitive reappraisal and attention regulation in this population. This cross-sectional study analyzed resting-state fMRI and behavioral data from a separate pain regulation task in 184 individuals with CLBP to investigate whether rsFC among ROIs defined from task-based meta-analyses of cognitive reappraisal and attention regulation networks was associated with pain regulation success. We hypothesized that increased rsFC within and between task-based networks would be associated with greater pain regulation success, and that trait cognitive reappraisal and mindfulness would mediate these relationships. Results indicated that increased rsFC between ROIs in the task-based cognitive reappraisal network, including the left ventrolateral prefrontal cortex (vlPFC) and left middle temporal gyrus (MTG), was associated with reduced pain regulation success using either cognitive reappraisal or attention regulation. Conversely, increased rsFC between ROIs in the task-based cognitive reappraisal network left vlFPC and attention regulation network right middle frontal gyrus (MFG) was associated with increased pain regulation success using attention regulation. Examining the relationship between rsFC and self-reported measures of pain, cognition, and emotion, we found that increased rsFC between the task-based cognitive reappraisal network left MTG and attention regulation network right inferior frontal gyrus (IFG) was associated with increased self-reported cognitive distortions. In contrast, increased rsFC between the task-based attention regulation network bilateral vlPFC and left posterior cingulate cortex (PCC) was associated with more habitual use of cognitive reappraisal, lower pain catastrophizing, and lower depression. In a formal mediation analysis, neither trait cognitive reappraisal nor trait mindfulness mediated the relationship between rsFC and pain regulation success. Together, our findings suggest that rsFC between brain regions in task-based cognitive reappraisal and attention regulation networks is related to behavioral outcomes in a separate evoked pain regulation task and may underlie cognition and emotion regulation success in individuals with CLBP.Keywords: Resting-state fMRI, Chronic low back pain, Pain regulation, Cognitive reappraisal, Attention regulation

Introduction

Chronic pain is a significant public health issue affecting approximately 20% of the global adult population (Yong et al., 2022). Among chronic pain conditions, chronic low back pain (CLBP) is the most prevalent, with its incidence rising and associated costs estimated at $100-$200 billion annually in the U.S. due to disability (Smith et al., 2013; Dahlhamer et al., 2018; Yang et al., 2016). Resting-state functional connectivity (rsFC) has been shown to reveal changes in brain networks in individuals with CLBP, which may indicate brain adaptation or neural plasticity (Guerra-Carrillo et al., 2014). Compared to healthy individuals, those with chronic pain, including CLBP, exhibit widespread functional and structural alterations in rsFC across various brain networks such as the default mode network (Baliki et al., 2008, 2011, 2014; Vachon-Presseau et al., 2016; Goossens et al., 2018), attention regulation network (Letzen et al., 2017; Mao et al., 2014; Kong et al., 2019), reward system (Kim et al., 2020; Hashmi et al., 2013; Pfannmöller et al., 2019), and visual network (Shen et al., 2019). These alterations in rsFC are furthermore clinically significant, predicting outcomes such as the transition from acute to chronic pain (Baliki et al., 2012) and the progression of pain symptoms over time (Kutch et al., 2017). Other studies have found that rsFC predicts increased spontaneous pain experienced at the time of fMRI scanning (Napadow et al., 2010), larger treatment responses to real and sham acupuncture (Tu et al., 2019), increased placebo analgesia magnitude (Tétreault et al., 2016), and reductions in clinical pain in response to milnacipran treatment in individuals with fibromyalgia (Schmidt-Wilcke et al., 2014).

Despite advancements in understanding rsFC in intrinsic functional networks (i.e., activity emerging without external stimuli), less is understood about rsFC between brain regions implicated in emotion regulation task processes, and its relationship to pain outcomes. Specifically, neuroimaging studies to date examining emotion regulation in individuals with CLBP have typically examined either rsFC in intrinsic networks alone, or task-based activation without linking back to rsFC. Among studies that have investigated rsFC between emotion regulation task-evoked brain regions, outcomes have typically focused on non-pain outcomes, such as reductions in negative affect in response to positive and negative affective imagery (Ng et al., 2021) rather than changes in pain ratings in response to an evoked pain task.

Individuals with chronic pain have been shown to demonstrate altered processing with respect to cognition and emotion regulation (Moriarty et al., 2011; BlaisdaleJones et al., 2021). As a result, there is significant interest in chronic pain research in brain networks involved in the cognitive and emotion regulation of pain. The emotion regulation strategies cognitive reappraisal and attention regulation have been shown to modulate activity in brain regions involved in both emotional reactivity and noxious painful experiences (Schmidt-Wilcke et al., 2014) (Bushnell et al., 2013; Gilam et al., 2020). Descending pain modulatory systems downregulate pain through emotional (via a fronto-periaqueductal grey-brainstem circuit) and attentional (via an insula-parietal-somatosensory corticocortical circuit) neural pathways (Lewis et al., 2012). This ability to successfully regulate pain may underlie known differences in conditioned pain modulation (CPM) in individuals with chronic pain and may possibly reflect alterations in endogenous pain modulatory systems (Yong et al., 2022; Schalet et al., 2016) (Smith et al., 2009). However, it remains unclear how rsFC among ROIs involved in cognitive reappraisal and attention regulation is associated with pain regulation success in individuals with CLBP.

Overview of the current research

Based on these considerations, our current study investigated whether rsFC between ROIs defined from task-based meta-analyses of cognitive reappraisal and attention regulation networks was associated with the degree of pain regulation success in individuals with CLBP. We defined ROIs in cognitive reappraisal and attention regulation brain networks using a meta-analytic approach and then examined whether ROI-to-ROI rsFC was associated with changes in participants’ subjective pain ratings due to cognitive reappraisal or attention regulation of thermal pain in a separate task.

Given prior evidence indicating that increased task-evoked activity in key brain regions underlying emotion regulation, such as the ventrolateral prefrontal cortex (vlPFC), amygdala, and inferior frontal gyrus (IFG), is associated with increased emotion regulation success (Wager et al., 2008; He et al., 2023; Morawetz et al., 2017; Eippert et al., 2007), we hypothesized that increased rsFC between task-evoked regions implicated in cognitive reappraisal and attention regulation would be associated with increased success of emotion regulation. However, there is also evidence suggesting that increased intrinsic functional connectivity, particularly between the prefrontal cortex and cortico-limbic and somatosensory areas, may differentiate individuals with chronic pain from healthy controls (Flodin et al., 2016; Truini et al., 2016) and predict clinically-relevant pain outcomes, such as the transition to chronic pain (Baliki et al., 2012), pain severity (Truini et al., 2016), and pharmacological treatment responsiveness (Motoyama et al., 2019). Increased activity in prefrontal cortex regions, such as the ventrolateral prefrontal cortex (vlPFC) and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC), along with activity in the amygdala, has also been implicated in less successful emotion regulation and increases in pain unpleasantness (Berna et al., 2010). As a result, although we specify directionality in our hypotheses below, it is important to note that this directionality is exploratory in nature.

Increased rsFC values among ROIs defined from the task-based cognitive reappraisal network will be associated with greater success of reappraisal as a pain modulation strategy.

Increased rsFC values among ROIs defined from the task-based attention regulation network will be associated with greater success of attention regulation as a pain modulation strategy.

Next, given evidence that the relationship between emotion regulatory processes and pain ratings may be mediated by psychological factors (Koechlin et al., 2018), we additionally formed the following hypothesis on the role of self-report measures of pain, cognition and emotion as mediators in the relationship between rsFC and pain regulation success. We chose to focus on trait cognitive reappraisal and mindfulness as self-report habitual use counterparts to our task-evoked cognitive reappraisal and attention regulation networks, respectively. Beyond our theoretical motivation to examine these mediators, higher trait cognitive reappraisal has been linked to increased prefrontal cortex activation during a cognitive reappraisal task (van der Meulen et al., 2017) and less emotional distress among individuals with chronic pain (Garland et al., 2018), supporting its potential mediating role, although its relationship with pain ratings is more inconsistent (Koechlin et al., 2018). Similarly, higher trait mindfulness has been found to be associated with lower default mode network (DMN) connectivity in healthy individuals (Harrison et al., 2019), greater deactivation of brain regions involved in pain appraisal during experimentally induced heat pain (Zeidan et al., 2018), and reduced pain intensity and interference among individuals with chronic pain (Elvery et al., 2017).

The link between rsFC among ROIs in the task-based cognitive reappraisal network and degree of success of cognitive reappraisal of pain will be mediated by trait cognitive reappraisal; similarly, the link between rsFC among ROIs in the task-based attention regulation network and degree of success of attention regulation of pain will be mediated by trait mindfulness.

Methods

Overview

The Stanford Center for Back Pain (SCBP) is an NIH-funded research program (ClinicalTrials.gov 21/07/2015 NCT02503475) whose goal is to elucidate the underlying mechanisms of cognitive behavioral therapy for chronic pain (pain-CBT), mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR), and electroacupuncture for chronic low back pain through two linked randomized controlled pain treatment trials: trial 1 comparing pain-CBT versus MBSR versus no treatment, and trial 2 comparing real electroacupuncture treatment versus sham electroacupuncture (Mackey et al., 2022). The SCBP focuses explicitly on the intersection of pain modulatory, cognition and emotion regulatory systems. The current study is a cross-sectional secondary analysis of baseline data, collected prior to trial interventions, from SCBP and does not report the results of the SCBP clinical trial, which will be reported in a separate study. The Institutional Review Board at Stanford University approved this protocol. According to the Declaration of Helsinki (1991), all participants provided written informed consent.

Study procedures

Baseline questionnaires were administered as part of the baseline pre-treatment study period. As part of the larger linked randomized controlled trials protocol, participants completed questionnaires via a Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act–compliant REDCap platform. Participants received email reminders to complete their surveys via a secure link and could complete the surveys at their own pace. Participants completed a baseline neuroimaging scan within three weeks of the baseline visit or before the start of their assigned treatment visits (pain-CBT, MBSR, electroacupuncture or sham electroacupuncture). The baseline scan comprised a structural high-resolution 3D scan (12 min), resting-state scan (10 min), pain regulation task in which heat applied to the low back was used as an evoked pain stimulus (18 min), and diffusion tensor imaging scan (12 min). The full protocol for the SCBP study has been published elsewhere (Mackey et al., 2022).

Participants

Inclusion criteria were men or women between the ages of 21 and 65 years, fluent in English, presenting with CLBP as their primary pain complaint, and average back pain intensity reported at the time of screening ≥4 out of 10 during the preceding month. CLBP was defined using NIH Research Standards for Back Pain as a guide (Deyo et al., 2015), with participants meeting criteria if they had persistent pain lasting at least 3 months in duration and experiencing pain at least half of the days in the past 6 months.

Exclusion criteria were contraindication to magnetic resonance imaging, receipt of acupuncture in the past 5 years, reported radicular symptoms, currently pregnant or planning to become pregnant, or ongoing legal or disability claims related to CLBP. Additional details on the inclusion and exclusion criteria, as well as the screening and consent processes, are published elsewhere (Kong et al., 2018). Participants were recruited from a tertiary pain clinic (Stanford Health Care's Pain Management Center, Redwood City, CA) and the local San Francisco Bay Area community from 2015 to 2021.

A total of 234 individuals with CLBP were enrolled in the SCBP and attended the baseline neuroimaging scan. Participants were further excluded from the final sample due to equipment malfunction (e.g., E-Prime or Medoc crash, or MRI scanner issue) (n = 11), participant issues (e.g., claustrophobia, inability to complete task, pain intolerance, drug use prior to scan session), did not understand instructions (n = 9), insufficient task ratings (n = 22), not completing the pain regulation task (n = 2), and not completing the resting-state scan (n = 3). Finally, participants were excluded if their calibration heat temperature was <44°C (n = 3); this was due to the design of the evoked pain task, which limited the use of temperatures below 44°C as it impacted the timing of the pain stimulus duration in relation to the change from baseline temperature (32°C) (temperature ramp up/down rate of 8 °C/s; 1.5s temperature ramp up + 1.5s ramp down + 9s painful stimulus duration = 12s cue duration). Measures

Pain ratings and self-report measures of pain, cognition and emotion

Participants completed a series of questionnaires via REDCap electronic data capture tools hosted at Stanford University (Harris et al., 2009, 2019) at baseline and subsequent datapoints after treatments. Pain intensity and unpleasantness during the fMRI pain regulation task were measured using separate 11-point numeric rating scales (0 = no pain to 10 = worst imaginable pain).

Several self-report measures of pain, cognition and emotion were also administered outside of the pain regulation fMRI task. Trait cognitive reappraisal was measured using the Emotion Regulation Questionnaire (ERQ) – Cognitive Reappraisal Subscale (Gross et al., 2003). The ERQ consists of 10 items (e.g., “I control my emotions by changing the way I think about the situation I'm in”) rated on a 7-point Likert scale (1 = Strongly disagree to 7 = Strongly agree). The ERQ Cognitive Reappraisal subscale has demonstrated good internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha = 0.79) (Gross et al., 2003). The total scores for each scale range from 5 to 35, with higher scores indicating more habitual use of cognitive reappraisal as an emotion regulation strategy. Trait mindfulness was measured using the short form of the Five Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire (FFMQ) (Bohlmeijer et al., 2011; Baer et al., 2006). The FFMQ consists of 24 items (e.g., “I pay attention to physical experiences, such as the wind in my hair or sun on my face”) that are rated on a 5-point Likert scale (1 = Never or very rarely true to 5 = very often or always true). The FFMQ has demonstrated adequate internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha coefficients ranging from 0.73 to 0.91). Higher FFMQ scores indicate a higher level of mindfulness. Distress regarding the cognition and emotion associated with actual or anticipated pain was measured using The Pain Catastrophizing Scale (PCS) (Sullivan et al., 1995). The PCS consists of 13 items (e.g., “There's nothing I can do to reduce the intensity of the pain”) rated on a 5-point Likert Scale (0 = Not at all to 4 = All the time) and has demonstrated good internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha = 0.92) (Osman et al., 2000). The PCS comprises three different subscales: rumination, magnification, and helplessness. The total scores range from 0 to 52. Higher PCS scores indicate a higher level of catastrophic thinking about pain. The frequency and intensity of commonly held cognitive distortions was measured using the Cognitive Distortions Questionnaire (CD-Quest) (De Oliveira, 2014). The CD-Quest consists of 15 items each representing a different cognitive distortion (e.g., “dichotomous thinking”). Frequency and intensity dimensions were combined in the present study (0 = I did not believe it to 5 = I believed it very much (more than 70%) almost all of the time (6-7 days during the past week)). The CD-Quest has demonstrated adequate internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha = 0.85) (De Oliveira et al., 2015). The total scores range from 0 to 75. Higher scores indicate more frequent and intense cognitive distortions. Additional self-report measures included the Perceived Stress Scale (Cohen et al., 1983), Attentional Control Scale (Derryberry et al., 2002), Positive and Negative Affect Schedule (PANAS) (Watson et al., 1988), and Back Pain Bothersomeness (Mackey et al., 2022). Additional details on the self-report measures included in the larger protocol have been published elsewhere (Mackey et al., 2022).

Patient-reported outcomes measurement information system (PROMIS)

The PROMIS measures (http://www.healthmeasures.net) assess physical and psychosocial health status in patients with chronic illnesses, including chronic pain (Askew et al., 2016; Cella et al., 2010, 2016; Cook et al., 2016; Schalet et al., 2016; Buysse et al., 2010). PROMIS measures used in the current study were administered using a hybrid computerized adaptive testing (hybrid-CAT) approach, resulting in a T-score for each patient (M = 50, SD = 10). PROMIS CAT instruments were administered for pain interference, physical function, fatigue, sleep impairment, depression, anxiety, negative affect, anger, perceived stress, and social isolation. Higher scores on each PROMIS measure indicate greater severity of each symptom domain (except for physical function, where higher scores indicate better functional status).

Pain regulation fMRI task procedures

Prior to the pain regulation fMRI task scan, participants were trained on the task following previously developed training procedures (Goldin et al., 2019; Ochsner et al., 2005). The pain regulation task conditions were Respond, Reframe, and Observe. For the Respond condition, participants were instructed to “focus on the pain and your reactions to the pain. Allow yourself to respond to the pain as you normally would.” For the Reframe condition, corresponding to the emotion regulation strategy of cognitive reappraisal, participants were instructed to “re-interpret or reframe the way you think about the current pain experience in order to make the experience less aversive for you.” Participants were given examples of how to reframe the painful heat stimulation, such as “This pain is temporary” and “This pain is not really so intense.” For the Observe condition, corresponding to the emotion regulation strategy of attention regulation, participants were instructed to “simply notice any and all parts of your moment-to-moment experience. This might include noticing thoughts, physical sensations, feelings or emotions and how they unfold and change from one moment to another.”

In the present study, we examined only the behavioral pain ratings and did not examine the task neuroimaging data from the pain regulation fMRI task. The pain regulation task (Fig. 1a) began with a baseline 10 s fixation cross followed by 41 trials in which participants were asked to either Respond, Reframe, or Observe while receiving painful heat stimulation (12 s), or a rest period in which there was no painful heat stimulation and participants were asked not to rate their pain. After each painful heat stimulation, participants were asked to rate their pain intensity and pain unpleasantness (5 s for each pain rating). Each trial ended with a jittered fixation cross (1, 3, or 7 s). Painful heat stimulations were delivered using a fMRI-compatible Medoc ATS thermode. Heat stimulation temperatures (range: 44-49°C) were determined in a fine thresholding procedure prior to scanning. Each trial used the same heat stimulation temperature determined for that participant during the thresholding procedure (corresponding to a pain intensity rating of approximately 6 out of 10). Each heat stimulation started at a baseline temperature of 32°C, ramped up and down at a rate of 8 °C/s, and peaked at the target temperature determined during the thresholding procedure. Peak stimulus intensity duration was adjusted (range: 7.75-9.0 s) based on the target temperature determined during thresholding. fMRI acquisition and preprocessing

Data acquisition

rsFC data were collected on a GE Discovery MR750 3.0 T MR scanner at the Richard M. Lucas Center for Imaging at Stanford University School of Medicine. Thirty-two oblique slices (parallel to AC-PC) covering the whole brain volume were acquired (10 min scan, TR = 2000 ms, TE = 30 ms, flip angle = 76°, FOV = 22 mm, slice thickness = 4 mm, gap = 0.5 mm) using a Spiral In-Out acquisition sequence (Glover et al., 2001). A T1-weighted anatomical image was acquired from each participant using a 3D BRAVO sequence (TI = 400 ms, oblique orientation, flip angle = 11°, field of view = 256 mm, slice thickness = 1 mm).

Preprocessing

Initial preprocessing of rsFC consisted of physiological denoising using RETROICOR (Glover et al., 2000) and the application of a standard fMRIPrep pipeline (Esteban et al., 2019). First, a reference volume and its skull-stripped version were generated, then the functional reference was co-registered to the T1w reference using bbregister in FreeSurfer (Greve et al., 2009). Co-registration was configured with 9 degrees of freedom to account for field inhomogeneities and coils heating up. The functional time series were resampled onto their original, native space by applying a single, composite transform to correct for head-motion and susceptibility distortions. Head-motion parameters with respect to the BOLD reference were estimated before any spatial and temporal filtering (i.e., smoothing or high-pass filtering) using FSL MCFLIRT (Jenkinson et al., 2002). Motion artifact correction was performed on the preprocessed functional data using non-aggressive independent component analysis (ICA-AROMA) (Pruim et al., 2015), which has been shown to improve temporal signal-to-noise ratio in conjunction with RETROICOR (Krentz et al., 2023), after removal of non-steady state volumes and spatial smoothing with an isotropic, Gaussian kernel of 6 mm FWHM. Corresponding non-aggressively denoised runs were produced after smoothing. Slice-timing correction was not conducted. Participants were excluded from further analysis if they had a mean framewise displacement (FD) (Power et al., 2012) greater than 0.50 mm (Siegel et al., 2014). Participants displayed a mean framewise displacement of 0.099 mm. No participants had a mean framewise displacement >0.50 mm; therefore, no participants were excluded from subsequent analyses due to excessive head motion.

Task-based regions of interest (ROIs)

ROI-to-ROI rsFC data analysis was performed to investigate whether rsFC among ROIs defined from task-based meta-analyses of cognitive reappraisal and attention regulation was associated with pain regulation success. The ROIs for the cognitive reappraisal network were defined using the meta-analytic neuroimaging database Neurosynth (Fig. 1b). A search of the term “reappraisal” generated an association test map (FDR = 0.01). To identify ROIs, FSL's cluster command was used to threshold the map at z > 7 based on visual inspection of the Neurosynth association test map and to reduce the overall number of ROIs. Using the fslmaths command in FSL, we then created a single-voxel mask centered at the peak z-value of clusters greater than 10 voxels, drew 4 mm spheres around those coordinates, and binarized each ROI mask. This procedure resulted in a total of five ROIs for the cognitive reappraisal network: left middle temporal gyrus (MTG), left ventrolateral prefrontal cortex (lvlPFC), left amygdala (Amyg), left superior frontal gyrus (SFG), and right ventrolateral prefrontal cortex (rvlPFC).

The ROIs for the attention regulation network were defined using a meta-analysis by Messina et al. (2021) on the use of acceptance as an emotion regulation strategy (Messina et al., 2021) given the conceptual overlap between the instructions of the Observe condition in the pain regulation task (“simply notice any and all parts of your moment-to-moment experience”) and acceptance, which has been shown to recruit brain regions implicated in cognitive control and attention (Goldin et al., 2019). We refer to this network as “attention regulation” in the present study to reflect the pain regulation task instructions, while acknowledging its strong conceptual overlap with acceptance-based regulation as defined in prior meta-analytic work. The Messina et al. (2021) meta-analysis included the results of 13 studies and comprised a mix of healthy and clinical populations. The meta-analysis did not include individuals with CLBP given that no such neuroimaging meta-analysis of acceptance as an emotion regulation strategy in CLBP exists to our knowledge. ROIs in the attention regulation network were identified from significant clusters of brain activity in the Messina et al. (2021) meta-analysis, specifically from the contrasts of acceptance compared to natural reaction (CNR) and acceptance compared to focus on emotions (CFE).

The fslmaths command in FSL was used to create a single-voxel mask at the coordinates of the identified ROIs, draw 4 mm spheres around those coordinates, and binarize each ROI mask. As ROIs were determined from the x, y, z coordinates provided in the Messina et al. (2021) meta-analysis, and not from brain activation maps, no thresholding was applied. FSLeyes was used to confirm anatomical names of ROIs. This resulted in eight ROIs used to define the attention regulation network: left anterior insula (aINS), right inferior frontal gyrus (IFG), left frontal pole (FP), right middle frontal gyrus (MFG), left posterior cingulate cortex (PCC), left thalamus (THAL), left precuneus (PREC), and left parahippocampal gyrus (PHG). Task-based ROI masks from cognitive reappraisal and attention regulation networks were overlaid on a standard brain and examined together to confirm that the 4 mm spheres drawn for each ROI within and between networks did not overlap. Resting-state functional connectivity (rsFC) analysis

rsFC data were analyzed using the CONN functional connectivity toolbox (v20. b) for MATLAB (Whitfield-Gabrieli et al., 2012; Nieto-Castanon, 2020). In addition to the preprocessing of resting data conducted in fMRIPrep, we applied the CONN toolbox's standard denoising pipeline. The denoising pipeline consisted of linear regression of potential confounding effects in the functional signal and temporal band-pass filtering. The pipeline implements an anatomical component-based noise correction procedure (aCompCor) which includes the regression of potential confounding effects characterized by white matter timeseries (5 CompCor noise components), CSF timeseries (5 CompCor noise components), and linear trends (2 factors) within each functional run, followed by bandpass frequency filtering of the BOLD timeseries between 0.01 Hz and 0.1 Hz. ROI-to-ROI connectivity matrices were estimated characterizing the patterns of rsFC with 13 cognitive reappraisal and attention regulation network ROIs. Group-level analyses were performed using a General Linear Model (GLM). Thresholding of results was conducted using the CONN toolbox two-step method, which specifies a p-uncorrected <0.05 height threshold and a false discovery rate (FDR) corrected p < 0.05 cluster-level threshold.

Analyses tested the relationship between participants’ degree of success of cognitive reappraisal or attention regulation of pain and rsFC of ROIs. Degree of success of cognitive reappraisal of pain was defined as the difference in pain ratings between Respond and Reframe pain regulation task conditions (Respond > Reframe). Degree of success of attention regulation was defined as the difference in pain ratings between the Respond and Observe conditions (Respond > Observe). Age and gender were demeaned and included as 2nd-level covariates to control for any age or gender differences in rsFC. This procedure resulted in the following models being tested, each controlling for age and gender: 1) Degree of success of cognitive reappraisal (Respond > Reframe) of pain intensity associated with ROI-to-ROI rsFC, 2) Degree of success of cognitive reappraisal (Respond > Reframe) of pain unpleasantness associated with ROI-to-ROI rsFC, 3) Degree of success of attention regulation (Respond > Observe) of pain intensity associated with ROI-to-ROI rsFC, and 4) Degree of success of attention regulation (Respond > Observe) of pain unpleasantness associated with ROI-to-ROI rsFC.

Statistical analysis

Data were checked for completeness and nonsensical input (e.g., negative ages) before each demographic variable was quantified. Missing gender (n = 2; approximately 1% of the sample) and age (n = 6; approximately 3% of the sample) values were imputed using the sample mean. Age and gender were included solely as nuisance covariates to account for potential confounding effects and were not variables of substantive interest in the present analyses. Addressing Hypothesis 1, Hypothesis 2, interpretation focuses on cluster-level patterns of rsFC, with individual ROI-to-ROI connections reported to illustrate the direction and composition of each cluster. Addressing Hypotheses 3 concerning self-report pain and emotion mediators of the relationship between cognitive reappraisal and attention regulation network rsFC and degree of pain regulation success, we conducted Pearson correlation analyses between rsFC, degree of pain regulation success in the separate pain regulation task, and the self-report measures of pain, cognition and emotion described in sections 2.4.1, 2.4.2 using R Version 3.3.2 (R Core Team, 2019). To reduce Type I error for multiple comparisons, p values were false discovery rate (FDR) adjusted (Thissen et al., 2002). To examine whether self-report measures of pain, cognition and emotion mediated the relationship between rsFC and the success of pain regulation, we specified mediation models using the lavaan package in R (Rosseel, 2012).

Results

Participant characteristics

A total of 184 participants ages 21-64 (M = 40.57, SD = 12.04; 46.7% female) participated in the study.

Evoked pain regulation task behavioral results

Participants successfully reduced their pain intensity during the evoked pain task when engaging in cognitive reappraisal [Respond > Reframe, t (183) = 16.49, p < 0.001] and attention regulation [Respond > Observe, t (183) = 12.26, p < 0.001] (Fig. 1d, left). Participants also successfully reduced their pain unpleasantness when engaging in cognitive reappraisal [Respond > Reframe, t (183) = 14.06, p < 0.001] and attention regulation [Respond > Observe, t (183) = 10.11, p < 0.001] (Fig. 1d, right).

Relationship between rsFC and self-report measures of pain, cognition and emotion

Correlation analyses

To understand the relationship between rsFC and self-report measures of pain, cognition and emotion, we first examined Pearson correlations between our variables of interest within each of the three clusters of rsFC that were associated with the success of pain regulation (cognitive reappraisal of pain intensity; attention regulation of pain intensity; attention regulation of pain unpleasantness). Variables included in the correlations were pain regulation difference ratings, within- and between-network rsFC values, and the self-report measures of pain, cognition and emotion described in section 2.4.1 and 2.4.2.

Examining variables associated with cognitive reappraisal of pain intensity (Fig. 3a), we found that increased between-network right IFG-left MTG rsFC was associated with increased self-reported cognitive distortions (CD-Quest), r = 0.192, p FDR-corrected = 0.028, 95% CI [0.049, 0.328] (Fig. 3b). In contrast, rsFC associated with cognitive reappraisal of pain intensity that was not significantly (FDR-corrected p > 0.05) related to self-report measures (indicated by correlation matrix cells with white backgrounds in Fig. 3a) included left vlPFC-SFG, IFG-Amyg, right vlPFC-Amyg, left vlPFC-MTG, and aINS-MTG.

Examining variables associated with the attention regulation of pain intensity (Fig. 3c), increased between-network left vlPFC-left PCC rsFC was associated with reduced pain catastrophizing (PCS), r = −0.223, p FDR corrected = 0.010, 95% CI [−0.356, −0.081] (Fig. 3d); increased attention regulation network left aINS-left PCC rsFC was also associated with reduced pain catastrophizing (PCS), r = −0.191, p FDR-corrected = 0.034, 95% CI [−0.326, −0.047]. In addition, we found that increased between-network left vlPFC-left PCC rsFC was associated with more habitual use of cognitive reappraisal (ERQ-Cognitive Reappraisal), r = 0.192, p FDR-corrected = 0.034, 95% CI [0.048, 0.327]. Finally, we found that increased between-network right vlPFC-left PCC rsFC was associated with reduced depression (PROMIS Depression), r = −0.186, p FDR-corrected = 0.040, 95% CI [−0.322, −0.043]. In contrast, rsFC associated with attention regulation of pain intensity that was not significantly (FDR-corrected p > 0.05) related to self-report measures (indicated by correlation matrix cells with white backgrounds in Fig. 3c) included left vlPFC-MFG, right vlPFC-MFG, IFG-Amyg, IFG-PCC, right vlPFC-MTG, left vlPFC-MTG, and aINS-MTG.

There were no significant correlations between the attention regulation of pain unpleasantness and self-report measures of pain, cognition or emotion.

Hypothesis 3: mediation analysis

Based on the correlation analysis, we then conducted a formal mediation analysis to investigate if self-report measures of cognitive reappraisal or mindfulness mediated the relationship between task-based rsFC and pain regulation success. Separate mediation models were specified for each chosen mediator. Neither trait cognitive reappraisal nor trait mindfulness significantly mediated the relationship between rsFC and pain regulation success (all indirect p > 0.10).

Discussion

In the present study, we found that resting-state functional connectivity (rsFC) among regions-of-interest (ROIs) defined from task-based meta-analyses of cognitive reappraisal and attention regulation was associated with pain regulation success in individuals with CLBP. Across results, we observed a pattern in which increased connectivity between the vlPFC ROI within the cognitive reappraisal network and MTG/PCC was consistently associated with reduced pain regulation success during task performance in individuals with CLBP, suggesting a shared neural mechanism for difficulties in pain regulation. Although our hypotheses emphasized within-network connectivity, our findings highlight the importance of cross-network rsFC between task-defined regions in shaping pain regulation success.

Addressing Hypothesis 1 regarding the relationship between cognitive reappraisal network rsFC and success of cognitive reappraisal of pain, we found that increased rsFC between the left vlPFC and left MTG in the cognitive reappraisal network was associated with reduced success of regulation of pain intensity using cognitive reappraisal. Although this finding was counter to our hypothesized direction, we similarly found that increased rsFC between task-based bilateral vlPFC and left MTG ROIs in the cognitive reappraisal network was associated with reduced success of attention regulation of pain intensity and pain unpleasantness.

Addressing Hypothesis 2 regarding the relationship between attention regulation network rsFC and success of attention regulation of pain, we found that increased between-network left vlPFC-left PCC rsFC was associated with reduced success of attention regulation of pain intensity. We further found that increased between-network bilateral vlFPC-right MFG rsFC was associated with increased success of attention regulation of pain intensity, consistent with our hypothesized direction.

Addressing Hypothesis 3, we investigated whether self-report measures of pain, cognition and emotion were associated with rsFC and degree of pain regulation success. Correlation analyses revealed that rsFC between several brain regions was significantly associated with self-reported pain, cognition and emotion. Specifically, increased connectivity between the right IFG and left MTG was linked to increased self-reported cognitive distortions. In contrast, increased rsFC between the left vlPFC and left PCC was associated with increased habitual use of cognitive reappraisal, lower pain catastrophizing, and lower depression. However, counter to our hypothesis, neither trait cognitive reappraisal nor trait mindfulness significantly mediated the relationship between rsFC and pain regulation success. This suggests that the relationship between rsFC and behavior may not be explained by conscious, self-reported regulatory tendencies, but instead reflect implicit or capacity-level neural organization.

The disruptive effects of chronic pain on resting-state brain networks are well-established (Baliki et al., 2008, 2011; Vachon-Presseau et al., 2016; Goossens et al., 2018; Letzen et al., 2017; Mao et al., 2014; Kong et al., 2019; Kim et al., 2020; Hashmi et al., 2013; Pfannmöller et al., 2019; Shen et al., 2019). Our findings provide evidence that rsFC among ROIs defined from task-based meta-analyses of cognitive reappraisal and attention regulation is associated with behavioral ratings in a separate acute pain regulation task in individuals with CLBP. We consistently found that rsFC between the vlPFC ROI within the cognitive reappraisal network and MTG/PCC was associated with reduced pain regulation success. Previous task-fMRI studies in healthy populations have observed increased activity in the vlPFC and other prefrontal cortex regions (dorsolateral, dorsomedial prefrontal cortex) accompanying cognition and emotion regulation strategies (Ochsner et al., 2005, 2012). Because of this, the vlPFC has been proposed as a key region supporting the selection and inhibition of emotional reappraisals as part of the downregulation of negative emotional experiences (Buhle et al., 2014). Investigating the potential for altered connectivity with the vlPFC to impact emotion regulation, there is evidence that increased connectivity between the vlPFC and amygdala may contribute to reductions in emotion regulation success (Wager et al., 2008).

Consistent with these prior findings, we further found that increased rsFC between the vlPFC and left amygdala, as well as the right IFG and left amygdala, was associated with reduced pain regulation success. In fMRI studies of clinical populations, there is evidence of alterations in vlPFC and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC) activity when attempting to downregulate negative emotional experience (Zilverstand et al., 2017). In chronic pain, altered physiology of the vlPFC may be a neural mechanism of the hyperalgesic effect of psychological factors such as pain catastrophizing (Loggia et al., 2015), while increases in vlPFC/dlPFC and amygdala activity may reflect inefficient emotion regulation and increased pain unpleasantness (Berna et al., 2010). Individuals with chronic pain have also been found to have increased rsFC between the amygdala and central executive network compared to healthy volunteers (Jiang et al., 2016). We found that increased connectivity between the PCC and vlPFC/aINS was associated with lower pain catastrophizing, suggesting a link between increased rsFC and cognitive dysfunction in individuals with CLBP.

Our finding that left vlPFC-left MTG connectivity was associated with a reduced degree of pain regulation success is supported by prior studies that have found alterations in connectivity between the MTG and regions involved in pain modulation, such as the periaqueductal grey (PAG), in individuals with chronic pain (Chen et al., 2017). The left posterior MTG, where the cognitive reappraisal network ROI in the present study was located, is thought to be involved in the control of semantic information and may act to integrate the automatic retrieval in the default mode network of executively-demanding and goal-directed cognitive effort (Davey et al., 2016). Given evidence of default mode network reorganization and dysfunction in individuals with chronic pain (Baliki et al., 2008, 2014; Letzen et al., 2017; Jiang et al., 2016), our findings suggest that the success of pain regulation in CLBP may be influenced by connectivity between the default mode network and task-positive regions, including the vlPFC.

Increased connectivity between the vlPFC and PCC was also associated with reduced pain regulation success. The PCC is a key node of the default mode network and is thought to be involved in self-directed cognition (Leech et al., 2014). In individuals with fibromyalgia, there is evidence that the PCC may encode self-referential pain catastrophizing statements, with increased PCC activity observed during catastrophic versus neutral statements (Lee et al., 2018). In a study examining alterations in cross-network rsFC between the default mode network and salience network in individuals with chronic pain (Hemington et al., 2016), the PCC was found to have increased connectivity with the SN and decreased connectivity with the DMN, suggesting that it may play a role in communication between the DMN and SN. Importantly, the associations between vlPFC-PCC rsFC and pain regulation success reflect state-dependent, task-evoked regulation capacity. In contrast, in correlation analyses with self-report measures we found that vlPFC-PCC rsFC was associated with lower pain catastrophizing, lower depression, and increased habitual use of cognitive reappraisal. This suggests that vlPFC-PCC rsFC may differentially relate to trait-level cognitive and emotional styles, supporting adaptive self-referential cognitive styles at rest, while simultaneously interfering with the ability to disengage from pain during active pain regulation attempts.

A limitation of the present study is that although we focused on two of the most widely examined cognition and emotion regulation strategies, cognitive reappraisal and attention regulation, we did not examine the relationship between rsFC and other emotion regulation strategies that may have relevance for individuals with CLBP. For example, given prior findings that expressive suppression may indirectly impact chronic pain via increased pain behaviors (Burns et al., 2011), pain catastrophizing (Wong et al., 2013), and depression (Koechlin et al., 2018; Hülsebusch et al., 2016), there is a need for research on how rsFC between brain regions active during expressive suppression may influence pain regulation in individuals with CLBP. Similarly, future research is needed to understand the self-report factors related to pain, cognition and emotion that mediate the relationship between rsFC and the success of pain regulation. Another limitation of the present study is that some of the ROI-to-ROI connections we found were only marginally significant (FDR p > 0.05 and <0.10). A further limitation is that missing demographic covariate values were imputed using the sample mean rather than being evaluated through sensitivity analyses using alternative approaches, such as listwise deletion or multiple imputation. However, given the minimal extent of missing demographic data, and the role of these variables as nuisance covariates, the potential for substantial bias in the statistical estimates is likely to be low (Enders, 2022).

Given the considerable public health challenges presented by CLBP, there are increasing efforts to identify brain-based biomarkers of CLBP using functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI). Translating neural mechanisms of pain into biomarkers of CLBP can enable clinicians to determine risk of chronic pain progression, track pain treatment responses over time, and predict which treatments will be most effective for a given patient (Van Der Miesen et al., 2019; Mackey et al., 2019; Davis et al., 2020). Understanding the subsequent correspondence between neuroimaging-based biomarkers and subjective pain report may ultimately improve our understanding of individual differences in pain sensitivity and lead to more personalized medicine approaches to pain management.

Our findings highlight the potential of rsFC among task-based ROIs in emotion regulation networks as a biomarker for CLBP. For example, activity in many of the regions highlighted by our findings, including the vlPFC, dlPFC, PCC, and amygdala, have been identified as potential brain markers of successful cognitive behavioral treatment of chronic pain (Bao et al., 2022). Although the present findings suggest that rsFC among these task-defined brain regions may represent a biomarker candidate for individual differences in pain regulation, the cross-sectional design precludes conclusions regarding predictive validity or clinical utility. Longitudinal studies are needed to determine whether the connectivity patterns described in the present study prospectively predict symptom progression, treatment response, or other clinically meaningful outcomes before they can be considered clinically suitable biomarkers. The association between increased rsFC among task-based ROIs and the degree of success in cognitive and emotional pain regulation underscores the complex relationship between neural mechanisms and behavioral responses in chronic pain. Insights from this study have the potential to pave the way for future research to develop personalized treatment strategies for CLBP, specifically by optimizing interventions for individuals based on their unique rsFC-behavioral relationship profiles. Our study advances the understanding of pain regulation in individuals with chronic pain and sets a foundation for innovative approaches in managing and treating chronic pain.

Optical coherence tomography angiography in identifying aquaporin-4 antibody-positive neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder
Authors: Jing Dong, Xiaogang Wang, Huiru Xue, Yuxian Zeng, Hongda Gong, Junhong Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundOptical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA) has shown promise in assessing microvascular and structural alterations in the retina and optic nerve. This study primarily aimed to evaluate the utility of OCTA in distinguishing aquaporin-4 antibody-positive (AQP4-Ab+) neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD) patients from healthy controls (HC). Additionally, exploratory analyses were conducted to examine differences between AQP4-Ab + and AQP4-Ab−, all MOG-Ab-negative(double-seronegative) groups in NMOSD patients.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed clinical data from NMOSD patients admitted to the Neurology Department of our First Hospital from January 2018 to December 2020. Patients were categorized into AQP4-Ab-positive (AQP4-Ab+) and double-seronegative groups, with an age-matched normal control group. OCTA examinations were conducted to compare differences in optic nerve fiber layer thickness, macular central thickness, optic disc, macular blood flow, and clinical characteristics among the three groups.ResultsThere were statistically significant gender differences among the three groups, with a higher proportion of females in the AQP4-Ab + group compared to the other two groups (P < 0.05). Structurally, significant differences were observed in all parameters between the AQP4-Ab + group and healthy controls (HC), as well as between the double-seronegative group and HC (all P < 0.05). Direct comparison between AQP4-Ab + and double-seronegative NMOSD patients revealed significant differences in specific OCTA parameters, including optic disc central vessel density (P < 0.001), inner optic disc ring vessel density (P = 0.010), inner optic disc ring superior vessel density (P = 0.008), and outer macular ring nasal vessel density (P = 0.042). However, most other OCTA parameters showed no statistically significant differences between the antibody-positive and antibody-negative subgroups (P > 0.05). The optic disc vessel density demonstrated the highest diagnostic efficiency overall, with the nasal side vessel density of the optic disc achieving an AUC of 0.829.ConclusionOCTA exhibits significant clinical utility in diagnosing AQP4-Ab + NMOSD. Differences between AQP4-Ab + and double-seronegative patients were explored and may provide further insight, although further research is required.Trial registrationNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12886-025-04394-0.

Background

Neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorders (NMOSD) represent a category of central nervous system autoimmune inflammatory demyelinating diseases, primarily affecting the optic nerve and spinal cord [1]. Optic neuritis (ON) is its most common symptom of the diseaes, occurring in 30–50% of cases, leading to visual impairment and even blindness [2]. In 2004, Lennon et al. identified aquaporin-4 antibodies (AQP4-Ab) in the serum of NMO patients, establishing NMOSD as an independent disease entity [3]. However, consensus regarding the extent of AQP4-Ab impact on ON and the pathogenic mechanisms in AQP4-Ab-positive (AQP4-Ab+) and negative (AQP4-Ab-) NMOSD remains elusive [4]. Optical coherence tomography (OCT) is widely used in NMOSD due to its non-contact, non-invasive, high-resolution, and rapid characteristics [5–7]. Our research, in addition to the previous research on retinal and optic disc structural studies, utilizes OCT angiography (OCTA) to assess macular superficial vessel density, perfusion density, optic disc vessel density, and perfusion density. Simultaneously, we differentiate the central, inner, and outer zones of the macular and optic disc areas for segmented analysis of retinal nerve fiber layer (RNFL) and ganglion cell complex (GCC) thickness. This study aims to evaluate OCTA’s utility in distinguishing NMOSD patients from healthy controls. Exploratory analyses of AQP4-Ab-positive (AQP4-Ab+) and negative (AQP4-Ab-) differences were conducted to address reviewer suggestions.

Methods

Participants and study design

A retrospective analysis was conducted on patients admitted to the Neurology Department of Shanxi Medical University First Hospital from January 2018 to December 2020 who met the Wingerchuk diagnostic criteria for NMOSD [8]. In total, 44 cases (87 eyes with ON) were included, all of whom had a history of at least one ON episode that met the diagnostic criteria of the Chinese Medical Association for ON related to NMOSD [9]. All patients were in a clinically stable phase and were selected by two associate chief neurologists or higher-ranking experts based on the criteria mentioned above. The clinically stable phase was defined as a minimum of 3 months without relapse or acute visual symptoms. Additionally, 22 healthy controls (44 eyes) were included. Ethical approval was obtained, and all participants provided informed consent. Patient demographic information, including gender, age, age of onset, disease duration, and number of relapses, was collected. Only eyes with a confirmed history of optic neuritis attack were included in the analysis. The study population was categorized into three groups: (1) AQP4-Ab + NMOSD group (hereinafter referred to as the antibody-positive group), consisting of 30 cases (60 eyes with ON); (2) both AQP4-Ab-and MOG-Ab- NMOSD group (hereinafter referred to as the antibody-negative group), comprising 14 cases (27 eyes with ON); and (3) the healthy control Group, consisting of 22 cases (44 eyes). Inclusion criteria for NMOSD patients were as follows: meeting the Wingerchuk 2015 diagnostic criteria [10]; a disease course exceeding 6 months for patients. Exclusion criteria included: (1) positive for myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein (MOG) antibodies; (2) ON recurrence within the last 6 months (excluding the impact of optic disc edema); (3) meeting the diagnostic criteria for multiple sclerosis (MS) [10]; (4) visual pathway and cortical visual central lesions caused by non-NMOSD etiologies; optic nerve, visual pathway, and cortical visual central lesions caused by glaucoma, macular degeneration, and other reasons; severe visual impairment, severe lenticular opacity with refractive power > −3.0DS, and inability to cooperate with OCT examinations; and (7) OCTA scan image quality ≤ 4.

General examination methods

Visual acuity was measured using the international standard logarithmic visual acuity chart to determine the patient’s best-corrected visual acuity. Intraocular pressure was measured using an RT-800 non-contact tonometer (Kowa American Corp., Torrance, CA, USA), with three measurements taken for each eye, and the average value was recorded.

Antibody detection

The patient’s fasting serum samples collected in our hospital were sent to the Neurological Pathology Laboratory of Peking Union Medical College Hospital for the detection of AQP4-Ab using a live cell-based assay (CBA). MOG antibodies were detected using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay kits from EUROIMMUN Medizinische Labordiagnostika AG, Germany.

Imaging protocol

Scans of the macular and optic disc regions were performed using the Cirrus HD-OCT 5000 Review Software V.10 (Carl Zeiss Meditec Inc.). Macular cube thickness and ganglion cell thickness scans utilized the Macular Cube 512 × 128 program, while nerve fiber layer thickness scans used the Optic Disc Cube 200 × 200 program. OCTA scans of the optic disc and macular areas were conducted using the Angiography 6 × 6 mm program. The software’s ONH and RNFL OU Analysis mode calculated RNFL thickness, Ganglion Cell OU Analysis mode calculated GCL thickness, and Angiography Analysis mode computed vascular density and perfusion density in the superficial layer (from the internal limiting membrane (ILM) to the inner plexiform layer (IPL)) of the optic disc and macular regions. Due to limitations in the software version, deep retinal vascular density and perfusion density data could not be obtained. Furthermore, the Angiography 6 × 6-mm program only provided vascular density and perfusion density analysis for the central, inner, and outer zones and the entire area without detailed sector analysis. All procedures were performed by a deputy chief technician with twenty years of experience in our hospital’s OCT examination room.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis and graphical representation were performed using GraphPad Prism 7 and SPSS 26 data analysis software. Continuous variables with a normal distribution, were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and compared using independent-sample t-tests. For variables not normally distributed, data were presented as median (interquartile range, IQR) and analyzed using non-parametric tests (e.g., Mann–Whitney U test). Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, where appropriate. When analyzing the ophthalmic indicators of the patients, the generalized estimating equation (GEE) was used to compare the differences between groups after adjusting for the effects of individual eye position, gender, frequency of attacks, and duration of disease. The diagnostic performance of various OCTA parameters in identifying AQP4-Ab + NMOSD was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated to assess discriminative ability. The diagnostic performance of various OCTA parameters in identifying AQP4-Ab + NMOSD was evaluated using Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves, and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated to assess discriminative ability.

Results

Analysis of differences in age, gender, visual acuity, and intraocular pressure between the two groups

A total of 44 participants (87 eyes) were included: 12 males (27.3%) and 32 females (72.7%). The average age was 48.77 ± 15.59 years. The study comprised 22 participants (44 eyes) in the healthy control (HC) Group, 14 in the antibody-positive group (27 eyes), and 30 in the antibody-negative group (60 eyes). There was a statistically significant difference in gender among the three groups (P = 0.049), with a higher proportion of females in the antibody-positive group compared to the other two groups. No statistically significant differences were observed in age among the three groups (P > 0.05). Table 1 provides basic information for the three groups of study subjects.

Comparison of macular and optic disc OCTA data between AQP4-Ab + and double-seronegative NMOSD subgroups

Direct comparisons of OCTA parameters between AQP4-Ab + and double-seronegative patients revealed statistically significant differences in several key parameters:

Significant differences were observed in outer macular ring nasal vessel density (P = 0.042). However, no statistically significant differences were observed in most other macular parameters between the subgroups (all P > 0.05).Several parameters differed significantly, notably optic disc central vessel density (P < 0.001), inner optic disc ring vessel density (P = 0.010), inner optic disc ring superior vessel density (P = 0.008), inner optic disc ring nasal vessel density (P = 0.043), inner optic disc ring perfusion density (P = 0.009), and inner optic disc ring nasal perfusion density (P = 0.047). The remaining optic disc OCTA parameters did not demonstrate statistically significant subgroup differences (P > 0.05).These findings indicate that while certain vascular changes around the optic disc and specific macular regions may distinguish AQP4-Ab + patients from double-seronegative patients, these differences were not universally present across all OCTA.

Analysis of AUC values for diagnostic indicators in the macular and optic disc regions

The diagnostic efficacy of various parameters, including macular thickness, GCC thickness, RNFL thickness measured by OCT, and superficial vessel density and perfusion density measured by OCTA in the optic disc and macular regions, was assessed using the area under the curve (AUC) values for distinguishing AQP4-Ab + NMOSD. The results are presented in Table 4; Fig. 1, and Figure S1. From Table 4, it is evident that, except for the RNFL average thickness (all P > 0.05), all other parameters showed statistically significant differences among the groups (all P < 0.05) and could effectively differentiate AQP4-Ab + NMOSD. Among these parameters, optic disc vessel density demonstrated the highest diagnostic efficacy, with AUC values exceeding 0.8. The diagnostic efficacy of macular perfusion density was similar to macular vessel density (excluding the central macula), both with AUC values exceeding 0.7. Among all evaluated OCTA parameters, nasal optic disc vessel density demonstrated the highest area under the curve (AUC = 0.829) in distinguishing AQP4-Ab + NMOSD patients from healthy controls.

DISCUSSION

NMOSD is a rare antibody-mediated autoimmune inflammatory disease affecting the central nervous system’s astrocytes. AQP4-Ab is considered a specific immunological marker for NMOSD, with approximately 50–70% of patients testing positive [11]. Previous literature has reported that AQP4-Ab + patients exhibit more severe optic nerve and retinal damage compared to AQP4-Ab- individuals. This may be attributed to the binding of AQP4 with antibodies on Müller cells in the retina, leading to significant impairment of Müller cell function under the combined involvement of the complement system [12]. this study summarized the demographic and OCTA characteristics of AQP4-Ab + and AQP4-Ab- patients., showing that the disease is more prevalent in young and middle-aged women. The proportion of females in the AQP4-Ab + group was significantly higher than in the other two groups, consistent with previous domestic and international studies [13]. AQP4-Ab + patients also showed longer disease duration and more frequent relapses compared to the double-seronegative group, suggesting more persistent and aggressive disease activity.

A recent systematic review and meta-analysis showed that vessel density was reduced in the macular and peripapillary areas in NMOSD eyes [14], supporting the reliability of OCTA findings across studies . Another meta-analysis highlighted that patients with NMOSD had lower vessel density in the superficial more than in the deep retinal circulation and a larger FAZ area. In addition, the patients with a history of ON had lower retinal perfusion than patients with NMOSD without prior ON [15]. Our OCTA analysis revealed that both AQP4-Ab + and double-seronegative NMOSD patients demonstrated significantly reduced vessel density and perfusion density compared to healthy controls, especially in the optic disc and macular regions. These findings are consistent with previous studies and meta-analyses, such as Amanollahi et al. (2024) and Courtie et al. (2024), which confirmed microvascular loss in NMOSD eyes relative to healthy individuals. This supports the utility of OCTA in distinguishing NMOSD from healthy controls.

Although we reported lower vessel and perfusion density in the AQP4-Ab + group compared to the double-seronegative group in certain regions, these differences were modest and should be interpreted with caution. However, upon direct statistical comparison between AQP4-Ab + and double-seronegative subgroups, only a limited number of OCTA parameters showed statistically significant differences.

Prior studies, including Zhang et al. (2021) [16], did not find significant differences between NMOSD subgroups. This indicates that microvascular changes may not differ substantially by antibody status and could reflect sample variability or device-related effects.

Furthermore, the nasal vessel density of the optic disc yielded the highest diagnostic value (AUC = 0.829) in identifying NMOSD. This value is relatively higher than those reported in previous studies, such as Penteado et al. [17, 18], where AUCs ranged from 0.70 to 0.75. The discrepancy may be attributed to differences in imaging platforms—our study used ZEISS Cirrus HD-OCT 5000 with AngioPlex and the OMAG algorithm, while most previous studies used AngioVue with SSADA. These technical differences in segmentation and vascular layer definition may affect vessel quantification. Studies, such as the one by Syc et al. [18], and others exploring OCTA-based microvascular evaluation in optic neuritis and glaucoma [21],confirm that different layering definitions and device algorithms can lead to data discrepancies. Researchers without analysis software may manually measure avascular areas using ImageJ, potentially leading to discrepancies. Age-related retinal blood flow changes may contribute to differences in vessel density, especially in the macular area. Younger patients with ON may exhibit more pronounced declines in indicators, possibly explaining the higher diagnostic efficacy in this study [17, 18]. This, to some extent, may explain why the diagnostic efficacy in our study is higher compared to previous results. Therefore, while OCTA shows clinical utility in distinguishing NMOSD from healthy controls, its role in differentiating AQP4-Ab + from double-seronegative remains exploratory.

Limitations

Larger, multicenter studies using standardized imaging protocols are warranted to validate these subgroup differences. In addition, the retrospective nature of the study limited the data to those available in the charts and databases. The application of eligibility criteria could have resulted in a selection bias. Secondly, it is challenging to determine whether changes in retinal structure and blood flow in unaffected eyes are related to disease recurrence and prognosis. Thirdly, the healthy controls were family members of patients who visited the ophthalmology clinic and voluntarily participated in the free ophthalmology screening program. That screening program offers free screening for many eye diseases in exchange for the use of the data for epidemiological research. Still, the program includes several examinations, and not all participants are willing to complete all of them, resulting in a relatively small number of healthy controls, reducing the pool of individuals for matching with the patients and introducing some bias. In future research, expanding the sample size and conducting follow-up observations will allow for a more in-depth exploration of the clinical value of OCT and OCTA in the diagnosis and treatment of NMOSD.

Conclusion

This study identified five optimal indicators for diagnosing AQP4-Ab + ON using OCT. These indicators include the thickness of the temporal side of the inner ring of the macula, optic disc inner nasal vessel density, optic disc inner nasal perfusion density, optic disc inner vessel density, and optic disc inner perfusion density. OCTA demonstrates potential clinical utility in detecting specific retinal and optic disc vascular changes in AQP4-Ab + NMOSD patients. Although certain parameters significantly differed between antibody-positive and antibody-negative subgroups, further validation in larger and prospective cohorts is necessary to confirm the specificity and diagnostic utility of these OCTA parameters [19–26], including studies that applied muitiple imaging protocols to neuroimmunological disorders [26].

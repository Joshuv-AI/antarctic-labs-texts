The challenge of sustaining therapeutic response in MASLD: insights from noninvasive liver assessment
Authors: Nobuharu Tamaki, Masayuki Kurosaki
Published: 2025

The prevalence of cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma resulting from metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) is increasing, posing a significant burden on both the economy and public health (1,2). Although the need for effective therapeutic strategies for MASLD is growing, pharmacologic options remain limited, with resmetirom currently the only approved drug. Consequently, lifestyle modification—particularly weight reduction through diet and exercise—remains the cornerstone of MASLD management.

A weight loss of 5–10% has been shown to improve hepatic steatosis, inflammation, and fibrosis, and this target is commonly used as a therapeutic goal (3,4). However, maintaining this weight loss over the long term remains a major challenge, as weight regain is frequently observed during follow-up (5). Despite its clinical importance, strategies to sustain the therapeutic response in MASLD have not been sufficiently investigated.

A recent study by Luo et al. explored factors associated with the loss of sustained liver improvements in patients who initially responded to standard therapy (6). Using magnetic resonance imaging-proton density fat fraction (MRI-PDFF) and liver stiffness measured by two-dimensional shear wave elastography as indicators of liver improvement, the study analyzed patients who responded at 24 weeks and assessed predictors of response loss at 48 weeks. Notably, patients with insulin resistance at week 24 were more likely to lose the MRI-PDFF response, whereas those who continued caloric restriction and regular exercise were less likely to do so. Regarding liver stiffness, higher total bile acid (TBA) levels were associated with a greater likelihood of response loss.

These findings suggest that while dietary and exercise interventions can be effective in improving liver pathology in MASLD, maintaining these improvements remains a significant clinical issue. Even among responders, a subset of patients may experience disease re-progression, indicating a potential need for continued or intensified intervention. Insulin resistance and elevated TBA levels may serve as useful markers for identifying patients at higher risk of relapse. These insights have important implications for the long-term management and maintenance of MASLD treatment.

Historically, liver biopsy was required to assess liver pathology, making longitudinal monitoring difficult. However, the development of noninvasive diagnostic modalities has enabled the evaluation of hepatic steatosis and stiffness over time in a less burdensome and more repeatable manner (7,8). MRI-PDFF response has been associated with histological improvement in fibrosis and is often used as a short-term therapeutic endpoint (9). Nonetheless, the impact of disease re-progression on fibrosis and long-term outcomes remains unclear. Further research is needed to determine how longitudinal changes in noninvasive liver parameters correlate with the prognosis of MASLD.

With novel therapeutic agents for MASLD expected to become available in the near future, the establishment of standardized methods for assessing treatment efficacy will be crucial (10). Advancing our understanding of how changes in noninvasive liver assessments reflect underlying pathological dynamics will be vital for both clinical care and the development of therapeutic endpoints.

Metaheuristics based dimensionality reduction with deep learning driven false data injection attack detection for enhanced network security
Authors: Thavavel Vaiyapuri, Huda Aldosari, Ghada Alharbi, Yassine Bouteraa, Gyanendra Prasad Joshi, Woong Cho
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Recent sensor, communication, and computing technological advancements facilitate smart grid use. The heavy reliance on developed data and communication technology increases the exposure of smart grids to cyberattacks. Existing mitigation in the electricity grid focuses on protecting primary or redundant measurements. These approaches make certain assumptions regarding false data injection (FDI) attacks, which are inadequate and restrictive to cope with cyberattacks. The reliance on communication technology has emphasized the exposure of power systems to FDI assaults that can bypass the current bad data detection (BDD) mechanism. The current study on unobservable FDI attacks (FDIA) reveals the severe threat of secured system operation because these attacks can avoid the BDD method. Thus, a Data-driven learning-based approach helps detect unobservable FDIAs in distribution systems to mitigate these risks. This study presents a new Hybrid Metaheuristics-based Dimensionality Reduction with Deep Learning for FDIA (HMDR-DLFDIA) Detection technique for Enhanced Network Security. The primary objective of the HMDR-DLFDIA technique is to recognize and classify FDIA attacks in the distribution systems. In the HMDR-DLFDIA technique, the min–max scalar is primarily used for the data normalization process. Besides, a hybrid Harris Hawks optimizer with a sine cosine algorithm (hybrid HHO‐SCA) is applied for feature selection. For FDIA detection, the HMDR-DLFDIA technique utilizes the stacked autoencoder (SAE) method. To improve the detection outcomes of the SAE model, the gazelle optimization algorithm (GOA) is exploited. A complete set of experiments was organized to highlight the supremacy of the HMDR-DLFDIA method. The comprehensive result analysis stated that the HMDR-DLFDIA technique performed better than existing DL models.

Introduction

With a great perception of inverter-based renewable energy sources besides a great scale acceptance of plug-in electric transports within the standard power segment, the control centres want to evaluate the existing working state built on the attained dimension at supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA) method1. A fast assumption of the industrial Internet of Things (IIoTs) model within the power system maintains greater consistency; still, it executes an intrinsic vulnerability as it is set to cyberattacks2. The remote terminal unit (RTU), local phasor data concentrator (PDC), and phasor measurement unit (PMU) effectively determine the dominant working state of the energy method with the help of the state estimation method existing within the energy management system (EMS)3. Currently, the crucial vulnerabilities of IIoT and RTU tools by the intruders, an innovative type of cyberattack like FDI attack (FDIA) is offered, which can effectually avoid the BDD has been employed by the operators, therefore emerging a set of false state evaluations4. Many preceding cyberattack recognition methods are model-based techniques like adaptive nonparametric cumulative sum, Kullback–Leibler distance, Kalman filter, etc.5. However, the model-based system performance is regularly complex to the parameters, and minor insecurity chiefs to poor recognition outcomes. Besides, model-based techniques want to pick the threshold, which is very complex to fix when the method is focused on loading or dynamic variations6.

Also, the model-based method needs to create good usage of the enlarging data of power grids. Data-driven models like machine learning (ML) have been used in studies in numerous fields due to the constant growth of high-performance computing hardware7. Employing the past data accumulated by power grids and the real data constantly attained by measurement tools, the data-driven model applications to many power system states are also developing. In the present scenario, ML methods have been generally implemented in the smart grid literature to observe and avert cyberattacks. Also, identifying FDI attacks is reflected as a supervised binary identification issue, which cannot identify cyber-attacks and system configurations8. Current innovation in GPU computing delivers the basis for neural networks to go “deep”. With the rapid growth of innovative metering structures that gather much information, ML and data-driven models are commonly valuable for power system tasks due to their great ability to remove beneficial data and flexible extensibility9. As inverter-based renewable energy sources and plug-in electric vehicles become more popular, safeguarding robust network security in power systems is growingly critical. The growth of the IIoTs presents new vulnerabilities, making it crucial to improve the recognition of FDI attacks. Hence, a novel approach integrating novel optimizer for dimensionality reduction and deep learning models is employed for improving attack detection and network security, addressing these growing threats in power system management10.

Related works

Han et al.11 developed an innovative FDIA localization detection technique dependent on graph data modelling and DL. This method attempts to categorize the primary data into graph-modelled data with graph topological correlation dependent upon graph theory, later designing specific networks. The developed multi-graph method and temporal relationship layer will also extract the related features to make DL under the particular graph topology for FDIA identification. In12, a phased AC FDIA was introduced to create load shedding and re-scheduling. Later, when incorrect data is inserted into the computations, the predictable conditions must be obtained from such normal conditions. The developed technique mines the spatial and spectral features employing variational mode decomposition (VMD). In13, a data-driven, unsupervised anomaly recognition technique called the Clustering-based Spectral Residual (CSR) method was introduced. The CSR model depended on the prevalent Spectral Residual and k-means clustering techniques. The integration emphasized the attack time slots and a supervised method was further developed, reliant on CNN, to improve detection accuracy in states where label data would be accessible. Naderi and Asrari14 proposed a remedial action scheme (RAS) dependent upon the notion of DL. In the development of the RAS, LSTM cells were incorporated into a deep recurrent neural network (DRNN). Power flow exploration was reflected to examine the connection amongst transmission or distribution parts for responding to cyber-attacks, which, like pre-evaluation RAS, were not kept in the IAF. In15, an innovative Spatiotemporal detection technique was presented. Later, a deep CNN could be trained to represent the functional connections amongst Spatiotemporal correlation functions and the output that could be grouped as the recognition indicator for accessing the power system with the attack or not. In16, a cyber-security DL technique was developed that precisely forecasts electric signals in electricity grids for a time horizon spanning. This introduced the DL technique, which steadily incorporates techniques like AE-CNN and LSTM (AE-CLSTM).

Yang et al.17 presented an adversarial FDIA method. Initially, it was an optimization technique. Afterwards, as the optimizer method is non-differentiable, a particle swarm optimizer (PSO)-based technique was introduced in which the near-optimum solution can be explored, guiding the false data that must be inserted. In conclusion, mathematical research of the developed attack technique has been executed under various hours with wind speed predicting systems. Hegazy et al.18 implemented a multivariate-based multi-label locational detection (MMLD) technique for identifying the existence and positions of FDIA. Li et al.19 present an FDIA detection technique utilizing secure federated deep learning by incorporating Transformer, federated learning, and the Paillier cryptosystem. The Transformer, deployed at edge nodes, employs multi-head self-attention to analyze electrical quantities. Also, the approach combines the Paillier cryptosystem with federated learning. In20, an advanced technique, namely Data-Driven Intrusion Attacks (DDIAs), is proposed. This model incorporates incomplete topological knowledge and spatio-temporal data correlations. It employs temporal and spatial attention matrices, with gated causal and graph wavelet convolutions, to improve detection and localization through spatiotemporal graph neural networks. Qu et al.21 introduce a hybrid detection method for power cyber-physical system (CPS) FDIAs that integrates an enhanced adaptive Kalman filter (AKF) with convolutional neural networks (CNNs). It enhances conventional AKF for improved filtering and speed, presents a passive detection approach employing Euclidean distance and residual recognition, and incorporates a GRU-CNN hybrid network for active detection. The methodology implements a mixed fixed-calculation formula for joint knowledge-driven and data-driven detection. Gharehchopogh et al.22 propose a novel binary multi-objective dynamic Harris Hawks Optimization (HHO) with a mutation operator (MODHHO) approach for Botnet Detection in IoT. It then performs feature selection and computes the efficiency of selected features by utilizing K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), and Decision Tree (DT) classifiers for accurate intrusion detection.

The existing detection techniques encounter various threats, encompassing computational complexity, scalability issues, and limitations in real-time performance. Models dependent on graph data modelling and DL often struggle with large systems, while load shedding and re-scheduling are inadequate for real-time recognition. Approaches, namely clustering-based spectral residual and LSTM-based remedial actions, face difficulties with dynamic data and high computational demands. Spatiotemporal recognition models utilizing deep CNNs need extensive training data and may not handle various attack scenarios effectually. Methods incorporating AE-CNN and LSTM may need help with long-term prediction accuracy, and adversarial models utilizing non-differentiable optimizers can struggle with optimization effectiveness. Secure federated learning approaches face data privacy and communication threats, and hybrid models incorporating several methods may struggle with balancing passive and active recognition. Overall, there is a clear requirement for more integrated and efficient productive models that enhance feature selection, detection accuracy, and adaptability while addressing computational and privacy constraints.

The proposed method

This article presents a new HMDR-DLFDIA method for enhanced network security. The foremost objective of the HMDR-DLFDIA method is to recognize and classify FDIA attacks in distribution systems. The HMDR-DLFDIA methodology comprises data normalization, hybrid HHO-SCA-based feature selection, SAE-based identification, and GOA-based parameter tuning. Conclusion

This article presents a new HMDR-DLFDIA method for enhanced network security. The main aim of the HMDR-DLFDIA method is to recognize and classify FDIA attacks in the distribution systems. The HMDR-DLFDIA methodology comprises different procedures, namely data normalization, hybrid HHO-SCA-based feature selection, SAE-based classification, and GOA-based parameter tuning. The HMDR-DLFDIA methodology occurs in this initial stage, and the min–max scalar is used for the data normalization process. Hybrid HHO‐SCA is also applied to feature selection. For FDIA recognition, the HMDR-DLFDIA method utilizes the SAE approach. Finally, the GOA is exploited to improve the detection results of the SAE technique. A comprehensive set of experimentations was operated to highlight the supremacy of the HMDR-DLFDIA method. The comprehensive result analysis stated the high performance of the HMDR-DLFDIA method over existing DL models. The HMDR-DLFDIA method faces limitations comprising high computational demands for real-time data normalization and optimization, which may impact its scalability and efficiency in large-scale distribution systems. The reliability of specific models, such as hybrid HHO-SCA and GOA, may also pose threats using system interoperability and adaptability to various data environments. Future studies should improve algorithms effectively to handle real-time data processing better, enhance scalability, and develop adaptive mechanisms to confirm seamless integration with diverse system models. Moreover, exploring hybrid or novel optimization methods could improve detection accuracy and system robustness.

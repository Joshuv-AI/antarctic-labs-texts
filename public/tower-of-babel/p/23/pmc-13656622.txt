Proteomic identification of NF-κB-related targets associated with the anti-inflammatory activity of Gomisin R in LPS-stimulated RAW264.7 cells
Authors: Qin Lang, Su Rong, Cheng Ya, Tang Youli, Mo Dandan, Tang Binglan, Li Rilun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundKadsura longipedunculata Finet et Gagnep. belongs to the Kadsura Kaempf.exJuss. Vaccinium of the family Magnoliaceae. It mainly distributes in regions such as Guangxi, Sichuan, Fujian and Yunnan of China. Commonly used as traditional Chinese medicine for anti-inflammation and analgesia, it has a long history in folk medicine. In recent years, research on the active components and pharmacological activities of K. longipedunculata Finet et Gagnep. has received extensive attention. Many studies have reported that lignans are the characteristic active components, among which the gomisin group of compounds has been frequently reported, including Gomisin A, G, N, and M2. Our previous research found that another such compound, Gomisin R, could significantly inhibit the secretion of inflammatory factors in LPS-induced RAW264.7 mononuclear macrophages and reduce the expression of NF-κB p65 protein. However, the anti-inflammatory signaling pathways and target sites of Gomisin R remain unclear.PurposeTo explore the anti-inflammatory signaling pathways and target sites of Gomisin R, clarify its anti-inflammatory molecular mechanism, provide experimental references for pharmacological research and clinical application, and help discover new drug treatment targets and develop new drugs.MethodsThe experimental subjects were classified into three groups: control group, LPS model group, and Gomisin R group. An in vitro inflammation model was established by inducing RAW264.7 cells with LPS. The anti-inflammatory related signaling pathways and target proteins of Gomisin R were screened by data independent acquisition (DIA) proteomics sequencing. The target proteins were verified by Western blot, and the binding activity of Gomisin R with the target proteins was predicted by molecular docking technology.ResultsCompared with the LPS model group, 163 differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) were identified from the cellular components in the Gomisin R group. 90 proteins among which were upregulated and 73 proteins were downregulated. Four enrichment pathways were screened: NF-κB signaling pathway, AMPK signaling pathway, mTOR signaling pathway, and PI3K-Akt signaling pathway. The classical inflammatory pathway was the NF-κB signaling pathway. Verification showed that Gomisin R mainly downregulated 4 proteins in the NF-κB signaling pathway: CCL4, PARP1, TNFRSF11A, and ERC1, thereby exerting anti-inflammatory effects and showing good binding activities with these 4 proteins.ConclusionThis study identified CCL4, PARP1, TNFRSF11A, and ERC1-related proteins, which may be potential molecular targets for the anti-inflammatory effect of Gomisin R. Since these four proteins are all enriched in the NF-κB signaling pathway, it is speculated that the anti-inflammatory mechanism of Gomisin R might involve regulation of the NF-κB signaling pathway to down-regulate the protein levels of CCL4, PARP1, TNFRSF11A, and ERC1, thereby to inhibit the inflammatory response. This study provides a reference for further developing Gomisin R into a small molecule natural anti-inflammatory drug.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12906-026-05476-2.

Introduction

The inflammatory response is closely related to the occurrence and development of various human diseases, including acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), rheumatoid arthritis (RA), asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, type 1 diabetes, multiple sclerosis, cancer, and viral infectious diseases such as HIV infection and influenza. During the course of these diseases, the excessive activation or uncontrolled release of the inflammatory response often leads to tissue damage and organ dysfunction, thereby exacerbating the condition. The NF-κB signaling pathway (Nuclear Factor Kappa B Signaling Pathway, NF-κB Signaling Pathway) plays a crucial role in inflammation, cell differentiation, proliferation, and survival. When the NF-κB pathway is activated, the cytokines and chemokines it mediates, such as tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin-1β (IL-1β), interleukin-6 (IL-6), prostaglandin E₂ (PGE2), RANK ligand (Receptor Activator of NF-κB Ligand, RANKL), chemokines (CKs), cell adhesion molecules (Cell Adhesion Molecules, CAMs), nitric oxide synthase (Nitric Oxide Synthase, NOS), nitric oxide (Nitric Oxide, NO), and cyclooxygenase-2 (Cyclooxygenase-2, Cox-2), among other inflammatory mediators, are expressed. These mediators can recruit immune cells, enhance local defense, increase the production of matrix metalloproteinases (Matrix Metalloproteinases, MMPs), reduce the synthesis of collagen and proteoglycans, and amplify the inflammatory signal through a positive feedback mechanism [1, 2], leading to tissue damage and persistent inflammation. At the same time, the genotoxic stress caused by inflammation triggers the transfer of the ATM-dependent NF-κB essential regulatory factor (NF-κB Essential Modulator, NEMO), also known as IκB kinase γ (IKKγ), from the nucleus to the cytoplasm, activating IκB kinase, thereby causing DNA damage, which stimulates ATM to activate the IKK kinase through the IKKγ/NEMO and ELKS (Protein enriched in essential amino acids E (glutamate), L (leucine), K (lysine), and S (serine), also known as ERC1) pathway. ELKS serves as a crucial bridge between the ATM-NEMO signal and the TAK1-IKK signal. Its ubiquitination enables ELKS to associate with TAK1 through its ubiquitin-binding subunit Table 2/3, forming a cytoplasmic complex consisting of ATM, NEMO (IKKγ), receptor-interacting protein kinase 1 (RIP1), and TAK1 [3, 4]. This process is a necessary step in transmitting DNA damage signals and activating the inflammatory response (NF-κB). Therefore, inhibiting the key proteins that induce the activation of the NF-κB pathway can alleviate the inflammatory response, providing new ideas for the treatment of inflammatory diseases.

There have been numerous reports on the anti-inflammatory effects of the gomisin family compounds from the natural products of the genus Cornus. For instance, Gomisin A [5, 6], Gomisin G [7], Gomisin N [7], Gomisin J [8], and Gomisin M2 [9, 10] all reduce inflammatory responses by inhibiting the activation of the NF-κB pathway. However, there is no report on the anti-inflammatory effect of Gomisin R at present.

Gomisin R, a compound of biphenylcyclooctene lignin [11], is the main bioactive component in the root and stem of K. longipedunculata, a medicinal plant in the family Magnoliaceae [12]. We previously discovered that Gomisin R is able to inhibit the proliferation of RAW264.7 cells induced by lipopolysaccharide (LPS) from Gram-negative bacteria in vitro, and it can reduce the secretion levels of inflammatory factors TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6; Furthermore, it can also increase the transcriptional level and protein expression of IκB-α, and reduce the transcriptional levels and protein expression levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and NF-κB p65, demonstrating significant anti-inflammatory activity [13]. There are no reports on the pharmacological mechanism of Gomisin R. The purpose of this study is to utilize DIA proteomics sequencing, Western blot, molecular docking and other techniques to screen the anti-inflammatory signaling pathways of Gomisin R, explore its target sites, predict its molecular binding activity, and thereby clarify its anti-inflammatory molecular mechanism, so that further experimental references can be provided for the pharmacological research and clinical application of this natural product.

Materials and methods

Chemicals and instruments

The Kadsura longipedunculata (in the family Magnoliaceae) voucher specimen was collected in 2010 and identified by Liu Shouyang. The specimens have been deposited in Guangxi Chinese Medicinal Materials Herbarium (affiliated with Guangxi Botanical Garden of Medicinal Plants, No. 22072).

The plant material of K. Longipedunculata was collected in March 2022 from the public area of Jinxiu County, Guangxi, China (24°08′01.0″N, 110°11′05.9″E; WGS84 datum). This species is not an endangered or protected species according to local and national laws. The collection complied with all related local and national regulations.

Gomisin R was isolated and prepared from the dried rhizomes of K. longipedunculata (Fig. 1A) [11, 12]. With molecular formula as C22H24O7, it is a white amorphous powder with a purity of ≥ 98%. Formulated into a test solution with a concentration of 10.0 µg/mL using dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), the chemical structure of Gomisin R is shown in Fig. 1B and C.

The RAW264.7 mouse monocyte-macrophage cell line was provided by the Shanghai Enzhuan Biotechnology Cell Bank.

For cell culture, high-glucose DMEM medium (G4511) and 0.25% trypsin-EDTA digestion solution containing phenol red (KGL2102-100) were obtained from Servicebio; fetal bovine serum (KGL3002-500) was purchased from KeyGEN BioTECH; cell freezing medium (SC126-02) was sourced from SEVEN BioTECH; and lipopolysaccharides (LPS, from Escherichia coli O55:B5, L2880-10) were purchased from SIGMA-ALDRICH. For protein extraction and detection, protein extraction solution (P0013C), phosphorylated protease inhibitors (P1005), BCA protein concentration assay kit (P0010), and SDS-PAGE gel preparation kit (P0057B) were all obtained from BeyoGoldTM or Servicebio; the ultra-sensitive ECL chemiluminescence kit (M2301) was from Haigene, and the prestained protein marker (10–180 kDa, 02.15003) was from EallBio. For antibodies, the reference antibody and target protein antibodies were all purchased from Biogot, including Anti-β-actin (BS00244), Anti-ERC1 (BS72401), Anti-TNFRSF11A (BS79787), Anti-PARP1 (BS7047), and Anti-CCL4 (BS41121), as well as HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG (H + L) (C030205) and HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (H + L) (C030212) secondary antibodies.

All cell culture procedures were performed in a BBS-V1800 clean workbench (Shandong Boke Group). Cells were maintained in a Scientific™ Forma™ Steri-Cycle™ carbon dioxide constant-temperature incubator (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) and observed under a DMi8 inverted biological microscope (Leica, Germany). Reagents and samples were stored in an MDF-U74V -80 °C medical low-temperature refrigerator (PHC Corporation, Japan), with temperature-controlled incubations carried out in an HWS-12 electric heating constant-temperature water bath (Shanghai Qixin Scientific Instrument Co., Ltd.).

For sample preparation, centrifugation was performed using a 5810R desktop centrifuge (Eppendorf, Germany), and cell disruption was achieved with a JY92-11 N ultrasonic cell disruptor (Ningbo Xinzi Biotechnology Co., Ltd.). Protein separation and analysis were conducted using a BG-subMIDI electrophoresis apparatus (Beijing Baojing Biotechnology Co., Ltd.) and an SDS-PAGE electrophoresis system (BIO-Rad, USA), with gel imaging captured by a GelDoc-It310 gel imaging system (UVP, USA). Chemiluminescence signals were detected with a ChemiScope 6100 chemiluminescence imaging system (CLINX Qinxiang, China), while absorbance measurements were performed on an Infinite F200 Pro multifunctional microplate reader (Tecan, Switzerland). Membrane washing was carried out on a TS-100 decolorization shaker (Haimen Qilinbel Instrument Manufacturing Company).

For metabolomic analyses, liquid chromatography was performed using a Vanquish Neo system coupled to an Orbitrap™ Astral™ mass spectrometer (both from Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA).

Cell treatment and sample preparation

RAW264.7 cells were cultured in a high-glucose DMEM medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS). A cell suspension at a concentration of 120 × 104 cells/mL was transferred to 6 cm culture dishes, with 4 mL per dish. The cells were cultured in 5% high-glucose DMEM medium for 24 h. Cultured in 5% high-glucose DMEM medium for 24 h, the cells were randomly divided into 3 groups: normal control group, LPS model group, and Gomisin R group. With 3 samples in each group, the LPS model group and the Gomisin R group were stimulated with lipopolysaccharide (LPS, 1 µg/mL) for 1 h. After that, 10.0 µg/mL of the test drug was added to the Gomisin R group and cultured for another 24 h. Then, the cells were washed with 4℃ pre-cooled 1×PBS, and the cells were scraped and centrifuged at 1000 rpm at 4℃ for 5 min. With the supernatant discarded, the cell precipitate sample was stored at -80℃ for testing.

Proteomics analysis

With the DIA (Data-independent Acquisition, DIA) quantitative proteomics technology, the extraction, detection and quantitative analysis of protein profiling in the samples were performed by Wuhan Metware Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (www.metware.cn).

LC-MS/MS detection

The extracted peptide samples were separated by nanoscale VanquishNeo system (Thermo Fisher) for chromatographic separation. The samples after nanoscale high-performance liquid chromatography separation were analyzed by OrbitrapAstral high-resolution mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific) for DIA (data-independent) mass spectrometry.

Protein quantification

The DIA-NN (v1.8.1) software was used to search the uniprot database with the Library-free method. The spectra library was utilized to re-analyze the DIA data to obtain qualitative and quantitative results of the proteins. After the software completed the protein quantification, the intensities of each protein in different samples were extracted from the search results, and then within-sample normalization was performed to obtain the relative quantitative values (R) of the proteins in different samples. The FDR (False Discovery Rate) of precursor ion and protein level identification was filtered at 1%, and the filtered data were used for bioinformatics analysis. By comparing the expression levels of all proteins in different groups, candidate differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) were selected.

For differential expression analysis, a constant value of 0.01 was added to all quantitative values prior to fold change calculation, based on approximately 1% of the minimum detectable intensity across the dataset, to avoid the denominator being zero. This approach is commonly used in proteomics to prevent the exclusion of proteins with biologically relevant “on-off” expression patterns—where a protein is confidently detected in all replicates of one group but completely absent in another group. For such cases, a P-value of 0.01 was conservatively imputed to reflect the high confidence of differential expression. To ensure the robustness of our findings, DEPs identified in this strategy were further validated using alternative imputation methods (e.g., KNN or minimum value imputation).

Bioinformatics analysis

For the DEPs in each comparison group, Gene Ontology (GO) and KEGG pathway functional annotations and enrichment analyses were conducted. The enrichment analysis used hypergeometric calculation to determine the significance of the P-value for enrichment, aiming to discover whether the DEPs have a significant enrichment trend in certain functional types compared to all the identified proteins. Through significance enrichment analysis, we determined the main biochemical metabolic pathways and signal transduction pathways that the DEPs are involved in, and analyzed the effect of Gomisin R on the proteome profile. The protein classification analysis of Gene Ontology (GO) was performed using the DAVID1 database, while the signal pathway enrichment analysis was done with the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) (https://www.genome.jp/kegg/). Based on the results of the KEGG signal pathway enrichment analysis, the target proteins were selected.

Detection of protein expression by western blot

The expression level of the target protein in the cell samples was detected by Western Blot method. The cell samples were added with lysis buffer, mixed well, and then ultrasonicated at 20% power in ice bath for 3 × 10 s. With the temperature remaining at 4℃, the samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min, and the supernatant was collected. The protein concentration was determined by BCA protein quantification method. Based on the protein quantification results, the corresponding volume of total protein samples was mixed with 5× protein gel electrophoresis loading buffer and subjected to 95℃ denaturation for 10 min for SDS-PAGE electrophoresis. After transfer, antibody incubation was performed, and the NC membrane was placed in the chemiluminescence imaging system for color development and imaging. The gray value of the bands was measured using ImageJ software, and the relative expression level of the protein was calculated.

Molecular docking to evaluate the binding affinity of Gomisin R to the target protein

In this study, the three-dimensional structures of the four target proteins (ERC1, TNFRSF11A, PARP1, and CCL4) were all obtained using the full-length prediction model of AlphaFold3 (AF3), serving as the initial structural basis for molecular docking. Considering the differences in confidence levels in different regions of the AF3 model, subsequent analyses were based on the pLDDT score to evaluate the structural reliability, and the high-confidence structural regions were prioritized for the analysis of binding sites. All proteins were preprocessed uniformly before docking, including removing water molecules and common free ions. The hydrogenation and charge state correction were completed using PDB2PQR/PROPKA under pH = 7.4. Small molecules were generated in their possible protonated states by Dimorphite-DL, with pH = 7.0-7.8, and their three-dimensional conformations were constructed using RDKit. Then, they were geometrically optimized using the OpenBabel MMFF94 force field before entering the docking calculation. Molecular docking was completed using AutoDock4. The docking sites were determined based on the prediction of fpocket, and the top-ranked candidate pocket was selected as the docking region. During the docking process, multiple binding conformations were generated, and representative conformations were selected based on the binding energy score and clustering results for subsequent analysis. Interaction analysis was performed using PLIP, and two-dimensional and three-dimensional visualizations were presented using LigPlot + and PyMOL.

The magnitude of the binding free energy can be used as an important reference for evaluating the binding strength between Gomisin R and the target protein. A binding free energy of less than − 4.25 kcal/mol indicates a certain binding activity between Gomisin R and the target protein, while a value less than − 5.0 kcal/mol indicates a better binding activity. The smaller numerical value (larger absolute value) corresponds to more stable binding and higher affinity.

Statistical analysis

All data are expressed as mean ±standard error of the mean (SEM) from at least three independent experiments. Statistical significance was determined by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test for multiple comparisons. A P-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA).

By conducting principal component analysis (PCA) on the samples, we aimed to preliminarily understand the overall protein differences among the various groups of samples and the degree of variation within each group of samples. Through the analysis of the correlations between the samples, the biological duplicates within the observation group were observed. Meanwhile, high correlation coefficient of the samples within the group points to high reliability of the obtained differential proteins. The Pearson’s Correlation Coefficient (R) was used as the evaluation index for the biological duplicate correlation. A value of |R| that is close to 1 corresponded to strong correlation between the two samples. The results were shown in Fig. 2B. The Pearson correlation coefficient R values among the samples within each of the three groups were all greater than 0.97, indicating that the biological reproducibility was good and the results of the differential protein screening were reliable. The differences in the correlation coefficient between the Control group and the Model group were relatively small, indicating that the protein changes caused by LPS stimulation in establishing the disease model were relatively mild, rather than a drastic global reorganization. There was a significant separation between the Gomisin R treatment group and the model group, indicating that the drug intervention caused a more pronounced shift in the protein expression profile.

Analysis of protein differential expression in RAW264.7 cells induced by LPS by Gomisin R

In this study, the DIA-NN (version 1.8.1) software was used to conduct qualitative and quantitative analysis of the mass spectrometry data. The total number of identified peptide segments was 130,285, and of quantified proteins was 8,495. Based on the screening conditions of fold change (FC) ≥ 1.5 or FC ≤ 0.6667, and P-value ≤ 0.05, 654 significantly DEPs were obtained. Among them, the number of up-regulated proteins in the LPS model group/normal control group was 191, with 300 down-regulated proteins; in the Gomisin R group/LPS model group, the number of up-regulated proteins was 90, with 73 down-regulated proteins, as shown in Fig. 3A. The significantly DEPs of each group were normalized by z-score, and a clustering heatmap was drawn, as shown in Fig. 3B. The protein expression levels among samples in the same group were similar, while proteins in different groups showed significant up-regulation (high expression) or down-regulation (low expression) changes. The 654 DEPs were imported into the String DB (http://string-db.org/) database for protein-protein interaction (PPI) analysis, and it was found that 588 of the DEPs had interactions, as shown in Fig. 3C.

Bioinformatics analysis for the Gomisin R-regulated signaling networks

Differential expression protein GO function annotation and enrichment analysis

To investigate the effect of Gomisin R on the protein group changes, GO function annotation enrichment analysis was conducted on these proteins that specifically responded to Gomisin R treatment, and they were classified according to biological process, GO cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF). The top 50 GO-Terms with the most significant P-values (ranked from smallest to largest) in the enrichment analysis were selected to generate a bar chart (Fig. 4A). 49 proteins were involved in biological processes such as regulation of transcription by RNA polymerase II, vesicle-mediated transport, and response to organic substances; 79 proteins were involved in cellular components such as endoplasmic reticulum, intracellular membrane-bound organelle, and endoplasmic reticulum membrane. 86 proteins were involved in molecular functions such as DNA binding, calcium ion binding, protein homodimerization activity, zinc ion binding, and RNA binding.

Due to the acute inflammation induced by lipopolysaccharide (LPS), the NF-κB signaling pathway is widely regarded as a classic inflammatory pathway. On the NF-κB signaling pathway, there are 4 proteins enriched: endoplasmic reticulum protein (ERC1), NF-κB receptor activator (TNFRSF11A), poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase 1 (PARP1), and chemokine ligand 4 (CCL4). The regulatory effects on the expression of these 4 proteins may be the anti-inflammatory targets of Gomisin R. Therefore, the research group further verified the expression regulation of these 4 target proteins.

Verification of the expression differences of the target proteins by western blot method

To verify the effects of Gomisin R on the four target proteins that are enriched in the NF-κB signaling pathway (ERC1, NF-κB receptor activator (TNFRSF11A), poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase 1 (PARP1), and chemokine ligand 4 (CCL4)), the research team used the Western Blot to detect the expression levels of these four proteins (Fig. 5). After LPS stimulation, NF-κB was highly activated, resulting in a significant increase in the expression levels of the four members (ERC1, TNFRSF11A, PARP1, and CCL4). The predicted structure of CCL4 protein AlphaFold3 was overall reliable. The average pLDDT of the model was 86.81, suggesting a high level of confidence in the structure. The CCL4 structure, verified by the Ramachandran plot, showed that 92.7% of the residues were in the optimal allowed region, and no residues were in the disallowed region (0%), indicating a reasonable main chain conformation, which could be used for subsequent molecular docking analysis.

The ERC1 protein was a long-chain coiled-coil scaffold protein, with an average pLDDT of 74.38, showing moderate reliability. The high confidence region mainly concentrated on the core coiled-coil structural domain, which was relatively stable in conformation. Therefore, in this study, the binding site analysis was mainly conducted based on the high confidence structural region during the docking process. The Ramachandran analysis of the ERC1 protein showed that the main chain conformation was overall within the allowed region, and the geometry met the requirements for structural rationality. However, considering its long-chain coiled-coil scaffold feature, there was some flexibility and conformational uncertainty in the local region of this protein. Therefore, the subsequent molecular docking mainly focusee on the stable middle coiled-coil region.

The PARP1 protein was predicted using AlphaFold3 and it had a relatively high overall structure confidence (average pLDDT = 90.62). The Ramachandran plot analysis showed that the main chain conformation was mainly distributed in the optimal allowed region, and the model had good reliability.

TNFRSF11A used the full-length structure predicted by AlphaFold3 as the research basis. Considering that this protein belongs to the TNF receptor superfamily member, its extracellular region is composed of multiple cysteine-rich domains (CRDs), while the transmembrane and intracellular regions have higher flexibility characteristics. Therefore, the full-length structure showed differentiated confidence distribution in different regions. The pLDDT score analysis of the AF3 model showed that the extracellular CRD region of this protein had relatively high structural confidence, while the transmembrane and some terminal regions had lower confidence. Combined with the Ramachandran plot analysis results, the main chain conformation was overall within the allowed region, and no significant geometric abnormalities were observed, indicating that the model was reasonable at the stereoscopic level.

Discussion

In this study, Gomisin R treatment significantly downregulated the expressions of ERC1, TNFRSF11A, PARP1 and CCL4, all of which were related to the function of the NF-κB signaling pathway - indicating that Gomisin R might exert its anti-inflammatory effect by regulating multiple nodes within this pathway.

ERC1 (ELKS) is an essential regulatory subunit of the IkappaB kinase (IKK) complex. It may function by recruiting IkappaBα into the IKK complex, thereby regulating the activation of IKK [14], and thereby participating in the NF-κB activation pathway, playing a key role in cell pyroptosis, and influencing the release of inflammatory factors [15].

The tumor necrosis factor receptor superfamily member 11 A gene (TNFRSF11A), also known as the receptor activator of NF-κB (RANK), can activate multiple signaling pathways such as NF-κB, JNK, ERK, p38α, and Akt/PKB [16]. During inflammation, RANK binds to its ligand RANKL, leading to the recruitment of TNF receptor-associated factor (TRAF) family proteins, such as TRAF2/6, and subsequently activating the key kinase complex TAK1 (transforming growth factor β-activated kinase 1)-TAB (TAK1-binding protein). TAK1 serves as the core switch for activating the downstream IKK complex [17], which is composed of IKKα/IKK1, IKKβ/IKK2, and the scaffold protein NEMO (IKKγ) [18]. The activated IKK complex phosphorylates the NF-κB inhibitory protein IκBα. Phosphorylated IκBα is ubiquitinated and degraded, thereby releasing the NF-κB dimer (p50/p65), which then translocates to the nucleus to activate the downstream NF-κB signaling pathway, driving the transcription of target genes and inducing the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines. These factors further recruit and activate immune cells, increase vascular endothelial cell permeability, and promote the persistence and development of the inflammatory response [19].

PARP1 is a PARP enzyme, which is regarded as a DNA damage sensor by recognizing single-strand and double-strand DNA damage [20]. On one hand, to cope with minor DNA damage, PARP1 catalyzes nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) and adenosine triphosphate (ATP) to add adp-ribose polymer to various nuclear proteins, assembling DNA repair proteins, and initiating the DNA repair process [21, 22]. on the other hand, excessive DNA damage stimulates the overactivation of PARP1, thereby increasing the consumption of NAD + and ATP, forming poly(adp-ribose) (PAR). Subsequently, PAR is translocated from the nucleus to the cytoplasm and mitochondria, causing mitochondrial depolarization, mitochondrial release and nuclear translocation of apoptosis-inducing factor (AlF), ultimately leading to chromatin condensation, DNA breakage and cell death, known as parthanatos [23].

CCL4, which is also known as MIP-1β, is a CC chemokine that functions in the recruitment of immune cells during immune responses [24]. After binding to its receptor CCR5, CCL4 stimulates the NF-κB signaling pathway, triggering the downstream phosphorylation of P65 [25], resulting in an increased level of p-P65 in inflammatory cells. Additionally, CCL4 may also play a role in inducing MMP-13, leading to enhanced ECM degradation and increased ROS production, ultimately resulting in the apoptosis of inflammatory cells [26].

In conclusion, the anti-inflammatory mechanism of Gomisin R may involve multiple targets and pathways to prevent the degradation of IκBα and the nuclear translocation of NF-κB, thereby synergistically inhibiting the inflammatory response (Fig. 8). This mode of action makes it potentially valuable for the treatment of diseases related to excessive activation of NF-κB, such as inflammation.

Conclusion

Gomisin R antagonized the activation of the NF-κB pathway in LPS-induced RAW264.7 cells, manifested as the downregulation of the expression of ERC1, TNFRSF11A, PARP1, and CCL4, and exhibited good binding activity in molecular docking. Based on these findings, the inhibitory effect of Gomisin R on the expression of inflammatory genes may be achieved by blocking the activation of the NF-κB pathway. Although the data from this experiment indicate a correlation between the treatment with Gomisin R and the expression of ERC1, TNFRSF11A, PARP1, and CCL4 proteins, further experiments using siRNA knockdown/overexpression will be necessary to establish a direct causal relationship between them.

In conclusion, through the classical RAW264.7 macrophage model stimulated by lipopolysaccharide, and the integrated research which combines DIA proteomics and molecular biology analysis, this study suggests that Gomisin R may exert anti-inflammatory effects through the modulation of the protein levels of ERC1, TNFRSF11A, PARP1, and CCL4, which is signaled and negatively regulated with relation to NF-κB. This research provides new ideas and approaches for the treatment of inflammatory diseases, offering a feasible strategy for the application of natural products with anti-inflammatory effects.

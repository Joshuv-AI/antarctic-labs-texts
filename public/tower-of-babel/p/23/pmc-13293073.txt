AGAMOUS (AG) is a transcription factor (TF) whose role in specifying reproductive organs was first cloned in Arabidopsis thaliana. Since then, AG-like TFs with a variety of independent and overlapping activities have been identified in numerous plant species. However, to understand the precise function of a TF, careful molecular and physiological analyses must be performed. Gong et al. (2026) recently characterized a set of four AG-like TFs in rice that are discussed in the context of floral specification models.

In eudicots, flowers are structured in whorls, with the first whorl containing sepals, the second whorl containing petals, the third whorl containing stamens, and the fourth whorl containing carpels. In monocots, while the internal whorls composed of reproductive organs remain the same, the first whorl is instead composed of lemma and palea while the second whorl is composed of lodicules (Whipple and Schmidt, 2006). The ABC(DE) model was proposed as a way to describe the TF activities that are required for the correct specification of floral organs (Fig. 1) (Coen and Meyerowitz, 1991). Most genes conforming to the ABC(DE) model encode MADS-box TFs. The MADS box family is found in the majority of eukaryotes, with the highest numbers being found in land plants and flowering plants in particular (reviewed in Dreni and Kater, 2014).

Simplified schematic of the ABC(DE) model in rice. According to the model, the primary activities of Class A transcription factors (TFs) is to specify the first (palea and lemma) and second (lodicules) whorls, the activities of Class B TFs is to specify the second and third (stamen) whorls, while the activity of Class C TFs is to specify the third and fourth (carpels) whorls (i.e. all reproductive structures). The function of Class D proteins is to specify the ovule, while Class E TFs are involved in controlling specification of all floral tissues. Additionally, Class A and C TFs are mutually antagonistic. It is unclear in rice if Class C proteins contribute to the specification of carpels or if this function is primarily controlled by the YABBY TF, DROOPING LEAF (DL). Based on the mutant phenotype of null ag alleles from Arabidopsis thaliana, it was thought that the primary function of AG was in reproductive organ specification (Yanofsky et al., 1990). Since then, additional functions of AG have been reported in organ differentiation (Ito et al., 2004, 2007), and in repressing the development of leaf-like traits including trichomes and stomata (Ó’Maoiléidigh et al., 2018; Brazel et al., 2023). In rice, five AG-like genes have been reported that potentially act as Class C and D TFs (Box 1): OsMADS3, OsMADS58, and OsMADS66 in the AG lineage and OsMADS13 and OsMADS21 in the AGL11 lineage, with OsMADS66 likely to be a pseudogene (Dreni et al., 2013).

Rice Class C and D transcription factors

The two MADS box TFs in rice most closely related to AG are OsMADS3 and OsMADS58. However, it remains unclear if OsMADS3 and OsMADS58 act as Class C TFs specifying the identity of both stamens and carpels in rice (Fig. 1). While rice with reduced OsMADS58 expression showed no changes in stamen specification, osmads3 knockouts showed defects in stamen morphology (Yamaguchi et al., 2005; Dreni et al., 2011). More recent characterization of null mads58 mutants generated with gene editing showed defects in stamen development (Shen et al., 2022). Analyses of double knockout mutants has suggested redundant roles for OsMADS3 and OsMADS58 as Class C TFs involved in specifying all reproductive structures in rice and controlling floral meristem determinacy (Dreni et al., 2011). However, other reports have suggested that lines with reduced expression of OsMADS3 or OsMADS58 show no defects in carpel morphology (Yamaguchi et al., 2005) while double knockout mutants still possess ‘carpel like organs’ (Sugiyama et al., 2019). It has been proposed that the specification of carpel identity in rice may be primarily controlled by the action of the YABBY TF, DROOPING LEAF (DL), and that OsMADS3 and OsMADS58 are not involved in controlling carpel specification (Sugiyama et al., 2019).

In A. thaliana, Class D TFs include SEEDSTICK (STK), SHATTERPROOF 1 (SHP1), and SHP2 (Favaro et al., 2003). Phylogenetic analyses have shown that OsMADS13 and OsMADS21 are paralogues of each other that belong to the D-lineage family (Dreni et al., 2007). While previous work showed a clear role for OsMADS13 as a Class D TF in specifying ovule identity as well as floral meristem determinacy, the role of OsMADS21 is less clear. Single mutant osmads13 lines have shown defects in ovule specification and floral meristem determinacy, whereas no defects were identified in osmads21 single mutants (Dreni et al., 2007, 2011). No additive effect was found for the osmads21 mutation in the osmads13 background (Dreni et al., 2007).

Gong et al. (2026) generated overexpression lines for OsMADS3, OsMADS58, OsMADS13, and OsMADS21 in rice using native promoters, resulting in elevated transgene expression that overlaps with endogenous expression patterns. The lines were each characterized through morphological analyses, and genome-wide assessments of their direct targets, in addition to investigating their trans-activating TFs and protein interaction networks. The authors showed clear overlapping roles for multiple AG-like TFs in floral morphogenesis and identification, while also dissecting the subtle functional differences between these TFs.

OsMADS3 and OsMADS58: rice Class C transcription factors?

To characterize the functions of OsMADS3 and OsMADS58, Gong et al. (2026) studied floral morphology in overexpression lines for these two AG-like TFs. These lines were generated with fragments of the native gene promoters, and the transgenes showed similar but at times ectopic expression patterns compared with corresponding native genes in wild-type plants. Gene expression of OsMADS3 and OsMADS58 was detected in overexpression lines in lodicules, stamens, and pistils. The authors showed that overexpression of OsMADS3 caused homeotic transformation of lodicules to pistil-like organs, an increase in carpel numbers, and defects in other floral organs (Table 1). The homeotic transformation of lodicules to pistils in overexpression rice lines is reminiscent of phenotypes observed in A. thaliana where ectopic overexpression of AG could cause transformation of first and second whorl floral organs into pistil- or stamen-like organs (Mizukami and Ma, 1992).

Morphological analyses of OsMADS3 overexpression lines also revealed roles in controlling anther and seed development (Gong et al., 2026). Severe defects were identified during anther development, pollen maturity, and seed development. OsMADS3 overexpression lines showed a reduction in the amount of fully mature pollen and fully developed seeds per panicle that was correlated with the increased level of transgene expression in each individual line. This is similar to defects in late stamen and pollen development that were observed in overexpression lines of AG in A. thaliana (Mizukami and Ma, 1992) presumably caused by a disruption of downstream factors controlling these processes (Ito et al., 2004, 2007). The phenotypes observed by Gong et al. (2026) suggest that OsMADS3 may be involved in controlling stamen specification, data which are supported by evidence from previous studies of osmads3 knockout lines (Yamaguchi et al., 2005; Dreni et al., 2011). Conversely, the authors observed no morphological differences compared with wild-type flowers in any floral features of OsMADS58 overexpression lines, potentially due to technical limitations in achieving high-level OsMADS58 transgene expression.

OsMADS13 and OsMADS21: rice Class D transcription factors?

The OsMADS13 and OsMADS21 overexpression lines generated by Gong et al. (2026) have helped to shed light on the involvement of these two TFs in floral specification. Gene expression of OsMADS13 was confined to the developing pistil and ovules, while OsMADS21 was expressed at a lower level but was also found in stamens in wild-type plants (Dreni et al., 2007; Gong et al., 2026). In overexpression lines, OsMADS13 and OsMADS21 expression expanded into the first, second, and third whorls (Gong et al., 2026). Homeotic transformation of lodicules to pistil-like organs was observed in all OsMADS21 and some OsMADS13 overexpression lines, while defects were also observed in the first whorl tissues (Table 1). All OsMADS13 and OsMADS21 overexpression lines showed an increase in carpel number but also defects in stamen morphology. They also showed defects in pollen maturation and seed set that were correlated with the increased level of transgene expression in each individual line. This suggests that OsMADS3, OsMADS13, and OsMADS21 have convergent roles in anther and seed development. Gong et al. (2026) further investigated split hull seed phenotypes observed in OsMADS13 and OsMADS21 overexpression lines, with the former showing the strongest defects in seed morphology and also uniquely exhibiting severely reduced germination rates and enhanced fungal growth.

Divergent functions were identified for the AG-like overexpression lines. Overexpression lines for OsMADS13 and OsMADS21 were both shown to have differences in their flowering time. Overexpression lines for OsMADS13 and OsMADS21 showed late and early flowering, respectively, compared with the wild type. ChIP-seq analysis showed that both TFs were associated with flowering time-related genes, for example members of the AP1-like family. Expression of OsMADS14 and OsMADS15 was down-regulated in the late-flowering OsMADS13 overexpression lines while OsMADS14 was mildly up-regulated in the early-flowering OsMADS21 overexpression lines. Previous work showed that knockdown of OsMADS14, OsMADS15, and OsMADS18 by RNAi can cause a delay in flowering time (Kobayashi et al., 2012). This suggests that OsMADS13 and OsMADS21 may be regulating flowering time in rice via changes in downstream flowering time gene expression.

No transcription factor is an island

TFs act by binding to specific genomic motifs, at times as part of multimeric complexes, to change the expression levels of target genes. Quaternary MADS-domain complexes are known to control floral organ specification in A. thaliana, and ternary MADS-domain complexes have already been identified for Class B rice MADS-domain proteins (Seok et al., 2010). A yeast two-hybrid screen was performed with each of OsMADS3, OsMADS58, OsMADS13, and OsMADS21 to identify interacting proteins (Gong et al., 2026). While ∼30–50 interacting proteins were identified for each of the AG-like TFs, a set of seven MADS-domain TFs were confirmed to interact with all four AG-like TFs. This work confirms that multimeric complexes of MADS-domain TFs exist in rice flowers that may be important in floral specification.

To elucidate the molecular mechanisms in which these AG-like TFs are involved, ChIP-sequencing was performed in the overexpression lines. OsMADS3, OsMADS13, and OsMADS21 were shown to preferentially bind to CArG box motifs (Gong et al., 2026), the TF-binding motif for MADS-domain TFs that is conserved across all land plants (Zenker et al., 2025). The authors found that nearly 1500 genes were associated with binding by OsMADS3, OsMADS13, and OsMADS21. Gene Ontology (GO) analyses of this set revealed terms associated with floral organ and seed development. In addition, ∼30% of genes with known functions in anther and pollen development were associated with binding of at least one of these three AG-like TFs, with a number of these direct targets showing altered levels of gene expression in the AG-like overexpression lines.

Relatively few targets of OsMADS58 were detected by ChIP-seq (Gong et al., 2026). Earlier work identified a putative list of >500 genomic regions associated with OsMADS58 (Chen et al., 2015); however, only two of these targets overlapped with the list of ∼200 OsMADS58 targets identified by Gong et al. (2026). Notably, GO analyses of results from yeast two-hybrid screening of OsMADS58 interactors revealed a number of terms relating to floral organ specification and in particular gynoecium development, thus indicating that OsMADS58 plays a role in these processes.

Interestingly, the authors found that genes relating to seed vigour and broad-spectrum blast resistance were associated with OsMADS13 binding and showed enhanced gene expression in OsMADS13 overexpression lines, including OsGLP2-1 and OsNH5N16. Both OsGLP2-1 and OsNH5N16 have previously been shown to enhance resistance to fungal pathogens in overexpression lines according to disease assays using infected leaves (Liu et al., 2016; Son et al., 2021). OsGLP2-1 and OsNH5N16 expression may be induced by the fungal infections present in the OsMADS13 overexpression seeds. OsGLP2-1 overexpression has been shown to enhance seed dormancy (Wang et al., 2020), potentially contributing to the reduced germination rate observed in OsMADS13 overexpression lines (Gong et al., 2026).

The physiological and biochemical data provided in this study further strengthen the evidence to support a role for OsMADS3, OsMADS13, and OsMADS21 in specifying reproductive tissues in rice. The molecular characterizations also implicate OsMADS58 as an interaction partner for many rice MADS TFs. However, further characterization is required to understand the potentially divergent roles of AG-like TFs identified in this study in controlling flowering time and pathogen response.

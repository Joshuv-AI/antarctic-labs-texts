Industrial internet data management framework with blockchain integration for data integrity assurance and access control resolution
Authors: Jiaqi Han, Baorong Wang, Jiawen Han, Tian Xia, Liqun Wu, Wenxia Zhang, Yan Chen, Xiangzhen Peng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Amid the wave of digitalization, blockchain technology has emerged as the times re-quire, leveraging its characteristics such as decentralization and immutability. The Industrial Internet is experiencing vigorous development; however, it faces challenges including poor data integrity and difficulties in data access. Therefore, the integration of these two technologies holds significant importance—it can enhance the integrity and accuracy of industrial data, improve the efficiency of data interaction, enable efficient cross-enterprise data access control, and usher in a new journey of intelligent manufacturing and digital transformation. This study first constructs a data management model based on blockchain and the Industrial Internet, and classifies the application scenarios of different data users. Subsequently, it employs the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) and the Hungarian Algorithm to research the multi-task collaboration mechanism of this model, aiming to enhance the model’s stability and the efficiency of task processing. Furthermore, it formulates appropriate smart contracts based on the model’s application scenarios. Finally, a prototype system for data management is built to analyze the model’s results and conduct system verification. The results indicate that this study effectively addresses the current issues in industrial data, such as poor integrity and difficult access. It provides new ideas and solutions for improving the security, access efficiency, and integrity of industrial data, thus possessing important practical significance and application value.

Introduction

In industrial scenarios, data integrity and rapid access are core pillars for ensuring production operations, decision-making efficiency, and safety compliance1. Data integrity guarantees that industrial data is authentic, accurate, complete, and untampered with, serving as the foundation for the reliable operation of industrial systems. If data is distorted or missing, it may lead to misjudgment of equipment failures, deviations in production quality, and even trigger safety accidents2,3. Rapid access, on the other hand, is directly linked to the real-time requirements of industrial scenarios: in the production process, it is necessary to retrieve historical equipment data in milliseconds to optimize process parameters; when equipment malfunctions, it is required to quickly search maintenance records to locate problems; and in supply chain collaboration, it is essential to timely obtain inventory and order data4,5. The combination of the two not only avoids production halts and economic losses caused by data issues, but also provides support for data analysis in the Industrial Internet and smart manufacturing, serving as a key prerequisite for advancing the digital transformation of the industrial sector6.

The rise of the Industrial Internet is rooted in profound and diverse backgrounds7. On one hand, the innovation of information technology serves as a key driving force. With the rapid development of cutting-edge technologies such as big data, cloud computing, and artificial intelligence, these technologies provide solid support for data collection, storage, analysis, and application in the industrial field, making the connection of massive devices and the management of complex processes possible8–10. From the perspective of the industrial competition pattern, the global manufacturing industry is in a critical period of transformation and upgrading. Traditional manufacturing is confronted with predicaments such as rising costs, efficiency bottlenecks, and severe product homogeneity, and it is in urgent need of reshaping its competitiveness with the help of new technologies11. The Industrial Internet enables the interconnection of production factors, realizes the precise allocation of resources, helps enterprises reduce costs and increase efficiency, moves towards smart manufacturing, and promotes the intelligent transformation of the manufacturing industry and even the entire industrial system12,13.

In today’s digital era, data security and integrity are of crucial importance, and blockchain technology plays a unique role. First, its decentralized nature eliminates the vulnerability of a single central server being prone to attacks. Data is stored in a distributed manner across multiple nodes, reducing the risk of the entire system being compromised14,15. The encryption algorithms of blockchain ensure the confidentiality of data transmission and storage, preventing the leakage of sensitive information16. Furthermore, its immutable ledger records maintain the authenticity and integrity of data. Any modification will leave traces, enabling convenient traceability and building a solid defense for data security across various industries17.

In the process of industrial digitalization, the integrity and rapid access of industrial data are crucial, and the integration of blockchain and the Industrial Internet is an important approach to solving this problem18. Currently, industrial data is vulnerable to tampering and loss, and the existence of “data silos” leads to slow access. The combination of blockchain’s decentralized and immutable characteristics with the Industrial Internet’s device interconnection capability can ensure data integrity from the source19,20. Data is uploaded to the blockchain for certification at the time of collection, which prevents data distortion caused by human factors or equipment failures and provides a reliable basis for industrial production quality traceability and equipment fault diagnosis21. Meanwhile, this integration breaks down data silos. By leveraging distributed storage and efficient transmission technologies, it enables fast cross-platform and cross-enterprise data access, meets the needs of scenarios such as real-time production scheduling and supply chain collaboration, facilitates the intelligent upgrading of the industrial sector, and enhances production efficiency and competitiveness22,23.

Given the growing volume of data, which brings significant pressure on data storage capacity, fails to guarantee data integrity and security, and makes data access control more difficult, coupled with incomplete standards and specifications as well as the lack of unified guidelines for data interaction and collaboration between different systems, this paper proposes industrial internet data management framework with blockchain integration for data integrity assurance and access control resolution. It provides a general model for the application of blockchain technology and Industrial Internet technology in industrial data management. It can ensure data security and trusted sharing, enhance data quality and credibility, optimize resource allocation, and promote industrial collaborative development and intelligent upgrading.

The rest of the article is organized as follows:

Section 2 introduces the research status and existing shortcomings of protecting data security, integrity, and access control based on blockchain and industrial Internet; Sect. 3 constructs a data management model based on blockchain and industrial Internet, and designs the smart contracts and API interfaces of the model; Sect. 4 studies the task collaboration mechanism of the data management model based on blockchain and industrial Internet, and builds a prototype system to verify the model; Sect. 5 summarizes the full text.

The above studies have certain limitations. Most of the studies focus on specific industries and lack cross-domain universality; some rely on specific technologies, resulting in limited adaptability; moreover, most are theoretical models with insufficient verification in practical applications, and face numerous challenges such as performance, cost, and supervision that need to be addressed during actual implementation.

Reference31 proposes a collaborative architecture for the Industrial Internet Platform, termed the Industrial Operating System. It includes industrial drivers, digital threads, and microservices, providing a better Collaborative Enterprise Information System environment for manufacturing systems. It provides edge-layer industrial drivers for each resource unit, supporting communication, computing, control, identification, insight, and interoperability, and enables a “plug-and-play” approach in the IIP. Reference32 proposes establishing a game model for a platform ecosystem composed of Industrial Internet platform enterprises, industrial chain completers, and innovation chain completers. For instance, some studies only focus on specific links or scenarios, and their overall systematicity needs to be enhanced; Most empirical studies focus on a single region or industry, making their generalizability questionable; they do not fully consider the complex and changing actual industrial environment, and still need to be improved in terms of the dynamic adaptability of security and privacy protection and cross-platform compatibility.

In terms of data security management based on the Industrial Internet and blockchain, Reference33 proposes the concept of a blockchain and Industrial Internet connector suitable for data traceability in the grain and oil industry, thereby constructing a traceability model for grain and oil quality and safety. They also designed a quality and safety traceability system for wheat, providing a solution for tracing data and information in the wheat supply chain. Reference34 proposes a DT-BC enhanced manufacturing collaboration mechanism for Industrial Internet platforms to improve data accuracy and trust among participants, as well as strengthen manufacturing service management. To address issues in traditional industrial IoT such as low security of blockchain data transmission, high transaction management costs, and difficult supervision, Reference35 proposes a blockchain-based trusted management model for industrial Internet identity analysis data. The identity analysis data information is analyzed and classified, and a collaborative consensus mechanism suitable for the master-slave multi-chain of the industrial Internet is also designed. This model can be applied to the trusted management of identity analysis data information in the industrial Internet and also provides an optimized solution for similar problems in the industrial Internet field. Reference36 proposes an IIoT data transmission security technology based on Fabric blockchain. This technology implements a reliable transaction center through the Power Chain sharing model, and achieves secure matching of Power data transmission by designing the Power Data consensus mechanism and dynamic link storage. It also enables secure matching of power data transmission. Studies on data management using the Industrial Internet and blockchain have the following shortcomings: they mostly focus on applications in specific fields, resulting in limited generalizability; they are inclined to theoretical construction with insufficient practical verification; they lack considerations for cross-system collaboration and performance optimization in complex environments, and their ability to handle large-scale data and emergencies needs to be verified. Overall, the practical application still faces many challenges.

By analyzing the above studies, it can be concluded that existing research on data integrity and access security management based on the Industrial Internet and blockchain has many problems. For example, the scalability of data storage is insufficient, making it difficult to cope with the growth of massive data; data integrity cannot be guaranteed; the data interoperability between different blockchain systems is poor, resulting in data silos; the computing resource consumption is high, and both access efficiency and access security need to be improved; there are vulnerabilities in the security of smart contracts, which are vulnerable to attacks, and effective solutions are urgently needed. Therefore, this study will focus on researching how to achieve complete transmission of data based on blockchain and Industrial Internet technologies, as well as addressing the issues of improving access efficiency and security37.

Blockchain and industrial internet-based data management mode

Construction of a data management model based on blockchain and the industrial internet

The data management model based on blockchain and the Industrial Internet aims to connect blockchain systems and the Identification and Resolution System of Industrial Internet. As shown in Fig. 1, it provides an innovative solution for the trusted circulation and secure sharing of industrial data. This model uses the Industrial Internet platform to comprehensively collect production, equipment, and environmental data, and uploads it to the blockchain network. Blockchain, with its decentralized, tamper-proof, and traceable characteristics, assigns a unique hash identifier to data, ensuring the integrity and credibility of the original data. This integrated architecture effectively addresses core issues faced by the Industrial Internet, such as data silos, lack of trust, and difficulty in traceability, and breaks down the interaction barriers of data between blockchain technology and the Identification and Resolution System of Industrial Internet. It establishes a decentralized, highly transparent, secure, and reliable data governance environment, laying a solid data foundation for intelligent manufacturing, supply chain collaboration, and product lifecycle management.

In the consortium blockchain architecture, we select the Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT) algorithm as the consensus mechanism. PBFT, a Byzantine fault-tolerant algorithm, is capable of handling malicious node behavior, which aligns highly with the industrial Internet of Things (IoT) application scenarios of consortium blockchains. Compared to Raft, a crash fault-tolerant algorithm that can only address node failures and cannot mitigate malicious activities, PBFT provides superior security. Meanwhile, IBFT, an enhanced version of PBFT, does not offer significant advantages under the network scale typical of consortium blockchains. Hyperledger Fabric adopted PBFT as its default consensus algorithm starting from version 0.6, and this choice has been validated in multiple industrial scenarios, ensuring data integrity and system security. Using the PBFT consensus mechanism, smart contracts are deployed on blockchain nodes to interact with data within the chain. by application API and Identification and Resolution System of Industrial Internet system for data interaction.

Data flow and core logic

The model includes three major application scenarios: users outside the blockchain, users within a single blockchain and cross-blockchain users.

Users outside the blockchain (red arrow). Off-blockchain users are users who are not in any blockchain system, the user scanned to identification and Resolution System of Industrial Internet The system assigns an identification code to the product, and parses it from the identification parsing system to obtain the data summary corresponding to the identification code, the complete data hash value, the blockchain where the data is located, and other information. At the same time, the identification code also stores the blockchain location where the data is located. Users can quickly and accurately obtain the same information on the identification code from the block. Although this method can also obtain data from the two systems, the process is relatively complex. The CBII can be configured via the external nodes of the blockchain, CBII Query through API interface Identification and Resolution System of Industrial Internet data on the system, perform data interaction to shorten data acquisition time. The system compares the information, and if it is consistent, it will provide verified data to the user; if it is inconsistent, it will provide the data and report the difference record to the regulatory authorities as questionable data, triggering the supervision process to ensure the authenticity and integrity of the data. The regulatory authorities will verify the data and supervise the relevant companies. Users interacting with enterprise nodes and blockchain, such as industry chain participants and regulators, can access industry chain data through enterprise nodes. At the same time, the data will be synchronized on the chain and stored as evidence, and the blockchain will be used to ensure the integrity and credibility of the data (to prevent data from being tampered with or forged).

Users within a single blockchain (yellow arrow). When the data the user is looking for is on the blockchain where he or she is located, and the user does not need to perform cross-chain query, it is deemed to be a user’s data access within a single chain. The system synchronously initiates a verification request: because the CBII is deployed on the blockchain, in-chain data processing can be performed through smart contracts.

The registration metadata of this data is obtained from the Identification and Resolution System of Industrial Internet via CBII, including its default ownership chain information. Subsequently, the system matches and verifies the ownership chain information in the metadata with the user’s current chain. If they are consistent, it proves that the data the user is querying is valid.

Cross-blockchain users (purple arrow). When the data the user is looking for is not on the blockchain where he or she is located, the user who needs to perform a cross-chain query is considered a cross-chain user for data access. For example, if a certain enterprise’s data is stored in chain B, and when enterprise chain C needs to call the data, the query request will be routed to the target blockchain and securely obtained through the cross-chain mechanism, ensuring collaboration and mutual trust of cross-enterprise and cross-chain data. The block chain that does not want to be connected realizes cross-chain data interaction through CBII and solves the problem of blockchain islands. This process encapsulates the complex cross-chain addressing and data acquisition process, provides users with a unified query entry, and effectively solves the problem of data islands.

Cross-chain communication mechanism

This data management model is designed to address the problems of data silos and cross-chain interaction in industrial Internet scenarios. The model adopts a multi-blockchain architecture design, which consists of multiple independent blockchains (e.g., Blockchain A, Blockchain B, Blockchain C). Each blockchain is composed of multiple nodes, and a Connector between Blockchain and Industrial Internet (CBII) is deployed on every blockchain. This connector is used for data interaction with the industrial Internet identification and resolution system, thus achieving cross-chain and cross-system interoperability. To support trusted data exchange between heterogeneous consortium blockchains, this paper proposes a cross-chain communication mechanism based on light client verification and event-driven relays. This mechanism consists of three core components:

Source Chain Event Listener: It is a smart contract deployed on the source chain (e.g., Hyperledger Fabric Chaincode). When key operations (such as data on-chain storage and data updating) occur, it writes events (including transaction hash, block height, and Merkle path) into the blockchain log. This component uses the identification information in the industrial Internet identification and resolution system to accurately mark the source and location of data.

Trusted Relay Service (Relayer): It is a trusted node running off-chain, which continuously monitors the event logs of the source chain and submits the proof package containing the Merkle Proof to the target chain. The Trusted Relay Service adopts an identity authentication mechanism based on the industrial Internet identification and resolution system to ensure the credibility of relay nodes and avoid reliance on centralized oracles.

Target Chain Verifier Contract: It is a smart contract deployed on the target chain, embedded with a Light Client of the source chain. It is used to verify the validity of the submitted block headers and confirm whether specific transactions are included in the block through Merkle Proof. This contract also interacts with the industrial Internet identification and resolution system to verify the integrity and consistency of data.

Compared with the Cosmos IBC protocol, this solution does not require the sharing of consensus logic between chains and is suitable for heterogeneous permissioned blockchain environments such as Hyperledger Fabric. In contrast to Chainlink CCIP, this solution reduces reliance on centralized oracles and is better suited to meet enterprise-level data sovereignty requirements. In industrial scenarios, this design balances security, performance and deployment complexity, providing a trusted foundation for cross-enterprise data collaboration.

The workflow of the cross-chain communication mechanism is as follows:

Data on-chain storage and event triggering First, Enterprise A performs a data upload operation on the source chain (Chain A). Then the source chain event listener captures the data upload event and generates an event log containing the transaction hash, block height, and Merkle path. Finally, the event log is associated with specific data through the identification information in the industrial Internet identification and resolution system.

Event relay and proof generation First, the Trusted Relay Service monitors the event logs of the source chain. Then the relay service collects the Merkle Proof and constructs a proof package containing the source chain block header, transaction hash, and Merkle path. Finally, the relay service verifies its own identity and confirms the credibility of the data source through the industrial Internet identification and resolution system.

Target chain verification and data synchronization First, the relay service submits the proof package to the target chain (Chain B). Then the target chain verifier contract verifies the validity of the source chain block header via the light client. Second, the verifier contract uses the Merkle Proof to verify whether the specific transaction is included in the block. Finally, after the verification is passed, the target chain synchronizes the data to the local storage and updates the data identifier through the industrial Internet identification and resolution system.

Data Consistency Verification: First, the target chain system performs a data consistency comparison with the industrial Internet identification and resolution system. Then, if the data is consistent, the verified data is returned to the user; if it is inconsistent, the supervision process is triggered and the exception is recorded.

This model can effectively ensure the integrity, trustworthiness and tamper-proofness of data, and improve the security and speed of access control.

Single task processing of CBII based on AHP

The main research content of this section is to use the Analytical Hierarchy Process to optimize the efficiency of single task uploading of data, which can well solve the problems of long upload time, low efficiency and incomplete data for a single enterprise in the supply chain, improve the efficiency of the enterprise, and save enterprise costs.

A brief introduction to the AHP

AHP is a mathematical model of multi-objective decision-making proposed by American operations researchers in the early 1970s. It is a method of analyzing decision-making problems. It is based on the hierarchical structure of the problem and helps people rank multiple options to determine the best decision-making option. This method can be used to evaluate the impact of various factors (such as cost, risk, benefit, etc.) on decision-making.

Comprehensive evaluation method of single-task performance of CBII

When a single task performs data transfer, CBII are needed to process tasks, this can effectively improve data transmission efficiency. However, there are differences in the performance of various CBII, individual tasks need to be decomposed in a targeted manner, and assigned to the corresponding CBII for processing. This study selects three CBII as examples, use three CBII to handle upload tasks simultaneously, At the same time, it will not occupy too many CBII, it will not affect the processing of other tasks, can greatly shorten the time for data to be uploaded to the chain, and improve the efficiency of enterprises in all links of the chain. The problem can be solved well by using the AHP.

The construction of the above judgment matrix is based on the results of a questionnaire survey conducted among 10 domain experts, combined with the measured data of different CBII performance indicators obtained in our pre-experimental environment. This approach ensures the rationality and objectivity of weight allocation. Weights of each indicator are calculated based on the judgment matrix. Currently, there are three commonly used methods for this calculation, including the Arithmetic Mean Method, Geometric Mean Method, and Eigenvalue Method. These three methods are applied separately, and the optimal one is selected.

1. Arithmetic mean method.

The arithmetic mean method refers to a method that calculates the average of a set of data by dividing the sum of all data by the number of data points. It is a commonly used method for computing averages and is applicable to various data processing scenarios, such as statistics, economics, physics, and biology.

2. The Geometric Mean Method.

The geometric mean method is a way to calculate the average of a set of data. It takes the nth root of the product of the data points as the average, where n is the number of data items. Compared with the arithmetic mean method, the geometric mean method is more suitable for measuring situations involving relative growth or decay.

3. The eigenvalue method.

Multi-task processing of CBII based on the Hungarian algorithm

The main research content of this section is to optimize the efficiency of multi-task data upload using the Hungarian Algorithm. This method can effectively solve problems such as low upload efficiency and incomplete data caused by the need to upload complex data, and plays a key role in improving the efficiency and integrity of data upload and query.

Introduction to the Hungarian Algorithm

The Hungarian Algorithm is a dynamic programming algorithm, also known as the Random Expansion Algorithm. Its core idea is to determine a set of feasible solutions through searching. The Hungarian Algorithm is a combinatorial optimization algorithm that solves assignment problems in polynomial time, and it is the most commonly used partial graph matching algorithm. The core of this algorithm lies in finding augmented paths, making it an algorithm for finding the maximum matching between bipartite graphs and augmented paths.

Analysis of multi-task allocation problem for CBII

When supply chain users upload data, the complexity of enterprise user groups results in diverse data types, including text, video, audio, and other formats. Since CBII differ in their ability to process various data types, there is a need to upload a maximum volume of data in the shortest possible time. This requires optimizing the task-matching method for CBII. In this section, the Hungarian Algorithm is adopted to achieve optimal matching between users’ data upload tasks and CBII, thereby enabling multiple enterprises to quickly upload data to the chain.

A task allocation structure for CBII is designed, where the number of data upload tasks is defined as N, and the number of CBII in the data management model is defined as M. As shown in Fig. 4, there are A_M^N types of CBII allocation methods. Therefore, it is necessary to select the strategy with the shortest time to quickly resolve the data upload tasks.

Safety analysis

Blockchain adopts distributed ledger technology. Data is not stored on a single central server but is distributed across multiple nodes in the network. In the Industrial Internet scenario, the massive amounts of data generated by devices can be distributed and stored on different nodes. Even if some nodes are attacked or fail, it will not lead to data loss or unavailability, ensuring the availability and integrity of the data. Blockchain generates a unique hash value for each data block through a hash algorithm. In the data management of the Industrial Internet, each batch of data collected by devices is assigned a hash value. Once the data is tampered with, its hash value will change, and the hash links of subsequent data blocks will also be broken, thus enabling quick identification and effectively preventing illegal modification of the data. The blockchain-based digital identity system assigns a unique digital identity to each entity in the Industrial Internet, such as devices, users, enterprises, etc. When accessing data, the system strictly authenticates the digital identity of the visitor. Only authorized digital identities can access the corresponding data resources. For instance, in industrial production workshops, workers need to pass digital identity authentication to access the operational data of equipment, effectively preventing unauthorized personnel from illegally accessing the data.

Scalability analysis

In the dimension of data storage, the distributed storage feature of blockchain provides the system with excellent scalability. With the expansion of the scale of devices and the sharp increase in the total amount of data in the Industrial Internet, the horizontal expansion of storage capacity can be achieved by adding blockchain nodes. Moreover, the process of accessing new nodes is simple and does not require large-scale adjustments to the overall architecture. For instance, when large manufacturing parks add new production line equipment, they only need to deploy new blockchain nodes at reasonable locations within the park to complete storage expansion and ensure the continuous and stable storage of data.

In terms of business functions, smart contracts, as the core components of blockchain, have the advantage of modular design. When the business scenarios of the Industrial Internet expand, functional iterations can be rapidly achieved by developing new smart contracts. For instance, when an enterprise expands its supply chain finance business and involves multi-party data interaction and complex rule execution, it only needs to develop a suitable smart contract and deploy it to the blockchain to seamlessly integrate with the existing system, efficiently complete functional expansion, and meet the diverse business needs.

In the dimension of network nodes, new enterprises and new devices are constantly joining the ecosystem in the Industrial Internet scenario. The P2P network structure of blockchain supports the convenient access of new nodes. Under the consortium chain model, new nodes that meet the access rules can directly join the network, participate in data verification and storage processes, achieve flexible expansion of node scale, and effectively adapt to the development needs of the continuous growth of the Industrial Internet ecosystem.

Usability analysis of the system interface

The data management prototype system constructed in this study adopts the Vue front-end framework, providing an intuitive and user-friendly interface. The system interface design adheres to the requirements of industrial Internet application scenarios, and mainly consists of four core interface modules: the main interface, data operation interface, management permission interface and database management interface. The interface design follows the following usability principles:

Conciseness and Intuitiveness: The interface layout is clear and functional modules are rationally divided, avoiding information overload. The main interface displays key data indicators through visual charts, enabling users to quickly grasp the system operation status.

User-Friendly Operation: Adopting a modular design, the system enables users to complete core functions such as data uploading, querying, and management through simple click operations. For example, the data operation interface is equipped with intuitive data upload buttons and query condition setting areas, allowing users to achieve data interaction without complex operations.

Consistent Design: The interface elements of the system (such as buttons, icons, and colors) are consistent across all modules, which reduces users’ learning costs and improves operational efficiency.

Timely Feedback: The system provides real-time feedback on user operations, such as upload success prompts and query result displays, enabling users to clearly understand the status of their operations.

Usability analysis of system functions

Targeting the core requirements of data management in the Industrial Internet, this model is designed with the following key functional modules, all of which feature excellent usability.

Data Security and Integrity Management: The immutability of data is achieved through blockchain technology. When data is uploaded, the system automatically generates a hash value and stores it on the blockchain, thereby ensuring data integrity and traceability. Users do not need to understand the underlying technical details; they only need to complete data upload according to the system prompts, and the system will automatically perform data security verification.

Data Operation Management: The system supports the collection, storage, and query of multi-source heterogeneous data, and automatically executes business rules through smart contracts. Users only need to select the required functional modules, and the system can complete complex data processing flows without the need for coding or complex configurations.

Permission Management: The model adopts a role-based access control (RBAC) mechanism, which grants corresponding data access permissions based on different user roles (enterprise users, regulatory authorities, and consumers). The permission setting interface is intuitive and easy to operate; administrators can complete permission assignment through simple checkbox operations without any professional technical background.

Cross-System Data Interaction: The system realizes seamless integration between the blockchain and the Industrial Internet Identification and Resolution System through CBII. When querying data, users do not need to understand the complex system architecture; instead, they only need to perform query operations through a unified interface, and the system automatically processes cross-system data interaction.

Usability analysis of system performance

The system delivers outstanding performance and is able to meet the high usability requirements in Industrial Internet scenarios.

Response Speed: System tests show that the efficiency of data query improves significantly with the increase in the number of CBII nodes (as illustrated in Fig. 9). When 4 CBII nodes are deployed, the average response time for 100 data query requests is reduced from 12.5 s to 4.3 s, representing an approximate 65% improvement in query efficiency, which can meet the real-time requirements of industrial scenarios.

Concurrent Processing Capability: The system supports high-concurrency data processing. Test results show that when handling 568 data upload requests, the system can process 182 valid transactions per second (as shown in Fig. 8), which is far higher than the performance requirements of ordinary industrial data processing systems.

System Stability: In a test involving 52,290 pieces of data, the system exhibited accuracy or integrity issues with only a tiny fraction of the data (approximately 0.1%) (as shown in Fig. 7). The overall stability of the system reached over 99.9%, meeting the requirements for industrial-grade applications.

Scalability: The system adopts a microservice architecture where all functional modules are deployed independently, enabling flexible expansion according to business requirements. For example, when an enterprise needs to add supply chain finance functions, it only needs to deploy new smart contracts without making large-scale adjustments to the system’s core architecture.

Overall, the system has not only achieved innovations in data management technically, but also met the high standards of Industrial Internet applications in terms of usability, providing a practical and efficient solution for industrial data management.

Conclusions

This study integrates blockchain and Industrial Internet technologies. Firstly, it designs a data management model based on blockchain and the Industrial Internet, using a CBII model to link the blockchain system and the Industrial Internet identifier resolution system. Meanwhile, according to different application scenarios of the CBII, it designs smart contracts that conform to the model for functions such as data query and upload in the model. Secondly, it conducts research on the task collaboration mechanism of industrial data, applies the AHP to study single-task processing, and uses the Hungarian Algorithm to research the multi-task coordination processing of the model, which can effectively improve the efficiency of multiple functions such as data upload and query. Finally, based on the Hyperledger Fabric platform, it designs a data management prototype system based on blockchain and the Industrial Internet, realizes and verifies the model through system implementation, achieves the collaboration between industrial data chains and networks, ensures data security and integrity, and improves data access control. This makes industrial data management more convenient and efficient, reduces the impact of human factors, and greatly enhances the safety and production efficiency of industrial production, which has a crucial impact on promoting industrial development.

Cardioprotective effects of right stellate ganglion block combined with dexmedetomidine in rats with atrial fibrillation-like atrial tachyarrhythmia
Authors: Renhao Xu, Jin Zhang, Qiongmei Guo, Deli Zheng, Xiangjian Zhang, Li Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo evaluate right stellate ganglion block (SGB) plus dexmedetomidine (Dex) in a rat model of atrial fibrillation-like (AF-like) atrial tachyarrhythmia. Methods: Male Sprague-Dawley rats were assigned to Sham, AF, AF+amiodarone, AF + SGB and AF + SGB+Dex groups. AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia was induced by daily CaCl₂-acetylcholine injections for 7 days, and interventions were administered 1 h before modelling. ECG, echocardiography, electrophysiology, histology, ELISA, Western blot/qPCR, immunofluorescence and electron microscopy were performed. Results: Compared with the AF group, SGB + Dex reduced arrhythmia duration (5.0 ± 1.5 vs. 16.3 ± 2.5 s, p < 0.001) and ventricular rate (344.3 ± 19.9 vs. 398.1 ± 19.2 bpm, p = 0.004), preserved LVEF (83.3% ± 9.2% vs. 72.2% ± 6.7%, p = 0.032), attenuated left atrial enlargement and fibrosis, partially reversed effective refractory period shortening, reduced inducibility, decreased collagen I/III, TGF-β1 and TNF-α, and altered SK2 expression in the stellate ganglion. Direct SGB versus SGB + Dex comparisons were not statistically significant across most major endpoints. Conclusion: Right SGB + Dex was associated with attenuation of electrical and structural remodelling in this AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia model. These findings support a protective SGB-based strategy but remain associative. Because no Dex-only group was included, the independent contribution of Dex and the incremental benefit of adding Dex to SGB require further investigation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12872-026-06114-y.

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common type of cardiac arrhythmia, affecting approximately 3% of the general population, with prevalence increasing to almost 10% among individuals aged > 80 years [1]. It is associated with a considerable risk of stroke, heart failure and mortality, leading to substantial medical and socioeconomic burdens. Current antiarrhythmic drugs and interventional therapies remain suboptimal with limited efficacy and high recurrence or invasiveness [2]. Increasing evidence suggests that the autonomic nervous system, particularly sympathetic overactivation, plays a key role in the initiation and maintenance of AF [3].

The stellate ganglion (SG), a major relay centre for sympathetic cardiac innervation, has been identified as a potential target for autonomic modulation. Studies have shown that SG block (SGB) can reduce sympathetic activity and attenuate atrial electrical remodelling, thereby reducing AF susceptibility [4–6]. Right SGB has been reported to reduce heart rate and arrhythmia burden more effectively than left-sided approaches, offering potential safety and efficacy advantages [7]. At the same time, dexmedetomidine (Dex), a selective α2-adrenergic receptor agonist commonly used for sedation, has demonstrated sympatholytic and cardioprotective effects, including heart rate reduction and improved myocardial oxygen balance [8–11]. However, their combined application in AF has not been systematically investigated.

Therefore, this study explores the combined application of right SGB and Dex in a rat model of AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia. By simultaneously assessing electrical remodelling, structural changes and molecular alterations, we aimed to determine whether this SGB-based combined strategy is associated with cardioprotective effects and to compare it with SGB alone and amiodarone within the available experimental design. In addition, we investigate the expression of small-conductance calcium-activated potassium channel 2 (SK2) channels in the SG, offering new insights into autonomic regulation as a potential therapeutic target.

Materials and methods

Animal grouping and induction of the atrial fibrillation model

A total of 30 specific pathogen-free male Sprague–Dawley rats (weight: 250 ± 50 g) were purchased from the Laboratory Animal Centre of Hebei Medical University (license no.: SCXK [Hebei] 2020-003). All animals were housed under standard laboratory conditions (22–25 °C, 12-h light/dark cycle and free access to food and water) for 1 week before the experiment for acclimatisation. The rats were randomly assigned to five groups (n = 6 per group): the Sham, AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia model (AF), AF + amiodarone treatment (Amio), AF + right SGB (SGB) and AF + right SGB combined with Dex (SGB + Dex) groups. The sample size was determined based on previous studies using the same AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia model [12–14], in which n = 6 per group was sufficient to detect a meaningful difference in arrhythmia duration (effect size d = 1.8) with 80% power at α = 0.05 (two-sided). A post hoc power analysis using the observed arrhythmia duration data (AF group: 16.3 ± 2.5 s vs. SGB + Dex group: 5.0 ± 1.5 s) confirmed that the achieved power exceeded 0.95 for the primary endpoint.

The AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia model was induced according to previously published protocols with slight modifications [12–14]. Anaesthesia was induced and maintained with 2%-3% isoflurane in 100% oxygen at a flow rate of 1 L/minute via a small-animal anaesthesia system (RWD Life Science, Shenzhen, China). Anaesthesia depth was monitored by the absence of the pedal withdrawal reflex. Rats were fixed supine, and subcutaneous needle electrodes were inserted for limb lead ECG recording. Rats with baseline ECG abnormalities were excluded. Except for those in the Sham group, all rats received arrhythmia induction via intravenous tail injection of a freshly prepared solution containing CaCl₂ (10 mg/kg) and acetylcholine (66 µg/kg). The solution was administered at a volume of 1 mL/kg once daily for 7 consecutive days. Sham rats received an equal volume of normal saline. AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia was identified by the appearance of rapid, irregular atrial activity with fibrillatory waves and the absence of discrete P waves in lead II ECG recordings. Spontaneous restoration to sinus rhythm marked the termination of the arrhythmia episode. It should be noted that, in the absence of direct intracardiac or optical mapping evidence, the observed rhythm disturbance is classified as AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia based on surface ECG criteria, consistent with the terminology widely adopted in similar rodent models [12–14]. The distinction between true AF and other organised atrial arrhythmias (such as atrial tachycardia or atrial flutter) cannot be definitively made by surface ECG alone in rodents with high baseline heart rates, and this limitation is further examined in the Discussion section.

In an additional long-term cohort (n = 6 per group for the Sham, AF and SGB + Dex groups), the same modelling protocol was extended to 14 consecutive days. In this cohort, AF induction (daily CaCl₂–acetylcholine injection) and corresponding interventions (SGB + Dex for the treatment group, saline for Sham) were administered every day for all 14 days, and all endpoint measurements were performed on day 14 following the final intervention. This extended cohort was designed to evaluate subchronic remodelling effects and was analysed separately from the primary 7-day cohort. The rats were randomly allocated into groups using a computer-generated random number sequence to ensure unbiased assignment. The investigators performing the interventions were aware of the group allocations, whereas all outcome assessments and data analyses were conducted by independent researchers blinded to the group assignments.

Experimental workflow

The experimental workflow for the primary 7-day cohort was as follows. On each day (days 1–7), the corresponding intervention was administered first (right SGB with or without Dex, amiodarone or normal saline in the Sham group), followed by AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia induction via tail vein injection 1 h later. Body weight was recorded daily. An ECG was recorded immediately before and after each arrhythmia-inducing injection. On day 7, transthoracic echocardiography was performed after the final ECG recording. Subsequently, electrophysiological studies were performed under terminal anaesthesia in a subset of animals. Following euthanasia, tissue and blood samples were collected simultaneously for histological, molecular and serological analyses. The 14-day extended cohort followed an identical daily protocol, with all endpoint assessments performed on day 14. Food and water intake were recorded daily, and animal health status (grooming, activity level and responsiveness) was monitored throughout the experimental period. No animals were excluded due to systemic illness or mortality during the study.

Right stellate ganglion block model

Rats in the SGB and the SGB + Dex groups underwent right SGB 1 h before AF induction each day [15, 16]. After inhalation anaesthesia with 2%–3% isoflurane in 100% oxygen at a flow rate of 1 L/minute, the right clavicle–sternum junction was identified as the surface landmark for puncture. The puncture site was located 3–4 mm lateral to the right sternoclavicular joint and 2–3 mm above the clavicle. A 30-gauge 13-mm needle was advanced perpendicular to the skin to a depth of 6–8 mm until slight resistance was felt, consistent with the anatomical location of the SG in rats. Successful puncture was indicated by tremors in the ipsilateral forelimb upon stimulation of the brachial plexus. A 0.2-mL injection of 0.25% ropivacaine (AstraZeneca AB, Södertälje, Sweden) was administered. Successful blockade was confirmed by the appearance of ipsilateral Horner’s syndrome (ptosis and enophthalmos) within 10 min post injection. In three rats, methylene blue injection confirmed the spread around the right SG (data not shown). To ensure the adequacy of blockade on a daily basis, the presence of ipsilateral Horner’s syndrome was verified 10 min after each injection throughout the 7-day protocol. If Horner’s syndrome was not observed, the injection was repeated once. All rats in the SGB and SGB + Dex groups exhibited consistent Horner’s syndrome following each daily injection, and no repeat injections were required.

Intervention treatments

Each group received its corresponding intervention 1 h before arrhythmia induction for 7 consecutive days. Rats in the Amio group received an intraperitoneal injection of amiodarone (30 mg/kg, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). This dose was selected based on prior animal studies demonstrating effective and safe antiarrhythmic effects via intraperitoneal administration [17, 18]. Rats in the SGB group received right SGB as described above. Rats in the SGB + Dex group received an intraperitoneal injection of Dex (50 µg/kg, MedChemExpress, Monmouth Junction, NJ, USA) in addition to right SGB with ropivacaine. The chosen doses were based on previous rodent studies [19–21]. The experimental design focused on the clinically relevant SGB-based combined regimen and its direct comparison with SGB alone and amiodarone; therefore, a standalone Dex-only arm was not included, which also limited animal use in accordance with the 3R principles. In an additional exploratory cohort, a lower dose of Dex (25 µg/kg) was tested to examine a possible dose–response trend (n = 5 per group: the Sham, AF, SGB+Dex25 and SGB+Dex50 groups). On day 7 (or day 14 for the extended cohort), following the final ECG and echocardiographic recordings, electrophysiological studies were performed in a subset of animals. Subsequently, the right SG, atrial tissue and venous blood samples were collected for further analyses. Experimental animals were deeply anaesthetised by continuous inhalation anaesthesia with 3% isoflurane using a small-animal anaesthesia system. Once a complete loss of pain reflex was confirmed (no response to toe pinch), euthanasia was performed via cardiac puncture or vascular blood collection. The heart tissue was then rapidly harvested for subsequent experiments.

Electrocardiogram and echocardiographic assessment

Rats were anaesthetised with 3% isoflurane delivered in 100% oxygen at a flow rate of 1 L/minute, and subcutaneous needle electrodes were placed on all limbs to record lead II ECGs before and after arrhythmia-inducing injections. The ventricular rate at rest and AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia duration after injection were recorded daily for 7 consecutive days. Heart rate data were extracted from ECG recordings at baseline (day 1) and day 7 in both sinus rhythm and during arrhythmic episodes.

Subsequently, on day 7, transthoracic echocardiography was performed using the Vevo 2100 Imaging System (VisualSonics, Toronto, ON, Canada) equipped with a high-frequency linear array transducer (MS400, 18–38 MHz). Imaging depth was set at 15 mm, frame rate was set at 233 fps and dynamic range was maintained consistently across all measurements. Two-dimensional imaging was used to measure the left atrial (LA) area in both systole and diastole to evaluate atrial remodelling. Motion-mode echocardiography in the parasternal short-axis view was used to measure the left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD), left ventricular end-systolic diameter (LVESD), left ventricular posterior wall thickness (LVPWT) and left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) to assess cardiac structure and systolic function. Left ventricular end-diastolic volume (LVEDV) and left ventricular end-systolic volume (LVESV) were automatically calculated by the software. All echocardiographic measurements were performed by a single sonographer blinded to group allocations, and each parameter was averaged from three consecutive cardiac cycles.

Haemodynamic monitoring

In an additional cohort (n = 5 per group), haemodynamic parameters were monitored during the experimental procedures. Heart rate was derived from ECG recordings. Mean arterial pressure (MAP) was measured using a tail-cuff system (Kent Scientific, Torrington, CT, USA) before anaesthesia induction, 10 min after anaesthesia stabilisation and after AF induction. Measurements were performed under standardised conditions to ensure comparability across groups.

Electrophysiological studies

In a subset of animals (n = 6 per group for the Sham, AF, SGB and SGB + Dex groups), atrial electrophysiological studies were performed on day 7 under terminal anaesthesia. After thoracotomy, a bipolar pacing and recording electrode was placed on the right atrial appendage. A programmed electrical stimulation protocol was used to assess right atrial effective refractory period (ERP) using an S1-S2 protocol with a basic drive cycle length of 150 ms and progressively decreasing S2 coupling intervals. Right atrial conduction time was recorded as the interval from the stimulus artefact to the local electrogram deflection. AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia inducibility was assessed by burst pacing (50 Hz for 10 s), and the duration of induced arrhythmic episodes was recorded.

Haematoxylin–eosin staining of atrial tissue

Following euthanasia, the hearts were excised, and atrial tissues were rinsed in cold saline. Samples were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for ≥ 24 h, dehydrated, embedded in paraffin and sectioned at 4 μm thickness. Sections were stained with haematoxylin–eosin and examined under a light microscope to assess myocardial morphology. For each animal, three non-consecutive sections were examined, and representative images were captured from the left atrial free wall and atrial appendage regions.

Masson’s trichrome staining

Atrial tissue sections were stained with Masson’s trichrome to evaluate myocardial fibrosis. For each animal, three non-consecutive sections were stained and examined. The collagen volume fraction (CVF) was quantified using ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA) by calculating the percentage of blue-stained fibrotic area relative to the total myocardial area. For each section, five randomly selected non-overlapping high-power fields (×400 magnification) were captured from the left atrial free wall, and the mean CVF per animal was used for statistical analysis. The colour threshold for blue-stained collagen was set using the ‘Colour Threshold’ function in ImageJ with a fixed hue range (130–190), and the same threshold parameters were applied uniformly to all images.

10 transmission electron microscopy of atrial myocytes

For ultrastructural analysis, fresh LA tissues (1 mm³) from three rats per group were rapidly excised within 3 min post euthanasia, minimising mechanical injury. Tissues were fixed in 2.5% glutaraldehyde (Servicebio, Wuhan, China) at 4 °C, post-fixed in 1% osmium tetroxide, dehydrated in graded ethanol, embedded in epoxy resin and sectioned ultrathin (70–90 nm). Sections were stained with uranyl acetate and lead citrate, then examined using a transmission electron microscope (Hitachi HT7700, Tokyo, Japan) for cardiomyocyte ultrastructural evaluation. To provide a quantitative assessment of ultrastructural changes, mitochondrial cross-sectional area and the number of mitochondria per field of view (at ×10,000 magnification) were measured in 10 randomly selected non-overlapping fields per animal using ImageJ software. The percentage of morphologically abnormal mitochondria (defined as those exhibiting swelling, loss of cristae integrity or disrupted outer membranes) was calculated for each group.

11 Western blot analysis

Left atrial tissues and right stellate ganglia were homogenised in radioimmunoprecipitation assay lysis buffer containing protease inhibitors. Protein concentrations were determined using a bicinchoninic acid assay. Equal amounts of protein (30 µg) were separated by 10% sodium dodecyl sulphate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk for 1 h and incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against collagen I (1:1,000, Abcam, Cambridge, UK), collagen III (1:1,000, Abcam), transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGF-β1) (1:1,000, Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA), SK2 (1:500, Alomone Labs, Jerusalem, Israel) and glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (1:5,000, Proteintech, Rosemont, IL, USA) as an internal control. After washing, membranes were incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:5,000) for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualised using enhanced chemiluminescence and quantified using ImageJ software. All Western blot experiments were performed in triplicate, and quantification was performed by an independent investigator blinded to the group allocations. The lane order was Sham, AF, Amio, SGB and SGB + Dex. The updated uncropped source image used to generate the displayed bands is provided separately as Original Data.png in the supplementary data to support verification of the Western blot presentation; representative cropped bands are reported with the Western blot results and figure legends so that the supplementary figures are cited in numerical order.

12 quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction

Total RNA was extracted from LA tissues and right SG using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA), and complementary DNA was synthesised using a reverse transcription kit (Takara Bio, Shiga, Japan). Quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) was performed using SYBR Green Master Mix (Roche Diagnostics, Basel, Switzerland) on a LightCycler 480 system (Roche Diagnostics). Primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table S1. Relative messenger RNA (mRNA) expression levels were calculated using the 2(−ΔΔCt) method with GAPDH as the internal reference gene. All qPCR reactions were run in triplicate.

13 immunofluorescence for small-conductance calcium-activated potassium channel 2 expression in right stellate ganglion

Three right stellate ganglia from each group were embedded in paraffin, sectioned and subjected to immunofluorescence staining. The selection of three ganglia per group was based on practical considerations related to the small size of the rat SG and the need to preserve sufficient tissue for parallel Western blot and qPCR analyses from the remaining animals. This approach is consistent with prior studies examining SG protein expression in rodent models [15, 16]. The sections were deparaffinised sequentially in three xylene substitutes (10 min each), rehydrated through graded ethanol (three changes, 5 min each) and rinsed in distilled water. Antigen retrieval was performed. Tissue sections were circled using a hydrophobic barrier pen and blocked with 10% donkey serum (bovine serum albumin) for 30 min at room temperature. The sections were then incubated overnight at 4 °C in a humidified chamber with primary antibody against SK2 (1:200, Alomone Labs). The next day, slides were washed in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) (pH 7.4) three times for 5 min each on a decolourising shaker and incubated with a fluorescent secondary antibody (1:500, Abcam) at room temperature for 50 min in the dark. After another series of PBS washes (3 × 5 min), nuclei were counterstained with 4’,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole for 10 min in the dark. Tissue autofluorescence was quenched using quenching reagent B for 5 min, followed by a 10-minute wash under running water. Slides were then sealed with an anti-fade mounting medium and imaged under a fluorescence microscope (Olympus BX53, Tokyo, Japan). Quantitative analysis of SK2 fluorescence intensity was performed using ImageJ software (NIH) by an investigator blinded to group allocations. For each section, five randomly selected non-overlapping fields were measured, and the mean fluorescence intensity per ganglion was used for statistical comparison.

14 serum tumour necrosis factor-alpha and interleukin-6 level measurement

Blood was collected from the abdominal aorta after the final interventions. Samples were left at room temperature for 2 hours or stored overnight at 4°C, then centrifuged at 3,000 rpm for 15 minutes at 2°C–8°C. Serum was collected, and tumour necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) and interleukin-6 (IL-6) levels were measured using commercial enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay kits (Elabscience, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, 100 µL of diluted serum samples, standards and blanks were added to pre-coated wells and incubated at 37°C for 1–2 hours. After washing 3–5 times, 100 µL of biotin-labelled antibody was added and incubated at 37°C for 1 hour. Following another wash, 100 µL of enzyme conjugate was added and incubated at 37°C in the dark for 30 minutes. Then, 3,3’,5,5’ tetramethylbenzidine substrate (100 µL) was added and incubated in the dark at 37 °C for 10–30 min. The reaction was stopped with 100 µL of sulphuric acid. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm, and sample concentrations were calculated using the standard curve.

15 statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 25.0 software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). All continuous data were tested for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Normally distributed variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s). Repeated-measures two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) (time × group) was used for comparisons of AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia duration over the 7 days. The area under the curve (AUC) for the 7-day arrhythmia duration was calculated for each animal and compared among groups using one-way ANOVA. Repeated-measures one-way ANOVA was used for other continuous variables measured over time (e.g. body weight and ventricular rate). One-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s honest significant difference (HSD) post hoc test was used to compare group differences in single-timepoint measurements (e.g. LVEF, LA area, CVF, cytokine levels, protein and mRNA expression levels). Direct SGB versus SGB + Dex comparisons for key endpoints were also reported using two-sided pairwise tests for continuous variables and Fisher’s exact test for arrhythmia inducibility. Fibrosis area from Masson’s staining, average fluorescence intensity from immunofluorescence and mitochondrial morphometric parameters from electron microscopy were quantified using ImageJ software (NIH).

Given the exploratory nature of this study, which assessed multiple interrelated endpoints to characterise the multidimensional effects of SGB + Dex, formal adjustment for multiple comparisons across outcome domains was not applied. This approach is consistent with current recommendations for exploratory preclinical studies, where overly conservative corrections may obscure biologically meaningful effects [22]. However, within each outcome domain, pairwise comparisons were controlled using Tukey’s HSD post hoc test. To provide further transparency, exact p-values are reported where available. The relatively small sample size (n = 6 per group) and the absence of formal multiplicity adjustment are acknowledged as limitations, and the present findings should be interpreted with appropriate caution. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

All quantitative analyses, including echocardiographic measurements, histological scoring, immunofluorescence quantification and Western blot densitometry, were performed by investigators blinded to the group allocation. Specifically, echocardiographic images were exported with coded identifiers and measured by a single sonographer unaware of group identity. Histological and immunofluorescence slides were coded by an independent technician before quantification. Western blot band densitometry was performed on de-identified images.

Results

Body weight changes and general health status

At baseline (day 1), there were no statistically significant differences in body weight among the five groups (p = 0.72), with mean weights as follows: Sham 336.83 ± 9.37 g, AF 330.50 ± 16.25 g, Amio 329.00 ± 6.87 g, SGB 330.17 ± 7.44 g and SGB + Dex 332.50 ± 10.71 g. After 7 days of intervention, rats in the Sham group exhibited a significant increase in body weight (363.50 ± 6.75 g), whereas the AF group showed a notable decrease (315.33 ± 20.57 g), with a statistically significant difference compared with the Sham group (p = 0.003). In contrast, rats in the Amio (337.00 ± 9.01 g), SGB (337.33 ± 8.91 g) and SGB + Dex (340.67 ± 13.79 g) groups showed attenuation of arrhythmia-associated weight loss, with body weights significantly higher than those in the AF group (Fig. 1). Daily food intake was recorded throughout the study and was reduced in the AF group (18.2 ± 2.3 g/day) compared with the Sham group (24.7 ± 1.8 g/day; p = 0.004), whereas the treatment groups showed intermediate values (Amio: 21.3 ± 2.1 g/day; SGB: 22.1 ± 1.9 g/day; SGB + Dex: 22.8 ± 2.0 g/day). Water intake showed a similar pattern. No signs of severe systemic illness, and no mortality, were observed in any group during the experimental period.

Electrocardiographic findings and ventricular rate

Electrocardiographic recordings showed a normal sinus rhythm in the Sham group (ventricular rate: 337.742 ± 9.817 bpm) after saline injection. In contrast, all other groups developed AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia following CaCl₂-acetylcholine injection, characterised by rapid, irregular atrial activity with fibrillatory waves and the absence of discrete P waves, accompanied by ocular hyperaemia. Analysis of arrhythmia duration over the 7 days revealed a significant time × group interaction (repeated-measures two-way ANOVA, p < 0.001), indicating differential temporal patterns among groups. The AUC for the 7-day arrhythmia duration was significantly higher in the AF group than in all treatment groups (p < 0.001). By day 7, the AF group exhibited a significantly prolonged AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia duration (16.333 ± 2.503 s) and elevated ventricular rate (398.113 ± 19.152 bpm) compared with the Sham group (p < 0.001 and p = 0.001, respectively). Daily treatment with Amio (arrhythmia duration: 6.167 ± 2.401 s, p < 0.001 vs. AF; ventricular rate: 347.637 ± 21.330 bpm, p = 0.006 vs. AF), SGB (5.500 ± 2.429 s, p < 0.001 vs. AF; 326.374 ± 18.489 bpm, p < 0.001 vs. AF) and SGB + Dex (5.000 ± 1.549 s, p < 0.001 vs. AF; 344.303 ± 19.861 bpm, p = 0.004 vs. AF) reduced arrhythmia duration and ventricular rate relative to the AF group (Figs. 2 and 3). Direct comparison between the SGB and SGB + Dex groups showed no statistically significant difference in day-7 arrhythmia duration (p = 0.681). The ventricular rate was numerically higher in SGB + Dex than in SGB, but this difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.137), and the value remained substantially lower than that in the AF group.

In the dose–response cohort, the lower-dose Dex group (SGB+Dex25, 25 µg/kg) showed intermediate effects on AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia duration (day 7: 7.8 ± 2.1 s) and ventricular rate (365.4 ± 19.7 bpm) compared with SGB+Dex50 (5.2 ± 1.6 s; 346.8 ± 18.3 bpm) and AF groups, suggesting a possible dose-dependent trend (Supplementary Figure S1). Because this exploratory cohort included only five animals per group and only two Dex doses, these findings should be interpreted as preliminary trend data rather than definitive dose–response evidence.

Haemodynamic parameters

Haemodynamic monitoring revealed no significant differences in MAP among all groups during anaesthesia (Sham: 95.3 ± 7.2 mmHg; AF: 92.8 ± 8.5 mmHg; Amio: 94.1 ± 6.8 mmHg; SGB: 96.2 ± 7.4 mmHg; SGB + Dex: 93.7 ± 8.1 mmHg; p = 0.85). Heart rate remained within the physiological range (300–360 bpm) across all groups, indicating haemodynamic stability throughout the experimental procedures (Supplementary Table S2).

Cardiac ultrasound evaluation

Transthoracic echocardiography revealed that both systolic and diastolic LA areas were significantly enlarged in the AF group (15.521 ± 2.072 mm² and 24.300 ± 4.379 mm², respectively) compared with the Sham group (8.698 ± 0.858 mm² and 17.711 ± 3.241 mm²; p = 0.001 and p = 0.024, respectively). These changes were alleviated in the Amio (11.856 ± 2.263 mm² and 16.705 ± 3.494 mm²), SGB (11.337 ± 1.882 mm² and 18.349 ± 2.425 mm²) and SGB + Dex (11.653 ± 1.555 mm² and 18.229 ± 1.962 mm²) groups (all p < 0.05 vs. AF for systolic LA area) (Fig. 4). Direct SGB versus SGB + Dex comparisons showed no significant differences in systolic LA area (p = 0.758) or diastolic LA area (p = 0.927).

Atrial electrophysiological properties

To explore the electrophysiological mechanisms underlying the observed reduction in arrhythmia burden, electrophysiological studies were performed on day 7 in a subset of animals from the Sham, AF, SGB and SGB + Dex groups (n = 6 per group). The right atrial ERP was significantly shortened in the AF group (48.3 ± 5.7 ms) compared with the Sham group (72.5 ± 6.2 ms; p < 0.001). Treatment with SGB (61.8 ± 6.9 ms; p = 0.002 vs. AF) and SGB + Dex (64.2 ± 7.3 ms; p = 0.001 vs. AF) partially reversed this shortening. Right atrial conduction time was prolonged in the AF group (18.7 ± 2.3 ms vs. 12.4 ± 1.8 ms in the Sham group; p < 0.001), and this prolongation was attenuated by SGB (14.6 ± 2.1 ms; p = 0.009 vs. AF) and SGB + Dex treatments (13.9 ± 1.9 ms; p = 0.003 vs. AF). AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia inducibility by burst pacing was significantly higher in the AF group (83.3%, 5/6 rats) than in the Sham group (0%, 0/6 rats; p = 0.015, Fisher’s exact test) and was reduced in the SGB (33.3%, 2/6 rats) and SGB + Dex (16.7%, 1/6 rats) groups (Fig. 6). Direct comparison between SGB and SGB + Dex showed no statistically significant difference in ERP (p = 0.571), conduction time (p = 0.558) or inducibility (p = 1.000).

Molecular markers of myocardial fibrosis

To further characterise the structural remodelling observed on histology, molecular markers of fibrosis were assessed. Semi-quantitative Western blot and qPCR analyses revealed that protein and mRNA levels of collagen I, collagen III and TGF-β1 were markedly increased in the AF group and significantly reduced in the Amio, SGB and SGB + Dex groups (Supplementary Figures S2 and S3). Specifically, collagen I protein expression (normalised to GAPDH) was 2.84 ± 0.42 in the AF group versus 1.08 ± 0.21 in the Sham group (p < 0.001) and was reduced to 1.92 ± 0.35 in the Amio (p = 0.003 vs. AF), 1.45 ± 0.28 in SGB (p < 0.001 vs. AF) and 1.38 ± 0.26 in SGB + Dex groups (p < 0.001 vs. AF). Collagen III protein expression was 2.51 ± 0.39 in the AF group versus 1.02 ± 0.18 in the Sham group (p < 0.001) and was reduced to 1.78 ± 0.31 in the Amio (p = 0.008 vs. AF), 1.35 ± 0.25 in SGB (p < 0.001 vs. AF) and 1.29 ± 0.22 in SGB + Dex groups (p < 0.001 vs. AF). Transforming growth factor-beta 1 protein expression followed a similar pattern (AF: 2.37 ± 0.36 vs. Sham: 1.00 ± 0.16, p < 0.001; Amio: 1.68 ± 0.29, p = 0.005 vs. AF; SGB: 1.32 ± 0.24, p < 0.001 vs. AF; SGB + Dex: 1.25 ± 0.21, p < 0.001 vs. AF). Direct SGB versus SGB + Dex comparisons were not statistically significant for collagen I (p = 0.663), collagen III (p = 0.669) or TGF-β1 (p = 0.603). Consistent patterns were observed at the mRNA level (Supplementary Figure S3), supporting anti-fibrotic associations at both protein and transcriptional levels.

11 extended AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia model results

In the 14-day extended cohort, the AF group demonstrated more pronounced LA enlargement (systolic LA area: 18.3 ± 2.8 mm²; diastolic LA area: 28.7 ± 4.1 mm²) and increased CVF (28.5% ± 4.2%) compared with the 7-day model, indicating progressive structural remodelling. Treatment in the SGB + Dex group maintained protective associations in the extended model, with significantly reduced LA area (systolic: 12.8 ± 2.1 mm²; diastolic: 20.1 ± 2.9 mm²) and CVF (5.8% ± 1.9%) compared with the AF group (all p < 0.01). The LVEF was preserved in the SGB + Dex group (80.2% ± 7.1%) compared with the AF group (67.4% ± 6.8%; p = 0.008). These 14-day cohort data correspond specifically to Supplementary Figure S4 and Supplementary Table S3.

Discussion

In this study, we established a rat model of AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia through tail vein injection of a mixture of CaCl₂ and acetylcholine. Our results showed that SGB-based interventions were associated with attenuation of atrial electrical and structural remodelling and functional decline. Compared with the untreated AF group, SGB + Dex significantly reduced arrhythmia duration, preserved LVEF, attenuated fibrosis and reduced TNF-α levels. However, direct comparisons between SGB and SGB + Dex did not demonstrate statistically significant superiority of the combined treatment across most major endpoints, including day-7 arrhythmia duration, LA size, LVEF, CVF, ERP, conduction time, inducibility, fibrosis-related protein expression and SK2 protein expression. Therefore, the present findings support cardioprotective associations of an SGB-based combined strategy but do not establish a definitive additive or synergistic Dex effect beyond SGB alone.

Mechanistically, the AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia model utilised in this study is based on autonomic imbalance and electrophysiological alterations. Acetylcholine acts on M receptors in atrial myocytes, increasing potassium efflux and shortening the action potential duration and ERP, thus enhancing atrial vulnerability [23]. Furthermore, CaCl₂ increases intracellular calcium levels, promotes transient inactivation of calcium channels, suppresses ICaα subunit mRNA expression and reduces calcium channel density, further promoting the formation of reentrant circuits [24]. This dual approach reproduces several electrophysiological features relevant to clinical AF, but surface ECG criteria in rodents cannot definitively distinguish true AF from other organised atrial tachyarrhythmias [25].

The SG, particularly the right SG, is a critical component of the cardiac sympathetic nervous system. It provides efferent sympathetic innervation to the atria and ventricles and plays a key role in initiating and maintaining arrhythmias [26, 27]. The stellate ganglion block has been reported to reduce sympathetic nerve activity, attenuate myocardial oxygen consumption and inhibit electrical remodelling [28]. Consistent with these findings, our electrophysiological assessment showed that SGB + Dex partially attenuated ERP shortening and reduced AF inducibility, providing direct evidence for improved atrial electrophysiology. Prior clinical and experimental studies have suggested that right SGB may exert stronger effects on atrial arrhythmias due to asymmetric cardiac sympathetic innervation [7, 29].

Dexmedetomidine is a highly selective α2-adrenergic receptor agonist with established sedative and sympatholytic properties. Although it has been traditionally used for perioperative sedation, recent evidence has highlighted its cardioprotective roles through multiple signalling pathways. Clinical studies and meta-analyses have shown that Dex is associated with a reduced incidence of postoperative AF in patients undergoing cardiac surgery [30]. In preclinical research, Dex has been demonstrated to attenuate myocardial ischaemia-reperfusion injury through activation of Nrf2-dependent antioxidant pathways, particularly the Keap1/Nrf2/HO-1 axis, thereby reducing oxidative stress and tissue injury [31]. Furthermore, Dex has been shown to exert anti-arrhythmic effects in a rat model of ischaemic cardiomyopathy by upregulating connexin 43 and reducing fibrosis and inflammation [32]. These findings support the biological plausibility that Dex may influence autonomic and cellular injury pathways relevant to atrial tachyarrhythmia. In our study, SGB + Dex significantly reduced TNF-α, whereas the reduction of IL-6 did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.11) and IL-6 was lower in the SGB group than in the SGB + Dex group. This pattern indicates that the inflammatory response was not uniformly improved by adding Dex to SGB. The day-7 ventricular rate was also numerically higher in SGB + Dex than in SGB alone, although the direct comparison was not statistically significant and both treatment groups remained substantially below the AF group. This finding may reflect the dynamic balance between peripheral sympathetic blockade, central α2-adrenergic modulation, anaesthetic context and compensatory autonomic regulation rather than a simple linear sympatholytic dose–response. Dexmedetomidine alone was not tested as a separate arm in this study; therefore, the independent contribution of Dex and its interaction with SGB cannot be separated from the current data. The observed results should be interpreted as evidence supporting a protective SGB-based combined strategy rather than proof of Dex-specific superiority. Although only two Dex doses were tested in a small exploratory subset of animals, the findings suggested a possible dose-dependent trend in arrhythmia duration, supporting further investigation of optimal dosing strategies. The assessment of downstream signalling pathways such as nuclear factor-kappaB (NF-κB), NOD-, LRR- pyrin domain-containing protein 3 (NLRP3) and Janus kinases/signal transducer and activator of transcription proteins (JAK/STAT) was beyond the scope of this study but represents an important direction for future mechanistic investigations [33, 34].

A noteworthy finding of this study is the observation of altered SK2 expression in the SG following arrhythmia induction and SGB-based interventions. Small-conductance calcium-activated potassium channel 2 contributes to afterhyperpolarisation in neurons, including those of the SG. In the atrial myocardium, SK2 channels contribute to atrial repolarisation, and their dysregulation has been implicated in AF pathogenesis [35, 36]. However, the role of SK2 channels in the SG is mechanistically distinct from their role in atrial tissue. In the SG, SK2 channels regulate neuronal excitability by mediating the afterhyperpolarisation that follows action potentials. Upregulation of SK2 in the SG could represent a compensatory response to sympathetic overactivation during atrial tachyarrhythmia, potentially serving to limit further neuronal firing. Conversely, attenuation of SK2 upregulation by SGB may reflect reduced sympathetic drive to the ganglion rather than a direct pro-arrhythmic effect. It is important to note that some studies have reported that SK2 downregulation in the atria is associated with pro-arrhythmic effects [37], and the relationship between SK2 expression in the SG and atrial arrhythmogenesis may not be straightforward. Our data showed upregulation of SK2 in the SG following arrhythmia induction, which was attenuated by SGB and SGB + Dex treatment. Multi-method validation, including immunofluorescence, Western blot and qPCR, provided consistent evidence for this expression pattern. However, these findings represent an association between SG modulation and SK2 expression changes, rather than a demonstrated causal mechanism. Whether the observed SK2 attenuation in the SG is a direct mediator of the anti-arrhythmic effects or a secondary consequence of reduced sympathetic activity remains to be elucidated. Future studies employing targeted SK2 overexpression or knockdown in the SG, or specific SK2 channel modulators, will be essential to establish the functional significance of these changes. These observations extend the current translational landscape of AF research, in which adjunctive rhythm-control strategies such as the Cox-maze IV procedure and exosome-associated biomarkers are increasingly being explored to refine mechanism-based management of AF [38, 39].

Our study also incorporated molecular analyses demonstrating that histological and molecular markers of fibrosis (collagen I, collagen III and TGF-β1) were reduced following SGB-based treatment, supporting anti-fibrotic associations at multiple levels. The standardised anaesthesia protocol (2%-3% isoflurane in 100% oxygen) with consistent monitoring of anaesthesia depth ensured that observed differences among groups were primarily related to the interventions rather than variations in anaesthetic effects. Haemodynamic monitoring confirmed that no major hypotension or bradycardia occurred, indicating haemodynamic stability throughout the experimental procedures. The detailed left ventricular structural parameters (LVEDD, LVESD and LVPWT) revealed that changes were primarily confined to the left atrium rather than reflecting left ventricular systolic dysfunction, which helps clarify the specific targets of arrhythmia-related remodelling.

Regarding the clinical translatability of our findings, it is important to acknowledge that our 7-day model represents an acute/subacute AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia condition rather than chronic clinical AF. However, in an extended 14-day cohort, similar trends were observed with more pronounced LA enlargement and fibrosis in the AF group, whereas SGB + Dex maintained protective associations. These findings suggest that the therapeutic strategy remains relevant in subchronic remodelling stages, although further studies with longer duration models are warranted to better simulate chronic clinical AF.

Compared with previous studies that explored SGB or Dex in isolation, our study investigated the combined application of SGB and Dex in an AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia model. This combination was associated with favourable effects on both structural and electrical remodelling compared with the untreated AF group, but the similarity between SGB and SGB + Dex across several endpoints requires cautious interpretation. Most prior animal studies have focused on short-term electrophysiological endpoints or inflammatory markers [40], whereas our study incorporated a comprehensive evaluation including echocardiography, electrophysiological studies, histopathology, molecular expression, quantitative ultrastructural analysis and immunofluorescence of the SG, providing multi-dimensional evidence for the effects of SGB-based intervention.

This work offers three contributions. First, it established a reproducible AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia model that captures several rhythm and remodelling features relevant to clinical AF while acknowledging the limitations of surface ECG-based diagnosis in rodents. Second, it investigated SK2 channel expression in the SG as a molecular correlate of autonomic modulation. Third, it demonstrated that SGB combined with Dex was associated with cardioprotective effects compared with untreated arrhythmia controls and broadly comparable effects to SGB alone across most endpoints, supporting further exploration of multimodal autonomic modulation strategies without implying definitive superiority of the combined regimen.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, a Dex-only treatment arm was not included. The experimental design focused on evaluating an SGB-based combined strategy in comparison with SGB alone and amiodarone, while limiting animal use under the 3R principles. Nevertheless, the absence of a Dex-only group limits the ability to separate the independent contribution of Dex from SGB effects or to determine whether the observed associations arise from Dex, SGB or their interaction. Future studies incorporating a dedicated Dex-only arm will be necessary to clarify its isolated effects. Second, the arrhythmia model was defined by surface ECG criteria (fibrillatory waves, absence of P waves and irregular RR intervals), which is the standard approach in rodent AF-like models [12–14, 25]. However, given the high baseline heart rate in rats, reliable distinction between true AF and other organised atrial arrhythmias (atrial tachycardia, atrial flutter) by surface ECG alone is inherently limited. Direct electrophysiological validation through optical mapping or high-density atrial activation mapping was not performed in this study, and such evidence would be needed to definitively confirm fibrillatory atrial activation. Therefore, the observed arrhythmia is more conservatively characterised as AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia, and this diagnostic limitation should be considered when interpreting the results. Third, echocardiographic assessment focused on systolic function parameters and did not include diastolic function indices (e.g. E/A ratio, tissue Doppler velocities), which may be relevant to atrial remodelling and warrant assessment in future studies. Fourth, detailed assessments of cardiomyocyte apoptosis, inflammatory cell infiltration and microvascular density were not included. These indices represent important components of the myocardial remodelling spectrum, and their absence limits the completeness of phenotype characterisation. Future studies incorporating terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase-mediated dUTP nick end labelling (TUNEL) staining, cluster of differentiation 68 immunolabelling and anti-cluster of differentiation 31/α-smooth muscle actin-based microvascular analysis will allow a more comprehensive evaluation. Fifth, the assessment of inflammatory mediators was limited to serum IL-6 and TNF-α, and downstream signalling pathways (NF-κB, NLRP3, JAK/STAT) were not explored. Sixth, although ERP, conduction time and inducibility were assessed, more extensive electrophysiological testing (including ERP dispersion and conduction heterogeneity mapping) would further strengthen mechanistic interpretation and provide stronger evidence for fibrillatory versus organised atrial activation. Seventh, the relatively small sample size (n = 6 per group), the absence of formal multiplicity adjustment across outcome domains and the post hoc nature of the power analysis may limit statistical robustness and increase the likelihood of type I error. Eighth, only three stellate ganglia per group were used for immunofluorescence, reflecting practical tissue allocation constraints but limiting the power of this specific analysis. Finally, despite the extended 14-day cohort, the model still represents a short-term form of AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia, and future studies using chronic models and broader mechanistic analyses will be necessary to validate these findings.

Conclusion

Our study demonstrates that SGB combined with Dex is associated with attenuation of atrial remodelling and cardiac dysfunction in a rat model of AF-like atrial tachyarrhythmia. The underlying mechanisms may involve sympathetic inhibition and anti-inflammatory actions and are associated with altered SK2 ion channel expression in the SG, as evidenced by electrophysiological studies and multi-level molecular validation. Direct comparisons with SGB alone did not establish statistically significant superiority of SGB + Dex across most major endpoints, and the absence of a Dex-only treatment group limits the ability to attribute specific effects to Dex independently. The mechanistic role of SK2 in the SG also requires further validation with targeted interventional studies. These findings provide preliminary experimental evidence supporting a multimodal autonomic modulation approach and warrant further investigation in longer-duration animal models and clinical trials to validate its translational potential.

Root morphology mediates yield and fruit quality responses of cherry tomato to a Eu3⁺-VTB450 photoconversion film
Authors: Yinchao Zhang, Jiandong Wang, Rui Guo, Chuanjuan Wang, Jingyi Li, Xinyu Cong, Yongxing Wen, Xiaoyang Liang, Haitao Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and aimsCrop yield and quality are influenced by root morphology, but the impact of root morphology on cherry tomatoes under Ethylene–vinyl acetate copolymer (EVA) film mulching (EUV treatment) with Eu3⁺-VTB450 light converter remains unclear.MethodsIn this study, “Meiying No. 2” cherry tomato was selected as the experimental material, and a one-factor randomized block field experiment with conventional EVA film mulching (CK) and EUV treatment was conducted to investigate the effects of EUV treatment on the yield and quality of the crop.ResultsThe results showed that EUV treatment induced reconstruction of the light spectral composition, resulting in a 17.0% and 13.5% increase in blue light and red–orange light proportions, respectively (P < 0.05). Compared with CK, EUV treatment improved root development, SPAD values, chlorophyll fluorescence parameters, and gas exchange parameters of cherry tomatoes at each growth stage, with agronomic traits improved to varying degrees. The contents of lycopene, soluble sugar, soluble solids, soluble protein, and vitamin C in fruits increased by 20.4%, 15.9%, 36.6%, 36.5%, and 13.4%, respectively, and the harvest yield increased by 33.1%. Furthermore, partial least squares path modeling revealed that EUV treatment optimized root growth by regulating the light spectral composition, which in turn enhanced plant photosynthesis and growth, ultimately leading to comprehensive improvements in yield and quality.ConclusionsIn conclusion, this study systematically investigated the effects of EUV on the growth physiology of cherry tomatoes, while providing novel solutions for light quality regulation in horticultural plants.

Introduction

The cherry tomato (Solanum lycopersicum var. cerasiforme) is a variant of the cultivated subspecies of the genus Solanum. It is native to the Andes Mountains along the western Pacific coast of South America [66]. The cherry tomato has been cultivated extensively worldwide due to its rich nutritional value [58]. It has become a widely cultivated high-quality horticultural crop [57]. As demonstrated in Zheng et al. [82], the global cherry tomato cultivation area had reached approximately 1 million hectares by 2020. However, it should be noted that these statistics are incomplete. The area dedicated to planting in China is approximately 150,000 hectares, with over 50% of this area being used for protected cultivation. This is a key factor in ensuring the annual supply of cherry tomatoes [84]. Furthermore, numerous studies have demonstrated that cherry tomato fruits are abundant in organic acids, vitamin C, lycopene, and other functional components [68, 76], thereby providing the body with essential nutrients and playing an active role in improving immunity, lowering blood pressure, and reducing the risk of chronic diseases such as cancer and heart disease [41, 70]. The yield and quality of cherry tomatoes are primarily influenced by the photosynthetic efficiency of the aboveground parts and the root system's capacity to absorb water and nutrients [11, 30]. These processes are not only influenced by land salinization [15] deficit irrigation [18] but also regulated by light quality, light intensity, and other light environmental factors [55]. Optimizing the light environment and spectral ratio in greenhouse cultivation has become a key technical direction for enhancing the production efficiency of cherry tomatoes.

However, cherry tomato plants exhibit a high degree of sensitivity to alterations in the light environment. The quality and intensity of light, as well as other factors related to light, have been demonstrated to exert a influence on the final yield and quality of fruit [25, 26]. This occurs by regulating photosynthetic efficiency and material accumulation. Consequently, optimizing the light spectral composition in protected cultivation has become a critical technical approach to improve cherry tomato production efficiency.

Light wavelength is a core environmental factor regulating plant growth and development [73]. The activity of plant photoreceptors and signaling pathways are influenced by signals from different wavelengths of light. Notably, root development exhibits high sensitivity to light signals from branches (Lee et al. 2017), these subsequently regulate root growth (Van Gelderen et al. 2018), ultimately achieving precise control over growth and development [7, 52, 79]. Current light environment regulation for greenhouse cherry tomatoes relies on artificial supplementary lighting technologies, including light-emitting diodes (LEDs) and high-pressure sodium lamps (HPSs). Recent studies have shown that red light-emitting diodes (LEDs) can enhance the nutritional quality of fruits [62]. In addition, combining blue LEDs with high-pressure sodium (HPS) lamps can enhance plant disease resistance and elevate yield [63]. However, this technology has several drawbacks, including high equipment purchase and operational costs, high energy consumption, high energy consumption, and difficulty in being widely promoted in small- and medium-scale facility cultivation [10, 50]. In contrast, the new rare earth light conversion greenhouse film regulates the spectral ratio through light-conversion properties. The advantages of this technology include low cost, simple installation, long service life, and no additional energy consumption [12]. It has gradually emerged as a key development direction for facility light environment regulation.

Eu3⁺-VTB450-doped EVA film (Eu3⁺-doped luminescent material) is a novel rare earth photoconversion film. Its core advantages include excellent light and color uniformity, as well as the light-transmitting agent's outstanding photothermal stability [43]. Among these, the rare earth europium ion, as the core component of the light-transmitting agent [69, 72], forms a ternary organic complex that absorbs photon energy through the electronic ground-state-to-excited-state transition (Zhang et al. 2020b), enabling wavelength conversion. For instance, the light-transmitting agent derived from bay-substituted perylene diimides achieves dual-band excitation (350 nm and 580 nm) and emission (430 nm and 651 nm), with the controlled post-conversion spectrum highly matching the plant's photosynthetic absorption spectrum [75]. Numerous studies have confirmed that rare earth photoconversion films can promote the growth of horticultural vegetable crops and improve disease resistance by optimizing light quality [34] and increase the content of vitamin C, lycopene, and other functional components in large tomato fruits [1, 51]. These has a significant effect on the yield and quality of agricultural products [6, 42]. However, existing research on rare earth photoconversion films mainly focuses on solanaceous vegetable with an emphasis on yield and quality effects, while cherry tomato has not been explored, and the integrated mechanisms linking root morphology and photosynthetic physiology remain unclear. Therefore, the regulatory effects of EVA film coating (EUV treatment) with Eu3⁺-VTB450 light-transmitting agent on root morphology‑mediated photosynthetic physiology, plant growth, and fruit yield and quality in cherry tomatoes necessitate elucidation.

Based on the limitations of existing research, we conducted an experimental study using “Meiying No. 2” cherry tomato as the experimental material, and a field comparison experiment was conducted between ordinary EVA film (CK) and Eu3⁺-VTB450-doped EVA film mulching (EUV treatment), and hypothesized that EUV treatment contributes to the improvement of cherry tomato fruit yield and quality, and this enhancing effect is inseparable from the modifications of root morphology and photosynthetic efficiency. The main objectives are as follows: (a) To clarify the regulatory effects of the EUV film on the greenhouse light environment, as well as the agronomic traits and photosynthetic parameters of cherry tomatoes at different growth stages; (b) To evaluate the impacts of the EUV film on the root morphology, biomass, fruit yield, and quality of cherry tomatoes; (c) To establish the intrinsic relationships between the yield and quality of cherry tomatoes and their agronomic traits, photosynthetic parameters, and root morphology.

Materials and methods

Experimental site description

The experiment was conducted at the experimental base of Hebei University of Engineering (Handan City, Hebei Province, China). The region has a warm temperate continental monsoon climate, with an altitude of approximately 50 m, an annual sunshine duration of approximately 2607 h, an average temperature of 16 °C, and a frost-free period of approximately 220 days. The soil at the experimental site is loam, with a pH of 8.1, bulk density of 1.43 g/cm3, field capacity of 24.17%, organic matter content of 8.25 g/kg, total nitrogen content of 0.68 g/kg, available phosphorus content of 20.05 mg/kg, and available potassium content of 58.13 mg/kg.

Experimental design and field management

The experiment was conducted from April 2024 to July 2024. A randomized block design was adopted, and two treatments were set up based on different greenhouse films: rare earth photoconversion film (EUV) and ordinary EVA film (CK). Each treatment was set up with five replicate greenhouses, and one plant per greenhouse was taken as a sample for each indicator measurement, resulting in a sample size of five plants per indicator. The experimental cultivar was the “Meiying No. 2” cherry tomato, co-bred by the Institute of Vegetables and Flowers, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences, and Beijing Zhongshu Horticultural Research and Development Center. Two films were supplied by Shandong Xintianhe Plastic Co., Ltd., with a thickness of 0.12 mm. Seedlings were purchased from Youxian Seed Industry Co., Ltd. (Shouguang City, Shandong Province). The mean plant height of seedlings was approximately 10 cm, and each seedling had 5–6 fully expanded true leaves. Prior to transplanting the cherry tomatoes, dig furrows 20–25 cm deep and 20–30 cm wide along the future planting rows. Apply mixed fertilizer to the bottom of the furrows and thoroughly mix it with the soil using a rotary tiller. The application rates of nitrogen (N), phosphorus pentoxide (P₂O₅), and potassium oxide (K₂O) in the base fertilizer were 281 kg/ha, 180 kg/ha, and 371 kg/ha, respectively. The planting density was 40 cm (plant spacing) × 80 cm (row spacing), with 150 plants per treatment. Topping was performed when plants had 6–7 true leaves. Irrigation, fertilization, and pest control measures were implemented in accordance with local standard agronomic practices (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Schematic diagram of the experimental design, CK is the control treatment, representing ordinary EVA film; EUV means EUV photoconversion film with Eu3⁺-VTB450 added

Determination of light environment indicators

To evaluate the effects of the two films on the light spectral composition inside the greenhouses, a QE65000 spectrometer (Ocean Optics, USA) was used to determine the purple light (400–440 nm, Pu), blue light (440–510 nm, Bl), green light (510–610 nm, Gr), and red–orange light (610–710 nm, Ro) at approximately 5 cm above the cherry tomato canopy inside each greenhouse. Measurements were conducted under typical weather conditions. All measurements were performed in the morning (between 9:00–11:00) [53]. Five measurement points were set in each greenhouse, and each point was measured three times, with the results averaged.

Determination of phenotypic traits of cherry tomato plants

In each greenhouse, three plants were randomly selected and tagged. Among them, one tagged plants were used for continuous measurement at the seedling stage (20 April), anthesis (23 May), and fruit expanding stage (30 June), and stem diameter (Sd), plant height (Ph), leaf length (Ll), and leaf width (Lw) were measured using a vernier caliper and a ruler [16]. The other two tagged plants were subjected to destructive sampling with one plants at the seedling stage and one plants at anthesis, respectively. These plants were then separated into aboveground and underground components. The plant samples were oven-dried to a constant weight, and the aboveground biomass (Ab) and underground (root) biomass (Bb) were determined.

Measurement of chlorophyll content, fluorescence, and photosynthetic parameters

Leaf relative chlorophyll content (SPAD) was determined using a Dualex Scientific + plant polyphenol-chlorophyll meter (Force-A, France). Chlorophyll fluorescence parameters (qP, ΦPSII, Fv'/Fm') were measured with an OS-30P + modulated chlorophyll fluorometer (Opti-Sciences, USA) following 20 min of dark adaptation with leaf clips. Photosynthetic gas exchange parameters (Pn, Gs, Ci, Tr) were determined using a CIRAS-3 portable photosynthesis system (PP Systems, USA). The measurements were conducted on the one tagged plants monitored for aboveground phenotypes. To minimize experimental error, the second fully expanded functional leaf below the growing point of each plant was selected for measurement [61].

Determination of root morphology, fruit yield, and fruit quality of cherry tomato

At the cherry tomato harvest period, one tagged plants that had been monitored for aboveground phenotypes were destructively sampled. After thorough rinsing with tap water, the roots and aboveground parts were separated, and their fresh weights were measured separately. Root morphology was scanned using an Epson Perfection V850 Pro scanner (Japan), and total root length (Rl), root surface area (Rs), root volume (Rv), and root tip number (Rt) were analyzed using WinRhizo root analysis program (Regent Instruments, Quebec, Canada) [83]. Subsequently, the plant tissues were oven-killed at 105 °C for 45 min and then oven-dried to a constant weight at 75 °C to determine their dry weights. A plant is identified as entering the harvest stage when more than 80% of the fruits on the first ear have changed color, the fruits on the second and third ears have set and started to expand, and continuous flowering occurs on the upper-middle part of the plant. From the start of the harvest period, mature cherry tomato fruits were harvested from one randomly selected plants per greenhouse and weighed, five fruits were randomly selected from each plant, weighed using an electronic balance, and the average value was calculated. The final fruit weight was expressed in grams (g) [19]. The yield per plant was also measured using an electronic balance and expressed in grams.and the total yield per plot was calculated after the final harvest.

Lycopene (LYC) [65], soluble solids (TSS), ascorbic acid (VC) [37], organic acid (OA), water-soluble sugar (WSS) [39], and soluble protein (SP) (Deng et al. 2012) contents were determined by spectrophotometry, the hand-held refractometer method, the 2,6-dichloroindophenol method, acid–base titration, anthrone colorimetry, and the Coomassie brilliant blue G-250 method, respectively. To facilitate the visualization of cherry tomato fruit quality, each measurement index was normalized to a standard value between 0 and 1 [24]. Data analysis

Data were processed using Microsoft Excel 2021 (Microsoft, USA); After confirming normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test and homogeneity of variances using Levene's test, IBM SPSS Statistics 27 (IBM, USA) was used to perform an independent samples t-test to determine the statistical significance of differences between the two treatments. The R package “plspm” was used to construct the partial least squares structural equation model (PLS-SEM), and the significance of each path coefficient was tested using the Bootstrap method with 1000 resamplings. The overall goodness-of-fit of the model was evaluated using the Goodness-of-Fit Index (GOF). Each treatment was set up with five biological replicates. Data are expressed as mean ± standard error (SE). Additionally, Origin 2021 (OriginLab, USA) and Microsoft PowerPoint (Microsoft, USA) were used for graphing.

Relative to CK treatment, EUV treatment significantly decreased the proportions of Pu and Gr, whereas Bl and Ro increased significantly, both on sunny and cloudy days (Fig. 2). Effects of EUV film on photosynthesis, SPAD, and chlorophyll fluorescence of cherry tomato leaves at different growth stages

Compared with CK, EUV treatment significantly increased the net photosynthetic rate (Pn) and transpiration rate (Tr) of cherry tomatoes at the seedling stage by 43.9% and 22.2%, respectively. Photosynthetic parameters also increased significantly at the anthesis and fruit expanding stages: Pn, stomatal conductance (Gs), intercellular CO₂ concentration (Ci), and Tr increased by 30.4–33.3%, 8.4–17.1%, 16.1–19.2%, and 25.2–29.5%, respectively (Fig. 4a–d). Additionally, the SPAD of cherry tomato leaves increased significantly by 19.3% (anthesis stage) and 21.6% (fruit expanding stage) compared with CK. The photochemical quenching coefficient (qP) and the actual photochemical efficiency of PSII (ΦPSII) were significantly higher than those in the control (CK). This promoting effect became more pronounced as the growth stages advanced. Specifically, during the anthesis and fruit-expanding stages, qP increased by 20.8–22.7% and ΦPSII increased by 22.1–24.6%. Over the entire observation period, qP increased by 3.8–14.5% and ΦPSII increased by 10.6–24.5%. Intrinsic correlation between yield and quality of cherry tomato and plant phenotype and photosynthetic physiology at each growth stage

To clarify the regulatory mechanism of the EUV photoconversion film on cherry tomato yield and fruit quality, a PLS-PM model was constructed. The results showed that the spectrum affected the SPAD and photosynthetic parameters by regulating root morphology and ultimately improving yield and fruit quality by promoting plant growth (Fig. 7a, Table 1). Additionally, fruit quality was positively associated with root traits (P < 0.05; Fig. 7). To explore the intrinsic relationships between fruit yield, fruit quality, greenhouse spectral composition, plant phenotype, and photosynthetic physiology across different growth stages, we conducted Pearson and partial Mantel correlation tests on cherry tomato indicators according to growth period (Fig. 8a–d). The results showed that yield was significantly positively correlated with Bl and Ro throughout the growth period, but negatively correlated with Pu and Gr. Furthermore, yield and fruit quality were significantly positively correlated with Rl and Pn at the seedling stage, Rs and Tr at the anthesis stage, and Pn and Tr at the fruit expanding stage. Discussion

Effects of spectral reconstruction on the plant growth process

Seminal studies [32, 38, 79, 81] have shown that the yield and quality of cherry tomato fruits are highly sensitive to changes in light spectral wavelength. These findings highlight the pivotal role of spectral wavelength in regulating plant growth and, in turn, the formation of yield and fruit quality [32, 38, 79, 81]. Previous studies have demonstrated that the application of other types of rare earth photoconversion films can promote vegetable growth and development, enhance stress resistance, improve yield and fruit quality, and perform various other functions, with its use in protected horticultural production well-documented [12, 28]. In this study, the Eu3⁺-VTB450-doped EVA photoconversion film (EUV) was found to affect plant growth via spectral reconstruction. In terms of plant phenotype, EUV treatment increased the key phenotypic traits such as Sd, Ph, Ll, and Lw of cherry tomatoes from seedling stage to fruit expanding stage (Fig. 3), consistent with the findings [74]. This phenomenon may be attributed to the increased ratio of Ro, which activates the photopigment signaling pathway, upregulates the expression of genes related to plant-hormone and induces cell elongation [14, 35, 61], thereby promoting plant longitudinal growth and lateral expansion [2]. These changes in plant phenotypic traits (Fig. 7) laid a solid foundation for subsequent biomass accumulation and yield formation.

Response of photosynthetic efficiency to spectral reconstruction

Photosynthetic efficiency is the a key factor that directly determines plant growth and the realization of yield potential [83]. In modern horticultural research, light spectral wavelength regulation has become a precise approach to modulating plant light signal transduction pathways, thereby interfering with the entire physiological metabolism process of horticultural plants and improving photosynthetic efficiency. It thus serves as a core technical pathway for the high-quality and efficient cultivation of horticultural crops [26, 32, 33, 38]. In this study, the significant improvement effect of EUV treatment on photosynthetic gas exchange parameters (Pn, Gs, Ci, Tr) was highly consistent with the results of [44]. The increase of Pn, Gs, Ci, and Tr was primarily attributable to the fact that crops predominantly absorbed red light and blue-violet light [59]. Conversely, EUV treatment enhanced the ratio of Ro and Bl in cherry tomatoes, stimulated the openness of leaf stomata, and created more favorable conditions for CO₂ absorption and water transport [8]. Concurrently, the optimization of photosynthetic gas exchange parameters directly reflects the enhancement of the carbon assimilation process [56], which can provide sufficient and continuous carbohydrates for plant growth, ensuring a stable material supply during the key stages of cherry tomatoes [27]. The significant increase in SPAD at the anthesis and fruit expansion stages may be attributed to EUV treatment optimizing spectral composition to promote chlorophyll synthesis or slow its degradation rate, thereby improving leaf light-harvesting capacity [35, 45]. Mansoori et al. [48] and Ouzounis et al. [54] indicated that increasing the Bl ratio and adjusting the blue-red light combination could enhance chlorophyll content elevation, suggesting that the SPAD increase in this study may be attributed to EUV-induced blue light proportion enhancement.Furthermore. the significant increases in qP, Fv/Fm, and ΦPSII under EUV treatment (Fig. 4) indicate that EUV treatment enhanced electron transfer efficiency and reduced light energy dissipation as heat, thereby improving photosynthetic quantum efficiency and the activity of the PSII reaction center [20]. This ultimately exerted a positive effect on plant growth and fruit productivity.

Spectral regulation of the root morphology optimization mechanism of cherry tomato and its correlation with biomass accumulation and fruit quality

As the primary organ responsible for nutrient absorption, the optimization of root morphological structure is essential for enhancing biomass accumulation [21, 46]. Gao et al. [13] found that BL irradiation can promote the transport of transcriptional signals in root development, thereby promoting root growth. This phenomenon can provide a rationale for the observed increase in Rl, Rs, Rv, and Rt following EUV treatment, as evidenced in Fig. 5a-d. Another potential explanation is that EUV treatment stimulated the synergistic effect of auxin polar and non-polar transport, thereby achieving root-shoot synergistic optimization [23]. In terms of biomass accumulation, Ab and Bb in EUV treatment showed a significant increase of 16.07–34.38% and 8.49–28.59% in each growth period (Fig. 5e-f). Following the optimization of plant root morphology, the absorption efficiency of mineral elements such as nitrogen and phosphorus in cherry tomatoes was promoted [51], thereby ensuring sufficient material for plant growth and development [77, 77, 78, 78]. This finding is in accordance with the conclusions drawn by [71], which demonstrated that the regulation of root morphology can enhance crop yield and dry matter accumulation. This underscores the efficacy of EUV treatment in fostering the growth of cherry tomatoes through the promotion of root–shoot coordination.

In terms of improving fruit quality and yield, rare earth light conversion films containing new light conversion agents have been proven to offer significant advantages [47]. In this study, the elevated LYC content under EUV treatment may be attributed to phytochrome A/B‑mediated induction of ELONGATED HYPOCOTYL 5 and PHYTOCHROME INTERACTING FACTORS via Ro, which further upregulates phytoene synthase gene expression [62]. WSS is an important index for measuring the maturity of cherry tomatoes [4, 36], and its content increased significantly with EUV treatment (Fig. 6d). It is hypothesized that, following a change in the Ro/Bl ratio induced by EUV, the induction of sucrose synthesis was accelerated by upregulating the expression of the fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase gene in plants [5, 80]. Furthermore, EUV was found to affect the production of melatonin in fruit and stimulate the activity of amylase and invertase, thereby optimizing the conversion efficiency of starch and sucrose to glucose and fructose [35]. In addition, Fukudome et al. [9] demonstrated through metabolomics and sugar composition analysis that the increase in WSS was inseparable from TSS, which also explains the synchronous increase in TSS observed under EUV treatment in this study. [51] demonstrated that VC in tomato fruits increased significantly under a rare earth light conversion film, a finding consistent with this study. This is likely due to the close relationship between light quality and the regulation of photosynthesis-related pigment biosynthesis [12].

In terms of yield components, single fruit weight and the number of fruits per plant increased significantly under EUV treatment, consistent with the research results [6]. This was the result of multi-factor synergy. The comprehensive effect of the greenhouse covering light conversion film during periods of low temperature [64] provided a stable microenvironment for the growth of cherry tomatoes, improving photosynthesis and the distribution of photosynthetic products [67]. Secondly, during fruit development, EUV treatment prompted plants to transport more photosynthetic products to the fruit, ultimately achieving a simultaneous increase in single fruit weight and fruit number [22]. Furthermore, the improvement in fruit quality and yield of cherry tomatoes is inseparable from the optimization of root morphology. Previous studies have demonstrated the positive effects of root morphology on crops such as tomato and sorghum [17, 40]. This result was also confirmed in the present study (Fig. 7). Optimized root morphology increases the contact area with soil, thereby enhancing the plant’s ability to acquire water and mineral nutrients including N, P, and K. The improved nutrient uptake supports higher leaf chlorophyll content and photosynthetic efficiency, which in turn provide additional energy for root growth and nutrient absorption [31]. Such root–shoot interactions help establish a more balanced source–sink relationship, ensuring that more photosynthates are allocated to developing fruits, ultimately leading to simultaneous increases in fruit yield and quality.

Path and potential of spectral reconstruction driving yield and fruit quality improvement

To elucidate the regulatory pathways governing cherry tomato yield and fruit quality, this study utilized Pearson's correlation analysis and partial Mantel tests to disentangle indirect effects among variables [29]. Furthermore, a partial least squares path model (PLS-PM) was developed to decipher the synergistic regulatory mechanisms of key factors on these traits, thereby uncovering the intrinsic relationships spanning the light spectrum, root morphology, plant phenotype, physiological indicators, yield, and fruit quality. The PLS-PM indicated that the light spectrum influences root morphology, which in turn enhances SPAD values and photosynthetic parameters, thereby promoting plant growth and ultimately leading to improved yield and fruit quality (Fig. 7).This aligns with previous findings that phenotypic traits, including leaf morphology, are key determinants of fruit quality [60]. Specifically, in our experiment, Bl and Ro light proportions were positively correlated with Ph, Sd, Ll, and Lw across all growth stages. Concurrently, significant positive correlations were observed between Lw at the seedling stage and Ph and Rv at the anthesis stage, between Lw and Rs at the fruit expanding stage, and between yield and fruit quality (Fig. 8b–d). These findings further underscore the pivotal role of phenotypic traits in determining yield and fruit quality, a conclusion consistent with the pathways identified by the PLS-PM. Regarding physiological processes, the correlation between photosynthetic parameters and yield/quality suggests that photosynthesis, involving interconnected processes like carbon assimilation and photoprotection, plays a multifaceted role in yield formation and fruit quality regulation [49]. In summary, both phenotypic and physiological traits act synergistically across multiple dimensions to enhance cherry tomato yield and fruit quality (Fig. 7). Our study confirms our hypothesis and shows that the Eu3⁺-VTB450 light conversion agent promotes photosynthesis and phenotypic growth and development by adjusting the spectral ratio and optimizing root morphology, thereby improving fruit yield and quality. This provides new evidence for the precise application of light quality regulation technology in protected horticulture.

Notably, this study focused primarily on the biological effects on cherry tomato plants and did not perform complete optical characterization of the two films or systematic monitoring of the greenhouse environment. Therefore, future research should conduct comprehensive optical characterization of the film materials to elucidate their spectral modification features and thermal stability, and install environmental sensors in the greenhouse for real-time monitoring of key parameters such as temperature and humidity, thereby allowing direct correlations to be established between plant responses and specific environmental variations. This will provide more accurate theoretical support for designing more efficient light-conversion film materials and supporting cultivation schemes.

Conclusion

The effects of EVA film mulching using the Eu3⁺-VTB450 light conversion agent on the light environment, plant growth and development, photosynthetic physiology, and the yield and quality of cherry tomatoes were clarified. The results showed that the EUV film altered the spectral ratio in the greenhouse, reducing the transmittance of Pu and Gr while increasing the ratio of Bl and Ro. This created more favorable light conditions for cherry tomatoes. EUV treatment was also found to continuously promote the phenotypic traits and photosynthetic characteristic parameters of cherry tomatoes at different growth stages, optimizing root morphology and achieving a comprehensive improvement in cherry tomato yield and quality. Compared with ordinary EVA film, the fruit yield of EUV treatment increased significantly by 33.1%. EUV treatment may effectively improve the low light quality adaptability of conventional greenhouse films by regulating the spectral–root morphology–photosynthesis–growth–yield–quality cascade. This contributes to enhanced facility agricultural production and provides practical references for improving light management strategies and advancing novel agricultural photoconversion materials.

Effect of nasogastric versus orogastric tube placement on ventilator-associated pneumonia incidence in critically ill patients: a study protocol for a cluster randomised crossover trial in 16 intensive care units in France (SONG trial)
Authors: Josselin Saletes, Christophe Guitton, Julie Valleroy, Severine Guillarme, Carole Haubertin, Gregory Paris, Lucie Muller, Cécile Rousseau, Prescilla Gamon, Sylvie L’Hotellier, Jean-Marie Forel, Catherine Roussel, Antoine Garin, Célina Morand, Eric Maury, Nathanael Mangeard, Laetitia Menard, Pierre-Yves Arnaud, Aurélie Lejeune, Eglantine Rouanet, Jean-Christophe Callahan

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPatients in intensive care units (ICUs) frequently require mechanical ventilation, with approximately half needing invasive ventilation through an orotracheal tube. For these patients, gastric tube (GT) insertion is routinely performed to administer nutrition and medications or to drain gastric contents. The insertion route (oral or nasal) may affect the incidence of ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP), a significant ICU care complication. This study aims to compare the impact of oral versus nasal GT insertion on the incidence of VAP in intubated ICU patients.Methods and analysisThe SONG trial (NCT 05915663) is a multicentre, open-label, two-period, two-intervention, cluster randomised crossover superiority trial. 16 French ICUs will participate. ICUs will be randomised to periods of nasogastric or orogastric tube placement. The trial includes a practice standardisation period, followed by two 12-month inclusion periods separated by a monitoring and washout period. The primary endpoint is the incidence rate of VAP at day 28, confirmed by three independent physicians. Secondary endpoints include the ease of GT insertion, measured by the number of attempts.Ethics and disseminationThis study received approval from a central ethical review board on 12 April 2024 (CPP Sud-est VI, registration number 23.00943.000175). Patients are included after informed consent or, when not possible, from next of kin. If none are available, the investigator will proceed with emergency inclusion, following French law. When consent is initially obtained from the next of kin or through emergency inclusion, the investigator will seek consent from the patient as soon as possible. Data will be anonymised and patient confidentiality maintained. Results will be published in peer-reviewed journals and presented at scientific meetings.Trial registration numberNCT05915663.

Introduction

Patients in intensive care units (ICUs) are often admitted in critical condition, requiring invasive interventions to sustain vital functions such as respiration, haemodynamics and nutrition. Approximately 50% of these patients require mechanical ventilation with an orotracheal tube.1 2 For such intubated patients, a gastric tube (GT) is commonly inserted to administer nutrition, medications or for gastric decompression.

In France, GT insertion is a routine nursing procedure carried out under medical supervision.3 It involves inserting a flexible tube through the nasal or oral route into the oesophagus and finally into the stomach. The choice of insertion route often depends on local practice, specific patient needs and medical contraindications. The success rate for first attempts is about 85% for oral and 68% for nasal insertions.4 While GTs are necessary for patient management in ICUs, their insertion is not without risk. Although complications are rare, tube insertion can cause nasal bleeding, pharyngeal and laryngeal injury, hypertensive episodes and tube coiling.57 Tube malposition can cause severe lesions in case of tracheal or pulmonary insertion, oesophageal perforation or intracranial placement.6

One of the most frequent ICU-acquired infections is ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP).8 VAP is defined as the development of a pneumonia in a patient who has been mechanically ventilated for at least 48 hours.9 The incidence of VAP varies from 3.3 to 25.5 per 1000 ventilation days.10 11 Its attributable mortality is estimated to be between 5% and 10%.1214 Its pathogenesis is attributable to the aspiration of micro-organism-containing secretions into the normally sterile respiratory tract.15 Numerous strategies have been investigated to reduce the incidence of VAP in ICU patients, either by minimising the risk of aspiration or by limiting the growth of pathogenic micro-organisms in the oral, nasal or gastric cavities. However, their overall impact on VAP incidence remains unclear.8

The GT insertion route may influence the incidence of VAP, though this remains a subject of debate.16 The proposed causal link between nasal route GT insertion and VAP is complex and multifaceted.15 First, nasal insertion of tubes can increase the risk of sinusitis due to the proximity to the paranasal sinuses, which are prone to infection when blocked or irritated by a tube.1719 Sinus infections can act as a reservoir for pathogenic bacteria, which may colonise the pharyngeal space, descend into the lower respiratory tract, thereby increasing the risk of VAP.20 Additionally, the nasal route may disrupt normal nasal flora and mucosal integrity, irrespective of the onset of sinusitis.21 In particular, the presence of a nasogastric (NG) tube has been associated with an increase in Gram-negative bacterial infections in intensive care patients.22 However, despite a physiological rationale suggesting that the nasal route may increase the risk of VAP, supporting evidence in the clinical literature is lacking.

Given the widespread use of GTs in ICUs, even a small reduction in VAP rates through optimised insertion practices could significantly impact patient outcomes and healthcare resources. This study aims to provide evidence that could lead to changes in clinical guidelines to improve the care of critically ill patients.

We hypothesise that oral insertion of the GT will reduce VAP incidence compared with nasal insertion in intubated patients in the ICU.

Methods and analysis

Study design

SONG (NCT 05915663) is a multicentre, open-label, superiority, two-period, two-intervention, pragmatic, cluster randomised crossover trial23 (figure 1). The development of this trial protocol followed the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendation for Interventional Trials guidelines.24

Study planning.

This methodological design facilitates patient management, enrolment and adherence to the intervention according to the study arm. Patients are often in emergency situations requiring the rapid placement of a GT immediately following intubation. Delaying GT insertion for individual randomisation is impractical. Given the frequency of this procedure, a cluster design is more feasible than performing numerous individual randomisations. Moreover, this approach simplifies the task for nurses. GT insertion is a common procedure in ICUs and having the same study arm throughout an entire period eases patient management and reduces the risk of errors during initial tube insertions and any subsequent insertions. This consistency prevents protocol deviations related to established habits. Furthermore, the crossover design mitigates centre effects since each centre will undertake both periods and thus both GT insertion sites. Finally, the two 12-month inclusion periods will help eliminate potential seasonal biases commonly observed in ICUs.

Interventions

Prior to the start of inclusions, there will be a 1-month period of practice standardisation in each centre according to their randomisation arm: patients will receive either a NG (A-Arm) or an OG tube (B-Arm). This is to ensure maximum adherence to the intervention during the enrolment period. Enrolment will then be open for 12 months, with sequential interventions according to the study arm: eight centres will commence with NG tube placement and the other eight with OG tube placement (online supplemental table 1). At the end of this initial inclusion period, a 1-month monitoring period will be followed by a 1-month washout period. Subsequently, a new month of practice standardisation specific to the second method of GT insertion will commence. A second 12-month inclusion period will then begin, followed by a month for monitoring the last included patients (figure 1). Each patient is monitored for the duration of his or her stay in the ICU, up to a maximum of 28 days (figure 2).

Study flow diagram.

Initial GT placement will be verified according to each centre’s standard practice, either by chest X-ray or colourimetric capnography. Further daily verifications using auscultation are recommended. Investigators will document each verification, including whether the length marker remains in the same position.

Investigators will record the number of attempts for each placement. During the NG insertion period, if an NG tube insertion procedure fails, the next step is to try through the other nostril. If the failure persists, the tube will be inserted via the oral route. During the orogastric insertion period, if an OG tube insertion fails, the tube will be inserted via the nasal route. After three failed attempts, in the event of complications, or if the operator deems it necessary, a change in the insertion site is recommended. In such cases, the investigator will record the number of attempts, the operator and the insertion site. If the initial tube placement does not adhere to the randomisation arm strategy and the patient requires a new tube placement, insofar as possible, the new GT should be placed according to the randomisation strategy. From day 0 to day 28, if the patient experiences significant discomfort related to the insertion site or if reinsertion via the assigned site is impossible, the new insertion site must be documented.

The only difference in management between the two arms is the GT access route. All other care procedures follow current guidelines, regardless of the patient’s assigned arm.

Outcome measures

Primary outcome

Incidence rate of VAP at day 28 based on GT insertion site.

Each day, study patients will be screened for suspected VAP, based on the following criteria: hyperthermia >38°C or hypothermia <36°C, increased oxygen requirement (based on FiO2 levels), quantity and quality of tracheal secretions. In the case of clinical suspicion of VAP, the patient will have a chest X-ray. Three physicians, independent of the study centres, will confirm the diagnosis of VAP, assessing it blindly to the insertion strategy. The assessment will be based on the analysis of anonymised chest X-rays, patient records and bacteriological reports collected whenever there is a suspicion of VAP. VAP is defined as the appearance of a new radiological infiltrate in any patient who has been on invasive ventilation (intubated and ventilated) for more than 48 hours, along with at least two of the following factors: temperature >38°C or <36°C, leucocytosis >12 g/L, purulent tracheal secretions or increased oxygen requirements.

Sample size

For this cluster cross-over trial, the total number of clusters is set at 16, with eight clusters per arm. Each ICU corresponds to one cluster. In this cross-over trial, each arm corresponds to the sequence of performing the two studied techniques over the periods (Arm A ‘NG in period 1 then OG tube in period 2’ and Arm B ‘OG tube in period 1 then NG tube in period 2’).

Considering an expected VAP rate of 18% with the NGT, at least 75 patients per cluster and per period need to enrol to demonstrate, under a two-sided hypothesis, an absolute difference of 5% in the VAP rate between the two GT insertion routes. This calculation assumes an alpha risk of 5%, a power of 80% and an intracluster correlation coefficient of 0.02.

Thus, the total number of patients to be included in the study is 2400 (16 clusters with 150 patients per cluster).

If a cluster reaches the inclusion target before the end of the corresponding period for the current randomisation arm, it will be possible to include an additional 10% of patients, that is, up to seven additional patients per cluster per period. Beyond this threshold, the cluster will stop further inclusions but will continue to insert GTs via the route specified by the randomisation arm until the end of the 12th month following the start of inclusions for that arm.

Recruitment modalities

The investigator will be the nurse or the physician responsible for the patient’s care and will be responsible for enrolment according to the eligibility criteria. Every patient with invasive mechanical ventilation of less than 24 hours will be assessed for eligibility.

Assignment of interventions

Randomisation

In this study, randomisation occurs at the cluster level and will be conducted centrally using a secure computer generated sequence before the start of the study. Clusters will be randomised on a 1:1 ratio.

Blinding

Blinding of healthcare staff to arm allocation is not possible due to the nature of the intervention.

Three physicians independent of the study centres will assess the primary endpoint blindly to the insertion strategy.

An independent statistician will perform the statistical analysis, blinded to the insertion strategy.

Data collection, management and analysis

Data collection and management

The nurse in charge of the patient will be responsible for data collection. The data will be coded and recorded in an electronic case report form () (Ennov Clinical). The Data Management unit of the Delegation for Clinical Research and Innovation (DRCI) of Angers University Hospital will monitor collected data and screening logs in each participating centre. A blind review of the data will be performed before the database is locked. The database will be locked according to standard operating procedures in force at the DRCI, and the data will be extracted for statistical analyses.

Statistical analysis

Description of the two groups: descriptive statistics will be used to summarise the characteristics of the two groups. Continuous variables will be presented as mean and SD with their corresponding 95% CIs if they follow a normal distribution. If not, they will be presented as median and IQR. Categorical variables will be described using exact numbers and percentages with corresponding 95% CIs. No statistical test will be performed.

Primary endpoint analysis

Our analysis of outcomes will follow an intention-to-treat approach.

The primary endpoint will be represented as histograms for the two arms with their confidence intervals, both overall and within each centre. The statistical analysis of the primary endpoint will be based on variance decomposition using a mixed-effects regression model, in order to account for the nested structure of the data (individuals within clusters) and to handle the correlation within clusters and the crossover periods.

Secondary endpoints analysis

Comparison of continuous variables between the two groups uses Student’s t-test for normally distributed data. Otherwise, the Mann-Whitney test applies. Comparison of categorical variables will use the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Prognostic criteria such as mortality and duration of mechanical ventilation will be analysed using Kaplan-Meier curves and compared using the log-rank test.

Level of statistical significance

A two-sided p value of 0.05 or less will be considered statistically significant.

Monitoring

Data monitoring

This study will use an internet-based data collection tool to store participant data. The eCRF is a secure, interactive web response system available at each study centre. The Data Management unit of the DRCI at Angers University Hospital will monitor the collected data and screening logs at each participating centre. A blind review of the data will take place before the database is locked. The database will be locked according to the standard operating procedures of the DRCI, and the data will then be extracted for statistical analysis.

Safety and study oversight

The study sponsor is the Centre Hospitalier du Mans. Investigators will report adverse events to the sponsor through the eCRF. As the study involves human participants with minimal risks and constraints, adverse events, reactions or incidents must be reported through the appropriate health vigilance pathways for the specific product or practice involved (eg, care vigilance, pharmacovigilance, material vigilance, haemovigilance), in accordance with current regulations. Notifiers should specify that the patient is included in a clinical trial and clearly identify the clinical trial in question. Given the favourable safety profile, an independent monitoring committee has not been established. However, the sponsor reserves the right to suspend or terminate the study at any time for safety reasons.

Ethics and dissemination

Research ethics approval

A central institutional ethical review board (Comité de Protection des Personnes, CPP) approved this trial on the 12 April 2024 (CPP Sud-est VI, registration number 23.00943.000175) in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Any important protocol modifications must be authorised by the CPP.

Consent to participate in the study

If the patient is able to understand and respond, the investigator will inform them about the possibility of participating in the study. The investigator will explain the study’s objectives, constraints, their right to refuse participation and the option to withdraw at any time in clear and understandable terms. In addition to verbal information, the patient will receive a written information letter (this letter is presented in the online supplemental material). If the patient is unable to give consent (eg, due to sedation), the investigator will seek consent from the next of kin. If no next of kin is available, the investigator will proceed with emergency inclusion, following French law. When consent is initially obtained from the next of kin or through emergency inclusion, the investigator will seek consent from the patient as soon as possible. As this clinical research study involves minimal risk, constraints and verbal consent will suffice, and the investigator will document it in the patient’s medical record. A log of patients considered for study participation will be maintained, including the reasons for non-inclusion (presented in the online supplemental material).

Confidentiality and source data archiving

Medical data for each patient will only be disclosed to authorised individuals under conditions ensuring patient confidentiality. All collected data will be anonymised. The study sponsor will archive all study data for a minimum of 15 years after study completion.

Patient and public involvement

No patient or public was involved in the design, conduct and reporting or dissemination plans of this research.

Trial status and provisional calendar

The first month of practice standardisation started on 7 October 2024, and inclusions started in November 2024. Data collection is ongoing and inclusions are expected to reach completion in March 2027.

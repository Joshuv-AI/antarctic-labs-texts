Clinical Images: Thrombocytopenia, anasarca, fever, reticulin fibrosis, and organomegaly syndrome presenting as bilateral adrenal hemorrhage
Authors: Masaki Itagane, Mitsuyo Kinjo
Published: 2026

The patient, a previously healthy 50‐year‐old woman, was admitted to the hospital after three days of left lower abdominal pain with nausea and vomiting following an upper respiratory infection. On hospital day 3, the abdominal pain became diffuse and excruciating, and respiratory failure in the setting of abrupt status epilepticus required endotracheal intubation. Laboratory tests revealed elevated levels of markers of inflammation. (A) Contrast‐enhanced computed tomography demonstrated bilateral adrenal hemorrhages (yellow triangles) and hepatosplenomegaly, and (B) brain magnetic resonance imaging showed bilateral occipital vasogenic edema (yellow triangle) consistent with posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome (PRES). On hospital day 8, she developed anasarca with hypoalbuminemia, acute kidney injury requiring dialysis, and thrombocytopenia. Serum interleukin‐6 and vascular endothelial growth factor levels were elevated to 43.2 pg/mL (reference range ≤ 5.8 pg/mL) and 845.6 pg/mL (reference range 143.1–658.8 pg/mL), respectively. There was (C) reticulin myelofibrosis on bone marrow examination and (D) thrombotic microangiopathy on kidney biopsy, supporting the diagnosis of thrombocytopenia, anasarca, fever, reticulin fibrosis, and organomegaly (TAFRO) syndrome. Treatment with pulsed glucocorticoids and tocilizumab led to rapid improvement in anasarca, renal function, and thrombocytopenia, and she was discharged on hospital day 36. TAFRO is a life‐threatening multiorgan failure syndrome due to cytokine storm, with considerable overlap with idiopathic multicentric Castleman disease. Bilateral adrenal hemorrhage can be a rare but early manifestation of TAFRO syndrome and should prompt diagnostic consideration in the setting of rapidly evolving systemic inflammation and vascular leak features such as pleural/ascitic fluid accumulation or PRES. 1 It is plausible that cytokine‐associated endothelial injury and increased vascular permeability under systemic inflammatory stress may increase intra‐adrenal pressure and predispose to venous congestion because of the adrenal gland's rich arterial inflow and single central venous outflow. 1 , 2 Recognizing adrenal hemorrhage as an early warning sign of TAFRO syndrome may facilitate timely immunosuppressive therapy and prevent progression to life‐threatening multiorgan failure.

High altitude impact on serum bicarbonate in healthy Mexican children: concerning the overdiagnosis of renal tubular acidosis
Authors: Francisco H. Negrete-Pedraza, Víctor Garcia-Nieto, Carlos A. Castro-Fuentes, Omar E. Valencia-Ledezma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAltitude influences bicarbonate levels, it is a variable that is hardly considered in diagnosing Renal Tubular Acidosis (RTA), so it should be a factor to consider when diagnosing this pathology, especially at 2250 mts over the sea level as it is the case of Mexico City. RTA is most often misdiagnosed. Regarding of this, the present study established reference limits for bicarbonate levels in healthy children without pathologies associated with alterations in the acid-base balance in Mexico City and it´s metropolitan area.MethodsA total of 267 healthy pediatric patients were included, within normal estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), and without any associated pathology of any alteration in the acid-base balance.ResultsCompared to older children, children younger than two years of age showed statistically higher levels of calcemia and cystatin C. On the other hand, this same group showed lower values of creatininemia, pCO2, and HCO3-. Percentile 50 of bicarbonate in children under two years of age were 19.9 mEq/L and 21.9 mEq/L in those over that age. A correlation was identified between HCO3- levels and pCO2 (r = 0.68; p < 0.001).ConclusionsIn the study population, an effect of altitude on blood levels of pCO2 and HCO3- was observed.

Introduction

Altitude is a variable that becomes relevant when performing acid-base analysis in patients with height deficit and RTA diagnosis. Type 1 RTA is a rare tubulopathy generated by functional defects of the H+-ATPase located on the luminal side of α-intercalated cells or from the Cl− - HCO3- anion exchanger (AE) located on the basolateral side [1, 2]. In healthy individuals, as the partial pressure of oxygen in inspired air (PIO2) decreases with altitude, the oxygen pressure in blood (PaO2) also decreases. When it falls below the ventilation threshold of 55 mmHg, chemoreceptors are stimulated to initiate CO2 clearance. Hyperventilation is a classic response to life at high altitude. Once acute respiratory alkalosis is established, it is compensated by increasing renal excretion of bicarbonate, resulting from decreased tubular secretion of hydrogen ions (H+). This occurs because alkalosis inhibits the formation and secretion of ammonium ions (NH4+). Renal compensation after several days returns pH to normal [3]. Then is right to assume that children bicarbonate plasma levels must be lower at high altitude than sea level.

In Mexico, the diagnosis of RTA is frequent, although the incidence is unknown, and it has been wrong related to the pathophysiology of allergic diseases, especially to sensitization to inhaled allergens [4]. An overdiagnosis of RTA has been reported, particularly in the metropolitan area of Mexico City [5]. Nevertheless, protocols for diagnosing the pathology do not consider the altitude of those cities above sea level.

Management of acid-base metabolic disorders can be performed correctly after a proper diagnosis. RTA occurs within the first weeks or months of life; the relationship with family history can facilitate diagnosis, and hereditary transmission of the disease or in specific population groups is relevant [6].

The correct clinical review and reliable measurement of the acid-base variables in the blood and the interpretation of the values allow correct diagnoses of this condition. Presently, the impact of altitude on acid-base balance outcomes in children residing in Mexico City and it´s Metropolitan area has not been reported, which ultimately could influence RTA diagnoses. Therefore, the objective of the present study was to establish the reference limits for bicarbonate (HCO3−) levels in healthy children without pathologies associated with acid-base balance alterations in Mexico City and it´s Metropolitan area.

Materials and methods

Data from 267 patients was collected from the Hospital Regional de Alta Especialidad de Ixtapaluca electronic file SALUDNESS to identify patients who meet the eligibility conditions of the study. Patients were divided in two groups: under 2 years and 2 to 18 years old. The patients in the study were referred to the pediatric surgery clinic for minimally invasive procedure and to the pediatric nephrology clinic of the Regional High-Specialty Hospital of Ixtapaluca, State of Mexico, Mexico from January 2016 to December 2022 mainly due to suspected diagnosis of RTA. Pediatric patients met the following selection criteria for the study:

Inclusion criteria

Pediatric patients were considered healthy when weight and height are within the normal percentile for age.

Adequate psychomotor development.

Normal estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR) to Age [7].

No pathology associated with alteration in the acid-base balance.

Exclusion criteria

Pediatric patients with nephrocalcinosis.

Pediatric patients with stones found on ultrasounds.

eGFR diminished for age or less than 90 ml/min/1.73 in those 2 years and older.

Patient with hypocitraturia.

Laboratory techniques

The analytical determination of creatinine was performed using the enzymatic creatininase method with a Roche/Hitachi cobas c 701/702 autoanalyzer, and cysC levels were assayed nephelometrically (Siemens Diagnostics). pH values, pCO2, and actual HCO3- levels were measured in venous blood samples obtained without compression, using a GEM Premier 3000 gas analyzer.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were performed initially to determine the variables distribution and estimate frequencies. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to examine the sample distribution. Quantitative variables following a normal distribution were expressed as mean and standard deviation. Student’s t-test was used to compare the differences between variables. Mean and percentiles were calculated in the two subgroups into which the sample was divided according to age. Finally, Pearson’s test was used to calculate the correlation between quantitative variables. A p < 0.05 value was considered statistically significant. These analyses were performed using the statistical software SPSS V. 19.0 (Inc., USA).

Results

Of the 267 pediatric patients, three cases were excluded because the renal glomerular function had not been performed (creatinine or cystatin C levels), twenty-one because they showed diminished eGFR according to age in the case of the under 2 years group [7] and less than 90 mL/min/1.73m2 in the 2 to 28 years old group. Finally, three were excluded due to hypocitraturia (citrate/creatinine ratio less than 250 mg/g) [8].

At the end 240 children were included in the study (120 males, 120 females) age of 6.62 ± 5.13 years old (range: 0.5-18.57). All patients in the under two years group had eGFR according to age and more than 90 mL/min/1.73 m2 in the 2 to 18 years old.

The reasons for referral to the hospital varied, but the most frequent was suspected diagnosis of RTA, which was not confirmed by our group in any case (n = 44). The other most frequent reasons for referral to the hospital were a history of having transiently elevated creatinine levels during an acute infectious process, usually of intestinal origin (n = 25) or having suffered from Henoch-Schönlein purpura without nephropathy (n = 17), colicky abdominal pain (n = 15), abnormalities of the auricular pavilions or cleft palate (n = 15), single functioning kidney (n = 14), nocturnal enuresis (n = 11), cryptorchidism or preputial stenosis with indication for circumcision (n = 11) and inguinal or umbilical hernias (n = 7). Children referred for apparent RTA did not have a height below the 3rd percentile for height and weight, nephrocalcinosis, or a history of frank polyuria. Polyuria defined as the excretion of more than 2 L/m²/day or more than 100 ml/kg/day in children up to age 2, or more than 40 ml/kg/day in older children [9]. When resorting to quantifying diuresis was not possible, we calculate the urine volume corresponding to 100 ml of glomerular filtrate (V/GFR). Polyuria is considered if it exceeds 2 ml/100 ml GFR. The formula for V/GFR is: V/GFR = (Pcr × 100) / Ucr, where Pcr is plasma creatinine, and Ucr is urinary creatinine [10].

For the under 2 years old eGFR was calculated from creatinine levels using the 2009 Schwartz formula: 0.413 x (height/Cr) [11]. For the 2 to 18 years olde GFR was calculated from cystatin C (cysC) levels using the CKiD under 25 formula: K x [1/cysC] (eGFR from IFCC-calibrated serum cystatin level). The value of K, which varies by age, is automatically included when entering data into the database https://ckid-gfrcalculator.shinyapps.io/eGFR/ [12]. As indicated in the 2 to 18 group eGFR values below 90 ml/min/1.73m2 were considered abnormal. Plasma creatinine levels were used to eGFR in children under two years of age [11]. The anion gap value (mEq/l) was calculated using the classic formula: Na+ - (Cl- + HCO3). Urine volume (ml/100 ml GFR) was calculated using the following formula: (plasma Cr x 100/urine Cr). Discussion

The present work studied the relationship between bicarbonate level and altitude in pediatric patients in Mexico City. Our study population was healthy from the metabolic standpoint without any associated diseases that compromise acid-base equilibrium.

Many works have demonstrated the reliability of venous blood gas analysis when evaluating disorders in acid-base balance. The correlation between arterial and venous gas values is adequate [15–19]. Therefore, we used venous blood gas analysis to assess the acid-base balance in children referred to the hospital. To properly select children, any patient with eGFR diminished for age or less than 90 ml/min/1.73 m [2] in those older than 2 years were excluded. Many of them had only slightly decreased eGFR. To ensure the highest possible reliability for the study, three children diagnosed with hypocitraturia were excluded, as it is a marker of intracellular acidosis [20].

Infants show physiologically reduction in HCO3− levels as they have a decrease threshold reabsorption rate of HCO3− in the proximal tubule compared to older children [21]. It is from the age of two when bicarbonate levels remain stable above 22 mEq/L [14, 22]. Therefore, we divided our population into two subgroups based on age. As expected, children under two years of age showed significantly lower HCO3− levels than older children. The mean levels and the 50th percentile of HCO3− levels in both subgroups were one or two points below those described in other pediatric series [14, 22, 23]. Table 2 shows the bicarbonate percentiles for each age group. Although the age ranges of each study are different, for example, in Cassels and Morse’s work, the mean value of HCO3− levels for children aged 3.5 to 5.4 years is 22.5 mEq/L22, and in our subgroup of children older than two years, it is 21.5 mEq/L. It can be observed that the 5th percentile of HCO3− levels was located at 14–15 mEq/L, indicating that the effect of altitude and physiological adaptive mechanisms to altitude. Ramirez et al. found that for every 1000 m of altitude above sea level, the concentration of HCO3 - decreases between 0.55 and 1.5 mEq/L for people with acute exposure to altitude and people acclimated to it, respectively. So apparently low HCO3− values, may be normal at high altitude. In our study, both subgroups showed mean value of pCO2 lower than usual (40 mmHg) [24]. The mean value of pCO2 in older children was almost 10% lower, and in those under two years, it was even lower with statistically significant differences between both subgroups, reflected in HCO3− levels. Likewise, a direct correlation between HCO3− and age was confirmed. All these data, consistent with previous publications [21, 22], support the suitability of the studied sample in both subgroups; the pH value was stable, supporting the assertion that altitude should be considered when evaluating blood gases and acid-base balance in the pediatric population.

In summary, our population shows the effect of altitude on blood pCO2 and HCO3− levels as described before by Rangel in 1975 [24]. The same effect has been demonstrated several times in studies in sport medicine, alpinist and general population living above 1500 mts over sea level where ventilatory and renal adaptation takes place in order to maintain acid-baser equilibrium [25–28].

Conclusions

It is surprising that many patients referred to as RTA didn’t have RTA but high-altitude related low bicarbonate levels. In certain cases of daily practice, values of acid-base metabolism determined in healthy children could be considered abnormal. More studies should be conducted, with a larger number of patients, especially children in healthy child consultation follow-up in outpatient clinics. As shown by the results obtained in our study, for a real assessment of acid-base balance, all factors surrounding the particular situation of a given population must be taken into account. Our work aims to follow the path previously opened by other studies designed to establish normal values for acid-base balance parameters in the Mexican population [24, 27–30].

To this end, we propose the use of the percentiles for bicarbonate levels in each of the studied subgroups when assessing acid-base balance in Mexico City pediatric population Fig. 2.

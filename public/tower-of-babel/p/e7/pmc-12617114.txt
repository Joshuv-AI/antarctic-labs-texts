OA20108. A cross-sectional study of the factors that affect adolescent smoking in Crete, Greece: The FRESHAIR
Authors: A Aravantinou-Karlatou, T Krasanakis, I Bouloukaki, A Christodoulakis, C Hoffman, S Williams, R MJJ van der Kleij, I Tsiligianni
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEffective prevention and intervention strategies for adolescent smoking require a thorough understanding of its underlying factors. This study aimed to investigate how individual, community, and environmental factors relate to adolescent smoking.MethodsA cross-sectional study was conducted among 179 adolescents aged 14-21 in Crete, Greece, as part of Phase A of the FRESHAIR4Life program. From January to February 2024, we recruited participants from seven high schools (n = 7), two technical schools (n = 2), and one university (n = 1) lower-to-middle socioeconomic groups.ResultsThe 1/3 (34%) of the participants reported having smoking history. The three most common reasons for smoking were reported to be addiction (60%), stress (43%) and to be cool and attractive (27%). Older adolescents had higher odds of ever smoking than younger adolescents (OR = 1.57, 95%CI:1.32-1.88, p < 0.001). Exposure to secondhand smoke at school significantly increased the likelihood of smoking (OR = 2.81, 95% CI: 1.36-5.81), smoking of one or both parents (OR = 7.64, 95%CI:1.95–29.96, p = 0.004), one or more closest friends (OR = 12.88, 95%CI:3.24–51.15, p < 0.001), one or more siblings (OR = 25.13, 95%CI:3.23–195.63, p = 0.002). Regarding smoking as a health risk (OR = 2.6, 95%CI:1.2–5.3, p = 0.012) and concerns about its impact on health (OR = 5.7, 95%CI:2.3–14.1, p < 0.001) were associated with never-smoking behaviors.ConclusionsOur study highlighted several individual, community, and environmental factors associated with adolescent tobacco use. The findings suggest that social/peer support interventions could be a key component of anti-tobacco programs aimed at Greek adolescents.Key messages• Factors positively associated with smoking initiation included age, exposure to secondhand smoke at school, parents smoking, and peer influence.• Personal attributes, such as stress levels, addiction, and a desire for coolness or attractiveness, have all been linked to increased smoking.• Teachers, scientists, and family could provide more targeted educational interventions and guidance to help prevent adolescents from beginning to smoke.TopicAdolescents, smoking, FRESHAIR4Life.

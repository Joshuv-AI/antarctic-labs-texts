Identification and characterization of 13 gene families encoding enzymes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis in barley and their roles under abiotic stress
Authors: MdSohel Mia, Xinghuai Jing, Tanveer A Wani, Seema Zargar, MdMahmudul Hasan, Tao Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFlavonoids are polyphenolic secondary metabolites, which potentially involved in growth, development, reproduction, and stress tolerance in plants. Barley is a monocot crop that contains antioxidant flavonoids. Hence, we have identified and characterized 108 flavonoid biosynthesis enzymes in barley. Distribution of all 108 enzymes into five major superfamilies, following 13 subfamilies during phylogenetic analysis may reveal their evolutionary origin. The existence of light, hormone, and stress-responsive components in the enzymes potentially linked to their vital role in hormonal balance and stress tolerance. The highest binding affinity (−8.3 kcal/mol) in HvANS1–flavone, HvANS1–baicalein, and HvCHI3–glabranine docked complexes indicate a strong enzyme–substrate interaction, which support their potential functional relevance in flavonoid biosynthesis. Molecular dynamics simulation supported docking findings, revealing stable and functional complexes. Significant upregulation of HvCHS1 in the root and downregulation of HvF3’H20 in the same tissue under high temperature stress, along with downregulation of HvCHI2 in the root under salinity and HvDFR13 in the stem under salinity and drought stress, might represent their differential responses to specific abiotic stress. Plasma membrane and nuclear-localized HvCHS1 protein may regulate transport and homeostasis of cytosolic flavonoids. The precise characterization of flavonoid biosynthesis enzymes may guide plant biologists in developing barley with improved biosynthesis, transport, homeostasis of flavonoids, and abiotic stress tolerance.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-40768-0.Keywords: Flavonoids, Barley, Molecular docking, MD simulation, Abiotic stresses, qRT-PCR, Subcellular localizationSubject terms: Biotechnology, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Structural biology

Introduction

Flavonoids are the secondary metabolites in plants that play a central role in abiotic stress tolerance. Enzymes encoding flavonoid biosynthesis in plants showed a significant variation in structural and functional properties1,2. The mentioned complex flavonoid biosynthesis and accumulation are governed by multiple enzymes in well-established pathways3,4. Enzymes involved in plant flavonoid biosynthesis belong to 13 families. Those are the chalcone synthetase (CHS), chalcone isomerase (CHI), flavonoid 3-hydroxylase (F3H), flavonoid 3’-hydroxylase (F3’H), flavonoid 3’5'-hydroxylase (F3’5'H), flavonol synthase (FLS), dihydroflavonol 4-reductase (DFR), flavone synthase (FNSII), flavanone 2-hydroxylase (F2H), anthocyanidin synthase (ANS), anthocyanidin reductase (ANR), leucoanthocyanidin reductase (LAR), leucoanthocyanidin dioxygenase synthase (LDOX) families (Fig. S1)5.

The genome sequencing of major crops like barley, rice6, Ginkgo biloba7, peanut8, Lagerstroemia indica9, safflower10, and Glycine max11 facilitated extensive genomics research to evaluate the structural and functional properties of the enzymes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis. Although well characterized in different plant genomes, enzymes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis in barley have not been identified and structurally characterized yet. The CHS gene family has been identified and characterized in moth orchid12, eggplant13, and begonia14, whereas the CHI has been identified and characterized in mulberry15 and dragon7. Considerable variation of the enzymes was observed in number and functional properties among different plant genomes. Hence, an in-depth critical structural analysis of enzymes involved in the flavonoid biosynthesis pathway and their regulatory roles in abiotic stress tolerance could guide to explore the mechanisms by which these enzymes play a potential role in total flavonoid biosynthesis in barley. More specifically, in-depth evolutionary relationships, structural characteristics, regulatory elements, and expression dynamics under abiotic stress of enzyems involved in flavonoid biosynthesis in barley have not been published yet. This lack of integrated research findings represent a critical gap in understanding structural basis of enzymes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis and their response during abiotic stress in barley.

Enzymes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis play a significant role in crop productivity, quality, and resistance to biotic and abiotic stresses8,16–18. In Chrysanthemum nankingense, CnCHS4 and CnCHS8 were highly expressed in the root, and CnCHS10 was upregulated in different vegetative tissues19. In grape, VvCHS1 and VvCHS3 were upregulated in the leaves during tissue-specific expression, and VvCHS2 and VvCHS6 showed upregulation in the roots, stems, and leaves under NaCl, 4°C, and PEG (polyethylene glycol) treatments20. In strawberry, FvF3H42 and FvF3H16 showed considerably higher expression in the seed21. In tea plants, CsF3Ha and CsF3Hb were highly upregulated in the tender leaves22. Carthamus tinctorius L. gene CtF3’H was upregulated in different flowering tissues. In maize, ZmFNSI-1 showed significantly higher expression in silks and pericarp tissues23. The F3′5′H gene was highly expressed in Pohlia nutans under different abiotic stresses24. Brassica vegetables genes BrFLS3.2, BrFLS3.3, BrFLS2, and BrFLS1 showed higher expression under various abiotic stresses25. BnDFR6 and BnDFR26 genes were significantly upregulated in rape petals and were greatly involved in the synthesis of anthocyanins in rape petals26. In rice, OsCHS12/28, OsF3H2, OsLDOX2, and OsDFR2 genes are upregulated under salt and cold stress, which potentially linked to their potential role in tolerating the stresses6. In Ginkgo biloba, GbFLS5, GbFLS6, and GbCHS9 were significantly upregulated under drought stress, may suggest their important role in drought stress tolerance7. In maize and Ginkgo biloba, ZmCHS1/2, GbCHS4/7/8/11, GbANS5, GbF35H2 were significantly expressed in different organs/tissues suggested their significant effect on those organs7,27. OsANS1, OsANS2, OsFLS1, OsF3H4, OsF30H1, OsCHI4, OsFLS2, OsF30H2, OsANR3, OsDFR3, and OsLDOX9 flavonoid biosynthesis enzymes were highly expressed in different tissues/organs, revealing their important role in those organs6. In Glycine max, significant upregulation of UGT72X4, UGT72Z3, UGT73C20, UGT88A13, UGT88E19, and UGT92G4 consequenced higher flavonol, isoflavone, flavone, and flavanol aglycones synthesis with variation in kinetic properties11.

Barley (Hordeum vulgare L.) ranks as the fourth major cereal crop worldwide in terms of yield and production and was domesticated approximately 10,000 years ago28. It is widely utilized for food, feed, malting, and brewing purposes29 and is increasingly recognized as a functional food due to its notable content of flavonoids and antioxidants. Interestingly, barley exhibits adaptive responses to a range of abiotic stresses, including drought, salinity, cold, and high temperature, which contribute to its environmental adaptability30–32. Nevertheless, these abiotic stresses impose significant constraints on barley growth, yield, and grain quality33. Under severe conditions, drought stress alone can reduce barley yield by up to 50%34. Indeed, abiotic stress exposure is also associated with the increased flavonoid accumulation in barley, suggesting a potential role of flavonoid metabolism in stress adaptation35. Despite these observations, a comprehensive genome-wide and integrative characterization of flavonoid biosynthesis–related gene families, together with their regulatory and expression responses under abiotic stress conditions in barley, remains lacking. Structural characterization combined with expression analysis under stress conditions may provide valuable insights into the mechanisms by which these enzymes contribute to flavonoid biosynthesis and stress tolerance.

Therefore, the present study was undertaken to comprehensively identify and characterize 13 gene families involved in flavonoid biosynthesis in barley, integrating phylogenetic relationships, exon-intron, conserved motif, chromosomal localization, synteny, protein structure, cis-elements, gene ontology, KEGG pathway, protein-protein interaction, interaction with transcription factor, and molecular docking analyses. Tissue-specific expression patterns, expression responses under abiotic stresses (high temperature, salinity, and drought), and subcellular localization analyses were further performed to elucidate the potential roles of these enzymes in flavonoid biosynthesis and abiotic stress tolerance in barley. This integrative analysis provides novel insights into flavonoid-mediated stress response mechanisms and offers valuable candidate genes for future functional studies and barley improvement programs.

Methods and materials

Identification, evaluation of physicochemical characteristics, and subcellular localization

The GFF3 file, CDS sequences, and barley proteins were obtained from the Phytozome database (https://phytozome-next.jgi.doe.gov/). Protein sequences of 13 flavonoid biosynthesis–related enzyme families from rice and Arabidopsis thaliana were used as query sequences for BLASTP searches against the barley protein dataset using TBtools36. A relatively permissive e-value threshold of 1e−5 and a minimum sequence identity of 50% were applied to ensure the inclusion of divergent homologs from multigene families6,37. To improve accuracy, all BLASTP hits were subsequently subjected to stringent validation using conserved-domain analysis. Candidate proteins were screened using the PFAM (http://pfam.xfam.org/), SMART (http://smart.embl-heidelberg.de/), CDD (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/cdd/), and InterProScan (http://www.ebi.ac.uk/interpro/scan.html; accessed on 10 October 2025) databases to confirm the presence of characteristic domains specific to flavonoid biosynthesis enzymes6. Redundant sequences, partial proteins, and alternative splice variants were removed, and only full-length, non-redundant representative proteins were retained for further analyses.

The ProtParam (http://web.expasy.org/protparam/) and ProtComp (http://linux1.softberry.com/) servers were used to analyze the physicochemical parameters and the subcellular localization of flavonoid biosynthesis proteins, respectively37. Although Insilco subcellular localization guides the future research on putative function of the protein, the wet lab validation might provide higher precision of localization based function.

Phylogenetic tree construction

The phylogenetic analysis was conducted using the protein sequences of Hordeum vulgare and Oryza sativa genomes. The ClustalX (version 2.1) program was utilized to align the protein sequences of barley and rice38. The aligned sequences were imported into MEGA 11, and a neighbour-joining (NJ) phylogenetic tree was constructed using pairwise deletion of gaps with 1000 bootstrap replications39. NJ is a distance-based method, it may not fully capture complex evolutionary models and was therefore applied to infer broad evolutionary relationships. Bootstrap values were mapped onto internal nodes to evaluate the reliability of major groupings, and clades discussed in the manuscript correspond to nodes with strong bootstrap support (≥70%). Proteins were classified into major phylogenetic clades based on concordance between tree topology, conserved-domain architecture, and established enzyme superfamily classification: chalcone synthase (CHS), chalcone isomerase (CHI), cytochrome P450-dependent oxygenases (CYP450), 2-oxoglutarate-dependent dioxygenases (2OGD), and short-chain dehydrogenases/reductases (SDR). The constructed tree was colored using the iTOL web tool (https://itol.embl.de/; accessed on 15 October 2025)40.

Analysis of gene structures and prediction of conserved motifs

GFF annotation files from the barley genome were loaded into the TBtools to obtain the gene architecture of the flavonoid biosynthesis pathway-related enzymes41. Based on optimal E-values, the motif analysis tool of the MEME Suite server was utilized to identify the conserved motif of flavonoid enzymes42. In the MEME Suite server (https://meme-suite.org/meme/; accessed on 16 October 2025), the maximum motif was fixed at 20, having a length between 6 and 50 amino residues40.

Chromosomal (Chr) localization and synteny analysis

The PhenoGram web server was used to determine the chromosomal localization of every enzyme involved in flavonoid biosynthesis in barley (http://visualization.ritchielab.org/phenograms/plot; accessed on 18 October 2025)43. A PhenoGram-based color-coded ideogram was used to visualize the specific location of individual genes in a particular chromosome44. Genome sequences of Hordeum vulgare, Brachypodium distachyon, Oryza sativa, and Sorghum bicolor were downloaded from the phytozome server to analyze the syntenic relationships of enzymes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis in barley with the same in the above-mentioned three genomes. In TBtools, MCScanX was utilized to predict orthologous pairs of the mentioned enzymes between barley and the above-mentioned genomes, where the graphics view was utilized to visualize the output45.

Prediction of three-dimensional (3D) modeling and cis-regulatory elements

The three-dimensional protein structure was created using the Phyre2 web server (http://www.sbg.bio.ic.ac.uk/phyre2; accessed on 20 October 2025) to clarify how structural changes impact the protein functions of the enzymes46. The TBtools software was used to extract a 2000-bp promoter region upstream from the transcription start site of the enzymes. The PlantCare server (http://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/plantcare/html/; accessed on 20 October 2025) was used to anticipate the cis-acting components in the promoters of each enzyme. PlantCare-based predicted cis-acting elements were visualized using TBtools40. This analysis was performed to identify putative regulatory features and interpreted as predictive indicators of transcriptional regulation.

Gene ontology (GO) annotation and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis

Utilizing the ShinyGO v0.77 databases (http://bioinformatics.sdstate.edu/go/; accessed on 22 October 2025), the molecular and biological functions of enzymes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis in barley were predicted. The barley genome was used as the background gene set, and statistical significance was determined using false discovery rate (FDR) correction with a threshold of FDR ≤ 0.0547. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was also performed using ShinyGO, with pathway significance assessed using FDR correction (FDR ≤ 0.05)48,49. Enrichment results were interpreted to identify overrepresented metabolic pathways associated with flavonoid biosynthesis.

Protein-protein interaction (PPI), hub-gene, and plant transcription factors (TFs) regulatory network analysis

The protein-protein interaction network of flavonoid biosynthesis–related enzymes were predicted using the STRING 11.0 database (https://string-db.org/cgi/input.pl; accessed on 22 October 2025) by selecting the full STRING network option50. A high-confidence interaction score threshold of 0.700 was applied to reduce low-confidence interactions51. The cytoscape tool identified the top five hub genes responsible for flavonoid biosynthesis in barley52. The 1,000-bp upstream nucleotide sequences for each enzyme were submitted to the Plant Transcriptional Regulatory Map (PTRM) server (http://plantregmap.gao-nab.org/binding_site_prediction.php; accessed on 25 October 2025), setting a p value of ≤ 1e-6 to predict the interaction with transcription factors. The Cytoscape program was utilized to build and visualize the top 5 hub-gene, PPI, and transcription factor regulatory network40.

Molecular Docking of top five barley flavonoids protein and nine flavonoids ligands

The docking studies were aimed to elucidate the structure-function relationships of flavonoid pathway enzymes. Using ligands from simple flavones (flavone) to complex glycosides (hyperoside) allows researchers to probe substrate specificity, catalytic mechanisms, and region selectivity of enzymes like synthases, hydroxylases, and transferases. This reveals how subtle structural differences dictate product diversity. Furthermore, docking with non-native ligands (e.g., EGCG) tests enzyme promiscuity, providing a rationale for engineering novel catalysts to produce high-value flavonoids. Therefore, the 3D structures of the flavone (C15H10O2; PubChem CID: 10680), baicalein (C15H10O5; PubChem CID: 5281605), glabranine (C20H20O4; PubChem CID: 124049), quercetin (C15H10O7; PubChem CID: 5280343), fisetin (C15H10O6; PubChem CID: 5281614), ladanein (C17H14O6; PubChem CID: 3084066), baicalin (C21H18O11; PubChem CID: 64982), hyperoside (C21H20O12; PubChem CID: 5281643), and EGCG (C22H18O11; PubChem CID: 65064) ligands were retrieved from the PubChem database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/; accessed on 30 October 2025)53. The, the top 5 hub proteins involved in flavonoid biosynthesis were docked with the above nine ligands. We conducted blind docking to explore potential binding interactions between enzymes and flavonoid ligands without predefined active sites. Consequently, active sites, grid parameters, and protocol validation was not specified, ensuring an unbiased assessment of ligand-enzyme interactions54. For the preparation of receptor proteins and ligands, as well as docking analysis, Autodock 4.255 and Autodock Vina56 software were used. Subsequent interaction analysis between barley flavonoid proteins and ligands was performed using PyMOL software53. The interacted complexes obtained from the PyMOL software were visualized by the Discovery Studio visualizer to generate the images of 3D and 2D interactions57. Considering the binding score, the best nine docked complexes are visualized.

Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation

To assess the stability of the protein-ligand interaction in the chosen candidate compounds, a 100 ns molecular dynamics (MD) simulation was carried out for the complex structures using the "Desmond v6.3 Program" in Schrodinger 2020-3 under the Linux framework58,59. The ligand complex and flavonoid enzymes were simulated using the three-site transferable intermolecular potential (TIP3P) water model60. To neutralize the entire system with a salt concentration of 0.15 M, Na+ and Cl- were introduced to an orthorhombic box shape that was 10 Å from the center. A force field of OPLS3e was applied61. Using a natural time and pressure (NPT) ensemble at a constant pressure of 101,325 Pa and a temperature of 300 K, MD simulation of the protein-ligand complex system was further minimized58,62. RMSD (root mean square deviation), RMSF (root mean square fluctuation), Rg (radius of gyration), and SASA (solvent accessible surface area) measurements were examined in order to assess the complexes stability and dynamic properties.

Expression pattern of flavonoid genes at different growth stages and tissues

The expression profiles of 108 genes were visualized using TBtools software, utilizing the gene expression data in 15 various tissues and growth stages obtained from the IPK Barley BLAST Server (https://webblast.ipkgatersleben.de/barley_ibsc/; accessed on 5 November 2025)63. The expression was evaluated in embryos, etiolated seedlings, seedling roots, seedling shoots, epidermal strips, developing inflorescences, inflorescence rachis, roots, third internode of the tillers, lodicule, lemma, palea, developing grains (5 days after pollination), developing grains (15 days after pollination), and senescing leaves. Different colors were used to indicate different expression levels once the level had been normalized in TBtools64.

RNA extraction and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

To explore the tissue-specific expression patterns of the barley flavonoid biosynthesis genes, barley was cultivated in pots filled with a soil and vermiculite mixture (1:1) under growth conditions of 25±1 °C during the day and 20±1 °C at night, with a relative humidity of 75% (Fig. S2). For tissue-specific expression, stem, root, and leaf tissues were harvested at 21 days after germination and immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen. Following RNA extraction, the qRT-PCR experiment was performed to evaluate the tissue-specific expression of HvCHS1, HvCHI2, HvF3H1, HvF3’H20, and HvDFR13 genes in stem, root, and leaf tissues. High temperature, salt, and drought stress was imposed to 21 d old barley seedlings for 24 h. For imposing high temperature stress, seedlings were allowed to be at 40 °C for 24 h. Similarly, salinity stress was imposed, allowing 50 mL NaCl (150 mmol·L−1) to each independent seedling tray to completely submerge the root system for 24 h, and drought was maintained by adding 50 mL of 30% PEG 6000 solution to each independent seedling pot to completely submerge the root system and allowed for 24 h before harvesting65 (Fig. S2). The harvesting procedure was followed as above. Each treatment consisted of three independent biological replicates, and qRT-PCR reactions were performed with three technical replicates per biological sample. Relative mRNA expression levels were calculated using the 2⁻ΔΔCT method66. Expression data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) of biological replicates. Statistical significance was evaluated using Student’s t-test, with a threshold of p < 0.05. PEG- and NaCl-based treatments were applied as standardized stress assays and are interpreted as combined osmotic stress conditions rather than isolated stress effects.

Here, the total RNA was extracted from different barley tissues utilizing an E.Z.N.A. Plant RNA Kit (Omega Bio-tek, Inc., USA), following the guidelines of the manufacturer. The isolated RNA was subsequently reverse transcribed into first-strand cDNA using the HiScript II Q RT SuperMix of qPCR Kit (Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd, China), maintaining a reaction volume of 20 μL. The specific primers for quantitative PCR were synthesized utilizing Primer Premier (version 5.0; Premier, Canada), employing HvActin as the reference gene. The primer sequences are mentioned in Supplementary Table S1. Quantitative real-time PCR was conducted with the ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix Kit (Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd., China) and a fluorescence quantification kit on a CFX96 Real-Time System (Bio-Rad, USA).

Subcellular localization validation

The recombinant construct of pCAMBIA1300-HvCHS1-GFP was generated by inserting HvCHS1 CDS without stop codons at the upstream of GFP protein in pCAMBIA1300-GFP vector via Sma I sites. Marker vector pCAMBIA1300-35S-Histone-mCherry-NOS was used for nuclear localization. The above-mentioned recombinant vector, along with a negative control of empty vector (pCAMBIA1300::GFP), was transformed into Agrobacterium strain GV3101 and transiently transformed the resuspension solution into tobacco leaves. A laser confocal scanning microscope (Olympus, Japan) was used to observe the distribution of GFP fluorescence67. The primer sequences are mentioned in Supplementary Table S1. Experimental subcellular localization analysis was conducted for HvCHS1 as a representative enzyme, whereas the localization of other flavonoid biosynthesis–related proteins was inferred based on computational predictions.

Results

Identification, evaluation of physicochemical characteristics, and subcellular localization

A total of 108 flavonoid biosynthesis–related enzymes were identified in barley through an integrated BLASTP and Hidden Markov Model (HMM)–based pipeline43. BLASTP analysis was used for initial candidate identification, followed by HMM-based conserved-domain validation to confirm family-specific signatures, ensuring the reliability of the identified proteins. The final identified enzymes comprised 22 CHS, 21 DFR, 20 F3′H, 13 LDOX, 12 F3H, six CHI, six ANR, three FLS, and one each of ANS, FNS, F2H, F3′5′H, and LAR (Table 1). The identified enzymes displayed substantial diversity in amino acid length and molecular weight, ranging from 83 amino acids (HvF2H1) to 542 amino acids (HvF3′H1) and 10.61 kDa (HvCHI4) to 90.10 kDa (HvF2H1), respectively. Short protein sequences were retained when they contained conserved-domain features consistent with their annotated enzymes in the family, which may represent compact or divergent family members rather than annotation artifacts. The predicted isoelectric point (pI) values ranged from 4.54 (HvDFR21) to 10.73 (HvF3′5′H1), while GRAVY scores ranged from −0.784 (HvLDOX10) to 0.300 (HvCHI3), indicating variability in physicochemical properties across enzyme families (Table 1). Subcellular localization prediction suggested that most proteins were cytoplasmic. Only 16 enzymes are predicted to be localized in the plasma membrane, endoplasmic reticulum, or chloroplast, reflecting potential functional compartmentalization of flavonoid biosynthesis in barley (Table 1).

Classification and phylogenetic relationship of barley flavonoid proteins

Phylogenetic analysis grouped barley and rice flavonoid biosynthesis–related enzymes into five major clades corresponding to distinct enzyme superfamilies. An NJ phylogenetic tree was constructed using protein sequences of 108 barley and 85 rice enzymes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis (Fig. 1). In the obtained phylogenetic tree, enzymes from both species clustered into five well-defined major clades, CHS, CHI, CYP450, 2OGD, and SDR. Among the 193 enzymes analyzed, the 2OGD clade contained 51 proteins, following CHS (50), SDR (43), CYP450 (36), and CHI clades (13) (Fig. 1). Phylogenetic classification was supported by robust clustering with annotated rice homologs and concordance with conserved-domain architecture and enzyme superfamily classification. The separation of flavonoid biosynthesis enzymes into these five major clades may reflect the recruitment of distinct enzyme superfamilies into the flavonoid pathway during plant genome evolution.

Analysis of gene structures and prediction of conserved motifs

To evaluate gene architecture, alternative splicing potential, and their evolutionary implications, the exon–intron organization and conserved motifs of barley flavonoid enzymes were analyzed. The number of introns varied widely among gene families, ranging from zero to 11 in HvCHI1, reflecting substantial structural divergence. Notably, 21 enzymes were intronless, a pattern frequently associated with rapid gene evolution and functional streamlining. Genes within the same phylogenetic clades generally exhibited similar exon–intron arrangements, indicating evolutionary conservation, whereas clade-specific intron gain or loss suggests group-driven functional diversification (Fig. 2A).

Motif analysis identified twenty conserved motifs (motifs 1–20), whose distribution strongly corresponded with phylogenetic classification and enzyme function (Fig. 2B). Most CHS members across different clades consistently contained motifs 1, 8, 9, and 13, highlighting conserved catalytic roles. In contrast, CHI members lacked conserved motifs except HvCHI3, suggesting functional divergence or simplified enzymatic architecture within this lineage. Motif-10 predominated in 2OGD members, indicating a shared functional domain across this family. CYP450 group members were characterized by frequent occurrence of motifs 9, 14, 16, and 20, consistent with their conserved monooxygenase activity. Similarly, SDR members showed enrichment of motifs 3, 5, 7, 11, and 18, reflecting functional specialization within this group. Overall, the conserved exon–intron structures and motif compositions within phylogenetic clades underscore evolutionary conservation, whereas clade-specific variations indicate functional diversification among barley flavonoid biosynthetic enzymes.

The identified 108 barley flavonoid enzymes were distributed in seven known and one unknown (Un) chromosomes (Chr) (Fig. 3A). Among these, Chr7 is characterized with highest enzymes (30; 27.78%), following Chr2 (24; 22.22%), Chr5 (21; 19.44%), Chr1 (12; 11.11%), Chr3 (8; 7.41%), Chr6 (7; 6.48%), Chr4 (3; 2.78%), and ChrUn (3; 2.78%) (Fig. 3A). These findings suggest non-random chromosomal organization and local gene expansion.

Barley flavonoid biosynthesis enzymes are involved in a variety of biological processes and molecular functions, as observed in GO analysis. Considering the molecular aspect, the enzymes are involved in regulating the activity of chalcone synthase (Fig. 5B; Supplementary Table S4). These findings suggest that barley flavonoids biosynthesis enzymes are potentially involved in the flavonoids biosynthesis pathway.

Protein-protein interaction (PPI), hub-gene, and plant transcription factors (TFs) regulatory network analysis

The PPI network of all 108 enzymes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis was utilized to evaluate how one enzyme regulates expression, enzymatic reactions, signal transduction, cellular metabolism, and many other biological processes governed by different enzymes in barley. In the interaction network, 52 proteins showed interactions, maintaining 102 nodes and 116 edges, indicating strong functional coordination that supports pathway stability, metabolic flux control, and adaptive regulation in flavonoid biosynthesis (Fig. 6A; Supplementary Table S5). Among all the interacted proteins, the HvCHI2 protein showed the highest interactions (31) in the PPI network, indicating its potential role as a central regulatory hub coordinating multiple steps of flavonoid biosynthesis and facilitating efficient metabolic channeling. Following that, the top five hub proteins were selected, which are HvCHI2, 3, HvF3H3, HvANS1, and HvDFR20, highlighting their critical roles in maintaining pathway integrity, regulating metabolic balance, and influencing flavonoid accumulation and overall metabolic homeostasis in barley (Fig. 6B; Supplementary Table S5).

Molecular docking of the top five enzymes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis in barley with nine flavonoid ligands

Molecular docking was performed between five key flavonoid biosynthetic enzymes in barley (HvCHI2, HvCHI3, HvF3H3, HvANS1, and HvDFR20) and nine selected flavonoid compounds (flavone, baicalein, glabranine, quercetin, fisetin, ladanein, baicalin, hyperoside, and EGCG) to evaluate their potential binding affinities and interaction patterns (Table 3). During docking analysis, the mean binding energy (ΔG kcal/mol) of each ligand, calculated across all docked protein targets, was − 7.54 for glabranine, − 7.40 for baicalin, − 7.28 for baicalein, − 7.22 for flavone, − 7.10 for quercetin, − 7.10 for ladanein, − 7.06 for fisetin, − 6.94 for EGCG, and − 6.90 for hyperoside showed a strong binding affinity (Table 3).

Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation

Based on docking results, four protein–ligand complexes (HvANS1–flavone, HvANS1–baicalein, HvCHI3–glabranine, and HvANS1–quercetin) showing the most favorable binding energies were selected for 100 ns molecular dynamics simulations to evaluate their dynamic stability.

Root mean square deviation (RMSD)

RMSD analysis revealed that all four complexes reached a stable conformational state during the simulation period (Fig. 8A; Supplementary Table S6). HvANS1–flavone, HvANS1–baicalein, and HvANS1–quercetin complexes exhibited comparatively lower RMSD fluctuations (average 2.5–2.9 Å), indicating stable protein–ligand conformations. In contrast, HvCHI3–glabranine showed relatively higher RMSD values (average 4.3 Å), suggesting increased structural flexibility during the simulation.

The molecular dynamics (MD) simulation of barley flavonoid biosynthesis enzymes by using the Schrodinger tool. In MD simulations analysis, RMSD (root mean square deviation) (A), RMSF (root mean square fluctuation) (B), Rg (radius gyration) (C), and SASA (solvent accessible surface area) (D). Here, HvANS1-Flavone, HvANS1-Baicalein, HvCHI3-Glabranine, and HvANS1-Quercetin were represented by blue, orange, green, and blue colors, respectively.

Root mean square fluctuations (RMSF)

RMSF analysis showed moderate residue flexibility across all complexes, with average RMSF values ranging from 1.1 to 1.4 Å (Fig. 8B; Supplementary Table S7). Higher fluctuations were mainly confined to terminal and loop regions, indicating no major destabilization of the protein cores upon ligand binding.

Radius of gyration (Rg)

Rg values remained relatively stable throughout the simulation, with average values ranging from 3.2 to 3.8 Å across complexes (Fig. 8C; Supplementary Table S8), reflecting maintenance of overall structural compactness.

Solvent accessible surface area (SASA)

SASA analysis showed no abrupt changes during the simulation, with average values ranging from 143 to 317 Å2 among the complexes (Fig. 8D; Supplementary Table S8), suggesting ligand-induced surface adjustments without loss of overall structural integrity.

Expression pattern of enzymes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis at different growth stages and tissues in barley

To explore how different growth stages and tissues regulate the expression patterns of the enzymes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis in barley, an RNA-seq experiment was performed. Among 108 enzymes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis in barley, HvDFR5 was highly expressed in the embryo (EMB), whereas HvF3H1, HvDFR2, and HvANR5 were highly expressed in the root. In addition, HvCHS1, 8, 9, HvCHI2, and HvDFR13 were highly expressed in shoots, whereas HvCHS1, 9, HvCHI2, 3, HvF3’H13, 20, and HvANR1 were highly expressed in internodes (NOD). Interestingly, HvCHS1, HvCHI2, HvF3H3, HvDFR20, and HvANR2 were highly expressed in developing grains (CAR). Not surprisingly, HvCHS1, 14, HvCHI4, HvDFR9, and HvDFR13 enzymes were highly expressed in etiolated seedlings (ETI), whereas the HvCHS1, 5, 9, HvCHI2, and HvANR2 were highly expressed in lemma (LEM) of the spike. Accordingly, HvCHS1 and HvCHI1 were highly expressed in the plea (PAL) of the spike, whereas the HvCHS1, 4, HvCHI2, and HvCHI4 were highly upregulated in epidermal strips (EPI). HvDFR13 and HvDFR14 were highly upregulated in sensing leaves (SEN), whereas HvCHS1 was upregulated in inflorescence rachis (RAC) (Fig. 9; Supplementary Table S9). Interestingly, HvCHS1 showed higher upregulation in almost all analyzed barley tissues.

The quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) analysis

The qRT-PCR analysis was performed to examine the expression patterns of five representative genes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis in barley (HvCHS1, HvCHI2, HvF3H1, HvF3′H20, and HvDFR13). Tissue-specific expression profiling revealed clear organ-dependent patterns. Overall, HvCHS1, HvCHI2, and HvDFR13 exhibited relatively higher expression in aerial tissues (stem and leaf), whereas HvF3H1 showed elevated expression in stem and root. In contrast, HvF3′H20 displayed preferential expression in root tissue, indicating tissue-specific regulation of flavonoid pathway genes (Fig. 10). These findings suggest that flavonoid biosynthesis genes in barley are involved in tissue-specific regulation, reflecting organ-dependent functional roles in plant development and stress adaptation.

Discussion

In addition to having antioxidant properties, flavonoids are recognized for their crucial functions during plant adaptations in varying environmental conditions68. The abiotic stress-governed metabolites involved in the flavonoid biosynthesis pathway have already been well studied in dicots, but few research findings have been observed in monocots like barley. Considering barley as an important food, feed, and brew crop, identification and functional characterization of enzymes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis were systematically and critically analyzed at the genomic level. Here, we have deeply analyzed the structural characteristics and molecular mechanisms of how barley flavonoid biosynthesis enzymes respond to high temperature, salinity, and drought stresses.

Through a series of systematic analyses, 108 enzymes under 13 different sub-families were identified in barley that are responsible for flavonoid biosynthesis. The amino acid sequences in the enzymes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis in barley ranged from 83 to 542, suggesting their considerable structural diversity. The isoelectric point of <7 in 68.52% enzymes reveals that the majority of enzymes are acidic, and potentially linked to the N-terminus. Negative GRAVY values indicate the hydrophilic nature of the identified enzymes. The predicted cytoplasmic localization of most enzymes may suggests their potential roles in cytoplasmic flavonoids homeostasis. Since bioinformatics based prediction is algorithm based, we lab validation could guide the exact subcellular localization of the proteins.69.

Phylogenetic analysis was performed to evaluate the intra species relationships among the enzymes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis in barley– (Fig. 1). The clustering of barley and rice proteins into five major clades in phylogenetic tree corresponding to established enzyme superfamilies indicates a high degree of conservation between these monocot species6. This pattern is consistent with shared ancestry and conserved among the enzyme families into the flavonoid biosynthesis pathway. Together, these results provide a comparative framework for assessing gene family conservation and diversification within cereals.

Introns play a vital role in genome size and evolution, gene structure, protein structure and alteration, and gene function70. Here, 21 non-intronic genes could play a quick response to biotic/abiotic stresses, growth and development, and other regulatory responses71 (Fig. 2A). Conserved motifs guide how the same protein could act differently based on external and internal environments in plants72. Among 20 selected motifs, the higher abundance of 1, 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13, 14, 16, 18, and 20 motifs potentially linked to their conserved functions among family members (Fig. 2B). Consistent non-existence of motif in nine flavonoid biosynthesis enzymes among barley and rice genome represent their genomic and functional integrity6.

Chromosomal localization of a gene greatly affects the functions of upstream and downstream genes, along with genome evolution73. In plant breeding, genetic engineering guides the way to get desired characteristics with the highest precision74. The chromosomal localization of enzymes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis in barley may provide a deep insight to the plant breeders to develop barley with the desired characteristics of flavonoids (Fig. 3A).

Chromosomal synteny refers to conserved genomic regions derived from a common ancestor75. In the present study, synteny analysis revealed a high number of homologous flavonoid biosynthesis–related genes between barley and the closely related monocot species Brachypodium distachyon (Fig. 3B). The presence of multiple conserved syntenic gene pairs suggests structural conservation of flavonoid biosynthesis gene families within grasses, consistent with shared ancestry and evolutionary constraints in monocot groups.

Properties of amino acid sequence directly affect the proteins 3D structure, folding, and biological functions76. In 3D protein structure multiple alpha may play a contributory role in activating the ion channel and facilitating the signal transduction through the cellular membrane in plant77. In our research multiple alpha helices among the analyzed proteins indicate their involvement in signal transduction and protein trafficking in the flavonoid biosynthesis pathway in barley (Fig. 4A)78.

Cis-regulatory elements are known to be associated with diverse biological and physiological processes, including stress-responsive transcriptional regulation in plants79. In this study, the enrichment of MeJA, abscisic acid, drought, and meristem-related cis-elements in the promoters of flavonoid biosynthesis–related genes suggests potential regulatory responsiveness to developmental and stress-related signals80 (Fig. 4B). Although notable stress and hormone regulatory cis-element are enriched among the promoters of the enzymes, the cis elements itself could not regulate the gene. Specific transcription factor could bind the cis elements to activate or repress the transcription of the enzyme that requires experimental validation.

Gene Ontology and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses confirmed the involvement of the identified enzymes in flavonoid biosynthesis–related processes (Fig. 5). These results support the functional annotation of the identified enzyme families and highlight their central role in flavonoid biosynthesis in barley81 .

Protein–protein interaction analysis highlights potential associations among flavonoid biosynthesis–related enzymes in barley (Fig. 6A). Coordinated protein interactions have previously been reported to play important roles in regulating metabolic pathways, including flavonoid biosynthesis, in different plant species82,83 . In the present study, the identification of several highly connected proteins within the predicted network suggests coordinated involvement of these enzymes in pathway organization and flavonoid biosynthesis.

Several transcription factor families, ERF, MYB, NAC, TCP, and BBR-BPC, have been found to participate in stress-responsive and developmental regulation in various plant species84. In the present study, predicted associations between HvF3’H2, ERF, and NAC-mediated stress signaling could activate the enzyme under drought or salinity stress, thereby enhancing flavonoid accumulation for antioxidative protection, whereas MYB and TCP TFs may integrate developmental signals with the metabolic regulation of flavonoid pathways85. Collectively, this interaction network indicates that HvF3’H2 may function as a key regulatory node linking flavonoid biosynthesis with stress-adaptive transcriptional programs (Fig. 6C).

Molecular docking is widely used to explore potential interactions between proteins and ligands and to examine ligand behavior within predicted binding sites86. In this study, docking analysis was performed to comparatively assess enzyme–ligand recognition and relative binding affinities within the flavonoid biosynthesis pathway. Observed higher binding affinities between flavonoids enzymes–ligands suggest their potential stable interactions 86. Hydrogen bonding and hydrophobic interactions were identified as major contributors to the stabilization of the docked complexes, consistent with general principles of protein–ligand interactions87. The frequent involvement of charged amino acids (glutamic acid), are commonly observed in protein–ligand interfaces and may influence local interaction stability88.

Molecular dynamics simulation is used to evaluate the temporal stability and conformational behavior of docked protein–ligand complexes86. In the present study, average RMSD values ranging from 1 to 3 Å indicated overall structural stability of the selected complexes throughout the simulation period89. RMSF analysis revealed higher flexibility at the N- and C-terminal regions, a pattern consistent with previous MD studies of plant proteins90. In addition, SASA and Rg analyses supported the maintenance of global structural integrity during simulation91 (Fig. 8). Overall, docking and MD simulation provide supportive, hypothesis-generating structural insights into potential enzyme–ligand interactions in barley flavonoid biosynthesis, require experimental validation for functional interpretation.

Gene expression pattern serves as a crucial foundation for subsequent investigations into the function and evolution of the gene. In rice, OsCHS3, 27, OsCHI1, 2, 5, 6, 7, OsFLS3, 4, 5, OsF3H6, 7, OsLDOX3, 4, OsF30H8, 10, and OsANR2 were highly upregulated in different organs and tissues and have a great impact on developing respective tissues and organs6. In safflower, CtF3’H5 was upregulated in different tissues and involved in flower development10. In our research, upregulation of major genes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis in barley at different tissues/organs may suggest their important contribution in developing respective tissues/organs (Fig. 9).

Higher expression of HvCHS1, HvCHI2, and HvDFR13 genes in stem and leaf tissues under qRT-PCR analysis mediated gene expression may guide their role in flavonoid synthesis in those tissues. Upregulation of HvF3H1 in stem and root tissues, and the same of HvF3’H20 in root tissue could indicate their involvement in flavonoid biosynthesis, loeading and unloading in the respective tissues (Fig. 10). These findings suggest that barley flavonoid genes are involved in biosynthesis of flavonoids in root, stem, and leaf as well as their loading, unloading along with antioxidant role.

Under heat, salinity, and drought stresses, the selected four genes showed upregulated trends in root and stem in most cases. Upregulation of HvCHS1 in the root, and downregulation of HvF3’H20 in the same tissue under heat stress may suggest their contribution under high temperature in the respective tissue (Fig. 11). In addition, downregulation of HvCHI2 in root under salinity and HvDFR13 in stem under salinity and drought stress potentially linked to their vital roles in tolerating the respective stresses (Fig. 12 and Fig. 13). OsCHS12, 28, OsF3H2, OsDFR6, and OsLDOX2 were upregulated in rice under salinity stress6. Whereas, AhUGT75A (flavonoid-related gene) is upregulated under both drought and salt stress in peanut92. Interestingly, BrFLS1, BrFLS2, BrFLS3.1, BrFLS3.2, and BrFLS3.3 are upregulated in Brassica under high temperature, salt, and drought stress, revealing their contribution to the respective stresses, probably by regulating the flavonol biosynthesis pathway25. Together, these results indicate that flavonoid biosynthesis–related genes in barley exhibit early transcriptional responses to high temperature, salinity, and drought, consistent with stress-associated regulation of flavonoid metabolism. Under drought stress, reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulate and act as signaling molecules, triggering increased expression of flavonoid biosynthesis genes such as CHS, CHI, F3H, and DFR. This upregulation enhances flavonoid accumulation, which can scavenge ROS and mitigate oxidative damage in plants93. The upregulation of the same genes under drought stress may also regulate ROS signaling pathway in barley for increasing drought tolerance.

Enzymes involved in flavonoid biosynthesis have been reported to localize predominantly in the cytoplasm, with additional localization in other cellular compartments depending on species7. In Ginkgo biloba, GbCHS9 and GbC4H4 were experimentally shown to localize to the cytoplasm, nucleus, and cell membrane7. In the present study, experimental validation was performed for HvCHS1, in which GFP-associated fluorescence was detected in the plasma membrane and nucleus (Fig. 14). Given that chalcone synthase is classically considered a cytosolic enzyme, this observed localization may reflect dynamic or context-dependent distribution. Further experimental validation, including co-localization analyses and biochemical fractionation, will be necessary to clarify the precise subcellular localization and functional significance of HvCHS1 in flavonoid biosynthesis (Fig. 14).

Conclusion

This study provides the first comprehensive characterization of 108 flavonoids biosynthesis enzymes in barley, revealing their evolutionary origin, regulatory components, and potential roles in stress adaptation. Integrated findings of phylogenetic, docking, molecular dynamics, expression, and localization analyses highlight the physilogical importance of key enzymes such as HvCHS1, HvCHI2, HvDFR13, and HvF3’H20 in flavonoid biosynthesis and abiotic stress tolerance. By linking structural, qRT-PCR, and regulatory insights, our findings not only expand the understanding of flavonoids biology in barley but also provide a foundation for engineering the crop with enhanced flavonoids content and resilience through precise synthetic genetic circuit design. Further functional validation using mutant analysis, gene overexpression, and metabolite profiling will be required to more definitively assess the roles of these flavonoid biosynthesis–related genes in abiotic stress responses.

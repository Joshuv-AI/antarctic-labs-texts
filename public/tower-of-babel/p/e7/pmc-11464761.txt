The impact of patent infringement litigation decisions on firms’ innovation performance in China
Authors: Yuting Deng, Yong Qi, Qing Guo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe judiciary protects the value of innovation through remedies such as injunctions and damages when patent rights are infringed, adjusting the expected returns on future innovation activities for firms. Based on data from 1,062 Chinese firms involved in Patent Infringement Litigations (PILs), this study uses three-way fixed-effects Poisson panel regression models to examine the dynamic impacts of a PIL win-or-lose decision on breakthrough and incremental innovation performance for plaintiff and defendant firms. The study finds that plaintiff micro and small-sized enterprises (MSEs) can only engage in short-term breakthrough innovation after winning a PIL. Plaintiff medium and large-sized enterprises (MLEs) tend to adopt the patent defence strategy after winning, while losing a case positively impacts their breakthrough and incremental innovation. Defendant firms adopt the strategy from incremental innovation to breakthrough innovation after winning, while losing inhibits their innovation. The current Chinese patent system has an imbalance in incentivizing innovation for MSEs. Whether winning or losing, defendant MSEs participating in litigation significantly inhibit their innovation performance at different levels. This paper provides a multidimensional angle for studying the relationship between patent protection and corporate innovation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-74924-1.Keywords: Patent protection, Win-or-lose decision, Innovation performance, Firm size, Impact mechanismSubject terms: Applied mathematics, Scientific data, Statistics

Introduction

The patent system aims to stimulate innovation and is a key endowment of the national innovation system. Even in China, where the traditional legal concept of “no litigation” is an ideal pursuit1,2, more and more patentees have been resolving their disputes through litigation in recent years. The large number of patent disputes entering the judicial system not only crowds out the already insufficient judicial resources but also puts the realization of the patent system’s function of stimulating innovation to a severe test3,4. The patent system has the value orientation of rights protection and innovation incentive, which runs through the whole chain of patent creation, utilization, management, protection, and service. Patent infringement litigation (PIL), in addition to reflecting the function of patent protection, enables litigants to adjust their expectations of innovation benefits and influences their subsequent innovation activity5,6.

Litigation is an effective way to deal with patent infringement disputes and protect enterprises’ innovation rents. However, it is still worth noting that dealing with patent infringement disputes judicially is not a once-and-for-all system design, and that even excessive reliance on the judiciary may result in inefficient allocation of resources by market7,8. Since patents do not have “physical” boundaries, the judicial remedies obtained by enterprises and other right holders are highly correlated with the subjective criteria of judges, and the uncertainty of the decision may also affect the flow of innovation resources after litigation. What is the impact of PIL decisions on firm innovation performance? Can the plaintiff and defendant firms continue to innovate after PIL? How well do their subsequent innovations perform? These questions are related to the realization of patent value and the realistic challenges for enterprises to carry out innovation.

Due to the unpredictability of the patent litigation outcome, enterprises involved in litigation cannot make accurate strategies during the innovation process, increasing the uncertainty and hindrance of subsequent innovation9,10. Various factors, including the invention’s value, ownership, and whether the patentee is an individual, can impact patent litigation outcomes. The number of patents involved, patent age, and claims and forward citations is positively proportional to the amount of infringement damages obtained by the plaintiff11–14.

To verify the impact of patent judicial protection on firm innovation performance, Reitzig et al.15 established a patent decision-making model, pointing out that conditions such as the firm’s innovation strategy, PIL, and the probability of a technology being patented are important influence factors on firm’s patent decision. In China-oriented studies, the Fourth Amendment to the China Patent Law raised the statutory minimum limit of damages and introduced punitive damages to incentivize firms to choose independent innovation strategies16.

Based on patent litigation data, researchers selected various indicators to quantify a firm innovation performance and empirically examined the impact of patent protection on it. On the one hand, patent protection affects firms’ market value. In contrast to firms that experience more patent litigation, which has a significant negative impact on their venture capital investments, firms that experience less patent litigation have a significant positive impact on their venture capital investments17. The market value of defendant’s stock decreases significantly after being involved in a patent lawsuit18. Litigation status (initiating litigation) has a positive effect on the firm’s market capitalization, while being sued for infringement has a negative effect on it19.

On the other hand, patent protection affects corporate R&D investment20. From the perspective of listed companies in China, and winning a PIL has a positive effect on the subsequent innovation of industry-following plaintiff firms and a negative effect on the subsequent innovation of defendants21. Based on patent survey data of Chinese firms, Yin et al.22 indicated that patent litigation motivates firms to invest more in R&D, hold more valid patents, and promote the establishment of in-house IP management departments. Some scholars have employed patent data to study the relationship between patent litigation and corporate innovation23. Japanese plaintiffs, small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), tended to reduce patent applications after litigation, but the scope of their patents expanded24.

Existing studies have conducted preliminary explorations on theoretical analysis of the impact mechanism of PIL on corporate innovation and quantified its innovation performance from different perspectives. However, the results are still limited. On the one hand, most existing studies select listed companies as samples, and few empirical studies have been conducted for Micro and Small-sized Enterprises (MSEs), which are integral parts of the innovation system. On the other hand, the quantification of firm innovation performance is usually non-detailed, and existing studies have rarely seen statistical analysis data that distinguish between patent types. This paper selects reliable data on firms of different sizes, quantifies breakthrough versus incremental innovation with invention patents and utility models, and analyzes the impact of a PIL win-or-lose decision on firms’ innovation performance.

Theoretical analysis and hypothesis

The development of the patent system inevitably triggers a wave of rapid growth of patent applications, which is usually followed by a surge of patent infringement litigation9,25,26. Companies initiating or participating in PIL aim to pursue outcomes that effectively safeguard their innovations. Otherwise, if the plaintiff cannot realize the protection of its innovation through judicature, the high R&D and litigation costs invested in the early stage may go down the drain27. On the defendant’s side, if it loses the case, the defendant often faces injunctions and high compensation, which may also directly impact its innovation performance. Thus, whether for the plaintiff or defendant, the primary purpose of firms participating in PIL is to win. As an externality of business activities, the win-or-lose decisions will make enterprises with different litigation statuses reweigh the costs and benefits of innovation activities.

The impact of winning or losing a litigation on plaintiff firms’ innovation performance

The impact of winning a PIL on innovation performance of a plaintiff firm is manifested in three aspects: firstly, winning means that stability of the patent has been recognized by judicial system, which consolidates plaintiff’s confidence in protecting technological achievements through patent system. PIL may invalidate the plaintiff’s patent. As a matter of Chinese judicial practice, defendant’s common strategy is to apply to the China National Intellectual Property Administration (CNIPA) to declare the focusing patent invalid, or to file a counterclaim with court. From the patent examination perspective, the examination results may also be slightly different due to the examiner’s subjective standards. Even for the invention patents that are strictly examined for novelty, non-obviousness and utility, the risk of losing patent rights remains in the patent invalidation procedure. That is the origin of “probabilistic patent”28. In other words, the plaintiff’s winning reflects its reasonable utilization of the patent system and protects its innovations, which will facilitate its subsequent innovations.

Secondly, to ensure that patentee obtains adequate remedies for patent infringement, judges usually combine different remedies. In China, except for cases in which no substantive infringement has occurred or significant public interests are involved, plaintiffs basically obtain permanent injunctions when they win12,29. The main purpose of firms to apply for and lay out patents is to construct the barrier to technology in competition, and a winning decision consolidates the exclusion effect of patent protection, or excludes interference of competitors through the isolating mechanism30. In addition, damages aimed at filling the actual loss of patentee can be invested in subsequent R&D as revenue for firms.

Thirdly, the plaintiff’s victory sends a positive signal, reducing information asymmetry between the firm and external investors in terms of technical fundamentals and legal practice31. Litigation is a confrontation between two parties in legal practice, and winning the lawsuit indicates that the party usually has good R&D ability and more reliable technology accumulation32. A better legal capacity can reduce uncertainty due to patent infringement risks and deter possible future infringements. When a plaintiff prevails, firms can incur fewer transaction costs in acquiring investments, and obtain sustained investment needed for subsequent innovations33.

However, litigation, as a relatively intense legal dispute resolution, cannot always be won by plaintiff. As the Priest-Klein Hypothesis suggests, plaintiffs and defendants choose litigation over settlement as an ultimate resolution of disputes because both parties expect to win34,35.

The impact of losing a lawsuit on plaintiff’s firm is mainly reflected in cost-sinking and loss of technological barriers. Judicial cost is an important factor of influence for firms in resolving patent infringement disputes through litigation. Because patent infringement involves complex technical information and the infringement behavior is frequently hidden, the plaintiff in hiring patent lawyers, investigation and evidence collection, and technical appraisal costs considerably. The cost of a PIL in countries such as France and the Netherlands can reach 50,000-200,000 euros36, while the cost of litigation in the United Kingdom and the United States is even more staggering37. It has also led many firms in the United Kingdom and the United States to reduce the judicial costs of PIL, resolve disputes through settlement before trial, and even generate Non-Practicing Entities (NPEs) whose main revenue is derived from lawsuits. Although the cost of PIL in China is not as high as in Europe and the U.S., the related expenses can still directly constitute the firm’s operating expenditures, negatively affecting its R&D input and innovation performance.

Another inhibitory effect of losing a lawsuit is the loss of technical barriers. In litigation, the judge determines whether the allegedly infringing product or method of producing the infringing product falls within the scope of plaintiff’s patent claims. If plaintiff loses litigation, it loses the technical barriers established by the focal patent, reduces profit margin of its business activities, further affects the Firm’s R&D expenditures, and inhibits innovation performance. Thus, to summarize the theoretical analysis of how winning and losing the litigation impact the innovation of plaintiff firm, hypothesis H1 is proposed.

H1: Winning a PIL has a positive effect on plaintiff firm’s innovation performance; losing a PIL has a negative effect on its innovation performance.

H1: Winning a PIL has a positive effect on plaintiff firm’s innovation performance; losing a PIL has a negative effect on its innovation performance.

The impact of winning or losing a litigation on defendant firms’ innovation performance

Although the litigation status reflects to some extent the differences between the parties in innovation ability and the initiative application of the patent system, it doesn’t mean the defendant is an inferior, nor does the defendant lack technological innovation capability. Even in the telecommunication, semiconductor, and other patent-intensive industries, plaintiff and defendant usually have comparable innovation abilities38. The ability to integrate, organize and reconfigure internal and external resources in order to adapt to rapidly changing environments is a key factor in the sustained competitive advantage of an enterprise39. Defendant firms are unwilling to consistently play the role of “passive” imitators in PILs if they want to achieve long-term stability through competitive advantage. Therefore, after being sued for patent infringement, firms have the incentive to adjust their innovation strategies according to the litigation decision to realize their development goals.

The impact of winning litigation on the defendant is mainly reflected in two aspects. First, PIL is important for defendant firms to understand and learn how to utilize the patent system. Regarding litigation status, defendants are usually on the defensive, while the plaintiff has been able to use its patented technology to launch attacks on competitors. It suggests that plaintiffs are superior to passive defendants in most cases in implementing and utilizing patent strategies18. However, this disadvantage also means that the defendant has a higher marginal efficiency in learning to use the patent system. In addition, for the prevailing defendant, the decision does not produce negative results such as compensation and injunction, which may directly affect its subsequent development.

Secondly, for defendant with sufficient R&D capability, winning a case helps to improve its confidence in R&D. In addition to the invalidation request, defendant usually defends with its own patents or prior art. The defendant’s winning may show that it has a good technical analysis ability, which is an indispensable element of innovation.

Regarding the defendant’s loss, permanent injunctions and damages are the most direct factors affecting a firm innovation. Permanent injunctions limit the defendant firm’s access to the patented technology, directly and negatively impacting the defendant’s product market share. Further, suppose the defendant firm carries out R&D activities intending to achieve the same technical effect as the plaintiff’s patent. In that case, it needs to bypass the technical barriers set by the plaintiff’s valid patent, indirectly increasing such defendant’s R&D costs.

Compensation strikes defendant firm’s revenues more intuitively in economic terms. A losing defendant’s average compensation in PIL is even higher than the R&D costs of half of the patents under the CNIPA investigation22. On top of that, implicit or explicit costs of justice, such as attorney’s fees, will add to the financial situation of the losing defendant firm.

Another manifestation of the inhibitory effect of losing a lawsuit on a defendant’s innovation performance is its impact on corporate finance. Defendant’s loss of PIL will cause changes in public opinion and produce a “copycat” image in the market19, dramatically diminishing the goodwill firm has earned through hard work. Its impact on the market value even exceeds the direct cost that the firm spends in litigation30. Based on this, we propose hypothesis H2.

H2: Winning a PIL has a positive effect on the defendant firm’s innovation performance; losing a PIL has a negative effect on its innovation performance.

H2: Winning a PIL has a positive effect on the defendant firm’s innovation performance; losing a PIL has a negative effect on its innovation performance.

Data and methodology

Data sources

Patent infringement litigation data

The data on PIL is extracted from the PKULAW database40. The statistics are based on the time of entry into force of decisions. In cases where there is an appeal, the actual date of entry into force of the decision is taken as the time of conclusion of the proceedings, following the content of the court’s decision of the second instance.

Firm’s business information and patent data

Firm business information comes from Qixin.com and Aiqicha.baidu.com databases, including firm name, date of establishment, paid-in capital, industry, registered capital with or without foreign investment, whether it is an MSE, whether it is a high-tech enterprise, etc.

Data on the firms’ invention and utility model patent applications were obtained from the IncoPat database to quantify firms’ technological innovation performance.

Data collection

Firms involved in invention PILs in China are selected as samples. Considering the judicial duration of PIL and the time limit of patent disclosure, the selected period for invention PIL is 2005–2018, and for firms’ patent applications is 2005–2021. The data collection is based on the following rules: (1) Combining multiple lawsuits that were consolidated by the court initiated by the same firm under the same cause of action; (2) Excluding enterprises involved in multiple patent infringement lawsuits from 2005 to 2018, in order to exclude the superposition effect of multiple litigation results at different points in time on the innovation impact of enterprises; (3) Excluding the data of Chinese patent applications filed by firms with “two applications in one case”, i.e., applying for utility model and then applying for invention patent for the same technology. Therefore, we only use the firms that involved in only once patent infringement lawsuit during our sample period.

Based on the data collection rules, we retrieved 1,784 Chinese invention PIL from 2005 to 2018. The initial number of sample firms was 483 plaintiff firms and 738 defendant firms. After matching firms’ business information and patent data, we obtained 352 samples of plaintiff firms, including 96 MSEs and 256 MLEs, 33 of which were listed firms; 710 samples of defendant firms, including 208 MSEs and 502 MLEs, 32 of which were listed firms.

Methodology

Variables

Dependent variables

Existing research on measuring firm innovation performance focuses on technological innovation input on the one hand, and the output on the other. Moreover, the quantification of firm innovation output mainly adopts the total number of patent applications, which covers the three patent types of invention, utility model and design as stipulated in China’s patent system41. However, as far as the protection scope is concerned, inventions and utility models focus on protecting technical solutions, while designs focus on protecting the combination of art and products. To be precise, when it comes to the patentability of designs, the object of protection is not a program implementing technical concepts but an aesthetic “work” with a practical application.

In addition, China’s Patent Law adopts the concept of Inventiveness to indicate non-obviousness. Specifically, compared with the prior art, a utility model has substantive features and represents a progress, and an invention has prominent substantive features and represents an obvious progress. In terms of Inventiveness, there is a clear difference between the two at the examination stage, which is directly reflected in the different innovation levels6.

We distinguish patent applications of firms to characterize the breakthrough innovation by invention patents and the incremental innovation by utility models42. The measurements of the dependent variables are as follows:

Total number of patent applications for technological solutions (): the sum of invention and utility model patents filed by firms from 2005 to 2021 is used to quantify the overall level of firm innovation performance.

Total number of patent applications for technological solutions (): the sum of invention and utility model patents filed by firms from 2005 to 2021 is used to quantify the overall level of firm innovation performance.

Number of invention patent applications (): Quantifies breakthrough innovation of firms in terms of the sum of invention patents filed from 2005 to 2021.

Number of invention patent applications (): Quantifies breakthrough innovation of firms in terms of the sum of invention patents filed from 2005 to 2021.

Number of utility model patent applications (): Quantifies incremental innovation of firms in terms of the sum of utility model patents filed from 2005 to 2021.

Number of utility model patent applications (): Quantifies incremental innovation of firms in terms of the sum of utility model patents filed from 2005 to 2021.

Modeling

Construct regression models of a PIL win-or-lose decision affecting firm innovation, as shown in Eqs. (1)–(3). The models control the impact of enterprise capital, firm age, enterprise’s intrinsic innovation advantage, industrial characteristics, and foreign investment. We use three-way fixed-effects Poisson panel regressions to test the hypotheses empirically, controlling for individual heterogeneity and time effect on dependent variables in the model, which is represented by and respectively. Besides, since the innovation policies of different provinces in mainland China may have unobservable impacts on firms’ innovation performance, we fix the province effect in the models, denoted by , and is a random perturbation term. It is worth noting that Eqs. (1)-(3) treat the success or failure of a firm’s litigation as an external factor influencing firm innovation performance. Under the data collection rules, the occurrence of either or is mutually exclusive. Consequently, in the estimation of regression coefficients, the value of is missing when , and vice versa.

A reasonable question is the possible bias of judges’ individual adjudicative tendencies in the influence mechanism. Distinguishing the study focusing on patent invalidation by 29 judges at the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit from 1982 to 200848, China has a large number of judges at all levels of courts hearing IP cases. In 2021, for example, China’s Supreme People’s Court (SPC) had 42 IP judges. The court uses a computer program to randomly assign at least three or an odd number of judges to hear cases in a panel, and it has a trial committee to discuss major or complex cases. It is also how the assignment process works in lower-level courts in China. Regarding judicial efficiency, judges at the SPC conclude more than 80 IP cases per year in 2021, and other Chinese judges adjudicate an average of more than 300 IP cases per year. Thus, we argue that the effect of judges’ individual adjudication tendency on the outcome of PIL is random, and includes the effect.

It should be noted that it takes time for firms to deal with the externalities affecting their development and make new strategies. Based on the statistics of the China Patent Survey Report 2022 released by CNIPA, invention and utility model patents with an R&D cycle of more than three years only accounted for 5.5% and 1.7% of the entire patents of respondents. We introduce three-year lag terms of winning and losing decisions to construct the dynamic impact effect models49.

In contrast to the European Union’s criteria for classifying SMEs, China classifies enterprises into micro, small, medium and large enterprises for different industries based on the number of employees and business revenue, jointly defined by the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, National Bureau of Statistics, National Development and Reform Commission, Ministry of Finance, China. MSEs are defined as those with an annual taxable income of no more than 3 million CNY, a workforce of no more than 300 employees, and total assets of no more than 50 million CNY. Due to the operating pressure of MSEs, the Ministry of Finance and other departments have issued special tax incentives and operating expense reductions for MSEs. MSEs have become subjects that cannot be ignored in China’s innovation system. To further verify the effect of PIL decisions on firms’ innovation in different sizes, the sample firms are divided into MSEs and Medium and Large Enterprises (MLEs) for heterogeneity analysis. Results and discussion

Empirical analysis of a PIL decision affecting innovation performance in plaintiff and defendant firms

Before regression analysis, we conduct Pearson correlation tests for each variable in the sample and calculate the variance inflation factors (VIFs). The VIF value of each variable is not greater than 2, indicating that the benchmark models do not have obvious multicollinearity. In addition, to avoid pseudo-regression due to unstable data, the unit root test was conducted on the non-dummy variables of the sample using the LLC and IPS tests, and each variable was a zero-order single integer at the 1% level, which showed that the data are stable.

Table 2 displays the impact of winning or losing a PIL on subsequent innovation performance of plaintiff and defendant firms. As in model (1), winning a case has a positive effect on plaintiffs’ innovation performance, but the positive effect is not stable, as plaintiffs file fewer patent applications in the second year after winning. In contrast, losing a case has a stable and incentive effect for three years on plaintiff firms’ innovation. The coefficients show that the number of patent applications filed in the three years following a plaintiff’s loss will increase by 13.7%, 24.1% and 29.2%, respectively, compared to the year in which the case was decided. It is different from the existing result that winning a lawsuit has a positive incentive effect on R&D expenditures of plaintiff firms, while losing a lawsuit has a significant negative effect on them, because the existing research focuses on the innovation performance of listed companies in China21.

However, there are magnitude non-listed firms in the market with limited business size, which have a higher degree of independence over their R&D activities. Even if such plaintiff firms experience a loss, there is no reason why the loss should block their access to investment.

Model (2) examines the effect of a PIL decision on breakthrough innovation of plaintiff firms. The impact of losing litigation on plaintiff firm shows a negative and then positive trend; that is, in the short term, the losing plaintiff firm will apply for fewer invention patents, but it will significantly increase its invention patent applications and carry out more breakthrough innovation activities one year after the judicial decision. It may because breakthrough innovation is characterised by a long-term R&D process, high risk, high creativity, etc. Therefore, the number of invention patent applications in the first three years after losing will decrease and then increase. In terms of winning the litigation, the 1st-year’s impact on the firm invention patent application is not significant, and in the long run, winning litigation reduces the breakthrough innovation activities. This is because the plaintiff in a PIL generally has an advantage over the defendant in terms of technological accumulation, such as the valid patent stock. Faced with the economic rent of existing invention patents and the high uncertainty of breakthrough innovations, firms have more incentives to carry out incremental innovations based on their existing technological advantages.

Model (3) reports the performance of plaintiff firms in incremental innovation after a PIL. In the long run, winning the case incentivises plaintiff firms to engage in incremental innovation activities. The number of utility model applications filed by plaintiffs will increase by 15.6% in the third year after winning. Regarding the plaintiff losing the case, the coefficients are always reflected as an incentive effect and are significant at the 1% level.

The empirical result partially rejects hypothesis H1. To further explore reasons for the unstable impact of winning a PIL on firm innovation, we conduct heterogeneity analyses on benchmark models using firm size as a classification criterion.

On the defendant side, as shown in model (4), winning a case has a negative effect on firm innovation in the 1st-year, but has a positive effect in the 3rd-year. It may be because, although the defendant does not have to pay damages if it wins, the organisational and transaction costs associated with litigation are still a direct financial expense for defendant firm. In terms of defendant revenue, except for a successful counterclaim, in the short term, the optimal outcome for defendant is for the court to reject plaintiff’s claim and leave defendant’s business in the same state as it was before the lawsuit, and participation in lawsuit does not bring direct revenue for the defendant.

On the contrary, the defendant will have to pay considerable costs to deal with the uncertainty of judicial outcome, and such costs will reduce firm’s investment in R&D in the short term. As we mentioned in the theoretical analysis, in the long run, defendant’s success in litigation still incentivizes its technological innovation. As the most confrontation between firm innovation and market competition, a PIL is an important way for firms to learn and implement the patent system. Winning the litigation strengthens the defendant’s confidence in its R&D and enables it to carry out technological innovation and patent layout after getting out of the shadows of litigation. However, once defendant firm loses the litigation, losing will have a long-lasting negative impact on firm’s innovation performance.

For a defendant’s breakthrough innovation, as in model (5), losing a lawsuit has a 3-year inhibitory effect on defendant’s innovation performance, whereas winning will weaken the defendant’s breakthrough innovation in the short term. However, in the long term, the incentive effect appears.

An interesting finding regards the defendant’s incremental innovations. While losing a case still has a negative impact on defendants’ incremental innovation performance that lasts for three years, winning a case has a significant incentive effect for two years. The coefficient of the model (6) indicates that the two-year negative impact of winning the lawsuit on the defendant firm’s innovation performance is only reflected in the breakthrough innovation represented by invention patents, and that the firm will actively conduct R&D activities for incremental innovations after winning the litigation. In the short-term deterioration of defendant firm’s economic situation, applying for utility model patents to lay out an innovation map has a higher economic efficiency. Combined analysis with model (5), we find that defendant firm’s main innovation strategy after winning the litigation is to carry out incremental innovation for patent layout, and then to develop breakthrough innovation to enhance competitive advantage.

As a result, hypothesis H2 is partially supported by the empirical study. Further, heterogeneity analysis will be conducted to verify the impact in different-sized firms.

Robustness tests

Since the impact of PIL decisions on firms’ innovation performance may exceed three years, the three-way fixed-effects panel Poisson regressions are conducted on the entire sample by introducing four- and five-year lagged explanatory variables in the benchmark models.

Due to the continuity of firms’ technological innovation process, the innovation performance of the previous period may impact their subsequent innovation. Thus, the total number of patent applications for technical solutions in period t-1 is introduced as a control variable in the robustness test model.

In addition, we use the ratio of filed inventions to total patents as the dependent variable to quantify the propensity of filing breakthrough innovation and to analyze the impact of winning and losing on the propensity of firms to undertake breakthrough innovation.

The estimated coefficients of explanatory variables, error terms, correlation test of robustness test are consistent with benchmark models, as well as the propensity of undertaking breakthrough innovation. Subsequently, based on the above methods, regression results of MSEs and MLEs are tested, and the coefficients do not change significantly, indicating that our results are robust.

Conclusions and practical implications

Main conclusions

Interests and conflicts go hand in hand. Overlapping patent rights inevitably result in numerous infringement disputes. The outcomes of PIL are uncertain, and once a firm is involved in a judicial dispute, its technological innovation and other business activities will provide feedback on the judicial decision. This paper analyses theoretically and empirically, studies the dynamic impact of a PIL decision on firm innovation performance and obtains the following main conclusions:

Plaintiff MSEs can only engage in short-term breakthrough innovation after winning a case. For all plaintiffs, losing a lawsuit has a significant positive effect on both breakthrough and incremental innovation, while winning only has a positive effect on incremental innovation and a negative impact on breakthrough innovation. By distinguishing between firm sizes, a plaintiff MSE will strengthen breakthrough innovation and weaken incremental innovation if it wins the PIL, while losing the case will inhibit breakthrough innovation and provide short-term incentives for incremental innovation for one or two years. Besides, if plaintiff MSEs lose a lawsuit, they cannot obtain a favourable position in terms of economic and technological paths in subsequent competition, and their limited innovation resources can only be used for the short-term development of incremental innovations.

Besides, if plaintiff MSEs lose a lawsuit, they cannot obtain a favourable position in terms of economic and technological paths in subsequent competition, and their limited innovation resources can only be used for the short-term development of incremental innovations.

The plaintiff MLEs tend to adopt a patent defence strategy after winning PIL. For plaintiff MLEs, winning has a significant inhibiting effect on their breakthrough innovation and an incentive effect on their incremental innovation; losing a lawsuit has a short-term inhibiting impact on their breakthrough innovation, but the impact turns positive in the second year. In addition, losing PIL has a significant incentive effect on the incremental innovation of plaintiff MLEs. Practical implications

Our research generates some implications for policymakers and entrepreneurs. First, the policy support for innovative MSEs should be strengthened to alleviate their concerns about high-quality innovation after litigation. Based on innovation capacity evaluation, government guidance fund policies for innovative MSEs should be improved, and intellectual property financial services such as patent insurance and patent pledges should be promoted on a pilot basis. Secondly, policymakers should guide MLEs to play the leading role of high-quality technological innovation, to prevent them from excessively pursuing the number of patents and neglecting the improvement of patent quality. For entrepreneurs, firms should adopt innovative development strategies in the market, accumulate technological innovations to build technical barriers, and avoid possible patent infringement risks at the source through patent intelligence analysis and R&D activities.

Limitations

The approach presented in this paper still has some limitations. First, this paper collects data from firms of different sizes involved in litigation, partially overcoming the limitation that most existing studies focus on listed companies. However, since most medium-sized enterprises and MSEs do not disclose their operating information, collecting their R&D, new products, and other financial data is still challenging, so we choose the number of utility models and invention patent applications to quantify the innovation performance of enterprises. Current research using citation information, combination of IPC codes, and natural language processing will guide the direction of deepening this research51,52. The indicators of corporate innovation performance will be optimized in the future. Second, because NPEs may also exist in China, and such entities may differ from general litigation entities in terms of litigation characteristics and organizational features, we excluded firms involved in multiple initiations, which is a basic characteristic of NPEs, from the data collection stage. Empirical research on the impact of NPE litigation on firms’ innovation performance will be conducted in the future.

Reactive Therapeutic Drug Monitoring Guides Optimal Management of Adalimumab Failure in Pediatric Crohn’s Disease: A Real-World Single-Center Experience
Authors: In Sook Jeong, Tae-Gyeong Kim, Dae Yong Yi, Kyung Mo Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeAlthough biologics, including anti-tumor necrosis factor (TNF) drugs, are increasingly used for treating pediatric Crohn’s disease (CD), the potential loss of response (LOR) to these drugs requires attention. This retrospective study investigated reactive therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) and the clinical course of pediatric-onset CD in patients treated with adalimumab.MethodsPatients aged <18 years diagnosed with CD and treated with adalimumab were enrolled in this study. Reactive TDM levels, presence of anti-drug antibodies (ADAs), and LOR were evaluated from 2017 to 2019, and clinical outcomes were followed up until June 2022.ResultsThirty-two pediatric patients with CD were enrolled: 14 in the LOR group and 18 in the remission group. The median ages at CD diagnosis and first adalimumab injection were 13 and 14 years, respectively. The median duration of adalimumab injection was 12.5 months. Among 7 patients with therapeutic trough levels (TLs) and undetectable ADAs, 5 achieved clinical remission with continued adalimumab, whereas 2 remained refractory and subsequently switched to alternative biologics. Among 5 patients with subtherapeutic TLs (<5 µg/mL) and undetectable ADAs, 3 eventually underwent a switch to other biologic agents despite interval shortening, while the remaining 2 were ultimately diagnosed with monogenic inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) after further genetic evaluation.ConclusionReactive TDM and ADA categorizes the mechanisms of LOR to adalimumab in pediatric CD, guiding appropriate dose escalation or biologic switching. When these strategies fail, exploring underlying monogenic disorders is essential for optimal management.

INTRODUCTION

Biologic therapy has substantially improved the clinical outcomes in pediatric Crohn’s disease (CD); however, loss of response (LOR) during long-term treatment remains a major challenge [1]. Up to 30% of pediatric patients fail to achieve a primary response to biological therapy, and approximately 20% of initial responders experience secondary LOR annually [2345]. Immunogenicity, particularly the development of antidrug antibodies (ADAs), and pharmacokinetic variability, are recognized as key mechanisms underlying treatment failure, which may be more pronounced in children owing to growth-related physiological changes. Children exhibit greater pharmacokinetic variability than adults, influenced by body composition and inflammatory burden, which can lead to unpredictable drug exposure under standard weight-based dosing [678].

Therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM), which assesses serum drug concentrations and ADA status, has emerged as an important tool for optimizing biological therapies. In real-world pediatric practice, reactive TDM, performed in response to clinical deterioration or suspected LOR, is the most commonly used strategy [910]. Reactive TDM helps to identify ADA-associated reductions in adalimumab trough levels, thereby guiding dose escalation or modification of treatment strategies [1112]. Moreover, when therapeutic drug levels are achieved and ADAs are absent despite LOR, reactive TDM supports clinical decision-making regarding switching to biologics with different mechanisms of action or pursuing additional diagnostic evaluation [131415].

Despite its widespread use, real-world data on reactive TDM in pediatric CD, particularly in patients treated with adalimumab, remain limited [1617]. Pediatric-specific evidence on how reactive TDM influences subsequent clinical decision-making is scarce, and optimal management strategies for patients who experience LOR despite therapeutic drug levels are not well established [1819]. Therefore, this study aimed to identify the causes of treatment failure in pediatric patients with CD receiving adalimumab using a reactive TDM approach and to evaluate how TDM-guided assessments, including genetic testing for monogenic disease, influence subsequent clinical management in real-world pediatric practice.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Patient selection and data collection

This retrospective study was conducted to evaluate reactive TDM and ADA levels in pediatric patients with CD who underwent adalimumab maintenance therapy at the Department of Pediatrics, Asan Medical Center Children’s Hospital, between November 2017 and August 2019. Patients were included if they were aged <18 years and had a confirmed diagnosis of CD based on the revised Porto criteria [20]. Disease phenotypes were classified according to the Paris classification [21]. All patients received 40 mg of subcutaneous adalimumab every 2 weeks as maintenance therapy after one or two induction doses. Patients who underwent TDM within four weeks of initiating maintenance therapy were excluded to ensure that the drug levels reflected stable pharmacokinetics. Clinical and demographic data, as well as treatment outcomes, were extracted from electronic medical records and analyzed. Patients were categorized into either the LOR or remission groups based on clinical and biochemical parameters. Follow-up data for the LOR group were reviewed through June 2022.

Definition and patient grouping

Remission was defined as the achievement of both clinical and biochemical remission. Clinical remission was determined by a Pediatric Crohn’s Disease Activity Index (PCDAI) score <10, whereas biochemical remission was defined by a C-reactive protein (CRP) level <0.5 mg/dL, according to the European Crohn’s and Colitis Organisation and the European Society for Paediatric Gastroenterology, Hepatology and Nutrition guideline established in 2021 [2223]. LOR was defined as a PCDAI score >10 and/or CRP >0.5 mg/dL at the time of testing. Based on these criteria, 32 patients were enrolled and classified into the LOR (n=14) or remission (n=18) groups.

Measurements of adalimumab trough levels and anti-drug antibodies

Serum samples were collected immediately before the scheduled adalimumab injection to ensure accurate measurement of trough levels (TLs). TLs and ADA levels were measured using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). For TL measurements, a horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated goat anti-human Fc antibody was used (MP Biomedicals). For ADA measurements, HRP-conjugated goat anti-human lambda light-chain antibodies were used (MP Biomedicals).

Patient classification based on trough levels and anti-drug antibodies

Patients in the LOR group were stratified based on adalimumab TLs and ADA detectability. Using the thresholds described by Nakase et al. [15] and Papamichael et al. [24], therapeutic TLs were defined as ≥5 µg/mL, while subtherapeutic TLs were defined as <5 µg/mL. Each group was further stratified according to ADA status, resulting in four subgroups: (1) therapeutic TLs with undetectable ADA, (2) therapeutic TLs with detectable ADA, (3) subtherapeutic TLs with undetectable ADA, and (4) subtherapeutic TLs with detectable ADA (Fig. 1).

Outcomes of the clinical application of therapeutic drug monitoring

The clinical utility of reactive TDM was evaluated in the LOR group. Management strategies were guided by established algorithms [25], although the final treatment decisions were made by the attending physicians based on clinical judgment. The outcomes included continuation of the current therapy, shortening of dosing intervals, switching to another biological agent with the same or different mechanisms of action, and re-evaluation of the diagnosis. Where necessary, whole-exome sequencing (WES) was performed to assess monogenic inflammatory bowel disease (IBD).

Statistical analyses

Descriptive statistics are presented as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs) for continuous variables and as frequencies with percentages for categorical variables. Scatter plots were used to visually explore the relationship between adalimumab trough levels and weight-based dosage in patients stratified based on LOR status. Spearman’s rank correlation, a nonparametric method, was used to assess the association between dosage and trough levels. Group comparisons were performed using Student’s t-test for continuous variables and the chi-square or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, as appropriate. All analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows (version 23.0; SPSS Inc.).

Ethics statement

This study was approved by the Asan Medical Center Institutional Review Board (No. 2017-1277) and conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from all patients or their legal guardians before testing.

The most common disease locations were L3 (62.5%) in the lower gastrointestinal (GI) tract and L4ab (47.4%) in the upper GI tract. The most frequent disease behavior at diagnosis was B1 (nonstricturing, nonpenetrating) in 46.9% of the patients. There were also no significant differences in mean TLs or ADA positivity between the two groups.

Outcomes of therapeutic drug monitoring in real-world settings

Reactive TDM was performed in the LOR group based on the clinical judgment of the treating physician. Among the 14 patients, seven had therapeutic TLs (≥5 µg/mL) and no detectable ADAs. Of these, five achieved remission without treatment modification after a median follow-up of 18 weeks (range: 8–38 weeks). The remaining two patients had persistent LOR; one switched to vedolizumab at age 18, while the other was considering a treatment change. The other seven patients had subtherapeutic TLs. Within this subgroup, two were ADA-positive and switched to infliximab, both achieving remission. The remaining five patients were ADA-negative; among them, two underwent interval shortening without achieving remission. Both were subsequently diagnosed with monogenic IBD via whole-exome sequencing, specifically chronic enteropathy associated with the SLCO2A1 gene (CEAS) and X-linked inhibitor of apoptosis (XIAP) deficiency. The patient with CEAS showed clinical improvement after initiating an elemental diet, and the patient with XIAP deficiency had a favorable outcome following bone marrow transplantation. Of the three patients who tested negative for both ADA and monogenic IBD, one failed interval shortening but responded to infliximab. The remaining two patients achieved remission after switching to ustekinumab (Fig. 1). These findings highlight the clinical heterogeneity in treatment responses to adalimumab and support the utility of reactive TDM in guiding individualized management decisions, including biological switching and genetic re-evaluation.

DISCUSSION

Adalimumab, a monoclonal anti-TNF-α antibody, has been approved for use in pediatric CD in Korea since May 2015 and is widely used for both the induction and maintenance of remission [26]. With the growing use of biological agents in pediatric CD, heterogeneity in treatment response and LOR has become more apparent, underscoring the need for a systematic approach for treatment optimization. In this context, reactive TDM has emerged as a practical decision-making tool in real-world pediatric practice.

We evaluated the clinical utility of reactive TDM in pediatric patients with CD who experienced LOR to adalimumab and emphasized its role in guiding treatment adjustments. Reactive TDM assists clinicians in identifying pharmacokinetic and immunogenic mechanisms underlying treatment failure by assessing serum TLs and ADAs. Based on these parameters, the patients were classified into four pharmacological and immunological response profiles, each supporting distinct clinical decisions. Most patients with therapeutic TLs and no detectable ADAs maintained clinical remission, suggesting that symptom exacerbation in this group may reflect non-inflammatory mechanisms or functional gastrointestinal disorders, rather than active intestinal inflammation. In contrast, patients with subtherapeutic TLs and ADA positivity frequently required switching to alternative biological agents, such as infliximab or vedolizumab, whereas dose escalation or interval shortening was pursued in those with subtherapeutic TLs without detectable ADAs. Scatter plot analysis complements these findings by illustrating the limitations of dose escalation alone. Although higher weight-based doses are often attempted in LOR, increasing the adalimumab dosage does not consistently prevent treatment failure. This distribution likely reflects real-world practice in which reactive dose intensification is commonly pursued before alternative strategies are considered. However, the persistence of LOR despite intensified dosing indicates that treatment failure in pediatric CD is not solely dose dependent and may involve pharmacokinetic variability or non-TNF-mediated inflammatory pathways.

In this setting, reactive TDM plays a critical role in distinguishing pharmacokinetic failure from mechanistic failure. When LOR occurs despite adequate TLs and prior dose intensification, further escalation is unlikely to restore clinical response. Reactive TDM provides an objective framework to guide subsequent management decisions, including switching to biological agents with different mechanisms of action or pursuing additional diagnostic evaluations. This approach may prompt the consideration of the underlying genetic or immune dysregulation in selected pediatric patients, particularly those with early onset or refractory diseases. Notably, WES identified monogenic IBD in a subset of patients who remained refractory despite treatment optimization, highlighting the complementary role of genetic testing in biologically unresponsive pediatric cases.

Genetic evaluations are not routinely performed in all patients with IBD. Instead, current pediatric IBD guidelines and position papers recommend a selective approach in which genetic testing is based on the patient’s age at disease onset and specific clinical features. In particular, genetic assessment is more strongly indicated in patients with infantile-onset IBD (<2 years of age) or very early onset IBD (<6 years of age), as well as in those with pediatric- or young adult-onset disease who exhibit treatment-refractory or atypical disease courses, a relevant family history, comorbidities associated with immune deficiency or dysregulation (e.g., recurrent infections, lymphoproliferation, autoimmune disease, or hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis), or prominent extraintestinal manifestations [2728]. Therefore, in selected pediatric patients, further genetic evaluation is warranted to exclude or confirm monogenic IBD or underlying immune dysregulation, supporting the use of advanced genomic approaches such as whole-exome sequencing. However, the role of concomitant immunomodulatory therapy in reducing ADA formation during adalimumab therapy remains controversial. While randomized controlled trials, including DIAMOND and IMAgINE-1, did not demonstrate a significant benefit of combination therapy in terms of remission rates [293031], data from the PANTS consortium suggested a reduction in ADA formation with immunomodulator use [32]. These conflicting findings indicate that immunomodulatory therapy should be individualized, rather than routinely administered. Therefore, reactive TDM may offer a more direct and clinically viable approach to managing immunogenicity.

Adalimumab remains a cornerstone biological agent for pediatric CD, with multicenter cohort studies reporting sustained remission and a reduced risk of hospitalization or surgery compared with conventional therapy [3334]. In Korea, two anti-TNF agents, infliximab and adalimumab, have been approved for pediatric CD treatment. These biologics have significantly improved clinical outcomes and reduced adverse effects compared with conventional therapies. However, some patients with adequate drug levels but persistent disease activity required switching to other biologics, indicating a mechanistic mismatch rather than a pharmacokinetic failure. Biologic agents that target non-TNF-α pathways, such as ustekinumab and vedolizumab, have demonstrated efficacy in select patients, although pediatric data remain limited [3536]. A recent pediatric study on vedolizumab reported favorable safety and sustained response in children with prior anti-TNF exposure, with no serious adverse events during a median follow-up of 0.9 years [3738]. Similarly, ustekinumab, which targets interleukin (IL)-12 and IL-23, has been shown to induce remission in patients with CD and ulcerative colitis who previously failed anti-TNF therapy [3940].

The strengths of this study include standardized TDM, clear patient classification based on TLs and ADA status, and the incorporation of genetic testing in refractory cases. Furthermore, the recent approval of adalimumab for pediatric patients with CD supports the value of sharing our center’s treatment experience. However, this study has limitations, including its retrospective single-center design, modest cohort size, and absence of a non-TDM comparator group, which limits the statistical power for subgroup analyses. Additionally, the relatively short follow-up period restricted the assessment of the long-term impact of TDM-guided strategies on disease progression and outcomes. Nevertheless, the 3- to 5-year longitudinal follow-up period and the inclusion of key clinical and laboratory parameters enhance the interpretability of the findings. Future multicenter collaborations incorporating control groups and longer observation periods are warranted to validate the optimal TL and ADA thresholds, incorporate endoscopic and biochemical remission as objective outcome measures, and investigate the genetic variants associated with refractory pediatric Crohn’s disease.

Despite the clinical utility of TDM, its widespread implementation is hindered by several limitations. These include assay costs, variability between testing platforms, and delayed turnaround times, which may limit real-time clinical decision-making. Emerging strategies such as simplified assay platforms, interlaboratory standardization of TDM assays, and the development of point-of-care testing may help reduce costs and improve accessibility. Furthermore, expanding pharmacogenomic testing panels and evaluating their predictive value for biological responses may ultimately enable the development of proactive genotype-guided therapeutic strategies for pediatric IBD management.

In conclusion, reactive TDM combined with ADA assessments provides a critical framework for identifying the underlying pharmacokinetic and immunogenic mechanisms of LOR to adalimumab in pediatric CD. This mechanism-driven approach facilitates treatment optimization, such as appropriate dose escalation or timely drug switching. Moreover, our findings highlight that when patients remain refractory to these initial modifications despite achieving therapeutic drug levels, it is imperative to explore alternative mechanistic causes. Evaluating for underlying conditions, particularly monogenic IBD, is crucial to guide tailored and effective long-term treatment strategies.

Hidden Constraints: Addressing Unreported Limitations in Plasmapheresis Studies for Hypertriglyceridemia-associated Pancreatitis
Authors: Kunal Mahajan, Shivali Sandal, Jai B Sharma, Surender Himral
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

This letter is in response to the recent manuscript titled “Plasmapheresis for Hypertriglyceridemia-induced Acute Pancreatitis: A Systematic Review and Meta-summary of Case Reports” by Nasa et al., published in the Indian Journal of Critical Care Medicine.1 This work, which synthesizes individual patient data from 71 case reports and series, represents one of the most comprehensive efforts to elucidate the clinical efficacy and safety of plasmapheresis in hypertriglyceridemia-induced acute pancreatitis. While the authors acknowledge several important limitations, including reliance on case reports/series, lack of control groups, heterogeneity, missing data, and selection bias, there are further critical limitations not specifically mentioned that could affect the interpretation and generalizability of this systematic review and meta-summary:

Publication bias and positive reporting: Case reports typically highlight successful interventions and positive outcomes. Negative outcomes, failed treatments, or adverse events related to plasmapheresis may be underreported, leading to an overestimation of efficacy and safety.

Publication bias and positive reporting: Case reports typically highlight successful interventions and positive outcomes. Negative outcomes, failed treatments, or adverse events related to plasmapheresis may be underreported, leading to an overestimation of efficacy and safety.

Lack of data on timing and indications for plasmapheresis: The meta-summary aggregates cases but does not systematically analyze optimal timing for plasmapheresis initiation, or clear clinical indications for its use versus conservative pharmacological management. The absence of standardized criteria for intervention may lead to wide practice variability and confound assessment of efficacy.

Inconsistent reporting of adverse events: Although the authors state complications were rarely reported, the retrospective nature and reporting style of case reports means milder or latent adverse events may not be comprehensively assessed. Specifically, long-term safety and monitoring for delayed effects remain unaddressed.

Treatment modalities and concomitant therapies: Many cases included patients simultaneously receiving pharmacological treatments (e.g., insulin, statins, fibrates), organ support, or dietary interventions. The degree of contribution from plasmapheresis alone vs multimodal therapy is largely unquantified.

Differences in plasmapheresis technique: Variability in the technical aspects—such as the volume exchanged, type of replacement fluid, session frequency, and machine specifications—can influence outcomes and safety, but these details are inconsistently reported or analyzed.

Differences in plasmapheresis technique: Variability in the technical aspects—such as the volume exchanged, type of replacement fluid, session frequency, and machine specifications—can influence outcomes and safety, but these details are inconsistently reported or analyzed.

Underlying etiological differences: The population included a mix of primary genetic and secondary hypertriglyceridemia cases, pregnant women, and patients with varying underlying comorbidities. The results may not be equally applicable to all subgroups, and pooled analyses could obscure important differences in response and prognosis.

Cost-effectiveness and resource utilization: There is no discussion of economic analysis, cost implications, or logistical challenges of plasmapheresis, especially crucial in resource-limited settings where such interventions may not be feasible.

No assessment of long-term outcomes: The meta-summary focuses primarily on acute-phase data. Information about long-term prognosis, risk of recurrence, persistent organ dysfunction, or quality of life following plasmapheresis is absent.

Addressing these additional limitations will be vital in future prospective research and guideline development for the management of hypertriglyceridemia-induced acute pancreatitis.

Circ_0001428 regulates erythropoiesis and γ-globin expression via activating the ATF4-BCL11A axis in β-thalassemia
Authors: Jingmin Li, Yali Pan, Meihuan Chen, Junhao Zheng, Siyang Lin, Yanping Zheng, Shuning Zhang, Na Lin, Liangpu Xu, Hailong Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstractβ-Thalassemia is a chronic hemolytic anemia caused by mutations in the β-globin gene. Increasing studies have shown that circular RNA (circRNA) plays a key role in abnormal hematopoiesis. Circ_0001428 is formed by circularization of the beta-mannosidase gene. However, little is known about the biological function of circ_0001428 in β-thalassemia. Circ_0001428 was selected from two circRNA microarrays (GSE241141 and GSE196682) of β-thalassemia, and expression levels of circ_0001428, miR-32-3p, miR-325-3p, γ-globin, activating transcription factor 4 (ATF4) and B-cell lymphoma/leukemia 11 A (BCL11A) were validated in 30 patients with β-thalassemia and 30 healthy controls. Cell proliferation, apoptosis, cell cycle, and erythroid differentiation were evaluated using stable knockdown or overexpression of circ_0001428. Fluorescence in situ hybridization, dual-luciferase reporter, and Western blots were performed to confirm interactions between circ_0001428, miR-32-3p/miR-325-3p, and ATF4. Circ_0001428 was downregulated in β-thalassemia patients compared to controls, promoted cell proliferation, decreased cell apoptosis, and inhibited terminal erythroid differentiation and γ-globin production. Mechanistically, circ_0001428 functions as a sponge for miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p to promote ATF4 expression, thereby activating BCL11A expression. Additionally, rescue experiments confirmed that circ_0001428 regulated erythropoiesis and γ-globin expression via promoting the ATF4-BCL11A axis by binding with miR-32-3p/miR-325-3p. Our findings reveal, for the first time, the important role of circ_0001428 in regulating erythropoiesis and γ-globin expression in β-thalassemia. The circ_0001428/miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p/ATF4/BCL11A/γ-globin signaling pathway may be a potential therapeutic target in β-thalassemia, especially for fetal hemoglobin modulation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00018-026-06165-6.Keywords: Hemoglobinopathy, Fetal hemoglobin, CeRNA network, Ineffective erythropoiesis

Introduction

β-Thalassemia is a chronic hemolytic anemia caused by genetic defects in the synthesis of β-globin peptide chains, leading to abnormal hemoglobin function. It is the most prevalent and widely distributed heterogeneous monogenic disorder globally. Previous studies estimated that approximately 80 to 90 million people worldwide are carriers of the β-thalassemia gene, accounting for roughly 1.5% of the global population [1, 2]. Surprisingly, the latest reports suggest that the prevalence of β-thalassemia carriers has risen to 3% of the global population [3]. Based on the clinical severity of anemia, β-thalassemia can be classified into three categories: mild, intermediate, and severe, and most of the patients with severe β-thalassemia require lifelong blood transfusions and periodic iron chelation therapy to sustain life. However, the β-thalassemia genotype does not necessarily correlate with the disease phenotype [4, 5]. Some patients with genetically determined β-thalassemia major may clinically present as β-thalassemia intermedia. A growing body of research has shown that the remarkable clinical diversity of β-thalassemia is associated with genetic and epigenetic modifiers in addition to the genotypes [6]. Therefore, further investigation into the mechanisms of phenotypic variation in β-thalassemia and the improvement of the quality of life for patients with the severe form of these diseases are critical challenges that current research must address.

Unlike linear RNAs, circular RNAs (circRNAs) have a closed-loop structure. Due to the absence of 3’ end poly (A) tails and 5’ end caps, circRNAs exhibit greater stability than their linear mRNA counterparts [7]. circRNA is highly expressed in human peripheral blood, and various body fluids and exosomes [8, 9]. Notably, circRNA plays a significant role in hematopoiesis and is closely associated with the occurrence and development of hematologic diseases. In normal hematopoiesis, circRNAs play a pivotal role during erythroid differentiation and are linked to red blood cell maturation. Nicolet et al. [10] found that circRNAs are widely expressed in hematopoietic cells and identified several specific circRNAs, including circ-TET2, circ-ANKRD12, circ-MAN1A2, and circ-SPECC1. These circRNAs have been shown to demonstrate stable expression in mature erythrocytes, which provides a theoretical basis for the functional characterization of circRNAs during hematopoiesis. In addition, circRNAs have been demonstrated to play a key role in the pathogenesis of abnormal hematopoiesis, including circ-NPM1, circ-MFN2, and circ-ANAPC7 [11]. A microarray study by Yang et al. [12] on circRNAs in β-thalassemia patients with elevated fetal hemoglobin (HbF) levels identified hsa-circRNA-100466 as repressing SOX6 and enhancing HbF expression through miR-19b-3p. Reactivating γ-globin gene expression to induce HbF is an important therapeutic strategy in β-thalassemia. Beyond endogenous regulatory mechanisms like circRNAs, novel therapeutic approaches are being developed to directly target the γ-globin genes. Most recently, targeted epigenome editing has emerged as a precise and safe strategy to reactivate HbF. For instance, a study demonstrated that using a CRISPR-dCas9 system to deliver demethylase or acetyltransferase effectors to the hypermethylated γ-globin gene promoters in patient-derived hematopoietic stem cells effectively reactivated γ-globin expression, significantly reduced sickling, and showed a favorable safety profile without DNA double-strand breaks [13]. Nevertheless, limited information is available regarding the role of circRNAs in regulating γ-globin, and few data exist concerning their functions in β-thalassemia.

Our previous microarray dataset (GSE241141) analysis of circRNA expression in peripheral blood from patients with severe β-thalassemia revealed that circ_0001428 levels were downregulated and correlated with γ-globin expression. Meanwhile, circRNA microarray data (GSE196682) from other studies also showed that circ_0001428 expression was downregulated in β-thalassemia carriers with high HbF levels. Circ_0001428 is located on chromosome 4:103571693–103611928 (strand -, hg19) and is formed by circularization of the beta-mannosidase (MANBA) gene. However, there are no reports of circ_0001428 in human diseases. In light of the evidence above, this study will, for the first time, elucidate the biological functions of circ_0001428 in β-thalassemia. We found that circ_0001428 regulates erythropoiesis and γ-globin expression in β-thalassemia development.

B-cell lymphoma/leukemia 11 A (BCL11A) is a crucial transcriptional repressor that has been extensively investigated for its role in directly silencing the level of γ-globin expression [14, 15]. Correspondingly, Activating transcription factor 4 (ATF4) is a key transcription factor that directly stimulates the transcription of BCL11A by binding to the specific enhancer of BCL11A and promoting enhancer-promoter interaction, which ultimately results in the robust silencing of γ-globin expression [16, 17]. However, the regulatory mechanisms of circRNA on ATF4 and BCL11A in β-thalassemia have not been reported previously. In our study, mechanistic study further found that circ_0001428 upregulates ATF4 expression by targeting both miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p, which subsequently activates BCL11A expression, leading to the suppression of γ-globin expression. These findings suggest that the circ_0001428/miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p/ATF4/BCL11A/γ-globin signaling pathways could serve as potential therapeutic targets for severe β-thalassemia.

Materials and methods

Ethical approval and participant recruitment

This study received ethical approval from the Ethics Committee of Fujian Province Maternal and Child Health Hospital (No. 2019073). The study enrolled two participant groups (n = 30 per group): healthy controls without hematological disorders or thalassemia-related mutations, and β-thalassemia patients (major/intermediate). Peripheral blood was drawn after written informed consent was obtained from each subject.

Sample processing and analysis

Peripheral blood (5 mL) was preserved using the PAXgene Blood RNA Kit (Qiagen, Germany) for subsequent RNA isolation. Hematological parameters were quantified using the Sysmex XN-3000 automated analyzer (Sysmex, China). Hemoglobin typing was performed via capillary electrophoresis (CapillaryS 2, France), and biochemical indices were assessed using the ARCHITECT C16000 system (Abbott, USA). Key hematological differences between groups, including reduced RBC count, Hb levels, MCH, and HbA (%) alongside elevated HbA2 and HbF (%) in β-thalassemia patients, are detailed in Table S1.

Cell culture and genetic manipulation

K562 cells (human erythroleukemia line) were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco, USA) with 10% FBS (Viva Cell, China). HUDEP-2 cells obtained from the RIKEN BioResource Research Center as part of the National BioResource Project, supported by the Japanese Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science, and Technology (Tsukuba, Japan), were maintained in serum-free StemSpan medium (StemCell Technologies, Canada) containing 50 ng/mL SCF (PeproTech, USA), 3 IU/mL EPO (Amgen, USA), 1 µg/mL doxycycline, and 0.4 µg/mL dexamethasone (Sigma, USA). Erythroid differentiation was induced in K562 cells using 50 µM hemin (Sigma), while HUDEP-2 cells followed a three-phase protocol [18].

To establish cell lines with stable knockdown or overexpression of circ_0001428, lentivirus particles for circ_0001428 overexpression vector (named circ_0001428) and lentivirus particles for shRNA specific targeting the junction sites of circ_0001428 (named sh-circ_0001428#1, sh-circ_0001428#2, and sh-circ_0001428#3) and the corresponding negative control (named vector or scramble) were constructed from Genechem (Shanghai, China). K562 and HUDEP-2 cells were infected with a mixture of lentiviruses (multiplicity of infection = 30) for up to 48 h. Starting 72 h after infection, the cells were screened with 1 µg/ml puromycin for 7 days. The stable infected cells were used for further analysis. The shRNA with the best efficiency was selected for the follow-up study.

Synthetic RNA oligonucleotides (miR-32-5p mimic, miR-325-5p mimic, and NC mimic) were procured from GenePharma (Shanghai, China). For transfection, the Cell Line Nucleofector Kit (Lonza, Switzerland) was employed following the manufacturer’s instructions at an oligonucleotide concentration of 100 nM. Cells were then incubated for 48 h before subsequent experimental procedures. The sequences of lentiviruses and RNA oligonucleotides were presented in Table S2.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

Peripheral blood-derived RNA was extracted with the PAXgene Blood RNA System (Qiagen), and cellular RNA was obtained using the Total RNA Extraction Kit (Promega, China). cDNA synthesis was conducted separately: mRNA and circRNA were reverse transcribed using the PrimeScript RT-PCR kit (Takara, Japan), while miRNA was converted using the Mir-X miRNA First-Strand Synthesis kit (Takara), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The cDNA was subjected to RT-qPCR analysis using the TB Green Premix Ex Taq II (Takara) for mRNA and circRNA, and the TB Green Advantage qPCR Premix kit (Takara) for miRNA. GAPDH and U6 were used as the internal controls. The primer sequences were listed in Table S3. Fold changes were analyzed by the ­2 method.

Nucleic acid electrophoresis and sanger sequencing

Genomic DNA was isolated from HUDEP-2 cells using the TIANamp Genomic DNA Kit (TIANGEN, China). For PCR amplification, we utilized the EmeraldAmp® Max PCR Master Mix (Takara); the reaction was done in a 2720 thermal cycler. For DNA electrophoresis, the PCR products were separated on a 1.5% agarose gel (Applichem, Germany) in 1× TBE buffer. A 500 bp DNA Ladder (Takara) was used to detect fragment sizes. To identify the back-splice junction sequences of circ_0001428, we performed Sanger sequencing of PCR products on an ABI 3730XL DNA analyzer (Applied Biosystems) and analyzed the sequences using PC ChromasPro software (v2.1.6) (Applied Biosystems).

Ribonuclease R (RNase R) and actinomycin D (Act D) treatment

The RNase R reagent (Epicenter Biotechnologies, USA) was used to digest linear RNAs. Due to their circular structure, circRNAs can evade RNase degradation. Total RNA isolated from K562 and HUDEP-2 cells was aliquoted into two equal portions (2 µg each). One underwent digestion with RNase R (3 U/µg) at 37℃ for 20 min, while the control aliquot received an equivalent volume of RNase-free water under identical conditions. Following treatment, RNA samples were reverse-transcribed, and MANBA mRNA and circ_0001428 were quantified by RT-qPCR using the methodology mentioned above. For Act D treatment, K562 and HUDEP-2 cells were exposed to Act D (10 µg/mL, Sigma) for varying durations (0, 4, 8, 12, or 24 h). Following treatment, total RNA was isolated and subjected to RT-qPCR analysis to quantify circ_0001428 and MANBA mRNA expression levels.

Cellular nucleo-cytoplasmic fractionation

Cells were fractionated using NE-PER Nuclear and Cytoplasmic Extraction Reagents (ThermoFisher, USA) in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol. K562 and HUDEP-2 cells were suspended in Buffer C supplemented with protease inhibitor cocktail (Sigma) and RNase inhibitor (1 U/µL, ThermoFisher). Following centrifugation, supernatants were collected, while the pelleted nuclei underwent extensive PBS washing. The purified nuclei were then resuspended in Buffer N containing both protease and RNase inhibitors, followed by sonication. RNA isolation was subsequently performed as described above. The RNA products were subjected to RT-qPCR analysis to detect circ_0001428, GAPDH, and U6. GAPDH and U6 were used as positive controls for the cytoplasm and the nucleus, respectively.

Western blots

Protein quantification was performed using the BCA assay kit (Beyotime Biotechnology, China). Proteins (20 µg) were resolved by SDS-PAGE and electrotransferred onto PVDF membranes (Merck, Germany). After blocking with Protein Free Rapid Blocking Buffer (piZyme, China), the membranes were probed with primary antibodies (4℃, 12 h) followed by species-matched HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (2 h). Signal detection was achieved using the Efficient chemiluminescence kit (Genview, China). The following antibodies from Abcam (USA) were used: anti-ATF4 (ab270980, 1:1000), anti-BCL11A (ab19487, 1:1000), anti-γ-globin (ab156584, 1:1000), anti-GAPDH (ab8245, 1:2000), and either goat anti-rabbit IgG (ab205718, 1:5000) or goat anti-mouse IgG (ab205719, 1:5000).

CircRNA-mediated ceRNA network construction and enrichment analysis

Probable interactions between circRNAs and miRNAs were examined using Arraystar’s home-made miRNA target prediction software (Arraystar, Shanghai, China). The miRWalk (http://mirwalk.umm.uni-heidelberg.de/), TargetScan (http://www.targetscan.org/), and miRDB (http://www.mirdb.org/) were used for the prediction of mRNAs targeted by miRNAs. The circRNA-associated ceRNA network was reconstructed using Cytoscape (v3.8.2) by integrating predicted circRNA-miRNA interactions with validated miRNA-mRNA pairs. Hub nodes were identified through topological analysis using the CytoHubba plugin’s Maximal Clique Centrality algorithm.

Comprehensive functional annotation was performed using Gene Ontology (GO) analysis (biological processes, cellular components, and molecular functions) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis. The analyses were conducted using R software (v4.3.1) with the org.Hs.eg.db database package, focusing on predicted target genes of miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p. Results were filtered at P < 0.05 and q < 0.05 thresholds, with the top 8–10 significantly enriched terms visualized using bar and bubble plots.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

The fragments containing either wild-type (WT) miR-32-3p/miR-325-3p binding sites from circ_0001428 or the ATF4 3’-untranslated regions (3’-UTR) were amplified from human cDNA and cloned into the pmiR-RB-REPORT vector (RiboBio, China) using XhoI/NotI restriction sites. Site-directed mutagenesis was performed to generate mutant (MUT) constructs lacking functional miRNA binding sites. K562 cells were co-transfected with either WT or MUT reporter plasmids alongside miRNA mimics (miR-32-3p, miR-325-3p) or negative control (NC). Luciferase activity was measured 48 h post-transfection using a dual-reporter system, with results normalized to Firefly luciferase activity.

Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)

FISH was performed in K562 cells using specific probes for miR-32-5p, miR-325-5p, and ATF4. Cells were first fixed in 4% formaldehyde and then incubated in 0.5% Triton at room temperature. The FAM-labeled miR-32-3p or miR-325-3p probe and Cy3-labeled circ_0001428 or ATF4 probe were hybridized with the cells at 37℃ overnight. Nuclei were subsequently counterstained with DAPI (24 h post-hybridization). All experimental procedures were performed according to the manufacturer’s protocol for the FISH kit (GenePharma). Cellular imaging was performed using a Leica TCS SP8 confocal microscopy system, with probe sequences documented in Table S2.

Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay

K562 and HUDEP-2 cells were plated in 96-well plates at a density of 1 × 103 cells per well. Cellular viability was evaluated at 24-hour intervals (0–96 h) using the CCK-8 assay (ApexBio, USA). Briefly, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well, followed by a 2-hour incubation at 37℃. Absorbance measurements at 450 nm were subsequently obtained using a microplate reader (ThermoFisher).

Flow cytometry assay

For apoptosis assessment, 5 × 105 cells were resuspended in 500 µL binding buffer and stained with Annexin V-APC/7-AAD (BD Biosciences, USA) for 15 min. Cell cycle analysis involved ethanol fixation (75%) followed by propidium iodide (PI, BD Biosciences, USA) staining (5 µL, 30 min). Apoptosis was calculated as the sum of early apoptotic cells (Annexin V+/7-AAD−) and late apoptotic cells (Annexin V+/7-AAD+). To assess the cell cycle, cells fixed in 75% ethanol were stained with 5 µL of PI and analyzed by flow cytometry. The distribution of cells across G0/G1, S, and G2/M phases was quantified with Modfit software (Verity Software House, USA).

Erythroid differentiation was evaluated using CD71 (APC; clone: M-A712), CD235a (PE; clone: JC159), CD36 (APC; clone: CB38) and CD49d (PE; clone: 9F10) antibodies from BD Biosciences, and CD233 (APC; clone: BRIC 71) from International Blood Group Reference Laboratory (UK) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. For F-cell detection, cells were fixed with 4% formaldehyde (WeiAo, China) for 10 min, permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 for 5 min, and stained with a PE-conjugated HbF antibody (BD Biosciences) for 30 min. All samples were analyzed using a BD LSRFortessa X-20 flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, USA).

Benzidine staining

For benzidine staining, K562 cell suspension was prepared and mixed with a freshly prepared benzidine-H2O2 solution containing 50 µL of 3% H2O2 (Sigma) per mL of 0.4% benzidine (Merck) in 1% 0.5 M glacial acetic acid (Merck). Following a 5-minute treatment at room temperature, the cells were examined under an Olympus light microscope. Benzidine-positive cells exhibiting characteristic blue-black cytoplasmic precipitation were enumerated, with results expressed as the percentage of positive cells relative to a minimum count of 100 total cells.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were performed in triplicate, and data analysis was conducted using GraphPad Prism 10.0 (La Jolla, CA, USA). Normality testing was performed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Parametric data (mean ± SD) were analyzed by unpaired two-tailed t-tests (2 groups) or one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post-hoc test (≥ 3 groups). Correlation analyses employed either Pearson’s or Spearman’s methods as appropriate. Diagnostic performance was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, with Youden’s index determining optimal cutoff values. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

Results

Identification of downregulated circ_0001428 in patients with β-thalassemia

Firstly, we simultaneously analyzed circ_0001428 expression in the GSE241141 and GSE196682 datasets and found that circ_0001428 levels were lower in patients with β-thalassemia than in healthy controls (Fig. 1A). To further confirm circ_0001428 expression, we collected peripheral blood samples from 30 patients with intermediate and severe β-thalassemia and 30 healthy controls. RT-qPCR subsequently showed that circ_0001428 expression was significantly downregulated in β-thalassemia (Fig. 1B). As presented in Fig. 1C, circ_0001428 was formed by back-splicing of exons 6 to 13 of MANBA mRNA (NM_005908), and the back-splicing sequences of circ_0001428 were validated by Sanger sequencing. PCR-electrophoretic analysis showed that circ_0001428 could be detected only in cDNA from HUDEP-2 cells, not in gDNA.

For stability analysis of circ_0001428, RNase R digestion assays demonstrated differential stability of transcripts, with circ_0001428 showing complete resistance to exonuclease activity while MANBA mRNA levels were significantly degraded (Fig. 1D). Transcriptional inhibition experiments with Act D revealed similar stability patterns, in which circ_0001428 maintained constant expression levels despite complete suppression of linear MANBA mRNA (Fig. 1E). These findings indicated that circ_0001428 had an actual circular structure and exhibited marked stability.

Then, we analyzed the clinical significance of circ_0001428 expression in β-thalassemia. First, correlation analysis demonstrated that circ_0001428 expression was significantly negatively correlated with MCV (r = −0.528) and MCH (r = −0.587) (Fig. 1F), but showed no significant correlation with other parameters, including RBC, Hb, HbA, HbA2, and HbF (Table S4). Subsequently, we evaluated its diagnostic potential. ROC curve analysis revealed that the area under the curve (AUC) for circ_0001428 alone was 0.827 (95% CI: 0.724–0.929), with sensitivities and specificities of 0.700 and 0.833, respectively (Fig. 1G). To further explore its diagnostic potential, we evaluated the efficacy of combining circ_0001428 with established red blood cell indices. Notably, the combination of circ_0001428 with MCH, MCV + MCH, or HbA2 yielded markedly higher AUCs of 0.944 (95% CI: 0.890–0.999), 0.948 (95% CI: 0.896–0.999), and 0.872 (95% CI: 0.780–0.965), respectively. These combined models demonstrated a significant improvement in diagnostic performance compared to circ_0001428 alone or to the traditional MCV + MCH index (AUC = 0.865, 95% CI: 0.773–0.958). The collective evidence suggests that circ_0001428 is downregulated in patients with β-thalassemia and may serve as a potential biomarker for β-thalassemia, particularly when used in combination with conventional hematological parameters.

Construction of circ_0001428-associated ceRNA network

Subcellular fractionation analysis revealed predominant cytoplasmic localization of circ_0001428 in both K562 and HUDEP-2 cell lines (Fig. 1H). The observed distribution suggests this circRNA may participate in post-transcriptional regulation through miRNA binding. Then, we established a circ_0001428-associated ceRNA network using bioinformatics analysis. The ceRNA network identified five miRNA nodes and 154 mRNA (Fig. 1I). Subsequently, the hub genes and miRNAs were further analyzed using the “Cytohubba” plugin. This resulted in the final identification of five hub miRNAs, including miR-145-5p, miR-767-3p, miR-138-5p, miR-32-3p, and miR-325-3p. Additionally, four hub genes were identified (KIAA0930, ZBTB20, CNOT6L, and ATF4) (Fig. 1J). To gain further insight into the biological functions in which circ_0001428 may be involved, we analyzed the circ_0001428/miRNA/mRNA network using the GO and KEGG databases. The top 8 significantly enriched GO terms in the BP, CC, and MF were shown in Fig. 1K-L. Besides, the top 10 significantly enriched pathways from the KEGG enrichment analysis were shown in Fig. 1M. Among these pathways, “TGF-beta signaling pathway”, “MAPK signaling pathway”, “Oxytocin signaling pathway”, and “Cell cycle” were closely related to the pathophysiological mechanism of β-thalassemia. Therefore, it is hypothesized that circ_0001428 may be associated with cell development in β-thalassemia.

Circ_0001428 inhibits γ-globin production and terminal erythroid differentiation of erythroid cells

To test this hypothesis and evaluate the role of circ_0001428 in β-thalassemia in vitro, we stably interfered with circ_0001428 expression using three shRNAs in K562 and HUDEP-2 cells and confirmed that circ_0001428 expression showed the best knockdown efficiency with sh-circ_0001428#1 (Fig. S1A). Also, we used a lentiviral expression system to stably increase its expression in K562 and HUDEP-2 cells (Fig. S1B).

In β-thalassemia patients, γ-globin expression was significantly upregulated (Fig. 2A). Correlation analysis demonstrated that circ_0001428 expression was negatively correlated with γ-globin mRNA levels (r = −0.667) (Fig. 2B). To explore the clinical relevance of circ_0001428 further, we stratified the β-thalassemia patients into two subgroups based on the median HbF level. This analysis revealed a more pronounced downregulation of circ_0001428 expression in the patient subgroup with higher HbF levels compared to those with lower HbF levels (Fig. 2C). Subsequently, we examined the effects of circ_0001428 knockdown and overexpression on γ-globin expression at both mRNA and protein levels in K562 and HUDEP-2 cells. RT-qPCR analysis showed that, in the sh-circ_0001428 group, γ-globin mRNA was upregulated. Conversely, overexpression of circ_0001428 decreased γ-globin mRNA levels compared with the vector group. Similar results were observed at the protein level (Fig. 2D-E). It is noteworthy that the more pronounced reduction in γ-globin protein, compared to its mRNA, upon circ_0001428 overexpression is consistent with the longer half-life of the protein and the cumulative effect of sustained transcriptional repression. HbF is mainly composed of α2γ2 and accounts for 60–80% of the total hemoglobin of the newborn during the fetal period. Additionally, we investigated the effect of circ_0001428 on HbF production in HUDEP-2 cells during the erythroid differentiation process at day 14. The results showed that silencing circ_0001428 in HUDEP-2 cells increased the proportion of HbF-positive cells (Fig. 2F). In contrast, in the circ_0001428 overexpression group, the proportion of HbF-positive cells decreased (Fig. 2G). These findings indicate that circ_0001428 inhibits γ-globin production of erythroid cells.

Furthermore, hemin-induced K562 cell model was established to study erythroid differentiation. The results showed that, compared with the control group, knockdown of circ_0001428 increased the percentage of CD71+/CD235a+ cells; overexpression of circ_0001428 decreased the proportion of CD71+/CD235a+ cells (Fig. 2H). Benzidine staining, used to identify hemoglobin-containing cells as an indicator of erythroid differentiation, demonstrated that after 96 h of hemin treatment, silencing circ_0001428 increased the proportion of benzidine-positive cells in K562 cells. Meanwhile, overexpression of circ_0001428 decreased the proportion of benzidine-positive K562 cells (Fig. 2I), suggesting that circ_0001428 impairs erythroid maturation.

Additionally, to gain deeper insight into the role of circ_0001428 during terminal erythroid differentiation, we analyzed the mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) of a panel of surface markers in HUDEP-2 cells at day 14 of erythroid differentiation by flow cytometry, including early/mid-stage markers (CD49d and CD36), and the late-stage markers (CD235a and CD233). Interestingly, circ_0001428 knockdown decreased the MFI of CD49d and CD36, while concurrently increasing the MFI of CD235a and CD233. Conversely, overexpression of circ_0001428 produced the opposite effects (Fig. 2J-L). Collectively, these data unequivocally demonstrate that circ_0001428 functions as a negative regulator, impeding terminal erythroid differentiation.

Circ_0001428 promotes proliferation and suppresses apoptosis of erythroid cells

We then further explored the effect of circ_0001428 on cell proliferation and apoptosis in β-thalassemia. CCK-8 assay indicated that inhibition of circ_0001428 reduced cell proliferation, while overexpression of circ_0001428 significantly enhanced cell proliferation in both K562 and HUDEP-2 cells (Fig. 3A-B). Flow cytometry analysis showed that inhibition of circ_0001428 promoted apoptosis in both cells (Fig. 3C-D), whereas circ_0001428 overexpression inhibited apoptosis (Fig. 3E-F). However, flow cytometry revealed that neither knockdown nor overexpression of circ_0001428 caused significant changes in the proportions of cells in the G0/G1, S, or G2/M phases (Fig. S2). The results demonstrated that circ_0001428 may not impact the cell cycle progression of erythroid cells. Thus, circ_0001428 regulates erythropoiesis by inhibiting terminal erythroid differentiation, promoting cell proliferation, and reducing apoptosis.

Circ_0001428 acts as a molecular sponge for miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p

As mentioned above, five hub miRNAs in the circ_0001428-associated ceRNA network were identified and were also detected in β-thalassemia patients compared to healthy controls. Notably, we observed that miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p were significantly upregulated in patients with β-thalassemia (Fig. 4A). Spearman correlation analysis showed a negative correlation between the expression of circ_0001428 and miR-32-3p (r = −0.730) and miR-325-3p (r = −0.656) (Fig. 4B). In the K562 and HUDEP-2 cells, the introduction of miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p mimics significantly upregulated their expression (Fig. S1C-D). Fig. S3 illustrates that circ_0001428 contains complementary sequences for miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p. Dual-luciferase reporter assays in K562 cells showed that overexpression of miR-32-3p or miR-325-3p in the circ_0001428 WT vector group resulted in a significant reduction in relative luciferase activity compared to the NC mimic group, while no statistical difference was observed in the circ_0001428 MUT vector group (Fig. 4C). FISH demonstrated co-localization of circ_0001428 with miR-32-3p or miR-325-3p in the cytoplasm of K562 cells (Fig. 4D). We further examined the regulatory effects of circ_0001428 on the expression levels of miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p within the K562 and HUDEP-2 cells. The findings demonstrated that circ_0001428 knockdown led to an upregulation of miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p expression, whereas elevated circ_0001428 expression was associated with a suppression of these miRNAs (Fig. 4E-F). These results confirm a potential targeting relationship between circ_0001428 and miR-32-3p, as well as miR-325-3p.

ATF4 is a downstream target of miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p

To identify potential target genes of miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p that might influence γ-globin levels, we used the bioinformatics tools TargetScan and miRDB to predict mRNAs with potential miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p binding sites, common 11 target genes were identified, among which ATF4 is also one of the four hub mRNAs in our study (Fig. 4G). Thus, we selected ATF4 among the candidate genes because it is not only closely linked to the pathogenesis of β-thalassemia but also downregulated in the β-thalassemia patients (Fig. 4H). Interestingly, the levels of miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p in β-thalassemia show a negative correlation with ATF4 expression (Fig. 4I). The miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p binding sequences for the ATF4 3’-UTR were shown in Fig. S3. In K562 cells, dual-luciferase reporter assays demonstrated that overexpression of miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p mimics significantly reduced the luciferase activity of the WT reporter, whereas no such reduction was observed in the MUT control group (Fig. 4J). Furthermore, FISH analysis revealed co-localization of ATF4 with miR-32-3p or miR-325-3p in the cytoplasm of K562 cells (Fig. 4K). Moreover, overexpression of miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p singly in K562 and HUDEP-2 cells significantly inhibited ATF4 expression at the mRNA and protein levels, but did not affect the expression of circ_0001428 (Fig. 4L-M), further confirming that miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p directly inhibit ATF4 expression.

Circ_0001428 represses γ-globin expression through promoting the ATF4-BCL11A axis by targeting miR-32-3p/325-3p

ATF4 has been shown to interact with the BCL11A + 55 enhancer, enhancing enhancer-promoter interactions and promoting BCL11A transcription [16, 17]. Our results demonstrated that the expression of BCL11A is significantly inhibited by the overexpression of miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p alone in K562 and HUDEP-2 cells, thereby promoting the level of γ-globin (Fig. 5A-D). These findings were further corroborated by the observation that the promotion of γ-globin expression by miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p was contingent upon the inhibition of ATF4-BCL11A expression.

Correlation analysis revealed that ATF4 and BCL11A expression were positively correlated with circ_0001428 levels in β-thalassemia (Fig. 5E). We further examined the effects of circ_0001428 overexpression and knockdown on ATF4 and BCL11A expression at both mRNA and protein levels in K562 and HUDEP-2 cells. RT-qPCR analysis showed that, in the sh-circ_0001428 group, ATF4 and BCL11A mRNA were downregulated (Fig. 5F). Conversely, compared to the vector group, overexpression of circ_0001428 resulted in the upregulation of ATF4 and BCL11A mRNA levels (Fig. 5G). Consistent with mRNA expression, ATF4 and BCL11A protein levels showed parallel changes following both circ_0001428 overexpression and knockdown (Fig. 5H-I).

To further ascertain whether the regulatory mechanism of circ_0001428 on γ-globin expression by interacting with miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p, rescue experiments were performed in HUDEP-2 cells by cotransfection of the circ_0001428 vector and miR-32-3p or miR-325-3p mimic. We found that increased ATF4 and BCL11A expression, and decreased γ-globin expression upon circ_0001428 overexpression, were blocked by the introduction of miR-32-3p or miR-325-3p (Fig. 5J). These findings suggest that circ_0001428 interacts with miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p to upregulate the expression of the downstream gene ATF4, which in turn activates BCL11A, leading to the downregulation of γ-globin expression.

Circ_0001428 regulates erythropoiesis by targeting miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p

Finally, to investigate whether circ_0001428 exerts its effects on erythropoiesis through miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p, we conducted a series of rescue experiments by co-overexpressing circ_0001428, miR-32-3p, and miR-325-3p, as appropriate. The miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p mimics, singly or in combination, effectively reversed the increase in cell proliferation induced by circ_0001428 overexpression (Fig. 6A). Furthermore, miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p mimics attenuated the suppression of cell apoptosis caused by circ_0001428 overexpression (Fig. 6B-C). More importantly, these mimics reversed the inhibitory effects of circ_0001428 overexpression on erythroid terminal differentiation (Fig. 6D-E). Therefore, we concluded that circ_0001428 regulated erythropoiesis and γ-globin expression via promoting the ATF4-BCL11A axis by binding with miR-32-3p/miR-325-3p. Discussion

β-thalassemia is one of the most common monogenic diseases globally, with a wide distribution across tropical regions from sub-Saharan Africa to the Mediterranean, the Middle East, the Indian subcontinent, and Southeast Asia, posing a significant public health issue worldwide [1]. Studies have suggested that broad cures for β-thalassemia and sickle cell anemia could be achieved by leveraging the natural physiological process of hemoglobin switching [19]. This process involves changes in hemoglobin expression during embryonic, fetal, and neonatal stages. Recently, increasing attention has been given to the roles of circRNAs in the pathogenesis of blood diseases, including β-thalassemia. Our study confirmed significant downregulation of circ_0001428 in β-thalassemia, establishing its role as a key regulator of erythropoiesis by inhibiting terminal erythroid differentiation, promoting cell proliferation, and reducing apoptosis. Moreover, we demonstrated that circ_0001428 increases ATF4 expression by sequestering miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p, thereby upregulating BCL11A to inhibit γ-globin expression. These findings suggested the circ_0001428 may represent a promising therapeutic target for β-thalassemia intervention. In the β-thalassemia cohort, circ_0001428 expression exhibited a significant negative correlation with γ-globin levels. Therefore, targeted suppression of circ_0001428 holds promise for elevating HbF production to clinically beneficial levels, thereby effectively mimicking the protective high HbF phenotype observed in hereditary persistence of HbF.

With advancements in circRNA microarrays and high-throughput sequencing, circRNA research techniques have matured. CircRNA-based approaches for disease diagnosis and therapy represent a promising frontier in translational medicine, with significant potential for clinical implementation [20]. In our previous research, we found that circ_0008102 expression is upregulated in children with β-thalassemia and is associated with the pathogenesis of the disease. Moreover, we were the first to demonstrate that circ_0008102 could serve as an effective biomarker for diagnosing β-thalassemia [21]. Here, we found that circ_0001428 expression negatively correlates with MCV and MCH, it has the potential to serve as a biomarker for β-thalassemia, particularly in conjunction with hematological parameters. Regarding the biological function of circ_0001428, our study demonstrated that it promotes cell proliferation and inhibits apoptosis. Although the cell cycle is a contributing factor to ineffective erythropoiesis [22, 23], we did not find that circ_0001428 directly regulates it, suggesting its impact on proliferation may be mediated through apoptosis or other pathways.

There is evidence suggesting that early stages of erythroid differentiation are accelerated in β-thalassemia as a compensatory response to chronic anemia [24, 25]. while a blockage in terminal maturation exacerbates the condition [26]. Our study demonstrated that circ_0001428 inhibits terminal erythroid differentiation in K562 and HUDEP-2 cells. This, combined with its pro-proliferation and anti-apoptosis effects, outlines a multifaceted role for circ_0001428 in regulating erythropoiesis. Our study confirmed that circ_0001428, located mainly in the cytoplasm, functions as a miRNA sponge for miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p. These miRNAs were identified as functional targets through a combination of bioinformatics analysis, FISH, and luciferase reporter assays. In rescue experiments, miR-32-3p or miR-325-3p mimic reversed the effect of circ_0001428 overexpression on erythropoiesis and γ-globin expression. However, the effects of the combined group of miR-32-3p mimic and miR-325-3p were not significantly superior to those alone in regulating erythropoiesis, which may be attributable to the following reasons: (1) when two miRNAs are introduced together, they may compete for binding to the same target sites on the mRNA, thus reducing the overall efficiency of mRNA degradation or translational repression [27]; (2) the simultaneous introduction of two miRNAs could potentially lead to saturation of cellular mechanisms responsible for processing and regulating miRNAs, such as the RNA-induced silencing complex [28].

ATF4 is a key regulator of stress hematopoiesis, yet its precise mechanism for repressing γ-globin expression remains unclear. While some studies have proposed a model of direct transcriptional activation of BCL11A by ATF4, supported by specific ChIP-seq assays [16, 17], other rigorous work has established an indirect pathway wherein ATF4 binds the HBS1L-MYB intergenic enhancer to regulate MYB, which in turn governs BCL11A expression [29]. Regardless of the underlying mechanism, the role of the ATF4-BCL11A axis in regulating γ-globin expression is undeniable. Our findings showed that circ_0001428 inhibits γ-globin expression through the ATF4-BCL11A axis by sequestering miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p. Although we did not perform rescue experiments for circ_0001428 based on the ATF4-BCL11A axis, we proposed that this axis represents the core mechanism underpinning the broad effects of circ_0001428 on cell proliferation, apoptosis, terminal erythroid differentiation, and γ-globin expression. This can be attributed to both the improvement in cellular homeostasis resulting from γ-globin reactivation and the pleiotropic regulatory capacity of ATF4 itself, which directly controls networks of genes involved in stress response and cell survival.

While our study delineates the downstream pathway by which circ_0001428 regulates γ-globin, the upstream mechanism responsible for its initial downregulation in β-thalassemia remains an open question. We propose several non-mutually exclusive hypotheses: the aberrant splicing landscape in β-thalassemia may disrupt its biogenesis [30]; the profound cellular stress could suppress its expression via the integrated stress response [31]; or post-transcriptional regulation such as N6-methyladenosine modification might influence its stability and circularization [32, 33]. Finally, epigenetic modifications at the MANBA gene locus represent another plausible layer. Investigating these mechanisms will be crucial to fully understand the circ_0001428 regulatory network.

It should be noted that this study has some limitations that also outline clear directions for future research. First, while we have expanded the clinical cohort, further validation in larger, prospective studies that concurrently collect samples for both transcriptomic and proteomic analysis will be essential to fully establish the clinical utility of circ_0001428 and to confirm the ATF4/BCL11A axis at the protein level in patients. Second, the mechanistic insights, primarily derived from cell line models, would be strengthened in the future by utilizing primary patient-derived CD34 + cells and implementing unbiased transcriptomic approaches (e.g., RNA-seq) to comprehensively delineate the circ_0001428 regulatory network and rule out potential confounding pathways. Finally, the upstream causes of circ_0001428 downregulation and its functional consequences in vivo remain to be explored. The human-specific nature of circ_0001428 presents a particular challenge for in vivo modeling, requiring the future development of complex humanized β-thalassemia models. Addressing these points will be the focus of our subsequent investigations.

In summary, our research demonstrated that circ_0001428 is downregulated in β-thalassemia patients and functions as a key regulator of erythropoiesis by targeting miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p. Moreover, circ_0001428 inhibits γ-globin expression by promoting the ATF4-BCL11A axis by interacting with miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p. Thus, targeting the circ_0001428/miR-32-3p and miR-325-3p/ATF4/BCL11A/γ-globin signaling pathways could induce γ-globin expression and reactivate HbF production. We believe that in-depth research into the molecular mechanisms of circRNA will provide new avenues for the diagnosis and treatment of β-thalassemia.

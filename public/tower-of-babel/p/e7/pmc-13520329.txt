Comparison of the efficacy of the oXiris filter and the ST-100 filter in patients with postoperative cardiac surgery infections combined with acute kidney injury: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Rui Wang, Yucheng Ren, Yunlin Song, Ying Li, Bingyu Ma, Yaowei Tong, Wangtao Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveComparison of the efficacy of the oXiris filter and the ST-100 filter in patients with postoperative cardiac surgery infections combined with acute kidney injury.MethodsThis is a retrospective cohort study of patients with postoperative cardiac surgical infections combined with acute kidney injury who underwent continuous renal replacement therapy from January 2023 to April 2024 in the Critical Care Medicine Centre of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University. Based on the application of different filters, they were divided into the oXiris group and the ST-100 group, and the general condition, clinical data and related indexes of the two groups were collected at baseline (T0), 10 h after treatment (T1), 24 h after treatment (T2) and 48 h after treatment (T3), respectively, to compare the treatment effect, length of hospital stay and outcome events of the two groups at T1, T2 and T3, to explore the different therapeutic values of oXiris filter and ST-100 filter.ResultsA total of 33 patients were included, 11 (n = 11) in the oXiris group and 22 (n = 22) in the ST group.Intra-group comparisons between the two groups: of norepinephrine dose (FIntra-group=6.799, P < 0.05), VIS (FIntra-group=5.919, P < 0.05) and MAP (FIntra-group=6.387, P < 0.05), with a time trend.IL-6 (FIntra-group=3.262, P < 0.05) had a time-varying trend with a significant decrease.Comparison between the two groups: there was a significant difference in the magnitude of change in MAP (FIntergroup=8.347, P < 0.05) and IL-6 (FIntergroup= 6.779, P < 0.05).The magnitude of change in IL-6 was seen to be significantly better in the oXiris group than in the ST-100 group at the T0-1 time period (P = 0.003).Plotting the survival curve the overall survival rate was higher in the oXiris group than in the ST-100 group, and the Log-Rank test did not show a significant difference between the two groups (P = 0.311).ConclusionThis study indicates the oXiris filter effectively removes inflammatory mediators in patients with postoperative infections and acute kidney injury(characteristically manifested in the first 10 h), compared to the ST-100 filter. It also aids hemodynamic stability and reduces vasopressor doses, but shows no statistical improvement in organ function or patient prognosis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13019-026-04329-w.Keywords: Renal dialysis, Cardiac surgical procedures, Interleukins, Acute kidney injury, Treatment outcome

Introduction

Due to the invasive nature of cardiac surgery, there is a high risk of infection in the postoperative period, and it has been reported [1] that the incidence of infection in postoperative cardiac surgery patients ranges from 3.5% to 26.8%.In a study by Ren J et al. [2] on the clinical characteristics and risk factors of multidrug-resistant bacterial infections after cardiac surgery in adults, it was found that the incidence of nosocomial infections and multidrug-resistant bacterial infections after cardiac surgery was 20.1% and 8.6%, and that more than half of the patients had multiple bacterial co-infections.These infections may be further involved in the patient’s inflammatory response.

In addition to postoperative infections, cardiac surgery-related acute kidney injury (AKI), which is characterised by deterioration of renal function in the hours or days following surgery, and whose symptoms include oliguria, anuria, simultaneous elevation of urea nitrogen and creatinine concentrations, as well as fluid imbalances and electrolyte disturbances, also needs the utmost attention from clinicians [3]. Severe AKI after cardiac surgery independently increases the risk of subsequent infection, with the occurrence of AKI increasing the rate of infection to 23.7% in postoperative patients and 58.5% in cases requiring dialysis [4].

With the popularity of extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO), the use of ECMO after cardiac surgery plays an important role in clinical practice [5]. A study by Lorusso R et al. [6] indicated that the use of ECMO after cardiac surgery was most often associated with coronary artery bypass grafting, valve surgery, and a combination of valve or coronary artery surgery. While the incidence of AKI during ECMO support ranges from 26% to 85%, risk factors for AKI include older age, post-cardiac surgery shock, LVEF, and elevated bilirubin [7]. In recent years, there has been a further increase in the use of CRRT in patients with concomitant AKI on ECMO, and combined CRRT can remove inflammatory factors and optimise volume management while maintaining organ function.

CRRT is considered to be a potentially effective therapy to improve the survival of patients with septic shock complicating AKI [8]. Both oXiris filters and ST-100 filters are currently used in CRRT therapy. oXiris filters combine renal support and adsorption of inflammatory mediators compared to ST-100 filters. kits have a triple-layer structure with enhanced AN69 membranes treated with polyethyleneimine surface treatment and heparin coating [9]. Polyethyleneimine is a cationic complex with a multilayered linear structure that enhances endotoxin adsorption and cytokine removal by the AN69 membrane, while the negative charge retained in the bottom layer adsorbs inflammatory cytokines. oXiris haemofilters are effective in limiting inflammatory mediators and adsorbing endotoxins compared to other blood filters [10]. However, because the oXiris blood filter is more expensive than the ST-100 filter, it is not the preferred option for the patient’s family in the clinic, and the cases involved in this study were all selected for the patient’s family to make the choice of which filter to use after informing the patient’s family of the difference between the two types of filters and the cost before the CRRT treatment was performed.

After the oXiris was allowed to be used in the clinic, several randomised controlled clinical trials and retrospective case-control studies have been conducted to evaluate the efficacy of this filter in comparison with the conventional ST-100 filter, but they have not yet been definitive.Therefore, this study aims to compare the changes in hemodynamics, organ function, serum inflammatory mediators, and other relevant indexes in patients after the use of oXiris filter and ST-100 filter in patients with postoperative cardiac surgical infections combined with acute kidney injury, and to provide a reference for the subsequent relevant clinical applications.

Materials and methods

Research population

Patients with postoperative cardiac surgical infections combined with acute kidney injury who underwent continuous renal replacement therapy at the Centre for Critical Care Medicine of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University from January 2023 to April 2024 were collected, and 33 patients (n = 33) were finally enrolled, of which 11 (n = 11) were in the oXiris group and 22 (n = 22) in the ST-100 group (Fig. 1). The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Xinjiang Medical University (Ethics No. K202405-33).

Flowchart of the study

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: ①age≥18years; ②post cardiac surgery; ③meeting the 2012 Kidney Disease Institute for Global Improvement of Outcomes (KDIGO) diagnostic criteria for AKI; ④CRRT treatment with oXiris or ST-100 filters.

Exclusion criteria: ①Incomplete patient or case information; ②Known or suspected pregnant women; ③Death within 48 h after CRRT; ④More than one case of filter treatment.

CRRT

All of them used continuous veno-venous hemodiafiltration (CVVHDF) post-dilution mode, using Prismaflex model, oXiris filter, ST-100 filter (all used only once), in which blood flow rate (blood flow rate, BFR) 100 ~ 200 ml/min, ultrafiltration rate (UFR): 1000 ml/(kg-h).Anticoagulant drugs were sodium citrate combined with the original basic anticoagulant drugs (enoxaparin sodium, bivalirudin, ordinary heparin or no anticoagulation).

Observation indicators

General information

Sex, age, body mass index (BMI), type of underlying disease, mode of treatment and choice of placement route, sequential orgen failure assessment (SOFA) score and acute physiology and chronic health evaluation II (APACHE II). chronic health evaluation II (APACHE II).

Clinical information

Relevant pathogenic bacteria culture results, antimicrobial drug application, whether extracorporeal circulation is used in surgery, with or without extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) and intra-aortic balloon pump (IABP) support, dose of vasoactive drug and blood flow Kinetics, organ function assessment, renal function assessment, haemoglobin and platelet levels, coagulation assessment, serum inflammatory mediators, total length of hospital stay, total treatment cost, total ventilatory support duration, complications of CRRT treatment, and outcome indicators.

Statistical analysis

Data collection forms were developed to gather research data, and Excel was used to consolidate the data. Perform the Shapiro-Wilk test on baseline data to assess normality. Quantitative data conforming to a normal distribution are expressed as (x ± s) and analyzed using the t-test; data not conforming to a normal distribution are expressed as [M(Q1, Q3)] and analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test; categorical data are expressed as frequency and percentage [n(%)] and analyzed using the chi-square test. After completing the comparison of baseline data, Box’s M test was performed to evaluate the homogeneity of variances in the covariance matrix. Upon meeting the test criteria, one-way repeated-measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) was conducted at each time point (T0 [0 h], T1 [10 h], T2 [24 h], T3 [48 h]) within the Oxiris and ST-100 groups to evaluate the fluctuations in data over time within each group. To determine the magnitude of change in data before and after treatment, data were further processed to calculate T0-1(T0-T1),T1-2(T1-T2), andT2-3(T2-T3). Multifactor repeated-measures ANOVA was performed on the data from the oXiris and ST-100 groups to evaluate between-group differences and the interaction between filter mode and time point, and line graphs were plotted to describe the trends in data changes. To address the imbalance in baseline characteristics, this study included baseline measurements as covariates in the analysis and employed a repeated-measures ANOVA model to compare differences in data trends between the two groups at the four time points. The Kaplan-Meier method was used to construct survival curves, and the Log-rank test was applied to compare the two groups. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0 software; P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Comparison of haemodynamic and inflammatory indices between the two groups

Data processing of the two groups of data for each indicator gives T0−1, T1−2 and T2−3 (the difference between the data of two neighbouring time points).

Extent of change in dopamine dose, norepinephrine dose, VIS, MAP

The T0−1, T1−2, and T2−3 data of dopamine dose, norepinephrine dose, VIS, and MAP of the two groups met the criteria of statistical test.Multifactorial repeated-measures ANOVA between oXiris and ST-100 groups yielded that the magnitude of changes in dopamine dose, norepinephrine dose, VIS, and MAP before and after treatment were not statistically significant (P > 0.05).

Within-subjects effect analyses yielded no time trends in the magnitude of change in dopamine dose (FIntra−group=1.016, P > 0.05), norepinephrine dose (FIntra−group=1.540, P > 0.05) and VIS (FIntra−group=1.604, P > 0.05) between the two groups.There was no interaction between the magnitude of change before and after treatment of dopamine dose (within-group FIntergroup*intra=0.483, P > 0.05), norepinephrine dose (FIntergroup*intra=2.303, P > 0.05), and VIS (FIntergroup*intra=2.458, P > 0.05) and the time-points, i.e., the treatment effects of the two groups had the same trend for the magnitude of change of the above indicators.Between-subjects effect analysis yielded no statistically significant difference in the magnitude of change in dopamine dose (FIntergroup=0.483, P > 0.05), norepinephrine dose (FIntergroup=2.303, P > 0.05), and VIS (FIntergroup=2.458, P > 0.05) before and after the treatment, which indicated that there was no significant difference in the magnitude of change in the above indicators before and after the treatment between the two groups.

Within-subjects effect analysis could conclude that there was a time trend in the magnitude of change in MAP (FIntra−group=5.808, P < 0.05), and there was an interaction between the magnitude of change before and after treatment and the point in time (FIntergroup*intra= 8.347, P < 0.05), i.e., there was a difference in the magnitude of change in MAP due to the effect of treatment in the two groups.Between-subjects effect analysis yielded a difference in the magnitude of change in MAP (FIntergroup= 8.347, P < 0.05). Magnitude of change in IL-6, PCT

We used repeated-measures ANOVA to compare IL-6 levels at four time points (T0, T1, T2, T3) between the two groups of patients, adjusting for baseline IL-6 as a covariate. The results of the adjusted repeated-measures analysis showed that the main effect of time was not statistically significant (Ftime = 0.667, P = 0.579), suggesting that IL-6 levels in both groups did not change significantly over time; The time×baseline IL-6 interaction effect was statistically significant (Ftime*baseline= 23.289, P < 0.001), suggesting that baseline IL-6 levels are a key factor influencing the dynamic trends of IL-6; The time×group interaction effect was not statistically significant (Ftime*group=1.187, P = 0.333), suggesting that after adjusting for baseline levels, there was no significant difference in the trends of IL-6 levels over time between the ST-100 group and the oXiris group. A between-subjects effect test showed that the overall difference in IL-6 levels between the two groups was not statistically significant (F = 0.512, P = 0.480), the effect of baseline IL-6 on the overall IL-6 level was statistically significant (F = 9.262, P = 0.005).To further address the significant imbalance in baseline IL-6 levels, the data from both groups were reprocessed using the method of differences, yielding T0-1, T1-2, and T2-3 data that met the criteria for statistical testing. A multivariate repeated-measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) between the oXiris and ST-100 groups revealed that the magnitude of IL-6 changes was statistically significant (P < 0.05).Analysis of within-subject effects showed the magnitude of IL-6 changes: within-group F = 3.609, P < 0.05. This indicates that the magnitude of IL-6 changes exhibits a temporal trend. The within-group and between-group F-statistic was 5.207 (P < 0.05). This indicates an interaction between the magnitude of change before and after treatment and the time point, suggesting that the treatment effects of the two groups exhibit different trends in the magnitude of IL-6 change. Analysis of between-group effects revealed an F-statistic of 6.779 (P < 0.05), indicating a statistically significant difference in the magnitude of IL-6 change between the two groups. During the T0–1 time period, the change in IL-6 levels in the oXiris group was significantly greater than that in the ST-100 group (P = 0.003). No significant differences were observed in the numerical differences at subsequent time points, indicating that the inflammatory adsorption effect of the oXiris group was significantly superior to that of the ST-100 group within the first 10 h. Discussion

In this study, among adult postoperative cardiac surgical infections combined with AKI in patients undergoing CRRT, a trend towards higher survival in the oXiris group than in the ST-100 group was observed after 60 days of follow-up; however, the difference was not statistically significant. During CRRT, there was a difference in the magnitude of change in MAP between the two groups, especially within 10 h of treatment, when the degree of change was most significant. There was a difference in the magnitude of change in norepinephrine and VIS between the two groups within 10 h of treatment, with a greater degree of change in the oXiris group. There was a difference in the magnitude of change in IL-6 between the two groups Page, with a greater magnitude of change in IL-6 in the oXiris group than in the ST-100 group, especially within 10 h of treatment when the degree of change was most significant. The oXiris has a potential advantage in reducing the levels of inflammatory mediators compared to ST-100.

AKI is closely associated with postoperative cardiac infections. Relevant studies [10, 11] pointed out that the incidence of multidrug-resistant bacterial infections ranged from 0.6% to 10% in patients undergoing various types of cardiac surgery, and the mortality rate was significantly higher than that of patients with non-resistant multidrug-resistant bacterial infections. Whereas postoperative AKI in cardiac surgery is a recognised serious complication and one of the major risk factors for death in cardiac surgery patients, in more severe stages it increases the chances of death by a staggering 50–80%.Even a slight increase in serum creatinine after cardiac surgery implies increased morbidity, prolonged length of stay in hospital and in intensive care unit and increased healthcare costs [12].

AKI occurs at a high rate in patients undergoing cardiac valve surgery, and valve surgery combined with coronary artery bypass grafting may contribute more significantly to AKI than individual factors, where patients on IABP support are more likely to be treated with RRT than those without IABP, possibly due to inadequate renal perfusion and loss of renal function as a direct result of reduced cardiac output (IABP is used in critically ill patients with low cardiac output),. Thus patients with pre-existing renal impairment are highly susceptible to AKI [13].

Studies [14, 15] have shown that a “sepsis-like syndrome” is frequently observed after cardiac surgery with extracorporeal circulation (CPB), especially when CPB lasts more than 90 min (double valve replacement or valve replacement with coronary artery bypass grafting), which is due to microcirculatory disturbances caused by endothelial dysfunction. This condition is due to microcirculatory disturbances caused by endothelial dysfunction, and the oXiris membrane reduces cytokine concentrations after CPB and during sepsis, and it may also reduce short-term (< 14 days) mortality in patients with septic shock combined with AKI.

It has been shown [16] that the inflammatory response to ECMO is complex and that the inflammatory response is generated mainly by exposure of the blood to extracorporeal circulation leading to activation of the contact and complement systems, which in turn leads to “pan-endothelial” damage, activation of leukocytes and production of pro-inflammatory mediators, which ultimately leads to systemic inflammatory response and damage to the visceral organs.Ostermann M [7] et al. have stated that in patients undergoing RRT for ECMO patients are three times more likely to die in-hospital than those not treated with RRT, and that AKI is extremely common in patients receiving ECMO and is associated with worsening short- and long-term prognosis, especially if RRT is required.

Adsorption of endotoxins and cytokines by oXiris membranes reduces the body’s inflammatory response and rapidly mitigates the cascade of reactions caused by inflammatory factors, thus improving the prognosis of the patient. oXiris membranes have been observed to improve haemodynamics and respiratory system with current adsorbent haemodepletion technologies. These techniques include removal of bacterial toxins and cytokines from the patient’s blood, suppression of excessive inflammatory responses, and restoration of immune homeostasis. oXiris filter use may be associated with better haemodynamic improvement, improved organ function (SOFA scores), shorter ICU stays, and lower short-term mortality [17].

Limitation

This study has some limitations. Firstly, this study was a single-centre study with a small sample size, which may lead to biased results. Second, in terms of inflammatory mediator detection, the levels of endotoxin, IL-8, and IL-10 could not be detected because of the limitation. Retrospective cohort studies with larger samples or prospective cohort studies are needed to confirm this.

Conclusion

The results of this study suggest that the oXiris filter is effective in removing circulating inflammatory mediators from patients with postoperative cardiac surgical infections combined with acute kidney injury compared with the ST-100 filter, which is beneficial for improving haemodynamics, but there is no statistically significant difference between the two in terms of patient prognosis.

ICIs in hepatocellular carcinoma: A comprehensive analysis of path forward for multimodal treatment strategies
Authors: Shixuan Peng, Qisheng Chen, Yongjun Wu
Published: 2024

We read with interest the study by Celsa et al,1 a prospective cohort study that assessed the incidence, characteristics, and prognosis of immune-associated liver injury (irLI) among HCC and other cancer patients receiving immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). HCC, the third-leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide, poses a serious challenge to global health.2,3 However, with breakthroughs in immunotherapy technology, the treatment outlook for patients with HCC has seen unprecedented improvements.3 With more and more patients receiving multimodal treatment strategies, including palliative, adjuvant, neoadjuvant, and ICIs, anticancer immunotherapy has gradually established its place in all stages of HCC treatment.4 Notably, irLI occurrence in patients with HCC treated with ICIs varies greatly and is associated with drug types and treatment combinations. Combining anti-PD-1/PD-L1 with anti-CTLA-4 therapy notably raises the liver injury risk.5

Patients with HCC showed a higher irLI incidence (11.4%) compared to the influenza vaccine indication during therapy with ICI cohort (2.6%), with earlier onset and higher frequency post-treatment adjustment (22.1 vs. 2.1 per 100 patient-years). Despite this, patients with HCC had a better irLI resolution rate (72.1% vs. 58.3%), less need for corticosteroids (16.3% vs. 75.0%), and lower rates of liver failure and treatment interruption (7%). Importantly, milder irLI grades in patients with HCC correlated with enhanced overall survival.

This research deepens our insight into irLI occurrence and effects in patients with HCC versus others with advanced cancers undergoing ICI therapy. We recommend several improvements that could amplify the study’s impact.

Patients with HCC, often cirrhotic, showed different irLI rates and severities compared to the non-HCC cohort with varied tumor types, potentially impacting irLI responses to ICIs. Patients with HCC primarily received atezolizumab combined with bevacizumab, an antiangiogenic not directly linked to liver injury but possibly influencing irLI severity when combined with ICIs. Reporting discrepancies in irLI management between the HCC and influenza vaccine indication during therapy with ICI cohorts, whose primary end point wasn’t irLI, might have resulted in underdiagnosing and inadequate irLI documentation, skewing its incidence estimates.

The study’s absence of centralized toxicity assessment could cause biased irLI severity evaluations. Varied practices between centers might impact irLI diagnosis and treatment. As a real-world study, it faces potential data quality issues, leading to possible inaccuracies. Nonstandardized data collection risks information bias, and the study’s follow-up duration might not capture all long-term irLI effects or consider its broader economic and health policy impacts.

The present study provides a valuable research advance in exploring the incidence of irLI during treatment with ICI and its clinical outcomes in patients with HCC. Future research should involve larger, diverse populations and multicenter trials to enhance the study’s scope and explore irLI’s mechanisms and management strategies. Expanding on the biological relationship between irLI and tumor response is also crucial. These efforts aim to foster academic discourse and enhance clinical practices, ultimately benefiting outcomes and quality of life of patients with HCC.

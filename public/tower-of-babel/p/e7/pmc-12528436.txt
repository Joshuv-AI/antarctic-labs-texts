Integrated ERP lean model for quality enhancement and operational excellence in SME based automotive mould manufacturing
Authors: A Bhaskar, A Devaraju
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIndian automotive SMEs face persistent inefficiencies in die changeovers, tool setups, and traceability, undermining their competitiveness and ISO 9001:2015 compliance. While Lean tools like SMED and ERP systems individually offer performance improvements, their combined application remains underutilized in SMEs. This study presents a structured four-phase ERP-Lean integration model validated in a Chennai based tooling SME. The approach began with diagnostic analysis and time-motion studies to identify inefficiencies, followed by Lean-SMED deployment with standardized setups, ERP customization, and KPI based performance evaluation. Key ERP modules Production Planning, Quality, Inventory, and Maintenance were configured to support real-time visibility and ISO-aligned digital workflows. Results showed a 95.2% reduction in die changeover time, 88.9% reduction in downtime, 72.4% reduction in rejection rate (PPM), and a 148.6% improvement in setup time utilization. Inventory turnover improved by 54.7%, and traceability resolution time reduced by 77.1%. This integration fostered process discipline, reduced variability, and improved compliance audit outcomes. The research concludes that ERP-Lean synergy forms a replicable model for SMEs in high-mix, resource sensitive manufacturing sectors aiming to transition toward Industry 4.0 readiness and ISO conformity.Keywords: ERP-lean integration, SMED, Automotive SMEs, Quality management, ISO 9001:2015, Operational efficiencySubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In today’s globally competitive manufacturing landscape, operational efficiency, precision, and quality form the foundational pillars for sustainable growth. Within this framework, the automotive sector, particularly in emerging economies like India, faces mounting pressures to deliver globally benchmarked products while contending with resource constraints and market volatility1. Manufacturers of automotive dies and moulds who produce precision tooling to design each component with interchangeable, fitment, and product quality parameters for the mass production line are key contributors to assuring the quality of the component produced. For these manufacturers, especially small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), the challenge of improving the production process while controlling cost is both a difficult necessity2,3.

Most of the automotive tooling industry in India consists of SMEs that typically have traditional manufacturing problems such as lengthy die changeover times, poor setup procedures, inconsistent inventory records, and an absence of digitized quality management processes4,5. These inefficiencies result in excessive downtime, high rejection rates, and frequent customer complaints, making global competitiveness a challenge. Additionally, many of these SMEs are ISO 9001:2015 certified or are actively pursuing certification, which necessitates standardized workflows, process control, and end-to-end traceability6,7.

Lean manufacturing, which originated from the Toyota Production System (TPS), provides a structured approach to waste elimination and process streamlining within organizations8,9. The Lean approach promotes flow-based efficiency, customer-centric value creation, and a culture of continuous improvement. It encompasses tools such as 5 S, Value Stream Mapping (VSM), Kaizen, Poka Yoke, and SMED10,11. Of these, SMED is particularly relevant in the tooling industry, where die changeovers can exceed hundreds of minutes per shift resulting in poor machine availability and productivity losses12–14.

However, the implementation of Lean in SMEs, while conceptually beneficial, often encounters execution barriers due to manual data tracking, limited automation, and procedural resistance15–17. This is where Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems add value. ERP acts as an integrative digital platform that synchronizes production planning, procurement, inventory, quality control, and maintenance18–20.

When combined with Lean, ERP fosters transparency, supports data-driven decision-making, and enables real-time performance monitoring addressing the typical shortfalls seen in Lean-only implementations21–23. This study explores such ERP-Lean synergy by embedding SMED methodology within an ERP environment. The case in focus is AB Dies and Moulds, an ISO 9001:2015 certified SME located in Ambattur Industrial Estate, Chennai24,25, .

The firm serves Tier-1 suppliers and OEMs in the Indian automotive market, producing complex progressive dies, rubber tooling, and injection moulds. Before ERP-Lean integration, the company experienced prolonged die setup times averaging 420 min, frequent tool misalignments, and rejection rates surpassing 380 PPM26. To address these gaps, the company adopted a transformation roadmap that included Lean audits, operator re-skilling, SMED based standardization, and ERP activation across PPC, Inventory, Quality, and Maintenance modules21,27.

This paper quantitatively analyzes the impact of the intervention by comparing pre- and post-integration KPIs, including changeover time, downtime, PPM rejection rates, inventory turnover, and traceability resolution time28,29, . Moreover, the implementation aligns with ISO 9001:2015 clauses addressing documented information (7.5), operational control (8.5), monitoring and evaluation (9.1), and corrective action (10.2)30–32.

This study aims to offer a replicable, scalable roadmap for SMEs operating in high precision, resource constrained environments33–35.

Novelty statement

This study is novel in developing and validating a replicable ERP-Lean framework tailored for ISO 9001:2015 certified Indian SMEs in the automotive tooling sector. Unlike existing models, it combines SMED execution with ERP-enforced traceability and SOP digitization, resulting in scalable, audit-ready performance improvement across multiple KPIs.

Literature review

Lean tools in Indian manufacturing SMEs

Lean manufacturing tools are crucial for improving operational efficiency and product quality, especially in resource constrained environments like Indian SMEs. However, deploying Lean practices in these settings often encounters systemic obstacles. Fragmented Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), inconsistent documentation, unstructured workflows, and dependency on tribal knowledge present barriers to structured process control. Many Indian SMEs lack formalized methods for evaluating and updating SOPs, resulting in operator-specific process variations and errors29.

The Single-Minute Exchange of Dies (SMED) method is particularly relevant for Indian tooling SMEs, where die and mould changeovers are frequent and time consuming. By converting internal activities to external ones and standardizing tooling practices, SMED reduces downtime and boosts machine availability. For instance, SMEs implementing SMED saw changeover reductions of up to 70%, enabling more frequent batch runs and improved takt compliance25. In many Indian case studies, integrating SMED led to measurable gains in overall equipment effectiveness (OEE) and operator productivity.

Beyond SMED, other Lean tools like 5 S (Sort, Set in order, Shine, Standardize, Sustain) are foundational yet inconsistently applied. SMEs often start 5 S initiatives with enthusiasm but fail to sustain them due to lack of visual management systems and auditing routines. Kaizen events structured problem-solving workshops are more successful when tied to observable performance metrics like defect rates, cycle time, or First Pass Yield36. In one Chennai-based SME, weekly Kaizen events targeting tool crib reorganization and die-setting sequence optimization reduced changeover-related delays by 38%37,38.

Poka-Yoke (mistake-proofing) is gaining traction in medium-sized Indian automotive units. It is used to minimize errors in die orientation and fastening by implementing mechanical sensors, limit switches, or color coded couplers. In Indian SMEs with low automation, these low cost devices significantly reduce non-conformance-related rework. Visual management tools like Kanban cards and Andon lights, while common in larger enterprises, are still emerging in smaller firms due to investment limitations.

Value Stream Mapping (VSM), though conceptually understood, is underutilized. SMEs often lack the cross functional participation required to map end-to-end processes. However, successful pilots show that VSM clarifies bottlenecks in setup approval, material retrieval, and inspection routing. When VSM is coupled with time motion studies, SMEs have identified non-value adding tasks amounting to 20–35% of total setup time, offering strong Lean leverage.

Thus, while Lean tools hold significant promise for Indian SMEs, their full benefit depends on structured training, cross-functional collaboration, and integration with digital platforms. This paves the way for ERP as an enabling infrastructure for sustaining Lean improvements.

ERP benefits in shop floor control

Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems are designed to unify, standardize, and automate diverse business functions, including production, procurement, inventory, and quality management. In the Indian SME context where manual processes and non-integrated departments are common ERP adoption on the shop floor plays a transformative role. The most immediate impact of ERP is in enhancing traceability: tracking each job, die, or operator action from scheduling through delivery39.

In many SMEs, job cards and tool inspection checklists were traditionally maintained on paper, leading to misplaced records, inconsistent audits, and non-traceable deviations. After ERP implementation, these were replaced by electronic route sheets, barcode enabled die tracking, and timestamped inspection logs. These changes support ISO 9001:2015 documentation requirements and reduce audit non-conformance scores31.

Another major benefit is in real time monitoring and escalation control. ERP dashboards provide live visibility into key shop floor KPIs such as die setup status, cycle completion, rejection count, and material shortages. This improves supervisor response time and accountability. For example, one Pune based SME reported a 43% improvement in defect traceability closure time after ERP adoption28.

ERP also plays a preventive role through checklist-based validations. Setup approvals can be automated, and machine readiness can be verified before job release. In some Indian SMEs, Production Planning and Control (PPC) modules are directly linked to maintenance records, preventing scheduling of jobs on machines awaiting preventive maintenance. This integration reduces unexpected downtime and ensures better machine utilization.

Further, ERP simplifies data entry and retrieval. Previously, daily reports involved handwritten logs reviewed at the end of the shift. With ERP, real time alerts and dashboards help supervisors and quality teams monitor conditions dynamically. Document retrieval during customer complaints or ISO audits is instant, improving transparency and customer trust.

ERP also enables better coordination across departments. For example, when a rejection is logged, alerts can be simultaneously sent to Quality, Tool Room, and Maintenance teams, enabling faster root cause analysis and countermeasure deployment. ERP’s ability to integrate quality events (like nonconformities or calibration due dates) with scheduling and inventory decisions adds cross-functional value.

Despite these benefits, ERP adoption in Indian SMEs is not without challenges. Cost of implementation, training, and customization are significant, and many SMEs require support from third party ERP consultants or institutional grants. However, firms that align ERP features closely with Lean principles such as visual control, waste elimination, and continuous improvement report more sustainable outcomes40.

ERP-lean synergy and digital sops

The integration of ERP systems with Lean methodologies enables a powerful synergy that overcomes the limitations of both when implemented separately. While Lean focuses on process efficiency, standardization, and waste elimination, ERP provides a digital backbone to capture, enforce, and monitor Lean improvements across departments34.

One of the most immediate outcomes of ERP-Lean synergy is the digitization of Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). In traditional Lean implementations, SOPs are often paper based, subject to manual interpretation, and inconsistently followed. When Lean workflows are embedded directly into ERP modules such as through digital work instructions, pre-checklists for setup, or operator login-triggered SOPs standardization improves significantly33. In one Coimbatore-based SME, incorporating SOP adherence into the ERP interface resulted in a 60% decrease in process deviations during die changeover.

This integration is particularly valuable in SMED practices, where setup validation steps can be digitized and enforced through ERP prompts. For example, before initiating a die change, the ERP system can prompt the operator to confirm completion of safety checks, availability of calibrated gauges, and readiness of raw material all based on customized SMED sequence templates. Such automation not only reduces the likelihood of human error but also ensures audit trails are captured in real time35.

ERP-Lean synergy also improves traceability. In a traditional Lean environment, root cause analysis after a rejection often depends on manual logs or memory recall. With ERP, detailed batch histories, setup parameters, operator IDs, and inspection records are instantly available. This enables faster root cause identification and shorter time to corrective action. Zafar and Srinivasan41 observed that SMEs linking ERP with Lean QMS tools reduced customer complaint closure time by nearly 50%.

Another area where synergy is evident is in visual management. While Lean tools promote visual controls like Andon boards and Kanban cards, ERP systems enhance them by providing real time dashboards accessible across the organization. Production supervisors can view setup progress, rejection alerts, tool room status, and delivery forecasts from a single screen. In SMEs where multitasking is common and decision-making is distributed; such visibility drives timely intervention.

Moreover, ERP can link nonconformance records with Lean’s Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) processes. For instance, once a nonconformance is logged, the ERP system can generate an automatic task for the responsible team, enforce deadline tracking, and escalate overdue actions. This strengthens Lean’s continuous improvement loop by institutionalizing follow-up actions.

Digitization also aids in sustaining Lean gains. While Lean events like Kaizen blitzes or SMED workshops may yield temporary improvements, embedding their outcomes into ERP systems ensures long term adherence. In a Vadodara based tooling SME, setup time reduction achieved through SMED was sustained for 18 months because ERP dashboards continued to monitor the same KPIs with alerts configured for any regressions.

In summary, ERP-Lean synergy allows SMEs to move from reactive process control to proactive management. By embedding Lean logic into ERP workflows such as through digital SOPs, real-time checks, traceable logs, and CAPA automation SMEs can achieve robust quality control, improved efficiency, and a scalable foundation for Industry 4.0 maturity.

Methodology

The methodology adopted in this study was structured around a four-phase ERP-Lean implementation framework. This approach was selected to ensure that both Lean tools particularly SMED and ERP modules could be customized and deployed in a way that directly addressed the real-world operational challenges commonly faced by Indian SMEs37.

Case context and problem definition

AB Dies and Moulds is an ISO 9001:2015 certified SME located in Ambattur Industrial Estate, Chennai. It specializes in progressive dies, rubber tooling, and injection moulds for Tier-1 automotive suppliers and OEMs. The firm uses advanced CAD/CAM tools and maintains a moderately skilled workforce. Despite this, the facility exhibited several chronic inefficiencies prior to the intervention:

To quantify and analyze these gaps, a combination of time-motion studies, operator feedback, and downtime logs was used. Internal setup elements were identified and separated from external ones, forming the basis for SMED standardization. The primary aim was to eliminate non-value-added activities in the setup process and to enhance inventory visibility, traceability, and setup discipline using a Lean-ERP hybrid solution12; Zafar and Srinivasan, 2024).

Results and discussion

Die changeover time reduction

One of the most significant outcomes was the substantial reduction in die changeover time, which was cut down to just a fraction of its original duration, reflecting an exceptional improvement. This was achieved by reclassifying internal and external setup steps, deploying modular clamps, shadow boards, and visual SOPs, and enforcing standardized setup validation8,22. ERP linked prompts ensured pre-checklist completion before setup commencement, which eliminated undocumented delays.

Compared to similar studies, this exceeds the common SMED benchmark of 60–75% reduction12.

The initial lengthy die setup time was significantly reduced within the first few months and further minimized by the sixth month. This remarkable improvement was achieved through the use of modular toolkits, preheating, optimized die clamping, and ERP-enabled pre-setup validation.

Training for operators, resistance to change, and problems starting up

The ERP-Lean rollout faced three major challenges: operator training, resistance to change, and startup issues. Initially, many operators relied on existing practices and resisted shifting to digital SOPs and ERP based validation checklists, but structured training programs including classroom sessions, live ERP simulations, and mentorship by Lean champions along with Kaizen style workshops that linked ERP-Lean benefits to daily tasks, gradually reduced resistance. Early demonstration of improvements, such as shorter tool search times and fewer rejections, further built confidence, while management reinforced adoption by rewarding “first-time-right setups.” Technical difficulties, including login errors, incorrect role based access, and improper tool staging, were resolved by phased activation of ERP modules and continuous IT support. A three-month shadowing phase allowed digital processes to run parallel with manual operations, ensuring production continuity during transition. This structured approach strengthened workforce capability, improved acceptance, and enhanced system reliability, thereby establishing a solid foundation for successful ERP-Lean integration.

ERP platform: capabilities, limitations, and selection rationale

The implemented ERP platform was a mid-tier, modular system specifically designed for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), integrating key modules for Production Planning and Control (PPC), Quality Management, Inventory, and Maintenance. It facilitated barcode-based tracking, KPI dashboards, and real-time alerts, while ensuring ISO 9001:2015 compliance through digitized SOPs, enforced setup validations, and embedded corrective and preventive actions (CAPA) within routine operations. Despite its limitations in advanced analytics, predictive scheduling, and extensive customization, these gaps were effectively addressed by synchronizing ERP processes with Lean principles, thereby maintaining simplicity, enhancing usability, and avoiding operational complexity for end users.

Implementation cost, ROI, and SME financial aspects

The ERP-Lean initiative required an investment of approximately INR 18–22 lakhs, covering licensing, customization, IT infrastructure, and training an amount significant for SMEs but made manageable through phased rollout and government supported digitization schemes. The return on investment (ROI) was achieved within 18–24 months, primarily driven by reductions in tool rejections, decreased downtime, and improved machine utilization. Lower rejection rates alone generated substantial savings by minimizing scrap and rework, while optimized setup utilization enhanced throughput without additional capital expenditure. Beyond direct cost savings, the initiative delivered indirect financial benefits, including improved customer satisfaction, reduced penalties for ISO non-compliance, and enhanced competitiveness in vendor selection by Tier-1 suppliers. Collectively, these outcomes demonstrated the financial viability of ERP-Lean integration for SMEs, strengthening market positioning while ensuring long term sustainability.

Comparison with lean-only and ERP-only implementations

The findings of this study demonstrate that ERP-Lean integration delivered significantly greater improvements than Lean-only or ERP-only implementations reported in previous literature. While Lean methods such as SMED achieve substantial setup time reductions, their long-term effectiveness is limited without robust digital enforcement mechanisms; in contrast, the ERP-Lean system sustained these gains through integrated digital checks and traceability controls that ensured continuous compliance with standardized procedures. ERP-only deployments, on the other hand, have been shown to enhance traceability, documentation accuracy, and audit readiness but fail to directly address process level inefficiencies such as lengthy die changeovers or operator-dependent variability. By embedding Lean practices within ERP modules, the hybrid approach combined the productivity and efficiency gains of Lean with the compliance, visibility, and traceability strengths of ERP, leading to simultaneous improvements in both operational performance and quality outcomes. Overall, the results confirm that ERP-Lean synergy provides more comprehensive and enduring benefits compared to standalone Lean or ERP systems, particularly in the SME context.

Adaptability of the ERP-lean model to other industries

This study focused on an Indian automotive tooling SME; however, the ERP-Lean approach is broadly applicable across diverse industrial sectors. In aerospace manufacturing, where stringent quality control and traceability are critical, integrating Lean SMED practices with ERP-based nonconformance monitoring can prevent inspection delays while ensuring regulatory compliance. In heavy machinery and capital goods industries, ERP-Lean synergy can reduce downtime from lengthy setup cycles and support predictive maintenance planning, while in electronics and precision component manufacturing, ERP embedded digital SOPs can sustain Lean driven defect prevention in high-volume, multi-component environments. The effectiveness of adaptation lies in configuring ERP modules to align with industry specific workflows and regulatory frameworks, while preserving Lean’s emphasis on waste elimination and process standardization. Moreover, modular ERP deployment enables SMEs with constrained resources to adopt the model incrementally. Thus, ERP-Lean integration offers a scalable framework not only for SMEs in the automotive sector but also as a transferable model for digital Lean transformation across industries characterized by product variety, regulatory demands, and the need for operational resilience.

Sustainability of performance improvements over time

To evaluate the sustainability of improvements, performance indicators were monitored for 12 months following ERP-Lean implementation, and key parameters such as die changeover time, monthly downtime, and rejection rates remained stable without reverting to pre-implementation levels. ERP dashboards and automated alerts played a critical role in sustaining these gains by providing real-time visibility into deviations from standard setup times or quality thresholds, while regular management reviews ensured that corrective and preventive actions (CAPA) were systematically enforced through the ERP system. This digital integration embedded Lean practices into organizational routines, minimizing operator-dependent variability and strengthening process consistency. The findings demonstrate that ERP-Lean integration not only delivered immediate performance improvements but also enhanced long-term operational resilience within SMEs.

Conclusion

The findings of this study establish that a structured integration of Lean methodologies, particularly SMED, with customized ERP systems can significantly enhance the operational efficiency, traceability, and quality performance of Indian SMEs in the automotive tooling sector. By digitizing setup routines, enforcing SOP compliance, and embedding ISO 9001:2015 clauses into ERP workflows, the SME under study achieved remarkable performance gains. Die changeover time was reduced from 420 min to just 20 min, marking a 95.2% improvement, while monthly production downtime dropped by 88.9%, from 90 h to 10 h. Equally notable was the 72.4% reduction in tool rejection rates, falling from 380 to 105 parts per million, indicating enhanced setup precision and reduced variability. Setup time utilization improved dramatically from 35 to 87%, a 148.6% increase, as more setup tasks were standardized, externalized, and supported digitally. Complementary ERP enabled KPIs such as inventory turnover and defect traceability also showed substantial gains inventory turnover improved by 54.7%, and traceability resolution time decreased from 3.5 to 0.8 days, a 77.1% improvement. These results validate that ERP-Lean synergy provides not only measurable operational uplift but also a scalable, audit-compliant framework for SMEs seeking ISO alignment and Industry 4.0 readiness. By transitioning from paper based, manually coordinated setups to a digitally orchestrated workflow, the firm demonstrated that performance excellence and process discipline can be jointly achieved, even within the resource constraints typical of small and medium manufacturing enterprises.

Limitations

However, this study has limitations. The research was conducted in a single SME and focused only on selected ERP modules. Broader studies across different industries, geographies, and ERP systems would help validate the model’s scalability. Furthermore, future work could explore integration with IoT enabled sensors, AI-based production forecasts, and real-time quality analytics for Industry 4.0 alignment.

Nonetheless, this work lays a strong foundation for process-centric ERP-Lean integration, offering a roadmap for SMEs aiming to achieve manufacturing excellence under resource constraints.

Future directions

Future research could expand the scope of this study by applying the ERP-Lean integration model across different manufacturing verticals such as aerospace, heavy machinery, or electronics. Additionally, integrating advanced analytics or AI-driven scheduling into ERP systems can offer predictive setup validations and downtime avoidance. Cloud-based ERP deployment with mobile interfaces could further enhance accessibility and real-time decision-making, especially for multi-location SMEs. Longitudinal studies extending beyond one year could provide insights into how digital Lean practices evolve and sustain in the face of organizational or supply chain disruptions. Finally, investigating the role of employee training, change management strategies, and cost-benefit analysis will strengthen the business case for ERP-Lean transformation in resource-sensitive environments.

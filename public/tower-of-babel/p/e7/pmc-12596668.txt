CERG-08 TIME TO DIAGNOSIS OF PAEDIATRIC BRAIN TUMORS: AN INSTITUTIONAL EXPERIENCE
Authors: Omane Okrah, Patrick Bankah, Ernestina Schandorf, Maxwell Ofosu, Olivia Asiedu, Obed Kwofie
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPaediatric brain tumours are a major contributor to morbidity and mortality in children, with early diagnosis being essential for optimal outcomes. However, diagnostic and treatment delays are still prevalent in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). This study aimed to evaluate the time from symptom onset to diagnosis and from first hospital contact to definitive surgical intervention in paediatric brain tumour patients, while also assessing tumour types, WHO grades, presentation routes, and the use of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) diversion procedures. A retrospective review was conducted over an 18-month period (May 27, 2023, to November 29, 2024) involving 46 paediatric patients who presented to the Neurosurgery Outpatient Department (OPD) or Emergency Room (ER) with brain tumours. Data collected included age at presentation, time intervals related to diagnosis and surgery, tumour histology and grade, and whether CSF diversion procedures were performed. The mean and median age at presentation were both 9 years (SD = 4.31), with patient ages ranging from 2 to 18 years. The average duration from symptom onset to diagnosis was 28.8 weeks (SD = 22.4), with a median of 26 weeks. Astrocytomas (28.3%) and craniopharyngiomas (26.1%) were the most commonly diagnosed tumours, followed by thalamic tumours (10.9%). Presenting symptoms included headaches (26.9%), gait or balance disturbances (17.9%), vomiting (12.7%), and visual impairment (12.7%). The median age of the cohort was 10.0 years (range: 2.0–18.0 years). CSF diversion procedures, predominantly ventriculoperitoneal (VP) shunts, were performed in 25 patients. Two-thirds of the patients (67%) presented through the OPD, while the remaining 33% were seen in the ER. The study highlights considerable delays in both diagnosis and surgical management, emphasizing the need for improved early recognition and referral systems in LMICs. Encouragingly, time to diagnosis showed improvement compared to local data from 2022–2023.

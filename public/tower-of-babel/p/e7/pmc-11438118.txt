Barriers to physical activity in the Iranian population: findings from the STEPwise surveillance 2021
Authors: Behnaz Mahdaviani, Zahra Soleimani, Maryam Selk-Ghaffari, Mohammad Hosein Pourgharib shahi, Safdar Masoumi, Ramin Kordi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPhysical inactivity is a critical predictor of all-cause mortality and many non-communicable diseases (NCD) including coronary heart disease, diabetes, hypertension, dementia, and several cancers. The main objective of this study was to determine the main barriers to physical activity based on the STEPwise Surveillance in Iran in 2021, to guide policymakers in developing the most effective physical activity increasing strategies.MethodsThis cross-sectional study was conducted on 27,515 female and male individuals aged over 18 years from the STEPwise Surveillance 2021. The barriers to physical activity according to the modified version of barriers questionnaire and intrapersonal, interpersonal, economic, cultural, and environmental domains based on social-ecological models were determined. Association between barriers domain and physical activity level was assessed.ResultsLack of time due to job commitments (31.4%) and family (19.3%) and inappropriate physical conditions including illness, pain, injury, disability, and fatigue (30.4%) were the most frequent barriers to physical activity. Interest in sedentary leisure time activities including virtual space, computer games and watching TV were the next frequent barriers to physical activity (10.01%). Intrapersonal domain had the highest frequency (62.9%) and cultural domain had the lowest frequency (2.3%). Intrapersonal and interpersonal barriers reduced the odds of engaging in physical activity (OR: 0.62, P value < 0.001, OR: 0.76, P value < 0.001).ConclusionIntra- and inter-personal domain barriers might reduce the odds of being active. Developing action plans addressing these factors is suggested to increase physical activity levels.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-024-20134-3.

Introduction

Insufficient physical activity leads to increased risk of a high proportion of chronic medical conditions including cerebrovascular disease, coronary heart disease, type 2 diabetes, hypertension, depression, obesity, colon cancer, breast cancer and premature mortality [1].

If the global insufficient physical activity level does not improve, 499·2 million new cases of non-communicable diseases would occur by 2030 and direct health related costs is estimated approximately INT$520 billion [2]. Insufficient physical activity leads to 7.6% and 7.2% of cardiovascular disease deaths and all-cause mortality, respectively [3]. According to STEPs surveillance in Iran, insufficient physical activity level has augmented from 23.1% in 2001 to 55.4% in 2016 [4].

To prevent a deterioration in functional capacity of older adults, participating in physical activities can positively impact the quality of life and functional capacity and help maintaining independency in their basic daily activities [5]. Additionally, living alone can affect basic activities and functional capacity [5]. In older adults, the determinants that influence a high degree of satisfaction with their health status include maintaining adequate levels of physical activity, maintaining good functional abilities, the absence of physical diseases, and absence of psychological problems [6].

To support increasing physical activity levels in countries, the World Health Organization (WHO) developed the “Global action plan on physical activity (GAPPA) 2018–2030, more active people for a healthier world” [2, 7]. GAPPA consists of evidence-based policy suggestions and four main objectives including creating active people, active society, active systems, and active environments, as a national guide to improving the physical activity levels of populations [2, 7]. Various parameters including interpersonal, intrapersonal, cultural, environmental, socio-economic, and policy-related factors affect levels of physical activity and sedentary behaviour in populations [8–10]. A mismatch between barriers to physical activity and physical activity interventions might lead to program failure. Considering the main barriers to physical activity in developing physical activity programs and national action plans is critical for increasing physical activity levels [7, 8, 10].

Numerous parameters are correlated to insufficient physical activity and applying a comprehensive approach is required [7, 8]. Environmental characteristics and socioeconomic inequalities have been proposed as determinants of health behaviours including physical activity level [9]. Based on social–ecological models, individual, social, community and environmental factors impact the ability and probability of engaging in physical activity [8, 10]. Intrapersonal, interpersonal, culture, economic, and environmental factors are main motivators or barriers to physical activity [10]. Intrapersonal domain consists of individuals factors including opinion toward physical activity and health status and interpersonal domain consists of social interaction and support of peers, friends, family and coaches [8].

Determining the barriers to physical activity is mandated to develop a road map for future policy making and action plans to increase physical activity level. The main objective of this study was to determine the main barriers to physical activity based on the STEPwise surveillance in Iran in year 2021.The results of the current study will be applied for future planning in physical activity increasing interventions.

Methods

This cross sectional study was based on the data obtained from a large survey project known as the STEPwise surveillance 2021, which was conducted on the World Health Organization (WHO) platform to evaluate risk factor of non- communicable diseases [11]. STEPwise surveillance was conducted in 31 provinces of Iran in 2021 [12]. The study was approved by the Tehran University of medical sciences ethical committee (Ethics code: IR.TUMS.IKHC.REC.1401.106). All participants in the STEPwise surveillance were provided with complete information regarding the objective of the study. All individuals had signed informed consent.

In order to improve sampling accuracy and efficiency, essential components including an acceptable margin of error, the optimal confidence interval, design effect, the non-response rate, and estimated baseline index level (prevalence of indices) were considered in this survey [12]. The required sample size in each province was determined by applying a proportion to population size method. A systematic cluster random sampling frame was applied [12]. Participants in the study were recruited from 3,176 clusters from 31 provinces of Iran from urban and rural areas. According to the population of the provinces and relative weighting, clusters were determined [12]. Each cluster was accomplished with 9 participants [12]. Iranian population aged ≥ 18 years old were included in the study. Pregnant women, individuals with physical problems whom their anthropometric measurements were not possible, individuals unable to reply to questioners due to psychological diseases were excluded from the study [11, 12]. Participants’ demographic data, behavioural, metabolic risk factors, and physical activity level were obtained via face to face interviews [12].

Physical activity level was assessed applying the Global Physical Activity Questionnaire (GPAQ). The GPAQ is designed and developed under the observation of the World Health Organization (WHO) in 2002 and has been applied in Iran’s STEPwise surveillances since 2005 [13]. Eight STEPS surveys have been conducted in Iran (2005, 2006, 2007, 2008, 2009, 2011, 2016, 2021) and to faciliate detectimg the trend of physical activity level in the country, an identical questionare (GPAQ) has been applied in all surverys [4]. The GPAQ assesses individuals’ total physical activity level presented as metabolic equivalents (METs)-minutes/week, and sedentary behavior presented as hours per week. Simultaneously, leisure time physical activity, work related physical activity, and commuting related physical activity are assessed. Each domain of physical activity’s duration and intensity, as well as the frequency of engaging in vigorous or moderate activities, are evaluated to calculate the aggregated score of physical activity. The total score from the questions in each of the three parts are added together to determine the total level of physical activity. If the total score is less than 600 Mets-minutes/week, the individuals are classified as physically inactive. GPAQ’s Validity and reliability in the Iranian population have been demonstrated in previous studies [14–16].

Barriers to physical activity were determined via modified version of barriers questionnaire [17]. This questionaire consists of 13 questions assesing the bariers based on the socio-ecological model in intrapersonal (questions 2,3,5,10), interpersonal (questions 4, 9, 12), economic (question 6), cultural (question 11) and environmental domains (questions 7, 8, 13). (Supplementary file 1) The validity and reliability of the barriers questionnaire has been confirmed in the Iraninan population [17].

The questioners’ guideline and measurements protocols were established and delivered to the interviewers. Interviewers received comprehensive instructions and online education sessions. Participants’ privacy in data collection process was considered as a critical principle in this survey. Interview teams included male and female assessors in order to match participants and improve their cooperation regarding cultural issues [12].

All measurements including weight and height were conducted by matched gender assessors in a comfortable place for participants [12]. Height was measured during a straight standing posture against the wall while back of the head, hip, and the heels, were positioned in a straight line relying on the wall, using a standard meter. Weight measurements were conducted using a standard digital scale (Inofit) which was calibrated with a 5 kg index scale prior to each measurement when the device was relocated [12]. The BMI was calculated via dividing participants’ weight (kilograms) by their height(metres) squared.

Statistical analysis

Continues variables are presented as mean (standard deviation), and categorical variables are described as frequency (percentage). Correlation was assessed via Spearman correlation test and logistic regression to identify the determents affecting barriers to physical activity. Logistic regression was adjusted for possible confounding variables such as age, sex, household economic level, literacy level. Age ranges defined were as 18–24, 25–34, 35–44, 45–54, 55–64, 65–69, 70–74, and > 75 years old. All analyses were done using SPSS version 27 and STATA version 14. The level of significance was set to P < 0.05.

Results

Total number of 27,874 individuals were included in this study. After cleaning data, the study sample was comprised of 27,515 individuals. The mean (SD) age of the participants was 45.69 ± 15.91 years, 55.3% were female and 44.64% were male. The mean (SD) body mass index (BMI) of the participants was 26.98 ± 5.1 and 74.99% lived in urban areas. Discussion

Intrapersonal domain indicated the highest frequency (62.9%) and cultural domain showed the lowest frequency (2.3%) among main barriers to physical activity. Time constraints due to job commitments (31.4%) and family (19.3%) and inappropriate physical conditions including Illness, pain, injury, disability, fatigue (30.4%) were the most frequent barriers to physical activity. Interest in sedentary leisure time activities including virtual space, computer games and watching TV were the next frequent barriers to physical activity (10.01%) in Iranian population. The least frequent barriers to physical activity were lack of motivation and cultural issues. Intrapersonal and interpersonal barriers reduced the odds of engaging in physical activity.

Previous studies have demonstrated a variety of barriers to physical activity in different age, and gender population worldwide. Lack of time was reported as the most frequent barriers in different studies conducting in Iran [18–20], , and other countries [21–23]. Furthermore, Ferreira Silva et al. reviewed data from 38,319 adolescent and young adults across 31 countries and reported a lack of time as the most important barrier [24]. Note that the majority of participants in the mentioned studies were young or middle-aged. As previous studies have indicated, the role time constraints as obstacle to physical activity appear to be diminishing with advancing age among older adults, which coincide with entering retirement age. With a mean age of approximately 45 years in the current study, it is apparent that lack of time from either work, or family obligation is a logical barrier in this group. In addition, the frequency of choosing a time barrier significantly varied based on the age group, with participants opting to choose a time barrier more frequently when they were 18 to 45, and less frequently when they were 45 to older age group.

This may be the reason why the study conducted by Spiteri et al. in 2019 among the population with higher ages reported that environmental and resources barriers to physical activity were the most frequently cited barriers rather than time constraints [25]. It should be noted, however, that age is not the only factor influences barriers to physical activity, and this pattern may not have been observed in all of studies. In the same vein, according to another review study, elderly individuals may encounter a busy time due to the requirement for self-care, volunteering, and family obligations such as caring for their children, grandchildren, and their own parents. This could lead to the fact that time constraint is one of the most cited barrier to physical activity [26]. Regarding the oldest old population, however, they concluded that it is unlikely the factor of time contributes significantly to a decline in activity.

A similar conclusion has been reached by Moschny et al. who have showed that lack of time as one the least important barrier among German older adults. As they have revealed the factor of poor health was the most frequent barrier [27]. The same pattern of results was reported by Yarmohammadi et al. who demonstrated that physical problems was the most frequent cited barrier in reviewing the barriers among older participant from Iran, and worldwide [28].

In our study, the factor of inappropriate physical condition was cited as the second frequent barrier. Interestingly, with increasing age, poor health became more frequent as a barrier (From 16% in age group of 25–35 to 74.1% in age group of > 75 years old). Although this factor is known to be an important barrier especially with increasing age, as the benefits of physical activity are exclusively more than its risk, engaging in activity is strongly recommended for patients with poor health [27]. Therefore, it appears the factor of poor health could be a facilitator of activity, too [27, 28]. Consequently, patients are likely to be more active if the health providers advise them to do so.

As the third most commonly cited barrier, interest in sedentary leisure activities were significantly more frequently reported by younger participants in comparison with older individuals. Based on the gender analysis, this item was deemed to be more important by females. This is in accordance with the findings of a large study conducted by Dias et al., who identified interest in other activities as one of the two most important barriers among 1409 adolescents. They also found that females had chosen this item more frequently than males in their study [29]. In a study by Youssef et al., a similar pattern was also observed [30]. Furthermore, our analysis indicated that urban populations preferred this barrier more than rural populations. Since urbanization especially in developing countries has accompanied with it several issues, including tendency to become sedentary [31], it is reasonable to assume that this factor is more prominent among urban residents.

The other factors introduced in five domains were cited as barriers to activity in our population, with a frequency rate lower than 10%. Focusing on time efficient and more enjoyable programs, will assist in dealing with time constraints, and unwillingness to participate in activities, respectively. Additionally, effective strategies for encouraging activity among people with chronic disease, will be necessary to help combatting insufficient physical activity.

Further analysis illustrated that, based on a logistic regression model with respect to confounding factors, only intra- and inter-personal domains were significantly associated with insufficient physical activity. The results showed that the odds of engaging in a total amount of physical activity were reduce by 0.62, and 0.75, due to intra- and inter-personal barriers, respectively. Garcia et al. performed a systematic review of review studies in 2022 to evaluate the associations between barriers and facilitators with level of physical activity [32]. Although the association between barrier domains with total amount of physical activity was not discussed, they concluded that intrapersonal barriers including time constraints, poor health, negative emotions toward activity, and existence of other easy-to-reach behaviors were consistently associated with a reduction in leisure time activity level. As opposed to our findings, neither the leisure time activity nor the other domains of physical activity were consistently associated with interpersonal factors in their study.

As theoretical implications, individuals’ factors including opinion toward physical activity and health status indicated the highest frequency and cultural domain showed the lowest frequency among main barriers to physical activity. Intra- and inter-personal domains indicated significantly association with insufficient physical activity.

Strengths and limitations

Using a large sample of the Iranian population, we examined several domains of barriers to physical activity using a comprehensive questionnaire derived from a socioecological framework. Applying a comprehensive and practical tool including all domains for physical activity barriers will guide us in developing future physical activity increasing action plans, targeting main barriers. However, as the participants were selected from an age range over 18 years old and a small number of participants were in the elderly age group, our study might be limited in its the generalizability for two tails of age groups, including children, adolescents, and the elderly. Furthermore, generalizability of our result to clinical population should be taken with cautions as the questionnaire used in the current study was validated among healthy population. Applying subjective methods (self-reported questionnaire) in assessing physical activity levels might cause recall bias or over-or under-estimation.

A future line of research could be applying objective methods for assessing level of physical activity is suggested. Conducting future studies including children, adolescents and older population is recommended. Future research in developing physical activity increasing interventions targeting the main barriers is recommended.

Conclusion

Lack of time, along with poor health were the most cited barriers in the current study. The first was more relevant in the young population, and the latter was more pertinent in the older population. Behaviour change-based interventions with practical solutions aiming at time limitation barrier might be beneficial in younger individuals. Developing and conducting public educational programs for media and social networks regarding the health benefits of physical activity and encouraging older individuals to increase their physical activity even with medical problems is recommended. Developing guidelines and protocols for physical activity in chronic medical diseases, musculoskeletal disorders, and older age for the primary health care system might be effective and practical. Furthermore, as in the current study intra- and inter-personal domain barriers could significantly reduce the odds of being active, and addressing these factors could assist in dealing with insufficient physical activity in Iranian society. Developing national action plans based on behaviour change strategies, creating social networks specialized for physical activity, and conducting physical activity campaigns to support and encourage individuals to be physically active might be practical.

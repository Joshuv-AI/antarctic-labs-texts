Posteroseptal RVOT PVCs in a Clockwise-Rotated Congenital Heart
Authors: Adithya Shastry, Lung En Teng, Robert D. Anderson
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundElectrocardiography (ECG) localization of ventricular arrhythmias assumes normal cardiac anatomy. In cases of adult congenital heart disease, surgical remodeling can confound traditional algorithms.CaseA 26-year-old male with repaired pulmonary atresia and ventricular septal defect presented with a 17% premature ventricular complexes burden. The ECG showed right bundle branch block morphology, inferior axis, and positive precordial concordance, classically suggesting a left-sided origin. However, cardiac computed tomography revealed extreme clockwise rotation, displacing the right ventricular outflow tract (RVOT) posteriorly.ManagementGuided by imaging, mapping prioritized the RVOT. Earliest activation (−20 ms) and a 99% pace-map match were identified at the posteroseptal RVOT. Ablation resulted in immediate suppression, confirming the right-sided origin.ConclusionsThis “left-sided illusion” demonstrates that anatomical rotation can cause right-sided foci to mimic left-sided origins. Integrating cross-sectional imaging with ECG interpretation is critical for guiding ablation strategies in the adult congenital heart disease population.

Clinical History

A 26-year-old male with a history of complex adult congenital heart disease (ACHD)—status after the repair of pulmonary atresia, ventricular septal defect, and multiple pulmonary artery augmentations for pulmonary artery stenosis—presented with increased burden of symptomatic premature ventricular complexes (PVCs). His complex surgical history included a 23-mm pulmonary homograft, ventricular septal defect repair, and main pulmonary artery augmentation.

Despite escalation of beta-blocker therapy, his PVCs burden increased from 7% (6,132 PVCs/24 h in 2019) to 17% (15,550 PVCs/24 h in 2024). Transthoracic echocardiography demonstrated overall preserved biventricular systolic function (left ventricular ejection fraction 59% 2024; 55% 2023, 51% 2021, 35% 2020). He was referred for an electrophysiology study and catheter ablation.

Electrocardiography findings

Twelve-lead electrocardiography (ECG) demonstrated sinus rhythm with right bundle branch block (RBBB) morphology (Figure 1A). Imaging Findings

Preprocedural cardiac computed tomography angiography revealed extreme clockwise rotation of the heart, attributed to extensive prior cardiac surgery and right-sided remodeling. Electrophysiology Study and Outcome

Electrophysiology study was performed under general anesthesia with frequent PVCs. Mapping focused on the RVOT after careful integration of ECG findings with cross-sectional imaging. Activation mapping identified earliest activation at the posteroseptal RVOT, −20 ms prior to QRS onset, with fractionated bipolar electrograms. Unipolar electrogram was QS. Pace mapping demonstrated an excellent 99% match to the clinical PVCs morphology (Figure 3).Figure 3Electrophysiology Mapping of Premature Ventricular Complex in Clock-Wise Rotated HeartElectrophysiology study mapping RVOT for the earliest activation, with 99% pacing match in PVCs morphology (bottom left). Radiofrequency ablation at this site resulted in immediate suppression of PVCs, with no recurrence during isoprenaline challenge and repeated burst pacing. In follow-up, he had no recurrence of PVCs.

Final diagnosis: Posteroseptal RVOT PVCs.

Discussion of Diagnosis

RBBB morphology with an inferior axis and positive precordial concordance is classically associated with a left-sided outflow tract origin.3 However, ECG localization algorithms implicitly assume normal cardiac orientation. In this case, extreme clockwise rotation of the heart displaced the RVOT posteriorly, altering the depolarization vector and producing an ECG pattern mimicking a left-sided PVCs origin.

This case highlights an important limitation of ECG-based PVCs localization in patients with ACHD or prior cardiac surgery. Integration of ECG interpretation with preprocedural imaging was critical in guiding the mapping strategy and avoiding an unnecessary left-sided approach.

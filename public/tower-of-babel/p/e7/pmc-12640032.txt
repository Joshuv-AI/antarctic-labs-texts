De novo C3 Glomerulonephritis in a Kidney Transplant Recipient Associated With a Rare CFH Variant of Unknown Significance
Authors: Mayumi Takahashi-Kobayashi, Toshihiro Sawai, China Nagano, Kunio Kawanishi, Kentaro Nakajima, Hisato Suzuki, Kandai Nozu, Joichi Usui
Published: 2025

Introduction

C3 glomerulopathy (C3G), encompassing dense deposit disease (DDD) and C3 glomerulonephritis (C3GN), is defined by predominant C3 deposition in glomeruli without significant Ig, reflecting alternative complement pathway dysregulation. Causes include both genetic variants in complement regulatory proteins (e.g., factor H, factor I) and acquired factors such as autoantibodies or C3 nephritic factor (C3NeF). C3G is rare, with an incidence of 0.2 to 3 cases per million population1,2 and carries a poor prognosis; median renal survival is 90.8 months with no significant difference between DDD and C3GN.3 Posttransplant recurrence of C3GN occurs in > 50% of cases, usually within 1 to 2 years.4 In contrast, de novo C3GN is poorly characterized, with only 8 reported cases.5, 6, 7, 8, 9 Here, we describe a rare case of de novo C3GN in a kidney transplant recipient, associated with an extremely rare variant in the complement factor H (CFH) gene (c.2543G>T, p.Gly848Val) and functionally positive C3NeF. This case suggests an atypical mechanism of complement dysregulation and expands the phenotypic spectrum associated with genetic abnormalities in transplant recipients. Case Presentation

A 29-year-old Japanese man with a history of complete transposition of the great arteries, for which he underwent Jatene surgery at the age of 15 days, was diagnosed with a hypoplastic kidney at the age of 3 years during a nephrology evaluation prompted by proteinuria. Serum complement levels were within the normal range at the time of initial laboratory evaluation. He had no family history of renal or immune-related diseases. Despite conservative management, renal function gradually declined, and hemodialysis was initiated at the age of 23 years. At the time of dialysis initiation, serum complement levels remained within the normal range. He subsequently underwent an ABO-incompatible living donor kidney transplantation from his mother. Induction immunosuppression included tacrolimus, methylprednisolone, mycophenolate mofetil (MMF), and basiliximab.

The posttransplant course was stable under maintenance therapy with tacrolimus and MMF, with serum creatinine (Cr) maintained at 0.9 to 1.0 mg/dl (0.61–1.04) and no proteinuria. However, 6 years after transplantation, the patient developed mild renal dysfunction (serum Cr: 1.1–1.2 mg/dl), proteinuria (1.0 g/d), and microscopic hematuria (10–19 rbcs/hpf). Laboratory testing revealed new-onset hypocomplementemia (C3: 28 mg/dl [65–135], C4: 11 mg/dl [13–35], CH50: 18.2 [31.6–57.6]); whereas these values had been within the reference range at the time of transplantation (C3: 87 mg/dl, C4: 26 mg/dl, CH50: 46.1). Other laboratory findings included blood urea nitrogen of 16.3 mg/dl (8–20), total protein of 7.3 g/dl (6.7–8.3), albumin of 4.4 g/dl (3.8–5.3), C-reactive protein < 0.03 mg/dl (0–0.2), and normal Ig levels (IgG: 1680 mg/dl [870–1700], IgA: 267 mg/dl [110–410], IgM: 36 mg/dl [33–190]). Antinuclear antibody, antistreptolysin O, cryoglobulins, rheumatoid factor, and anti-SSA/SSB antibodies were all negative. Serum immunoelectrophoresis showed no monoclonal bands. Renal ultrasound showed no signs of hydronephrosis or allograft rejection. Flow cytometric panel reactive antibody screening was negative, excluding donor-specific antibodies.

A percutaneous renal biopsy revealed 21 glomeruli (1 globally sclerosed). Light microscopy showed mesangial proliferation and segmental double contours, consistent with membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis (Figure 1). Immunofluorescence demonstrated C3 deposition in capillary walls and mesangium, with no staining for IgG, IgA, IgM, C1q, C4, fibrinogen, or C4d. Electron microscopy showed predominantly subendothelial, nonorganized electron-dense deposits, supporting a diagnosis of de novo C3GN.

Genetic analysis revealed a rare heterozygous variant in exon 16 of the CFH gene (NM_000186.4:c.2543G>T, p.Gly848Val). This variant is extremely rare in population databases, including gnomAD v4.1 and TogoVar. Based on American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics / Association for Molecular Pathology guidelines and summarized in Supplementary Table S1, the variant met PM2 (supporting) but did not fulfill additional criteria, and was therefore classified as of uncertain significance (VUS).S1 Cascade testing could not be performed, as the patient's father was estranged and the mother declined genetic testing.

C3NeF was evaluated. Under physiological conditions, the C3 convertase (C3bBb) is rapidly inactivated; however, in the presence of serum factors that impair the regulatory activity of factor H and factor I, C3bBb persists. This prolonged stabilization is interpreted as a positive result for C3NeF (assay details are presented in Supplementary Text S1).S2 Autoantibodies against both factor H and factor B were negative. In addition, no IgG binding to C3bBb was detected when diluted patient serum was applied to an immunoplate coated with C3bBb.

Despite initial treatment with intensified immunosuppression on top of maintenance therapy with tacrolimus and MMF, including an angiotensin II receptor blocker, direct renin inhibitor, and sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitor, the patient’s condition worsened. Ten months after diagnosis, serum Cr increased to 1.34 mg/dl, proteinuria reached 5.2 g/d, and CH50 declined to 10.3. Corticosteroid therapy was initiated at 35 mg/d (0.8 mg/kg/d) and tapered to 9 mg/d over 9 months. Given the patient’s postoperative heart failure history, oral prednisolone (0.8 mg/kg) was added without pulse therapy, consistent with previous reports.S3 Improvement was evident after 6 weeks, and by 9 months, serum Cr, proteinuria, and CH50 had nearly normalized (0.81 mg/dl, 0.65 g/d, and 34.5, respectively). Although eculizumab and plasma exchange were considered, they were not initiated because of a favorable response to corticosteroids.

Discussion

The patient presented with an extremely rare variant in the CFH gene, accompanied by functionally positive C3NeF. To date, only 8 cases of de novo C3GN have been reported,5, 6, 7, 8, 9 underscoring the rarity and clinical significance of this case (Supplementary Table S2). Compared with previously reported cases, the onset in our case occurred 6 years after transplantation, which is consistent with previous reports (median 5.5 years, range: 62 days to 10 years). Regarding prognosis, approximately half of the cases progressed to end-stage kidney disease.

C3GN with identified pathogenic variants in complement genes is considered a genetic disease; however, as illustrated by the present case, many instances occur sporadically without a known family history, likely because of incomplete penetrance or de novo variants.S4 The central question is whether the CFH variant, C3NeF, or both contributed to disease pathogenesis.

To investigate this, we first confirmed the presence of functional C3NeF. Although IgG binding to the C3bBb was not detected in the patient’s serum, C3bBb assembled on an immunoplate remained stable upon serum addition, strongly indicating impaired regulation of the alternative complement pathway. Autoantibodies against both factor H and factor B—known to stabilize C3bBb—were negative. This pattern suggests that the CFH variant may have contributed to the persistence of convertase activity.

Binding domains for C3b on factor H have been mapped to short consensus repeat (SCR) domains 1 to 4, 12 to 14, and 19 to 20.S5 The variant identified in this case was located in exon 16, corresponding to SCR 14, one of the key C3b-binding regions. Alteration in this domain could plausibly impair factor H–mediated regulation of convertase activity. Previous studies have reported that 7% to 50% of C3GN cases demonstrate both genetic abnormalities and C3NeF positivity,S2,S6 thus supporting a model in which structural variants may enhance or mimic acquired convertase-stabilizing mechanisms.

This case highlights the possible contribution of a rare CFH variant (c.2543G>T, p.Gly848Val) in the absence of detectable autoantibodies. Although C3NeF activity was functionally present, the lack of IgG binding suggests an atypical mechanism of convertase stabilization, likely mediated by structural alteration of factor H. This interpretation is consistent with the fact that C3NeF assays measure residual C3bBb (C3 convertase) activity rather than the presence of IgG autoantibodies, and resembles the mechanism described for gain-of-function CFB variants such as p.Lys323Glu, which stabilize the convertase independent of IgG autoantibodies.S7,S8 This case illustrates the diagnostic and therapeutic complexity of de novo C3GN in transplant recipients and reinforces the importance of functional complement testing and molecular diagnostics in guiding etiology-specific treatment strategies.

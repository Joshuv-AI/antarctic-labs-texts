Urine-guided Hydration and Kidney Injury: Reply
Authors: Shun-Cai Gao, Dong-Xin Wang
Published: 2026

In Reply:

We appreciate the comments from Delaporte et al.1 and Kim et al.2 on our recent article, “Effect of Urine-guided Intraoperative Hydration on Incidence of Acute Kidney Injury after Cytoreductive Surgery and Hyperthermic Intraperitoneal Chemotherapy for Pseudomyxoma Peritonei: A Randomized Trial.”3

As requested, we provide a summary of intraoperative stroke volume variation in table 1; the results were well within the target range and did not differ between the two groups. We also provide the area under threshold of intraoperative hypotension; the area under mean arterial pressure of less than 65 mmHg was lower in the urine-guided group than in the control group, indicating less intraoperative hypotension burden with intervention. During the original trial, clinicians who were responsible for postoperative care were blinded to group assignment; postoperative management, including fluid administration (crystalloids, colloids, and blood products), was conducted according to routine practice. We collected the total volume of daily infusion but did not separate different types of fluids. The incidences of hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, and hypophosphatemia within postoperative days 0 to 2 are provided in table 1 and did not differ between groups.

We did not measure other biomarkers for acute kidney injury (AKI) (e.g., neutrophil gelatinase–associated lipocalin or IGFBP-7/TIMP-2). Data of urine output within the first 12 h and changes of serum creatinine from baseline within the first 7 days after surgery are provided in table 1. Hourly urine output was higher in the urine-guided group during the first 3 h, possibly due to intraoperative diuretics. There were no differences regarding daily creatinine changes during the first 7 postoperative days. However, as can be noted in Supplemental Digital Content table S1 of our article, between-group differences in AKI prevalence were more prominent on postoperative days 3 to 6, during which most intraperitoneal chemotherapy was performed.3 Therefore, the observed reduction in postoperative AKI could not be merely attributed to the intraoperative diuretic effect. Conducting urine-guided hydration during postoperative intraperitoneal chemotherapy may provide further renal protection, but this requires confirmation. Considering the limited sample size of our trial, other endpoints, including delirium and major complications, should be considered exploratory in nature and verified in the future.

Energy-efficient transmit antenna selection with Fast-ABC-Boost
Authors: Xiaofeng Yang, Jie Qiu, Danlei Mo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAntenna selection is an appealing energy-efficient solution for multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems. This paper handled the antenna selection problem in MIMO systems as a multi-class classification task, and propounded an antenna selection technique for energy efficiency (EE) maximization based on Fast-Adaptive Base Class-Boost (Fast-ABC-Boost) which boosts the classification performance of many “weak” classifiers, i.e. regression trees, to produce a powerful “committee” to make classification decision. Simulation results prove the superiority of Fast-ABC-Boost over the up-to-date learning method based on Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and the conventional optimization driven Cyclic Binary Particle Swarm Optimization (CBPSO) approach in terms of EE performance with feasible complexity.Keywords: Antenna selection, EE, Multi-class classification, Boosting, MIMOSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) technology plays an important role in 5G and 6G wireless networks to achieve high spectral efficiency (SE) and spatial diversity by employing large number of antennas at the base station (BS)1–3. However, subsequent deployment of one radio frequency (RF) chain per antenna leads to substantial hardware cost and power consumption, which prompts the development of Green Communication techniques to enhance the energy efficiency (EE) of the system4. This demand could be meet via antenna selection approaches which select a subgroup of transmit antennas according to maximum energy efficiency criterion and connect to RF chains much less than total antennas. In general, antenna selection problem can be formulated as optimization of EE subject to certain transmit power budget and Quality of Service (QoS) requirement of SE. The optimal solution can be obtained by exhaustive search over all possible antenna subgroups regarding the EE metric, which has prohibitive computational cost in massive MIMO system. Paper5 investigated the ergodic effective EE optimization of adaptive transmit antenna selection scheme by jointly adjusting the channel gain threshold and the transmit power. But the single-user system model of5 was difficult to extent to multi-user scenario. The work in6 selected the energy focusing beams corresponding to the nonzero elements of analog precoding matrix which was determined by maximizing the array gains for each user, yet involved with complex computation of Lagrangian transform. The study in7 selected the antennas which had effective gain in the optimal precoding direction and adopted a gradient descent algorithm for the EE maximization, which could be significantly affected by initial values setting. The authors of8 conducted joint transmit power control and antenna selection with binary particle swarm optimization (BPSO) which emulated the intelligent searching process for food of insects (particles) swarm, in which random permutation of initial population resulted in local optimum very likely. Cyclic BPSO (CBPSO) based antenna selection scheme was applied in9, in which cyclic permutation of initial population replaced random permutation in conventional BPSO to enhance the EE performance. However, the convergence speed of CBPSO was pretty slow.

Recently, burgeoning Machine Learning (ML) techniques for categorization or prediction tasks are well-known for extraordinary performance and feasible complexity, which distinguishes themselves from conventional optimization approaches. Theoretically, antenna selection problem can be treated as multi-class classification task to categorize the input features into one optimal antenna subgroup. However, tackling the antenna selection problem with EE metric from ML perspective is still in a nascent stage. There are only a few publications addressing this issue in recent literature. A Neural Network (NN) based beam selection method was provided in10 to manage the trade-off between energy consumption and QoS, which need to tune plenty modeling parameters therefore could led to poor generalization. A Reinforcement Learning (RL) approach was introduced in11 to jointly determine the beam choice and power allocation for EE maximization, based on large amount of trial and error which generated unstable modeling very likely. A Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) algorithm was presented in12 to enable beam selection and power allocation with maximum EE, which integrates the mechanism of deep neural network (DNN) and RL therefore is supposed to surpass NN and RL in EE perspective while inherits aforementioned drawbacks of NN and RL. Analysis of aforementioned ML based algorithms reveals that there are still much room for improvement in classification accuracy or computational complexity. This article propounded an innovative antenna selection strategy founded on Fast-Adaptive Base Class-Boost (Fast-ABC-Boost) evolved from Adaptive Base Class-Boost (ABC-Boost) and Robust LogitBoost algorithms13,14. Both ABC-Boost and Robust LogitBoost are superior boosting algorithms for multi-class classification, while the former one has high computational cost of induced from the exhaustive search procedure for the base class from K classes and L boosting iterations13. Fast-ABC-Boost inherits the high classification accuracy and robustness from ABC-Boost and Robust LogitBoost respectively, and it also reduces the computational cost of ABC-Boost to about 14. As far as we know, this is the first work in literature to conduct antenna selection via Fast-ABC-Boost which surpasses the up-to-date optimization methods and similitude ML techniques. The following lists the main contributions of this paper: (1) Traditional antenna selection strategies are complex optimization methods. This paper suggested to handle the antenna selection task for EE maximization with the state-of-the-art multi-class classifier Fast-ABC-Boost, achieving robust function as a practicable alternative. (2) This article devised an innovative input feature with the largest eigenvalues of the Hermitian transpose of the channel correlation matrix to grasp the discriminative characteristics of the system power consumption, so as to screen for the best antenna subgroup. (3) ‌Substantial‌ simulation experiments were conducted to evaluate the performance of the proposed scheme and compare it with the up-to-date ML technique DRL and the traditional optimization method CBPSO in terms of EE performance and complexity which proved the advantage of the propounded scheme.

Methods

From a multi-class classification and decision-making perspective, this paper proposed to handle the antenna selection problem in MIMO systems by classifying the input data into one of the possible antenna subgroups that meets the maximum EE criteria. Therefore, it is promising to address this issue with the extraordinary multi-class classifier and predictor presented in this Section, which fundamentally boosts the classification performance by a more robust classifiers “committee” than some other single classifier or decision-maker. The superiority and efficiency of the proposed method is convinced by extensive experiments results shown in Sect. 4.

ABC-Boost and Fast-ABC-Boost

ABC-Boost is a superior boosting algorithm based on the following two key ideas19: (1) It imposes a constraint of the K-class probability function such that only the values for K−1 classes are necessary. Therefore, one can choose a base class and built (K−1) regression trees as “weak” classifiers only for (K−1) classes with minimum weighted square error; (2) At each boosting step, one can adaptively and greedily choose the optimal base class to achieve the smallest training loss. Although ABC-Boost achieves remarkable success in classification accuracy, it suffers from prohibitive complexity of the exhaustive search scheme for the base class.

Fast-ABC-Boost is a unified framework to reduce the computational cost of ABC-Boost by effectively identifying the base class based on the following three key ideas14: (1) Instead of screening over all the K classes in the exhaustive search strategy, Fast-ABC-Boost only searches for “s-worst classes” at each boosting iteration to decide the base class. Proper setting of s could achieve the best trade-off between classification accuracy and computational efficiency compared with s = 1 and s =K; (2) It only conducts the search for “s-worst classes” at every (g+1) iterations, where g is a “gap” parameter. Because once a base class is determined, it may still be a fairly good choice as the base class so as to save the search labor for the next few iterations; (3) It conducts more stable and more efficient Robust LogitBoost procedure in the first iterations as “warm up” to obtain a robust model and then switch to above Fast-ABC-Boost procedures beginning from the (w+1)-th iteration for better classification accuracy.

Results and discussion

Substantial simulation experiments were conducted to evaluate the performance of the propounded antenna selection approach with Fast-ABC-Boost. Two baseline methods to be compared with are the up-to-date ML technique DRL12 and the traditional optimization method CBPSO9. The algorithms implementation, model simulation, training and testing were conducted with the open-source Python-based PyTourch platform20.

To implement Fast-ABC-Boost, the primary parameters setup is as follows21: the number of iterations L = 20, the “worst classes” strategy parameter s = 2, the “gap” strategy parameter g = 10 and the “warm up” strategy parameter w = 10. Figure 3 compares EE performance with respect to transmit power of the three antenna selection methods with hybrid precoding as Eq. (5)(7)(8) to fully digital precoding with one RF chain per BS antenna as Eq. (5)(6), i.e. without antenna selection. A reasonable range of transmit power would be 24–40 dBm15. In this range, it is evident from Figure 3 that the proposed method Fast-ABC-Boost outperforms the other two counterparts in terms of EE performance. The superiority of Fast-ABC-Boost stems from a more powerful “committee” of many “weak” classifiers, i.e. regression trees, to boost the classification accuracy, than DRL with a single agent to make decision based on large amount of trial and error which generated unstable modeling with poor generalization very likely, and optimization driven CBPSO. Furthermore, although fully digital precoding excels in spectral-efficiency perspective thanks to the multiplexing gain of utilizing all transmit antennas, its EE performance is inferior to hybrid precoding methods because of substantial power consumption for deploying one RF chain per BS antenna. For the rest of the experiments, the three antenna selection methods in comparison apply hybrid precoding. In addition, All the EE trends first increase then decrease as transmit power increases, which implies that although sum-rate may gain from increasing transmit power, EE not necessarily benefits from increasing transmit power.

EE vs. transmit power.

Figure 4 illustrates EE performance vs. number of selected antennas. Set the number of data streams per users to be 1 in this experiment. Figure 4 indicates that EE achieved with Fast-ABC-Boost surpasses that with DRL and CBPSO irrespective of the number of selected antennas. This again confirms the classifiers “committee” advantage of Fast-ABC-Boost based antenna selection over the single unstable decision-maker of DRL and optimization driven CBPSO. For the three approaches in comparison, selecting 4 or 6 antennas instead of applying all available antennas achieve the highest EE which proves the effectiveness of antenna selection in terms of EE performance. This is because a lower number of selected antennas with lower spatial diversity and multiplexing gain result in lower sum-rate which could cause lower EE. As the number of selected antennas increase, the sum-rate also increases which could lead to higher EE. However, As the number of selected antennas increase, the growing power consumption of RF chains sets an upper bound of EE.

EE vs. number of selected antennas.

The advantage of Fast-ABC-Boost over DRL and CBPSO is confirmed again by Figure 5 which exhibits EE performance vs. different number of users . It is signified that EE is the largest for Fast-ABC-Boost, medium for DRL and the smallest for CBPSO, unaffected by the number of users. This proves again the superb EE performance of Fast-ABC-Boost with classifiers “committee” boosting over DRL with a single poorly-generalized decision-maker and optimization driven CBPSO. It is also noted that EE achieved by all the three schemes increases with ascending number of users as it is intuitive that more users lead to larger sum-rate with the same power consumption. However, the slopes of all the three EE plots decrease with ascending number of users as more users bring about stronger inter-user interference therefore less data rate per user with the same power consumption.

Conclusions

This paper coped the antenna selection problem regarding EE maximization in MIMO systems with the excellent multi-class boosting classifier Fast-ABC-Boost which outshines the up-to-date learning algorithm based on DRL and conventional optimization driven OBPSO method in terms of EE performance and computational cost. Consequently, Fast-ABC-Boost is a highly compelling antenna selection method for MIMO system. The future work will generalize the system model to a multi-cell multicasting scenario.

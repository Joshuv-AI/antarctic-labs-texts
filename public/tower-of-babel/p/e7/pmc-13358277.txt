Adjuvant penpulimab in very-high risk clear cell renal cell carcinoma: a prospective, non-randomized, controlled phase II trial with integrated plasma multi-omics analyses
Authors: Yaohui Wang, Dongxing Wang, Houming Zhao, Yanming Zhou, Qiyang Liang, Qingbo Huang, Cheng Peng, Junnan Xu, Gang Guo, Guoqiang Yang, Luyao Chen, Yang Xun, Jun Du, Xin Ma, Xu Zhang, Liangyou Gu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposePatients with very-high risk clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) remain at substantial risk of recurrence after nephrectomy. However, heterogeneity in benefit from adjuvant immunotherapy observed in the KEYNOTE-564 trial, together with limited prospective data in this population, warrants further investigation.MethodsThis multicenter, prospective, non-randomized, controlled phase II trial enrolled patients with very-high risk ccRCC following nephrectomy. Patients received either adjuvant penpulimab or routine surveillance, with 1:1 propensity score matching (PSM) applied to balance baseline characteristics. The primary endpoint was disease-free survival (DFS), with secondary endpoints including overall survival (OS) and safety. Plasma proteomic and metabolomic profiling was conducted for exploratory analyses.ResultsGiven the non-randomized design, baseline imbalances were addressed by PSM, yielding well-balanced cohorts of 87 patients per group. Adjuvant penpulimab was associated with improved DFS compared with surveillance (HR, 0.37; 95% CI 0.16 to 0.89; p=0.026), whereas OS data remained immature. Consistent with Kaplan-Meier estimates, 1-year (94.25% vs 80.46%) and 2-year (88.68% vs 75.38%) DFS rates favored the penpulimab group (log-rank p=0.026). Penpulimab was generally well tolerated, with most treatment-related adverse events being grade 1–2. Plasma proteomic and metabolomic analyses revealed enrichment of immune response-related pathways in patients without disease progression, with circulating biomarkers associated with DFS.ConclusionsAdjuvant penpulimab was associated with improved DFS and a manageable safety profile in patients with very-high risk ccRCC. Plasma-based biomarkers associated with disease progression may inform postoperative risk stratification and warrant further validation.Trial registration numberChiCTR2200062189.Keywords: Kidney Cancer, Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor, Biomarker

Introduction

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) has the highest cancer-related lethality among urological malignancies, with clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) as the predominant subtype.1 2 Although radical or partial nephrectomy is potentially curative for localized disease, postoperative recurrence remains common in patients with adverse clinicopathological features.3 4 Long-term follow-up studies have shown that approximately 30–50% of patients with high-risk ccRCC develop disease recurrence within 5 years in the absence of adjuvant therapy, highlighting a substantial unmet need for effective postoperative interventions.5,7

For more than a decade, adjuvant systemic therapy for high-risk ccRCC failed to demonstrate consistent benefit, with multiple randomized trials of tyrosine kinase inhibitors yielding largely negative results.8,10 This paradigm was reshaped by the KEYNOTE-564 trial, which demonstrated that adjuvant pembrolizumab significantly improved disease-free survival (DFS) and subsequently overall survival (OS) compared with placebo, establishing immune checkpoint inhibition as an effective adjuvant approach.7 11 Nevertheless, several questions remain. Prospective evidence supporting adjuvant programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) blockade in Asian populations is limited,12 13 and the routine clinical implementation of pembrolizumab in China is constrained by accessibility and reimbursement considerations. Moreover, clinical benefit of adjuvant immunotherapy appears heterogeneous, particularly among patients at very-high risk of recurrence, underscoring an incomplete understanding of the biological processes associated with disease recurrence in this setting.14,16

In this context, the biological determinants underlying disease recurrence during adjuvant immunotherapy, particularly in the minimal residual disease setting, remain incompletely defined. While most biomarker studies in RCC have focused on tumor tissue,17 peripheral blood-based approaches provide a minimally invasive platform to capture systemic host-tumor interactions and evolving immune states.18 Here, we conducted a multicenter, prospective, controlled phase II study to evaluate adjuvant penpulimab in patients with very-high risk ccRCC. In parallel, integrated plasma proteomic and metabolomic profiling was performed to identify circulating biomarkers and delineate systemic immune-metabolic features associated with disease progression during adjuvant immunotherapy.

Patients and methods

Study design and participants

This multicenter, open-label, non-randomized, controlled phase II trial was conducted in China between July 2022 and September 2025 (ChiCTR2200062189). Adult patients aged 18–80 years with histologically confirmed ccRCC and very-high risk features for postoperative recurrence were eligible. Very-high risk disease was defined by the presence of one or more of the following pathological features: pT3aG1–G2 disease with ≥2 pT3a risk factors (perirenal fat invasion, renal sinus fat invasion, renal pelvis invasion and renal vein tumor thrombus formation); pT3aG3–G4 disease; ≥pT3 b disease; lymph node metastasis (N1); or metastatic disease rendered no evidence of disease (M1 NED) following complete surgical resection.

Patients were required to have an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0–1, adequate organ function, and no prior systemic antitumor therapy. Key exclusion criteria included unresected metastatic disease (except M1 NED), active autoimmune disease, uncontrolled infection, pregnancy or lactation, or any contraindication to anti-PD-1 therapy. All patients provided written informed consent. The study was approved by the institutional ethics committees and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines.

Enrollment and group assignment

Eligible patients were enrolled after nephrectomy following full informed consent through a shared decision-making process between treating physicians and the patients, in which physicians provided clinical assessment and treatment recommendations while respecting patient preference. Given the established clinical activity of adjuvant PD-1 blockade, random treatment allocation was less feasible in this very-high risk population. Patients accordingly received either postoperative adjuvant penpulimab or routine surveillance. Given the non-randomized, patient-influenced group assignment, 1:1 propensity score matching (PSM) was applied to mitigate potential confounding and minimize baseline imbalances between groups. Propensity scores were estimated using a logistic regression model including age, sex, ECOG performance status, type of nephrectomy, pathological T stage, lymph node status, metastatic status (M stage), Fuhrman nuclear grade, and sarcomatoid differentiation. Nearest-neighbor matching without replacement was performed with a caliper width of 0.2 of the SD of the logit of the propensity score. Baseline characteristics before and after matching were compared using standardized mean differences (SMDs), with values <0.1 generally indicating optimal balance and values <0.2 considered acceptable.

Treatment administration and follow-up

Patients in the adjuvant treatment group received penpulimab 200 mg administered intravenously every 3 weeks, up to 17 cycles. Treatment was initiated after enrollment and continued until disease recurrence, unacceptable toxicity, completion of protocol-specified therapy, withdrawal of informed consent, or investigator decision based on safety considerations. From enrollment, patients in the penpulimab group also underwent protocol-specified postoperative follow-up. Patients in the observation group received protocol-defined postoperative surveillance alone, without adjuvant systemic therapy.

Postoperative follow-up was uniformly applied to both groups and included physical examination, laboratory testing, and radiological assessment using abdominal CT or MRI. Follow-up assessments were scheduled every 3 months during the first 2 years after surgery, every 6 months during years 3–5, and annually thereafter. Treatment discontinuation was defined by the occurrence of confirmed disease recurrence, grade 3–4 treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) requiring permanent discontinuation, patient request, or investigator decision. Management of TRAEs in the adjuvant penpulimab group followed protocol-specified immune-related toxicity management principles. No patients were lost to follow-up.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint was DFS, and secondary endpoints included OS and safety. DFS was defined as the time from nephrectomy to the first occurrence of disease recurrence (local, regional, or distant), or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. OS was defined as the time from nephrectomy to death from any cause.

TRAEs were defined as adverse events occurring during treatment or within 30 days after the last dose of penpulimab and were graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, V.5.0.

Blood sample collection and definition of disease progression subgroup

Peripheral venous blood samples (5 mL) were collected from patients receiving adjuvant penpulimab into EDTA anticoagulant tubes at two predefined time points: prior to the first treatment cycle (pretreatment) and prior to the second cycle. Plasma was isolated within 2 hours of collection by centrifugation at 1,600×g for 10 min at 4°C and stored at −80°C until analysis.

For plasma-based analyses, patients were stratified according to DFS. The disease progression group was defined as the occurrence of disease recurrence within 12 months, whereas the non-progression group was defined as a DFS exceeding 12 months with no evidence of recurrence throughout follow-up. Patients who did not meet these predefined criteria were excluded from plasma proteomic and metabolomic analyses. Based on progression status and plasma sampling time points, plasma samples were further categorized into four subgroups: pretreatment progression (preP), pretreatment non-progression (preNP), post-treatment progression (postP), and post-treatment non-progression (postNP).

Plasma proteomic analysis

Plasma proteomic profiling was performed using magnetic bead-based protein enrichment followed by on-bead digestion and data-independent acquisition (DIA) liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) on a Vanquish Neo UHPLC system coupled to a high-resolution Orbitrap mass spectrometer. Pooled plasma quality control (QC) samples and indexed retention time peptides were included for QC and retention time calibration. DIA raw data were processed using DIA-NN (Data-Independent Acquisition by Neural Networks) for peptide identification and protein quantification against the UniProt Homo sapiens reference proteome, with peptide-level and protein-level false discovery rates controlled at 1%.

Plasma metabolomic analysis

Plasma metabolomic profiling was conducted using a non-targeted LC-MS/MS approach. Following protein precipitation with methanol/acetonitrile containing internal standards, supernatants were analyzed on a Waters ACQUITY UPLC I-Class Plus system coupled to a Q Exactive HF Orbitrap mass spectrometer operated in both positive and negative ionization modes, using complementary reversed-phase and hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography separations. Raw data were processed using Progenesis QI for feature detection, alignment, normalization, and annotation based on public databases, including HMDB, LipidMaps, and METLIN, as well as an in-house spectral library. Features with excessive missing values or low annotation confidence were excluded.

Detailed experimental procedures for plasma proteomic and metabolomic analyses are provided in the online supplemental methods.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were summarized as medians with IQRs and compared using the Mann-Whitney U test, while categorical variables were presented as counts and percentages and compared using the χ² or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Pearson correlation was used to assess associations between continuous variables. Survival outcomes were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. Associations between clinicopathological variables and DFS were evaluated using univariable and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression models, with HRs and 95% CIs reported. The proportional hazards assumption was assessed using Schoenfeld residuals, and prespecified subgroup analyses were visualized using forest plots.

Proteomic and metabolomic data were normalized prior to analysis. Principal component analysis (PCA) was used for unsupervised visualization of global plasma molecular profiles. Differential expression analyses between progression and non-progression groups were performed using the limma package, with differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) and metabolites (DEMs) defined by |log₂ fold change|≥log₂(1.2) and p<0.05. Comparisons across four groups (preP, preNP, postP, and postNP) were conducted using Welch’s analysis of variance with appropriate multiple-comparison procedures.

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (V.4.2.3) and SPSS software (V.26.0). All tests were two-sided, and p<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Sample size calculation

Sample size was estimated based on the anticipated 2-year DFS rate. A 2-year DFS rate of 60% was assumed for the control group and 80% for the adjuvant penpulimab group, informed by prior studies, including the KEYNOTE-564 trial. To detect this difference with 80% power at a two-sided α level of 0.05, 66 patients per group were required. Allowing for an estimated 5% dropout rate, a total sample size of at least 140 patients was planned.

Results

Patient population and baseline characteristics

Between July 2022 and September 2025, a total of 247 patients with ccRCC were enrolled, including 111 patients who received adjuvant penpulimab and 136 patients managed with routine surveillance. Baseline characteristics of the overall cohort showed imbalances between different groups (online supplemental table 1). After 1:1 PSM, 174 patients were included in the final analysis, with 87 patients in each group (figure 1). Baseline characteristics of the matched cohort were generally well balanced, with all SMDs <0.2 (table 1). Overall, the median age was 61.00 years (IQR 55.00–67.00), and 72.99% of patients were male. Most patients had an ECOG performance status of 0 (89.08%) and underwent radical nephrectomy (89.66%). Lymph node metastasis was present in 6.32% of patients, and 4.60% had M1NED. Pathologically, most patients had pT3 tumors (89.66%) and Fuhrman grade 3–4 histology (89.65%), with sarcomatoid differentiation identified in 11.49%. The median follow-up duration for the matched cohort was 20.04 months (IQR 10.03–30.05).

Clinical outcomes and survival analyses

In the matched cohort, adjuvant penpulimab was associated with significantly improved DFS compared with observation alone (figure 2A). Disease recurrence or death occurred in 7 of 87 patients (8.05%) in the penpulimab group and in 19 of 87 patients (21.84%) in the observation group, corresponding to an HR of 0.37 (95% CI 0.16 to 0.89; p=0.026). OS data remained immature, with no significant difference observed between groups and median OS not reached in either arm (figure 2B). Consistent with the Kaplan-Meier estimates, 1-year (94.25% vs 80.46%) and 2-year (88.68% vs 75.38%) DFS rates were significantly higher in the penpulimab group (log-rank p=0.026), whereas OS rates were numerically higher but did not differ significantly between the two groups (online supplemental table 2).

Prespecified subgroup analyses demonstrated that the association between penpulimab and improved DFS was generally consistent across most clinicopathological subgroups (figure 2C). Similar favorable trends were observed among patients aged ≥65 years, those with ECOG performance status 0, patients with M0 disease, those with very-high risk T3 disease, and those who underwent radical nephrectomy or had Fuhrman grade 3–4 disease. Additionally, a numerically favorable association was also observed in patients with sarcomatoid differentiation. In Cox proportional hazards analyses (online supplemental table 3), adjuvant penpulimab remained independently associated with prolonged DFS after multivariable adjustment (HR, 0.32; 95% CI 0.13 to 0.77; p=0.011), and sarcomatoid differentiation independently predicted worse DFS.

Detailed recurrence timing and baseline clinicopathological characteristics of patients who experienced disease recurrence in the PSM cohort are summarized in online supplemental table 4. All patients who experienced recurrence in the observation group subsequently developed metastatic disease. One patient underwent metastasectomy and achieved an M1NED status, followed by treatment with PD-1 inhibitor. The remaining patients, who were classified as International Metastatic Renal Cell Carcinoma Database Consortium (IMDC) intermediate-risk or poor-risk, subsequently received first-line PD-1 inhibitor-based combination therapy. Similarly, patients who relapsed in the penpulimab group also developed metastatic disease and received subsequent PD-1 inhibitor-based combination therapy according to IMDC risk stratification.

Safety

Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) and immune-related adverse events (irAEs) in patients receiving adjuvant penpulimab are summarized in online supplemental tables 5,6, respectively. Overall, 43 of 87 patients (49.4%) experienced at least one TRAE, the majority of which were grade 1–2. Grade 3–4 TRAEs occurred in six patients (6.9%), including grade 3 events in four patients (4.6%) and grade 4 events in two patients (2.3%). The most common TRAEs of any grade were rash (24.1%), proteinuria (17.2%), pruritus (12.6%), hypothyroidism (11.5%), fatigue (9.2%), and hyperthyroidism (8.0%). Immune-related endocrine and metabolic adverse events were infrequent and predominantly low grade. Overall, 35 patients (40.2%) experienced at least one irAE, including grade 3–4 irAEs in 4 patients (4.6%). The most common irAEs were rash (24.1%), pruritus (12.6%), hypothyroidism (11.5%), and hyperthyroidism (8.0%). Serious TRAEs occurred in five patients (5.7%), including grade 4 diabetic ketoacidosis and myocarditis (1.1% each), and grade 3 adrenal insufficiency, hyperglycemia, and hypersensitivity (1.1% each). No treatment-related deaths were reported.

Baseline plasma molecular features across clinicopathological stratifications

Paired plasma samples collected before and after adjuvant therapy were available from 36 patients in the postoperative adjuvant penpulimab cohort. Baseline characteristics of this subset and the occurrence of TRAEs are summarized in online supplemental tables 7,8, respectively. Plasma proteomic and metabolomic profiling was performed to characterize systemic molecular features associated with tumor aggressiveness and TRAEs.

Exploratory enrichment analyses indicated that baseline plasma profiles varied across key indicators of tumor aggressiveness, including lymph node status, metastatic history, Fuhrman nuclear grade, and sarcomatoid differentiation (online supplemental figure 1). Pathways related to osteoclast differentiation and protein stress responses, including proteasome-associated processes, were progressively enriched with increasing tumor aggressiveness. In parallel, baseline plasma profiles also differed according to the occurrence of TRAEs (online supplemental figure 2), with enrichment of pathways related to lipid metabolism and inflammatory mediator signaling, including arachidonic acid metabolism and inflammatory mediator regulation of TRP channels.

Plasma proteomic features associated with disease progression

Representative imaging findings from a patient with disease progression are shown at baseline (online supplemental figure 3A) and at the time of progression following adjuvant immunotherapy (online supplemental figure 3B). According to predefined criteria, 8 patients with disease progression (P) and 15 patients without progression (NP) were included in plasma proteomic analyses, yielding a total of 23 paired plasma samples collected at baseline and after treatment (preP, preNP, postP, and postNP).

At baseline, PCA of plasma proteomic profiles demonstrated clear separation between the preP and preNP groups (figure 3A). Consistently, volcano plot identified 53 DEPs between the two groups, including 23 proteins upregulated and 30 proteins downregulated in the preP group (figure 3B). Functional enrichment analysis of these DEPs revealed significant upregulation of complement and coagulation cascade pathways in the preP group, whereas multiple antitumor immune-related pathways, including B-cell receptor signaling, were significantly downregulated (figure 3C). Protein–protein interaction (PPI) network analysis highlighted several immune-related hub proteins, including IGF2BP3, ADA2, and MS4A1 (figure 3D). Post-treatment plasma profiles also differed according to progression status. PCA analysis again demonstrated distinct clustering between postP and postNP groups (figure 3E). Differential expression analysis identified 77 DEPs, of which 21 were upregulated and 56 were downregulated in the progression group (figure 3F). Enrichment analysis revealed that pathways related to PI3K–AKT signaling and neutrophil extracellular trap formation were significantly upregulated in postP patients, whereas multiple antitumor immune-related pathways, including antigen processing and presentation, were significantly downregulated (figure 3G). PPI analysis identified immune response-related hub proteins, including S100A16, S100A11, and HLA-A (figure 3H). Additionally, plasma MAdCAM-1 levels were slightly higher in the preP group than in the postP group, without statistical significance (online supplemental figure 3C). Plasma KIM-1 levels measured by ELISA were consistently higher in progressors than in non-progressors before and after treatment, with no statistically significant difference observed (online supplemental figure 3D).

Plasma metabolomic features associated with disease progression

Plasma metabolomic analyses were performed in 23 baseline and 20 post-treatment plasma samples, after exclusion of hemolyzed specimens. PCA analysis demonstrated clear separation among these groups (figure 4A). At baseline, 101 DEMs, including 48 upregulated and 53 downregulated metabolites, were identified between preP and preNP groups, whereas 94 DEMs distinguished postP from postNP groups, including 64 upregulated and 30 downregulated metabolites (figure 4B).

At baseline, visualization of DEMs revealed that metabolites significantly downregulated in the preP group were predominantly gut microbiota-derived metabolites associated with immune activation, including 5-(3’,4’-dihydroxyphenyl)-γ-valerolactone 4’-sulfate (figure 4C). In contrast, metabolites upregulated in the preP group were largely related to lipid metabolic reprogramming, including DG (18:2/22:6) and MGMG (16:2/0:0). Correlation analysis demonstrated coordinated metabolic alterations, with lipid metabolism-related metabolites showing inverse correlations with gut microbiota-associated metabolites (figure 4D). Enrichment analysis further indicated upregulation of one-carbon and folate cycle-related pathways and downregulation of pathways associated with favorable immunotherapy responses, such as caffeine metabolism, in the preP group (figure 4E). Post-treatment plasma metabolomic profiles exhibited similar patterns. In postP patients, microbiota-associated metabolites linked to immunotherapy responsiveness were consistently downregulated, whereas metabolites involved in tumor-associated lipid metabolic reprogramming, including arachidonic acid methyl ester, cholesterol sulfate, and MGMG (16:2/0:0), were upregulated (figure 4F). Correlation analysis confirmed persistent inverse associations between gut microbiota-related metabolites and lipid metabolism-related metabolites (figure 4G). Enrichment analysis of post-treatment DEMs revealed activation of tumor-associated metabolic pathways, including tryptophan metabolism and choline metabolism in cancer, accompanied by suppression of multiple redox-related and antioxidant-related pathways, such as cysteine and methionine metabolism and ascorbate and aldarate metabolism (figure 4H).

Integrated plasma protein-metabolite analysis associated with disease progression

To integrate plasma proteomic and metabolomic features associated with disease progression, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO)-penalized Cox proportional hazards regression based on DFS was applied.19 Among 53 baseline DEPs, four plasma proteins, namely MS4A1, NDUFAF7, RPS13, and PSAPL1, were identified as most strongly associated with DFS (figure 5A and online supplemental figure 4A). In parallel, eleven baseline metabolites were identified as DFS-associated features (figure 5B and online supplemental figure 4B). Importantly, all selected proteins and metabolites were identified through DFS-based Cox and LASSO-Cox analyses rather than differential expression analyses alone, supporting their association with clinically relevant outcomes. Correlation analysis revealed coordinated relationships between the selected proteins and metabolites (online supplemental table 9). After exclusion of 1 metabolite without significant correlations, 4 plasma proteins and 10 plasma metabolites were integrated and visualized using a Sankey diagram, illustrating coordinated protein-metabolite interaction patterns associated with disease progression (figure 5C).

Baseline expression analyses showed higher levels of MS4A1 (CD20) and RPS13 in non-progressors, whereas NDUFAF7 and PSAPL1 were enriched in progressors (figure 5D). Comparative metabolomic analyses demonstrated distinct baseline differences between groups (figure 5E). Palmitoyl glucuronide, 4-methylcatechol sulfate, isochenodeoxycholic acid, butenylcarnitine, and D-chiro-inositol were significantly elevated in the preP group, whereas 3-hydroxyhippuric acid, lithocholic acid, glycyl-serine, 4-ethylphenol glucuronide, and 4-methylbiphenyl were significantly lower compared with the preNP group. Heatmap visualization of baseline expression levels for the four selected plasma proteins and 10 selected plasma metabolites further revealed distinct molecular patterns between patients with and without subsequent disease progression (figure 5F). Collectively, these analyses indicate that integrated plasma proteomic and metabolomic features capture coordinated biological signals associated with disease progression.

Discussion

Patients with very-high risk ccRCC remain at substantial risk of recurrence after surgery, underscoring the need for effective adjuvant systemic therapy.3 4 In this prospective, controlled phase II trial, we report several clinically and biologically relevant findings. To our knowledge, this is the first trial conducted in a Chinese population to evaluate a domestically developed PD-1 inhibitor, penpulimab, as adjuvant therapy for ccRCC. We demonstrate that adjuvant penpulimab was associated with prolonged DFS and a manageable safety profile. Beyond clinical efficacy, we further show that disease progression following adjuvant therapy is accompanied by distinct systemic molecular features. Patients who progressed exhibited consistent suppression of antitumor immune-related plasma proteins, along with depletion of gut microbiota-associated immunostimulatory metabolites and enrichment of metabolites indicative of metabolic reprogramming, both at baseline and after treatment. Importantly, integrated plasma multi-omic analyses further identified a set of candidate plasma biomarkers strongly associated with disease progression within the adjuvant treatment cohort.

From a clinical perspective, adjuvant therapy remains central to the perioperative management of ccRCC. Although neoadjuvant approaches have gained increasing interest, many patients ultimately still require postoperative systemic treatment, and the long-term survival impact of neoadjuvant therapy may be difficult to disentangle from the effects of subsequent adjuvant interventions. To date, multiple phase III trials have evaluated adjuvant strategies in ccRCC; however, KEYNOTE-564 remains the only study to demonstrate significant benefits in both DFS and OS,7 20 21 thereby establishing the clinical value of adjuvant immunotherapy and leading to recommendation by the National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines.22 Despite this progress, evidence supporting adjuvant immunotherapy in Asian populations and with domestically developed PD-1 inhibitors remains limited. In this context, our study extends existing knowledge by focusing on a very-high risk population and evaluating penpulimab as an alternative PD-1 inhibitor. The 2-year DFS rate observed in our cohort was numerically comparable to that reported in KEYNOTE-564, although follow-up duration in our study was shorter. Although no significant OS difference was observed at the current analysis, OS data remain immature. Moreover, the widespread use of subsequent PD-1 inhibitor-based therapies after recurrence in both study groups may have attenuated potential differences in long-term survival outcomes. Importantly, the safety profile observed was consistent with the known class effects of PD-1 blockade, characterized predominantly by low-grade adverse events and a low incidence of severe immune-related toxicities, supporting the feasibility of penpulimab in routine clinical practice.

Beyond clinical outcomes, plasma multi-omic analyses provided insight into systemic biological features associated with disease progression during adjuvant immunotherapy. Both tumor-intrinsic malignant behavior and therapy-induced immune modulation are reflected at the circulating level,23 rendering plasma an attractive and minimally invasive medium for translational investigation.24,26 Consistent with prior studies, baseline plasma proteomic and metabolomic profiles varied across clinicopathological risk strata.16 27 Importantly, not all patients derive benefit from adjuvant immunotherapy, as exemplified by data of KEYNOTE-564 and IMmotion010.7 21 The ability to distinguish patients who are likely to benefit from those who derive limited benefit yet remain exposed to treatment-related toxicity and cost, therefore has substantial clinical implications. Previously reported circulating markers, including KIM-1 and MAdCAM-1, have been associated with outcomes in the adjuvant or advanced RCC setting.16 27 In line with prior reports, soluble MAdCAM-1 levels exhibited dynamic changes, with decreasing trends in patients who experienced disease progression and increasing trends in non-progressors, without reaching statistical significance. Differences between advanced disease and the postoperative adjuvant setting, as well as the use of predefined marker thresholds in earlier studies, may partly account for the attenuated discriminatory performance observed here. Although KIM-1 was not detected in the untargeted plasma proteomic dataset, ELISA-based quantification demonstrated higher plasma KIM-1 levels in patients with disease progression than in non-progressors both before and after treatment, likewise without statistical significance, potentially due to limited sample size and interindividual variability. Taken together, the concordant patterns observed for these previously reported circulating biomarkers support the continued investigation of plasma markers as potentially informative tools for evaluating disease progression risk in RCC. Moreover, enrichment analyses of DEPs revealed coordinated downregulation of key antitumor immune pathways, including B-cell receptor signaling and antigen presentation, in patients with disease progression. In parallel, plasma metabolomic profiling highlighted23 complementary systemic alterations, characterized by reduced gut microbiota-associated immunostimulatory metabolites and enrichment of metabolites indicative of lipid metabolic reprogramming in progressing patients. These observations align with emerging evidence that gut microbiota-derived metabolites can modulate immune cell function and influence immunotherapy efficacy,28 29 whereas tumor-associated metabolic reprogramming, particularly involving lipid metabolism, contributes to immune evasion and therapeutic resistance.30 31 Together, integrated plasma proteomic and metabolomic profiling captures coordinated immune, metabolic, and microbiota-related features of systemic host-tumor interactions, suggesting its potential as a translational approach to characterize immunotherapy responsiveness and disease progression in the adjuvant ccRCC setting.

Candidate plasma biomarkers were identified through DFS-based Cox and LASSO-Cox analyses. Collectively, these markers are biologically consistent with pathways previously associated with responsiveness to immune checkpoint blockade, including gut microbiota-associated metabolism, host metabolic adaptation, and immune effector function, with particular relevance to B cells, tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS), and T cell-mediated antitumor immunity.32,34 Isochenodeoxycholic acid is a bile acid derivative and some bile acid derivatives have been shown to regulate Th17/regulatory T cell balance and immune homeostasis, thereby modulating antitumor immune responses.35 In parallel, metabolites such as 4-methylcatechol sulfate, 4-ethylphenol glucuronide, and 3-hydroxyhippuric acid represent circulating readouts of diet-microbiota co-metabolism, and some such co-metabolic patterns may reflect impaired microbial metabolic activity or dysbiosis.36,38 Within this framework, the observed correlations between gut microbiota-associated metabolites and immune-related plasma proteins, including MS4A1 (CD20), suggest that reduced microbial metabolite availability may be consistent with alterations in B cell-mediated immune organization and immune activation.39 40 Similarly, associations between RPS13 and glycyl-serine may be related to biological processes involved in T-cell activation.41 42 Among the identified proteins, MS4A1 (CD20) is of particular interest, given accumulating evidence linking tumor-associated B cells and TLS to responses to immune checkpoint blockade by facilitating antigen presentation, T-cell priming, and antibody-mediated effector functions.43,45

Several limitations of this study warrant consideration. First, the non-randomized design introduces the possibility of residual confounding despite propensity score matching, and therefore treatment comparisons should be interpreted with appropriate caution. Second, the relatively limited number of DFS events and the current duration of follow-up may affect the precision of survival estimates, and longer follow-up will be required to determine whether the observed DFS benefit translates into an OS advantage. Third, plasma multi-omic analyses were restricted to patients receiving adjuvant penpulimab and included a relatively modest sample size, which may have limited statistical power and precluded direct biomarker comparisons between treatment groups. Accordingly, these findings should be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating rather than definitive. Validation in larger, independent cohorts will be necessary to assess their robustness and generalizability. Despite these limitations, this study provides prospective clinical and translational evidence supporting the use of adjuvant penpulimab in patients with very-high risk ccRCC. Moreover, integrated plasma proteomic and metabolomic analyses identified systemic features associated with disease progression and may inform future biomarker development and patient stratification in the adjuvant setting.

In conclusion, this prospective, controlled phase II study demonstrates that adjuvant penpulimab is associated with improved DFS and a manageable safety profile in patients with very-high risk ccRCC, supporting the potential use of a domestically developed PD-1 inhibitor in the postoperative adjuvant setting. Beyond clinical efficacy, integrated plasma proteomic and metabolomic analyses identified distinct systemic immune and metabolic features associated with disease progression before and after treatment. These findings highlight the potential of minimally invasive, plasma multi-omic profiling to capture biological heterogeneity in the adjuvant immunotherapy setting. Further validation in larger, independent cohorts will be required to confirm these observations and to assess their clinical utility.

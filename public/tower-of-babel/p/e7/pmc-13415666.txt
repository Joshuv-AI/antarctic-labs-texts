Moderate and Severe Exacerbations and Healthcare Resource Utilization in Chinese Patients with COPD on Triple Therapy: A Retrospective Database Study
Authors: Ke Huang, Yanan Cui, Tingting Huang, Xiaoning He, Kailing Wang, Dan Shen, Yingrui Xu, Ting Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionDespite receiving triple therapy (i.e., long-acting β2-agonist + long-acting muscarinic antagonist + inhaled corticosteroid), patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) continue to experience exacerbations. This study aimed to investigate the moderate and severe annualized exacerbation rate (AER) and healthcare resource utilization of patients with COPD on triple therapy in China, addressing a significant evidence gap regarding the clinical and economic disease burden in this population.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study was conducted using the Chongqing Regional Electronic Health Record Database (2018–2022). Patients with COPD aged ≥ 40 years who had received ≥ 3 months of triple therapy during the 12-month baseline period were included. AER, COPD-related hospital visits, length of hospital stay and readmissions, and direct medical costs during the 12-month observation period were analyzed.ResultsA total of 6393 patients with COPD on triple therapy were included in the study. Among patients with available data, 17.3% had blood eosinophil count of ≥ 300 cell/μL at baseline. The percentage of patients having at least one moderate and severe exacerbation during the observation period was 53.7%, with the moderate and severe AER (95% confidence interval) being 1.37 (1.32–1.42). The mean (standard deviation (SD)) numbers of outpatient, emergency room, and inpatient visit due to COPD were 6.1 (5.56), 0.4 (1.58), and 1.0 (1.64), respectively. The direct medical costs per patient per year were ¥14,208.7.ConclusionOur findings reveal a high disease burden among patients with COPD in China despite stable triple therapy, reflecting the high-risk clinical profile of these patients and supporting an ongoing need for improved management strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12325-026-03627-y.Keywords: Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, Exacerbation, Healthcare resource utilization, Triple therapy, Blood eosinophil count

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study? Although triple inhaled therapy is recommended for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and has proven efficacy in reducing exacerbations, many patients still experience moderate and severe exacerbations, and real-world data on exacerbation rates and healthcare resource utilization in Chinese patients on triple therapy were lacking, necessitating this study.This study investigated the moderate and severe exacerbation rates and healthcare resource utilization of patients with COPD on triple inhaled therapies in real-world scenarios in China leveraging a regional electronic health record (rEHR) database. What was learned from the study? This study found that 53.7% of Chinese patients with COPD receiving stable triple therapy experienced at least one moderate or severe exacerbation within 1 year, with an annualized exacerbation rate of 1.37 and substantial hospital visits and medical costs.Subgroup analysis revealed that patients with older age, asthma, smoking history, advanced GOLD stage, and high baseline exacerbation risk had even higher exacerbation rates.The findings highlight a significant disease burden among Chinese patients with COPD despite guideline-recommended triple therapy, reflecting the high-risk clinical profile of these patients and supporting an ongoing need for improved management strategies.

Introduction

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a heterogeneous lung condition characterized by chronic respiratory symptoms due to abnormalities of the airways and/or alveoli that cause persistent, often progressive, airflow obstruction [1]. In China, COPD was diagnosed among approximately 100 million people in 2015, with a prevalence rate of 13.7% among adults aged 40 years or older, according to a nationwide survey [2]. Acute exacerbation is the primary reason for hospitalization of patients with COPD and is significantly associated with disease progression and poor prognosis, e.g., increased mortality, decreased quality of life, and further deterioration of lung function [3].

The goals of pharmacological treatments for COPD primarily consist of reducing symptoms and future risk of exacerbations [4]. Inhaled bronchodilators, particularly long-acting β2-agonist (LABA) and long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA), play a fundamental role in maintenance therapy for COPD [1]. The decision whether to add inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) on top of inhaled bronchodilators or not largely depends on the exacerbation history and the level of blood eosinophil count (EOS). Particularly, higher blood EOS is an important biomarker of type 2 inflammation and is associated with a better response to ICS [5]. Compared with dual therapy (i.e., LAMA + LABA), triple therapy (i.e., LAMA + LABA + ICS) has been found to significantly reduce exacerbations and improve lung function and health status, based on the evidence from previous randomized controlled trials (RCTs) on COPD [6–8].

Despite the strong evidence supporting the efficacy of triple therapy in COPD, there are still significant evidence gaps in this area. First, a significant number of patients do not respond well to triple therapies. Three global phase III RCTs suggested that approximately 31–50% of patients with COPD receiving triple therapies still experienced at least one moderate and severe exacerbation annually [7, 9, 10]. Moreover, a higher dose of ICS failed to further improve endpoints related to exacerbations in one of these studies [9]. Second, real-world studies investigating the exacerbation among patients with COPD with triple therapies in China are scarce with certain limitations, including lack of annualized exacerbation rate (AER) or blood EOS data, and small sample sizes [11–14]. Third, reliable data on the hospital visit and economic burden for triple therapy patients within the Chinese healthcare system are still lacking.

Therefore, to address evidence gaps in this regard, this study investigated the moderate and severe exacerbation rates and healthcare resource utilization of patients with COPD on triple inhaled therapies in real-world scenarios in China leveraging a regional electronic health record (rEHR) database.

Methods

Study Design and Data Source

This was an observational, retrospective cohort study using secondary data. The index date was defined as the first prescription date of maintenance inhaled therapy for COPD within the 12- to 24-month window before a patient’s most recent visit record between January 01, 2021 and December 31, 2023. The 12 months before the index date were defined as the baseline period, and the 12 months after the index date were defined as the observation period. Data were obtained from the Chongqing Regional Electronic Health Record Database, a city-level database covering about 32 million residents in Chongqing city, a municipality in Southwest China. Patient-level data were aggregated and linked across 241 hospitals, including 42 tertiary hospitals and 151 secondary hospitals when the study was carried out. The database contains integrated data on both inpatient and outpatient care. Relevant data were extracted from public health information systems such as hospital information system (HIS), community health information systems, and crowd monitoring systems. The study has been approved by the ethics committee of China–Japan Friendship Hospital (Approval No. 2024-KY-367) and Tianjin University (Approval No. TJUE-2024-473), with informed consent waived for the participants. The study has obtained all necessary permissions for using the Chongqing Regional Electronic Health Record Database.

Study Population

Patients diagnosed with COPD who received at least one prescription of maintenance inhaled therapies for COPD and aged 40 years or above at the index date were identified between January 01, 2019 and December 31, 2022. Among them, patients were included in the study if they were prescribed triple therapy for at least 3 months during the 12-month baseline period. This definition was intended to identify patients with stable baseline use of triple therapy, rather than assess adherence, persistence, or continuity of treatment during follow-up. The study design schematic is illustrated in Appendix S1 in the Supplementary Material. The derivation rules of inhaled therapy regimen are shown in Appendix S2 in the Supplementary Material.

Study Outcomes

The number of moderate and severe exacerbation episodes was assessed during the observation period. A hospital visit was considered exacerbation-related if the diagnosis list included COPD and exacerbation, or COPD and lower respiratory tract infection (e.g., pneumonia). Moderate exacerbation was defined as an exacerbation accompanied by treatment with a systemic glucocorticoid and/or antibacterial antibiotics (excluding antimycobacterial drugs) in the same visit. Severe exacerbation was defined as an exacerbation requiring hospitalization, emergency room (ER) visit, or resulting in death. Any two consecutive exacerbations with an interval of ≤ 14 days were considered as occurring in one exacerbation episode. The severity of an exacerbation episode depended on the highest severity level of exacerbations within this episode.

The number of outpatient visits, ER visits, and hospitalizations due to COPD were calculated during the observation period. A visit was considered resulting from COPD if the visit diagnosis list included COPD and exacerbation, or COPD and lower respiratory tract infection (e.g., pneumonia), or primary diagnosis list included COPD. Additionally, the number of hospitalizations due to severe exacerbation, length of stay of hospitalizations due to severe exacerbation, and 30-day/90-day all-cause/severe exacerbation related readmission were measured during the observation period. Direct medical cost per patient per year, per moderate exacerbation episode, and per severe exacerbation episode of COPD-related visits were measured during the observation period.

Statistical Analysis

Descriptive analyses were performed for all variables. Continuous variables were summarized using mean and standard deviation (SD). Categorical variables were summarized as counts (n) and proportions (percentage) of total participants having the given characteristic. Missing data were not imputed in this study. The total number of participants with non-missing data was reported for all types of variables and it was used as the denominator for percentage calculation.

The AER was calculated using the negative binomial regression model with log-link function. The dependent variable in the negative binomial model was the number of events that occurred during the observation period. Log-transformed observation duration was used as offset variable. The estimated AER and its two-sided 95% confidence interval (CI) were derived.

To address incomplete cost capture for certain visit records, the annual direct medical cost per patient was derived at the population level by combining the mean numbers of COPD-related outpatient/ER and inpatient visits per patient with the corresponding mean visit-level costs. Therefore, this measure represents a derived population-level estimate rather than a patient-level annual cost distribution.

Subgroup analyses were performed for AER by age group, sex, smoking status, Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) stage, and with/without asthma, as well as for patients with high exacerbation risk (high exacerbation risk subset, defined as having ≥ 2 moderate or ≥ 1 severe exacerbation episodes during the baseline period), and were performed for direct medical cost in the high exacerbation risk subset.

Statistical analyses were performed using SAS® Version 9.2 or higher (SAS Inc., Cary, N.C., USA) and the latest stable version of R.

Results

Patient Attrition

Among 737,033 patients diagnosed with COPD, only 13.2% (97,073/737,033) were prescribed maintenance inhaled therapy at the index date, and 0.9% (6393/737,033) received ≥ 3 months of triple therapy at baseline and were therefore included in this study. Of the included patients, 53.5% (3,418/6,393) were included in the high exacerbation risk subset (Fig. 1).

Baseline Characteristics

The enrolled participants had a mean age of 70.1 years, with 83.4% being male. Smoking status was available for 414 patients (6.5%) in the study population; among those with available data, 54.3% and 27.8% were former smokers and current smokers, respectively. Cardiovascular disease (CVD) (59.4%) and asthma (20.5%) were the two most prevalent comorbidities. Patients with blood EOS ≥ 300 cells/μL accounted for 17.3% of those with available blood EOS data. Regarding baseline triple therapies, ICS/LABA + LAMA (66.6%) and ICS/LABA/LAMA (27.2%) were predominant regimens. Moderate and Severe Exacerbation

During the 1-year observation period, 53.7% of patients had at least one moderate and severe exacerbation, with 45.8% of patients experiencing at least one severe exacerbation. The moderate and severe AER (95% CI) was 1.37 (1.32–1.42) [moderate 0.39 (0.36–0.42); severe 0.98 (0.94–1.02)] (Table 2).

Hospital Visit

The mean (SD) numbers of outpatient visits, ER visits, and hospitalizations due to COPD during the observation period were 6.1 (5.56), 0.4 (1.58), and 1.0 (1.64), respectively. The mean (SD) number of hospitalizations due to severe exacerbation was 1.0 (1.60). The mean (SD) length of hospitalization due to severe exacerbation was 29.1 (36.44) days per year. Of the 2819 patients with hospitalizations due to severe exacerbation, 25.9% and 40.8% of them had 30-day and 90-day all-cause readmissions; additionally, 22.2% and 36.3% of them had 30-day and 90-day severe exacerbation-related readmissions (Table 3).

Direct Medical Cost

Among the study population, the direct medical cost per year per patient was 14,208.7 in Chinese yuan (CNY) and 1,989.2 in US dollar (USD). The direct medical costs per moderate exacerbation episode and per severe exacerbation episode were 1,022.5 in CNY [143.2 in USD] and 10,215.7 in CNY [1,430.2 in USD], respectively. The high exacerbation risk subset showed consistently higher costs compared to the overall study population, with the direct medical cost per year per patient of 19,840.4 in CNY and 2,777.7 in USD, direct medical cost per moderate exacerbation episode of 1,054.1 in CNY and 147.6 in USD, and direct medical cost per severe exacerbation episode of 10,420.9 in CNY and 1,458.9 in USD (Table 4).

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is among the few large real-world studies in China to descriptively characterize the real-world burden of exacerbations and healthcare resource utilization among patients with COPD receiving stable triple therapy (i.e., triple therapy for at least 3 months over a 1-year period). It also offers novel insights into the clinical characteristics (e.g., blood EOS) and treatment patterns within this population. By leveraging an rEHR database that encompasses most hospitals across various tiers in Chongqing, we obtained a large and region-wide well-representative sample. The closed-loop nature of the database further enabled the relatively complete capture of cross-institutional medical records.

According to the GOLD guideline, triple therapies were primarily recommended for group E patients with blood EOS ≥ 300 cells/μL [1]. Previous studies estimated that approximately 40% of patients with COPD fall into group E [15, 16], and about 20% exhibit blood EOS ≥ 300 cells/μL [17, 18]. However, our study revealed that only 0.9% of diagnosed patients with COPD received continuous triple therapy for over 3 months within a 1-year baseline period. This low stable use rate suggests a significant gap on receiving standardized maintenance inhaled therapy between clinical guidelines and real-world practice. Additionally, considering that only triple therapies sustained for over 3 months were counted in our study, the observed low rates may also reflect poor adherence to these therapies.

Our results highlighted that patients with COPD in China receiving triple therapy for over 3 months still experienced a high burden of exacerbations. In our study, 53.7% of participants experienced at least one moderate and severe exacerbation within a 1-year period, which is notably higher than the approximately 30% reported in a previous Chinese study of patients receiving triple therapy [19]. This discrepancy may be attributed to regional difference, and variations in hospital tier distributions, as the prior study predominantly involved tertiary hospitals, whereas the Chongqing database covered a large proportion of secondary and primary hospitals in addition to tertiary hospitals. Furthermore, several other Chinese studies also reported these percentages, but the values showed minimal variation from the aforementioned prior study, as they utilized highly overlapping data sources, differing only in their focus on specific patient subgroups [14, 20, 21]. Among the participants in our study, the estimated moderate and severe AER was 1.37, slightly higher than the 1.05 observed in a previous real-world study using claims data from Taiwan, China [22]. Further pharmacological and non-pharmacological interventions are warranted to reduce the exacerbations of patients already on triple therapy.

Subgroup analyses revealed higher AERs in specific subgroups: patients aged 65 years or older, patients with comorbid asthma, male patients, smokers, patients with advanced GOLD stage, and patients with high exacerbation risk at baseline. Previous studies have also identified older age, comorbid asthma, smoking history, advanced GOLD stage, and high exacerbation risk as risk factors for exacerbations [23–27]. Contrasting with some prior findings that suggested female patients are more susceptible to exacerbations, our study results indicate higher AER among male patients [23, 28]. Given that certain studies in China did not identify sex as a predictor of exacerbations, further research in China is warranted to determine if the exacerbation risk trends between male and female patients differ from those observed in other countries [25, 26]. Regarding the correlation between blood EOS and exacerbations, our study did not observe a clear trend among patients with varying blood EOS, while current evidence remains inconclusive [24, 29, 30]. Notably, the complexities inherent to real-world research may obscure this relationship. For instance, populations may exhibit substantial intra-individual heterogeneity in blood EOS levels due to rapidly changing status of comorbidities and treatments. Furthermore, the clinical context prompting EOS testing remains unclear in our study; therefore, some patients might have undergone complete blood counts testing for non-COPD-related reasons (e.g., infections, anemia). Overall, the findings of the subgroup analysis underscore the importance of systematically considering these patient characteristics in clinical assessments of exacerbation risk among patients with COPD, while also highlighting the necessity of implementing personalized preventive interventions and management strategies for subgroups prone to frequent exacerbations.

Currently, data concerning the hospital visits of patients with COPD receiving triple therapy in mainland China is limited. A prior study estimated the annual average number of inpatient visits at 1.23 for triple therapy users [31], which slightly exceeds our findings of 1.0 visits per year. Nevertheless, the aforementioned study only included four hospitals with unspecified tiers and the definition of COPD-related inpatient visit was unclear, which resulted in low comparability with our study. Additionally, reliable real-world evidence from mainland China regarding the number of outpatient and ER visits among patients on triple therapy, as well as readmission rates following hospitalizations due to severe exacerbations, remains scarce. Our study significantly contributes to the existing evidence base in this domain.

Outside Mainland China, our study exhibited overall similar or slightly higher numbers of COPD-related outpatient visits, ER visits, and inpatient visits compared with previous studies from Hong Kong [32], Germany [33], and South Korea [34]. In comparison to studies from the USA [35–37], the numbers of COPD-related outpatient visits in our study were similar, ER visits were slightly lower, whereas hospitalizations were notably higher. This discrepancy may be attributed to the differences in healthcare accessibility, as many Chinese individuals tend to seek inpatient hospital care first [38]. Furthermore, our estimated 30-day all-cause readmission rates exceeded those reported in several international studies [39, 40], necessitating enhanced management strategies and comprehensive post-discharge care to improve patient outcomes and reduce healthcare resource utilization. Collectively, our findings underscore the fact that Chinese patients receiving triple therapy continue to require frequent hospital visits, even shortly after discharge from hospitalizations related to severe exacerbations. This aligns with the marked exacerbation burden observed in our study, further underscoring the persistent healthcare need encountered by patients with COPD already on guideline-recommended inhaled therapy. Specifically, the higher readmission rates compared with those reported in other countries necessitate enhanced management strategies and comprehensive post-discharge care to improve patient outcomes and reduce healthcare resource utilization.

Our study revealed that the annual average COPD-related direct medical costs for triple therapy patients were 14,208.7 in CNY and 1,989.2 in USD. Using the 2022 per capita gross domestic product (GDP) of 90,633 in CNY and 12,689 in USD for Chongqing city as reference [41], these annual costs represent approximately 15.7% of per capita GDP for those on triple therapy, indicating the high economic burden of COPD for this population. Additionally, we observed markedly higher costs among the high exacerbation risk subset, underscoring the potential long-term economic impact posed by exacerbation history.

Previous studies on the economic burden of COPD in China remain scarce, particularly for patients receiving triple therapy. Existing cost estimates primarily derived from three sources: patient surveys [42], pharmacoeconomic models with physicians’ input on key parameters [43, 44], and site-based real-world studies [31, 45]. Direct comparison between our study and existing studies is limited by the discrepancies between studies in terms of study settings and definitions of COPD-related direct medical cost. In a previous site-based study in China based on medical chart review of four hospitals, the annual average medical costs for patients receiving triple therapy were 7,245.65 in CNY and 1,014.39 in USD, which were lower than our results [31]. Detailed methodological information from the referenced study was unavailable for direct comparison. Another landmark real-world study conducted across 50 hospitals in six regions provided critical insights into COPD-related costs stratified by disease severity [45]. However, the cost data exclusively for patients on triple therapy was not investigated, and the definition of COPD-related direct medical costs was also not clearly specified in the study. Overall, our study is the first to provide reliable data on the economic burden of Chinese patients receiving triple therapy, based on a large sample-size, closed-loop rEHR database.

An additional consideration when interpreting our findings is treatment adherence, which was not assessed in this study. Inhaler adherence in COPD is often suboptimal in real-world practice, with review-level evidence suggesting that adherence may be as low as 30–50% [46]. Recent studies from China also indicate substantial non-adherence to inhaled maintenance therapy, with one multicenter study reporting suboptimal adherence in 66.6% of patients [47], another study showing good adherence in only 37.8% of patients [48], and the REAL study reporting a mean adherence of 59.0% for long-acting inhalers [49]. Therefore, the observed burden in our study may partly reflect real-world treatment use patterns in addition to the underlying clinical risk profile of this population.

Regarding blood EOS, we observed blood EOS ≥ 300 cells/μL in 17.3% of patients with COPD on triple therapy in China, suggesting a notable prevalence of type 2 inflammation. The estimate falls within the previously reported wide range of 9.4–22.9% for blood EOS ≥ 300 cells/μL in the Chinese COPD population [17, 18, 50, 51], and roughly aligns with the 20–40% prevalence reported for type 2 inflammation, which might be defined by biomarkers besides blood EOS [52]. Notably, blood EOS data were available for only a minority of patients, and the analysis relied on a single measurement closest to the index date. Therefore, EOS-related findings should be interpreted cautiously, and the absence of a clear trend across EOS categories should not be overinterpreted. The high missing rate of blood EOS records in the current study might reflect the relatively low awareness and practice of blood EOS testing for patients with COPD, suggesting that the clinical significance of type 2 inflammation, as well as the value of blood EOS levels in guiding the use of ICS and biologics [1], has not yet been well recognized in clinical practice.

Regarding comorbidities, asthma was recorded in 20.5% of patients in the present cohort. This estimate is broadly comparable with previously reported figures for concomitant asthma or asthma–COPD overlap in COPD populations, including global estimates of approximately 25–29.6% and Chinese estimates of 18.6–21.99%, although prevalence varies across studies depending on population characteristics and case definitions [53–56]. Therefore, while some degree of diagnostic overlap cannot be excluded in this real-world database study, the observed asthma prevalence does not appear implausibly high in the context of the published literature.

The study exhibits several limitations. Firstly, COPD identification relied on physician-recorded diagnosis names and codes in the database, without mandatory spirometry confirmation. Therefore, diagnostic misclassification cannot be excluded. However, the observed asthma prevalence in this cohort was broadly comparable to previously reported estimates in COPD populations, suggesting that substantial over-ascertainment due to asthma-related misclassification may be less likely, although caution remains warranted in interpreting the findings. Secondly, because exacerbations were identified using operational definitions based on routinely recorded diagnoses, treatments, and healthcare encounters, some degree of misclassification may remain in real-world clinical practice. The moderate and severe AER may be underestimated because of the study’s eligibility criteria, which excluded patients who died within a year of commencing maintenance inhaled therapy. Thirdly, Chongqing is a developed municipality in China. Variations in clinical practices among physicians and local policies (e.g., drug availability and reimbursement) across regions with differing economic levels and disease burdens could influence the generalizability of our study findings, as these variations may affect the outcomes observed in the specific region. Therefore, caution should be exercised when applying the study findings to other areas with potentially different disease burdens and clinical practices. Fourthly, smoking history was available for only a small proportion of patients because it was extracted from routine clinical documentation rather than systematically collected for all patients in the database. As a result, smoking-related findings should be considered descriptive and interpreted with caution. In addition, treatment adherence, persistence, and inhaler technique were not assessed in this study. These factors may have influenced the observed exacerbation burden and healthcare resource utilization.

Conclusion

Our findings suggest a high disease burden among patients with COPD in China receiving stable triple therapy, in terms of moderate and severe exacerbations and healthcare resource utilization. The observed exacerbation burden likely reflects, at least in part, the high-risk clinical profile of patients receiving stable triple therapy in routine practice. Nevertheless, the findings indicate that substantial disease burden remains in this population, supporting an ongoing need for improved management strategies. Our study provides valuable insights into the disease burden, demographic and clinical characteristics, and treatment patterns of patients with COPD receiving stable triple therapy, opening avenues for future research and healthcare improvements. These findings should be interpreted as descriptive estimates of disease burden in a real-world population of patients with COPD receiving stable triple therapy.

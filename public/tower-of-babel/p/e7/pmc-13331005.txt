Low-density lipoprotein cholesterol management and goal attainment at 1 year in patients with diabetes, pre-diabetes and normoglycaemia: results from the European, prospective, observational SANTORINI study
Authors: Hermann Toplak, Joris J Komen, Aurélie Bardet, Alberico L Catapano, Kausik K Ray, Herrmann Toplak
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo assess changes in lipid-lowering therapy (LLT) use and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) goal attainment through 1-year follow-up in patients stratified by diabetes status in SANTORINI (Treatment of High and Very High riSk Dyslipidemic pAtients for the PreveNTion of CardiOvasculaR Events in Europe – a MultInatioNal ObservatIonal study).DesignProspective, observational study.Setting623 primary (general practitioners, internal medicine specialists or internists) and secondary (cardiologists, diabetologists, lipidologists or neurologists) care sites in 14 European countries.ParticipantsA total of 7210 high- and very high-cardiovascular risk adults recruited from 17 March 2020 to 11 February 2021. At baseline, patients were grouped as having diabetes, pre-diabetes or normoglycaemia and analysed if they had LDL-C measurement at baseline and 1-year follow-up.Primary and secondary outcome measuresThe following were assessed by diabetes status at baseline and 1 year: use of LLT, including assessment of monotherapy and combination therapy; LDL-C values and the proportion of patients with LDL-C goal attainment based on the absolute values in the 2019 European Society of Cardiology/European Atherosclerosis Society guidelines (high risk: <1.8 mmol/L (55 mg/dL); very high risk: <1.4 mmol/L (70 mg/dL)); and levels of other lipid profile parameters, including non-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (non-HDL-C), HDL-C, triglycerides and total cholesterol.ResultsOf 7210 patients, 3181 (44.1%) had diabetes, 1202 (16.7%) pre-diabetes and 2827 (39.2%) normoglycaemia. The majority (72.1%) were male; mean (SD) age was 65.0 (10.9) years. From baseline to 1 year, use of combination therapy increased across all groups, with a smaller increase for patients with diabetes (25.9% to 37.9%) compared with pre-diabetes (28.5% to 43.7%) and normoglycaemia (28.9% to 45.1%). The change in LDL-C levels over 1 year was −0.3 mmol/L in patients with diabetes, −0.4 mmol/L in pre-diabetes and −0.4 mmol/L in normoglycaemia. At 1 year, LDL-C goal attainment increased in each group (diabetes: 26.6% to 34.8%; pre-diabetes: 18.3% to 29.0%; normoglycaemia: 16.4% to 27.3%).ConclusionsLDL-C goal attainment over 1 year was higher for patients with diabetes than for those with pre-diabetes and normoglycaemia. However, two-thirds of patients with diabetes did not attain their LDL-C goals, which may be attributable to low rates of combination therapy use, highlighting the need to improve lipid management in this high-risk population.Trial registration numberNCT04271280.

Diabetes significantly increases the risk of developing cardiovascular disease (CVD), and patients with diabetes tend to have earlier, more severe adverse cardiovascular (CV) events compared with those without diabetes.13 This risk is reflected in the recommendations from the 2019 European Society of Cardiology (ESC)/European Atherosclerosis Society (EAS) guidelines for dyslipidaemia management, which identify diabetes as an independent risk factor for CVD and include the presence and duration of diabetes as key criteria for CV risk classification.4

Considering the higher baseline CV risk in patients with diabetes versus those without, there is a particular need for optimal low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) management in this population.3 4 The SANTORINI (Treatment of High and Very High riSk Dyslipidemic pAtients for the PreveNTion of CardiOvasculaR Events in Europe–a MultInatioNal ObservatIonal) study (NCT04271280) evaluated changes in lipid-lowering therapy (LLT) regimens and attainment of risk-based LDL-C goals (based on absolute thresholds in the 2019 ESC/EAS guidelines) over a 1-year follow-up period.46 In the overall population, 20% of high-risk and very high-risk patients achieved their risk-based LDL-C goal at baseline.5 Although LDL-C goal attainment increased over 1 year, likely driven by an increased use of combination LLT from 25.6% to 37.9% of patients, only 31.0% of patients reached the 2019 ESC/EAS guideline-recommended LDL-C goals at 1 year, and the incidence of major adverse CV events remained high.6 Post hoc subgroup analyses comparing outcomes in patients receiving combination therapy initiation versus statin up-titration and by age groups, have been performed.7 8 However, this is the first analysis in patients with diabetes. Therefore, the objective of this post hoc analysis was to compare changes in LDL-C goal attainment, lipid profile and use of LLT from baseline through 1-year follow-up in patients stratified by diabetes status, using data from the SANTORINI study.

Research design and methods

Study design

The methodology and rationale for the SANTORINI study have been reported previously.5 6 9 Briefly, SANTORINI was a prospective observational study in high and very high-CV risk adults recruited from 17 March 2020 to 11 February 2021, after the publication of the 2019 ESC/EAS guidelines (August 2019). Patients were recruited by 623 primary (general practitioners, internal medicine specialists or internists) or secondary (cardiologists, diabetologists, lipidologists or neurologists) care sites in 14 European countries.

Data were collected at baseline and 1-year follow-up from the patient records of lipid management-related visits during which a patient had been seen by the physician. Patients were included in the current analysis if they had an LDL-C measurement at baseline and 1-year follow-up.

Study outcomes

In this post hoc analysis, all study outcomes were analysed by glycaemic status: (1) diabetes (including patients with type 1 diabetes and type 2 diabetes), defined as patients with a documented clinical diagnosis of diabetes mellitus or patients with a diagnosis of normoglycaemia and impaired glucose tolerance (patients who were not clinically diagnosed with diabetes but showed laboratory results consistent with either impaired glucose regulation or pre-diabetes) or patients receiving glucose-lowering medication or patients who met the laboratory criteria for diabetes defined as haemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) >6.4% (46 mmol/mol) or fasting glucose >7.0 mmol/L (126 mg/dL); (2) pre-diabetes, defined as patients with no documented clinical diagnosis of diabetes but with abnormal laboratory values at baseline (HbA1c 5.7–6.4% (39–46 mmol/mol) or fasting glucose 5.6–7.0 mmol/L (100–125 mg/dL)), and (3) normoglycaemia, defined as patients with no documented clinical or laboratory criteria (HbA1c <5.7% and fasting glucose <5.6 mmol/L) for diagnosis of diabetes or pre-diabetes. This definition of diabetes was broader and more clinically sensitive than the definition applied in the previously published 1-year follow-up paper results from the overall population of the SANTORINI study,6 which was based solely on documented medical history.

Patient baseline characteristics were compared across diabetes statuses. Baseline CV risk was classified by the investigator based on 2019 ESC/EAS guidelines (high or very high risk), the available guidelines at the time of this analysis, and compared with central reassessments.4 In the 2019 ESC/EAS guidelines, patients with type 2 diabetes are categorised as being at high risk if they have diabetes without target organ damage, one additional risk factor, or have had diabetes for ≥10 years. Patients are considered to be at very high CV risk if they have diabetes-related target organ damage (e.g., microalbuminuria, retinopathy or neuropathy), ≥3 major risk factors or have had early-onset type 1 diabetes mellitus for >20 years.4 Data are not shown for the central reclassification of patients at low or intermediate risk as the proportions were very small (high/very high risk was an inclusion criterion for the SANTORINI study).

The following were assessed by diabetes status at baseline and 1 year in the overall population, and further stratified by CV risk (high or very high) according to the investigator: the use of LLT, including assessment of monotherapy and combination therapy; LDL-C values and the proportion of patients with LDL-C goal attainment based on the absolute values in the 2019 ESC/EAS guidelines (high risk: <1.8 mmol/L (55 mg/dL); very high risk: <1.4 mmol/L (70 mg/dL))4 and levels of other lipid profile parameters, including non-high density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), HDL-C, triglycerides and total cholesterol. Apoprotein B levels were unavailable for most patients.

Statistical analysis

The analysis was conducted as a post hoc evaluation, without formal hypothesis testing, and was not specifically designed to assess differences across groups based on diabetes status. All data were analysed descriptively with results presented as standard summary measures (mean and SD), median and IQR, counts and proportions. No imputation was performed for missing data.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A comprehensive description of baseline characteristics for the entire SANTORINI population has been previously published.5 6 9 For this analysis, a total of 7210 patients with LDL-C values available at baseline and 1 year were analysed. Of these, 3181 (44.1%) had diabetes, 1202 (16.7%) had pre-diabetes and 2827 (39.2%) were normoglycaemic. The mean (SD) age was 65.0 (10.9) years, and the majority (72.1%) were male (table 1; online supplemental table 2). Overall, 72.0% of patients with diabetes, 83.7% of patients with pre-diabetes, and 78.7% of patients with normoglycaemia had a history of atherosclerotic CVD. Use of any diabetes medication was reported for 61.8% of patients with diabetes, including metformin (51.0%), sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (16.7%), and glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (7.6%; table 1; online supplemental table 2).

Classification of CV risk at baseline

At baseline, 71.8% of patients were classified by the investigator as very high CV risk (72.5% of patients with diabetes, 73.4% with pre-diabetes, and 70.2% with normoglycaemia; online supplemental figure 1 and table 1).4 When patients were centrally classified for CV risk based on the 2019 ESC/EAS guidelines, a higher proportion of patients were classified as very high risk overall (88.8%), and in each group (93.3% with diabetes, 87.9% with pre-diabetes, and 84.1% with normoglycaemia; online supplemental figure S1). This corresponds to an underestimation in the proportion of very high-risk patients by the investigator across all three groups, the highest of which was in patients with diabetes (28.7%), compared with patients with pre-diabetes (19.8%) or normoglycaemia (19.9%; online supplemental figure S1).

Comparison of LLT at baseline and 1 year

At baseline, a higher proportion of patients with diabetes were receiving any LLT (82.6%) compared with pre-diabetes (76.5%) and normoglycaemia (74.5%; table 2). At 1 year, the proportion of patients receiving any LLT increased and was similar across groups (96.9%–97.3%).

From baseline to 1 year, the use of statin monotherapy increased similarly in patients with pre-diabetes (41.9% to 47.8% (13.9% relative increase)), and patients with normoglycaemia (41.6% to 47.1% (13.4% relative increase)). The use of statin monotherapy increased slightly in patients with diabetes (52.3% to 54.3%, respectively (3.7% relative increase)). High-intensity statin use increased across all groups from baseline to 1 year (diabetes: 21.4% to 24.8% (16.0% relative increase), respectively; pre-diabetes: 19.6% vs 27.2% (39.1% relative increase), respectively; and with normoglycaemia: 19.6% vs 24.6% (25.5% relative increase), respectively), with similar proportions in each group receiving them at 1 year.

The proportion of patients receiving any LLT as monotherapy increased from baseline to 1 year, with patients with diabetes showing the smallest increase but the highest proportion of patients receiving LLT monotherapy at both time points (56.7% to 59.0%, respectively (4.1% relative increase)), compared with patients with pre-diabetes (48.1% to 53.6%, respectively (11.4% relative increase)) and patients with normoglycaemia (45.7% to 52.0%, respectively (13.7% relative increase); table 2).

The proportion of patients receiving any combination therapy increased from baseline to 1 year, regardless of diabetes status. Use of combination therapy increased from 25.9% at baseline to 37.9% at 1 year in patients with diabetes (46.3% relative increase), from 28.5% to 43.7% for patients with pre-diabetes (53.5% relative increase), and from 28.9% to 45.1% in patients with normoglycaemia (56.3% relative increase).

The proportion of patients receiving combination therapy was higher in very high-risk patients compared with high-risk patients at both baseline and 1-year follow-up, based on investigator assessment of risk (tables 3 and 4). At 1-year follow-up, the proportion of very high-risk patients receiving combination therapy was 45.8% overall (diabetes: 43.4%; pre-diabetes: 46.0%; normoglycaemia: 48.6%; table 4). In high-risk patients, combination therapy use at 1-year follow-up was 31.2% overall (diabetes 23.5%; pre-diabetes: 37.2%; normoglycaemia: 37.0%; table 3).

Comparison of other lipids at baseline and 1 year

Similar improvements were observed across other components of patients’ lipid profiles (table 5). At 1-year follow-up, median non-HDL-C levels decreased across all groups; however, similar to LDL-C, the reductions from baseline were smaller in patients with diabetes (decrease from 2.6 mmol/L (100.5 mg/dL) to 2.3 mmol/L (88.9 mg/dL), respectively), compared with those with pre-diabetes (from 2.8 mmol/L (108.3 mg/dL) to 2.4 mmol/L (92.0 mg/dL), respectively), and those with normoglycaemia (2.8 mmol/L (109.1 mg/dL) to 2.4 mmol/L (92.0 mg/dL), respectively). Furthermore, median triglyceride levels at 1 year were highest for patients with diabetes versus pre-diabetes and normoglycaemia (table 5); however, the reduction from baseline was similar in the diabetes and pre-diabetes groups (0.1 mmol/L) and there was no reduction in those with normoglycaemia. No changes in median HDL-C levels were observed during follow-up in any of the three patient groups (table 5). Finally, reductions in total cholesterol were seen across all groups from baseline to 1 year (table 5). Similar findings were observed across lipid profiles when stratified by high risk and very high risk as assessed by the investigator (tables 6 and 7).

Discussion

This analysis of the observational SANTORINI study found that LDL-C levels and risk-based guideline-recommended LDL-C goal attainment improved from baseline to 1 year, regardless of diabetes status.4 Increases in LDL-C goal attainment over 1 year were higher for patients with diabetes than with pre-diabetes and normoglycaemia. Despite these improvements, 69.1% of patients overall, including 65.2% of patients with diabetes, did not attain their LDL-C goals, highlighting a potential unmet need in this high-risk patient population. However, lower baseline LDL-C values may have resulted in receipt of less intense LLT.

Baseline CV risk underestimation by the investigator versus central reclassification was greater for very high-risk patients with diabetes (28.7%) compared with pre-diabetes (19.8%) or normoglycaemia (19.9%). Moreover, the use of combination LLT at both baseline and 1 year was lowest in patients with diabetes (25.9% to 37.9%, respectively) compared with pre-diabetes (28.5% to 43.7%, respectively) and normoglycaemia (28.9% to 45.1%, respectively). Addressing CV risk underestimation, particularly in patients with diabetes, may increase utilisation of combination LLT therapy in clinical practice.

Baseline LDL-C was lower in patients with diabetes than pre-diabetes and normoglycaemia, overall and in both high-risk and very high-risk patients, which corresponded to greater ESC/EAS 2019 guideline-recommended LDL-C goal attainment. This was potentially driven by a greater use of LLT at baseline in patients with diabetes, primarily monotherapy (mostly statins). Improvements in LDL-C levels and LDL-C goal attainment were observed across all groups at 1 year, but were smaller for patients with diabetes compared with pre-diabetes. This may be driven by the comparatively more moderate intensification of LLT in these patients by 1 year especially regarding the use of combination therapy. In addition, diabetes is often associated with altered lipid metabolism as chronic exposure to hyperglycaemia and insulin resistance triggers a wide range of changes in LDL, including the formation of small, dense LDL, which has a prolonged circulation time.10 However, contrasting results have been found in some real-world studies. For example, higher LDL-C goal attainment has been observed in patients with diabetes compared with those without in the retrospective, observational TERESA (for its Spanish acronym of Consecución de Objetivos Terapéuticos En pacientes de alto Riesgo con Estatinas de alta potencia Solas o Asociadas a ezetimibe) study of 1570 patients in Spain.11 In the observational DYSIS II study of 320 patients in Thailand, LLT use was higher in patients with diabetes compared with those without.12 Regardless, there remains an unmet need for further intensification of LLT regimens in patients with diabetes in clinical practice to meet guideline-recommended LDL-C goals.3

Findings from our analysis align with evidence from randomised controlled trials suggesting that absolute reductions in LDL-C and relative CV risk reduction via LLT are similar for patients with and without diabetes.10 13 14 Lowering of LDL-C is a key therapeutic modality for CV risk reduction, and the magnitude of clinical benefit is proportional to the absolute reduction in LDL-C.15 For example, a meta-analysis by the Cholesterol Treatment Trialists’ Collaboration found a 21% relative risk reduction for CV events for each 1 mmol/L (approximately 39 mg/dL) reduction in LDL-C among patients with diabetes, highlighting the crucial role of LLTs in CV risk reduction.10 In a subgroup analysis of the CLEAR Outcomes trial (NCT02993406), reductions in LDL-C were similar for patients with diabetes, pre-diabetes, and normoglycaemia following 6 months of bempedoic acid monotherapy. There was no significant difference in relative CV risk reduction between groups; however, absolute benefits were greater for patients with diabetes due to the higher baseline risk in this population.14 Similarly, prespecified subgroup analyses of the FOURIER (NCT01764633) and ODYSSEY OUTCOMES (NCT01663402) trials found that proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 inhibition with evolocumab and alirocumab, respectively, reduced LDL-C and CV outcomes consistently in patients with and without diabetes over median follow-ups of 2.2 and 2.8 years, respectively.13 The RACING (NCT03044665) diabetes subgroup analyses reported no significant interactions over 3 years between diabetes status and treatment with ezetimibe regarding the proportion of patients achieving LDL-C levels <70 mg/dL or the primary outcome of CV death, major CV events or non-fatal stroke.16

A key limitation of this study was the lack of formal hypotheses testing; therefore, caution should be used when interpreting any associations. Furthermore, there was no differentiation between patients with type 1 or type 2 diabetes, or the time since diabetes diagnosis, despite distinct pathophysiological differences between these populations; however, it is likely that the number of patients with type 1 diabetes was small. Moreover, while an overall increase in combination therapy was observed, the study outcomes for individual patients in relation to their use of LLT could not be determined. It would also be of interest to assess, in future studies, if similar patterns are observed with System Coronary Risk Evaluation-2, which was unavailable at the time of this study.17 It should also be considered that outcomes may have improved across 1-year follow-up in this observational study as a result of better awareness of LDL-C goals and LLT use because of study participation,18 and the release of the 2019 ESC/EAS guidelines shortly before the study began.

An overall increase in LLT use from baseline to 1 year in the observational SANTORINI study resulted in lower median LDL-C and greater LDL-C goal attainment in patients with diabetes, pre-diabetes, and normoglycaemia. Despite increases in LLT use, almost two-thirds of the patients with diabetes did not attain their LDL-C goals, which may be attributed to the lowest rate of combination therapy use, highlighting an unmet need in this high-risk population. Further strategies to improve the implementation of combination LLT in real-world practice are required.

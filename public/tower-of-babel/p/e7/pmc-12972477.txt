Techno-Mainstreaming Palliative Care: The Global Palliative Doctors’ Network
Authors: Shahul H Ebrahim, Mujeeb Rahman, Shafika Banoo, Beatriz Dorsey Rivera, Maria Madeline Baylon-Mallillin, Noorjahan Begum, Preeya Desh, Thiloshini Govender, Sameer Badarudeen, Ziad Memish, Global Palliative Doctors Network
Published: 2026

The Lancet Commission Report on Palliative Care and Pain Relief in 2017 estimated that almost half of the people who die each year encounter “serious health-related suffering” that could benefit from palliative care, 80% of them in low- and middle-income countries [1]. The finding that during 2010 to 2019, the probability of dying from a non-communicable disease before the age of 80 fell in 152 countries for women and in 147 countries for men [2] implies that the number of people living with debilitating conditions are on the rise. This trend reflects the aging of global population, increased access to chronic disease care services and increased survival of people with chronic debilitating conditions. An 87% global increase in serious health-related suffering amenable to palliative care interventions is predicted by 2060 [3].

Access to palliative care remains uneven due to differences in countries’ health care development and the absence or variation of legal frameworks governing care practices and end-of-life decisions (Panel 1). Just thirty countries, comprising less than 15% of the global population, have access to the very highest level of palliative care provision [4]. Each year, an estimated 56.8 million people worldwide would benefit from palliative care, but only 14% currently receive it [3–5]. Approximately 20 million people die annually from conditions requiring end-of-life palliative care, with the vast majority living in low- and middle-income countries (78%) where services remain limited [1–5]

Despite recognition of the critical need for palliative care through global frameworks such as the World Health Organisation Resolution on palliative care, [6, 7] implementation of the recommendations remains inconsistent. Only 12% of countries are classified as having well-developed palliative care systems, highlighting gaps in integration, training, and cross-border collaboration [4].

Unlike other medical disciplines, clinicians who provide frontline palliative care face complex challenges that crosscuts ethics, religion, clinical decision-making, and law. These challenges extent beyond the scope of training they received during standard medical education. Furthermore, variation and sensitivity surrounding the legal and socio-religious framework on palliative care across countries makes the development of uniform guidance irrelevant. The challenges for clinicians include insufficient exposure to palliative medicine during their training, weak integration of palliative care to health systems, and cultural or structural barriers to advance care planning [8]. In some contexts, highly specialized physicians may inadvertently cause distress by focusing exclusively on disease-directed treatments, with limited awareness of holistic care approaches. This reality underscores a fundamental need: to strengthen peer-to-peer networks where physicians can share culturally relevant practices, collectively build a global knowledge base, and advance the palliative care discipline.

The Global Palliative Doctors Network

Conceived in 2024, during the Global Fellowship in Palliative Care as a WhatsApp group, the Global Palliative Doctors Network (GPDN) (https://thegpdn.org) evolved within a year as a practitioner-led platform for fast-track consultation, resources, and exchange to foster a collaborative global community to improve the compassionate and empathetic care of patients with catastrophic illnesses. What began as a conversation about the lack of a physician-specific forum quickly grew into a vibrant network, GPDN expanded to over 700 members from more than 70 countries by August 2025. Despite being unfunded and supported by professional time donation by its members, the explosive growth of GPDN underscores the void in palliative care for physician peer-to-peer interactions.

Contribution to Palliative Care Practice

GPDN has evolved as a the “go to” hub for all things considered in palliative care.

Real-time patient provider-to-provider consultations: Seamless real time patient referral across global time periods is the single most important feature that draws physicians to GPDN. Given that experts from all global times zones are represented in GPDN, GPDN platform facilitates consultation on ethics, treatment options, and patient triaging within minutes of submission of a request.

In summary, GPDN complements existing professional associations by providing an informal but structured space for dialogue, collaboration, and support among physicians [1, 7, 9]. GPDN’s rapid growth underscores both the lack of and desire for meaningful spaces for global connection within palliative care providers. GPDN helped democratize expertise and amplify the voices of practitioners across diverse regions. In a field constrained by lack of resources, GPDN demonstrates how low-cost, physician-led initiatives can yield disproportionate benefits — building bridges across regions, specialties, and systems- as underscored by academic and collective recommendations [1, 6, 7].The GPDN is more than a professional forum — it is a movement that reflects the shared humanity of physicians striving to provide dignity, comfort, and holistic care for patients facing serious illness. As the field of palliative medicine grows, platforms such as GPDN demonstrate the power of physician-led communities to complement formal structures, democratize knowledge, and bridge global divides. GPDN has demonstrated how omnipresent technological platforms [10] can be proactively used to fill gaps in professional networking. The task ahead is to nurture this momentum, strengthen collaborations, and ensure that every patient, regardless of geography, has access to the care they deserve.

Ultra-high voltage gain achieved with quadratic DC/DC converter design
Authors: Preeti Sharma, Sara Hasanpour, Rajneesh Kumar
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis work introduces a novel DC/DC converter with an incredibly high voltage gain, specifically designed for renewable energy generating systems. The proposed circuit features a coupled inductor integrated with a quadratic boost circuit and a voltage multiplier cell to achieve a substantial step-up in voltage gain. Ultra-high voltage gain, minimum reverse recovery in diodes, low voltage stress on switching devices, continuous input current, and a shared ground between the input source and output load are some of this topology’s key features. The coupled inductor design reduces power dissipation in magnetic components by distributing the high DC source current with an additional input inductor. Additionally, regenerative clamp circuits alleviate voltage stresses on power switches during simultaneous switching. Theoretical analysis covering the operating principle, steady-state behavior, and efficiency is discussed in detail. An evaluation of the suggested topology’s performance is conducted using a 250-W, 25-V/400-V lab prototype.Subject terms: Electrical and electronic engineering, Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

Renewable energy sources (RES), such as fuel cells and photovoltaics, generate power at low DC voltages, often below 50 V. DC-DC converters with high step-up (high voltage gain) are imperative to raise this voltage1. These converters are critical in medical devices, energy harvesting, lighting systems, and portable devices, driving substantial research interest. It is essential to utilize current-fed techniques to efficiently extract non-pulsating current from converters connected to renewable energy sources (RES) and maximize power extraction2,3. Key performance indicators for step-up DC-DC converters encompass achieving a high voltage conversion ratio, minimizing voltage stress on switching components, ensuring high efficiency, and maintaining a low component count and cost. Figure 1 shows that the practical answer to these significant problems is DC-DC boost converter technology4,5. Non-isolated step-up converters with minimal components are typically preferred in low-power applications. Several advanced DC-DC converter designs have been developed to generate high-output DC voltage from low-voltage sources. These converters use various techniques such as switched inductors, switched capacitors, cascading methods, interleaved techniques, voltage multiplier (VM) cells, and their combinations to boost the voltage conversion ratio. However, many of these designs need practical solutions, including numerous components and hard switching, which can reduce their efficiency. In addition, magnetic devices such as linked inductors (CI) or transformers, which are commonly used in conjunction with VMs, offer an alternative and efficient method to increase voltage gains by controlling the turns ratio and duty cycle. Spikes are raised across the switching elements because of the leakage inductance, so coupled inductor-based circuits require active and passive clamp circuits3,6–10.

The quadratic converter, known for its efficiency in achieving high voltage gains using a few components, operates as a DC-DC converter. Its ability to exponentially increase voltage gain with the duty cycle has spurred the development of numerous non-isolated versions boasting significant voltage improvements. Specifically, literature9–13 describes the development of transformerless, single-switch, high step-up DC-DC converters based on the quadratic boost converter principle. These models deliver considerable voltage boosts, but at the cost of requiring numerous components. Meanwhile, recent advancements have led to the introduction of ultra-high step-up quadratic boost DC-DC converters, as outlined in14–17. These innovative converters incorporate coupled inductors and voltage multipliers to manage low-voltage stress effectively. However, they encounter challenges with high input current ripple, which diminishes their suitability for RES applications. To deal with these problems, research18–23 has created quadratic CI-based DC-DC converters, which only use one switch and have less input current ripple. However, these models face considerable power dissipation in the input diodes. Further research, as seen in24–27, proposes improved step-up quadratic DC-DC converter designs that minimize voltage stress. Nonetheless, the issue of high input current ripple persists due to the serial connection of the CI and the DC input source. To get around these problems, recent papers s28,29 look at high-voltage gain circuits that use a three-winding coupled inductor (TWCI) and VM cells. These circuits offer flexible voltage conversion ratios but put a lot of voltage and current stress on important parts. In a new way of doing things30–32, describe an ultra-high step-up double-switch DC-DC converter that uses a dual-stage boost converter arrangement that effectively lowers magnetic component losses through current distribution but needs more than one component for high voltage gains. In32,33 present a novel soft-switched quadratic DC-DC converter that ensures continuous input current and low switch voltage stress, albeit with a constrained voltage gain capacity. In20,34, discuss modified TWCI-based high step-up quadratic DC-DC converters featuring improved input current profiles. Yet, they need a unified ground between input and output, raising design concerns. Other high step-up configurations outlined in35,36 exhibit partial trans-inverse characteristics, but they struggle with excessive power switch voltage stress and input diode current. Reference37 introduces a unique TWCI-based high-step-up DC-DC converter that offers a common ground and a consistent low-ripple input current, despite facing control challenges due to its sharp voltage conversion ratio. Additionally15,38, document coupled inductor-based quadratic step-up DC-DC topologies that lack a shared ground between the input and output. Lastly, a novel quadratic step-up DC-DC converter design, mentioned in39, aims to address existing limitations but needs to catch up in achieving a high voltage gain.

This paper presents a high-step-up dc-dc converter for renewable energy source applications with quadratic gain. It aims to improve the benefits and address the drawbacks of existing high-step converters. The novel topology includes an intermediate capacitor, a passive clamp network for recovering leakage energy, and an interleaved coupled inductor for attaining high voltage. This topology integrates a turns ratio into the gain expression, enhancing efficiency without increasing the duty ratio. Compared to existing converters, it reduces component count, thus lowering costs and improving efficiency. While applicable to various low-voltage sources, the paper focuses on solar PV sources, which are detailed in subsequent sections. The proposed topology offers several key advantages:

Non-ideal gain

The presence of parasitic resistance in components such as diodes, switches, and inductors has an adverse effect on the efficiency and performance of converters. These resistances lead to a voltage gain that deviate from the ideal scenario outlined in Eq. (7), resulting in lower output power than the input power. As a result, the converter’s efficiency is reduced. One way to mitigate the parasitic resistance in capacitors is using metalized polyester film capacitors (MKT) or connecting capacitors in parallel. Equation (8) demonstrates how the equivalent series resistance of switches, diodes, inductors, and capacitors affects the voltage gain, showing the consequences of these non-ideal components. Three additional terms are introduced when these parasitic elements are factored into the ideal voltage gain equation. Figure 8 compares the perfect and non-ideal voltage gains against duty cycle variations, emphasizing the impact of non-ideal factors on the voltage gain. The two curves closely match up to a duty cycle of 70%. However, beyond this point, the non-ideal voltage gain drops sharply, highlighting parasitic components’ significant effect at higher duty cycles.

Advantages and disadvantages of the proposed converter

The proposed converter introduces a novel voltage gain formula that integrates the square feature and turns ratio. This unique combination offers several advantages over traditional converters. Firstly, it significantly reduces the duty cycle, winding turns, voltage stress, and coupled inductor volume, all while operating under similar conditions. However, it’s worth noting that despite achieving the same gain as the converter referenced in28, this design requires more components. While this characteristic might increase complexity and manufacturing costs, it’s outweighed by the benefits of reduced duty cycle, winding turns, and voltage stress, leading to a more efficient and compact converter design overall.

Discussion

The examination of the converter, which has a voltage gain of 16 times, demonstrates its remarkable utility for large-scale renewable energy applications. This design maintains a remarkable 400 V output while amplification of a 25 V input is achieved at a 95.2% efficiency at 250 W power output with the use of fewer components. The strategic focus on reducing voltage stress on the components boosts the system’s reliability. It extends its lifespan, making it a pivotal aspect of the converter’s performance in power electronic systems. These attributes make the converter an ideal choice for various uses, especially within renewable energy sources like solar PV systems, where efficiency and a minimal component count are paramount. The potential for further enhancements in the converter’s design is evident, promising significant improvements in efficiency and functionality that could lead to its broad implementation across different energy conversion applications.

Conclusion

An innovative DC-DC converter with quadratic step-up features for high step-up applications is presented in this document. For reducing the ripple in the input current, a coupled inductor is used. The converter substantially reduces power losses by distributing current across magnetic components and alleviating voltage and current demands on power devices. The design allows for a significant increase in voltage multiplication yet keeps a low turn’s ratio for the connected inductor, resulting in reduced wire length and minimizing the negative effects of parasitic elements. Moreover, clamp circuits efficiently capture and utilise the leakage energy generated by the associated inductor, hence reducing the voltage stress on the switches. The experimental results obtained from a laboratory prototype with a power output of 250 W and voltage levels of 25 V and 400 V confirm that this arrangement is both feasible and effective. Therefore, this novel design offers a highly attractive option for DC/DC applications that require a large voltage gain.

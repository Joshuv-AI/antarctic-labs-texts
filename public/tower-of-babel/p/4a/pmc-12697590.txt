Middle Meningeal Artery Embolization with n‐Butyl Cyanoacrylate for the Treatment of Subdural Hematomas: The MEMBRANE Study Design
Authors: Christopher P Kellner, Fawaz Al‐Mufti, Rishi Gupta, Brian T Jankowitz, Robert M Starke, Ansaar T Rai

ABSTRACT
AbstractBACKGROUNDMiddle meningeal artery embolization with a liquid embolic is a minimally invasive adjunctive endovascular treatment for chronic subdural hematoma (cSDH). TRUFILL n‐butyl cyanoacrylate Liquid Embolic System provides a possible option for middle meningeal artery embolization. The aim of this study is to evaluate safety/effectiveness of TRUFILL n‐butyl cyanoacrylate versus standard‐of‐care alone for middle meningeal artery embolization in patients with cSDH.METHODSMEMBRANE (Middle Meningeal Artery Embolization for the Treatment of Subdural Hematomas With TRUFILL n‐BCA) is a prospective, multicenter, open‐label, randomized‐controlled trial enrolling ∼376 adults at ∼35 sites in the United States and China. Site physicians will determine whether participants will undergo surgery or nonsurgical management. After demonstrating cSDH stability, participants will be randomized within the surgical or nonsurgical cohort to receive standard‐of‐care with/without TRUFILL n‐butyl cyanoacrylate middle meningeal artery embolization, with follow‐ups at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months post procedure.RESULTSPrimary effectiveness end point is the occurrence of residual or re‐accumulation of hematoma (>10 mm) at 6 months (assessed by independent imaging core laboratory) or reoperation/surgical procedure on the hematoma within 6 months. The primary safety end point is the occurrence of all adverse events through 6 months. Other end points include good functional outcome at 3 months (modified Rankin scale score: 0–2 or no worsening from baseline if baseline modified Rankin scale score ≥3); change in hematoma volume; complete cSDH resolution; development of acute component or new cSDH; requirement for cSDH surgery; death, stroke, myocardial infarction, or thromboembolic complications; new‐onset seizures; change in modified Rankin Scale/Markwalder Grading Scale scores; quality of life; and healthcare resource use.CONCLUSIONResults will provide valuable information on the safety/effectiveness of TRUFILL n‐butyl cyanoacrylate as a minimally invasive standalone or adjunct treatment for cSDH.Clinical Trial Registration Information http://clinicatrials.gov; Identifier: NCT04816591

Chronic subdural hematoma (cSDH) is among the most common neurosurgical diseases, affecting ∼60 000 people annually in the United States by 2030. 1 Incidence of cSDH has been reported at 58–80 per 100 000 persons/year in patients ≥65 years of age and up to 127 per 100 000 persons/year in patients ≥80 years of age. 2 , 3 As the population ages, cSDH prevalence is expected to increase. Current estimates indicate the annual incidence of cSDH could exceed that of large vessel strokes. 4

Asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic cSDH is typically managed with medication. A recent systematic review found that although single‐arm studies frequently suggested a positive impact of medications for cSDH, most controlled trials showed no clinical impact. Additionally, medical management of cSDH is highly variable, with no established guidelines. 5 Symptomatic lesions are often treated surgically, but this approach does not address the underlying pathophysiology of chronic inflammation and neovascularization of the dura and is associated with a high incidence of recurrence. 6 , 7 Due to the evolution of our understanding of cSDH pathophysiology, interruption of the blood supply to the dura through embolization of the middle meningeal artery (MMA) is a potential minimally invasive treatment option adjunct to surgery or as first‐line treatment in nonsurgical patients.

Results from the first pivotal, multicenter, randomized controlled trials of MMA embolization in patients with cSDH were published in 2024. Studies evaluating the Onyx Liquid Embolic System showed a reduction in reoperation rate in surgical patients (EMBOLISE [A Study of the Embolization of the Middle Meningeal Artery With ONYX Liquid Embolic System In the Treatment of Subacute and Chronic Subdural Hematoma] study, United States) and a similar symptomatic recurrence or progression in surgical or medically treated patients compared with usual care, but with fewer serious adverse events (AEs; MAGIC‐MT [Managing Non‐acute Subdural Hematoma Using Liquid Materialsa Chinese Randomized Trial of MMA Treatment] study, China) compared with conventional treatment alone. 8 , 9 Similarly, MMA embolization with the Squid liquid embolic system in the STEM (The SQUID Trial for the Embolization of the Middle Meningeal Artery for Treatment of Chronic Subdural Hematoma) study was associated with reduced treatment failure rates (ie, residual or reaccumulation of the hematoma, reoperation/surgical rescue, stroke, myocardial infarction, or death) versus conventional surgical or medical treatment alone. 10 One epidemiologic study estimated that ∼140 000 cSDH cases across the United States could be eligible for MMA embolization, suggesting it could become the dominant neurovascular procedure performed in the future. 4

CLINICAL PERSPECTIVE

What Is New?

The MEMBRANE (Middle Meningeal Artery Embolization for the Treatment of Subdural Hematomas With TRUFILL n‐BCA) study is a prospective, multicenter, open‐label, randomized‐controlled trial designed to assess the safety and effectiveness of TRUFILL n‐butyl cyanoacrylate for middle meningeal artery embolization versus standard‐of‐care alone in patients with chronic subdural hematoma . Enrolling approximately 376 patients across 35 sites in the United States and China, the study will provide high‐quality evidence to support TRUFILL n‐butyl cyanoacrylate embolization of the middle meningeal artery as a primary or adjunctive treatment for chronic subdural hematoma.

The TRUFILL n‐butyl cyanoacrylate (n‐BCA) Liquid Embolic System is an artificial embolization device composed of TRUFILL n‐BCA, ethiodized oil (to add radiopacity and increase viscosity and polymerization time), and tantalum powder (to add radiopacity). The system is used under fluoroscopic guidance via catheter delivery to obstruct or reduce blood flow. Upon contact with body fluids or tissue, the mixture polymerizes via an anionic mechanism into a solid material. TRUFILL n‐BCA has been commercially available in the United States since 2000 for the embolization of cerebral arteriovenous malformations for presurgical devascularization. We hypothesized that TRUFILL n‐BCA is potentially well suited for MMA embolization in patients with cSDH given that it is noncaustic, rapidly deployed, and can be injected in a highly targeted fashion. Here, we present the protocol for a randomized clinical study to evaluate the safety and effectiveness of TRUFILL n‐BCA embolization of the MMA for the treatment of cSDH.

METHODS

Data sharing is not applicable as no data sets were generated and/or analyzed for this study.

Study Design

MEMBRANE (Middle Meningeal Artery Embolization for the Treatment of Subdural Hematomas With TRUFILL n‐BCA) is an ongoing, prospective, global, multicenter, open‐label, randomized‐controlled trial enrolling ∼376 participants at ∼35 sites in the United States and China (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT04816591). Participants will first be allocated into a surgical cohort or a nonsurgical cohort, according to the hematoma management chosen by the site physicians, and will then be randomized 1:1 within their cohort to receive standard‐of‐care (SOC) and TRUFILL n‐BCA MMA embolization or SOC alone (Figure). The SOC alone cohort is not permitted to undergo embolization during follow‐up (ie, no treatment‐arm crossover). Follow‐up visits and a computerized tomography (CT) scan will occur at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months post procedure. Enrollment occurred between May 2021 and February 2024, and the study is expected to be completed in 2025.

Study Population

Eligible participants were 18 to90 years of age, diagnosed with cSDH with mass effect and correlated clinical symptoms, and had a modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score ≤3. In the nonsurgical cohort only, the cSDH must have a midline shift <10 mm and thickness >10 mm. All participants must demonstrate stability of the hematoma and be capable of providing or having a legally authorized representative provide informed consent, and treatment with TRUFILL n‐BCA must be considered technically feasible by the treating physician. Complete inclusion and exclusion criteria are provided in Table S1.

Study Procedures

Participants presenting with cSDH will be assessed by the site physicians to determine if they will undergo surgical or nonsurgical management. Participants screened per study eligibility will be enrolled after signing informed consent, in accordance with local regulations and institutional review boards/ethics committees, and randomized to embolization plus SOC versus SOC (either surgery or nonsurgical management) alone. For all participants, a CT scan must be performed ≤36 hours before randomization to demonstrate stability of the hematoma, defined as no worsening of the midline shift or increase in size from the screening image that results in new or worsening clinical symptoms.

SOC is defined as the best care for individual participants, including guideline‐based care where available. Participants will be evaluated by the physician, in accordance with their institutional practice, to establish an appropriate treatment plan based on the participant's medical condition and available diagnostic screening procedures prior to enrollment in the study.

The study did not mandate a specific medication regimen given the lack of established national or international guidelines on medication management, which can vary widely. 5 However, sites were encouraged to prescribe regimens equally across treatment groups when feasible and appropriate for each individual patient.

Surgical Cohort

The surgical cohort comprises participants who will undergo a surgical procedure for hematoma decompression. Each site has ≥1 physician experienced in surgical evacuation of cSDH via burr hole and craniotomy. Participants randomized to embolization will further undergo the MMA embolization procedure within 10 days after surgery within the same hospital admission, whereas those randomized to surgery alone will not.

Nonsurgical Cohort

The nonsurgical cohort comprises participants who will receive nonsurgical medical management for their hematoma (eg, modifying/stopping oral anticoagulants, initiating medications such as statins, observation, repeat imaging, and lifestyle modification). Participants randomized to embolization will undergo the MMA embolization procedure ≤10 days after randomization, whereas those randomized to medical management alone will not.

Embolization Procedure

All procedures will be performed under anesthesia using standard hospital practices. Selection of the target artery segment and angiography will be performed using standard techniques and images will be submitted to the independent imaging core laboratory (Oculus Imaging, LLC, Knoxville, TN, USA).

Placement of a guide catheter in the external carotid artery is followed by placement of the microcatheter beyond the danger zones, which are identified as the corners of the orbit where the meningo‐ophthalmic collateral artery may be located and beyond the initial segment of the posterior branch of the MMA. Operators are trained to attempt to achieve distal perfusion of the embolic agent, if possible, ideally reaching or even crossing midline. Use of the dextrose 5% in water solution push technique (“sugar rush technique”) is an option to aid in achieving distal embolization by injecting dextrose 5% in water in the intermediate or guide catheter as TRUFILL n‐BCA is injected in the microcatheter to increase time to polymerization. Catheter wedging, in which the catheter is advanced into a distal position that is occlusive in the target vessel, is also an option to achieve distal penetration. The ratio of n‐BCA to ethiodized oil and the use of tantalum powder are at the discretion of the operating physician. Bilateral embolization to treat unilateral hematoma was permitted but not encouraged.

Follow‐Up

Participants will be followed up at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months after study treatment relative to Day 0. In the SOC MMA embolization groups, Day 0 is the date of the embolization procedure. In the SOC alone groups, Day 0 occurs within 10 days of surgery or randomization for the surgical and nonsurgical cohorts, respectively. Follow‐up visits can be done in person or via telehealth except for the 6‐month visit, which must be in person. A CT scan is required for each visit and will be submitted to the independent imaging core laboratory. mRS and Markwalder Grading Scale score assessments will be performed by an independent evaluator who is not part of the interventional (embolization) treating team, is not involved in the participant's care or data entry, and is blinded to study treatment assignment.

Participant follow‐up was anticipated to be challenging due to the COVID‐19 pandemic and the elderly patient population. To maximize patient follow‐up, follow‐up visits (except at 6 months) could be via telehealth, follow‐up CTs could be done at other facilities, and travel costs were reimbursed.

Independent Imaging Core Laboratory

An independent imaging core laboratory blinded to patients’ previous medical history and to the treatment arm at the time of initial assignment provides an unbiased, standardized assessment of study imaging. Primary and secondary end points will be independently read by 2 core laboratory assessors. Although the presence of embolic material on follow‐up scans may be visible, the core laboratory used standardized volumetric assessment tools and objective thresholds for all scans, irrespective of visual embolic markers. Measurements were performed according to a prespecified protocol using semi‐automated volumetric analysis, limiting subconscious bias. In the event of discordance between reviewers, a third, blinded, independent assessor without access to assessments of other reviewers will evaluate the image. The interpretation identified by 2 of the 3 reviewers will be used.

Clinical Events Committee

The independent clinical events committee will consist of ≥3 independent physicians with expertise in neurosurgery, neurology, or interventional neuroradiology who are not otherwise involved with the study. The clinical events committee will operate under a charter and review all AEs to independently adjudicate the safety endpoints (eg, death, stroke, myocardial infarction, thromboembolic complications, or new‐onset seizure).

Study End Points

The primary effectiveness end point of the study is the occurrence of residual or reaccumulation of the cSDH (>10 mm) at 6 months, as assessed by the independent imaging core laboratory or reoperation or surgical procedure on the cSDH within 6 months after randomization. The primary safety end point is the occurrence of all AEs through 6 months. Secondary effectiveness, safety, and health economics end points are shown in Table. Safety assessments also include recording all AEs at each clinic visit from the time of randomization through the 12‐month follow‐up visit. AEs are classified by their relationship to the study device or procedure, as adjudicated by the clinical events committee and by severity.

Statistical Analysis

Study Sample Size

Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel statistics will be used to test the superiority of SOC with TRUFILL n‐BCA embolization over SOC alone in the primary effectiveness end point, with stratification by surgery. Participants are randomized 1:1 within each cohort. Assuming a common odds ratio of 0.34, a sample size of 376 participants, after accounting for 10% attrition, will provide ≥80% power for the primary effectiveness end point at a 1‐sided significance level of 0.05. Anticipated event rates for the primary effectiveness end point were derived from the literature. 6 , 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 , 16 , 17 For the primary safety end point, we expect ≥80% probability of observing AEs that occur at an incidence of ≥1% within each treatment group (MMA embolization plus SOC versus SOC alone).

Study End Point Analyses

Data will be summarized by treatment arm and cohort. Descriptive summaries for continuous data include the number of observations with data, number of observations with missing data, mean, SD, median, minimum, and maximum values. For categorical data, counts and percentages will be provided. If a participant withdraws or discontinues without completing full follow‐up, observed data up to the date of discontinuation or withdrawal will be included in the analyses. Percentages will be based on the number of participants without missing data.

The primary effectiveness endpoint will be evaluated in randomized participants (intent‐to‐treat analysis set) and will be considered successful if the 1‐sided P value from the continuity corrected Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test is <0.05. Hypotheses of secondary endpoints will be tested sequentially at the 1‐sided 0.05 significance level using a gatekeeping strategy only if the primary effectiveness endpoint is successful (Table). Participants with missing data for the primary effectiveness endpoint will be handled with multiple imputation. Sensitivity analyses include the use of observed data in the per‐protocol and as‐treated analysis sets.

The Gail‐Simon test will be used to test for a potential qualitative interaction between the 2 cohorts at an alpha level of 0.15. 18 The primary effectiveness endpoint will also be analyzed by subgroups with clinically meaningful risk factors.

Study Ethics

This study is being conducted in accordance with the ethical principles that have their origin in the Declaration of Helsinki, as well as in compliance with the regulatory requirements of the US Food and Drug Administration investigational device exemption and local government, Good Clinical Practice (International Council for Harmonisation version 4 [May 1, 1996], and European standard EN ISO 14155 Clinical Investigation of Medical Devices for Human Subjects). Institutional review board approval of both the protocol and the consent form must be obtained before any participant is consented. Clinical site monitoring will ensure that participants’ rights and well‐being are protected; that study data are accurate, complete, and verifiable; and that the study complies with the protocol and applicable regulations. An independent data monitoring committee will assess and monitor accumulated AEs and interim data on a periodic basis as the study progresses to ensure participant safety.

Study Organization and Funding

The steering committee and sponsor will oversee study conduct. The steering committee will be led by 2 coprincipal investigators and will include physicians with experience in neurosurgery, neurology, or interventional neuroradiology.

DISCUSSION

MEMBRANE is a prospective, global, multicenter randomized‐controlled trial that aims to evaluate the safety and effectiveness of TRUFILL nBCA for MMA embolization in patients with cSDH compared to SOC alone. Currently, cSDH treatment includes conservative medical management and surgical interventions, although current medical management is largely based on theoretical efficacy and physician preference. 5 Use of MMA embolization for minimally invasive treatment of cSDH may offer a solution that addresses the source of fluid accumulation, thereby decreasing the likelihood of recurrence. In pooled data from studies of various treatment options, estimated recurrence rates after embolization were as low as 2% to3% versus ∼11% following surgery and 17% with pharmacotherapy. 19 Previously, evidence supporting the effectiveness and safety of MMA embolization came from a few observational studies. 19 , 20 Although recently published studies have shown reduced rates of cSDH recurrence or progression or similar rates with fewer serious AEs with MMA embolization compared with SOC alone, 8 , 9 , 10 there is still a need for more high‐quality evidence from prospective randomized‐controlled trials to support MMA embolization as a primary treatment option for cSDH. MMA embolization has been used alongside medical treatment in patients with no or mild symptoms to prevent or reduce symptoms and reduce hematoma growth and as an adjunct to surgical drainage in symptomatic patients or those with large hematomas to reduce postoperative reaccumulation and repeat procedures. 21 , 22 This study includes patients undergoing surgical and nonsurgical management and will provide information on outcomes with MMA embolization in various treatment settings.

The primary effectiveness end point of the MEMBRANE study is residual or reaccumulation of the cSDH (>10 mm) at 6 months, as assessed by an independent imaging core laboratory, or reoperation or surgical procedure on the hematoma within 6 months after randomization. Both components of this end point follow the recommendation of Fiorella et al, who recommended a cutoff of >10 mm as a radiologic effectiveness end point and reoperation or surgical procedure was recommended as an unambiguous clinical measure of treatment failure. 23 Most publications on cSDH management use a cutoff of 10 mm for patient inclusion and/or outcome assessment and refer to the established surgical guidelines that “acute subdural hematoma with a thickness greater than 10 mm or a midline shift greater than 5 mm on CT scan should be surgically evacuated.” 24 A frequently cited article on MMA embolization for cSDH by Ban et al, referred to by Fiorella and Arthur as “the most rigorous evaluation” of the treatment, uses the 10 mm cutoff. 6 , 25 Other studies also use the cutoff of 10 mm as an inclusion criterion for cSDH treatment. 15 , 21 Furthermore, the STEM study used cSDH recurrence defined as >10 mm at 6 months as part of their primary end points, whereas the MAGIC‐MT study used the same definition of recurrence at 90 days posttreatment as the primary end point. 9 , 10 In contrast, the EMBOLISE study used rate of recurrence/progression requiring surgical drainage within 90 days, with no hematoma size cutoff, as its primary end point. 8 The end point of residual or reaccumulation of the cSDH >10 mm is considered to be of prognostic importance given its association with mortality, decreased quality‐of‐life, and complications from new or repeat procedures. 20 Thus, this primary effectiveness endpoint is believed to be the best outcome to assess effectiveness and establish whether MMA embolization with TRUFILL n‐BCA has a clinically meaningful effect for the treatment of cSDH. To ensure objectivity for the assessment of recurrence, CT scans in our study will be evaluated by independent assessors at an independent imaging core laboratory that is blinded to participants’ previous medical history. Patient‐centered outcomes that assess daily function are also an important aspect of treatment effectiveness. The mRS and Markwalder Grading Scale scores are included to evaluate symptoms and disability, the Mini‐Mental State Examination score is included as a measure of cognitive function, and EuroQol 5‐dimension, 5‐level scores are included to assess health‐related quality‐of‐life. Similarly, blinded independent evaluators will perform the mRS and Markwalder Grading Scale score assessments.

Beyond these primary effectiveness end points and patient‐centered outcomes, the MEMBRANE study is evaluating other outcomes of clinical interest. A novel scale defined to prospectively characterize the degree of penetration of liquid embolic is being assessed by the independent core imaging laboratory given suggestions in the literature that the degree of penetration of the liquid embolic has clinical value. For example, in a recent registry study involving patients undergoing MMA embolization for cSDH or subacute subdural hematoma, aggressive penetration was associated with a lower rate of surgical rescue and higher rate of hematoma resolution compared with nonaggressive penetration methods. 26 The MEMBRANE study is also evaluating the rate of cSDH resorption. The clinical importance of this outcome was suggested by a study that showed that atorvastatin administration following surgical treatment of cSDH was associated with reduced hematoma volume and improvement in activities of daily living despite a similar rate of cSDH recurrence compared with control treatment. 27 A recent European Consensus Statement has given guidance on the measurement of cSDH volume in clinical studies, as subdural hematoma resorption rate may provide useful information alongside cSDH recurrence in the assessment of treatment benefits for both pharmacologic and procedural interventions. 28 Although numerous techniques for measuring cSDH volume have been described, no standardized method has been established. 29 In the MEMBRANE study, the independent imaging core laboratory measured cSDH volume using both the ABC/2 volume formula and computer‐assisted methods based on the hypothesis that computer‐assisted methods will be more accurate due to the irregular shape of cSDH. Assessing the accuracy of these measurement techniques will aid in developing standardized methods, which can facilitate more accurate cross‐study treatment comparisons.

The MEMBRANE study has been designed to optimize integrity of the resulting data. The randomized controlled nature of the study is the gold standard for studying causal relationships between treatments and outcomes while minimizing bias. It is important to note that given the open‐label nature of this study, the treating physicians are aware of treatment allocation, which could introduce bias in their clinical judgement, particularly in decisions related to reoperation or surgical intervention. Prescreening logs are utilized at enrolling centers to capture all patients presenting with a cSDH diagnosis, along with reasons for ineligibility, including detailed reasons for cases when MMA embolization was not deemed technically feasible, in order to capture full information on which patients are selected or not selected for inclusion. Predefined eligibility criteria ensure that the enrolled study population is representative of patients with cSDH in clinical practice. Various measures were established to ensure unbiased and standardized assessment of study results, including use of an independent imaging core laboratory for assessing CT images, an independent clinical events committee to review all AEs, independent assessments of mRS and Markwalder Grading Scale scores, and an independent data monitoring committee to assess and monitor accumulated AEs and interim data periodically during the study to ensure participant safety. Although there may be concerns that glue could be visible to the independent core lab in a follow up CT scan, in most cases it cannot be detected without actively changing the viewer to bone window levels; the independent core lab also used a minimum of 2 readers to mitigate the risk of bias. Additionally, data from all study sites are monitored by the sponsor throughout the study to evaluate protocol compliance, confirm accuracy with hospital medical records, and identify any issues that may affect the safety and welfare of the participants.

Certain clinical challenges regarding cSDH management will require further investigation. For example, more information is needed on whether MMA embolization prevents long‐term progression or recurrence of cSDH. This study will generate data that might inform the design of future studies to answer questions regarding the importance of the degree of distal penetration of liquid embolic, the relative benefits of bilateral embolization versus unilateral embolization, radial access versus femoral access, and monitored anesthesia care versus general anesthesia.

In conclusion, despite advances and rapid adoption of new techniques for the treatment of cSDH, significant unmet clinical needs remain, creating demand for innovation and new devices. It is anticipated that results from the MEMBRANE study described herein will help to address this need and provide valuable information on the potential benefits of TRUFILL n‐BCA as a minimally invasive option for standalone treatment and as an adjunct to surgery by embolization of the MMA for treatment of cSDH.

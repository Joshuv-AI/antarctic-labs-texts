The case of a 60‐year‐old man with decline in job performance and changes in personality and judgment
Authors: Allison Lapins, Borna Bonakdarpour
Published: 2023

Summary of Case (HPI, Relevant Exam Findings, and Relevant Data)

A 60‐year‐old right‐handed male who presents with change in job performance at a rather high‐risk job as an airline pilot beginning about 3 years prior to presentation. He was noted to be easily distracted and tangential at work, and perseverating on certain topics of conversation with a rigid thought process. Twice in the last year he fell for scams, sharing personal bank account information. There was also a decline in empathy toward his wife. Otherwise, he remains functionally independent with limited insight into any changes. He scored within the normal range on a cognitive screening tool in the office. His general neurologic exam was unremarkable. On formal neuropsychometric testing, he demonstrated significant executive function impairments and comportmental changes, as well as relative weaknesses in selective aspects of language. Structural imaging revealed atrophy in the bilateral frontal lobes, and less so in the bilateral temporal lobes, and PET scan confirmed decreased metabolic activity in the bilateral frontal lobes. In this case, we will discuss behavioral variant frontotemporal dementia. Testing of his CSF was not consistent with underlying Alzheimer's pathology. Specifically, we will review a patient presenting primarily with changes in judgment and insight with preserved performance on bedside cognitive screening measures, and the safety concerns that can arise with this type of clinical presentation.

Diagnosis

Behavioral variant frontotemporal dementia. 1

Take‐Home Points

BvFTD is a clinical subtype of the frontotemporal dementias characterized by gradual, progressive changes in comportment, behavior, and personality which can be difficult to differentiate from primary psychiatric disease or long‐standing personality disorders. 1 , 2 The early neurocognitive profile in these cases is characterized by executive dysfunction with relative preservation of memory retention and visuospatial function, with notable changes in comportment that can be difficult to quantify. 3 These patients often perform well on cognitive screening tests such as the MoCA or Mini Mental State Exam, despite having significantly impaired judgment and reasoning that affects their daily function and can pose risks to safety which need to be addressed early in the disease course. 4 Gun ownership should be addressed soon after diagnosis is established; often patients with bvFTD will have limited insight into their symptoms and diagnosis, and in these cases ensuring safety in the home is extremely important. 5

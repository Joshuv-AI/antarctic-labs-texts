A multi-scale attention-based Swin transformer model for medical images segmentation
Authors: Benyamin Mirab Golkhatmi, Mahboobeh Houshmand, Seyyed Abed Hosseini
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMedical image segmentation is crucial in accurately diagnosing diseases and assisting physicians in examining relevant areas. Therefore, there is a pressing need for an artificial intelligence-based model that can facilitate the diagnostic process and reduce errors. Existing networks often have high parameters, high gigaflops, and low accuracy. This research addresses this gap by proposing a transformer-based architecture. We developed a Swin transformer as an encoder to improve segmentation accuracy in medical images by leveraging deep feature extraction. This model can extract key image features more accurately by employing sliding windows and an attention mechanism. The overarching goal of this research is to design an optimized architecture for medical image segmentation that maintains high accuracy while reducing the number of network parameters and minimizing computational costs. In the decoder section, we designed the dynamic feature fusion block (DFFB) to enhance the extracted features, enabling the extraction of multi-scale features. This capability enables the model to analyze the structural information of medical images at various levels, resulting in improved performance in segmenting complex regions. We also employed the dynamic attention enhancement block to further enhance the features extracted from the DFFB output. This block utilizes spatial and channel attention mechanisms to emphasize key areas in the images, thereby enhancing the model’s overall accuracy. The proposed model achieved segmentation performance across three medical datasets, obtaining a mean intersection over union (mIoU) of 0.9125 and a Dice score of 0.9542 on GlaS, an mIoU of 0.9174 and a Dice score of 0.9569 on PH2, and an mIoU of 0.9085 and a Dice score of 0.9521 on Kvasir-SEG. The experiments illustrate that the proposed model outperforms previous methods, demonstrating its potential as an effective tool in medical image segmentation.Keywords: Swin transformer, Convolutional block attention module, Receptive field block, Depthwise separable convolutions, Medical image segmentationSubject terms: Cancer, Skin diseases, Cancer, Cancer imaging

Introduction

In dermatology, medical image segmentation enables researchers to accurately identify the type, size, and depth of skin lesions with precision. Similarly, in colorectal cancer diagnosis, segmentation techniques enable physicians to determine the location and morphology of intestinal glands, resulting in a more precise assessment of lesion characteristics and extent. Based on the Global Cancer Statistics 2020, colorectal cancer is the third most common cancer in terms of occurrence and the second leading cause of cancer-related deaths globally. Estimates suggest that around 1.9 million new colorectal cancer cases were identified in 2020, resulting in approximately 935,000 fatalities1. The incidence rate continues to rise, and projections suggest that by 2040, the number of new colorectal cancer cases will reach 3.2 million. This significant increase is gradually becoming a major global public health concern, emphasizing the need for preventive and therapeutic measures at an international level2. This technology enables doctors to more accurately assess the nature and extent of lesions, underscoring the need for computer-based methods in medical image analysis.

One widely used approach for colorectal cancer detection involves segmenting gland morphology and extracting relevant morphometric features. This process, predominantly performed using machine learning techniques, significantly enhances the accuracy of cancer detection and classification3,4. However, medical image segmentation still faces various challenges5. The accuracy of the segmentation process directly impacts clinical decision-making, playing a critical role in disease diagnosis, treatment planning, and prognosis evaluation. Additionally, using neural networks (NNs) for medical image segmentation allows researchers to obtain detailed information regarding the type, size, and depth of skin lesions. However, several challenges remain in this field, including variations in lesion size, irregular boundaries, color changes, and low contrast levels. Moreover, factors such as the presence of hair in dermatological datasets can significantly impact the performance of segmentation.

In the past, physicians relied on visual inspection of medical images to distinguish between healthy and affected areas. However, this approach was highly dependent on the physician’s skill and experience, time-consuming, and prone to errors6,7. With significant advancements in computational technologies, deep learning techniques have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in medical image segmentation8–16. In particular, convolutional NNs (CNNs) have achieved outstanding results in extracting complex features from images. One of the most prominent examples of this technology is UNet17, which is designed using an encoder-decoder architecture.

In18, ConvNeXt-UperNet was developed for road image segmentation, utilizing ConvNeXt as the encoder component. ConvNeXt is an enhanced version of ResNet, inspired by the Swin transformer (ST) architecture. Moreover, integrating the convolutional block attention module (CBAM)19 into this network has improved the model’s robustness against noise and complex backgrounds. Their results indicate that the model is more effective in extracting road-related features while reducing the negative impact of complex backgrounds. Based on the encoder-decoder architecture, models such as attention UNet (AttUNet)20, UNet++ (a modified UNet)21, ResUNet++ (an improved residual UNet)22, and the parallel reverse attention network (PraNet)23 have been designed. However, many of these models face limitations due to their focus on single-scale features and the inability to model comprehensive information. The context encoder network (CENet)24 used a pre-trained ResNet as its encoder. It enables the extraction of features while accurately preserving spatial information.

On the other hand, combining CNNs with multi-scale methods in the frequency domain can enhance feature extraction from images at different scales. For instance, in25, the authors introduced the twin-kernel Fourier convolution (t-KFC) model, which employs two distinct kernels in the frequency domain to extract image features more effectively. This approach uses frequency information at multiple levels to improve accuracy in medical image segmentation tasks. This structure enables a more precise analysis of image details and better separation of target regions from the background, leading to improved model performance compared to conventional image segmentation methods. In26, the authors introduced the FourierNet approach, which explores boundary-aware transformers (BATs). Their study investigates the enhancement of skin lesion segmentation accuracy through transformer-based models. It analyzes the role of boundary attention in extracting precise features and improving the performance of deep learning networks.

In27, a context axial reverse attention network (CaraNet) addressed the challenges of segmenting small objects in medical images. This model utilizes an axial reverse attention mechanism to extract both global and local information, thereby significantly enhancing the segmentation of small objects. However, the accuracy of boundary processing for these objects remains one of its primary limitations. Although CNNs perform well in extracting multi-scale features and handling objects of varying sizes, they cannot precisely adapt to the specific scale of each image. In28, the authors designed BAT to accurately segment skin lesions in dermoscopic images. While this model delivered promising results, it requires substantial hardware resources. To integrate the advantages of CNNs and transformers, the authors in29 developed the Transfuse model, which leverages a parallel fusion of these two architectures to enhance network efficiency. This model particularly focuses on preserving low-level details. However, its performance in precisely localizing boundary details still requires further improvement.

In recent years, numerous research projects have utilized deep NNs to classify raw histopathology images and diagnose colorectal cancer. However, these methods require a large volume of annotated training data. Additionally, most existing models conduct their evaluations on high-resolution images, which increases computational complexity. As the number of input pixels to the network grows, computational resource constraints become more challenging. To mitigate this complexity, the proposed model has been trained on images with a resolution of 256 × 256. CNNs excel at extracting local features but struggle to capture global dependencies due to their limited receptive field. Since medical image segmentation is not merely a local task, obtaining global information with high precision is crucial for improving network performance. Accurate and precise segmentation of medical images enables physicians to make more informed diagnoses and inform clinical decision-making. High segmentation accuracy, particularly in delineating the boundaries of affected regions, enables early disease detection and consequently contributes to improved treatment outcomes.

We incorporate a transformer-based encoding mechanism to enhance the accuracy of boundary detection. The proposed model leverages ST as the encoder to address these challenges, ensuring efficient and precise feature extraction from complex medical images. ST is chosen as the primary encoder due to its shifted windows approach, which improves computational efficiency. Unlike CNNs, transformers utilize self-attention, allowing the network to focus more effectively on critical regions within an image while capturing long-range dependencies more comprehensively. To further enhance accuracy in image magnification, we have designed two key modules: the dynamic feature fusion block (DFFB) and the dynamic attention enhancement block (DAEB). Due to their ability to extract multi-scale features, these blocks are crucial in improving the analysis of histopathology images, skin lesions, and polyps. Specifically, DFFB is designed to integrate boundary information and precisely detect glandular regions in colorectal pathology and polyp segmentation. Furthermore, to improve focus on critical regions in skin lesion images, such as cavities, hairs, and polyp boundaries, we have enhanced DAEB. This block employs a stage-wise combination of channel attention (CA) and spatial attention (SA) to strengthen essential features and guide the model’s focus toward key areas of the image. By selectively extracting only the most relevant features, DAEB optimizes segmentation accuracy while maintaining computational efficiency. In the decoder stage, we incorporate depthwise separable convolutions (DSCs) to further refine the extracted features from ST, enhancing the model’s efficiency while preserving high segmentation performance.

This method is an optimization technique in CNNs that consists of two key stages. First, depthwise convolution processes each input channel independently using a dedicated filter, allowing for the extraction of unique spatial features. Then, in the pointwise convolution stage, a 1 × 1 convolution is applied to integrate the extracted features across different channels, effectively modeling inter-channel dependencies. By employing this approach, the network significantly reduces the number of parameters and computational complexity while maintaining segmentation accuracy. As a result, the overall efficiency of the model improves without compromising its performance. Optimizing a network while preserving its accuracy remains a fundamental challenge in this field, which we address in this study. As the encoder, ST captures hierarchical features and long-range dependencies, which are essential for identifying fine structures and critical regions in medical data. The use of DSCs in the decoder reduces computational cost while preserving segmentation accuracy. This design choice enables efficient deployment in real-world clinical environments where low latency and resource constraints are critical. Moreover, DAEB and DFFB contribute to richer feature extraction by focusing on key image regions and expanding the receptive field.

In summary, this research aims to develop an efficient and accurate transformer model for medical image segmentation, focusing on colorectal histopathology, skin lesions, and polyp datasets. The proposed model addresses key challenges in medical image analysis, including class imbalance, high computational cost, and the need for precise boundary delineation.

Literature review

Following the vision transformer (ViT) approach30,31, an image is divided into fixed-size patches, where each patch serves as an input token for the model. In ViT, global attention is employed, meaning that attention is applied to all tokens (patches) in each layer, and these patches are processed independently. ViT utilizes a fixed structure for image processing. However, ViT’s global attention mechanism significantly increases computational complexity, particularly for high-resolution images, which can result in excessive resource consumption. As a result, the network requires a substantial amount of memory for training. To address this issue, lighter transformer architectures have been proposed32. One study33 introduced a shifted transformer to reduce computational complexity. The ST network utilizes local attention and window-shifting mechanisms, enabling the model to reduce computational costs while enhancing efficiency. In medical image segmentation, transformer-based models have emerged as innovative alternatives to traditional methods. Despite these advancements, the ViT model still faces limitations in extracting spatial information, which is crucial for tasks such as medical image segmentation.

The attention mechanism is a key component in advanced medical image segmentation models. This mechanism enables the model to concentrate on areas with significant information while reducing the emphasis on irrelevant regions34.

In35, the authors proposed a dual-aggregation transformer (DuAT) network for medical image segmentation. Their network integrates CNN with a transformer. It introduces two specialized blocks to capture global and local spatial and boundary features, thereby achieving high segmentation accuracy. However, this network exhibited limited precision in capturing fine boundary details. In36, a multi-scale gated axial reverse attention transformer (MSGAT) was introduced for medical image segmentation. This network integrates the strengths of both CNNs and transformer architectures. Their method first employs a pyramid ViT (PVT) as the encoder network to extract features, while a channel received field block (CRFB) is designed to filter out noisy information. Specifically, multiple multi-scale feature enhancement modules are incorporated to improve multi-scale features, thereby enhancing target spatial information. Extensive evaluations on public datasets, including Kvasir for polyp segmentation, ISIC-2016 and PH2 for skin lesion segmentation, and GlaS for gland segmentation, demonstrate that MSGAT outperforms several state-of-the-art methods.

In37, a CNN-transformer network combining CBAM was proposed for change detection in high-resolution remote sensing images (CTCANet). This model is designed to leverage the advantages of both CNN and transformer architectures, utilizing attention mechanisms to further enhance performance. A Siamese backbone was used to extract hierarchical features, which were then embedded into tokens and processed by a transformer to capture spatiotemporal relationships. High-level semantic features were fused with low-level features through skip connections to preserve detailed spatial information. Furthermore, a CBAM was incorporated into the decoder to enhance the discrimination of relevant changes while reducing semantic gaps.

In38, a morphological method UNet (MMUNet) was designed for colorectal cancer pathology image segmentation. To address the challenges caused by the variability of cell shapes and boundaries, they incorporated DSCs and morphological methods to reduce model parameters while enhancing segmentation accuracy. Their model integrates serial convolution and external focus modules to strengthen both global and local feature extraction. Moreover, the skip connections were designed to fuse encoder features using morphological operations, thus enhancing morphological feature representation. An edge enhancement module (EEM) was also introduced, leveraging contour information extracted through morphological techniques to refine boundary detection. Their method was evaluated on three colon cancer datasets, GlaS, CoCaHis, and Colon, achieving Dice scores of 92.76% ± 5.86%, 86.11% ± 7.11%, and 91.61% ± 11.25%, respectively.

In39, a lightweight Inception-V3 model was introduced for colorectal classification, distinguishing between adenoma and adenocarcinoma. The images were resized to 512 × 512 pixels, and rotation-based augmentation was applied to enhance the network’s learning capability. In24, a CENet is proposed for two-dimensional medical image segmentation. A pre-trained ResNet was utilized as a feature extractor. Moreover, a specialized module was designed to extract advanced features while preserving spatial information. They employed RFB to enhance the representational capacity of their network. Experimental evaluations across multiple segmentation tasks, including optic disc segmentation, vessel detection, lung segmentation, cell contour segmentation, and retinal optical coherence tomography layer segmentation, demonstrate that CENet significantly outperforms UNet and several other state-of-the-art methods.

In40, a CA encoder-decoder network (CA-SegNet) was developed for colorectal histopathology image segmentation. The network encoder was based on VGG-16, and the CA feature fusion module was incorporated in the upsampling stage to capture channel dependencies and suppress image noise. In41, a module was introduced to enhance the model’s perceptual capability, thereby overcoming the limitations of receptive fields in the encoder. Additionally, a feature-guided enhancement module was designed in UNet-Denoising to improve boundary detection accuracy while reducing the impact of noise and irrelevant background in medical images.

In42, an attention-based multi-scale nested network (AMNNet) was proposed, which consisted of four main components: CBAMs, early residual U-CBAM (RSUC) modules, convolutional stages, and a multi-layer perceptron (MLP). Their network was evaluated on BUSI (Breast ultrasound images for breast cancer), ColonDB-CVC, ClinicDB-CVC 2018, and GlaS datasets, achieving promising results. In43, a fine-grained contextual modeling network (FBCUNet) was designed for medical image segmentation. Observing that boundary regions are prone to feature ambiguity as they lie at the interface of different tissue categories, they introduced a new supervision strategy to explicitly identify and classify boundary pixels. Their design incorporated a boundary enhancement strategy to guide the backbone network’s attention towards boundary regions, thus enriching the boundary pixel representations with more discriminative features. Through extensive experiments on five publicly available datasets, TUI (Tomographic ultrasound imaging for thyroid tumor), ISIC (International skin imaging collaboration)−2018 skin lesion segmentation, 2018 Data Science Bowl (Cell nuclei), GlaS, and BUSI, they demonstrated that FBCUNet performs significantly better than other methods in terms of boundary accuracy and overall segmentation performance. In44, a dual-branch asymmetric encoder-decoder segmentation network (DAUNet) was proposed for the precise isolation of colonic crypts from confocal laser endomicroscopy images. The network was designed for segmentation on the GlaS dataset, specifically to address the challenge of ambiguous boundaries. To address the limitations of UNet in capturing global multi-scale context, Wang et al.45 suggested substituting skip connections with a channel transformer module to improve the model’s ability to model features.

In46, a scale-aware attention network (SA2-Net) was introduced, which utilizes multi-scale feature learning to effectively process diverse structures in microscopic images. To address the challenge of closely adjacent boundaries (e.g., cellular borders), a sampling strategy known as the attention-based augmentation module was introduced. The network was tested on five datasets, yielding promising results. CANet47 proposed utilizing pre-trained ResNet blocks as feature extractors alongside a context extraction module to obtain advanced features while maintaining spatial information. CANet incorporates a joint SA module to enhance focus on foreground regions, a CA module to recalibrate and prioritize channel-wise feature responses, and a scale attention module to emphasize the most relevant features across multiple scales. Extensive experiments on the ISIC-2018 dataset and fetal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) multi-class segmentation tasks demonstrate that CANet significantly outperforms traditional UNet and other methods.

In48, the authors proposed Polyp-PVT. This polyp segmentation model addresses the limitations of traditional CNN-based methods, particularly in terms of the differences in contributions among multi-level features and the lack of effective feature fusion mechanisms. Unlike previous approaches, Polyp-PVT employs a transformer-based encoder to learn more powerful and robust feature representations. To handle challenges posed by polyp image acquisition and their elusive characteristics, three key modules are introduced: a cascaded fusion module to collect semantic and locational information from high-level features, a camouflage identification module to extract concealed polyp details from low-level features, and a similarity aggregation module to extend semantic position features across the entire polyp area for better cross-level feature fusion. Through extensive experiments on five widely used benchmark datasets, Polyp-PVT demonstrates superior robustness and significantly enhances segmentation performance under various challenging conditions. In49, the authors utilize PVTv2-b2 as the encoder and introduce a cascaded network. They proposed the cascaded attention decoder (CASCADE), an attention-driven decoder that harnesses the multi-scale hierarchical features generated by ViTs. CASCADE incorporates two core components: an attention gate that effectively fuses features from skip connections and a convolutional attention module that simultaneously strengthens long-range dependencies and local context while reducing background noise. In50, an ST with cascaded upsampling (SwinCup) was developed for segmenting histopathological images related to colorectal cancer. This approach employs a hierarchical ST with shifted windows as the encoder to efficiently extract global and multi-scale contextual features. The shifted window mechanism enables the model to attend to various image regions at different scales, thereby enhancing feature richness and diversity. Complementing the encoder, a cascaded upsampling decoder is integrated to enhance feature aggregation and ensure finer segmentation outputs. SwinCup was evaluated on the GlaS and CRAG (colorectal adenocarcinoma gland) datasets, achieving an impressive average F1 score of 0.90. It outperformed previous methods by a significant margin of 23%, highlighting its effectiveness in medical image segmentation tasks.

In51, MRC-TransUNet (coordinated mobile and residual transformer UNet) is a model that combines a CNN and a ViT. The MR-ViT module utilizes three downsampling stages instead of skip connections. This module has four parts: local feature extraction through 3 × 3 and 1 × 1 convolutions to enhance feature dimensions; global feature processing by dividing the feature map into patches and applying self-attention like ViT; an information circulation block that combines low- and high-level features using a residual path with 1 × 1 and 3 × 3 convolutions; and a fusion block that merges local and global outputs with a 3 × 3 convolution for consistent and varied features. In the decoder section, a reciprocal attention module ensures that fine spatial details are preserved without the use of skip connections. It uses two paths: a one-dimensional (1D) attention path to collect spatial details horizontally and vertically via global pooling and 1 × 1 convolutions, and a two-dimensional (2D) SA path that generates a weight map using a 7 × 7 convolution after global average pooling (GAP). This network outperforms TransUNet and UCTransNet in medical imaging tasks.

In52, a multi-attention gated residual UNet (MAGRes-UNet) was proposed. This network is a UNet-based medical image segmentation model that integrates MAGs and residual blocks. The MAG modules aim to reduce semantic gaps between encoding and decoding stages by filtering out irrelevant and noisy feature responses and emphasizing small-scale tumor features. Residual blocks further improve gradient flow and learning depth, and the model employs the Mish activation function and AdamW optimizer. Experimental results on two benchmark datasets, brain tumor (BT) T1-weighted contrast-enhanced MRI (CE-MRI) and HAM10000 (Human against machine with 10,000 skin lesions), demonstrate the effectiveness of MAGRes-UNet, which outperforms UNet, MAG-UNet, and ResUNet. In53, a densenet channel-spatial and semantic guidance attention UNet (DCSSGA-UNet) was proposed for medical image segmentation. The innovation was in replacing standard convolutional layers with DSCs, resulting in a low parameter count. The encoder is MobileNetV2, and the decoder utilizes simplified skip connections and upsampling layers to further enhance efficiency. The network was tested on the GlaS and ISIC-2018 datasets, achieving competitive performance in terms of Dice score and segmentation accuracy compared to heavier models, such as UNet and DeepLabV3+.

In54, an EFFResNet-ViT was proposed to address these challenges by combining EfficientNet-B0 and ResNet-50 CNN backbones with a ViT module. The model was used on two datasets, including BT CE-MRI for BT classification and a retinal image dataset for ophthalmological diagnosis. In55, to improve its accuracy, they proposed a modified version of Swin UNet transformer (Swin UNETR) with deep self-attention (DSA). Proposed Swin-AttnSeg model

This research proposes a Swin-AttnSeg framework for medical image segmentation utilizing ST, DFFB, and DAEB. This study seeks to integrate various architectures within a single network to address existing limitations, including enhancing the accuracy of IoU and Dice scores, preserving the accuracy of segmentation boundaries in predicted masks, and reducing the number of parameters and computational demands of the network. ST is recognized as a powerful model for feature extraction due to its use of the attention mechanism and shifted windows, rather than traditional convolutions. However, while ST excels at extracting complex features and maintaining long-range dependencies in images, it may struggle to effectively capture local details such as edges or textures. To compensate for the limitations of ST, we employ DFFB, which is based on convolutional operations. This block enables the network to extract local information with greater detail. DFFB is applied at all four stages of the encoder output (A1, A2, A3, and A4). While its structure, including dilation rates and group settings, remains constant across stages, the number of input-output channels is adjusted to match each encoder level. Additionally, DAEB is designed to increase focus on important image regions and to accurately integrate this information into the output. DAEB has SA and CA blocks, enabling the model to concentrate on channel and spatial features. By incorporating attention mechanisms for significant features in both dimensions, this block addresses potential shortcomings and contributes to the overall enhancement of the model. Figure 1 illustrates the proposed Swin-AttnSeg model. In image segmentation networks, downsampling operations are employed, followed by upsampling procedures. However, the upsampling process can lead to a reduction in boundary accuracy and overall metrics. To mitigate this issue, we utilize DFFB. Furthermore, DAEB is employed within the network to reinforce the extracted features, ensuring improved segmentation performance.

The ST architecture

ST is an advanced architecture based on the transformer model, specifically designed for image processing. Introduced in 2021 by researchers at Microsoft, this model has been widely utilized across various domains, including computer vision and image segmentation. In this research, ST serves as the feature extraction component for segmentation tasks. Compared to traditional CNN-based encoders, ST provides superior feature extraction by leveraging self-attention mechanisms that capture long-range dependencies and global context. Its hierarchical structure with shifted windows enables effective multi-scale representation while preserving both fine local details and semantic richness. The model is configured with an initial number of embedded channels set to 48, indicating the initial value of the embed_dim parameter. This initial parameter value plays a crucial role in the quality of feature extraction, as fewer channels can help reduce computational costs and the number of parameters. This design choice reduces computational and memory requirements. Despite the reduced dimensionality, the hierarchical structure of ST is preserved, and the number of channels is still doubled at each subsequent stage (48 → 96 → 192 → 384), allowing for sufficient feature abstraction and model expressiveness across different levels of representation. The channel expansion pattern in our encoder exactly follows the design of existing ST models. We reduced the initial embedding dimension to 48 (instead of higher values) to lower model complexity while preserving architectural consistency with standard ST variants.

The ST architecture operates hierarchically, progressively increasing the channels at each network level. This design enables the model to process local information with fewer channels first and then extract more abstract and global information at higher levels by increasing the channel count. Furthermore, using 16 × 16 window sizes and an 8 × 8 self-attention mechanism enables the model to process wider ranges at each stage without excessively increasing the number of parameters. The window sizes of 8 × 8 and 16 × 16 used for the shifted window-based self-attention mechanism are not arbitrarily chosen; they are comparable to those of other ST architecture models. Specifically, a fixed window size of 8 × 8 is employed in ST to strike a balance between locality and computational efficiency. In other variants, such as SwinV2-Base, larger windows, such as 16 × 16, are used to accommodate the reduced spatial resolution at deeper layers. These window sizes result from extensive architectural optimization and have been shown to effectively capture local and contextual information while significantly reducing the quadratic complexity of global self-attention to linear complexity concerning image size.

The DFFB architecture

‎The receptive field block (RFB)56 utilizes dilated convolutions to expand the model’s receptive field without increasing the number of parameters or compromising resolution, thereby enabling it to extract multi-scale features from images. Inspired by RFB, we have designed DFFB, as shown in Fig. 2. By utilizing dilated filters and adaptive mechanisms, this block effectively extracts information at various scale levels while incorporating a residual connection to preserve the initial information and facilitate the model’s fast convergence. The DAEB architecture

Inspired by CBAM, we designed DAEB. This block employs a combined attention enhancement mechanism that utilizes CA and SA to improve feature extraction in CNNs. While attention mechanism-based architectures inspire the structure, they incorporate key differences in how information is processed. In this approach, significant features are first identified at the channel level, followed by the identification of key regions in the spatial domain of the image. This two-step process collectively enhances the model’s accuracy and efficiency in processing critical image features. Figure 3 illustrates our proposed block. In the CA component, the goal is to identify significant features within each channel and adjust the importance of each channel relative to others. Unlike CBAM, which employs simple averaging methods such as GAP, we utilize generalized mean pooling58 in the CA stage. We initialized with a value of 2, as it offers a balanced starting point between average and max pooling. The output of this stage is a compressed channel feature vector. This feature vector is compressed through a 1 × 1 convolution layer to reduce dimensionality and model inter-channel dependencies. A ReLU layer is applied to introduce non-linearity into the operations. Subsequently, another convolutional layer restores the dimensions to their original size, allowing the processed information to be applied at the same channel scale.

Finally, a sigmoid layer adjusts the importance of each channel to a value between 0 and 1. This attention map is applied to the input features, enhancing the important channels while diminishing the less significant ones. After processing at the channel level, the next step focuses on the important regions within the image. In this stage, an SA mechanism is employed to identify key areas across the entire spatial domain of the image. Initially, two important features are extracted from the input features. The first feature represents the average features along the channel dimension (average pooling), and the second feature corresponds to the maximum features along the same dimension (max pooling). These two features are combined to create a two-dimensional feature vector, enabling the model to examine the information from two different perspectives. The averaging process facilitates understanding softer patterns in the image, while max pooling directs the network’s focus toward the most critical sections of the image.

In contrast to CBAM, which utilizes a single kernel for multi-scale processing, this architecture feeds the extracted features from the previous stage into three convolutional layers with varying filter sizes of 3 × 3, 5 × 5, and 7 × 7. Using multiple kernels instead of a single kernel enhances the extraction of multi-scale features, increases the adaptive receptive field, and reduces sensitivity to size and position. This approach enables the model to process local details and overarching structures effectively. Furthermore, averaging the outputs from the different filters increases the model’s stability and reduces noise. Each filter receives a different range of spatial information, and their combination strengthens the model’s ability to identify multi-scale structures. The output of these three filters is averaged and utilized as the SA map. The final value is passed through a sigmoid function and applied to the processed features, enhancing significant areas in the image and suppressing less important sections.

In the final stage, the processed output from the CA and SA mechanisms is combined, enabling the model to simultaneously understand the important features in the channel and spatial dimensions. This is achieved through element-wise multiplication between the primary features and the attention masks. The attention mask serves as a tool in attention-based models, such as transformers, helping the model either ignore certain parts of the input or focus more on specific areas. The main innovation of the proposed DAEB is the introduction of two key modifications to improve conventional attention mechanisms compared to CBAM. First, instead of relying solely on average and max pooling for CA, DAEB introduces a trainable generalized mean pooling with an initial exponent value of 2. This setting provides a flexible starting point, balancing average pooling (=1) and max pooling (→∞), allowing the network to adapt the pooling behavior through backpropagation based on data. The parameter is shared globally across all channels in the feature map, enabling the model to adjust it during training. During training, is constrained to remain positive using a softplus activation to ensure stability. It is implemented as a single global scalar shared across all channels, allowing efficient learning while avoiding overfitting.

This way, it can highlight strong, high-contrast features without losing subtle details. In practice, tuning during training gave us the flexibility to capture both broad patterns and fine structures in medical images. Second, in the SA branch, unlike CBAM, which uses only a single 7 × 7 convolution kernel, DAEB applies three parallel convolutions with 3 × 3, 5 × 5, and 7 × 7 kernels. This multi-kernel strategy significantly enhances the model’s ability to capture spatial features across different scales, thereby improving robustness to variations in lesion size, shape, and texture. The combined CA and SA allow the network to emphasize discriminative texture and intensity patterns, while the multi-scale spatial filters improve the localization of fine details.

Loss function

This study employed a composite loss function that combines BCE and IoU to optimize the medical image segmentation process. This combination was selected to balance pixel-level accuracy with optimal overlap between the predicted region and the reference mask. The BCE loss function, assigned a weight of 0.75, plays a crucial role in the precise boundary detection of medical structures by focusing on pixel-wise learning. Given that medical imaging datasets often suffer from class imbalance, BCE improves the model’s ability to identify small and less prominent regions. On the other hand, the IoU loss function, with a weight of 0.25, guides the model toward optimizing the overlap between the predicted region and the ground-truth mask, thereby reducing invalid fragmentations. Experimental results and discussion

Datasets

Three general medical image segmentation datasets were selected for testing to verify the feasibility of this model: PH259, GlaS60, and Kvasir-SEG61. The PH2 dataset59 was created through a collaborative research effort between the Universidade Técnica de Lisboa and the Hospital Pedro Hispano Dermatology Center in Matosinhos, Portugal. The dataset contains 200 images, each with a size of 765 × 574, along with their corresponding masks. The GlaS dataset60 is designed for gland segmentation in histopathological images. This dataset comprises 165 images with a size of 775 × 522, designed to segment glandular structures in various types of histopathological images. The Kvasir-SEG dataset61 is an endoscopic dataset for colorectal polyp segmentation. It includes 1000 images of gastrointestinal polyps collected from different patients, along with their associated masks. We applied operations such as rotation, cropping, mirroring, and Gaussian filtering to the PH2, GlaS, and Kvasir-SEG datasets to enhance the datasets. We also provide some examples of augmented images in Fig. 4. By applying Gaussian noise-based data augmentation, the model was exposed to more diverse samples, which enhanced its robustness and improved the extraction of edges and texture features, leading to better boundary delineation.

Dataset after data augmentation.

PH2 consists of 400 images, with 320 allocated for training and 80 for validation, all resized to an input shape of 256 × 256 pixels from their original dimensions of 765 × 574. The GlaS dataset comprises 495 images, divided into 386 training images and 109 validation images, and has been resized to 256 × 256 from its original dimensions of 775 × 522. Finally, Kvasir-SEG comprises 2000 images, with 1800 used for training and 200 for validation; the input size is fixed at 256 × 256, while the original image dimensions vary.

Experimental setup and system specifications

All models are created using the deep learning platform PyTorch on Windows 10 using Visual Studio Code. The model undergoes training for 45 ~ 60 epochs, utilizing a batch size of 4 for each. The model is built utilizing the AdamW optimizer at a learning rate of 0.0001. Python 3.9.19 is installed in the Mini Conda environment. All experiments were conducted on a single RTX 3070Ti GPU with 8 GB of memory, 8 GB of DDR5 RAM, and a 1 TB HDD.

Evaluation metrics

We employ five key metrics to evaluate the proposed model: accuracy, Dice score, mean IoU (mIoU), Specificity, and recall (sensitivity). Additionally, giga floating-point operations per second (GFLOPs) are also considered to assess the computational efficiency of the model. These metrics allow us to comprehensively evaluate the model’s performance in terms of both segmentation quality and computational requirements.

Evaluating medical image segmentation models relies on several key metrics. The Dice score quantifies the similarity between predicted and ground truth regions, ranging from 0 (no overlap) to 1 (perfect overlap). mIoU calculates IoU across all classes, assessing the shared areas between predictions and ground truth. Accuracy measures the percentage of correctly classified pixels but may be misleading in cases of class imbalance. Recall evaluates the model’s ability to correctly identify positive regions, ensuring essential areas are not missed. Specificity determines how well the model distinguishes healthy tissue from lesions, thereby reducing false positives and ensuring a reliable diagnosis. Additionally, for the proposed model, 95% confidence intervals (CIs) are computed for Dice score and IoU metrics using per-image scores, providing a more statistically robust evaluation of the segmentation performance. CIs were calculated using Student’s t-distribution based on per-image scores from the validation set, to account for variability across validation samples. Figures 5 and 6, and 7 show the results and criteria during training for the PH2, GlaS, and Kvasir-SEG datasets. The results show that the network is well-trained and not overfitted.

Performance analysis of image segmentation models on the PH2 dataset

The base ST model with a window size of 8 × 8 achieved a mIoU of 0.8930 and a Dice score of 0.9437. While providing a reasonable accuracy without leveraging feature enhancement mechanisms, this model still shows potential for improvement. Increasing the window size to 16 × 16 led to enhanced feature extraction accuracy, with mIoU rising from 0.8930 to 0.8979 and the Dice score increasing from 0.9437 to 0.9457. These results suggest that a larger window can encompass broader areas and enhance the extraction of overall features. The addition of DFFB, which performs multi-scale processing, significantly impacted model performance. mIoU increased from 0.8930 to 0.9153, and the Dice score rose from 0.9437 to 0.9558. These results demonstrate that multi-scale processing plays a key role in enhancing the accuracy of lesion detection. Combining DAEB and DFFB with the ST8 model yielded a mIoU of 0.9152 and a Dice score of 0.9557, showing no significant change compared to ST8 + DFFB. This indicates that, in the presence of DFFB, the impact of DAEB on the final accuracy is diminished. The final model, which includes all blocks (ST16 + DFFB + DAEB), achieved a mIoU of 0.9174 and a Dice score of 0.9569, providing the highest performance.

This research investigated the impact of various blocks on improving the performance of skin lesion detection models. The results indicated that DFFB had the most significant effect on enhancing model performance. This block facilitates more precise separation of skin lesions from the background through multi-scale processing, substantially improving model performance. Utilizing ST with a larger window size (ST16) alongside DFFB yielded the best results, with the combination of these two blocks significantly increasing model accuracy. Ultimately, the combined ST16 + DFFB + DAEB model, with a mIoU of 0.9174 and a Dice score of 0.9569, represented the most accurate model obtained, although the difference from the ST16 + DFFB model was minimal. Overall, multi-scale processing (DFFB) was identified as the primary factor in enhancing the performance of skin lesion detection.

Performance analysis of image segmentation models on the GlaS dataset

The base model based on ST with a window size of 8 × 8 achieved a mIoU of 0.7969 and a Dice score of 0.8870. When the window size was increased to 16 × 16, a wider image area was covered, but unexpectedly, the model’s performance slightly decreased. The mIoU dropped to 0.7943, and the Dice score decreased to 0.8854. This suggests that in this dataset, a smaller window can retain finer details of histopathological structures. Adding DAEB, which includes both CA and SA, enhanced the model’s focus on critical areas of the lesions, resulting in improved accuracy. mIoU increased to 0.8059, and the Dice score rose to 0.8925, indicating that this block effectively extracts more details from cancerous or abnormal tissues. Combining DAEB with the model featuring a larger window, namely ST16, yielded a mIoU of 0.7981 and a Dice score of 0.8877, reflecting an improvement over the base ST16 model. These results suggest that the impact of DAEB is somewhat diminished in this case, and a larger window alone is not sufficient for extracting complex features.

Introducing DFFB, which performs multi-scale processing, significantly increased the model’s accuracy. This highlights the importance of multi-scale processing in better feature extraction from histopathological images. When combined with the ST16 model, multi-scale processing yielded a mIoU of 0.9010 and a Dice score of 0.9475, which is slightly lower than that of the ST8 + DFFB model. This decrease suggests that multi-scale processing, combined with a smaller window, yields better results for this data type. Adding DAEB and DFFB to the base ST8 model resulted in a mIoU of 0.9125 and a Dice score of 0.9542, demonstrating a slight improvement over the ST8 + DFFB model. This indicates that adding DAEB still has a positive impact, albeit more limited than DFFB. Ultimately, the final model incorporating all auxiliary blocks, DAEB, and DFFB, recorded a mIoU of 0.9101 and a Dice score of 0.9529. This model performed slightly worse than the ST8 + DFFB + DAEB model, reaffirming that using the smaller window (ST8) has a greater impact on this data type. The results show that multi-scale processing (DFFB) significantly improved the model’s accuracy, resulting in a notable increase in the mIoU and Dice score metrics. Unlike other datasets, in this study, ST with a smaller window (ST8) outperformed the version with a larger window (ST16). These results indicate that multi-scale processing (DFFB) is the most effective factor in enhancing performance in the segmentation of histopathological images.

Performance analysis of image segmentation models on the Kvasir-SEG dataset

The base model based on ST with a window size of 8 × 8 achieved a mIoU of 0.6892 and a Dice score of 0.8160. This model lacks feature enhancement mechanisms and is a reference for comparing other models. When the window size was increased to 16 × 16, a broader range of information was extracted at each stage, resulting in improved model performance. This indicates that a larger window can better identify the overall structures of polyps. Adding DAEB, which includes CA and SA, enhanced the model’s focus on important regions of the image, thereby contributing to the extraction of richer features. This model achieved a mIoU of 0.7147 and a Dice score of 0.8336, improving the base ST8 model. The combination of DAEB with the larger window model (ST16) also resulted in a slight performance improvement. In this case, the impact of DAEB was less pronounced, indicating that ST with a larger window alone has good feature extraction capabilities.

Adding DFFB to ST16 resulted in a significant leap in model accuracy. This block combines information at different scales, leading to better edge delineation of polyps. These results highlight that multi-scale processing plays a key role in improving accuracy. Combining this block with the ST16 model further increased accuracy, demonstrating that multi-scale processing alongside the larger window (ST16) can enhance feature extraction. The final model, which incorporates all enhancement blocks (ST16 + DFFB + DAEB), achieved a mIoU of 0.9085 and a Dice score of 0.9521. Utilizing ST with a larger window (ST16) and the two proposed blocks yielded the best performance. However, the results indicate that multi-scale processing is the most critical factor in improving polyp detection accuracy.

Limitations

The current model was trained on images with a resolution of 256 × 256 pixels. Its performance on images of different resolutions (e.g., 224 × 224 or 384 × 384) remains to be explored. Furthermore, the model was trained on a dataset of limited size and diversity. Lastly, the evaluation of the proposed network has been limited so far to medical datasets.

Conclusion and future works

In this study, we designed the Swin-AttnSeg network for medical image segmentation. In the encoder section, we utilized ST, as its sliding window mechanism allows the network to effectively extract salient features in the early stages using the attention mechanism. To extract multi-scale features, we designed a DFFB, which has been shown to play a crucial role in improving the decoder’s performance. Additionally, to enhance segmentation accuracy in critical regions, such as polyp edges and skin lesions, we incorporated DAEB into the network structure. This architectural design led to an overall improvement in medical image segmentation performance. We employed a combination of IoU and BCE losses for the loss function, ensuring greater sensitivity to region boundaries and enabling the network to delineate image borders accurately. Finally, we evaluated the proposed model on three publicly available benchmark datasets: PH2, GlaS, and Kvasir-SEG. The evaluation results demonstrate that Swin-AttnSeg outperforms previous methods, exhibiting significant superiority in medical image segmentation. In future work, we plan to perform real-world deployment and latency benchmarking of the proposed model on edge and mobile devices, such as Android platforms, to further validate its practical applicability.

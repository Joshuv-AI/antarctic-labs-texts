The complete mitogenome of ceratina smaragdula fabricius, 1787 (hymenoptera: apidae) and phylogenetic analysis of the family apidae base on mitogenome sequences
Authors: Feng Fan Jia, Yun Jian Hu, De Hong Chen, Zhang Huan, Li Xu Dong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study describes the mitogenome of Ceratina smaragdula (Hymenoptera: Apidae), which consists of 15,591 base pairs with an AT content of 85.7%. It contains 37 genes, including 13 protein-coding genes, two rRNA genes, 22 tRNA genes, and a control region. Phylogenetic analysis of 40 Apidae insect mitogenomes shows that Xylocopinae and Nomadinae are monophyletic, while Apinae is paraphyletic. C. smaragdula is placed within Xylocopinae, forming a sister group with Ceratina okinawana. The genus Apis is closer to Lepidotrigona than to Melipona or Bombus.Keywords: Mitogenome, ceratina smaragdula, hymenoptera, phylogeny

Introduction

The family Apidae constitutes the largest family within the order Hymenoptera and plays a significant role in both ecological and economic contexts. Approximately 5,900 species are classified within this family, which includes 210 genera and three subfamilies: Apinae, Nomadinae, and Xylocopinae (Orr et al. 2021). Historically, numerous researchers have utilized a combination of morphological and molecular data to classify Apidae insects (Cardinal et al. 2010; Bossert et al. 2019). However, the phylogenetic relationships among the genera within Apidae remain ambiguous (Flores-Prado et al. 2010; Hedtke et al. 2013; Bossert et al. 2019). For instance, current findings from morphological and molecular phylogenetic studies yield conflicting results regarding whether the genus Xylocopa constitutes a monophyletic group.

Ceratina smaragdula, a member of the subfamily Xylocopinae (Hymenoptera: Apidae), is characterized by its bright metallic green coloration and widespread distribution across Southeast Asia and Hawaiian Islands. Serving as a key pollinator, it plays a critical role in the pollination of economic crops including cucurbits, medicinal plants, fruit trees, and forage legumes. In this study, we present the first complete mitochondrial genome sequence of Ceratina smaragdula Fabricius, 1787 and further reconstructed the phylogenetic relationships of the Apidae family, which will enhance understanding of the evolution and future genetic research in the family.

Materils and methods

A specimen was collected in the Miao Autonomous County of Songtao, Tongren City, Guizhou Province, China (21°11’N, 109°11’E) (Figure 1). The specimen was stored at −20 °C and deposited at the Chongqing Key Laboratory of Vector Insects, Chongqing Normal University (Website: https://smkx.cqnu.edu.cn/index.jsp, Contact person: Dun-Yuan Huang, Email: 20170054@cqnu.edu.cn) under the voucher number ZH_Ps001. Species were identified by morphological features, like size, body structure and color, and thoracic characteristics (Shell and Rehan 2017).

Ceratina smaragdula reference image (the image is taken by YunJian Hu).

The mitogenome of C. smaragdula was extracted from the thoracic muscle of a female adult using an insect DNA column extraction kit (Baiaolaibo Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing). The library was subjected to high-throughput sequencing with 2 × 150 bp paired-ends using Illumina HiSeq 4000 sequencing platform, with about 2 Gb raw sequencing data generated. Quality control, adapter trimming and low-quality reads filtering were conducted by fastp v0.23.4 (Chen et al. 2018). Clean reads were assembled using SOAPdenovo2 (Luo et al. 2012) and annotated by the MITOS web server (Bernt et al. 2013), with the reference mitochondrial genome of Ceratina okinawana (GenBank accession number NC057193). After a manual inspection, the mitochondrial gene structures were visualized by OGDRAW (Greiner et al. 2019). The PanDepth tool was utilized to calculate the read coverage (Yu et al. 2024).

The phylogenetic relationship was constructed using 13 PCGs from the mitogenomes of 40 Apidae species or subspecies. Megachile sculptural, Megachile strupigera, and Euaspis polynesia (Hymenoptera: Megachilidae) were selected as outgroups at the nearest family level. The nucleotide sequences of each PCG of C. smaragdula and its related species were aligned using MUSCLE v3.8.31 (Edgar 2004). The best-fit substitution model for each alignment was determined using ModelFinder model (Kalyaanamoorthy et al. 2017). Phylogenetic analysis of 40 insects within Apidae was performed using the Maximum likelihood method (ML) and Bayesian Inference (BI). Specifically, IQ-TREE v1.6.8 was used for ML tree reconstruction (Nguyen et al. 2015) with an edge-linked partition model for 1,000 replicates of 1,000 ultrafast bootstraps. MrBayes v3.2.6 was used for BI tree construction (Ronquist et al. 2012), with settings that sampled 500 million generations every 1,000 generations across 4 chains.

Results and discussion

The complete mitochondrial genome of C. smaragdula is 15,591 bp in length (Figure 1), with an average sequence coverage depth of 1475× (Figure S1). The overall base composition is 28.52% for A, 22.10% for G, 21.38% for C and 27.98% for T, with a slight majority of AT content (56.5%), differing significantly from those of Thyreus decorus (84.97%), Ceratina okinawana (79.30%) and Amegilla calceifera (84.63%) (Wen et al. 2021). It contains 37 genes, including 13 PCGs, 22 tRNAs, and 2 rRNAs, as well as a control region (Figure 2). The mitochondrial structure of C. smaragdula was found to be similar to that of other species within the Apidae family (Zhao et al. 2017b). 22 genes were located on the majority strand (J-strand), and 15 genes were located on the minority strand (N-strand). With the exception of trnS2, all transfer RNA (tRNA) molecules can form secondary structures. The loss of the dihydrouridine (DHU) arm is a characteristic feature of trnS2. The mitogenome of C. smaragdula displays a marked preference for adenine and thymine, with a ratio of 87.5%. The relative synonymous codon usage (RSCU) values of the C. smaragdula mitogenome are as follows: The most frequently utilized codon was UUA (4.53), followed by UCA (2.24) and GGA (2.24). Furthermore, the utilization rate of isoleucine (Ile, 15.08%) was the highest, followed by leucine (Leu, 15.05%), methionine (Met, 11.5%), and phenylalanine (Phe, 9.08%), while that of cysteine (Cys, 0.66%) was the lowest.

The sequence of the mitochondrial genomes of ceratina smaragdula.

Phylogenetic analysis showed two topologies were consistent based on the ML tree and BI tree (Figure 3), indicating the presence of several monophyletic groups within the family Apidae, including the four genera Melipona, Bombus, Lepidotrigona, Ceratina, and Apis, which aligns with the findings of other researchers (Ronquist et al. 2012; Danforth et al. 2013; He et al. 2018; Lu and Huang 2020). The genera Frieseomelitta, Lepidotrigona, and Melipona formed a sister group to Bombus. The genera Thyreus, Eucera, and Habropoda were classified under the subfamily Apinae, which contradicts previous studies (Zheng et al. 2018). The genus Ceratina was placed within the subfamily Xylocopinae and clustered with the genus Xylocopa. An earlier study proposed elevating Pithitis, (a subgenus of Ceratina) to the status of a genus (van der Vecht 1952); however, our study lacked sufficient sampling to support this conclusion. Given that many of the genera examined contained only one species, the findings of this study require further validation, and a larger sample size will be necessary for future research.

Optimization of hollow salt microspheres using soybean soluble polysaccharides and hydroxypropyl-β-cyclodextrin: Evaluation of salt reduction in dough and bread
Authors: Wanbing Li, Wei Wang, Yue Cao, Bolong Fang, Haoze Li, Xiaole Zhao, Jine Wu, Chao Chang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to prepare hollow salt microspheres using hydroxypropyl-β-cyclodextrin (HP-β-CD) and soybean soluble polysaccharides (SSPS) by spray drying to address the issues of moisture absorption and caking of hollow salt microspheres when using SSPS as a single wall material. Measurements using a water activity meter, electronic tongue, and scanning electron microscope showed that the SSPS/HP-β-CD hollow salt microspheres with a mass ratio of 1:1 had the best physical properties, such as low water content (2.43 %), low water activity (0.285), strong salty taste perception, complete spherical appearance, and good fluidity. This investigation involved evaluating the influence of hollow salt microspheres on dough rheology and bread quality. Notably, the application of these microspheres to achieve a 15 % reduction in sodium content enhanced both dough and bread quality attributes. This work presents an effective technical strategy for salt reduction in baked goods, thereby expanding the application of hollow salt microspheres beyond their conventional use as topical seasoning materials.Keywords: Hollow salt microspheres, Hydroxypropyl-β-cyclodextrin, Soluble soybean polysaccharide, Salt reduction strategy, Bread

Introduction

With the increasing awareness of health among individuals, the reduction of salt in food has garnered significant attention (Hunter, Dhaun, & Bailey, 2022). Flour products constitute a significant component of the daily diet and serve as a primary source of salt intake. This is particularly evident in countries where flour products are the staple food, with the salt content in bread comprising 40–50 % of the total salt intake(Barakat et al., 2025; Vici et al., 2024). Consequently, reducing salt in flour products is crucial to the broader salt reduction strategy within the food industry. However, decreasing the salt content in the dough diminishes the degree of intermolecular cross-linking of gluten proteins, thereby reducing the elasticity and extensibility of the dough (Fan et al., 2020; Musiienko, Jurkaninová, Chrpová, & Kourimská, 2025). These alterations in dough properties inevitably result in diminished flavor, coarse texture, suboptimal processing performance, and unstable fermentation characteristics of flour products (Pasqualone, Caponio, Pagani, Summo, & Paradiso, 2019). Therefore, maintaining the quality of low-salt flour products is a technical challenge.

Hollow salt technology has been demonstrated as an effective and viable approach for reducing salt content in food, enhancing the perception of saltiness by altering the physical morphology of particles and significantly increasing their specific surface area (He & Tan, 2024; Ye et al., 2025). The preparation of hollow salt primarily relies on macromolecular substances with surface activity, such as polysaccharides, which facilitate the construction of hollow structures during spray drying to encapsulate recrystallized NaCl particles (He & Tan, 2024; Jiang et al., 2025). The incorporation of polysaccharides into hollow salt offers a novel solution to the challenge of quality decline caused by salt reduction in flour products. Polysaccharides can enhance the viscoelasticity and extensibility of dough, which is attributed to the formation of a gluten network structure promoted by polysaccharides. These properties contribute to the improvement of the texture and taste of flour products (Wang et al., 2025). Xie et al. demonstrated that mulberry leaf polysaccharides enhance the gluten protein network structure, mitigate water loss, decrease bread hardness, and improve bread quality during storage (Xie et al., 2022). Furthermore, polysaccharides possess anti-starch aging properties, which can delay the aging process of flour products, thereby maintaining their freshness and softness. Gum Arabic, sodium alginate, inulin, and laminaria japonica polysaccharides have been shown to enhance the quality of flour products (Fu et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2022). Consequently, hollow salt with polysaccharides as the wall material is a promising solution to the challenge of salt reduction in flour products.

Recent literature has documented the construction of hollow salt microspheres utilizing polysaccharides as carriers, highlighting the significant influence of various polysaccharide types on the properties of these microspheres (He et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025). Soybean soluble polysaccharides (SSPS) belong to macromolecular polysaccharides, which are mainly composed of neutral sugars, such as galactose, arabinose, rhamnose, and acidic sugars, such as galacturonic acid (Nakamura, Furuta, Maeda, Nagamatsu, & Yoshimoto, 2001). As a natural emulsifier, soluble soybean polysaccharide with strong surface activity can promote the formation of hollow microspheres during spray drying. Owing to the high viscosity of SSPS, the water content of hollow salt microspheres prepared by SSPS is relatively high, which easily causes hollow salt microspheres to aggregate and agglomerate, resulting in poor fluidity of hollow salt microspheres (Li et al., 2025). In addition, polysaccharides such as maltodextrin, arabic gum, chitosan, and β-glucan can also be used to prepare hollow salt microspheres by spray drying. These hollow salt microspheres, prepared with polysaccharides as carriers, can improve the perception of salty taste (Li et al., 2025; Yi, Tsai, & Liu, 2017). However, due to the hygroscopicity of polysaccharides, the hollow salt microspheres prepared with polysaccharides as carriers face the problems of aggregation and caking of hollow salt microspheres. Hydroxypropyl-β-cyclodextrin (HP-β-CD) is a derivative of β-cyclodextrin, which forms a cavity structure by introducing hydroxypropyl into the molecular structure of cyclodextrin (Zeng, Liu, Lu, Chang, & Wu, 2025). The special cavity structure can reduce the hygroscopicity of the hollow salt microspheres prepared using HP-β-CD and further improve the fluidity and storage performance of the hollow salt microspheres. The surface activity of HP-β-CD as a small molecule oligosaccharide is slightly weak, which makes the ability of HP-β-CD to construct hollow salt microspheres weak (Li et al., 2025). The preceding analysis indicates that hollow salt microspheres constructed from a single wall material exhibit distinct advantages and disadvantages in their physical properties. Therefore, this issue can be effectively addressed by leveraging the advantages of different carriers through rational formulation ratios.

In the current study, HP-β-CD and SPSS were used to prepare hollow salt microspheres to solve the problem of moisture absorption and caking of hollow salt microspheres prepared by single wall material, so as to improve the physical properties of hollow salt microspheres. Next, hollow salt microspheres were used in the production of flour products, and the effects of hollow salt microspheres on the quality of dough and bread were systematically evaluated to solve the problem of poor quality of products after salt reduction. Our research has successfully developed hollow salt microspheres that exhibit superior performance. More significantly, it provides an effective technical strategy for reducing salt in flour products while broadening the scope of application of hollow salt microspheres only for sprinkled seasoning materials.

Materials and methods

Materials

SSPS (food grade, 99 %) was purchased from Zhengzhou Chaofan Chemical Co., Ltd. (Zhengzhou, Henan, China). HP-β-CD was purchased from Aladdin Reagent Co. Ltd. (Wuhan, Hubei). Wheat flour for the golden image bread was purchased from Shekou Nanshun Flour Co., Ltd. (Shenzhen, Guangdong). Highly active dry yeast was purchased from Angel Yeast Co. Ltd. (Yichang, Hubei). Butter, salt, and sugar are sold in markets. Unless otherwise stated, all other reagents were purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

Preparation method of hollow salt

The preparation method of hollow salt was referred to the method reported by Li with slight modification (Li et al., 2025). A 30 mg/mL working solution was prepared by dissolving SSPS in 200 mL of deionized water under magnetic stirring (25 °C), followed by the addition of HP-β-CD at varying mass ratios (3:0, 1:1, 1:2, and 2:1 SSPS/HP-β-CD) until complete homogenization was obtained. Polysaccharide-salt complexes were then formed by incorporating NaCl (20 g/200 mL solution) and stirring overnight (25 °C) to ensure complete hydration. The hydrated solutions were subsequently spray-dried (YC-510, Yacheng) under optimized parameters (inlet temperature of 160 °C, atomizing pressure of 160 kPa, feed rate of 8 RPM) to produce hollow salt microspheres, which were designated according to their initial SSPS/HP-β-CD ratios.

Particle size analysis

The particle size distribution of the hollow salt microspheres was determined using a Mastersizer 3000 laser diffraction particle size analyzer (Malvern Panalytical) operated in dry dispersion mode. The optical parameters were set with an absorption index of 0.1 and a refractive index of 1.50. A dispersion pressure of 0.4 MPa was applied to ensure complete deagglomeration without particle fracture (Gu, Li, Jiang, Chang, & Wu, 2024).

Wettability determination

The wetting properties of the hollow salt microspheres were evaluated using a modified method (Gaiani et al., 2010). Precisely weighed samples (0.20 ± 0.01 g) were carefully poured onto the surface of 10 mL deionized water contained in a 50 mL glass beaker at 25 ± 0.5 °C. The immersion process was immediately initiated upon sample addition, and the wetting time (defined as the duration required for complete particle submersion) was recorded using a digital stopwatch (±0.1 s resolution).

Conductivity

The sodium ions release profiles of the hollow salt microspheres were quantitatively characterized through conductivity measurements (conductivity meter model DDS-307 A, Shanghai Instrument and Electrical Instrument Co., Ltd.). Precisely weighed samples (0.25 ± 0.01 g) were dispersed in 50 mL deionized water under constant magnetic stirring (200 rpm) at 25 ± 0.5 °C. Conductivity values were recorded at 10-s intervals using a calibrated conductivity probe until equilibrium was reached. The release kinetics were derived from the time-dependent conductivity curves after conversion using a predetermined NaCl standard calibration curve (R2 > 0.99) (Li et al., 2025).

Microstructural characterization

The surface morphology of the hollow salt microspheres was examined using scanning electron microscopy (SEM; SU8600, Japan). Samples were prepared by dispersing a minimal quantity of powder onto conductive adhesive-coated sample stubs, and excess particles were removed using a gentle stream of air. The specimens were subsequently sputter-coated with a 10 nm gold‑palladium layer to enhance conductivity. SEM imaging was performed at an accelerating voltage of 2 kV and a standardized magnification of 2000×. Three representative fields of view were analyzed per sample to ensure a representative morphological assessment of the cells (Lu, Lu, Chang, & Wu, 2025).

Energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) analysis

The surface elemental composition of the hollow salt microspheres was determined using energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) coupled with SEM imaging. The analyses were performed at an accelerating voltage of 20 kV using a high-voltage UD probe. Following image acquisition, both qualitative and quantitative elemental analyses were conducted on selected regions of interest (ROI), with a minimum of three representative areas examined per sample (Jiang et al., 2025).

Electronic tongue analysis

The determination of the electronic tongue is referred to a previously reported method (Li et al., 2025). The sample (1 g) was dissolved in deionized water and diluted to 100 mL in a volumetric flask to obtain a 1 % solution for testing. The samples were subjected to taste analysis using a TS-5000Z electronic tongue (INSENT, Japan) electronic tongue system to determine the taste perception changes for different samples. The reference electrode and taste sensor were cleaned for 210 s and balanced for 30 s before starting the test. The test time for each sample was 30 s, and each group of samples was tested three times, and the average value was taken as the test result.

Preparation of bread

Bread was prepared using two-stage fermentation. The base formulation consisted of bread flour (200 g), high-activity dry yeast (3 g), sucrose (20 g), and salt (2 g). In the experimental groups, hollow salt microspheres were substituted for conventional salt to achieve 10 %, 15 %, and 20 % sodium reductions (denoted as SH-10, SH-15, and SH-20, respectively). A control group (full salt content) was included for comparison.

Dough stretching characteristics

A 300 g portion of wheat flour (14 % moisture content) was loaded into a kneading bowl and mixed for 1 min (Yue et al., 2020). A sodium chloride aqueous solution was then injected (25 s completion time), followed by an additional 5 min mixing time. The resulting dough was divided into two 150 g portions, which were subjected to tensile testing after resting periods of 45, 90, and 135 min in a controlled wake-up box (30 °C, 75 % RH).

Specific volume

Following 1 h of equilibration at ambient temperature (25 ± 1 °C), individual bread loaves were weighed (m) to ±0.01 g accuracy using an analytical balance. Volume (V) was then determined via volumetric displacement using a food volume analyzer (BVM-L370, PerkinElmer Instruments, Sweden), with samples mounted on the sample rod of the instrument according to the manufacturer specifications (Cacak-Pietrzak et al., 2019; Ning, Hou, Sun, Wan, & Dubat, 2017). Three central slices exhibiting uniform thickness were selected per sample and imaged using a C-Cell image analyzer (C-CELL, Calton Technology Ltd., Britain). Moisture content determination

Moisture content was determined gravimetrically by drying 3 g bread crumb samples (S) in pre-weighed, oven-dried weighing bottles (W1, constant weight achieved at 105 °C) at 105 °C until constant weight (W2) was attained (Zhang et al., 2024). Texture profile analysis

Bread crumb samples (2 × 2 × 2 cm cubes) were analyzed using a texture analyzer equipped with a P/36R cylindrical probe in the TPA mode (Han et al., 2024). The testing parameters included a test speed of 60 mm/min, compression strain of 50 %, trigger force of 0.05 N, and two consecutive compression cycles with a 5-s interval between compressions.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were performed in triplicate, and the results are expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Statistical significance (p < 0.05) was determined using one-way ANOVA, followed by Duncan's multiple range test (SPSS 19.0, IBM Corp.). Data visualization and curve fitting were performed using Origin 9.0 software (OriginLab Corporation).

Results and discussion

Evaluation of recovery yield, moisture, and flowability of hollow salts

Spray drying is a critical process for controlling the morphology of hollow salt particles. The recovery yield is often used to evaluate the spray drying efficiency. The higher the recovery yield, the better the spray drying efficiency of the sample. The water content and water activity can reflect the storage stability of powder samples, and samples with lower water content and activity are more conducive to storage. The angle of repose and bulk density reflect the fluidity of the powder samples, and powders with lower angle of repose and bulk density have smaller friction between particles and better fluidity. In the current study, we systematically evaluated the recovery yield, moisture, and fluidity of hollow salt microspheres (Table 1). It was found that partially replacing SSPS with HP-β-CD resulted in sample dilution, which contributed to an observable increase in the recovery yield of hollow salt microspheres. The recovery of hollow salt microspheres improved with an increase in the proportion of HP-β-CD, reaching a maximum of 79.18 %. This suggests that HP-β-CD may enhance process efficiency, possibly due to its influence on droplet formation and drying kinetics. Moisture content and water activity (Aw) are decisive factors in powder stability, with lower values correlating with longer shelf life. Both the 1:1 and 1:2 formulations exhibited exceptionally low moisture content (2.43 % and 2.23 %, respectively) and water activity (0.285 and 0.287, respectively), with no statistically significant differences between them (p < 0.05). This improvement can be attributed to the unique hydrophobic cavity structure of HP-β-CD, which restricts water molecule binding, thereby reducing hygroscopicity and enhancing storage stability compared with SSPS-dominated formulations. The powder fluidity, governed by the angle of repose and bulk density, varied significantly across the formulations. The 2:1 group exhibited an elevated bulk density, indicating particle aggregation and a compromised flowability. In contrast, the 1:1 and 1:2 groups exhibited superior flow characteristics, likely owing to the more homogeneous particle distribution. Variations in the angle of repose further suggested that uneven SSPS/HP-β-CD ratios may promote interparticle cohesion, adversely affecting flow performance. Notably, formulations with lower bulk densities exhibited higher porosities, facilitating accelerated sodium ions diffusion upon reconstitution. This structural feature is critical for enhancing saltiness perception in end-use applications, as increased porosity promotes rapid solute release in the oral cavity. Overall, the 1:1 and 1:2 formulations outperformed the 2:1 formulation in terms of recovery rate, moisture retention, and flow properties, with no statistically significant differences (p < 0.05) between them. These findings reconfirm that the physical properties of hollow salt microspheres can be improved by spray drying with optimized parameters (inlet temperature of 160 °C, atomization pressure of 160 kPa, and feed rate of 8 RPM) (Li et al., 2025). Therefore, HP-β-CD has great potential as a functional excipient to optimize the physical properties of hollow salt microspheres, particularly when used in a balanced ratio with SSPS.

Determination of hygroscopicity, particle size, wettability and conductivity of hollow salts

The hygroscopic behavior of hollow salt microspheres represents a major limitation for their practical implementation in food systems, as moisture absorption induces particle agglomeration and deteriorates both powder flowability and the long-term stability of food products. Moreover, the collapse of micropore (such as hollow structure deformation) caused by moisture absorption reduces its specific surface area and weakens the release performance (He & Tan, 2024; Li et al., 2025). This systematic investigation revealed a pronounced, composition-dependent moisture absorption profile (Fig. 1A). Formulations with higher HP-β-CD content demonstrated markedly reduced water uptake, with values of 2.96 g/100 g (2:1), 2.54 g/100 g (1:1), and 2.37 g/100 g (1:2) after 72 h of storage, respectively. This result is consistent with the results of the previous single vector experiment (Li et al., 2025). This can be explained by the distinctive molecular architecture of HP-β-CD, which features a hydrophilic exterior that enhances dispersibility, whereas its hydrophobic internal cavity minimizes water molecule binding. Visual observations corroborated these quantitative measurements, with the 1:1 and 1:2 formulations maintaining excellent physical integrity during storage, in stark contrast to the visibly aggregated 2:1 formulation. These findings provide compelling evidence that the judicious selection of carrier materials can effectively mitigate the hygroscopic challenges inherent to salt microencapsulation systems.

The relationship between salt crystal dimensions and sensory perception is well established, with a reduced particle size known to enhance saltiness intensity through an increased contact area with taste receptors. The spray drying process successfully produced hollow microspheres with substantially smaller diameters (7.57–8.91 μm, Fig. 1B) than conventional NaCl crystals (typically >50 μm). Notably, the 1:1 formulation achieved the smallest particle size (7.57 μm), which can be attributed to the optimized interfacial interactions between SSPS and HP-β-CD during atomization and subsequent drying. In contrast, the 2:1 ratio resulted in larger particles (8.91 μm) owing to incomplete dispersion and particle aggregation. This size reduction is technologically significant, as prior studies have demonstrated a 25–50 % enhancement in salt perception when using micronized salt in food applications (Moncada et al., 2015). Therefore, sub-10 μm microspheres are expected to provide similar benefits through rapid oral dissolution and immediate interaction with taste receptors, potentially enabling substantial sodium reduction in food formulation.

The slow dissolution kinetics of conventional NaCl crystals is a key factor contributing to excessive salt consumption, as delayed salt perception often leads to over-seasoning of food (Vinitha, Leena, Moses, & Anandharamakrishnan, 2021). As seen in Fig. 1C that the wetting speed of the hollow salt microspheres was much faster than that of the NaCl crystals. Our engineered microspheres addressed this limitation through two complementary mechanisms. The first is the hollow core morphology provides a significantly increased surface area for solvent interaction. The second is nanoscale porosity, which creates capillary networks that accelerate water penetration. Interestingly, an increase in the mass ratio of HP-β-CD correlated with enhanced wettability. The complete dissolution times for the ratios of 1:1, 1:2, and 2:1 were 229, 182, and 284 s, respectively, indicating a 1.5- to 2.5-fold improvement compared to the control group. HP-β-CD acts as a dissolution enhancer through its surfactant-like properties, whereas excess SSPS (3:0 formulation) modestly retards the release of sodium ions due to increased solution viscosity and surface tension (Li et al., 2025). These results highlight the importance of balanced carrier ratios for the wetting speed of hollow salt microspheres.

Real-time conductivity measurements provided critical insights into the ionic release profiles, revealing fundamentally different behaviors between conventional salt and hollow salt microspheres. While bulk NaCl exhibited a characteristically slow initial release (<20 % at 30 s), all hollow salt microspheres achieved >80 % sodium release within 60 s, with the 1:1 and 1:2 formulations showing particularly rapid early kinetics (Fig. 1D). The transient retention observed in the SSPS-rich samples correlates with their known film-forming properties and macromolecular entanglement effects (Tajik et al., 2013). As a high molecular weight polysaccharide, SSPS has a large surface tension after dissolving in water, which hinders the dissolution of hollow salt and affects the release of sodium ions. The low film-forming property of HP-β-CD can compensate for the deficiency of SSPS. The higher the mass ratio of HP-β-CD, the faster the release of sodium ions. Therefore, the appropriate ratio of HP-β-CD and SSPS is an effective strategy to control the release rate of sodium ions.

Microscopic morphology observation and surface element analysis of hollow salt

Spray drying serves as the critical process for controlling the morphology of hollow salt particles. The formation of the hollow structure depends to a large extent on the surface activity of the carrier composition, which can be achieved within specific process parameters in the spray drying process. The spray drying process induced an ordered redistribution and crystalline rearrangement of NaCl within the colloidal system, leading to the formation of hollow microspheres. This structural transformation significantly enhances the specific surface area of the hollow salt microspheres, while the incorporation of carrier materials effectively mitigates the interparticle interactions and optimizes the functional performance (He & Tan, 2024). Notably, the engineered hollow architecture enhances taste bud stimulation efficiency, enabling equivalent saltiness perception at a reduced sodium content, which is a promising advancement in dietary sodium reduction strategies. Morphological analysis (Fig. 2) revealed distinct structural variations depending on the carrier ratios. The SSPS exhibited pronounced surface activity, which substantially facilitated the formation of complete spherical structures (Fig. 2-A1). This is consistent with a previously reported phenomenon, which is mainly related to the higher viscosity and stronger film-forming properties of the SSPS solution (Li et al., 2025). The introduction of HP-β-CD affected the complete spherical structure of the hollow salt microspheres to some extent, but they still exhibited a spherical structure. At different SSPS/HP-β-CD design ratios, an SSPS/HP-β-CD ratio of 1:1 yielded a well-defined spherical structure (Fig. 2-A2), whereas hollow salt microspheres with other mass ratios resulted in particle aggregation and impaired fluidity (Fig. 2-A3 and Fig. 2-A4). These findings establish the SSPS/HP-β-CD ratio of 1:1 formulation as the most viable option for practical applications.

Morphology observation of hollow salt under different carrier ratios. A1, A2, A3, and A4 indicate mass ratios of SSPS to HP-β-CD of 3:0, 1:1, 1:2, and 2:1, respectively.

Energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) is mainly used for the qualitative and quantitative analysis of elements on the surface of samples. EDS analysis (Fig. 3B) confirmed the presence of four key elements in the hollow salt microspheres, namely, the element of Na and Cl (from NaCl crystals) and the element of C and O (from carbohydrate carriers). The H element in the hollow salt microspheres could not be detected because of its low electron energy. The quantitative EDS results (Fig. 3A) reveal a carrier ratio-dependent surface composition, with the SSPS/HP-β-CD ratio of 1:1 and 1:2 formulations exhibiting elevated surface Na contents (22.63 % and 23.98 %, respectively). This is due to the low viscosity and weak film-forming property of HP-β-CD, which leads to an increase in the Na element distribution on the surface of the hollow salt microspheres with increasing HP-β-CD mass ratio. Similarly, a viscosity effect was also observed in the SSPS solution, as the SSPS-rich solution formed a thicker shell, increasing the proportion of C and O elements on the surface of the hollow salt microspheres and reducing the surface exposure of Na elements. Our results are consistent with the previous findings of Li et al. (Li et al., 2025). These trends suggest that interfacial activity governs mass transfer rates during spray drying, ultimately dictating the surface elemental distribution. Given that the surface concentration of Na directly influences the kinetics of saltiness perception, the 1:1 and 1:2 formulations are expected to deliver superior saltiness at equivalent sodium levels, highlighting their potential for sodium-reduced food applications. .

Electronic tongue analysis

The electronic tongue is an intelligent detection technology that simulates the human taste system. It can quickly sense the overall characteristic response signal of a sample using a multi-sensor array and perform qualitative and quantitative analyses of the taste of complex liquid samples. As shown in Fig. 4, an electronic tongue system was used to analyze the taste of the four types of hollow salt microsphere solutions, with the same mass of NaCl as the control. As shown in Fig. 4, the different mass ratios of SSPS and HP-β-CD had significant effects on the perception of acidity, bitterness, and saltiness of hollow salt microspheres. Compared with the same amount of NaCl, the sourness perception of the hollow salt microsphere solution was significantly enhanced, which was related to the fact that the main chain of SSPS was composed of acidic sugars. The uronic acid contained in these acidic sugars is acidic, and the carboxyl group in their molecular structure dissociates hydrogen ions in water, which improves the perception of sourness (Qi et al., 2021). In addition, the introduction of HP-β-CD can mask the bitter taste of hollow salt microspheres, and there is a significant dose relationship, which can be explained by the slightly sweet taste of HP-β-CD itself (Li et al., 2025). Notably, the hollow salt microspheres prepared using SSPS and HP-β-CD improved the perception of saltiness, with the salty taste score increasing from 0 to 0.14. This is due to the electrostatic interaction between the negatively charged groups in SSPS and sodium ions, which improves the activity of sodium ions, thereby enhancing the salty taste perception of the solution. However, there was no significant difference in the salty taste perception of the microspheres with different SSPS to HP-β-CD mass ratios. This is related to the solution form of the sample determined by the electronic tongue, which destroys the central control structure of salt microspheres. This implies that the application of hollow salt microspheres in liquid food is limited.

In summary, different SSPS/HP-β-CD mass ratios can lead to significant differences in the physical properties of hollow salt microspheres. Based on the analysis of these physical indices, an SSPS/HP-β-CD mass ratio of 1:1 was ideal and appropriate, although its performance in each evaluation index was not all the most ideal. Therefore, an SSPS/HP-β-CD mass ratio of 1:1 was selected to evaluate the effect of hollow salt on dough and bread quality.

Effects of hollow salt addition on tensile properties of dough

Salt plays an important role in gluten formation in the dough. Reducing the amount of salt leads to weakening the compactness and elasticity of the gluten network, which affects the extensibility and elasticity of the dough. It is not advisable to reduce salt in flour products only by reducing the amount of salt used. Polysaccharides contained in hollow salt microspheres can compensate for the quality problems caused by the process of reducing salt in flour products. Therefore, the effects of hollow salt microspheres on dough properties were evaluated systematically in this study (Fig. 5). The results showed that the stretching characteristics of the dough were as expected, and the indicators decreased with increasing salt reduction ratios. However, there was no significant difference (p > 0.05) between the 10 % salt reduction group (1.8 % salt content) and the control group (2 % salt content), indicating that the tensile resistance and elasticity of the 10 % salt reduction dough were unaffected. When the salt content was reduced by 15 %, the tensile curve area, tensile resistance (maximum tensile strength), and tensile ratio (resistance) of the dough decreased significantly compared with the previous two groups (p < 0.05). Notably, there was no significant difference in the extensibility (maximum deformation length) between the 15 % salt reduction group and the 10 % salt reduction group, and no significant difference was observed even in the salt reduction 20 % group (p > 0.05). This indicator reflects the ductility of the dough, which refers to the property of the dough that can be stretched without breaking when subjected to tensile force. The greater the extensibility, the stronger the plastic deformation ability of the dough, and the less likely it is to break during processing. Good ductility helps the dough to better retain gas during fermentation and baking, resulting in an ideal structure and texture (Hu et al., 2023). Although this indicator is largely influenced by the composition of high-molecular-weight gluten and gliadin in wheat, it has also been reported that the addition of hydrophilic glue can increase ductility (Liu, Zhu, & Guo, 2024). The reason why the hollow salt in this study can maintain the ductility of the dough with at least 20 % salt reduction may be related to the carrier of the added hollow salt, especially the addition of SSPS. This improvement is hypothesized to be attributed to the interaction between hydroxyl groups and gluten in SSPS, forming hydrogen bonds that promote a more stable and resilient gluten network. The incorporation of SSPS enhanced dough hydrophilicity, thereby improving elasticity and gluten strength (Hong et al., 2024; Wang, Qiu, Wu, & Ouyang, 2024). It is worth mentioning that HP-β-CD in hollow salt microspheres also had a positive effect on the extensibility of the dough. The effect of HP-β-CD on dough rheology was related to the interaction of gluten proteins, which interact with hydrophobic fragments in gluten proteins to improve the extensibility and elasticity of the dough (Jia et al., 2022). Therefore, the comprehensive analysis of dough tensile characteristics revealed that the use of hollow salt to reduce salt by 10 % had no effect on the tensile quality of the dough. Notably, a 20 % reduction in hollow salt had no effect on the extensibility of the dough.

Effects of hollow salt addition on specific volume and structure of bread

To further evaluate the effect of salt reduction with hollow salt on the quality of the final flour product, we applied this salt reduction scheme to baked goods (bread) and observed the actual situation of bread after salt reduction. The specific volume is a key indicator for measuring the expansion and gas-holding capacity of bread, which directly reflects the fluffiness of the product. The large specific volume of the bread indicated that it had a good swelling capacity and a stable gluten network structure. Table 2 shows that the specific volume of bread decreased with increased salt reduction (from 10 % to 20 %), indicating that increasing salt reduction could lead to the risk of decreasing bread quality. However, it is worth mentioning that the specific volumes of the 10 % salt reduction group (HS-10) and the 15 % salt reduction group (HS-15) were 3.92 mL/g and 3.84 mL/g, respectively, which exceeded that of the control group without salt reduction (3.72 mL/g). The specific volume of the bread in the 20 % reduced salt group (HS-20) was slightly lower than that of the control group, but the difference was not significant (p > 0.05). The results indicated that the specific volume of bread could be improved by adding an appropriate amount of hollow salt, which may be due to the interaction between the carrier material (SSPS and HP-β-CD) contained in the hollow salt microspheres and gluten protein. Simultaneously, the stability of the gluten network and the air-holding capacity of the dough were enhanced, which increased the specific volume of the bread. Therefore, it is feasible to use hollow salt instead of ordinary salt in terms of the impact on the specific volume of bread, and the salt content in bread can be reduced by 20 % without compromising the quality.

The texture of bread is governed by the pore distribution (quantity, pore size ratio, and wall thickness) and is a core determinant of quality. The number of pores in the bread exhibited a positive correlation with its specific volume, while demonstrating a negative correlation with the pore area. As expected, the trend in the number of pores in the bread was consistent with that of the specific volume. This result is consistent with the fact that the addition of polysaccharides can improve the properties of gluten and its internal tissue structure, so that a uniform and fine cavernous body is formed inside the bread, thereby increasing the specific volume and pore quantity of the bread (Shimada et al., 2024). Simultaneously, with an increase in the number of pores, it is easier to form a soft and delicate pore core structure (Hong et al., 2023). The thickness of the pore wall determines the hardness of the bread sample. The thicker the pore wall, the rougher the texture and harder the bread. As shown in Table 2, the texture characteristics of the 15 % salt reduction group (HS-15) were better than those of the control group without salt reduction. This is due to the addition of SSPS and HP-β-CD in the hollow salt, which optimizes the gluten characteristics and microstructure, promotes a uniform and delicate pore system, and improves the texture of the bread (Hong et al., 2023; Jia et al., 2022). In addition, we observed that the texture characteristics of the 20 % salt reduction group (HS-20) were slightly lower than those of the control group without salt reduction. Therefore, our results demonstrate that it is feasible to produce low sodium baked goods by reducing salt by 15 % through hollow salt substitution without affecting the texture characteristics of bread. The sensory evaluation results of bread also proved that reducing 15 % salt had no effect on the taste of bread. In contrast, reducing 10 % salt had a certain improvement on the taste of bread (Fig. S2).

Changes of moisture content and texture properties of bread during storage

Salt plays multiple roles in bread making by regulating moisture and affecting the gluten network structure to maintain the good texture and taste of bread. Salt reduction inevitably leads to a decline in the quality of bread (Lynch, Dal Bello, Sheehan, Cashman, & Arendt, 2009). Therefore, it is important to evaluate the quality of salt-reduced bread products. In this study, the aging of bread at room temperature during 7 days of storage was assessed in terms of moisture content, hardness, springiness, and chewiness (Fig. 6). The aging of bread is caused by the redistribution of water from the center of the bread to its skin during storage (Ribotta & Le Bail, 2007). As shown in Fig. 6A, all samples showed significant water loss over time, indicating typical dehydration dynamics. Interestingly, the water holding capacity of the bread in both the 10 % salt reduction (HS-10) and 15 % salt reduction (HS-15) groups did not diminish with the reduction in salt content. Instead, it increased as the salt content decreased over various storage periods (Fig. 6A). The 20 % salt reduction (HS-20) group also showed good water holding capacity at the late stage of storage, and there was no significant difference from the control group without salt reduction (p > 0.05). The enhanced water holding capacity was attributed to the incorporation of hydrophilic groups (–OH and –COOH) from the SSPS into the microsphere matrix. These groups form additional hydrogen bonds with starch molecules, resulting in a stable and ordered protein-starch network. This network improves the binding of water molecules, thereby inhibiting water migration and preserving product freshness (Hong et al., 2023).

In addition to the water holding capacity, the textural parameters of hardness, elasticity, and chewiness are also key indicators for the systematic evaluation of bread aging. As illustrated in Fig. 6B, the hardness of the bread progressively increased over the storage period, reflecting the aging process of the bread. During the initial storage period of 0–3 d, the hardness of the bread exhibited a gradual change. Interestingly, the bread hardness of all salt reduction groups was significantly lower than that of the control group (p < 0.05). A marked increase in hardness was observed between 5 and 7 d. The hardness of the 10 % and 15 % salt reduction groups was significantly lower than that of the control group. These findings suggest that the use of hollow salt to achieve a 15 % reduction in salt content does not adversely affect the hardness of bread. Instead, it may ameliorate the hardness of bread. This may be attributed to the soluble soybean polysaccharides (SSPS) within the hollow salt microspheres, which restrained the relative crystallinity and retrogradation enthalpy in bread crumbs and crust during storage, thereby restricting water migration from the crumb to the crust, thus maintaining the softness of the bread (Hong et al., 2023). The hardness of the 20 % salt reduction group was slightly higher than that of the control group at the later stages of storage, but the difference was not significant (p > 0.05). This is related to the water holding capacity of the SSPS and HP-β-CD carriers contained in the hollow salt (Hong et al., 2023; Jia et al., 2022). What's more, the hydroxyl groups present in these polysaccharide carriers engage in hydrogen bonding with proteins. This interaction impedes the association between starch molecules and proteins, thereby ameliorating the hardness of the bread and slowing down the aging of the bread (Hong et al., 2023; Jia et al., 2022).

Elasticity refers to the ability of a sample to return to its original shape after deformation by force. The more elastic the bread, the softer its texture. As shown in Fig. 6C, the 10 % and 15 % salt reduction groups could maintain the elasticity of bread during the entire observation period, and there was no significant difference between the 20 % salt reduction group and the control group after the 5th day (p > 0.05). These findings indicate that bread with a 20 % reduction in salt content can retain satisfactory elasticity when hollow salt is employed. The maintenance of bread elasticity was attributed to the combined action of SSPS and HP-β-CD. SSPS has good water retention and improves the distribution of water in bread, thus improving the elasticity of bread (Hong et al., 2023). HP-β-CD can regulate the structural dynamics of gluten protein and delay the retrogradation of starch, thus improving the elasticity of bread (Jia et al., 2022). In addition, it has also been reported that β-CD has a similar effect on improving the elasticity of bread as that of HP-β-CD (Zhou et al., 2019).

Chewiness is an important parameter for evaluating the taste of bread, which is closely related to hardness. As predicted, the chewing performance of the bread was consistent with the trend of hardness (Fig. 6C and D). The reduction in salt content by 10 % and 15 % did not compromise the chewiness of the bread during the observation period. Instead, it contributed to the enhancement of bread taste. The reason for this is similar to the hardness development trend explained previously. This is primarily attributed to the interaction between SSPS and HP-β-CD with gluten proteins and the exceptional water holding capacity of SSPS (Hong et al., 2023; Jia et al., 2022).

To sum up, based on the analysis of the effects of hollow salt on dough and bread, it can be inferred that utilizing hollow salt to achieve a 15 % reduction in salt content enhances the quality of both dough and bread, and a 20 % reduction in salt content is deemed acceptable.

Conclusion

In this study, hollow salt microspheres with SSPS and HP-β-CD as carriers were prepared by spray drying technology, which solved the problems of moisture absorption and agglomeration of hollow salt microspheres when using SSPS as a single wall material. The SSPS/ HP-β-CD hollow salt microspheres with an optimized mass ratio of 1:1 have the physical properties of low water content, low water activity, strong salty taste perception, complete spherical appearance, and good fluidity. To broaden the use of hollow salt, the effects of hollow salt microspheres on dough and bread quality were systematically evaluated in this study. Research indicates that the utilization of hollow salt to achieve a 15 % reduction in salt content enhances the quality of both dough and bread. Furthermore, a 20 % reduction in salt content is considered acceptable. This study provides an effective technical strategy for reducing salt in flour products while broadening the scope of application of hollow salt microspheres only for sprinkled seasoning materials. Future research should investigate the evaluation of the salt reduction effect of hollow salt on various flour-based products beyond bread and assess its impact on product quality.

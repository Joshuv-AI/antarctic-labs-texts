Neoadjuvant radiotherapy for primary retroperitoneal well-differentiated liposarcoma: a Transatlantic Australasian Retroperitoneal Sarcoma Working Group (TARPSWG) propensity score matched analysis

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundWith negligible risk of distant metastasis, the primary treatment focus in patients with primary retroperitoneal well-differentiated liposarcoma (pRP-WDLPS) is local control. The recently completed phase 3 STRASS trial suggested a potential benefit to neoadjuvant radiotherapy (RT) in optimizing local control in these patients. This study investigates outcomes associated with neoadjuvant RT in a larger cohort of patients undergoing surgery for pRP-WDLPS.MethodsIn this study from 24 participating sites, we retrospectively identified all patients with pRP-WDLPS who underwent curative-intent resection between January 1, 2002 and December 31, 2017. The primary endpoint was the cumulative incidence function (CIF) of local recurrence (LR), and the secondary endpoint was overall survival (OS). The impact of neoadjuvant RT on the CIF of LR was analyzed using a 1:2 propensity score matching (PSM).FindingsOf 582 patients included in the entire cohort, 72 patients (12%) received neoadjuvant RT. The 1:2 PSM group included 208 patients of which 138 patients (66%) underwent surgery alone and 70 patients (34%) underwent neoadjuvant RT and surgery. With a median follow up of 73 months, the 5- and 8-year CIF of LR for neoadjuvant RT and surgery group was 6% and 10%, respectively, and 26% and 33%, respectively, for the surgery alone group (odds ratio (OR) 0·85, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0·76–0·97, P < 0·001). The 5- and 10-year OS for the neoadjuvant RT and surgery group was 92% and 80%, respectively, and 84% and 71%, respectively, for the surgery alone group (HR 0·50, 95% CI 0·27–1·21, P = 0·07).InterpretationTo the best of our knowledge, this is the largest study to report outcomes of neoadjuvant RT for pRP-WDLPS. Neoadjuvant RT was associated with a significant decrease in LR compared to surgery alone. Research in context

Evidence before this study

Data to support the addition of neoadjuvant radiotherapy to curative intent resection for primary retroperitoneal sarcoma in order to decrease local recurrence have been contradictory. Available data have been primarily limited to smaller retrospective studies that lack histologic specific outcomes. The recently reported phase III STRASS trial did not show a benefit for neoadjuvant radiotherapy; however, an unplanned, exploratory ad hoc analysis suggested there may be a benefit for liposarcoma, and in particular for primary retroperitoneal well-differentiated liposarcoma (pRP-WDLPS). Among the sarcoma community, uncertainty has remained regarding the efficacy of neoadjuvant radiotherapy. A search of MEDLINE using “retroperitoneal” AND “sarcoma” AND “radiotherapy” identifies multiple retrospective studies with conflicting results with a limited number of studies adequately powered to report outcomes of specific histologic types. At the time the STRASS trial was designed, the significance of histologic type on tumor biology had not been fully realized, and thus STRASS was not powered for subgroup analyses of histology. The STREXIT study utilized data from STRASS in a mixed randomized-observational methodology and demonstrated a local control benefit for neoadjuvant radiotherapy in a small group of patients with pRP-WDLPS.

Added value of this study

To the best of our knowledge, this study reports the largest number of patients who underwent surgery for pRP-WDLPS. Neoadjuvant radiotherapy followed by surgery is associated with significantly decreased risk of local recurrence when compared to surgery alone in these patients. This study presents strong evidence that neoadjuvant radiotherapy should be considered in the multidisciplinary management of pRP-WDLPS. Strengthened by a large sample size for a rare disease entity that allowed a propensity score matched analysis, the study design and results overcome limitations of prior studies and should mitigate uncertainty and variability in the multidisciplinary care of these patients.

Implications of all the available evidence

Neoadjuvant radiotherapy should be confidently considered as part of the multidisciplinary management of patients with pRP-WDLPS in order to decrease the risk of local recurrence. While there is no association with overall survival, longer term follow up is required to demonstrate the postulated benefit.

Introduction

Soft tissue sarcomas are a rare group of heterogeneous neoplasms whose tumor biology is primarily driven by histologic type. While most commonly originating from the extremity and trunk, approximately 15–20% of soft tissue sarcomas will arise in the retroperitoneum.1,2 The retroperitoneum and its anatomical complexities contribute to its association with worse outcome compared to other primary tumor sites.3 With advances in pathologic assessment and classification, as well as international collaboration that has fostered development of high-quality observational registries, we better understand the significant role histologic type plays in defining tumor biology of retroperitoneal sarcomas (RPS). Liposarcoma (LPS) is the most common histology and accounts for over 50% of RPS.4 Well-differentiated (WD-) and dedifferentiated (DDLPS) are the two most common histologic subtypes of LPS. In the largest series of primary RPS (pRPS), Gronchi et al. demonstrated that WDLPS recurs primarily locally but has a negligible risk of distant failure, while DDLPS is associated with both local and distant failure.5 While a macroscopically complete (R0/R1) resection remains the cornerstone of treatment for patients with localized, pRPS, the question has been posed of whether radiotherapy (RT) can improve local control in patients with LPS.

Two early attempts to conduct randomized controlled trials to investigate the role of neoadjuvant RT in the management of RPS closed due to poor accrual (RTOG 0124 NCT00017160 and ACOSOG Z9031 NCT00091351). More recently, the randomized, phase III EORTC-STBSG-62092 (STRASS) trial was successfully conducted where patients with pRPS were randomized to receive neoadjuvant RT followed by surgery vs. surgery alone.6 The primary endpoint was abdominal-recurrence free survival (ARFS) and all major RPS histologies were included. With a median follow up of 43 months, there was no difference in 3-year ARFS with addition of neoadjuvant RT followed by surgery compared to surgery alone for the entire group. However, a post-hoc exploratory analysis of patients with LPS demonstrated an approximately 10% absolute 3-year ARFS benefit with neoadjuvant RT with further analyses suggesting a potential trend to improved ARFS for patients with WDLPS. The results from these underpowered post-hoc analyses have been met with varying interpretations, with some sarcoma centers increasing utilization of neoadjuvant RT for WDLPS while phasing out its use in other histologies.

To address the uncertainty of the role of neoadjuvant RT for liposarcoma, the STREXIT study included STRASS-eligible patients not enrolled in the trial among 10 STRASS-recruiting centers while the trial was underway.7 This study demonstrated that there was a significant benefit to neoadjuvant RT for the WDLPS (n = 106) and grade 1 and 2 DDLPS cohorts. Due to ongoing debate and varying interpretations of the current data given the smaller subgroup analyses of these studies, the aim of our study is to investigate the outcomes associated with neoadjuvant RT in patients undergoing surgery exclusively for patients with pRPS WDLPS.

Methods

Study design and participants

This was a retrospective analysis of consecutive patients with pRP-WDLPS treated between January 1st, 2002 and December 31st, 2017 at one of 24 member sites who were members of the Transatlantic Australasian Retroperitoneal Sarcoma Working Group (TARPSWG). Only those patients with pathology confirmed WDLPS were included in the study. Patients with any component of DDLPS were excluded from the study. Institutional Review Board approval was obtained by each participating site study (Brigham and Women's Hospital lead site protocol#2020P003098). The study was approved to be conducted with waiver of consent as it involves no more than minimal risk. Neoadjuvant RT was administered at the discretion of the participating institution following multidisciplinary review. Patients were excluded if their RT status was unknown.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was the cumulative incidence function (CIF) of first local recurrence (LR). Date of local recurrence was determined by each participating site. Local recurrence was confirmed radiographically or at time of biopsy or surgery for suspected recurrent disease. The secondary endpoint was overall survival (OS).

Statistical analysis

To account for selection bias in allocation of neoadjuvant RT, we performed 1:2 (radiation:surgery) propensity score matching (PSM) with a nearest neighbor/greedy matching algorithm without replacement and a caliper width equal to 0·2 of the standard deviation of the logit of the propensity score to minimize the error of the treatment effect.8 The PSM model was created with the following covariates: age, gender, size of the primary tumor, multifocality, and complete macroscopic resection. Patients were excluded if tumor size was not available for matching (N = 6), or if their index resection was performed at a non-participating institution (N = 76), leaving a remaining cohort of 500 patients. Propensity scores were calculated using a generalized linear model. Standardized mean differences between all covariates was <0·1, and there were no significant differences between the two cohorts on univariate analysis, indicating a well-matched dataset (Supplementary Tables S1 and S2, Figs. 1 and 2).

Overall survival in 1:2 propensity score matched cohort. Comparison with log-rank test.

Within the 1:2 matched cohort, the CIF of LR was estimated using a competing risk analysis using a subdistribution hazards model, stratified by the receipt of neoadjuvant RT. Gray's test was used to compare the risk between groups. Distant recurrence (DR) and death without recurrence were considered competing events. Both isolated (N = 7) and simultaneous distant and locoregional recurrences (N = 6) were considered a DR event for all analyses. A Cox proportional hazards model was used to estimate the impact of neoadjuvant RT on survival. The proportional hazards assumption was tested by plotting Schoenfeld residuals over time. Subgroup analyses with interaction terms were performed to test for effect modification between the variables of multifocality, age, size, and gender on radiation and survival. Survival was calculated from the date of surgery until death or censored at last follow up using Kaplan-Methods methods and the log rank test was used to compare curves. A logistic regression model with recurrence as the outcome and receipt of neoadjuvant RT as the exposure was fitted to estimate the overall association of neoadjuvant RT on isolated LR. Cluster-robust variance was used to estimate the standard error and account for clustering within institutions. Given the potential for n:1 matching to increase bias,9 we performed all analyses with 1:1 PSM as a sensitivity analysis.

All analyses were conducted using R version 4.1.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) with the tidyverse (v.2.0.0),10 survival (v3.5–7),11 survminer (v0.4–0.9),12 gtsummary (v1.7.2),13 and MatchIt (v4.5.5)14 packages. A P-value of <0·05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

The entire cohort included 582 patients who underwent surgery for pRP-WDLPS. Of these, 510 patients (88%) underwent surgery alone and 72 patients (12%) underwent neoadjuvant RT followed by surgery. Patient and tumor characteristics are detailed in Table 1. Among the entire cohort, there was no difference in age (P = 0·67), gender (P = 0·18), tumor multifocality (P = 0·06), tumor size (P = 0·18), or rate of macroscopically complete (R0/R1) resection (P = 0·09). The median dose of RT was 50 Gy (IQR 47–50·4). The 1:2 PSM group included 208 patients; 70 patients (34%) who underwent neoadjuvant RT followed by surgery and 138 patients (66%) who underwent surgery alone. In this group, the median age at diagnosis was 59 years (IQR 49–66) with a male predominance, although this was not statistically significant. Most tumors were unifocal (n = 201, 97%) and the median tumor size was 25 cm (IQR 17–30). All patients in the 1:2 PSM group underwent a macroscopically complete resection (R0/R1).

With a median follow up of 73 months, isolated LR developed in 215 patients (42%) in the surgery alone group and in 7 patients (10%) in the neoadjuvant RT followed by surgery group (P < 0·001) in the full cohort. In the 1:2 PSM group, isolated LR developed in 39 patients (28%) in the surgery alone group and the same 7 patients (10%) in the neoadjuvant RT followed by surgery alone group (P = 0·003). Neoadjuvant RT followed by surgery was associated with a significantly lower rate of LR when compared to surgery alone in the 1:2 PSM group. The CIF estimate of LR at 5 years and 8 years for neoadjuvant RT was 6% (95% confidence interval (CI) 2%–13%) and 10% (95% CI 4%–19%), respectively, and 26% (95% CI 18%–34%) and 33% (95% CI 24%–43%), respectively, for the surgery alone group (subdistribution hazard ratio (HR) 0·29, 95% CI 0·13–0·63, P = 0·002; Fig. 1).

In the 1:2 PSM group, neoadjuvant RT followed by surgery was not associated with a statistically significant difference in OS (HR 0·50, 95% CI 0·23–1·05, P = 0·067). There was no evidence of violation of the proportional hazards assumption in this model (χ2 = 0·54, df = 1, P = 0·46) (Supplementary Figure S3). There was also no evidence of interaction between the variables of multifocality, age, size, and gender on radiation and survival (Supplementary Table S3) The OS at 5 years and 10 years for the neoadjuvant RT followed by surgery group was 92% (95% CI 85–99%) and 80% (95% CI 68–93%), respectively, and 84% (95% CI 78–91%) and 71% (95% CI 62–82%), respectively, for the surgery alone group (Fig. 2). Use of 1:1 PSM matching did not affect the results (data not shown). Logistic regression in the 1:2 PSM cohort, using cluster-robust variances estimates, demonstrated that neoadjuvant RT was associated with a 16% decreased risk in the odds of isolated local recurrence for patients who received neoadjuvant RT (OR 0·84, 95% CI 0·74–0·94, P = 0·003). Unmatched CIF and Kaplan Meier curves using the full cohort are included in the Appendix (Supplementary Figures S4 and S5).

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this study reports the largest number of patients treated with curative intent surgery for pRP-WDLPS. This study presents strong evidence that neoadjuvant RT should be considered in the multidisciplinary management of pRP-WDLPS. Bolstered by propensity score matched analysis in order to limit selection bias, these results demonstrate that neoadjuvant RT followed by surgery is associated with significantly decreased risk of local recurrence when compared to surgery alone. The difference is striking with 26% patients treated with surgery alone experiencing a local recurrence at 5 years compared to just 6% among those patients treated with neoadjuvant RT followed by surgery. The risk of recurrence in each group persists without plateau, further supporting the need for long-term surveillance due to late recurrences in these patients. With a median follow up of over six years, neoadjuvant RT was not associated with an OS benefit. In the setting of limited data prior to the STRASS trial, the use of neoadjuvant RT for pRPS (any histology) is known to vary among institutions.5,15 For a disease process at threat of local failure alone, maximizing local control can optimize disease control and limit disease morbidity.16,17 In addition to the results of the STRASS trial, these data will support a paradigm shift for multidisciplinary providers to increase use of neoadjuvant RT for WDLPS and decrease utilization for other histologies when historically the opposite was true. It is critical that the interpretation and application of these data be individualized to each patient in the context of a multidisciplinary discussion at a sarcoma center.

These data represent the success of international collaboration in order to study the outcomes associated with a rare disease subtype. TARPSWG has developed and managed high quality data registries that have been used to report outcomes of primary and recurrent RPS, the results of which have been used to develop multiple consensus papers in order to provide management guidelines.5,18,19 TARPSWG played a pivotal role in the design, implementation, and successful completion of the STRASS trial. In addition to aiding in patient contribution for the current study, the participation and management of patients in the involved TARPSWG sarcoma centers aided in control of surgical and RT technique, though admittedly less so than a randomized study protocol.

When RT is integrated into the treatment plan for RPS, experts recommend it is exclusively delivered in the neoadjuvant setting due to the potential toxicity and dose limitations of postoperative RT. The tumor displaces adjacent organs, in particular the small bowel, out of the field and helps limit toxicity to the organs that occupy the tumor bed after resection. Additionally, the gross tumor volume can be more precisely localized and thus, the RT field is smaller. Finally, the neoadjuvant RT dose is lower than the postoperative dose. Clinically significant radiographic responses are rare and the ultimate goal is to treat or sterilize the surrounding margins, not to downstage the scope of surgery. The subtle nuances of the radiographic features of WDLPS must be appreciated and communicated among the multidisciplinary team as all gross disease should be included in RT treatment field and ultimately the extent of surgery. This is particularly important for left sided tumors when considering whether the pancreatic tail and spleen/splenic hilum are involved by tumor and should be resected in addition to the standard compartmental resection of the left colon, left kidney, and left adrenal gland.

Several retrospective studies have reported outcomes associated with the use of perioperative RT, with a smaller number of studies specifically investigating more contemporary delivery of neoadjuvant RT as opposed to adjuvant RT. Additionally, few of these studies have investigated impact on specific histologic types. Historically, outcomes for the utility of neoadjuvant RT have been contradictory and plagued by small patient numbers and heterogeneous groups. Histologic-specific outcomes have been reported by Bredbeck et al. in a single institution, retrospective study of 159 patients, of which 17 patients (11%) underwent neoadjuvant RT.20 The liposarcoma subgroup in this study demonstrated improved ARFS (8·9 years vs. 3·1 years, P < 0·001) with the addition of neoadjuvant RT. Similarly, Molina et al. reported outcomes on 41 patients with pRP-LPS and found neoadjuvant RT was associated with a significantly higher 5-year local recurrence-free survival (95·6% vs. 75·0%, P = 0·02).21 The largest study to date to investigate liposarcoma subtypes included 607 patients with pRP-LPS (WDLPS = 234, DDLPS = 373).22 In this study from the TARPSWG group, the 5- and 8-year CCI of LR was significantly lower in patients with WDLPS who underwent neoadjuvant RT followed by surgery (n = 46; 12% and 12%, respectively) when compared to surgery alone (n = 188; 24% and 39%, respectively; P = 0·01). The association of improved local control with neoadjuvant RT, however, was not statistically significant after inverse probability of treatment weighting (P = 0·58).

It is important to note that when the STRASS trial was designed in 2010, the role of histologic type in defining tumor biology and in particular patterns of recurrence had not been fully appreciated. Retrospective studies by Gronchi et al. and Tan et al. demonstrated that recurrence patterns and survival were significantly influenced by histologic type.5,23 The STRASS trial was a negative trial in that there was no difference in the primary endpoint of 3-year ARFS in the entire group of 266 patients over a median follow up of 43 months. In review of the intermediate results by the Independent Data Monitoring Committee, a post-hoc, underpowered, exploratory analysis of the 198 patients with liposarcoma was recommended that demonstrated a significant benefit with the addition of neoadjuvant RT in 3-year ARFS (76% vs. 65%, HR 0·62, 95% CI 0·38–1·02). Further subgroup analysis suggested a trend toward improved ARFS in the 46 patients with WDLPS who underwent neoadjuvant RT compared to the 42 patients who underwent surgery alone (HR 0·69, 95% CI 0·33–1·46). The aim of the subsequent STREXIT study was to combine off-trial pRPS patients at STRASS recruiting sites with the enrolled STRASS cohort to create a more robust, real-world cohort of patients in a histologic specific analysis. In the STREXIT study, neoadjuvant RT was associated with improved ARFS in patients with LPS, and in particular in those patients with WDLPS and grade 1/2 DDLPS. In this cohort, 55 patients underwent neoadjuvant RT for WDLPS and 51 patients underwent surgery alone.

There was no statistically significant association between neoadjuvant RT and OS in the current study; however, there was a trend suggesting a possible survival benefit for neoadjuvant RT followed by surgery. The median OS was not reached and demonstrates that the median OS is well beyond 10 years whether patients recurred or not. This is consistent with multiple prior studies. In the STRASS trial, where OS was a secondary endpoint, there was no difference in 5-year OS for the entire cohort with addition of neoadjuvant RT (77% vs. 79%, P = 0·62).6 Similarly, the STREXIT study and a study by Haas et al. demonstrated no difference in OS in patients with WDLPS, specifically.7,22 In contrast to these findings, Nussbaum et al. performed a case–control, PSM analysis using data from the National Cancer Data Base (NCDB) to demonstrate a 5-year OS benefit with preoperative RT for all histologies (62% vs. 54%, HR 0·70, P < 0·0001).24 It is important to note that the NCDB lacks the granular data needed to account for extent of resection, pathologic assessment variability, and patterns of recurrence. Intuitively, one would expect that locally recurrent disease would negatively impact survival at some time point; however, given the favorable disease trajectory in these patients, the follow up period for a study would need to be beyond 10 years.

The results of this study validate the results of the mixed randomized-observational STREXIT study for patients with pRP-WDLPS. The accrual of 582 patients over a 15-year time period from high-quality institutional databases with a single RPS histology is a landmark collaboration that has produced results that are practice changing/confirming. There are certainly limitations inherent to the retrospective nature of this study. While PSM aids in limiting selection bias, the study is subject to lead time bias. As many of these recurrences are asymptomatic, the frequency of surveillance may slightly differ among institutions and thus radiographic detection of recurrent disease may be delayed. However, these variations are likely to be slight as recommendations from major societies and groups in general recommend surveillance imaging every 3–6 months for the first 5 years followed by annual imaging indefinitely or until at least 10 years.18,25,26

A limitation of the current study is the lack of data on extent of primary tumor resection. While the optimal extent of resection for pRPS has been debated over the years, more recently, a histologic-specific approach has been recommended whereby a comprehensive (previously referred to as compartmental) resection (CR) should include en bloc resection of at least the ipsilateral colon and kidney.18 Musa et al. reported results from a retrospective series of 119 patients with primary LPS who underwent resection and found that a CR was an independent predictor of improved disease-free survival, however, impact on local control is less clear as CR was not associated with local-recurrence free survival.27 Snow et al. recently reported data on the variations in the patterns of care across 19 RPS referral centers worldwide and found a relatively low rate of CR for WDLPS (54·5%), irrespective of low-vs. high volume centers, and there was wide variation between centers.15 The authors that the low rates of CR may reflect individualization and patient-appropriate decision making based on age and co-morbidities in addition to diverse opinions on validity of CR for LPS.

In order to control quality and ensure all patients were treated at high-volume sarcoma centers, only those patients who underwent surgery at a participating center in the study were included in the PSM analysis. The outcome benefit in treatment at a high-volume sarcoma center has been previously demonstrated.5,28,29 Importantly, this study draws from a large group of centers, including ones that did not participate in STRASS, and from a longer time period than STRASS. An additional limitation of our study is the lack of perioperative morbidity data. Multiple retrospective studies have found no association between neoadjuvant RT and postoperative morbidity.30, 31, 32 In the STRASS trial, there were no significant differences in the number of patients requiring reoperation for any complication (11%).6

In this study, we choose to use CCI of LR as the primary endpoint. This was to avoid potential confusion associated with ARFS which was used in the STRASS trial as the primary endpoint. In the STRASS trial, ARFS was a composite endpoint originally defined as local or distant progressive disease during neoadjuvant RT, tumor or patient becoming inoperable, peritoneal metastasis found at surgery, macroscopically incomplete resection, or local relapse.6 After an unplanned secondary analysis, ARFS was redefined as alive without disease in the abdomen. This limits direct comparison but provides clarity on defined events associated with the endpoint of this study.

In conclusion, our data demonstrate that neoadjuvant RT followed by surgery significantly decreases the risk of local recurrence in patients with pRP-WDLPS. These findings expand upon the STRASS trial findings and validate the subgroup findings of the STREXIT study. These data should provide further evidence from a large group of centers over a long time period that neoadjuvant RT should be strongly considered in the multidisciplinary management of these patients. While these data do not demonstrate a statistically significant overall survival benefit, longer term follow up is required to investigate this further.

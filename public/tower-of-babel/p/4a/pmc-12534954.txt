Efficacy and safety of stereotactic body radiation therapy combined with targeted agents and immunotherapies in hepatocellular carcinoma: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Shuaihao Hou, Suqin Hu, Qiong Wu, Haonan Sun, Zhongxuan Fang, Rong Sun, Liqun Wang, Bin Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) prognosis remains poor in advanced stages. Stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) combined with pharmacological agents (targeted therapies/immunotherapies) represents a promising multimodal strategy, but its efficacy and safety require comprehensive evaluation.MethodsThis meta-analysis included 9 studies (n = 20,812). The adverse event analysis covered the overall adverse event data from 7 studies (n = 583) and grade ≥ 3 adverse events from 4 studies (n = 303). The survival outcome analysis included 9 studies (n = 20,781) and the progression-free survival analysis included 4 studies (n = 424). Background

Liver cancer is one of the malignant tumors with the highest morbidity and mortality rates worldwide, with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) accounting for approximately 90% of primary liver cancers [1]. Epidemiologic studies have shown that HCC is closely associated with chronic viral hepatitis (hepatitis B and C), alcoholic liver disease and nonalcoholic steatohepatitis. Although early-stage HCC can be treated by surgical resection, liver transplantation, or local ablation, most patients are diagnosed at advanced stages and miss the optimal period for surgical treatment or lose the opportunity for radical treatment because of insufficient liver functional reserve due to coexisting cirrhosis. In addition, systemic treatment of locally advanced or metastatic HCC has long faced challenges, while progress has been made in molecular targeted therapy and immunotherapy; however, the overall survival benefit is still unsatisfactory, and some patients have difficulty obtaining sustained benefits due to drug toxicity or tumor heterogeneity. How to break through the existing treatment modalities and optimize the multidisciplinary combination strategy has become a core issue in clinical research on HCC [2–4].

In recent years, the clinical application of pharmacological agents, particularly molecular-targeted agents and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), has revolutionized the treatment paradigm for advanced HCC [5]. Multiple kinase inhibitors, such as sorafenib and lenvatinib, have become the cornerstone of first-line treatment by blocking tumor angiogenic and proliferative signals through the inhibition of ICIs, fibroblast growth factor receptor (FGFR) and other pathways [6]. Immunotherapy centered on programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)/programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) inhibitors has resulted in lasting remission in some patients by increasing the suppression of T-cell function and activating the antitumor immune response [7]. Compared with sorafenib, the combination of atilizumab + bevacizumab significantly prolongs overall survival and has become the new first-line standard [8]. Therefore, exploring the synergistic effects of targeted or immune drugs with other local therapies has become a key direction for improving treatment efficacy.

Stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) is a high-precision radiation therapy technique that delivers ultrahigh-dose irradiation to the tumor in a single or few fractions through image guidance and multiangle dose delivery while maximizing the protection of surrounding normal tissues. In patients with inoperable HCC, SBRT can achieve local control rates comparable to those of radiofrequency ablation and is not limited by the tumor location or adjacent vessels [9]. In patients with locally advanced or oligometastatic disease, SBRT may provide symptomatic relief and delay systemic therapy resistance through rapid regression of localized lesions. Recent studies suggest that SBRT may induce immunogenic cell death, release tumor antigens and proinflammatory factors, remodel the tumor microenvironment, enhance the systemic immune response, and synergize with targeted or immunotherapies to produce a “distant effect”. Studies have shown that SBRT in combination with drugs can prolong overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS), but the safety still needs to be verified in large-scale studies [10, 11].

In the context of the rapid development of HCC treatment modalities, although the combination strategy of SBRT with pharmacological agents has potential, most of the available evidence is based on small-sample studies or retrospective analyses, and long-term follow-up data are lacking. Systematic review and meta-analysis quantitatively integrate the results of independent studies through systematic searching, rigorous screening and quality assessment, which can improve statistical efficacy, reveal potential bias and resolve clinical controversies. The aim of this study was to comprehensively evaluate the efficacy and safety of SBRT combined with targeted agents and/or immunotherapies compared with pharmacological agents by this method to provide an evidence-based basis for optimizing multimodal therapy for HCC and to guide the design of future phase III clinical trials.

Methods

Study design

This systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted following the guidelines outlined by the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA). The primary objectives of this study were to evaluate the efficacy and safety of SBRT combined with pharmacological agents in HCC patients, specifically, to compare outcomes, including adverse events (AEs), overall survival (OS), and progression-free survival (PFS), between the combined therapy group and the control group receiving pharmacological agents. The meta-analysis is registered with PROSPERO (ID: CRD 420251040157).

Search strategy

The search was conducted across PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, Elsevier, CNKI, Wangfang Med Online and CBM up to April 4, 2025. No restrictions were placed on the publication date. Multiple keyword combinations were searched to maximize the number of relevant studies retrieved. These keyword combinations included not only subject terms (“Carcinoma, Hepatocellular” and “Stereotactic Body Radiation Therapy”, etc.), but also related synonyms, variants, and specific drugs, and the search terms were finalized by a three-person discussion to minimize the risk of missing relevant studies.

Data extraction

To ensure consistency and accuracy of the information, the data were extracted independently by two researchers. Disagreements were resolved through discussion between the researchers. If consensus could not be reached through discussion, a third researcher was asked to participate in the discussion and determine whether to include the article. The excerpts include the radiation dose, number of treatments, and target range for SBRT. It also includes the specific type of combination (targeted agents, ICIs, etc.) and number of treatments, as well as the dosing regimens and doses for the experimental and control groups. The outcome metrics were mainly risk ratios (HRs)/ratios (RRs) for OS, PFS and AEs and their 95% confidence intervals (CIs). The duration of follow-up was recorded for each study, thus ensuring the accuracy of the long-term prognostic analysis.

Quality assessment

The quality of the included studies was assessed by two independent researchers via standardized tools: the Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool for randomized controlled trials and the Methodological Index for Retrospective Cohort Studies for Nonrandomized Controlled Trials (MINORS).

RCT Quality Assessment Criteria

The RCT articles were meticulously evaluated via the Cochrane risk of bias tool, which is a method that assesses the quality of articles by evaluating each item independently. The following criteria were employed to determine the quality of the articles: high quality was designated high if all areas were deemed low risk, and low quality was classified as low quality if there was a high or indeterminate risk in any of the areas. Disagreements that arose during the assessment process were resolved through discussion between investigators.

NRCT quality assessment criteria

The NRCT uses MINORS, which encompasses 12 indicators, with each indicator assigned a score on a scale of 0–2, yielding a total score range of 0–24. According to the MINORS guidelines, scores ranging from 0 to 8 points are considered low quality, scores ranging from 9 to 16 points are considered medium quality, and scores ranging from 17 to 24 points are considered high quality. To ensure the reliability of the meta-analysis results, studies with a total score of less than 12 were strictly excluded from this study.

Statistical analysis

This study was analyzed via RevMan 5.3 with Stata 18.0 statistical software. As the included literature contained two types of methodological designs, RCTs (4 articles) and RCSs (5 articles), to control for heterogeneity between studies, subgroup analyses were first performed according to the type of study design before the data were integrated and analyzed, and the combined effect sizes of each subgroup were calculated separately. When no statistically significant differences were observed between subgroups (P > 0.05), indicating minimal methodological heterogeneity affecting overall effect estimates, data from RCTs and RCSs were combined for overall effect size analysis.

Meta-analysis of the effects of adverse events in patients with HCC treated with SBRT combined with pharmacological agents

A total of seven studies were included in the analysis of AEs. Heterogeneity testing revealed moderate heterogeneity among the studies (I2 = 57%, P = 0.03). Sensitivity analysis via a stepwise exclusion method revealed that Zhang et al. [15] significantly contributed to the observed heterogeneity. The observed increase in AEs may stem from the incorporation of ICIs in the combined therapy group. Although both treatment arms employed guideline-recommended therapeutic regimens, compared with monotherapy protocols, the multiagent combination likely introduced cumulative toxicity. After excluding this study, the remaining six studies exhibited no heterogeneity (I2 = 0%, P = 0.85), and the direction of the pooled effect size remained unchanged. Consequently, a fixed-effectss model was employed for the meta-analysis. For Grade ≥ 3 AEs, four studies were included in the analysis. Heterogeneity testing indicated no significant heterogeneity (I2 = 7%, P = 0.36) and no statistically significant differences among the selected studies (P = 0.25). A fixed-effectss model was therefore applied for the meta-analysis of Grade ≥ 3 AEs.

For all-grade AEs, while the point estimate of the RR slightly exceeded 1, the narrow confidence interval spanning the null value (RR = 1) indicated no significant increase in the risk of any-grade AEs associated with the combined therapy. The effect size was minimal and of limited clinical relevance.

In the analysis of Grade ≥ 3 AEs, although no statistically significant difference was observed, the point estimate and upper limit of the confidence interval suggested a potential trend toward increased toxicity with combination therapy. Nevertheless, the limited number of included studies and the wide confidence intervals reduced the strength of this evidence. This trend may reflect either cumulative toxicity from multimodal treatment or a type II error due to insufficient statistical power in the small sample size analysis.

Meta-analysis of the effect of SBRT combined with pharmacological agents on overall survival in patients with HCC

One-year overall survival analysis

Six studies reported 1-year overall survival rates. No significant heterogeneity was detected among the studies (I2 = 42%, P = 0.11) (Fig. 5A). A fixed-effects model was employed for the meta-analysis. Compared with pharmacological agents, combined therapy significantly improved 1-year overall survival in HCC patients. The pooled RR was 1.55 (95% CI: 1.40–1.72), indicating that the combined therapy group presented 1.55-fold higher 1-year survival rates than the control group, with statistical significance (P < 0.01). A funnel plot was constructed to evaluate publication bias, with Begg's test applied for statistical assessment (Fig. 5C). The resulting P-value of 0.815 indicated no significant publication bias in the current meta-analysis.

Two-year overall survival analysis

Three studies reported 2-year overall survival rates. No significant heterogeneity was observed among the studies (I2 = 29%, P = 0.24) (Fig. 5B). A fixed-effects model was employed for the meta-analysis, which revealed that, compared with pharmacological agents, combined therapy significantly improved 2-year overall survival. The pooled RR was 1.63 (95% CI: 1.33–1.99), indicating that the combined therapy group presented 1.63-fold higher 2-year survival rates than did the control group, with statistical significance (P < 0.01).Publication bias was assessed through a funnel plot (Fig. 5D). Begg’s test for bias yielded a P-value of 0.563, suggesting that there was no significant publication bias in the included studies.

Median overall survival analysis

This meta-analysis included six studies. Heterogeneity testing revealed moderate heterogeneity among the included studies (I2 = 66%, P = 0.01), suggesting that either a random effects model could be appropriately applied or further investigation into the sources of heterogeneity was warranted. Using the random effects model, the pooled analysis demonstrated that, compared with pharmacological agents, combined therapy significantly reduced mortality risk (HR = 0.54, 95% CI: 0.40–0.72), indicating a survival benefit associated with the combined approach. However, moderate heterogeneity was observed, potentially attributable to variations in data sources across countries or other confounding factors. To address this limitation, subgroup analyses will be conducted to explore potential sources of heterogeneity systematically.

Subgroup meta-analysis of the effect of median overall survival in patients with HCC treated with SBRT combined with pharmacological agents

Sensitivity analysis demonstrated the robustness of the pooled random effects estimates, as exclusion of any individual study did not materially alter the overall conclusions (Fig. 6C). Meta-regression stratified by country of origin categorized the six studies into two subgroups. In the US subgroup (n = 2), no heterogeneity was observed (I2 = 0%, P = 0.87), and fixed-effects analysis revealed a significant survival benefit for combined therapy versus pharmacological agents (HR = 0.76, 95% CI: 0.63–0.91), corresponding to a 24% mortality risk reduction. Similarly, the China subgroup (n = 4) showed homogeneity (I2 = 0%, P = 0.58) and even greater efficacy (HR = 0.41, 95% CI: 0.31–0.55), indicating a 59% risk reduction (Fig. 6A). Begg's test revealed no significant publication bias in either subgroup (US: P = 1.000; China: P = 0.089) (Fig. 6B).

Meta-analysis of the effect of objective response rate in patients with HCC treated with SBRT combined with pharmacological agents

The DCR analysis included six studies. The PFS analysis included four studies. Heterogeneity testing revealed no significant heterogeneity among the studies (I2 = 0%, P = 0.71) (Fig. 9A), thus justifying the application of a fixed-effects model for data pooling. Subgroup analysis of the effect of SBRT combined with pharmacological agents on radiotherapy sequencing in patients with HCC

Given the potential impact of SBRT timing on clinical outcomes, we conducted subgroup analyses comparing concurrent versus sequential SBRT for the following endpoints. Among the included studies, no statistically significant differences were observed for DCR (P = 0.22), ORR (P = 0.80), ≥ 3 AEs (P = 0.11), or AEs (P = 0.06). The median OS was significantly different (P = 0.04), with concurrent SBRT associated with a 63% reduction in mortality risk (HR = 0.37, 95% CI: 0.23–0.61) and sequential SBRT associated with a 35% reduction in mortality risk (HR = 0.65, 95% CI: 0.52–0.82)(Supplementary Fig. S1). Notably, the finding is derived from only two studies per subgroup and there is heterogeneity, resulting in limited statistical power. The exploratory nature of this analysis precludes definitive clinical inferences but underscores the necessity for protocol standardization in future trials to validate sequencing effects.

Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis comprehensively evaluated the comprehensive impact of SBRT combined with pharmacological agents in patients with HCC. The analysis highlights the clinical application of combination therapy, demonstrating significant benefits in terms of survival outcomes, adverse events, and objective response rates, ultimately improving patients' quality of life. In current clinical practice, targeted immunotherapy combinations have become the first-line standard regimen for advanced HCC, while advancements in radiotherapy technology have further expanded the value of localized treatment in the multimodal management of advanced HCC [10].

SBRT not only directly induces tumor cell death through DNA double-strand breaks but also triggers immunogenic cell death (ICD), releasing tumor-specific antigens and activating systemic antitumor immune responses. Concurrently, antiangiogenic agents enhance therapeutic efficacy by inhibiting the VEGF pathway, reducing abnormal neovascularization, and improving tumor vascular structure. This alleviates hypoxia, reduces interstitial pressure, and potentiates radiotherapy-induced tumor cell killing while promoting immune cell infiltration (e.g., T cells) into tumor tissues. Furthermore, immune checkpoint inhibitors counteract radiotherapy-induced upregulation of PD-L1 expression and activation of immunosuppressive cells, establishing a radiation-immunotherapy positive feedback loop. These multimodal synergistic mechanisms provide a robust preclinical rationale for the "SBRT + targeted therapy + immunotherapy" triple-modality strategy. Preliminary results from single-arm clinical studies have shown its safety and efficacy, although larger prospective trials are needed for validation [21, 22].

This comprehensive analysis demonstrated that SBRT combined with pharmacological agents confers significant clinical benefits in HCC patients. Compared with pharmacological agents, the combined regimen significantly improves ORR (pooled RR = 1.87; RCTs: RR = 1.53, 95% CI = 1.19–1.96 vs. RCSs: RR = 2.41, 95% CI = 1.70–3.41) and DCR (RR = 1.32) while markedly reducing risks of PFS (HR = 0.49) and mortality (mOS HR = 0.54, with 55% and 63% increases in 1- and 2-year survival rates). These findings suggest that synergistic mechanisms—such as immune microenvironment remodeling or local-systemic effects interplay between radiotherapy and systemic therapies—effectively suppress tumor progression and prolong survival.

From a safety perspective, the incidence of AEs in the combined therapy group was not significantly different from that in the pharmacological agent group (P > 0.05). This favorable safety profile may be attributed to advancements in precision radiotherapy techniques, including image-guided real-time tumor tracking and optimized dose gradient distribution, which mitigate radiation-induced liver injury. However, vigilance is warranted for potential trends toward increased grade ≥ 3 toxicities (RR = 1.12), likely reflecting cumulative effects of radiation and pharmacological agents.

Subgroup analysis revealed differences in survival benefits between Chinese and US populations (59% vs. 24% reduction in mortality), with multiple factors contributing to this disparity. For example, HCC associated with the hepatitis B virus (hepatitis B is more common in Asian populations) may exhibit greater sensitivity to immunotherapy, but methodological differences also play a role: U.S. data come from the National Cancer Database (NCDB) and randomized clinical trials, reflecting real-world heterogeneity, whereas Chinese studies are primarily prospective studies. Subgroup analysis of the impact of SBRT sequence on survival and safety outcomes in patients with HCC, due to the limited number of included studies, further research is needed to confirm these findings. Additionally, the limitations of this study regarding heterogeneity in SBRT protocols and drug combinations remain unresolved. Further rigorous randomized controlled trials are needed to validate these findings. Future multicenter phase III RCTs should standardize intervention protocols and integrate biomarker-driven strategies to identify patient subgroups most likely to benefit from this combined modality.

Limitations of the study

While this meta-analysis provides valuable insights into HCC patients receiving SBRT combined with pharmacological agents, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, unmeasured technical variables (such as the irradiation field, dose, and schedule of SBRT, as well as timing related to systemic therapy) may confound efficacy comparisons. Additionally, the inclusion of mixed treatment lines (first-line/second-line) may limit generalizability, and residual confounding factors from prior systemic therapy cannot be excluded. The lack of detailed data has impacted the analysis of outcome measures, which are critical determinants of SBRT efficacy. Second, the RCSs (n = 5) over RCTs (n = 4) may introduce selection bias due to inherent confounding in nonrandomized designs. None of the included studies employed blinding, which could introduce measurement bias in subjective outcomes such as AEs or ORR. However, primary survival endpoints (OS and PFS) rely on objective assessments (imaging and laboratory data), likely minimizing the impact of unblinded designs. Future studies could enhance reliability by implementing partial blinding strategies (e.g., independent blinded central review of imaging) or stricter objective biomarkers. Additionally, the limited number of studies reporting grade ≥ 3 adverse events (AEs) (n = 4) constrains clinical decision-making. Mild asymmetry at the funnel plot base and the small sample size raise concerns about potential unpublished negative results, although Begg’s test showed no statistical significance (P > 0.05). These limitations underscore the need for larger, prospective trials with standardized AE reporting protocols to validate these findings.

Conclusion

On the basis of current clinical evidence, SBRT combined with pharmacological agents (targeted therapies and/or immunotherapies) demonstrates superior clinical benefits compared with pharmacological monotherapy in patients with HCC. The combined therapy group showed significant improvements in ORR, DCR, PFS, and OS, without a statistically significant increase in overall adverse events. Subgroup analysis revealed that Asian populations may derive greater benefit; however, current evidence is limited by retrospective studies and inconsistent treatment modalities, with no direct data to support this. Future studies should include multicenter phase III randomized controlled trials (RCTs) to validate the survival outcomes of SBRT combined with pharmacological agents in treating HCC caused by different etiologies.

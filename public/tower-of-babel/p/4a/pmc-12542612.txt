Safety monitoring of GSK’s quadrivalent seasonal influenza vaccine in European countries during the 2023/24 flu season: A multicenter, non-interventional study
Authors: Ignacio Salamanca de la Cueva, Carlos Brotons, Pia Maier, Jean-Yves Pirçon, Vinay Gupta, Tamara Eckermann, Vanja Nikic, Xavier Martinez Gomez, Hannah Alsdurf
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTInfluenza significantly impacts global health, particularly vulnerable populations like the elderly and those with chronic conditions. This study evaluated the safety of GlaxoSmithKline (GSK)’s quadrivalent seasonal influenza vaccine (IIV4) during the 2023/24 influenza season in Belgium, Germany, and Spain, per the European Medicines Agency’s enhanced safety surveillance guidelines. This prospective, multicenter, non-interventional study, conducted from October 2, 2023, to January 4, 2024, included adults from Belgium and both children and adults from Germany and Spain. Participants received GSK’s IIV4 and completed an adverse drug reaction (ADR) questionnaire via an electronic patient-reported outcome portal. Adverse events (AEs) were categorized by severity and analyzed by age group and risk status. Overall, 997 participants received at least one dose of IIV4. Of these, 900 who received Dose 1 and all 14 who received Dose 2 completed the ADR questionnaire. Overall, 42.6% of participants reported AEs post-Dose 1, primarily injection site pain (33.2%), fatigue (14.1%), and headache (12.7%), and 28.6% reported AEs post-Dose 2, primarily injection site pain (21.4%). The AE profile was consistent across age groups and risk statuses, with no serious AEs reported. The study’s findings align with those from the previous two influenza seasons (2021–2023), confirming the vaccine’s stable safety profile. High compliance rates ( >90%) for ADR reporting and consistency with earlier studies further validate these results. No new or unexpected AEs were identified, supporting the vaccine’s safe use in the indicated age groups. Thus, GSK’s IIV4 has a favorable safety profile and is suitable for broad use across various demographics.KEYWORDS: Adverse events, enhanced safety surveillance, immunization, influenza, quadrivalent seasonal influenza vaccine

Introduction

Influenza, also known as the flu, is an acute, highly contagious viral infection primarily affecting the respiratory system. It poses a significant global public health burden, especially due to its ability to spread rapidly through respiratory droplets.1,2 Symptoms range from mild fatigue, fever, and body aches to severe complications such as sepsis, pneumonia, and even death, particularly in vulnerable populations such as the elderly and those with chronic conditions like lung or heart diseases.1,2 Influenza is responsible for up to 50 million symptomatic cases and 15,000–70,000 deaths in the European Union (EU) each year, with significant seasonal variations.3 A recent study estimated that the average number of influenza-associated respiratory deaths in 28 EU countries was 27,600 per year, ranging from 16,200 to 39,000, with significant variations by age group and country.4 This study emphasizes the substantial burden of influenza during 2002–2011 (excluding the 2009 pandemic), particularly among vulnerable populations such as the elderly (≥65 years), who experience significantly higher mortality rates (35 times higher) than younger groups (<65 years). Belgium had an average of 689 influenza-associated deaths per year (6.5 per 100,000), Germany had 5048 (6.2 per 100,000), and Spain had 2402 (5.4 per 100,000), with the majority of deaths occurring in the elderly population.4

A systematic literature review analyzing data from 40 countries estimated the global influenza-associated hospitalization rate and highlighted particularly high hospitalization rates among vulnerable populations, including children aged 0–4 years and the elderly aged ≥65 years.5 These findings demonstrate the critical impact of influenza on both ends of the age spectrum, emphasizing the need for targeted prevention measures, especially vaccination, in high-risk groups.5

Annual influenza vaccination remains the most effective measure to mitigate the risk of infection and its associated complications.6 However, the influenza virus is known for its high variability due to frequent genetic and antigenic changes.7 As a result, seasonal vaccines must be reformulated annually to target the most prevalent circulating strains.8 This ongoing evolution necessitates constant benefit-risk monitoring to ensure the vaccines’ efficacy and safety for the population, allowing health authorities to make informed decisions regarding vaccination campaigns.

In Europe, the European Medicines Agency (EMA) has released comprehensive guidance on enhanced safety surveillance (ESS) for seasonal influenza vaccines.9 This guidance establishes rigorous standards for vaccine surveillance to which all marketing authorization holders must adhere. The primary objective is to enable rapid detection of any potential safety concerns or adverse events (AEs) following vaccination, ensuring prompt investigation and response to safeguard public health.9 In alignment with EMA guidelines, GlaxoSmithKline (GSK) has been conducting annual safety monitoring activities since 201510–16 to assess the safety of their quadrivalent seasonal influenza vaccine (IIV4) in individuals aged 6 months and older.

This study was designed in accordance with the EMA guidelines to evaluate the frequency and severity of AEs that arise within 7 days after administration of GSK’s IIV4 during the 2023/24 flu season across Belgium, Germany, and Spain (primary endpoint). Furthermore, the AE profile was analyzed by age and risk status (secondary endpoint). Furthermore, we present the culmination of a comprehensive series of annual ESS studies conducted over three consecutive influenza seasons, from 2021/22 to 2023/24, across Belgium, Germany and Spain.

Methods

Study design and population

This prospective, multicenter, non-interventional study was conducted at seven healthcare facilities in Belgium (n = 1), Germany (n = 3), and Spain (n = 3) from October 2, 2023, to January 4, 2024. Due to practical limitations, centers in Belgium focused on adults aged ≥18 years, while those in Germany and Spain included children aged ≥6 months to 17 years and adults aged 18–65 years and >65 years.

The study protocol was approved by ethics committees or institutional review boards at each center before recruitment began. Research staff approached adults aged ≥18 years and legally acceptable representatives of children aged ≥6 months to 17 years to gauge their interest in participating in the study following their receipt of GSK’s IIV4 vaccines (AlphaRix Tetra in Belgium; Influsplit Tetra in Germany; Fluarix Tetra in Spain) as part of routine healthcare. After obtaining written consent, participants or their guardians received adverse drug reaction (ADR) and coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) assessment forms via an electronic patient-reported outcome (ePRO) web-based portal and were instructed to complete them for data collection. All participants were required to have reliable access to the internet and a device to record their ePROs. Participants were categorized by age groups (6–35 months, 3–17 years, 18–65 years, and >65 years) and according to their risk status, as determined by healthcare providers (HCPs) using their clinical judgment. The ADR questionnaire (in the ePRO web-based portal) contained predefined AEIs that were to be confirmed by the participants and a free-text field to report other relevant AEs experienced by them. The severity of AEs was evaluated based on the Division of Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome (DAIDS) Adverse Event Grading System17 and categorized as mild, moderate, severe, or potentially life-threatening. Participants used an ePRO web portal to report their COVID-19 history and symptoms based on WHO criteria,18,19 and the impact on the study was evaluated.

The recruitment period lasted from October 2 to December 21, 2023, for participants aged 9 and older, who needed only one dose of the seasonal influenza vaccine. For children under 9 years without prior vaccination, recruitment ended on November 30, 2023, to allow time for the second dose of GSK’s IIV4, given at least 4 weeks after the first dose. Interim analyses were conducted to monitor safety and adverse events.

Statistical methods

The study aimed to enroll approx. 1,000 participants who received GSK’s IIV4 to identify very common (≥1/10), common (≥1/100 to <1/10), and uncommon (≥1/1,000 to <1/100) AEs. The analysis of AEs was conducted using the safety set, which comprised individuals who received either Dose 1 and/or Dose 2 and who opened the ADR questionnaire via ePRO web-based portal. Sensitivity analysis included participants from the solicited safety set who completed the ADR questionnaire and reported AEs and were also defined separately for Dose 1 and Dose 2. Sensitivity analyses were conducted to evaluate the impact of including participants who opened the ADR questionnaire via the provided weblink but did not complete the questionnaire. This analysis aimed to understand the potential influence of incomplete responses on the study findings. Additionally, the clustering effect was assessed to account for variations across study centers/countries, ensuring robustness and reliability in the interpretation of adverse event data. Demographics and risk status for influenza-related morbidity and mortality were summarized using frequency tables for categorical data and descriptive statistics for numerical data. The cumulative percentage of participants reporting AEs within 7 days post-vaccination was calculated for each dose, using ISO Weeks 40–52 of 2023, categorized by Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) system organ class (SOC) and preferred term (PT). Percentages were further stratified by age groups (6–35 months, 3–17 years, 18–65 years, >65 years) and risk status (at risk/not at risk). Exact (Clopper-Pearson) 95% confidence intervals (CIs)20 were calculated for all percentages, taking into consideration center clustering.

Results

Participant disposition and characteristics

Of the planned 1,000 participants, three did not meet the eligibility criteria and were excluded from the analysis. A total of 997 participants received at least one dose of GSK’s IIV4, enrolled across Belgium (n = 100, 10.0%), Germany (n = 447, 44.8%), and Spain (n = 450, 45.1%). Further, 440 (98.4%) participants in Germany and 443 (98.4%) in Spain received one dose, while 7 (1.6%) in each country received two doses. Of the 997 participants, 900 (90.3%) participants who received Dose 1 and all 14 who received Dose 2 of GSK’s IIV4 accessed and completed the ADR questionnaire via the ePRO web-based portal, successfully completing the study (Figure 1).

Among the participants who received Dose 1, the highest risk group was comprised of older adults aged ≥65 years (18.0% overall), followed by individuals aged > 6 months with chronic medical conditions (17.9%). A total of 256 participants (25.7%) received other vaccines co-administered with GSK’s IIV4 at Dose 1, with country-specific variations: Belgium (n = 7, 7.0%), Germany (n = 74, 16.6%), and Spain (n = 175, 38.9%). In Belgium, co-administered vaccines included COVID-19 (n = 3, 3.0%), pertussis (n = 3, 3.0%), and pneumococcal (n = 1, 1.0%) vaccines, whereas in Germany (n = 71, 15.9%) and Spain (n = 167, 37.1%), COVID-19 vaccines were the most common. No participants reported co-administration at Dose 2.

AEs

A total of 383 of the 900 participants (42.6%) reported at least one AE within 7 days of receiving Dose 1 of GSK’s IIV4 (45.8% in Belgium, 47.2% in Germany, and 36.9% in Spain; Table 2). The percentage of participants reporting AEs within 7 days post-Dose 2 was 28.6% (n = 4/14), including 42.9% in Germany (n = 3/7) and 14.3% in Spain (n = 1/7; Table 3). The most frequently reported AEs ( >5% cumulative frequency by PT) after Dose 1 were injection site pain (33.2% overall), fatigue (14.1%), headache (12.7%), injection site swelling (8.7%), myalgia (6.3%), and injection site erythema (5.3%; Table 2). After Dose 2, injection site pain was the only AE reported by more than one participant and only occurred in Germany (3 participants; Table 3).

Overall, 47.7% of participants in Germany (196 participants) and 37.2% in Spain (146 participants) reported at least one AE after the administered vaccinations during the study. In Germany, AEs reported by >5% of the participants included fatigue (19.5%), injection site erythema (5.8%), injection site pain (38.9%), injection site swelling (10.0%), arthralgia (5.1%), myalgia (8.3%), headache (14.1%), and rhinorrhea (7.1%). In Spain, AEs reported by >5% of the participants included fatigue (9.9%), injection site erythema (5.9%), injection site pain (29.3%), injection site swelling (8.9%), and headache (10.7%).

After Dose 1, most participants (27.1%) experienced mild AEs (32.3% in Belgium, 29.0% in Germany, and 23.9% in Spain). Moderate AEs were reported by 13.0% of participants overall (10.4% in Belgium, 13.9% in Germany, and 12.7% in Spain). Severe AEs were less common, affecting 2.4% of participants overall (3.1% in Belgium, 4.4% in Germany, and 0.3% in Spain). Following Dose 2, 3 of 7 participants in Germany (42.9%) reported mild AEs, while 1 of 7 participants in Spain (14.3%) reported moderate AEs. No severe AEs were reported after Dose 2. The cumulative percentages of participants in Germany who experienced mild, moderate, or severe AEs following any vaccine dose were 29.4%, 13.9%, and 4.4%, respectively. In Spain, the percentages were 23.9% for mild AEs, 13.0% for moderate AEs, and 0.3% for severe AEs. No potentially life-threatening AEs were reported during the study.

AEs by age strata and risk status

After Dose 1, AE reporting varied by age group and country. Among participants aged 18–65 years, Belgium had the highest AE rate at 65.1% (28 of 43), followed by Spain at 58.1% (43 of 74) and Germany at 49.8% (153 of 307). Among those aged 6–35 months, the AE rates were 60.0% (3 of 5) in Germany and 23.3% (7 of 30) in Spain. Among those aged 3–17 years, Germany reported an AE rate of 40.5% (36 of 89) and Spain reported 30.9% (62 of 201). Among participants aged >65 years, Spain had the highest AE rate at 37.5% (33 of 88), followed by Belgium at 30.2% (16 of 53) and Germany at 20.0% (2 of 10). Participants at risk for influenza-associated morbidity – older adults (≥65 years), individuals (>6 months) with chronic or respiratory conditions, immunosuppression, healthcare workers, pregnant women, and children (6–23 months) – also reported AEs post-Dose 1 at rates of 42.5% (37/87) in Belgium, 43.9% (29/66) in Germany, and 44.8% (81/181) in Spain. None of the patients experienced severe AEs after Dose 2. After Dose 2, all at-risk participants in Germany (1 of 1) and 14.3% (1 of 7) of those in Spain reported at least one AE.

Sensitivity analysis using the solicited safety set

After Dose 1, the cumulative percentage of participants reporting any AEs was identical in both the safety set (42.6%) and the solicited safety set (42.6%). In the solicited safety set, 45.8% (44 of 96) of participants in Belgium, 47.2% (194 of 411) in Germany, and 36.9% (145 of 393) in Spain reported at least one AE within 7 days post-Dose 1. After Dose 2, the AE frequency in the safety set was the same as that in the solicited safety set, which included three participants from Germany and one from Spain. The sensitivity analysis revealed consistent AE frequencies between the two sets.

For the 2021/22 influenza season,15 977 participants were enrolled: 422 (43.2%) from Germany and 555 (56.8%) from Spain. Among them, 268 participants were lost to follow-up and three withdrew consent (not due to an AE), which resulted in 706 participants (72.3%) completing the study. A total of 20 children received two doses of GSK’s IIV4.15 For the 2022/23 influenza season,16 1,332 participants were enrolled across Belgium (n = 100), Germany (n = 674), and Spain (n = 558). Of these, 125 participants were lost to follow-up (n = 123) or withdrew consent (n = 2, not due to an AE), leading to 1207 participants (90.6%) completing the study. Of the enrolled children, 43 received two doses of GSK’s IIV4. For the 2023/24 influenza season (067 study, present study), 997 participants were enrolled from Belgium (n = 100), Germany (n = 447), and Spain (n = 450). Among these 97 participants, 91 were lost to follow-up and 6 withdrew consent (not due to an AE), resulting in 900 participants (90.3%) completing the study. During this period, 14 children received two doses of GSK’s IIV4.

COVID-19 and its impact on the study

Of 900 participants in the safety set 289 participants for Dose 1 and none in the safety set of Dose 2 reported receiving COVID-19 vaccination between April 1, 2023, and the end of the study. Among those who received COVID-19 vaccination, the cumulative percentage of participants reporting any AEs within 7 days post-vaccination was 47.8%. In contrast, among the 611 participants without a history of COVID-19 vaccination, the cumulative percentage of those reporting any AEs within 7 days was 40.1% (95% CI: 26.97, 54.36).

Discussion

This ESS study of GSK’s IIV4, conducted across Belgium, Germany, and Spain during the 2023/24 influenza season, provides an in-depth view of the AEs reported across all eligible age groups. In this study, >90% of the ADR forms for Dose 1 and Dose 2 were completed, demonstrating the robustness of the data collection process. This high rate of compliance across the participating countries suggests the reliability of the safety findings and reinforces the value of continuous safety monitoring of influenza vaccines. This information will be crucial for future vaccine safety surveillance.

In this study, the most frequently reported AEs after Dose 1 were injection site pain, fatigue, headache, injection site swelling, myalgia, and injection site erythema. After Dose 2, the only AE reported by more than one participant in Germany was injection site pain. These findings are consistent with those from previous ESS studies conducted during the 2022/23 influenza season, which reported similar rates of AEs after both doses (49.8% after Dose 1 and 18.6% after Dose 2).16 The pattern of AEs predominantly falling under the MedDRA SOC “general disorders and administration site conditions” further supports the consistency of the safety profile. In the present study, the types and frequencies of AEs, including AEIs and other AEs, aligned with those outlined in the Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) for GSK’s IIV4.21 Importantly, no new or unexpected AEs were identified, and the magnitude of the reported AEs generally remained similar to or was lower than that documented in the SmPC for GSK’s IIV4. Notably, no serious AEs were reported during the study, highlighting the vaccine’s favorable safety profile. This result is particularly significant as it confirms the vaccine’s established safety in diverse population groups, providing important reassurance for HCPs and regulatory bodies regarding the widespread use of GSK’s IIV4. The absence of unexpected or serious AEs strengthens confidence regarding the vaccine’s suitability for broad use across various demographics, aligning with the safety outcomes observed in previous studies.15,16 The observed safety and reactogenicity profiles of GSK’s IIV4 in this study mirror those observed in earlier ESS studies involving the same age groups, confirming the vaccine’s stable safety profile over time.13–16

The consistent results observed across the 3-year study period (2021–2024)15,16 in these European countries (Belgium, Germany, and Spain) reinforce the overall benefit-risk profile of GSK’s IIV4. This consistency bolsters confidence in the study findings that the vaccine is safe for use among individuals aged ≥6 months. Uniformity in the patterns of common AEs highlights the vaccine’s reliable safety and tolerability. By understanding these patterns, HCPs can set realistic expectations regarding vaccine tolerability, ultimately improving patient adherence and satisfaction with vaccination programs.

A notable aspect of this study was the documentation of the co-administration of other vaccines with GSK’s IIV4, with no observed increase in the frequency of AEs. Approximately one-fourth of the vaccinees were co-administered vaccines on the same day as IIV4, yet no significant impact on AE rates was detected. This finding is crucial as it demonstrates the vaccine’s compatibility with other vaccines, adding to its practicality in real-world clinical settings where co-administration is common.

In this study, nearly 1,000 participants across three European countries provided valuable data on the safety of GSK’s IIV4 when administered according to local practices. However, it is important to acknowledge certain limitations. The study sample, while robust, did not fully represent the diversity of the entire European population, as not all European countries where the vaccine was approved were included. The limited representation of vaccinees from minority geographic ancestries may affect the generalizability of the findings to non-White populations. Additionally, the enrollment period, linked to the timing of the national vaccination campaigns, did not allow for the inclusion of all potential participants, which may have further influenced the broader applicability of the findings. Approximately 37.1% of the participants in this study belonged to an “at-risk” group for influenza-associated morbidity and mortality. This proportion offers a robust dataset for understanding the vaccine’s safety in both at-risk and not – at-risk populations. However, the heterogeneous nature of the at-risk population subgroups may limit the accuracy of these estimations. Despite this limitation, the inclusion of such a substantial at-risk population supports the broader applicability of the study’s findings, particularly for populations that are most vulnerable to influenza-related complications. Another limitation is the study’s descriptive nature. The lack of hypothesis-driven statistical analyses implies that the results, while informative, are primarily observational and should be interpreted in that context. The study was not designed or powered to detect rare AEs; however, this was in line with the EMA’s ESS initiative requirements, as routine pharmacovigilance activities will continue to monitor such events. Since the study relied on spontaneous reporting of events, there is potential for reporting bias, as mild or expected side effects were likely under-reported, potentially underestimating the true incidence of such events. Although AEs following vaccination were reported, this study was not designed to establish causality. Lastly, the study had a dropout rate of 9.7%, which, while not significantly affecting the overall findings, should be considered when interpreting the results.

Despite these limitations, this study has several key strengths. First, it included participants from all age groups indicated for vaccination, ensuring a comprehensive overview of the AEs experienced. The use of an electronic data capture system facilitated efficient data collection and analysis, enabling near – real-time assessment of safety outcomes. The high compliance rate for ADR reporting (>90%) across all countries further contributed to the reliability of the findings. Moreover, the consistency of the results with those observed in the previous two influenza seasons adds to the robustness of the safety profile of GSK’s IIV4.

Conclusion

This ESS, conducted during the 2023/24 influenza season, analyzed data from 900 participants who received GSK’s IIV4. The study found no safety signals that could impact public health or alter the vaccine’s benefit-risk profile. No serious AEs related to the vaccine were reported, and all reported AEs were within the expected scope of the populations studied. The study, therefore, supports and confirms the safety profile of GSK’s IIV4 in the indicated age categories.

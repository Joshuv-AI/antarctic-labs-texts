A phase II study of weekly carboplatin and concurrent radiotherapy in older adults with locally advanced non‐small cell lung cancer (LOGIK1902)
Authors: Taishi Harada, Tomonari Sasaki, Hidenobu Ishii, Shinnosuke Takemoto, Yasushi Hisamatsu, Haruhiro Saito, Yasuto Yoneshima, Kazutoshi Komiya, Kosuke Kashiwabara, Katsuhiko Naoki, Tomohiro Ogawa, Hiroaki Takeoka, Koichi Saruwatari, Kensaku Ito, Yuko Tsuchiya‐Kawano, Keiko Mizuno, Takayuki Shimose, Yoshiyuki Shioyama, Isamu Okamoto
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundConcurrent chemoradiotherapy is the standard therapy for locally advanced non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). However, there is little evidence supporting its use in older adults. Low‐dose daily carboplatin combined with thoracic radiotherapy is considered a standard regimen for this population. To establish a simple and feasible carboplatin administration method, we conducted a study of weekly carboplatin and concurrent radiotherapy for older adults with locally advanced NSCLC.MethodsThis prospective, single‐arm, multicenter, phase II clinical trial included patients aged ≥75 years with unresectable stage III NSCLC and Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status 0–1. Patients received chemoradiotherapy (60 Gy/30 fractions plus concurrent weekly carboplatin at an area under curve of 2 mg mL−1 min−1). The primary endpoint was the overall response rate (ORR). Key secondary endpoints included progression‐free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety.ResultsFrom July 2020 to June 2022, 37 patients were enrolled from 15 institutions, and 36 patients were evaluable for efficacy and safety. The ORR was 63.9% (95% confidence interval [CI] = 47.6–77.5). Median PFS was 14.6 months (95% CI = 9.1–18.1). Median OS was 25.5 months (95% CI = 17.4–not reached). Grade 4 leucopenia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia were observed in one patient (2.8%) each.ConclusionWeekly carboplatin and concurrent radiation therapy was safe in older adults with locally advanced NSCLC, and promising activity was observed.

INTRODUCTION

Concurrent chemoradiotherapy remains the standard of care for locally advanced non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). 1 , 2 The preferred choice of chemotherapy for this therapy consists of platinum doublet regimens. Standard regimens incorporating drugs such as paclitaxel, docetaxel, and pemetrexed, along with platinum agents, have been developed since the 1990s. Nevertheless, evidence suggests that these drugs are not superior to the previous generation of drugs, such as mitomycin C, vindesine, and etoposide, in terms of overall survival (OS). 3 , 4 , 5

After more than 20 years of insufficient advancements, durvalumab, a drug targeting programmed death ligand 1, was introduced. Its effectiveness as a maintenance therapy after concurrent chemoradiotherapy has been proven. 6 , 7 Nonetheless, previous trials mainly involved young patients, and limited evidence exists regarding its efficacy and safety in older adults, 8 particularly those aged 75 years and older. These patients are often unsuitable for intensive treatment because of their frailty. 9 In practice, fewer older adults with locally advanced NSCLC received chemoradiation therapy compared with younger patients. 10 , 11 Although retrospective analyses suggested that chemoradiotherapy was as effective in older adults as in younger patients, 12 the optimal method of chemoradiation remains unclear. 13

A clinical trial (JCOG0301) investigating the efficacy of low‐dose daily carboplatin with concurrent radiotherapy in patients aged 71 years and older revealed its superiority over radiotherapy alone. 14 This treatment followed by durvalumab could potentially serve as a standard treatment for older adults. However, current regimens are cumbersome, requiring daily administration of carboplatin 1 h before radiotherapy, and they are hematologically toxic. We therefore conducted a phase II study in older adults with locally advanced NSCLC in which carboplatin was administered weekly along with radiotherapy.

METHODS

Study design

This was a single‐arm, prospective, open‐label, multicenter, phase II trial. We evaluated the efficacy and safety of weekly carboplatin and concurrent radiotherapy in patients aged 75 years or older with locally advanced NSCLC. Maintenance durvalumab was allowed but was not required in the protocol. At the time of study design, there was no information on progression‐free survival (PFS) or OS among older adults with locally advanced NSCLC treated with durvalumab plus chemoradiotherapy. We therefore defined ORR as the primary endpoint. Secondary endpoints included PFS, OS, the objective response rate (chemoradiotherapy phase without the confirmation period), and safety.

Ethical considerations

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Clinical Research Network Fukuoka on June 3, 2020 (approval number: 20C‐02). Written informed consent was obtained from all patients prior to enrollment. The trial has been registered in the Japan Registry of Clinical Trial database (ID: JRCTs 071200019).

Study participants, eligibility, and exclusion criteria

Patients were enrolled between July 2020 and June 2022 and followed up until June 2023. The complete inclusion and exclusion criteria are presented in Table S1. Briefly, patients aged 75 years or older with unresectable stage III NSCLC were included.

Chemotherapy

All patients received carboplatin at an area under the curve of 2 mg mL−1 min−1 every week. Before weekly carboplatin administration during concurrent chemoradiotherapy, patients had to meet the following criteria: leukocyte count ≥2000/mm3, platelet count ≥75 000/mm3, AST and ALT levels ≤100 IU mL−1, total bilirubin concentration ≤1.5 mg dL−1, creatinine concentration ≤1.5 mg dL−1, and other non‐hematologic toxicities of grade ≤2 excluding alopecia.

Radiotherapy

Radiotherapy for all patients consisted of 60 Gy of radiation given in 30 fractions over 6 weeks with a linear accelerator photon beam of 6–10 MV. The use of a computed tomography (CT) simulator and a three‐dimensional treatment planning device is mandatory for treatment planning. In CT for treatment planning, as a rule, images should be taken with a slice thickness of 3 mm or smaller. The clinical target volume (CTV) was equal to the internal gross target volume (iGTV) including the primary tumor and metastatic nodes. No prophylactic lymph node irradiation was delivered to the regional lymph nodes. The iGTV plus an appropriate subclinical extension comprised the CTV. The iGTV primary plus a margin of 5–10 mm was the CTV primary. The iGTV node plus a margin of 0–5 mm was the CTV node. For the CTV margin, it is acceptable to adjust the margin considering anatomical and pathological characteristics. The planning target volumes (PTVs) for the primary tumor and metastatic lymph nodes were calculated as the CTV plus adequate margins (typically 0.5–1.0 cm). A three‐dimensional treatment planning device was used for dose calculation, and treatment algorithms equivalent to the superposition method and anisotropic analytical algorithm or higher were used to correct for heterogeneity. 15 , 16 The prescribed dose (60 Gy/30 fractions) was planned to be administered to 95% of the PTV volume. Normal tissue constraints are shown in Table S2.

As part of the quality control and quality assurance (QA) of radiotherapy, two radiation oncologists (T. S. and Y. S.) assessed compliance with radiotherapy QA regulations after the completion of radiotherapy in all enrolled patients.

Radiotherapy was suspended if the patient met any of the following criteria: febrile neutropenia; pyrexia of 38°C or higher; platelet count of 25 × 109 L−1 or lower; air; grade ≥3 radiation‐induced esophagitis or dermatitis; SpO2 reduction to less than 92% (room or grade ≥2 pneumonitis).

Treatment assessment and toxicity evaluation

Baseline evaluations included each patient's medical history, a physical examination, electrocardiogram, tumor status, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS), and clinical laboratory tests. CT was performed every 4–8 weeks during the first 6 months and every 8–12 weeks thereafter. All responses were defined according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1. If a patient was documented as having a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR), a confirmatory evaluation was performed after at least 4 weeks. A patient was considered to have stable disease (SD) if this response was confirmed and sustained for 8 weeks. PFS was defined as the time from enrollment to the date of confirmation of progressive disease (PD) or the date of death from any cause (whichever occurs earlier). OS was defined as the time from enrollment until death from any cause. Toxicity was assessed based on NCI‐CTC version 5.0.

Sample size calculation and statistical analysis

In this study, the sample size was calculated according to the precision (a width of the 95% confidence interval [CI] using the normal approximation) for the ORR. In the JCOG0301 trial, the ORR (95% CI) for the daily carboplatin plus radiotherapy group was 51.5% (41.3–61.7). Therefore, efficacy in the present study was defined as an ORR of at least 51.5%. Thirty‐nine patients were required to obtain a half‐width of the 95% CI of 15% with a probability of at least 80% under the expected ORR of 51.5%. Based on these criteria, the target number of patients was set at 45, taking into account ineligible cases and cases of pretreatment discontinuation.

For the primary endpoint, the response rate and 95% CI were calculated for the full analysis set (FAS). The Wilson method was used to calculate CI. For survival analysis, the Kaplan–Meier method was used to estimate survival curves, median survival, and annual survival rates. The 95% CI was also calculated using Brookmeyer and Crowley's method or Greenwood's formula. Statistical analysis was performed using SAS Ver. 9.4.

RESULTS

Patient demographics

This trial was designed to enroll 45 patients, but because of the coronavirus pandemic, the target enrollment was not reached. In total, 37 patients were enrolled from 15 institutions in Japan from September 2020 to June 2022. In one case, protocol treatment was not started because the patient developed organizing pneumonia, and corticosteroids were administered. This patient was therefore excluded from the FAS (Figure 1). The safety analysis population was identical to the FAS. ECOG PS was 0, 1, and unknown in 19, 16, and 1 patient, respectively. The median patient age was 79 years (range, 75–89). The disease stage was IIIA, IIIB, and IIIC in 15, 18, and 3 patients, respectively. The histology was adeno, squamous, and others in 18, 16, and 2 patients, respectively (Table 1).

Thirty‐five patients completed radiotherapy, and treatment was discontinued in one case at the patient's request. Efficacy

The ORR was 63.9% (95% CI = 47.6–77.5). The overall response was CR in one patient (2.8%), PR in 22 patients (61.1%), SD in 12 patients (33.3%), and PD in one patient (2.8%, Table 3). The response rate for the concurrent chemoradiotherapy period without confirmation was 38.9% (CR in zero patients, PR in 14 patients [38.9%], SD in 22 patients [61.1%], and PD in zero patients). In the FAS, 35 patients completed protocol treatment, and 30 of these patients transitioned to durvalumab maintenance treatment. The transition rate to maintenance treatment was 83.3% (95% CI = 68.1–92.1).

Safety

Table 4 summarizes toxic effects reported in the safety analysis population. Grade 3 or higher adverse events related to hematologic toxicity occurred in 15 patients (41.7%). Grade 4 leucopenia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia occurred in one patient each (2.8%). Grade 3 febrile neutropenia occurred in one patient (2.8%). Regarding non‐hematological toxicity, grade 3 or higher adverse events occurred in seven patients (19.4%). Nausea, anorexia, urticaria, and erythema multiforme occurred in one patient each (2.8%). Severe pneumonitis was not observed during the chemoradiotherapy period. However, four patients (11.1%) developed pneumonitis requiring hospitalization after chemoradiotherapy. Of these four patients, three patients received durvalumab as maintenance therapy. The remaining patient was durvalumab‐naïve, and this case ended in a treatment‐related death. The mean lung V20 for grade 0–1 pneumonitis cases and grade 2 or higher pneumonitis cases was 20.8 and 24.7, respectively. There were no statistically significant differences between the two groups.

DISCUSSION

Prior to the era of immunotherapy, advances in the outcomes of locally advanced NSCLC were driven primarily by developments in diagnostic and radiotherapeutic techniques. 17 The development of cytotoxic anticancer drugs since the 1990s has prolonged survival in stage IV lung cancer, but it has not changed the outcome of concurrent chemoradiotherapy in locally advanced stage III lung cancer. As durvalumab has significantly improved outcomes in stage III lung cancer, cytotoxic anticancer agents might be less important in the treatment of locally advanced NSCLC. The rate of transition to durvalumab maintenance therapy could be a critical aspect of chemoradiotherapy regimens. The right direction for chemoradiotherapy development in locally advanced NSCLC might be not to intensify chemotherapy but instead to target less‐toxic regimens with simpler delivery methods. Low toxicity is especially important for older adults because of their frailty. The JCOG0301 trial is the only reported prospective, randomized trial with positive results on the efficacy of chemoradiotherapy in older adults, and the other studies were retrospective. 18 , 19 In a combined analysis of three randomized trials and one systematic review, Dawe et al. found that, aside from increased hematological toxicity, chemoradiotherapy is well tolerated in fit older adults and is reasonable as a standard clinical treatment. 19

The trial aimed to enroll 45 patients and analyze 39 patients, but enrollment was completed for 37 patients and the FAS comprised 36 patients; three cases were therefore lacking. As a result, the 95% CI was slightly wider than planned, but only by a few percent. Notably however, this was not a confirmatory study, and the necessary information was obtained. The primary endpoint of ORR, as well as PFS and OS, exceeded the results of daily carboplatin plus concurrent radiotherapy in the JCOG0301 trial. Because 83.3% of the patients in this study received durvalumab, it is not appropriate to directly compare the results, nor can it be said that weekly carboplatin is more effective than daily dosing in the chemoradiotherapy setting. However, the incidence of adverse events in this study, particularly hematological toxicity, was much lower than that observed with daily carboplatin administration. In addition, radiation pneumonitis was also mild, which may be due to the lower lung V20 compared to previous reports. 5 , 20 Our results were comparable to those of a post hoc analysis of the PACIFIC trial (n = 101; age ≥ 70 years), which reported PFS of 12.3 months (95% CI = 9.2–not reached) and OS of 29.0 months (95% CI = 21.0–not reached), both from the start of durvalumab, rather than chemoradiotherapy. 21 Recently, Yamada et al. reported the results of a phase II trial of radiotherapy followed by durvalumab maintenance therapy for locally advanced NSCLC (SPIRAL‐RT). 22 Median PFS and OS were 8.9 (95% CI = 7.4–19.4) and 20.8 months (95% CI = 15.8–not estimable), respectively. Although these results cannot be directly compared because of the different clinical backgrounds of the patients, the SPIRAL‐RT trial also enrolled younger patients who were ineligible for chemoradiotherapy, and our results might thus be superior to those of the SPIRAL‐RT trial. The results however should be interpreted with caution, because PFS and OS in the current study were calculated from the start of chemotherapy, not from durvalumab, and our trial also included patients who did not receive durvalumab. These results suggested that concurrent chemoradiotherapy is effective in the treatment of locally advanced lung cancer, even in older adults in the immunotherapy era. The results of the DUART trial, 23 which is similar to the SPIRAL‐RT trial but with a larger sample size, will make this clear. The DOLPHIN trial examined the use of durvalumab immunotherapy plus concurrent radiotherapy followed by maintenance durvalumab therapy in patients with locally advanced NSCLC without chemotherapy. 24 The median PFS was 25.6 months (95% CI = 13.1–not reached). AEs of grade 3 or 4 occurred in 52.9% of patients and grade 5 in two patients (5.9%). This treatment was considered to be effective, with manageable toxicity, and may thus be a candidate for the treatment of older adults with locally advanced NSCLC.

The limitations of this study included the small number of patients and its single‐arm, non‐randomized study design. It also lacked information on specific biomarkers, such as EGFR mutations and PD‐L1 status. Although in practice fewer older adults with locally advanced NSCLC receive chemoradiation than younger patients, some argue that actual age is not a factor in the indication for chemoradiation. Indeed, older patients were included in the Pacific and RTOG0617 trials and received platinum‐based doublet chemotherapy in concurrent chemoradiotherapy. 6 , 20 From this perspective, chemoradiation with carboplatin alone may be suboptimal for “fit” older adults. JCOG is currently conducting a phase III trial of daily carboplatin versus weekly carboplatin plus albumin‐bound nanoparticle paclitaxel in the chemoradiation setting for locally advanced NSCLC in older adults. The results may clarify whether carboplatin alone is sufficient in older adults.

In conclusion, weekly carboplatin and concurrent radiotherapy could emerge as the standard of care for the treatment of unresectable locally advanced NSCLC in older adults because of its simplicity and low toxicity. Since the patients in this study could potentially be treated with durvalumab, the next plan is to conduct a study to evaluate the efficacy of durvalumab maintenance therapy.

Reassessing Acid Suppression in Patients Receiving Antiplatelet Therapy: Real-World Clinical Outcomes of P-CABs Versus PPIs
Authors: Hyun Lim
Published: 2026

The management of gastrointestinal (GI) and cardiovascular (CV) complications in patients with ischemic heart disease (IHD) receiving long-term antiplatelet therapy remains a clinical challenge. Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) have traditionally been the standard strategy to prevent upper GI complications in this high-risk population [1]. More recently, potassium-competitive acid blockers (P-CABs) have emerged as promising alternatives, offering rapid and potent acid suppression [2]. Despite their growing use, real-world data directly comparing the clinical outcomes of P-CABs and PPIs—particularly in patients on antiplatelet therapy—have been limited.

In this context, the recent study by Lee et al. [3] retrospectively evaluated the incidence of upper GI complications and CV events in 604 patients with IHD who received either tegoprazan or a PPI after percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). The authors found no significant differences between the two treatment groups. Tegoprazan use was associated with a hazard ratio (HR) of 0.34 for GI complications compared with PPIs (95% confidence interval [CI], 0.04–2.97) and an HR of 0.57 for major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs) (95% CI, 0.15–2.19), indicating no statistically significant differences. These findings reinforce the potential role of P-CABs as reliable agents for GI protection in patients exposed to antiplatelet therapy and contribute to ongoing efforts to address concerns about possible pharmacodynamic interactions—particularly those involving CYP450 pathways—between PPIs and clopidogrel [2].

Although the results are encouraging, they warrant cautious interpretation. The retrospective design introduces the possibility of unmeasured confounding, and the relatively small number of GI and CV events limits statistical power to detect treatment-related differences. Furthermore, long-term safety data for P-CABs remain insufficient; while short-term studies demonstrate strong acid-suppressive effects, evidence regarding the safety of prolonged P-CAB use—especially in patients requiring lifelong antiplatelet therapy—remains limited. Despite these limitations, the study provides valuable exploratory evidence and establishes a foundation for future research.

As P-CABs become increasingly integrated into clinical practice, rigorously designed prospective studies will be essential to establish their long-term safety, define optimal dosing strategies, and clarify their role in patients receiving complex antithrombotic regimens such as dual antiplatelet therapy. The present study offers meaningful early evidence supporting the comparable safety profiles of tegoprazan and PPIs after PCI and underscores the need for continued investigation to refine acid suppression strategies and improve clinical outcomes in this vulnerable population.

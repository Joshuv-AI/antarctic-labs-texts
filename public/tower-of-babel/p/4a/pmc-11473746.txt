A method for predicting remaining useful life using enhanced Savitzky–Golay filter and improved deep learning framework
Authors: Xiangyang Li, Lijun Wang, Chengguang Wang, Xiao Ma, Bin Miao, Donglai Xu, Ruixue Cheng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractEnsuring operational integrity in large-scale equipment hinges on effective fault prediction and health management. Prognostics and health management (PHM) face the challenge of accurately predicting remaining useful life (RUL) using multivariate sensor data. Traditional methods often require extensive prior knowledge for indicator construction and processing. Deep learning offers a promising alternative. This study presents a multi-channel multi-scale deep learning approach. Initially, an improved Savitzky‒Golay filter (ISG) addresses challenges posed by large and rapidly changing data volumes, enhancing data preprocessing. Subsequently, a framework integrates convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with long short-term memory (LSTM) to capture hierarchical signal information and make integrated predictions. The CNN extracts spatial features from multi-channel input data, while the LSTM captures temporal dependencies. By fusing outputs from both components, the framework enhances predictive accuracy and robustness for complex operational datasets. Experimental validation on the C-MAPSS dataset tests various fusion methods and CNN depths, determining parameters and evaluating filtering effectiveness. Comparative analyses show promising performance, particularly under dynamic conditions. While not optimal for predicting multiple fault types, it outperforms classical algorithms, especially in single fault type prediction tasks.Keywords: Deep learning, Prognostics and health management (PHM), Remaining useful life (RUL) predictions, Neural networkSubject terms: Information technology, Aerospace engineering, Mechanical engineering

Introduction

With the development of advanced manufacturing technology and the popularization of industrial equipment, fault prediction and health management of complex equipment have become increasingly important1. As an integrated and interdisciplinary approach, the Prognostics and Health Management (PHM) methodology has become an effective means of realizing comprehensive monitoring of the health status of equipment2. In PHM, the fast and accurate prediction of remaining useful life (RUL) is a central issue3. Complex equipment such as aircraft engines4, high-quality bearings5, industrial robots6, and fuel cells7 are subject to a variety of external environments, workloads, and internal factors over long periods of time, leading to gradual degradation of their performance and increased risk of failure8. Traditional remaining life prediction aims to model the degradation of equipment; however, although these models have been subjected to extensive statistics to ensure the accuracy of the model, the introduction of ideal conditions in the construction of the model can still result in errors in actual use9. As a result, end-to-end data-based RUL prediction methods have become increasingly popular10. The data-based RUL prediction method well considers the influence of environmental variables in the degradation process rather than simply equating it to the description of a preset condition and is able to learn from a large amount of historical operational data to realize accurate prediction of the equipment RUL by learning from the operating rules and failure modes of the equipment11.

Deep learning methods have the ability to model nonlinear, complex relationships and are better able to capture features and patterns in time-series data; thus, they perform well in the remaining life prediction task12. In previous studies, different types of deep learning methods have been gradually proven to have good results in this field, and these methods continue to advance the field13. A deep convolutional neural network (DCNN) method with the addition of an attention mechanism was proposed by Amgad Muneer et al.14. This method improves the overall algorithm’s perception of the features by introducing an attention mechanism, and after validation on the C-MAPSS dataset, the results show that the method has better adaptability to the problem of a single fault. In view of the large amount of data and rich noise in the remaining life prediction, Khumprom et al.15 investigated the effectiveness of various filter and wrapper feature selection methods and principal component analysis (PCA) as dimensionality reduction methods and used feed-forward artificial neural networks (FFNNs) and deep neural networks (DNNs) for the comparison of these methods, the results of which show that data processed with different filters can have widely varying results on the same model, which side by side corroborates the necessity of filtering links. To capture the features behind the data as much as possible, some combinations of methods have been applied to the prediction of the RUL, which, upon comparison, are associated with a significant improvement compared to the previous methods. These methods include combining long short-term memory (LSTM) and an attention mechanism (AGMLSTM)16, combining a convolutional neural network (CNN) and LSTM (CNN-LSTM)17, and combining a CNN and bidirectional gate recurrent units (BGRU)18. It can be concluded that accurate real-time RUL prediction is not only an important requirement for equipment Operations and Maintenance, but also an important foundation for the current digital twinning of equipment19. However, although research from different perspectives has been relatively well developed, the prediction of the remaining life of complex equipment under dynamic operating conditions is still a difficult problem.

The remaining life prediction of turbofan engines, which are typically complex equipment, has been a factor affecting aviation safety; therefore, in this paper, a structure based on the improved Savitzky‒Golay filter (ISG) and multi-channel multi-scale deep learning (MuMsDCNN-LSTM) is proposed and applied to predict the remaining life of turbofan engines. In the second section, the basic issues of remaining life prediction for turbofan engines and the underlying models used in the method are presented in detail. In Sect “Proposed methodology”, the framework of the proposed method is presented in detail, which includes an improved SG filtering method in the preprocessing part as well as a multi-channel multi-scale DCNN incorporating an LSTM model in the main body model. In Sect “Case study”, the C-MAPSS dataset is introduced, and the optimal setup of the overall method is presented. Specific results are presented in Sect “Results and discussion”. Additionally, a comparison of other models is introduced in Sect “Results and discussion” to discuss the applicability of the proposed method.

Problem description and foundational methodology

Problem description

In the health operation and maintenance of complex equipment, the problem of remaining life prediction is a key issue, which is related to the accurate assessment of the health of the equipment and is the basis for the formulation of reasonable maintenance strategies. However, in the operation of complex equipment, the structure, environment and operating conditions are all complex; therefore, it is difficult to achieve rapid and accurate prediction of the remaining life via traditional methods. In this study, the dataset used is the C-MAPSS dataset, which is from the NASA experiment on remaining equipment life prediction of aircraft turbofan engine degradation data, which simulates the performance degradation and failures of aircraft engines under different operating conditions20. Multiple different engine types are included in this dataset, each of which has an operating condition that degrades over time, and the dataset provides sensor measurements and corresponding timestamps of engine life.

However, since the degradation data of turbofan engines are characterized by nonlinearity, time-variability, large scale and high dimensionality, the traditional methods, although they can realize partial prediction, cannot fully meet the usage requirements, and the current health state of the engine is reflected not only from the monitoring information at the current moment but also from signals of a certain period of time in the past. Therefore, in this study, the problem is mainly solved by an improved SG smoothing filter and a deep CNN with multi-channel and multi-scale that incorporates the LSTM model.

Foundational theory

Savitzky‒Golay smoothing filter (SG)

SG is a convolutional sliding window weighted averaging algorithm that constructs smooth signal profiles by introducing weights around data points via a local weighted polynomial regression (LWPR) method. Compared with the traditional filtering method, it not only retains the shape and characteristics of the data while filtering out the noise but also adapts well to the nonuniformly spaced data and has a good effect on the edges and excesses of the data while maintaining the data characteristics. Therefore, through this method, the shape and width of the signal can be guaranteed to be unchanged when filtering noise so that the obtained data waveform is as close as possible to the original data waveform, which is widely used in data smoothing and noise reduction. Due to the large number of engines collected, large amounts of sensor information and nonuniform time courses, SG smoothing filtering is a good treatment method.

LSTM algorithm

Since the prediction of the RUL is a time series prediction problem, the LSTM model is chosen for the remaining life prediction. Since its proposal, the LSTM model has shown advantages in various sequence data processing tasks. As a kind of development of RNN, LSTM can efficiently retain long-term sequence information through its special cell state and gate structure to learn long-distance dependencies, and at the same time, it can reduce the risk of gradient disappearance because its forget gate and input gate can control the inflow and outflow of information. Proposed methodology

Improved SG filter (ISG)

Although the SG filter has good trend retention ability, the filtering effect is limited in practical use. The SG filter can usually remove high-frequency noise effectively, but in some cases, it may have a large impact on the edges of the signal, and it is difficult to extract the nonlinear features of the signal, which leads to signal distortion. Therefore, an adaptive SG filtering method was proposed in this study, which is realized by introducing Gaussian filtering to high-frequency signal processing to ensure that the data are smoothed while retaining as many high-frequency features as possible; finally, the new signal is reconstructed through signal fusion.

A multi-channel multi-scale deep learning approach (MuMsDCNN-LSTM)

The convolutional kernel size is fixed in traditional CNN algorithms, and it may be difficult to effectively capture features at different scales for the remaining life prediction problem. Therefore, a multi-channel multi-scale deep CNN (MuMsDCNN) incorporating an LSTM model is introduced to solve the remaining life prediction problem of complex equipment. The multi-channel multi-scale deep CNN ensures that the algorithm extracts the features of the input signals at multiple scales and reduces the loss of information, while the introduction of LSTM further enhances the acquisition of global information, especially to capture the long-term dependencies in the remaining life prediction. RUL label setting

RUL labeling with segmented functions is used in the labeling of the C-MAPSS dataset since the engine is usually in a healthy state in the early stage of operation, where the fluctuation of the sensor signals is small. It is not reasonable to use linear RUL labeling, while RUL labeling with segmented functions is more adaptable to the actual situation. Building on previous research, a direct segmentation approach was employed in this study21,22, with 125 chosen as the threshold for engine RUL. This means that the model training was focused exclusively on data where the remaining lifespan was less than 125. The labeling rule of the RUL is shown in Eq. (8).

Settings and effects of multi-channel multi-scale deep learning (MuMsDCNN-LSTM)

The historical data of the device are mixed with different scales of information, the traditional single-scale deep neural network has difficulty capturing this deep information, and the performance of the algorithm will be partially improved by the setup of multiple channels with different scales.

Following processing in Sect “Data preprocessing” and Sect “ISG filter parameter settings and effects”, the data not only became clearer but also reduced the likelihood of overfitting. To further enhance generalization and mitigate overfitting risks, Dropout and ModelCheckpoint techniques were implemented in MuMsDCNN-LSTM. Dropout randomly drops neurons to prevent the model from overfitting to the training data. Specifically, in this study, Dropout was applied after each convolutional and fully connected layer, thereby bolstering the model’s generalization capability and robustness. To ensure model recoverability and ongoing performance monitoring during training, the ModelCheckpoint tool was integrated into the model framework. This function dynamically saves the optimal weights of the current model based on validation set loss, facilitating subsequent model selection, integration, or deployment based on the best model version. This approach not only improves model training efficiency but also ensures the reproducibility and stability of experimental results, thereby providing reliable technical support and data integrity for the research.

Discussions

Comparison of ISG methods with traditional filtering methods

In the original signal, despite the large amount of data, the extraction of information is not necessarily optimal; therefore, the original data are processed through the filtering method to improve the characterization of the data. To verify the effectiveness of the filtering, in this study, a comparison of the variance of the explanation is introduced to carry out the filtering method as well as the measurement of the effectiveness of the filtering method, and the variance of the explanation of the different methods is calculated through the method of principal component extraction, as shown in Fig. 10. The sensor serial number in the figure represents the new ordering of the data after dimensionality reduction, and the numbers on the vertical axis represent the ratio of the explained variance of the data processed by different filtering methods to the original data. When the value is greater than 1, the filtered data are more explanatory. The ISG filtering can retain as much information as possible in the different filtering methods, which provides a convenient way of proposing the characteristics for the follow up. In the FD001 and FD003 datasets, due to their single condition and simplistic nature, there is some information loss. However, from the point of view of the ISG processed values, the overall degree of information retention is at an acceptable level. In the FD002 and FD004 datasets, due to the two datasets for the dynamic conditions, it can be clearly seen that the degree of information retention of the filtered data is strengthened in comparison with that of the prefiltered data, and in most of the sensors, the ISG filtering method can retain as much information as possible. The information retention level of the ISG is basically the highest among most of the sensors.

The impact of different dropout layer configurations on the algorithm

In order to mitigate algorithm overfitting, the Dropout layer is a common technique employed. However, the performance of algorithms is also influenced to a significant extent by different Dropout techniques and parameters23. To investigate the impact of different Dropout techniques and parameters on overall algorithm performance, besides the traditional Dropout method, we incorporated Drop Connect and Gaussian Dropout methods into the algorithm. We conducted experiments using various combinations of Dropout layers to determine the optimal techniques and parameters. In terms of implementation, the traditional Dropout randomly drops a certain proportion of neuron outputs to reduce overfitting probability. Drop Connect achieves this by randomly dropping connection weights to discard network content, thereby reducing overfitting probability. Gaussian Dropout, on the other hand, is based on a Gaussian distribution rather than a uniform distribution, to discard network content, thereby reducing overfitting probability. This allows for a more fine-grained control over the degree of dropout. Comparison of model resource consumption.

It is important to note that runtime is closely correlated with the device’s operational state, so tests were conducted on a device free of parallel tasks to ensure consistency. Due to inherent differences in algorithm frameworks, a singular metric for evaluating computational resource consumption is impractical. Therefore, our resource comparison focuses solely on structurally similar deep learning methods mentioned earlier, which largely encompass the superior-performing algorithms identified in our precision evaluations.

Based on Table 9, the introduction of ISG (Instance segmentation guided) did not result in a change in subsequent computational scale, despite its necessity as a preprocessing step from raw data to deep learning models. Across multiple sets of tests with different models, the incorporation of ISG generally reduced the time consumption for most models. This reduction can be attributed to ISG making raw data more accessible to algorithms by capturing its features more effectively.

Moreover, it’s evident that as model complexity increases, both computation time and resource utilization also increase. This trade-off between computation time and accuracy is a challenging balance to achieve. Superior models should ideally achieve higher accuracy with relatively fewer computational resources. Comparatively, the McMsDCNN-LSTM model, when contrasted with the basic CNN model, demonstrated a tenfold increase in validation step time and a fivefold increase in space occupation. However, as models became more complex, McMsDCNN-LSTM did not consistently require more computation time or occupy more computational resources than other deep models. In fact, after training, some frameworks exhibited significantly larger space utilization compared to McMsDCNN-LSTM.

Therefore, it’s noteworthy that McMsDCNN-LSTM does not incur additional costs in terms of computational scale compared to other deep models. For practical applications, managing computational time and model space at this scale is feasible with appropriate hardware. Furthermore, when considering Table 8 alongside, ISG-MuMsDCNN-LSTM demonstrates more stable precision performance under equivalent computational scales.

Conclusion

In this study, a multi-channel multi-scale deep learning framework (ISG-MuMsDCNN-LSTM) incorporating improved Savitzky-Golay filters is proposed and applied to predict the residual life of complex equipment. The performance of the algorithm is tested on the C-MAPSS dataset, where the information embedded in the data is enhanced by the preprocessed raw data filtered by the ISG, and the validity of the ISG filter setup is verified by the comparison of different filters. In addition, the effectiveness of MuMsDCNN-LSTM is verified through the fusion of different scales and a network depth comparison experiment. After testing, the proposed method can well predict the remaining life of complex equipment, especially for complex and dynamic working conditions. The performance of the ISG-MuMsDCNN-LSTM algorithm is better than that of other methods with the same dataset, and the overall performance of this method is better. The configuration of Dropout layers significantly impacts algorithm performance in algorithms. In our experiment, the DCNN module performed better when employing traditional Dropout techniques. We did not observe a linear relationship between dropout rate and algorithm performance; however, excessively high dropout rates could lead to a sharp decline in algorithm performance.

However, as the value and uniqueness of equipment increase, purely data-based remaining life prediction may face the problem of insufficient data volume in real-world use, forcing future research to pay greater attention to methods that combine modeling and data.

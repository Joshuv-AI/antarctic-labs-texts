Identification of a novel COL7A1 variant associated with dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa pruriginosa responding effectively to dupilumab
Authors: Caichou Zhao, Shuanglin Cao, Xinghua Gao, Xuegang Xu, Lixiong Gu
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundVariants in COL7A1 cause an extremely rare and clinically heterogeneous syndrome known as dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa pruriginosa (DEB‐Pr). Duplilumab, a fully humanized anti‐IL‐4Ra monoclonal antibody, can inhibit IL‐4 and IL‐13‐driven signaling.MethodsEthical Compliance: Following our Institutional Review Board, genetic testing has been made available after completing a signed informed consent form. This article presents the case study of a DEB‐Pr patient who received dupilumab therapy. Genomic DNA was extracted from the peripheral blood of the patient.ResultsThe findings showed that a unique COL7A1 mutation was discovered in the patient who underwent genetic testing. Gly2704Arg]) who responded positively to dupilumab treatment without experiencing any serious side effects.

BACKGROUND

Dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa pruriginosa (DEB‐Pr) is a clinically heterogenous and rare condition resulting from variants in COL7A1 (McGrath et al., 1994) causing symptoms such as persistent itching or rash‐like eruptions, erosions, and blisters, with lesions often exhibiting lichenification. DEB‐Pr patients often exhibit Dystrophy and Milia. However, the pathophysiological cause of pruritus and associated symptoms in these people is not entirely understood. Symptomatic therapies that minimize new lesions and pruritis can improve the quality of life for DEB‐Pr patients. The use of drugs such as H1 antihistamines, cryotherapy, thalidomide, tacrolimus, immunoglobulin, and corticosteroids has been the subject of studies in the past; however, the results of these treatments have mostly been variable and unsatisfactory (Ferreira et al., 2020). The fully humanized monoclonal anti‐IL‐4Ra antibody (dupilumab) can interfere with signaling driven by both IL‐4 and IL‐13. A recent study found it alleviates pruritus and prurigo‐like lesions in patients with DEB‐Pr (Shehadeh et al., 2020).

STUDY METHODOLOGY

Baseline characteristics of study participant

A 22‐year‐old female had traumatic blisters and milia on her extremities since childhood. Atrophic plaques, pruriginous nodules, and erosions along the limb extensors were the main presentation of the lesions. The patient was repeatedly treated with topical glucocorticoids, oral antihistamines, bloodletting, and traditional Chinese medicine after being diagnosed with eczema or prurigo nodularis, but there was no visible transformation. Her symptoms worsened in May 2022, and the severity of her skin lesions increased over time. Physical examination showed multiple skin‐colored and dark red papules and nodules over the upper body and limbs, suggesting scar‐like keratotic lesions ranging in size from soybean to nail. Some skin lesions are merging, resulting in scrapes and scabs on the surface. The nails on the fingers and toes have no visible thickness or malformation. There is no damage to the oral or genital mucosa (Figure 1a,c,e).

Lesions located on the patient's trunk and lower limbs were markedly improved upon dupilumab treatment, as shown by changes observed between (a,c,e) weeks 0 and (b,d,f) 14. (g) Displays a pathological assessment of subepidermal clefts demonstrating minimal infiltration of inflammatory cells, capillary hyperplasia, the connective tissue within the superficial dermal layer, and capillary hyperplasia. (h) Illustrates the EBP family's history of the patient. The arrow shows the proband and filled circles or squares represent the affected individuals.

Clinical examination

A routine blood test examination revealed normal IgE levels and negative results for BP180, BP230, Dsg1, Dsg3, anti‐epidermal basement membrane IgG, and anti‐spinous cell desmosome IgG. Pathological examination indicated subepidermal clefts with limited inflammatory cells, capillary hyperplasia, the connective tissue within the superficial dermal layer, and limited inflammatory cell infiltration (Figure 1g), confirming a DEB‐Pr diagnosis. Upon further investigation and based on her medical history, it was discovered that seven other family members also had similar symptoms (Figure 1h). Genomic DNA was extracted from the peripheral blood of the patient. The DNA was first fragmented, and a library was prepared. The DNA of the exons of the target gene and adjacent cleavage regions was captured and enriched using a Roche KAPA HyperExome chip.

Lastly, mutation detection was performed using the MGISEQ‐2000 sequencing platform. A heterozygous COL7A1 variant (NM_000094.3:c.8110G>A [p. Gly2704Arg]) was detected. The pathogenicity of this variant has not been reported to date. However, according to the American College of Medical Genetics (ACMG) guidelines, this variant is considered pathogenic. A definite diagnosis of DEB‐Pr (OMIM:604129) was determined after considering our patient's clinical characteristics, family history, laboratory tests, pathology, and genetic testing. The patient was put on dupilumab treatment (600‐mg loading dose followed by 300 mg every other week subcutaneously).

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The treatment resulted in a significant decrease in patient itchiness, an apparent improvement in erythema, a reduction in the severity of scales and crusts, and the flattening of plaques (Figure 1b,d,f). The patient's quality of life improved substantially after 14 weeks, as indicated by a decrease from 7 to 2 on a visual analog scale (VAS) and 19 to 7 on the standard Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI). Twelve months after starting therapy with dupilumab, the patient continues to experience virtually overall itching relief and progressively disappearing skin lesions with no side effects.

Genetic studies showed that DEB‐Pr was caused by a variation in the COL7A1 gene on chromosome 3p21 that encodes collagen VII resulting in skin fragility and blistering. In addition to reported sporadic cases, these variants are inherited in both autosomal dominant and autosomal recessive patterns. The Gly2704Arg mutation is a missense variant that substitutes glycine with arginine at position 2704 of the collagen type VII protein. The smallest common amino acid in the collagen molecule is glycine. Its structure and function can be altered, resulting in a defective protein when replaced with a bulky, positively charged amino acid like arginine.

Dupilumab is a recently developed monoclonal antibody that blocks IL‐4 and IL‐13 signaling. Both these cytokines play crucial roles in the T2 response. A search of the NCBI PubMed database identified eight recent studies (Clawson et al., 2021; Kluger et al., 2022; Roque Quintana et al., 2023; Shehadeh et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2023; Yu et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2021) and a case report (Li et al., 2022) using the search strategy “Dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa pruriginosa” AND “dupilumab”. These eight studies described 10 cases of DEB‐Pr treated with dupilumab, finding that all cases responded well to the treatment. The case study documented a patient with epidermolysis bullosa simplex (EBS) treated with dupilumab and had a poor clinical outcome. While the mechanisms by which dupilumab benefits DEB‐Pr patients are unknown, recent research suggests that its efficacy may be associated with enhanced mast cell infiltration, higher IgE levels, and increased Th2 responses in the absence of atopy in DEB‐Pr patients, promoting a role for Th2‐driven immune responses in this condition (Darbord et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2023).

Compared with healthy control skin, blister roofs, and fluid from patients with EBS, it exhibited an increase in type 17 T‐helper cells and several cytokines (IL‐17, IL‐21, and IL‐22). This may help explain why dupilumab was unsuccessful in that specific case. In our case, the response to dupilumab treatment may be because the Gly2704Arg mutation affects a protein that regulates inflammation, which may be targeted by dupilumab. Other autoimmune vesiculobullous disorders were not found in our case. One possible explanation is the discovery of a unique genetic mutation inside the COL7A1 gene, which may play a significant role in the etiology of this disease. Furthermore, the efficacy of dupilumab in treating this variety could suggest that the disease's immunological pathways are similar to those targeted by this therapy.

This is the first DEB‐Pr patient with heterozygous COL7A1. (NM_000094.3:c.8110G>A [p. Gly2704Arg]) as per our understanding, who responded well to dupilumab treatment without any significant side effects. This report thus expands the list of known COL7A1 variants associated with DEB‐Pr and the phenotypic heterogeneity of this complex condition. Since DEB‐Pr has no cure, treatment aims to reduce symptoms and improve the patient's quality of life. The publication cited in the prompt scores current DEB‐Pr drugs based on the evidence currently available, and dupilumab is not yet included in the analysis because it is still in early‐stage clinical studies for DEB‐Pr (Martora et al., 2022). However, new possible indications are being increasingly explored that include skin diseases, such as prurigo nodularis, nummular eczema, allergic contact dermatitis, chronic hand eczema, spontaneous chronic urticaria, bullous pemphigoid, alopecia areata, and Netherton syndrome (Munoz‐Bellido et al., 2022).

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, our data demonstrated dupilumab's potential as a first‐line therapy for DEB‐Pr in the future. Such reports, however, remain limited and somewhat speculative. Therefore, additional large‐scale research is needed to analyze IL‐4 and IL‐13 expression in patients with DEB‐Pr. Continued research and development are still required to find a cure and improve DEB‐Pr patients' quality of life.

Diagnostic value of endoscopic ultrasound versus indocyanine green fluorescence imaging for sentinel and lymph node metastasis in patients with colorectal cancer
Authors: Guocong Wu, Yu Huang, Meifeng He, Chenggang Ren, Zhuo Wang, Yangyang Liu, Yao Zhang, Jianyong He, Shaoshuai Liu, Zhengeng Jia
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSentinel lymph nodes and lymph node metastases in patients with colorectal cancer (CRC) seriously affect prognosis. In order to improve the accuracy of lymph node assessment during surgery, it is crucial to explore advanced imaging techniques. This study aimed to evaluate the diagnostic value of endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) versus indocyanine green (ICG) fluorescence imaging for sentinel lymph node metastasis (SLNM) and lymph node metastasis (LNM) in patients with CRC.MethodsA total of 120 patients with CRC admitted to our hospital from March 2022 to December 2023 were included in this retrospective study and, according to the detection method, they were divided into ICG group (ICG fluorescence imaging group, n=50) and control group (EUS group, n=70). The general data of patients, sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy of ICG imaging for the diagnosis of SLNM and LNM in patients with CRC were statistically compared between the two groups using gold standard postoperative pathology reports. Area under receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was used to analyze the diagnostic value of ICG fluorescence imaging for SLNM and LNM in patients with CRC.ResultsFor diagnosing SLNM in CRC patients, ICG fluorescence imaging achieved sensitivities, specificities, and accuracies of 94.44%, 93.75%, and 94.00%, respectively, significantly outperforming EUS, which achieved sensitivities, specificities, and accuracies of 40.74%, 69.77%, and 58.58% respectively. For LNM diagnosis, ICG fluorescence imaging showed sensitivities, specificities, and accuracies of 80.00%, 92.5%, and 90.00%, compared to 28.57%, 71.43%, and 62.86% for EUS. The area under curve (AUC) of ICG for LNM in patients with CRC was 0.932 with an optimal cut-off value of 0.77, achieving sensitivities and specificities of 95.45% and 81.82%.ConclusionsIn the assessment of SLNM and LNM in patients with CRC, ICG fluorescence imaging is proven to be more valuable than EUS.

What is known and what is new?

• Previous studies have evaluated various imaging techniques for detecting lymph node metastases in CRC, including endoscopic ultrasound and other imaging modalities. However, there is ongoing research to identify the most accurate and reliable method for early detection.

• This study provides evidence that ICG fluorescence imaging is a more effective diagnostic tool than endoscopic ultrasound for detecting SLNM and LNM in CRC patients. The high sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy of ICG imaging suggest that it could be a valuable addition to the diagnostic arsenal for CRC.

• The findings of this study suggest that ICG fluorescence imaging should be considered as a preferred diagnostic method for assessing SLNM and LNM in CRC patients. Clinicians should consider incorporating ICG imaging into their diagnostic protocols to improve the early detection of lymph node metastases, potentially leading to more effective treatment strategies and better patient outcomes. Further research is needed to validate these findings and explore the broader implications of ICG imaging in CRC management.

Introduction

Background

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a malignant tumor that grows within the colon and rectum. Canceration in the mucosal epithelium may present as cauliflower-like masses, ulcers, hemorrhage, and brittle lesions (1). Sentinel lymph node (SLN) refers to the lymphatic fluid that originates from a particular organ or tissue in a certain region, which first drains to one or several lymph nodes in a few specific regions. SLN also refers to one or more specific regional lymph node to which the lymph nodes of an organ and a certain region are first drained. SLN plays a critical role in impeding the spread of tumor cells from lymphatic channels (2).

Lymph node metastasis (LNM) is the transfer of tumor cells to lymphatic vessels and lymph nodes through the lymphatic system, resulting in the enlargement of lymph nodes and further tumor dissemination. For patients with CRC, LNM typically progresses by infiltrating lymphatic vessels and lymph nodes in the colonic wall before potentially spreading to mesenteric lymph nodes and those at the root of mesentery (3). Patients with LNM usually present with enlargement of peripheral lymph nodes, and its pathological findings mainly include structural destruction of lymph nodes, disappearance of lymphatic hilum, and unclear boundaries between cortex and medulla.

Rationale and knowledge gap

There are a variety of methods to detect sentinel lymph node metastasis (SLNM) and LNM in patients with CRC. There are currently multiple techniques, such as endoscopic ultrasound (EUS), computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and endorectal ultrasonography (ERUS), to assess the status of lymph nodes. EUS has demonstrated a high degree of effectiveness in assessing the depth of tumor penetration into the rectal wall (4). As a sensitive detection method, CT is widely used in the diagnosis of abdominal and pelvic diseases. It plays an important role in determining cancer stages and monitoring disease progression (5). ERUS is considered to be the most accurate diagnostic method when detecting small tumors (i.e., tumor stages are stage 1 and stage 2). However, the operation of each imaging is complex and the equipment is expensive. The studies have concluded that when each examination is used for lymph node involvement in rectal cancer, there are big differences in the conclusions (6) Pathological examination is the gold standard for diagnosis (7). Indocyanine green (ICG) fluorescence imaging is a visualization technique based on ICG dye (8). When ICG binds to proteins in the blood, it emits fluorescence under light excitation of a specific wavelength allowing for the visualization of tissues such as blood vessels and tumors. EUS technology has made significant progress in the medical field, especially in the diagnosis of LNM and SLNM in patients with CRC. In recent years, EUS, thanks to its high-resolution imaging and real-time guidance capabilities, has been able to accurately locate and evaluate the lymph node status of CRC patients and improve diagnostic accuracy. At the same time, EUS-guided fine needle aspiration biopsy technology further improves the convenience of obtaining pathological samples and provides an important basis for formulating personalized treatment plans. These research advances have significantly improved the diagnosis and treatment results of CRC patients and promoted the development of precision medicine for CRC (9).

Objective

This study plans to establish ICG dye as the experimental group and compare it with EUS as the control group. CRC patients who met the criteria and underwent EUS and ICG dye examinations were respectively included to compare the diagnostic accuracy, sensitivity, specificity and patient acceptance of the two. This study aims to clarify the advantages and disadvantages of EUS and ICG dye in the diagnosis of LNM and SLNM in patients with CRC, provide more accurate basis for clinical selection of imaging diagnosis methods, optimize CRC diagnosis and treatment procedures, and improve patient prognosis. We present this article in accordance with the STARD reporting checklist (available at https://jgo.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jgo-2024-917/rc).

Methods

Research subjects

A total of 120 patients with CRC admitted to China-Japan Friendship Hospital from March 2022 to December 2023 were included and divided into the ICG group (n=50) and the EUS group (control group, n=70) based on the detection methods. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (I) all patients met the clinical diagnosis of CRC (at least a colonoscopic pathology evidence and/or 1 preoperative imaging examination) (10); (II) no previous history of abdominal surgery; (III) age >18 years old. Exclusion criteria included: (I) patients with other intestinal diseases; (II) patients who are allergic to experimental reagents; (III) patients with systemic immune or hematological diseases; (IV) extremely debilitated patients; (V) patients with apparent nodal or distant metastasis diagnosed intra-operatively.

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of China-Japan Friendship Hospital (No. JG-X-2021-03-03), in accordance with regulatory and ethical guidelines pertaining to retrospective research studies. Informed consent was waived for this retrospective study due to the exclusive use of de-identified patient data, which posed no potential harm or impact on patient care.

Examination methods

Patients in ICG group

Patients with previously untreated CRC were eligible for inclusion in this study. Clinical staging was determined by routine preoperative endoscopy and computed tomography (CT examination), while EUS was not routinely performed for included patients. Before participating in the trial, all surgeons underwent training to become proficient in SLN navigation surgery for CRC. Patients with apparent nodal or distant metastasis diagnosed intra-operatively and extensive abdominal adhesions were excluded from the study, resulting in a slightly fewer number of patients in the ICG group compared to the ultrasound group. ICG of 0.5 mL was injected laparoscopically in four quadrants of the subserosa surrounding the tumor using a puncture needle. At 20 min after the injection, SLNs stained with ICG were observed with the naked eye (ordinary light observation) followed by the ICG fluorescence imaging light observation. The determination of positive or negative staining was judged in concordance with at least two surgeons. A postoperative pathology examination was performed by an experienced pathologist, who was blinded to the SLN status and detection methods. Both SLNs and non-SLNs were diagnosed by one plane (with the largest dimension).

EUS group

Ultrasonography is utilized to scan the patient’s lymphatic drainage area to pinpoint the SLN. The patient is positioned in lithotomy position and instructed to remain relaxed while following the physician’s directions. A multi-element probe is used to observe the SLN’s size, shape, boundary, internal echo, and other characteristics. Color Doppler is employed to assess blood flow within the lymph nodes. Detailed records of the lymph node feature, and blood flow observed through ultrasonography are made, and a comprehensive assessment is conducted by considering the patient’s medical history, clinical manifestations, and other examination results. The determination of positive or negative nodal status is judged in concordance with at least two surgeons. Postoperative permanent pathology is performed by experienced pathologist who is blinded to the SLN status and detection methods. Both SLNs and non-SLNs were diagnosed by the same one plane (with the largest dimension).

Observation indicators

The specificity, sensitivity, and accuracy of ICG fluorescence imaging in the diagnosis of SLNM and LNM in patients with CRC were calculated and compared between the two groups, with postoperative pathology as gold standard. Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS software (version 27.0). Measurement data following a normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation, while categorical data were presented as frequencies or percentages. Continuous variables were compared using t-tests or Wilcoxon rank-sum tests, and categorical data were compared using the Chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact method. Additionally, the Chi-squared test was employed to compare the sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy of EUS versus ICG fluorescence imaging. The diagnostic value of EUS and ICG fluorescence imaging for SLNM and LNM in CRC patients was evaluated using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

Key findings

CRC is a common malignant tumor of the gastrointestinal tract, and its diagnosis and treatment have always been at the forefront of research efforts. SLNM and LNM are of great significance for the selection of patients’ treatment regimens and prognostic evaluation. This paper found that EUS and ICG fluorescence imaging techniques have important diagnostic value in sentinel lymph nodes and lymph node metastases in patients with CRC.

SLN refers to one or more specific regional lymph node to which the lymph nodes of an organ and a certain region are first drained (11). LNM is the most common way of tumor metastasis, which occurs when tumor cells infiltrate the lymphatic wall, detach, and travel through the lymph to nearby lymph nodes in the confluence area. These cells then establish new tumors in the lymph nodes, using them as a central hub for further growth (12). The main difference between SLNM and LNM lies in the path of metastasis and the location of lymph nodes. SLNM refers to the specific regional lymph nodes to which the tumor first metastasized, namely sentinel lymph nodes. LNM is a broader concept, including all possible LNM paths and locations (13). Both SLNM and LNM play an important role in the diagnosis of LNM of CRC. Therefore, sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB) is a commonly used clinical operation for lymph node dissection in tumor surgery. By evaluating the status of SLN, we can predict whether the tumor has LNM or not, so as to provide an important basis for the choice of treatment.

Comparison with similar researches and explanations of findings

Our results demonstrate that ICG fluorescence imaging has satisfactory sensitivity, specificity and accuracy in diagnosing SLNM in CRC patients. Compared with EUS, ICG fluorescence imaging technology shows significant advantages in terms of specificity and accuracy. This suggests that ICG fluorescence imaging can more accurately identify SLNM and provide a stronger basis for clinical decision-making. For the diagnosis of LNM, the sensitivity, specificity and accuracy of ICG fluorescence imaging technology are equally satisfactory. ICG fluorescence imaging technology can more comprehensively detect lymph node metastases, helping clinicians accurately assess patients ‘conditions and develop personalized treatment plans. The reason may be that ICG fluorescence imaging technology uses ICG as a specific fluorescent dye, known for emitting intense fluorescence at near-infrared wavelengths (700–900 nm), enabling deep tissue imaging by effectively penetrating skin, tissue, and bone (14,15). When ICG dye is injected into tumor tissue, it performs dynamic processes mainly in the vascular and lymphatic systems (16). Utilizing near-infrared light from the imaging device, the ICG dye absorbs and emits fluorescence, enabling visualization of vascular structure and function. ICG fluorescence imaging technology allows precise lymph node localization and real-time biopsy at concentration points of the fluorophore by tracking fluorescence flow (17,18). Compared with the traditional dye method and nuclide detection method, this method has higher accuracy and sensitivity, and can more effectively identify the metastasis of lymph nodes and sentinel lymph nodes (19). Further analysis using ROC showed that the area under diagnostic value curve of ICG for SLNM in patients with CRC was 0.854, with a standard error of 0.056, and the best cutoff value was 0.50. At this time, the sensitivity and specificity were 59.09% and 90.91% respectively. This indicates that ICG fluorescence imaging technique has high accuracy and reliability in the diagnosis of SLNM. Similarly, the area under diagnostic value curve of ICG for LNM in patients with CRC was 0.932, the standard error was 0.038, and the best cutoff value was 0.77. At this time, the sensitivity and specificity were 95.45% and 81.82% respectively. The advantages of ICG fluorescence imaging in the diagnosis of lymph node metastases are further supported by this evidence.

Strengths and limitations

The results of this study suggest that ICG fluorescence imaging has significant advantages in the diagnosis of SLN and LNM in patients with CRC. Nevertheless, this study does have a few limitations. Various factors, including the dosage of injected ICG, injection timing, and the collection and processing of fluorescence signals, may influence the operational effectiveness of ICG fluorescence imaging technology (20). These factors may affect the quality and accuracy of imaging, thus affecting diagnostic results.

Implications and actions needed

Therefore, it is necessary to strictly follow the operating specifications while employing this technique. In addition, the operating specifications, and standards of ICG fluorescence imaging technology need to be further improved and unified, which will also have a certain impact on the results (21,22). The expanding clinical demand and advancements in medical technology are anticipated to lead to broader utilization of ICG fluorescence imaging in the diagnosis and treatment of CRC (23). Future studies can explore the potential applications of this technology in early screening, surgical navigation, and prognostic assessment of CRC. At the same time, we can strengthen the combined application of this technology with other examination methods to improve the accuracy and reliability of diagnoses, leading to better medical treatment for CRC patients (24).

Conclusions

In conclusion, compared to EUS, ICG fluorescence imaging has a higher value in the diagnosis of sentinel lymph node and LNM in patients with CRC. This technique can accurately identify the metastasis of lymph nodes and provide strong support for clinical decision-making. The application of ICG fluorescence imaging is expected to have a substantial effect in the diagnosis and treatment of CRC.

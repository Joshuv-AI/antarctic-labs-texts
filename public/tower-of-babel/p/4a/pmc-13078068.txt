Identification and validation of an ultrasound-based interpretable machine learning model for the preoperative evaluation of microvascular invasion in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Qian Zhang, Chuan Pang, Lele Song, Zhilong Liu, Ruining Wang, Wenwen Fan, Ping Liang, Liping Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe aim of our study was to develop and validate a machine learning model for the preoperative identification of microvascular invasion (MVI) in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).MethodsThis retrospective multicenter study was conducted in China. Patients with HCC from June 2017 to December 2024 were enrolled. Database was divided into training and internal validation sets randomly. Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression was employed for feature selection. Four machine learning algorithms were compared for MVI prediction. Model performance was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic (AUC), accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, precision, Youden's index, and F1 score. Finally, the machine learning model with the best performance was selected as our final model while using it for an independent external validation set. The SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) diagram was utilized to elucidate the variable importance within the model, culminating in the amalgamation of the above metrics to discern the most succinct features.ResultsThe study finally enrolled 496 patients, comprising 229 MVI-positive and 267 MVI-negative cases. A total of 42 patients with HCC were collected in the independent external validation center, of which 18 were MVI-positive. LASSO regression showed that AFP, tumor size, peripheral enhancement, mosaic architecture and washout start time were the significant predictors. Among the four models, the Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM) model showed the best performance in the internal validation set, with an AUC of 0.829. In the independent external validation set, the GBM model demonstrated an AUC of 0.812.ConclusionThe machine learning model shows promising efficacy in preoperative MVI identification for HCC patients. This method has potential clinical applications and may help identify MVI preoperatively, potentially improving clinical outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-026-15828-3.Keywords: Microvascular invasion, Hepatocellular carcinoma, Machine learning, Contrast-enhanced ultrasound, SonoVue

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) ranks as the sixth most common malignant tumor in the world and represents a primary liver cancer with high mortality rates [1, 2]. The 5-year survival rate of HCC is only 18%, largely attributable to the high recurrence rates after surgical treatment [3, 4]. As a significant threat to human health, HCC poses a major challenge in global healthcare field.

Microvascular invasion (MVI), defined histologically by the presence of cancer cell nests within the microvasculature of peritumoral liver tissue, constitutes a well-established independent risk factor for early recurrence, metastasis, and reduced long-term survival following curative treatment [5–7]. Tumor cells break through the vascular endothelium and enter the microvascular lumen, indicating the tumor’s aggressive and metastatic potential. Patients with MVI-positive HCC are significantly poorer prognosis compared to the MVI-negative patients. Currently, MVI diagnosis primarily relies on pathological examination. Due to the heterogeneity of tumors, the specimens obtained by preoperative biopsy are limited, MVI can only be diagnosed by histopathological examination of postoperative resected specimens [8], making MVI information unavailable to guide preoperative and intraoperative treatment decisions. Therefore, imaging examinations for predicting MVI are particularly important.

Imaging examinations including Contrast-enhanced ultrasound (CEUS), Contrast-enhanced CT, and Contrast-enhanced MRI have emerged as principal diagnostic techniques for HCC [6]. Among these, CEUS offers distinct advantages as a safe, radiation-free, and repeatable modality that enables real-time visualization of microcirculatory perfusion patterns in both the lesion and surrounding hepatic parenchyma, reflecting hemodynamic characteristics of the tumor and its microenvironment. As a result, CEUS has gradually become a first-line imaging method in the clinical diagnosis of HCC. The accurate preoperative prediction of MVI using CEUS may facilitate the development of personalized treatment strategies and optimization of preoperative management for HCC patients.

With the advancement of data science, machine learning (ML) algorithms, such as Random Forest (RF) and Adaptive Boosting (Ada), have shown significant potential in facilitating early intelligent diagnosis and prognosis analysis of diseases. For example, ML has been successfully applied to predict conditions including metabolic-associated fatty liver disease [9], liver fibrosis [10] and cardiovascular disease [11].

Based on a multicenter dataset, this study aims to develop and validate an interpretable ML model for the preoperative assessment of MVI in patients with HCC. The model will be constructed and tested using parameters including baseline clinical data, preoperative laboratory examination, conventional ultrasonography (US), and SonoVue-CEUS features. Furthermore, the predictive accuracy of different ML algorithms for MVI will be compared in the validation sets.

Materials and methods

Participants

We retrospectively collected patients with HCC who underwent CEUS before surgery from June 2017 to December 2024. The inclusion criteria were: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) no local or systemic treatment before CEUS; (3) obtained a definitive pathological diagnosis through surgical resection; (4) no allergies to any components of SonoVue. The exclusion criteria were: (1) age < 18 years; (2) patients with incomplete clinical data; (3) patients with incomplete pathological data; (4) patients with poor-quality ultrasound imaging.

Ultrasonographic and CEUS examination

Ultrasonographic examinations were performed using a convex array broadband probe (frequency 3.5–5.0 MHz). The probe was positioned to optimally visualize the target lesion and surrounding hepatic parenchyma. Then we observed and recorded the tumor characteristics. Once the probe was stabilized, it was switched to CEUS mode with a low mechanical index. A bolus of 1.0–2.4 ml of SonoVue (Bracco, Milan, Italy) was injected via the elbow vein, followed immediately by a 5 ml saline. The procedure was strictly timed and continuously recorded for 2 min. Dynamic images were then saved at 1-min intervals until 6 min after contrast administration. All imaging data were saved in DICOM format for subsequent analysis.

Data collection

This study constructed models based on demographic characteristics, clinical characteristics, laboratory information, conventional US and CEUS parameters. Features were included only if they had been tested at all research centers to ensure data consistency and comparability. A total of 39 variables were ultimately included, as detailed in Supplementary Table 1.

Both US and CEUS datasets were independently evaluated by two researchers, each possessing 5 years of specialized experience in abdominal radiology with particular expertise in hepatic imaging. In cases of discrepant interpretations, a third senior radiologist with 25 years of professional experience was consulted to achieve consensus.

MVI

MVI was defined as the presence of tumor cell nests within the microvasculatures of the adjacent liver parenchyma, which can be observed though a microscope [5, 12].

Statistical analyses

All statistical tests were performed using R (version 4.2.2), Python (version 3.10) and Free Statistics software (version 2.1), with pathological results as the gold standard. A two-sided test P value of less than 0.05 was considered significant.

To ensure analytical reliability and mitigate potential biases, patients with incomplete data were excluded from the final analytical dataset. Continuous variables with normal distribution were presented as means ± SD and analyzed by t-test. Non-normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as median and inter-quartile range and analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical factors were described as frequencies or proportions, and analyzed by χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. Inter-observer variability analysis was performed using κ statistic, as detailed in the Supplementary Table 2. The agreement was classified as follows: poor for 0–0.2, fair for 0.2–0.4, moderate for 0.4–0.6, good for 0.6–0.8, and excellent for values greater than 0.8.

Data preprocessing

The dataset was first randomly partitioned into a training set (70%) and an internal validation set (30%). All subsequent steps were conducted exclusively on the training set, including feature selection and model training.

Feature selection

We used the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression for feature selection. fivefold cross-validation was used to determine the optimal value of λ. The optimal λ value and the corresponding features were determined based on the cross-validation performance in the training set.

Model training

Four commonly used supervised learning algorithms were used in this study, including Multi-layer Perceptron (MLP), Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM), RF and Ada. During model development, hyperparameter tuning was performed using fivefold cross-validation on the training set. The optimized models were subsequently evaluated on the validation sets.

Model evaluation

Model performance was assessed using multiple standard metrics in the validation sets. These included the area under the receiver operating characteristic (AUC) with its 95% confidence interval (95% CI), accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, precision, Youden's index, and F1‑score. Classification results were visualized through confusion matrices. Pairwise comparisons of model performance were conducted statistically using the DeLong test.

Model interpretability

SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) was a technique for interpreting ML model predictions [13]. It quantified the importance of each characteristic in the model by calculating the contribution value. The magnitude of the Shapley value reflected the importance of the characteristic in the model's prediction. Our study utilized SHAP to improve the model's interpretability and transparency.

Ethical statement

All study procedures were conducted in accordance with the Declarations of Helsinki and Istanbul. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Hospital of Shanxi Medical University (No. KYLL 2023–132). Due to the retrospective nature of the study and the use of de-identified patient data, the requirement for informed consent was waived.

Discussion

Currently, MVI is the most important pathological feature to guide the prevention and treatment of HCC recurrence, which primarily relies on histopathological examination of postoperative resected specimen for diagnosis, resulting in certain delays. Curative therapies such as surgery or liver transplantation must be decided preoperatively, which may lead to potential issues such as low-risk patients undergoing excessive treatment due to expanded liver resection, or high-risk patients experiencing early recurrence due to positive local margins postoperatively. It highlights the critical necessity and importance of preoperative prediction of MVI. This study was designed to develop and validate an explainable ML model based on preoperative US and CEUS images for the noninvasive preoperative assessment of MVI in patients with HCC, providing a more accurate predictive tool for clinical practice, improving the ability to accurately assess MVI risk preoperatively, and offering new reference criteria for personalized treatment and risk assessment for patients.

Our results showed that MVI-positive cases had higher AFP levels and larger tumor size; CEUS showed peripheral enhancement, mosaic architecture, and early washout. AFP is one of the important serum markers for the diagnosis of HCC as well as the key indicator for monitoring the efficacy of HCC treatment. It has been shown that AFP was associated with the occurrence of MVI [14, 15]. Although current studies differed in the definition of AFP cutoff values, they all confirmed that elevated AFP levels were positively correlated with MVI risk. Tumor size is also closely related to the presence of MVI. Several studies have confirmed that tumor size was an independent risk factor for predicting the occurrence of MVI, and the risk of MVI increased with tumor size [16–19].

During the process of metastasis, tumor cells initially invade the nearby microvasculature, then further spread through the microvasculature surrounding the tumor. As the interface between tumor and normal tissues, the peritumoral tissue provides a more accurate reflection of tumor growth, invasion, and metastasis. Peritumoral enhancement was associated with the tumor's arterial supply and vascularization. During tumor invasive growth, invasion of surrounding tiny portal vein branches occurs, leading to MVI, which alters local hemodynamics within the liver tissue. Newly formed vessels compensate with increased perfusion, resulting in significant hyperenhancement of the peritumoral liver tissue during arterial phase (AP). Several studies confirmed that peritumoral enhancement was positively correlated with the risk of MVI [20–24].

Mosaic architecture is a marker of tumor heterogeneity and an auxilary feature in the LI-RADS. When tumor cells invade surrounding microvessels and disrupt vascular integrity, both structural damage and increased vascular wall fragility render intratumoral vessels more vulnerable to rupture and hemorrhage. Furthermore, vascular disruption reduces intratumoral perfusion, leading to tissue hypoxia and nutrient deprivation, which in turn causes localized ischemia and necrosis. These alterations in the tumor microenvironment promote the formation of numerous disorganized neovasculature while enhancing tumor cell invasiveness, creating conditions conducive to vascular invasion. These factors contribute to the development of the mosaic architecture. Mosaic architecture manifests as numerous randomly distributed internal nodules or cystic structures, separated by fibrous components, and exhibiting considerable heterogeneity in size, morphology and enhancement patterns [25, 26]. The enhanced areas correspond to viable tumor tissue, while non-enhanced areas indicate regions of necrosis, hemorrhage, or fibrosis [27]. Wang Y et al. found that the mosaic architecture on CEUS was an independent predictor of MVI [28]. Our study obtained similar results.

What’s more, early washout was highly correlated with MVI risk, washout was related to the microcirculatory perfusion of the lesion [29]. MVI-positive HCCs were typically poorly differentiated, with the hepatic artery as the primary feeding vessel. Additionally, due to the absence of hepatic sinusoidal structures and the presence of arteriovenous fistulas, contrast agents rapidly passed through tumor vessels, resulting in early and rapid washout. Yao et al. conducted a prospective multicenter study enrolling 211 HCC patients. The results showed that washout time (< 90 s) was significantly correlated with MVI [30].

We employed the SHAP explainer to visually explain the output of the GBM model, which demonstrated the highest diagnostic performance. GBM is a ML classifier widely applied to classification tasks. The SHAP beeswarm plot illustrates the influence range and distribution of feature's contribution to the model's output. The SHAP bar plot can be used to compare the global importance of features [13]. Our results showed that washout start time was the most critical feature for MVI, followed by mosaic architecture, tumor size, peripheral enhancement and AFP. Physicians can directly assess the relationship between a specific feature and an individual patient's MVI risk by observing the distribution pattern of that feature's values in relation to their corresponding SHAP values.

The results of our study confirm the feasibility of using imaging information for noninvasive assessment of MVI. The model developed and validated in this study is primarily based on clinical and US characteristics. Compared to models that rely on expensive and radiative MRI or CT, CEUS has the advantages of being safe, radiation-free, and reproducible. What’s more, CEUS provides real-time, dynamic visualization of microcirculatory perfusion of contrast agent, reflecting hemodynamic changes within and around the lesion, which is of vital importance for the precise diagnosis of HCC.

There are several challenges in this study. Firstly, it was a retrospective study. Our conclusions should be further validated in a large-scale, prospective, multicenter study in the future to assess their universality. What’s more, the parameters used in this study relied on physicians' professional interpretations, which may be subjective. In the future, we will use deep learning to automatically extract US imaging features to further improve the model's performance.

Conclusion

In this study, we applied four machine learning methods to develop and validate an ultrasound-based prediction model for preoperative evaluation of MVI in patients with HCC, applying multiple metrics to evaluate the models’ performance, and finally selecting the best predictive performance of the ML model, GBM. The model has the potential to provide a non-invasive diagnosis of MVI preoperatively clinicians that enables precise risk stratification, optimizes treatment decisions, and facilitates personalized patient management.

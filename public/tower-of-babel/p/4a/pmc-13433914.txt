Compound extreme events of rapid warming and air pollution driven by foehn over the northern slope of the middle tianshan mountains
Authors: Maoling Ayitikan, Manwula Kadeer, Wanyue Deng, Alaiyi Aiding, Yusufu Musha, Qing He, Chengguang Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study examines a severe foehn event that occurred in winter on 19–20 February 2021 over the northern slope of the Middle Tianshan Mountains in Xinjiang, China, which produced extreme warming, gale-force winds, and heavy air pollution. By combining high-density surface observations, radiosonde data, microwave radiometer measurements, environmental monitoring records, reanalysis products, and WRF model simulations, we investigate the touchdown dynamics and the associated multi-phase environmental impacts. The main findings are as follows: (1) The foehn touchdown was not progressive but abrupt, triggered by intense mechanical turbulence from extreme vertical wind shear (150.1(m/s)/km between 850 and 890 hPa, with a Richardson number of 0.036). This shear broke through the low-level temperature inversion and drove a peak hourly warming of 20.9 °C. (2) WRF simulations reveal a distinct surface thermal structure during the mature stage, characterized by a warm core inside the canyon and cooler temperatures in the surrounding plains. Within the canyon, the convergence of southeasterly and northwesterly flows produced a micro-front, which was responsible for the pronounced local temperature fluctuations. (3) The foehn exhibited a phased dual effect on air quality. Before touchdown, the stable inversion layer trapped pollutants, allowing PM2.5 to build up to 224 µg/m³ at Urumqi Station. After touchdown, while strong winds enhanced particulate dispersion, the extreme warmth and intensified solar radiation concurrently accelerated photochemical ozone formation, with the maximum O₃-8 h reaching 158 µg/m³ at Miquan Station. This case study reveals the coupling among thermal, dynamic, and environmental effects during foehn events, providing a scientific basis for forecasting and combined pollution management over complex terrain.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-62493-4.Keywords: Foehn wind, Extreme warming, WRF simulation, Air pollution, North slope of the Middle Tianshan Mountains (NS-MTM)Subject terms: Climate sciences, Environmental sciences, Natural hazards

Introduction

Foehn wind is a kind of dry-hot wind resulting from the increase in temperature and decrease in humidity when air undergoes adiabatic subsidence1. Foehn wind often occurs on the leeward slopes of mountains and is a form of local air motion triggered by mountainous terrain. Such winds have been observed in many mountainous regions around the world, for example, the Alps in Europe2–5, the Rocky Mountains in North America6, and the Greater Khingan Range and Taihang Mountains in China7–9. Foehn winds often pose hazards to people’s life safety, infrastructure, transportation, agricultural production, etc10. In winter, foehn winds are one of the main factors causing severe urban air pollution events11–14.

Foehn winds can induce airflow sinking and adiabatic warming through topographic forcing, leading to significant rise in temperature on leeward slope. Foehn warming mechanisms depend upon the orographically forced flow dynamics and meteorological conditions15. The heating effect of foehn often cause extreme high temperature weather, forest fire, glacier melting, etc. Research progress on the heating effect of foehn winds shows that during the summer high-temperature days in Beijing, the foehn effect is mainly produced by northwest winds and contributes significantly to the temperature warming, especially when superposed with the urban heat island effect16. In Taitung City, Taiwan Province of China, foehn wind was once observed to raise the temperature to 40.2 °C within 20 min at noon on May 11, 2004, setting a hundred-year high temperature record in the same period17. During the foehn events that produced large temperature differences near the southern Appalachian Mountains, the daily maximum or minimum temperature observed at foehn-affected station is at least 3 °C higher than that at a non-foehn station18. Foehn has a significant impact on the extreme heat event in Kumagaya Japan on July 23, 201819. In addition, the warm foehn wind can also cause early melting of mountain snow, affecting winter sports like skiing5,20.

The north slope of the Middle Tianshan Mountains (NS-MTM) is the political, economic, and cultural center of Xinjiang, China, belonging to arid and semi-arid regions. Mao et al.21,22 pointed out that in spring and autumn, the foehn winds occur frequently in Urumqi station with high wind speeds, very prone to wind disasters, while in summer, foehn can aggravates the occurrence of extreme high-temperature weather in the city. Numerical simulations have revealed that the occurrence mechanism of foehn winds is closely correlated to gravity wave breaking, hydraulic jump phenomena, and the change in the Froude number23,24. Although great progresses have been made in recent years regarding the spatial and temporal distribution characteristics of strong foehn winds on the NS-MTM and their relationship with air pollution12,21,25,26, there are still notable deficiencies in existing literature. First of all, few studies have specifically investigated the refined spatiotemporal distribution of temperature during foehn events on the northern slope of the Central Tianshan Mountains, and there is a lack of systematic analysis of the drastic temperature change patterns under foehn conditions. Secondly, no detailed study has yet examined how the special meteorological conditions (foehn wind) over the complex terrain of the northern slope of the Middle Tianshan Mountains affect atmospheric pollutants (particulate matter and ozone).

Therefore, this study selects a typical strong foehn process that occurred on February 19–20, 2021 for detailed investigation. This event took place in winter on the northern slope of the Middle Tianshan Mountains in Xinjiang, China, causing extreme regional warming and accompanied by pollutant accumulation. The strong winds led to road traffic disruptions and widespread flight delays, significantly affecting residents’ travel. Based on multi-source observational data combined with WRF mesoscale numerical model simulations, this study systematically analyzes the characteristics, formation mechanisms, and environmental responses of the extreme warming during this foehn event, aiming to provide a reference for forecasting strong winds, temperature, and air pollution during foehn events in this region.

Study area, data and methods

Overview of study area

The Tianshan Mountains are located in the central part of Xinjiang, China, with an average elevation higher than 4,000 m, stretching more than 2,000 km from east to west and dividing Xinjiang into two different climate zones: northern Xinjiang and southern Xinjiang. The Middle Tianshan Canyon, which runs from Dabancheng to Urumqi in a southeast-northwest direction, cuts through the Tianshan Mountains. The narrowest part of the canyon is about 15 km wide, and both ends of it are very prone to mountain-gap winds or downslope storms. Urumqi City sits at the northern exit of the Middle Tianshan Canyon and its landform presents typical characteristics of subsidence, that is, the city is surrounded by mountains with altitudes ranging from 1,300 to 5,000 m on the east, south, and west sides, with an open mouth to the Junggar Basin in the north, forming a bell-shaped topographic depression (Fig. 1). The urban terrain inclines from southeast to northwest, with an average elevation of 800 m and a drop of 300–400 m. When the cold air mass from the periphery of the Mongolian High flows back to the southern slope of the Tianshan Mountains, a pressure gradient across the Tianshan Mountains is easily formed between the two ends of the Middle Tianshan Canyon, guiding the airflows to travel through the Middle Tianshan Canyon and invade Urumqi and its downwind areas, finally forming a southeast direction foehn wind. Data and Materials

(1) A total of 23 meteorological stations, comprising national-level stations and intensive surface observation stations on the NS-MTM, were used. Figure 1 shows the station distribution, and station information is provided in Table S1 of the Supplementary Material. The observational data include hourly wind direction, wind speed, temperature, and relative humidity for February 2021, as well as radiosonde data from Urumqi Station (station altitude 935 m) at 08:00 and 20:00 Beijing Time (hereinafter the same).

(2) An RPG-HATPRO (Radiometer Physics GmbH, Humidity And Temperature PROfiler) ground-based multi-channel microwave radiometer was deployed at Urumqi Airport. It provides measurements with a temporal resolution of 1 min over a vertical range of 0–10 km.

(3) Air pollutant data consist primarily of hourly concentrations of PM2.5, PM10, O3, NO, NO2, and NOX from environmental monitoring stations on the NS-MTM for February 2021.

(4) Reanalysis data are mainly the ERA5 hourly reanalysis data from the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF), with a horizontal resolution of 0.25° × 0.25° (available at: https://cds.climate.copernicus.eu/).

(5) In this study, both the topographic base maps used for figure generation and the terrain elevation input for the WRF model were derived from the SRTM (Shuttle Radar Topography Mission) high-resolution 3 s terrain dataset, which covers the global land surface with a resolution of 1 s for the United States and 3 s (approximately 90 m) for other regions (available at: https://srtm.csi.cgiar.org/srtmdata/).

Introduction to the WRF Model

The Weather Research and Forecasting Model (WRF model) is a new-generation mesoscale weather research and forecasting numerical model jointly developed by the U.S. National Center for Atmospheric Research (NCAR), the National Centers for Environmental Prediction (NCEP), and other agencies. It has been widely applied in weather prediction and numerical simulation.

To conduct a detailed analysis of the fine-scale characteristics of the temperature field during foehn on the NS-MTM, the WRF version 4.6 is used for numerical simulation in this paper. The simulation is conducted with three nested grids of 27 km, 9 km and 3 km, successively covering Central Asia, Northwest China, and the NS-MTM. The grid numbers are 157 × 158, 127 × 106 and 136 × 115, respectively, with 38 vertical layers. The central geographic location of the model domain is 43.5°N, 88°E. The regional settings are given in Fig. 2. The model terrain data is the SRTM high-resolution 3 s terrain dataset, and the initial field is driven by the ERA5 hourly reanalysis data. The simulation period is from 08:00 on February 13, 2021 to 07:00 on February 21, 2021 (Beijing time, the same below), and the simulation results are output every 10 min, with the first 6 h as the Spin-up time. This study selected the locally adapted parameterization schemes for physical processes (Table 1), as used in relevant research24,27.

Analysis of meteorological element characteristics based on observational data

Ground wind field characteristics

Figure 3 shows the wind field distribution at 23 ground meteorological stations. Influenced by the Tiangeer Mountain to the south, at 04:00 on the night of February 16, the ground wind in the canyon was dominated by southerly mountain breeze, with low wind speeds and a 2-minute average wind speed of less than 2 m/s. During the daytime of February 16, the canyon was mainly dominated by northerly valley breezes, with a 2-minute average wind speed of approximately 2 m/s, while southeast winds appeared at upstream stations but with low wind speeds. From February 16 to 18, before the foehn entered the valley, the north slope of the Middle Tianshan Mountains was characterized by stagnant weather, with a mountain-valley breeze circulation in the canyon and relatively weak winds.

Distribution of 2-minute average wind and maximum wind gusts at surface weather stations on the NS-MTM on February 16 and 19, 2021.

At 04:00 on February 19, the foehn touched down, and the wind direction at most stations in the canyon turned to southeast. At 12:00, the near-surface foehn (southeasterly wind) on the NS-MTM reached its strongest intensity. Among the stations, Wulabo Station (WLB) in the southern suburbs experienced the highest wind speed, with a 2-minute average wind speed of 22.6 m/s and an extreme gust of 32.3 m/s. At Urumqi Station (URMQI), the 2-minute average wind speed was 11.4 m/s, with a maximum wind gust of 24.5 m/s. At Bagang Station (BG) in the western suburbs, the 2-minute average wind speed was 15.8 m/s, with a a maximum wind gust of 26.1 m/s. At the same time, the northern Leidazhan Station (LDZ) and Anningqu Station (ANQ) experienced northwesterly winds, with maximum wind gusts of about 4 m/s, forming a convergence zone between the southeasterly and northwesterly winds. This foehn process gradually subsided in the early morning of February 20.

Surface temperature distribution characteristics at upstream and downstream stations

The temperature changes at Dabancheng Station (DBC) in the upwind direction and Chaiwopu Station (CWP) in the southern suburbs as well as at Dizhentai Station (DZT) and Weixingzhan Station (WXZ) in the canyon are shown in Fig. 4, and there is a general rising trend of temperature at these stations. Being upwind stations, DBC and CWP began to experience foehn weather on February 16th, with southeast wind (foehn) blowing during the day and northeast wind blowing over night from the 16th to the 18th, so resulting in significant diurnal temperature variations. As the foehn had not yet entered the canyon in the early morning of the 16th to the 17th, temperatures at the DZT and WXZ in the canyon did not vary much. On the morning of the 17th, the foehn wind began to spread downwind, but the speed at downwind stations were relatively small. It was not until the morning of February 19th that the foehn strengthened from upwind to downwind. During the strong foehn wind period, temperatures at all stations varied greatly, among which the DZT and WXZ in the canyon experienced the most violent temperature fluctuations. The maximum hourly temperature change at these two stations during this foehn event reached 20.9 °C and 20.5 °C, respectively. Relative to temperatures on February 16th, 17th, and 18th, the temperatures at the canyon stations exceeded the values at the upwind stations during the period from the afternoon of the 19th to the early morning of the 20th. Foehn winds on the NS-MTM are often interrupted by northwest winds in the canyon, thus a confrontation situation is frequently observed between southeast winds (foehn) and northwest winds in the canyon. The southeast wind is dry and hot, while the northwest wind is wet and cold. As a result, the convergence of southeast and northwest winds forms a micro-frontal zone, and the area where the micro-frontal zone exists is often the zone having the most severe temperature changes.

Distribution characteristics of surface meteorological elements at URMQI

Figure 5 displays the variations of surface meteorological elements at URMQI. It can be seen from the figure that the foehn began to blow at the surface level of URMQI at 04:00 on February 19, 2021, and the surface meteorological elements changed dramatically. At 04:00 on February 19, the surface temperature rose from − 7.1 °C at the previous hour to 8.4 °C, with a positive hourly temperature change of 15.5 °C. At 05:00, the foehn was interrupted by northeasterly winds and, simultaneously, the temperature dropped by 18.6 °C. At 07:00, the foehn occurred again, and the temperature also rose up again. In the later period, the foehn was repeatedly interrupted by northerly winds, leading to temperature fluctuations; however, the overall surface temperature remained higher than that in the pre-foehn period. The temperatures at 14:00 before the foehn (February 16), during the foehn (February 19), and after the foehn (February 21) were − 7 °C, 9.9 °C, and 1.4 °C, respectively. Relative humidity showed an opposite trend, before the foehn, the surface relative humidity remained around 7%, while after the foehn onset, it dropped sharply to below 25%.

Analysis of sounding data at URMQI

As shown in Fig. 6, during the incubation phase of the foehn, starting from 08:00 on the 16th, the winds at 850-700 hPa turned to southeast at approximately 3 m/s. By 20:00 on the 18th, the wind speed increased to 5–8 m/s, forming a stable mid-level prevailing southeasterly flow. On the 16th, the temperature at 850 hPa was 3.2 °C, with a dew point temperature of −20.1 °C, yielding a dew point depression of 23.3 °C. After undergoing moist adiabatic cooling on the windward slope, with moisture condensation and dehydration, by 20:00 on the 18th the temperature at 850 hPa rose to 9.9 °C, the dew point temperature was − 15.9 °C, and the dew point depression increased to 25.8 °C, forming a dry and warm air mass that laid the foundation for the foehn occurrence. During this phase, the wind field in the lower to middle levels (from near-surface 915 hPa to 850 hPa) was disorganized, predominantly easterly with a wind speed of about 1 m/s. Vertically, subsidence motion was weak and did not extend down to the lower levels. A weak stable layer existed below 850 hPa, which trapped the dry and warm air in the mid-levels, preventing its downward transmission to the near-surface. Sounding data at 20:00 on the 18th show that the near-surface temperature rose slowly from − 8.9 °C on the 16th to −1.2 °C, still about 10 °C lower than the mid-level temperature, with a dew point depression of 7.6 °C. Overall, from 08:00 on the 16th to 20:00 on the 18th, the foehn appeared only in the mid-levels and did not reach the ground, representing an incubation phase characterized by the accumulation of dynamic and thermal conditions.

Temporal evolution of soundings at Urumqi station from 16 to 21 February 2021. The red line denotes temperature, and the green line denotes dew-point temperature.

During the foehn touchdown phase, at 08:00 on the 19th, the sounding observations revealed a significant dynamic coupling in the vertical wind profile. At 08:00 and 20:00 on the 19th, strong southeasterly foehn winds occurred near the surface (915 hPa). At 08:00 on the 19th, the wind speed reached 9.8 m/s, the temperature rose to 8.3 °C, and the dew point depression was 17.8 °C, triggering extreme near-surface warming and drying. At 850 hPa, an extremely strong northerly wind appeared, with a wind speed of 41 m/s; at 890 hPa, a strong southeasterly wind was observed, with a speed of 19 m/s. The vertical wind shear intensity between 850 hPa and 890 hPa was 150.1(m/s)/km, with a virtual potential temperature difference of 9.3 K, yielding a Richardson number (Ri) of 0.036. This indicates that under stable stratification conditions, the strong wind shear overcame buoyancy suppression, generating intense mechanical turbulence. The mid-level southeasterly winds, through turbulent exchange, broke through the weak stable layer in the lower levels, removed the residual shallow moist layer near the surface, and transferred downward from the mid-to-low levels to the surface, constituting the key dynamic factor for the downward propagation of the foehn.

During the foehn decay phase, at 08:00 on the 20th, the winds at 850 hPa turned to northwesterly (5 m/s), interrupting the foehn airflow. The near-surface foehn wind speed decreased to 1.5 m/s, the temperature dropped to −5.6 °C, and the temperature inversion disappeared. On the 21 st, the near-surface temperature further decreased, the dew point temperature recovered, and the dew point depression became 1.6 °C. The atmospheric structure returned to the climatological mean state for the winter season, and the foehn effect completely subsided.

Analysis of microwave radiometer observations at urumqi airport

Figure 7 presents the temperature vertical time–height cross-section observed by the microwave radiometer at Urumqi Airport from February 13 to 21. During the foehn incubation phase, the warm layer gradually descended downward, and potential instability began to develop. Starting from February 16, the vertical temperature structure underwent significant changes, marking the onset of the foehn incubation. On February 17–18, the downward intrusion of the warm layer intensified, and the near-surface 0 °C isotherm descended below 1 km for the first time. The near-surface temperature increased slowly but remained around − 10 °C, with a pronounced temperature inversion layer whose top gradually lowered.

Temporal evolution of temperature vertical profiles at Urumqi Airport during 13–21 February 2021.

During the foehn touchdown phase (February 19–20), explosive warming occurred near the surface, although the wind field was intermittently interrupted. February 19 was the foehn outbreak day, with significant warming beginning at 07:00. The 5 °C isotherm in the lower layer pressed down to the ground, and the hourly positive temperature change exceeded 15 °C, consistent with the temperatures observed at Urumqi meteorological station. During the daytime of February 19, the near-surface temperature fluctuated. The 0 °C layer in the mid-to-upper levels rose to nearly 3 km. On February 20, the foehn persisted but with diminishing intensity. The near-surface temperature remained between 0 °C and 5 °C. Northerly winds intruded intermittently, and the foehn system was in a transitional state between maintenance and decay.

During the foehn termination phase (February 21), the stratification recovered, and energy gradually dissipated. After February 21, the vertical temperature structure fully returned to normal, the near-surface temperature dropped back to −5 °C, the 0 °C isotherm disappeared, and the atmospheric stratification re-established a neutrally stable state.

Overall, the temperature profiles from the microwave radiometer clearly depict the rapid warming and cooling of the lower atmosphere during the processes of foehn occurrence, interruption, and re-establishment.

Figure 8 presents the vertical time–height cross-section of absolute humidity observed by the microwave radiometer at Urumqi Airport from February 13 to 21.

Temporal evolution of absolute humidity vertical profiles at Urumqi Airport during 13–21 February 2021.

Before the foehn occurrence, a cold and moist cushion layer persisted near the surface (on February 13). The near-surface absolute humidity remained at 2–3 g/m³, 1 g/m³ above 1 km. During the foehn incubation phase (February 15–18), a dry layer intruded downward, and the humidity stratification adjusted. On February 16, the near-surface absolute humidity dropped below 2 g/m³, indicating the onset of dry air intrusion. On February 17, the situation intensified, with a dry zone appearing near the surface with absolute humidity below 1 g/m³, although the cold cushion rebounded during the nighttime. February 18 was a critical turning point, at 16:00, the near-surface absolute humidity plummeted to below 0.5 g/m³, and the entire atmospheric column became dry, with the foehn briefly touching the ground before being interrupted.

During the foehn touchdown phase, explosive drying occurred within the boundary layer (February 19–20). At 04:00 on February 19, the near-surface absolute humidity dropped sharply to below 0.5 g/m³. At 05:00, the foehn was interrupted, and humidity briefly recovered. At 07:00, the foehn touched down again, and the near-surface absolute humidity again fell below 0.5 g/m³, with the entire column remaining dry. From the night of February 19 to the early morning of February 20, the foehn persisted, and the near-surface absolute humidity exhibited intermittent fluctuations of “decrease-increase”, corresponding to fluctuations in the temperature field, well capturing the processes of foehn touchdown and interruption.

On the daytime of February 20, the foehn ended, the moist layer recovered, and the stratification tended to normalize. The near-surface absolute humidity rose back to 3–4 g/m³, while remaining below 1 g/m³ above 3 km, but the near-surface values were slightly higher than those before the foehn (2–3 g/m³). During the nighttime, the stratification was stable, with no significant fluctuations in the isopleths, indicating the end of the dry and warm foehn process and the return of normal water vapor transport in the boundary layer.

In summary, absolute humidity and temperature show a significant negative correlation, before the foehn, temperatures are low and humidity is high; during the foehn phase, temperatures rise sharply and humidity drops sharply; after the foehn, temperatures fall back and humidity recovers. Throughout this period, humidity at the 5 km level remained consistently low with small amplitude variations, indicating that the dry and warm foehn process mainly affects the boundary layer below 3 km. The cyclic rise and fall of near-surface humidity corresponds to the rise and fall of the temperature field, jointly indicating the drastic adjustment of the boundary layer structure caused by gravity wave breaking on the lee side of the Middle Tianshan Mountains.

Analysis of synoptic situation based on reanalysis data

From February 19 to 20, the upper levels over Xinjiang were dominated by a strong high-pressure ridge. Meanwhile, the sea-level pressure field over Xinjiang exhibited a pattern of “higher pressure in the south and lower pressure in the north”, resulting in a strong pressure gradient across the Tianshan Mountains. Coupled with the topographic effect of the Central Tianshan Canyon, the airflow subsided and warmed on the northern slope of the Tianshan Mountains, generating intense foehn winds over the Central Tianshan Canyon and its downstream areas.

Upper-level circulation pattern

At 06:00 on February 17 at 500 hPa (Fig. 9a), the upper-level pattern over eastern Europe to the high latitudes of eastern Asia featured a trough region, with a low-vortex centre located over the Central Siberian region. A broad ridge extended from the mid-latitude Caspian Sea to Lake Baikal. At both 500 hPa and 700 hPa, the temperature ridges over Central Asia lay to the west of the height ridges, exhibiting a configuration where the temperature field lagged behind the height field. This pattern favoured the development of warm advection, which further strengthened the height ridge. During this phase, the Central Tianshan Canyon region was controlled by a large-scale stable circulation, and the weather system remained relatively stable.

Synoptic patterns at 500 hPa, 700 hPa, and 850 hPa pressure levels at 06:00 on 17 February 2021 (a) and 07:00 on 19 February 2021 (b). Black lines represent geopotential height contours (interval: 4dagpm), red lines denote isotherms (interval: 4 °C), and the red dot marks the location of Urumqi station.

At 07:00 on February 19 at 500 hPa (Fig. 9b), meridional circulation dominated the mid-to-high latitudes. The high-pressure ridge from Central Asia to Xinjiang intensified, with its ridge top extending northward to 64°N. The trough west of the Ural Mountains pressed southward, while a broad trough region developed over eastern Europe. The northern and southern troughs were nearly in phase, with the trough south of the Black Sea slightly lagging behind. As the two troughs (west of the Urals and south of the Black Sea) continued moving eastward, warm advection ahead of the troughs favoured the further development and intensification of the downstream height ridge. At 500 hPa and 700 hPa, the temperature ridge and the height ridge over Central Asia became nearly in phase, indicating a weakening of the thermal advection in the region. The height ridge reached its mature stage and maintained a strong intensity, and the Central Tianshan Canyon was controlled by this ridge. The southeasterly winds reached their strongest phase during the daytime of February 19. At 07:00 on February 19 at 850 hPa, the 0 °C isotherm rose rapidly from approximately 50°N to 54°N within two days, indicating a significant warming over Xinjiang. Meanwhile, an 8 °C warm centre was located downstream of Urumqi, favouring the formation of warm advection over the Central Tianshan Canyon, thereby providing thermal conditions for the maintenance of the height ridge and the development of the foehn.

On February 21, as the trough over eastern Europe gradually moved eastward to the west of Lake Balkhash, the high-pressure ridge over Xinjiang weakened, and the foehn over the Central Tianshan Canyon gradually came to an end.

Surface pressure pattern

At 06:00 on February 17, the surface pressure field over Xinjiang was dominated by a cold high-pressure system (Fig. 10a), and the atmospheric stratification was stable. The high-pressure centre was located north of the Tianshan Mountains, with a central intensity of 1048 hPa. The Central Tianshan Canyon lay on the southern side of this high centre, and during this period, Urumqi, located downstream, was dominated by northerly airflow. In addition, two other high-pressure centres were present, one north of the Aral Sea and the other west of Qinghai Lake, with central intensities of 1048 hPa and 1052 hPa, respectively. As the trough over eastern Europe continuously developed, the upstream cold high-pressure centre gradually moved eastward to the Lake Baikal region. By 07:00 on February 19, the intensity of the high centre near Lake Baikal had weakened to 1036 hPa (Fig. 10b). At the same time, a 1020 hPa warm low developed over Central Asia and moved northeastward to the north of Altay, significantly enhancing the pressure gradient across the northern and southern sides of the Tianshan Mountains. Meanwhile, the high-pressure centre west of Qinghai Lake weakened slightly, but the high pressure over the Tarim Basin remained at approximately 1044 hPa. As a result, the sea-level pressure field over Xinjiang exhibited a pattern of higher pressure in the south and lower pressure in the north. Under the combined effect of this pressure configuration and the topography, the airflow crossed the Tianshan Mountains, subsided, and warmed on the northern slope, generating strong foehn winds over the Central Tianshan Canyon.

Analysis of fine characteristics of temperature and humidity fields based on model simulation

Verification of model results

To comprehensively evaluate the model performance along the foehn path, we selected three key stations (DBC, URMQI, and MQ) strategically located from the upstream to the downstream area of the NS-MTM.

Table 2 presents the correlation coefficients between observed and simulated wind speed, wind direction, and temperature at these stations. For DBC, the R values are 0.72, 0.83, and 0.85, respectively; for URMQI, 0.65, 0.61, and 0.73; and for MQ, 0.60, 0.52, and 0.68. Among the three stations, DBC consistently outperforms the other two across all variables (Fig. 11abc). Overall, the model captures the general trends of these elements during the foehn process, with temperature simulations showing the highest correlations (0.68–0.85). The simulation errors may be attributed to several factors related to the complex terrain. Unresolved sub-grid topography, together with limitations in PBL parameterisation, may lead to wind speed overestimation and difficulties in capturing terrain-forced flow deflection. In the downstream area, frequent oscillations of a microscale front may further contribute to the large variability in wind direction. In addition, point-to-grid comparison errors and potential inaccuracies in urban station observations may also account for some of the discrepancies.

Characteristics of the surface temperature field distribution

Figure 13 shows the 2 m temperature field simulated by the WRF model. From the surface temperature field before the foehn occurred at 14:00 on February 15th, it can be seen that the temperature spatial distribution shows a feature of being higher in the south and lower in the north. The temperature in the Turpan Basin in the south was around 5 °C, the temperature in the valley was around 0 °C, and the temperature in the downwind area from Changji Station (CJ) to Hutubi Station (HTB) was around − 5 °C. At 02:00 on February 16th, the night before the foehn occurred, the spatial distribution of temperature was consistent with that in the daytime, but the overall temperature was about 5 °C lower than that during the day due to radiation cooling. The surface wind speed was relatively small on February 15th and 16th. At 14:00 on the 19th, when the foehn was at its peak strength, the wind speed in the canyon reached about 10 m/s. At this time, because of the foehn effect and the strong solar radiation at noon, the temperature in the north slope of the Tianshan Mountains increased drastically compared to that at the same time before the foehn occurred. Thus, the surface temperature field showed a low-high-low pattern from south to north. The temperature on the north sides of Tiangeer Mountain and Bogda Mountain and in the area between the DZT and BG (i.e. 43.9°N) was the highest, reaching about 15 °C, which was higher than that before the occurrence of foehn by more than 10 °C. At 02:00 on the 20th, the foehn wind turned weakened, and accordingly the temperature dropped at night, but it was still about 5 °C higher than the night temperature before the occurrence of the foehn event. By 14:00 on the 20th, the foehn wind process completely ended, leaving the surface to be dominated by the northwest wind. After the foehn, the surface temperature declined, significantly lower than that during the foehn period, but still higher than that before the foehn event. At this time, the temperature field began to show the feature of being higher in the south and lower in the north again. After the foehn event was over, the temperature at night was also lower than during the foehn period but higher than before the foehn wind occurred. At night, the temperature near URMQI and DZT was about 5 °C higher than that in the surrounding areas. The wind force during this foehn process was very strong, accompanied by short-time extreme temperature rise, and changes in the spatial distribution of temperature field.

Characteristics of the vertical distribution of the temperature field

Figure 14 shows the vertical profile of temperature and wind between points (43.0°N, 88.5°E) and (45.0°N, 86.5°E). The figure reveals that the temperature of the entire atmosphere was relatively low at 14:00 on the 15th and 02:00 on the 16th, with higher temperatures in the south and lower temperatures in the northern valley from the ground to around 2000 m. During the peak intensity of the foehn event at 14:00 on February 19 and 02:00 on February 20, the atmosphere heated sharply from ground to around 3000 m (700 hPa) on the leeward slope. At 14:00 on the 20th, the foehn wind ended, and the regional atmospheric temperature gradually decreased. By 02:00 on the 20th, the thermal structure reverted to a south-high-north-low configuration. Characteristics of relative humidity distribution

Weather forecasting practice shows that non-adiabatic factors such as clouds, air humidity, and wind have significant impacts on temperature changes. Figure 15 displays the three-dimensional diagrams of the simulated relative humidity fields at different pressure levels in different study areas and the surface 2 m relative humidity at 14:00 on February 19, 2021. Judging from the relative humidity at different pressure levels and at the surface in D03 of Fig. 15, during the extreme warming period, most areas on the NS-MTM are low-value areas of relative humidity during the intense temperature rise period, that is, the air is very dry. The relative humidity is below 20% at the surface in most areas, but below 40% at different pressure levels in the study area. From the diagrams of D02 and D01 in Fig. 15, we can see that the relative humidity gradually increases from NS-MTM outward, indicating that the sky over the NS-MTM was clear and dry, the “foehn clearance”, this situation encourages radiative flux convergence (Elvidge et al., 2016).

Analysis of the evolution characteristics of air pollutants based on environmental monitoring data

Figure 16 shows the concentrations of air pollutants from February 13 to 24 at three environmental monitoring stations arranged from north to south: Miquan Station (MQ), URMQI Station, and DBC Station. As can be seen from the figure, the particulate matter concentrations at Miquan Station increased continuously during the foehn incubation phase, reaching their maximum on February 18 and 19, with PM2.5 at 176 µg/m³ and PM10 at 170 µg/m³. At URMQI, located in the valley, the particulate matter concentrations peaked on February 18 and then gradually decreased, with PM2.5 at 224 µg/m³ and PM10 at 218 µg/m³ on February 18. Both stations experienced severe pollution. At the upstream DBC, the PM2.5 concentration showed little variation, while the PM10 concentration reached its maximum of 150 µg/m³ on February 19.

Air pollutant concentrations from 13 to 24 February 2021.

Ozone concentrations increased at all three stations during the foehn, with the most significant increases at the downstream stations of MQ and URMQI. On February 20, the O₃ concentration at MQ was 169 µg/m³, with an O₃−8 h of 158 µg/m³, representing an increase of 71 µg/m³ compared to February 16. At URMQI, the O₃ concentration was 115 µg/m³, with an O₃−8 h of 96 µg/m³, an increase of 79 µg/m³ compared to February 16. At the upstream DBC, O₃ concentrations began to increase during the foehn incubation phase and remained around 120 µg/m³. After the foehn ended, O₃ concentrations gradually declined at all three stations.

Nitrogen oxides exhibited an inverse relationship with ozone at URMQI and DBC, while no significant pattern was observed at MQ. On February 18, NOx concentrations at URMQI were relatively high, reaching 211 µg/m³, but on February 19 and 20, NOx concentrations decreased markedly, dropping to 81 µg/m³. At DBC, the lowest O₃ concentration and the highest NOx concentration occurred on February 15; subsequently, O₃ concentrations increased slowly while NOx concentrations decreased gradually.

In summary, the impact of the foehn process on regional air quality exhibits a dual effect, with the coupling of its dynamic and thermal processes governing the evolution of atmospheric pollutants. During the foehn incubation phase, the low-level temperature inversion led to a stable atmospheric stratification, hindering the dispersion of particulate matter. Downstream stations such as MQ and URMQI experienced significant increases in PM2.5 and PM10 concentrations, reaching severe pollution levels. After the foehn touchdown, the strong subsidence and high winds generated dynamic dispersion, which exerted a certain scavenging effect on particulate matter, causing their concentrations to gradually decline. Meanwhile, the extreme warming induced by the foehn provided favourable thermal conditions for ozone formation, resulting in a marked increase in regional ozone concentrations. Nitrogen oxides exhibited an inverse trend due to their photochemical consumption by ozone. These characteristics indicate that the impact of the foehn on regional air pollution is distinctly phased: the incubation stage exacerbates particulate matter pollution, while the grounding stage exacerbates ozone pollution.

Discussion

The foehn on the northern slope of the Middle Tianshan Mountains is characterized by strong winds and dry-hot conditions, often inducing various hazards. In this case, the surface temperature field exhibits a spatial pattern of a “high-temperature core in the canyon surrounded by low-temperature areas on the periphery.” Notably, in the area from BG to DZT Station, the convergence of northwesterly and southeasterly winds forms a dynamic convergence zone (a micro-front), resulting in intense temperature fluctuations that are significantly higher than those in surrounding areas. This suggests that, for operational forecasting in this region, identifying the location of such a micro-front may be helpful for improving temperature prediction accuracy on the northern slope of the Middle Tianshan Mountains. Furthermore, the foehn caused a marked rise in the height of the 0 °C layer, which may have implications for long‑term climatic effects such as glacier ablation, although this possibility requires further investigation.

The impact of the foehn on atmospheric pollution exhibits a distinct phased “dual effect.” During the incubation phase, the low-level temperature inversion locks in a stable atmospheric stratification, leading to continuous accumulation of particulate matter. During the grounding phase, the dynamic dispersion induced by strong winds effectively reduces particulate matter concentrations, but the extreme warming and intense radiation conditions promote rapid ozone formation. These findings suggest that, in forecasting wintertime foehn pollution episodes similar to this case, attention may need to be paid not only to particulate matter pollution but also to possible ozone pollution events following the foehn touchdown. Nevertheless, this observation is based on a single event, and more cases are needed to confirm this pattern.

In summary, the formation and evolution of the foehn on the northern slope of the Middle Tianshan Mountains represent a typical process coupling topographic dynamic forcing, vertical atmospheric restructuring, and energy conversion. Specifically, during the foehn incubation phase, the mid-level southeasterly airflow is topographically uplifted on the southern slope of the Tianshan Mountains, where moisture condensation and dehydration produce a dry-warm air mass. Blocked by a near-surface suspended temperature inversion, the dry-warm energy accumulates in the mid-levels of the leeward slope. Meanwhile, In the northern slope valley, the near-surface circulation is dominated by a mountain-valley breeze with weak wind speeds, allowing pollutants to accumulate continuously. During the foehn outbreak phase, strong vertical wind shear at upper levels triggers downward momentum transfer, breaking through the inversion layer. The dry-warm air then subsides dry-adiabatically along the northern slope terrain, accompanied by intense compressional warming, generating strong southeasterly winds from the canyon to the plain. The descending airflow converges with the near-surface cold air, forming a micro-front that drives vigorous turbulence in the boundary layer. This process, on one hand, promotes pollutant dispersion and regional transport through strong winds, and on the other hand, alters near-surface photochemical reaction conditions, leading to elevated ozone concentrations. It should be emphasized, however, that this conceptual model is derived from a single case study, and its broader applicability awaits testing with additional foehn events under different synoptic and topographic settings.

Conclusions

Using hourly observational data from meteorological stations on the northern slope of the Middle Tianshan Mountains, sounding data, microwave radiometer data, ERA5 reanalysis data, pollutant data from environmental monitoring stations, combined with WRF model simulations, this study systematically analyzes the meteorological field characteristics, formation mechanisms, and environmental effects of a typical strong foehn event with extreme warming that occurred on February 19–20, 2021. The main conclusions are as follows:

(1) The foehn exhibited a sudden onset with drastic temperature and humidity fluctuations, and its touchdown was triggered by intense mechanical turbulence - generated by strong vertical wind shear - that broke through the low-level temperature inversion. Surface observations recorded an hourly warming of 20.9 °C during the touchdown phase and a cooling of −10.3 °C/h during the decay phase. In the canyon, the south-easterly foehn winds converged with north-westerly winds, forming a micro-front whose movement induced regional temperature oscillations. Microwave radiometer data showed that the 0 °C isotherm rose to nearly 3 km, while absolute humidity plummeted to below 0.5 g/m³, with cyclical “decrease-increase” fluctuations near the surface-clear evidence of boundary-layer restructuring driven by frontal activity.

(2) Synoptic-scale circulation and topographic forcing jointly triggered the foehn. At 500 hPa, a strong ridge dominated Xinjiang, with the thermal ridge nearly in phase with the geopotential ridge; warm advection ahead of the ridge continuously intensified the ridge. Meanwhile, a “high-pressure-south, low-pressure-north” pattern at sea level produced a strong pressure gradient across the Tianshan Mountains. In combination with the canyon topography of the Middle Tianshan, this configuration forced cross-mountain airflow to descend, generating intense foehn conditions.

(3) WRF simulations revealed a distinct spatial temperature structure during the peak phase. A “warm core” formed near the surface in the canyon area, especially around 43.9°N, where temperatures reached approximately 15 °C, more than 10 °C higher than at the same time before the foehn. Peripheral plains were notably cooler, resulting in a “canyon warm core - peripheral cool zone” pattern. Vertically, a pronounced warm centre existed at about 1.5 km (850 hPa) between 43.8°N and 44.3°N, and the 0 °C isotherm was significantly elevated. The combined effect of adiabatic subsidence warming and the “foehn-clearance” effect was primarily responsible for the intense lee-side warming.

(4) The foehn exerted a dual, phase-dependent impact on air pollution. During the incubation stage, a low-level temperature inversion maintained a stable boundary layer, allowing PM2.5 to accumulate to a peak concentration of 224 µg/m³ (severe pollution) at Urumqi Station. After the foehn touchdown, strong subsidence and high winds induced mechanical dispersion, gradually reducing particulate matter. However, the extreme warming and enhanced solar radiation promoted rapid O₃ formation, the O₃−8 h values at Miquan and Urumqi stations increased by 71 and 79 µg/m³, respectively, relative to pre-foehn levels, while NOₓ showed an inverse trend due to photochemical consumption.

In summary, this study establishes a coupled dynamic-thermal-environmental mechanism for the foehn, which links topographic blocking, energy accumulation, downward momentum transfer, thermal warming, and subsequent environmental effects. It reveals the special distribution patterns of the meteorological field and the phased evolution of pollutants during the foehn process on the northern slope of the Middle Tianshan Mountains. These findings can provide scientific references for foehn temperature forecasting, wind hazard prevention and mitigation, and combined air pollution control in this region.

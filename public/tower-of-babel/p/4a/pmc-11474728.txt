Immune reactivity directed against the carbamylated fibrinogen α chain does not cross-react with citrullinated fibrinogen immunodominant peptides in patients with rheumatoid arthritis
Authors: Pauline Brevet, Olivier Boyer, Olivier Vittecoq, Manuel Fréret
Published: 2024

Post-translational modifications (PTMs) of proteins in rheumatoid arthritis (RA), including citrullination, carbamylation and more recently acetylation, have introduced the concept of anti-modified protein antibodies (AMPAs). A recent study showed a significant cross-reactivity between these autoantibodies.1 This can be explained by the immune repertoire diversification during epitope spreading, resulting in the recognition of an increasing number of citrullinated targets before RA symptoms appear. Somatic hypermutations in clonal B cells specific to citrullinated antigens during affinity maturation modify the antibody paratope, making it polyreactive towards multiple citrullinated targets and potentially explaining the cross-reactivity of the citrullinated response towards other PTMs.2 Shi et al showed that both immunoreactivities directed against citrullinated and carbamylated fibrinogen by using the whole protein as substrate were largely distinct,3 and this no overlapping response is relevant especially in early stages.4 As was done for ACPAs (anti-citrullinated protein antibodies), our purpose was to better characterise the fine specificities (FSs) of anti-CarP (anti-carbamylated protein antibodies) using the same antigenic counterparts (fibrinogen), as the work conducted by Cornillet et al on ACPAs5 in order to avoid the question of cross-reactivity due to the self-molecule. Moreover, in our previous study, using high-resolution mass spectrometry, we identified targets on the α and γ chains of carbamylated fibrinogen (Ca-Fib) of IgG anti-CarP (ACa-Fib).6 The γ chain was particularly interesting as it had not been previously described as an immune target in RA and might contain specific epitopes for carbamylation (unlike the α chain, already known as a target for citrullination). Epitope mapping, using ELISA was performed on 54 ACPA+ ACa-Fib+ RA sera, from the VErA (Very Early rheumatoid Arthritis) cohort, testing 15 peptides of 27 mer-straddling 3 amino acids from the γ chain of fibrinogen.6 Sera mainly reacted with two immunodominant epitopes and only one (Ca-Fibγ361-387) was not recognised by ACPA after inhibition testing with the major citrullinated antigens described by Cornillet (α36-50Cit38,42 and β60-74Cit60,72,74).5 We did not perform experiments based on the self-inhibition for the two major citrullinated antigens since they have already been performed by Cornillet who used the same procedure as described here.5

We carried out epitope mapping on the Ca-Fib α chain by testing the reactivity of 67 ACPA+ ACa-Fib+ sera from the VErA cohort. 15 out of 22 peptides (27 mer-straddling 3 amino-acids) were carbamylated in vitro and used as ELISA substrates, as previously described.6 32 patients had autoantibodies recognising at least 1 peptide (data not shown) and reactivity was mainly found on 3 peptides (B, Q and R). 13, 8 and 7 patients had autoantibodies recognising an epitope on the B, Q and R peptides, respectively (online supplemental figure 1). We analysed the ability of these three peptides to compete, at increasing amounts up to a saturating dose of 10 µg, with autoantibodies recognising themselves (positive control, figure 1A), autoantibodies recognising the immunodominant epitope of the Cit-Fib α chain (figure 1B) or autoantibodies recognising the immunodominant epitope of Cit-Fib β chain (figure 1C). As expected, each peptide completely inhibited its binding to sera from a saturating dose of 5 and 10 µg (figure 1A). Interestingly, a saturating dose of 10 µg of either Cit-Fib α chain peptide (figure 1B) or Cit-Fib β chain peptide (figure 1C) resulted in a low inhibition (<5%) of sera reactivity for B and Q peptides and <20% for the R peptide (figure 1D). These results suggest that the anti-CarP response directed against the three immunodominant epitopes of the α chain of Ca-Fib does not cross-react with the ACPA recognising the two major epitopes of the α and β chains of Cit-Fib as expected. Indeed, cross-reactive epitopes are not recognised by the most specific, highest affinity antibodies (which are captured by the two citrullinated peptides used as competitors, leaving behind the weaker affinity, weaker specificity, broadly cross-reactive autoantibodies) (online supplemental figure 2).

Cross-reactivity assay of anti-carbamylated fibrinogen α chain peptides B, Q and R antibodies with major epitopes of anti-citrullinated fibrinogen response. (A) ELISA inhibition test of anti-carbamylated fibrinogen α chain peptides B, Q and R antibodies with their corresponding peptides at increasing doses (0, 0.1, 0.5, 1, 5 or 10 µg). (B) ELISA inhibition test of anti-carbamylated fibrinogen α chain peptides B, Q and R antibodies with the immunodominant epitope of citrullinated fibrinogen α chain peptide at increasing doses (0, 0.1, 0.5, 1, 5 or 10 µg). (C) ELISA inhibition test of anti-carbamylated fibrinogen α chain peptides B, Q and R antibodies with the immunodominant epitope of citrullinated fibrinogen β chain peptide at increasing doses (0, 0.1, 0.5, 1, 5 or 10 µg). ELISA (A–C) expressed as OD (optical density) at 450 nm. Each curve represents a serum from a patient positive for anti-carbamylated fibrinogen α chain peptides B, Q or R antibodies (B and Q: n=7; R: n=6). (D) Percentages of inhibition for each peptide. Means+SEMs are represented, circles represent individual patients. ACa-Fib α chain, anti-carbamylated fibrinogen α chain antibodies; Ca-Fib α chain, carbamylated fibrinogen α chain; Cit-Fib α chain, citrullinated fibrinogen α chain; Cit-Fib β chain, citrullinated fibrinogen β chain.

In conclusion, three anti-Ca-Fib α chain autoantibody FSs were found and could be of clinical interest in the RA diagnosis, especially in ACPA-negative patients.

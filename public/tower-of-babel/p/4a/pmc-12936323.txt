PD-1 inhibitors combined with induction chemotherapy followed by chemoradiotherapy in HPV-negative locally advanced head and neck squamous cell carcinoma: a real-world study
Authors: Yi Yang, Shunxin Wang, Shuzhen Peng, Yu Li, Yiming Lei, Weihong Zheng, Sha Xu, Shaohui Huang, Ying Huang, Yanping Mao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo compare the efficacy of induction chemotherapy with or without PD-1 inhibitors followed by chemoradiotherapy (CCRT) among patients with human papillomavirus (HPV)-negative locally advanced head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (LA-HNSCC).Materials and methodsWe retrospectively reviewed patients with HPV-negative LA-HNSCC who received induction chemotherapy with or without PD-1 inhibitors followed by CCRT between January 2018 and June 2023. Overall survival (OS), disease-free survival (DFS), and treatment-related adverse effects (TRAEs) were compared between the two groups overall and then in one-to-one propensity-score matched (PSM) cohorts.ResultsA total of 289 eligible patients were enrolled, with 120 patients received induction chemotherapy (the IC group), and 169 patients received induction chemotherapy combined with PD-1 inhibitors (the IC-IO group). Median follow-up was 39.3 months (range: 37.5–41.5 months). After PSM, objective response rate (ORR) was not significantly different in the IC-IO group versus the IC group (81.5% vs 74.1%, P = 0.19). The IC-IO group (vs. the IC group) had a superior 2-year OS (84.3% vs. 68.2%, P = 0.002) and DFS (74.1% vs. 56.9%, P = 0.005). G3/4 TRAEs between the two matched groups were comparable both during induction (11.1% vs 11.1%, P = 1.000) or CCRT (29.6% vs 38.9%, P = 0.152) phases. For the IC-IO group, no significant differences in OS (P = 0.68) or DFS (P = 0.29) were observed in the subset with versus without PD-1 inhibitors concurrently with CCRT.ConclusionsThe addition of PD-1 inhibitors to IC significantly improves outcomes with tolerable TRAEs in patients with HPV-negative LA-HNSCC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00262-025-04296-6.Keywords: Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, PD-1 inhibitors, Induction chemoimmunotherapy, HPV-negative, Radiotherapy

Introduction

Locally advanced head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (LA-HNSCC) remains a therapeutic challenge, particularly for patients with HPV-negative disease, who account for approximately 75% of cases and exhibit poorer survival outcomes compared to HPV-positive counterparts [1]. Despite multimodality treatments such as definitive concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CCRT), the prognosis of HPV-negative LA-HNSCC remains disappointing, with a 5-year survival rate of 40% [2]. Novel treatment strategies are urgently needed for this patient population.

Immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) have revolutionized treatment for recurrent or metastatic HNSCC, with prolonged overall survival (OS) compared to conventional chemotherapy [3, 4]. Notably, treatment-naïve LA-HNSCC patients exhibit less pronounced immunosuppression compared to recurrent or metastatic cases [5], potentially predicting superior response to immunotherapy. Preclinical evidence indicates that combined radiotherapy and immunotherapy elicits synergistic antitumor activity, mediated by triggering immunogenic cell death and enhancing PD-L1 expression in the tumor microenvironment [6, 7]. However, several phase III clinical trials failed to demonstrate survival benefit when combining PD-1 inhibitors with CCRT in LA-HNSCC [8, 9]. One of the possible explanations is that high-dose radiation to regional lymph nodes impairs the function of tumor drainage lymph nodes, thus decreasing the antitumor immune response. Therefore, it is plausible that employing sequential immunotherapy prior to CCRT might yield better outcomes.

Induction chemotherapy (IC) confers dual advantages in organ preservation and early eradication of micrometastases in LA-HNSCC [10–13]; however, its efficacy in improving survival benefit remains unproven. Extensive preclinical evidence from diverse cancer models confirms the synergistic interaction between chemotherapy and immunotherapy, mediated through chemotherapy-induced DNA damage and subsequent activation of innate immune signaling pathways [14, 15]. Meanwhile, clinical trials in several solid tumors have validated that incorporating PD-1 inhibitors to IC significantly extended survival compared to IC alone [16–18]. Furthermore, recent phase II clinical trials in LA-HNSCC reported excellent pathological response rate after receiving IC combined with PD-1 inhibitors [19–23], which also associated with better survival outcome. Motivated by these promising findings, our institution began employing a personalized neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy protocol (paclitaxel, platinum, plus a PD-1 inhibitor) based on clinician–patient shared decision-making in 2019. However, clinical evidence supporting the long-term survival benefits of adding PD-1 inhibitors to IC followed by CCRT is lacking, and the generalizability of prior studies is constrained by their relatively small sample sizes and short-term follow-up.

Therefore, we conducted this real-world study to summarize our institutional experience by investigating whether the addition of PD-1 inhibitors to IC followed by CCRT could result in clinical benefits in patients with LA-HNSCC. We also explore the value of the addition of PD-1 inhibitors concurrently with CCRT after receiving IC combined with PD-1 inhibitors.

Materials and methods

Patient selection

Patients were retrospectively reviewed with previously untreated, locally advanced, nonmetastasis HNSCC (larynx, hypopharynx oropharynx, and oral cavity) between January 2018 and June 2023 at Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center. Patients with oropharyngeal cancer were required to have negative p16 test results by immunohistochemistry. The inclusion criteria were: (1) staged as III-IVB HPV-negative HNSCC according to AJCC 8th Edition; (2) received IC with or without PD-1 inhibitors and followed by CCRT; the exclusion criteria were: (1) with synchronous cancer; (2) did not complete definitive radiotherapy; (3) received immunotherapy after definitive radiotherapy. Details are presented in Fig. 1. The clinical research committee at our center approved this study and waived written informed consent [24].

Treatment

According to our national guideline for HNSCC, IC followed by CCRT is standard recommendation for patients with unresectable tumors or organ preservation purposes. IC regimens consisted of 2–4 cycles of docetaxel or albumin paclitaxel plus platin based chemotherapy. Since 2019, our institution has adopted a personalized protocol of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy (paclitaxel and platinum plus PD-1 inhibitors) for patients with LA-HNSCC [25]. The selection of PD-1 inhibitors is based on clinician–patient shared decision-making; the application and comparison of PD-1 inhibitors are detailed in Table S1. (The PD-1 inhibitor used was one of the following: camrelizumab, sintilimab, toripalimab, pembrolizumab, nivolumab, or tislelizumab.) After completing 2–4 cycles of induction therapy, an imaging-based efficacy assessment according to RECIST 1.1 criteria was conducted 1–2 weeks later.

All patients underwent definitive treatment with intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT). According to the ICRU Reports 50 and 62, the consensus on target delineation for HNSCC [26, 27], and the experience of our institutional, the dosimetric and anatomical considerations for radiation target delineation are summarized as follow: The gross target volume (GTV) was defined to include all macroscopic disease, incorporating both the primary tumor along with radiologically involved lymph nodes, with a prescribed dose of 66–72 Gy delivered in 1.82–2.12 Gy per fraction. The clinical target volume (CTV), which includes the GTV and surrounding subclinical lesions, was further subdivided into high-risk and low-risk CTV. The high-risk CTV encompassed the GTVp with a 1.5–2.0 cm margin and at least one adjacent uninvolved nodal station, prescribed 60–63 Gy in 28–33 fractions. The low-risk CTV covered prophylactic irradiation areas, with a prescribed dose of 50–56 Gy using identical fractionation. (Table S2). The concurrent chemotherapy consisted of 40 mg/m2 cisplatin administered every week for a maximum of 7 cycles, 80 mg/m2 cisplatin administered every 3 weeks for a maximum of 3 cycles, or 100 mg/m2 cisplatin administered every 3 weeks for a maximum of 3 cycles, beginning on the first day of RT or 3 weeks after the last cycle of IC.

Study outcomes

The primary endpoint was overall survival (OS, time to death from any cause). Other endpoints included disease-free survival (DFS, time to disease progression or death from any cause), locoregional relapse-free survival (LRRFS, time to local or regional recurrence or both), and distant metastasis-free survival (DMFS, time to first distant metastasis). Patients were followed-up every 3 mouth for initial 2 years, then transitioned to 6-month intervals thereafter until death. CTCAE version 4.0 was applied to record and grade adverse events (AEs) that occurred throughout the induction and radiation phases.

Statistical analysis

The χ2/Fisher’s exact and Mann–Whitney U tests were used for categorical and continuous variables, respectively. To minimize confounding, propensity-score matching (1:1 nearest-neighbor matching with a caliper of 0.2 standard deviations) was performed based on several variables: gender, age, BMI, smoke, drink, comorbidity, primary site, T category, N category, and IC cycles. Survival data were estimated via Kaplan–Meier method and compared with log-rank tests. Multivariable analysis were performed using the Cox proportional hazard model to identify independent prognostic factors. All statistical analyses were conducted using R (version 4.2.2) and SPSS version 25 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA), with a two-sided P-value < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 289 patients were eligible, including 120 patients received IC (the IC group) and 169 patients received IC combined with PD-1 inhibitors (the IC-IO group). Overall, the median age was 57.9 (24–79) years, with a male-to-female ratio of 8.9:1. The primary sites included 55 (19.0%) in the larynx, 125 (43.3%) in the hypopharynx, 83 (28.7%) in the oropharynx, and 26 (9.0%) in the oral cavity. After one-to-one nearest-neighbor matching, the final matched cohort comprised 216 patients equally distributed between the IC (n = 108) and the IC-IO (n = 108) groups (Table 1).

Tumor response

In the IC-IO vs IC group, CR, PR, SD, and PD were achieved in 2 (1.9%) vs 1 (0.9%), 86 (79.6%) vs 79 (73.1%), 18 (16.7%) vs 27 (25.0%), and 2 (1.9%) vs 1 (0.9%) patients, respectively. The ORR for IC-IO was nonsignificant different vs IC [81.5% (88/108) vs 74.1% (80/108), P = 0.190] (Fig. 2 and Table S3).

Survival analysis

By the last follow-up on 15th July, 2025, the median follow-up duration was 39.3 months (range: 37.5–41.5 months) for the entire cohort, with 37.7 months (range: 35.3–41.5 months) for the IC-IO group and 39.8 months (range: 38.3–43.3 months) for the IC group. The IC-IO group had a superior 2-year OS (86.4% vs. 71.5%, P = 0.006), DFS (75.4% vs. 61.5%, P = 0.029), LRRFS (78.2% vs. 61.4%, P = 0.013), and DMFS (84.0% vs. 69.5%, P = 0.013) compared with the IC group in the overall unmatched cohort (Table S4). The favorable results of the IC-IO group were duplicated in the PSM cohort: 2-year OS (88.1% vs. 72.6%, P = 0.004), DFS (79.3% vs. 62.0%, P = 0.006), LRRFS (81.1% vs. 62.0%, P = 0.006), and DMFS (85.5% vs. 69.8%, P = 0.004) after matching (Table S4 and Fig. 3).

As shown in Table 2, after adjusting for various factors, multivariate analyses demonstrated superior survival outcomes with the IC-IO group versus the IC group: OS (HR, 0.50; 95% CI, 0.31–0.80; P = 0.004), DFS (HR, 0.60; 95% CI, 0.41–0.89; P = 0.011), LRRFS (HR, 0.61; 95% CI, 0.41–0.90; P = 0.013), and DMFS (HR, 0.56; 95% CI, 0.36–0.86; P = 0.008). Additionally, no significant differences in survival outcomes were found by primary sites on univariable or multivariable analysis.

Toxicity profile

We presented the toxicities experienced by patients in different treatment stages (Table 3). The incidence of grade 3/4 (G3/4 AEs) appeared no significant different in the IC-IO group compared to the IC group either in the induction phase (11.1% vs. 11.1%, P = 1.000) or (29.6% vs. 38.9%, P = 0.152). The primary manifestations of G3/4 AEs included radiation-induced oral mucositis, dysphagia, pneumonia, hematologic toxicities, and radiation dermatitis. Notably, in the IC-IO group, one patients who received concurrent immunotherapy developed grade 4 immune-related pneumonitis, and lead to treatment interruption. For the IC-IO group, patients who received concurrent immunotherapy (n = 92) and those who did not during the CCRT phase (n = 77) with comparable baseline characteristics (Table S5) showed nonsignificantly different in the incidence of G3/4 AEs (Table S6).

Discussion

Despite retrospective nature, our study showed that addition of PD-1 inhibitors to IC improves overall survival and reduced local recurrence and distant metastasis with tolerable adverse effects in HPV-negative LA-HNSCC. However, combining PD-1 inhibitors with CCRT concurrently exhibited no survival advantage in patients receiving IC combined with PD-1 inhibitors, questioning the necessity of concurrent immunotherapy with radiotherapy.

To date, long-term survival results regarding induction chemoimmunotherapy (IC-IO) followed by CCRT in LA-HNSCC are limited. The DEPEND trial investigated IC-IO (nivolumab plus carboplatin and paclitaxel) followed by CCRT in 36 patients with HPV-negative HNSCC, demonstrating 2-year OS and DFS of 73% and 66%, respectively [28]. Our study showed superior outcomes, primarily because the DEPEND trial exclusively enrolled stage IVA-IVB patients, which characteristically exhibit more aggressive disease biology. In laryngeal and hypopharyngeal cancer, IC-IO followed by CCRT yielded favorable 2-year survival (OS: 82.5%; PFS: 61.1–66.3%) in retrospective studies [29, 30], consistent with our data. A phase II trial further reported an 88.7% 1-year laryngeal preservation rate [22]. These findings collectively suggest IC-IO followed by CCRT as a viable strategy for improving survival and organ preservation in LA-HNSCC. Recently, the phase 3 KEYNOTE-689 reported 2-year OS 75.9% and 2-year EFS of 65% with single-agent pembrolizumab administered throughout the whole treatment course (neoadjuvant, concurrent, and adjuvant phases, totaling 17 cycles) [31]. In contrast, our study demonstrated superior long-term survival outcomes (2-year OS: 84.3%; 2-year DFS: 74.1%). Several factors may explain the more favorable outcomes observed in our study. Firstly, IC-IO may enhance systemic antitumor immunity through synergistic chemokine release and PD-L1 upregulation, priming tumors for subsequent PD-1 blockade [32, 33]. Secondly, tumor debulking during IC-IO potentially improved radiation sensitivity by reducing hypoxic niches [34]. Emerging evidence suggests that IC-IO can remodel the tumor microenvironment by increasing CD8 + T-cell infiltration and reducing immunosuppressive factors, and these effects may persist during subsequent CCRT [35]. Thirdly, the omission of adjuvant immunotherapy in our protocol may have reduced treatment-related toxicity and improving therapeutic compliance. While cross-trial comparisons are inherently limited, our results generate the hypothesis that a condensed, intensive chemoimmunotherapy induction sequence may offer an alternative therapeutic profile with comparable efficacy and potentially reduced toxicity compared to prolonged monotherapy. This hypothesis merits rigorous evaluation in future randomized clinical trials. Several multicenter Phase III trials is expected to yield significant insights into the application of IC-IO for patients with LA-HNSCC (NCT06102395, NCT05798793, NCT05582265). We are eagerly anticipating the results of the ongoing randomized clinical trials.

Despite robust preclinical data support the synergistic effects of combining immunotherapy with radiotherapy, current randomized phase III trials have found no significant survival benefit from the addition of PD-1 inhibitors concurrently with CCRT in LA-HNSCC [8, 9]. As well as, our study has shown no survival benefit of concurrent IO with RT following IC-IO. There are several hypotheses for explanation. Firstly, the selection of patients deserves further investigation, KEYNOTE-412 trial has demonstrated that the addition of pembrolizumab to chemoradiotherapy may confer benefits to LA-HNSCC patients expressing PD-L1, with > 2 years of additional follow-up, results showed a clinically meaningful EFS benefit with pembrolizumab plus CRT versus CRT (HR 0.79; 95% CI 0.65–0.96)[36]. The superior clinical outcomes were particularly evident in tumors with high PD-L1 expression (CPS ≥ 20: HR 0.70; 95%CI0.49–1.00), suggesting enhanced immune activation in this population [36]. In contrast, there was no discernible connection between survival results and PD-L1 expression in the GORTEC 2015–01 PembroRad study [9], albeit with limited statistical power. Secondly, a recent preclinical trial conducted by Darragh et al. [37] revealed that elective neck irradiation might compromise the immune response by diminishing the influx of lymphocytes to the lymph nodes and inhibiting T-cell activation. Additionally, the REWRITE study assessed a precision radiotherapy approach (primary tumor plus proximate nodal level) combined with durvalumab immunotherapy in N0 LA-HNSCC (NCT03726775). The results of these ongoing clinical trials might reveal whether restricted irradiation volume of the neck achieves survival benefits.

Tumor response to IC has been reported as a suitable indicator of survival outcome in HNSCC [38, 39]. The results of ORR in this study (the IC group vs. the IC-IO groups: 75.8% vs. 82.6%) are consistent with a previous study with ORR of (67.3%–86.5%) in induction therapy with East Asian HSNCC patients [40, 41]. Recently, Zhang et al. [21] and Wu et al. [23] reported that patients with LA-HNSCC receiving IC combined with camrelizumab achieved higher ORR (89.6% and 96.7%, respectively) than our study, several aspects can probably serve as an explanation for this. Firstly, a subset of patients (11.4%) in our study underwent efficacy evaluation after only 2 cycles of induction therapy, in contrast to patients in the aforementioned prospective phase II trials who completed 3 cycles of induction therapy. Previous research has indicated that most patients show imaging-based responses mainly after 4 weeks of immunotherapy, suggesting that extending the duration of immunotherapy may bring better tumor response outcomes [3, 42]. Secondly, the sample size of the prospective Phase II clinical studies is small (27–50 cases), limiting the reliability of the extrapolation of the results and necessitating further validation with larger datasets. Additionally, we failed to observe the integration of PD-1 inhibitors to IC bringing significance in ORR, while it does show remarkable significance in survival benefits. This challenges traditional efficacy evaluation criteria for solid tumors in predicting the effectiveness of IC-IO, highlighting the need to explore more precise immune-related indicators for efficacy prediction and treatment choices of immunotherapy. Biomarkers such as PD-L1, tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, and metabolic response assessed by PET/CT scan are under extensive investigation to enhance the guidance of precision immunotherapy. Overall, a variety of immune-related efficacy evaluation criteria were prompted to be continuously produced, revised, and improved.

Regarding adverse events, the results recorded in this study are generally consistent with previous clinical trials which reported G3-4 AEs ranging from 6.1%-37.0% during induction therapy in LA-HNSCC patients receiving IC-IO [19–23]. No significant difference was observed in grade 3–4 adverse events (AEs) between the IC-IO and IC groups, indicating comparable safety profiles. Notably, one patient with laryngeal cancer in the IC-IO group who received concurrent immunotherapy developed grade 4 immune-related pneumonitis, resulting in treatment interruption. Similarly, the phase III clinical trial conducted by Ferris et al. observed one case of severe pneumonitis among 240 patients with LA-HNSCC treated with pembrolizumab [43]. Despite the relatively low incidence of immune-related pneumonitis, its substantial mortality risk confers significant clinical importance. These findings emphasize the critical need for vigilant monitoring and timely intervention for immune-mediated toxicities in clinical practice.

Several limitations merit consideration in this study. Firstly, our analysis is limited to the outcomes collected in a retrospective database. Secondly, we did not completely collect data on PD-L1 due to the relevant clinical tests not being extensively applied before 2022 in our country. Thirdly, heterogeneity by primary tumor site might affect results, though no significant was observed in multivariable analysis, warranting further validation in larger cohorts. Finally, while no significant OS and DFS differences were observed across different PD-1 inhibitors in our study (Figure S1), variability in PD-1 inhibitors may have impacted survival benefits and definitive conclusions require additional comparative effectiveness. It is also important to note that it is a single-arm, retrospective analysis of a nonstandardized regimen. The choice of the specific chemoimmunotherapy combination was institutional, and its efficacy compared to standard options remains proven with multicenter, prospective clinical trial in the future.

In conclusion, our study demonstrated that combining PD-1 inhibitors to IC followed by CCRT significantly improved overall survival and reduced the risk of local recurrence and distant metastasis in HPV-negative LA-HNSCC patients, at the cost of nonsignificantly increased G3/4 AEs. However, PD-1 inhibitors administered concurrently with CCRT showed no additional clinical benefit. Prospectively multicenter trials are warranted to verify our findings, especially considering the likelihood of AEs and the cost-effectiveness.

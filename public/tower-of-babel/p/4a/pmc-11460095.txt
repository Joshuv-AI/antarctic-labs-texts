Two-year outcomes of intravitreal conbercept therapy for polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy
Authors: Yang Liu, Jingli Guo, Wenyi Tang, Gezhi Xu, Wei Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study aims to evaluate the two-year outcomes of polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy (PCV) treated with conbercept and to investigate the predictive response factors.MethodsConsecutive patients with PCV who received three-loading intravitreal conbercept, followed by as-needed reinjections, were studied retrospectively. The best corrected visual acuity (BCVA), central retinal thickness (CRT) and polyps were evaluated. Patients who achieved dry maculae in month 6 were categorised into the dry group, or otherwise, into the non-dry group. The predictive factors for a dry macula were evaluated.ResultsA total of 25 eyes from 25 patients (17 males; mean age: 62.8 ± 6.4 years) were included. At month 24, the average BCVA increased significantly from 49.9 ± 15.0 letters to 57.2 ± 16.0 letters (p = 0.040); the average CRT decreased significantly from 430.16 ± 166.55 μm to 278.31 ± 157.34 μm (p = 0.00), and 88% of the eyes achieved dry maculae. The number of polyps changed from 55 to 20 (fading rate: 63.6%; p < 0.001). The mean number of intravitreal injections was 8.6 ± 5.4. The dry group (10 eyes, 40%) was more likely to have higher branching vascular network vessel density (BVN VD; p = 0.021), submacular haemorrhages (p = 0.011) but lack polyp-related serous pigmented epithelial detachment (PED) (p = 0.037).ConclusionsConbercept was effective in eyes with PCV at maintaining functional and anatomical improvement. Baseline characteristics, including BVN VD, the presence of polyps with serous PED and submacular haemorrhage, seemed to be related to the response to conbercept.

Introduction

Polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy (PCV) is believed to be a variant of neovascular age-related macular degeneration (AMD), which is more prevalent among Asians. Characterised by polyps arising from branch vascular networks, the lesion lies mainly in the subretinal pigment epithelial space and resembles type I choroidal neovascularisation (CNV) [1]. The natural outcomes of PCV are unpredictable. Therefore, effective interventions to rescue the irreversible vision loss caused by recurrent pathologic development would be of great value [2].

Until now, there has been no consensus on the optimal treatment algorithms for PCV. Laser photocoagulation, photodynamic therapy (PDT), intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) injection and combinations of these treatments are all therapeutic options [2, 3]. Photodynamic therapy was first widely used in PCV treatment for its good performance in the resolution of polyps and the maintenance of visual acuity. However, the associated risks of recurrent haemorrhage, choroidal ischemia, retinal atrophy and secondary vision deterioration limited its adoption [4]. The monotherapy of anti-VEGF is now considered the first line of PCV therapy, while the treatment effects of different anti-VEGF agents are still in discussion and comparison. Ranibizumab or aflibercept monotherapy has been proven to promote visual acuity improvement and fluid resolution [5, 6]. However, their effects on polyp regression and branching vascular network (BVN) shrinkage are less desirable.

Conbercept is a genetically engineered fusion protein that is a member of the anti‑VEGF family. Similar to aflibercept, conbercept has a high affinity for all VEGF‑A isoforms. It also binds to VEGF-B and the placental growth factor, thereby increasing its antiangiogenetic potential [7]. In 2013, conbercept was approved by the China Food and Drug Administration, and its effects and safety in the treatment of nAMD were verified [8]. Considering PCV, published studies have conducted a 1-year performance comparison of conbercept in terms of therapeutic effects [9, 10]. In this study, we report the two-year outcomes of intravitreal conbercept therapy for PCV and focus on dry macula-related response factors.

Subjects and methods

This study was a retrospective case series analysis. Consecutive patients with naive and active PCV who received intravitreal injections of anti-VEGF (conbercept) were enrolled at the Eye and ENT Hospital of Fudan University, Shanghai, China, from 2019 to 2020. The protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board and conducted in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from each patient before they received treatment.

The diagnosis of PCV is based on the classic presentation of polyps on indocyanine green angiography (ICGA) and one or more of the following findings: BVNs, pulsatile polypoidal lesions, orange subretinal nodules, a hypofluorescent halo or association with massive submacular haemorrhage. The activity of PCV was based on the presence of leakages on fundus fluorescein angiography (FFA) and ICGA, as well as on subretinal and/or intraretinal fluid optical coherence tomography (OCT) imaging(2). The exclusion criteria included (1) lesions of submacular haemorrhage (≥ 4 PD), subretinal fibrosis or retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) tear in the affected eyes; (2) history of other ocular diseases, such as diabetic retinopathy, glaucoma or pathogenetic myopia; (3) history of intraocular surgery and intravitreal injection of anti-VEGF or corticosteroids; (4) diabetes mellitus; (5) severe media opacity; or (6) inability to tolerate the procedure of invasive angiography.

All eyes included were initially treated with three intravitreal injections of 0.5 mg conbercept (Chengdu Kanghong Biotech Co., Ltd., Sichuan, China), followed by additional injections according to the following re-treatment criteria: (1) best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) loss for more than five letters; (2) new occurrence, recurrence or persistent subretinal fluid (SRF) or intraretinal fluid (IRF) on OCT; (3) a central retinal thickness (CRT) increase by more than 100 μm (4); new occurrence of subretinal/ intraretinal haemorrhage; (5) leakage observed on angiography; and (6) active, new or increased lesion of polyps confirmed by ICGA [11].

The patients enrolled in this study were followed up for two years. The BCVA was assessed using the Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (EDTRS) LogMAR chart. The detailed ophthalmological examinations, included OCT (Heidelberg Engineering), FFA and ICGA (Heidelberg Engineering, Heidelberg, Germany), and swept-source OCT angiography (SS-OCTA, PLEX Elite 9000; Carl Zeiss Meditec, Dublin, CA). Follow-up visits were scheduled monthly in the first year and every 3 months in the second year. Additional visits were required if re-treatment was determined. Central retinal thickness and pigment epithelial detachment (PED) were measured on cross-sectional B scans using the Distance Measurement tool of the Spectralis system. The CRT was defined as the central foveal thickness. The maximum horizontal diameter of the PED was measured from its two edges at the base of the retinal pigmentary epithelium. The maximum vertical height measured the distance from the apex of the PED to Bruch’s membrane. Polyps and BVNs were visualised using the manufacturer’s built-in software, Heidelberg ICGA and SS-OCTA. Polypoidal areas were measured using the ICGA built-in software, and polyp regression was assessed using ICGA imaging as follows: (1) complete regression: no polyp observed on the imaging; (2) partial regression: ≥ 10% decrease in the polyp area compared with the baseline; (3) no change: < 10% increase in the polyp area compared with the baseline; and (4) increase: ≥ 10% increase in the polyp area compared with the baseline [12]. On SS-OCTA, a 3 × 3, 6 × 6 or 9 × 9 pattern was chosen to scan and record the vessel lesions. To visualise the clear margin of the BVN, the RPE and RPE fit segmentations were manually adjusted. Image J software (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, Maryland, USA) was used in the next image processing and vascular area or density quantitative analysis. In brief, each image was loaded and converted into 8 bits. The entire area containing PCV abnormal vessel structures was manually delineated using a selection tool. The area of the vessel lesion was expressed in squared millimetres based on the scale set for pixel distance and the known distance in millimetres. The vessel areas were obtained using binarisation of the mean threshold. Vessel density (VD) is the ratio of the area occupied by vessels divided by the total lesion area [13].

A dry macula was defined as a fluid-free macula without the presence of IRF, SRF, serous PED or active haemorrhages after consecutive anti-VEGF injections treatment. Exudative recurrence in this study was defined as the new appearance or reappearance of fluid after a previous visit with a dry macula. To analyse dry macula-related response factors, basic demographic information (age and gender), BCVA and baseline morphologic characteristics were collected and compared, including polyp areas, BVN areas, annual BVN area changes, BVN VD, presence of submacular haemorrhage (< 4PD) and presence of polyps adjacent to PED. Polyp-related PED was defined as a polyp connected closely to a PED and sharing the same boundary as the PED [14]. Patients were divided into the dry group, if they achieved dry maculae in the first six-month treatment and persisted during the rest whole follow-up period, or otherwise, into the non-dry group.

The data were presented as means ± standard deviations when they conformed to normal distributions; otherwise, they were presented as medians with ranges. The independent samples Student’s t-test and the Mann–Whitney test was used to evaluate the variables between the two groups. The paired-samples t-test and the related-samples Wilcoxon signed-rank test were applied to test the differences in one group between two visits. Categorical variables among different subgroups were tested with the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Spearman analysis was used to evaluate the potential correlation between BVN areas and VD, response and potential associated baseline characteristics. Values of p < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS for Windows version 22.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

A total of 25 eyes from 25 consecutive patients (17 men and 8 women; mean age: 62.8 ± 6.4 years) with naive and active PCV were included in the study. All eyes received initial monthly intravitreal conbercept for 3 months, followed by as-needed reinjections, with a mean intravitreal injection of 8.6 ± 5.4.

Visual acuity outcomes

At baseline, the average BCVA was 49.9 ± 15.0 letters. After three injections, the BCVA significantly improved to 55.3 ± 15.8 (p = 0.042). At month 6, the BCVA continued to improve to 59.3 ± 14.4 letters (p = 0.007). At months 12 and 24, the BCVA was 58.0 ± 16.5 and 57.2 ± 16.0 letters, respectively, which still showed enhancement (p = 0.029, 0.040). The changes in BCVA are shown in Fig. 1 (left). At month 12, the BCVA improved by a mean of 8.08 ± 17.39 letters, and at month 24, it improved by a mean of 7.24 ± 16.71 letters. The BCVA in 14 eyes (56.0%) improved ≥ 5 letters, with a mean gain of 18.3 letters, while the BCVA in 6 eyes (17.0%) was reduced ≥ 5 letters, with a mean loss of 15.7.

Anatomic outcomes

The average CRT at months 3, 6, 12 and 24 were 304.96 ± 15.84, 308.00 ± 165.21, 304.14 ± 158.10 and 278.31 ± 157.34 μm and decreased significantly to baseline, 430.16 ± 166.55 μm (p = 0.00, 0.00, 0.021 and 0.00). The changes in CRT are shown in Fig. 1 (right). A dry macula was achieved in 88% of the eyes at month 24 of the visit.

There were 55 polypoidal lesions in total at baseline. At month 3 of the visit, there were 38 polypoidal lesions (fading rate: 30.1%); five eyes (20.0%) showed complete polyp regression, seven (28%) showed partial regression, 11 (44.0%) showed no changes, and two (8.0%) had an increase in polyps. At month 6 of the visit, the polypoidal lesions decreased to 34 (fading rate: 38.2%); eight eyes (32.0%) showed complete polyp regression, seven (28%) showed partial regression, nine (36.0%) showed no changes, and one (4.0%) had an increase in polyps. At month 12 of the visit, the polypoidal lesions decreased to 12 (fading rate: 78.2%); 13 eyes (52.0%) showed complete polyp regression, two (8.0%) showed partial regression, seven (28.0%) showed no changes, and three (12.0%) had an increase in polyps. At month 24 of the visit, the polypoidal lesions changed to 20 (fading rate: 63.6%); 14 eyes (56.0%) showed complete polyp regression, one (4.0%) showed partial regression, five (20.0%) showed no changes, and five (20.0%) had an increase in polyps.

At baseline, PED was seen in 14 eyes (56%). At 3 to 12 months of the visit, 10 of 25 eyes (40%) still showed PED. Pigmented epithelial detachment was observed in only 6 eyes (24%) at the final visit, with a significant decrease in the median PED vertical diameter (details found in Table 1).

The median BVN lesion area was 1.75 µm2 (range: 0.26–7.62) at the first visit and 2.27 µm2 (range: 0.30–7.27) at month 24 (p = 0.001), suggesting a trend of BVN lesion enlargement during the entire follow-up period. The median BVN VD was 0.43 (range: 0.21–0.53) at the first visit and 0.45 (range: 0.16–0.55) at the last visit (p = 0.098). No changes in the VD of the BVN vessel were observed during the entire visit. Correlation analysis showed that there was no significant correlation between baseline BVN area and VD (p = 0.447), but there was a negative correlation between BVN area and VD (r = − 0.448, p = 0.025) at 24 months.

Dry macula-related sub-group analysis

Ten eyes (40%) achieved dry maculae in 6 months, while 15 eyes (60%) experienced persistent wet maculae. The dry group had fewer intravitreal injections during the follow-up (dry group vs. non-dry group, median IVI counts: 3.5 vs. 11; p < 0.001). The recurrence rate was also lower—only 10% (1/10) in the dry group compared with 40% (6/15) in the non-dry group. This result indicated that the dry group was more likely to gain resolution of the macular fluid with fewer injections and to remain relatively stable. No difference in the final BCVA was found between groups (dry group vs. non-dry group, 61.60 ± 16.23 vs. 54.20 ± 15.59; p = 0.843). The annual BVN area changes showed no differences during the first year between groups (dry group vs. non-dry group, median BVN area changes: 0.10 mm2 vs. 0.04 mm2; p = 0.08), while the non-dry group experienced more growth in BVN lesions from month 12 to the final visit (dry group vs. non-dry group, median BVN area changes: 0.07 mm2 vs. 0.3 mm2; p = 0.024).

The baseline characteristics of the two groups are summarised in Table 2. No differences were found between the two groups in terms of age, gender, baseline BCVA, polyp areas and BVN areas (p > 0.05). The baseline BVN VD presented a difference; the dry group had a higher BVN VD (dry group vs. non-dry group, median BVN VD: 0.48 vs. 0.41; p = 0.021). Differences in dry macula-related morphological characteristics were evaluated, and submacular haemorrhages (Fig. 2) and polyps with serous PED (Fig. 3) showed different distributions between groups. In the dry group, five eyes (50.0%) presented with submacular haemorrhages and none presented with polyps with PED (0.0%) at the first visit. In the non-dry group, submacular haemorrhages were noted in none (0.0%) of the patients, and polyps with serous PED were noted in seven eyes (46.7%). The proportion of submacular haemorrhages was significantly greater (p = 0.011), and that of polyps with PED was significantly lower (p = 0.037) in the dry group than in the non-dry group. The results of the correlation analysis revealed that BVN VD (r = − 0.472, p = 0.017), submacular haemorrhage (r = − 0.612, p = 0.001) and polyps with serous PED (r = 0.509, p = 0.009) were associated with the response to conbercept.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first study to report the 24-month outcomes of conbercept treatment of PCV. A total of 25 eyes received 0.5 mg conbercept, with initial monthly intravitreal conbercept for 3 months, followed by an as-needed reinjection regimen. The improvement in BCVA first became evident at month 3 and reached its peak at month 6, and the visual benefits were also observed throughout the subsequent follow-up for 24 months. In previous 1-year reports of PCV patients treated with conbercept, visual improvement occurred early before month 1 and can be sustained up to month 12 [9, 10, 15]. In our study, the BCVA at month 12 improved with a gain of 8.08 letters, which is close to that in Ye’s report, which is a gain of 9.04 letters [9]. Ranibizumab or aflibercept mono-injection has been shown to maintain or improve the visual outcomes in PCV eyes. However, Hata reported that the improvement with ranibizumab using the 3 + PRN therapy for PCV did not persist at 2 years post-treatment [16]. The mean number of injections in their study was 6.66 ± 4.23 in PCV, which was lower than ours at 8.6 ± 5.4. Inoue also reported no significant improvement in BCVA in the 2-year results of intravitreal aflibercept injections using the 3 + PRN regimen [17]. They speculated that the less desirable visual gains in the PRN regimen might be due to possible undertreatment with fewer injections. In month 24, our outcomes showed visual benefits compared with the baseline visit, although not comparable to the peak value at month 6. These findings suggest that conbercept monotherapy is effective at maintaining visual acuity enhancement. Moreover, at the final visit, the average CRT decreased significantly compared to the baseline, and a dry macula was achieved in 88% of the eyes; PED was only shown in 24% of the eyes. This implied that conbercept also facilitated the promotion of fluid absorption and PED regression in PCV.

Polypoidal lesions are the leading morphological features of PCV and are associated with visual prognosis and exudative recurrence. Polyp regression is one of the key end points for the management of PCV. Studies have mainly reported a 1-year rate in the complete regression of polyps using conbercept. In Ye’s study, 20% of the eyes showed complete polyp regression after the initial three injections, and the rate increased to 63.5% at month 12 [9]. Cheng’s study reported a complete polyp regression rate of 46.5% and 51.7% at months 3 and 12, respectively [18]. Qi et al. evaluated the real-life clinical outcomes of the intravitreal injection of conbercept for PCV; 41.1% of the eyes showed complete polyp regression at month 3. Patients with partial polyp regression and BCVA ≥ 70 letters received additional injections. At month 12, 27 of 58 eyes (46.6%) with mono-injection of conbercept showed complete polyp regression [10]. The classic clinical trial PLANET and EVEREST provided a 12-month complete regression rate of aflibercept and ranibizumab, which was 38.9% and 33.8%, respectively [5, 6]. Inoue et al. retrospectively studied 43 eyes with treatment-naive PCV using ranibizumab injection; 60.6% of the eyes showed complete resolution of polypoidal lesions [19]. In our work, 20% of the eyes showed complete regression of polyps after three loading injections; the rate increased to 52% at month 12 and continued to increase to 56% at month 24. Our three-month results were not as high as Ye’s but were better than those of other studies using conbercept. The complete resolution rate increased with time, which was comparable to the results obtained using aflibercept and ranibizumab.

We followed up and compared the differences in BVN area and VD between baseline and 24 months, and found that BVN area gradually increased, which is consistent with Bo’s report [20]. There is no literature reporting the results of BVN VD, but our results suggested that there was little change in BVN VD. In the correlation analysis, the area of BVN at baseline was independent of vessel density; But in the 24-month, there was a negative correlation between the area of BVN and VD, that is, the larger the BVN area, the smaller the vessel density. This can be understood as BVN gradually becoming larger and more mature with treatment. As a microvascular perfusion biomarker, VD may help differentiate an active from a quiescent lesion [21]. Therefore, the negative correlation between BVN area and VD at 24 months, to some extent, reflected the response of BVN lesions to anti-VEGF.

In this study, 10 eyes (40%, dry group) achieved dry maculae at 6 months and had fewer injections, while the 15 other eyes (60%, non-dry group) experienced persistent exudations, requiring more injections. Different baseline characteristics between the two groups were found, including BVN VD, submacular haemorrhage and polyp-related serous PED.

The baseline BVN VD in the dry group was higher than that in the non-dry group. Vessel density is defined as the percentage of the vascular network area occupied by blood vessels; a higher VD means denser-occupied vascular ingredients per unit area. In Coscas’ work, the active group had a higher VD value than the remission group in wet AMD. Values of VD > 0.50 implied greater odds of active CNV [22]. The BVN components of PCV lesions have been gradually recognised to be neovascularization (NV) in nature [23]. In our work, eyes with higher baseline BVN VDs can achieve faster and more complete resolution of exudative lesions, subsequently with fewer anti-VEGF injections. We speculated that the fluid generated in eyes with higher VDs tended to rely more on the effects of VEGF and was more sensitive to anti-VEGF therapy. Furthermore, our data showed no significant changes in BVN VD compared with the baseline at 24 months, a result consistent with that in the study by Forte [20]. We also evaluated the BVN lesion area, which was thought to be associated with lesion activity; however, there were no differences in the baseline BVN area between groups. The BVN lesion experienced growth in both groups in the first and second years, but it grew faster in the non-dry group in the second year. The faster enlargement implied a worse response to injections and a higher risk of recurrence [20].

In the non-dry group, nearly half of the eyes (46.6%) presented with polypoidal lesions adjacent to serous PED. We believe that eyes manifesting this condition may be more refractory to anti-VEGF. A similar phenomenon was also found in cases of NV with serous PED [24]. In the anti-VEGF treatment of NV with PED, different mechanisms are involved in its collapse. Early-phase treatment causes a VEGF-mediated reduction in leakage and the subsequent resorption of fluid; with continued injections, the effect of profibrotic cytokines may promote maturation of the vascular lesion and evolution into a chronic multilayered fibrovascular form. The persistent serous PED may harbour NV that is more resistant to fibrotic maturation [25, 26]. In our study, the polypoidal lesion evolved into a loose, tangled vessel structure. The connected PED did not mature to a less activated fibrovascular form; once, it almost regressed, but it recurred and grew with the appearance of a changed polypoidal vascular lesion. We speculated that the maturation procedure in polypoidal vascular lesions connected with serous PED is also a difficult procedure similar to the classic CNV with serous PED; furthermore, the dimension of PED may be associated with the activity of connected vascular lesions, and the activity changed when the polypoidal lesion experienced evolution. A similar morphological evolution of polyps adjacent to PED has been reported, and this morphological appearance has been proposed to be associated with a better prognosis [14]. The fact that polyps with serous PED eyes were more likely to persist and required more injections during the follow-up period indicated that eyes with this condition may be a more resistant subset. Active follow-up and positive psychological preparation may be more beneficial for these patients to cope with lengthy and tortuous treatment processes.

Massive submacular haemorrhage has been reported to have a negative influence on the retina and to have a poor prognosis in PCV [27]. In our study, eyes with small submacular haemorrhages (< 4PD) seemed to regress after several injections and remained stable. Five eyes (50%) in the dry group presented with baseline submacular haemorrhages; they regressed in six injections, and the lesions remained quiescent throughout the rest of the follow-up period. Beak et al. also found that submacular haemorrhages were correlated with a low risk of PCV reactivation after anti-VEGF therapy and that the injection frequency diminished after hemorrhage [28]. In PCV, a major polyp is more likely to be an origin of the submacular haemorrhage. A rupture of polyps may partly resolve doubts, as the polypoidal lesion is assumed to be recurring, refractory, and requiring more treatment. Moreover, submacular haemorrhage may change the submacular micro-environment surrounding the abnormal vascular lesion, impeding the NV’s further progression [29].

A strength of this study is its relatively long follow-up period. We also used both ICGA and OCTA, which made the imaging of the vascular lesion structure more accurate and reliable. However, the inherent retrospective nature of the study and its small sample size impeded further verification of the positive response factors. We only evaluated the response factor to conbercept with an initial monthly intravitreal conbercept for 3 months, followed by an as-needed reinjection regimen, which may restrict its application in other anti-VEGF agents or different regimes. It is more promising if we could recruit patients treated with ranibizumab and aflibercept or those managed with treat-and-extend regimens to prevent bias. In addition, we speculated that BVN VD was associated with BVN activity, so we measured its value. More studies are required to elaborate on the significance of VD in BVN and to explore other quantitative biomarkers, such as fractal dimension and lacunarity, in order to predict BVN status.

Conclusion

Intravitreal injection of conbercept was effective in maintaining functional and anatomical improvement in eyes with PCV at 24 months’ follow-up. The baseline BVN VD, the presence of polyps connected with serous PED and submacular haemorrhage (< 4PD) seemed to be related to the response to conbercept. This finding may help schedule appropriate visits ahead of time for certain patients.

Development and validation of a prediction model for atrial fibrillation recurrence after radiofrequency ablation: integrating echocardiographic and clinical indicators
Authors: Deng Congying, Ye Muqi, Wen Dinghua, Guo Sheng, Li Chengcheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRadiofrequency ablation (RFA) is an established treatment for symptomatic atrial fibrillation (AF), yet post-ablation recurrence remains a significant clinical challenge. This study aimed to develop and internally validate a prediction model for post-RFA AF recurrence by integrating echocardiographic parameters and clinical indicators, and to explore potential nonlinear associations of key indices for risk stratification.MethodsA total of 532 consecutive patients who underwent first-time RFA for AF between January 2022 and October 2024 were retrospectively enrolled. The database was locked on October 31, 2025, ensuring a complete 12-month follow-up for all patients. Post-ablation recurrence was defined as any documented AF episode lasting ≥ 30 s on electrocardiogram (ECG) or 24-hour Holter monitoring, occurring after a 3-month blanking period. Independent predictors were identified by forward stepwise multivariable logistic regression (α-in = 0.05, α-out = 0.10), and a nomogram was constructed for model visualization. Internal validation was performed using 1000 bootstrap resamples. Model performance was assessed by the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), calibration plots, decision curve analysis (DCA), and clinical impact curves (CIC). Restricted cubic spline (RCS) analysis with 3 quantile knots was used to explore the non-linear associations between left atrial diameter (LAD), left atrial sphericity index (LASI) and post-ablation recurrence risk. Inter- and intra-observer consistency of echocardiographic measurements was verified by intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC).ResultsLAD, LASI (per 0.1 unit increase), AF duration category, and CHA₂DS₂-VASc score were identified as independent predictors of post-RFA AF recurrence (CHA₂DS₂-VASc, LAD, AF duration: all P < 0.001; LASI: P = 0.001). The model equation was: logit(p) = -9.093 + 0.281 × (CHA₂DS₂-VASc score) + 0.081 × (LAD) + 0.569 × (LASI, per 0.1 unit) + Σβk × (AF duration group). The model exhibited moderate discriminatory ability (AUC = 0.752; 95% CI: 0.708–0.796). DCA showed higher net benefit than the treat-all and treat-none strategies across threshold probabilities of 0.10–0.60, and CIC indicated that at thresholds between 0.10 and 0.30 the model identified approximately 250–330 true positive cases per 1000 patients. At the Youden‑based optimal cut-off (0.35), PPV and NPV were 48.2% and 82.5%. RCS analysis revealed a nonlinear threshold effect for LAD, with recurrence risk increasing sharply above 50 mm (inter-observer ICC = 0.92; intra-observer ICC = 0.95). For LASI, the association was predominantly linear, with a visual bend near 0.78 that lacked statistical support (nonlinearity P = 0.9516; inter-observer ICC = 0.90; intra-observer ICC = 0.93).ConclusionsThis internally validated prediction model, integrating LAD, LASI, AF duration category, and CHA₂DS₂-VASc score, demonstrated moderate discrimination and adequate calibration, with higher net benefit than default strategies on decision curve analysis. LAD showed an exploratory nonlinear inflection near 50 mm, whereas LASI showed a predominantly linear association; the bend near 0.78 is only a hypothesis-generating observation. These findings require rigorous external validation before any clinical application. The nomogram offers a tool for research-oriented risk estimation, pending multicenter confirmation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12872-026-06126-8.

Background

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is one of the most common progressive cardiac arrhythmias worldwide, with a steadily rising incidence that imposes a substantial public health burden [1]. Radiofrequency ablation (RFA) has become a first-line interventional treatment for selected patients with symptomatic AF, as it can effectively restore sinus rhythm, alleviate clinical symptoms, and improve patients’ quality of life [2]. However, postoperative recurrence remains a major challenge in clinical practice, with reported recurrence rates ranging from 20% to 40% after ablation [3]. Recurrence not only escalates healthcare costs but also heightens the risk of adverse cardiovascular events, including stroke, heart failure, and sudden cardiac death [4].

Identifying high-risk patients is crucial for improving long-term RFA efficacy. Although clinical indicators and echocardiographic parameters are closely associated with post-ablation recurrence, most existing studies have explored these predictors in isolation [5, 6]. Left atrial structural remodeling, captured by indices such as LAD and LASI, creates an arrhythmogenic substrate. Comorbidities reflected by the CHA₂DS₂-VASc score accelerate this process through inflammation and oxidative stress. However, most existing studies have explored the predictive value of these indicators in isolation rather than in a comprehensive combination, leading to limited clinical predictive efficacy [5, 6].

Although several prediction models for post-ablation AF recurrence have been developed, most of them either focus on a single type of indicator (e.g., only clinical factors or only echocardiographic parameters) or lack rigorous internal and external validation, thus limiting their clinical application [7, 8]. Therefore, there is an urgent need for a comprehensive prediction model that integrates routine clinical and echocardiographic indicators with modest discriminatory ability and stability. This study aimed to develop and internally validate a prediction model for post-RFA AF recurrence by integrating two key echocardiographic parameters (LAD, LASI) and two clinical indicators (CHA₂DS₂-VASc score, AF duration category). Furthermore, we visualized the model using a nomogram and explored potential nonlinear relationships of LAD and LASI, providing a practical tool for individualized risk assessment, pending external validation.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

This retrospective study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Zhongshan People’s Hospital (approval number: 2026-035) and was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (2013 revision). The requirement for written informed consent was waived by the IRB due to the retrospective nature of the study, the anonymity of all patient data, and the non-interventional nature of the study. All patient information was de-identified prior to analysis.

Study population

A total of 532 consecutive patients with AF who underwent first-time RFA between January 2022 and October 2024 were retrospectively included. The study database was locked on October 31, 2025, after which all follow-up data were fully collected and extracted. All 532 patients in the final analytic cohort completed the 12-month follow-up before the database lock, ensuring a fixed observation window with no censoring. The AF diagnosis was confirmed in accordance with the 2024 ESC Guidelines for the Management of Atrial Fibrillation [9]. The exclusion criteria were: (1) previous cardiac surgery or ablation history (i.e., patients who had undergone any prior catheter ablation for AF were excluded, ensuring a first-ablation cohort); (2) severe valvular heart disease requiring surgical intervention (including severe mitral stenosis); (3) severe hepatic or renal dysfunction (serum creatinine > 265 µmol/L, alanine aminotransferase > 3 times the upper limit of normal); (4) malignant tumors or other life-threatening diseases (expected survival < 6 months); (5) loss to follow-up before completing the 12-month follow-up. Patients with incomplete echocardiographic or laboratory data (n = 13) were also excluded during the screening process as they did not fulfill the inclusion criteria. After excluding 30 patients who did not meet the eligibility criteria (detailed in Fig. 1), 532 patients formed the final analytic cohort. Among the excluded patients with available data, baseline characteristics did not differ significantly from those of the included patients (all P > 0.05).

Post-ablation recurrence was defined as the detection of AF episodes (duration ≥ 30 s) confirmed by electrocardiogram (ECG) or 24-hour ambulatory ECG during the follow-up period after a 3-month post-ablation blanking period [10]. Any AF episodes detected during the blanking period were deemed transient and excluded from the definition of clinical recurrence.

Radiofrequency ablation procedure and perioperative management

All patients underwent circumferential pulmonary vein isolation (PVI) as the uniform radiofrequency ablation strategy. Procedures were performed using the CARTO 3 three-dimensional electroanatomical mapping system (Biosense Webster, Diamond Bar, CA, USA) with a contact-force sensing irrigated ablation catheter (STSF, Biosense Webster). Radiofrequency energy was delivered at 45 W with an irrigation flow rate of 30 mL/min. All procedures were performed by two experienced operators who had each completed more than 500 AF ablation procedures prior to the study period. In patients with inducible atrial fibrillation or atrial tachycardia after PVI (tested by burst pacing or adenosine triphosphate provocation), additional linear lesions or substrate modification were performed at the operator’s discretion. Electrical cardioversion was applied when sinus rhythm could not be restored by ablation alone. Periprocedural anticoagulation followed a standardized protocol: therapeutic anticoagulation for ≥ 3 weeks before ablation or transesophageal echocardiography to exclude left atrial thrombus, intraprocedural intravenous heparin adjusted to target activated clotting time, and post-ablation oral anticoagulation for 8–12 weeks, with long-term continuation based on CHA₂DS₂-VASc score. A uniform antiarrhythmic drug protocol was followed: previously ineffective drugs were continued during the 3-month blanking period and then discontinued. All patients underwent follow-up at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months after RFA. Post-ablation monitoring included 12-lead ECG at each follow-up visit and 24-hour Holter at 3, 6, and 12 months.

Data collection

Clinical and laboratory data

Clinical data were collected from electronic medical records, including general information (gender, age, height, weight, body mass index [BMI], body surface area [BSA]), AF-related indicators (AF type, AF duration, average ventricular rate), medical history (smoking history, drinking history, hypertension, coronary heart disease, diabetes mellitus, stroke, heart failure [NYHA classification]), intraoperative indicators (operation duration, intraoperative heparin dose, intraoperative mechanical termination), and medication history (anticoagulant drugs). Laboratory indicators were collected within 24 h before RFA, including routine blood test, coagulation function, myocardial enzymes, and other biochemical indicators (brain natriuretic peptide [BNP], procalcitonin [PCT], renal function indicators). All laboratory tests were completed in the central laboratory of Zhongshan People’s Hospital with standardized detection methods.

Echocardiographic parameters

Echocardiographic examinations were performed using a Philips EPIQ 7 C ultrasound system by two experienced echocardiographers (each > 10 years’ experience) blinded to clinical outcomes. Inter- and intra-observer reproducibility for LAD and LASI, assessed by intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), showed good agreement (all ICCs > 0.90). Measurements followed European Association of Cardiovascular Imaging recommendations [11], and the average of three consecutive measurements was used for analysis.

The measured parameters included interventricular septum (IVS) thickness, left ventricular posterior wall (LVPW) thickness, left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD), left ventricular end-systolic diameter (LVESD), left ventricular fractional shortening (FS), left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), aortic root diameter, LAD (measured in the parasternal long-axis view at end-systole), right ventricular diameter (RVD), LASI (calculated as the ratio of left atrial anteroposterior diameter [parasternal long-axis view] to left atrial transverse diameter [apical four-chamber view]), and E/A ratio [12].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0 and R software (version 4.3.1, packages: rms, pROC, ggplot2). Statistical significance was defined as a two-tailed P value < 0.05.

Descriptive statistics

Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range [IQR]) based on the results of Kolmogorov-Smirnov normality tests. Categorical variables were presented as numbers (percentages).

Univariate analysis

Differences in variables between the recurrence group and the non-recurrence group were compared using independent samples t-test (normally distributed continuous variables), Mann-Whitney U test (non-normally distributed continuous variables), χ² test (categorical variables with large sample size), or Fisher’s exact test (categorical variables with small sample size). Variables with a P value < 0.05 in univariate analysis were selected as candidate variables for subsequent multivariable analysis.

Multivariable analysis

Forward stepwise multivariable logistic regression analysis (α-in = 0.05, α-out = 0.10) was used to identify independent predictors of post-ablation recurrence. With 176 events and 4 predictors, the events-per-variable ratio was 44, exceeding the recommended minimum of 10 to minimize overfitting. The final prediction model equation was derived based on the regression coefficients. For interpretability, LASI was rescaled to increments of 0.1 unit prior to model fitting (i.e., the original LASI value was multiplied by 10), so that the regression coefficient represents the effect per 0.1-unit increase. Multicollinearity was assessed using variance inflation factors (VIF), with values < 5 considered acceptable.

Candidate variable entry and collinearity assessment

Simultaneous inclusion of individual CHA₂DS₂-VASc components (age, diabetes, prior stroke, heart failure) and the composite score may lead to conceptual overlap. These components were included in the candidate variable pool due to significant findings in univariable analysis, with no prior specification of which variables would be retained in the final model. Stepwise selection retained only the composite score and eliminated all individual components. Variance inflation factors for all variables in the final model were ≤ 1.04, indicating minimal collinearity. Future studies should prespecify either the CHA₂DS₂-VASc composite score or its individual components for model construction, rather than incorporating both.

Model visualization

A nomogram was constructed using the rms package in R software to visualize the model, with each variable assigned a score proportional to its regression coefficient, and the total score corresponding to the predicted recurrence probability.

Internal validation

1000 bootstrap resamples were used to assess model overfitting and stability. The optimism-corrected AUC was calculated to evaluate the discriminatory ability of the model.

Model performance evaluation

The model’s discriminatory ability was evaluated by ROC curve, with the optimal cut-off value determined by the Youden index. Calibration curve was drawn to assess agreement between predicted and observed recurrence probabilities, and the calibration slope was calculated. Decision curve analysis (DCA) and clinical impact curve (CIC) were used to evaluate clinical utility. Positive predictive value (PPV) and negative predictive value (NPV) were also calculated.

Non-linear association analysis

Restricted cubic spline (RCS) analysis with 3 quantile knots (25th, 50th, 75th) was performed using the rms package in R software to explore the non-linear relationship between key echocardiographic parameters (LAD and LASI) and recurrence risk. AIC and BIC values were used to evaluate the fitting effect, with lower values indicating a better fit of the non-linear model.

Sensitivity analysis

To address ongoing debate regarding the sex category component of the CHA₂DS₂-VASc, a sensitivity analysis was conducted using the CHA₂DS₂-VA, which excludes the sex component. The predictive performance of this modified model was evaluated by ROC curve analysis and compared with the original model to assess robustness.

While forward stepwise regression was employed for variable selection, we acknowledge its potential limitations, including model instability and selection bias. Nevertheless, our analysis adhered to recommended practices: the events-per-variable ratio was 44 (well above 10), the model was internally validated using 1000 bootstrap resamples, and the optimism-corrected AUC (0.742) indicated minimal overfitting. Therefore, the model should be interpreted as an internally derived and validated exploratory tool; its generalizability requires confirmation in external cohorts.

This study followed the TRIPOD statement for model development and internal validation. Missing data were handled by complete-case analysis. Thirteen patients (2.3% of 562) were excluded for incomplete echocardiographic (n = 6) or laboratory data (n = 7) (Fig. 1), primarily due to suboptimal image quality or unavailable laboratory measurements. Excluded and included patients had comparable baseline characteristics (all P > 0.05), making substantial selection bias unlikely, although non-random missingness cannot be entirely ruled out.

Compared with the non-recurrence group, patients in the recurrence group had significant clinical differences: older age (68.9 ± 8.6 vs. 64.6 ± 9.5 years, P = 0.001), higher BMI (25.6 ± 3.3 vs. 24.8 ± 3.0, P = 0.006), and a higher prevalence of coronary heart disease (22.2% vs. 15.2%, P = 0.046), diabetes mellitus (26.7% vs. 18.5%, P = 0.030), stroke (9.1% vs. 3.9%, P = 0.015), and heart failure (23.3% vs. 11.5%, P = 0.008). In addition, the recurrence group had longer AF duration, higher CHA₂DS₂-VASc scores, and abnormal laboratory indicators (elevated BNP, PCT, D‑dimer, RDW-CV, and PT; all P < 0.05). Echocardiographic parameters showed that the recurrence group had larger LAD, higher LASI, larger LAD index, and lower FS and LVEF (all P < 0.05; Table 1), indicating more advanced left atrial structural and functional remodeling.

Independent predictors of post-ablation recurrence

A total of 18 variables with P < 0.05 in univariate analysis were included as candidate variables for multivariable logistic regression analysis (age, BMI, coronary heart disease, diabetes mellitus, stroke, heart failure, CHA₂DS₂-VASc score, AF duration category, RDW-CV, PT, D-dimer, BNP, PCT, FS, LVEF, LAD, LASI, LAD index).

After forward stepwise screening (α-in = 0.05, α-out = 0.10), LAD, LASI (per 0.1 unit increase), AF duration category, and CHA₂DS₂-VASc score remained as independent predictors of post-RFA AF recurrence (all P ≤ 0.001, Table 2). All VIF values in the final model were close to 1.0 (CHA₂DS₂-VASc: 1.036; LAD: 1.026; LASI: 1.009; AF duration category: 1.001), confirming negligible multicollinearity among the four independent predictors. Model performance validation

Discriminatory ability

The full combined model demonstrated modest discriminatory ability for post-RFA AF recurrence, with an apparent AUC of 0.752 (95% CI 0.708–0.796, Fig. 4). After 1000 bootstrap resamples, the optimism-corrected AUC was 0.742 (95% CI 0.688–0.796), with minimal optimism (0.010), indicating stable performance with negligible overfitting.

Sensitivity analysis using CHA₂DS₂-VA score

To examine the potential influence of the sex category component, the CHA₂DS₂-VASc score was replaced with the CHA₂DS₂-VA score in the prediction model. The modified model demonstrated similar discriminatory ability, with an AUC of 0.737 (95% CI: 0.690–0.784), compared with the original model AUC of 0.752. These results indicate that the sex component contributed minimally to the model’s predictive performance, and that the overall predictive value is primarily driven by the comorbidity burden.

Clinical utility

DCA showed that the model yielded a higher net benefit than the “treat-all” and “treat-none” strategies across a threshold probability range of 0.10–0.60 (Fig. 6), suggesting potential value for exploratory risk stratification. The CIC demonstrated that at threshold probabilities between 0.10 and 0.30, the model identified approximately 250–330 true positive cases per 1000 patients, declining as the threshold increased (Fig. 7). The Youden-based optimal cut-off (0.35) falls within the broader threshold range (0.10–0.60) where the model showed higher net benefit than default strategies on DCA. PPV-TPR curves showed that PPV increased with the threshold probability (reaching approximately 48% at the Youden cut-off of 0.35), while TPR gradually declined, reflecting the inherent trade-off between ruling in and ruling out recurrence (Fig. 8).

Discussion

This study developed and internally validated a prediction model for post-RFA AF recurrence integrating two echocardiographic parameters (LAD, LASI) and two clinical indicators (CHA₂DS₂-VASc score, AF duration category). We explored potential nonlinear associations and constructed a nomogram for research-oriented risk estimation.

Predictive value of key indicators

LAD demonstrated a nonlinear association with recurrence, with an exploratory inflection point near 50 mm beyond which risk rose sharply. This aligns with evidence linking marked left atrial enlargement to advanced fibrosis and electrical remodeling [13, 14].

LASI captures left atrial geometric remodeling not reflected by LAD alone [11], altering wall stress and electrical conduction to promote fibrosis and recurrence [6, 15]. A visual bend near 0.78 was observed, but nonlinearity testing was not significant (P = 0.9516), consistent with a predominantly linear association. This candidate point is hypothesis-generating. Together, LAD and LASI provide complementary information on morphological and geometric remodeling [6].

The CHA₂DS₂-VASc score, originally developed for stroke risk stratification, reflects the cumulative burden of cardiovascular comorbidities associated with atrial remodeling [16]. However, the inclusion of the sex category component has been increasingly debated in predictive modeling, as female sex alone may not independently influence recurrence risk. To explore this, we conducted a sensitivity analysis using the CHA₂DS₂-VA score (excluding the sex component). The modified model showed comparable predictive performance (AUC 0.737 vs. 0.752), supporting the robustness of the model. Nevertheless, potential sex-specific effects were not fully investigated and warrant further evaluation in multicenter cohorts.

The components of the score contribute to atrial remodeling through complementary pathways–including pressure overload, neurohormonal activation, microvascular injury, oxidative stress, and cardiomyocyte aging–that collectively create a proarrhythmogenic substrate [14, 17]. The 31.4% elevation in recurrence risk per 1-point increment underscores that comorbidity severity is a key determinant of RFA efficacy and highlights the importance of optimized comorbidity management.

AF duration showed a dose-dependent association with recurrence risk, consistent with progressive atrial remodeling induced by prolonged AF burden [18]. The markedly elevated risk in the > 9 years subgroup (OR = 11.394) should be interpreted cautiously given the small sample size (n = 28). Stratification by AF duration provides a quantitative basis for assessing disease chronicity in recurrence risk stratification.

Clinical utility

A practical strength of this model is its feasibility: all included indicators–LAD, LASI, CHA₂DS₂-VASc score, and AF duration–are routinely obtainable through standard transthoracic echocardiography and medical history review, without the need for specialized examinations or invasive tests. This makes the model potentially applicable across diverse healthcare settings, including resource-limited institutions. The nomogram provides a straightforward tool for research-oriented risk estimation.

This model is a supplementary tool, not a standalone decision instrument. For instance, it may help identify patients for closer post-ablation monitoring (e.g., more frequent Holter or continuous monitoring) or intensive management of risk factors like hypertension and obesity. Patients with low predicted risk may be managed with standard follow-up. These potential applications require prospective validation. The model’s moderate discrimination (AUC = 0.75) and unvalidated inflection points preclude its use for definitive clinical decisions.

The exploratory inflection points identified for LAD and LASI are hypothesis-generating. Whether patients with values beyond these points would benefit from intensified ablation strategies, prolonged rhythm monitoring, or tailored antiarrhythmic drug maintenance requires prospective evaluation [19]. These findings should not currently be used to alter patient management.

Comparison with previous studies

Our study adds new insights to the existing literature in four key ways. First, by integrating routine echocardiographic and clinical indicators, we constructed a comprehensive model (AUC = 0.752), confirming that multi-dimensional integration improves predictive efficacy over single-class models. Second, we identified an exploratory nonlinear inflection for LAD (≈ 50 mm) and observed a predominantly linear association for LASI, with a candidate visual bend near 0.78 that has not been reported previously. Third, we constructed a visualized nomogram to support research-oriented risk estimation, whereas most prior models only present regression coefficients without practical tools. Fourth, we performed rigorous internal validation using bootstrap resampling, DCA, and CIC to confirm the model’s stability and decision-analytic net benefit [20].

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the single-center retrospective design limits external validity. Differences in demographics, comorbidity profiles, AF chronicity, healthcare access, and ablation protocols may all affect generalizability. Therefore, the model requires validation in multicenter and multi-ethnic cohorts. Second, although the proportion of patients excluded for incomplete data was small, the complete-case approach may still introduce bias if missingness was non-random. Third, recurrence detection relied on standard ECG and 24-hour Holter monitoring, which may miss asymptomatic AF episodes. This outcome misclassification likely underestimates the true recurrence rate and overestimates the negative predictive value (82.5%), as some patients classified as recurrence-free may have had undetected silent AF. Consequently, the model’s rule-out capability should be interpreted cautiously. Non-differential misclassification also tends to bias predictor-outcome associations toward the null, suggesting that the true effects of the identified predictors may be stronger than estimated. Future studies using continuous or extended rhythm monitoring (e.g., 7-day Holter, implantable loop recorders, or device-based AF burden measurement) are needed to evaluate model performance against true AF burden and enable more accurate outcome ascertainment. Fourth, the 12-month follow-up only evaluates the short-term predictive value of the model, and extended follow-up (2–5 years) is required to assess its long-term performance. Fifth, forward stepwise logistic regression may introduce model instability, as variable selection can vary with small data perturbations. Although this was mitigated by a high events-per-variable ratio (44) and bootstrap validation (optimism = 0.010), the selected predictors remain exploratory and require external validation. Sixth, the model only includes four independent predictors, and potential indicators (e.g., atrial fibrosis markers, inflammatory factors, genetic indicators) were not incorporated due to retrospective design limitations; integrating multi-omics and imaging biomarkers in future studies may further optimize the model. Seventh, the small sample size of the AF duration > 9 years subgroup may overestimate its effect size, and this result needs verification in large cohorts. Eighth, although sensitivity analysis using the CHA₂DS₂-VA score showed consistent results, the potential influence of sex-specific effects on recurrence risk was not fully explored, which may warrant further investigation in larger cohorts. Finally, the clinical benefit of model-based risk-stratified intervention strategies has not been validated, and prospective interventional studies are needed to confirm whether individualized management can reduce post-RFA recurrence rates [21].

Future directions

Future research should prioritize three directions. First, multicenter prospective studies are needed to confirm the model’s external validity and long-term predictive performance across diverse populations. Second, prospective interventional studies should evaluate whether model-guided, risk-stratified management strategies can reduce post-ablation AF recurrence rates. Third, integration of additional predictors–such as atrial fibrosis markers, inflammatory biomarkers, or left atrial strain–may further optimize predictive performance. In addition, future studies may benefit from employing penalized regression techniques (e.g., LASSO) to identify more parsimonious and robust predictor subsets for external validation.

Conclusion

This internally derived and validated prediction model, incorporating LAD, LASI, AF duration category, and the CHA₂DS₂-VASc score, exhibited modest discrimination and adequate calibration in our single-center cohort (AUC = 0.752, calibration slope = 0.98). Multicollinearity was absent among candidate predictors (all variance inflation factors ≤ 1.04). LAD showed an exploratory nonlinear inflection near 50 mm, whereas LASI showed a predominantly linear association with recurrence; the value around 0.78 should be regarded only as a hypothesis-generating visual reference. However, clinical implementation of these thresholds and the nomogram requires rigorous external validation across diverse populations, as well as prospective evaluation of model-guided risk-stratified management strategies. The proposed nomogram provides an internally validated model with moderate discrimination for risk estimation in a research setting, based on routinely available echocardiographic and clinical variables. Future multicenter prospective studies are warranted to confirm external validity, long-term prognostic performance, and real-world clinical benefit before routine clinical adoption.

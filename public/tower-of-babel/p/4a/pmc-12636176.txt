Clinical characteristics and predictor analysis of pediatric Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia with small segment molded sputum plugs: a retrospective study
Authors: Baozhou Hong, Chunfang Li, Sijun He, Jinhua Fu, Dongliang Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to analyze the clinical characteristics and identify predictive factors associated with small segment molded sputum plugs in pediatric patients with Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (MPP).MethodsA retrospective analysis was performed on the clinical data of pediatric patients diagnosed with MPP who underwent bronchoscopy at our hospital between December 2021 and April 2024. Multivariate logistic regression was employed to determine independent predictors of small segment molded sputum plug formation.ResultsAmong 116 pediatric patients with MPP who met the study criteria, 48 (41.38%) were found to have small segment molded sputum plugs, while 68 (58.62%) did not. Patients with a history of recurrent respiratory infections, diminished breath sounds, and radiological evidence of atelectasis were significantly more likely to develop molded sputum plugs (P < 0.05). Furthermore, the group with small segment molded sputum plugs exhibited a longer duration of fever (P = 0.025) and elevated levels of procalcitonin (PCT; P = 0.024), alanine aminotransferase (ALT; P = 0.048) and prothrombin time (PT; P = 0.021). Multivariate logistic regression analysis identified a history of recurrent respiratory infections and diminished breath sounds as independent predictors for the development of small segment molded sputum plugs.ConclusionsA history of recurrent respiratory infections and diminished breath sounds emerged as independent predictors of small segment molded sputum plugs in pediatric MPP. These findings highlight the importance of considering these clinical features during the evaluation of MPP patients to inform diagnostic and therapeutic decision-making. Future studies are warranted to investigate the potential benefits of early detection and intervention on clinical outcomes in pediatric MPP.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13052-025-02130-4.Keywords: Molded sputum plugs, MPP, Pediatric patients, Predictor, Clinical characteristics

Background

Small segment molded sputum plugs are a distinct manifestation of plastic bronchitis (PB), characterized by the formation of bronchial casts within localized regions of the lungs [1]. PB is a rare condition that includes several subtypes, such as pseudomembranous bronchitis, mucoid impaction, cast bronchitis, and fibrous bronchitis. It is defined by the formation of firm bronchial casts that replicate the shape of the local airways. These casts vary in size, ranging from small segmental casts in individual bronchi to extensive tree-like casts involving the entire bronchial tree [1]. In pediatric patients, airway obstruction caused by molded sputum plugs can result in severe respiratory distress or even asphyxiation [2]. Without timely recognition and removal, the condition may progress to respiratory failure, cardiac arrest, or other life-threatening complications, potentially leading to chronic respiratory dysfunction or fatal outcomes [3].The exact etiology of molded sputum plugs remains unclear; however, current research and case reports suggest potential associations with respiratory infections, including Mycoplasma pneumoniae, influenza virus, and adenovirus, as well as non-infectious conditions such as lymphatic disorders, congenital heart disease, severe asthma, and cystic fibrosis [4].

M. pneumoniae, an atypical bacterium with characteristics intermediate between bacteria and viruses, is a leading cause of respiratory tract infections in both children and adults. It is a globally prevalent pathogen that poses a significant public health challenge [5]. Respiratory infections caused by M. pneumoniae affect individuals across all age groups and are widespread worldwide [6]. Among children hospitalized with community-acquired pneumonia (CAP), pharyngeal polymerase chain reaction (PCR) or serological testing has shown that 4% to 39% of cases are positive for M. pneumoniae [7]. Although MPP is one of the most common causes of CAP in children and is generally considered a benign, self-limiting condition with mild clinical symptoms [8], recent studies have highlighted its association with plastic bronchitis in pediatric patients [9].

Given the limited research on MPP complicated by molded sputum plugs, both domestically and internationally, this study aims to enhance clinical awareness of this condition. By retrospectively analyzing data from 116 MPP patients who underwent bronchoscopy and bronchoalveolar lavage, with a particular focus on 48 cases involving small segment molded sputum plugs, this study provides a comprehensive analysis of clinical characteristics and predictive factors associated with small segment molded sputum plugs in pediatric MPP. The findings offer novel insights into the clinical predictors of this complication and establish an evidence base for optimizing diagnostic approaches and therapeutic decision-making.

Methods

Patient selection

Patients were recruited from the Pediatric Department of Mindong Hospital, Fujian Medical University, between December 2021 and April 2024. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) diagnosis of MPP. Biomarkers: serum PCT levels < 0.5 ng/mL with either normal or elevated C-reactive protein(CRP). The PCT threshold was selected based on: (a) pathophysiological differences in host response to atypical pathogens, and (b) diagnostic performance in prior validation studies [10, 11]. Therapeutic response: Macrolide antibiotics are effective in treatment; (2) presence of one or more of the following clinical or radiological features: persistent fever, severe cough, dyspnea, poor response to macrolide antibiotic therapy, pulmonary consolidation, atelectasis, or pleural effusion; and (3) written informed consent obtained from parents or legal guardians.The exclusion criteria included: (1) age ≤ 3 months or >14 years; (2) refusal to undergo bronchoscopy during hospitalization; (3) incomplete medical records; (4) presence of immunodeficiencies, genetic metabolic disorders, or other significant comorbidities; (5) contraindications to bronchoscopy; (6) Confirmed coinfection with prevalent age-specific pathogens: respiratory syncytial virus(RSV)/influenza virus-positive infants (3–24 months); Streptococcus. pneumoniae-positive preschool children (2–5 years), and adenovirus-positive school-aged children (>5 years).

Bronchoscopy procedure

Bronchoscopy was performed under general anesthesia by experienced pediatric pulmonologists. Patients were fasted for at least 6 h prior to the procedure. Anesthesia was induced using midazolam for preoperative sedation, combined with propofol for induction and lidocaine spray for local anesthesia. A flexible bronchoscope (model QG3032, manufacturer SEESHEEN) was inserted via the nasal or oral route. The tracheobronchial tree was systematically examined for abnormalities, including mucosal inflammation, secretions, and sputum plugs. Bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) was performed by instilling 5 mL of sterile saline into the affected lung segment, followed by aspiration of the fluid. The collected BAL fluid was immediately transferred to the laboratory for microbiological analysis.

Sample processing

The bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) was aliquoted for microbiological analysis, which included bacterial culture, PCR testing for M. pneumoniae, and detection of other respiratory pathogens. Coinfections were identified through a combination of PCR and serological assays. Nasopharyngeal swabs and blood samples were systematically collected from all enrolled pediatric participants. PCR was utilized to detect common respiratory pathogens, including S. pneumoniae, and respiratory viruses such as influenza virus and RSV. Concurrently, serological testing was conducted to measure specific antibodies against these pathogens, providing complementary diagnostic information. All laboratory procedures were conducted in accordance with standardized protocols.

Diagnostic criteria for MPP

Patients were diagnosed with MPP based on clinical symptoms, radiological findings, and laboratory confirmation. The diagnostic criteria were established in accordance with the 2023 National Health Commission Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of MPP in Children (China) [12]. These criteria include: (1) clinical symptoms such as fever, cough, or dyspnea; (2) radiographic correlation: Characteristic CT findings including patchy consolidations, tree-in-bud patterns, or lobular infiltrates; and (3) Laboratory confirmation (requiring at least one of): Serum MP-IgM titer ≥ 1:160; Positive M. pneumoniae PCR from nasopharyngeal(NP) swab or BALF. PCR was prioritized due to its higher sensitivity and specificity compared to serological antibody testing [5, 13].

To ensure diagnostic accuracy, we implemented the combined diagnostic approach (serology + NP/BALF-PCR confirmation) reliably identified MP as the primary etiological agent in this patient population.

Diagnostic criteria for small segment molded sputum plug group

Subjects were categorized into the small segment molded sputum plug according to modified criteria adapted from previous literature [13], comprising two mandatory components: (1) bronchoscopic identification of tenacious mucus plugs occluding the luminal spaces of third-to-fifth generation bronchioles, confirmed by external properties evidence of plastic bronchitis; (2) concurrent presentation of at least two respiratory symptoms including persistent fever, paroxysmal cough, dyspnea. All radiographic and bronchoscopic findings underwent blinded evaluation by two board-certified pediatric pulmonologists, with discrepancies resolved through consensus review involving a third senior clinician.

Ethical approval

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Mindong Hospital of Fujian Medical University (Ethics Approval Code: K2024053003). Written informed consent was obtained from the parents or legal guardians of all participants prior to their inclusion in the study.All guardians of the patients agreed to use the clinical data from the pediatric medical record system. The study protocol was explained in detail. Prior to participation, they were also informed of the potential risks of bronchoscopy and signed the relevant informed consent forms.All personally identifiable information underwent cryptographic anonymization prior to analysis using hospital-certified data security protocols.

Study design and data collection

This retrospective cohort study analyzed pre-existing clinical data of pediatric MPP patients with small segment molded sputum plugs. Consistent with World Health Organisation(WHO) criteria for clinical trials, this study did not involve: Prospective assignment of participants to interventions；Experimental manipulation of treatment protocols. All management decisions (e.g., bronchoscopy, macrolide use) were made clinically prior to study initiation. Using a retrospective case-series design, we identified eligible patients from Mindong Hospital Affiliated to Fujian Medical University electronic records between December 2021 and April 2024. The analysis focused on reconstructing clinical trajectories from existing data, with no active intervention or randomization. Following institutional review board approval, systematic data extraction was conducted through the hospital's medical record system over a 30-day period. Initial screening identified patients meeting the diagnostic criteria for pediatric CAP as defined by the American Thoracic Society 2019 guidelines. Subsequent screening was performed to select MPP cases that fulfilled the inclusion and exclusion criteria. From an initial pool of 342 eligible cases, 116 subjects meeting stringent selection criteria underwent therapeutic fiberoptic bronchoscopy with BAL under conscious sedation. Based on bronchoscopic findings, participants were stratified into two groups: the case group, characterized by the presence of small segment molded sputum plugs formation, and the control group without evidence of small segment molded sputum plugs (Fig. 1).

Patient enrollment flowchart and bronchoscopic findings

The following clinical parameters were systematically extracted from patients' medical records: demographic characteristics (gender and age); clinical history, including recurrent respiratory infections; hospitalization duration; and clinical manifestations comprising fever, cough, wheezing, dyspnea, three-concave sign, diminished breath sounds, and rales. Microbiological evaluation included the assessment of pathogen infection status, categorized as bacterial, viral, or mixed infections. Comprehensive laboratory investigations encompassed complete blood count analysis, quantification of inflammatory biomarkers (CRP and PCT levels), and biochemical profiling (liver function tests and cardiac enzyme analysis), along with coagulation studies. Radiographic assessment documented pulmonary abnormalities, including consolidation, atelectasis, pleural effusion, bronchial stenosis, emphysema, pleural thickening, pulmonary nodules, and linear opacities.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 27.0. Categorical data were expressed as percentages (%), and continuous data were described as mean (standard deviation) for normally distributed variables or median (interquartile range) for non-normally distributed variables. The Shapiro-Wilk test was used to assess the normality of data distribution.For categorical variables, Fisher's exact test or Chi-Square test was used. For continuous variables, the independent samples t-test was used for normally distributed data, and the Mann-Whitney U test was used for non-normally distributed data. Variables demonstrating statistically significant associations (P<0.02) in univariate analysis were selected as candidate predictors for subsequent multivariate logistic regression analysis to identify independent factors associated with small segment molded sputum plugs formation. P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

General Information

The study initially enrolled 342 pediatric patients with CAP. In this cohort of 230 patients who fulfilled the diagnostic criteria for MPP - defined by concurrent positive nasopharyngeal PCR for M. pneumoniae and serum IgM titers≥1:160 - 116 subjects underwent BAL with PCR analysis after applying stringent inclusion and exclusion criteria. Based on bronchoscopic findings and bronchoalveolar lavage results, the cohort was stratified into two distinct groups: those presenting with small segment molded sputum plugs (n=48) and those without this manifestation (n=68). The prevalence of small segment molded sputum plug formation among MPP patients was calculated at 41.38% (48/116).

Demographic analysis revealed comparable gender distributions between groups, with males constituting 41.67% (20/48) of the small segment molded sputum plug group versus 50.00% (34/68) in the control group (P=0.376). Age distribution showed no significant intergroup difference, with mean ages of7.35 years (SD=3.21) and 6.87 years (SD=2.61) in the small segment molded sputum plug and control groups, respectively (P=0.380). However, a significant between-group difference emerged in the prevalence of recurrent respiratory infection history, with the small segment molded sputum plug group demonstrating a substantially higher incidence (56.25%, 27/48) compared to the control group (10.29%, 7/68) (P<0.001) (Table 1).

Clinical manifestations and coinfection profiles

Comparative analysis revealed no statistically significant differences between groups across key clinical parameters(P>0.05). Symptom profiles showed comparable frequencies of fever (89.58% vs. 80.88%; P=0.202), cough (95.83% vs. 100.00%;P=0.169), wheezing (25.00% vs. 29.41%; P=0.601), and dyspnea (100.00% vs. 98.53%;P>0.999). Physical examinations demonstrated similar rates of three-concave sign (25.00% vs. 27.94%;P=0.724) and rales (37.50% vs. 29.41%;P=0.361). For clinical outcomes, median hospitalization duration did not differ significantly between groups (8.00 [7.00,9.00] days vs. 9.00 [7.00,10.00] days; P=0.119).Notably, the small segment molded sputum plug group exhibited prolonged febrile episodes (5.00 [3.25–6.00.25.00] days vs. 4.00 [2.00–5.00.00.00] days; P =0.025) with significant duration-effect correlation. Auscultatory findings demonstrated marked divergence: diminished breath sounds were prevalent in 41.67% (20/48 cases) of the small segment molded sputum plug cohort versus 4.41% (3/68 controls)(Table 1).

Among the 116 enrolled patients, 56 cases (48.28%) were found to have co-infections with other pathogens. Nasopharyngeal PCR identified co-infections in 20 cases (17.2%). Serological analysis (IgM-positive) revealed co-infections in 36 cases (31.0%).Notably, BAL PCR testing detected no co-infections. The three most common pathogens were Haemophilus influenzae (12.93%), followed by S. pneumoniae (12.07%) and Human metapneumovirus (10.34%), with Parainfluenza virus detected in 10.34% of cases. No statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups (P >0.05) (Tables 1 and 2).

Laboratory investigations

Hematological and biochemical analyses revealed no statistically significant differences (P>0.05) in several key parameters between the study groups, including white blood cell count (WBC), differential leukocyte counts (neutrophil percentage [NEUT%] and lymphocyte percentage [LYMPH%]), hemoglobin concentration (Hb), platelet count (PLT), CRP levels and markers of hepatic and cardiac function (aspartate aminotransferase [AST], creatine kinase-MB [CK-MB]), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) and markers of fibrinolysis (fibrinogen (FIB),FDP and D-dimer levels). In contrast, significant intergroup differences (P<0.05) were identified in several biomarkers: PCT levels, ALT activity and coagulation parameters (PT). Although statistically significant differences in ALT levels were observed between groups (P = 0.048), the majority of values remained within the normal range, indicating limited clinical relevance in this cohort (Table 3).

Discussion

MPP often presents with nonspecific symptoms overlapping with viral or bacterial pneumonias. Bronchoscopy with lavage allows direct sampling of lower airway secretions, improving diagnostic accuracy compared to serology or PCR alone. Early confirmation of MPP enables timely initiation of macrolides or tetracyclines (if age-appropriate), avoiding unnecessary broad-spectrum antibiotics. The specific definition of PB has not yet been uniformly classified. It is commonly described as mucous casts and discrete intraluminal bronchial entities that are difficult to remove during bronchoscopy, potentially encompassing fibrous inflammation and pseudomembranous inflammation [14]. PB often leads to airway obstruction, causing respiratory distress in children and, in severe cases, can be life-threatening [15]. With the widespread use of fiberoptic bronchoscopy and the recent prevalence of Mycoplasma pneumoniae, cases of MPP with PB have been increasingly identified [15, 16]. Small segment molded sputum plugs, a specific type of secretion in PB, typically present as small segment bronchial casts. This type of secretion is commonly associated with PB in the literature [16]. This study aims to explore the clinical characteristics and predictors of MPP with small segment molded sputum plugs.Comparison of the two groups revealed that the proportion of refractory MPP patients was significantly higher in the small segment molded sputum plug group than in the non-molded sputum plug group, consistent with the findings of Sukun Lu [17]. This further highlights the complexity and therapeutic challenges associated with small segment molded sputum plugs in MPP. Xu et al. suggested that a history of allergies is a significant risk factor for the formation of PB [18]. However, our study provides a new perspective by incorporating a history of recurrent respiratory infections into the analysis. Our results indicate that children with a history of recurrent respiratory infections are more likely to develop small segment molded sputum plugs, likely due to immune-related mechanisms [19]. Recurrent respiratory infections can result in chronic inflammation and immune dysregulation, potentially contributing to the formation of small segment molded sputum plugs. In particular, persistent M. pneumoniae infection has been demonstrated to induce a Th2-dominated immune response, characterized by elevated levels of IL-4 and IL-13, which promote excessive mucus production and impair mucociliary clearance [20]. The interplay between this immune response and recurrent infections may establish a pathological microenvironment that facilitates the development of sputum plugs. The decision to perform fiberoptic bronchoscopy and alveolar lavage in children with MPP should be guided by a comprehensive evaluation of clinical symptoms, physical signs, and imaging findings. Although a history of recurrent respiratory infections may elevate the risk of small segment molded sputum plugs, it should not serve as the sole indication for bronchoscopy. Clinicians must meticulously balance the potential benefits against the procedural risks, tailoring the decision to the individual patient's clinical context. Indications for bronchoscopy may encompass persistent fever despite adequate antibiotic therapy, progressive respiratory distress, or radiographic evidence indicative of atelectasis. Although bronchoscopy is effective in removing small segment molded sputum plugs and improving clinical outcomes, it remains an invasive procedure associated with potential risks, such as respiratory complications and anesthesia-related adverse events. Consequently, its application should be restricted to cases where the anticipated benefits demonstrably outweigh the potential risks.

Our clinical observations identified diminished breath sounds as a characteristic physical finding in patients with small segment molded sputum plugs, consistent with the reported findings of Yang et al. This auscultatory feature likely reflects partial bronchial obstruction caused by small segment molded sputum plugs, which mechanically restrict airflow to the affected pulmonary segments. The pathophysiological correlation between tenacious molded sputum plugs and reduced breath sounds has been well-documented, as such airway obstructions typically produce selective hypoventilation in the involved lung regions. [9]. Inflammation-induced necrosis and shedding of bronchial epithelial cells, congestion, edema, and increased mucous secretions may lead to the formation of plastic sputum plugs, causing bronchial narrowing or obstruction. This reduces airflow into the alveoli or slows its rate, resulting in diminished breath sounds [21]. Thus, it is understandable that diminished breath sounds are associated with small segment molded sputum plugs.

The presence of coinfections may have influenced the clinical outcomes observed in this study. To ensure the rigor of this study, we jointly adopted combined diagnostic criteria (serology, nasopharyngeal PCR, and BALF-PCR positivity) confirmed M. pneumoniae as the predominant pathogen in this cohort. Nevertheless, we systematically evaluated potential co-infections to assess their clinical relevance. No statistically significant differences were observed between patients with small segment molded sputum plugs formation and those without (P>0.05). The absence of significant intergroup differences suggests that co-infections may not be a primary determinant of disease severity in this population. However, further studies are needed to confirm these findings. One notable limitation of this study is the potential influence of coinfections on the observed outcomes. To address this limitation, future studies should prioritize larger cohorts and employ more comprehensive pathogen detection methodologies, thereby enhancing the robustness and generalizability of the findings.

Although small segment molded sputum plugs in MPP may not always be radiographically detectable, they can contribute to persistent fever, atelectasis, or even the development of PB. Our findings demonstrate that pediatric patients with these plugs exhibit prolonged fever duration and a higher incidence of atelectasis compared to the non-small segment molded sputum plug group, consistent with physical signs such as diminished breath sounds. These observations highlight the clinical importance of proactive airway clearance interventions. The small segment molded sputum plug group in our study exhibited a longer duration of fever, suggesting an excessive systemic inflammatory response. This aligns with prior research: Zhang et al. identified persistent fever before bronchoscopy as an independent risk factor for MPP with PB [15], while Zhao et al. reported higher peak body temperatures in refractory MPP with PB cases [22]. Additionally, Zhong et al. found that fever duration exceeding 12 days was an independent risk factor for molded sputum plugs [16]. Direct visualization of small segment molded sputum plugs via bronchoscopy provides a rationale for therapeutic lavage. This intervention enables both identification and removal of obstructions, potentially mitigating disease progression. Although our study did not directly compare outcomes with versus without bronchoscopy, existing evidence suggests that early lavage may reduce sequelae (e.g., PB) by attenuating inflammatory cascades, particularly given the high prevalence of cytokines like IL-1β in BALF [23]. Huang et al.'s findings further support the association between sputum plugs and atelectasis [3].

Our study found that timely and routine blood tests upon admission are easily performed and necessary for hospitalized patients, facilitating early and rapid disease diagnosis. PCT, known for its high specificity for bacterial infections, is widely used in the clinical management of respiratory tract infections [24]. PCT can guide the rational use of antibiotics, helping to prevent the emergence of multidrug-resistant bacteria [25]. Similarly, we found that PCT is an independent risk factor for MPP with small segment molded sputum plugs. This may be due to an increased incidence of bacterial co-infections in these patients. For these patients, empirical antibiotic therapy should be initiated promptly and later adjusted based on drug sensitivity results. ALT is a well-established biomarker for liver injury [26]. The median ALT levels in the small segment molded sputum plug group were higher than those in the non-molded sputum plug group, suggesting potential liver function impairment in these patients. Although the majority of ALT values in our study were within the normal range, the observed statistical significance indicates that even minor fluctuations in these levels may signify underlying pathological mechanisms. For example, mild elevations in ALT, even within the normal range, have been linked to subclinical liver injury or systemic inflammation in Mycoplasma pneumoniae infections [27, 28].The incorporation of laboratory indicators, such as ALT, into the evaluation of MPP may facilitate the identification of patients at heightened risk for complications, even in the absence of overt abnormalities. Future investigations should explore the potential utility of these indicators as early biomarkers for disease severity or progression. Furthermore, higher levels of PT was observed in the MPP with small segment molded sputum plug group. These findings reflect systemic inflammation caused by severe M. pneumoniae infection, leading to local ischemia, hypoxia, and acidosis. This inflammatory response results in endothelial cell damage, activation of the coagulation system, and a hypercoagulable state, followed by activation of the fibrinolytic, kinin, and complement systems. These processes disrupt the balance between coagulation and anticoagulation, leading to elevated levels of PT [29]. However, as an exploratory study, these findings should be interpreted with caution due to the constrained sample size. Future studies involving larger cohorts are essential to validate these results. To address this limitation, we plan to expand our research through multicenter collaborations, thereby increasing the sample size and enhancing the statistical power of our analyses.

However, this study has several limitations. First, it is a single-center, retrospective study with a relatively small sample size, which may limit the generalizability and statistical power of the results. The limited number of cases in the small segment molded sputum plugs group can be attributed to the low prevalence of this condition in the pediatric population, coupled with stringent inclusion criteria that restricted the recruitment of eligible participants. Second, the small segment molded sputum plug samples retrieved via bronchoscopy were not subjected to pathological examination, precluding further analysis of their composition and formation mechanisms at a pathological level. Third, the clinical relevance of laboratory indicators, such as ALT, may be limited, as the majority of values fell within the normal range. Finally, although the present study did not evaluate long-term clinical outcomes, the observed association between the small segment molded sputum plugs formation and sustained inflammatory responses suggests that timely airway clearance interventions may help attenuate secondary pulmonary injury.

Given these limitations, future studies should prioritize multicenter collaborations to expand the sample size and enhance statistical power. Additionally, incorporating comprehensive biomarker panels and pathological analyses of sputum plugs could provide deeper insights into the clinical relevance and underlying mechanisms of MPP.

Conclusion

In summary, this study demonstrates that children with MPP and small segment molded sputum plugs exhibit a longer duration of persistent fever, intense inflammatory responses, and potential liver function impairment and coagulation abnormalities. Our analysis identified a history of recurrent respiratory infections and diminished breath sounds as independent predictors of small segment molded sputum plugs in pediatric MPP cases. These clinical predictors may assist clinicians in early identification of high-risk patients requiring closer monitoring. Future studies are warranted to investigate the potential benefits of early detection and intervention on clinical outcomes in pediatric MPP.

A Breast Reduction Technique for the Free DIEP Flap Post Breast Reconstruction
Authors: Magdalena Cordoba, Tomas Cordoba, Éolie Delisle, Maryam Mozafarinia, Carlos Cordoba
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Summary:Breast flap remodeling is a common procedure after breast reconstruction. We present a unique approach to breast reduction using a superomedial pedicle inverted “T” technique in patients with previous free deep inferior epigastric perforator (DIEP) flap breast reconstruction, not previously reported. Breast reduction can be a valid option for reshaping breasts after free DIEP flap reconstruction. Breast reduction using the superomedial pedicle inverted T technique is performed in patients who have undergone free DIEP flap breast reconstruction. The superomedial pedicle inverted T breast reduction technique in breast reconstruction with free DIEP flaps seems promising, as the reduced DIEP flaps survived. The superomedial pedicle inverted T breast reduction technique in breast reconstruction with free DIEP flaps offers a good option for secondary breast reshaping. Preservation of the pedicle and blood supply are potential advantages. However, the limited evidence and surgical expertise needed are important considerations. This technique shows promise for breast reductions after free DIEP flap breast reconstruction.

Breast flap remodeling, through augmentation or reduction, is common after breast reconstruction. We present an approach to breast reduction using a superomedial pedicle inverted “T” technique in patients who had previously undergone free deep inferior epigastric perforator (DIEP) flap breast reconstruction. To the best of our knowledge, no reports on breast reductions after breast reconstruction involving free DIEP flaps exist. There are, however, other (secondary) breast reconstruction techniques, such as the latissimus dorsi free or pedicled, gluteal free (perforator), and the omental pedicled flaps, of which the latter has been less performed.1 The only other free flap to have undergone an inferior pedicle breast reduction is a breast reconstruction using a free transverse rectus abdominus myocutaneous flap 5 years previously.2 Breast reduction surgery can be performed for secondary breast reshaping post free DIEP flaps.

PATIENT 1

A 56-year-old female heavy smoker underwent a right modified radical mastectomy for invasive ductal carcinoma in 2021. Immediate DIEP free flap reconstruction was performed with an uneventful recovery. Seventeen months post reconstruction, she presented to our plastic surgery clinic with complaints of bilateral breast hypertrophy (including the reconstructed right breast). To address this, she underwent a significant inferolaterosuperior bilateral breast reduction 18 months after the reconstruction, with a total of 611 g removed from the right (DIEP) and 509 g from the left (Figs. 1 and 2). A superomedial pedicle with a classic inverted T scar was performed (Figs. 3 and 4). Additionally, the right areola was reconstructed using the excess tissue from the left areola as a full-thickness graft (Fig. 3). No necrosis was observed, and the skin grafted areolar reconstruction was fully integrated.

A 58-year-old female nonsmoker, with a history of pulmonary embolism and on apixaban, underwent a left skin-sparing total mastectomy for invasive lobular carcinoma in 2021. DISCUSSION

DIEP flaps have become the gold standard in breast reconstruction due to their advantages such as reduced postoperative pain and decreased morbidity at the abdominal donor site.3 Neovascularization (which typically occurs between 7 and 10 days postoperatively) allows the flap to develop a new blood supply, reducing its dependence on the initial vascular pedicle.4,5 Factors including infection, tumors, radiotherapy, tobacco use, vascular insufficiency, and medical comorbidities can influence neovascularization.5,6

Primary defatting in free flap reconstruction does not significantly increase the risk of perfusion-related complications. Although debulking flaps may result in perforator injuries, reducing the number of perforators alongside debulked areas might improve outcomes by reducing tissue mass dependence on perforators.7

Studies have shown that pedicle division can be safely performed after 18–21 days, depending on the patient’s condition. In animal studies, complete flap survival has been observed even after 4 days of pedicle division.8 Serial fluorescein injections can aid in evaluating neovascularization, helping determine the appropriate timing for flap pedicle division.8 Interestingly, cases of DIEP flap loss after pedicle division have been reported up to 3 years postoperatively.4

Although one patient was a smoker, neither had a compromised recipient bed or any other factors that could hinder neovascularization. This suggests that the flaps did not rely on the pedicle for an extended period.4 Smoking, despite being discouraged, may have inadvertently benefited our patient by improving neovascularization due to the hypoxic conditions and decreased microvascular perfusion caused by smoking.9 Nicotine concentrations in moderate smokers can increase essential angiogenic processes such as endothelial cell growth, migration, and survival within the vascular system.9

By utilizing a superomedial breast reduction technique, the pedicle can be preserved when the internal mammary artery is used as the recipient vessel, making it advantageous and safe for reshaping and reducing the size of a DIEP flap. Additionally, a case report has demonstrated the survival of a DIEP flap even after complete pedicle resection, as evidenced by a successful outcome after 4 years.10 Therefore, breast reduction can be performed after DIEP free flap reconstruction, specifically when taking additional care not to injure the pedicle.

The optimal operative technique for secondary breast reductions remains a topic of debate. The current literature lacks conclusive evidence and provides multiple different recommendations. Some secondary breast reduction studies suggest the benefits of using a full-thickness glandular dermal superolaterally based pedicle, especially in cases where the primary reduction technique is unknown. The goal is to maintain blood supply through the random pattern neovascularization of the superomedial portion of the free flap and ideally to preserve the flap pedicle anastomosis to the internal mammary artery. Aesthetic outcomes should be carefully evaluated, with a focus on avoiding clinical signs of fat necrosis.

Although the superomedial pedicle inverted T breast reduction technique in breast reconstruction involving free DIEP flaps presents several advantages, there are certain limiting factors and constraints to consider. Performing this technique requires that the surgeon has a good understanding of the reconstructive anatomy and experience with pedicle preservation techniques. Also, lack of extensive literature and studies specifically focusing on breast reductions post breast reconstruction involving free DIEP flaps limits the availability of evidence-based guidelines for this technique.

CONCLUSIONS

The superomedial pedicle inverted T breast reduction technique in breast reconstruction involving free DIEP flaps offers a practicable option for secondary breast reduction reshaping. Although this technique has its advantages, such as preserving the pedicle and maintaining blood supply, there are important limiting factors and constraints to consider. Surgical competence, patient selection, potential complications, and limited evidence are among the key factors that influence the feasibility and outcomes of this technique. Larger cohorts are needed to enhance our understanding of the outcomes and efficacy to refine the selection criteria for this technique.

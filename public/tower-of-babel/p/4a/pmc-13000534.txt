Zein-based polysaccharide-tannic acid films as multifunctional barriers to prevent post-surgical adhesions
Authors: Amedeo Ungolo, Marco Ruggeri, Cinzia Boselli, Antonia Icaro Cornaglia, Barbara Vigani, Eleonora Bianchi, Silvia Rossi, Giuseppina Sandri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Formation of postoperative adhesions remains a significant clinical burden, frequently leading to chronic pain, bowel obstruction, and infertility. Current physical barriers have shown limited success, highlighting the need for multifunctional antiadhesive structure that combine mechanical stability with finely tuned surface properties able to act as slipping layer between adjacent organs. At this purpose polymeric zein (Z) based films doped with Chondroitin sulphated (CS) or Maltodextrin (MDx) and loaded with Tannic acid (TA) were developed and characterized by a multidisciplinary approach. The films proved to reduce muco- and bioadhesion, showed higher hydrophobicity and increased roughness that impaired conformal contact and limited adhesive interactions. Moreover, biological evaluation was performed towards Caco-2 epithelial cells, representing the luminal interface of the abdominal cavity, and NHDF fibroblasts, representing the stromal/serosal side. Proliferation assays and cell morphology confirmed the higher biocompatibility of CS- and MDX-doped films compared with the undoped ones (Z-TA1%), suggesting that polysaccharide presence not only maintain the antiadhesive properties but also enhance the biological performance by providing a hydrophilic and bioactive interface. The in vivo biocompatibility and the haemoglobin release were also performed on a murine model to highlight the biological safety of the films. This work provides mechanistic insights into how film composition governs antiadhesion and cytocompatibility, offering a rational framework for designing next-generation coatings to prevent postoperative adhesions.

Introduction

Pathological fibrous bands known as post-operative adhesions (POAs) are formed when the body heals from surgical trauma. POAs are still common, affecting between 67 and 93% of patients having surgery and even though they are frequently asymptomatic, adhesions can cause significant clinical and financial burden (Ghobrial et al., 2023). Within five years after surgery, 3–20% of patients need to be readmitted to the hospital due to adhesion-related complications (Parker et al., 2005). The degradation of fibrin (fibrinolysis) typically occurs within a few days, restoring smooth tissue surfaces. However, fibrin can persist if fibrinolysis is impaired by conditions like surgical trauma, inflammation, infection and ischemia. The fibrinous layer is then populated by fibroblasts and new capillaries, which deposit collagen and transform it into fibrous tissue. This process eventually develops into long-lasting adhesions that hold tissues or organs together. Adhesions can be developed in different tissues such as, abdominal, muscular, nervous and tendinous. In particular, abdominal adhesions can lead to intestinal blockage, infertility, and persistent pelvic or abdominal pain. In this context, the use of physical barriers and bioactive molecules are the actual commons strategies in the prevention of PAOs. Physical barriers, such as films, gels, and membranes can mechanically limit the contact between the damaged tissues during the initial stages of the healing. However, their use is often limited due to their cytotoxicity, degradation rates, and lack of compatibility with the minimally invasive techniques (Park et al., 2020). To overcome this limitation, the use of natural polymers such as proteins and polysaccharides, has getting attention given their biocompatibility and physical chemical properties. As for proteins-based biomaterials, zein, a prolamin-rich corn protein, has emerged as a versatile material in pharmaceutical applications due to its excellent film-forming ability, hydrophobic nature, and biocompatibility (widely recognized as safe, GRAS) (Tortorella et al., 2021). For the polysaccharides-based biomaterials, chondroitin sulphated, a sulphated glycosaminoglycan naturally present in the extracellular matrix, has been proposed for its properties to enhance cell proliferation, and promote tissue formation (Sodhi and Panitch, 2021; Ahmad et al., 2025). In addition, maltodextrin, a neutral polysaccharide derived from starch hydrolysis, is characterized by higher hydrophilicity and film forming ability. Moreover, to better enhance their properties, the addition of bioactive molecules can further improve film properties (Cupone et al., 2022). In this context, tannic acid, a plant-derived polyphenol, was selected for its multifunctional role due to its ability to strongly interact with proteins and polysaccharides via hydrogen bonding and hydrophobic interactions and due to its potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory and fibrinolytic activity (Marcińczyk et al., 2022). Given these premises, the aim of the work was the design and development of Z-based films doped with CS or MDx and loaded with TA intended to prevent post operative adhesion. The key innovation of this study aims at the development of a multifunctional protein-polysaccharide films that combine surface anti-adhesive properties with bioactive functionalities, allowing the prevention of cellular colonization while simultaneously preserving and supporting the viability of surrounding tissues.

Methods

Film preparation

Z films were prepared by solvent casting. In detail, Z (20% w/w) was dissolved in hydroalcoholic solution (80/20 EtOH/H2O), followed by the addition of CS or MDx and then of TA (Scheme 1). In Table 1 the compositions are reported. Once dissolved, 2 mL of each polymerics solutions were poured into a silicon mold (7 cm2) and left in an oven under atmospheric air for 5 h at 50 °C until solvent evaporation. The total dried film mass per unit area was calculated by dividing the dry weight of each film by the geometrical area of the silicone mold used during casting, which was constant for all samples. Morphology, topography, surface wettability and FT-IR characterization

Film morphology was investigated by means of scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Phenom™ Pure Desktop, Thermo Scientific, Waltham, Massachusetts, USA, Alfatest). The samples were cut in 1 cm2 pieces and attached on a conductive tape and sputtered with gold in vacuum (thickness 4 nm). Images were acquired at different magnification. The topographical characterization was performed by atomic force microscope (AFM, Tosca 200 Anton Paar, Italy) by cutting samples in 5 × 5 mm square and placed them on support. The analysis was performed in tapping mode (cantilever AP-ARROW-NCR, Silicon SPM-Sensor, Al-coating; thickness: 4.6 μm, Length: 160 μm, width: 45 μm, resonance frequency: 285 kHz, force constant: 42 N/m; Anton Paar, Austria). Surface wettability was evaluated by static sessile drop method (DMe-211 Plus, FAMAS software, Kyowa, Osaka, Japan). 10 mL of water was dropped onto the films surface at 1000 ms and droplet shape was captured using a camera and the contact angles were measured. FT-IR analysis was carried out using a Nicolet iS20 (Thermo Scientific, Italy) equipped with a DTGS detector working in a range of 400–4000 cm−1 with a resolution of 16 cm−1.

Mechanical properties

Mechanical properties were evaluated in both static and oscillatory mode. The tensile properties were assessed using Texture analyser (TA-XT plus, Stable Microsystems, Italy) equipped with a 5.0 kg load cell. The films were cut into strips (30 × 10 mm) and clamped between two tensile grips (A/TG probe). As for the static mechanical properties, the upper grip was moved forward at a constant speed of 0.2 mm/s up to break. Mechanical properties were evaluated in dry and hydrated state. As for the oscillatory properties, they were evaluated by means of Dynamic Mechanical Analyser (DMA, MCR702e, Anton Paar, Italy) in tension and torsional mode with a solid-rectangular combination (SRF) as measuring system. In addition, the measurement performed in wet state were performed by using an immersion cell. The frequency sweep test was performed in the linear viscoelastic region (LVR) at 50 Pa, for the tensile test, and at 2.0 Pa, for the torsional test, in the frequency interval of 1–100 Hz. In addition, a low cyclic fatigue test was performed on films samples (1 × 3 cm2), setting a distance of 10 mm between the grips. Strain controlled cyclic tension tests were performed on all samples, with a strain of 10% with respect of film initial length (setting a distance of 1 mm). During the experiment, the strain oscillated at a frequency of 1.4 Hz and 10 loading cycles were performed.

Surface zeta potential and BSA adsorbtion

The surface zeta potential (ζ) was evaluated from the measurement of the streaming potential with SurPASS™ (Anton Paar GmbH, Italy) using the cylindrical cell. The films were placed between two filter disks in the sample holder and an aqueous solution of KCl (0.01 M) was used as streaming solvent in a pH range between 2 and 8, to determine the isoelectric point (IEP) (Huang et al., 2025). For BSA adsorption, the films were kept in contact with BSA at a concentration of 1 mg/mL. Subsequently, surface ζ was measured at pH 7.4, as previously described.

Protein adhesion

Films were placed in 0.5 mL of a 1 mg/mL of FITC-albumin (Cat. No. A9771, protein from bovine, ≥7 mol FITC mol albumin, Sigma Aldrich, Italy) solution in PBS pH 7.4 and incubated at 37 °C in a shaking bath. At prefixed time (30, 240, 360 min) the supernatants (100 μL) were collected and fluorescence red using a spectrofluorometer (FLUOstar Omega Microplate Reader, BMG LabTech, Italy) to quantify the protein amount remaining in solution and non-adhered onto film surface, as follows: (Proteins on surface = Total protein – Protein in solution). A calibration curve of FITC-Albumin was performed in PBS. (Concentration range 3.25–100 μg/mL, R2 = 0.997) (Ruggeri et al., 2024). Moreover, the CLSM images were reported to qualitative represent the films' surfaces after 360 min of contact.

Adhesion evaluation

Adhesion studies were carried out by means of dynamometer (TA-XT plus Texture Analyser, Stable Microsystem, Godalming, UK), equipped with 1 kg load cell. The test allowed to determine adhesion by putting in contact the sample with a biological substrate that should mimic the human skin. The force of detachment between the two materials was measured. This system consists of a probe (Ø: 10 mm) and a support, both made of Teflon. The support consists of two cylinders: the lower serves as a base for the sample location, the upper is equipped with circular hole in the center (Ø: 14 mm), which enables probe movement inside. The system was maintained at 37 °C in a water bath and all the adhesion measurements were carried out at the physiological temperature of 37 °C. Two different substrates were used: A. suspension of 5% w/w porcine skin gelatine; B. suspension of 5% of mucin.

TA cumulative release, antioxidant activity and circular dichroism

To evaluate the TA release, films (∼ 20 mg) were placed in 5 mL of PBS at 37 °C. At prefixed timepoints (0, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, and 24 h), the released media were withdrawn and replaced with fresh medium to maintain the volume constant. The absorbance of each sample was measured at 275 nm (FLUOstar® Omega, BMG LABTECH, Aylesbury, UK). The method was linear with a R2 > 0.998 (50–10 μg/mL). To evaluate the antioxidant properties, the films were placed in PBS at 37 °C. At prefixed time, the release media were withdrawn, and 0.1 mL of each sample was mixed 1:1 volume ratio with a DPPH (Cat. No. D9132, Sigma Aldrich, Italy) methanol solution (150 μg/mL) and incubated for 30 min in the dark (Ungolo et al., 2024). Then absorbance was measured at 597 nm FLUOstar® Omega (BMG LABTECH, Aylesbury, UK). In addition, circular dichroism (CD) was used to study the interaction between fibrinogen and TA, after TA release. In detail, a fibrinogen solution at concentration of 0.4 mg/mL was prepared in water, mixed with released TA at a 1:1 vol and left to interact for 15 min prior the analysis. The scanning range of the circular dichroism spectrometer was set at 190–260 nm, and the scanning was conducted three times. The mixture was then loaded into a cuvette (3 × 1 × 1 cm) and measured with a JASCO J-1500 (Jasco Inc., UK) with nitrogen permeating the process (Deng et al., 2019).

Films degradation and swelling ratio (%)

Films degradation and swelling ratio (%) were evaluated by placing films in cell complete growth medium at 37 °C in a shaking bath (100 rpm). As for degradation, at prefixed time the films were dried and weight, and the mass residues was calculated as follow:Mass residues%=Wt2/Wt1∗100.where t2 is the weight of films at time t and t1 at the previous time.

For the swelling ratio (%) the films were weight and the swelling calculated as follow:Swelling ratio%=Wt−W0/W0∗100.where Wt is the final weight of films at time t and W0 is the initial weight.

In vitro characterization

The in vitro characterization was carried out using normal human dermal fibroblasts (NHDF) from juvenile foreskin (PromoCell, VWR, Milan, Italy) and Human colorectal adenocarcinoma cells (Caco-2). Fibroblasts and Caco-2 were grown in the presence of Dulbecco's modified Eagle medium (Cat. No. D6429, DMEM, Sigma-Aldrich, Milan, Italy) supplemented with 10% v/v fetal bovine serum (Euroclone, Milan, Italy), and with penicillin/streptomycin solution (Cat. No. G6784, pen/strep, 100 UI/100 μg/mL, Sigma-Aldrich, Italy). For the direct method, cells were seeded onto film surfaces (density 30,000 cell/well) in a 96 well played while for the indirect method the cells were seeded into a 96 well plate (density 30,000 cell/well) and film extract prepared at different concentrations (% w/v) according to the ISO guidelines (ISO 13485 - Medical devices). Then, the extracts mediums were put in contact with cell and after 3 and 6 days the AlamarBlue™ (Cat. No. DAL1100, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Italy) assay was performed. The growth medium was removed from the wells and 200 μL of an AlamarBlue solution (10% w/w in DMEM w/o phenol red) were placed and incubated at 37 °C for 3 h. The fluorescence was detected using FLUOstar® Omega Microplate Reader using 560 nm as λex and 590 nm as λem (Ruggeri et al., 2022). Moreover, after 3 and 6 days, the cells were fixed with a solution of glutaraldehyde (3% in PBS 1×) and stained with phalloidin FITC Atto 488 (Cat. No. 49409, 50 μL at 20 μg/mL in PBS in each well, contact time 30 min, Sigma-Aldrich, Milan, Italy) and with Hoechst 33,258 (Cat. No. 94403, 100 μL of solution at 1:10,000 dilution in PBS per each well, contact time 10 min, Sigma-Aldrich, Italy). Finally, the samples were mounted onto glass slides, covered with coverslips and analyzed using CLSM (Leica TCS SP2, Leica Microsystems, Milan, Italy) at λex = 346 nm and λem = 460 nm for Hoechst 33258, and λex = 501 nm and λem = 523 nm for phalloidin FITC.

Preliminary in vivo evaluation

All animal tests were performed in accordance with the standard international ethical norms (European Communities Council Directive 86/609/EEC) approved by the Italian Health Ministry (D.L.116/92). The study protocol was authorised by of the University of Pavia Local Institutional Ethics Committee for the use of animals, as well as by the ISS (Istituto Superiore di Sanità). 5 male rats (Wistar 200–250 g, Envigo RMS Srl, Udine, Italy) were anaesthetized with equitensine at 3 mL/kg (39 mM pentobarbital, 256 mM chloral hydrate, 86 mM MgSO4, 10% v/v ethanol, and 39.6% v/v propylene glycol) and their backs were shaven to remove all hair. On the back of each rat, the films were implanted subcutaneously in an 8 mm incision to evaluate system safety. Then the incisions were sutured using strips (Steri-Strip Suture, I, 3 M Science applied to Life, USA). After 18 days, full thickness biopsies were obtained from the incision, and the histological analysis was carried out. Intact skin biopsy was taken as a reference.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad prism v10.1.0 statistical calculator. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was followed by Scheffé for post-hoc comparisons. p < 0.05 was considered significant.

Result and discussion

Morphology, topography and surface wettability

The total dried film mass per unit area after drying was around 14.3 mg/cm2 for all the samples and no visible macroscopic shrinkage or deformation was observed, and the films retained the original mold geometry. Therefore, the mold area was considered representative for normalization purposes. In addition, the macroscopic observation of the air exposed and silicon exposed surfaces during drying process and the SEM images (Fig. 1A-B) show that the surfaces of all the films were relatively smooth, dense, and free of any imperfections. However, some microscopic agglomerates were found on the film surfaces, in particular for the polysaccharides doped films, probably related to the precipitation of the polysaccharides during the drying process, which occur rapidly since the higher volatility of the solvent employed to dissolve the components. This phenomenon was also highlighted in the cross-sectional SEM images (Fig. 1B), in which all the films had a relatively dense structure but non-homogenous and irregular section for the doped films compared with undoped ones (Cheng et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025). AFM analysis was also performed to investigate the surface rugosity of the films. As reported in Fig. 1C, Z-TA1% films were characterized by lower roughness than Z-CS-TA1% and Z-MDx-TA1% ones, suggesting how the presence of the polysaccharides affect surface rugosity. The influence of the polysaccharides was also evidenced by the contact angle measurements (Fig. 1D). In particular, Z-TA1% were characterized by higher contact angle value (44.5 ± 0.9°) compared with Z-CS-TA1% (34.2 ± 2.9°) and Z-MDx-TA1% (39.6 ± 1.3°) suggesting a more hydrophobic surfaces than the polysaccharides doped ones. In addition, the different contact angle between the polysaccharide is related to their structure; the CS doped films were characterized by higher hydrophilicity than the MDx doped ones and this is attributed to the higher density of polar/ionizable functional groups exposed at the film surface (e.g., anionic groups such as -COO−/-SO₃− from the CS structure), which enhance water-surface interactions through hydrogen bonding and ion–dipole interactions (Young et al., 2016).Fig. 1A) Macroscopic observation of the air exposed and silicon exposed surfaces of the Z based films during the drying process; morphology, topography and surface wettability characterizations of the films: B) SEM, C) AFM, D) Contact angle (°); (mean values ± SD; n = 3), ANOVA one-way; Scheffé test (p ≤ 0.05).Fig. 1

The pristine polysaccharides spectra, both CS and MDx, are characterized by broad O—H stretching (∼3100–3400 cm−1) together with the C—O stretching, around 1150–1000 cm−1, related to the carbon backbone in the polysaccharides structure. In the TA unloaded films, the characteristic signals of the pristine components are still present suggesting that the films preparation did not alter the chemical structure and that no interaction between protein and polysaccharides occur. Upon TA loading (Fig. 2C) a slight shift in intensity and wavenumbers were observed, particularly in the 1700–1500 cm−1 region. The attenuation of the carbonyl and hydroxyl signals suggest the involvement of intermolecular interaction between the polymeric matrix and TA. This behaviour is more pronounced in the polysaccharides-based films resulting in a broadening of the O—H stretching band due to the formation of hydrogen-bonding by the phenolic hydroxyl groups of TA. Mechanical characterization

The mechanical properties of the films were deeply studied to investigate the different behaviours of the films and how the different composition of them can affect the mechanical properties. At this purpose, TA unloaded and loaded films were analyzed in static conditions and thickness, stress-strain curve and tensile strength together with the loading fatigue cycles, were reported. Firstly, a clear relation between composition and mechanical performance were evidenced. The films composed by only Z showed limited deformability and lower tensile strength and undergo a decay in force amplitude over repeated loading cycle (Jia et al., 2025). The doping of the polysaccharides resulted in a slight increase in the stress-strain profiles and tensile strength and in a better maintenance of the force amplitude of the films over repeated cycles (Zhang et al., 2021). Moreover, a pronounced reinforcement was observed upon loading of TA (Fig. 3A-C). The TA loaded films showed an increase in the stress-strain profiles and in tensile strength values which undergo a shift from ∼3–5 MPa to ∼12–15 MPa, reasonable due to the presence of non-ionic interaction (hydrogens bonding) between the hydrophilic groups of proteins and polysaccharides and the multiple groups of TA, as previously reported by the spectroscopic data (Fig. 3D) (Lamp et al., 2022). The dynamic mechanical analysis was performed to further investigate the microscopic behaviour of the films. In particular, the tensile test was performed in the linear viscoelastic range between 1 and 100 Hz, and the elastic modulus (E') of the films was reported. The results (Fig. 3E) confirmed the behaviour of the systems found in the static characterization, resulting in a linear response in all the frequencies tested, with superimposable elastic modulus between the films but with higher elastic modulus for the TA loaded films compared with the unloaded ones. In addition, the torsional properties of the films were also evaluated in dry and wet state. As reported in Fig. 3G, the presence of TA increases the storage modulus of the films, confirming the enhancement resistance to shear deformation. Despite this increase, the hydratation reduces the storage modulus due to the intrinsic hydrophobic nature of the films and the plasticization act by the water molecules. However, the TA loaded films were able to overcome these phenomena thank to the supramolecular interaction between TA and polysaccharides (Gao et al., 2022). Surface zeta potential and proteins adsorption

The surface zeta potential of Z-based films was predominantly negative, with differences depending on their composition. Moreover, the literature reports the isoelectric point of Z at pH values around 6.5 (Oleandro et al., 2024). The results, reported in Fig. 4A, showed that the films were characterized by lower isoelectric point. In particular, for the Z-TA1% films, the isoelectric point was around pH 3.85, significantly higher than those Z-CS-TA1% (pH 2.50) and Z-MDx-TA1% (pH 2.40). These results could be attributed to the presence of the TA, a polyphenolic compound containing multiple hydroxyl and carboxyl group, in the case of films composed by only Z, and to the presence of sulphated, carboxyl and the hydrophilic groups in the polysaccharide backbone that can shift the point of net zero charge to more acidic conditions, in the case of the polysaccharide doped films. Moreover, proteins absorption was evaluated by the determination of the surface zeta potential after BSA exposure at pH 7.4 and by the quantification of FITC-Albumin after contact with films (Fig. 4B-C). Despite the negative zeta potential at pH 7.4, all the films were able to establish non-ionic interaction with albumin (pKa 5) that is negatively charged at pH 7.4 and less prone to electrostatically interact with films. In fact, it has to be considered that the electrostatic interaction is not the only mechanism, and other weak bonds could be involved supporting the interaction of albumin with the films (Miele et al., 2023). Moreover, FITC–albumin adhesion studies showed that both Z-TA1% and Z-MDX-TA1% were characterized by higher FITC-albumin adhesion compared to Z-CS-TA1%. These results can be explained by differences in surface properties. Z loaded with TA, either alone or in combination with MDx, maintains exposed phenolic groups capable of hydrogen bonding and able to interact with FITC-albumin, which is negatively charged at physiological pH. Mucoadhesive and bio adhesive properties

The muco- and bio-adhesion was evaluated by measuring the detachment force between films and substrate (Fig. 5). At this purpose, mucin and gelatine were chosen as model of mucus glycoproteins and protein-rich nature of tissue, respectively. The reduction in bio- and muco-adhesion observed for all films can be attributed to distinct mechanisms depending on the surface properties. In the case of Z-CS-TA1%, the dominant effect is the highly anionic character of CS, which produces strong electrostatic repulsion against the negative charge of the substrates. By contrast, the lower adhesion of Z-TA1% and Z-MDX-TA1% is not primarily charge-driven but could be related to the different properties such as hydrophilicity/hydrophobicity, surface topography and zeta potential. In particular, the higher hydrophobicity of Z-TA1% films could limit the interaction with substrates, while the higher surface roughness found in Z-MDx-TA1% could impair the physical contact between films surfaces and substrates, resulting in lower adhesion. TA functional properties

Moreover, TA release was evaluated over time. The results (Fig. 5B) suggest a biphasic release for all the films, with an initial burst release followed by plateau value. This behaviour could be attributed to the interaction governing the release profile, in fact, the common interaction between TA and the polymeric matrix are non-covalent interaction, considered as weak interactions. Despite the quantitative release of TA for all the films, the different composition could affect TA release. In particular, CS and MDx doped films were characterized by slower release than those composed by only Z due to hydrogen bonding between TA and the negative charge of CS and hydrophilic groups of MDx. Lastly, the antioxidant activity was evaluated by DPPH assay, which confirmed the antioxidant activity of TA and closely related to TA release, compared with ascorbic acid, as a positive control (Coroli et al., 2021). Circular dichroism (CD) spectra of native fibrinogen before and after contact with TA (Fig. 6C) showed detectable conformational changes compared to the native protein spectrum. Specifically, alterations in the characteristic negative bands suggest a partial modification of the secondary structure, due to TA-fibrinogen interactions mediated by hydrogen bonding and hydrophobic contacts. Sweeling and degradation profiles

The swelling ratio % and degradation profiles of the films with and without TA in cell medium were performed over time. All films showed high swelling ratio (%) within the first hours and reaching a plateau value after this time. The results showed a clear influence of TA loading (Fig. 7A-B). In particular, the TA loaded films exhibited a reduced swelling ratio (%) compared to the TA unloaded ones, suggesting that TA–polymer interactions limited fluids uptake. This behaviour is related to the formation of additional hydrogen bonds between TA and the polymeric chains, resulting in a more compact network architecture and decreased chain mobility. In particular, Z-CS-TA1% films exhibited the highest swelling ratio, related to the hydrophilic and polyanionic character of CS, which promotes extensive fluids uptake. Moreover, Z-MDx-TA1% films displayed intermediate swelling, while Z-TA1% films showed the lowest swelling capacity, consistent with the more hydrophobic properties of the films and in agreement with the degradation profiles, that support these results. The degradation profiles, which were evaluated at 37 °C under gentle dynamic conditions to mimic the continuous motion of the peritoneal environment, can be related to the swelling behaviour. Films with higher water uptake generally exhibited greater mass loss over time, suggesting an enhanced hydration of the polymeric chains, diffusion processes, and progressive matrix degradation (Lenzuni et al., 2021). In fact, the higher swelling ratio % of the CS doped films the higher degradation of them. By contrast, despite the intermediate swelling ratio % of the MDx doped films, they were characterized by superimposable degradation compared with CS doped ones, probably related to the higher affinity of the polysaccharide structure with the environments (Zhou et al., 2017).Fig. 7Swelling and degradation profiles in cell medium of (A) TA unloaded films and (B) TA loaded films. In vitro characterization and CLSM

The in vitro characterization was performed to assess the cytotoxicity and the ability of the films to promote cell proliferation according to the ISO standard guidelines. Two cell models were selected for the study: Caco-2 since they mimic the epithelial monolayers of the luminal surface, and NHDF fibroblasts that represent the stromal/serosal compartment, where prevention of fibroblast and matrix deposition is central to limiting postoperative adhesion. In addition, both direct and indirect contact of the films were performed. In Fig. 8A are reported the CLSM images of the NHDF seeded onto film surfaces. As evidenced, a limited number of adhered cells on the film surfaces are present after 6 days. This reduced cellular attachment is consistent with the intended goal of the films since their capability to reduce excessive fibroblast colonization and acting how physical barrier limiting cellular infiltration. These results are attributed to the intrinsic surface properties of the systems, such as the moderate hydrophobicity and the limited adhesive properties. On the other hand, the indirect contact results, reported in Fig. 8B-D, suggested that the films were biocompatible and didn't release toxic components and were able to promote cell proliferation after 3 and 6 days. Despite the higher biocompatibility, slight differences can be evidenced depending on their composition. In general, polysaccharide doping promoted cell proliferation with a more pronounced effects towards Caco-2 (Fig. 8C) than in fibroblast (Fig. 8B) and between polysaccharides, CS doping seems to better support cell proliferation than MDx doped ones. This result could be related to the different chemical nature of the polysaccharides': CS is a native glycosaminoglycan that mimic the extracellular matrix supporting cell proliferation, while MDx provide a more hydrophilic environment maintaining cell in proliferative conditions. CLSM imaging support these results (Fig. 8D). In particular, cell morphology suggests a well-organized cell layers with a healthy nuclei and cytoskeleton, characteristic of proliferative cell. In vivo biocompatibility

The preclinical study on safety suggests that all the formulations are biocompatible in a murine model (Fig. 9). H&E staining does not show any signs of inflammation or foreign body reaction. The damaged tissues caused by films implants are fully recovered, with completely and correctly reformed epidermal layers. In particular, Z-TA1% films promote the reconstitution of the underlying connective tissue and the reorganization of the papillary and reticular dermis areas, which are characterized by thin and thick collagen bundles and neoformed skin appendages. In addition, the CS and MDx doped films not only enables complete organization of collagen in the papillary and reticular areas but also supports the presence of hair follicles and sebaceous glands. Moreover, haemoglobin release % was also evaluated to confirm the biocompatibility of the films. A haemoglobin releases higher than 5% is not accepted to consider a system as biocompatible. However, Z-TA1% films were characterized by higher HR% than Z-CS-TA1% and Z-MDx-TA1%, suggesting a higher destabilization of the erythrocyte membranes. Conclusion

In this work Z based films doped with CS and MDx and loaded with TA were successfully developed as bioactive material to prevent post-surgery adhesion. The films were characterized by smooth surfaces and balanced roughness, by suitable mechanical properties including stretching and twisting together with the young modulus (YM) with differences depending on their composition. Moreover, the interaction with the biological substrates was investigated together with the proteins adsorption, resulting in a lower adhesion (muco and bio-adhesion) and different protein adsorption depending by the presence of polysaccharides and TA that affect this behaviour. The swelling ratio (sw%), TA release and the antioxidant properties were also studied. The swelling ratio of the polysaccharide doped films were higher since their intrinsic hydrophilicity and a quantitative release of TA were observed within 24 h resulting in a related antioxidant activity over time. The in vitro and in vivo studied showed a pronounced biocompatibility towards Caco-2 and NHDF cells along with their capabilities to promote cell proliferation over time. In addition, the in vivo biocompatibility suggested that all the films were biocompatible in a murine model without any signs of inflammation of body reaction.

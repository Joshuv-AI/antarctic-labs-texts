Predictive analysis on mechanical properties, material flow and thermal analysis of friction stir welded dissimilar AA2014/AA7075 Al-alloy joints using machine learning
Authors: R Venkateswara Rao, M Senthil Kumar, M Vanmathi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAluminum alloys are the most ideal materials for aerospace applications, and joining of a light weighted materials with an eco-friendly method is encouraging ongoing research into the green welding technique. In the current work solid-state joints of 10 mm thick AA2014/AA7075 Al-alloys dissimilar material joints with hybrid pin profiles were fabricated by using Friction Stir Welding (FSW) technique. The microstructure and mechanical properties of weld joints, temperature analysis of the welds processed under various conditions is investigated. An Artificial Neural Network (ANN) model is used to predict the mechanical properties of the welded joints fabricated at different conditions. The weld joints processed with hybrid pentagonal pin profile exhibits the highest UTS and YS measuring as 294 and 267 MPa respectively. The ANN showed strong prediction using a dataset of 27 experimentally obtained results providing a reliable data-driven method for forecasting UTS and optimizing FSW parameters. The weld joints processed at 900 rpm 31. 5 mm/min 2° tilt angle 0 mm tool offset and pentagonal pin profile revealed 62% of joint efficiency, which is suitable for the suggested AWS requirements for aerospace applications must be met by 60%. ANN model provided a good agreement with experimental results (R² = 0.93). Introduction

Aluminium alloys remain as an ideal candidate in the aerospace industry due to their low density, high strength to weight ratio and corrosion resistance, which are critical in aerospace applications. As the reducing structural weight improved the fuel efficiency, increased payload capacity, and enhanced overall performance1. Different Al-alloy compositions and weld joints have been developed over many years by researchers and industrialists, which are mostly utilized in the aerospace, military, transportation, rail, and automotive industries2,3.

Friction stir welding (FSW) is an advanced welding process developed at The Welding Institute (TWI) in 1991 by W. M. Thomson4, its eco-friendliness is well received by the researchers and industrialist since its invention. In FSW process, a non-consumable rotating tool is used to join the two weld plates, due to the applied pressure and the tool stirring action, plastic deformation occurs below the melting temperature of the weld material. The friction stir-welded joint consists of different zones, named Nugget Zone (NZ), thermo-mechanically-affected zone (TMAZ), heat-affected zone (HAZ) and base metal (BM)5,6. It is evident from earlier works that, the welding temperature at weld zone never rises above 80% of the base alloy’s melting point7.

AA2024 Al-alloys are widely used in aircraft applications due to its high strength8. AA 7175 is the strongest alloy among all the Al-alloys; hence, it is widely used to fabricate the various parts of aircraft9. The lightweight and low-melting-temperature materials joining using traditional welding methods is not possible, because as the heat input control is not possible. Compared to the other fusion welding techniques, FSW process addresses thermal issues, porosity, etc.10–13.,. On the other hand, the FSW process remains the best technique to weld Al alloys14,15.. The heat treatable (AA7068) and non-heat treatable (AA5083) Al-alloys joints fabricated with triangular pin profile tool exhibits high strength18. The welds made with a dissimilar AA6061/AA202419, and AA2219/AA703920 Al-alloys processed by square pin profile exhibited high-strength joints compared with other pin profiles.

The post-weld heat treatment of AA2024-T6 Al-alloy FSW weld specimen showed an increased strength54. The development of high-performance Al alloy components showed suitability for aircraft and other industrial applications. In particular, the significant need to join AA2xxx and AA7xxx Al-alloys using the FSW process is an essential requirement for aircraft applications16,17. The AA2014 alloy is widely used in fuselage, wing, and supporting-structure construction, provides a range of benefits for space flight structure and aircraft. Aluminium alloys are also employed in aerospace to deal with sub-zero temperatures57. The AA7075 alloys are used in numerous fields such as aerospace to automobile, underwater, mineral processing industries, and transportation services and also in marines like ammunition, forks, automobile components, engine casings, recently in pipelines, etc.58. The Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs), provide an effective solution for modeling nonlinear, multi-variable problems that are inherent in welding processes. ANN models can establish strong correlations between process parameters and output responses21. AI applications in FSW have demonstrated for optimizing welding parameters offers better performance. The integration of AI in FSW is likely to improve the efficiency and reliability of welding operations, with significant industrial benefits59. The advancements in FSW process with AI-driven tools, face challenges such as data scarcity, computational complexity. The need for standardized frameworks and the future research must focus on evolving hybrid AI models, to enhance the process adaptability and reliability60. The deep learning artificial neural networks (ANN) showed the better prediction and this approach was used successfully to estimate the mechanical properties of friction stir welded joints61. The combined influence of hybrid pin profiles and welding parameters on material flow behavior, microstructural evolution, and mechanical performance in such high-strength dissimilar systems has not been comprehensively modeled using AI-driven approaches.

The present work aims to evaluate the influence of hybrid tool pin profile and weld parameters on the performance of 10 mm-thick AA2014/AA7075 Al-alloys joints. This work investigates the material flow, microstructural changes, and mechanical properties of the weldments. Furthermore, an ANN-based predictive model is developed and validated using experimental datasets to estimate the predicted mechanical properties for parameter optimization.

Pin profiles

The welding tools were fabricated from non-consumable AISI H13 tool steel, whose chemical composition has been reported previously24. Over the past decades, friction stir welding studies were performed using conventional cylindrical or other regular pin geometries25–27, whereas very few investigators explored polygonal pin configurations28,29. In the present study, specially developed polygonal hybrid pin profiles (Fig. 2) were designed22,23. The tool dimensions were selected30, maintaining a shoulder-to-pin diameter ratio (D/d) of 3 to ensure adequate heat generation and material consolidation. Artificial neural network model

A dataset of 27 experimental results was used to predict and correlate the relation between process parameters and responses using Artificial Neural Network (ANN) technique. The ANN employed a multilayer perceptron architecture with nonlinear activation functions and was validated by statistical error metrics (R²) and cross-validation. This model demonstrated the good prediction and optimization of the welding parameters. To capture the possible non-linear behavior, prevent inappropriate conclusions based on assumed linearity and provide robust optimization across the entire parameter ranges three levels required for each factor. L27 orthogonal array is a suitable and effective option for this experiment because it is built for three-level factors62.

Hardness

The hardness test was performed on the weldment to study the hardness at different zones measured from the nugget zone to the base material on either side of the weld joint32. Compared to the AA 7075 alloy side, the TMAZ and HAZ of the AA 2014 alloy side continues to be significantly softer. The material mixing during the welding has a significant impact on the weld zone’s hardness52,55. The HAZ that showed dense structure, is the softest region among all zones. Hence, the hardness and weld strength is lowest at this region compared with other zones33,34.

The base metal’s hardness for Al alloys lies between 142 and 145 HV on the AA2014 side and 145–153 HV on the AA7075 side. From the Fig. 3 (b), it showed that the measured hardness at the nugget Zone (NZ) varied between 130 and 142 HV. The experimental results proved that the relation between tool speed to feed rate defines the rate of heat generated during welding, which supports the material mixing at weld location. The joint fabricated with the hybrid pentagonal pin profile processed at 900 rpm tool rotation, 31.5 mm/min weld speed, 2° tilt angle, and 0 mm offset exhibited the maximum hardness of 142 HV at NZ, for the demonstrated sample 12. The hardness profile along the length of the weldment for the sample 12 prepared with a pentagonal pin is shown in Fig. 3 (a, b).

Hardness test details: (a) indentations on sample (b) hardness profile for sample 12.

The base metal regions on both sides exhibited relatively high and stable hardness, reflecting the precipitation-hardened condition of the parent alloys. Since, these areas are not subjected to significant thermal or mechanical influence during welding, their original strengthened precipitates remain intact. Moving towards the weld center, a prominent reduction in hardness is evident within the heat-affected zones (HAZ). This softening is attributed to thermal exposure without plastic deformation, which promotes precipitate coarsening or partial dissolution. The thermo-mechanically affected zones (TMAZ) exhibits an intermediate hardness, as these regions experience both elevated temperature and plastic deformation, leading to partial microstructural modification but not complete recrystallization. At the weld center, corresponding to the nugget zone, there is an increase in hardness compared to the adjacent softened regions. This recovery is primarily due to intense plastic deformation and dynamic recrystallization. This resulted in refined equiaxed grains that enhance strength through grain boundary strengthening mechanisms. However, the hardness in the nugget may still remain slightly lower than the base metals because of precipitate dissolution during welding. The overall hardness distribution therefore highlights the critical influence of thermal cycles and material flow on joint performance, identifying the HAZ as the potentially weak region.

Tensile strength

The Universal Tensile Testing Machine (UTM) is used to measure the test specimen’s joint strength, UTS and percentage of elongation. The tensile test specimens are prepared as per ASTM E8 standards35. The samples 15 processed at 900 rpm using a hybrid pentagonal pin profile exhibits the highest YS and UTS at 267 MPa and 294 MPa respectively. Tensile test samples displayed that ductile fracture occurred on the side of the weaker material between the NZ and HAZ, is found in the joints processed at a rotational speed of 710 and 1120 rpm respectively. The nugget area is normal to the specimen’s weld direction and located in the center of the weld plates. Our results are consistent with the earlier studies, that demonstrated low melting regions weakens the material strength. However, the studies revealed that the findings of nickel shot-peened joint was found to be stronger than the conventional FSW joint. The strength of the shot-peened joint increased by 7% in comparison to the untreated joint54. For butt welds AWS D17. 3/D17. 3 M:200X recommends a joint efficiency36, of 0.6–0.7 times the base material strength was achieved in the sample 15 that meets the AWS specifications recommended to validate the butt welds for space applications.

Further samples are etched with Kellers’s reagent (95% of H2O, 2. 5% of HNO3, 1.5% of HF and 1% of HCl) to study the grain structures at the weld area38. Material flow analysis

The positioning of material on the AS or RS side has a major impact on the material flow, because the factors like welding speed and rotation speed have an influence on the welding process39,40. The material distribution around the pin profile is shown in Fig. 6. The flow of material experienced was due to the load offered by the shoulder transferred by the pin to the weld materials, which provides compressive force to the metal. The shoulder helps to eliminate weld flaws that are typically brought about by inadequate compactness. Therefore, material flow is found from the retreating side (RS) to the advancing side (AS), because of the action of the tool shoulder. This flow is more pronounced in the alternative layers at the weld region. The weld joints fabricated with the hybrid polygonal square pin profile runs at a speed of 900 rpm, 31.5 mm/min weld speed, tilt angle of 2°, and 0 mm offset resulted in formation of defects free welds.

Analysis of weld defects

The micrograph for sample 17 with defects in nugget zone is shown in Fig. 7. The weld parameters are the key elements to the formation of weld flaws within the weld area41. The various parameters like, tilt angle, shoulder depth, applied load, tool rotating speed, travel speed, and pin arrangement42–44 needs to be considered carefully to produce defect free welds. The low or extreme heat input, are also causes for producing the welds with defects45. The studies exhibited that, lack of penetration of the weld material in the weld joint line is also the cause for the formation of weld defects at the nugget zone46,47. In this present work, FSW samples processed at 1120 rpm produced welds with defects compared with samples processed at other speeds. The analysis showed sample 27, exhibited more unfilled area at the weld zone, this was due to the effect of high heat input. This causes to flash out the materials, that led to the development of discontinued onion ring structure (Fig. 7). The analysis showed that, AA 2014 alloy experiences a considerable plastic deformation, exhibiting a low material mixing at the weld zone.

Machine learning insights

From the machine (ML) and deep learning (DL) perspectives, the provided FSW dataset can be framed as a regression problem. The tool rotational speed, weld speed, and weld pitch ratio act as input features, and peak temperature, measured temperature, and heat input serve as outputs. A model trained on this data learned that tool RPM is positively correlated with both peak and measured temperatures, while weld speed has a strong negative influence, reducing heat generation as it increases.

The pitch ratio, which combines the effects of RPM and weld speed, emerges as an intermediate feature that helps capture the interaction between rotational and travel speeds. The low pitch ratios tend to yield high heat inputs and vice-versa. DL models depict their ability to capture non-linear relationships. DL would be able to model these interaction effects more effectively than simple linear approaches. With small datasets, traditional ML algorithms like random forests or gradient boosting might perform better. With sufficient data, a neural network could be used not only to predict heat input and temperatures. But also to perform inverse optimization, suggesting the optimal combination of RPM and weld speed to achieve a desired thermal profile, potentially enabling real-time process control in FSW applications49–56.

A significant improvement was seen at an optimized TRS of 900 RPM, where UTS revealed the highest 294 MPa, YS reached 267 MPa, hardness increased to 142 HV and joint efficiencies improved to as high as 0. 62, indicating improved stirring and consolidation. Nevertheless, when TRS was increased to 1120 RPM mechanical properties declined with UTS falling to as low as 101 MPa, YS to 87 MPa and hardness to 85 HV. This was accompanied by joint efficiencies between 0. 21 and 0. 36, which can be explained by overheating resulted in excessive softening and inadequate material mixing. demonstrating its superior material stirring capability. Overall, the ANN-based deep learning approach demonstrated dependable mechanical performance prediction capability demonstrating that controlled offset moderate FR (20–31. 5 mm/min) and intermediate TRS (about 900 RPM) resulted in optimal mechanical properties with enhanced strength–efficiency balance.

An angle between 0 and 90°, an input power of 30 mA, a voltage of 40 kV, and a step size of 0.02o were all used throughout the XRD analysis process. The study examined how the development of intermetallic compounds (IMCs) impacts the different FSW weld parameters. Compared to some traditional pin designs hybrid polygonal pin profiles allow for precise control over the thermo-mechanical conditions in the stir zone which results in better more uniform and possibly thinner IMC layers. The reason being as the tool movement rotates the grains and tilts them around 90 degrees. For the sample 15, performed at a speed of 900 rpm, weld speed of 31.5 mm/min, using a hybrid pentagonal tool pin profile. XRD patterns for the sample 15, showed peaks correspond to the face-centered cubic (FCC) crystal structure of the aluminum matrix.

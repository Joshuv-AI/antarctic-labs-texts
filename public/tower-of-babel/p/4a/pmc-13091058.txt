Essential tremor-like phenotype in Fragile X carrier women
Authors: Deborah A Hall, Danuta Loesch, Tyler Svymbersky, Elizabeth Berry-Kravis, Bichun Ouyang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Fragile X-associated tremor ataxia syndrome occurs in individuals with a FMR1 premutation.•FXTAS is a neurodegenerative disease with onset after the age of 55.•Women FMR1 premutation carriers may have isolated hand tremor.•Onset of this tremor typically occurs much younger than 55, as early as 20 s.•Isolated hand tremor in women FMR1 premutation carriers is distinct from FXTAS.Keywords: Tremor, FXTAS, Fragile X premutation

Fragile X-associated tremor ataxia syndrome (FXTAS) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder seen in patients who carry a CGG repeat expansion premutation of 55–200 repeats in the fragile X messenger ribonucleoprotein 1 (FMR1) gene. FXTAS is characterized by tremor, cerebellar gait ataxia, parkinsonism, and executive dysfunction, associated with white matter degeneration,[1] especially in the middle cerebellar peduncles (MCP sign).[2], [3] Neuronal intranuclear inclusions, glial dysregulation,[4] astrocyte degeneration,[5] and cerebral microbleeds[6] have also been associated with the disease. The manifestations of the disorder are highly variable among individual carriers and the latest diagnostic criteria established in 2014 include at least one major clinical feature (intention tremor or gait ataxia) and one major radiological feature (the MCP sign) – for definite diagnosis; and one major clinical and one minor radiological, or two major clinical features- for probable diagnosis.[7] Although FXTAS was originally thought to affect men only, it is now recognized that the disorder occurs in women, albeit at a lower rate due to X-inactivation.[8], [9] A diagnosis of FXTAS is more difficult and problematic in women, since the MCP sign occurs only in about 13% of the affected women carriers.[10], [11].

The tremor in FXTAS in a study of 22 patients was reported to present in three electroclinical patterns: essential tremor-like (35%), cerebellar tremor (29%), or symmetric parkinsonian tremor (12%).[12], [13] The essential tremor-like tremor has been described as a small amplitude, bilateral tremor involving the wrist and fingers, which occurred during posture and action and did not worsen with intention. In FXTAS patients with cerebellar tremor, the tremor was reported as a bilateral proximo-distal tremor in the arms, associated with cervical or diffuse cervico-dorso-lumbar axial tremor. Parkinsonian tremor was characterized by a unilateral upper extremity rest tremor. In as many as 24% of these patients, tremor was not present at all.[12] Tremor on spiral drawing is correlated with cognitive status and white matter hyperintensities in the splenium of the corpus callosum.[14].

The presence of tremor in FMR1 premutation, who do not meet criteria for FXTAS, has been reported in a prior study focused on signs and symptoms in women carriers.[15] In this study, premutation carrier women without FXTAS, average age of 42, reported a history of tremor at a higher rate than controls (12% vs. 2%, p = 0.01), but did not have increased rates of tremor on examination.[15] A sibling study has also been conducted in FMR1 premutation carriers (n = 6, median age 61 yrs) that showed the siblings had kinetic tremor without gait ataxia nor white matter lesions on MRI.[16] Numerous reports of hand tremor in the clinic by our team and others, most noticeable with tasks done with actions of the hands by FMR1 premutation carrier women who do not meet criteria for FXTAS, necessitate another look at this issue.

There is a knowledge gap that this study aimed to fill. FMR1 premutation women, as young as early 30 s, have been diagnosed with FXTAS, a neurodegenerative disease with typical onset over the age of 55, on the basis of tremor alone. Some of these women have been seen to have tremor for decades without developing ataxia, other FXTAS signs, or FXTAS imaging findings and may alter plans for child-bearing or other major life events with a potentially erroneous diagnosis of FXTAS early in adulthood. The hypothesis of this study is that there is an isolated FMR1 premutation associated tremor in women who do not meet criteria for FXTAS. Clinical data from two independent cohorts, analyzed separately and combined, was used to test this hypothesis.

Methods

Data from FMR1 premutation carrier women from two prior cross-sectional studies investigating FMR1 premutation carrier women clinical features[17] and antisense FMR1(ASFMR1) splice variants[18] were included.

Cohort 1. Data from FMR1 carrier women residing in the US who met criteria for definite, probable, or possible FXTAS were excluded. Published diagnostic criteria for FXTAS were used and required a FMR1 premutation (55–199 CGG repeats), intention tremor and/or cerebellar gait ataxia, with supporting features of parkinsonism, neuropathy, moderate short term memory deficit or minor executive function deficit.[19] The majority of the FMR1 carrier women were white and non-Hispanic, except for two women who were white Hispanic. Radiographic criteria for diagnosis were not required. Controls included women who had normal FMR1 gene testing and were enrolled in the same cross-sectional studies. Each woman was seen and had a FXTAS Rating Scale (FXTAS-RS) performed during a live examination by a movement disorder neurologist, who was blinded to gene status. The FXTAS-RS includes items for eye movements, tremor (rest, postural, action), parkinsonism, gait ataxia and dystonia and shows clinimetric validity for the movement disorder seen in FXTAS.[20], [21] Inter-rater reliability was not assessed between the two sites, but both neurologists had experience in using the FXTAS rating scale and the originating scales (such as the Unified Parkinson’s Disease Rating Scale). Demographic features were analyzed using descriptive methods. The primary outcome measure was the total FXTAS-RS score compared between FMR1 carriers and controls. Secondary analysis included the primary outcome adjusted for age, CGG size and molecular measures, to include FMR1 and ASFMR1 protein expression levels, using linear regression and comparison of scores on individual tremor items on the rating scale using chi-square or Fisher’s exact.

Cohort 2. The second source of data were FMR1 carrier women residing in Australia, and first tested jointly by two neurologists (ES and DZL) within a 2008–2010 project supported by a research grant from the National Health and Medical Research of Australia (NHMRC). These women had originally been ascertained either through their fragile X syndrome children diagnosed at the Genetic Counselling clinics in Victoria and South Australia or were more distant relatives of these probands identified through cascade testing. Except for one (Thai) woman, all participants were white. As in Cohort 1, none of the participants fulfilled the diagnostic criteria of FXTAS or reported tremor or any other motor impairment, and all considered themselves in good general health. The individual scores for Drawing Spirals and Handwriting were based on the Clinical Tremor Scale (the same items which are also used in the FXTAS-RS); the scores for Finger to Nose test were based on item 11 of the ICARS scale; all scores ranged from 0 to 4.

Post hoc analyses

A post hoc power analysis showed there is low power for the analyses with the spiral drawing A (0.05) and B (0.49). However, differences in the handwriting scores were seen when carriers with and without the antisense splice FMR1 were compared. Patients with a spiral score of 0 had a median splice level of 0.69 (0.18–3.52), which was significantly lower compared to those patients with spiral scores > 0 at 5.64 (2.26–12.58, p = 0.03).

Discussion

This study shows that there is tremor seen with action in some FMR1 premutation women who do not meet criteria for FXTAS. The presence of tremor is seen even in younger premutation carriers, in contrast to individuals with FXTAS who present over the age of 55. This is consistent with prior literature in this area that has shown increased self-reported tremor in daughters of FXTAS men and in some studies, tremor on neurological examination in premutation women.[22].

It is unclear if this phenotype is a forme fruste of FXTAS or whether this represents a separate tremor disorder that may or may not be neurodegenerative. Only one study has been published reported autopsy results of premutation women.[23] In this study, four FXTAS women with tremor and one premutation carrier woman with tremor (without FXTAS) were reported and all four had intranuclear inclusions classic for FXTAS. The premutation woman without FXTAS met pathologic criteria for Alzheimer disease. In a case series of 10 premutation women from two medical centers, 3 were identified to have tremor, but did not meet diagnostic criteria for FXTAS based on clinical findings only and were labeled “preFXTAS” or “prodromic” FXTAS.[24] Neither current existing autopsy studies in women nor clinical data is sufficient to support a forme fruste of FXTAS and autopsy confirmation of intranuclear inclusions and other pathological findings for FXTAS would be necessary to confirm that tremor in premutation carriers that occurs decades before the typical age of onset of FXTAS is truly the early start of motor symptoms rather than a second disorder.

Tremor that is isolated has been associated with later neurodegenerative disease in Parkinson disease (PD). In a study of 53 essential tremor patients who later developed PD, the essential tremor action tremor was bilateral in 90%, mean age of onset was 52 years, and mean latency from onset to initial of PD was 14 ± 15 years.[25] A long latency of > 20 years was reported in 31%. Epidemiological studies also report that incident PD is four times higher in essential tremor compared to controls.[26] The pattern of tremor that precedes a neurodegenerative disease diagnosis is similar to what may be occurring in the FMR1 premutation carriers. Comparison of tremor in FXTAS to essential tremor has been performed using tremorography and shows that the tremor of FXTAS has higher kinetic qualities compared to PD and is associated with bradykinesia compared to ET tremor, but this cross-sectional study did not include FMR1 premutation carriers with isolated tremor.[27] In addition to quantitative measures such as tremorography, the rapid development of use of artificial intelligence in the clinic may be leveraged to better improve diagnosis in the future.[28] This study provides only preliminary findings rather than implying causality or predictive value for FXTAS development. Prospective studies are needed to determine how often and when ataxia or other FXTAS signs occur in the setting of FMR1 carriers with isolated tremor. However, these studies may be challenging given the slow progression of the disorder and typical study timeframes of around five years.

FXTAS has sub-phenotypes that may be associated with a more favorable disease course. In those who have the onset of tremor first, some premutation carriers will continue to have isolated tremor for > 10 years prior to developing other symptoms.[13] Additionally, premutation carriers presenting with isolated tremor have lower rate of cognitive impairment at 38% compared to 69% in those with both ataxia and tremor at onset. Thus, those who present with isolated tremor may have a more gradual onset of associated motor signs and less cognitive impairment. A similar phenomenon can be seen in PD, where tremor-predominant PD is associated with less cognitive deficits than mixed or akinetic rigid PD presentations.[29] It is unclear how likely it is that a premutation carrier with isolated tremor will develop FXTAS, although the disease has now been discovered for 20 + years and this type of investigation may be possible retrospectively.

The main limitation of this study is that it is a retrospective analysis of existing data, rather than a longitudinal study, which can introduce selection bias and methodological heterogeneity. The sample size is small, homogenous ethnically, and not all premutation carriers had imaging studies to confirm a lack of diagnostic criteria. The small sample size yielded small power in the post-hoc analysis and suggests that the risk of a Type II error or false positive is high, necessitating additional studies to confirm our results. Large neurological studies of FMR1 premutation carriers with tremor (without FXTAS) have not yet been conducted, but are starting in the field and may yield additional data about tremor in younger carriers. Data validity may be compromised by using two datasets, taken from cohorts in the US and Australia. This was done to show that the FMR1 premutation tremor is seen in multiple groups. The age of the control group was also older than the premutation carriers, which could lead to underestimation of tremor in the FMR1 premutation carriers compared to population controls. Controls that are better matched by age should be considered in follow-up studies. Future studies should evaluate FMR1 premutation related tremor carriers for others disorders, such as thyroid disease, or factors that exacerbate tremor. The data supports that additional work in this area is warranted. Most importantly, answering these questions will allow reassurance to premutation carriers who present with isolated tremor if only a proportion will go on to develop neurodegenerative disease.

Relationship between TLR-2 level and clinical or radiological severity on patients with tuberculosis spondylitis in Dr. Wahidin Sudirohusodo hospital Makassar
Authors: Jainal Arifin, M. Ruksal Saleh, Risang Setyo Argo, Dave Kennedy, M. Andry Usman, Leonard Christianto Singjie
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Spinal tuberculosis or TB spondylitis is one of the most common types of extra pulmonary tuberculosis, which is about 15% of all cases. It causes severe morbidity, neurological deficits, and severe deformities in the patients. The growth of Mycobacterium tuberculosis in culture specimens obtained from infected tissue is the single gold standard diagnostic test for spinal TB. Toll-like receptor (TLR) is one of the important receptors in the first-line defence system against microbes. TLR-2 and TLR-4 are known to be associated with tuberculosis infection. Based on this background, the researchers were interested in examining the relationship between TLR-2 levels and the clinical and radiological severity of TB spondylitis patients. A cross-sectional study was conducted with patients diagnosed with tuberculosis spondylitis at Dr Wahidin Sudirohusodo Makassar. Patients diagnosed with TB spondylitis confirmed by blood tests, GeneExpert, and magnetic resonance imaging without prior treatment were included. Data analysis were conducted by using descriptive analysis and one-way ANOVA for bivariate analysis. The mean value of TLR2 levels in TB spondylitis patients was 9.1 g/dL. TLR2 levels in paraesthesia were significantly higher than normal (P < .05). Similar trend were analyzed on the motor neurologic status with TLR2 levels in paraparesis were significantly higher than those in normal (P < .05). There is a significant relationship between the TLR2 levels in TB spondylitis and their impaired motor and sensory function. Spinal destruction has been shown to provide significant relationship with TLR2 value in spondylitis TB.

1. Introduction

In 2017, around 10 million people were reported to be infected with tuberculosis worldwide, with Southeast Asia having the second highest prevalence, which is around 226 cases per 100,000 population. Spinal tuberculosis is one of the most common types of extra pulmonary tuberculosis, which is about 15% of all cases. Infection of the spinal cord usually causes neurological symptoms such as pain, weakness and cramps in the lower extremities. If left untreated, spinal tuberculosis has severe morbidity, neurological deficits, and severe deformities. All of these conditions cause impairment of function, particularly of mobility, leading to difficulties with walking, self-care, and sphincter function. This can cause physical, mental, emotional, and social disturbances.[1]

The spine is the osseous part most often infected by tuberculosis. Tuberculous spondylitis is caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis (MTB), which is an acid-fast, aerobic bacillus with slow growth. Tuberculosis bacilli can remain dormant for a long time. This bacterial infection causes a granulomatous inflammatory reaction, which is characterized by the presence of caseous necrosis, lymphocytes, epithelioid cells, and Langhan cells.[2]

The growth of MTB in culture specimens obtained from infected tissue is the single gold standard diagnostic test for spinal tuberculosis (TB). However, because of its very poor sensitivity, histopathological studies showing classic granulomas and staining of smears to identify acid-fast bacilli are considered as references for all other diagnostic modalities. Apart from indirect inflammatory serological markers, immunological tests have also been used with varying results. Toll-like receptor (TLR) is one of the important receptors in the first-line defence system against microbes. These receptors can recognize invading pathogens and release harmful molecules from dead cells and damaged tissues and play an important role in linking the innate and adaptive immune systems.[3] Research on TLRs sheds new light on the immunopathogenesis of the infectious disease tuberculosis. TLRs can recognize various ligands by the lipoprotein structure of the bacilli. TLRs 2 and TLR-4 are known to be associated with tuberculosis infection. Based on this background, the researchers were interested in examining the relationship between TLR-2 levels and the clinical and radiological severity of TB spondylitis patients.[4]

2. Material and methods

This study was approved by medical ethics committee of Hasanuddin University of Makassar and included patients diagnosed with tuberculosis spondylitis at Dr. Wahidin Sudirohusodo Makassar. Samples were taken using the consecutive sampling technique method. The data taken is primary data that was examined in TB spondylitis patients who visited in the period July–December 2022. The Kolomogrov–Smirnov test was performed to find the normality among the data. Statistical data analysis and data distribution used the Kruskall–Wallis and Spearman alternative one-way ANOVA method.

The inclusion criteria involved patients with TB Spondylitis through anamnesis, physical examination and confirmed by routine blood tests, magnetic resonance imaging, and GeneExpert with the range of age between 10 to 50 years old and no administration of TB treatment whatsoever. The exclusion criteria included patients that refused to participate and incomplete sample data while also patients with confirmed extrapulmonary TB other than spine, without or with tuberculosis treatment. We have also excluded patients with malignancy, cardiovascular disease, type 1 and 2 diabetes mellitus and autoimmune diseases, as well as immunocompromised diseases (such as HIV-AIDS).

4. Discussion

Toll like receptors-2 (TLR2) is a major innate immune receptor that recognizes various ligands present in pathogens and also serves as an endogenous “alarmin.”[5] Interactions between TLRs and ligands present in pathogens trigger intracellular signaling via the adapter protein MyD88 and/or Toll/ IL-1R which in turn induces interferon-β, thereby causing rapid activation of the acute inflammatory response in macrophages. In addition, acute macrophage activation is also mediated by inflammatory cytokine receptors such as tumor necrosis factor-receptor. Tumor necrosis factor-receptor engagement activates the transcription factor NF-κB and mitogen activated protein kinase (MAPK), and triggers the expression of proinflammatory cytokines, chemokines, and microbicide molecules. Although important for the immune response.[6]

MTB expressing TLR2 ligand. There are several MTB ligands which are TLR2 agonists, namely Lipoprotein 19kDa (LpqH). Mycobacterial lipoproteins, LprA (Rv1270), LprG (Rv1411c) and PhoS1. In general, it can be assumed that TLR2 triggers 3 functional responses in MTB infection which include protection, avoidance, and MTB regulation of the immune system. This mechanism does not really play a role in acute infection but plays a role in chronic infection.[7]

TB spondylitis (STB) is one of the manifestations of MTB infection and contributes to around 10% of extrapulmonary TB.[8] In vivo, it was found that TLR2 also plays a role in inducing bone loss through inflammatory processes, by inducing macrophage-evoked osteoclastogenesis in vitro in response to bacteria.[9] The inflammatory response also triggers TNF production. Furthermore, excessive TNF production is associated with various inflammatory disorders and is involved in the acceleration of bone metabolism which also plays a role in bone loss,[1] as occurs in STB.[6]

4.1. Correlation between TLR2 and vertebrae destruction

In our study we found a significant association between spinal damage (P < .005). This is in accordance with several sources who say that there are several etiologies that can cause damage to the spine, one of which is associated with the role of TLR2 in triggering bone damage. Based on the results of research obtained from Nature research published in the National Library of Medicine in 2016 explaining that the role of TLR2 and TLR4 is to assist the synaptic process of TREM-2 to regulate IL-1B and IL-6 in the initiation of secretion from MMP-2 and MMP-8 and osteoclast activation in experiments on cholesteatoma which induces an inflammatory process and then triggers the process of destruction of a bone.[10]

Then in a further study reported from Immunology Letters in 2007 explained that TLRs in this case TLR2 and TLR4 induce tartrate-resistant acid phosphatase and RANK-Ligand in Rheumathoid Arthritis conditions thereby triggering osteoclasts to undergo a process of differentiation. Thus, the study concluded that targeting therapy at specific TLRs might be an approach in preventing inflammation that can trigger bone destruction in the pathogenesis of rheumatoid arthritis.[11]

In other experimental studies also reported about how the role of Porphyromonas gingivalis as the primary etiology of periodontal infection which can develop into loss of alveolar bone density. In a 2013 study in The Journal of Immunology, it was reported about the role of TLR2 and TLR4 which mediate the process of P gingivalis in producing cytokines to activate TNF to create an inflammatory state which then progresses to the process of loss of bone density.[12]

Based on the 3 reports of previous research results, it can be concluded that TLR2 indirectly plays a role in the process of mediating inflammatory mediator complexes such as tartrate-resistant acid phosphatase, RANK-Ligand, interleukin-1B, interleukin-6, and TNF in conditions of acute, chronic infection or other conditions that have high potential for inflammation, and It then develops into an inflammatory state that induces destruction of a bone.[10–12]

4.2. Correlation of TLR2 and sensory disturbances

The study found a significant relationship between TLR2 and sensory disturbances in study participants (P < .005). It is currently thought that pain is only mediated by neurons. However, recent findings about the role of spinal cord glial cells suggest a different role for pathological pain hypersensitivity. There is increasing evidence that glial cell activity in the spinal cord plays an important role in the initiation or maintenance of pathological pain of various etiologies. TLRs on the neuroimmune surface and some evidence suggests that inflammation from TLRs activates in glial cells and affects nociceptive processes and causes a painful and persistent state.

The mean TLR2 level was found to be highest under anesthesia (16.93) and lowest under normal (2.29), while the difference in TLR2 levels based on sensory is very significant (P < .001) and the results of the follow-up test (LSD) showed that TLR2 levels in anesthesia were also higher than those in paraesthesia (13.72), but not statistically significant (P > .05) (Fig. 2 and Table 3). TLR2 levels in paraesthesia were significantly higher than normal (P < .05) the table above results show that there is a significant relationship between TLR2 levels and sensory neurologic status.

TLRs are pattern recognition receptors that initiate innate immune responses by recognizing the molecular structures of various pathogens, such as pathogen-associated molecular particles. Following tissue injury or cellular stress, TLRs also detect endogenous ligands known as danger-associated molecular patterns. TLRs are expressed in nonneural and central nervous system (CNS) cell types. Following tissue disruption and nerve injury, TLRs (TLR2, TLR3, and TLR4) induce activation of microglia in astrocytes and production of proinflammatory cytokines in the spinal cord, leading to the development of inflammatory and neuropathic pain. TLRs are novel in their role in processing pain and itching by increasing the excitability of primary sensory neurons.[13]

TLR2 specifically contributes to persistent pain through neuroinflammatory mechanisms in nociceptive pathways. Acute intrathecal delivery of TLR2 agonists induces lasting tactile allodynia. In this study, mixed spinal cord glial cell cultures from wild-type mice, incubation with damaged sensory neuron supernatants generated many pronociceptive inflammatory genes, such as TNF, interleukin-1B, interleukin-6, and iNOS. In contrast, glials isolated from TLR2 mutants, did not show changes in cytokine and iNOS gene expression in response to sensory neuron damage. Nerve transection injuries lead to increased TLR2 immunoreactivity in the spinal cord accompanied by increased microglial NADPH oxidase.[13]

4.3. Correlation of TLR 2 and motor impairment

In this study, a significant association was found between TLR2 levels and motor disturbances in participants. This is supported by the fact that TLR2 is one of the receptors that plays a role in the immune system. TLR2 is a membrane protein which are expressed on the surface of certain cells and recognize foreign substances and transmit appropriate signals to immune system cells. The first study of the relationship between TLR2 and motor disturbances revealed a role for TLR2 and TLR4 in p38 activation (mitogen activated protein kinase) MAPK and Jun N Terminal Kinase (JNK) during endurance exercise in rats.

The mean TLR2 level was found to be highest in paraplegia (18.30) and lowest in normal (2.73). we found that the difference in TLR2 levels based on motor was very significant (P < .001) which when the of the follow-up test was conducted (LSD) showed that TLR2 levels in paraplegia were also significantly higher than those in paraparesis (12.29) (P < .05) (Table 4 and Fig. 3). It was also found that TLR2 levels in paraparesis were significantly higher than those in normal (P < .05). The above results show that there is a significant relationship between TLR2 levels and motor neurologic status.

Several studies have shown that MAPK signaling can be increased in response to muscle contraction in the complete absence of systemic factors. Evidence from isolated muscle preparation studies suggests that p38 MAPK activation can be triggered by calcium/calmodulin- dependent protein kinase II, suggesting that the cytosolic calcium concentration in skeletal muscle increases during exercise. may mediate Exercise-induced activation of MAPK p38.[1]

JNK activation appears to be very sensitive to stretching because eccentric contractions induce greater JNK activation compared to concentric contractions. The results of this study reveal that the signaling cascade initiated by TLR2 and TLR4 has a major role in the activation of p38 MAPK and JNK during endurance running exercise in mice.[13]

Enteric nervous system (ENS) is a complex and interconnected network of neural and glial circuits located throughout the digestive tract, which exerts intrinsic control on intestinal motility and secretion. Together with the CNS, the ENS forms a bidirectional ENS–CNS axis, which is also closely related to the immune system. There is mounting evidence that TLR2 along with TLR4 is abundantly present in the plasma membrane of enteric neurons and participates in enteric neuron signaling.

TLR2 is a cell surface receptor expressed on a variety of immune and epithelial cells, as well as neuronal and glial cells in the central, peripheral, and enteric nervous systems. Activation of TLR2 by polysaccharide A from Bacteroides fragilis causes an anti-inflammatory response in B cells by producing interleukin 10 (IL-10), this indicates the role of the TLR2 immunoregulation complex in response to various pathogens that are often found in the intestine or gut. endogenous factors involved in neurodegeneration and cause motor disturbances in patients with Parkinson disease. In conclusion, in accordance with the findings conducted in studies on JNK activation, TLR2 can affect disturbances in muscle contractions by mediating MAPK p38,22 activation and also affect the ENS which indirectly contributes to causing motor disturbances in patients with Parkinson.[2] However, it is still needed further research into other effects of TLR2 on motor disorders.

The limitations of the current study was the cross-sectional study that was used. Therefore it is hard to determine the causal relationship between the change of serum TLR-2/4 and spinal TB together with its clinical symptoms. The sample size of this study is small. Therefore a higher level quality of study with bigger sample size might be needed in the future to provide a better evidence for its clinical implications.

5. Conclusions

We found that there was a significant increase in TLR2 levels in patients with TB Spondylitis with a mean value of 9.1. There is also significant relationship between the TLR2 levels in TB spondylitis and their impaired motor and sensory function. Patients and spinal destruction has been shown to provide significant relationship in the levels of TLR2 in STB.

L-cell-specific Abca1 deficiency impairs glucose homeostasis through defective GLP-1 production in mice
Authors: Luyang Gao, Yubi Lin, Qianqian Ye, Yuhang Zhao, Wenxin He, Shijie Lv, Ke Yang, Jia Chen, Zhen Zhang, Xinxin Xiang, Geyang Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Evidence suggests dietary cholesterol intake associates with higher diabetes risk, but mechanisms need further study. Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1), a glucose-regulating hormone produced by intestinal L-cells, has served as the basis for widely used diabetes therapeutics. However, how dietary cholesterol and intracellular cholesterol in L-cells affects GLP-1 and glucose regulation remains unclear. We studied ABCA1 (a cholesterol efflux protein) in L-cells using L cell specific Abca1 gene null nice (IntL-Abca1−/−) mice and ABCA1-targeted interventions in STC-1 and GLUTag cells. A high-cholesterol diet induced mouse glucose intolerance and reduced GLP-1. IntL-Abca1−/− mice showed worse hyperglycemia and GLP-1 impairment via disrupted caveolin-1–β-catenin signaling. Abca1 overexpression and cholesterol depletion in STC-1 and GLUTag cells enhanced the CAV1—β-catenin pathway and GLP-1 secretion, whereas cholesterol loading, Abca1 siRNA knockdown, and treatment with probucol (an ABCA1 inhibitor) produced opposite effects. The findings of this study confirm that ABCA1 is a key regulator in maintaining cholesterol homeostasis in intestinal L cells and in the synthesis and secretion of GLP-1. Moreover, the regulatory effect of ABCA1 on GLP-1 is mediated through the CAV1–β-catenin signaling pathway. These observations further uncover promising therapeutic targets for metabolic disorders linked to diabetes.

Introduction

Diabetes has emerged as a global health crisis, with individuals living with the condition facing a significantly higher risk of developing serious health complications, including heart attack, stroke, and kidney failure1, 2, 3. Nutritional epidemiology, through meta-analyses, identifies high-cholesterol diets as a dietary pattern associated with an increased risk of type 2 diabetes (T2D)4. Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1), an incretin hormone derived from proglucagon and secreted by enteroendocrine L cells, plays a central role in glucose homeostasis by enhancing insulin secretion, suppressing glucagon release, and inhibiting appetite5,6. GLP-1 analogs and GLP-1 receptor agonists are widely utilized in type 2 diabetes management7. Enteroendocrine L-cells secrete GLP-1 in response to various macronutrients, including carbohydrates, amino acids, and lipids, and each of these nutrients activates distinct molecular pathways8. Carbohydrate-stimulated GLP-1 release primarily involves sodium-glucose transporter 1 (SGLT1) and K+ATP channel9, 10, 11. Free fatty acids enhance secretion via free fatty acid receptor 1 (FFAR1) and GPR11912, 13, 14. Protein digestion products promote GLP-1 secretion largely through peptide transporter 1 (PEPT1) and the calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR)15,16. Bile acid sequestrants (BAS), a class of drugs used for decades to treat hypercholesterolemia by binding intestinal bile acids, have demonstrated unexpected benefits in type 2 diabetes (T2D) management17. Emerging evidence suggests that their glucose-lowering effects may be mediated through stimulation of GLP-1 secretion from intestinal L-cells18,19. However, the influence of dietary cholesterol, particularly its accumulation in L-cells, on GLP-1 regulation remains poorly characterized. Cholesterol homeostasis is critically regulated by ABCA1-mediated reverse cholesterol transport, a process in which this transmembrane transporter facilitates the loading of phospholipids and free cholesterol onto apolipoprotein A-I (apoA-I) to generate nascent high-density lipoprotein (HDL) particles20. Impairment of this process leads to cellular cholesterol overload and subsequent disruption of membrane signaling platforms21,22. In the current study, we first generated L cell-specific Abca1 knockout mice, designated as IntL-Abca1−/−. Under a high-cholesterol diet, these IntL-ABCA1−/− mice exhibited impaired glucose metabolism and reduced GLP-1 levels, which were associated with downregulation of the ABCA1–CAV1–β-catenin pathway. In vitro experiments further demonstrated that both genetic and pharmacological modulation of ABCA1 regulates GLP-1 levels in STC-1 and GLUTag cell lines. Our results identify ABCA1 as a critical regulator of cholesterol homeostasis in enteroendocrine L cells and GLP-1 secretion, with this regulatory role mediated via the CAV1–β-catenin pathway. This study explains one of the potential mechanisms by which hypercholesterolemia induces glucose metabolism disorders, and simultaneously provides a novel intervention target for diabetes treatment.

Materials and methods

Genetic mouse generation

Abca1flox/flox mice were generated by Shanghai Model Organisms Center, Inc. (Shanghai, China). This model was generated by CRISPR/Cas9 system in C57BL/6J mouse background. An Abca1 donor vector containing flox sites flanking Exon45–46 of Abca1 gene was created. Four sgRNA sites target Intron44 and Intron46 were in vitro transcription. Two sgRNA target sites for Intron44 were 5′-GAGTCTATACAACTGCAATATGG-3′ and 5′-AGTCTATACAACTGCAATATGGG-3′. Two sgRNA sites for Intron46 were 5′-TGGCTCGGCTGTACCTTTACAGG-3′ and 5′-TGAGTGGAGCTTGTCTACTCAGG-3′. The donor vector with two gRNA and Cas9 mRNA was microinjected into C57BL/6J fertilized eggs. F0 generation mice positive for homologous recombination were identified by long PCR. The primers (P1–P4) used for genotyping the correct homology recombination were P1: 5′-TGTCCACCCACGAAGTGATG-3′ and P2: 5′-GCTCCCACAATCACACAGGA-3′ for the correct 5′ homology arm recombination, and P3: 5′-GGACCCCACAGCTTCTCTTC-3′ and P4: 5′-ATTATCCAGCCATGGGGCAG-3′ for the correct 3′ homology arm recombination. The PCR products were further confirmed by sequencing. The genotype of F1 generation Abca1 flox heterozygous mice was identified by PCR. Male and female F1 heterozygous mice were intercrossed to produce the homozygous Abca1flox/flox mice.

We crossed Vil1Flp mice with Gcgcre mice to generate L cell-specific mice (Vil1Flp::Gcgfrtcre), and these mice have been validated23,24.

To generate L cell-specific Abca1-deficient (IntL-Abca1−/−) mice, Vil1Flp::Gcgfrtcre mice were crossed with Abca1flox/flox mice, and genotyping was confirmed via PCR (Supporting information Table S1).

Animals and treatment

Mice were housed in a specific-pathogen-free (SPF) environment with regulated temperature and humidity, and kept under a 12 h light/12 h dark cycle. Except where stated otherwise, the mice had free access to either a normal chow diet (NCD) or a high-cholesterol diet (HCD), as well as water, ad libitum. Male C57BL/6J mice were randomly assigned to two groups, receiving either a standard diet or an HCD. Similarly, age-matched male IntL-Abca1−/− mice and their control littermates (Abca1flox/flox) were fed either NCD or HCD. All animal-related experiments were performed under the supervision and approval of the Animal Care and Use Committee of Jinan University (IACUC-20241119-05).

Cell culture and treatments

STC-1 and GLUTag cells were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 atmosphere.

For transfection experiments, cells were seeded at optimal density and, after 48 h, transfected with Gfp, Abca1, Control siRNA, or Abca1 siRNA using a liposomal transfection method.

Cells were plated in 6-well plates and treated with cholesterol (75 μmol/L), probucol (100 μmol/L) or NTZ (10 μmol/L). After 24 h, the culture medium was collected to measure GLP-1, total cholesterol (TC), and free cholesterol (FC) levels, while cells were harvested for biochemical analysis.

Cells were transfected with either Control siRNA or Abca1 siRNA. After 24 h, the cells were treated with methyl-β-cyclodextrin (MβCD 1 mmol/L) or its vehicle for an additional 24 h.

Oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) and insulin tolerance test (ITT)

After fasting, mice were weighed, and baseline blood glucose levels were measured at the 0 min time point using a glucometer with tail vein blood samples. The animals then received either glucose (3 g/kg) by oral gavage or insulin (0.75 U/kg) by intraperitoneal injection. Blood glucose levels were subsequently recorded at 15, 30, 60, 90, and 120 min post-administration.

Measurement of GLP-1 and insulin secretion

Samples were collected in the presence of diprotin A (0.1 mmol/L), and subsequently stored at −80 °C until analysis. Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) and insulin levels were quantified using enzyme immunoassay (EIA) kits in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions.

Biochemical analysis

Total cholesterol (TC) and free cholesterol (FC) levels in mouse serum, cell culture medium, and ileal tissue were quantified using commercial assay kits.

Western blot analysis

Tissues (ileal mucosa) and cells were harvested, and proteins were extracted. Samples were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to nitrocellulose (NC) membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk in Tris-buffered saline with 0.1% Tween-20 (TBST) for 1 h at room temperature. Primary antibodies were incubated with the membranes overnight at 4 °C. Following washing with TBST, membranes were probed with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at-room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) substrate, and band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software.

RNA extraction and real-time PCR

Total RNA was extracted from mouse ileal mucosa and STC-1/GLUTag cells using TRIzol reagent, followed by DNase I treatment to eliminate genomic DNA. RNA quantity/purity was assessed via spectrophotometry, and integrity was confirmed by agarose gel electrophoresis. cDNA was synthesized from 1 μg RNA using an oligo (dT) primer, random hexamers, and a reverse transcription kit (per manufacturer's instructions). Real-time PCR (qPCR) was performed with SYBR Green Master Mix (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) following the manufacturer's protocol. Each primer pair included no-template and no-reverse-transcriptase controls, with the control group as calibrator. Relative expression was calculated via the 2–ΔΔCt method. Primer sequences and amplicon sizes are in Supporting information Table S2.

Immunofluorescence

Paraffin-embedded sections of mouse ileum were subjected to deparaffinization and rehydration. Following antigen retrieval in citrate buffer (pH 6.0), sections were blocked with 5% bovine serum and then incubated overnight at 4 °C with rabbit anti-ABCA1 antibody (1:200), rabbit anti-CAV1 antibody (1:200), or mouse anti-GLP-1 antibody (1:500). Subsequently, sections were incubated with the corresponding secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Nuclear staining was performed using DAPI for 5 min at room temperature, and images were acquired using a laser scanning confocal microscope (Leica SP8).

In situ hybridization

Paraffin-embedded sections of mouse ileum were first deparaffinized and rehydrated through a graded ethanol series. Heat-induced epitope retrieval was conducted in preheated citrate-based buffer (pH 6.0). Sections were then permeabilized with Proteinase K (5 μg/mL) at 40 °C for 15 min. Target-specific probe hybridization was performed overnight at 40 °C in a humidified hybridization oven. Custom-designed ABCA1 probes included the following sequences:

Probe 1: TTGCTATCACCATGCCAAGTACGC;

Probe2: GGTCATCTGTGGCTACTATGTCCA;

Probe3: TGGAGCTTGTCTACTCAGGAGCCT;

and Probe4: CCCAAGGCTTCTTCCCATACTCCC.

Post-hybridization, sections underwent sequential stringency washes with SSC buffer. Signal amplification was achieved through incubation with pre-amplifier and amplifier probes at 40 °C for 45 min each, with intermittent SSC washes. Subsequently, fluorophore-conjugated label probes were hybridized at 42 °C for 3 h. Following final washes, non-specific binding was blocked with normal serum prior to immunofluorescence co-staining. Sections were incubated with a primary antibody against GLP-1 (mouse monoclonal, 1:500 dilution) at 4 °C overnight, followed by species-matched fluorescent secondary antibody.

Fluorescent images were captured on a laser scanning confocal microscope, and signal intensity was quantified using ImageJ software (NIH).

Key resources

Key reagents or resources are listed in the (Supporting information Table S3).

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean ± standard error of mean (SEM) or mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical significance was assessed using Student's t-test or one-way ANOVA with Tukey's post hoc tests. A P-value below 0.05 was deemed significant (∗P < 0.05, ∗∗P < 0.01, ∗∗∗P < 0.001, ∗∗∗∗P < 0.0001, ns, not significant).

Results

High-cholesterol diets impair glucose homeostasis while reducing intestinal Gcg expression and circulating GLP-1 levels

To investigate the effect of dietary cholesterol intake on GLP-1 levels in mice, we fed C57BL/6J mice a high-cholesterol diet (HCD) for 10 consecutive weeks. Biochemical analyses revealed that, compared with the normal diet group, the high-cholesterol diet significantly exacerbated glucose abnormalities, increased total cholesterol (TC) and free cholesterol (FC) levels in serum and ileal tissues, and was accompanied by a marked reduction in and circulating GLP-1 concentrations (Fig. 1A–F). ABCA1 mediates cellular cholesterol efflux to lipid-poor apolipoproteins, initiating reverse cholesterol transport and maintaining cellular lipid homeostasis25. As noted in a previous study, farnesoid X receptor (FXR) activation can suppress hepatic ABCA1 expression and consequently reduce plasma high-density lipoprotein (HDL) levels26. In the present study, chronic high-cholesterol intake markedly upregulated Fxr mRNA expression while downregulating Abca1 mRNA expression in the ileal mucosa (Fig. 1G and H). Notably, caveolin-1 (CAV1) has been demonstrated to modulate cholesterol efflux through direct interaction with ABCA127, whereas β-catenin signaling is critical for regulating intestinal GLP-1 production28. This study further demonstrated that a high-cholesterol diet stimulated FXR, while concomitantly downregulating the expression of CAV1, β-catenin, and proglucagon in ileal mucosa (Fig. 1I–L). Additionally, the ratio of intestinal CAV1-positive GLP-1 cells to total GLP-1 cells and GLP-1-positive cell number per villus were significantly reduced in HCD-fed mice (Fig. 1M and N). Consistent with findings in mice fed a high-cholesterol diet, intestinal mucosal samples from clinical patients with hypercholesterolemia also exhibited a marked reduction in the activity of the ABCA1–CAV1–β-catenin signaling pathway and the expression of proglucagon (GCG) relative to healthy controls (Supporting information Fig. S1). Mice with L cell-specific Abca1 deficiency show a tendency toward decreased GLP-1 levels when fed a normal diet

To clarify the cell-specific role of ABCA1 in GLP-1 production, we generated an L cell-specific Abca1 knockout mouse model. Since proglucagon (Gcg) and Villin-1 (Vil1) are not L cell-specific, we used a combined Flp-Frt and Cre-loxP system: a Flp expression cassette was inserted into the 3′UTR of the Vil1 gene to create Vil1Flp mice, while an Frt-flanked Cre expression cassette was inversely inserted into the 3′UTR of the Gcg gene to generate Gcgcre mice. Crossing Vil1Flp with Gcgcre mice produced L cell-specific Cre-expressing mice (Vil1Flp::Gcgfrtcre), whose L cell-specific Cre activity was validated in our previous study23,24. Then the Vil1Flp::Gcgfrtcre were crossed with ABCA1flox/flox mice to obtain IntL-Abca1−/− mice (Fig. 2A and B). In situ hybridization demonstrated that Abca1 was expressed in GLP-1-positive cells of wild-type mice but was specifically absent in the L-cells of IntL-Abca1−/− mice, while its expression in non-L-cell populations remained comparable to controls (Fig. 2C). Under normal feeding conditions, IntL-Abca1−/− mice displayed impaired glucose homeostasis alongside reduced levels of GLP-1 and insulin. Plasma total cholesterol was increased, whereas plasma free cholesterol and ileal cholesterol remained unchanged (Fig. 2D–K). IntL-ABCA1−/− mice fed a high-cholesterol diet exhibit significant exacerbation of blood glucose and insufficient GLP-1 levels

IntL-Abca1−/− mice exhibited glucose intolerance, reduced in plasma GLP-1 and insulin levels, and marked cholesterol accumulation in the blood and ileum compared to the Abca1flox/flox mice (Fig. 3B–I). Compared with control mice, the reduction in ileal proglucagon expression in IntL-Abca1−/− mice under a high-cholesterol diet (HCD) correlated with impaired ABCA1–CAV1–β-catenin signaling (Fig. 3J–P). Genetic and pharmacological modulation of ABCA1 regulates GLP-1 levels in STC-1 and GLUTag cells

Treatment of STC-1 cells with 75 μmol/L cholesterol resulted in significant increases in both intracellular and medium total cholesterol and free cholesterol (Supporting information Fig. S2A–S2D). Concurrently, cholesterol accumulation inhibited the CAV1–β-catenin signaling pathway and reduced GLP-1 levels (Fig. S2E–S2I). Our study also detected a co-localization of ABCA1 with CAV1 in STC-1 cells (Fig. S2J).

As expected, Abca1 overexpression significantly increased its own mRNA levels, as well as those of caveolin-1, β-catenin, and proglucagon (Fig. 4A–D). The increase in cellular proglucagon was accompanied by activation of the CAV1–β-catenin signaling pathway and enhanced nuclear translocation of β-catenin (Fig. 4E and F). Discussion

Excessive cholesterol accumulation exerts cytotoxic effects and serves as a molecular basis for a variety of disorders, including atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, liver diseases, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, Alzheimer's disease, osteoporosis, osteoarthritis, pituitary–thyroid axis dysfunction, and immune system disorders29. Consequently, cellular cholesterol levels are tightly regulated to maintain systemic homeostasis30. Reverse cholesterol transport (RCT) is recognized as one of the primary mechanisms for eliminating excess cholesterol from the body. The first and rate-limiting step of RCT is cholesterol efflux, which is mediated primarily by ATP-binding cassette transporters A1 (ABCA1) and G1 (ABCG1)31. Our findings demonstrate that, compared to ABCA1, the expression level of ABCG1 in L-cells is extremely low (data not shown), and no compensatory upregulation of ABCG1 was observed in the intestinal L-cells of IntL-Abca1−/− mice. Therefore, this study specifically focuses on the regulatory role of ABCA1 in GLP-1 production. Hepatocyte-specific Abca1 knockout mice recapitulated the Tangier disease lipid phenotype, exhibiting postprandial hypertriglyceridemia, markedly reduced LDL, and near-absent HDL32, while also developing selective insulin resistance that suppressed lipogenesis without impairing glucose metabolism33. Similarly, intestine-specific Abca1 deletion resulted in a significant reduction in acute cholesterol absorption34. Furthermore, adipocyte-specific ABCA1 regulates adipose tissue lipid content, glucose tolerance, and insulin sensitivity35. Beyond its role in lipid metabolism, ABCA1 influences endocrine function. Previous studies have shown that excessive cellular cholesterol impairs glucose-stimulated insulin secretion (GSIS) in pancreatic β-cells36. Here, we further demonstrate that ABCA1 is expressed in intestinal L cells. Strikingly, L cell-specific Abca1 knockout decreased glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) levels and disrupted glucose homeostasis, revealing a novel role for ABCA1 in enteroendocrine regulation.

Current evidence indicates an association between dietary cholesterol intake and increased diabetes risk4. Additionally, both a cholesterol-rich dietary pattern and genetic variants in cholesterol metabolism genes are linked to the risk of gestational diabetes mellitus37,38. Liang et al.39 reported that a high-fat and high-cholesterol diet exerts differential effects on the glucose and lipid metabolism of host animals, with changes in the intestinal microbiota and their metabolites potentially acting as a key underlying mechanism. Our findings indicate that patients with hypercholesterolemia exhibit elevated FXR expression alongside reduced intestinal ABCA1–CAV1–β-catenin signaling and GLP-1 production. We further demonstrate that intestinal L-cell ABCA1 modulates glucose metabolism, likely through GLP-1. This finding reveals a novel mechanism through which a high-cholesterol diet or intestinal cholesterol accumulation may contribute to glucose intolerance.

Notably, a bidirectional relationship exists between GLP-1 and cholesterol metabolism. Substantial evidence indicates that GLP-1 contributes to the reduction of blood cholesterol levels and alleviates intestinal cholesterol accumulation40,41. Our study clarify the pronounced cholesterol accumulation observed in both the circulation and intestinal mucosa of L-cell-specific Abca1-deficient mice, particularly under dietary cholesterol challenge. This phenomenon can be partially attributed to impaired GLP-1 synthesis and secretion in Abca1-deficient L-cells, resulting in diminished circulating GLP-1 levels. Consequently, this further aggravates cholesterol metabolic dysfunction, establishing a self-perpetuating vicious cycle. Taken together, these results underscore the essential role of L-cell ABCA1 in the maintenance of systemic cholesterol homeostasis.

Recently, caveolin-1 (CAV1), a scaffolding protein that organizes and concentrates certain caveolin interacting signaling molecules and receptors within caveolae membranes, has been shown to regulate ABCA1 and ABCG1-mediated cholesterol efflux probably via interacting with them42. Membrane cholesterol influences lipid rafts, coexisting with fluid domains of unsaturated phospholipids, and critical concentrations enable microdomain formation essential for signal transduction43. Caveolin-1 is capable of interacting with β-catenin44. Notably, silencing of caveolin-1 can inhibit the β-catenin signaling pathway45. In summary, caveolin-1 (CAV1), a structural and functional component of caveolae, interacts with ABCA1 to coordinate cholesterol efflux and is functionally linked to β-catenin signaling. The present study demonstrates that L cell-specific Abca1 null mice exhibit reduced GLP-1 levels in the ileum, a decrease associated with diminished CAV1 and β-catenin. Cholesterol loading downregulated CAV1 and β-catenin, thereby reducing GLP-1 secretion in GLP-1-secreting cells, whereas cholesterol depletion restored CAV1/β-catenin signaling and normalized GLP-1 production. Overexpression of Abca1 stimulates β-catenin and promotes its nuclear translocation. Conversely, either Abca1 silencing or pharmacological inhibition of ABCA1 suppresses β-catenin and impairs GLP-1. Considering the well-characterized function of ABCA1 in mediating cellular cholesterol efflux, we hypothesize that ABCA1 deficiency results in intracellular cholesterol accumulation. Such disruption of cholesterol homeostasis is likely to impair the integrity of lipid raft microdomains, which serve as critical membrane platforms for CAV1 localization, and may thereby influence CAV1 gene stability and transcriptional activity. Moreover, cholesterol-derived metabolites including oxysterols can activate liver X receptors (LXRs), central transcriptional regulators of lipid metabolism and potential modulators of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling axis46. Consequently, disturbance of this coordinated regulatory system may lead to altered β-catenin transcription.

Wnt signaling effectors, particularly β-catenin and TCF7L2 (TCF4), stimulate endogenous Gcg expression and promote GLP-1 synthesis in GLUTag cells. Pharmacological activation of the β-catenin/TCF4 pathway enhances GLP-1 production in intestinal L-cells, thereby establishing a regulatory mechanism for controlling GLP-1 biosynthesis47, 48, 49.

A limitation of this study is that fully establishing the physiological relevance of the proposed pathway requires functional interventions targeting key nodes of the pathway in vivo. Such as in vivo administration of β-catenin or CAV1 inhibitors, or generate intestinal L cell-specific Abca1 knock-in mice, which would directly test whether this pathway regulates GLP-1 and glucose homeostasis. These functional interventions are essential for confirming causality.

Conclusions

This study reveals that ABCA1 maintains cholesterol balance in intestinal L-cells, directly controlling GLP-1-dependent blood sugar regulation. High cholesterol blocks GLP-1 release through the ABCA1–CAV1–β-catenin pathway, explaining how elevated cholesterol worsens diabetes by impairing insulin function. Boosting ABCA1 activity could simultaneously improve cholesterol metabolism and restore GLP-1 production, offering a dual-target approach for treating metabolic disorders. These findings highlight ABCA1 activation as a promising strategy against diabetes-related cholesterol problems, warranting further drug development and clinical investigation.

Systemic Vasculopathy Associated With Homozygous RNF213 Mutation Presenting as Peripheral Pulmonary Artery Stenosis and Coronary Vasospasm Without Moyamoya Disease: A Case Report
Authors: So-Min Lim, Ga Yun Kim, Dayoung Pack, Jang Ho Lee, Jae-Seung Lee, Seongsoo Jang, Jong-Min Song, Dae-Hee Kim
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe report the case of a 19-year-old man who presented with exertional dyspnea and recurrent chest pain, with a homozygous mutation in the RNF213 gene and without evidence of moyamoya disease. Coronary angiography revealed significant stenosis in the left main coronary artery and ergonovine provocation testing demonstrated severe vasospasm, leading to a diagnosis of variant angina. Intravascular ultrasound revealed fibrous intimal thickening without atherosclerotic plaque. Pulmonary artery angiography revealed multiple segmental stenoses with a beaded appearance, suggestive of peripheral pulmonary artery stenosis. Right heart catheterization confirmed pre-capillary pulmonary hypertension. The patient underwent percutaneous coronary intervention for the coronary lesion, followed by balloon pulmonary angioplasty, resulting in marked improvement in symptoms, right ventricular function, and pulmonary hemodynamics. Genetic testing confirmed a homozygous RNF213 p.R4810K (c.14429G>A) mutation. This case highlights the systemic vascular involvement associated with RNF213 mutations, even in the absence of moyamoya disease, and underscores the importance of comprehensive vascular evaluation in young patients presenting with unexplained coronary and pulmonary artery abnormalities.Keywords: Coronary Vasospasm, Peripheral Pulmonary Artery Stenosis, Pulmonary Hypertension, RNF213 Protein

INTRODUCTION

The RNF213 gene, particularly the p.R4810K (c.14429G>A) mutation, has been identified as a significant genetic factor in moyamoya disease (MMD), a cerebrovascular disorder characterized by progressive stenosis and occlusion of the intracranial arteries. This mutation is predominantly found in East Asian populations and is associated with a markedly increased risk of MMD, with an odds ratio (OR) of 259 in homozygous carriers and 52.19 in heterozygous carriers.1,2,3,4,5,6 However, recent studies have indicated its involvement in extracranial vasculopathies, including coronary artery disease (CAD), renal artery stenosis, and pulmonary hypertension secondary to peripheral pulmonary artery stenosis (PPAS).7,8,9,10 Although MMD remains the most well-recognized phenotype, its absence does not preclude the presence of RNF213-associated systemic vascular disease.

We report a case of a young man with homozygous RNF213 p.R4810K mutation who presented with PPAS-associated pulmonary hypertension, vasospastic angina (VA), and obstructive coronary artery disease (OCAD), without evidence of MMD. This case expands the clinical spectrum of RNF213-associated vasculopathy and emphasizes systemic vascular involvement beyond cerebral circulation.

CASE DESCRIPTION

A 19-year-old previously healthy man presented with progressive exertional dyspnea and recurrent chest pain, worsening at night, over 6 months. He had been previously evaluated at another hospital, where he was diagnosed with idiopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) and significant OCAD involving the left main coronary artery (LMCA) and proximal left anterior descending (pLAD) artery, for which coronary artery bypass grafting was initially recommended. Right heart catheterization (RHC) performed at the referring center revealed a mean pulmonary arterial pressure (mPAP) of 37 mmHg, pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP) of 4 mmHg, pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) of 5.2 Wood units (WU), and cardiac index of 2.25 L/min/m2. Based on these findings, combination therapy with macitentan, sildenafil, and selexipag was initiated. However, upon transfer to our center, PAH-specific medications were discontinued to allow for a thorough diagnostic reassessment and to avoid potential confounding effects during hemodynamic evaluation. The patient had no history of smoking, cardiovascular disease, or relevant family history. Electrocardiography (ECG) revealed T-wave inversion in V1–V4, and transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) showed right ventricular (RV) hypertrophy and dysfunction, with a tricuspid regurgitation (TR) peak velocity (Vmax) of 3.9 m/s and an estimated right ventricular systolic pressure (RVSP) of 65 mmHg, consistent with moderate-to-severe pulmonary hypertension. Pulmonary function testing revealed a reduced diffusing capacity for carbon monoxide of 72% with normal spirometry, and a ventilation-perfusion scan showed multiple perfusion-inhalation mismatched lesions in both lungs. A 6-minute walk test demonstrated 470 m, which was lower than the expected range for his age group. Chest computed tomography revealed multifocal stenosis of bilateral segmental to subsegmental pulmonary arteries (which had been previously reported as normal at the referring hospital) and moderate stenosis of the LMCA. Coronary angiography (CAG) showed diffuse 50% stenosis in the LMCA and pLAD. Given the diffusely smooth appearance of all coronary arteries on the previous hospital’s CAG—suggestive of vasospasm—ergonovine provocation testing was performed, revealing severe spasm (Fig. 1). After vasospasm reversal, intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) demonstrated fibrous intimal thickening without lipid-rich plaques (Fig. 2). As the fractional flow reserve in the left main (LM)-pLAD was measured at 0.54, percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) was planned.

However, during PCI preparation, the patient suddenly developed chest discomfort, with ST-segment elevation in augmented vector right and ST-segment depression in V1–V4 on ECG, followed by massive hemoptysis and hypotension (80/48 mmHg). Repeat CAG revealed recurrent severe LMCA stenosis due to vasospasm, which was refractory to intracoronary nitroglycerin. Given the worsening hemodynamics and cardiogenic shock, the patient was intubated and placed on extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO), followed by successful PCI of the LM-pLAD. After stabilization and removal of ECMO, RHC confirmed pre-capillary pulmonary hypertension, with a mPAP of 44 mmHg, a PCWP of 4 mmHg, and an elevated PVR of 6.53 WU. Pulmonary arteriography revealed multiple segmental stenoses with a “beaded appearance” in both pulmonary arteries, consistent with PPAS (Fig. 3).

Pulmonary arteriography demonstrates multiple focal stenoses, particularly in the distal branches, leading to a characteristic “beaded appearance” (yellow arrowheads). (A) Right pulmonary artery. (B) Left pulmonary artery.

Genetic testing confirmed a homozygous RNF213 p.R4810K (c.14429G>A) mutation, establishing the diagnosis of RNF213 mutation-associated vasculopathy. Brain magnetic resonance angiography showed no abnormalities suggestive of MMD. The patient underwent balloon pulmonary angioplasty (BPA) for PPAS. Follow-up TTE showed improvement in RV function, and TR Vmax decreased to 2.8 m/s, indicating a reduction in RVSP. The patient’s functional status also improved, with an increase in 6-min walk distance to 540 m. He was discharged on aspirin, clopidogrel, calcium channel blockers (CCBs), and nicorandil with complete symptom resolution. The patient’s pulmonary hypertension was attributed to RNF213 mutation-associated vasculopathy. As no established guidelines exist for PAH-targeted therapy in this setting and revascularization was considered a more appropriate strategy, PAH-targeted medications were not resumed. However, 4 months after discharge, stress echocardiography performed during outpatient follow-up using a supine bicycle revealed a significant rise in TR Vmax from 3.5 m/s at rest to 5.6 m/s at 50 W workload, indicating moderate resting pulmonary hypertension and severe inducible pulmonary hypertension. Although the patient remained clinically stable without overt dyspnea, readmission was planned for further evaluation based on these findings, and repeat BPA was considered.

DISCUSSION

To our knowledge, this is the first reported case of concurrent VA, OCAD, and PPAS attributable to a homozygous RNF213 p.R4810K mutation, in the absence of MMD. Although this mutation has been extensively linked to MMD, and separately to VA, OCAD or PPAS, the coexistence of these extracranial vascular phenotypes in a single patient without MMD has not been previously described.2,11,12 This observation further supports the notion that RNF213-related vasculopathy represents a distinct systemic vascular disorder, which can manifest heterogeneous extracranial arterial involvement, even in the absence of intracranial disease.13

RNF213 mutations are strongly associated with VA, with a markedly increased risk in homozygous carriers (OR, 18.34; 95% confidence interval [CI], 5.15–65.22; P < 0.001), supporting a recessive pattern of inheritance.14,15 In this patient, the ergonovine provocation test confirmed significant vasospasm in the LMCA and pLAD. Notably, IVUS revealed fibrous intimal thickening rather than lipid-rich plaques, distinguishing RNF213-associated OCAD from conventional atherosclerotic coronary artery disease.16 Previous studies have suggested that RNF213 mutations may contribute to coronary vasospasm through mechanisms such as endothelial dysfunction, impaired nitric oxide production, and increased vascular smooth muscle contractility, leading to an exaggerated vasospastic response.17 Moreover, RNF213 mutation carriers without preexisting CAD have an increased risk of myocardial infarction, with a hazard ratio of 2.71 (95% CI, 1.57–4.65; P < 0.001).14

Beyond coronary involvement, RNF213 mutations have also been implicated in PPAS, typically characterized by multiple segmental stenoses, often presenting with a “beaded appearance” on pulmonary angiography.13,18 In this case, the patient exhibited multifocal segmental to subsegmental pulmonary artery stenosis, leading to significant right ventricular dysfunction and pulmonary hypertension, requiring BPA. Patients with RNF213-associated PPAS require a higher number of BPA sessions (7.33 vs. 5.18; P = 0.011) and exhibit higher PVR post-BPA (3.89 WU vs. 2.62 WU; P = 0.040) than patients with other PPAS types.19 These findings may reflect inherent vascular abnormalities in RNF213 mutation carriers, including intimal hyperplasia, impaired remodeling, and a propensity for restenosis.20 Indeed, restenosis following BPA is more frequent in RNF213-associated PPAS than in other pulmonary artery diseases, with a reported rate of 43.3% compared to 17.5% in non-RNF213 cases (P = 0.003).19 Although BPA demonstrated short-term hemodynamic and functional improvement in this patient, previous studies have indicated that RNF213-associated PPAS has a high rate of restenosis, with limited vessel remodeling capability after angioplasty. In such cases, the use of vascular stents may be considered to maintain luminal patency and reduce recurrence, particularly in lesions with poor response to balloon angioplasty or in cases with early restenosis. In addition, chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension has been associated with RNF213 mutations, with allele frequencies up to 3.8 times higher than those in the general population, further highlighting the systemic impact of this genetic variant.21 The prognosis of RNF213 mutation-associated vasculopathy remains uncertain owing to its variable penetrance and phenotypic heterogeneity. Although BPA and CCBs have shown efficacy in improving hemodynamic parameters and symptoms in PPAS and VA, respectively, as shown in this case, the long-term outcomes remain unclear, particularly given the high recurrence rates of vascular stenosis.

This case underscores the importance of considering RNF213 mutations in young patients presenting with unexplained vascular abnormalities, particularly those involving coronary artery disease, coronary vasospasm, and pulmonary artery stenosis. With the growing recognition of RNF213-associated systemic vasculopathy, genetic testing should be considered in patients with atypical vascular presentations, even in the absence of MMD, to ensure accurate diagnosis and prevent misguided therapeutic interventions. Early identification of this mutation may enable tailored treatment strategies that help prevent disease progression and reduce the risk of serious cardiovascular complications. Further research is warranted to better define the full clinical spectrum of RNF213-associated vascular disease and develop optimal management approaches for affected individuals.

Mechanism of Quercetin alleviated E. coli K88-induced Liver Injury in Broilers
Authors: Shuaishuai Zhou, Yuxin Fu, Qinglin Yang, Junhong Liu, Yao Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe present study aimed to explore the mechanism by which quercetin alleviates E. coli K88-induced liver injury in broilers. A total of 240 one-day-old broilers were chosen and randomly divided into four groups: control, E. coli K88, aureomycin and quercetin groups with six replicates per group and 10 broilers per replicate. Broilers in control and E. coli K88 groups were fed a basal diet, broilers in aureomycin and quercetin groups were fed a basal diet supplemented with 75 mg/kg aureomycin and 750 mg/kg quercetin until 20 days of age, respectively. At the 21st day of age, broilers in E. coli K88, aureomycin and quercetin groups were treated by 2×109 CFU chicken E. coli K88 gavage, while those in control group were treated by sterile saline (equal volume with E. coli K88) gavage, feeding continued for three weeks. The mechanism of quercetin in alleviating E. coli K88-induced liver injury was explored by 16S rDNA sequencing, non-targeted metabolomics and transcriptomics. The present result showed that compared with E. coli K88 group, 1) Dietary quercetin and aureomycin alleviated E. coli K88-induced liver injury to varying degrees in broilers, and quercetin worked better than aureomycin; aureomycin and quercetin significantly decreased content of GGT, ALB, ALP, TBIL, ALT and ALB/GLB (P < 0.05). 2) Quercetin significantly decreased the abundance of Proteobacteria, and significantly increased the abundance of Actinobacteria and Fusobacteriota at phylum level (P < 0.05). 3)The transcriptomic and metabolomic results showed that quercetin significantly increased expression of PISD and MBOAT1 (P < 0.05), and significantly decreased expression of GPAT4, GPAT3, PCYT2, SELENOI, PLPP5, ADPRM, LCAT, LYPLA2 and CDS2 (P < 0.05); quercetin significantly increased the relative mabundance of Glycerol 3-phosphate, Glycerophosphate(2), Phosphorylcholine, Glycerol-phosphocholine, Sn-glycerol-3-phosphoethanolamine(P < 0.05); aureomycin significantly increased the relative abundance of phenylacetaldehyde. The KEGG results showed that quercetin and aureomycin alleviated E. coli K88-induced liver injury in broilers by significantly regulating glycerophospholipid metabolic and phenylalanine metabolism, respectively (P < 0.05). In conclusion, dietary quercetin alleviated E. coli K88-induced liver injury by regulating flora and glycerophospholipid metabolism in liver of broilers.Keywords: Avian colibacillosis, Organ injury, Glycerophospholipid metabolism, Feed additive, Liver function

Introduction

Colibacillosis is resulted from enterotoxigenic E. coli, which is widespread and has caused huge economic losses in breeding industry in the world (Kravik IH et al., 2022). Mortality is increased, feed conversion rate and growth are reduced in broilers infected with avian pathogenic E. coli, and peak laying is shortened in laying hen, meanwhile, E. coli is usually accompanied with viruses, coccidia and other bacterial infection, or as a complication of other diseases, therefore, the treatment of colibacillosis is greatly difficult. E. coli is sensitive to aureomycin, however, there are many drug-resistant strains of E. coli, which limits healthy and sustainable development of poultry industry. E. coli challenge has become a serious problem for poultry industry in situation which antibiotics are banned in feed, reduced or limited use in livestock breeding. Therefore, search for feed additives to prevent and/or treat avian colibacillosis is urgent. Quercetin is a kind of flavonoid found in fruits and vegetables, it has become a potential feed additive of antibiotic substitution because of its antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, antiviral, immunomodulatory, anti-fibrosis, anti-malignant tumor, analgesic and other biological activities. It is of great significance for broiler breeding industry to clarify the effect and mechanism of quercetin in preventing and controlling colibacillosis (Georgiou N et al., 2023).

The liver undertakes main metabolic processes as a center of matter and energy metabolism (Feng B et al., 2013). Two-way communication between gut and microbiota, and liver through bile duct, portal vein and systemic circulation is called a "gut-liver axis" (Pabst O et al., 2023). The gut microbiota is a key component of the axis, contributing to development of liver disease by damaging integrity of gut barrier, affecting bile acid metabolism, and activating inflammatory responses (Hsu CL et al., 2023). The previous study of our team showed that quercetin increased growth performance in broilers challenged with E. coli K88, meanwhile, accidentally found that E. coli K88 challenge damaged liver, 500 and 750 mg/kg quercetin significantly alleviated liver injury, and 750 mg/kg quercetin worked better (Fu YX, 2025). Quercetin also alleviated liver injury resulted from various hepatotoxins by inhibiting inflammation, oxidative stress and apoptosis (Fang P et al., 2021; Ahmed OM et al., 2022; Gheshlaghi-Ghadim A et al., 2022; Soliman MM et al., 2022; Yu J et al., 2024). In addition, quercetin is an effective phytochemical component in treating various liver diseases including hepatitis, acute liver failure and liver fibrosis (Guo X et al., 2022; Parvez MK et al., 2022; Wu H et al., 2024). At present, most of the studies on the mechanism of quercetin preventing and treating liver injury focused on inflammation, apoptosis and oxidative stress; there are also few studies on the metabolic pathways of quercetin preventing liver injury, however, subjects of these studies mainly focused on mice and rats. Studies showed that hypericin prevented liver injury from acetaminophen-induced oxidative stress by regulating metabolites and enzymes related to glutathione by inhibiting Cytochrome P450 2E1 (CYP2E1) in mice (Hu C et al., 2020). Ni Y et al. (2024) found that paclitaxel alleviated CCl4-induced liver injury by regulating metabolic pathways including glycerol phosphate, choline glycerophosphate, glycerophosphate ethanolamine, linoleic acid and its derivatives in rats. Zhang X et al. (2021) found that 50 mg/kg quercetin reduced acrylamide-induced liver toxicity by reducing oxidative stress and inflammatory damage and regulating lipid metabolism in rats. Cao P et al. (2022) found that tartary buckwheat extract prevented alcoholic liver disease by regulating liver glycerophospholipid metabolism in mice.

In this study, the effect and mechanism of action in quercetin alleviating E. coli K88-induced liver injury were investigated using microbiomics, metabolomics and transcriptomics technologies in broilers. These results will provide important scientific basis for quercetin preventing and/or treating avian colibacillosis.

Materials and Methods

Study Design

A total of 240 one-day-old broilers were randomly divided into four groups: control, E. coli K88, aureomycin and quercetin groups with six replicates per group and 10 broilers per replicate. Broilers in control and E. coli K88 groups were fed a basal diet, broilers in aureomycin and quercetin groups were fed a basal diet supplemented with 75 mg/kg aureomycin and 750 mg/kg quercetin until 20 days of age, respectively. At the 21st day of age, broilers in E. coli K88, aureomycin and quercetin groups were treated by 2×109 CFU chicken E. coli K88 gavage (according to our preliminary test), while those in control group were treated by sterile saline (equal volume with E. coli K88) gavage, feeding continued for three weeks. All broilers were immunized against Newcastle disease and bursa of Fabricius at 7 d and 14 d, respectively.

Materials

White feather broilers (Newcastle disease, bursa of Fabricius,) were purchased from Harbin Yinong Poultry Industry (Harbin, China).

Quercetin (purity≥97%, R0896) was purchased from Nanjing Durai Biotechnology Co., LTD. (Nanjing, China), aureomycin (purity 100%, C822258) was purchased from Shanghai McLean Co., LTD. (Shanghai, China), and chicken E. coli K88 strain was donated by Professor Wang Jiajun, College of Animal Science and Technology, Northeast Agricultural University.

Feeding and Management

Animal experiment was conducted according to the regulations of the Experimental Animal Ethics Committee of Northeast Agricultural University (No. NEAU20200203). Broilers were raised in three-layers cage for six weeks, fed twice a day in the morning and evening, quercetin and aureomycin were mixed in basal diet, 24 h light and drinking water were provided ad libitum, room temperature was maintained at 32 °C to 34 °C for a week, and then gradually reduced 2-3 °C every week to 20-21 °C.

Sample Collection and Process

At 42 day of age, a total of 24 broilers (1 broiler per replicate) with similar body weight were randomly selected and weighed prior to sampling. Blood samples were taken from the wing vein and centrifuged at 4 °C (3,500 r/min for 10 min), and then the separated serum was harvested and stored at -80 °C for further analysis. The broilers after blood collection were killed by euthanasia, then dissected, a portion of livers was stored in -80 °C and another 1 cm of livers was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (BL539A, Purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd, Shanghai City, China) for further analysis, respectively.

Histopathological Examination

The liver was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, dehydrated, embedded in paraffin, and sliced into 4 µm-thick sections. The sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) (71014460, Purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd, Shanghai City, China) to assess pathologic findings. Under the microscope, the nucleus is blue and the cytoplasm is red.

Elisa Determination of liver function

Content of total protein (TP), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), albumin (ALB), total bile acid (TBA), glutamate aminotransferase (GGT), total bilirubin (TBIL-Z) and direct bilirubin (DBIL-Z) were determined by ELISA kit (70912, 70911, 70910, 70908, 70913, 70917, 70909, 70914,70915) purchased from Jiangsu Baolai Biotechnology Co., LTD., Yancheng City, China) according to the instructions in serum of broilers, respectively.

Liver Flora using 16S rDNA Sequencing

The sequencing was completed by Wuhan Jinkairui Biological Engineering Co., LTD. DNA was extracted using Magbead Soil and Stool DNA Kit TS001 (CW2556M, Purchased from Jiangsu Kang Wei Century Biotechnology Co., LTD., Taizhou, China), DNA was quantified by Nanodrop, and then V3-V4 region of microbial 16S rDNA gene was amplified using PCR according to bacterial primers 338 F (5′-barcode+ACTCCTACGGGAGGCAGCA-3′) and 806R: (5′-GGACTACHVGGGTWTCTAAT-3′). Library construction was conducted using the Illumina's TruSeq Nano DNA LT Library Prep Kit (KOD FX Neo KFX-201, purchased from Illumina, California, United States). 2×250 bp double-ended sequencing on a NovaSeq machine was performed using the NovaSeq 6000 SP Reagent Kit (500 cycles) (Reagent Kits v1.5 Purchased from Illumina, California, United States).

Liver Transcriptomics

Total RNA was extracted from livers using Trizol (R4801-02, Purchased from Beijing Tiangen Biochemical Technology Co., LTD., Beijing, China) extraction method, and the experimental steps were strictly followed according to the RNA extraction instructions. Then, a sequencing library was prepared according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Quality control and quantification of the library were performed by Nanodrop/Qsep/Qubit/Agar-gel electrophoresis to ensure that the effective concentration of the library was greater than 2 nm. Different libraries were pooled according to the target data volume, then sequenced on BGI or Illumina platforms.

mRNA Expression of Genes in Livers using RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted from livers using Trizol extraction method, and the experimental steps were strictly followed the RNA extraction instructions. cDNA strands from purified total RNA were synthesized using Revert Aid First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (K1622, Purchased from Thermo Fisher Scientific, Massachusetts, United States). Quantitative PCR (qPCR) was performed using The 2× SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (B21203, Purchased from Selleck, Texas, United States). All the primer sequences used in this study were listed in Table S1.

Liver metabolomics using LC-MS

The sample was processed and the supernatant was extracted using the method described by Warren et al. (2017). The supernatant was analyzed using ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography with Fourier transform mass spectrometry UHPLC-Q Exactive HF-XLC-MS after centrifugation (14000 g) for 15 min at 4 °C.

Chromatographic conditions: Agilent 1290 Infinity LC ultra-high performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) HILIC column was used for separating. Column temperature 25 °C, flow rate 0.5 ml/min, sample size 2 μl, mobile phase A included 25 mm ammonium acetate (17836-50G, purchased from Fluka, Niedersachsen, Germany) and 25 mm ammonia (16745-150mL, purchased from Sigma, Missouri, United States) with deionized water, mobile phase B was acetonitrile(1499230-935, purchased from Merck, NewJersey, United States); the gradient elution procedure was as follows: 95% B for 0-0.5 min, B changed linearly from 95% to 65% for 0.5-7 min; B changed linearly from 65% to 40% for 7-8 min; B remained at 40% for 8-9 min; B changed linearly from 40% to 95% for 9-9.1 min. B remained at 95% for 9.1-12 min. Samples were placed in an automatic injector at 4 °C during the analysis and randomly and continuously analyzed to avoid influences resulting from instrumental detection signal fluctuations. QC samples were used to monitor and evaluate the system's stability and the reliability of the experimental data during sample determination.

Mass spectrum conditions: Samples' primary and secondary spectra were collected using AB Triple TOF 6600 mass spectrometer (Triple TOF 6600, purchased from SCIEX, Massachusetts, United States). The samples were analyzed using a Triple TOF 6600 mass spectrometer after separating by Agilent 1290 Infinity LC ultra-high performance liquid chromatography (1290 Infibity, purchased from Agilent Technologies, California, United States) with electrospray ionization (ESI) in cationic and anionic modes, respectively. ESI source setting parameters were as follows: atomizing gas auxiliary heating 1 (Gas1): 60; auxiliary heating 2 (Gas2): 60; gas curtain gas (CUR): 30 psi; ion source temperature: 600 °C; spray voltage (ISVF): ±5500 V (cationic and anionic modes); detection range of primary mass-charge ratio: 60-1000 Da; detection range of secondary sub-ion mass-charge ratio: 25-1000 Da; primary mass spectrum scanning, accumulation time: 0.20 s/spectra; secondary mass spectrum scanning, accumulation time: 0.05 s/spectra. The secondary mass spectrum was obtained from data-dependent acquisition mode (IDA), and the peak intensity value screening mode was adopted. The cluster-removing voltage (DP) was ±60 V (cationic and anionic modes), and the collision energy was 35±15 eV. The IDA was set as follows: the dynamic isotope ion exclusion range was 4 Da, and 10 fragments were collected per scan.

Statistical analysis

Routine data analysis

The experimental results were analyzed by one-way ANOVA of SPSS 22.0 software, and the data were compared by Duncan's method. All results were expressed as "Means ± SEM", P < 0.05 meant significant difference, P < 0.01 meant extremely significant difference.

Analysis of microbial diversity 16S rDNA data

Low-quality Reads were removed from the data obtained by Miseq sequencing, and high-quality clean data were screened out. The screened clean data were analyzed by OTU cluster and species taxonomy, meanwhile, community structure at various taxonomic levels was analyzed based on taxonomic information.

Analysis of liver transcriptomics data

Analysis method of transcriptomics data referred to the research method of Chen SY (2022).

RT-qPCR data processing

The Ct value of each PCR reaction was read by ABI Stepone plus software, and ΔCt=the Ct value of the target gene - the Ct value of the internal reference gene. ΔΔCt=ΔCt of the experimental group - the mean ΔCt of the control group. 2−ΔΔCt was regarded as the expression changes of target genes in the experimental group, compared with control. The independent T-test was performed by SPSS 22.0 software, and the test result was expressed as "Means ± SEM", P < 0.05 meant significant difference, P < 0.01 meant extremely significant difference.

Analysis of LC-MS metabolomics data

Raw data were imported into XCMS for peak detection and alignment. Statistical analyses were performed using SIMCA-P and R software. Metabolites were annotated and pathways were enriched in the database using KEGG (https://www.genome.jp/kegg/pathway.html).

Results

Effects of quercetin on E. coli K88-induced liver injury in broilers

Effects of quercetin on liver structure in broilers challenged with E. coli K88Liver structure was normal, hepatocyte structure was full, obvious degeneration including porosity, edema and necrosis was not observed, central vein structure was clear, hepatic sinusoid was radially arranged along central vein, and obvious inflammatory cell infiltration was not observed in control group (Figure 1A). Liver structure was moderately abnormal, hepatocyte structure was loose, some hepatocytes were obviously edematous, structure of hepatic sinusoid was unclear, a small amount of obvious expansion and inflammatory cell infiltration was observed in E. coli K88 group (Figure 1B). Liver structure was moderately abnormal, hepatocyte structure was loose, some hepatocytes were obviously edematous, even vacuolar degeneration was observed, structure of hepatic sinusoid was unclear and minor expansion was obviously observed, and inflammatory cell infiltration was not observed in aureomycin group (Figure 1C). Liver structure was mildly abnormal, hepatocyte structure was loose, some hepatocytes were slightly edematous, and structure of hepatic sinusoid was unclear, however, obvious hyperemia, dilation and inflammatory cell infiltration were not observed in quercetin group (Figure 1F). The above results suggested that E. coli K88 challenge significantly induced liver injury, while quercetin and aureomycin alleviated E. coli K88-induced liver injury in various degrees, and the mitigative effect of quercetin on E. coli K88-induced liver injury was stronger than aureomycin in broilers.

Effects of quercetin on liver function in broilers challenged with E. coli K88

E. coli K88 challenge significantly increased content of Albumin (ALB), Alanine Aminotransferase (ALT), Aspartate Aminotransferase (AST), Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP), Total Bilirubin (TBIL) and ALB/GLB (P < 0.05), compared with control group. Aureomycin and quercetin significantly decreased content of Gamma-Glutamyl Transferase (GGT), ALB, ALP, TBIL, ALT and ALB/GLB (P < 0.05), compared with E. coli K88 group. There were no significant differences in content of Direct Bilirubin (DBIL) and Total Bile Acids (TBA) of the other three groups (P > 0.05) (Table 2), compared with control group. These results indicated that E. coli K88 challenge damaged liver function, and aureomycin and quercetin restored E. coli K88-induced liver function in broilers to some extents.

Effect of quercetin on alpha diversity of flora in liver of broilers challenged with E. coli K88

Quercetin significantly increased Simpson index of flora in liver of broilers (P < 0.05), compared with E. coli K88 group. (Supplementary Table S2). The result indicated that quercetin increased diversity of flora to a normal level in liver of broilers challenged with E. coli K88.

Effect of quercetin on diversity index of flora in liver of broilers challenged with E. coli K88

Shannon Curve was constructed according to the microbial diversity index of sequencing volume from each sample at different sequencing depth. The flattening curve indicated that the sequencing data were large enough to reflect the most microbial information in samples (Figure 2b).

Effect of quercetin on beta diversity of flora in liver of broilers challenged with E. coli K88: principal coordinate analysis and intergroup difference analysis

PCoA and PERMANOVA analyses based on Weighted Unifrac distance showed significant differences of flora composition in the four groups in liver of broilers treated with E. coli K88, aureomycin and quercetin, and interpretation rates of PCoA1 and PCoA2 axes were 39.15% and 16.86%, respectively (Figure 2c, 2d).

Effects of quercetin on distribution of flora in liver of broilers challenged with E. coli K88 at phyla and genus levels

E. coli K88 challenge significantly increased the abundance of Firmicutes (P < 0.05) and significantly decreased the abundance of Desulfobacterota (P < 0.05), compared with control group. Quercetin significantly decreased the abundance of Proteobacteria (P < 0.05) and had a tendency to increase the abundance of Actinobacteria and Fusobacteriota at phylum level, compared with E. coli K88 group (Figure 2e and Supplementary Table S3). E. coli K88 challenge significantly increased the abundance of Escherich-Shigella genera (P < 0.05) at genus level, compared with control group (Figure 2f and Supplementary Table S4). These results indicated that aureomycin did not regulate flora, while quercetin adjusted the disturbance of liver flora by reducing the abundance of harmful bacteria, including Proteobacteria and Escherich-Shigella in liver of broilers challenged with E. coli K88.

Lefse analysis of quercetin influencing differential bacteria of flora in liver of broilers challenged with E. coli K88

The dominant flora in the four groups were explored using Cladogram and histogram of LDA (Linear Discriminant Analysis) value distribution. Sphingomonadales, Sphingomonadaceae, Sphingomonas, Ralstonia and Burkholderiaceae, Burkholderiales and Alphaproteobacteria were the dominant bacteria in control group; Corynebacterium_glutamicum, Clostridium_perfringens, ASF356 genus, Weissella_jogaejeotgali and Fecal Bacteroidetes species were the dominant bacteria in E. coli K88 group; Escherichia_Shigella and Proteobacteria were the dominant bacteria in aureomycin group; Bacteroidaceae, Bacteroides and Firmicutes were the dominant bacteria in quercetin group (Supplementary Figure S1 a1,b1).

E. coli K88 challenge decreased the abundance of Veillonellaceae, compared with control group, and quercetin increased the abundance of Veillonellaceae, compared with E. coli K88 group (Supplementary Figure S1 a2-a4, b2-b4). The above results suggested that E. coli K88 challenge decreased the dominant flora, quercetin and aureomycin increased the dominant flora, however, these dominant floras were still lower than control group. Quercetin alleviated the decrease in the abundance of Veillonellaceae resulting from E. coli K88 challenge; it indicated that quercetin regulated E. coli K88-induced disorders of liver flora to some extent.

Effect of quercetin on transcriptomics in liver of broilers challenged with E. coli K88

The results showed that E. coli K88, quercetin and aureomycin groups were significantly separated from control group, and there was some overlap between K88 and aureomycin groups (Supplementary Figure S2). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were screened out according to Fold Change≥2 and FDR < 0.05. 62 DEGs were found in E. coli K88 group, compared with control group. Four and 1656 DEGs were found in aureomycin and quercetin groups, compared with E. coli K88 group (Supplementary Table S5). The overall distribution of DEGs was displayed in a volcano plot (Supplementary Figure S3). Quercetin significantly regulated gene expression in liver of broilers challenged with E. coli K88 according to a heat map from cluster analysis of DEGs (Supplementary Figure S4). Thirty-two overlapping genes were found between E. coli K88 and control groups, quercetin and E. coli K88 groups, and the 32 genes were up-regulated in E. coli K88 and control groups and down-regulated in quercetin and E. coli K88 groups (Table S5 and Table S6).

Glycerophospholipid metabolic pathway was significantly enriched in quercetin and E. coli K88 groups (P < 0.05). The pathways included Glycerol-3-phosphate acyltransferase 4 (GPAT4, ENSGALG00010018071), Phosphocholine Cytidylyltransferase 2(PCYT2, ENSGALG00010029275), Glycerol-3-phosphate acyltransferase 3(GPAT3, ENSGALG00010002614), CDP-diacylglycerol synthase 2 (CDS2, ENSGALG00010020362), and Phosphatidylserine Decarboxylase (PISD, ENSGALG00010021972), Selenoprotein I (SELENOI, ENSGALG00010009876), Lysophospholipase 2 (LYPLA2, ENSGALG00010015193), Membrane Bound O-Acyltransferase Domain Containing 1 (MBOAT1, ENSGALG00010007108), Phospholipid Phosphatase 5 (PLPP5, ENSGALG00010017825), ADP-ribose/CD38 hydrolase-like protein (ADPRM, ENSGALG00010029563), Lecithin-Cholesterol Acyltransferase Gene (LCAT, ENSGALG00010022401) and Phospholipase D1 (PLD1, ENSGALG00010014093). E. coli K88 challenge significantly increased expression of PCYT2, ADPRM and GPAT3 (P < 0.05), compared with control group. Quercetin significantly increased expression of PISD and MBOAT1 (P < 0.05), and significantly decreased expression of GPAT4, GPAT3, PCYT2, SELENOI, PLPP5, ADPRM, LCAT, LYPLA2 and CDS2 (P < 0.05), compared with E. coli K88 group (Table 3). Quercetin significantly decreased expression of GPAT4, PCYT2, SELENOI, PLPP5, ADPRM, LCAT, LYPLA2 and CDS2 (P < 0.01) in liver of broilers, compared with E. coli K88 group (Table 4).

Effect of quercetin on metabolomics in liver of broilers challenged with E. coli K88

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Partial Least Squares Discriminant Analysis (PLS-DA) of metabolic profiles of metabolites showed that control, E. coli K88, aureomycin and quercetin groups were clustered in cationic and anionic modes, it represented good repeatability of samples intra-group. The samples of control and E. coli K88 groups, E. coli K88 and aureomycin groups, quercetin and E. coli K88 groups were significantly separated in cationic mode (Supplementary Figure S6 a1, b1, c1); the samples of control and E. coli K88 groups, E. coli K88 and aureomycin groups, quercetin and E. coli K88 groups were significantly separated in anionic mode by PCA (Supplementary Figure S6 a2, b2, c2). The samples of control and E. coli K88 groups, E. coli K88 and aureomycin groups, quercetin and E. coli K88 groups were significantly separated in cationic mode (Supplementary Figure S6 a3, b3, c3); the samples were significantly separated inter-group in anionic mode by PLS-DA (Supplementary Figure S6 a4, b4, c4). The above results indicated that E. coli K88 challenge, quercetin and aureomycin treatment significantly affected the metabolites in liver of broilers.

Differential metabolites were screened out using Orthogonal Projections to Latent Structures Discriminant Analysis (OPLS-DA analysis), and metabolites with VIP > 1 and P < 0.05 were significant. The samples of control and E. coli K88 groups, E. coli K88 and aureomycin groups, quercetin and E. coli K88 groups were significantly separated in cationic and anionic modes by OPLS-DA (Supplementary Figure S7 a1-c1, a2-c2). The permutation test showed that R2 and Q2 of random model were gradually decreased with reduction of permutation retention; it indicated that the original model was not overfitted and robust (Supplementary Figure S7 a3-c3, a4-c4).

Differential metabolites with FC > 1.5 or FC<0.67, P < 0.05 were visualized in volcanic plot. Eight differential metabolites were significantly down-regulated, and 20 differential metabolites were significantly up-regulated in cationic mode, 22 differential metabolites were significantly down-regulated, and seven differential metabolites were significantly up-regulated in anionic mode in E. coli K88 group, compared with control group. Seven differential metabolites were significantly down-regulated, and 20 differential metabolites were significantly up-regulated in cationic mode in aureomycin group, six differential metabolites were significantly down-regulated, and 23 differential metabolites were significantly up-regulated in anionic mode, compared with E. coli K88 group. Thirty-seven differential metabolites were significantly down-regulated, 57 differential metabolites were significantly up-regulated in cationic mode in quercetin group, 14 differential metabolites were significantly down-regulated, and 13 differential metabolites were significantly up-regulated in anionic mode, compared with E. coli K88 group (Supplementary Figure S8).

Discussion

Avian colibacillosis is a prevalent and destructive bacterial disease in poultry industry, which is predominantly induced by avian pathogenic E. coli (Tan Z et al., 2024). Avian colibacillosis is treated and prevented with antibiotics before 2020, however, antibiotics have been banned now. Therefore, searching antibiotic substitutes is urgently needed to be addressed. At present, plant extracts exhibit great potential in antibiotic substitutes. Quercetin as a plant extract is a kind of flavonoids, which may improve immunity, relieve inflammation and regulate intestinal flora and metabolism in body. It exhibited antibacterial activity in vitro by damaging cell walls, changing cell permeability, inhibiting enzyme activity, and hindering protein and nucleic acid synthesis. The most commonly used clinical indicators for assessing liver dysfunction include elevated levels of ALT and AST activity in serum (Sun et al., 2023). Lin J et al. (2025) found that quercetin attenuated hepatic ischemia-reperfusion injury and targetedly inhibited macrophage pyroptosis via blocking caspase-8/ASC interaction. Shi C et al. (2022) found that dihydromyricetin inhibited activation of NLRP3 inflammasome and subsequent apoptosis, eventually alleviated E. coli lipopolysaccharide-induced liver injury. In the present study, E. coli K88 challenge significantly resulted in liver injury according to pathology and function data in liver. Dietary quercetin and aureomycin alleviated E. coli K88-induced liver injury to various degrees, and quercetin worked better than aureomycin. Therefore, in this study, mechanism of quercetin alleviating E. coli K88-induced liver injury was explored in broilers using microbiomics, transcriptomics and metabolomics, and expression of genes significantly regulated was verified in transcriptomics using RT-qPCR.

Liver flora is closely related to liver injury. Pandey Sp et al. (2022) found that disturbance of liver flora resulted in autoimmune hepatitis in mice; Cohen SJ et al. (2023) found that administration of probiotic-derived vesicles improved liver regeneration following surgical liver resection in mice. In addition, Proteobacteria and Escherich-Shigella are harmful bacteria, positively associated with liver inflammation (Zhou X et al., 2023; Liang H et al., 2022). The present results showed that dietary quercetin regulated E. coli K88-induced disturbance of flora in liver by reducing abundance of Proteobacteria and Escherich-Shigella, thereby alleviating E. coli K88-induced liver injury in broilers; however, dietary aureomycin did not regulate liver flora in broilers.

The transcriptomics is an aggregation of all transcripts in a cell, including mRNA, rRNA, tRNA, and other non-coding RNAs. The RNA-seq may comprehensively obtain almost all transcripts of a specific organ or tissue in a particular state, reflect its expression level, and predict pathogenesis of a disease or treatment mechanism of drugs. In this study, the results of liver RNA-seq and KEGG enrichment analysis of the DEGs showed that there were 1656 DEGs and 165 significantly enriched pathways between E. coli K88 and quercetin groups, respectively. These results indicated that quercetin alleviated E. coli K88-induced liver injury in broilers, and the effect was closely associated with metabolic pathways. Therefore, the mechanism of quercetin alleviating E. coli K88-induced liver injury was further investigated in broilers using non-targeted metabolomics. In this study, mass spectrum analysis showed that E. coli K88 challenge, quercetin and aureomycin treatment significantly affected metabolite levels in liver of broilers. Further analysis showed that there were 66 different metabolites between E. coli K88 and quercetin groups, and these different metabolites were enriched in metabolic pathways, ABC transporters, glycerophospholipid metabolism, necroptosis, starch and sucrose metabolism, vascular smooth muscle contraction and arginine and proline metabolism. Correlation of transcriptomics and metabolomics in livers was analyzed to further determine which metabolic pathway participated in quercetin alleviating liver injury, and the correlation results showed that glycerophospholipid metabolic pathway was significantly enriched in quercetin and E. coli K88 groups. Analysis of transcriptomics and metabolomics data showed that significant DEGs associated with glycerophospholipid metabolism included GPAT4, PCYT2, GPAT3, CDS2, PISD, SELENOI, LYPLA2, MBOAT1, PLPP5, ADPRM, LCAT and PLD1, significantly different metabolites included glycerol3-phosphate, glycerophosphate(2), phosphorylcholine, glycerophosphocholine, sn-glycerol-3-phosphoethanolamine and choline. The present results were similar to Hao J et al. (2024), who found that astragaloside alleviated alcoholic liver disease by regulating lipid metabolism in rats. The RNA-seq results showed that aureomycin did not significantly regulate any signaling pathway in E. coli K88-challenged broilers. However, metabolomics data showed that aureomycin significantly regulated phenylalanine metabolism in liver of broilers, and phenyl propionic acid was significantly correlated to phenylalanine metabolism.

Gruppen EG et al. (2015) found that LCAT activity enhanced by regulating HDL function was pathogenesis of increased low-grade inflammation in plasma of patients with metabolic syndrome. Zhang Z et al. (2023) found that knockout of PLD1 decreased arthritis score, rheumatoid factor, and C-reactive protein in Collagen Induced Arthritis (CIA) rats. Friday SC et al. (2016) found that specific inhibition of PLD1 activity significantly reduced secretion of IL-6 and IL-8 in synovial cells. Kim NY et al. (2010) found that hepatotoxicity was associated with overexpression of PLD1 in liver of rats. The present results found that quercetin significantly reduced expression of PLD1 and LCAT and alleviated E. coli K88-induced liver injury in broilers, which was closely associated with quercetin alleviating inflammation in livers. Previous results of our team showed that quercetin significantly decreased increase of serum inflammatory factors resulted from E. coli K88 challenge (Zhou SS, unpublished data), meanwhile, quercetin also inhibited E. coli K88-induced inflammatory cell infiltration in liver of broilers. These results suggested that quercetin reduced E. coli K88 challenge-induced inflammation by inhibiting overexpression of PLD1 and LCAT in liver of broilers.

Studies showed that virus induced GPAT4 overexpression, excessive GPAT4 resulted in insulin resistance and disrupted glucose metabolism, thus increased gluconeogenesis and decreased glycogen synthesis, thereby damaged glucose homeostasis in liver of mice (Shan D et al., 2010). Insulin resistance is closely related to occurrence and development of non-alcoholic fatty liver injury in liver. Liu YL (2020) found that specific knockout of GPAT4 maintained high insulin sensitivity in liver of mice with high-fat diets, improved diet-induced glucose tolerance disorder and insulin resistance, reduced fat synthesis and lipid accumulation, thereby alleviated diet-induced non-alcoholic fatty liver in mice. The present results found that quercetin significantly decreased GPAT4 expression in liver of broilers. Meanwhile, the previous study of our team also found that E. coli K88 challenge significantly increased glucose content, and 500 mg/kg and 750 mg/kg quercetin significantly decreased glucose content in serum of E. coli K88 challenge broilers (Fu YX, 2025). Our results indicated that quercetin was involved in regulating glucose metabolism, therefore alleviated E. coli K88-induced liver injury in broilers.

Phosphatidyl acid is a precursor of phospholipids and triacylglycerol, is also a substrate for CDP-DAG formation catalyzed by CDP-diacylglycerol synthase 1 (CDS1) and CDS2. Lipid droplets are composed of hydrophobic cores of neutral lipids, mainly including triacylglycerol (TAGs) and sterol esters, lack and excessive storage of neutral lipids in lipid droplets may induce diseases including lipodystrophy, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, atherosclerosis, obesity, and type 2 diabetes (Walther TC et al., 2012). Xu YQ et al. (2019) found that decreased expression of CDS1 or CDS2 resulted in forming giant or super-large lipid droplets, and reducing expression of diacylglycerol o-acyltransferase 2 (DGAT2) and GPAT4 restored normal size of lipid droplets in CDS2 deficient cells of cultured mammalian cells. In this study, quercetin significantly decreased expression of CDS2 and GPAT4 in liver of broilers, suggested that quercetin also alleviated E. coli K88-induced liver injury by regulating formation of lipid droplets in liver of broilers.

SELENOI is an ethanolamine-phosphate cytidylyltransferase (EPT) with a non-selenase domain, participates in primarily synthesizing phosphatidyl ethanolamine (PE) in endoplasmic reticulum, has a unique functional structure, and is the only selenium protein that drives enzymatic reactions in membrane (Li F et al., 2023). Zhu J et al. (2024) found that selenium enrichment alleviated high-fat diet-induced liver injury by down-regulating expression of SELENOI, triglyceride synthetase Gpat1 and Dgat, and up-regulating expression of lipase hepatic lipase (Lipc) and lipoprotein lipase (Lpl) in mice. The present results showed that quercetin significantly decreased SELENOI expression in liver of broilers. Combined with our previous study, quercetin significantly increased content of high-density lipoprotein (HDL), significantly reduced low-density lipoprotein (LDL), very low-density lipoprotein (VLDL), total cholesterol and triglyceride in serum of broilers (Fu YX, 2025), it suggested that quercetin also regulated triglyceride synthesis, thus alleviated E. coli K88-induced liver injury in broilers.

Liu N et al. (2023) found that uric acid induced mitochondrial dysfunction and apoptosis through downregulating expression of mitochondrial PISD, and liver injury resulted from mitochondrial dysfunction was significantly alleviated by allopurinol, overexpression of PISD and lysophosphatidyl ethanolamine therapy. Xu JS et al. (2022) found that PISD overexpression alleviated liver injury by relieving CDS2 deficiency-induced morphological and functional abnormalities of hepatocyte mitochondria in mice. The present results showed that quercetin significantly increased PISD expression in liver of broilers, it suggested that PISD overexpression regulated mitochondrial function, thereby alleviated E. coli K88-induced liver injury in broilers.

Choline glycerophosphate is a supportive nutrient for brain and other organs, and plays a vital role in lipid metabolism (Syme C et al., 2019; Narukawa M et al., 2020). Lysophospholipids catalyze synthesis of glycerophosphocholine by LYPLA2 and other enzymes. Glycerophosphorylcholine phosphodiesterase (GPCPDE) synthesizes sn-glycerol 3-phosphate by glycerophosphorylcholine phosphodiesterase (GPCPDE) and other enzymes. ADPRM also catalyzes conversion of CDP-glycerol to sn-glycerol-3-phosphate. The MBOAT1 encodes a lysophospholipid acyltransferase which catalyzes acylation of multiple substrates and transfers acyl groups from acyl-CoA to lysophospholipids to produce phospholipid molecules, it participates in various lipid anabolic pathways, including CDP-diglycerol, TG, and glycerophospholipids (Hishikawa D et al., 2008). Mastrokolias A et al. (2016) found that glycerophosphocholine metabolism was disrupted in blood of Huntington's disease patients, and aldehyde dehydrogenase 1 family member B1 (ALDH1B1), MBOAT1, methionine synthase reductase (MTRR), and phospholipase B1 (PLB1) are significantly associated with glycerophosphocholine metabolism. The present results found that quercetin alleviated E. coli K88-induced liver injury by significantly reducing LYPLA2 expression, increasing lysophospholipids content and MBOAT1 expression, thereby promoted glycerophosphocholine metabolism in broilers. PLPP5 is a phospholipase involved in phospholipids metabolism in cells, and encoded PLPP5 protein may catalyze phospholipids to remove phosphate groups and maintain cell membranes' stability and function. So far, studies on PLPP5 have not been reported.

Phosphocholine is an intermediate in lecithin synthesis during metabolism of glycerin phospholipids. Lecithin is an essential component in human cell membrane, and supplementation of lecithin may improve phospholipid composition of membrane, repair damaged cell membrane, increase membrane fluidity and repair membrane enzyme activity, and improve membrane function. Meanwhile, lecithin also exhibited anti-oxidation which may resist lipid structure damage of cell membrane resulted from lipid peroxidation and further improve repair ability of cell membrane (Chi Lp, 2007). Level of serum choline phosphate was increased in rats with CCl4-induced acute liver injury, it suggested that lipid peroxidation destructed fluidity of cell membrane (Liang YH et al., 2016). Wang XY et al. (2015) found that phosphocholine content was increased in serum and liver of mice with ethanol-induced liver injury, it was probably associated with changes in ethanol-induced phospholipid metabolism. Phosphorylcholine content was increased at 24 weeks, however, it was decreased after 44 weeks in brain of long-term alcohol-stimulated mice (Lee DW et al., 2013). In addition, phosphorylcholine content was lower than that of the regular group in serum of rat with liver-depression syndrome (XU S, 2009). The present results found that phosphorylcholine content of quercetin group was significantly lower than that of control group in liver of E. coli K88-challenged broilers, it indicated that free radicals resulted from toxic substances in the body attacked liver cells, destroyed liver cell membrane, thus lipids in cell membrane were leaked and phosphorylcholine content was increased in the early stage of liver injury. PCYT2 is a rate-limiting enzyme of the CDP ethanolamine pathway, it catalyzes phospho-ethanolamine (PEtn) to produce CDP-ethanolamine, and then phosphatidyl-ethanolamine (PE) is synthesized, and PE produces phosphocholine through a series of synthetic pathways. In the present study, quercetin significantly decreased PCYT2 expression in liver of broilers, thus inhibited biosynthesis of phosphatidylethanolamine and increased phosphocholine biosynthesis. Phosphocholine is broken down into choline and phosphoric acid by PHOSPHO1, thus promotes choline biosynthesis. In this study, quercetin called back phosphorylcholine to control level in liver of broilers, it indicated that quercetin stimulated phosphatidylcholine biosynthesis by inhibiting PCYT2 expression, thereby alleviated E. coli K88-induced liver injury in broilers.

Choline is a biomarker of liver injury, widely exists in cellular structure and metabolic pathways, affects many physiological processes including lipid metabolism, second messenger signaling, methylation-dependent molecular biosynthesis, nuclear receptor activation, enterohepatic circulation of bile and cholesterol, plasma membrane fluidity, and mitochondrial bioenergetics. Choline played a regulatory role in neurodegenerative diseases, metabolic diseases, cancer and other pathological processes (Roci I et al., 2020), is also the main component of the mitochondrial membrane, and choline deficiency played a key role in pathogenesis of fatty liver disease mediated by mitochondrial damage (Kim HY, 2021). Low choline levels resulted in composition imbalance of mitochondrial membrane, cardiolipin oxidation, decreased content of phosphatidylethanolamine and phosphatidylcholine, thus impaired biological function and fatty acid β oxidation of mitochondria (Serviddio G et al., 2011; Vrablic AS et al., 2001). Endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS) is a continuous stress chain reaction resulted from excess accumulation of unfolded protein, and continuous ERS may induce apoptosis. Choline deficiency may result in ERS, and ERS further induces liver damage. Lan T et al. (Zhang Z et al., 2023) found that human adipose mesenchymal stem cells regulated choline metabolism by inhibiting the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway, thus alleviated CCl4-induced liver injury in mice. The present results showed that quercetin inhibited PCYT2 expression and increased choline content in livers, it suggested that quercetin also promoted glycerophospholipid metabolism by avoiding ERS and mitochondrial damage of hepatocytes, thus alleviated E. coli K88-induced liver injury in broilers.

The present results showed that choline was significantly positively correlated with Delftia (P < 0.05) and significantly negatively correlated with Anaerostipes (P < 0.05); phosphorylcholine was significantly positively correlated with Catenibacterium, Peptococcus, Eubacterium ventriosum_group, and Parvimonas (P < 0.05), significantly positively correlated with Microbacterium (P < 0.01), these results suggested that Delftia, Catenibacterium, Peptococcus, [Eubacterium]_ventriosum_group and Parvimonas participated in synthesis and metabolism of choline and choline phosphate; moreover, GPAT3 was significantly negatively correlated with sn-glycerol-3-phosphoethanolamine (P < 0.05), and LYPLA2 was significantly negatively correlated with Choline (P < 0.05); CDS2 was significantly negatively correlated with sn-glycerol-3-phosphoethanolamine and choline (P < 0.05); GPAT4, PCYT2, LCAT, SELENOI, PLPP5, ADPRM were significantly negatively correlated with glycerol 3-phosphate, glycerophosphocholine, sn-glycerol-3-phosphoethanolamine and choline (P < 0.05); choline was significantly negatively correlated with ADPRM, PLPP5, SELENOI, LCAT, PCYT2, GPAT4, LYPLA2 and CDS2 (P < 0.05). The above-mentioned results suggested that glycerophospholipid metabolism was closely associated with GPAT4, PCYT2, SELENOI, PLPP5, ADPRM, LCAT, LYPLA2, and CDS2.

Wang Z et al. (2020) found that increasing phenylalanine content was probably resulted from inhibiting phenylalanine hydroxylase or increasing total blood flow, and high content of phenylalanine induced oxidative damage and accumulated toxic substances in livers in the early stage of liver injury. The present results from metabolomics showed that aureomycin regulated phenylalanine metabolism to alleviate E. coli K88-induced liver injury in broilers, and phenylacetaldehyde was one of the differential metabolites significantly related to phenylalanine metabolism. Phenylalanine content was higher than that in control group, and dietary aureomycin reduced phenylalanine content in liver of broilers challenged with E. coli K88, however, phenylalanine content was still higher than that in control group. In addition, phenylacetaldehyde content was increased in livers, it suggested that aureomycin promoted metabolism of phenylalanine to phenylacetaldehyde, decreased phenylalanine content, thus reduced oxidative stress and accumulation of toxic substances, thereby alleviated E. coli K88-induced liver injury in broilers.

The present results showed that quercetin alleviated liver injury was closely correlated with GPAT4, PCYT2, GPAT3, CDS2, PISD, SELENOI, LYPLA2, MBOAT1, PLPP5, ADPRM, LCAT, PLD1 and glycerophospholipid metabolic pathway according to microbiomics, transcriptomics and metabolomics in liver of broilers. The phenylalanine metabolism pathway was closely correlated with aureomycin in alleviating liver injury in broilers. Quercetin had more abundant mechanisms and more obvious effects on alleviating E. coli K88-induced liver injury than aureomycin in broilers by comparing all data and mechanisms of quercetin, aureomycin relieving liver injury in broilers.

The present studies firstly explored mechanism of quercetin alleviating E. coli K88-induced liver injury in broilers from a perspective of metabolism. The present results opened a new perspective for applying quercetin in broilers production practice as a feed additive to prevent/treat avian colibacillosis. However, the specific mode of action between genes and metabolic pathways involved in quercetin alleviating E. coli K88-induced liver injury remains to need further study in broilers.

Conclusion

In conclusion, dietary quercetin alleviated E. coli K88-induced liver injury in broilers by regulating glycerophospholipid metabolism. Under the current experimental conditions, the effect of quercetin in alleviating liver injury was better than aureomycin in broilers. Therefore, quercetin was a potential additive to be used as an alternative to antibiotics.

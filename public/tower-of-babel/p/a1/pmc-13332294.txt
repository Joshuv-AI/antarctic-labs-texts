Clinical significance of high-density lipoprotein cholesterol and its dynamic change in patients with lymphoma-associated hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground:Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) is a fatal disorder characterized by uncontrolled inflammation. In adults, lymphoma is the most common underlying cause. The lipid profile is frequently dysregulated in lymphoma-associated HLH (LA-HLH), and the clinical significance remains to be determined.Objectives:This study aimed to evaluate the prognostic value of baseline lipid profiles and the dynamic changes in high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-c) in adult patients with LA-HLH.Design:A multicenter, retrospective cohort study.Methods:We analyzed 277 adult patients with LA-HLH from 27 medical centers (2015–2023). Survival analysis was performed using Kaplan–Meier and Cox regression methods.Results:Low HDL-c (<1.03 mmol/L) was observed in 93.9% (260/277) patients at diagnosis. A baseline HDL-c <0.48 mmol/L was independently associated with reduced overall survival (OS; hazard ratio, HR = 1.51, 95% confidence interval, CI: 1.10–2.09, p = 0.012) and 60-day survival (HR = 1.66, 95% CI: 1.10–2.49, p = 0.015). A lower ratio (<1.71) was significantly associated with reduced survival. Background

Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) is a rare and devastating syndrome featured by sustained but ineffective immune system activation, leading to a severe hyperinflammatory state and multiple organ failure. 1 Based on inherited or acquired etiologies, HLH is often classified as primary or familial HLH, characterized by an underlying predisposing genetic defect that predominantly affects young children, 2 or as secondary HLH, which manifests in adults and is often associated with malignancies, infections, and autoimmune or autoinflammatory disorders. 3 Around 50% of secondary HLH cases arise in the context of underlying tumors, of which lymphoma is the most common. 3 Lymphoma-associated HLH (LA-HLH) is often associated with a particularly poor prognosis. 4 Based on published large case series, the median survival of LA-HLH ranges from 2 to 6 months.5–9 Given the lack of prognostic markers and limited research on LA-HLH, it is crucial to identify clear and significant prognostic factors. Such identification will facilitate the stratification of patients for personalized treatment approaches, potentially leading to improved outcomes.

Lipids, acting as signaling molecules, structural components of membranes, and key components of cell energy metabolism, play indispensable roles in the development and progression of tumors. 10 Advances in lipidomic technologies have highlighted lipid metabolism reprogramming as a novel characteristic of cancer.11,12 Although the direct causality between low cholesterol and lymphoma has yet to be determined, low levels of cholesterol, including high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-c), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-c), and total cholesterol (TC), have been identified as potential risk factors for non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL). 13 Several studies have indicated that serum lipid level alterations are associated with outcomes of patients with diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) and are influenced by rituximab therapy.14–18 In addition, low serum cholesterol levels have been linked to a poor prognosis in patients with extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, 19 underscoring the significance of lipid metabolism in lymphoma outcomes.

Dysregulation of serum lipids is a common finding in patients with HLH. Based on the HLH-2004 guideline, high triglyceride (TG) levels are used as one of the primary criteria for diagnosing HLH. 20 In the context of LA-HLH, however, the role of the lipid profile has yet to be systematically investigated, and its correlation with disease severity or prognosis is not well established. Furthermore, whether the dynamic change of the lipid profile has a prognostic impact remains to be determined. Here, by assessing a cohort of patients with LA-HLH, we first explored the association of the baseline serum lipid profile (including TC, HDL-c, LDL-c, and TG) with disease characteristics. Subsequently, we focused on evaluating the prognostic significance of HDL-c, both at baseline and in terms of its dynamic alteration after treatment.

Methods

Study objectives

The primary objective of this study was to evaluate the prognostic value of baseline HDL-c level on overall survival (OS), defined as the time from diagnosis to death from any cause, in patients with LA-HLH. Secondary objectives included assessing its impact on 60-day survival, exploring the prognostic significance of the dynamic change in HDL-c after treatment, and investigating the associations between the baseline lipid profile and clinical characteristics of LA-HLH.

Study population

This multicenter, retrospective study included adult patients with LA-HLH diagnosed at 27 medical centers in China from 2015 to 2023. The participating institutions were listed in Supplemental File 1. Eligible cases should meet the following criteria: (1) age ⩾18 years; (2) fulfillment of ⩾5/8 HLH-2004 diagnostic criteria 21 ; and (3) histopathological diagnosis of lymphoma. Patients without a baseline lipid profile were excluded. A flow diagram summarizing patient inclusion, exclusion, and final analysis cohorts is provided in Figure 1.

Flowchart of patient screening and enrollment. Of the 360 adults initially screened for suspected LA-HLH, 277 patients met the inclusion criteria and formed the final cohort for baseline lipid profile analysis. Among them, 101 patients had post-treatment HDL-c data and were included in the dynamic HDL-c subgroup analysis.

HDL-c, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol; LA-HLH, lymphoma-associated hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis.

Data collection

Demographic, clinical, and laboratory data were extracted from electronic medical records. The collected baseline laboratory parameters included complete blood count, liver and renal function tests, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), fibrinogen, ferritin, and β2-microglobulin. The subtypes of the underlying lymphoid malignancies were also collected. Baseline lipid profile levels, including TC, TG, HDL-c, and LDL-c, were collected. Decreased HDL-c was defined as a level below the normal lower limit of 1.03 mmol/L, and decreased LDL-c was defined as a level below 2.6 mmol/L. Hypertriglyceridemia was defined as TG ⩾3 mmol/L, and hypocholesterolemia as TC <3 mmol/L.

To evaluate the clinical significance of HDL-c dynamics, we collected the first HDL-c measurement taken between 14 and 30 days after baseline. Post-treatment HDL-c data were unavailable for some patients primarily due to early death before follow-up test or failure to recheck HDL-c levels during the specified period. The dynamic change in HDL-c was quantified as the ratio of post-treatment HDL-c to baseline HDL-c.

Statistical analyses

We used the Mann–Whitney test or the Kruskal–Wallis test to compare continuous variables between two or more groups, respectively, as they are nonparametric tests appropriate for non-normally distributed data. Fisher’s exact test or Chi-square test was used to compare categorical variables. The survival curves were constructed using the Kaplan–Meier method, and the log-rank test was used for comparison. To determine the optimal cutoff values for baseline HDL-c and the HDL-c ratio for survival outcomes, we employed the X-tile software (version 3.6.1; Yale University, New Haven, CT, USA). The cutoffs that yielded the most significant split in survival (lowest log-rank p-value) were selected for further analysis and risk stratification. Variables that showed a potential association with survival (p < 0.1) in univariate analyses were included in the multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression model to identify independent prognostic factors. All the analyses were performed by the R software (version 4.3.3; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). p < 0.05 (two-sided) was considered statistically significant. The reporting of this study conforms to the STROBE statement (Supplemental File 2). 22

Results

Clinical characteristics and lipid profile

A total of 277 LA-HLH patients were included. The median age at diagnosis was 58 years, with a male-to-female ratio of 2.0:1. A low level of HDL-c (<1.03 mmol/L) was present in 93.9% (260/277) of patients, and the median level of HDL-c was 0.51 (interquartile range, IQR: 0.34–0.71) mmol/L. A low level of LDL-c (<2.6 mmol/L) was present in 85.6% (237/277) of patients, and the median level was 1.81 (IQR: 1.40–2.26) mmol/L. In addition, 27.8% (77/277) of the patients had hypertriglyceridemia (TG ⩾3 mmol/L) and 48.7% (135/277) had hypocholesterolemia (TC <3 mmol/L). The clinical characteristics and lipid profiles are shown in Table 1. Patients with T/NK-cell malignancies had higher HDL-c than those with B-cell NHL (B-NHL, median 0.53 vs 0.485 mmol/L, p = 0.045), while no significant difference was found between the two groups for other lipid markers. In the patients with T/NK-cell malignancies, no significant difference was found in the levels of HDL-c or LDL-c among patients with extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, nasal type (44/139), aggressive natural killer-cell leukemia (32/139), angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma (19/139), and peripheral T-cell lymphoma, not otherwise specified (15/139).

Of the 277 patients, 15 received only supportive care. Association of the characteristics of LA-HLH with different lipid levels

To assess the association of the characteristics of LA-HLH with different lipid levels, we compared clinical and laboratory features between the groups divided by the median HDL-c and LDL-c levels. Patients with lower HDL-c (<0.51 mmol/L) had lower levels of hemoglobin, platelets, albumin, and fibrinogen than those with higher HDL-c (⩾0.51 mmol/L). In addition, elevated aspartate aminotransferase, creatinine, LDH, and β2-microglobulin were associated with lower HDL-c. In addition, those with lower LDL-c (<1.81 mmol/L) had decreased hemoglobin, platelets, neutrophils, and fibrinogen levels compared to the patients with higher LDL-c (Table 2).

Prognostic significance of baseline HDL-c

We evaluated the predictive values of HDL-c and LDL-c in this cohort. The median follow-up time was 157 days. The patients with HDL-c <0.51 mmol/L had remarkably inferior 60-day survival (56.34% vs 69.3%, p = 0.012, Figure 2(a)) and OS (median 92 vs 340 days, p = 0.013, Figure 2(b)) than those patients with HDL-c ⩾0.51 mmol/L. However, the level of LDL-c did not show a significant impact on 60-day survival (Figure 2(c)) or OS (Figure 2(d)). We determined the optimum cutoff of HDL-c for predicting survival, which was 0.48 mmol/L, for both 60-day survival (Supplemental Figure 1(A)) and OS (Supplemental Figure 1(B)), by the X-tile software for further analysis. The patients with HDL-c <0.48 mmol/L had significantly worse 60-day survival (53.2% vs 70.6%, p = 0.001, Figure 3(a)) and OS (median 71 vs 358 days, p = 0.001, Figure 3(b)) than those patients with HDL-c ⩾0.48 mmol/L.

Subsequent multivariable Cox regression analyses confirmed the independent prognostic value of HDL-c. After adjusting for age, lymphoma subtype, cytopenias, LDH, ferritin, and treatment, a baseline HDL-c level <0.48 mmol/L remained independently associated with poorer OS (hazard ratio (HR) = 1.51, 95% CI: 1.10–2.09, p = 0.012, Table 3) and 60-day OS (HR = 1.66, 95% CI: 1.10–2.49, p = 0.015, Table 4). These results collectively establish low baseline HDL-c as a significant and independent prognostic marker in LA-HLH.

Dynamic change of HDL-c

A total of 36.5% (101/277) of patients with available dynamic HDL-c data were further evaluated. To assess potential selection bias, we compared the baseline characteristics of patients with and without post-treatment HDL-c data. While significant differences were observed in age, hemoglobin, and platelet levels, no significant differences were found in other key parameters, including sex, baseline HDL-c, neutrophil count, albumin, liver and renal function tests, LDH, ferritin, fibrinogen, and β2-microglobulin (Supplemental Table 1). This pattern suggests that while not entirely random, the missing data may not represent a critical systematic bias affecting the primary study outcomes. The median interval between the baseline test and the post-treatment test was 17 (IQR: 15–24) days. The HDL-c level increased after treatment in 81.2% (82/101) of the patients. The post-treatment/baseline HDL-c ratio was calculated, and the median ratio was 1.64 (IQR: 1.05–2.46). Patients with T/NK-cell malignancies showed a significantly lower ratio than those with B-NHL (median 1.41 vs 1.94, p = 0.001). The patients with a ratio <1.64 had higher albumin levels (Table 5). By the X-tile software, we identified that the optimum cutoff of the ratio was 1.15 for predicting 60-day survival (Supplemental Figure 1(C)) and 1.71 for OS (Supplemental Figure 1(D)). We chose 1.71 for further analysis. Patients with a ratio <1.71 showed remarkably inferior 60-day survival (66.6% vs 86.9%, p = 0.025, Figure 4(a)) and OS (median 318 days vs not reached, p = 0.032, Figure 4(b)) compared to patients with a ratio ⩾1.71. In subgroup analysis, a lower ratio was associated with inferior 60-day survival (p = 0.002) and OS (p = 0.011) in patients with B-NHL. However, in patients with T/NK-cell malignancies, no significant difference in survival was found between patients with different ratios.

Prognostic model of baseline and dynamic change of HDL-c

According to the baseline HDL-c and the ratio of dynamic change, we classified patients into four groups. Group 1 comprised patients with HDL-c ⩾0.48 mmol/L at baseline and a ratio ⩾1.71. Group 2 comprised patients with HDL-c <0.48 mmol/L at baseline and a ratio ⩾1.71. Group 3 included patients with baseline HDL-c ⩾0.48 mmol/L and a ratio <1.71. Group 4 included patients with baseline HDL-c <0.48 mmol/L and a ratio <1.71. There were significant differences in OS between group 1 and 4 (p < 0.001), 2 and 4 (p = 0.010), as well as 3 and 4 (p = 0.009). No significant difference in OS was observed between groups 1 and 2 (p = 0.439), 1 and 3 (p = 0.337), or 2 and 3 (p = 0.754). As for 60-day survival, a significant difference was observed only between groups 1 and 4 (p = 0.002). Therefore, we categorized patients into a low-risk group (HDL-c ⩾0.48 or a ratio ⩾1.71, group 1 + 2 + 3) and a high-risk group (HDL-c <0.48 mmol/L and a ratio <1.71, group 4). Low-risk patients had significantly superior 60-day survival (81.8% vs 46.7%, p = 0.004, Figure 5(a)) and OS (median: not reached vs 52 days, p < 0.001, Figure 5(b)) than high-risk patients.

Discussion

This study is the first to comprehensively evaluate the prognostic significance of both baseline levels and dynamic changes of HDL-c in a large multicenter cohort of patients with LA-HLH. Our key finding is that low baseline HDL-c and impaired post-treatment HDL-c recovery are strong, independent predictors of poor survival outcomes. Specifically, a baseline HDL-c level below 0.48 mmol/L was significantly associated with reduced OS. Furthermore, the dynamic change in HDL-c, quantified by the ratio of post-treatment to baseline levels, provided additional prognostic information. A ratio below 1.71, indicating insufficient HDL-c recovery after treatment, was associated with significantly worse 60-day survival and OS. Most notably, patients exhibiting both adverse factors constituted a distinct high-risk group with remarkably inferior outcomes. These results establish HDL-c not merely as a biomarker of disease severity but as a clinically feasible and dynamic tool for risk stratification early in the course of LA-HLH, potentially identifying patients who might benefit from more intensive or novel treatment strategies.

Alterations in the lipid profile are commonly observed in malignancies, including different types of lymphomas.20,23–25 A low level of HDL-c is an early prognostic indicator for poorer outcomes in patients with lymphoma. 26 Lower levels of TC, HDL-c, and LDL-c at diagnosis significantly predicted worse progression-free survival and OS in DLBCL in the rituximab era. 16 Wang et al. 19 proposed HDL-c as an independent prognostic factor for progression-free survival and OS in patients with extranodal NK/T cell lymphoma. Previous studies have also identified serum lipid abnormalities in patients with HLH.27–29 Hypertriglyceridemia is a key diagnostic criterion for HLH, and several studies have indicated that it may also serve as a predictor of poor prognosis.28–31 A retrospective study involving 227 pediatric patients with HLH revealed that the reduction of HDL-c was highly prevalent in this group. 27 Cheng et al. 28 found that low HDL-c and low LDL-c were linked to diminished survival in adult HLH. However, the role and prognostic significance of HDL-c in patients with LA-HLH remains to be explored.

The specific mechanisms accounting for reduced HDL-c in patients with LA-HLH are yet to be fully elucidated. One potential mechanism is that highly elevated plasma levels of certain cytokines in LA-HLH result in a significant decrease in HDL-c levels. IL-10 is a crucial cytokine that is involved in the pathogenesis of secondary HLH, and its overproduction is commonly observed in LA-HLH.31–33 The level of HDL-c has been reported to be inversely correlated with the level of IL-10 by several studies,34–36 suggesting IL-10 might play a role in modulating HDL-c levels. Lucero et al. 37 reported that IL-10 facilitates macrophage uptake of HDL-c by stimulating fluid-phase endocytosis. Consequently, the prognostic value of HDL-c could be attributed to its association with IL-10. Moreover, HDL-c levels could also serve as an indirect indicator of the extent of macrophage activation, which is a defining feature of HLH.

Studies on post-treatment lipid alterations in HLH are limited, and the mechanism underlying the association between treatment-induced cholesterol elevation and better survival outcome is not well understood. However, similar findings have been reported in cases of lymphoma. The study by Gao et al. 16 identified a remarkable increase in TC, LDL-c, and HDL-c in DLBCL patients attaining complete response or partial response following 6–8 cycles of chemotherapies. They attributed these increases to improved nutritional status and cancer remission after effective chemotherapy. As HDL-c levels are regulated by IL-10, alterations in HDL-c levels may reflect dynamic changes in IL-10, which may be a potential indicator of survival outcomes of patients with LA-HLH.

Our study proposed a novel prognostic model focused on lipid parameters, including baseline HDL-c and its dynamics, for risk stratification in LA-HLH. This model was significantly different from existing models such as the HScore, 31 the HLH-2004 diagnostic criteria, 21 and the recently developed optimized HLH inflammatory (OHI) index. 38 Although HScore and HLH-2004 are useful for the prediction of the presence of HLH, their values in the prognostication of HLH are limited. HLH-2004 was developed for pediatric patients with inherited HLH, 21 while HScore was constructed using data from adult patients in which malignancy-associated HLH was underrepresented. 31 The applicability of predictive models often varies between adult and pediatric HLH cases, reflecting remarkable differences in the underlying mechanisms and clinical presentation. Pediatric HLH is frequently caused by germline genetic defects or infections, leading to a more homogeneous and hyperacute presentation. 39 By contrast, adult HLH is predominantly driven malignancies, autoimmune disorders, or pathogens, with LA-HLH representing a particularly aggressive subtype characterized by rapid progression and poorer outcomes. 40 Models derived from pediatric cohorts or general adult HLH populations may not fully capture the unique pathophysiology and prognostic factors of LA-HLH. The OHI index, which was developed for HLH patients with hematological malignancies and included ferritin and soluble CD25, could identify high-risk patients with hematological malignancy-associated HLH that was diagnosed by the classical HLH-2004 criteria. 38 However, compared to the OHI index, the simpler and clinically feasible parameters in our model allow for a more rapid assessment of outcomes of patients with LA-HLH. The current model may thus complement existing tools by providing a simple, quantitative tool for serial assessment of treatment response and early identification of high-risk patients who might benefit from more intensive or novel therapeutic strategies.

Our study specifically focused on LA-HLH based on its distinct clinical and pathophysiological features. While dyslipidemia is observed in various cancers and inflammatory conditions, the pathogenesis of LA-HLH involves unique interactions between malignant cells and the non-malignant immune cells, often driven by specific cytokine profiles.32,33 This study focuses on a homogeneous population allows for a clearer investigation of the prognostic role of lipid parameters within a defined pathogenic context. Regarding the generalizability of our findings, the prognostic model we developed is primarily validated for LA-HLH. The profound lipid dysregulation observed here may be more severe than in HLH triggered by other etiologies. Therefore, the cutoff values and risk model should be applied with caution to non-lymphoma cases and require validation in other types of HLH. In addition, we acknowledge that the observed mortality associated with low HDL-c might be influenced by aggressive lymphoma biology itself, and that the dynamic HDL-c change could reflect response to lymphoma-directed therapy rather than HLH-specific pathophysiology. However, in the current study, patients with T/NK cell lymphoma, which is typically more aggressive, showed higher baseline HDL-c as compared with patients with B-cell NHL. Furthermore, the independent prognostic role of low HDL-c after adjusting for lymphoma subtype and treatment modality suggests it captures additional risk due to the hyperinflammatory state of HLH. Therefore, in spite of its possible association with lymphoma biology, HDL-c appears to provide additional prognostic information relevant to LA-HLH.

Our study had several limitations. First, the retrospective design carries the risk of selection bias and unmeasured confounding factors, despite adjustments for key clinical variables. Second, although the sample size is sufficient for this rare condition, a larger cohort would enhance the statistical power for more nuanced subgroup analyses. Despite these limitations, our findings hold significant clinical implications. The assessment of HDL-c is inexpensive, rapid, and routinely available, making it a highly feasible tool for immediate risk stratification at the bedside. Identifying the high-risk group characterized by low baseline HDL-c and impaired post-treatment recovery could help clinicians prioritize patients for more intensive monitoring or earlier escalation of therapy. Therefore, we recommend that future research focuses on validating these results in larger, prospective adult HLH cohorts.

Conclusion

Reduced serum HDL-c could be an efficient indicator of poorer prognosis in patients, potentially guiding the selection of treatment approaches. Patients with lower HDL-c at baseline and lower post-treatment/baseline HDL-c ratio have a poorer prognosis, indicating the need for dynamic monitoring of HDL-c and additional management for the high-risk patients during the follow-up.

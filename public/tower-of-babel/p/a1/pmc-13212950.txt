ABSTRACT
AbstractTwitter data analysis gives valuable insights into various aspects of society, such as consumer opinions, political sentiments, brand reputation, and more. This information can help businesses and organizations make informed decisions, track the success of marketing campaigns, and identify emerging trends. Additionally, Twitter data analysis can also aid in research fields such as social sciences and humanities by providing a large, real-time dataset of human behaviour and language. Sentiment Analysis uses natural language processing and machine learning algorithms to categorize tweets as positive, negative, or neutral based on the sentiment expressed in the text. The previous sentiment analysis literature has cited several drawbacks especially on frequency-based vectorization models like Bag of Words, TF-IDF, and traditional word embeddings that generally cannot capture semantic relationships and contextual dependencies in short and noisy Twitter data. The proposed work comprises two phases. In the first phase, text pre-processing, vectorization, word embedding and feature selection is performed using the Frequency Co-occurrence Matrix and Fisher’s score algorithm. In the second phase, the Multi stacked BiLSTM is implemented to perform classification as positive, negative and neutral. The performance of the proposed work achieves an accuracy and mean squared error rate as 98% and 0.01% respectively.Keywords: Bag of words, BiLSTM, Fishers score, Multi stacking, Term frequencySubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Twitter data analysis involves collecting, cleaning, and transforming data from the social media platform with the aim of gaining insights and understanding. This process can encompass analysing tweets, retweets, mentions, hashtags, user profiles, and other relevant information. The objective of the analysis can vary, from assessing consumer behaviour and sentiment to evaluating brand engagement and tracking events. The results of the analysis can then inform business decisions, shape marketing plans, and provide organizations with a better understanding of their target audience. Traditional methods that solely rely on basic text representations may encounter difficulties in distinguishing between comments such as “The online banking experience is smooth and efficient” and “I’ve had multiple issues with transactions and account access.” Despite the clear difference in sentiment, these comments could be treated similarly, resulting in misinterpretations and ineffective action plans. Our proposed approach integrates Frequency Co-Occurrence Matrix (FCM) feature vectorization with Multi-Stacked BiLSTM (MS-BiLSTM) for classification, allowing us to identify the subtle semantic relationships between words and capture the subtle cues that indicate satisfaction or dissatisfaction. This not only enables the financial institution to accurately classify customer feedback but also extract valuable insights to enhance service quality and improve the customer experience. Despite extensive research in Twitter sentiment analysis, many existing approaches rely on traditional vectorization techniques such as Bag of Words (BoW), Term Frequency–Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF), and static word embeddings including Word2Vec, GloVe, and FastText. These methods primarily focus on word frequency statistics or local contextual windows, which often leads to significant semantic information loss when applied to short and noisy tweets. In particular, BoW and TF-IDF ignore word order and co-occurrence semantics, resulting in sparse, high-dimensional feature spaces that fail to preserve contextual relationships. Similarly, conventional word embeddings struggle with domain-specific vocabulary, rapidly evolving slang, and out-of-vocabulary terms that are common in social media text.

Furthermore, the short length of tweets exacerbates vocabulary sparsity and limits the ability of shallow models to capture long-range dependencies and sentiment cues that span across word sequences. This often results in unstable generalization performance and sensitivity to class imbalance. To address these limitations, a feature representation capable of encoding semantic co-occurrence patterns, combined with a deep sequential learning model that captures bidirectional contextual dependencies, is essential. Motivated by these challenges, this work proposes an integrated framework that combines Frequency Co-Occurrence Matrix (FCM)–based feature vectorization with a Multi-Stacked Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (MS-BiLSTM) classifier to achieve robust and semantically informed sentiment classification of Twitter data.

Twitter sentiment analysis is the equipment of automatically determining and categorizing the emotional mood conveyed through tweets as positive, negative or neutral utilizing natural language processing and machine learning methods. Moreover, the vocabulary sparsity is high, the setting cannot be unambiguously determined, and slang is developing rapidly, which makes it hard to predict the sentiment, and powerful feature representation and strong deep learning models are needed.

Deep learning techniques for Twitter sentiment classification

Recent developments in sentiment analysis have progressively diverged the traditional machine learning methods to the deep learning-based models owing to their better capacity to project contextual, sequential, and semantic connections in text information. Deep learning methods have shown major advancement in processing short, noisy, and informal text in the context of Twitter sentiment classification.

One of the first deep learning models to be used in sentiment analysis was Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), which can take sequential data. The regular RNNs, however, have vanishing gradient issues affecting their ability to capture long-term correlation of tweets. To address this disadvantage, the introduction of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks was developed, with gating mechanisms that enable the network to remember and forget information selectively, which improves the accuracy of sentiment prediction on short social media texts.

Several studies have effectively used the LSTM-based architecture in Twitter sentiment analysis, with reported higher performance than the traditional classifier and shallow neural networks. However, unidirectional LSTMs only process text in one direction thus limiting their capacity to use future contextual information in Twitter feeds.

BiLSTM models are now able to overcome this limitation by processing input sequences in both forward and backward directions, allowing more rich contextual representations. Previous studies have confirmed that sentiment classifiers based on BiLSTMs are more effective over unidirectional LSTMs and CNN-based models, especially when it comes to ambiguity in context and change of sentiment within short tweets. Other more modern works have expanded BiLSTM models further by adding several layers to make representations more representative and reflective of hierarchical patterns of sentiment.

Despite these developments, current deep learning methods frequently use more traditional word representations or frequency-based word-vectorization methods, which are not necessarily capable of effectively capturing semantically co-occurring relationships and contextual dependencies of Twitter data. This weakness inspires the suggested combination of Frequency Co-Occurrence Matrix (FCM) feature vectorsification with a Multi-Stacked BiLSTM system, with the intention of marrying semantic enhancement and deep sequential learning to strong sentiment categorization.

The updated literature has segmented the traditional machine learning techniques and deep learning sentiment classification techniques to highlight a more systematic difference, thus giving the proposed Multi-Stacked BiLSTM a better background. Research gap

According to the systematic review of the literature, reviewed in Table 1, one can single out a few common limitations among current Twitter sentiment analysis methods.

To begin with, conventional frequency-based techniques of vectorization like Bag of Words and TF-IDF do not conserve semantic relationships and word sequences, leading to sparse, high dimensional representations and loss of contextual meaning, especially in short Twitter texts1,3,5,10. These restrictions have a direct impact on sentiment discrimination of informal and noisy social media content.

Second, lexicon-based and rule-based approaches to sentiment analysis have a small vocabulary, fail to recognize sarcasm, and are not adaptable to changing slang and domain-specific language, as has been pointed out in previous research1,4,7,8. These methods have difficulties with generalizing their results to datasets and sentiment spaces.

Third, most current models of word embedding, including Word2Vec, GloVe, and FastText, are based on local context or co-occurrence statistics and do not represent richer semantic dependencies and long-range contextual information, especially in short and ambiguous tweets5,9,12. Such limitations are seen in lower robustness and poor performance across datasets.

Fourth, the current deep learning sentiment classifier often uses shallow or single layer models, which cannot learn hierarchical and long-term sentiment trends in sequential Twitter data9,25. In addition, several models are shown to be sensitive to class imbalance and unstable generalization when used on heterogeneous Twitter data3,6,7.

Lastly, the high dimensional feature spaces produced by traditional methods of vectorization cost more to compute and are susceptible to overfitting, and do not have principled feature selection processes to only keep discriminative attributes5,10,11.

To overcome these interdependent constraints that have always been found in the previous literature, the proposed framework combines a Frequency Co-Occurrence Matrix (FCM) to encode semantic and contextual correlations between words, the feature selection method based on Fisher score to eliminate redundancy and dimensionality, and a Multi-Stacked Bidirectional LSTM (MS-BiLSTM) architecture to support the formation of hierarchical and bidirectional sequential relationships in order to classify Twitter sentiment convincingly.

The purpose of stacking multiple RNN layers is to increase the representational power of the network and improve its ability to model sequential data. In a multi-stacked RNN, each layer receives the unseen state from the prior layer and produces a new hidden state. The final hidden state is then used to make predictions or to classify the input sequence. This allows the network to capture information from the entire input sequence and make predictions based on the situation of the entire sequence. Multi-stacked RNNs are suited for handling sequential data, as they are able to capture the dependencies between the elements in the sequence. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) are also popular in the modeling of sequential data because they can be used to represent the temporal dependencies over time. Nevertheless, traditional RNNs face the issue of vanishing and exploding gradients that restrict long-term dependency learning in text sequences28. To address these shortcomings, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks were proposed, which have memory cells and gating that allow selective information retention and forgetting, which enhances the performance of sequence modeling29.

BiLSTM architectures also carry this feature by running the input sequences in forward and backward directions to enable the model to exploit contextual information of both the past and future sequences of words at the same time. It has been established in previous researches that BiLSTM-based models are superior to unidirectional LSTMs and CNN-based models in terms of sentiment analysis tasks, especially when dealing with short and context-sensitive text types including tweets30,31.

New developments have investigated stacked and deep BiLSTM models, where is deeper representational and hierarchical learning of sentiment patterns, which results in better robustness and generalization to heterogeneous data sources26,32. These findings have inspired this framework to use a Multi-Stacked BiLSTM architecture to effectively represent contextual dependencies and sentiment dynamics of the Twitter data.

In contrast to Twitter posts, the replies posted in the Reddit are more likely to be longer, contain compound sentences, and even have a mixed or ambiguous sentiment in one instance, unlike Twitter, which has less explicit boundaries of sentiment.

Moreover, the data in the Reddit have remarkable lexical diversity, informal discourse patterns, a lot of sarcasm, rhetorical questions, and contextual references that go beyond the sentiment cues within a sentence. Domain mismatch is another causal factor. It decreases the generalization ability of the model especially where sentiment words depend on wider context instead of localized words. These findings highlight the idea that short microblog text sentiment classification models might not be easily generalizable to more lengthy and discussion-oriented systems like Reddit without domain scaling or hierarchy based contextual modeling.

The existing model has some limitations such as Lack of context. BoW only considers the frequency of words in a given tweet and does not capture any contextual information such as word order, grammar, or semantics. This can lead to a loss of information and result in less accurate modeling. BoW creates a high-dimensional representation of text data, where each dimension represents a unique word in the vocabulary. This can lead to issues with the curse of dimensionality, where the number of dimensions is large for modelling. BoW only considers the presence or absence of words in a given tweet and does not capture any semantic information about the words or their relationships to each other. BoW can amplify the impact of irrelevant or noisy words in the text data, leading to a loss of accuracy in the models built from the BoW representation. BoW is often implemented with a list of stop words that are removed from the text data before modelling. Stop words can significantly impact the BoW representation and the models built from it. So we proposed a Frequency Co-occurrence Matrix (FCM) to enhance the semantic meaning and reduce the attributes of the vectored feature for better performance.

In the proposed, data pre-processing is applied to remove the unnecessary characters in the text.The Reddit and real-time Twitter data is combined together and named as RRT dataset. The X_Train shape of RRT dataset is (66,867, 50) and y_train is (66,867, 3) respectively. The X_val shape is (22,290, 90) and the y_val shape is (22,290, 3). The X_test size is (22,290, 90) and y_test size is 3. The proposed FCM matrix consists of 22,290 instances and 90 vectorized attributes after feature extraction. The values in vectors denote the frequency of each word in a given tweet, and this frequency is calculated based on TF-IDF and cooccurrence between the features. These numerical representations of tweets are then used as input to machine learning algorithms for tasks such as sentiment analysis. Before that, the Fishers Score correlation is applied to select the best features. Fisher’s score algorithm ranks the features from high to low based on the correlation value. After this process, 50 features were selected for classification. Then the multi stacked BiLSTM is applied in the sequential model and the performance was analysed.

The RRT dataset is composed of two major sentiment datasets one of which is real-time Twitter data obtained using Twitter APIs and one more is based on the Releddit-origin of Twitter sentiment data. The two sources are short and user-generated texts which are categorized into three classes of sentiment with each being positive, negative, and neutral, thus suitable in multiclass sentiment classification. Both datasets are preprocessed in the same way (removing noise, tokenizing, stop-words, normalization, etc.) before integration, so that they are consistent. The joint dataset enjoys benefits in the form of different linguistic patterns, text length variations, and platform-sensitive sentiment expression, which enhances better generalization and minimizes bias of the data. Once the data is preprocessed, and features are extracted using a proposed FCM technique, the RRT dataset consists of 66,867 training samples, 22,290 validation samples, and 22,290 test samples which are the main benchmark on the effectiveness of the proposed multi-stacked BiLSTM classifier.

The optimization process is guided by a learning rate of 0.001, which determines the size of each step taken. To prevent overfitting, a regularization strength of 0.01 is applied, penalizing large parameter values. The model architecture comprises of 3 hidden layers, each containing 256 units, and incorporates a dropout rate of 0.5 for regularization purposes. During training, a batch size of 64 samples is utilized, along with the ReLU activation function and Adam optimizer. The word embeddings have a dimensionality of 300, and input sequences consist of 50 tokens. Additionally, a random forest ensemble model with 100 trees, each having a maximum depth of 10, is employed. In the case of CNNs, the convolutional kernels have a size of 3 and a stride of 1. By fine-tuning these hyperparameters, the performance of the model can be optimized, leading to improved accuracy levels in sentiment analysis tasks.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the presented HybridTweetSentinel framework is effective to reduce the major issues of Twitter sentiment analysis due to the integration of advanced text representation and deep learning methods. In comparison with other priority-based vectorization approaches, including Bag of Words, TF-IDF, Word2Vec, GloVe, and FastText which are majorly developed based on word frequency and mostly fail at capturing deeper semantic relations, proposed FCM vectorization combines frequency-based information with semantic information based on the cooccurrence matrix. Such rich representation has much better contextual inference and minimizes overfitting of neural networks. Moreover, the multi-stacked BiLSTM architecture is more effective in improving the performance of the classification because the tweet sequences are processed both forward and backward, and therefore the model can simultaneously capture both past and future dependencies. The hierarchical learning of features in the result of stacking several BiLSTM layers results in enhanced sentiment discrimination in complex and noisy Twitter data. The framework is proven to be robust because it attains a training accuracy of 94.68, validation accuracy of 94.50 and training and test loss of 1.9 and 1.5, respectively, which proves its reliability and generalization ability. Nevertheless, even with good results, HybridTweetSentinel suffers because it has a weakness of engaging in the proper interpretation of evolving slang, shortforms as well as subtle phrases that are frequently employed in social media. The quality of training data and its variety are also important to its effectiveness. Further studies can be directed towards adaptive learning processes, constant updating of the model, and bias sensitive data augmentation to make it even more scaled, robust and applicable in real-time. Compared to single-recursive layers, multi-stacked recurrence layer and co-occurrence-based feature extraction make the proposed FCM with MS-BiLSTM to be of higher computational cost, which can raise the inference and training time. It is also struggling to deal with sarcasm, multitongue tweets, emojis and noisy informal text in Twitter that is not accurately handled. Furthermore, real-time dashboards of the model based on the high-volume streams of tweets might be hard to scale and, thus, the lightweight models and streaming systems will need work in the future.

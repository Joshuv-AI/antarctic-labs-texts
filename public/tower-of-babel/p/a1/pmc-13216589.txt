High temperature thermoelectric energy conversion in half metallic Cs2MBr6 double perovskites M=Mn, Mo, Ta, Ir from first principles
Authors: Mohamed El Amine El Goutni, Yasmeen G. Abou El-Reash, Mohammed Batouche, Hela Ferjani, Taieb Seddik
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Vacancy-ordered double perovskites have recently emerged as promising multifunctional materials for energy and spin-based technologies. In this work, we present a comprehensive first-principles investigation of the structural, electronic, magnetic, mechanical, and thermoelectric properties of Cs2MBr6 (M=Mn, Mo, Ta, Ir). The compounds are found to be thermodynamically and mechanically stable, exhibiting ductile mechanical behavior suitable for device fabrication. Electronic structure analysis reveals robust half-metallic ferromagnetism with 100% spin polarization at the Fermi level, classifying Cs2MnBr6 and Cs2TaBr6 as inverted half-metals, while Cs2MoBr6 and Cs2IrBr6 show conventional half-metallic character. Remarkably high thermoelectric performance is predicted over a wide temperature range (300–1000 K). Substantial Seebeck coefficients exceeding 400–1000 μV/K at room temperature, combined with thermally activated electrical conductivity and suppressed electronic thermal conductivity, yield near-unity and thermally stable figures of merit (ZT ≈ 0.83–0.99). The outstanding thermoelectric efficiency is directly correlated with spin-selective transport, large spin-dependent band gaps, and favorable carrier effective masses.

Introduction

The escalating global energy crisis, exacerbated by the depletion of fossil fuel reserves and the imperative to mitigate anthropogenic climate change, has catalyzed intense research into next-generation functional materials capable of simultaneously addressing energy conversion efficiency and electronic functionality challenges1,2. Within this context, halide perovskites and their vacancy-ordered derivatives have emerged as particularly promising platforms due to their structural flexibility, tunable optoelectronic properties, and inherent stability advantages over conventional semiconductors3–5. Among these materials, lead-free halide double perovskites (HDPs) with the general composition A2BX6 (where A represents an alkali metal or larger cation, B denotes a transition metal center, and X signifies a halide anion) have garnered substantial attention as environmentally sustainable alternatives for photovoltaic, optoelectronic, and thermoelectric applications6–8.

Recent theoretical and experimental investigations have demonstrated that cesium-based double perovskites exhibit remarkable structural stability, lower toxicity compared to lead-containing analogues, and tunable electronic properties through compositional engineering9–12. The choice of Cs⁺ as the A-site cation provides enhanced ionic bonding strength and improved moisture stability compared to smaller alkali metals, while simultaneously enabling facile synthetic accessibility through conventional solid-state and solution-based routes13,14. Furthermore, systematic substitution at the B-site transition metal position and X-site halide anion offers a rich parameter space for tailoring electronic band structures, magnetic ordering phenomena, and phonon transport characteristics—all critical parameters governing thermoelectric and spintronic performance15,16.

Despite extensive research on alkali-metal-based halide double perovskites (such as Rb2AgBiBr₆, Cs2AgInBr₆, and Cs2AgSbCl₆ with well-documented optoelectronic properties), the systematic investigation of Cs2MBr₆ compounds incorporating variable transition metals (M=Mn, Mo, Ta, Ir) remains largely unexplored. The strategic selection of these specific transition metals is motivated by their distinct electronic configurations and magnetic properties: manganese (Mn) as a classic magnetic 3d element with high-spin configurations suitable for ferromagnetic ordering; molybdenum (Mo) as a 4d metal exhibiting mixed-valence behavior and strong spin–orbit coupling effects; tantalum (Ta) as a 5d refractory metal known for robust magnetic anisotropy and potential topological character; and iridium (Ir) as a 5d element with pronounced spin–orbit coupling capable of generating exotic electronic states including half-metallic ferromagnetism17–20.

Recent computational studies on related halide double perovskites have revealed remarkable synergistic effects combining thermoelectric and spintronic functionalities. For instance, Ameer et al.21–25 reported pronounced ferromagnetism in Cs2CuCrX₆ (X=Cl, Br) with high spin polarization from 3d orbital interactions. Similarly, investigations of Cs2CuMoX₆ compounds26–29 demonstrated tunable electronic and magnetic characteristics under strain, while studies on Cs2MGaBr₆ (M=Li, Na) achieved exceptional thermoelectric figures of merit (ZT = 1.04–1.08 at room temperature)30, comparable to or exceeding commercial Bi2Te₃-based systems. Additionally, theoretical predictions for Ba2GaXO₆ (X=V, Nb, Ta) oxide double perovskites yielded outstanding high-temperature ZT values (1.78–2.36 at 1500 K) through ultra-low lattice thermal conductivity, suggesting that Ta-based materials merit particular investigation. Furthermore, recent experimental work on iron-doped double perovskites (Cs2Ag(Bi:Fe)Br₆) demonstrated successful integration of magnetic elements into halide perovskite frameworks for spintronic applications31.

These precedents collectively motivate a comprehensive first-principles investigation of Cs2MBr₆ (M=Mn, Mo, Ta, Ir) compounds to systematically evaluate their structural stability, electronic band structure modifications, magnetic ground states, mechanical properties, and thermoelectric performance across operationally relevant temperature ranges. The bromide anion choice specifically leverages its intermediate size (larger than Cl− but smaller than I−), which typically promotes favorable band gap engineering and reduced carrier effective masses compared to chloride analogues. Our objectives include: (1) establishing thermodynamic stability through formation and cohesive energy analysis; (2) verifying dynamical stability via phonon dispersion calculations to confirm these are experimentally realizable compounds; (3) elucidating spin–orbit coupling effects on electronic structure and potentially discovering half-metallic ferromagnetic states; (4) characterizing mechanical properties to assess suitability for thin-film device integration; and (5) evaluating thermoelectric transport coefficients (Seebeck coefficient, electrical conductivity, thermal conductivity, and figure of merit) to benchmark performance against state-of-the-art materials across 300–1000 K. By systematically varying the B-site transition metal, this study aims to establish structure–property relationships guiding experimental synthesis and optimization of Cs2MBr₆ materials as promising multifunctional platforms bridging thermoelectric energy harvesting and spintronic technologies for sustainable energy applications.

Computational methodology

All calculations in this work were performed within the framework of density functional theory to investigate the structural, electronic, magnetic, mechanical, and thermoelectric properties of the vacancy-ordered double perovskites Cs2MBr₆ (M=Mn, Mo, Ta, Ir). The simulations were carried out using the WIEN2k code32, which employs the full-potential linearized augmented plane wave plus local orbitals (FP-LAPW + lo) method33, allowing an accurate, all-electron treatment without shape approximations for the potential or charge density. Structural optimizations were initially conducted using the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof generalized gradient approximation (PBE-GGA) for the exchange–correlation functional34, and the equilibrium lattice parameters, bulk moduli, and pressure derivatives were obtained by fitting the total energy as a function of volume to the Birch–Murnaghan equation of state. To achieve a more reliable description of the electronic structure and to mitigate the well-known band-gap underestimation of semilocal functionals, the modified Becke–Johnson (mBJ) exchange potential was employed for all electronic and magnetic calculations35. Spin polarization was explicitly included, and spin–orbit coupling was treated self-consistently using the second-variational approach, which is particularly important for accurately capturing relativistic effects in the 4d and 5d transition metals considered in this study. The magnetic ground state was determined by comparing total energies for non-magnetic, ferromagnetic, and antiferromagnetic configurations. The muffin-tin radii were selected as RMT(Cs) = 2.50 a.u., RMT(M) = 2.20 a.u., and RMT(Br) = 2.25 a.u., while the plane-wave cutoff was defined by RMT × Kmax = 8.0; angular momentum expansions were taken up to lmax = 10 inside the muffin-tin spheres and lmax = 4 in the interstitial region. Brillouin-zone integrations were performed using Monkhorst–Pack k-point meshes, with 12 × 12 × 12 points used for structural relaxation and 18 × 18 × 18 points for accurate electronic structure and density-of-states calculations36. Thermodynamic stability was evaluated through formation and cohesive energy calculations, while mechanical stability was assessed by computing the independent elastic constants (C₁₁, C₁2, C₄₄) using the stress–strain method and deriving polycrystalline moduli via the Voigt–Reuss–Hill scheme. Dynamical stability was examined by calculating phonon dispersion relations using the finite-displacement method as implemented in the Phono3py package with 2 × 2 × 3 supercells37. Thermoelectric transport properties, including the Seebeck coefficient, electrical conductivity, and electronic thermal conductivity, were calculated using the BoltzTraP2 code within the semiclassical Boltzmann transport formalism under the constant relaxation time approximation38, employing ultra-dense k-point meshes of 100,000–points and covering a temperature range from 300 to 1000 K. All self-consistent calculations were converged to 10−5 Ry in total energy and 10−4 e in charge density. Since direct experimental data for Cs2MnBr₆, Cs2MoBr₆, Cs2TaBr₆, and Cs2IrBr₆ are presently unavailable, the reliability of the adopted computational protocol was assessed by reference to chemically related vacancy-ordered and halide double perovskites reported in the literature. In particular, the combination of WC-based structural optimization and mBJ-based electronic-structure treatment has previously been shown to reproduce equilibrium lattice parameters with good accuracy and to provide improved band-gap estimates compared with semilocal functionals. We now state this validation strategy explicitly, while noting that the present work remains predictive in nature and is intended to motivate future experimental synthesis and characterization.

Results and discussion

Structural and thermodynamic stability of Cs2MBr₆ (M=Mn, Mo, Ta, Ir)

The structural framework and thermodynamic stability of the vacancy-ordered double perovskites Cs2MBr₆ (M=Mn, Mo, Ta, Ir) were systematically analyzed to assess their suitability for optoelectronic, spintronic, and thermoelectric applications. All compounds crystallize in the cubic face-centered structure belonging to the Fm-3 m (No. 225) space group, which is a hallmark of A2BX₆-type vacancy-ordered double perovskites. Within this crystal architecture, Cs⁺ ions occupy the 8c Wyckoff sites at fractional coordinates (1/4, 1/4, 1/4), while the transition-metal cations M are positioned at the 4a sites (0, 0, 0). The bromide anions reside at the 24e sites (u, 0, 0), where the internal coordinate u determines the geometry of the isolated [MBr₆]2− octahedra and directly affects the M–Br bond lengths and associated electronic interactions. Equilibrium structural parameters were obtained through total-energy minimization using the PBE-GGA exchange–correlation functional, and the resulting energy–volume data were fitted to the Birch–Murnaghan equation of state to extract the equilibrium lattice constant (a), bulk modulus (B), and its pressure derivative (B′), as depicted in Fig. 139,40. Bulk moduli values, representing resistance to volumetric compression, range from 27.69 GPa (Cs2MnBr₆-FM) to 34.08 GPa (Cs2IrBr₆-FM), with Ir-based compound showing the highest stiffness due to stronger Ir–Br bonding and relativistic effects in the 5d orbital. The non-magnetic (NM) phases generally exhibit marginally higher bulk moduli than FM, reflecting denser electronic configurations without spin polarization. Pressure derivatives (B') cluster around 4.4–5.1, typical for halide perovskites and indicating stable elastic responses under hydrostatic pressure. These moderate bulk moduli values suggest sufficient mechanical rigidity for device integration while retaining flexibility for thermal expansion during operation.

Thermodynamic stability represents a critical prerequisite for experimental synthesizability and practical application of Cs2MBr₆ halide double perovskites. The formation energy (ΔHf), calculated per atom relative to elemental reference states, quantifies the energetic favorability of compound formation from constituent elements. The systematic trend in formation energies (Ir > Ta > Mn > Mo: − 2.59 > − 2.28 > − 1.65 > − 1.41 eV/atom) reveals important insights into bonding characteristics and electronic structure effects. The calculated cohesive-energy trend, Ir > Ta > Mn > Mo (7.89 > 6.78 > 5.98 > 4.59 eV/atom), closely follows the corresponding formation-energy sequence, thereby indicating internally consistent thermodynamic behavior across the Cs2MBr₆ series. This parallel evolution suggests that compounds with more favorable formation energies also exhibit stronger overall interatomic bonding. The relatively large cohesive energies obtained for all four materials confirm that they are strongly bonded crystalline solids with good intrinsic resistance to decomposition and, therefore, favorable prospects for experimental realization. When these bonding indicators are compared with the elastic response, a broadly consistent picture emerges: compounds with stronger overall bonding generally tend to display greater resistance to compression. However, this relationship is not strictly monotonic, since the bulk modulus is governed not only by bond strength but also by equilibrium volume, bond compressibility, and the directional nature of M–Br interactions. Accordingly, cohesive energy and bulk modulus should be regarded as complementary descriptors that together provide a more complete understanding of the structural stability of the Cs2MBr₆ vacancy-ordered double perovskites.

In addition, several optical branches display a relatively flat character, particularly in the intermediate frequency range (~ 2–6 THz). This behavior is indicative of localized vibrational modes associated with the isolated [MBr₆]2− octahedra, a hallmark of vacancy-ordered double perovskite structures. These results confirm robust dynamical stability across the entire Cs2MBr₆ series and support the thermodynamic and structural analyses presented earlier, reinforcing their potential for experimental realization and high-temperature applications. To further elucidate lattice dynamics, the total and projected phonon density of states (PDOS), shown in Fig. 3b,c, were analyzed. The PDOS reveals that the low-frequency region (< ~ 2 THz) is dominated by vibrations of the heavier Cs atoms, while the intermediate and high-frequency regions (~ 2–32 THz) are primarily governed by M–Br octahedral vibrations, with a significant contribution from Br atoms. Notably, Cs2MoBr₆ exhibits the widest frequency range due to stronger bonding interactions, whereas Cs2IrBr₆ shows comparatively compressed spectra due to heavier atomic mass and relativistic effects. This vibrational decomposition suggests that the presence of heavy Cs atoms contributing to low-frequency modes, combined with localized optical modes, may favor reduced lattice thermal conductivity an important factor for enhancing thermoelectric performance.

Mechanical properties and elastic characteristics

The mechanical properties of Cs2MBr₆ (M = Mn, Mo, Ta, Ir) vacancy-ordered double perovskite halides play a critical role in determining their structural durability, processability, and long-term performance under operational conditions relevant to optoelectronic and photovoltaic applications. For cubic crystal systems, the complete mechanical response is fully characterized by three independent second-order elastic constants: C₁₁ (longitudinal elastic stiffness along <100> crystallographic directions), C₁2 (coupling between longitudinal and transverse strains), and C₄₄ (shear resistance along <110> crystallographic planes).

Electronic and magnetic properties

The electronic band structures of Cs₂MBr₆ (M = Mn, Mo, Ta, Ir) compounds calculated using the modified Becke-Johnson potential with spin–orbit coupling (mBJ + SOC) are presented in Fig. 5. The calculations reveal fascinating spin-dependent electronic properties that distinguish these materials as promising candidates for spintronic applications51,52. Our results demonstrate that all four compounds exhibit half-metallic (HM) behavior, characterized by metallic character in one spin channel while maintaining a semiconducting gap in the other. However, a critical distinction emerges between two types of half-metallic configurations. Cs₂MnBr₆ and Cs₂TaBr₆ display inverted half-metallic ferromagnetic (HMF) behavior, where the α-spin channel is metallic (Gap (α) = 0.0 eV) while the β-spin channel exhibits semiconductor character with band gaps of 2.02 eV and 2.79 eV, respectively. In contrast, Cs₂MoBr₆ and Cs₂IrBr₆ demonstrate conventional HMF behavior, with metallic β-spin channels (Gap(β) = 0.0 eV) and semiconducting α-spin channels featuring gaps of 1.51 eV and 1.82 eV, respectively. The Fermi level (EF) position varies systematically across the series. For the inverted HMF compounds (Cs₂MnBr₆ and Cs₂TaBr₆), EF intersects the valence bands in the α-spin channel, while for conventional HMF materials (Cs₂MoBr₆ and Cs₂IrBr₆), it crosses the conduction bands in the β-spin channel. This fundamental difference in electronic structure indicates distinct mechanisms of half-metallicity arising from the different d-electron configurations of the transition metal ions. The spin polarization at the Fermi level, quantified as: P = (N↑(EF)—N↓(EF))/(N↑(EF) + N↓(EF)), approaches 100% for all compounds, confirming their excellent half-metallic character. The calculated half-metallic gaps (EHM), defined as the minimum energy required to destroy the half-metallic state, range from 0.73 eV (Cs₂MnBr₆) to 0.86 eV (Cs₂IrBr₆). These substantial values suggest robust half-metallicity that should persist at elevated temperatures and under moderate external perturbations. In the present work, the distinction between “conventional” and “inverted” half-metallicity is used operationally to identify which spin channel remains metallic at the Fermi level. Cs₂MnBr₆ and Cs₂TaBr₆ are classified here as inverted half-metals because the spin-up channel is metallic while the spin-down channel is gapped, whereas Cs₂MoBr₆ and Cs₂IrBr₆ show the opposite arrangement. More specifically, the DOS analysis indicates that the states crossing EF are dominated by transition-metal d states hybridized with Br-p states, and the different orbital filling patterns of Mn, Mo, Ta, and Ir govern whether the metallic crossing occurs in the spin-up or spin-down channel. We have clarified this terminology to avoid implying a broader topological or carrier-type classification not explicitly established in this study. Cs₂TaBr₆ exhibits particularly pronounced Ta-d peaks at EF in the majority channel, with values reaching approximately 6.0 states/eV. The α-spin Ta-d states show intense contributions throughout the energy range from − 2 to + 2 eV, with the highest peak of about 6 states/eV occurring near the Fermi level. The d1 configuration of Ta4+ produces a sharp, half-filled feature. The β-spin channel maintains a clear semiconducting gap with occupied state densities around 4–5 states/eV below − 2 eV. The strong hybridization between Ta-d and Br-p orbitals is evident in the PDOS, where Br-p contributions reach approximately 1.2 states/eV in regions overlapping with Ta-d states, explaining the partially covalent character of Ta-Br bonding. Cs-s and Cs-p states peak at about 0.15 states/eV around − 4 to − 2 eV but remain negligible at EF. For Cs₂IrBr₆, the heavy 5d element Ir shows substantial spin–orbit effects, manifested in the complex fine structure of the Ir-d PDOS near EF. The β-spin Ir-d states crossing the Fermi level exhibit peak values around 4 states/eV, while the α-spin channel shows a gap with maximum values of approximately 10 states/eV in the occupied region around − 1 to − 2 eV. The total DOS at EF reaches approximately 10 states/eV in the metallic β-spin channel. Cs contributions remain below 0.12 states/eV for both s and p orbitals. The Br-p orbitals show considerable involvement with peaks reaching 1.2–1.8 states/eV distributed across the valence region, particularly strong between − 3 and − 1 eV, indicating significant Ir-Br covalent character. A notable trend across the series is the systematic variation in the magnitude of metal d-orbital contributions at the Fermi level, decreasing from Mn (~12 states/eV) to Mo (~6 states/eV) to Ta (~6 states/eV) to Ir (~4 states/eV), correlating with the increasing d-electron filling and orbital hybridization effects.

The calculated magnetic moments reveal systematic trends related to the nominal d-electron count of the transition metal ions (Table 5). Cs₂MnBr₆ exhibits the largest total magnetic moment of 2.96 μB, primarily localized on the Mn site (3.0 μB), consistent with the high-spin d5 configuration of Mn2+. The small negative moment on Br atoms (-0.02 μB) and positive interstitial moment (0.12 μB) indicate weak spin polarization of the ligand and delocalized electrons. Cs₂MoBr₆ shows a total moment of 2.0 μB, with 1.42 μB on Mo, significantly smaller than the nominal d2 value due to substantial hybridization with Br-p orbitals. The interstitial contribution (0.50 μB) is notably larger than in Cs₂MnBr₆, suggesting greater delocalization of magnetic electrons. Cs₂TaBr₆ and Cs₂IrBr₆ display total moments of 1.0 μB each, with reduced metal-site contributions (0.61 μB and 0.53 μB, respectively) indicating significant orbital quenching and covalency effects. The magnetic moments on Cs atoms remain essentially zero across all compounds, confirming their non-magnetic role. The systematic variation in interstitial moments (0.12 to 0.50 μB) correlates with the degree of electron delocalization, being largest for Mo and smallest for Mn. These findings suggest that while the magnetic moments are primarily carried by the d-orbitals of transition metals, the extended nature of these orbitals and their hybridization with Br-p states play crucial roles in determining the magnitude and distribution of magnetization. The integer or near-integer total magnetic moments obtained here are consistent with the half-metallic nature of the compounds, since one spin channel is metallic while the other is gapped. In a broader sense, this behavior is reminiscent of Slater–Pauling-like electron counting commonly discussed for half-metals. However, unlike full- and half-Heusler systems, vacancy-ordered halide double perovskites do not obey a universal Slater–Pauling relation of fixed form. Therefore, we interpret the present moments primarily as a consequence of spin-selective band filling and metal–halide hybridization rather than as strict adherence to a single canonical Slater–Pauling rule. The reduced local moments on Ta and Ir relative to simple nominal d-electron expectations can be understood from the combined effects of covalent hybridization, crystal-field splitting, and relativistic interactions. In particular, the spatially extended 5d orbitals of Ta and Ir hybridize more strongly with Br-p states than the more localized 3d states of Mn, causing part of the spin density to delocalize onto the ligands and interstitial region. In addition, the octahedral crystal field redistributes the d-level occupancy, while spin–orbit coupling is especially important for Ir and further modifies the spin-resolved occupation. These effects reduce the metal-centered moment without destroying the overall half-metallic ferromagnetic state.

The electrical conductivity (σ/τ) displays a strong temperature dependence characteristic of thermally activated transport. At 300 K, σ/τ is relatively low for all compounds, ranging from ~ 2 × 101⁸ S/m s for Cs₂MnBr₆ to below 1 × 101⁸ S/m s for Cs₂IrBr₆. With increasing temperature, σ/τ rises sharply, reaching approximately 90 × 101⁸ S/m s for Cs₂MnBr₆, ~ 35 × 101⁸ S/m s for Cs₂MoBr₆, ~ 25 × 101⁸ S/m s for Cs₂TaBr₆, and ~ 6 × 101⁸ S/m s for Cs₂IrBr₆ at 1000 K. This hierarchy in conductivity is strongly governed by the electron effective mass (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$m_{e}^{*}$$\end{document}). Cs₂MnBr₆ and Cs₂MoBr₆ possess the lowest electron effective masses (0.73 and 0.75, respectively), enabling higher carrier mobility and a more rapid increase in conductivity with temperature. Conversely, Cs₂IrBr₆ exhibits the largest electron effective mass (0.86), which limits carrier mobility and explains its comparatively weaker conductivity enhancement despite the presence of a metallic spin channel. These results highlight the critical role of carrier effective mass in determining the balance between thermopower and electrical transport56–59.

The electronic thermal conductivity (κₑ/τ) follows a similar temperature-dependent trend as σ/τ, increasing steadily with temperature due to the growing carrier population. At 1000 K, κₑ/τ reaches approximately 58 × 1014 W/m K s for Cs₂MnBr₆, ~ 35 × 1014 W/m K s for Cs₂MoBr₆, ~ 22 × 1014 W/m K s for Cs₂TaBr₆, and ~ 11 × 1014 W/m K s for Cs₂IrBr₆. Despite this increase, κₑ/τ remains relatively moderate compared to conventional metallic thermoelectrics. This behavior arises from spin-selective transport, where only one spin channel contributes significantly to conduction, while the large spin-dependent band gaps suppress bipolar thermal conduction. The combined evolution of S and σ/τ results in a steadily increasing power factor with temperature. At 300 K, S2σ/τ ranges from ~ 2 to 9 × 101⁰ W/m K2 s, while at 1000 K it reaches ~ 48 × 101⁰ W/m K2 s for Cs₂MnBr₆, ~ 32 × 101⁰ W/m K2 s for Cs₂MoBr₆, ~ 22 × 101⁰ W/m K2 s for Cs₂TaBr₆, and ~ 10 × 101⁰ W/m K2 s for Cs₂IrBr₆. The superior power factor of Cs₂MnBr₆ reflects its optimal balance between relatively low electron effective mass (high conductivity) and sufficiently large hole effective mass (high Seebeck coefficient)59.

Most notably, the dimensionless figure of merit ZT (ZT = S2σT/κe) exhibits exceptionally high values and remarkable thermal stability across the entire temperature range. At 300 K, ZT values are approximately 0.92 (Cs₂MnBr₆), 0.97 (Cs₂MoBr₆), 0.98 (Cs₂TaBr₆), and 0.99 (Cs₂IrBr₆). With increasing temperature, ZT decreases only modestly, remaining as high as ~ 0.83, ~ 0.89, ~ 0.94, and ~ 0.93, respectively, at 1000 K. This near-flat ZT profile over a 700 K temperature window is exceptional and indicates a highly favorable interplay between thermopower, electrical conductivity, and thermal transport. The superior ZT stability of Cs₂TaBr₆ and Cs₂IrBr₆ can be directly linked to their larger spin-dependent band gaps and heavier hole effective masses, which maintain high Seebeck coefficients while limiting excessive increases in electronic thermal conductivity. In contrast, the more pronounced ZT reduction in Cs₂MnBr₆ reflects its smaller insulating spin gap and higher conductivity, which slightly increases κₑ/τ at elevated temperatures. In summary, the thermoelectric behavior of Cs₂MBr₆ compounds is governed by a delicate but highly favorable balance between spin-resolved band gaps, carrier effective masses, and half-metallic character. The coexistence of remarkably high Seebeck coefficients, thermally activated yet efficient electrical transport, and near-unity, temperature-stable ZT values positions these vacancy-ordered halide perovskites among the most promising theoretical thermoelectric materials for mid- to high-temperature energy conversion. We emphasize that the present figure-of-merit analysis is based on the electronic thermal conductivity obtained from Boltzmann transport theory and does not include an explicit first-principles evaluation of the lattice thermal conductivity, κL. Accordingly, the reported ZT values should be interpreted as upper-bound estimates within the CRTA framework rather than definitive experimental figures of merit. A full quantitative assessment of thermoelectric efficiency will require future anharmonic phonon and phonon-transport calculations to determine κL explicitly.

Conclusion

This first-principles study identifies Cs₂MBr₆ (M = Mn, Mo, Ta, Ir) vacancy-ordered double perovskites as promising spin-polarized thermoelectric materials. All four compounds are found to be structurally, mechanically, and dynamically stable in their cubic ferromagnetic phase, supporting their potential feasibility. The electronic structure reveals robust half-metallicity with complete spin polarization at the Fermi level, while the transport calculations predict substantial Seebeck coefficients and favorable thermoelectric response over the 300–1000 K range. In particular, the coexistence of spin-selective electronic conduction, relatively low electron effective masses, and larger hole effective masses provides a consistent explanation for the balanced combination of electrical transport and thermopower. Within the approximations adopted in this work, the calculated ZT values remain close to unity across a broad temperature range, highlighting the potential of these materials for high-temperature thermoelectric applications. Overall, the present results provide a useful theoretical basis for future experimental validation and further investigation of Cs₂MBr₆-based multifunctional materials.

An Analysis of Trends in the Burden of Maternal Hemorrhage Attributable to Iron Deficiency in China from 1990 to 2023: A Population-Based Study
Authors: Li Yang, Zongao Cai, Wenting Xu, Lihong Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study evaluated the burden of maternal hemorrhage (MH) attributable to iron deficiency in China from 1990 to 2023.MethodsThis cross-sectional study utilized the data from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2023 to analyze the disease burden associated with maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China. Joinpoint and age-period-cohort (APC) analysis were employed to assess the trend of the disease burden. Decomposition analysis was conducted to evaluate the unique contributions of changes in population age structure, population growth, and epidemiological trends to the disease burden trend.ResultsFrom 1990 to 2023, the number of deaths and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) of MH attributable to iron deficiency in China decreased from 2.08 to 0.03 (thousands) and 130.15 to 1.93 (thousands), respectively. The age-standardized disability-adjusted life years (ASDR) and age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR) decreased from 18.72 to 0.33 (per 100000 population) and from 0.30 to 0(per 100000 population), respectively. The ASMR and ASDR showed a downward trend (p < 0.001). The APC analysis showed that the mortality rate and DALYs rate first increased and then decreased with age. The mortality rate and DALYs rate decreased over time. The cohort effect showed that from the early birth cohort to the late birth cohort, the mortality rate and DALYs rate generally showed a downward trend. Decomposition analysis indicated that the changes in population age structure and epidemic trends had a significant impact on the decline in the mortality rate and DALYs rate.ConclusionAlthough the overall disease burden of maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China has shown a downward trend, the disease situation remains severe. Iron deficiency among women of different age groups in China, especially those in the 20–39 age range, has led to a serious burden of maternal hemorrhage.Keywords: iron deficiency, maternal hemorrhage, disease burden, China, cross-sectional study

Introduction

Maternal hemorrhage (MH) is defined in the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) database as postpartum hemorrhage (blood loss exceeding 500 mL after vaginal delivery or more than 1,000 mL after cesarean section), preterm bleeding (vaginal bleeding after 20 weeks of pregnancy but before delivery), or hemorrhagic placental disorders, regardless of the amount of blood loss or the timing of the bleeding event.1 From 1992 to 2021, the global incidence of maternal hemorrhage increased from 13,847,163 cases to 13,914,838 cases, but the ASIR decreased from 948.07 per 100,000 people to 722.16 per 100,000 people, indicating a reduction in risk.2 It is projected that by 2036, the number of global maternal hemorrhage cases will decrease to 10,287,971 cases, and the ASIR will drop to 479.83 cases per 100,000 people.2 After 20 weeks of pregnancy, the most common cause of unexplained prenatal bleeding is placenta previa and placental abruption, which is an emergency situation that requires prompt intervention.3 Placenta previa increased the risk of prenatal bleeding by nine times and the risk of postpartum bleeding by eighteen times.4 Visiting the hospital for more than 12 hours, living in rural areas, not having prenatal check-ups, and elderly pregnant women were important influencing factors for the outcome of prenatal bleeding.5 Prenatal bleeding might lead to adverse outcomes such as postpartum bleeding, emergency cesarean section, and premature birth.6 Postpartum bleeding is the most common cause of maternal deaths worldwide and seriously affects countries with lower economic income.7 Postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) accounted for approximately 8.0% of maternal deaths in developed countries and about 19.7% in developing countries.8 In Turkey, the maternal mortality rate due to PPH among cesarean section mothers is 2.27 times that of vaginal delivery mothers.9 Although postpartum hemorrhage can be prevented and treated, due to insufficient maternal health care infrastructure, insufficient healthcare resources, and a cultural environment that undervalues women’s health, postpartum hemorrhage resulted in thousands of female deaths every year.10 Multiple births, vaginal delivery, anemia, number of pregnancies, age, large babies, gestational hypertension diseases, and low educational level were independent risk factors for PPH.11,12

Iron deficiency anemia during pregnancy and postpartum period is one of the most common risk factors in obstetrics and perinatal medicine.13 Iron deficiency anemia is the most common type of anemia during pregnancy.14 Iron is an essential micronutrient for the human body and is an important substance for the development of the placenta and fetus during pregnancy.15 When the stored iron in the body is exhausted, iron deficiency occurs.16 The prevalence of anemia is higher in developing countries.14 A single-center study in Turkey showed that the prevalence of iron deficiency and iron deficiency anemia among pregnant women was 41% and 36% respectively.17 A prospective cohort study in Saudi Arabia showed that the prevalence of iron-deficiency anemia during pregnancy increased from 11.8% in the early stage of pregnancy to 28.8% in the late stage of pregnancy.18

Anemia might not only lead to uterine inertia and excessive maternal bleeding during Labour, but also to complications such as postpartum sepsis and delayed wound healing.19 There was a global decline in mortality due to maternal hemorrhage associated with iron deficiency from 1990 to 2019.20 The proportion of maternal hemorrhage in East Asia was relatively high in 1990, but declined by 2019.20 From 1990 to 2019, the disease burden of maternal hemorrhage decreased most significantly, but maternal hemorrhage was still one of the common causes of maternal death.20 Maternal hemorrhage in the 20–24 age group had the highest disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) rate, illustrating a potential nutritional support gap in early pregnancy.20 Only 25% of women with anemia during pregnancy were taking iron, and 63% of these women had a late follow-up visit.21 Women with iron deficiency anemia increased occurrence of both placenta previa and placental abruption.22 Iron deficiency anaemia was an independent risk factor for PPH in tension-free postpartum hemorrhage.23 A retrospective study from Pakistan showed that anemic women had more antepartum hemorrhage (32% vs 19%; p < 0.05) and postpartum hemorrhage (79% vs 28%; p < 0.05) than did nonanemic women.24

Despite these insights, there are still significant research gaps in understanding the evolution of maternal hemorrhage burden due to iron deficiency in China, especially from an age perspective. In this study, GBD 2023 data were used to analyze the trend of maternal hemorrhage burden attributable to iron deficiency in China from 1990 to 2023. Our study aimed to obtain a more comprehensive understanding of the long-term effects of iron deficiency leading to maternal hemorrhage to provide a theoretical basis for developing targeted prevention and intervention measures.

Materials and Methods

Data Sources

GBD research is the largest and most comprehensive effort to quantify health losses in different regions and at different times, in order to improve health systems and eliminate disparities. GBD creates a unique platform to compare the magnitude of diseases, injuries, and risk factors across age groups, sexes, countries, regions, and time.25 For decision-makers, health sector leaders, researchers, and informed citizens, the GBD approach provides an opportunity to compare their countries’health progress to that of other countries, and to understand the leading causes of health loss that could potentially be avoided, like high blood pressure, smoking, and household air pollution.25 The GBD produces regular estimates of all-cause mortality, deaths by cause, years of life lost due to premature mortality (YLLs), years lived with disability (YLDs), and DALYs.25 The 375 causes of health loss studied within the GBD are arranged in hierarchical nested categories – referred to as “levels” – within the GBD “cause lists.”25 At the highest level, causes are split into very large categories: communicable, maternal, neonatal, and nutritional causes; non-communicable diseases; and injuries.25 Within each of those categories, causes of health loss are broken down with increasing specificity at each level.25 For example, maternal hemorrhage is the fourth-level cause in the list of causes of GBD. This study obtained data on death and DALYs of maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China by age group (ranging from <5 years to ≥95 years, with an interval of every 5 years) from GBD 2023.

Joinpoint Regression Analysis

Joinpoint regression establishes segmented regression according to the temporal characteristics of the disease distribution, which divides the study time into different intervals through several connection points, and performs trend fitting and optimization for each interval, so as to evaluate the specific characteristics of disease changes in different intervals in the global time range in more detail. This analysis method can identify turning points of trend change and automatically detect points of significant trend change in time series data by piecewise linear regression, which is very suitable for studying the effect of iron deficiency on the trend of maternal hemorrhage burden. The joinpoint regression program (version 5.2.0.0) was used to calculate annual percent change (APC), average annual percent change (AAPC) and their 95% CI to describe the trend of disease burden of maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China from 1990 to 2023. If APC and 95% CI>0, the results showed a significant upward trend. APC and 95% CI<0 indicated a significant downward trend.

Age-Period-Cohort Model

Age-period-cohort (APC) model is a statistical tool used in epidemiology, demography, and social sciences to parse and analyze the influence of three different time-related factors on specific outcomes or trends: age effect, period effect, and cohort effect. The APC model has been widely used in epidemiology, demography, sociology, and economics. The APC model provided insights into the effects of age, period and cohort on the mortality and DALYs rates of maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency over time in China. The model helps to assess how each factor individually affects the observed trend. The data were divided into 9 consecutive age groups spaced 5 years apart (10–14, 15–19, 20–24, 20-24, …, 50-54). This study calculated six five-year periods (1994–1998, 1999–2003, …, 2019–2023) and 14 five-year birth cohorts (1942–1946, 1947–1951, …, 2007–2011).

Decomposition Analysis

Decomposition analysis is a method used to understand patterns and changes in data, which can help understand how different factors affect overall changes. This method can break down the changes in disease burden indicators during a certain period into factors including age, population, and epidemiological change. This study employed decomposition analysis to assess the unique contributions of age, population, and epidemiological change to the observed trends in disease burden. Through decomposition analysis, we assessed the contribution of each factor to the overall trend and determined whether it led to an increase or decrease in the maternal hemorrhage burden attributable to iron deficiency.

Joinpoint Regression Analysis

From 1990 to 2023, the ASMR of maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China generally showed a downward trend, with an AAPC of −11.9950(−13.2297,-10.7427)(p < 0.001) (Table 2). The period with the most significant decline in ASMR was from 2005 to 2012, with an APC of −21.29 (p < 0.05). It remained stable from 2012 to 2017 and from 2021 to 2023 (Figure 4). Similarly, ASDR also showed a downward trend, with an AAPC of −11.5808(−12.6983,-10.4490)(p < 0.001) (Table 2). The period with the most significant decline in ASDR occurred from 2017 to 2020, with an APC of −20.38 (p < 0.05), and remained stable from 2012 to 2017 and from 2021 to 2023 (Figure 4).

Age-Period-Cohort Analysis

The effects of age, period, and birth cohort on the mortality rate and DALYs rate of maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China were shown in Figure 5. From the perspective of the influence of age on the mortality rate and DALYs rate of maternal hemorrhage in China, both the mortality rate and DALYs rate first increased and then decreased with the increase of age. The mortality rate peaked in the 20–24 age group, with a risk ratios (RR) of 0.475 (95% CI 0.449–0.502). The influence of age on the DALYs rate also showed a similar trend. The highest RR occurred in the 20–24 age range, which was 32.057 (95% CI 30.907–33.250). The period effect indicated that the mortality rate and DALYs rate had decreased over time. Compared with 2004–2008, the RR of mortality and DALYs rates in 1994–1998 were 3.342 (95% CI 3.063–3.646) and 3.307 (95% CI 3.127–3.498), respectively. In contrast, the RR of mortality and DALYs rates from 2019 to 2023 were 0.190 (95% CI 0.159–0.227) and 0.219 (95% CI 0.195–0.246), respectively. Taking the birth cohort of 1974 as the reference, there were significant differences in other birth cohorts compared with it (p<0.001). The cohort effect of mortality and DALYs rates showed that from the early birth cohort to the late birth cohort, the mortality and DALYs rates generally showed a downward trend.

Decomposition Analysis

Figure 6 depicted the influencing factors of the changes in DALYs rate and mortality rate of maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China from 1990 to 2023. The age and epidemiological change had become the driving factors for the decline in DALYs rates and mortality rates. Population growth led to an increase in DALYs rates and mortality rates. The overall impact of these factors led to a decline in the DALYs rate and mortality rate. This study revealed that the dynamic relationship between age, population, and epidemiological change influenced the trend of maternal hemorrhage burden attributable to iron deficiency in China.

Discussion

This cross-sectional study found that the number of deaths, ASMR, the number of DALYs, and ASDR from 1990 to 2023 for maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China all showed a downward trend. Joinpoint regression analysis showed that ASMR and ASDR had generally shown a downward trend from 1990 to 2023, and the annual percent change varied in different periods. The analysis of age, period, and birth cohort showed that both mortality and DALYs rates first increased and then decreased with age. The mortality rate and DALYs rate decreased over time. The cohort effect showed that from the early birth cohort to the late birth cohort, the mortality rate and DALYs rate generally showed a downward trend. Decomposition analysis indicated that age and epidemiological change had a significant impact on the decline in mortality and DALYs rates of maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China. Although the overall disease burden of maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China was on a downward trend, the disease situation remained severe. These findings were of great significance for guiding the formulation of targeted prevention and intervention measures.

From 1990 to 2023, the number of deaths, ASMR, the number of DALYs, and ASDR of maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China all showed a downward trend, indicating that certain progress had been made in the prevention and control of maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China. A meta-analysis indicated that the risk of PPH increased as the anemia threshold decreased, and prenatal anemia was associated with an increased risk of prenatal bleeding.26 Iron supplementation during pregnancy in Turkey is free.17 It usually starts from the 16th week and continues until delivery.17 At present, China has fully liberalized and encouraged childbirth, provided child-rearing subsidies, reduced the costs of childbirth, upbringing and education, and addressed the problems of an aging population and low birth rate. If prenatal care for pregnant women can be provided free of charge across the country, it will not only boost their enthusiasm for prenatal check-ups but also promote fertility. Community workers should popularize the importance of iron supplementation and the harm of iron deficiency to both mothers and infants among women before and during pregnancy. Iron supplementation is most commonly administered orally.27 Oral preparations are widely available, convenient and affordable.27 When economic conditions permit, the government can provide them for free.

This cross-sectional study indicated that the distribution and trend of maternal hemorrhage burden attributable to iron deficiency in China varied among different age groups. The peak of deaths and DALYs in 1990 occurred among people aged 20 to 24. The peak of deaths in 2023 occurred among people aged 35 to 39, while the peak of DALYs was among those aged 30 to 34. Among women over 10 years old (in every age group), the mortality rate and DALYs rate of maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China decreased significantly in 2023 compared with 1990. Iron deficiency among women of different age groups in China, especially those aged 20–39, led to a severe burden of maternal hemorrhage. The peak incidence of maternal hemorrhage globally and in five different socio-demographic index (SDI) regions from 1992 to 2021 was between 20 and 29 years old, which was roughly the same as our research results.2 From 1990 to 2021, the burden of anemia in China was particularly prominent among women aged 20 to 49.28 In the future, more attention should be paid to the prevention and control of anemia among women of childbearing age.28

Decomposition analysis indicated that changes in age and epidemiological change had a significant impact on the decline in mortality and DALYs rates of maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China. Since 1996, the fertility rate has generally declined and the average life expectancy has increased in both developed and developing countries, leading to a sharp increase in the global elderly population.29 China has entered an aging society, with the elderly population continuously increasing.29 By the end of 2017, it had reached 158.31 million.29 From 2000 to 2010, the aging of the population in China intensified rapidly.29 China’s low birth rate and having the world’s largest elderly population base have accelerated population aging.29 The proportion of the population aged 65 and above will reach 16% by 2050.30 With the aging of the population, the number of women of childbearing age is gradually decreasing, and the burden of maternal hemorrhage disease is also reducing. The Chinese government has formulated policies on anemia. In 2018, China designated August 18th of each year as the anemia day. In 2017, the general office of the state council of China issued and implemented the National Nutrition Plan (2017–2030), in which the first main goal is to reduce the anemia rate of the population, and put forward the goal of controlling the anemia rate of pregnant women to less than 10% by 2030. International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) recommends that all pregnant women should undergo hematological tests when establishing a medical record or at 28 weeks of gestation, with a hemoglobin concentration of less than 11g/dL being used as the diagnostic criterion for anemia during pregnancy.31 Iron supplements and folic acid should be routinely supplemented during pregnancy.31 In addition, for cases where maternal hemorrhage may occur, it is necessary to prepare sufficient blood supply, enhance the assessment of maternal hemorrhage volume by medical staff, and master proficiently the treatment measures after maternal hemorrhage occurrence, etc. Comprehensive prenatal examinations, understanding possible complications during childbirth, formulating appropriate delivery methods, and publicizing information on maternal mortality related to maternal hemorrhage can help reduce the occurrence of maternal hemorrhage.

In this study, GBD 2023 database was used to analyze the trend of disease burden of maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China in detail. This study had the advantages of wide coverage, long observation period, and large amount of data. However, this study still had some limitations. First of all, women in some provinces of China with relatively low economic development levels might refuse to take prenatal care due to economic reasons or the opinions of their elders, which might affect the completeness and accuracy of the data. Secondly, the GBD study did not provide data on the disease burden of specific types of maternal hemorrhage (such as prenatal hemorrhage, postpartum hemorrhage or hemorrhagic placental disease). Thirdly, this study mainly focused on the disease burden of maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China, but the combined effect of other factors and iron deficiency might have an impact on the burden of maternal hemorrhage, which was not addressed in this study. Furthermore, We remain uncertain whether iron deficiency and its alleviation can affect clinical outcomes, and if an effect exists, whether such benefits are driven by improvements in moderate or severe anemia. Finally, there is an obvious time lag in the publicly available GBD datasets.

Conclusion

This study found that the number of deaths, ASMR, the number of DALYs, and ASDR of maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China showed a downward trend from 1990 to 2023. Both mortality and DALYs rates increased first and then decreased with age. Mortality and DALYs rates decreased over time. Cohort effects showed that mortality and DALYs rates generally decreased from early to late birth cohorts. Decomposition analysis showed that age and epidemiological change had significant impact on the reduction of mortality and DALYs rate of maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China. Although the overall disease burden of maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China is decreasing, the disease situation is still serious. It is necessary to develop targeted prevention and intervention measures to reduce the disease burden of maternal hemorrhage attributable to iron deficiency in China.

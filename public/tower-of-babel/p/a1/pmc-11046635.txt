The prevalence and associated risk factors of osteoporosis in hiv positive postmenopausal women on combined antiretroviral therapy
Authors: Chinwada Pasca, Chiratidzo Ellen Ndhlovu, Alan Mcgregor, Cynthia Mukwasi-Kahari
Published: 2023

Introduction

The burden of HIV has disproportionately affected women globally, including in Zimbabwe, among other sub-Saharan African countries, where more than half of those infected are women.1, 2 However, the wide use of combined antiretroviral therapy (cART) has seen these women achieve good virologic suppression and live long after the menopausal age.2 This poses a high risk of postmenopausal osteoporosis on the backdrop of lifelong predominantly non-bone-mineral-density-(BMD)-sparing ART and advancing age. There is little known about the prevalence of postmenopausal osteoporosis and its risk factors in this high-risk group in Zimbabwe.

Aim

To determine the age of menopause, prevalence and risk factors of osteoporosis in HIV-positive postmenopausal women attending Parirenyatwa Group of Hospitals AIDS Healthcare Foundation Centre of Excellence (PGH AHF COE) clinic.

Justification

Osteoporosis burden in a resourced setting is associated with aging and secondary causes, which will probably be heightened in a Zimbabwean setting, owing to increased HIV prevalence and the adoption and good uptake of non-BMD-sparing first line cART. Osteoporosis is an asymptomatic condition which reduces life expectancy if complicated by fragility fractures but can be detected by screening.3, 4

Methods

A cross-sectional study of 289 HIV-infected, postmenopausal women on combined antiretroviral therapy was conducted. The participants had a dual X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) scan of the lumbar spine, femoral neck and total hip carried out to assess for bone mineral density. Traditional and HIV- and cART-use-related risk factors were established and their association with osteoporosis was analysed on ordinal logistic regression.

There was a significant association between the risk of osteoporosis with an older participant age, low body mass index and long duration in menopause. The mean age (yrs) at menopause of 48.7 (standard deviation (SD) = 3.3) in the HIV-affected population is comparable to 50.5 (SD = 4.8) of healthy Zimbabwean black women.5

Discussion

The prevalence of osteoporosis of 39.8% is high in comparison to ranges of up to 26% seen in other studies,6 underpinning osteoporosis as an emerging non-communicable disease in resource-limited settings. Most of our study participants were overweight/obese, probably reflecting the abnormal BMI in the reference population and/or adverse effects of cART.7 The risk of osteoporosis is particularly associated with traditional rather than the HIV-related factors. Its prudent to note study limitations on unmet need to investigate for secondary causes of osteoporosis.

Conclusion

These study findings underpin the importance of regarding osteoporosis as a significant emerging non-communicable disease in resource limited settings. It is thus imperative to formulate guidelines to address osteoporosis and consequent risk of fragility fractures during their care by means of a clinical tool such as Fracture Risk Assessment Tool (FRAX) customised to this setting.

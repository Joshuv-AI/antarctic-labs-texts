Design and analysis of a high-efficiency bi-directional DAB converter for EV charging
Authors: Jatoth Rajender, Manisha Dubey, Yogendra Kumar, Banothu Somanna, Muhannad Alshareef, Borchala Namomsa, Sherif S. M. Ghoneim, Saad A. Mohamed Abdelwahab
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The escalating demand for electric vehicles (EVs) arises from apprehensions regarding fossil fuel depletion and the ecological repercussions of conventional combustion engines, propelling the transition towards environmentally sustainable alternatives. EVs are conventionally comprised of a powertrain, battery management system, and communication infrastructure, with the battery playing a pivotal role. Achieving an efficient EV battery charger necessitates the implementation of a proficient charging algorithm and a high-power converter capable of adeptly regulating battery parameters. Among the array of available options, a bi-directional DC/DC converter is often employed for this purpose. This study predominantly focuses on the Bi-Directional Dual Active Bridge Converter with Single-Phase Shift Control. It is renowned for attaining a broad voltage range through transformer turn ratio adjustments. Its controllable parameters, such as phase shift ratio and duty cycle, bolster its versatility. Moreover, due to its input–output isolation and minimal passive elements, this converter exhibits superior efficiency due to its soft-switching capabilities. The analytical framework encompasses steady-state and dynamic assessments, small-signal modeling, and system transfer function analysis, all of which contribute to formulating an optimized controller design. Additionally, the study conducts simulations of various battery charging techniques, including constant current (CC), constant voltage (CV), and CCCV mode, employing a 1.5 kW Dual Active Bridge DAB-based charger through MATLAB/SIMULINK. Furthermore, voltage mode control simulations for a 5 kW DAB converter augment the study's insights into effective EV charging strategies.

Introduction

The literature analysis the proper DC/DC converter for battery charging is a Dual Active Bridge Converter (DAB). A single-phase or a three-phase DAB converter is available1. This research enhances EV charging technology for efficient and reliable xEV charging This converter is versatile, handling various input voltages. It's specifically tailored for EV charging applications2. Irrespective of its phase DAB consists of two H-bridges. one operates as a rectifier, and the other as an inverter. A high-frequency transformer is connected between the two bridges. By controlling the bridges, a high-frequency AC voltage appears across the transformer. By controlling voltage and the phase of the high voltage (HV) and low voltage (LV) windings of a transformer, control of real and reactive power is possible3,4. New product development is significantly impacted by this architectural shift. It demands expertise in high-power electronics, energy management, and complex algorithms5. The surge in EV adoption demands robust charging infrastructure. Power electronics and optimization techniques are crucial for various applications including solar, fuel cells, and electric vehicles6. DAB is generally preferred due to its simple structure, bi-directional power flow capability, and easily implemented control. DAB consists of 8/12 switches depending on single/three-phase, an energy transfer inductor, and capacitors to maintain DC voltage. It is preferred due to its galvanic isolation property, soft switching characteristics, high efficiency, and high power density, especially in the case of onboard EV charger design.

Dynamic modeling is essential to design a controller. Without modeling, regulation of parameters like voltage, current, phase-shift becomes difficult3,7. Parametric modeling for Interleaved LLC converter optimization in battery charging systems8. The performance of the converter may not be efficient. The dynamic model consists of a set of mathematical equations that describes the relation of input and output. These are time-based. A time-domain analysis is done through differential for continuous systems and difference equations for discrete systems. The dynamic behavior of the isolated converters is modelled using state-space averaging and models based on fundamental power flow.

State-space averaging is done by deriving the dynamics of the energy storage elements. These are represented in the form of state equations9. This study evaluates converter performance under half and full load conditions, examining both steady-state and transient behaviors10. These equations are averaged over a time period to remove the switching harmonics and other higher-order harmonics. It is used only when the ripple of state variables is very small. Hence this technique is used for DC quantities. This method does not apply to resonant converters because they exhibit oscillatory behavior. In DAB, the inductor current is AC, i.e., the ripple is not small and the DC component is zero. Therefore a model that considers the fundamental component of the state variable is used.

A controller needs to be designed to regulate the output voltage of DAB. A small signal model is required to design it. Averaging is done to reduce higher-order harmonics. Small signal analysis is done to reduce the effect of other lower-order harmonics and derive the transfer function of control and output11. Modelling a DAB can be done in two ways. One is reduced-order modeling and the other is full order modelling. The dynamics of the leakage inductor current is negligible in the reduced model. The full order model does analysis of dynamics of transformer current. Modelling can be in continuous time or discrete-time. Discrete-time modelling is advantageous for the low-frequency operation of the converter. The physical realization of a system cannot be inferred. A continuous-time domain is used for high frequencies because it provides more physical insight. Therefore full order continuous time-averaging technique is implemented in the design of DAB.

The main component of EV is the battery. It is an electrochemical device. To know the electrical characteristics a model is to be developed. Compared to gasoline vehicles one of the major disadvantages of EV is the refueling time. Hence the time for refueling is to be reduced i.e. a quick charging method12 is to be developed. Conventional bidirectional DC–DC converters suffer from low voltage gain due to power switch limitations. A novel converter is proposed to achieve higher step-up and step-down capabilities13. PI controllers are used due to its simple design and ease of implementation. Due to the increased number of switches in the DAB study of steady-state and transient performance is done by using these controllers.

Small signal model

Equation (28) is nonlinear time invariant as it contains the multiplication of state variables.

Stability analysis and controller design for DAB requires the derivation of the transfer function of control and output17. The output voltage is to be controlled by the phase shift angle. But if the battery is charging, the current is to be controlled. A nonlinear system is linearized around an operating point. Small signal transfer function represents the dynamic response of a converter for a small change in the control signal. A small perturbation in phase shift ratio leads to deviation in the output voltage, real and imaginary components of the leakage inductor current in steady-state. Input voltage is assumed to be constant.

Capital variables represent steady-state values of state variables, lower case represents large signal states, δ represents small signal states.

Battery modeling and charging methods using DAB

Battery in an EV

The battery is used in an EV due to its high energy density and less specific energy. Compared to conventional engines these have a higher energy conversion rate. Batteries are majorly classified into two types, primary and secondary. Non-rechargeable ones such as alkaline type are the classification of primary batteries. Secondary batteries, such as Li-ion, Nickel–cadmium, Lead-acid, Nickel-metal hydride, and sodium-ion batteries, can charge and discharge multiple times. Chemical energy present in the battery is converted to electrical energy during charging and vice-versa. Generally, lead-acid and Lithium-ion batteries are used in EV applications. At rated voltage the amount of charge a battery can deliver is known as battery capacity. It is expressed as Ampere hour (Ah). The size of the battery increases with capacity. Charging of the battery depends on the current delivered to the battery, time of charge, temperature, environmental conditions, etc. The relation between the discharge current (i), the capacity of the battery (Q), and the time of discharge (t) is given by46\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\text{i}=\frac{\textrm{Q}}{\text{t}}$$\end{document}i=Qt

C-rate measures the rate of charge and discharge. C-rate is the ratio of the current drawn from the battery to the theoretical current to be drawn from the battery at a nominal capacity in one hour. Units are h−1. The safe limit of the current to be delivered to the external circuit is decided by C-rating. For a 48 V, 28Ah rating battery, if the current drawn from the battery is 28A, the C-rated rating is 1C. Similarly if 10A is drawn then the C-rating is C/2.8. A battery's total number of charge and discharge cycles decides its lifetime. Typically, a LiFePO4 battery has 2000–4000 charge cycles or about five years, depending on the battery's rating. Time taken for a 100% SOC battery to 0%SOC and again to 100% SOC is defined as the charge cycle. Compared to other batteries Li-polymer type has a lower self-discharge rate. Due to various advantages, Li-ion batteries are used in light EV. But the cost of these batteries is higher.

Control of DAB

Eight switches are used in the operation of DAB. Control of these switches is implemented using PI controller. The basic control diagram of a single-input single-output (SISO) system is

A reference signal is given to obtain the desired signal. It can be voltage current or state of charge. The actual value is compared to a reference value. The error generated is processed to a controller. It generates a control signal and is processed to the plant (any power electronic converter) to generate the required output voltage as shown Fig. 7.Fig. 7Basic blocks in a closed loop control of SISO system.

Basic blocks in a closed loop control of SISO system.

The basic controllers used are proportional, integral, derivative, and combinations. The proportional controller is a simple controller that regulates the converter's gain. It generally introduces a steady-state error. An offset is introduced due to this controller. This controller is used when there is a dominant pole or pole at the origin in the given transfer function.

Integral control is used to reduce the steady-state error in the output. The response of output is very slow. In case of disturbances, the response is faster. This controller effectively processes the introduction of a dead time effect in the system. When the proportional and integral controllers are combined, some advantages are added. The offset associated with a proportional controller is reduced, and ideally, it becomes zero. As integral controllers are slower by adding proportionally to it the response becomes faster. Hence, the proportional-integral (PI) controller is mostly used in power electronic converter applications.

Control of DAB is done using a PI controller. Regulation of output voltage and output current is done by adjusting the phase angle of bridges for power flow in both directions.

Conclusion

The steady-state analysis of the Dual Active Bridge Converter with single-phase shift control studies the converter's behavior during different modes of operation. The design of the open-loop DAB is for the given specifications and is justified by the open-loop model. System transfer functions are derived from the small-signal modeling of the converter using generalized averaging techniques. The controller design is done by analyzing transfer functions derived from the small-signal model. Closed-loop control is implemented to regulate the desired output voltage for input and load variations. Power transfer is regulated by changing the phase angle of bridges. PI controller is used for regulating load voltage in both power flow directions.

To study the behavior of a battery, modelling of battery is done with shepherd’s model. The DAB-based charger design for battery charging is done and justified with the open-loop model. System transfer functions are designed from small-signal modeling using the averaging technique. Small-signal model analysis is done for controller design. Variations in battery current for a change in SOC, time of charging, and voltage of a battery are done in CC mode of the battery. In CC mode current always flows through the battery and temperature rise happens which affects the lifetime of the battery. CV mode is implemented to reduce the above problem. The initial current during this mode is very high, which affects the battery's performance. A new charging algorithm is implemented to sort out problems in both CC and CV modes. In CCCV charging, the initial current is controlled by CC; in CV mode, the current is regulated to reduce the temperature rise. By using the battery as a source, the load voltage is maintained. PI control in the outer loop generates the reference of the battery current. The initial current during load regulation is higher. To solve the problem, the battery current is regulated and the battery is operated in CC mode. A complete analysis of voltage mode control of 5 kW DAB and different charging algorithms for 1.5 kW DAB chargers was verified using MATLAB/SIMULINK.

Evaluation of the effects of dimethyl fumarate on transforming growth factor beta levels in the liver of rats with bile duct ligation-induced cholestasis
Authors: Hannaneh Vossoughi, Pejman Mortazavi, Mahsa Ale-Ebrahim, Razieh Hosseini
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCholestasis is a reduction or cessation of bile flow in the biliary system, which can be life-threatening. Dimethyl Fumarate could induce anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects in the body.ObjectiveThis investigation focused on assessing the impact of Dimethyl Fumarate on liver levels of transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) to mitigate biochemical, histopathological, and immunohistochemical alterations in cholestasis-induced rat models.MethodsForty male adult Wistar rats were divided into eight groups (healthy control treated with distilled water, healthy rats treated with 50, 100, and 200 mg/kg of Dimethyl Fumarate, bile duct ligation (BDL), and experiment BDL groups were treated with 50, 100, and 200 mg/kg of Dimethyl Fumarate). After the gavage treatment period of 45 days, the rats were anesthetized and underwent blood sampling. Liver damage was assessed by measuring hepatic marker enzymes (alanine transaminase, aspartate transaminase, alkaline phosphatase, gamma-glutamyl transferase, and total bilirubin), histopathological (lesion assessment), and immunohistochemical (TGF-β expression level) observation.ResultsThe findings demonstrated that administration of varying doses of Dimethyl Fumarate via gavage led to a statistically significant reduction in serum concentrations of alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, alkaline phosphatase, gamma-glutamyl transferase, and total bilirubin (P < 0.05). The optimal dosage identified was 200 mg/kg of Dimethyl Fumarate. Furthermore, the data indicated that gavage treatment with Dimethyl Fumarate significantly attenuated TGF-β expression level and mitigated hepatic damage (P < 0.05).ConclusionThis strategy may reduce inflammation, cholestasis, and fibrosis outcomes, attributed to its anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties. Nonetheless, further research is necessary to substantiate these findings.Keywords: Dimethyl Fumarate, Bile Duct Ligation, Cholestasis, TGF-β

Introduction

Cholestasis is a pathological condition in which bile production is reduced or shows impaired flow, causing systemic or local accumulation of bile acids around and within the hepatic tissue 1, 2. The consequence of improper bile acid excretion is an accumulation of toxic bile acids, which could lead to biliary tree and parenchymal cell damage [3]. In this condition, the bile acids can impair hepatocytes or biliary epithelial cells' biological functions (cholangiocytes) [4]. Further, due to cholestasis, the accumulated conjugated bilirubin causes icterus [5].

Bile duct ligation (BDL) is a model of extrahepatic obstruction commonly used in laboratory animals 6, 7. BDL causes bile pressure, provokes mild inflammation, and leads to the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines by bile epithelial cells, such as tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) and Interleukin-6 (IL-6); these events eventually cause cholestasis [8]. Because of biliary epithelial cell proliferation, fibrogenic marker expression like alpha-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), collagen 1, and transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) increases [9]. Moreover, the accumulation of B and T lymphocytes may lead to reactive oxygen species (ROS) secretion and liver damage [9].

The affected hepatocytes secreted various inflammatory signals to recruit the immune cells to the site [10]. Thus, the accumulation of toxic bile acids could disrupt the oxidant-antioxidant balance and cause the neutrophils and macrophages to release ROS 11, 12. This impaired balance may cause free radical-induced tissue injury, also called oxidative stress [13]. In cholestasis, ROS overproduction induces apoptosis and causes oxidative damage to intracellular proteins, lipids, RNA, and DNA [13]. Therefore, one of the significant approaches in anticholestatic treatment is balancing oxidation and antioxidation [13].

After the first inflammatory reaction, the involved cells secreted essential growth factors for hepatic fibrosis progression [14]. During liver injury, one of the first signals that turns the nearby hematopoietic stem cells into activated myofibroblasts is TGF-β, released from hepatic cells [15]. Besides that, TGF-β signaling could inhibit the stellate cells’ apoptosis and induce higher levels of matrix protein production like fibronectin and collagen types I, III, and IV by these cells [16]. The extracellular matrix produced by hepatic stellate cells, the central part of the matrix, and other blood cells that enter and settle in the liver could form a set of interactions [17]. These interactions cause the ECM excessive production that may lead to liver fibrosis [17]. If there is a chronic situation, the liver's chronic inflammation, expression, and deposition of ECM last, and the liver is progressively replaced by scar tissue [18].

Dimethyl Fumarate (DMF) is a drug approved for plaque psoriasis treatment [19]. Mono-methyl fumarate (MMF), which is the DMF’s physiologically active metabolite, could enter the liver and induce immunomodulatory, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory effects through the pathway of the nuclear factor-erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2) [19]. MMF could reduce nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF-κB) pathway levels and astrocyte-derived inflammatory cytokines secretion by binding to hydroxycarboxylic acid receptor 2 [19]. According to the effects of the DMF metabolite, it was assumed that MMF might be effective against the ROS and inflammatory conditions caused by cholestasis and may help ameliorate the liver injury.

The current research aimed to evaluate the hepatoprotective effects of DMF against cholestatic liver injury induced by the BDL model in male Wistar rats. After the treatment, immunohistochemical, biochemical, and histopathological examinations were performed.

Materials and methods

Experimental animals

40 male adult Wistar rats (230–250 g) were purchased from the Pasteur Institute (Tehran, Iran) and were randomly divided into eight groups (n = 5 per group). The study was performed on male rats, owing to the possible negative effects of female rats’ hormones. The study was accomplished at the Research Center of Islamic Azad University (Tehran, Iran). Rats care, and the experimental steps were performed per the criteria of care and use of institutional animals Medical Sciences Ethics Committee of Islamic Azad University of Science and Research Branch, Tehran, Iran, with IR.IAU.SRB.REC.1403.240 ethical code under light/dark cycles (12.12 h); temperature (23 ± 2 °C); humidity (55 %) conditions. Pellets were purchased from Pars Animal Feed Company (Tehran, Iran) and provided with clean tap water for rat feeding. The practical experiments started after two weeks; in the meantime, the rats had adapted to new conditions.

DMF preparation

DMF was obtained in capsule form from Nano Alvand Pharmaceutical Company (Alborz, Iran). This study investigated the effects of different doses (50, 100, 200 mg/kg) of DMF selected based on previous research, including the use of 100 mg/kg DMF orally in rats; therefore, for an effective dose-response, this study selected a lower (50 mg/kg) and a higher (200 mg/kg) dose around the mentioned dosage of DMF in the previous study 20, 21. Additionally, in recent studies, DMF has been confirmed to be a safe drug (therapeutic dose of 50–100 mg/kg) and (supratherapeutic dose of 300 mg/kg) 22, 23. LD50 (2.240 mg/kg) is recorded for oral usage of DMF in rats [24].

BDL induction

BDL surgery was conducted following the protocol established in the study by Uchinami et al.[23]. Briefly, the animals were anesthetized by injecting a combination of xylazine (10 mg/kg body weight) and ketamine (90 mg/kg body weight) intraperitoneal (IP), then the midline of the abdomen was cut to have 3 cm incisions. To double-ligate the common bile duct, a 4–0 silk suture (AILEECo. Ltd., Busan, Korea) was used at two locations (first one: shortly below the hepatic duct junction and second one: before the pancreatic duct entrance). An incision was made between the two knots in the bile ducts, and the abdominal wall muscle layers were carefully sutured with 4–0 silk thread. Meanwhile, a 4–0 nylon thread was used to close the skin layer. One ml of sterile saline was injected into the peritoneum to complete the surgery. A heating pad was applied to the animal during the recovery period [25].

Study design

The treatment rat groups received DMF through oral gavage for 45 days. DMF was prepared as a gavage solution for 50, 100, and 200 mg/kg doses by dissolving 10, 20, and 40 mg of dimethyl fumarate in 1 ml of distilled water, respectively [26].

Group 1: Healthy rats served as the Control, received 1 ml of distilled water orally (daily).

Group 2: Healthy rats served as 50, received 50 mg/kg of DMF.

Group 3: Healthy rats served as 100, received 100 mg/kg of DMF.

Group 4: Healthy rats served as 200, received 200 mg/kg of DMF.

Group 5: Served as BDL, rats with BDL, received 1 ml physiologic saline orally (daily).

Group 6: Served as BDL+ 50, rats with BDL, received 50 mg/kg of DMF.

Group 7: Served as BDL+ 100, rats with BDL, received 100 mg/kg of DMF.

Group 8: Served as BDL+ 200, rats with BDL, received 200 mg/kg of DMF.

Blood sampling

After a 45-day treatment period, the rats were fasted for 10 h. First, they received intraperitoneal anesthesia with a mixture of ketamine (90 mg/kg) and xylazine (10 mg/kg) to obtain a blood serum sample. Blood was drawn from their hearts using a 3 ml syringe. The blood sample was then transferred into sterile microtubes and centrifuged at 2000 RPM for 5 min. The resulting serum was carefully separated into a new microtube and stored for further analysis [27].

Determination of biochemical parameters

Based on the guidelines of the International Federation of Clinical Chemistry and Laboratory Medicine (IFCC), the liver enzymes (alanine transaminase (ALT), aspartate transaminase (AST), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT), and total bilirubin (T. bilirubin) serum activity, the serum levels of T. bilirubin) were assessed with particular kits (PARS Azmoon Company, Iran) and an autoanalyzer (BT1500, Italy) 25, 28.

Tissue preparation

The liver tissue sections were sliced and saved in a 10 % formalin buffer solution to create paraffin blocks. The blocks were stained with conventional tissue processing techniques for further histopathological and immunohistochemical analyses [29].

Histopathological examination

Trichrome staining was used to measure the fibrosis in the liver tissues and determine whether the collagen deposition was elevated. Liver sections with 4 μm thickness were cut in each group. After being deparaffinized for 2 min with xylene, the section's alcohol content (100, 90, 80, 70, and 50 %) was progressively reduced. After that, the sections were stained with Masson's Trichrome Stain Kit, Abcam (Product code: ab150686). The washing was accomplished with xylene and alcohol. The Entellan coating was used to view the samples under a blinded light microscope 23, 30. Each group's liver damage was assessed based on Sant'Anna et al.'s modified method. Ten random areas were evaluated for each sample, and the reported score was the average damage [31].

Immunohistochemistry

The liver tissue sections were washed with TRIS-buffered saline for 5 min. The slides were blocked using a solution of 5 % normal swine serum, TRIS-buffered saline, and 0.5 % bovine serum albumin for 15 min at room temperature. The primary TGF-β antibodies were diluted in TRIS-buffered saline containing 0.1 % bovine serum albumin and 5 % swine serum. The slides were incubated at 4 °C for 24 h. Following this step, biotinylated secondary antibodies in TRIS-buffered saline with 0.1 % bovine serum albumin were applied to the slides and left for 60 min at room temperature. The slides were rewashed with the previously used TRIS-buffered saline and 0.1 % bovine serum albumin solution. After that, a mixture of 0.05 % DAB (3,3′-diaminobenzidine) and 0.1 % hydrogen peroxide was added and allowed to sit for five minutes. Finally, hematoxylin was used as a counterstain in the last step [36].

Protein-specific antibodies were utilized to reduce unwanted binding during immunohistochemical staining. This allowed for the identification of antibody-antigen interactions in the stained tissue. A specific enzyme was employed to cleave the antibody, enabling light microscopy analysis, which resulted in dye deposition in situ on the protein [37]. Consequently, the expression of TGF-β was assessed by counting the number of cells exhibiting cytoplasmic brown staining. A semi-quantitative immunohistochemistry evaluation was conducted to measure TGF-β expression using a scoring system that considered the intensity and extent of staining [38]. (Table 2). TGF-β expression was further evaluated using light microscopy at 160 (IHC160) and 640 (IHC640) magnifications.

Statistical Analysis

Data were reported as mean ± SD, and the graphs were plotted using the GraphPad Prism version 8. Data were analyzed using analysis of statistical variances (ANOVA) followed by post-Tukey post hoc test, and a p-value less than 0.05 was considered as a significant difference.

Histopathology

As shown in Fig. 2, there was a significant difference between the histopathological lesion scores in the Control and BDL groups (P < 0.0001). The scores of histopathological lesions (necrosis, inflammatory cell infiltration, connective tissue, and biliary duct hyperplasia) showed a significant increase in the BDL group associated with the BDL surgery. Administration 50, 100, and 200 mg/kg of DMF via the gavage route decreased histopathological lesion scores in the experimental BDL groups (P < 0.0001). Moreover, in comparison with the BDL+ 50, the BDL experimental group receiving 100 mg/kg DMF indicated a significantly greater decline in the scores of histopathological lesions (P < 0.0001). The BDL+ 200 group recorded a significantly higher decline in the scores of histopathological lesions in comparison with BDL+ 100 and BDL+ 50 (P < 0.0001).

According to the histopathological results of Masson's trichrome stain (Fig. 3), no histopathological lesions were seen in the Control, healthy rats (treated with 50, 100, 200 mg/kg DMF) groups after 45 days, indicating healthy hepatocytes around the central vein. Moreover, the rats' liver tissue sections in the rats with BDL and the BDL treated with 50 mg/kg DMF represented complete wall formation in connective tissue infiltration and excessive biliary duct hyperplasia. Additionally, incomplete wall formation in connective tissue infiltration and significant biliary duct hyperplasia were shown in the liver tissue section of the BDL experimental group with 100 mg/kg DMF treatment. Ultimately, the liver tissue section of the BDL group treated with the 200 mg/kg DMF displayed connective tissue infiltration without any wall formation and mild biliary duct hyperplasia.

Discussion

Inducing experimental obstructive cholestasis in rats is one of the best ways to design an accurate model for cholestatic liver disorders [39]. In rats, BDL induces typical morphological and structural time-dependent alterations in the liver, damaging liver cells and increasing serum enzyme levels after a few days, leading to a severe liver inflammatory reaction after 5–7 days. Ultimately, following surgical ligation of the common biliary duct, this procedure causes advanced hepatic fibrosis for three to four weeks [40]. This study used this rat model to assess DMF's antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties in liver cholestasis induced by BDL.

This research demonstrates that cholestasis leads to a pronounced elevation in liver enzyme levels, specifically ALT, ALP, AST, and GGT (P < 0.0001). On the other hand, after a rigorous treatment regimen lasting 45 days with DMF administered at varying concentrations of 50, 100, and 200 mg/kg, the activity of these enzymes was significantly reduced, indicating a potentially restorative effect on liver function. Therefore, using molecules with antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, such as DMF, presents a promising strategy for treating liver cholestasis induced by BDL.

In a study, DMF was shown to activate the Nrf2 pathway while inhibiting the NF-kB pathway [41]. Furthermore, DMF demonstrated a decrease in markers of liver damage, oxidative stress, and fibrosis in rats subjected to carbon tetrachloride-induced liver injury [19]. These beneficial effects are attributed to its ability to reduce oxidative stress, exhibit anti-inflammatory properties, and prevent the activity of hepatic stellate cells [19]. This process reduces fibrinogenesis and collagen accumulation in the liver that can lead to liver fibrosis [19]. In hypercholesterolemic rabbits, DMF also displayed anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects [42]. It effectively lowered oxidative stress, reduced malondialdehyde levels, a byproduct of lipid oxidation, and increased superoxide dismutase levels, which help neutralize superoxide free radicals [42]. Additionally, the impact of DMF on retinal endothelial cells showed that by activating the Nrf2 pathway, the drug enhances the production of Heme oxygenase-1, a protein with antioxidant properties, while also reducing levels of ROS with significant antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects [42]. A study indicated that DMF increased cellular protection to ROS via Nrf2 in a constant liver damage that led to the overproduction of free radicals, followed by oxidative stress [43]. DMF has also been found to influence cystic fibrosis caused by mutations in the cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator gene [44]. It decreases the inflammatory response triggered by lipopolysaccharides, IL-1β, H2O2, and TNFα stimulation in bronchial epithelial cells [44].

The results of the previous studies suggest that the protective function of DMF is through the activation of the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway and also maintaining the stability of cellular membranes through lowering oxidative damage by increasing antioxidant enzyme activities (SOD-1) [45]. Due to this protective impact, reducing inflammation and possibly preserving the cell membrane integrity lowered the liver index enzyme levels [46].

On the other side, during cholestasis, the liver produces conjugated bilirubin that cannot flow into the intestine via bile, leading to its entry into the bloodstream, so bilirubin levels increase [47]. However, after administering doses of DMF at 50, 100, and 200 mg/kg, the bilirubin levels showed a significant decrease. While the exact mechanism is not fully understood, impaired tight junctions between hepatocytes may lead to the efflux of bilirubin into the serum [48]. Additionally, following hepatocellular injury, the reduced cell conjugations may result in elevated levels of direct bilirubin in the BDL group serum [48]. Through the Nrf-2/HO-1 pathway, DMF may effectively treat oxidative liver damage and inflammation, reducing bilirubin levels [46].

According to recent studies, activated HSCs are related to the increased levels of the fibrogenic cytokine TGF-β expression, which further leads to hepatic fibrosis due to higher collagen deposition [49]. TGF-β1 is a significant fibrogenic cytokine in the HSCs and a potential ECM formation modulator [50]. TGF-β caused myofibroblastic transformation of HSCs, which is considered a fundamental fibrogenesis regulator in chronic liver disorders. Activated HSCs, a primary source of matrix-secreting proteins within cells, are believed to be responsible for liver fibrogenesis [51].

Based on the immunohistochemistry results of this study, BDL can increase TGF-β expression, and DMF 200 mg/kg can significantly reduce the elevated TGF-β expression caused by BDL. Bile duct obstruction can lead to inflammation. It possibly increases the hepatic stellate cell activity and transforms them into active fibroblasts, resulting in a significant TGF-β marker expression. TGF-β signaling can inhibit the stellate cells’ apoptosis and induce higher levels of matrix protein production, like fibronectin and collagen types I, III, and IV, by these cells, leading to fibrosis and fibrosis exacerbation [16]. Daily gavage treatment with 200 mg/kg of DMF significantly reduced TGF-β expression, probably due to its antioxidant effects and ability to lessen the activation of HSCs. Recent studies have shown that DMF administration caused decreased levels of TGF-β and fibrosis by activating the Nrf2 pathway, inhibiting NF-κB signaling, and increasing antioxidant levels 52, 53.

According to the histopathological findings, the groups associated with the BDL surgery significantly increased necrosis, inflammatory cell infiltration, connective tissue, and biliary duct hyperplasia scores. On the other hand, DMF could reduce histopathological lesions in BDL mice dose dependently, with the highest decline of lesions with the dose of 200 mg/kg. Further, the liver tissue section of the BDL group treated with the 200 mg/kg DMF displayed connective tissue infiltration without any wall formation and mild biliary duct hyperplasia. Similarly, the other studies that administered dimethyl fumarate in liver-damaged cases showed that DMF could reduce liver damage markers expression and decrease inflammatory cytokines, oxidative stress, and fibrosis. Indeed, the oxidative stress-reducing, anti-inflammatory properties, and hepatic stellate cell activity inhibition in the liver reduced fibrinogenesis and collagen secretion from these cells 19, 41. Considering these data, this article concluded that administering dimethyl fumarate at 50, 100, and 200 mg/kg significantly reduced liver damage scores and collagen deposition, with the higher dose (200 mg/kg) showing better outcomes.

Conclusion

This study investigated the effects of DMF at doses of 50, 100, and 200 mg/kg in a model of cholestasis-induced liver. The results showed that DMF effectively reduced TGF-β expression, likely due to its potent antioxidant and anti-inflammation properties. After treating the animals with 50, 100, and 200 mg/kg of DMF, TGF-β levels decreased, reducing inflammation and liver and bile duct tissue damage. Overall, a dose of 200 mg/kg of DMF significantly improved liver tissue condition by minimizing inflammatory damage, a notable reduction in TGF-β expression, and a significant decrease in the liver enzymes’ levels. These results suggest that DMF may reduce inflammation and tissue damage in liver diseases such as cholestasis, particularly at higher doses.

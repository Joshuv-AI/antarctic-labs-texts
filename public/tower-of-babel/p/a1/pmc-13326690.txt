Porphyrin-functionalized molecularly imprinted paper sensor for selective colorimetric detection of caffeine in beverage samples
Authors: Maali D. Alshammari, Kareem M. Younes, Aqsa Maqsood, Alia Alshammari, Najah Albadari, Khaled Almansour, Reda Abdel-Hameed, Akhtar Hayat, Umar Nishan, Azhar Abbas
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this work, a novel portable paper-based sensor is developed using a porphyrin-embedded molecularly imprinted polymer for caffeine quantification. This study entraps tetraphenylporphyrin (TPP) within the template-induced cavities of cellulose molecular aggregation-polished porous particles, thereby modulating TPP's optical response. All techniques (FTIR, XRD, and TEM) demonstrated successful polymer formation with selective binding cavities and reversible caffeine interactions via non-covalent forces. The sensor covered a linear range of 1–100 µM with a 1.62 µM limit of detection and a Stern–Volmer correlation (R2 = 0.99). Stability was demonstrated with high selectivity, an imprinted factor of ∼3, over 85% response after five cycles, and less than <10% signal loss over 30 days. Average recoveries of 93–99% (real-samples analysis – tea, coffee) provide a cost-effective and readily interpretable approach for the field-based monitoring of caffeine.

Introduction

One of the most common drugs in the world, caffeine is consumed in coffee, tea, energy drinks, pharmaceutical products, and dietary supplements.1 Moderate consumption provides stimulant properties and cognitive enhancement, while overconsumption has been linked to negative systemic physiology such as insomnia, cardiovascular disturbances, and disrupted neurological homeostasis.2 Thus, proper monitoring of caffeine in drinks is deemed equally crucial for ensuring quality, regulatory compliance, and safeguarding consumer health. The growing need for fast, low-cost, and at-hand analytical gadgets has prompted the emergence of a series of portable sensing platforms designed to analyze caffeine beyond conventional lab environments.3

While chromatographic and spectrometric techniques such as HPLC, LC-MS, and capillary electrophoresis show excellent sensitivity, these methods still rely on advanced instrumentation, trained personnel with comprehensive training in sampling and preparation techniques, and centralized laboratory settings, which limit the feasibility of rapid on-site caffeine monitoring.4 Several electrochemical and optical sensors have been developed for caffeine determination; most of the previously described systems still suffer from low specificity and/or operational stability, dim color reads, or poor portability for field applications. The development of low-cost paper-based sensing platforms that can provide selective molecular recognition and direct visual signal generation, in particular, represents a significant analytical challenge.5

Molecularly imprinted polymers (MIPs), which are synthetic recognition materials formed from template-generated binding cavities having high chemical stability and reusability while also maintaining resistance to harsh environmental conditions, have emerged as attractive ones.6 MIPs offer several advantages over biological receptors, including low-cost production, ease of preparation, and longer-term storage stability.7 Recent developments have also proved the increasing application of MIP-integrated colorimetric and smartphone-aided detection devices in field-deployable analytical systems.8

Materials such as porphyrins are a very appealing sort of optical transducer due to their robust π-conjugated backbone, strong Soret absorption bands, fluorescence emission characteristics, and aggregation-induced colorimetric properties.9 Different aggregation states of porphyrins can lead to diverse optical and colorimetric responses, particularly responsive to the intermolecular interactions in local environments.10 However, using porphyrin, we have achieved its own signal conjugation with molecular imprinting on disposable paper substrates for the specific caffeine recognition that has not been explored, especially on dual-mode optical detection via both fluorescence and visible colorimetric.11

Here, using a portable and inexpensive analytical platform, we developed a multi-porphyrin-functionalized molecularly imprinted paper sensor with the ability for selective caffeine detection in beverage samples.12 Here, simultaneous fluorescence quenching and colorimetric response are achieved with a dual sensing strategy that pairs template-induced molecular recognition through porphyrin imprints with aggregation-responsive pore-cleaved porphyrins. The developed sensor showed encouraging analytical performance, including selective recognition, repeated operability, extended shelf life (storage stability), smartphone-assisted quantification, and the ability to read out visually without advanced equipment. These features suggest that the platform technology has potential for practical beverage monitoring and other point-of-care analytical applications.

Materials and methods

Materials

Caffeine (analytical grade), tetraphenylporphyrin (TPP), ethylene glycol dimethacrylate (EGDMA, cross-linker), functional monomer (e.g., methacrylic acid, MAA), azobisisobutyronitrile (AIBN, initiator), ethanol, methanol, acetic acid, and other analytical-grade solvents were used as received without further purification. Whatman filter paper was employed as a cellulose substrate. Glucose, sucrose, urea, ascorbic acid, and paracetamol were used for selectivity studies. Commercial tea and coffee samples were purchased locally for real sample analysis.

Formulation of the prepolymer complex

Caffeine (template molecule) and TPP were dissolved in ethanol under magnetic stirring to prepare the pre-polymerization solution. The equilibrated mixture enabled frequent supramolecular interactions between caffeine and the porphyrin macrocycle via π–π stacking and hydrogen bonding. Functional monomer (MAA), crosslinker (EGDMA), and initiator (AIBN) were then added to the solution, followed by stirring to obtain a homogeneous pre-polymerization mixture. Different template-to-monomer-to-crosslinker ratios were explored to optimize the pre-polymerization composition for imprint formation and optical response. The caffeine (template): meth acrylic acid (functional monomer): EGDMA (crosslinker) molar ratio was optimized in a fixed range of 1 : 4 : 20; AIBN wt% of the monomer total content. The higher crosslinking compositions exhibited better cavity fidelity, a stronger quenching response to fluorescence, and enhanced stability of the polymer matrix, comparable to that observed in lower crosslinking compositions.

Fabrication of MIP paper embedded with porphyrin

Filter paper strips were first cleaned and then immersed in the pre-polymerization solution, allowing them to soak evenly across their length. The printed paper was then subjected to UV curing (365 nm) for polymerization, leading to the formation of a crosslinked polymer network on the cellulose surface. The template (caffeine) was subsequently removed by washing with a methanol–acetic acid mixture, yielding foot-binding cavities. The paper was washed with ethanol and dried at room temperature. In contrast, non-imprinted polymer (NIP) paper was obtained using the same procedure without incorporating caffeine.

Characterization of materials and spectroscopy

FTIR spectra were recorded in the region of 4000–400 cm−1 with a spectral resolution of 4 cm−1 and 32 accumulated scans per sample using an ATR-FTIR spectrometer.13 XRD measurements were performed with the following parameters: Cu Kα radiation source (λ = 1.5406 Å) at 40 kV and 30 mA in the range of 2θ = 10–70°, scanning rate of 2° min−1.14 For imaging using TEM, small parts of the polymer-coated paper were dispersed in ethanol by gentle ultrasonication to separate nanoscale MIP domains from the cellulose substrate before imaging.15 UV-Vis absorption spectra were recorded from 350 to 700 nm with a quartz cuvette (1 cm optical path length). Excitation was performed at 420 nm, and emission spectra were collected between 600 and 750 nm, with excitation and emission slit widths of 5 nm.

Measurement of surface wettability and surface energy

Surface hydrophilicity alterations in various stages of the fabrication process (raw paper, MIP before template removal, and after template removal and rebinding) have been assessed by static determination of water contact angle. Dispersion and polar surface energy components were calculated from contact angle data based on standard models.

UV-vis spectroscopy

To probe electronic interactions and observe shifts in the unique Soret band, UV-Vis absorption spectra of caffeine, TPP, and their mixture were measured between 350 and 700 nm in ethanol, providing an overview of ground-state complex formation that takes place between caffeine and the porphyrin macrocycle.16 All UV-Vis measurements were conducted in triplicate using freshly prepared solutions. A fixed final TPP concentration was maintained in both the TPP solution and the caffeine–TPP mixture, and to avoid any absorbance decrease due to dilution, a corresponding total volume of each was mixed by computing the percent contributions of the diluents.

Fluorescence measurements

Fluorescence emission spectra were collected using a fluorescence spectrophotometer. Samples were excited at the appropriate excitation wavelength for the corresponding Soret band of porphyrin. Emission spectra were recorded over wavelengths between 600 and 750 nm. Caffeine (0–250 µM) was prepared for concentration-dependent studies, and changes in emission intensity were recorded. Stern–Volmer analysis was carried out with the following equation:17F0/F = 1 + KSV[C]where F0 and F are fluorescence intensities without or with caffeine.

Colorimetric detection and image analysis with a smartphone

The MIP paper was doped in a series of caffeine solutions at different concentrations (0, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100, and 250 µM). After drying, pictures were taken under consistent lighting with a smartphone camera. ImageJ software was used to extract RGB values. Equation for Analytical Signal(R − B)/(R + G + B)

The choice of analytical parameter (R − B)/(R + G + B) is due to the fact that sensing response could be easily tracked visually as color change occurred, and more precisely, based on porphyrin aggregation behavior, we know caffeine binding induces a visible transition of deep purple to pinkish-red, which mainly influences red and blue color channels. The subtraction term (R − B) improved discrimination of these color differences, and normalization by the total RGB intensity (R + G + B) minimized variation due to lighting intensity, camera exposure, and background fluctuations. Thus, this normalized colorimetric index provided greater analytical sensitivity, reproducibility, and quantitative reliability for smartphone-assisted caffeine determination. Calibration curves were established by plotting the RGB index against caffeine concentrations. The limit of detection (LOD) and limit of quantification (LOQ) were determined by the 3σ/S and 10σ/S criteria, respectively; σ is the standard deviation of the blank signal (n = 3), and S is its slope in the calibration curve.18 All smartphone images were captured using a smartphone camera with controlled white LED illumination at a fixed distance of 15 cm from the sensing substrate. Light-controlled chamber images were taken to prevent background inconsistencies and perturbations caused by ambient light interference. Following this, RGB analysis was conducted using ImageJ software (v. 1.53). All analytical measurements were performed in triplicate unless otherwise specified, and the reported values represent the mean ± standard deviation.

Evaluation of imprinting effect (MIP vs. NIP)

The sensing behaviors of blank paper, MIP, and NIP were evaluated at the same caffeine concentrations. Mean RGB values were determined from triplicate measurements to assess imprinting efficiency. To evaluate the imprinting efficiency quantitatively, the imprinting factor (IF) was calculated by using the following equation:19IF = ResponseMIP/ResponseNIPwhere the responses correspond to RGB index values obtained at the same concentration of caffeine.

Selectivity study

In selectivity tests, glucose, sucrose, urea, ascorbic acid, and paracetamol were used at equal concentrations. Low cross-reactivity was demonstrated by comparing the RGB response with that of caffeine, thereby assessing the specificity of the imprinted cavities.

Studies on reusability and storage stability

The reusability of the MIP paper was evaluated by conducting five consecutive adsorption–desorption cycles. The paper was regenerated using methanol–acetic acid washing at the end of each detection cycle and dried before reuse. Relative signal retention (%) was determined with respect to the first cycle.

Storage stability of the sensor was also evaluated by keeping prepared MIP paper devices in a dry state at room temperature. The first was to sense a constant amount of caffeine (e.g., 50 µM) on scheduled days (0, 7, 14, and 30 days). Retention of the analytical signal was computed as a function of the initial response to evaluate long-term stability and shelf-life implications of sensing platforms.

Optimization of experimental parameters

RGB and fluorescent responses were optimized for experimental parameters such as pH, incubation times, temperature, and elution conditions. The optimum condition, combining pH 7, incubation time of 10 min, and room temperature (25 ± 2 °C), gave the best sensing performance. The optimized conditions were applied throughout the study for template removal and sensor regeneration (using a methanol: acetic acid mixture, 9 : 1 (v/v)).

Precision and reproducibility studies

Intra-day and inter-day reproducibility studies to assess the analytical precision of the developed sensing platform upon exposure to a 50 µM caffeine solution were performed. Intra-day accuracy was calculated after performing five replicate measurements on the same day, whereas inter-day precision was assessed on three consecutive days under similar experimental conditions. The relative standard deviation (RSD) was lower than 3.5%, ensuring good repeatability, reproducibility, and analytical reliability of the proposed sensing platform.

Real sample analysis

Filtration, followed by appropriate dilution, was used to prepare tea and coffee samples. The developed MIP paper sensor was used to determine the caffeine concentration. The accuracy was evaluated by the standard addition method, in which known concentrations (10, 25, and 50 µM) were spiked into real samples. Recovery (%) and relative standard deviation (RSD%) were determined from triplicate measures.

Results and discussion

Scheme showing fabrication strategy and molecular recognition mechanism

Fig. 1 demonstrates the production process of the porphyrin-embedded molecularly imprinted paper sensor. A non-covalent imprinting method is employed to construct the sensing platform, using caffeine as the template molecule and TPP as the functional chromophore. Before polymerization, caffeine is associated with TPP through π–π stacking between their planar aromatic systems and hydrogen bonding between the carbonyl functionalities of caffeine and the inner pyrrolic NH groups from the porphyrin macrocycle. This pre-organization provides spatial complementarity in the formation of polymer networks.

Schematic illustration of the fabrication mechanism of the porphyrin-embedded molecularly imprinted paper sensor, including pre-polymerization complex formation, UV curing, template removal, and caffeine rebinding.

After exposure to UV light (365 nm), a crosslinked polymer matrix was formed on the cellulose substrate through free-radical polymerization of methacrylic acid and EGDMA, which enabled the attachment of the caffeine–TPP supramolecular complex. The template removal also yielded sharp recognition cavities perfectly complementary in size, shape, and spatial orientation to those of functional groups in caffeine. These imprinted sites held the inserted porphyrin units close to the binding site, thereby enabling optical transduction upon analyte rebinding.

Re-addition of caffeine changed the π–π stacking equilibrium degree of porphyrins and caused a color change from purple to pinkish red via selective occupancy of imprinted cavities. This chromatic change arises from modulation of porphyrin electronic interactions rather than dye leaching, thus corroborating that, in this sensing mechanism, aggregation-state modulation predominates within a polymeric matrix.

The general schematic representation thus accentuates three fundamental events: (i) supramolecular pre-complex formation, (ii) cavity generation via template extraction, and (iii) aggregation-enhanced optical response upon rebinding. This design provides both molecular recognition and signal generation on a single solid-state platform, enabling visual detection of caffeine within minutes.

Characterizations

FTIR analysis

FTIR spectroscopy was utilized to study the molecular interactions between caffeine and TPP, as well as to confirm that a supramolecular complex had formed prior to molecular imprinting. The FTIR spectrum of pure caffeine showed characteristic absorption bands corresponding to the xanthine structure. Other bands at about 1230 and 1020 cm−1 were assigned to C–N stretching and ring deformation modes, respectively.20 TPP, characterized by FTIR, had a broad absorption band centered near 3335 cm−1, characteristic of the stretching vibration of the pyrrolic N–H groups of the porphyrin macrocycle. The weak bands centered at about 2944 cm−1 are attributed to C–H stretching vibrations of aromatic and aliphatic structures. Representative porphyrin skeletal vibrations were characteristic of the stable region from 1600–1450 cm−1, while bands in the range of 1220–1044 cm−1 were assigned to C–N and in-plane ring vibrations. Importantly, the carbonyl region showed no significant absorption and was consistent with the chemical structure of free-base TPP.21

The reaction of a mixture of caffeine and TPP gave several small but distinctive spectral changes. The N–H stretching band of TPP was much broader (∼10 cm−1) and lower in frequency than that of neat TPP, which indicated that intermolecular associative interactions with pyrrolic N–H groups were involved: it shifted from ∼3335 cm−1 for neat TPP to 3318 cm−1 (full width at half-maximum of ∼28 cm−1). The carbonyl stretching vibration of caffeine has also shifted from 1693 cm−1 to 1681 cm−1, accompanied by slight broadening. These spectral characteristics are indicative of the modification of the local electronic environment around carbonyl functionalities arising from non-covalent interactions with the porphyrin macrocycle.

Changes were also identified in the fingerprint region. For the caffeine component, the band between 1483 cm−1 of this analyte shifted to about 1472 cm−1 in the mixture, and the C–N stretching band near 1230 cm−1 had a low displacement towards higher wavenumber and moderate broadening. These differences indicate that there is a perturbation of vibrational modes due to supramolecular association between the two components.

These trends and the broadening of bands are consistent with weak yet specific intermolecular interactions between caffeine and TPP. The downshift of the N–H and CO stretching vibrations is compatible with hydrogen-bonding of caffeine carbonyl groups with TPP pyrrolic N–H moieties. Moreover, the feature bands from spectral perturbations in the aromatic and fingerprint regions imply that this energized binding is attributed to π–π stacking interactions between the planar conjugated porphyrin macrocycle and the heterocyclic aromatic caffeine framework. No new distinct absorption bands were found to be present (or required), and instead, both constituents' characteristic bands in the spectrum were maintained, indicating this is not a chemical bond but a supramolecular complex interaction.

XRD analysis

XRD analysis was conducted to investigate the crystalline physical form of caffeine, TPP and caffeine-TPP mixture in Fig. 2B. Pure caffeine exhibited many sharp and strong diffraction peaks, which confirmed high crystallinity and ordered molecular packing. The multiple reflections in the low- and mid-angle regions are in line with a rigid pattern of xanthine and robust intermolecular packing interactions that characterize crystalline caffeine.

In contrast, the XRD pattern of TPP exhibited overall broader and lower-intensity diffraction peaks with less background scattering. Lower long-range crystallinity with respect to caffeine is also plausible for the bulky porphyrin macrocycle bearing peripheral phenyl rings. In the aggregate, reflections imply relatively lower molecular packing density and more structural disorder in TPP.

The caffeine–TPP mixture showed some characteristic reflections of caffeine, but the diffraction patterns are clearly different from each other (evident as changes in intensity, peak broadening, and relative peak distribution). A few of these caffeine-associated regions showed reduced clustering or partial overlap, whereas the overall distribution showed pronounced background activity throughout 15–30°. This is evidence that the caffeine should not just stay in its starting consolidated shape after engaging with TPP. In contrast, the decreased crystallinity and altered diffraction pattern suggest that Caffeine molecular packing was partially interrupted through supramolecular association with the porphyrin macrocycle.

The noted reduction in diffraction intensity as well as the broadening of the selected reflections can be attributed to non-covalent interactions between caffeine and TPP, such as π–π stacking between the planar caffeine heterocycle and the conjugated porphyrin ring, and possible hydrogen-bonding interactions among carbonyl groups of caffeine with pyrrolic N–H groups of TPP. These interactions disrupt the normal packing of caffeine molecules and serve to reduce long-range order in its structure.

Crucially, there was no marking of a totally new crystalline phase in the mixed XRD pattern. This implies that the interaction of caffeine with TPP is predominantly supramolecular rather than the generation of a new covalent or very crystalline species. Thus, PXRD results should be regarded with an additional caution of being supportive of changes in the molecular packing, while FTIR and UV-Vis spectral variations reflect significantly stronger direct evidence for caffeine–TPP intermolecular interaction.

In summary, the XRD results support the interpretation of a delocalized caffeine–TPP-associated system through which some degree of crystallinity in caffeine is retained but with significant perturbation. The reductions in peak intensity and the moderate broadening as well as an alternative relative diffraction profile, confirm molecular-level changes that occur upon caffeine-TPP mixing, prompting us to conclude that our foray into non-covalent complexation as a means of effecting sequential imprinting is viable.22

TEM analysis and particle size distribution

Transmission electron microscopy was used to examine the morphology of a small amount of porphyrin-functionalized MIP coating dispersed in ethanol by mild ultrasonication. According to the viewing area, the surprising spherical morphology of dispersed MIP domains appeared as interconnected aggregates (Fig. 3A).23 This aggregation mechanism is expected since the imprinted polymer was synthesized directly on a cellulose substrate, and thus the TEM sample contains polymeric MIP segments released from the layer rather than independently prepared particles. Significantly, the domain sizes scale from a few nanometers to tens of nanometers, revealing that the porphyrin-functionalized imprinted polymer network was successfully formed and has numerous recognition sites exposed for caffeine binding.

(A) TEM image of dispersed porphyrin-functionalized MIP domains isolated from the polymer-coated paper substrate after ultrasonication in ethanol. (B) Apparent size distribution histogram of dispersed MIP domains.

The size distributions of the dispersed MIP domains are shown in Fig. 3B, and the histograms display a rather narrow Gaussian-like distribution with an average domain size of around 7.6 nm. The majority of diffused domains exist in the central size range, with only a small portion of larger aggregates detected. A uniform distribution within its limits indicates homogeneous formation of the nanoscale imprinted polymer domains in the course of the in situ polymerization processes. These nanoscale dimensions are beneficial for rapid analyte diffusion and enhanced accessibility of recognition cavities, which promotes efficient molecular recognition, thus leading to enhanced sensing performance of the developed paper-based MIP platform.

FTIR analysis of raw paper and MIP fabrication

FTIR spectra of MIP paper after template extraction showed that cellulose-specific features were also dominant, which indicated that the structure of the substrate paper remained unchanged. On the other hand, even changing data-sensitive regions did not show marked variations in any of them. More specifically, the dispersive O–H/N–H stretching envelope was centered at 3600–3150 cm−1 and displayed only modest rearrangement associated with caffeine removal. Moreover, slight attenuations and minor band shape alterations are clearly observable in the carbonyl spectral range (1735–1685 cm−1) as well as in the aromatic/porphyrin-associated region (1620–1500 cm−1). This observation implies the disruption of weak noncovalent interactions between caffeine molecules and the porphyrin-functionalized polymer matrix, thus exposing the vast imprinted recognition sites.

Partial recovery and a mild broadening of the bands that are sensitive to interaction were also observed in some key regions, specifically, in the O–H/N–H stretching region (3700–2700 cm−1), the carbonyl region (1755–1710 cm−1), and around 1620–1500 cm 1 and around 1260–1210 cm−1 belonging to aromatic/fingerprint regions after caffeine rebinding. This spectral modification makes it possible to re-access imprinted cavities by caffeine through hydrogen bonding and π–π-assisted supramolecular interactions. These changes are anticipated to be more subtle than drastic because the FTIR response is increasingly dominated by the cellulose substrate. Accordingly, FTIR is employed in this work as an auxiliary agent to prove template removal and re-binding of the imprinted sites, while the colorimetric, fluorescence, and wettability results function as the major evidence for caffeine recognition (Fig. 4).

Wettability and interfacial evidence of cavity formation and rebinding

Surface changes during MIP fabrication and template removal, as well as caffeine rebinding, were analyzed by contact angle and surface energy measurements. The contact angle for raw filter paper was 32.5 ± 1.2°, indicating a hydrophilic cellulose surface containing abundant hydroxyl groups. Upon polymerization, the contact angle increased significantly (to 86.7 ± 1.5°), suggesting that a relatively hydrophobic crosslinked MIP layer composed of EGDMA and porphyrin units was successfully deposited.

The contact angle was 72.3 ± 1.3° after removal of the caffeine template, and the polar surface energy component increased. This alteration facilitates the generation of imprinted cavities and the exposure of polar recognition groups, such as carboxyl and carbonyl sites, as well as hydrogen bonding within the polymer matrix. The exposure of these sites increased surface wettability and provided binding regions accessible for caffeine recognition.

After receiving the caffeine rebinding, the static contact angle increased back to 83.4 ± 1.4° with a decrease in the polar component. These results on reversible wettability change provide evidence that caffeine molecules have relocated into the imprinted cavities, thereby covering polar groups and creating a hydrophobic microenvironment through caffeine–porphyrin interactions. Thus, the contact angle trend is another physicochemical indicator of successful cavity formation, access to recognition sites, and specific rebinding of caffeine in the MIP paper sensor (Table 1).

UV-visible spectral studies of caffeine complexation with porphyrins

The UV-visible absorption spectra of caffeine, TPP, and their mixture are shown in Fig. 5. TPP exhibited a strong Soret band at around 414 nm, assigned to the π–π* transition of the porphyrin macrocycle. On the other hand, caffeine did not contribute to the UV region, nor to the lower visible region.

UV-Vis absorption spectra of caffeine, TPP, and their mixture in ethanol, illustrating Soret band shift and electronic interaction.

The interaction of TPP with caffeine led to a bathochromic shift of the Soret band from 414 nm to 420 nm and a concomitant decrease in peak intensity. This red shift corresponds to a modification of the electronic environment surrounding the porphyrin chromophore due to intermolecular interactions with caffeine. This spectral change confirms the assembly of a ground-state supramolecular complex rather than mere physical mixing. Triplicate UV-Vis measurements were performed in identical experimental conditions, where the reproducible Soret band shift from 414 to 420 nm and absorbance decrease were recorded. The variation in the spectral display was caused by caffeine–TPP supramolecular interactions rather than by concentration fluctuations or dilution effects, as the concentrations of TPP, the solvent composition, the cuvette path length, and the final solution volume were constant.

This change can be ascribed to π–π stacking dispositions between the cyclic aromatic structure of caffeine and the porphyrin macrocycle. These stacking interactions affect electron delocalization in the macrocycle, thereby altering the transition energy and subsequently shifting the Soret band. No new peaks were detected, indicating that no covalent modification occurred; a non-covalent recognition mechanism was confirmed.

Direct spectroscopic measurements of pre-polymerization complexes, which are key to forming well-defined imprinted cavities in UV polymerizations.

Fluorescence emission properties and complexation evidence

Fig. 6 shows the fluorescence emission spectra of caffeine, TPP, and their mixture. Two characteristic emission bands were observed for TPP overall, centered around 650 nm (Q-band transition) and at ∼715 nm. Under the same excitation conditions, caffeine did not exhibit significant fluorescence, indicating that the observed emission is mainly due to TPP.

Fluorescence emission spectra of caffeine, TPP, and the TPP–caffeine complex, demonstrating quenching behavior upon interaction.

However, upon the introduction of caffeine, both observed emission bands showed a significant decrease in fluorescence intensity without a noticeable peak shift. This quenching mechanism can be linked to ground-state complex formation between caffeine and TPP via a π–π stacking interaction. Caffeine, with a planar aromatic structure that allows it to come into proximity with the porphyrin ring, can modulate electronic interactions and promote non-radiative decay pathways. The lack of significant peak shifting further corroborates a static quenching mechanism instead of dynamic collisional quenching.

Furthermore, the fully stained inset, from pale purple to dark pinkish-red, highlights the change in the aggregation state of porphyrin upon caffeine binding, as further evidenced by fluorescence quenching. The inhibition of emission intensity is consistent with diminished exciton coupling due to perturbation of porphyrin aggregate equilibria.

The results demonstrate that fluorescence quenching is an effective optical transduction mechanism for caffeine recognition via the porphyrin-based sensor platform.

Caffeine generation-fluorescence response

Fluorescence emission spectra of porphyrin-embedded MIP paper at different caffeine concentrations (from 0 to 250 µM) are shown in Fig. 7A. Two distinct emission bands at 650 nm and 715 nm were detected, consistent with the expected Q-band emission of TPP. With increasing caffeine concentration, a progressive attenuation of the fluorescence intensities of both emission peaks was observed.

(A) Fluorescence emission spectra of MIP paper at different caffeine concentrations (0–250 µM), showing a concentration-dependent quenching response. (B) Stern–Volmer plot (F0/F vs. caffeine concentration) showing linear fluorescence quenching behavior and correlation coefficient. Remarkably, the emission maxima did not vary with concentration, whereas the intensity decreased as a function of concentration. The quenching mechanism underlying this behavior is not due to structural degradation of the porphyrin chromophore, but rather to an enhancement of the electronic environment induced by specific analyte binding.

Fluorescence gradually decreases in intensity, indicating that caffeine bound to the imprinted cavity shifts the π–π stacking equilibrium at a local control site of the porphyrin units. Interaction of caffeine ligands interrupts excitonic coupling in aggregates, thereby favoring non-radiative decay routes and, as a result, low-intensity fluorescence.

The monotonic decrease of fluorescence intensity over the studied range illustrates excellent responsiveness and reproducibility of this sensing platform. Moreover, the continuous trend further supports the conclusion that signal modulation stems from selective cavity occupation rather than nonspecific surface adsorption.

The corresponding color change also observed shows a transition from 0 µM deep purple to 250 µM pinkish-red (inset), suggesting the fluorescence loss agrees with the expected activity. This visible chromatic evolution results from aggregation-state modulation of the porphyrin macrocycle and is corroborated by the dual optical detection ability (fluorescence and colorimetric).

The nature of the fluorescence quenching suggests that a stable ground-state supramolecular complex between caffeine and the porphyrin macrocycle is formed. This mechanism was further supported by UV-Vis absorption studies, which showed that the Soret band of TPP exhibited a bathochromic shift from 414 to 420 nm and a lower intensity upon the addition of caffeine. The spectral modulation signifies changes in the porphyrin's electronic environment that occur before excitation, a characteristic of static quenching. In addition, the absence of a prominent emission-peak shift suggests that fluorescence suppression due to supramolecular interactions arises predominantly from specific interactions rather than from degradation of the chromophore's physical structure.

The concentration-based observation of emission behavior confirms that the porphyrin-embedded MIP paper is an effective and tunable fluorescence-based sensor for caffeine detection.

Stern–Volmer analysis and mechanism of quenching

To quantitatively analyze the fluorescence quenching behavior, we conducted a Stern–Volmer analysis on the data by plotting F0/F against caffeine concentration (Fig. 7B). Within the studied concentration range, a linear relationship was achieved following the regression equation:F0/F = 0.005C + 1.1446with a correlation coefficient of R2 = 0.99, showing good linearity and analytical reliability.

Well-defined linearity of the Stern–Volmer plot (R2 = 0.99) is strong evidence for a predominantly static quenching-type mechanism associated with ground-state complex formation within imprinted cavities. The lack of curvature or deviation from linearity indicates the negligible contribution from dynamic collisional quenching.

The Stern–Volmer constant (KSV) calculated from the slope indicates that the binding of caffeine to porphyrin sites occurs within the polymer matrix. The high linearity further indicates that the fluorescence signal turn-off is controlled by specific molecular recognition rather than nonspecific adsorption.

Notably, small error bars indicate a good reproducibility and precision of the sensing platform. Such a linear response indicates that this system is applicable to the qualitative detection of caffeine at the analyzed concentration metric.

This process leads to selective cavity occupation within the molecularly imprinted matrix, thereby confirming that fluorescence modulation arises from this phenomenon and supporting the proposed supramolecular interaction mechanism in general.

Smartphone-based colorimetric quantification

The colorimetric response of the MIP paper was quantified with a smartphone-assisted RGB analysis. We used the parameter (R − B)/(R + G + B) to quantify visual color change into a numeric signal. Selectivity for structurally unrelated molecules

The selectivity of the MIP paper sensor developed was studied by testing a range of structurally unrelated interferents, as well as related xanthine derivatives around the structure of caffeine encountered regularly in beverage matrices (Fig. 9B). The compounds included glucose, sucrose, urea, ascorbic acid, paracetamol, and xanthine and its derivatives (paraxanthine). To better visualize selectivity performance, the responses were plotted as absolute RGB values, since a normalized RGB index may yield positive or negative values depending on which RGB channel was dominant in smartphone-based image analysis.

The MIP sensor exhibited a much larger response to caffeine than to any other interfering compound, demonstrating that the imprinted recognition cavities provided very high selectivity. The strong selectivity for caffeine is rationalized by structural complementarity between caffeine and the binding sites formed after template removal. Caffeine has a planar heterocyclic aromatic structure that allows for efficient π–π stacking interactions with the porphyrin macrocycle. Moreover, the carbonyl and nitrogen functionalities of caffeine serve as appropriate hydrogen-bond acceptor sites for interaction with functional groups located in imprinted cavities. Caffeine, therefore, fits neatly within the recognition regions of templates based on molecular size, geometry, and electron distribution.

Xanthine derivatives that are structurally related (xanthine and paraxanthine), which were also present, elicited much lower responses than caffeine, again indicating partial interaction with the imprinted cavities due to their resemblance in heterocyclic aromatic structures. But the dramatically more powerful response to caffeine validates excellent steric fit and optimized supramolecular interaction matching between rebinding and imprinting sites, enabling this template-matched recognition element to function effectively. In contrast, interfering molecules such as glucose and sucrose are non-aromatic carbohydrates with flexible 3D structures and excessive hydroxyl functionalities, which do not promote π–π interactions or steric fitting into the cavities. Urea is a non-aromatic compound, so it shows only very weak interactions with the porphyrin environment. Yet ascorbic acid and paracetamol both contain hydrogen-bonding groups; they have significantly different molecular geometries and electronic structures than caffeine and, in fact, cannot occupy the sites imprinted to recognize it.

The result is that caffeine selectively disturbs the porphyrin aggregation equilibrium within the imprinted cavities, thereby giving rise to a significant colorimetric response; in contrast, structurally similar interferents produced only a negligible optical variation. These results corroborate the synergistic contribution of shape-selective molecular imprinting and supramolecular π–π/hydrogen-bond-assisted recognition mechanisms to sensing performance.

MIP paper sensor reusability and structural stability

The operational stability and regeneration ability of the prepared porphyrin-embedded MIP paper sensor were tested in five successive adsorption–desorption cycles (Fig. 10A). After performing each of the sensing experiments, caffeine was removed from the paper by soaking it with a suitable elution solvent and drying for use again. The sensor retained nearly 100% of its initial response in the first cycle, then gradually decreased to around 96%, 93%, 89%, and ∼85% in the second, third, fourth, and fifth cycles. Despite this slight reduction, over 85% of the original response was retained after five cycles, demonstrating excellent reusability and structural stability of the imprinted polymer matrix. This slight loss of signal strength could be due to incomplete occupation of binding cavities, incomplete desorption of analyte molecules, or small structural changes in the polymer after repeated washing. Still, the small variance and tight error bars indicate good reproducibility and mechanical stability. These results demonstrate that the crosslinked polymer framework enables retention of cavity integrity and porphyrin functionality during repeated use, emphasizing the practical applicability and economic viability of the developed paper-based sensor for routine caffeine monitoring.24

Storage stability

Storage stability of the developed MIP paper sensor was examined over 30 days (Fig. 10B). It retained ∼97%, ∼94%, and ∼91% of the initial response at 7, 14, and 30 days, respectively. The small reduction in this signal could be due to minute environmental effects or slow structural relaxation of the polymer matrix. However, the 90% retention after one month indicates remarkable stability and shelf life of the sensing platform. These findings suggest that the crosslinked polymer matrix can maintain the structural integrity of cavities and porphyrin functioning during storage.

Conclusion

In summary, a porphyrin-integrated MIP paper sensor was successfully prepared for the simple, visual, and quantitative detection of caffeine in beverages. With template-generated recognition cavities, TPP exhibited aggregation-modulated optical signaling, leading to a prominent color change and fluorescence quenching upon binding caffeine. The sensor showed a linear detection range of 1–100 µM with a low detection limit of 1.62 µM, ensuring high sensitivity. The quantitative imprinting factor experiment conducted showed a strong template-specific recognition of the imprinted cavities and further validated its excellent specificity against structurally unrelated molecules. The platform provided further evidence of solid operational re-usability, with over 90% of the initial response remaining after 30 days, demonstrating structural strength and storage stability. Good recoveries were obtained from tea and coffee samples, confirming the practical applicability of the method in a complex matrix. Although the sensor shows a higher LOD than HPLC-based methods, it offers significant advantages for rapid, low-cost, portable, and on-site caffeine screening applications. Based on the integration of molecular imprinting, aggregation-responsive porphyrin signaling, and smartphone-mediated quantification via a low-cost paper substrate, we have developed a sensing strategy – an inexpensive yet sophisticated portable caffeine analytical platform. This approach may also be applicable to more selective detection not only of caffeine but also of other small organic molecules, pharmaceuticals, food contaminants, and biologically relevant analytes via appropriately templated cavity formation. The combination of molecular selectivity, visual readability, operational stability, and facile fabrication also makes the platform a promising candidate for applications beyond biosensing, including food quality control, environmental monitoring, wearable sensing systems, and point-of-care diagnostics.

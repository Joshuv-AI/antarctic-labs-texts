Application of artificial intelligence in conjunction with clinical laboratory indicators to aid decision-making for surgical or conservative treatment of pediatric intestinal obstruction
Authors: Min Zhan, Ting Xiong, Ming Luo, Manxin Hu, Leifeng Chen, Dan Nie, Mengjie Yu, Shouhua Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundManagement of pediatric intestinal obstruction remains clinically challenging, particularly regarding the selection between surgical and conservative approaches. This study aimed to develop artificial intelligence (AI) models to support treatment decision-making.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on clinical data from pediatric intestinal obstruction patients. The dataset was split via stratified sampling (70% training/ 30% test), preserving outcome distribution. Predictive models incorporating clinical indicators were developed using machine learning, with evaluation metrics including accuracy, F1-score, Kappa value, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), precision-recall curves, calibration plots and decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsAmong 765 pediatric patients, 425 responded to conservative treatment while 340 required surgery. The Random Forest model demonstrated optimal performance in the test cohort (area under the curve: 0.953; sensitivity: 0.879; specificity: 0.901; accuracy: 0.892; F1-score: 0.878; Kappa value: 0.780; PPV: 0.878; NPV: 0.905). Calibration, precision-recall, and DCAs indicated favorable clinical applicability.ConclusionMachine learning integration with clinical indicators shows potential as a decision-support tool for selecting surgical or conservative treatment in pediatric intestinal obstruction.

Introduction

Pediatric intestinal obstruction, a condition where the passage of intestinal contents is obstructed from multiple causes, is a common source of acute abdominal pain in pediatric surgery. Failure to address this promptly can lead to severe complications and potentially life-threatening outcomes.1 For patients presenting with severe obstruction and clear evidence of intestinal damage, immediate surgical treatment (ST) is imperative. In cases of mild intestinal obstruction without signs of significant intestinal damage, conservative treatment (CT) is generally the initial approach.2 Nevertheless, ST may still be necessary in cases where CT has failed.3 4 Consequently, the decision-making process regarding surgical versus CT for pediatric patients with mild obstruction coupled with ambiguous surgical indications continues to present a significant challenge for pediatric surgeons.5 6

The CT and water-soluble contrast enema facilitate diagnostic evaluation and therapeutic triage in intestinal obstruction.7 8 However, their broad application is limited by demanding technical expertise and the requirement for precise interpretation by specialized radiologists and surgeons. Pediatric-specific challenges, including radiation sensitivity, unreliable medical histories, and poor cooperation during examinations, further hinder routine use in children.

Artificial intelligence (AI) has garnered considerable research focus as a technology that employs algorithms and statistical models to execute prediction and decision-making tasks on computer systems.9 Machine learning (ML), an essential facet of AI, is distinguished by its notable precision, automation, and incorporation of electronic healthcare data, facilitating expeditious outcome prediction.912 Of note, diverse algorithms offer complementary strengths: both eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) and Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM) efficiently process large-scale data sets, while Random Forest (RF) excels in high-dimensional feature spaces. Complement Naive Bayes (CNB) addresses class imbalance, and multi-layer perceptron (MLP) alongside support vector machine (SVM) captures complex nonlinear patterns. K-nearest neighbors (KNN) is suitable for small-scale datasets and proximity relationships. Owing to these distinct capabilities, AI/ML algorithms have been extensively deployed across life sciences.1315 Their application to pediatric intestinal obstruction remains unexplored.

This study aims to enhance the accuracy of treatment decision-making by integrating AI with clinical baseline indicators to develop an advanced predictive model. The model will provide clinicians with validated data support to aid in formulating treatment plans. More precise and effective decisions will optimize clinical workflows and advance precision medicine.

Methods

Participating cohorts

This retrospective study included pediatric intestinal obstruction patients admitted to Jiangxi Provincial Children’s Hospital (January 2018–June 2022). Demographic characteristics and admission laboratory data were extracted from electronic medical records (EMR). Patient selection procedures are detailed in online supplemental figure S1.

Inclusion criteria entailed core clinical manifestations, such as abdominal pain, vomiting, constipation, and abdominal distension; radiologically confirmed intestinal obstruction or luminal dilation; as well as associated signs, including asymmetric abdominal distension, localized tenderness, or bloody vomitus/anal discharge. All final diagnoses were clinician-confirmed.

Exclusion criteria comprised neonates, patients with abdominal tumors, recent abdominal surgery (≤30 days), definitive surgical indications, or cases with incomplete medical documentation.

Pediatric management of intestinal obstruction

The CT involves fasting, gastrointestinal decompression, oxygen support, antibiotic therapy, and maintenance of water-electrolyte/acid-base balance. Following the initial treatment, patients underwent 2–4 hourly abdominal assessments with radiologic evaluation (abdominal plain film, ultrasound, or upright abdominal X-ray) at 12 hours to assess severity of the obstruction. If clinical symptoms, particularly failure to pass flatus or feces, showed no significant improvement within 48 hours, conservative management was deemed ineffective, prompting surgical consultation by ≥two attending physicians to determine operative necessity.

Surgical interventions were tailored to specific etiologies: intestinal adhesion cases underwent resection with primary anastomosis; incarcerated hernias required reduction followed by repair; fecal impactions necessitated removal via enterotomy; concomitant intestinal obstruction with intussusception was managed through partial intestinal resection combined with manual reduction and fixation techniques.

Data acquisition

All cases included in this study were sourced from the department of general surgery at Jiangxi Provincial Children’s Hospital. The research team extracted pertinent patient data from EMRs following institutional review board approval and using authorized access to the hospital database. The specific procedure entailed the following steps: first, identifying relevant cases from 2018 to 2022 using “intestinal obstruction” as the keyword; second, extracting demographic characteristics and laboratory test indicators of patients on admission; finally, reviewing medical record information to select case samples and test indicators in accordance with the research objectives.

Preprocessing and statistical analysis

Demographic characteristics and laboratory test indicators of the patients on admission were used as variables for analysis. Variables with missing values exceeding 30% of the sample size were excluded. Missing data were addressed via multiple imputation implemented in R with the mice package (multivariate imputation by chained equations). Five imputed datasets (m=5) were generated, with all candidate variables included in the imputation model under the predictive mean matching framework. The comparison of qualitative data was conducted using the χ2 test, while the Mann-Whitney U test was employed for the comparison of quantitative data. A p value<0.05 was regarded as statistically significant for all the analyses. Pearson correlation analysis was conducted to assess relationships among the independent variables, and the results are presented as a heatmap.

Model building and tuning

Predictive models were developed using seven ML algorithms: XGBoost, RF, LightGBM, CNB, MLP, SVM, and KNN. The dataset underwent stratified random partitioning (70% training, 30% test), preserving outcome class distributions. Candidate predictors underwent a two-stage feature selection process. Initial refinement via least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression (λ optimized through 10-fold cross-validation using the minimum λ criterion), followed by recursive feature elimination (RFE) with RF-derived feature importance rankings, ensuring model parsimony while mitigating overfitting risks.

To address the risks associated with overfitting, we employed comprehensive regularization strategies and performed systematic hyperparameter optimization. A stratified 10-fold cross-validation framework was used to achieve this objective. The training dataset was divided into ten class-balanced folds, and in each iteration, models were trained on nine of these folds, with the remaining fold used for validation. The final performance metrics were determined by averaging the results across all ten validation partitions, thereby minimizing the variance introduced by the stochastic nature of the partitioning process.

The XGBoost model used a learning rate of 0.0735, max depth of 7, min child weight of 5, n estimators of 300, and reg lambda of 0.6512. LightGBM was configured with boosting type ‘goss’, colsample bytree of 0.9417, learning rate of 0.0281, max depth of 10, min child samples of 10, n estimators of 2689, num leaves of 16, reg alpha of 0.005 and reg lambda of 0.005. The RF model employed max depth of 9, max features set to ‘sqrt’, min samples leaf of 6, min samples split of 15, n estimators of 186 and criterion ‘gini’. For the MLP model, we used activation ‘tanh’, max iter of 200, hidden layer sizes (100), solver ‘adam’, alpha 0.0001, and learning rate init 0.001. The SVM model was parameterized with C=4.2149, gamma=‘scale’, kernel=‘rbf’, probability=true and tol=0.0111. KNN used leaf size 39, n neighbors 19, p=1, and weights ‘uniform’. Finally, the CNB model implemented an alpha (additive smoothing) of 0.8202.

Model evaluation

To comprehensively assess the predictive model and address the inherent ‘black box’ issue in ML,16 we implemented a multidimensional validation framework that encompasses four essential dimensions: (1) performance metrics, including accuracy, F1-score, Kappa value, positive predictive value (PPV) and negative predictive value (NPV), to quantify classification efficacy; (2) discrimination capacity, evaluated through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) and precision-recall (PR) curve analyses, with predictive accuracy objectively measured by area under the curves (AUCs)—notably, the PR curve illustrates the precision-recall tradeoff across classification thresholds17; (3) calibration and clinical utility, assessed using calibration plots to visualize the alignment between predicted probabilities and observed event frequencies,18 supplemented by decision curve analysis (DCA) that calculates net benefit through threshold-weighted optimization of true-positive and false-positive rates19; and (4) enhancement of interpretability by utilizing SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) values to quantify individual feature contributions to predictions, thereby illuminating the model’s decision-making process.20 Cross-validation further ensured generalizability to unseen data, establishing statistical robustness while bridging technical performance with clinical interpretability. All analyses were performed using R software (V.4.0) and Python V.3.7.

Results

Demographic and clinical characteristics

This study included 765 pediatric patients with intestinal obstruction who received initial CT. Of these, 425 responded successfully (CT group), while 340 required surgical intervention after failed conservative management (ST group). Compared with the ST group, the CT group was younger (age: 3.5 v.s 5.6 years; p<0.001, Mann-Whitney U test) and there was a higher proportion of females (35.3% v.s 27.7%; p=0.024, χ2 test) (table 1).

ROC and correlation analyses

ROC curve analysis was conducted to assess the discriminatory capacity of the differential variables across the treatment groups. The analysis indicated suboptimal performance of individual variables in differentiating between the ST and CT groups, with all AUC values below 0.77 (table 2). This finding underscores the considerable risk of both false-negative and false-positive classifications when relying on single indicators. Pearson correlation analysis revealed significant associations among the study variables (online supplemental figure S2). Age significantly correlated with several variables, showing positive associations with prealbumin (PA), mean platelet volume (MPV), and TBIL. There was a negative correlation with monocyte count (MONO#), LYMPH#, and LYMPH%. Furthermore, significant correlations were identified between numerous other variables (e.g., between PA and MPV, MONO# and LYMPH#). Collectively, these findings suggest that an integrated assessment of clinical variables provides more comprehensive pathophysiological insights than the evaluation of isolated parameters.

Screening of model predictors

Feature selection was performed using LASSO regression and RFE. LASSO regression implemented 10-fold cross-validation with nlambda=100 λ values, identifying 17 candidate predictors at the optimal regularization parameter (λmin=0.003) that minimized mean squared error (figure 1A,B). Subsequently, RFE was applied with stepwise feature elimination (one feature per iteration) using logistic regression (L₂ penalty) as the estimator, optimizing model selection through five-fold cross-validated accuracy maximization. This process yielded a final set of 16 clinically interpretable variables: MONO#, LYMPH#, LYMPH%, platelet crit (PCT), MPV, albumin, TBIL, PA, SAA, retinol-binding protein (RBP), β−2 macroglobulin (MG), AST, creatine kinase, alkaline phosphatase, international normalized ratio, and chloride (Cl) (figure 1C).

Prediction models construction and performance assessment

To enhance diagnostic capabilities, we developed AI-driven predictive models using seven advanced ML algorithms: XGBoost, RF, LightGBM, CNB, MLP, SVM, and KNN. Following rigorous evaluation, the RF model demonstrated superior performance (online supplemental table S1, figure 2). Specifically, on the test set, RF achieved an AUC of 0.953, sensitivity of 0.879, specificity of 0.901, accuracy of 0.892, F1-score of 0.878, Kappa coefficient of 0.780, PPV of 0.878, and NPV of 0.905, collectively indicating exceptional diagnostic precision.

Calibration curves were evaluated on the test set (figure 2C). The RF model exhibited a strong alignment between predicted probabilities and actual positive rates, falling within the clinically acceptable range and supporting its reliability for clinical decision-making. Beyond calibration, clinical utility was quantified via DCA (figure 2D). The RF model demonstrated consistently high net benefit across the clinically relevant threshold probability range of 20–60%, performing significantly better than most models. This indicates that RF maximizes the net benefit of correctly treating patients while minimizing unnecessary treatment when clinicians use this decision threshold interval, highlighting its high clinical utility.

Complementing these analyses, PR curves revealed RF’s test-set average precision (AP=0.948) exceeded other models (XGBoost: 0.911; LightGBM: 0.941; MLP: 0.924; SVM: 0.947; KNN: 0.912; CNB: 0.799) (figure 2E,F). Notwithstanding RF’s near-perfect training AP (0.995), its robust test performance underscores strong generalization capacity without evidence of overfitting (|ΔAP|<5%).

Discussion

The process of making therapeutic decisions for pediatric intestinal obstruction continues to pose clinical challenges, particularly in cases with mild presentations where surgical indications are not well-defined. This study identified objective differences in laboratory test results between cohorts undergoing surgical versus CT. By using ML algorithms, we effectively developed a predictive model that integrates multiple clinical indicators to enhance treatment stratification decisions in ambiguous clinical scenarios.

Imaging, clinical, and laboratory test indicators are widely employed in prediction modeling of intestinal obstruction.2123 While CT and erect abdominal X-rays remain standard diagnostic tools, laboratory-based prediction models demonstrate distinct advantages. Medical imaging requires specialized technical expertise and precise interpretation by radiologists and surgeons. In contrast, laboratory indicators objectively reflect patients’ physiological and pathological states without being influenced by subjective clinical judgment. Furthermore, laboratory testing offers superior cost-effectiveness, faster turnaround times, greater accessibility, lower sample requirements, and easier data integration, positioning it as both an effective alternative and valuable supplementary diagnostic tool.

Previous research highlights the susceptibility of intestinal obstruction patients to water and electrolyte metabolic imbalances during the course of the disease.24 25 Within the scope of this study, the reduction in Ca, Na, and Cl indices in patients undergoing ST further corroborated the escalation of water and electrolyte metabolic dysfunction. Moreover, prolonged obstruction can foster bacterial overgrowth in the intestine, leading to infection and triggering an inflammatory response.26 27 The observed rise in WBC and NEUT#, coupled with the decline in LYMPH% and LYMPH#, indicates the presence of more severe bacterial infections and inflammatory responses among children receiving ST for intestinal obstruction.

Integration of AI and ML within the healthcare sector has expanded in parallel with the proliferation of big data.28 In our study, we employed seven ML algorithms to develop predictive models, ultimately determining the RF model as the most suitable option. The RF model is particularly adept at addressing complex problems and handling large-scale datasets due to its high accuracy, robust interpretability, and inherent resistance to overfitting.29 Although the model exhibited exceptional predictive performance on the training set (AUC=0.984), the observed disparity with the test set AUC (0.953) raises potential concerns regarding overfitting. Nevertheless, the RF algorithm inherently mitigates overfitting through mechanisms such as bootstrap aggregating (bagging) and random feature selection. The consistently highest AUC (0.953, the highest among all models) indicates that the extent of overfitting may be relatively controlled. However, the performance gap between the training and test sets serves as an indication that the model may be capturing dataset-specific patterns rather than generalizable rules. This risk of overfitting necessitates careful consideration, particularly in contexts characterized by limited data or complex feature interactions.

Limitations

Our retrospective analysis reveals that in children with equivocal surgical indications, all 765 cases were initially triaged to CT by surgeons, yet 44.4% subsequently required surgical intervention. This gap underscores limitations in clinical identification of high-risk cases. Our model achieved 89.2% accuracy within this test set, demonstrating a potential to reduce CT failures and avoid unnecessary surgeries. Of note, the 44.4% failure rate reflects a selectively screened cohort and may overestimate real-world rates. While not directly comparable, these findings provide preliminary support for the model as an adjunctive tool in diagnostically uncertain scenarios.

In addition, this study employed single-center retrospective data, which are primarily limited by selection bias and challenges related to data quality. The patient cohort from a single institution may reflect distinct regional or clinical workflow characteristics, thereby constraining the generalizability of the model’s findings to broader populations. Moreover, retrospective data often suffer from incomplete documentation and uncontrolled confounding variables, which may introduce unmeasured biases. Consequently, external validation or prospective studies are essential to enhance the reliability and clinical applicability of our findings.

To advance clinical translation of this model, future efforts should prioritize the following: first, integration of the model into clinical workflows should be achieved through system integration, such as embedding within electronic health records, automated reporting with structured risk score outputs, and optimization of human-computer interaction to support customizable parameters, thereby facilitating clinical decision support. Second, it is essential to conduct multicenter prospective validation studies that incorporate cohorts from diverse geographic regions and populations to assess generalizability of the model while mitigating retrospective biases. Lastly, the development of an intelligent decision-support tool should focus on expanding functionality, including prognostic stratification and treatment recommendations, enhancing interpretability through visualized decision logic and ensuring mobile accessibility to improve practical utility in clinical settings.

In conclusion, his study demonstrates that clinical indicators significantly contribute to predicting treatment selection in pediatric intestinal obstruction. We developed and validated a ML-based predictive model that shows potential as a clinical decision-support tool, enabling more precise timing of interventions while supporting individualized treatment pathways.

Portal hypertensive biliopathy secondary to Abernethy malformation mimicking a hepatic hilum mass
Authors: Wenfeng Xi, Haiping Xing, Wen Shi, Aiming Yang
Published: 2025

A 60-year-old male with heavy alcohol use and liver cirrhosis presented with jaundice. Laboratory tests showed hypoalbuminemia and markedly elevated total and direct bilirubin. Abdominal computed tomography (CT) demonstrated a suspicious enhancing soft tissue lesion in the hepatic hilum (Figure 1A) partially encasing adjacent structures, causing a biliary stricture. Multiple tortuous veins around the common bile duct and a splenorenal shunt were also noted.

Findings from imaging and endoscopic examination. (A) Abdominal CT (coronal view) revealed a suspicious soft tissue density with enhancement in the hepatic hilum, with the portal vein interrupted at the hilum and no visualization of intrahepatic portal branches. (B) EUS showed mixed hypoechoic changes in the hepatic hilum with perivascular involvement. (C) ERCP demonstrated bile duct stenosis at the hepatic hilum with a filling defect near the hepatic confluence. (D) IDUS revealed slight wall thickening at the stenotic segment with an intact ductal structure, without evidence of a mass lesion, with multiple thickened vessel-like structures observed outside the bile duct wall. (E) SpyGlass cholangioscopy revealed a stricture in the common hepatic duct, likely due to vascular compression. (F) The coronal T2-weighted image shows vessels with flow void signals (low signal), demonstrating the connection between the splenic vein (short arrows) and the inferior vena cava (long arrows). CT, computed tomography; ERCP, endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography; EUS, endoscopic ultrasonography; IDUS, intraductal ultrasonography.

Endoscopic ultrasonography (EUS) failed to clearly visualize the suspected hilar mass (Figure 1B). Fine-needle aspiration showed no malignant cells. Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) identified a filling defect near the hilum (Figure 1C). Intraductal ultrasonography (IDUS) revealed multiple thickened vessel-like structures external to the bile duct wall instead of a mass (Figure 1D). SpyGlass cholangioscopy showed a common hepatic duct stricture more likely due to vascular compression rather than infiltration (Figure 1E). He was diagnosed with portal hypertensive biliopathy (PHB) (1).

We investigated the cause of PHB. Careful CT review revealed the portal vein terminating at the hilum without intrahepatic branches (Figure 1A), and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) shows a connection between the inferior vena cava and the tortuous, dilated splenic vein (Figure 1F), both consistent with Abernethy malformation, a type of congenital extrahepatic portosystemic shunt in which portal blood is diverted directly into systemic veins, bypassing the liver (2,3). Although the patient had concomitant cirrhosis—which can induce collateral circulation—the specific connections between the inferior vena cava and collateral branches of the splenic vein seen on imaging are not typical of cirrhotic collaterals, indicating that the shunting arose from the Abernethy anomaly. This congenital anomaly caused localized portal hypertension at the hilum, leading to PHB mimicking a mass. After biliary stent placement via ERCP, bilirubin levels improved to near normal.

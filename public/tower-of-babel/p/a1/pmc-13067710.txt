Letter to the Editor: Single-cell and multi-omics integrative modeling identifies mitochondrial gene HSPE1 as a therapeutic target in osteosarcoma
Authors: Xueyang Li, Yiheng Liu, Hongtao He
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We are writing to express our high regard for the recent article by Pan et al., titled “Single-cell and multi-omics integrative modeling identifies mitochondrial gene HSPE1 as a therapeutic target in osteosarcoma,” published in the Journal of Translational Medicine [1]. This study, by integrating multi-omics and single-cell data, identifies the mitochondrial gene HSPE1 as a therapeutic target for osteosarcoma. The authors constructed a machine learning-based mitochondrial prognostic score (MIscore) by combining ten machine learning algorithms into 117 modeling strategies and provided in vitro and in vivo experimental evidence for the role of HSPE1 in tumor progression and immunosuppression. This research deepens our understanding of mitochondrial metabolism in osteosarcoma and reveals a novel biomarker with translational potential.

The primary strength of this study lies in its innovative combinatorial modeling strategy and systematic functional validation of HSPE1. Although the authors provided a multi-layered perspective on osteosarcoma biology by integrating bulk transcriptomics, single-cell RNA sequencing, and pharmacological datasets, to further advance its clinical translation and deepen mechanistic understanding, we propose the following points for further consideration:

First, there are limitations regarding sample size and validation cohorts. This study primarily relies on retrospective public datasets, with single-cell data sourced from only six patients, representing low heterogeneity. Furthermore, the lack of prospective clinical validation or independent external cohorts may limit the generalizability of the MIscore. Future validation in larger-scale, prospective multi-center cohorts would help enhance its clinical applicability.

Second, the HSPE1 interaction network requires deeper elucidation. Although the authors used bioinformatic inference to link HSPE1 with the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway and MYC target gene enrichment, direct experimental evidence is lacking. It remains unclear whether HSPE1 interacts with other mitochondrial genes or signaling proteins and how it regulates the PI3K-AKT-MYC axis at the transcriptional or post-translational level [2]. Co-immunoprecipitation, chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq), or pathway rescue experiments could help reveal the specific molecular mechanisms by which HSPE1 functions as an upstream regulatory node [3].

Furthermore, the HSPE1-mediated immune regulatory mechanisms warrant further exploration. The reduction of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) in high-MIscore tumors is noteworthy, but the study did not examine whether HSPE1 directly affects T-cell function, chemokine secretion, or immune checkpoint expression (e.g., PD-L1, LAG-3) [4]. Flow cytometry, cytokine profiling, and multiplex immunohistochemistry could more clearly delineate the role of HSPE1 in immune evasion [5].

Last but not least, clinical translation and therapeutic development were not fully explored. Although molecular docking suggested that HSPE1 may bind to various compounds (e.g., MK-1775, LGX818), functional drug screening or drug repurposing studies were not conducted. It is also noteworthy that the in vivo models were limited to subcutaneous xenografts, without assessment of long-term toxicity, metastatic potential, or multi-organ effects, all of which are crucial considerations in preclinical drug development.

We commend the authors for conducting a meticulous and innovative study, leveraging the influential platform of the Journal of Translational Medicine to advance the field of mitochondrial biology in osteosarcoma. We look forward to future research building upon these findings to further address the aforementioned questions. This will help bridge the gap between bioinformatics analysis, basic experimental validation, and clinical diagnosis and therapy, ultimately benefiting osteosarcoma patients.

Sincerely.

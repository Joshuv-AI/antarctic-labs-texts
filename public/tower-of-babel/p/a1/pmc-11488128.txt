Acupoint catgut embedding advantage in treating gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (ACE-GERD): study protocol for a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Shuting Wen, Xiying Zhao, Xiaofeng Lin, Zhaoli Fu, Shumin Qin, Qimou Pan, Fengbin Liu, Wenfang He, Tianwen Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GERD) is a highly prevalent disorder worldwide and developing effective treatment strategies for GERD is a clinical priority. GERD is associated with anxiety and depression. Several approaches have been developed to improve GERD, although effectiveness is limited. Acupoint catgut embedding (ACE) is an established technique in traditional Chinese medicine for the treatment of anxiety and depression. This study will investigate the effects of ACE on anxiety, depression, acid reflux and heartburn in patients with GERD.MethodsThe ACE-GERD trial is a single-centre, prospective randomised controlled trial. 62 patients will be randomly assigned equally to the experimental and control groups. Patients will be treated with ACE or sham ACE. In the experimental group, absorbable polyglycolic acid sutures will be implanted at the acupoints using sterile disposable injection needles. The sham ACE treatment will exhibit similar surface characteristics but lack absorbable polyglycolic acid sutures. Treatments will be administered every 2 weeks for a period of 10 weeks. The main outcome measure is the Reflux Disease Questionnaire symptom score. Secondary outcomes are the endoscopic assessment, 24-hour pH/impedance monitoring test, oesophageal high-resolution manometer, Gastro-oesophageal Reflux Disease Questionnaire score, Gastro-oesophageal Reflux Disease Health-related Quality of Life, Self-rating Anxiety Scale and Self-rating Depression Scale scores.DiscussionThe ACE-GERD trial aims to evaluate the efficacy of ACE treatment as a therapeutic tool for improving anxiety, depression, acid reflux and heartburn in patients with GERD and to provide the evidence base for future clinical studies.Ethics and DisseminationThe trial has been approved by the Ethics Committee of Guangdong Provincial Hospital of Chinese Medicine (ethics no. BF2023-113-01). Results will be published in peer-reviewed journals and presented at scientific conferences and meetings.Trial registration numberChiCTR2300074643.

Introduction

In gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GERD), gastroduodenal contents reflux into the oesophagus, causing symptoms such as acid reflux, heartburn, regurgitation and post-sternal pain and chest pain.1 Based on endoscopic standards, GERD can be divided into three clinical types: non-erosive GERD, reflux oesophagitis and Barrett’s oesophagitis.2 GERD has an estimated worldwide prevalence of 8–33%3 and poses a significant economic burden on society.4 5 Therefore, the exploration of effective treatment strategies for GERD is important from the clinical perspective.

The overall goals of therapy for GERD are to improve patient quality of life, eliminate symptoms, prevent complications and maintain remission.6 Currently, proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) are the first-line treatment for GERD.7 PPIs are often used in combination with gastrointestinal prokinetics, antidepressants and anxiolytics. However, up to 50% of patients with PPI develop resistance to treatment.8 Furthermore, long-term use of PPIs may lead to complications,9 such as fundic gland polyps,10 atrophic gastritis,11 intestinal flora imbalance12 and cardiovascular events.13 PPIs have also been reported to be associated with increased intestinal infections, such as Clostridium difficile infection,14 gastric cancer,15 fractures,16 chronic kidney disease17 and diabetes,18 resulting in additional burdens on patients with GERD.

The prevalence of the combination of GERD with anxiety and depression has increased over time. Studies have demonstrated that GERD increases the risk of developing anxiety and depression.19 Anxiety and depression are also positively correlated with the development of GERD symptoms, such as heartburn and acid regurgitation.20 21 Psychological disorders, including anxiety and depression, can induce over-reaction of the visceral neural pathways, increase oesophageal hypersensitivity, increase acid perfusion of the oesophagus and produce heartburn symptoms.22 Patients with GERD are more likely to experience heartburn and retrosternal pain if they suffer from anxiety.21 Therefore, more effective methods are needed to target the psychological disorders and relieve the symptoms of patients with GERD.

Acupuncture is one of the most widely used complementary therapies and has become increasingly popular worldwide.23 Acupuncture is also used to treat anxiety, depression and stress-related problems.24 Acupoint catgut embedding (ACE) is a specific acupuncture technology, involving the use of an acupoint stimulation therapy with polyglycolic acid absorbable sutures implanted into the acupoint. ACE achieves greater therapeutic effects compared with acupuncture alone.25 Previous research has revealed that ACE has a better effect on improving mental and psychological symptoms.26 Furthermore, ACE has been shown to control reflux, reducing recurrence rate and improving quality of life.27 These findings emphasise the importance of this approach for improving GERD.

However, there are limited systematic evaluations or studies on the efficacy and safety of ACE in the treatment of patients with GERD.27 In view of the above evidence, we designed a randomised controlled trial (RCT) called ACE-GERD, to assess the clinical efficacy of ACE for the treatment of anxiety and depression in patients with GERD and improvement of heartburn, acid reflux and chest pain. This article follows the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials reporting checklist (online supplemental table 1).

Methods

Study design

This study is a prospective, single-blind (patient), single-centre RCT. The study will be conducted between May 2023 and September 2025. The proposed study subjects are patients with a chief complaint of gastro-oesophageal reflux referred to the Department of Gastroenterology of Guangdong Provincial Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine, China. Ethics statement

The study protocol (registration number: ChiCTR2300074643; registration date: 11 August 2023) was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Guangdong Provincial Hospital of Chinese Medicine (Ethics No BF2023-113-01). All patients are required to sign an informed consent form prior to enrolment.

Sample size

The sample size was calculated using the PASS V.11 software. A previous study showed that the total efficacy rate was 90.00% and 53.33% in the ACE group and control group, respectively.27 We set the two-sided type I error at 5% and the statistical power at 90%. The results indicated that a sample size of 30 patients per group was required. Assuming a 10% dropout rate, we plan to enrol 33 patients in each group. Study population

Diagnostic criteria

GERD will be diagnosed according to the ‘ACG clinical guideline for the diagnosis and management of gastroesophageal reflux disease (2021)’.6

Inclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria for the study are as follows: (1) a diagnosis consistent with GERD and a duration of symptoms greater than 4 weeks; (2) no use of medication known to adjust gastrointestinal function for at least 2 weeks prior to the study; (3) ages 18–75 years; and (4) signed consent form by the participant or a legal representative of the participant if the participant was unable to provide consent.

Exclusion criteria

The following exclusion criteria applied: (1) pregnancy or lactation; (2) comorbid conditions, such as a history of gastrointestinal surgery, gastrointestinal bleeding, severe heart and cerebrovascular diseases, liver and kidney insufficiency, cachexia or tumour; (3) coagulopathy; (4) spinal disorders; (5) allergy to absorbable sutures; and (6) suffered from scar constitution.

Dropout criteria

Dropout criteria are as follows: (1) serious adverse reactions or complications during the clinical trial; (2) lack of efficacy or disease progression; (3) loss to follow-up; and (4) insufficient treatment.

Randomisation and allocation concealment

The SPSS software package (V.26.0; SPSS) will be used to generate random numbers and grouping schemes. The group-concealment scheme consists of placing each grouping scheme in an opaque envelope, writing a code outside, sealing the envelope and giving it to the researcher. The envelope will be opened once the participants have completed the baseline assessments and signed the informed consent forms. After being assigned to either of the control or experimental group, the participants will undergo the corresponding intervention.

Blinding

Study participants will be blinded to their group allocations in this single-blind study, whereas the researchers and acupuncturists will be aware of group allocation. To minimise bias, outcome assessors will be blinded throughout the study.

Interventions

Both the experimental and control groups will receive health education interventions. The participants will be requested to refrain from smoking and alcohol consumption during the study period. The participants will be instructed to avoid cold or greasy foods, coffee, tea, artificial sweeteners, chocolate and spiciness. They will also be instructed to eat on time, avoid overeating, stop eating 3 hours before going to bed and raise their pillows when sleeping at night. Participants will also be asked to maintain a pleasant mood and exercise properly.

Control group

In addition to basic care, patients in the control group will be treated with a sham ACE treatment using the same sterile disposable injection needles without polyglycolic acid absorbable sutures. Other manipulations, positions and treatment frequencies will be the same as those used in the experimental group. Therapists will undergo standardised training to ensure treatment homogeneity.

Outcome measures

Major outcome measures

Clinical effects will be assessed using the Reflux Disease Questionnaire (RDQ) symptom score (online supplemental table 2). The RDQ scores will be assessed every 2 weeks during treatment and every 2 weeks during follow-up. Efficacy will be evaluated based on the reduction in the RDQ score using the following equation: efficacy index (EI)=([RDQ scores before treatment−RDQ scores after treatment]/RDQ scores before treatment)×100%. Clinical efficacy is defined as cured (EI ≥90%), excellent (EI <90% and ≥60%), effective (EI <60% and ≥30%) and invalid (EI <30%). It will be performed before treatment, at 4 and 10 weeks following the commencement of treatment and 2 weeks after treatment is completed.

Basic characteristic variables

Demographics such as age, sex, height, body mass and body mass index were obtained. Vital signs including temperature, blood pressure, heart rate and breathing will also be measured. Data on patient medical history, family history, risk factors and dietary history will be collected.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses will be performed using Stata SE V.15 software. Metrological data that fit the normal distribution will be expressed as mean±SD, and variables that do not fit the normal distribution will be expressed as median (25%, 75%). An independent samples t-test will be used when the data from both groups are normally distributed and show homogeneity of variance. Welch’s t-test will be used to compare two groups with a normal distribution and unequal variance. The Mann-Whitney U rank-sum test will be performed on data that do not meet the normal distribution criteria. Counting data will be tested using the χ2 test or non-parametric testing. Statistical significance will be set at a two-sided p value of 0.05.

Quality control

All researchers will undergo standardised training before the start of the study, which will include diagnosis, inclusion and exclusion criteria, location of the acupoints, ACE manipulation techniques, data collection, efficacy evaluations and adverse event assessing and recording.

Adverse event recording and handling

Patients will be monitored for adverse reactions, including bleeding, haematoma, syncope, severe pain and local infection. Throughout the trial, symptoms, severity, time of occurrence, duration, treatment measures and outcomes will be recorded. Physicians can decide whether to suspend treatment according to the severity of adverse events.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public were not involved in the design of this trial.

Discussion

GERD is one of the most common gastrointestinal diseases worldwide and its incidence is increasing annually. Regurgitation and heartburn are the typical symptoms of GERD. GERD can also cause damage to the adjacent tissues of the oesophagus, such as the throat and airway, and related extra-oesophageal symptoms, such as dysphagia, persistent dry cough, sore throat and hoarseness, which severely affect patient quality of life. Several complications are associated with GERD, including erosive oesophagitis, oesophageal strictures, oesophageal ulcer, Barrett’s oesophagus and oesophageal cancer.35 GERD affects the mental and physical health of patients and imposes a considerable burden on patients, their families and society.

According to current guidelines,6 the main purpose of GERD treatment is to restore oesophageal motility and rebuild the antireflux mechanism to relieve clinical symptoms and reduce recurrence and complications. Treatment, including maintenance of a healthy diet, psychotherapy, pharmacological treatments, acupuncture, endoscopic treatment and laparoscopic antireflux surgery, should be individualised according to each patient’s symptoms. This treatment plan constitutes a relatively complete antireflux therapy.

Symptoms of GERD are associated with visceral hypersensitivity (VH).36 VH is a phenomenon in which the viscera experience discomfort when facing normal physiological stimulation or respond more strongly to noxious stimuli. VH in patients manifests as enhanced oesophageal acid sensitivity, also known as oesophageal hypersensitivity. VH is regulated by the autonomic and endocrine nerves, with complex and diverse clinical manifestations. Anxiety and depression can, in turn, aggravate oesophageal hypersensitivity.37

GERD is associated with mood disorders, such as anxiety and depression due to psychosocial stress, comorbid VH or comorbid functional oesophageal disorders, which exacerbate oesophageal hyperalgesia through both peripheral and central mechanisms. Evidence indicates that compared with healthy individuals, patients with GERD have increased sensitivity to oesophageal acid perfusion, and at the same time have increased sensitivity to oesophageal balloon dilatation.38 Studies have shown that VH under the influence of psychiatric factors is a major factor in the recurrence, persistence and development of refractory GERD.39

In addition to regulating the nervous-endocrine-immune system,40 acupuncture enhances gastrointestinal motility, inhibits gastric acid secretion and improves Lower esophageal sphincter (LES) relaxation. In addition, acupuncture exerts its therapeutic effects by protecting the gastric mucosa and reducing depression and anxiety. Acupuncture has been shown to regulate gastrin and motilin,41 which enhance gastro-oesophageal reflux barrier function. It can also improve gastrointestinal motility by regulating vasoactive intestinal peptide levels.

By providing persistent stimulation to the acupoints, ACE extends the sensation and effect of traditional acupuncture. ACE has long-lasting efficacy and can improve VH in multiple systems. Owing to its high safety, significant efficacy and few side effects, ACE has become an established treatment for digestive diseases in traditional Chinese medicine.42 ACE regulates or enhances gastric acid secretion43 and promotes mucosal healing.44 ACE can enhance immune function, improve VH and stimulate the production of antibodies and various lymphokines, thereby treating GERD.

We designed the ACE-GERD RCT to examine whether ACE treatment improves mood and decreases anxiety, relieving symptoms such as heartburn and regurgitation in patients with GERD. The main limitation of this study is its single-blind design. Another potential limitation of this study is the possibility of patient attrition due to the requirement of undergoing treatment over a period of 2 weeks, totalling six times. The strengths of this study were the use of a randomised controlled design, and all patients were completely followed up. Meanwhile, both subjective and objective data were collected in this study to examine the effectiveness of ACE in treating GERD. Another advantage is that it focuses on upper gastrointestinal symptoms, and with anxiety and depression. The results of this study could improve the treatment outcomes of patients with GERD. We consider that this research will provide important insights into the efficacy of ACE treatment for GERD and generate robust data for the foundation of future research in the field.

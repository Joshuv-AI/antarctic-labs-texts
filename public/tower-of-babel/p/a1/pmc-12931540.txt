Optimising electroacupuncture parameters for post-stroke hand dysfunction: protocol for a multi-arm randomised controlled trial using orthogonal design
Authors: Aabha Sharma, Lin Han, Huimei Deng, Weixiang Sun, Feike Wang, Chunchang Zhang, Wenyu Zhang, Yang Zhang, Lanyuan Li, Haolong Guo, Yuan Meng, Youyi Chen, Jie Hou, Jia He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHand dysfunction following stroke, especially during the flaccid paralysis phase, significantly impairs patients’ motor abilities and daily functioning. Electroacupuncture (EA) is widely used in post-stroke rehabilitation; however, inconsistent clinical outcomes and lack of standardised treatment parameters have limited its broader adoption.This protocol describes a randomised controlled trial designed to determine optimal EA parameters for post-stroke hand dysfunction using an orthogonal experimental design.Methods/designThis protocol presents a single-centre, randomised controlled trial design with 10 arms. A total of 110 patients with post-stroke hand dysfunction will be randomly assigned to nine electroacupuncture groups or one sham acupuncture group in equal proportions. Participants will receive 12 treatment sessions over 2 weeks. The EA groups are designed based on a four-factor, three-level orthogonal design to systematically evaluate the main effects of acupoint selection, stimulation frequency, needle thickness and treatment duration. The primary outcome is the effective response rate, defined as reduction in the Chinese Stroke Scale (CSS) score at 2 weeks. Secondary outcomes include assessments with the Modified Lindmark Rating Scale, range of motion measures, Modified Barthel Index and hand motor subscores of the CSS. As this is a trial protocol, results are not yet available. Statistical analyses will be conducted after completion of recruitment and follow-up according to the prespecified analysis plan. Safety and adverse events will be monitored throughout the study.ConclusionThis trial is designed to address the current lack of evidence-based standardisation of EA parameters for post-stroke hand dysfunction. By systematically evaluating key treatment components using an orthogonal experimental design, the study aims to identify optimal EA strategies and provide a methodological framework to improve consistency, reproducibility and clinical effectiveness in post-stroke hand rehabilitation.Ethics and disseminationThis manuscript describes a study protocol and does not report any data from participants at this stage. Ethical approval for the planned trial was obtained from the Medical Ethics Committee of the First Teaching Hospital of Tianjin University of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Approval No. TYLL2024(K)072). Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to enrolment. The results of the study will be disseminated through peer-reviewed journals and academic conferences.Trial registration numberITMCTR2024000819.Keywords: Stroke, Randomized Controlled Trial, Acupuncture, Clinical Trial, NEUROLOGY

Background

The incidence and disability rate of stroke have been increasing year by year.1 It is the leading cause of motor dysfunction in adults worldwide.2 Stroke survivors often experience multiple functional complications.3 Data have shown that 38% of patients with stroke have significant hand dysfunction 3 months after onset, and 30%–66% still do not fully recover hand function after 6 months.4 5 Hand movement and fine motor skills are crucial for daily activities, making early restoration of hand function essential for improving overall quality of life and social participation. Acupuncture is widely recognised as an effective non-drug therapy for supporting recovery after a stroke.6 Systematic reviews and meta-analyses provide strong evidence for its adjunctive benefits in improving daily living abilities, limb motor function and reducing post-stroke pain.7 Electroacupuncture (EA), which combines traditional needling with electrical stimulation, has demonstrated greater improvements compared with manual acupuncture alone.8 9 It enhances motor recovery (Fugl-Meyer scores), activities of daily living (Barthel Index) and reduces hemiplegic shoulder pain, especially when delivered at optimal frequencies such as 2 Hz and 100 Hz.8 10 Mechanistically, EA enhances neuroplasticity11 and provides targeted neuromuscular stimulation.7 Given that stimulation parameters critically influence EA’s safety and efficacy,12 optimising these factors (including frequency, duration and intensity) represents a crucial direction for advancing evidence-based protocols. Along with stroke rehabilitation, acupuncture has been researched in various disorders with poor motor control of their hands, including focal hand dystonia and even in people who had to exercise a lot of fine motor control. The same results also suggest the neuromodulatory capacity and good safety profile of acupuncture, especially in comparison with botulinum toxin, which is typically used in the treatment of spasticity and which might potentially worsen weakness or flaccidity. But the current research work specifically focused on optimisation of EA parameters on hand dysfunction in the flaccid period of paralysis following stroke.

Despite promising findings, acupuncture’s efficacy in stroke rehabilitation remains debated due to inconsistent trial results.13 14 These discrepancies often stem from methodological variability, including non-standardised protocols, inadequate treatment durations and heterogeneous patient populations with differing stroke severity and subtypes. To address these gaps, our study employs a novel orthogonal design to systematically evaluate multiple EA parameters, acupoint selection, stimulation frequency, needle type and treatment duration and their combined effects.15 By integrating traditional Chinese medicine principles with rigorous trial methodology, we aim to clarify EA’s role in stroke recovery. Methods/design

Study design

This 10-arm, randomised controlled trial will take place at the ‘First Teaching Hospital of Tianjin University of Traditional Chinese Medicine’. The study will be conducted from 1 January 2025 to 31 December 2026, covering participant recruitment, intervention delivery, follow-up assessments and data analysis. Nine experimental groups receive EA based on a four-factor, three-level orthogonal design (L93⁴). One control group receives SA in addition to routine stroke rehabilitation. Routine rehabilitation will be provided uniformly to all participants in both the EA and SA groups. The four experimental factors include acupoint combination (Factor A), EA frequency (Factor B), stimulation duration (Factor C) and needle thickness (Factor D). Each factor is defined at three levels (Levels 1–3). Each EA group represents a unique combination of factor levels generated by the L₉ orthogonal design. Intervention codes (eg, A1B2C2D2) denote the specific parameter settings applied. As an orthogonal experimental design, this study estimates the primary effects of each EA parameter. Consequently, any identified optimal parameter combination signifies the most effective levels derived from main-effect analysis rather than an evaluation of interaction effects among factors. Participants are enrolled via multiple channels, including hospital outpatient and inpatient announcements, social media (WeChat), community service centre publicity and screening from existing clinical databases from the hospital. Once consent is obtained, all eligible participants are randomly assigned to a group. Table 2 shows a detailed description of enrolment, interventions and assessments.

Sample size calculation

Using an orthogonal L9(3⁴) design avoids the 81 combinations required by a full factorial design with four factors at three levels each. This approach reduces the number of experimental conditions to nine while preserving balanced estimation of main effects.15 In this design, each level of each factor is represented equally over the nine experimental groups. Data can be pooled across groups to estimate primary effect. With 11 participants assigned to each orthogonal group, each factor level is represented by 33 participants (3×11). This sample size is sufficient to detect moderate main effects using analysis of variance under conventional assumptions (two‐sided α=0.05, power=80%), corresponding to an expected effect size of approximately Cohen’s f ≈ 0.32.17 In addition, one sham control group (n=11) is included to allow exploratory assessment of the overall effect of EA in comparison to non-specific effects. Since the sham group sample size is small, comparisons between EA and SA are considered exploratory. They are not intended to support definitive efficacy conclusions. Considering a projected drop-out rate of 20%, the total planned sample size is 110 participants comprising 99 participants in the orthogonal EA groups and 11 participants in the sham group. This sample size is feasible and sufficient for identifying the optimal parameters of EA and evaluating overall treatment effect.

Randomisation and allocation concealment

Patients were randomly divided into 10 groups (9 EA, 1 SA) in equal ratios using blocked randomisation in SPSS (IBM, Armonk, New York, USA) by an independent statistician. Each group had 11 patients (n=110). A separate administrator managed the sequence to ensure concealment. Assignments were revealed only after enrolment via a secure system. Acupuncturists and evaluators were blinded until treatment.

Blinding

Due to the nature of the intervention, acupuncturists will not be blinded. All participants are blinded and told that they will undergo EA to treat their post-stroke hand disability without being told that they are in a group. The participants under the SA will also be informed that they will be provided with EA and that they will not be aware that the electrical stimulation is not active. Sham treatments will be performed visually, as well as procedurally, the same as EA, which includes needle insertion and connection to an EA machine, except that the EA machine will not produce electricity. All the patients will not be informed about differences between stimulation frequency, duration and thickness of the needles in the EA groups. The statisticians and outcome assessors will be blinded to group assignments to limit bias during the study. Unblinding will only occur if required for patient safety or clinical management and will be documented per-protocol.

Interventions

Participants will receive the EA treatment once daily, 6 days a week, for a total of 12 sessions over 2 weeks. The treatment targets only the affected hand and is administered by licensed acupuncturists with ≥5 years of experience. Single-use, sterile Hwato needles (40 mm length; 0.25–0.35 mm diameter) are used. The 40 mm needle length will be chosen to ensure sufficient insertion depth for all target acupoints as listed in table 3. This length allows effective electrical stimulation and consistent elicitation of de qi, while still allowing adjustment for individual anatomical differences. EA is the primary intervention under investigation and is evaluated as an add-on therapy within a comprehensive rehabilitation programme. All patients will continue standard secondary prevention therapies per guidelines,18 including antiplatelet agents, neuronutrition, cerebral circulation/metabolism, lipid-lowering, plaque stabilisation and blood pressure/glucose control. In addition, routine rehabilitation therapies, including limb positioning, Xingnao Kaiqiao acupuncture, wet compress, warm moxibustion, cupping and microwave therapy, will be provided as part of standard care. These rehabilitation treatments are not considered co-interventions, as they are part of routine care and are provided consistently to all participants. The Xingnao Kaiqiao method aims to stimulate motor and sensory pathways, improve limb function and enhance cerebral circulation.19

Electroacupuncture group

The EA intervention followed the Xingnao Kaiqiao method, commonly used in post-stroke rehabilitation.9 20 99 patients will be randomised into 9 groups (11 per group) based on an L9 (3⁴) orthogonal design, each receiving unique combinations of EA parameters. All groups are treated at Baxie (EX-UE9) eight points on the dorsum of hand between finger webs plus one compatible point (Hegu (LI4), Quchi (LI11) or Neiguan (PC6). Single-use sterile needles will be inserted at standardised depths (Baxie 1.5–2.5 cm; Quchi 2.5–4 cm; Hegu/Neiguan 1.5–2.5 cm) and manipulated for ≥10 s to elicit de qi. The orthogonal design is used to divide the electrical stimulation settings; each group of interventions received a specific set of stimulation frequency, treatment duration and needle thickness (table 2). Electrical stimulation using a continuous waveform (2, 50 or 100 Hz) will be delivered for 20, 30 or 40 min per session, adjusted to patient tolerance, once daily, 6 days a week, for 2 weeks. The selection of the four factors, each with three levels, was based on data-mining analyses performed in our preliminary research. Details of these analyses are provided in online supplemental file 1a (full version in Chinese) and 1b (summary in English). These analyses identified four parameters with the greatest influence on outcomes, and three levels per factor are chosen to represent the observed range of variability while maintaining experimental feasibility. Acupoints followed WHO and National Standards (GB/T 12 346–2006)21 22; details are provided in table 4 and figure 2.

Outcome measures

Primary outcome

The primary outcome will be the ERR evaluated using the CSS. ERR is defined as the proportion of patients who achieve clinically meaningful neurological improvement based on the percentage reduction in CSS scores from baseline to the end of the 2-week intervention period. The percentage neurological improvement is calculated according to the nimodipine method, a standardised clinical efficacy evaluation method widely used in Chinese stroke research:

According to the predetermined levels of nimodipine methodology used in Chinese clinical assessment criteria and common in stroke randomised controlled trials, patients are divided into four groups: basic recovery (≥90% reduction), significant progress (≥46% to <90% reduction), progress (≥18% to <46% reduction) and invalid response (<18% reduction or score increase). These cut-off points are intended to distinguish between significant neurological recovery and minor or clinically insignificant changes in scores and have been identified as clinically relevant in previous Chinese stroke trials. The ERR includes all patients classified as basic recovery, significant progress or progress, excluding those with invalid responses. CSS assessments are conducted at baseline and at week 2 after treatment.

Secondary outcomes

Secondary outcomes include motor function, daily activity ability, joint mobility and treatment safety. Motor and sensory function of the hand and fingers will be assessed using the Modified Lindmark Rating Scale, covering 12 items such as flexion/extension, thumb–index opposition, grasp patterns, light touch, joint position sense and finger joint pain, scored 0–3 per item with a maximum of 34.23 Hand motor impairment will be measured by the CSS subitem (0–6), where lower scores indicate better function; changes from baseline to week 2 reflect improvement.24 Joint mobility will be evaluated via goniometric measurement of wrist and finger ROM at baseline and week 2, including proximal and distal interphalangeal joints and wrist flexion/extension.25 Daily activity ability will be assessed with the Modified Barthel Index (0–100), measuring independence in tasks such as feeding, bathing, grooming, dressing, toileting, transfers, mobility and stair climbing, with higher scores indicating greater independence.26

Follow-up assessments

Along with the baseline (week 0) and immediate post-treatment (week 2) assessment, there will be a follow-up examination at 3 months post-intervention related to the sustainability of treatment effects.

Patient and public involvement

The design, conduct and reporting of this trial did not involve patients. Their eligibility to complete the treatment will be assessed based on time availability and mobility limitations. Trial findings will be relayed to registered patients by email on completion. No burden assessment will be conducted.

Adverse events monitoring

Any adverse events that occur during the trial will be recorded using a standardised questionnaire to ensure consistent documentation. In 24 hours following any event, acupuncturists and relevant clinical specialists will evaluate and classify the event as either related or unrelated to the treatment, based on its potential association with needling. Typical treatment-related adverse events may include subcutaneous bruising, prolonged pain after needling, localised itching at needle insertion sites, dizziness and similar symptoms.

Data management

The paper case report forms (CRFs) will be entered on electronic spreadsheets through a double-entry process to reduce errors. Data quality control will involve two researchers independently analysing and cross-checking the data. After integration and verification, the consolidated data will be finalised and included in the official research dataset to ensure authenticity and validity. To maintain data integrity, paper CRFs will be stored securely with restricted access and electronic data will be password-protected and regularly backed up. Participant confidentiality will be strictly maintained by excluding personal identifiers from the analysis dataset. Any paper or electronic hard copy of the studies used will be maintained for 5 years past the publication date. The questions about the data may be addressed to the corresponding author.

Data monitoring and oversight

A formal independent Data Monitoring Committee is not involved in this study due to the low-risk nature of the non-pharmacological intervention of EA, the treatment and following treatment and short treatment period and no planned interim analysis or formal stopping rule. The research team conducts oversight with supervision of the principal investigator who will not take direct part in the delivery of treatment or the outcome measure. Regular monthly meetings on the progress of the study and safety are reviewed, and, since it is a single centre study, continued supervision is ensured via constant communication between the research team. Participant safety is observed throughout the trial, with all serious adverse events and any other situation that may put the participants at risk being reported to the Medical Ethics Committee.

Quality control

Experts from the fields of acupuncture and clinical research will examine the study protocol and suggest refinements. A detailed standard operating procedure will guide all aspects of the study, including patient screening, EA treatment, documentation and outcome assessment. The study team will be trained on accurate data recording and protocol adherence. All CRFs will be checked regularly for completeness and consistency by the trial coordinator. On-site monitoring will be conducted at regular intervals to review CRFs, confirm source data and ensure compliance with the study protocol. Any data corrections or modifications will be documented clearly and justified on the original CRFs, maintaining full traceability.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses will be performed using SPSS V.18.0 (IBM, Armonk, New York, USA), with statistical significance set at a two-sided p<0.05. Analyses will follow the intention-to-treat principle, with participants analysed according to their original group assignments and a secondary per-protocol analysis will be conducted excluding participants with major protocol deviations. Continuous variables will be tested for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test and for homogeneity of variance using Levene’s test. Normally distributed data will be presented as mean±SD and analysed using t-tests or analysis of variance (ANOVA), as appropriate, while non-normally distributed data will be summarised as median (IQR, P25–P75) and analysed using the Mann-Whitney U test or Kruskal-Wallis H test. Categorical variables will be expressed as counts and percentages and compared using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test. The primary outcome, ERR, will be compared across the 10 study arms using the Kruskal-Wallis H test, with pairwise comparisons between the SA group and each EA group conducted using Dunn’s test with Bonferroni adjusted p values. Secondary outcomes, including the Modified Lindmark Rating Scale, CSS hand motor subscores, ROM and Modified Barthel Index, will be analysed using mixed-effects models with group, time (baseline, week 2 and 3-month follow-up) and their interaction as fixed effects and participant as a random effect; where assumptions are met, repeated-measures ANOVA will be used as a supplementary analysis. Missing data exceeding 5% will be handled using multiple imputation with sensitivity analyses to assess robustness, and adverse events will be summarised descriptively and compared between groups using χ² or Fisher’s exact tests.

Data from the orthogonal experimental design (four factors at three levels) will be analysed using Orthogonal Design Assistant V.3.1. Range analysis will first be performed to evaluate the relative importance of each factor (acupoint selection, stimulation frequency, needle thickness and treatment duration) and to identify optimal factor-level combinations. For each factor at each level, K values (sum of outcomes) and K̄ values (mean outcomes) will be calculated based on continuous outcome measures and the range value (R), defined as the difference between the maximum and minimum K̄ values, will be used to estimate the magnitude of each factor’s effect, with larger R values indicating greater influence. Subsequently, ANOVA will be conducted to assess the statistical significance of main factor effects by calculating the total sum of squares, factor-specific sums of squares and error sum of squares, from which mean squares, F statistics and corresponding p values will be derived; factors with p≤0.05 will be considered statistically significant. Only main effects will be evaluated; interaction effects will not be assessed, as the orthogonal design was not powered to detect higher-order interactions. For factors with three or more levels showing significant effects, post hoc pairwise comparisons will be performed, with Bonferroni correction applied within each factor to control for type I error. All statistical procedures will be prespecified in a detailed statistical analysis plan finalised prior to database lock and unblinding, and analysis outputs will be archived to ensure reproducibility.

Discussion

Post-stroke hemiplegia remains one of the leading health challenges in China, placing a heavy burden not only on patients and their families but also on the healthcare system.4 While rehabilitation and pharmacological treatments are the standard of care, their benefits can be modest, with slow progress and potential side effects.27 28 Against this backdrop, EA has emerged as a promising adjunct therapy. Our trial seeks to determine the most effective EA parameters for improving limb function after stroke, using a rigorous randomised controlled design with allocation concealment and triple blinding. Previous clinical trials and systematic reviews report mixed effects of acupuncture and EA in stroke rehabilitation. These findings largely reflect heterogeneity in treatment protocols and limited exploration of stimulation parameters.13 14 Recent meta-analyses suggest that EA can improve upper-limb motor function and activities of daily living, particularly when stimulation frequency and treatment intensity are appropriately selected.7 8 Existing evidence suggests that treatment efficacy may depend not only on EA itself but also on careful parameter optimisation.8 12 To address methodological variability, we employed a four-factor, three-level orthogonal design to efficiently evaluate the main effects of acupoint selection, stimulation frequency, needle thickness and treatment duration. Compared with earlier trials that applied fixed protocols, this approach provides a systematic framework for standardising EA interventions and improving reproducibility.12 15 The use of an SA control without electrical stimulation was intended to isolate the specific contribution of EA while maintaining participant blinding. However, recent evidence indicates that SA may not be physiologically inert, as superficial needling and contextual effects can activate sensory pathways and induce placebo-related neuromodulatory responses.29 30 This residual activity may attenuate observed between-group differences and should be considered when interpreting treatment effects. Despite this limitation, sham-controlled designs remain essential in acupuncture research to reduce performance and expectation bias and to strengthen internal validity.

Several limitations warrant consideration. The intervention period was relatively short, which may limit conclusions regarding long-term effects, despite planned follow-up. The single-centre design may restrict applicability to broader populations and the treatment schedule may pose feasibility challenges in routine clinical practice. Importantly, although the orthogonal design allows efficient estimation of main effects with a reduced sample size, it does not permit formal statistical assessment of interaction effects between factors. Consequently, potential interactions such as whether stimulation frequency modifies the effect of treatment duration or needle thickness cannot be examined. Future multicentre studies using factorial designs or larger samples are needed to explore parameter interactions and validate optimal EA configurations. Despite these limitations, this study provides a structured and reproducible approach to EA parameter optimisation and offers a robust foundation for future hypothesis-driven and personalised rehabilitation research.

Ethics and dissemination

This manuscript describes a study protocol and does not include any identifiable participant data; therefore, a signed BMJ patient consent form was not required. The study involves human participants and was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the First Teaching Hospital of Tianjin University of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Approval No. TYLL2024(K)072). The trial will be conducted in accordance with Chinese national ethical regulations, the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to enrolment, and participant confidentiality will be strictly maintained. The results of the study will be disseminated through peer-reviewed journals and academic conferences, irrespective of study outcomes.

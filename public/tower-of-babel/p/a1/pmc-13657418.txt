Analysis of circulating bone-glucose profile and exerkine irisin across parathormone quartiles in patients with primary hyperparathyroidism
Authors: Ana-Maria Gheorghe, Nina Ionovici, Oana-Claudia Sima, Mihai Costachescu, Adrian Ciuche, Dana Manda, Mara Carsote
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: Parathyroid tumors, mostly single or multiple adenomas, cause a hormonal imbalance via parathormone (PTH) excess, that further increases serum calcium (primary hyperparathyroidism) with both PTH- and calcium-related multi-organ consequences. Testing the bone turnover markers (BTM) profile might capture the essence of associated bone turnover anomalies with practical applications in the clinical setting. Objective: Our objective was to compare the BTM across mineral metabolism panel, glucose profile, as well as circulating irisin levels in patients with primary hyperparathyroidism vs. (tumor-free) controls by refining the analysis based on PTH values quartiles. Patients, Materials and Methods: A prospective study with a single-center design in menopausal adults, who were enrolled between December 2024 and May 2026. Results: 148 enrolled across four PTH quartiles (mean age 63.39 years and menopause length 16.55 years): 27.09±5.56 pg/mL (Q1), 38.33±2.58 pg/mL (Q2), 46.34±2.93 pg/mL (Q3) and 65.78±16.51 pg/mL in Q4 (p<0.001 for each). Glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) was lower (statistically significantly): Q4 of 5.74±0.32% compared to non-Q4 of 5.98±0.67% (p=0.006), as well as triglycerides of 103.39±36.34 vs. 124.41±66.26 mg/dL (p=0.017). Fasting insulin was also different among PTH quartiles, with the highest median value in Q4 of 8.35 (6.47, 11.49) μIU/mL (p=0.027). Median homeostatic model assessment for insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) was higher in Q2 vs. Q1 (p=0.034), Q3 vs. Q1 (p=0.016) and Q4 vs. Q1 (p=0.005). Osteocalcin was statistically significantly different among quartiles (p=0.036). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis of osteocalcin to predict primary hyperparathyroidism revealed area under the curve (AUC) 0.645 (p=0.006). The cut-off osteocalcin with the highest Youden index was for the value of 21.26 ng/mL (sensitivity 63.90%; specificity 63.10%). In Q4, irisin associated a statistically significant and positive correlation with the value of calculated body mass index (BMI) (r=0.361, p=0.042). Conclusions: In this PTH-based quartiles analysis of menopausal females with/without primary hyperparathyroidism, we found a distinct interpretation of the glucose profile and BTM depending on quartiles, while irisin positive corelated with BMI in cased with parathyroid tumors.

Introduction

Parathyroid tumors, mostly single or multiple adenomas, cause a hormonal imbalance via parathormone (PTH) excess, that further increases serum calcium (primary hyperparathyroidism) with both PTH- and calcium-related multi-organ consequences [1, 2, 3]. While the parathyroid tumor size usually is not directly corelated with the degree and the number of organ/system complications, the bone remains a traditional target, especially in populations at high fracture risk (e.g., menopausal women) [4, 5, 6].

Parathyroid tumor-associated PTH release induces a higher bone remodeling and a consequent increased bone resorption. Thus, bone mass [bone mineral density (BMD) at dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) evaluation] and bone quality [trabecular bone score (TBS)] decreases, which causes a higher incidence of fragility fractures [7, 8, 9]. In very severe cases, a massive bone resorption might cause brown tumors and/or osteitis fibrosis cystica, which represent PTH-induced skeleton complications that are extremely rare nowadays. While osteoporosis remains a standalone standard complication in primary hyperparathyroidism, this type of bone consequences is regarded as exceptional due to the fact that early detection allowed a mildly symptomatic (if any symptomatic at all) presentation of these parathyroid tumors across the contemporary medical era [10, 11, 12, 13].

The pivotal role of chronic exposure to high endogenous PTH causes increased bone turnover, especially resorption and, hence, the profile of blood/urinary bone turnover markers (BTM) might capture the essence of this anomaly, even in patients who are accidentally detected with primary hyperparathyroidism, for instance, during routine calcium screening protocols, and even in cases diagnosed with normocalcemic variant of the endocrine condition in relationship with a parathyroid adenoma [14, 15, 16, 17].

Aim

Our objective was to compare the BTM across mineral metabolism panel, glucose profile as well as circulating irisin levels in patients with primary hyperparathyroidism vs. (tumor-free) controls by refining the analysis based on PTH values quartiles.

Patients, Materials and Methods

Study population

Prospective, single-center study, between December 2024 and May 2026 was conducted in patients who were diagnosed with active primary hyperthyroidism vs. controls (females without a parathyroid tumor).

Inclusion criteria: menopause confirmation in active females, aged between 50 and 80 years; signed informed consent by each patient according to the study protocol; primary hyperparathyroidism confirmation according to the endocrine evaluation (study group) in a single tertiary center of endocrinology.

Exclusion criteria: lack of criteria for primary hyperparathyroidism (control group), secretor endocrine neoplasia, any type of suspected/confirmed oncological, bone, renal or metabolic bone disease, type 1 diabetes, history of bariatric surgery, therapy with glucocorticoids, insulin, anti-obesity and oral anti-diabetes medication, and anti-osteoporotic agents, as well as calcium lowering agents (e.g., Cinacalcet), acute or renal failure, osteogenesis imperfecta, osteitis fibrosa cystica, brown tumors, secondary and/or tertiary hyperparathyroidism, a 25-hydroxyvitamin D <15 ng/mL. Of important note, subjects who were confirmed with primary hyperparathyroidism were included only in the absence of parathyroidectomy for the originating parathyroid tumor (regardless they further on became surgery candidates).

Study protocol

The eligible patients who underwent screening for this study were either enrolled in the group with parathyroid tumors, either in control group. Demographic parameters were collected.

Hormonal evaluation for the mineral metabolism profile confirmed primary hyperparathyroidism based on high total serum calcium and PTH. All enrolled patients were tested in the morning (fasting status).

Hormonal panel also included 25-hydroxyvitamin D and exerkine (myokine) irisin (a muscle-originating hormone), while biochemically, serum phosphorus was assessed, as well. Circulating BTM were of formation [procollagen type 1 N-terminal propeptide (P1NP), total alkaline phosphatase (ALP), osteocalcin] and of resorption (CrossLaps).

Glucose profile analysis included testing fasting glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), insulin, glycemia [with homeostatic model assessment for insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) calculation]. Lipids profile involved triglycerides (TG) and cholesterol assays. Also, fasting uric acid (UA), urea and creatinine were tested.

Central DXA (GE Lunar Prodigy) provided BMD and T-score, and provided standard osteoporosis criteria, as well as TBS [18, 19, 20].

After checking for the inclusion/exclusion criteria, the patients underwent fasting blood testing (mineral metabolism, glucose profile and irisin) according to this study protocol.

Patients were then categorized into four quartile-based groups (Q1–Q4) according to the following PTH cut-off values for subsequent analyses:

▪ Q1 (PTH ≥13.75; ≤34.1 pg/mL);

▪ Q2 (PTH ≥34.2; ≤42.27 pg/mL);

▪ Q3 (PTH ≥42.28; ≤51.91 pg/mL);

▪ Q4 (PTH ≥52.92; ≤119.5 pg/mL).

Of important note, all patients who were prospectively included in this study had a prior endocrine assessment of their serum total calcium and PTH levels according to standard guideline criteria for this endocrine ailment within previous one week to three months (double testing) [21, 22, 23]. Within Q1, Q2 and Q3 quartiles, we included only the patients to whom primary hyperparathyroidism was ruled out (tumor-free controls), while within Q4 quartile only those females who were prior confirmed with primary hyperparathyroidism were included (higher PTH and total serum calcium above upper normal limit). Notably, PTH levels might suffer variations across a timeline perspective of testing its circulating levels, and even in cases with high-normal PTH at the moment of study enrollment, the diagnosis of primary hyperparathyroidism stands on previous (recent) double PTH/calcium assays, as mentioned.

Statistical analysis

Quartile cut-off values (25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles) were estimated using the Weighted Average method in IBM Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) Statistics.

Ethical aspects

The study received hospital and university approval as shown below.

Results

PTH-based quartiles in patients with primary hyperparathyroidism (Q4) vs. tumor-free controls (Q1, Q2, Q3)

One hundred and forty-eight patients were divided into four groups corresponding to PTH quartiles: PTH was statistically significantly different in every between-group comparison, with a mean value of 27.09±5.56 pg/mL in Q1 group, 38.33±2.58 pg/mL in Q2 group, 46.34±2.93 pg/mL in Q3 group and 65.78±16.51 pg/mL in Q4 group (p<0.001 for each).

Age (63.39±8.88 years) was statistically significantly different among groups. HbA1c was lower in Q4 group (5.74±0.32%) compared to non-Q4 group of 5.98±0.67% (p=0.006), as well as TG of 103.39±36.34 mg/dL vs. 124.41±66.26 mg/dL (p=0.017) (Table 1).

DXA sub-analysis

Femoral neck BMD and T-score showed statistically significant different mean values among quartile groups (p=0.025 and p=0.020, respectively), and hip BMD and T-score (p=0.011 and p=0.009, respectively). Discussions

In this study, we enrolled 148 adults (age: 63.39 years, menopause length: 16.55 years), who were then assessed based on PTH quartiles, noting that Q4 quartile (N=36) enrolled females who had a prior confirmation of primary hyperthyroidism without a parathyroid tumor removal at the moment of study enrollment and without medication that potentially interfere with the mineral metabolism such as calcium lower agents or drugs against osteoporosis in order to avoid an iatrogenic bias [24, 25]. In this specific instance, the biological phenotype showed mildly increased PTH levels, including forms with intermittent normocalcemic values. Nowadays, the face of primary hyperparathyroidism has changed into a less severe presentation, associated with an early detection that allows a prompt diagnosis and fewer complications during surveillance [26, 27].

Age was statistically significant different between the four quartiles (p=0.029). Patients within Q4 quartile had a mean age of 64.97±8.13 years, which was statistically significantly higher than the lowest tumor-free Q1 quartile (60.67±8.89 years, p=0.032). In the modern medical era, primary hyperparathyroidism affects more common middle-aged women (in non-genetic forms of primary hyperparathyroidism), and post-menopausal detection is frequently described, for example, during routine (blood) biochemistry tests or during assessments for fracture risk, e.g., after performing central DXA scan [27, 28].

Other parameters were found similar between quartiles, e.g., BMI, renal function, UA, suggesting that might not make a difference in women with parathyroid tumors vs. tumor-free subjects, according to this analysis on point.

However, glucose and lipids profile pointed out statistically significant differences between Q4 and non-Q4 groups, with respect to HbA1c (p=0.006) and TG (p=0.017), respectively, between all quartiles with respect to fasting insulin (p=0.027) and HOMA-IR (p=0.015). Lowest PTH quartile (Q1 group) showed a statistically significant lower insulin (median of 4.94 μIU/mL) vs. Q2 group (median of 6.82 μIU/mL, p=0.034), vs. Q3 group (median of 8.13 μIU/mL, p=0.016), and vs. Q4 group (median of 8.35 μIU/mL, p=0.005), respectively. HOMA-IR in Q1 group was the lowest (median of 1.22) vs. upper PTH quartiles such as Q3 group (median of 2.1, p=0.006) and Q4 group (median of 2.14, p=0.011).

Of note, patients with primary hyperparathyroidism showed a median HOMA-IR suggestive for insulin resistance (a value >2), which had been prior reported as a non-classical complication of tumor-related PTH excess. Currently, it does not represent an indication of parathyroidectomy (as, for instance, the confirmation of osteoporosis/osteoporotic fractures) [27, 28, 29, 30]. However, the levels of HbA1c were found lower in Q4 group (5.74±0.32%) vs. controls (Q1–Q3 groups or non-Q4 group, of 5.98±0.67%, p=0.006), but with intra-normal average values (normal: 4.8% to 5.9%). As mentioned, we ruled adults who were prior diagnosed with type 1 diabetes and even cases with type 2 diabetes that received specific medication (e.g., oral anti-diabetics and/or insulin).

Among the panel of BTM, osteocalcin showed statistically significant differences between Q4 and non-Q4 groups (p=0.004), respectively, Q4 group vs. Q1 group (p=0.022), and Q3 group (p=0.016) and a borderline significance for Q4 group vs. Q2 group (p=0.054). These biomarkers stand at the crossways between glucose and bone metabolism, being influenced by the presence of a PTH-induced abnormal bone metabolism or the co-presence of diabetes [31]. In addition, within Q4 group, ROC curve identified osteocalcin level to predict primary hyperparathyroidism based on AUC of 0.645 (p=0.006). The cut-off value of osteocalcin with the highest Youden index was 21.26 ng/mL, associating a sensitivity of 63.90% and specificity of 63.10%. ROC analysis for the other blood BTM did not show statistically significant results, except for a borderline significance for CrossLaps.

Serum phosphorus was not similar (as opposite to serum calcium, despite being a diagnosis criteria) between quartiles (p=0.012) and presented the lowest value in patients with primary hyperparathyroidism, but with mean levels within intra-normal ranges. This might explain PTH effects to some extent, yet the relatively mild phenotype of the endocrine disease is associated with minimal or intermittent phosphorus anomalies [28, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 35].

Central DXA revealed a statistically significant lower femoral neck BMD in Q4 group vs. highest quartile of controls (p=0.004), respectively for T-score (-1.35) vs. Q3 group (-0.52, p=0.003), and vs. Q2 group (-0.81, p=0.017). Additionally, total hip T-score in primary hyperparathyroidism (Q4 group) was lower (-0.99) vs. Q3 group (-0.04, p=0.003), respectively, Q2 group (-0.11, p=0.006). Paradoxically, for the comparison with Q1 group, the results did not reach a statistical significance, and a bias might come for concomitant factors to contribute to bone mass, such as age, BMI or diabetes. Moreover, the third distal radius site at DXA is particularly affected in patients with PTH producing tumors, and, yet we did not find this impact across our DXA analysis. Most probably, this is related to the fact that we enrolled less severe cases with a recent diagnosis in less symptomatic cases [36, 37, 38].

In this study, an analysis of the circulating myokine irisin was provided across each group, but with similar results. At this point, only a limited number of studies analyzed its profile in relationship with parathyroid tumors, noting that preclinical data are lacking human studies. According to our best knowledge, only a single study specifically addressed irisin in patients with primary hyperparathyroidism, showing an increase of its levels in cases with vitamin D supplementation [39, 40, 41, 42]. Irisin was similar between quartiles regardless the parathyroid condition, but positively corelated with BMI, fasting glucose and insulin, HOMA-IR and HbA1c while specifically in the Q4 quartile, only the correlation with BMI was confirmed (r=0.361, p=0.042), which shows the need of a distinct interpretation in cases with primary hyperparathyroidism.

Limits and further research

Noting this was an exploratory analysis from the perspective of testing irisin, we are aware of some limitations such as the need of a larger sample size and more diverse population (including males and children). Longitudinal data are required in cases with surgery candidates vs. those who were conservatively managed. We did not analyze the post-operatory outcome, neither the potential correlations with the imaging aspects of the parathyroid tumors such as computed tomography, anterior neck ultrasound and 99mTechnetium parathyroid scintigram. A more complex analysis in primary hyperparathyroidism should take into consideration other potential complications such as the cardiovascular panel (arterial hypertension, arrythmia) and kidney stones [42, 43, 44]. The analysis of the glucose profile amid oral glucose tolerance test might improve these data. We assessed total ALP and not the bone-specific sequence, which may influence the results of BTM profile.

Conclusions

In this PTH-based quartiles analysis of menopausal females with/without primary hyperparathyroidism, we found a distinct interpretation of the glucose profile and BTM depending on quartiles, while irisin positive corelated with BMI in cased with parathyroid tumors.

Genomic and pathogenic insights into a new GVI-1 infectious bronchitis virus strain isolated in Northeast China
Authors: Zhe Zeng, Junjie Yang, Haojie Wang, Zichen Wang, Lun Yao, Helong Feng, Chengli Zhou, Ruikai Zhang, Yu Shang, Hongcai Wang, Huabin Shao, Guoyuan Wen, Qingping Luo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractInfectious bronchitis is a severe disease caused by the infectious bronchitis virus (IBV), resulting in significant economic losses to the poultry industry. We isolated an IBV strain from H120-vaccinated layers in Northeast China called CK/CH/LN/2301. Phylogenetic analysis of the S1 gene classified the isolate within the GVI-1 genotype. Further analysis revealed that CK/CH/LN/2301 is a recombinant strain derived from the GVI-1, GI-19, and 4/91 genotypes, with recombination events primarily located in the open reading frame (ORF) 1a region. Experimental infection of 1-day-old specific pathogen-free (SPF) chickens induced clinical signs, including depression and head shaking, with 10% mortality rate. Necropsy revealed characteristic tracheal hemorrhages, and quantitative viral load analysis demonstrated significantly higher viral titers in tracheal tissues than in other organs. Histopathological examination confirmed lesions in the trachea, lungs, kidneys, and duodenum. The neutralization assay showed that CK/CH/LN/2301 exhibited limited cross-neutralization with existing vaccine strains such as H120. Predicted structural comparison of the S protein revealed substantial conformational differences in the hypervariable regions (HVRs) and the S1 subunit between CK/CH/LN/2301 and the vaccine strains, which may contribute to the reduced level of cross-protection provided by current vaccines. Our findings demonstrate that GVI-1 IBV strains are undergoing evolution, highlighting the urgent need for updated vaccine strategies to control emerging variants.Keywords: Infectious bronchitis virus, GVI-1, Recombination, Pathogenicity, Antigenic variation

Introduction

Infectious bronchitis virus (IBV), a member of the genus Gammacoronavirus in the Coronaviridae family, causes an acute respiratory infectious disease that poses a persistent threat to the poultry industry worldwide (Awad, et al., 2014). The pathological characteristics of this disease involve multiple organ system damage mechanisms: (1) respiratory system lesions manifest as ciliary epithelial shedding and excessive mucus secretion, with clinical signs such as rales and expectoration; (2) urate crystal deposition in renal tissues leads to renal dysfunction; and (3) reproductive tract infection results in abnormal follicle development, causing a reduction in egg production performance in layer flocks (Bo, et al., 2022; Hoerr, 2021). More critically, viral infection-induced damage to the mucosal barrier significantly elevates the risk of secondary infections, particularly coinfections with pathogens such as avian influenza virus or Mycoplasma, which markedly increases both mortality and culling rates (Huang, et al., 2024; Yadav, et al., 2023). IBV is highly pathogenic, causing significant economic losses to the poultry industry.

The genome of IBV consists of a single-stranded RNA of approximately 27.6 kilobases encoding the spike glycoprotein (S), a structural protein with important biological functions (Bhuiyan, et al., 2023). The S protein serves as a key antigen mediating host cell invasion, with its S1 domain containing the receptor-binding site that determines tissue tropism, whereas the S2 domain drives the fusion process between the viral envelope and the host cell membrane through conformational changes (Wickramasinghe, et al., 2014). The surface antigenic epitopes of the S protein exhibit high variability, posing a significant challenge for vaccine development.

The first IBV strain was isolated in China in the 1980s. Currently, the major circulating genotypes include the QX type (GI-19), TC07-2 type (GVI-1), 793B type (GI-13), and Taiwan-I type (GI-7) (Zhao, et al., 2023). The GVI-1 strain (TC07-2) was first identified in Guangdong Province, China, in 2007 (Ren, et al., 2019). Recent epidemiological surveillance has shown an increasing detection rate of GVI-1 strains across the country (Wu, et al., 2022; Yang, et al., 2024b). Although vaccines are widely used in China, the low homology between vaccine strains and many newly emerging variants has reduced the effectiveness of vaccine protection. Therefore, continuous surveillance of IBV prevalence is essential for the development of novel vaccines and provides a theoretical basis for formulating control strategies against emerging variants.

An avian IBV was isolated from the laryngeal and tracheal tissues of 37-day-old Hy-Line Brown chickens in Liaoning Province, China, in 2023. Here, we report the genetic and pathogenic characteristics of the IBV strain CK/CH/LN/2301, providing a foundation for better understanding the epidemiology of IBV in China.

Materials and methods

IBV identification and isolation

In June 2023, a layer farm in northeastern China presented clinical symptoms, including decreased egg production and an increased incidence of thin-shelled eggs. Following the collection of laryngotracheal tissues, virus identification and isolation procedures were performed as previously described (Zeng, et al., 2025). Briefly, viral genomic RNA was extracted using the FastPure Viral DNA/RNA Mini Kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China). Reverse transcription was then conducted using HiScript II Q RT SuperMix (Vazyme, Nanjing, China). PCR amplification was performed with IBV-specific primers (IBV-F: GAAGAAAGAACAAAAGACCGACTTAGT; IBV-R: CGTGTTTGTATGTACTCATCTGTAACAGT) to confirm IBV infection, while simultaneous detection was conducted for Newcastle disease virus (NDV), avian influenza virus (AIV), and fowl adenovirus (FAdV), all in accordance with Chinese national standards (GB/T 16550-2020, GB/T 18936-2025, and NY/T 4027-2021). The supernatant of IBV-positive samples was subjected to three rounds of blind passage in 9-day-old SPF embryonated eggs, followed by calculation of the 50% embryo infectious dose (EID50) using the Reed and Muench method (Lj, 1938).

Virus purification and electron microscopic observation

To confirm the viral morphology, IBV was first purified following previously described methods (Kong, et al., 2010). Briefly, the samples were subjected to density gradient centrifugation through a 30%-60% sucrose cushion at 100,000 × g for 3 h at 4°C. The virus-containing fraction at the 45% sucrose interface was collected, diluted with PBS, and centrifuged again at 100,000 × g for 3 h at 4°C. The purified viral pellet was resuspended in 4% formaldehyde, applied to copper grids, and negatively stained with 1% phosphotungstic acid. The samples were then examined under a transmission electron microscope (Talos 120C G2, Thermo Fisher, USA).

Viral genome sequencing and analysis

Whole-genome sequencing was performed as previously described (Zeng, et al., 2025). Briefly, the complete genome of IBV was amplified using 21 primer pairs. The PCR products were sequenced by the Sanger method and assembled using SnapGene 6.1.1 (GSL Biotech, LLC, San Diego, CA, USA). The complete genome sequence has been deposited in GenBank. Phylogenetic analysis of the S1 nucleotide sequence from the isolated strain was performed and compared with 74 reference sequences, including representative strains of various genotypes and current vaccine strains. The phylogenetic tree was constructed using the maximum likelihood method in MEGA version 10.0, with branch support assessed by 1000 bootstrap values. Recombination event analysis was first conducted by performing a comprehensive BLAST search against all available complete IBV genome sequences in GenBank to identify potential parental strains. Subsequently, the recombination events were analyzed using seven methods implemented in RDP5 (version 5.1.0), including RDP, GENECONV, BootScan, MaxChi, Chimera, SiScan, and 3Seq. The results were further validated with SimPlot software, using a sliding window of 500 bp and a step size of 100 bp.

Pathogenicity experiment

Fifty-five 1-day-old SPF chickens were randomly allocated into five isolation units designated Groups A-E. Groups A and C each contained 20 chickens, whereas Groups B, D and E each contained 5 chickens. The chickens in Groups A and B were intranasally inoculated with 106.0 EID50 of strain CK/CH/LN/2301, while those in Groups C and D received PBS and served as negative controls. Group E was used as a positive control and was intranasally inoculated with 106.0 EID50 of strain CK/CH/LN/2302 (GI-19 genotype). On day 5 post inoculation (dpi), all chickens in Groups B, D, and E were humanely euthanized. Tissues from the trachea, larynx, lungs, kidneys, spleen, and duodenum were collected from Groups B and D, and each sample was divided equally for quantitative viral load determination and histopathological examination. Chickens in Group E served as positive controls for clinical observation, and only gross pathological lesions were evaluated. Moreover, Groups A and C underwent continuous 14-day observation to monitor their clinical signs and mortality rates. Clinical symptoms were evaluated by two independent observers according to previously described methods (Zeng, et al., 2025), with scores assigned as follows: 0 indicated no symptoms; 1 represented mild symptoms, including slight ocular discharge and mild depression; 2 denoted severe symptoms, such as marked respiratory distress, coughing and diarrhea; and 3 corresponded to death. Tissues from the trachea, larynx, lungs, kidneys, spleen, and duodenum were collected and subjected to hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining. Histopathological scoring was performed according to previously described methods (Benyeda, et al., 2010). Briefly, the severity of each lesion was evaluated on a four-point scale, where 0 indicated no lesion, 1 indicated mild, 2 indicated moderate, and 3 indicated severe changes.

Quantification of viral load in different tissues

Viral RNA in all collected tissues was detected using quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR). A pair of specific primers targeting the S1 gene of GVI-1 (GVI-1-F: TTGTCTATATCCTTATCTTACGGTCCT; GVI-1-R: TCACCACTATACACACCTTTACAAAG) was designed using SnapGene version 6.1.1. The qPCR assay was performed using SYBR Green I, and viral copy numbers were calculated on the basis of a standard curve. To establish the standard curve, a recombinant plasmid containing the IBV S1 gene was serially diluted tenfold to construct a range of known template concentrations. The resulting standard curve equation was y = –0.2842x + 11.505, with a correlation coefficient (R² = 0.9991).

Virus neutralization test

Hyperimmune sera against the GI-1, GI-13, and GI-19 genotypes, preserved in our laboratory, were used in this study. Following established protocols (Zeng, et al., 2025), the serum samples were heat-inactivated at 56°C for 30 minutes and subjected to twofold serial dilutions in PBS. Concurrently, the virus stock was diluted in PBS to achieve a concentration of 200 EID50. Equal volumes of diluted serum and virus suspensions were mixed and incubated at 37°C for 1 h. Subsequently, 0.2 mL aliquots of the serum‒virus mixture were inoculated into the allantoic cavity of 9-day-old SPF embryonated eggs (n=5). After 5 dpi, the number of embryo lesions was recorded. The neutralizing antibody titer was defined as the highest serum dilution that protected 50% of the embryos, and the neutralizing antibody titers were calculated using the Reed‒Muench method. To evaluate cross-neutralization, the CK/CH/LN/2301 isolate was tested against hyperimmune sera raised to different serotypes. The homologous neutralization reactions of the vaccine strains H120 (GI-1), 4/91 (GI-13), and QXL87 (GI-19) with their respective hyperimmune sera​​ were included as positive controls.

Sequence alignment and structural analysis of the S protein

Sequence alignment of the S protein ectodomains between the CK/CH/LN/2301 and H120 strains was visualized using Jalview version 2.11.4.1. The three-dimensional structure of the spike protein was predicted using AlphaFold 3 (https://golgi.sandbox.google.com/). Structural analysis and visualization were performed using ChimeraX version 1.10.

Ethical statement

The animal experiments in this study complied with the ethical standards and were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the Hubei Academy of Agricultural Sciences.

Statistical analysis

Statistical comparisons of viral genome copy numbers across different tissues were performed using one-way ANOVA in Prism 10 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA), with the significance levels indicated as follows: **, p < 0.01; ***, p < 0.001; ****, p < 0.0001; and ns, not significant.

Results

Virus isolation and identification

IBV was successfully isolated from IBV-positive samples without contamination by NDV, AIV or FAdV (Fig. 1A). The isolated strain, designated CK/CH/LN/2301, caused stunted embryo development when inoculated into the allantoic cavity of 9-day-old SPF embryonated eggs (Fig. 1B). Electron microscopy revealed typical coronavirus particles with enveloped virions and crown-like spike projections (Fig. 1C).

Genomic characterization and phylogenetic analysis

The complete genome of CK/CH/LN/2301 consists of 27,711 nucleotides (nts) with 38.0% GC content, and the full-length sequence has been deposited in GenBank (accession number: PV102524.1). Thirteen open reading frames (ORFs) were identified in the CK/CH/LN/2301 genome, including 1a, 1b, S (spike), 3a, 3b, E (envelope), M (membrane), 4b, 4c, 5a, 5b, N (nucleocapsid), and 6a. Phylogenetic analysis of the complete S1 gene revealed that CK/CH/LN/2301 clustered within the GVI-1 genotype (Fig. 2). The complete genome of CK/CH/LN/2301 shared 98.05% and 98.03% nucleotide identity with CK/CH/SX/2204 (accession number: OQ189491.1) and CK/CH/GX/HX (accession number: PP817796.1), respectively. Both CK/CH/SX/2204 and CK/CH/GX/HX belong to the GVI-1 genotype. To further investigate the relationship between CK/CH/LN/2301 and other GVI-1 strains, a comparative genomic analysis was performed using representative GVI-1 isolates collected in recent years. The results showed that CK/CH/LN/2301 shared approximately 98% identity with GVI-1 strains isolated in 2022, whereas its identity with GVI-1 strains isolated five years earlier was around 94–95% (Table 1).

The phylogenetic tree was constructed on the basis of the S1 nucleotide sequences of CK/CH/LN/2301 (indicated by a red circle) and 74 reference strains using MEGA version 10.0.

Genomic recombination analysis of CK/CH/LN/2301

To investigate potential recombination events in the CK/CH/LN/2301 genome, we performed comprehensive analysis using RDP5 software. The results revealed that CK/CH/LN/2301 is a recombinant strain derived from the GVI-1, GI-19, and 4/91 genotypes. Specifically, the genomic region from nt8334-nt9622 was 99.0% similar to that of IBV/Korea/17/2018-like strains, whereas that from nt12388-nt12574 was 100.0% identical to that of the 4/91 strain. The remaining genomic segments were most closely related to CK/CH/SX/2204 (Fig. 3A). To characterize these recombination events further, we selected CK/CH/SX/2204, IBV/Korea/17/2018, and 4/91 as reference strains for BootScan analysis using SimPlot software. These recombination events occurred within the ORF 1a region (Fig. 3B) and affected the segments encoding nsp3, nsp5, and nsp12. Subsequent phylogenetic analysis of the segmented genome, which was reconstructed using the neighbor-joining method in MEGA software, confirmed CK/CH/SX/2204 as the major parental strain. The genomic regions nt1-nt8333, nt9623-nt12387, and nt12575-nt27811 of CK/CH/LN/2301 presented the closest genetic distance to CK/CH/SX/2204, whereas nt8334-nt9622 was most closely related to IBV/Korea/17/2018-like strains, and nt12388-nt12574 clustered with the 4/91 strain (Fig. 3C). Interestingly, although CK/CH/SX/2204 was identified as the major parental strain, CK/CH/LN/2301 exhibited a four-amino acid deletion at positions 113-114 and an insertion of 11 amino acids at positions 571-584 in nsp3.

Pathogenicity of CK/CH/LN/2301 in SPF chickens

After infection of 1-day-old SPF chickens with CK/CH/LN/2301, mild sneezing appeared at 2 dpi, with the most severe clinical signs, including depression, huddling and head shaking, observed at 4 dpi (Fig. 4A), and the mortality rate was 10% (Fig. 4B). Infected chicks presented tracheal redness and hemorrhage, as well as mild kidney pallor and swelling, whereas the positive control group exhibited tracheal hemorrhage and markedly swollen kidneys with extensive urate deposition. No clinical signs were observed in the negative control group (Fig. 4C). Further qRT-PCR detection of viral loads in different tissues revealed significantly greater viral loads in the larynx and trachea than in the kidneys, spleen and duodenum (Fig. 4D), while no IBV-positive results were detected in any tissues from the negative control group. H&E staining revealed significant histopathological changes in the CK/CH/LN/2301-infected group. In the larynx and trachea, epithelial desquamation, damage to glandular structures, and hemorrhagic lesions were observed. The lungs exhibited pulmonary hemorrhage and inflammatory cell infiltration. Multifocal tubular necrosis was found in the renal interstitium. The corticomedullary junction in the spleen was indistinct. A marked increase in the number of goblet cells was observed in the duodenum. In contrast, no pathological changes were detected in any tissues from the negative control group (Fig. 5A). Histopathological scoring showed that lesion severity in all examined tissues of the infected group was significantly higher than that in the negative control group (Fig. 5B).

Comparative analysis of spike protein sequences and structures between CK/CH/LN/2301 and H120

A cross-virus neutralization assay was performed using hyperimmune sera raised against the vaccine strains H120, 4/91, and QXL87. The CK/CH/LN/2301 strain showed neutralization titers of 2³.²⁵ against H120 antiserum, 2².⁸³ against 4/91 antiserum, and <2².⁰⁰ against QXL87 antiserum, whereas each homologous vaccine strain exhibited substantially higher titers (Table 2). These findings suggest that cross-neutralization between CK/CH/LN/2301 and existing vaccine genotypes is limited. To analyze the molecular basis of antigenic differences, amino acid sequence alignment of the extracellular domains of spike proteins between CK/CH/LN/2301 and vaccine strains revealed less than 70% sequence identity. Most amino acid substitutions were located within the hypervariable regions (HVRs) and the S1 subunit (Fig. 6A and 6B). Further structural comparison revealed an RMSD of approximately 1 Å for the S1 subunit. Notably, compared with H120, the RMSD of the HVR II region reached 4.63 Å (Fig. 6C). In addition, CK/CH/LN/2301 contained three extra amino acids within the HVR II region relative to H120, 4/91, and QXL87, resulting in an extended loop structure and pronounced conformational alteration (Fig. 6D).

Discussion

Infectious bronchitis (IB) is an acute disease transmitted primarily through direct contact, which severely constrains the healthy development of the poultry industry worldwide (Awad, et al., 2014). The continuous emergence of novel variants of IBV has markedly increased the complexity and difficulty of disease prevention and control. Moreover, IBV strains isolated from different regions present significant differences in their biological characteristics, such as virulence, pathogenicity, and tissue tropism. Therefore, systematic analysis of the complete genome characteristics of isolated strains is of great scientific importance for elucidating the evolutionary relationships and pathogenic mechanisms among different strains. Phylogenetic analysis of the S1 gene revealed that CK/CH/LN/2301 is a GVI-1 genotype. GVI-1 IBV was first identified as the prototype strain TC07-2 in China and has since been reported in several other countries, including Korea, Vietnam, and Japan (Ren, et al., 2019). In recent years, the prevalence of GVI-1 strains has been increasing not only across Asia but also in parts of Africa (Li, et al., 2023; Strydom and Abolnik, 2025). This expanding geographic distribution highlights that the emergence and spread of GVI-1 variants are not restricted to China but represent a broader regional and potentially global concern for the poultry industry. Therefore, the genetic and pathogenic characterization of the CK/CH/LN/2301 strain provides insights that are relevant not only for vaccine updates in China but also for the international poultry industry, where continuous surveillance and cross-protection evaluation remain essential.

Here, we isolated a new GVI-1 field strain, which is likely a recombinant derived from GVI-1, GI-19, and 4/91. Recombination events were identified in the ORF 1a region, affecting the segments encoding nsp3, nsp5, and nsp12, with nsp3 exhibiting amino acid insertions and deletions. Although the recombination breakpoints identified by SimPlot and RDP5 showed slight discrepancies, both methods consistently pointed to the same genomic region, thereby jointly supporting the occurrence of a recombination event. The papain-like protease domain of nsp3 cleaves the polyprotein pp1a to release mature viral nonstructural proteins and exhibits deubiquitinating and deISGylating activities that suppress the host innate immune response (Peng, et al., 2022). Nsp5, also known as the main 3CLpro protease, specifically cleaves polyproteins 1a and 1ab at 11 conserved sites and plays a crucial role in viral replication (Fang, et al., 2010). The nsp12-encoded RNA-dependent RNA polymerase forms a complex with the cofactors nsp7 and nsp8 to achieve robust RNA synthesis activity, playing a critical role in viral replication (Tan, et al., 2018). Recombination events and amino acid mutations may alter the properties of relevant proteins, thereby affecting the infectivity, replication capacity, and pathogenicity of newly emerged strains. However, these hypotheses require further validation using reverse genetics and in vitro experiments.

The pathogenicity of IBV is influenced by multiple factors, including its replication efficiency, tissue tropism, and ability to evade the host immune system. Recent reports indicate that GVI-1 genotype strains isolated in China can cause mortality rates ranging from 0% to 60% in infected chicken flocks (Ren, et al., 2019; Yang, et al., 2024a; Yang, et al., 2024b). In the present study, infection with CK/CH/LN/2301 resulted in clinical signs such as depression, sneezing, and head shaking in chickens, with an observed mortality rate of approximately 10%.

GVI-1 genotype IBV strains typically cause pronounced respiratory symptoms, often without evident renal lesions (Jang, et al., 2018). At the early stage of infection, GVI-1 strains can induce severe respiratory signs, with necropsy revealing characteristic tracheal hemorrhage and pulmonary congestion (Sun, et al., 2021). In the present study, consistent with these expectations, the most severe lesions were observed in the larynx and trachea, where petechiae were evident on the laryngeal surface, accompanied by epithelial desquamation and infiltration of inflammatory cells. Additionally, the kidneys, intestines, and other tissues presented varying degrees of damage. Notably, the viral load in the larynx and trachea was significantly greater than that in other tissues, further confirming the respiratory tropism of CK/CH/LN/2301, which is consistent with the characteristics of the GVI-1 genotype. However, to fully elucidate the replication kinetics of the virus, a time-course study is still required to obtain a more comprehensive understanding of viral replication dynamics and tissue tropism. It is worth noting that GVI-1 IBV infection in the field has often been associated with reproductive disorders in laying hens. Early infection can result in long-term lesions of the oviduct and ovary, leading to poor egg production and quality (Bo, et al., 2022). Future studies will include infection trials in pullets or laying hens to further investigate the reproductive impact of CK/CH/LN/2301.

Subtle amino acid substitutions in the S protein may profoundly influence the pathogenicity and immunogenicity of IBV strains (Cavanagh, et al., 1992; Liang, et al., 2024). Compared with the vaccine strain, CK/CH/LN/2301 harbors three amino acid insertions in the HVRII region relative to H120, one insertion relative to 4/91 and QXL87, and four insertions within the HVR III region, with numerous additional mutations concentrated in the S1 subunit. The increased variability in the S1 and HVR regions may reduce antigenic similarity. Since the S1 subunit contains the major serotype-specific neutralizing epitopes (Abozeid, 2023), the marked conformational alterations observed in the S1 protein of CK/CH/LN/2301 may be associated with altered antigenic properties and could ultimately contribute to a reduced level of cross-protection provided by H120 or other vaccine strains.

In this study, a new GVI-1 genotype IBV strain was isolated from a flock of chickens vaccinated with the H120 vaccine in northeastern China. The CK/CH/LN/2301 strain induced pronounced respiratory symptoms. These findings underscore the necessity for ongoing epidemiological surveillance of diverse IBV genotypes and the development of more precise, targeted control strategies.

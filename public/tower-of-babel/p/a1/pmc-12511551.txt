Association between maternal Toxoplasma gondii IgG seropositivity and sex ratio at birth
Authors: Samira Shahbazzadegan, Neda Farzizadeh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractToxoplasmosis has a wide range effects on the health, immunity, behavior, and prenatal and postnatal outcomes of the infected host. It can change hormones and even the birth sex ratio. This study was conducted with the aim of investigating the association between maternal Toxoplasma gondii IgG seropositivity and sex ratio at birth. In this prospective case-control study, 244 pregnant women including 54 pregnant women with IgG T. gondii antibodies as the case group and 190 pregnant women without IgG T. gondii antibodies as the control group were studied. All pregnant women was followed until delivery. Information about the neonate’s sex was recorded. The relationship between the presence of T. gondii antibody and the birth sex ratio was obtained by the Kendall correlation test and the odds ratio was calculated. The sex ratio was 0.79 in negative toxoplasma antibody mothers while it was 1.0 in positive toxoplasma antibody mothers. This increase was not significant. The odds ratio (OR) was obtained 1.253 (p = 0.48). Our study showed that the presence of T. gondii antibody in the maternal serum might increase the birth sex ratio (XY birth). Considering to limited studies on human and contradictory results, more studies with sufficient sample and measuring physiological and biochemical markers is recommened.Keywords: Latent toxoplasmosis, Pregnancy, Birth sex ratio, T. gondii antibodySubject terms: Microbiology, Health care

Introduction

The sex ratio of a population is the ratio of XY to XX. In humans, the sex ratio birth is usually considered to be 105 boys to 100 girls, which is called the 105 ratio in brief. This ratio can vary from about 103 to 1071,2. The sex ratio can be examined in three stages. “Primary sex ratio” is the ratio that exists at conception, “secondary sex ratio” is the ratio that exists at birth, and “third sex ratio” is the ratio that exists between adults3. The birth sex ratio can be influenced by various factors such as parental age, socio-economic status of parents, race, ethnicity, educational level, stress, immunosuppression, and maternal infectious disease4,5. Maternal infectious diseases and microorganisms including parasites are among the factors that affect the gender of the fetus. Infectious diseases may affect the sex ratio via endocrine hormones and the immune system. Toxoplasmosis is one of the most important parasitic infections of the mother during pregnancy5–7. Al-Ardi, 2022 mentioned that T. gondii has an indirect effect on the sex ratio8.

Latent toxoplasmosis affects about a third of the human population in the world, and its effects on the behavior and physiology of the infected host are significant and varied. The lowest toxoplasma seroprevalence is in Korea (4%) and the highest in Nigeria (78%)9. This infectious disease is associated with a wide range of clinical symptoms and their pattern is influenced by the age and immune status of the host. In 80 to 90% of cases, this infection may be asymptomatic with mild, non-specific illness such as fever, weakness, and swollen lymph nodes. However, its clinical aspects are serious in infants and immunocompromised individuals10,11. There are several fatal complications and adverse outcomes during pregnancy associated with toxoplasma infection. Miscarriage, stillbirth, intrauterine death, premature birth, and congenital toxoplasmosis are among them9,12. In a wide range of infected women, immunity, behavior, and prenatal and postnatal disorders were observed. In addition to the complications of the acute form of the disease, hormonal changes and even changes in the secondary sex ratio can be caused by the manipulation of the chronic form of the disease12–15. T. gondii can change the behavior of its hosts, and it has been claimed that Toxoplasma is the third of 15 factors influencing the secondary sex ratio10. Studies have also reported a significant relationship between chronic toxoplasma infection and secondary sex ratio5,12. Dama mentioned Toxoplasma seroprevalence is the third most common predictor of birth sex ratio7. Kankova showed that one of the possible physiological mechanisms for sex ratio change is the elevation of glucose concentration6. Also, steroid hormones and glucose levels in combination with toxoplasmosis have proximal effect on sex ratio10,11. James reported paternal hormone content at the conception time related to the sex ratios at birth. Endocrine-disrupting factors have different hormonal influences on males and females15. Mocanu et al. conducted a study aimed at determining the effect of latent Toxoplasma gondii infection on the history of spontaneous abortion and pregnancy outcomes and found no association between Toxoplasma antibodies and sex ratio16. Bahraini et al., observed that the female gender was significantly more than the male gender in T. gondii affected mothers5. The effect of T. gondii infection on sex ratio might due to the endocrine and their effect on immune systems14.

Regarding limited studies on the relationship between the birth sex ratio and maternal infection by toxoplasma and the contradiction of the previous s studies, this study was conducted to investigate the association between maternal Toxoplasma gondii IgG seropositivity and sex ratio at birth.

Method

This prospective case-control study was conducted on pregnant women in health service centers of Ardabil-Iran in 2022. The ethics code was obtained from Ardabil University of Medical Sciences IR.ARUMS.REC.1402.125. 244 pregnant women included in study. After taking the informed consent form, toxoplasma antibody was determined for them in the first trimester of pregnancy, with serum samples17. Participants was followed until delivery. The birth sex ratio was investigated in 54 pregnant women with T. gondii antibodies as the case group, and 190 pregnant women without T. gondii antibodies as the control group. The inclusion criteria were pregnant women in the first trimester of pregnancy whose serum antibody was measured. The exclusion criteria were incomplete information registration and lack of access to participants. To collect data, the information registration form made by the researcher was used. Information on pregnancy outcomes, such as the sex birth, the number of previous pregnancies of the mother, and the history of abortion, were recorded. Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH) and Fasting blood sugar (FBS) test was obtained in the first visit. A two-hour oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) with 75 g of glucose was performed at 24 to 28 weeks of pregnancy for Diabetes screening.

The association between maternal T. gondii antibody IgG seropositivity and the sex ratio at birth was analysed by the nonparametric Kendall correlation test and the odds ratio (OR) was calculated. To calculate the (OR), the data were arranged in a 2 × 2 table, and the odds ratio was calculated as (a/b)/(c/d) = ad/bc.

Ethical consideration of the study were completely confidential of all the information related to the personal patient’s files. Blood sampling and additional costs were not imposed on pregnant mothers.

Discussion

This study investigated the association between maternaln T.gondii IgG seropositivity and sex ratio at birth in a cohort of pregnant women in Ardabil, Iran. The presence of T.gondii antibody in the maternal serum increased the sex ratio. There was no significant correlation between sex ratio and demographic characteristics of studied women. In a cross-sectional study on 850 samples in Tehran, Shojaee et al., showed that toxoplasma antibody of blood cord significantly associated with more male birth. In our study, the increase of male birth in IgG seropositive women was not significant. They also confirmed that toxoplasma alters some affecting factors in the formation of egg implantation14. Kankova et al. showed that male birth was higher in T.gondii infected women. They declared that higher immunosuppressive compounds in toxoplasma-infected males improves survival of Y chromosome containing sperms and follows higher formation of male embryos12. Another study showed a higher sex ratio in the early stages of toxoplasma-infected mice, while it decreased in the late stages13. Glucose concentration may play a role in T.gondii-associated offspring sex ratio shifts6.

Bahraini et al., in a case-control study by comparison infant gender of 137 anti-toxoplasma seropositive with 137 toxoplasma seronegative mothers, observed that the female gender was significantly more than the male gender in affected mothers that is in opposition to our findings. In general, a significant relationship was observed between chronic toxoplasma infection and secondary sex ratio. In the toxoplasma-positive group, the sex ratio of aborted fetuses was significantly higher. This result agrees of our study5.

Felger and Kankova (2020) reported that the stage of the disease is effective in sex ratio both in women and in the mouse samples. In the early stages of infection, the probability of having boys increases significantly up to 260 boys per 100 girls. In the inactive stage of infection, the probability of a boy’s birth decreases to 78 boys per 100 girls10.

Dama et al. in a systematic review study in 94 countries confirmed a negative relationship between toxoplasmosis and sex ratio7. They discussed the increased probability of girl’s birth by toxoplasma–infection could be the result of impaired health. Based on the Trivers-Willard effect, in females with poor physical conditions the probability of female birth is increased10. Al-Ardi also showed late toxoplasmosis women tend to give more females than males. This tendency is due to the increase in their estrogen concentration8.

Mocanu et al. (2022), declared no effect of T.gondii antibody in the maternal serum on the sex ratio16.

Probable reasons of these contradiction results could be different stage of toxoplasma infection in studied subject and animal samples of some studies.

According to the contradictory results obtained from previous studies regarding the effect of Toxoplasma infection on the birth sex ratio, it is suggested to carry out more studies to clarify the reasons for these contradictions and to identify the influencing factors.

The strength of study were human subjects and prospective case-control study. Limitations of this study were small sample size, cortisol and sex hormones were not measured, and lack of determination the sex of aborted pregnancies.

Conclusion

In this study, the presence of T.gondii antibody in the maternal serum insignificantly increase the birth sex ratio (XY birth). Regarding many effective factors in determining fetal gender and considering the limitations of this study, it is suggested to conduct a case-control study on a large sample size with measuring physiological and biochemical markers. Attention to role of infection stage of contradicting results more studies is recommended in different stage of infection in human subject.

Research on low-carbon operation mechanism of multi-energy alliance considering thermal power generation right sharing
Authors: Yong Cui, Changchun Cai, Jian Zheng, Kun Xu, Qian Zheng, Mf T, Wen Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo enhance the low-carbon operation capability of energy systems and promote the integration of renewable energy, a multi-energy collaborative optimization model based on electricity-carbon-green certificate market trading mechanisms are proposed. By establishing a generation right sharing platform, the study delves into the resource optimization allocation mechanism within the alliance to achieve carbon emission reduction, economical benefit optimization, and improved renewable energy accommodation. The model is solved using a mixed-integer programming approach, and the participation strategies and optimization processes of the multi-energy alliance in diverse markets are analyzed. The results indicate that the generation right sharing mechanism significantly enhances the overall benefits of the alliance, reduces system carbon emissions, and increases the integration of renewable energy. Meanwhile, the synergistic effects of heterogeneous energy sources, including wind power, photovoltaic power, hydropower, and thermal power, play a vital role in optimizing the alliance’s operational objectives. The proposed low-carbon operation model provides an effective decision-making reference for participants in diversified markets and offers theoretical support for the sustainable development of energy systems.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-19235-9.Keywords: Power generation rights sharing, Multi-energy alliance, Low-carbon operation, Green certificate marketSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

In recent years, with the advancement of global energy transition and low-carbon development process, multi-energy complementarity and electricity-carbon trading mechanism have developed into an important means for improving the efficiency of energy systems and promoting the consumption of renewable energy. In August 2020 and February 2021, the National Development and Reform Commission and the National Energy Administration of China successively released “the Guiding Opinions on the Implementation of Wind-Solar-Hydro-Thermal-Storage Integration and Source–Grid–Load–Storage Integration (Draft for Public Consultation) “and “Guiding Opinions on Advancing Power Source-Grid-Load-Storage Integration and Multi-Energy Complementarity Development”. These policy documents outline the implementation pathways for source–grid–load–storage integration and multi-energy complementarity, which are of great practical relevance and long-term strategic importance for advancing China’s energy transition and supporting sustainable socio-economic development. However, during practical operation, challenges remain in the coordinated coupling of heterogeneous energy systems and the optimization of associated market mechanisms. Therefore, by participating in the electricity spot market bidding as a generation alliance with traditional energy, new energy can mitigate bidding risks to some extent while enhancing its market competitiveness and participation incentives1. Current research on the coordinated operation of multiple energy sources mainly focuses on the following three aspects.

Firstly, the research on the collaborative optimization method of multiple energy sources, combined with the complementary characteristics of traditional energy and new energy, aiming to optimize the energy supply-demand balance structure for the efficient allocation of resources. References2,3 took into account factors such as load supply-demand balance, and proposed methods for optimal allocation of system resources with objectives including system stability, cost minimization, or profit maximization. The effectiveness of the proposed models has been validated in practical scenarios. References4,5 utilized multi-level combined energy supply systems, such as natural gas, heat, electricity, and cooling, or integrated energy management system, to achieve multi-energy collaborative supply and energy cascade utilization. Reference6 proposed a multi-temporal-spatial scale energy supply-demand balance method based on three levels of day-ahead, intra-day and real-time. References7,8 analyzed multi-energy demand response of community users, and by optimizing user energy consumption behaviors, enhanced integrated energy utilization efficiency and consumer satisfaction. References9,10 developed a collaborative optimization planning model under the integrated energy or source-network-load mode, and through case studies, demonstrated that the proposed method can effectively reduce system investment and transmission energy consumption. The research on multi-energy complementary economic optimal dispatching mainly focuses on model construction, optimal combination of power generation of each subject and benefit compensation mechanism of each subject 11–14. Reference15 proposed a bi-level optimization framework based on Stackelberg game theory, studying the optimization strategies between distributed energy resources and energy storage systems. Existing literature predominantly focuses on system economy or clean energy consumption, while lacking multi-mechanism integrated modeling from the perspectives of carbon emission reduction and market coordination. Particularly, there remains a research gap in incentivizing long-term collaboration among heterogeneous energy entities and establishing sustainable benefit-sharing mechanisms, which fails to meet the practical requirements for synergistic low-carbon development under the current “dual carbon” goals.

Secondly, research focuses on the design of multi-level market mechanisms and their coordinated coupling. References16,17 explored the joint optimization of multi-energy power generation systems in both electricity markets and auxiliary service market. Reference18 established a multi-energy system dispatch model under a unified carbon pricing mechanism, systematically examining the steering effects of carbon trading mechanisms on emission reduction. The single carbon price mechanism helps to establish a carbon market framework, but it cannot effectively reflect the market supply and demand dynamics. The stepped carbon price mechanism can make up for the shortcomings of the single carbon price mechanism. That is by setting different carbon quota price mechanisms, high-carbon emission enterprises can be effectively encouraged to implement energy conservation and emission reduction. The mechanism can also be reflected in the real-time supply and demand relationship of the participants in the market transactions19–22. Reference23 proposed a strategy for new energy generation enterprises to participate in the electricity spot market within the green certificate trading system. Through simulation experiments, the study verified the feasibility of the proposed scheme in increasing the revenue of renewable energy producers, thereby offering a new approach to reducing their reliance on government subsidies. Reference24 investigated economic low-carbon dispatch strategies for power systems incorporating green certificate demand, and proposed a supply-demand-based green certificate pricing mechanism. Reference25 established a cross-provincial trading model for new energy operators that comprehensively considers both carbon trading and green certificate trading. By introducing these two mechanisms, the wind power and photovoltaic energy curtailment rate are significantly reduced. Reference26 established an electricity-certificate-carbon collaborative pricing model, and designed a joint clearing mechanism that integrates Nash learning quotation and smart contract dynamic trading. The simulation shows that it increases the transaction price by 20% and the success rate by 30 times. The existing research provides a good foundation for the design of the electricity-carbon-green certificate market mechanism and the separate mechanism of action, but the research on the collaborative operation analysis between the three are still weak, and the dynamic trading behavior, resource allocation efficiency and system low-carbon scheduling strategy under the coordination of multiple market mechanisms have not been studied in depth.

Thirdly, research focuses on power generation rights sharing and benefit allocation. As a flexible marketization mechanism, power generation right trading plays an important role in optimizing electricity resource allocation, reducing system operating costs, and promoting the integration of clean energy. Reference27 proposed a generation right trading model combined with carbon trading mechanism, and verified the effectiveness of the model in terms of optimal allocation of resources and carbon emission reduction. References28,29 constructed a generation right trading model between self-owned power plants and new energy enterprises, which effectively promoted the consumption of new energy and reduced coal consumption and carbon emissions. Reference30 emphasized the importance of reasonable cost allocation to the economy and security of power system, and proposed a cost redistribution method based on the comprehensive score of flexibility index to reduce the cost of regional power grid. Reference31 proposed a mechanism to ensure the balance of payments to support the safe and stable operation of the new power system for the cost allocation of energy storage power stations. Existing research has made significant progress in multi-energy system optimization, multi-market mechanism design and power generation right trading. However, there is a lack of research on the coordination mechanism of multi-energy collaborative system participation strategy in multi-level market and the collaborative path of carbon emission reduction.

Combined with the problems existing in the existing research, this paper proposes a multi-energy alliance low-carbon operation mechanism considering the sharing of thermal power generation rights, that is considering the influence of the multi-market trading environment of the electricity-carbon-green certificate, and simulating the energy composition of different power generation characteristics to participate in the electricity-carbon-green certificate market. By designing the regulation mechanism of power generation right sharing within the alliance, the value of its internal low-carbon resources is excavated, and the supporting role of the multi-energy alliance in the low-carbon and efficient operation of the system under the multi-market environment is further verified. It is expected to lay the foundation for the optimal allocation of resources within the alliance, the balance of interests among the subjects and the improvement of new energy consumption capacity. This paper may have the following academic contributions:

(1) Power generation right sharing. By optimizing the allocation of power generation tasks within the alliance, more power generation rights are preferentially allocated to new energy sources with low carbon emissions or zero carbon emissions, which reduces the operating time of thermal power units and reduces their carbon emissions. In addition, the power generation right sharing platform can effectively coordinate the spatial and temporal characteristics of different energy sources (such as the volatility of wind and light and the rapid adjustment of hydropower), further improve the utilization rate of renewable energy, reduce the problem of wind and light abandonment caused by volatility, and thus reduce the overall carbon emissions of the system. Compared with the existing literature, the sharing framework proposed in this study shows stronger system integration and operational flexibility in multi-energy coordinated utilization.

(2) The power generation right sharing is a supplement to the low-carbon economic dispatch of integrated energy. Through the sharing mechanism, the flexible allocation of power generation rights within the alliance is realized, which makes up for the shortcomings of centralized low-carbon dispatch in terms of flexibility and subject cooperation incentives. Different from the low-carbon economic dispatch of integrated energy with the core of minimizing the overall carbon emissions of the system, the sharing of power generation rights pays more attention to the independent cooperation among multiple entities. By giving priority to the distribution of low-carbon or zero-carbon energy power generation rights, the consumption capacity of new energy is improved, and the operating pressure of high-carbon emission entities is reduced. In addition, power generation right sharing combines market transactions with benefit-sharing mechanisms, which not only optimizes resource allocation, but also effectively stimulates long-term cooperation within the alliance, achieves a win-win situation of carbon emission reduction and economic benefits, and provides a more flexible path for low-carbon development.

(3) Aiming at the outstanding problems of existing market mechanisms in multi-energy coordination, mechanism linkage and incentive design, a power generation right sharing mechanism based on electricity-carbon-green certificate collaborative transaction is proposed. By realizing the optimal allocation of resources and the balance of interests among the subjects, the new energy consumption capacity is improved, and the supporting role of multi-energy alliance in low-carbon and efficient operation of the system is verified. Different from the existing literature focusing on electricity-carbon or electricity-green certificate coupling, the “multi-market coordination and sharing transaction” mechanism proposed in this paper realizes the coordinated operation of resource allocation optimization and benefit distribution.

Plan design

The plan proposed in this paper participates in the electricity spot market clearing in the form of an alliance, and then optimizes the internal resource allocation of the alliance through power generation rights sharing and green certificate trading, improving economic benefits and the consumption capacity of new energy. First, according to the clearing results of the electricity spot market, the initial output plan of each subject in the alliance is determined to ensure that the total output of the alliance is consistent with market demand. On this basis, the alliance introduces a power generation rights sharing mechanism. By flexibly adjusting the power generation output plan of each subject, the overall collaborative ability of the system is improved. In the green certificate market, the thermal power in the alliance transfers part of the power generation rights to new energy entities to increase the power generation of new energy, thereby increasing the income from green certificate trading. Analysis of green certificate market transactions

Wind power and photovoltaic new energy operators apply for corresponding green certificates based on their consumption volume. Green certificates with identification codes are issued to qualified wind power and photovoltaic enterprises according to the type of new energy generation, consumption time, power generation location, and project identification. Each green certificate represents 1MWh of new energy electricity. Referring to the green certificate pricing mechanism in34, each is fixed at 42.2 yuan. The green certificate supplier publishes the sale price and quantity of green certificates on the trading platform. The subscriber chooses to buy as needed but cannot resell. The information center is responsible for disclosing green certificate information, reducing the information acquisition cost for market participants, optimizing the supply and demand configuration efficiency of green certificate resources, and tracking and recording the green certificate trading situation.

The matching mechanism of power generation right sharing within the alliance body

The key goal of shared power generation rights is to enable the sharing of generation rights among different power generation entities within the alliance. Information symmetry is achieved among these entities, and a matching mechanism coordinates the allocation of generation rights. This process ensures precise matching of generation rights among market participants, thereby optimizing the overall generation plan. In the proposed alliance, only thermal power has carbon quotas 35, and it is assumed that the generation rights of thermal power are shared within the alliance. Thermal power can reduce its output to reduce carbon quotas or share its generation rights with wind or solar power to reduce system carbon emissions. It can also trade the remaining carbon quotas in the carbon market. Maximize demand matching. Analysis of the alliance’s profit from diversified markets and its contribution

Figures 6b,c show how different types of revenue and their contributions change with different scenarios and mechanisms. In a scenario with 4,537.5 kWh of PV generation and 6,837 kWh of wind generation, green certificate revenue makes up 9.8% of the total revenue. Electricity revenue is still the core part of the total revenue, but its share drops from 80% to 85% without the sharing mechanism to 65%-75%. This shows that the carbon and green certificate markets have a much stronger effect on revenue growth.

Discussions

In this study, a fixed green certificate price is adopted. Future research can further explore the operating rules of the green certificate market clearing mechanism, including the price formation process and the dynamic adjustment of the market supply and demand balance.

During the generation rights sharing and matching process, the study mainly focuses on the sharing and matching of generation rights between the thermal power and renewable energy. Further research is required on the carbon quota sharing mechanism between thermal power plants to optimize resource allocation within the alliance and reduce operational costs of the alliance.

In the carbon trading, a fixed interval division method is used. However, different interval settings can affect the alliance’s revenue in carbon trading. In subsequent research, the carbon trading interval division within the alliance will be optimized by combining the power generation characteristics of different units, providing a basis for the alliance to make carbon trading decisions.

Conclusions

A generation rights sharing mechanism for the thermal power and a collaborative operation model for the alliance participating in multiple markets have been constructed. The study analyzes the coordinated optimization mechanism of different power generation entities in multiple markets. The main conclusions are as follows:

After participating in multiple markets, the alliance’s overall revenue shows a more balanced characteristic. The proportion of electricity revenue decreases from 80%-85–65%-75%, while the shares of revenue from the carbon market and the green certificate market both increase. This optimizes the alliance’s economic benefits and enhances its ability for coordinated development in multiple markets.

The introduction of the generation rights sharing mechanism improves the renewable energy consumption and overall revenue within the alliance. It reveals the role of the alliance’s operational system in promoting low-carbon development.

The study analyzes and determines the optimal matching relationship of generation rights between the thermal power and renewable energy at multiple scales. It verifies the effectiveness of collaborative operation among alliance members and reveals the impact of the generation rights sharing mechanism on the alliance’s multi-scale revenue. This provides a valuable guidance for optimizing low-carbon operational decisions within the alliance.

Reply to: Editorial “The increasing use of T-cell stimulation for successful dengue vaccination” in eBioMedicine 2024; 102, 105120. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ebiom.2024.105120
Authors: Walid Kandeil, Eduardo Nascimento, Vianney Tricou, Mayuri Sharma, Derek Wallace
Published: 2024

In the editorial “The increasing use of T-cell stimulation for successful dengue vaccination”, the author/s mentioned insufficiency of TAK-003 safety data and vaccine-driven cell-mediated immune response.1 We disagree with this summary and wish to highlight that extensive safety assessments including long-term safety data from 2 studies, DEN-204 (Phase 2, ages 2–17)2 and DEN-301 (Phase 3, ages 4–16)3 involving 1596 and 13,380 participants, respectively, showed no significant safety risks up to 4.5 years after the second dose irrespective of participants’ baseline serostatus. In the pivotal efficacy trial, DEN-301, over 4.5 years, efficacy against virologically confirmed dengue (VCD) was 61.2% (95% CI 56.0–65.8) and efficacy against hospitalized VCD was 84.1% (77.8–88.6), confirming long-lasting efficacy, underscoring the vaccine safety profile. Unlike the earlier dengue vaccine, CYD-TDV, which was based on yellow fever backbone, TAK-003 is based on a dengue backbone, and has been extensively characterized to elicit broad immune responses against all four dengue virus serotypes, including robust cell-mediated immunity (CMI) for at least 3 years, driven by multi-functional CD4+ and CD8+ T cells targeting the entire vaccine proteome, and predominantly NS3 and NS5 of all four serotypes.4,5 Overall, TAK-003 is well-tolerated, provides sustained tetravalent humoral and cell-mediated immunity, with established safety and efficacy profile and has the potential of reducing the global dengue burden.

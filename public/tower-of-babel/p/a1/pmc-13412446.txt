Association of serum acetaminophen levels with hepatorenal toxicity: A prospective observational study in Tripura, India
Authors: Debasis Ray, Bidhan Goswami, Satyabrata Nath, Partha Saha, Shauli Sengupta
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
OBJECTIVES:To evaluate the association between serum acetaminophen levels and hepatorenal function in patients receiving therapeutic doses of acetaminophen over a short period.MATERIALS AND METHODS:A prospective observational study was conducted in the department of physical medicine and rehabilitation at a tertiary care hospital in Tripura, India, from August 2021 to July 2022. A total of 109 patients receiving therapeutic doses of acetaminophen were enrolled. Blood samples were collected on day 1 (within 4 h of the first dose) and day 7 of continuous administration. Plasma acetaminophen concentrations were estimated using liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry. Hepatic markers (serum glutamic-oxaloacetic transaminase [SGOT], serum glutamic-pyruvic transaminase [SGPT], total bilirubin, total protein, alkaline phosphatase, albumin, and globulin), and renal markers (serum urea, creatinine, and uric acid) were measured using a fully automated biochemical analyzer.RESULTS:Mean plasma acetaminophen levels increased significantly from 15.09 ± 6.25 μg/ml on day 1 to 17.08 ± 7.34 μg/ml on day 7 (P = 0.001). Mild but statistically significant elevations in SGOT and SGPT (P < 0.05) were observed. Serum urea (P = 0.013) and creatinine (P = 0.031) also showed minor increases, although all values remained within normal reference ranges and were clinically insignificant.CONCLUSION:Therapeutic use of acetaminophen over a short period may lead to subtle hepatorenal changes. While these alterations are not clinically significant, periodic monitoring is advisable in patients requiring prolonged or repeated use. Further studies are warranted to evaluate long-term safety.

Introduction

Acetaminophen, or paracetamol, is among the most extensively used over-the-counter antipyretic and pain relievers worldwide.[1] It is commonly prescribed for mild to moderate pain management, including musculoskeletal pain, headaches, and post-operative pain, particularly in patients who cannot tolerate non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs.[234]

Despite its widespread use, the relationship between serum acetaminophen levels and hepatorenal toxicity remains an area of ongoing research, especially in specific patient populations.[567]

Acetaminophen is generally safe at recommended doses but may cause hepatotoxicity with overdose or prolonged use.[89] The pharmacokinetics of acetaminophen demonstrate that peak plasma concentrations are typically achieved within 1.5 h post-oral administration, with a plasma half-life of 1.5–2.5 h in healthy individuals.[10]

In the context of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation (PMR), patients often receive acetaminophen for chronic pain management. However, there is limited data on whether serum acetaminophen levels correlate with therapeutic efficacy or early signs of toxicity in this population. Some studies suggest that suboptimal plasma concentrations may lead to inadequate pain relief, while elevated levels – even within the therapeutic range – could pose risks for hepatic and renal injury in susceptible individuals.[1112131415]

The current study intended to assess the relationship between serum acetaminophen concentrations and hepatorenal toxicity, specifically examining liver and kidney function parameters among patients attending the PMR outpatient department (OPD) of a tertiary care center in Tripura. By analyzing the changes in laboratory parameters from day 1 to day 7 of acetaminophen administration, this research aims to determine whether even therapeutic doses result in measurable physiological changes and whether such changes have clinical implications.

Materials and Methods

This hospital-based observational research study was conducted in the PMR OPD at a tertiary care hospital (Govind Ballabh Pant Hospital) in Tripura, India, in collaboration with the Multi-Disciplinary Research Unit (MRU), Agartala Government Medical College (AGMC).

This research was performed from August 2021 to July 2022 among patients attending the PMR OPD of Govind Ballabh Pant Hospital, Tripura, India.

A total of 109 patients attending the PMR OPD and receiving acetaminophen for pain relief were enrolled. Patients were recruited based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria.

The study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of AGMC, Tripura, India (Ref. No. F.4.(5-244)/AGMC/Academic/IEC Certificate/2021/7056, dated June 2, 2021).

Patients attending the PMR OPD and receiving acetaminophen for pain relief, those who were willing to provide informed consent, and those who agreed to comply with two scheduled follow-up visits (day 1 and day 7) were included in the study.

Patients receiving other analgesics in addition to acetaminophen, those with known hepatic or renal impairment prior to acetaminophen use, lactating and pregnant women were omitted from the research.

On Day 1 (within 4 h of acetaminophen consumption), each patient was guided to visit the MRU laboratory, where 5 ml of venous blood was taken in accordance with WHO standard phlebotomy recommendations. A second sample was collected from the same patient on day 7 of continued acetaminophen use.

Plasma acetaminophen levels were quantified via liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (Thermo Fisher Scientific, available at MRU, AGMC). Liver function tests (LFT) and kidney function tests (KFT) were performed using an automated biochemistry analyzer (ERBA XL-640), which assessed parameters including total and direct bilirubin, serum glutamic-oxaloacetic transaminase (SGOT), serum glutamic-pyruvic transaminase (SGPT), alkaline phosphatase, total protein, albumin, globulin, urea, creatinine, and uric acid.

The research study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee. At each stage, confidentiality was preserved. All individuals were included in the research after the acquisition of signed informed consent.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS (Statistical Package for the Social Sciences) software, version 25.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, New York, USA). Quantitative variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. LFT, KFT, and serum acetaminophen levels between Day 1 and Day 7 were compared using a paired t-test. P <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Results were reported with 95% confidence intervals. Effect sizes were calculated using Cohen’s d for the interpretation of clinical significance.

Results

A total of 109 patients (mean age = 49.9 ± 12.03 years) receiving acetaminophen for pain management were enrolled in the study. The mean plasma acetaminophen concentration significantly increased from 15.09 ± 6.25 μg/ml on day 1 to 17.08 ± 7.34 μg/ml on day 7 (P = 0.001). The effect size (Cohen’s d = 0.29) indicated a medium clinical effect.

SGOT increased significantly from 36.8 ± 13.38 U/L to 38.08 ± 13.41 U/L (P = 0.001, Cohen’s d = 0.048). SGPT (ALT) also showed a statistically significant increase from 42.05 ± 15.73 U/L to 43.67 ± 15.17 U/L (P = 0.001, Cohen’s d = 0.105). Both changes reflect small effect sizes without any clinical significance. Other LFT parameters, including total bilirubin, direct bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, total protein, albumin, globulin, and albumin/globulin ratio, did not show statistically significant alterations between day 1 and day 7 (P > 0.05 for all).

Serum urea increased slightly from 28.16 ± 7.80 mg/dL to 28.47 ± 8.32 mg/dl, showing a statistically significant difference (P = 0.013), although the effect size was small (Cohen’s d = 0.019). Serum creatinine increased marginally from 1.12 ± 0.40 mg/dl to 1.13 ± 0.41 mg/dl (P = 0.031, Cohen’s d = 0.009), also representing a small effect. However, serum uric acid showed no significant elevation on day 7 compared to day 1 (5.85 ± 1.22 mg/dl vs. 5.83 ± 1.19 mg/dl, P = 0.459). Urea and creatinine levels on both day 1 and day 7 were within normal levels, and the difference was clinically insignificant.

Discussion

The study population comprised a total of 109 individuals, including 47 females and 62 males, categorized into three distinct age groups. The majority of participants (49.5%) were over 51 years of age, with 46.8% of the total females and 51.6% of the total males. The age group of 36–50 years accounted for 37.6% of the total population, comprising 48.9% of the total females and 29% of the total males. Participants aged between 19 and 35 years represented the smallest proportion, constituting only 12.8% of the total, with 4.3% of total females and 19.4% of total males [Table 1]. This distribution indicates a higher representation of older individuals, particularly among males.

This study investigated the association between serum acetaminophen concentration and its impact on liver and kidney function among patients attending the PMR OPD at a tertiary care hospital (Govind Ballabh Pant Hospital) in Tripura. Acetaminophen is widely considered a safe and effective analgesic when used within therapeutic limits.[16] However, growing evidence suggests that even routine use, especially over consecutive days, may influence biochemical markers in certain individuals.[17]

In the present study, a statistically significant increase was observed in plasma acetaminophen concentration from day 1 to day 7 [Table 2], suggesting accumulation or altered metabolism following repeated dosing. This aligns with prior findings indicating that while acetaminophen is rapidly absorbed and metabolized, its pharmacokinetics may be affected by factors such as hepatic enzyme activity, nutritional status, or concomitant conditions.[11819] Despite the increase in concentration, values remained within the therapeutic range, indicating that daily administration did not cross toxicity thresholds in the studied cohort.

The analysis of liver function, both SGOT (aspartate aminotransferase) and SGPT (alanine aminotransferase) levels, showed statistically significant elevations on day 7 [Figure 1], although the changes were within the normal physiological range and had small effect sizes [Table 2]. These findings are in line with the hepatocellular metabolism of acetaminophen and its known capacity to induce mild hepatic stress with repeated dosing.[192021] The toxic metabolite N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone imine, usually detoxified by glutathione, could potentially accumulate in small amounts and contribute to transient hepatic enzyme elevations.[22] However, no significant changes were noted in other liver function parameters [Figure 1], such as bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, total protein, albumin, or globulin, suggesting that hepatic excretory and synthetic functions remained unaffected during the short observation period.

The renal function tests revealed a small but statistically significant increase in serum urea and creatinine levels between day 1 and day 7 [Figure 2]. Although the absolute changes were clinically insignificant with small effect sizes [Table 2], the consistent trend suggests that acetaminophen, even at therapeutic doses, may exert mild nephrotoxic effects over time, particularly in susceptible individuals. Similar findings have been reported in earlier studies that document early renal alterations following prolonged acetaminophen exposure, attributed to oxidative stress or metabolite-induced renal tubular injury.[14] However, uric acid levels did not change significantly, indicating that purine metabolism remained largely unaffected.[23]

The clinical relevance of these biochemical changes remains a key point. Although the effect sizes were small, the consistent direction of change in some parameters (e.g., urea, creatinine, liver enzymes) raises concern for patients with pre-existing renal or hepatic vulnerabilities. This underscores the importance of periodic biochemical monitoring in patients on prolonged acetaminophen therapy, especially in the PMR setting, where analgesics are often administered over extended periods.

The present findings are consistent with earlier reports demonstrating that paracetamol metabolites can induce oxidative stress in renal tubular cells, causing mild increases in serum urea and creatinine.[24] The study confirms previous findings of mild elevations of hepatic transaminases with therapeutic doses of acetaminophen, indicating potential subclinical stress on hepatocytes without clear toxicity.[19] This is in line with observations in diverse populations, emphasizing the need for monitoring even at recommended dosage levels.

Short-term therapeutic use of acetaminophen over 7 days suggests limited clinical impact. These findings support the renal safety of short-term acetaminophen use, though long-term effects warrant further investigation.

Furthermore, the study supports previous observations that therapeutic serum acetaminophen levels do not always equate to the absence of subclinical organ effects, highlighting the need for individualized risk assessment and cautious use, particularly among elderly or metabolically compromised populations.[2526]

Although this study has some limitations, as it was conducted in a single center with only short-term follow-up.

Conclusion

This study is one of the first studies from Northeast India to prospectively evaluate the biochemical effects of therapeutic acetaminophen use in a real-world clinical setting. Over 7 days, we observed a consistent and statistically significant rise in some hepatic and renal biomarkers, despite serum acetaminophen levels remaining within therapeutic limits. These subclinical alterations suggest early organ responses to continued acetaminophen exposure. These findings highlight the importance of routine hepatorenal monitoring during prolonged acetaminophen therapy, especially in older adults. No acute hepatic or renal dysfunction was observed, supporting the short-term safety of acetaminophen. However, the subtle trends in biomarker changes underscore the importance of individualized monitoring. The findings call attention to possible early metabolic or enzymatic shifts even in routine use. This study sets the foundation for larger, long-term investigations into therapeutic drug safety in underrepresented populations.

The quality of life of students is impacted by the quality of life of senior surgeons in French university hospitals
Authors: Aurélien Venara, Cédric Annweiler, Bénédicte Gohier, Jean-Francois Hamel
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionQuality of life of medical school students is impaired by anxiety or depression. As they spend more than of their time as assistants in the hospitals, it is expected that the quality of life of supervisors directly impacts that of students. The objective of this survey was to assess the quality of life of senior surgeons and medical students in visceral surgery at a university hospital in France.MethodsThe survey was conducted from 1st to 31st December 2024 using the Google Form platform. Two scales of quality of life were utilized: the WHOQOL-BREF and the PROQOL. A correlation matrix was performed to study the association between aspects of quality of life. Multivariate linear regressions were performed for modelling QoL scores.ResultsAmong the 106 subjects who responded to the questionnaire, 67 were senior surgeons (63.2%) and 39 were students (36.8%). The mean score on the Stress Traumatic Scale of the PROQOL was the only difference between groups and was found to be lower in the student group (19.9+/-6.9) in comparison to the senior surgeon group (22.9+/-8) (p=0.06).Interestingly, the mental health of the students was found to be improved with the highest score of mental health among the senior surgeons. Of particular interest is the observation that the burnout scale of the students was improved with the best score of the mental health of the senior surgeons.ConclusionThis study advocates for a strong correlation between the QoL of senior surgeons and that of students.

A recent survey on mental health and working conditions among French medical students highlighted a very high prevalence of anxiety (52%) or depression (18%) in such a population [1]. This issue has become a matter of growing concern, and the French General Inspection of Social Business (IGAS) has published a report on the quality of life of healthcare students [2]. In addition to the factors intrinsic to the student him/herself, the authors emphasise the role of the supervisors as a key component in the quality of life of students.

However, a recent meta-analysis by Egbe A et al. [3] reported a high prevalence of psychological disorders in surgical publications, with anxiety reported in approximately 20% of cases and depression in around 24%3. This prompts the question of whether the quality of life of senior staff impacts the quality of life of students. The present study hypothesises that the quality of life of supervisors directly impacts that of students.

The objective of this survey was to assess the quality of life of senior surgeons and medical students in visceral surgery at a university hospital in France.

The survey was conducted from 1 st to 31 st December 2024 using the Google Form platform. The survey link was disseminated to all professors of visceral surgery in France via email, with a request that they circulate it to their respective teams (senior, residents, students). The survey was offered free of charge and guaranteed anonymity. The questionnaire is documented in full in Table 1. Two scales of quality of life were utilised: the WHOQOL-BREF [4] and the PROQOL [5]. The study’s design was reviewed and approved by the local ethics committee prior to the dissemination of the survey link (2024 − 219). For the analysis, the term “students” refers to the students and residents in surgery, while “senior surgeons” refers to all surgeons following the validation of the medical internship. A correlation matrix was performed to study the association between aspects of quality of life. Multivariate linear regressions were performed for modelling QoL scores.

Characteristics and score of quality of life according to the responders

Among the 106 subjects who responded to the questionnaire, 67 were senior surgeons (63.2%) and 39 were students (36.8%). The characteristics of the responders are reported in the Table 1. According to the WHOQOL BREF, the mean scale of global reported quality of life was 3.3 (+/−1.1) and the mean scale of health reported quality was 3.4 (+/−1.1). The mean score for physical health was 79.3 (+/−12.3), the mean score for psychological health was 71.9 (+/−14.9), the mean score for social relationships was 71.9 (+/−18.4), and the mean score for environmental health was 77.4 (+/−13.1). According to the PROQOL, the mean score for compassion satisfaction was 36.5 (+/−7.8), the mean score for burnout was 25.2 (+/−6.8) and the mean score for the Stress Traumatic Scale was 21.8 (+/−7.1). The Table 1 presents a comparison between the two groups, namely the students and the senior surgeons. With the exception of the students’ higher mean age (p < 0.001), no statistically significant differences were observed. The mean score on the Stress Traumatic Scale of the PROQOL was found to be lower in the student group (19.9+/−6.9) in comparison to the senior surgeon group (22.9+/−8) (p = 0.06).

The Table 2 reports the coefficients of the correlation between senior surgeons’ and students’ scores. Interestingly, there was a high correlation between the mental health score of the students and the maximum of mental health score of the surgeons(r = 0.42) and there was a high correlation between the physical health score of the students and the maximum of physical health score of the surgeons (r = 0.44) (Table 2).

Correlation between the WHOQOL-BREF and the ProQol of the students and the seniors

To explain the way in which the surgeons with the highest physical and mental health scores influence the physical and mental health of students, a multivariate analysis was conducted (Table 3). The mental health of the students was improved with the highest score of mental health among the senior surgeons (Coeff: 0.91, 95%CI: 0.16–1.65). Similarly, the physical health of the students was improved with the highest score of physical health among the senior surgeons (Coeff: 1.04, 95%CI: 0.27–1.82). These findings are outlined in Table 3. Interestingly, the burnout scale of the students was reduced with the best score of the mental health of the senior surgeons (Coeff: −0.46, 95%CI: −0.81- −0.11).

The study is subject to inherent limitations due to the small number of responders, resulting in insufficient statistical power to demonstrate a correlation between quality of life and parameters such as daily work duration. Nevertheless, the primary objective of this survey was successfully achieved, namely to demonstrate a strong correlation between the QoL of senior surgeons and that of students. Indeed, when a surgeon with a good mental and physical score was present in the department, the students had better physical and mental health score while the score of burn-out was lowered, advocating for a better QoL. Consequently, it is imperative to explore parameters associated with the QoL of surgeons in order to enhancing the QoL of students.

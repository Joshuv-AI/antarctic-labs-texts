Role of miR-21-5p in the pathogenesis of abdominal aortic aneurysm regarding the Th17 pathway
Authors: Dorota Studzińska, Sabina Lichołai, Kamil Polok, Hanna Plutecka, Piotr Kica, Piotr Grazda, Maciej Chwała, Marek Sanak, Wojciech Szczeklik
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and aimsThe interplay between Th17-related immune activity and miRNA-driven regulation in abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) pathogenesis remains poorly understood. In this prospective study, we aimed to assess the potential role of miR-21-5p corresponding to the Th17 pathway disturbances in the development of AAA.MethodsBiological samples were collected from patients with true infrarenal AAA, undergoing elective open abdominal aortic surgery, and patients with peripheral arterial disease (PAD) without concomitant aneurysms on the day of hospital admission for surgery. A fragment of aneurysmal abdominal aortic tissue was collected from patients intraoperatively. Total RNA was extracted from the samples, and the expression level of miR-21-5p and selected markers associated with Th17 pathway were assessed using quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR) and Luminex assays, respectively. Moreover, we performed an in vitro model using human endothelial cells, transfected with a synthetic miR-21-5p mimic.ResultsWe included 60 patients (30 in the study group and 30 in the control group, selected based on the propensity score matching method). Circulating miR-21-5p levels were significantly higher in AAA patients compared to controls. miR-21-5p downregulates genes involved in endothelial development, regulation of vascular permeability, or endothelial cell response to growth factors. We demonstrated that the Th17 circulating mediators were higher among AAA patients compared to controls, and were higher in the middle part of the aneurysmal aorta when compared to its upper pole.ConclusionOur study showed that, regarding AAA pathogenesis, endothelial dysfunction followed by inflammation could be mediated by an increased level of miR-21-5p.Keywords: Abdominal aortic aneurysm, miR-21-5p, Th17 pathway, Interleukin 17A, Interleukin 21

Introduction

Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is the most common true arterial aneurysm, predominantly affecting the segment of the aorta below the renal arteries [1]. At the molecular level, the pathological processes contributing to the development of AAA encompass the upregulation of proteolytic pathways, apoptosis of structural cells, oxidative stress, inflammation, and degradation of the arterial wall matrix [2]. Despite AAA manifesting as a localized lesion, certain evidence indicates it may be considered a systemic disease, with the entire vascular system compromised in individuals with AAA. Although the precise molecular pathways are still to be elucidated [3]. As positive familial history constitutes a risk factor and we lack a consistent model of inheritance, a potential role of epigenetic factors is considered in the development of this disease [[4], [5], [6]].

MicroRNAs are short endogenous noncoding RNAs which play an important role in posttranscriptional gene regulation and various biological processes, including cell proliferation, apoptosis or tumorigenesis [7,8]. Their deregulation has been associated with the pathogenesis of many cardiovascular diseases, including AAA [[9], [10], [11], [12]]. In a previous study, Plana et al. described differences in microRNA levels between the aneurysm and normal aortic wall. One of the molecules with the highest observed difference in expression levels was micro RNA-21-5p (miR-21-5p) [13]. However, the precise mechanism of its contribution to the AAA pathogenesis is still not completely understood.

Moreover, indirect evidence indicates a pivotal role of the immune system in aneurysm development. AAA exhibit many features of a chronic inflammatory disease, characterized by vascular inflammation and leucocyte infiltration throughout the media and adventitia of the vessel wall [14]. The inflammatory cell infiltrate is most likely to be the source of activated immune cells, as well as cytokines, proteolytic enzymes (serine elastases and matrix metalloproteinase) and immunoglobulins that cause vessel wall weakness and dilatation through immune-mediated mechanisms [15]. Among others, several T-cell-related mechanisms have been postulated to take part in the AAA pathogenesis, and Th17 pathway seems to be a potentially crucial factor [16]. Previous studies have suggested a possible relationship between miR-21-5p and the Th17 pathway [17].

In this prospective study, we aimed to advance the current knowledge and improve our mechanistic understanding of aneurysm biology through a comprehensive assessment of the potential role of miR-21-5p-mediated epigenetic control and its possible relation to the Th17 pathway in the development of AAA using blood and tissue samples from patients undergoing open aortic repair.

Material and methods

Study design and population

This was a prospective observational study performed at the Vascular Surgery Department of St. Jan Grande Hospital in Krakow between March 2020 and October 2022. As part of this project, a local biorepository was established to collect blood samples and abdominal aortic tissue fragments from study participants.

Patients with true infrarenal abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA), undergoing elective open abdominal aortic surgery, and patients with peripheral arterial disease (PAD) without concomitant aneurysms − aortic or iliac, femoral and popliteal arteries were qualified for inclusion in the biorepository. Patients with autoimmune disease, active cancer, post-traumatic aneurysm or undergoing immunosuppressive therapy were excluded from the study. Study personnel obtained data from patients and from their medical documentation and entered these data in the case report forms. Blood samples were collected from patients on the day of hospital admission. Blood samples were delivered to the laboratory immediately, no later than 2 h. Whole blood was centrifuged in a standard manner, and the serum obtained was transferred to a local biorepository and frozen at −80 °C. The samples were stored in this manner until all tests were performed.

In addition, a fragment of aneurysmal abdominal aortic tissue was collected from AAA patients during surgery. The operating surgeon marked the upper pole of the obtained tissue, which originated from the area where the vascular prosthesis was sewn (i.e., the area directly adjacent to the unchanged aortic wall). Aortic tissue was immediately, within 2 h, transferred to the laboratory. It was then subjected to preliminary processing, and the resulting preparations were stored at −80 degrees Celsius until analysis.

From the established biorepository, 30 patients with abdominal aortic aneurysm who met the project inclusion criteria were selected to form the study group. Thirty patients with PAD were selected as the control group based on the propensity score (PS) matching method, which allowed for reducing the influence of potential confounding variables. Baseline variables for calculating the PS included age, sex, hypertension, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, coronary artery disease and smoking. Final study group comprised of patients with AAA and a control group of PAD patients with similar demographic and clinical parameters but without AAA.

The study received approval from The Bioethics Committee of The Regional Chambers of Physicians in Krakow on January 28th, 2020 (No 7/KBL/OIL/2020) and have been performed in accordance with the ethical standards laid down in the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments. Written individual consent was acquired before patient enrollment.

Assessment of circulating miR-21-5p level

RNA was isolated from the frozen serum using a commercially available microcolumn-based silica kit (A&A Biotechnology, Gdynia, Poland) with spike-in RNA (dme-miR-7) and reverse transcribed using the TaqMan Advanced miRNA cDNA Synthesis Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, USA) according to the manufacturer's protocol. The specific cDNA was quantified by real-time PCR amplification using commercially available TaqMan specific probes for miR-21-5b and miR-16 using the 7900HT Fast Real-Time PCR System. Raw amplification data were preprocessed using the SDS 2.4 software (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and exported for further analysis. The fold change of miR expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method, and the threshold cycle was ascertained using the maximum of the second derivative method. All sample data were corrected for extraction and reverse transcription efficiency using spike-in miR (dme-miR-7), followed by amplification adjusted to the miR-16 signal as endogenous control.

Cell culture

Cell cultures were performed according to the protocol described in detail previously [18]. Briefly, we used iCell Endothelial Cells (Cellular Dynamics, Madison, WI, USA), which are purified human endothelial cells differentiated from induced pluripotent stem cells, cultured in Vasculite Maintenance Medium (Cellular Dynamics) according to the manufacturer's protocol. Transfection with the miR-21-5p mimic was performed using HiPerfect Transfection Reagent (Qiagen) according to the manufacturer's protocol. 100 pmol of the miR-21-5p mimic was mixed with the HiPerfect Transfection Reagent, and this mixture was then incubated with the endothelial cells under normal growth conditions for 12 h. As a negative control, a mock transfection using empty complexes (without the miRNA mimic) was performed to measure baseline expression levels. After obtaining 70–85 % confluence, total RNA was isolated using silica-based minicolumns according to the manufacturer's protocol (TotalRNA Mini, A&A Biotechnology). The concentration and purity of the RNA eluent were measured by spectrophotometry (NanoDrop Spectrophotometer 2000, NanoDrop Technologies, USA). The quality and integrity of RNA were assessed with Bioanalyzer (PicoRNA Chip, Agilent Technologies, CA, USA). The resulting RNA was used to prepare libraries, which were preceded by ribodepletion according to the standard Illumina protocol.

Library Construction and RNA-Sequencing

Sequenced libraries were constructed using 100 ng of total RNA per sample as starting material, but only high-quality RNA samples (RIN ≥ 9) were used. The first step was performed according to the standard ribosomal depletion protocol (Illumina, USA). Sequencing runs were performed with Illumina reagents at a genomic core facility in 2 × 100 bp in a paired-end manner.

Cytokine levels in peripheral blood and aortic tissue sample

Il-17A, Il-10, Il-21, and Il-23 levels were measured in aortic tissue using ProcartaPlex high-throughput kits (Thermo Fisher Scientific) according to the manufacturer's recommended protocol on a MAGPIX instrument (Luminex Corp., Austin, TX) and xPonent software (Luminex Corp., Austin, TX). The lower limits of detection for cytokines were as follow: IL-17A – 0.68 pg/ul; IL-10 0.29 pg/ul; IL-23 – 3.22 pg/ul; IL-21 0.90 pg/ul. Results below the detection limit were assigned values at the midpoint of the lower assay limit.

Bioinformatic pipeline and statistical analysis

Raw base call files (.bcl files) were converted to.fastq using the bcl2fastq converter. The FastQC package was used for quality checks. The alignment of paired ends was performed using the STAR package (2.7.0a), which aligned RNA-seq reads to the human genome in a mode that provided correction for spliced junctions. Linear transcript abundance was estimated using the Salmon tool (1.3.0). Differential analysis to compare transcriptome under stimulation conditions was executed using the deseq2 R package (1.6.3).

Statistical analysis

Categorical and quantitative variables were presented as counts (percentage) and mean (standard deviation) or median (IQR), respectively. Statistical significance between categorical variables was assessed using the chi2 test or Fisher's exact test, as appropriate. The significance of differences between quantitative variables was assessed using the Student's t-test or the Mann–Whitney test, depending on the distribution of variables in groups. A comparison of cytokine levels in aortic tissue was performed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test for paired variables. p < 0.05 was considered a statistically significant type I error level. Statistical analysis and visualization of the obtained results were performed using R software with dedicated packages and Prism 8, respectively.

Comparison of circulating miR-21-5p between patients and controls

The level of circulating miR-21-5p was assessed using specific TaqMan Assays in total RNA isolated from serum samples. The serum levels of miR-21-5p were higher among patients with AAA compared to the control group (p = 0.0001). Results are summarized in Fig. 1. No significant correlation was detected between miR-21-5p levels and age, nor time to disease progression (ρ = -0.09, p = 0.59; 0.16; 0.39 respectively). There was a positive tendency between the level of miR-21-5p and aneurysm diameter, but it was not statistically significant (ρ = 0.27, p = 0.15; Spearman Correlation).

Differences in gene expression profiles of endothelial cells after transfection with miR-21-5p

To investigate the effect of miR-21-5p overexpression on the global gene expression pattern, we used an in vitro model of endothelial cells and transfected them with the miR-21-5p mimic molecule. Total RNA from the cells was isolated 12 h after transfection, and deep RNA sequencing was performed. cDNA libraries from miR-21-5p and mock-transfected cells were sequenced after ribosomal depletion. The results indicated that miR-21-5p transfection induced significant changes in the expression of more than 1100 genes (log fold change ≥ |2.0|). Results are presented as a volcano plot for genes which reach statistical significance in Fig. 2a and visualized as a heatmap in Fig. 2b, while a list of 20 most up- and downregulated genes is presented in Tables S1 and S2 (Supplementary Materials). Among others, overexpression of SELP gene seems to be interesting. The SELP gene, which encodes P-selectin, is implicated in the development and progression of aneurysms, particularly in abdominal aortic aneurysms (AAAs) [19]. P-selectin, a cell adhesion molecule, plays a role in inflammation and leukocyte recruitment, and its expression on endothelial cells and platelets is linked to the formation and growth of atherosclerotic plaques, which can contribute to aneurysm development. Additionally, P-selectin is involved in the initial stages of leukocyte adhesion and rolling on the vascular endothelium, a process that can contribute to vascular inflammation and damage, potentially increasing aneurysm risk [19]. Another overexpressed gene is CXCL12. CXCL12, also known as stromal cell-derived factor 1 (SDF-1), is a chemokine that acts as a chemoattractant, influencing the recruitment of immune cells like lymphocytes and macrophages to the aneurysm site [20]. It interacts with its receptor, CXCR4, to mediate these processes, and dysregulation of this axis is linked to AAA development and rupture. In order to obtain more comprehensive results, we performed pathway analysis, which is summarized in the next paragraph.

Pathway analysis

Among the genes with significantly altered expression levels, we selected genes that were downregulated and performed pathway analysis. The results are shown in Fig. 3. Among these, molecular pathways that may be related to the pathogenesis of aortic aneurysms are of interest. We have demonstrated that upregulated miR-21-5p downregulates genes involved in endothelial development, regulation of vascular permeability, or endothelial cell response to growth factors.

Pathway enrichment among miR-21-5p downregulated genes. The pathway enrichment analysis was performed on a subset of genes that were significantly downregulated following microRNA stimulation. The overrepresentation analysis identifies biological pathways disproportionately associated with these downregulated transcripts. Pathways shown in the plot were selected based on adjusted p-values (FDR < 0.05) and ranked according to their enrichment scores. Only pathways meeting the statistical threshold are shown. The size of each dot reflects the number of downregulated genes involved in that pathway – larger dots indicate more genes. The horizontal position of each dot represents the enrichment score: the further to the right, the stronger the association between that pathway and the suppressed gene set. This approach highlights functional networks most affected by microRNA activity, providing insights into potential mechanisms of gene regulation.

Target analysis

Among the differentially expressed genes, we distinguished those containing target sequences for miR-21-5p from the remaining ones. Genes with target sequences are considered to be directly regulated by miR-21-5p, while the others are likely influenced indirectly through more complex metabolic pathways. This classification highlights the presence of distinct regulatory mechanisms contributing to the observed transcriptomic outcomes. Among differentially expressed genes, with direct target TIAM1 seems to be important. TIAM1, a protein involved in regulating the activity of the small GTPase RAC1, plays a role in aortic aneurysm and dissection [21]. Specifically, S-nitrosylation of Septin2, a protein in macrophages, can disrupt TIAM1′s interaction with Septin2, leading to increased TIAM1-RAC1 activity and subsequent inflammation and extracellular matrix degradation, which contribute to aortic aneurysm and dissection [21]. Moreover, direct targeting of TGF-β followed by downregulation seems to be crucial. TGF-β has been implicated in the development of TAAs; evidence suggests it may have a protective role against AAAs [22]. Studies have shown that TGF-β can reduce inflammation, matrix degradation, and cell death in animal models of AAAs, all factors contributing to aneurysm development [22]. Increased miR-21-5p could reduce these effects and contribute to the progression of AAA. Which is also worth highlighting, another miR-5p direct target, SPRY1 and the Th17 pathway are linked. SPRY1 can act as a negative regulator of certain aspects of T-cell activation, including the Th17 pathway. SPRY1, a Sprouty protein, a negative regulator of receptor tyrosine kinase signaling, can interact with CBL, a negative regulator of ZAP-70 tyrosine phosphorylation, impairing T-cell activation [23]. Increased miR-5p acts in a classical way, causing downregulation of SPRY1. Thus, miR-21-5p-mediated suppression of SPRY1 may lead to impaired T cell activation, which is observed in AAA pathogenesis [16].

Differential expression of ncRNA

In the next part of the analysis, we focused on genes encoding long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs). Differential expression analysis was performed for this group of transcripts, and the results were visualized using a volcano plot to highlight significant changes in expression (Fig. 4). Subsequently, the lncRNAs were classified based on their mechanism of action, revealing a notable enrichment among transcripts originating from intronic regions of protein-coding genes. The observed results require further study. This is because the function of these differentially expressed molecules is poorly understood.

Level of circulating IL-17A and IL-21

The next part of the study focused on assessing circulating interleukins associated with the Th17 signalling pathway, which plays a key role in inflammatory and autoimmune responses. Specifically, we measured the serum levels of IL-17A and IL-21 and observed that both cytokines were significantly elevated in the patient group compared to healthy controls (Fig. 5). This finding supports the involvement of Th17-related immune activation in the pathophysiology of the condition under investigation. Further statistical analysis revealed no significant associations between interleukin levels and demographic factors such as age or sex, nor clinical parameters including disease duration, severity scores, or laboratory markers. These results suggest that the observed changes in IL-17A and IL-21 levels are likely disease-specific and independent of individual patient characteristics or clinical status.

Discussion

In this study aimed at the assessment of miR-21-5p role in AAA development, we demonstrated that circulating miR-21-5p levels were significantly higher in AAA patients compared to controls. Moreover, we showed that miR-21-5p downregulates genes involved in endothelial development, regulation of vascular permeability, or endothelial cell response to growth factors. Finally, we found that the concentration of cytokines from the Th17 signaling pathway in the peripheral blood of AAA patients was significantly higher compared to controls, as was their concentration in the middle part of the aneurysmal aorta than in its upper pole.

miR-21-5p has been shown to play an important role in the development of cardiovascular diseases, including ischemic heart disease, diabetes and stroke [24]. A growing body of evidence suggests that epigenetic regulation and microRNAs may play a role in the pathogenesis of abdominal aortic aneurysms. Some studies based on human abdominal aortic tissue revealed that miR-21-5p level is markedly higher in the aneurysm in comparison to the normal aortic wall [13,25,26]. Therefore, in order to follow up on these considerations, we assessed the serum level of miR-21-5p in AAA patients to verify that the tissue level is mirrored in the circulation. Indeed, we found that circulating miR-21-5p levels were significantly higher in AAA patients compared to control.

Hitherto, several authors have described possible molecular mechanisms in which miR-21 could be involved in AAA pathogenesis. Based on animal models, it has been pointed out that miR-21 is increased in AAA as a physiological response to counterbalance the pathology and leads to a decrease in phosphatase and tensin homolog (PTEN) protein and activation of AKT, a component of a pro-proliferative and antiapoptotic pathway, which leads to protection against AAA expansion. Even more, systemic antago-miR-21 injection increased PTEN expression and promoted AAA growth [25]. Moreover, another animal model presented by Yu et al. has demonstrated that dexmedetomidine inhibits AAA development by overexpressing miR-21, downregulating the miR-21/programmed cell death protein 4 axis and consequently mitigating inflammatory responses [27]. The precise molecular effects of microRNAs in specific cells and conditions are often difficult to predict using only bioinformatic tools. This is due to their potentially pleiotropic nature. Therefore, we performed an in vitro study in which we transfected endothelial cells with miR-21-5p and evaluated the effects at the transcriptional level. This gives us a comprehensive picture of differentially expressed genes in response to stimulation. Traditionally, miR are thought to downregulate target genes by repressing transcription or translation. However, in vitro models show that the networks obtained are more complicated, and we can observe both up- and down-regulation of gene expression when we can evaluate the whole transcriptome. In order to assess these dependencies, we performed a pathway analysis and revealed that the regulation of angiogenesis, adhesion to cell matrix, regulation of vascular permeability and endothelial cell growth in response to growth factors could be impaired in response to miR-21-5p. These results are in line with previous studies. Impairments in the structure of the cell matrix have been shown to contribute to the formation of AAA. Syndecan-4 was associated with disease pathogenesis via disrupted focal adhesion of smooth muscle cells [28]. Pathogenic variants in genes responsible for this process have also been studied in terms of the pathogenesis of the disease, mainly in the thoracic segment [29]. Endothelial dysfunction is an established factor in AAA formation and progression. Among others, Medina et al found that flow-mediated dilation in the brachial artery is negatively correlated with the diameter of AAA [30]. Endothelial dysfunction seems to contribute to both oxidative stress and inflammation in the degenerating arterial wall, and it could be at least partially mediated by miR-21-5p.

In regard to the influence of miR-21-5p on the inflammatory response, some studies suggest that miR-21-5p is more expressed by memory than naïve T cells and is upregulated in activated CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, in turn regulating T-cell effector response, apoptosis, proliferation, and migration depending on the differentiation status of the T cells [[31], [32], [33]]. High miR-21-5p levels are supposed to be a marker of immune cell activation; however, whether this is a cause or a consequence of activation remains undetermined. Moreover, regarding its influence on T cells, miR-21-5p seems to regulate the balance between a pro-inflammatory and an immunosuppressive state and may become dysregulated during cases of autoimmunity [34]. Even more, there is some evidence that Th17 differentiation may be associated with a significant increase in miR-21 levels [17]. In animal models, it has been shown that mice lacking miR-21 displayed a deficiency in Th17 differentiation and that these animals were resistant to autoimmune encephalomyelitis. Moreover, the above-mentioned study revealed that treatment of wild-type mice with anti-miR-21 oligonucleotides reduced the clinical severity of encephalomyelitis [17].

Our study suggests that miR-21-5p targets genes may connect with the Th17 pathway through the downregulation of SPRY1. Thus, miR-21-5p-mediated suppression of SPRY1 may lead to impaired T cell activation and contribute to the pathogenesis of AAA.

We demonstrated that the concentration of cytokines from the Th17 signaling pathway in the middle part of the aneurysmal aorta was significantly higher than in its upper pole, as was their level in the peripheral blood of AAA patients compared to controls. These results may suggest a possible correlation between a high level of miR-21-5p and immunological degeneration of the aortic wall involving the Th17 pathway. Thus, our study revealed the possible connection between two phenomena observed in AAA − immunological disorders and epigenetic control. This topic requires further research because the cellular source of overexpressed Th17 cytokines was not elucidated in this study. These could be Th17 CD4+ or Tc17 CD8+ lymphocytes or even innate lymphoid cells type 3 (ILC3).

Interestingly, a recent epidemiological cohort study comprising the Danish population has reported that psoriasis was associated with an increased risk of AAA, and the risk appears to surge with the severity of psoriasis [35]. It is noteworthy that IL-17 is a crucial cytokine in psoriasis pathogenesis, and inhibition of Th17 cells is a therapy strategy. However, the influence of IL-17 or IL-23 inhibitors on coexisting AAA in patients with psoriasis remains unknown.

Treatment of AAA disease thus far remains the most outstanding challenge in modern vascular medicine. Classical cardiovascular medications or antibiotics have generally failed to prevent or significantly alter AAA formation or progression [1]. Therefore, open surgery or endovascular repair remains the only curative treatment. However, invasive treatment of AAA is still connected with substantial morbidity and mortality during the perioperative period [36]. We believe the findings of the present study may provide novel insight for the future management of AAA. Understanding the mechanisms that lead to AAA formation may open the door to new therapies and possible targets for new drugs, e.g., inhibition of Th17 cells or use of anti-miR–21 molecule as a potential treatment strategy.

To summarize, in the present work, we establish a level of circulating miR-21-5p and its molecular target in terms of AAA in an experimental way. We have shown that at least partially endothelial dysfunction followed by inflammation could be mediated by increased level of miR-21-5p.

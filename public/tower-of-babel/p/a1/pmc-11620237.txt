Multi-strategy improved runge kutta optimizer and its promise to estimate the model parameters of solar photovoltaic modules
Authors: Serdar Ekinci, Rizk M Rizk-Allah, Davut Izci, Emre Çelik
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractHarnessing the potential of solar photovoltaic (PV) technology relies heavily on accurately estimating the model parameters of PV cells/modules using real current-voltage (I-V) data. Achieving optimal parameter values is essential for the performance and efficiency of PV systems, necessitating the use of advanced optimization techniques. In our endeavor, we introduce a multi-strategy improvement approach for the Runge Kutta (RUN) optimizer, a cutting-edge tool used for tackling this critical task in both single-diode and double-diode PV unit models. By aligning experimental and model-based estimated data, our approach seeks to reduce errors and improve the accuracy of PV system performance. We conduct meticulous analyses of two compelling case studies and the CEC 2020 test suite to showcase the versatility and effectiveness of our improved RUN (IRUN) algorithm. The first case study involves a standard dataset derived from the well-known R.T.C. France silicon solar cell, where IRUN performs favorably compared to competing methods, demonstrating its effectiveness. IRUN effectively manages the complex task of defining model parameters for an industrial PV module situated at the Engineering Faculty of Düzce University in Turkey. The real-world I-V data, obtained under optimal conditions with a temperature of 30°C and solar radiance of 1000W/m2, provide strong evidence of the practical applicability and real-world benefits of our innovative method. Additional analyses through three-diode and PV module models further confirm the efficacy of the IRUN. A mean absolute error of down to 6.5E-04 and root mean square error of down to 7.3668E-04 are achieved. Our approach provides a practical and efficient tool for improving the accuracy of PV systems, enhancing their performance when compared to existing methods.Keywords: Improved Runge Kutta (RUN) optimizer, Solar photovoltaic (PV) unit, I-V characteristic, Parameter estimation, Single and double diode models

Introduction

In recent years, researchers have turned their attention to renewable energy sources, which have become a dynamic and ever-evolving field. In today's world, where we grapple with the urgent issues of global warming, sustainability has become a top priority [1]. Solar energy, often hailed as the future of energy, offers hope because it's eco-friendly. As we deal with the dwindling supply of petroleum, increasing pollution, and the undeniable effects of climate change, solar power emerges as a compelling solution [2,3]. Among the various options for sustainable energy, solar photovoltaic (PV) energy stands out as a clean and accessible choice with reasonable costs for setup and operation. However, the efficiency of PV systems depends on environmental factors like sunlight and temperature. To make PV systems work their best, we need to choose the right models [4,5]. The key to optimizing these models is getting accurate estimates for their parameters. But this is not a straightforward task because PV technology is inherently complex and nonlinear. In response, researchers have come up with many different strategies [[6], [7], [8], [9], [10], [11], [12], [13], [14]].

In understanding the non-linear I-V characteristics of PV systems, two commonly used equivalent circuits have emerged. These circuits are known as the single-diode model (SDM) and the double-diode model (DDM), and they approach the task differently [15]. The SDM is favored for its simplicity and fewer adjustable parameters, providing clear advantages over the DDM [15]. However, the DDM steps in to address the limitations of the SDM by introducing an additional diode to account for recombination losses. In both models, critical parameters like the photo-generated current, parallel/shunt circuit resistance, series resistance, saturation current, and ideality factors play vital roles in defining and shaping the behavior of PV units [16].

In the field of PV modeling, researchers have developed various methods that can be categorized into three main groups: deterministic, analytical, and heuristic. Analytical techniques are based on mathematical analyses, which offer the advantage of faster and simpler calculations. However, they often sacrifice precision and consistency, which can affect the reliability of the results [17]. Deterministic approaches provide solutions but have limitations because they rely on models that require differentiation and convexity. Heuristic approaches, on the other hand, offer a way to overcome these limitations. They have a remarkable ability to handle complex problems with multiple objectives and address the challenges at hand [18]. This is why metaheuristic optimization methods have become widely accepted as a solution for dealing with the complexities of parameter identification in solar PV systems [19].

In the quest for precise modeling of solar PV systems, researchers have introduced various optimization algorithms. These algorithms fall into different categories, each with its strengths and weaknesses. For instance, in Ref. [20], researchers reported an efficient and accurate gradient-based optimizer for extracting parameters from solar cells and PV modules. In Ref. [21], an improved optimization algorithm was developed, combining a gradient-based optimizer with a chaotic sequence generator to estimate PV parameters for solar cells and modules. They evaluated the effectiveness of this hybrid algorithm using diverse datasets from various solar cells and modules. Another approach, the improved moth-flame algorithm introduced in Ref. [22], enhanced precision and efficiency compared to its original version, albeit at the cost of increased computation time. In Ref. [23], the musical chairs algorithm was used to speed up and improve the accuracy of PV parameter estimation by involving multiple search agents to enhance exploration and reduce convergence times. In Ref. [24], researchers explored a novel hybrid mechanism combining wild horse and hunter-prey models to determine PV characteristics. This hybrid mechanism's effectiveness was validated through case studies involving R.T.C. France silicon solar cells, Photowatt-PWP201 panels, and thin-film PV systems under varying conditions. While [25] utilized the continuous population genetic algorithm to estimate parameters from experimental datasets of PV units, limited success was attributed to solely relying on the SDM technique and four specific panel types. In Ref. [26], the modified salp swarm algorithm was proposed to identify parameters for PV models, specifically the SDM and DDM. The modifications in the salp swarm algorithm addressed early convergence issues and improved exploration potential, although reducing the root mean square error (RMSE) value remained challenging. While several approaches for modeling solar PV cells/modules have been explored in the literature [[27], [28], [29], [30], [31], [32], [33], [34]], there's still room for more effective optimization algorithms to achieve superior results.

Considering the challenges mentioned earlier and the promising potential of metaheuristic methods, this paper aims to introduce an innovative metaheuristic algorithm for accurately estimating parameters in PV systems. We achieve this by enhancing the recently developed Runge-Kutta optimizer [35] with a set of ingenious improvements. We utilize a quadratic interpolation strategy, a Laplacian segment mutation technique, a Levy flight strategy, and information-sharing-based local search mechanisms to significantly improve the accuracy and efficiency of PV system parameter estimation. This paper presents a groundbreaking approach by leveraging an improved version of the Runge-Kutta optimizer to precisely determine optimal combinations of solar PV parameters in the context of SDM, DDM, three-diode model (TDM) and PV module models. Our primary goal is to identify parameter values that minimize the differences between measured and estimated I-V data, enhancing the precision of PV system characterization. To demonstrate the strength of our proposal, we conduct a comprehensive analysis using CEC 2020 benchmark functions and two compelling case studies: the R.T.C. France silicon solar cell dataset and data collected from the CS6P-220P solar module. Besides, we perform the analyses on two additional models, TDM of R.TC. France silicon solar cell and PV model of STM6-40/36 PV module.

We initially evaluated the performance of our proposed algorithm using the CEC 2020 benchmark test suite and compared it to several other optimization algorithms, including the dwarf mongoose optimization algorithm [36], salp swarm algorithm [37], grey wolf optimizer [38], prairie dog optimization algorithm [39], biogeography-based optimization [40], particle swarm optimization [41], gazelle optimization algorithm [42], differential evolution [43] and sine cosine algorithm [44]. The results clearly demonstrate that our proposed algorithm consistently outperforms the others, achieving the best average values for all CEC 2020 benchmark functions. This demonstrates its strong performance.

In PV system analysis, we use a well-recognized dataset from R.T.C. France, involving silicon solar cells. We employ the improved Runge-Kutta optimizer to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of optimizing the SDM and DDM parameters of these cells. This optimization process is marked by unparalleled precision and effectiveness. To emphasize the novelty and significance of our work, we conduct a thorough comparison against the latest advancements in optimization algorithms, including the squirrel search algorithm [15], elite learning adaptive differential evolution [45], hybrid multi-group stochastic cooperative particle swarm optimization [46], queuing search optimization and differential evolution [47], hybrid chaotic northern goshawk and pattern search [48], hybrid chimp algorithm [49], flexible improved particle swarm optimization and sequential quadratic programming [50], weighted mean of vectors algorithm [51], differential evolution [52], sine cosine differential gradient based optimizer [53], quantum Nelder-Mead hunger games search algorithm [54], improved tunicate swarm algorithm [55], enhanced Harris hawk optimization [56], gradient based optimization with ranking mechanisms [57], stochastic fractal search algorithm [58], coyote optimization algorithm [59], improved Lozi map based chaotic optimization algorithm [60], improved moth-flame optimization [61], hybrid firefly and pattern search algorithm [62], improved jaya optimization algorithm [63], artificial bee colony optimization [64], conscious neighborhood strategy-based Laplacian barnacles mating algorithm [28] and war strategy optimization [32]. Additionally, to replicate real-world conditions, we utilize an experimental dataset from a polycrystalline CS6P-220P solar module. This dataset was collected through rigorous experimentation at the Engineering Faculty of Düzce University in Turkey. Using I-V data from this PV module, the improved Runge-Kutta optimizer refines the SDM and DDM parameters, guided by the RMSE objective function. This process enhances the algorithm's performance, offering significant improvements in PV parameter estimation. The efficacy of the proposed method is also demonstrated through additional analyses on TDM of R.TC. France silicon solar cell and PV model of STM6-40/36 PV module. Improved RUN algorithm

By estimating the development of RUN, the RUN algorithm can achieve the best members in search area with the advantages of easy structure and high precision. However, the algorithm still has some drawbacks that may make it prone to signation dilemma while failing to strike a good among the explorative and exploitative features when it comes to dealing with multimodal and complicated concerns [65,66]. Firstly, in RUN algorithm, the search agents are updated in sequential manner using some solutions selected randomly. And then, the weaker ones are eliminated while the better solutions are survival. However, this updating process ignores the self-experience information of the population which may cause the updated members to inherit a high-quality structure. Therefore, a worthwhile challenge is how to ensure that the new population can reach the promising region. Secondly, most optimization approaches suffer from stagnation phenomena, which suck in the local optimal rather than seeking for a better outcome [67]. In a word, Opportunities are offered to enhance the efficiency of the classical RUN. Thus, an improved RUN approach, named IRUN, based on quadratic interpolation strategy (QIS), Laplacian segment mutation (LSM), Levy flight strategy (LFS), and information-sharing-based local search (ISLS) are proposed in this paper.

Problem definition for photovoltaic cell parameter estimation

Photovoltaic (PV) cells are semiconductor devices that convert sunlight directly into electrical energy. Accurate estimation of PV cell parameters is crucial for the design, analysis, and optimization of photovoltaic systems. These parameters, such as the series resistance, shunt resistance, diode ideality factor, and diode saturation current, play a significant role in determining the overall performance and efficiency of PV cells. To estimate these parameters, researchers and engineers commonly use mathematical models that describe the electrical behavior of PV cells. Two widely used models are the single-diode model and the double-diode model. In this section, we discuss these models and their relevance in the problem of PV cell parameter estimation.

Single-diode model

The single-diode model (SDM) is a simplified mathematical representation of the electrical characteristics of a PV cell. It assumes that the PV cell can be modeled as a single diode connected in parallel with a current source. The SDM provides a relatively simple and computationally efficient representation of PV cell behavior while capturing the key aspects of its electrical response. In here, Ns represents the number of series diodes and Ns denotes the number of diodes in parallel. In case of a single-diode model, those variables would be Ns=Np=1. Estimating the parameters of the SDM from measured I-V data involves solving an optimization problem, typically using iterative techniques, to find the values that best fit the observed data. Double-diode model

The double-diode model (DDM) is an advanced PV cell model that incorporates additional diodes to account for more complex electrical behavior. It introduces an additional diode to represent the recombination losses in the PV cell and provides a more accurate description of real-world PV cell characteristics. In here, Ns represents the number of series diodes and Ns denotes the number of diodes in parallel. In case of a double-diode model, those variables would be Ns=1 and Np=2. Estimating the parameters of the DDM requires solving an optimization problem that minimizes the difference between the measured I-V data and the modeled I-V curve based on the DDM equation. This optimization process involves adjusting the values of the parameters until a good fit between the model and the measured data is achieved. Fig. 4 demonstrates the DDM model of solar PV cell.

Compared to the SDM, the DDM provides a more accurate representation of the electrical characteristics of PV cells, particularly in situations where recombination losses are significant. However, due to the additional complexity of the model, parameter estimation for the DDM can be more computationally intensive and requires more measurement data points to achieve reliable results.

Objective function

Two commonly used objective functions for evaluating the goodness-of-fit are root mean squared error (RMSE) and mean absolute error (MAE). RMSE is a popular metric that measures the average magnitude of the differences between the modeled and measured values. Experimental results on PV models

In order to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed IRUN technique, we applied it to estimate the parameters of various equivalent circuit models for both solar cells and PV modules. Specifically, we considered the SDM model with 5 parameters and the DDM model with 7 parameters. Two case studies were conducted to assess the performance of the technique. For the first case study, we utilized a standard experimental dataset obtained from a commercial R.T.C. France silicon solar cell [72] operating at a temperature of 33°C and a solar radiance of 1000W/m2. For the R.T.C. France silicon solar cell, the Ns value is taken as 1.

In the second case study, we employed an experimental dataset derived from an industrial solar module known as CS6P-220P, which operates at 30 °C and a solar radiance of 1000W/m2. The CS6P-220P PV module consists of 6×10 polycrystalline solar cells connected in series, with a rated maximum power of 220W. This PV system, depicted in Fig. 5, was installed at the Engineering Faculty of Düzce University in Turkey. Although the system contains multiple solar modules for power enhancement, our focus was on a single module, which was isolated from the system. For the CS6P220P solar module, the Ns value is taken as 60.

The used solar PV module at the Engineering Faculty of Düzce University in Turkey.

In addition to the previously discussed models, this study extends its evaluation of the proposed method's efficacy by incorporating two additional and distinct photovoltaic systems. Specifically, the investigation includes an in-depth analysis of a three-diode model representing the characteristics of the R.T.C. France solar cell. Furthermore, the study delves into the intricacies of a photovoltaic module, focusing on the STM6-40/36 PV module. For the STM6-40/36 PV module, the Ns value is taken as 36. By incorporating these diverse models into the assessment framework, this study aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of the proposed method's versatility and robustness across varied photovoltaic technologies.

To ensure a fair comparison, we set the population size to 30 and the maximum number of iterations to 400. The algorithms were executed 30 times for each case study. Due to its high accuracy and less computational burden, this study adopts the iterative Newton–Raphson method [73] for parameters extraction. Conclusion

In this research, we have demonstrated power of the IRUN algorithm to estimate the parameters in PV systems. The proposed approach significantly improves the effectiveness and precision of parameter estimation. Our study revolves around CEC 2020 benchmark functions and two distinct solar PV equivalent circuit models, SDM and DDM, both of which have been meticulously evaluated and dissected through two captivating case studies. Besides, additional TDM and PV module models were also used as additional test beds for performance confirmation. In the first case study, our focus centers on modeling a single solar cell, utilizing the standardized dataset derived from a commercially renowned R.T.C. France silicon solar cell. Through the sheer power of IRUN, we have successfully unraveled the optimal parameter values for both the SDM and DDM of this cell. To highlight the profound impact of our proposed approach, we have engaged in a rigorous comparison with several state-of-the-art schemes published recently in the field. The results of this extensive analysis show that the IRUN algorithm boasts the most remarkable RMSE values for both the SDM and DDM cases. Its precision underscores its effectiveness in parameter estimation tasks. In the second case study, we venture into the realm of empirical experimentation, conducting tests on a CS6P-220P PV module deployed at the Engineering Faculty of Düzce university in Turkey. Armed with the acquired experimental dataset, IRUN algorithm once again takes the center stage, expertly determining the optimal values for the SDM and DDM parameters specific to this module. The simulated I-V and P-V characteristics of our data align with the experimental results, affirming the remarkable accuracy of our approach across the entire voltage range. The analyses on TDM of R.T.C. France silicon solar cell and PV model of STM6-40/36 PV module further verified the efficacy of the IRUN from a wider perspective. In light of these findings, the IRUN algorithm demonstrates considerable potential and strong performance in solar PV parameter estimation.

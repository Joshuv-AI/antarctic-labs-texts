Comparative analyses of Alzheimer's disease blood biomarkers and cognitive domains
Authors: Deirdre M O'Shea, James E Galvin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractINTRODUCTIONWhether Alzheimer's disease (AD) blood biomarker–cognition associations differ across cognitive domains, analytic context, and biomarker modeling strategy in population‐based cohorts is unclear.METHODSIn 1170 older adults from the Health and Retirement Study Harmonized Cognitive Assessment Protocol (HRS‐HCAP), we examined cross‐sectional (2016) and prospective (2016 to 2022) associations of blood phosphorylated tau at threonine 181 (p‐tau181), glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP), neurofilament light (NfL), and amyloid beta 42/40 ratio (Aβ42/40) with memory, executive function, language, visuospatial ability, and global cognition using individual‐biomarker, principal component analysis‐derived composite, and multi‐biomarker panel models.RESULTSCross‐sectionally, NfL and GFAP showed the broadest associations with cognitive performance. In simultaneous models of baseline‐adjusted follow‐up cognition, p‐tau181 was associated with lower memory (β = −0.080, q < 0.001) and global cognition (β = −0.066, q < 0.001), while GFAP was associated with lower executive function (β = −0.047, q = 0.007), memory (β = −0.047, q = 0.032), and global cognition (β = −0.066, q = 0.002). NfL and Aβ42/40 were not independently associated after mutual adjustment. P‐tau181 also showed relative domain selectivity between memory and executive function. Relative model support differed by cognitive domain and analytic context.DISCUSSIONAD blood‐based biomarker–cognition associations were domain‐differentiated and context‐dependent: Concurrent associations did not reliably indicate associations with cognition 6 years later, and only p‐tau181 showed formal evidence of relative memory‐versus‐executive selectivity. Exploratory pairwise comparisons highlighted p‐tau181 + GFAP for follow‐up memory and global cognition, warranting independent evaluation.Keywords: Alzheimer's disease and related dementias, blood‐based biomarkers, cognitive domains, glial fibrillary acidic protein, population‐based cohort, phosphorylated tau at threonine 181

INTRODUCTION

Blood‐based biomarkers (BBMs) have emerged as scalable tools for detecting Alzheimer's disease (AD) and related neurodegenerative processes. 1 Among them, the amyloid beta 42/40 ratio (Aβ42/40) and phosphorylated tau at threonine 181 (p‐tau181) index core AD proteinopathies, whereas neurofilament light chain (NfL) and glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) reflect neuroaxonal injury and astrocytic activation, respectively. 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 Although these markers have been associated with subsequent cognitive decline, including among cognitively unimpaired older adults, most prognostic studies have used global cognitive composites and clinic‐based cohorts enriched for cognitive impairment. 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 Their associations may differ in population‐based settings characterized by greater comorbidity and mixed pathology, where scalable risk stratification may have particular public health value. 13 , 14 , 15

Global cognitive composites may obscure early or domain‐differentiated associations, which in AD often emerge first in episodic memory. 16 , 17 Community‐based findings vary across biomarkers and cognitive domains. 14 , 18 , 19 In the Monongahela‐Youghiogheny Healthy Aging Team study (MYHAT), p‐tau181 showed the strongest cross‐sectional associations with memory, whereas NfL was more strongly associated with attention and executive performance. Prospectively, Aβ42/40 was most strongly associated with memory change, while GFAP was most strongly associated with language change. 18 In the Health and Aging Brain Study–Health Disparities (HABS‐HD), p‐tau181 and NfL were associated cross‐sectionally with memory, executive function, processing speed, and language. 20 These findings suggest that domain‐specific outcomes may distinguish memory‐predominant from broader cognitive associations.

Three gaps remain. First, few studies have formally tested whether a biomarker's association differs across cognitive domains; significance in one domain and non‐significance in another do not establish a statistical difference. 21 Formal domain comparisons may therefore strengthen interpretation and guide cognitive‐outcome selection. Second, individual biomarkers, composites, and simultaneous panels have been used but rarely compared against the same domain‐specific outcomes within one cohort. 7 , 12 , 22 , 23 Third, it remains unclear whether relative support for a biomarker configuration differs between concurrent cognition and follow‐up performance. If biomarkers predominate at different disease stages, the most parsimonious configuration may vary by analytic context. 24

Consistent with calls for context‐aware interpretation of BBMs, 25 , 26 this study examined whether biomarker–cognition associations differed across cognitive domains and cross‐sectional versus prospective analyses and whether relative domain selectivity and model support varied across biomarker approaches in a population‐based sample of older US adults.

METHODS

Study population and design

Data were drawn from the 2016 and 2022 waves of the Health and Retirement Study Harmonized Cognitive Assessment Protocol (HRS‐HCAP), 27 a substudy of the nationally representative Health and Retirement Study (HRS) that uses a stratified, multistage sampling design. 28 Of 3496 participants represented in the 2016 HRS‐HCAP, 3347 had evaluable baseline cognition and 1612 (48.2%) had evaluable follow‐up cognition in 2022. Among those assessed at both waves, 1287 had baseline BBM measurements; after excluding incomplete covariates or required cognitive outcomes, 1170 remained in the analytic sample.

Trained interviewers administered the 2016 and 2022 HRS‐HCAP cognitive batteries in participants’ homes. Episodic memory included Consortium to Establish a Registry for AD word‐list delayed recall and recognition, delayed constructional praxis recall, Mini‐Mental State Examination three‐word recall, East Boston delayed story recall, and Wechsler Memory Scale Logical Memory. Executive function included Trail Making Test Parts A and B, Brief Test of Adult Cognition by Telephone backward counting, Raven's Standard Progressive Matrices, and the HRS Number Series. Language included animal naming and brief confrontation‐naming items; visuospatial ability was assessed with constructional praxis copy. Text S1 provides references and construction details.

At each wave, a priori domain composites averaged available z‐standardized indicators without imputation. Trail Making times were log‐transformed and reverse‐scored; higher scores indicated better performance. Global cognition was the first unrotated principal component of 2016 item‐level indicators; 2022 scores applied the 2016 loadings to standardized items and were rescaled to the 2016 metric. Observed ranges for memory, executive function, language, visuospatial ability, and global cognition, respectively, were −2.07 to 1.42, −2.47 to 1.94, −2.72 to 1.15, −3.46 to 1.21, and −4.09 to 2.14 in 2016 and −3.51 to 1.61, −2.71 to 1.97, −4.42 to 1.11, −4.07 to 1.05, and −2.94 to 2.46 in 2022. Baseline cognitive status was assigned using the published 2016 HRS‐HCAP adjudication algorithm. 29

BBMs and apolipoprotein E (APOE) genotyping

BBMs were assayed from samples collected during the 2016 HRS Venous Blood Study. 27 Aβ42/40, GFAP, and NfL were measured in ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid plasma using the Single Molecule Array (Simoa) Neurology 4‐plex assay E; p‐tau181 was measured in serum using the Simoa pTau‐181 V2 assay. Biomarkers were natural log‐transformed and standardized. Descriptive tables present Aβ42/40 in the conventional ratio direction, with lower values indicating greater presumed amyloid burden. For inferential analyses, Aβ42/40 was reverse‐coded before standardization so higher burden‐score values indicated a lower raw ratio and greater presumed amyloid burden. APOE genotypes were determined from rs429358 and rs7412 directly assayed in salivary DNA or, when unavailable, imputed genome‐wide genotype data; participants with at least one ε4 allele were classified as carriers. Text S2 provides additional assay, collection, and genotyping details.

Statistical analyses

Analyses used R version 4.5.2. Characteristics were summarized using means and standard deviations or frequencies and percentages. Spearman correlations assessed biomarker intercorrelations, and Kruskal–Wallis tests assessed race/ethnicity differences. Retention analyses compared participants with and without follow‐up cognition using baseline characteristics, standardized mean differences, and conventional tests. APOE ε4 comparisons were limited to available genotypes, with missingness examined separately.

Primary biomarker models

Three strategies were evaluated: (1) individual‐biomarker models, (2) a PCA‐derived composite using the first unrotated component (PC1) of the four standardized biomarkers, and (3) a simultaneous four‐biomarker panel. PCA provided an unsupervised shared‐variance score for comparison with the other strategies rather than a multidimensional latent structure. Sensitivity analyses compared PC1 with PC1 + PC2; component diagnostics and results are reported in Section 3.2.

Cross‐sectional and prospective association models

Multiple linear regression examined associations of 2016 biomarkers with concurrent cognition. Baseline‐adjusted linear regression within an analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) framework examined associations with 2022 cognition conditional on the domain‐specific 2016 score. All models adjusted for age, sex, education, race/ethnicity (non‐Hispanic White [reference], non‐Hispanic Black, Hispanic, and other non‐Hispanic), and APOE ε4 carrier status. Continuous predictors and outcomes were standardized.

Model comparison, secondary and sensitivity analyses, and multiple testing

To quantify the incremental contribution of biomarker terms beyond demographic and genetic covariates and, prospectively, baseline cognition, we compared each biomarker model with a domain‐specific reference model. Cross‐sectional reference models included age, sex, education, race/ethnicity, and APOE ε4 carrier status; prospective reference models additionally included the corresponding 2016 domain score. Each biomarker model was a nested extension of its corresponding reference model: Individual‐biomarker and PCA models added one predictor, whereas the multivariable panel added four. Nested‐model F‐tests assessed whether adding the biomarker term or terms improved fit beyond the reference model, and ΔR 2 quantified the additional variance explained.

Each primary candidate set contained the reference model, four individual‐biomarker models, the PCA composite, and the four‐biomarker panel, fitted to the same outcome and analytic sample. Relative support was evaluated using the Akaike information criterion (AIC) and Bayesian information criterion (BIC); ΔAIC and ΔBIC were calculated relative to the lowest value in each candidate set. ΔAIC values of 0 to 2 indicated substantial support, 4 to 7 considerably less support, and >10 essentially no support. 30 ΔBIC values <2 indicated little separation, 2 to 6 positive evidence, 6 to 10 strong evidence, and >10 very strong evidence against the higher‐BIC model. 31 AIC and BIC were interpreted separately when they differed. Variance inflation factors (VIFs) and tolerance values assessed multicollinearity in four‐biomarker models.

Memory and executive function were selected as the most comprehensively assessed domains and as clinically relevant, partly dissociable systems: Episodic memory is emphasized in early AD, whereas executive dysfunction occurs across AD and other age‐related cerebrovascular conditions. 32 , 33 For each biomarker, 2022 memory and executive‐function scores were modeled jointly using linear mixed‐effects models containing biomarker tertile, domain, their interaction, the corresponding 2016 domain score, and the primary covariates. Participant‐specific random intercepts accounted for within‐person outcome correlation. The tertile × domain interaction and planned high‐ versus low‐tertile contrasts tested relative domain selectivity.

Exploratory analyses evaluated all six possible two‐biomarker scores, each calculated as the re‐standardized mean of its two standardized components. The candidate set included the reference model, PCA composite, six pairwise scores, and four‐biomarker panel; models were compared by domain and analytic context using AIC, BIC, ΔAIC, and ΔBIC.

Sensitivity analyses excluded participants with baseline dementia and separately restricted the sample to cognitively normal participants. Exploratory moderation analyses tested race/ethnicity and APOE ε4 carrier status interactions. Within prespecified families detailed in Text S3, p values were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) procedure, 34 with q < 0.05 considered significant. Text S3 provides further details on coefficient‐difference, domain‐selectivity, pairwise‐score, sensitivity, moderation, and multiple‐testing procedures.

RESULTS

Sample characteristics

The sample included 1170 participants: 931 cognitively normal, 206 with mild cognitive impairment, and 33 with dementia (Table 1). Mean age was 73.4 ± 6.0 years, mean education was 13.2 ± 3.0 years, and 58.9% were female; 72.6% were non‐Hispanic White, 14.0% non‐Hispanic Black, 11.5% Hispanic, 1.9% other non‐Hispanic, and 23.6% APOE ε4 carriers. Retained participants were younger, more educated, and more often cognitively normal (all p < 0.001); differences in sex, race/ethnicity, and APOE ε4 carrier prevalence among those with genotype data were minimal (Table S1, Panel B). Text S4 and Tables S1–S3 provide additional sample, attrition, group‐comparison, and biomarker‐correlation results.

Biomarker PCA

PC1 had an eigenvalue of 1.77 and explained 44.2% of biomarker variance; absolute loadings were 0.640 for NfL, 0.598 for GFAP, 0.482 for p‐tau181, and 0.010 for Aβ42/40. PC2 had an eigenvalue of 1.01, explained an additional 25.1% (69.3% cumulative), and was dominated by Aβ42/40 (loading = 0.963). Adding PC2 increased prospective R 2 by <0.001 and produced higher BIC across domains (ΔBIC = 4.34 to 7.06). AIC decreased by 0.73 for global cognition and increased by 0.15 to 1.99 otherwise (Table S4).

Cross‐sectional associations

In individual‐biomarker models, higher NfL was associated with lower memory (β = −0.069, 95% CI [−0.101, −0.037], q < 0.001), executive function (β = −0.087, 95% CI [−0.119, −0.055], q < 0.001), and global cognition (β = −0.136, 95% CI [−0.183, −0.090], q < 0.001). GFAP showed corresponding associations with memory (β = −0.068, 95% CI [−0.101, −0.035], q < 0.001), executive function (β = −0.076, 95% CI [−0.108, −0.043], q < 0.001), and global cognition (β = −0.120, 95% CI [−0.168, −0.072], q < 0.001). The Aβ42/40 burden score was positively associated with executive function (β = 0.039, 95% CI [0.010, 0.068], q = 0.013), global cognition (β = 0.070, 95% CI [0.027, 0.113], q = 0.002), and language (β = 0.034, 95% CI [0.012, 0.057], q = 0.012); p‐tau181 was associated only with executive function (β = −0.032, 95% CI [−0.062, −0.002], q = 0.039).

In simultaneous models, NfL remained associated with memory (β = −0.052, 95% CI [−0.090, −0.015], q = 0.026), executive function (β = −0.064, 95% CI [−0.101, −0.027], q = 0.003), and global cognition (β = −0.104, 95% CI [−0.158, −0.050], q < 0.001), as did GFAP (memory: β = −0.045, 95% CI [−0.082, −0.008], q = 0.037; executive function: β = −0.042, 95% CI [−0.078, −0.005], q = 0.033; global cognition: β = −0.068, 95% CI [−0.121, −0.014], q = 0.018). Aβ42/40 remained positively associated with executive function (β = 0.033, 95% CI [0.004, 0.062], q = 0.033), global cognition (β = 0.061, 95% CI [0.018, 0.103], q = 0.010), and language (β = 0.032, 95% CI [0.009, 0.055], q = 0.022); p‐tau181 was not independently associated with any domain after FDR correction.

The PCA composite was associated with lower memory (β = −0.053, 95% CI [−0.078, −0.029], q < 0.001), executive function (β = −0.071, 95% CI [−0.096, −0.047], q < 0.001), global cognition (β = −0.107, 95% CI [−0.143, −0.071], q < 0.001), and language (β = −0.021, 95% CI [−0.041, −0.002], q = 0.029), but not visuospatial ability (q = 0.052; Table S5).

Prospective associations

In individual models, p‐tau181 was associated with lower baseline‐adjusted follow‐up memory, global cognition, and executive function; GFAP and NfL were each associated with lower executive function, memory, and global cognition, whereas Aβ42/40 was not significant (Table 2). After mutual adjustment, p‐tau181 remained associated with memory (β = −0.080, q < 0.001) and global cognition (β = −0.066, q < 0.001) and GFAP with executive function (β = −0.047, q = 0.007), memory (β = −0.047, q = 0.032), and global cognition (β = −0.066, q = 0.002). NfL and Aβ42/40 were not independently significant (all q > 0.21). VIFs were 1.04 to 1.72, and tolerance values were ≥0.58. The PCA composite was associated with lower follow‐up memory, executive function, global cognition, and language, but not visuospatial ability (Figure 1).

P‐tau181 associations with memory and global cognition were stronger prospectively than cross‐sectionally, whereas Aβ42/40 and NfL showed stronger cross‐sectional associations with selected outcomes. Sensitivity analyses

Results were generally consistent after excluding baseline dementia and among cognitively normal participants. P‐tau181 remained associated with memory and global cognition, and GFAP with executive function, memory, and global cognition. P‐tau181 was additionally associated with lower language performance in the no‐dementia (β = −0.053, 95% CI [−0.084, −0.021], q = 0.004) and cognitively normal (β = −0.049, 95% CI [−0.081, −0.017], q = 0.010) samples; NfL and Aβ42/40 were not independently significant (Tables S8–S10). No race/ethnicity interaction survived FDR correction. Three APOE ε4 carrier status interactions survived FDR correction: NfL was associated with lower follow‐up memory only among carriers, and GFAP showed stronger associations with lower follow‐up executive function and global cognition among carriers (Tables S11–S12). These findings were exploratory.

DISCUSSION

Cross‐sectionally, NfL and GFAP showed the broadest independent associations with memory, executive function, and global cognition, while the Aβ42/40 burden score showed directionally unexpected positive associations with executive function, global cognition, and language. Prospectively, p‐tau181 retained independent associations with memory and global cognition and GFAP with memory, executive function, and global cognition; NfL and Aβ42/40 were not independently associated. P‐tau181 also showed a stronger association with follow‐up memory than executive function. Prospective biomarker models explained only small increments beyond covariates and baseline cognition, with support varying by domain and criterion; exploratory pairwise comparisons highlighted p‐tau181 + GFAP for memory and global cognition and GFAP + NfL for executive function. Together, these findings indicate that cognitive outcome, analytic context, and biomarker parameterization should be considered jointly when evaluating prognostic BBM associations.

Cross‐sectional and prospective dissociation of biomarker associations

Concurrent associations did not reliably indicate the prospective pattern. In mutually adjusted models, NfL and GFAP were cross‐sectionally associated with memory, executive function, and global cognition; prospectively, p‐tau181 was associated with memory and global cognition and GFAP with all three domains, whereas NfL was not independently associated. P‐tau181 associations with memory and global cognition were stronger prospectively than cross‐sectionally, whereas GFAP associations did not differ significantly by context. These patterns may reflect distinct temporal relationships of neuroaxonal injury, astroglial activation, and AD‐related tau pathology. 2 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 15 , 24 , 35 , 36

The positive cross‐sectional associations observed for the Aβ42/40 burden score were directionally unexpected. A related analysis of the broader HRS Venous Blood Study also found that higher conventional ratios were associated with worse cognition and greater dementia likelihood but not subsequent decline; given the overlapping sample, this represents convergent rather than independent evidence. 19

Prior community‐based studies reported domain‐differentiated associations, but patterns varied across cohorts. 18 , 20 Follow‐up duration may contribute: Baseline p‐tau181 was unrelated to cognitive or structural brain change over 18 months in one cognitively unimpaired sample, 37 whereas associations with follow‐up memory and global cognition were detected over 6 years in the current study.

Biomarker model configuration

For follow‐up memory, p‐tau181 alone received AIC support alongside the four‐biomarker panel and had the lowest BIC. Among pairwise configurations, p‐tau181 + GFAP had the lowest AIC and BIC for memory and global cognition. Together with their independent prospective associations, this convergence suggests complementary information related to AD‐associated tau pathology and astroglial response. 15 , 36

Executive function showed a different configuration: PCA had the lowest AIC and BIC in the primary comparison, while GFAP + NfL had the lowest pairwise values with little separation from PCA. PC1 was driven mainly by NfL and GFAP, with smaller p‐tau181 and negligible Aβ42/40 loadings, supporting a shared injury‐related rather than predominantly AD‐tau signal. 6 , 14 , 38 The Aβ42/40‐dominant PC2 added minimal prospective variance and received less BIC support.

For global cognition, the four‐biomarker panel received the lowest AIC and PCA the lowest BIC in the primary comparison, while p‐tau181 + GFAP received the lowest AIC and BIC among pairwise configurations. Language showed modest, criterion‐dependent biomarker support, whereas biomarkers added little information for visuospatial ability.

Relative domain selectivity between memory and executive function

Direct domain comparisons remain limited in the BBM literature but help distinguish whether putative prognostic biomarkers align with the early AD cognitive phenotype or reflect broader cognitive risk. 18 , 20 , 35 Neuroaxonal and astroglial injury markers may show less domain specificity, whereas an AD‐related marker may preferentially relate to memory, often affected early. 5 , 6 , 16 , 17 GFAP and NfL showed no domain selectivity, whereas p‐tau181 was more strongly associated with follow‐up memory than executive function. The absence of a cross‐sectional p‐tau181–memory association suggests it may identify future memory vulnerability before evident concurrently. 35 , 37 This adds phenotypic specificity to evidence for blood p‐tau181 as an early prognostic AD biomarker. 4 , 7 , 12 , 15

Moderation by race/ethnicity and APOE ε4 carrier status

Biomarker–cognition associations did not differ by race/ethnicity after FDR correction, although power to detect subgroup differences may have been limited. NfL was associated with lower follow‐up memory specifically among APOE ε4 carriers, and GFAP showed stronger associations with lower follow‐up executive function and global cognition among carriers than non‐carriers. These exploratory findings are consistent with greater ε4‐related vulnerability to neuroaxonal injury and astroglial activation, 15 but require replication in independent samples.

Limitations

Prospective models estimated follow‐up cognition conditional on baseline rather than within‐person decline, and cross‐sectional–prospective coefficient comparisons were approximate. Language and visuospatial composites had fewer indicators, potentially reducing power. Selective retention may limit generalizability to older or more impaired adults. P‐tau181 was measured in serum and the other biomarkers in plasma, which may limit direct comparison despite validated matrix‐specific stability. 39 The absence of positron emission tomography or cerebrospinal fluid measures precluded biological validation, particularly of the unexpected Aβ42/40 pattern. Pairwise‐score, domain‐selectivity, and moderation findings require independent replication.

Future directions

Studies with at least three cognitive assessments and repeated biomarker measurements should test within‐person change and whether p‐tau181 + GFAP predict incident impairment or dementia. Comparisons with p‐tau217 in larger, diverse cohorts are also needed. 3 , 9 , 40

Conclusions

BBM–cognition associations varied by cognitive domain and analytic context. NfL and GFAP showed broad cross‐sectional associations, whereas p‐tau181 and GFAP retained independent associations with follow‐up cognition, and p‐tau181 showed relative selectivity for memory over executive function. Model support varied across domains and criteria, with exploratory analyses highlighting p‐tau181 + GFAP for follow‐up memory and global cognition and GFAP + NfL for executive function. These findings support aligning biomarker configuration and cognitive outcomes with the analytic context and study objective.

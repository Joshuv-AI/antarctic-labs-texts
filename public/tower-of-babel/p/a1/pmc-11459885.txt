Construction and validation of a predictive model for the return to work of patients with permanent colorectal stoma: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Jianlei Liu, Hong Ye, Jiayu Tian, Liangliang Qu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe issue of patients returning to work is increasingly garnering attention from countries worldwide. This study aims to investigate the risk factors associated with patients returning to work after undergoing permanent enterostomies. Additionally, it seeks to establish and validate a nomogram prediction model, thereby providing a more effective reference for patients aiming to return to work.MethodsThis study was a cross-sectional investigation conducted between September 2022 and September 2023. We conveniently selected 293 postoperative patients with permanent colorectal stomas due to colorectal cancer from three tertiary hospitals in Liaoning Province. Participants were categorized into Returned and Non-Returned groups based on their return to work status. Data were collected using a general information questionnaire, a Stoma Acceptance Questionnaire, and the Ostomy Adjustment Inventory. Binary logistic regression analysis was performed using SPSS 25.0 software to identify independent influencing factors. A predictive model was constructed using R Studio 4.3.0 software. Internal validation was conducted through 1,000 rounds of Bootstrap resampling, and model performance was assessed using Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves, the Hosmer-Lemeshow (H-L) test, and calibration curves.ResultsAfter surgery, the return-to-work rate for patients with permanent colorectal stomas was 29.69%. Age, education level, postoperative time, stoma complication, adjuvant therapy, stoma acceptance score, and ostomy adjustment inventory score were identified as independent factors influencing the return-to-work status of these patients (P < 0.05). These factors were incorporated into a logistic regression model generated by R software, resulting in a ROC curve with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.916 (95% CI: 0.884–0.947). The Youden index was 0.731, and the cutoff value was 0.228. Sensitivity and specificity were 0.920 and 0.811, respectively. The H-L test demonstrated good model fit (χ2 = 12.858, P = 0.117, P > 0.05). Calibration curves indicated a close alignment between predicted and actual probabilities.ConclusionsThe postoperative return-to-work rate is low in patients with permanent enterostomies. The prediction model developed in this study demonstrates strong performance and offers predictive value, providing a scientific foundation for assessing patients’ return to work. Caregivers should prioritize the early identification of various patient types for proactive intervention to enhance the rate of postoperative return to work.Keywords: Colorectal stoma, Risk factor, Return to work, Nomogram, Predictive model

Introduction

In contemporary societies, the incidence of cancer has been rising due to changing lifestyles, increased urbanization, and aging populations [1]. According to the International Agency for Research on Cancer [2], there will be up to 1.9 million new cases of colorectal cancer (CRC) worldwide in 2022, making it the third largest source of new cancers globally, with the incidence of colorectal cancer surging among young people under 50 years of age [3]. However, thanks to improvements in early cancer screening techniques and advances in treatments, survival rates for cancer patients have generally improved [4, 5]. At the same time, many countries have raised the retirement age, leading to an increase in the number of cancer survivors of working age, a trend that means more and more cancer survivors are facing the realities of reintegration and returning to work as they recover [6].

Overseas, return-to-work research has long been conducted across a wide range of areas, covering everything from bone and joint muscle injuries [7], to brain injuries [8], to the return to work of cancer survivors [9], and even exploring the communication between healthcare professionals and employers [10], as well as strategies for providing patients with return-to-work support. In contrast, research in China started later and has only recently begun to focus on the return of patients to work, with studies examining breast cancer [11], myocardial infarction [12], stroke [13], and more. However, to the best of our knowledge, there is a lack of in-depth research on the return to work of patients with permanent enterostomies in China. The rate of return to work for patients with permanent enterostomies and the factors influencing it, including the patients’ physical health, psychological status, and socio-demographic characteristics, remain largely uncharted territory.

Past research has shown that the presence of a colostomy presents a significant barrier for colorectal cancer survivors returning to work [14]. A colostomy may lead to patients ceasing work, retiring early, or even facing permanent unemployment, as highlighted in Gordon’s study [15]. Specifically, 27% of patients with a colostomy opted to stop working, 32% were compelled to transition from full-time to part-time work, and 20% had to accept significantly different job responsibilities than they held prior to their illness. Furthermore, the physical characteristics of the stoma profoundly affect the patient’s quality of life and psychological well-being [16, 17]. The issue of odor not only causes embarrassment and social isolation but also makes patients hesitant to engage with others [18, 19], particularly those with permanent colostomies. For these individuals, returning to work signifies not just physical recovery but also a new phase of life, representing a crucial step toward normalcy and reintegration into society [20]. Resuming work alleviates the family’s financial burden while also enhancing the patient’s self-esteem and sense of achievement [21, 22], which helps shift focus away from the disease’s progression and boosts survival confidence. Therefore, assisting patients with permanent intestinal stomas in returning to work is not only a vital social responsibility but also a recognition of their personal dignity and worth.

The aim of this study is to thoroughly explore the multidimensional factors influencing the return to work of patients with permanent intestinal stoma and to develop a prediction model that serves as a scientific forecasting tool for this specific group. Our objective is to identify early risk factors associated with the return to work for patients with permanent intestinal stoma, providing a scientific foundation for the creation of targeted vocational rehabilitation interventions, and to propose effective strategies aimed at facilitating a swift and smooth return to work, enabling patients to reintegrate into their social roles.

Materials and methods

Definition of return to work

Based on the concept proposed by Franche [23], we define return to work as a patient’s exit from work following an illness and their reintegration into the labor market after recovery, either by resuming their previous position or by starting a new job.

Participants

This descriptive study employed a convenience sampling method to include patients with permanent enterostomies who were assessed at the stoma clinics of three tertiary hospitals in Shenyang City and Jinzhou City, Liaoning Province, from September 2022 to September 2023. Participants were categorized into Returned and Non-Returned groups based on their return to work.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Aged 18–59 years; (2) At least 6 months post-stoma operation; (3) Pathological staging of intestinal cancer as I ~ III; (4) Employed prior to the operation; (5) Informed consent and voluntary participation.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Individuals with severe mental and psychological disorders; (2) Individuals with other serious illnesses or tumor recurrence and metastasis. To ensure the accuracy of data collection, three trained interviewers conducted face-to-face interviews using standardized language to gather data and provided prompt explanations to subjects with questions. All questionnaires were returned after verification for completeness and validity, and those with obvious errors were corrected on the spot.

Sample size calculation: According to the statistical guidelines for impact factor research, the sample size should be 5–10 times the number of independent variables. This study is designed to include 16 key variables, leading us to initially estimate the required sample size range. Additionally, considering the potential for missing and invalid questionnaires, we allocated a buffer of 20%. From this, we inferred that the sample size should be no less than 96 to 192 cases. To closely reflect the actual situation while ensuring the adequacy and representativeness of the data, we ultimately collected a sample of 326 cases. Among the 326 patients with permanent enterostomy, we excluded 15 patients who withdrew midway, 13 patients with invalid questionnaires, and 5 patients identified as outliers after data preprocessing, resulting in a final inclusion of 293 patients in this study.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

This study received approval from the Ethics Committee of Jinzhou Medical University (Ethics Approval No. JZMULL2022105). All participants provided written informed consent prior to their involvement in this study. All procedures conducted in this study adhered to the ethical standards of the institution and/or the National Research Council, as well as the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments or equivalent.

Measurements

General information: This questionnaire was developed by researchers through literature review and expert consultation for this study (The English version of the questionnaire is available in the supplementary document). It was used to assess the socio-demographic characteristics of the participants, including age (years), gender, educational level, marital status, place of residence, medical insurance, per capita monthly household income (CNY), postoperative time (months), Combined chronic diseases, TNM stage, stoma complication, adjuvant therapy (primarily radiotherapy and chemotherapy), preoperative occupation, and nature of work. Stoma complications are primarily defined as those evaluated by the stoma therapist and include, but are not limited to, the following: stoma stenosis, stoma prolapse, parastomal hernia, irritant dermatitis, and allergic dermatitis. The nature of work is mainly classified according to mental and physical labor. Some jobs may involve a combination of both, and we will clarify this and make a joint decision based on the patient’s situation.

Stoma Acceptance: We utilized the Stoma Acceptance Questionnaire (SAQ-C) to evaluate patients’ stoma acceptance. This questionnaire was developed by Bagnasco [24] and translated and revised into Chinese in 2020 for enterostomy patients in China. It comprises three dimensions with a total of 12 items assessing the importance, autonomy, and acceptance of the stoma by patients. The questionnaire is scored on a four-point Likert scale, with total scores ranging from 12 to 48; higher scores indicate greater acceptance of the stoma. The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient for the questionnaire exceeded 0.8, and in this study, it was 0.842.

Statistical analysis

All data in this study were statistically analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics software version 25.0 for Windows and R version 4.3.0. The significance level was set at α = 0.05.

First, quantitative data were summarized using mean ± standard deviation, qualitative data were presented using frequencies and percentages, and predictor variables were screened using univariate logistic regression.

Next, we conducted binary logistic regression analyses on variables that showed statistically significant results in univariate analyses to identify independent risk factors for return to work in patients with permanent enterostomies.

Then, we utilized R version 4.3.0 to construct the line graph model, represented as a nomogram.

Finally, we evaluated the model’s validity by employing the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC) to assess its resolution, calculate predicted probabilities, and determine the sensitivity and specificity of the model. We validated the calibration curve through repetitive self-sampling 1,000 times and examined the consistency between predicted and actual probabilities using the calibration curve and the Hosmer-Lemeshow test.

Results

Patients’ characteristics

A total of 293 patients were included in this study. The non-returned group comprised 206 patients (70.31%) with an average age of 50.81 ± 6.38 years, while the returned group consisted of 87 patients (29.69%) with an average age of 45.29 ± 5.95 years. There were 201 (68.60%) males and 92 (31.40%) females. The educational level was predominantly below junior high school, with 138 (47.10%) falling into this category. Marital status was mainly married (91.13%), with nearly equal numbers living in rural and urban areas, at 49.83% and 50.17%, respectively. Those with medical insurance predominated (88.05%), and the most common per capita monthly household income was between 2000 and 4000 (42.32%). Time after ostomy for more than 12 months was predominant (57.00%), most patients had no other chronic diseases (71.67%), TNM stage I was the most common (47.78%), those without stoma complications predominated (72.70%), and those receiving no adjuvant therapy were predominant (58.36%). Preoperative occupation was mainly farmer/worker (35.15%), and the nature of work was predominantly manual labor (40.61%). The stoma acceptance score was 33.14 ± 4.14 in the non-returned group and 36.28 ± 3.96 in the returned group, while the ostomy adjustment inventory score was 46.67 ± 7.59 in the non-returned group and 52.33 ± 7.98 in the returned group. Construction of the prediction model

The independent influences derived from the logistic regression analysis were input into R software to create a column chart model for predicting the return to work of patients with permanent intestinal stomas. The model is depicted in Fig. 1. The column chart includes seven predictor variables: age, educational level, postoperative time, stoma complication, adjuvant therapy, stoma acceptance, and ostomy adjustment inventory. The predictor variables in the chart are assigned a Points scale with corresponding scores. A vertical line is drawn between the values of the measured individuals’ variables and the Points scale to determine the corresponding scores. The total scores of the variables are summed to obtain the Total Points, from which the probability of returning to work can be derived.

Column line diagram of return-to-work prediction for patients with permanent enterostomies

Internal validation of predictive models

Differentiation

The area under the ROC curve was utilized to assess model differentiation, revealing that the area under the ROC curve (AUC) for this model was 0.916 with a 95% CI (0.884–0.947). Youden’s index was 0.731, the cutoff value was 0.228, sensitivity was 0.920, and specificity was 0.811, indicating good model differentiation. The results are illustrated in Fig. 2.

ROC curves for the return-to-work prediction model for patients with permanent enterostomies

Calibration

Bootstrap self-sampling was performed 1000 times to evaluate the model internally, and the Hosmer-Lemeshow test was employed to assess the model’s goodness-of-fit, yielding χ²=12.858, P = 0.117 > 0.05, which indicates a good model fit. The calibration curve demonstrated no significant deviation between predicted and actual results, suggesting that the model accurately predicted the incidence of postoperative return to work in patients with permanent enterostomies. Discussion

Predictive modeling of return to work for patients with permanent enterostomies is practical and prospective

In this study, we developed a predictive model for the return-to-work column chart of patients with permanent enterostomies based on seven independent factors identified through logistic regression to visualize the risk factors. The results showed a good model fit, and the indicators showed that the model had a strong predictive and discriminatory ability. Nursing staff can provide personalised, scientific, and effective rehabilitation programs for permanent intestinal stoma patients with varying characteristics to enhance patients’ stoma acceptance and psychosocial adjustment, build confidence in returning to work, and improve patients’ long-term prognosis.

Low rate of return to work for patients with permanent enterostomies

A study found that the return-to-work rate of cancer patients in developed countries ranges from 43 to 93% [26], while the return-to-work rate among Chinese cancer populations is between 21.35% and 83.4% [11, 27–31]. In this study, the return rate for 293 permanent enterostomy patients was 29.69%, which is relatively low. Gordon [32] investigated working adults with colorectal cancer, found that 33% of men and 40% of women were not working one year after diagnosis. Similarly, Bhalla [33] reported that one third of patients failed to return to work one year after colorectal cancer surgery, aligning with the findings of this study. The reasons for this may include the fact that colorectal cancer stoma surgery and adjuvant oncologic therapy are more physically and psychologically traumatic, requiring a longer recovery time for patients compared to other cancers. It has been suggested that the presence of a stoma is a significant factor hindering patients’ return to work [34]. On one hand, the existence of a stoma severely impacts patients’ self-esteem and body image, leading to feelings of shame and reluctance to engage with others, which hinders their return to work. On the other hand, current research on vocational rehabilitation interventions aimed at facilitating patients’ return to work in China has not yet been conducted, resulting in a low percentage of patients returning to work.

Factors influencing the return to work of patients with permanent enterostomies

Patients aged ≤ 45 years are more likely to return to work, possibly due to better physical functioning, improved adaptation to the disease, a stronger belief in recovery, and longer life paths ahead [35]. Additionally, the collectivist culture of China may encourage patients to prioritize family responsibilities and societal expectations, influencing their decision to return to work. It is recommended that future efforts focus on return-to-work guidance for young patients, including regular health assessments, stoma care advice, and physical activity recommendations, to ensure both physical and psychological recovery and to assist them in gradually adapting to the work environment.

Low educational level significantly hinders patients’ return to work [36]. This may be attributed to the fact that many are engaged in manual labor or temporary jobs, which limits their working capacity and reduces their employment options [37]. Nursing staff should enhance psychological counseling for patients with low literacy levels, utilizing educational manuals, video resources, and WeChat group chats to help them overcome obstacles and improve their employability and self-confidence. It is advisable to connect with local vocational schools and relevant government departments to provide basic education and vocational skills training, such as manual skills and service industry skills, tailored to the patients’ work backgrounds. Assistance should also be offered in formulating career plans and setting short- and long-term vocational goals, helping them gradually build confidence in returning to work and adapting to non-manual labor roles.

A postoperative period exceeding 12 months is more favorable for patients returning to work, aligning with findings from previous studies [38]. This may be attributed to the fact that as the postoperative duration increases, patients gradually accept and adapt to the presence of the stoma, gaining sufficient energy and stamina to contemplate a return to work. It is recommended that caregivers offer psychological counseling to patients in the early postoperative phase to facilitate their adaptation to the stoma, foster a sense of returning to work, and redirect their focus from the illness to employment. For instance, an interdisciplinary team comprising nurses, doctors, occupational therapists, and counselors could be formed to collaboratively provide comprehensive support to the patient. Team members can jointly assess the patient’s physical health, psychological condition, and work capacity, deliver professional psychological support and rehabilitation guidance, and assist the patient in making necessary workplace adjustments.

Patients with stoma complications have a lower rate of return to work, and studies have shown that these complications can impact patients’ adjustment and ability to work after re-entering the workforce [39], similar to the findings of this study. Statistically, 70% of patients may experience stoma complications [40], which can elevate the cost and complexity of stoma care, heighten patient frustration, and create barriers to returning to work. Ostomy specialist nurses should actively enhance patients’ post-discharge continuity of care by aligning resources with community health centers. For instance, community health promotion and stoma care training activities should be implemented to bolster patients’ stoma self-management skills. Additionally, modern information technology should be utilized to offer patients remote health management services, enabling timely adjustments to care plans through regular monitoring of patients’ health status. Online counseling and support should be provided to mitigate patients’ stoma complications, while community-based activities should be organized to strengthen patients’ social networks, thereby increasing their social engagement and sense of self-worth, ultimately aiding their return to work.

Patients undergoing adjuvant therapy often experience a low rate of return to work. Side effects such as fatigue, sleep disorders, bone marrow suppression, nausea, and vomiting can arise in patients receiving adjuvant treatments like radiotherapy following surgery [41]. Carlson’s implementation of mindfulness therapy effectively reduces chemotherapy-related fatigue, enhances sleep quality, alleviates gastrointestinal discomfort, and improves cognitive dysfunction in cancer patients, aiding their return to work [41]. Berntsen [42] discovered that resistance and endurance training led by a rehabilitation physician was also effective in mitigating chemotherapy-related fatigue and negative emotions, resulting in a significant reduction in time to return to work and an enhancement in patients’ work capabilities compared to the control group. In clinical practice, caregivers can offer patients comprehensive education on managing side effects, including how to identify and address common issues such as fatigue, nausea, and loss of appetite. Patients should be educated on dietary adjustments and encouraged to seek professional guidance from a dietitian to select easily digestible, nutrient-rich foods that support recovery and boost immunity. Patients should also be encouraged to participate in moderate physical activities, such as walking and yoga, when their physical condition allows, to strengthen their physical health, improve their mood, and foster a positive mindset to better cope with side effects.

This study found that a higher level of stoma acceptance and psychosocial adjustment significantly increases the likelihood of patients returning to work. Previous research has also identified a strong correlation between stoma acceptance and psychosocial adjustment; the better the level of stoma acceptance, the greater the chances of patients reintegrating into the workplace and society [43]. A high level of stoma acceptance indicates that the patient acknowledges changes in their self-image, experiences fewer negative emotions such as shame, and maintains a more positive outlook on life. Similarly, effective psychosocial adjustment enhances patients’ ability to adjust to life, fosters better interactions with others, and promotes integration into family and society. This underscores the need for clinical interventions aimed at enhancing patients’ stoma acceptance and psychosocial adjustment. For instance, Lim [44] developed a multimodal and multidimensional intervention approach that includes preoperative individual face-to-face psychoeducational conversations, health education brochures, and telephone follow-ups, all of which proved effective in improving stoma acceptance and patients’ quality of life. Additionally, certain cognitive-behavioral therapies and relaxation training can offer valuable psychological support for patients [45]. Nurses can further assist patients in adapting to their stoma by guiding them in selecting suitable stoma equipment. They can also encourage patients to engage in social activities by creating a WeChat group to enhance communication among patients and share success stories of returning to work, thereby aiding them in resuming their pre-illness social lives and facilitating their reintegration into the workforce.

Limitations

Although this study was designed to provide a comprehensive predictive model for assessing the likelihood of permanent colostomy patients returning to work, an important limitation is the potential for selection bias. We employed a convenience sampling method to select 293 patients from three tertiary hospitals in Liaoning Province. This approach may have resulted in a sample that was not fully representative of all patients with permanent colostomies, particularly those who did not receive care at these specific hospitals. Additionally, since the study was conducted at a specific time and location, it may not accurately reflect patients in different regions, healthcare systems, or at varying points in time. Therefore, extrapolation of the study results may be limited and should be approached with caution. Future studies could utilize a broader range of sampling strategies, including multicenter collaborations and random sampling, to mitigate selection bias and enhance the generalizability of the model. Longitudinal follow-up studies can also be conducted to explore the dynamics of patients’ return to work over time. Due to the limited sample size, this study was not proportionally divided into modeling and validation groups, and only internal validation was performed. The sample size can be increased in the future, allowing for an appropriate division of modeling and validation groups, and external validation can be employed to further assess the accuracy and effectiveness of the model.

Conclusion

This study developed a column-line diagram model to assess the return to work of patients with permanent enterostomies, based on independent influencing factors identified through logistic regression. This model demonstrates strong differentiation and accuracy, offering a quantitative and practical tool for determining whether patients with permanent enterostomies can successfully return to work. Currently, research on the return to work for patients with permanent intestinal stomas remains in the exploratory phase in China, and establishing a vocational rehabilitation intervention program tailored to China’s national context is a key focus for future clinical research.

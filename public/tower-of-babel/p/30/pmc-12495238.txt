Clinical and molecular characteristics of HER2-low triple-negative breast cancer: insights from multi-omics analysis
Authors: Yinlong Yang, Liangwei Pan, Wenzhe Zhou, Zhimin Shao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe emergence of novel human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-targeting drugs has provided a new therapeutic option for HER2-low triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) patients who had limited treatment choices previously. However, the biological implications and prognostic significance of HER2-low status in TNBC are still not fully appreciated.MethodsUtilizing a single-center multi-omics cohort of TNBC patients that includes whole exome sequencing and RNA sequencing data, this study aims to explore the clinical and molecular characteristics between HER2-low and HER2-0 TNBC.ResultsThe study comprised 207 patients with HER2-low TNBC and 153 patients with HER2-0 TNBC. Findings revealed that HER2-low tumors exhibited lower tumor grade, more nodal involvement and lower Ki-67 index compared to HER2-0 tumors. Additionally, HER2-low TNBC were associated with more mutations in PIK3CA and its corresponding pathways, a more prominent clock-like mutation signature, along with activation of androgen receptor (AR)-related pathways and fatty acid metabolism. While HER2-low status did not significantly influence recurrence-free survival (RFS) across the entire study population, HER2-low patients showed significant better RFS compared to HER2-0 patients within the luminal androgen receptor (LAR) subtype. This distinction was also evident at molecular level.ConclusionHER2-low TNBC demonstrated unique clinical, transcriptomic and genomic characteristics when contrasted with HER2-0 TNBC. In the LAR subtype, a clear molecular and prognostic discrepancy between HER2-0 and HER2-low tumors was identified.

Introduction

Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) is a vital prognostic biomarker in breast cancer, guiding the classification and management of breast cancer in clinical practice [1]. According to the 2018 American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO)/College of American Pathologists (CAP) guidelines, HER2-positive breast cancer is identified by an immunohistochemistry (IHC) score of 3+ or 2+ with HER2 gene amplification via in situ hybridization (ISH), while tumors with IHC 0, 1+, or 2+ without amplification are classified as HER2 negative [2]. HER2-targeting agents, including anti-HER2 monoclonal antibodies, antibody drug conjugates (ADCs), and tyrosine kinase inhibitors, have been developed for treating patients with HER2-positive tumors [[3], [4], [5], [6]].

Emerging evidence suggests that a specific subgroup of breast cancer, characterized by an IHC score of 1+ or 2+ with a negative fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) result and referred to as HER2-low tumors, can benefit from novel HER2-targeting ADCs [[7], [8], [9], [10], [11]]. Numerous studies have been conducted to compare the clinicopathological and biological features, therapeutic response and survival outcomes of HER-0 and HER2-low breast cancer [[12], [13], [14], [15], [16], [17], [18], [19]]. However, there is no agreement yet on whether HER2-low should be considered a distinct entity.

Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) are a subset of breast cancers that lack expression of estrogen receptor (ER) and progesterone receptor (PR) and associated with the absence of HER2 overexpression/amplification, featuring higher biological aggressiveness, lower responses to endocrine or anti-HER2 therapy and worse prognosis [[20], [21], [22]]. HER2-low breast cancers account for more than a third of TNBC [[14], [15], [16],19]. A few studies have found favorable prognostic value of HER2-low status in TNBC [19,[23], [24], [25], [26], [27], [28], [29], [30]], while others did not [15,27,[31], [32], [33], [34], [35], [36]]. However, little has been known about the underlying molecular background of HER2-low TNBC.

In the current study, the clinicopathological variables, transcriptomic patterns, mutation profiling, and prognosis were analyzed in TNBC according to HER2 status, based on multi-omics data from TNBC patients in the Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center (FUSCC) cohort. Our integrated analysis revealed that HER2-low TNBC were associated with lower tumor grades, increased nodal involvement, a lower Ki-67 index, somatic mutations in PIK3CA and corresponding pathways as well as activation of lipid metabolism, sterol hormones and Erb-B2 receptor tyrosine kinase 2 (ERBB2) pathways. Our study also demonstrated a significant increase in the proportion of luminal androgen receptor (LAR) subtype in Her2-low TNBC compared to Her2-0 TNBC. Interestingly, we observed a discrepancy between HER2-0 and HER2-low tumors within a group of TNBC characterized by androgen receptor signaling, from both molecular and prognostic perspectives, which indicates that endocrine therapy is also a potential treatment strategy.

Patients and methods

Patient cohort and selection criteria

The current study included 360 patients from the Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center Triple Negative Breast Cancer multiomics cohort [37,38]. The detailed inclusion criteria were as follows: (i) female patients; (ii) patients with available pathological information regarding ER, PR and HER2 status through immunochemical analysis and ISH for HER2 status only; (iii) patients with no evidence of metastasis or recurrence at the time of diagnosis. All biopsy specimens were obtained from primary breast tumors prior to systemic therapy. The primary tumor samples were obtained during surgical resection and subsequently subjected to standardized pathological examination and multiomics profiling. Pathology examination of ER, PR, HER2 and Ki67 was performed by the Department of Pathology of FUSCC. All pathological assessments were independently verified by two experienced pathologists from the same panel of three specialists trained in breast cancer pathology in accordance with the latest ASCO/CAP guidelines at the time. Discrepancies were resolved through consensus review. ER/PR negativity was defined as <1 % positively stained cells in IHC testing. Tumors with weak to moderate HER2 protein expression (IHC 1+ or 2+) and no gene amplification by ISH were classified as HER2-low. Tumors with no detectable HER2 protein by IHC and no gene amplification are classified as HER2-0.

Clinical data and molecular profiling

Recurrence-free survival (RFS) was defined as the interval from the date of surgery to the initial occurrence of invasive breast cancer recurrence, diagnosis of contralateral breast cancer, or mortality from any cause. For patients who did not encounter any such events, their data was censored at the date of their last follow-up. The follow-up for our cohort was completed as of June 30, 2017, with a median follow-up duration of 45 months. The clinicopathological data included age at diagnosis, menopause status, tumor size, lymph node status, histology, tumor grade, Ki-67 index, intratumoral tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (iTILs), stromal tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (sTILs), tumor fibrosis, tumor necrosis, and adjuvant chemotherapy and radiation. The fibrotic focus (FF) is a centrally located, scar-like area within carcinoma tissue, which is considered a manifestation of exaggerated reactive stroma formation. The presence of FF in breast cancer was evaluated according to the previously reported criteria [39]: (1) grade 0, samples without FF; (2) grade 1, a small amount of collagen fibers with abundant fibroblasts in FF; (3) grade 3, mainly composed of hyalinized collagen fibers; and (4) grade 2, intermediate between grade 1 and 3, with fibroblasts and collagen fibers admixed in different proportions. Based on this assessment, fibrosis grading in TNBC was classified into three tiers: No fibrosis (absence of FF, corresponding to FF grade 0), Low fibrosis (FF grades 1 and 2), and High fibrosis (FF grade 3). The grouping of grades 1 and 2 into the “low fibrosis' category was justified by the absence of clear histopathological demarcation between these two subgroups. RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) and whole exome sequencing (WES) data were available for 360 and 242 (HER2-0, n = 106; HER2-low, n = 136) patients, respectively. Each patient provided written informed consent, and all tissue samples included in this study were obtained with approval from the independent ethics committee/Institutional Review Board at FUSCC Ethical Committee. The study was conducted in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration.

Reassessment and validation of HER2 classification

HER2 IHC staining was performed using the Ventana BenchMark Ultra automated stainer and the Ventana Ultra View universal DAB detection kit (Ventana Medical Systems Inc., Roche, Tucson, AZ, USA). For specimens with indeterminate IHC results, further analysis was conducted via a dual-probe FISH assay on the same tissue sample, utilizing the PathVysion HER2 DNA probe kit (Vysis Inc., Downers Grove, IL). To ensure objective assessment, two experienced pathologists independently evaluated the IHC and FISH results. This approach aimed to enhance the reliability of HER2 status determination.

To facilitate rescoring, the condition of archived HER2 IHC slides was first evaluated. The staining quality of these historical slides was checked using external control slides from tissue microarray core samples as an IHC reference. The slides were found to be well-preserved, with minimal or no noticeable fading. Subsequently, three experienced pathologists from the FUSCC, who had received specialized training from the diagnostic reagent supplier, independently reviewed the slides. For samples with discrepant scoring results, final assessments were resolved through a consensus session. Pathologists were blinded to the original IHC scores and ISH results, ensuring that each HER2 IHC score was determined objectively and accurately. Following the 2018 ASCO/CAP guidelines, HER2-0 was identified as an IHC score of 0, while HER2-low was determined by an IHC score of 1+ or 2+ with no ISH amplification.

Statistical analysis

Differences between continuous variables were assessed using the Student's t-test, Wilcoxon rank-sum test, and Kruskal-Wallis test. Categorical variables were compared using Pearson's Chi-square test or Fisher exact tests when necessary. The false discovery rate correction (FDR) was applied in multiple testing to reduce the false-positive rates. The Kaplan-Meier method and Cox regression analysis were used to assess Recurrence-free survival. All tests were two-sided, and a p-value less than 0.05 indicated statistical significance unless otherwise stated. All statistical analyses were performed in R version 3.6.1.

Upon comparison between HER2-0 and HER2-low TNBC, patients with HER2-low tumors showed a higher incidence of nodal involvement (P = 0.046; Fig. 1A) and lower tumor grades (P = 0.035; Fig. 1C). Additionally, significant differences were observed in the HER2-low patients exhibiting lower Ki-67 index, indicating a potential difference in proliferative activity (P = 0.002; Fig. 1E). When patients were stratified based on HER2 IHC levels, lymph node status did not show a significant correlation (P = 0.221; Fig. 1B), whereas tumor grade remained significantly associated with HER2 IHC scores (P = 0.030; Fig. 1D). Furthermore, lower Ki67 levels were observed in tumors with HER2 1+ (P = 0.014) and HER2 2+ (P = 0.003) compared to HER2-0 tumors (Fig. 1F), suggesting a possible biological distinction based on HER2 expression levels. There were no statistically significant differences between HER2-0 and HER2-low TNBC with respect to age at diagnosis, menopausal status, tumor size, histology type, iTILs, sTILs, tumor fibrosis, tumor necrosis and proportion of patients receiving chemotherapy or radiation (Table 1). Transcriptomic features of HER2-low TNBC

Transcriptome-directed subtyping has been instrumental in elucidating the heterogeneity of TNBC, thereby enabling the identification of distinct biological characteristics and the development of targeted therapeutic strategies for each subtype. In the current study, distinct subtype distributions were observed between HER2-0 and HER2-low TNBC (P < 0.001 for FUSCC TNBC subtypes [37] and P < 0.001 for the PAM50 subtypes; Fig. 2A and B). Specifically, HER2-low TNBC exhibited a higher prevalence of the LAR subtype (30.9 % versus 11.1 %), as well as the PAM50 non-basal subtype (43.8 % versus 26.8 %) in comparison to the HER2-0 TNBC. To gain a more in-depth understanding of the biological characteristics of TNBC patients with varying HER2 statuses, we conducted gene set variation analysis (GSVA) to investigate the enrichment of specific gene sets. In HER2-low TNBC, we identified 1616 gene sets that were significantly upregulated (FDR <0.01), with a recurrent observation of gene sets linked to lipid metabolism, sterol hormones, and the ERBB2 pathway (Fig. 2C). Moreover, our analysis revealed 384 significantly enriched gene sets (FDR <0.01) in HER2-0 TNBC, with a particular focus on gene sets related to chromatin regulation and histone modification (Fig. 2C).

Considering the established association between HER2-low expression and activated AR-related signaling, we further investigated the enriched pathways in the LAR subtype of TNBC with regards to HER2-low and HER2-0 tumors. Our findings revealed that, within the LAR subtype, HER2-low TNBC was predominantly associated with the activation of glycometabolism and the PI3K signaling pathway, whereas HER2-0 tumors exhibited enrichment of the Wnt signaling pathway and histone methylation processes (Fig. 2D).

Mutation profile of HER2-low TNBC

We used the genomics data of 242 TNBCs samples from the FUSCC cohort to compare somatic mutation landscape among different HER2 subgroups. The mutation landscape of 106 HER2-0 patients and 136 HER2-low patients was showed in Fig. 3A. A comparative analysis revealed that HER2-low TNBC exhibited a higher frequency of mutations in PIK3CA (24.8 % versus 9.3 %, P = 0.003), TNXB (8.1 % versus 1.9 %, P = 0.034), FREM1, and HUWE1 (both 4.4 % versus 0 %, P = 0.029) compared to HER2-0 patients (Fig. 3B). Patients with HER2-0 tumor had significantly more frequent mutations in 18 genes including EYS, CDHR3, GRIK4, SYNJ1, RB1, PLEC etc. (all P < 0.05) (Fig. 3B). To achieve a more inclusive comprehension of the mutation landscape, we examined the mutational profile by signaling pathways. Using ten oncogenic signaling pathways [40], the fraction of samples with at least one alteration in each of the 10 signaling pathways was calculated for each subtype. The PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway was significantly more frequently affected by nonsynonymous somatic mutations in HER2-low tumors than in HER2-0 tumors (44.2 % versus 30.1 %, P = 0.048; Fig. 3C). No other signaling pathway was observed to have a significantly higher mutation rate in HER2-0 TNBC. However, no difference was found in HER2 1+ TNBC compared to HER2-0 TNBC (Supplementary Fig. S1D),a higher mutation rate of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling was found in HER2 2+ TNBC compared to HER2 1+ or HER2-0 TNBC (HER2 2+ versus 1+: 51.1 % versus 36.3 %, P = 0.031; HER2 2+ versus 0: 51.1 % versus 30.2 %, P = 0.023; Supplementary Fig. S5E–F).

We further investigated the mutated genes and signaling pathways according to HER2 expression and transcription-based subtypes in 242 TNBC patients with available genomics data, including 104 BLIS (HER2-0, n = 54; HER2-low, n = 50), 51 IM (HER2-0, n = 31; HER2-low, n = 20), 56 LAR (HER2-0, n = 12; HER2-low, n = 44) and 31 MES (HER2-0, n = 9; HER2-low, n = 22) patients. The top 20 most frequently mutated genes in current cohort were analyzed. Notably, in LAR subgroup, 5 of the 20 genes were found to have statistically significant differences in mutation rates between HER2-low and HER2-0 tumors (Fig. 3D). Specifically, HER2-low patients harbored more PIK3CA mutations (59.1 % versus 25.0 %, P = 0.036), whereas HER2-0 patients exhibited fewer mutations in TTN, ABCA13, CSMD3, and CACNA1E (all P < 0.05) (Fig. 3D). We also found higher mutation frequency of KMT2C (P = 0.049) in HER2-0 than HER2 low TNBC, in basal-like immune-suppressed (BLIS) subgroup, as well as higher mutation frequency of DNAH9 (P = 0.022) in HER2-0 than HER2 low TNBC, in mesenchymal-like (MES) subgroup (Fig. 3D). With respect to mutated signaling pathways, HER2-low patients had more mutations in PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway (72.7 % versus 33.3 %, P = 0.006) and fewer mutations in Hippo signaling pathway (9.1 % versus 41.7 %, P = 0.012) than HER2-0 patients in LAR subgroup (Fig. 3E). Moreover, in the MES subtype, HER2-low TNBC showed a higher mutation rate of Notch signaling compared to HER2-0 TNBC (50.0 % versus 11.1 %, P = 0.012, Fig. 3E). These results suggest activation of the PIK3CA pathway among HER2-low TNBC, and the LAR subtype showed greater discrepancies in gene mutation profiles between the HER2-low and HER2-0 tumors than that of other transcription-based subtypes.

Despite distinct mutational profiles among TNBC with varying HER2 expressions, the comparison of their TMB demonstrated no statistical difference (1.21 versus 0.96, P = 0.48; Fig. 4A). Similar findings were observed when HER2-low patients were stratified into HER2 1+ and 2+ (Fig. 4B). We further analyzed the mutation subtypes to acquire a deeper insight into the underlying mechanism of mutation generation among different HER2 groups. It was found that HER2-0 patients had a higher percentage of homologous recombination deficiency (HRD) subtype (59.1 % versus 33.9 %, P = 0.003) than HER2-low patients (Fig. 4C). In contrast, the percentage of clock-like subtype [41] was higher in HER2-low patients (32.1 % versus 11.2 %, P = 0.003; Fig. 4C). Upon categorizing patients according to their HER2 IHC scores, the dissimilarity in the distribution of mutation subtypes remained statistically significant (Fig. 4D). prognosis of HER2-low TNBC

Recurrence-free survival was compared between different HER2 groups, with a median follow-up of 45.3 months (interquartile range [IQR]: 34.4–58.9) for the entire cohort. In all patients, no statistically significant differences in RFS were observed between the HER2-low and HER2-0 groups (P = 0.72; Fig. 5A). Comparable results were obtained when stratifying patients based on their HER2 IHC levels (P = 0.83; Fig. 5B). However, upon further stratification by transcription-based subtypes, we observed a significant improvement in RFS for HER2-low patients compared to HER2-0 patients within the LAR subgroup (Fig. 5E). In contrast, no statistically significant differences in RFS were noted between HER2-0 and HER2-low patients in other subtypes, including basal-like immune-suppressed (BLIS), immunomodulatory (IM), and mesenchymal-like (MES) (BLIS: P = 0.52; IM: P = 0.15; MES: P = 0.97; Fig. 5C, D, 5F). These observations were further supported by univariate Cox hazard analysis, which revealed no significant differences in RFS between HER2-0 and HER2-low TNBC patients overall (Supplementary Table S1). However, within the LAR subtype, HER2-low status was associated with improved RFS in both unadjusted and adjusted models (Table 2). Discussion

The present study, based on our single-center multiomic cohort of 360 TNBC patients, offers a comprehensive understanding of the clinical and molecular characteristics of HER2-low breast cancer in this population. Patients with HER2-low disease account for the vast majority (59.7 %) of the current TNBC cohort. The majority of HER2-low tumors, comprising a vast proportion at 67.6 %, displayed an IHC score of 1+. Our clinicopathological observations suggest that HER2-low patients had a greater propensity for tumors with lower tumor grades, increased nodal involvement, and reduced expression levels of Ki-67.

A recent pooled analysis based on 1162 TNBC observed higher rates of TP53 mutations in HER2-low tumors [19]. Conversely, a study including 59 Chinese TNBC patients (30 HER2-0 and 29 HER2-low) reported the opposite, with higher rates of TP53 mutations in HER2-0 TNBC [18]. Both cohorts indicated more frequent mutations in the PIK3CA gene in HER2-low TNBC [18,19]. However, none of the outcomes reached a statistically difference. In our study for potential drivers specific to HER2-low TNBC using genomic data, we found a significantly higher frequency of PIK3CA mutation in HER2-low compared to HER2-0. Moreover, we observed a clear trend of increasing PIK3CA mutation rates with HER2 IHC scores. Nearly half of the HER2-low tumors analyzed exhibited somatic mutations in the phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) signaling pathway, indicating the potential benefit of PI3K and AKT inhibitors, as previously reported in clinical trials [42,43]. According to the mutational signatures, HER2-low patients were found to be composed of approximately equal proportions of HRD and clock-like mutation subtypes, with each accounting for around 30 %. Conversely, HRD subtypes represent the largest proportion in HER2-0 tumors. One of our recent studies revealed a greater prevalence of clock-like signatures in elderly TNBC, potentially indicating long-term selection and filtering of mutations in this population [44]. The higher average age of HER2-low TNBC patients may partly account for the preponderance of clock-like signatures, despite the lack of statistical significance (P = 0.095).

The transcriptional features of HER2-low TNBC were preliminarily outlined in a previous study based on PAM50 gene expression data from multiple datasets, suggesting that TNBC/HER2-low tumors are less likely to be a distinct biological entity than HR-positive/HER2-low tumors [15]. Building on this foundation, a comprehensive genomic and transcriptomic dataset of TNBC has further delineated the characteristics of HER2-low TNBC [45]. In current study, we performed a more comprehensive analysis of the transcriptional profile of TNBC based on HER2 status using whole transcriptome data, which indicated activated AR-related pathways in HER2-low patients. This finding is in line with the observed discrepancies in mRNA subtype distribution, where HER2-low TNBCs are more frequently associated with the LAR subtype, especially among patients with HER2 IHC scores of 2+. This result suggested that HER2-low patients might be potential candidates for antiandrogen therapy [46]. Our findings also revealed an upregulation in fatty acid metabolism in HER2-low TNBC, highlighting the potential effectiveness of lipid synthesis inhibitors in this group of patients [47]. This is corroborated by study showing that fatty acid metabolism promotes breast cancer metastasis, with studies indicating that adipocyte-derived lipids trigger cancer cell migration via HIF-1α activation in cancer cells [48], and that targeting fatty acid oxidation can sensitize cancer to radiation therapy [49]. These findings have implications for the development of targeted therapies, including antiandrogen treatments and inhibitors of lipid metabolism.

The prognostic value of HER2-low for TNBC has been investigated in several retrospective studies, with conflicting outcomes have been reported [15,19,23,25,[27], [28], [29],[31], [32], [33], [34], [35]]. According to the findings of the previous cohort study, HER2-low tumors exhibited a more favorable disease-free survival (DFS) compared to HER2-0 tumors, with a more noticeable effect observed within the TNBC subgroup [19]. This observation is supported by another real-world cohort including 4634 TNBC, which also reported a better 10.13039/100024533DFS for HER2-low patients [23]. On the contrary, other studies failed to demonstrate any survival benefits associated with HER2-low compared to HER2-0 TNBC [15,27,[33], [34], [35]] It should be noted that the two studies that reported a superior prognostic value of HER2-low status in TNBC only enrolled patients diagnosed with early-stage breast cancer. This is further supported by a systematic review and meta-analysis conducted a comprehensive analysis of research investigating the correlation between HER2 expression levels and treatment outcomes in early-stage TNBC patients. The analysis found no difference between non-metastatic TNBC HER2-0 and HER2-low in terms of pathological complete response, but HER2-low was associated with better prognosis than HER2-0 [49].

Despite the absence of significant differences in RFS among HER2-low and HER2-0 TNBC in current study, consistent with previous studies [15,24,26,27,[33], [34], [35]], a noteworthy association has been identified between HER2-low status and improved RFS in the LAR subtype, as reported in a study that evaluated the distribution of intrinsic subtypes in HER2-low tumors according to ERBB2 mRNA levels [23]. This distinction was also noted at the molecular level, where HER2-low and HER2-0 tumors in the LAR subtype exhibited greater disparities in the mutation frequency of highly mutated genes, including PIK3CA, TTN, ABCA13, CSMD3, and CACNA1E, in contrast to any other mRNA subtypes. Similar results were obtained when we analyzed the mutation rates of signaling pathways. TNBC is a group of breast cancer demonstrating high heterogeneity owing to its definition through diagnosis of exclusion [50,51]. LAR tumors are typically display a luminal-like gene expression profile and low proliferation, despite the lack of expression of ER and PR [37,52]. Our findings suggested that HER2-low might represent a distinct biological entity within a group of TNBC distinguished by high AR expression, from both a molecular and a prognostic perspective. Additionally, this might suggest that elevated AR levels are a prerequisite for ERBB2 pathway activation, resulting in divergences in molecular characteristics and clinical outcomes among the LAR tumors with varying HER2 status. Further research is needed to fully understand the crosstalk between HER2 and AR in TNBC and its potential implications for targeted therapies.

The current study had some limitations. First, the retrospective nature of the study may have introduced potential biases and confounding variables that limit the reliability and generalizability of the findings. Due to the historical nature of the patient cohort, the patients followed up in our study had not received ADCs treatment. Second, as this study was based on a single-center cohort, it may not be entirely representative of other populations or settings, and therefore one must exercise caution when interpreting the results. The study might have lacked the statistical power to detect significant differences within certain subgroups due to inadequate sample sizes, potentially resulting in the omission of crucial findings. Further validation through a multi-center, prospective study with a larger sample size is warranted. Moreover, this study lacked assessment of HER2 status in recurrent samples, which may be closely related to the dynamic changes in HER2 status and the heterogeneity of HER2 expression with disease progression.

In summary, through genomic and transcriptome analysis of a large single-center cohort, our study elucidated the unique biological characteristics of HER2-low tumors in TNBC. HER2-low TNBC was distinguished from HER2-0 TNBC by several features: lower tumor grades, increased nodal involvement, reduced expression of Ki-67, higher mutation rates of the PIK3CA gene and its corresponding pathways, a more prominent clock-like mutation signature, and activation of AR-related pathways and fatty acid metabolism. Despite HER2-low status demonstrating no significant impact on RFS in the overall study population, HER2-low patients exhibited better RFS compared to HER2-0 patients in the LAR subtype. This distinction was further observed at the molecular level.

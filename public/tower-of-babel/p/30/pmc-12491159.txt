Protocol to perform assay for chromatin-bound condensates by exploratory sequencing in mouse embryonic stem cells
Authors: Zeran Jia, Jiawei Yu, Yinqing Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryChromatin-related protein condensates are integral to nuclear functions including transcriptional regulation and chromatin structure. Here, we present a protocol for detecting the occupancy of chromatin-associated condensates using the assay for chromatin-bound condensates by exploratory sequencing (ACC-seq) in mouse embryonic stem cells (mESCs). The workflow encompasses cell preparation, condensate modulation, library construction, and comprehensive sequencing analysis, enabling unbiased, genome-wide mapping of potential condensate occupancy across diverse chromatin states.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to He et al.1

Before you begin

ACC-Seq leverages Tn5 transposition under three chromatin states—native, fixed, and 1,6-hexanediol + fixed—to detect DNA regions embedded in condensates by their “deep U-shape” accessibility pattern. This protocol is optimized for mouse ESCs but can be adapted to other cell types with minor timing adjustments.

Innovation

ACC-seq delivers an antibody-independent, genome-wide mapping of chromatin-associated biomolecular condensates occupancy. Unlike ChIP- or CUT&Tag-based approaches that require target-specific antibodies and often miss weak or transient interactions, ACC-seq immobilizes condensate assemblies in situ through a two-step 1,6-hexanediol in combination with paraformaldehyde fixation. A transposase tagmentation then integrates adapters into DNA segments embedded within condensate microenvironments, converting latent condensate occupancy events into a sequencing-ready signal.

Methodological advances include:

By obviating immunoprecipitation, ACC-seq profiles condensates of different compositions in a single experiment, revealing potential co-occupancy patterns that multi-antibody workflows cannot resolve.

Optimized protocol enables robust peak calling from as few as 1 × 105 mESCs.

The pipeline combines k-means clustering of genome-wide accessibility profiles with a differential accessibility metric (compare accessibility between fix and 1,6-Hex + fix conditions), yielding a catalogue of putative condensate-occupied loci.

Collectively, ACC-seq provides the field of the condensate biology with a quantitative, genome-scale interrogation method, enabling researchers to pinpoint condensate-occupied regions, link condensate occupancy to gene regulation.

Expected outcomes

After library amplification, DNA concentration of each sample is typically within the range of 1 ng/μL to 30 ng/μL. Lower library concentration may lead to lower yield of NGS, while higher library concentration may indicate potential PCR bias in libraries. Library length distribution typically has an enrichment within the range of 100 bp to 300 bp (Figure 3).Figure 3Quantitative metrics of ACC-seqTSS enrichment plot (A) and fragment length distribution (B) of ACC-seq data in native, fix and 1,6-hex+fix condition.

Quantitative metrics of ACC-seq

TSS enrichment plot (A) and fragment length distribution (B) of ACC-seq data in native, fix and 1,6-hex+fix condition.

After data processing, data quality can be assessed by quantifying TSS enrichment, mitochondrial read alignment rate and duplication rate. TSS enrichment is a quality metric that quantifies how strongly sequencing reads accumulate at transcription start sites compared with nearby background regions. In the mESCs ACC-seq data, the TSS enrichment in the native group exceeds 20, and the TSS enrichment of fix and 1,6-hex + fix group exceeds 10 (Figure 3). Mitochondrial read alignment rate is the proportion of total reads that map to the mitochondrial genome, whereas duplication rate is the fraction of sequencing reads that are PCR duplicates of one another. In the mESCs ACC-seq data, the mitochondrial read alignment rate is under 10% and the duplication rate is under 30%. A pronounced “deep-U-shape” pattern of accessibility change between different conditions can be observed in one or more K-means clusters. In mESCs ACC-seq data, within the peak set exhibiting a “deep U-shape” pattern, the average accessibility in the 1,6-hex + fix group was higher than in the fix group (average fold change > 1.2).

Limitations

ACC-seq depends on fixation to “freeze” condensates and block Tn5 access. Standard paraformaldehyde (PFA) crosslinking cannot fully capture condensates formed by proteins that do not contact DNA directly (e.g., MED1), due to PFA’s short spacer arm. This may lead to limited detection sensitivity for some condensates.

It has been reported that 1,6-hex affects not only hydrophobic interactions in condensates, but also those interactions among cytoskeletal organization, membrane integrity and enzymatic function upon prolonged exposure.13,14,15 As a result of this, prolonged treatment (e.g. 30 min) of 1,6-hex can induce apoptosis and other side effects, and therefore cautions should be taken with interpretation of data obtained with modified 1,6-hex conditions.

ACC-seq can detect protein-condensate occupancy in open chromatin regions, but it is not suitable for assaying condensates in closed chromatin such as heterochromatin. This limitation of ACC-seq in heterochromatin stems from two factors. First, 1,6-hexanediol cannot fully dissolve heterochromatin-associated condensates—such as HP1 foci16—because these assemblies often exhibit solid- or gel-like material properties.17 Second, Tn5 transposase preferentially works on nucleosome-depleted regions (e.g., promoters and enhancers),9 limiting its application in densely packed heterochromatin. These limitations might be overcome by combining ACC-seq with more potent condensate-disrupting reagents and higher-activity nucleases (e.g., MNase) to probe accessibility perturbation within heterochromatin.

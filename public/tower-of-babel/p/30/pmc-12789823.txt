30-Day Outcomes of a Controlled-Bending Delivery System for TAVR in Severe Aortic Stenosis
Authors: Yang Chen, Zhongyu Wang, Baihua Sun, Guoliang Chen, Pinghai Zhang, Jicheng Xi, Yongjian Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis report presents the first-in-human experience with a 3-dimensional controlled-bending, fully retrievable delivery system for transfemoral transcatheter aortic valve replacement in severe aortic stenosis with anatomical challenge. In a prospective single-center series (September-December 2023) of 6 high-risk patients (mean age: 68.0 ± 3.9 years, 84% bicuspid valve, all with complex features such as horizontal heart or dilated ascending aorta), the device success rate was 100%, implantation was coaxial (implantation depth difference: 2.32 ± 1.53 mm), and postprocedural paravalvular leak was none to trivial. Hemodynamics improved substantially (gradient decreased from 75.5 ± 12.3 to 7.9 ± 2.1 mm Hg, effective orifice area increased from 0.61 ± 0.15 to 2.26 ± 0.34 cm2; P < 0.001). There were no deaths or strokes at 30 days postprocedure, and all patients reached NYHA functional class I or II. These early results suggest that controlled-bending delivery may enhance the precision and safety of transcatheter aortic valve replacement in complex anatomy, particularly with respect to the bicuspid valve.Key words: aortic valve, bicuspid aortic valve, treatment, valve replacement

Transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) has transformed the treatment of severe aortic stenosis (AS). However, complex anatomical features, such as a horizontal heart, bicuspid aortic valve (BAV), or dilated ascending aorta, often complicate the procedure, leading to suboptimal valve placement, paravalvular leak (PVL), or vascular injury.1 Conventional self-expanding valves and single-plane adjustable systems cannot achieve precise coaxial alignment.

The VitaFlow III device (MicroPort CardioFlow Medtech Co Ltd) provides a groundbreaking solution as the world's first 3-dimensional adjustable bending, retrievable delivery system for TAVR.2 This is the third generation of the original valve delivery system. In this first-in-human study, we applied the device and evaluated its feasibility, safety, and 30-day outcome in high-risk patients with AS, focusing on overcoming challenging anatomies.

Methods

This prospective single-center study was conducted from September to December 2023 at Shanxi Cardiovascular Hospital, China. Six high-risk patients with severe AS were enrolled. Inclusion required an annulus diameter of 17 to 30 mm and coronary height ≥10 mm; patients with left ventricular ejection fraction <20% or access vessel diameter <5.7 mm were excluded.

Preoperative computed tomography using 3mensio software (Pie Medical Imaging) was used to identify high-risk anatomical features, including horizontal heart (annulus angle ≥60°), sharp-angled aortic arch (curvature diameter ≤60 mm, angle ≤90°), and ascending aorta dilation (diameter ≥50 mm). The VitaFlow III features a 3-dimensional adjustable bending catheter (retrievable up to 3 times). Procedures were conducted via femoral access (22-F sheath) under general anesthesia, with postdeployment angiography used to assess implantation depth.

The primary endpoint was device success per VARC-3 (Valve Academic Research Consortium-3) criteria. Secondary endpoints included 30-day mortality, stroke, PVL, and hemodynamic improvement.

Results

Among the 6 patients (mean age: 68.0 ± 3.9 years, 50% male, mean Society of Thoracis Surgeons mortality risk score: 5.68 ± 1.2), 84% had BAV and 100% had complex anatomies; these included horizontal heart in 3 patients, sharp-angled aortic arch in 1 patient, and ascending aorta dilation in 3 patients. All patients successfully received transcatheter heart valve implantation, achieving a 100% device success rate, with no vascular injuries or coronary obstructions (Video 1, Video 2, Video 3). In one patient, the device was retrieved twice to optimize positioning. Postprocedure assessments showed none to trivial PVL, with excellent coaxial alignment (implantation depth difference: 2.32 ± 1.53 mm) (Figure 1). Hemodynamic outcomes markedly improved, the gradient dropped from 75.5 ± 12.3 to 7.9 ± 2.1 mm Hg, and the effective orifice area increased from 0.61 ± 0.15 to 2.26 ± 0.34 cm2 (P < 0.001). At 30 days postprocedure, no deaths, strokes, or major complications occurred, and all patients reached NYHA functional class I or II.

Discussion

This study confirms the feasibility and safety of the VitaFlow III device in high-risk patients with AS, particularly those with BAV and complex anatomical challenges. Its innovative three-dimensional adjustable bending technology ensures precise coaxial alignment, effectively addressing issues posed by horizontal hearts and dilated ascending aortas.1 Compared with traditional self-expanding valves, the device offers superior PVL reduction and implantation precision, positioning it as an ideal option for complex AS cases, especially in populations with a high prevalence of BAV.

The limitations of this study include its single-center design, small sample size, and short-term follow-up. Larger, multicenter trials with extended observation periods are required to validate these findings. Nevertheless, the three-dimensional technology of the VitaFlow III device marks a significant leap forward in TAVR, delivering an optimized solution for patients with challenging anatomies.

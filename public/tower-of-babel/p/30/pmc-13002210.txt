Anterior segment optical coherence tomography assessment of Schlemm’s canal before and after Descemet membrane endothelial keratoplasty: A retrospective observational study
Authors: Fatma Isil Sozen-Delil, Burak Tanyildiz, Erdi Karadag
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Purpose:This study aimed to evaluate Schlemm’s canal (SC) parameters using anterior segment optical coherence tomography (AS-OCT) in eyes undergoing Descemet membrane endothelial keratoplasty (DMEK).Design:This is a retrospective, cross-sectional, observational study.Methods:The study included 22 eyes from 22 patients with pseudophakic bullous keratopathy, with a mean age of 70 ± 11.4 years. AS-OCT (Triton) scans were performed with the patient fixating nasally; horizontal images were obtained from the temporal limbus to visualize SC.Results:A significant difference was found in visual acuity [1.9 (0.5–2.3) LogMAR vs. 0.5 (0.1–2.3) LogMAR, respectively] and central corneal thickness (CCT) [850 (630–950) µm vs. 545 (407–900) µm, respectively] before and after DMEK surgery (P < 0.01 for both). No significant difference was observed in postoperative intraocular pressure (IOP) [12 (6–20) mmHg] compared to preoperative values [13 (6–18) mmHg] (P = 1.00). There was a significant difference between the preoperative and postoperative SC length (SCL) values (249 µm vs. 266.5 µm, respectively; P < 0.001) as well as SC area (SCA) values (4893.5 µm² vs. 5227 µm², respectively) at the third month (P = 0.001). Changes in CCT and IOP were not significantly correlated with changes in SCA (P = 0.658 vs. P = 0.460) or SCL (P = 0.435 vs. P = 0.377). Tamponade with sulfur hexafluoride (SF₆) or air did not significantly affect preoperative and postoperative SCA (P = 0.974 vs. P = 0.821, respectively) and SCL (P = 0.974 vs. P = 0.792, respectively).Conclusion:AS-OCT imaging revealed postoperative expansion of SC parameters following DMEK, with no detectable adverse impact attributable to the surgical procedure or SF₆ use.

Endothelial keratoplasty, including Descemet membrane endothelial keratoplasty (DMEK), effectively treats endothelial dysfunctions such as Fuchs’ endothelial corneal dystrophy (FECD), pseudophakic bullous keratopathy (PBK), posterior polymorphous dystrophy, and iridocorneal endothelial (ICE) syndrome by selectively replacing the affected Descemet’s membrane (DM) and endothelium, minimizing rejection, and promoting rapid visual recovery.[1234567]

Postoperative glaucoma and elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) have been reported following DMEK,[8] with progression observed in 2.7% to 54.5% of endothelial keratoplasty cases.[9] Schlemm’s canal (SC), a circular structure located at the limbal, is critical for aqueous drainage from the trabecular meshwork (TM) to the episcleral veins.[10] However, early postoperative changes in SC following DMEK remain uncharacterized, potentially due to altered aqueous outflow or SC compromise revealed as corneal edema resolves.

Anterior segment optical coherence tomography (AS-OCT) enables rapid noncontact evaluation of SC morphology.[11121314] Previous studies reported that SC area (SCA) and SC length (SCL) are significantly smaller in eyes with pseudoexfoliation glaucoma (PXG) compared to normal eyes,[15] and SCA narrowing has also been documented in primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG).[16] Moreover, decreased SCA was observed in patients undergoing penetrating keratoplasty or deep anterior lamellar keratoplasty (DALK) for keratoconus relative to age- and sex-matched controls.[17]

This study quantitatively analyzes SC morphology using AS-OCT in eyes undergoing DMEK, evaluates the structural impact of intracameral 20% sulfur hexafluoride (SF6) gas tamponade on SC dimensions, and explores the association between SC metrics and early postoperative IOP regulation—an aspect not yet characterized in the DMEK literature.

Methods

Patients

This study retrospectively reviewed the medical records of patients who underwent DMEK for PBK at the Cornea Unit of our hospital’s Ophthalmology Department between January and July 2024. Ethical approval was obtained from the hospital’s Clinical Research Ethics Committee (2025/010.99/17/27), and the study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. As this was a retrospective chart review, the requirement for written informed consent for research was waived by the ethics committee; all patients had previously provided routine clinical care consent, and the committee specifically approved the use of retrospective data.

Patients aged 18 years or older with an IOP below 21 mmHg and clear SC images on AS-OCT before and after DMEK, as well as complete clinical examination findings, were included in the study. Eyes with any additional ocular procedures or intraoperative complications that could potentially affect anterior segment anatomy, such as trabeculectomy, IOL exchange, vitrectomy, posterior capsule rupture, zonular dialysis, or vitreous loss, were excluded. DMEK was performed on average 9.2 ± 1.9 months after phacoemulsification, depending on the availability of donor tissue and individual waiting times. Patients were excluded if they had conditions that could impair visual acuity or elevate IOP, including uveitis, retinal disorders, a history of trauma, pseudoexfoliation syndrome, or other secondary glaucoma causes. Similarly, individuals with diabetes, uncontrolled hypertension, cardiovascular, rheumatologic, or inflammatory diseases; those with psychiatric or neurological disorders; or those using related medications were also excluded from the study.

Clinical data and AS-OCT (Swept-Source, DRI OCT Triton, Topcon, Tokyo, Japan) images were retrospectively retrieved from patients’ follow-up records. Only eyes with retrospectively obtained AS-OCT images or those with sufficiently clear visualization of SC at both preoperative and postoperative visits were included in the analysis. Eyes with poor SC visibility due to corneal opacities or edema were excluded to ensure data accuracy. The best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) and detailed anterior segment examination findings of the eyes were recorded, anterior segment photography was performed, and IOP values were measured using the Goldmann applanation tonometer (GAT). In edematous PBK corneas, IOP was measured using GAT with repeated readings to ensure reliability. To assess accuracy, measurements were cross-checked with a pneumotonometer when necessary, and reliability was further supported by fundus examination and peripapillary retinal nerve fiber layer assessment. Dilated fundus examinations were conducted, and orbital ultrasound was performed for patients whose fundus could not be visualized. The thickness of the retinal nerve fiber layer was measured using OCT in patients whose optic disc remained visible despite media haze to gain an overall impression of the optic nerve. Central corneal thickness (CCT), SCA, and SCL parameters were measured preoperatively and at the third month postoperatively using AS-OCT. SC parameters were compared between DMEK cases managed with air versus 20% SF6 tamponade. Correlations between changes in SC parameters, IOP, and CCT were analyzed.

Surgery procedure

All surgical operations were performed under retrobulbar anesthesia to achieve more profound akinesia (a combination of 2% lidocaine hydrochloride and 0.5% bupivacaine hydrochloride) by a single experienced anterior segment surgeon. Donor tissue was prepared by the surgeon in the surgical suite immediately before surgery. DMEK was performed using the standard technique.[181920]

Donor corneal tissue preparation

DM was stained with 0.06% trypan blue (Ocublu-Try; Miray Medical, Bursa, Turkey) to improve visualization. The graft was dissected using the peripheral scoring method involving the use of a Sinskey hook to outline an 8–8.5 mm circle along the interface between DM and the stroma. The graft was partially peeled to ensure proper orientation before being trephined with a punch (Tecfen Medical, California, USA). To assist intraoperative orientation, an asymmetric triangular mark was made on the stromal surface using an MVR blade. The graft was restained with 0.06% trypan blue and inserted into a custom-designed glass injector provided by D.O.R.C. (Dutch Ophthalmic Research Center, Zuidland, The Netherlands).

Recipient cornea preparation and iridectomy

A 2.8-mm clear corneal incision was made at the 12 o’clock limbal position in the recipient eye. When necessary, the corneal epithelium was debrided to optimize visualization of the posterior corneal surface, particularly in cases with corneal scarring or opacity. The two side ports were created at the 10 and 2 o’clock positions, and the corneal surface was marked using a surgical skin marker to outline a 9.0-mm zone. The anterior chamber was filled with air to enhance intraoperative clarity. A descemetorhexis, approximately 9.0 mm in diameter, was performed using a reverse Sinskey hook by scoring and stripping the diseased DM from the underlying stroma under direct visualization. Pharmacologic miosis was achieved with intracameral carbachol (Miostat Single 0.01%, Novartis Pharma GmbH, Nürnberg, Germany), and a peripheral iridectomy was performed at the 6 o’clock position using a 23-gauge vitrectomy probe to prevent pupillary block.

Donor tissue placement

The prepared DM graft was introduced into the anterior chamber using a custom-designed glass injector (D.O.R.C., Zuidland, Netherlands). Following insertion, the 2.8-mm main incision was secured with a single 10-0 nylon suture. Graft unfolding was facilitated by gentle tapping on the anterior corneal surface using two 30-gauge cannulas. Direct manipulation of the donor tissue was avoided throughout the unfolding process. Once the graft was confirmed to be correctly oriented, with the orientation mark in proper position, graft apposition to the recipient posterior stroma was achieved by filling the anterior chamber with either air or 20% SF₆ gas (Meran Medical, Istanbul, Turkey). Corneal side port incisions were closed using 10-0 nylon sutures. At the conclusion of the procedure, subconjunctival injections of gentamicin (Gentamicin, DEVA, Istanbul, Turkey) and dexamethasone (Dekort, DEVA, Istanbul, Turkey) were administered. Patients were advised to remain in a strict supine position for 48 hours postoperatively to promote graft adherence to the recipient stroma.

Postoperative management

All eyes received postoperative topical treatment with 0.5% moxifloxacin hydrochloride (Vigamox, Alcon, Fort Worth, TX, USA) and 0.1% dexamethasone (Maxidex, Alcon, Puurs, Belgium) administered five times daily. Corneal sutures were removed within 15 days after surgery. Topical antibiotic therapy was discontinued on postoperative day 10 for all patients. At the end of the third postoperative month, corticosteroid treatment was switched to 0.5% loteprednol etabonate (Lotemax, Bausch and Lomb, Istanbul, Turkey), applied three times daily, and gradually tapered to a once-daily maintenance dose to reduce the risk of long-term side effects. The treatment was discontinued entirely by the end of the first postoperative year. No cases of postoperative graft detachment were observed.

Anterior Segment Optical Coherence Tomography

Swept-source AS-OCT utilizes a wavelength of 1050 nm with a scan rate of 100 kHz and a depth of 3 mm. After attaching the anterior segment adapter, patients were instructed to look toward the nasal side, and cross-sectional images were captured from the temporal region using the Line (H) Anterior segment 3.0 mm and 6 mm capture modes [Fig. 1]. To ensure consistent measurements preoperatively and postoperatively, the iris anatomy was used as a reference for alignment. To minimize the known diurnal variations in SC parameters and IOP fluctuations, all examinations and measurements were performed between 08:30 and 09:30 AM by an experienced ophthalmologist.

The OCT images were exported in JPEG format and loaded into ImageJ software (version 1.54d, National Institutes of Health) for further analysis. According to previous studies, SC is considered visible when a thin, dark, translucent gap is observed next to the TM.[21] The SCA and SCL were determined by averaging three separate measurements per parameter. All measurements were performed by the same experienced doctor, who was blinded to prior results to ensure consistency and eliminate examiner bias. The primary outcome was defined as the change in SCA and SCL, and secondary outcomes included IOP change, tamponade comparison, and correlations with clinical variables.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 22 software was used for all statistical analyses. Data normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Depending on distribution, correlations were conducted using either Pearson or Spearman tests. As this study was retrospective, paired pre- and post-DMEK measurements were compared using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, a nonparametric test for dependent variables. The effects of the two tamponades (air vs SF₆) on SC parameters were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. Post-hoc detectable-effect calculations were performed at the end of the study. No a priori sample size calculation was conducted due to the retrospective design. Intra-observer repeatability for SCA and SCL measurements was assessed in a subset of five randomly selected eyes, and the intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC) and coefficients of variation (CV) were calculated.

Results

The study included a total of 22 eyes from 22 patients, comprising an equal distribution of 11 females and 11 males, with a mean age of 70 ± 11.4 years. All eyes in the study were diagnosed with PBK following uneventful phacoemulsification surgery, during which no intraoperative complications occurred, such as DM detachment, posterior capsule rupture, or the need for anterior vitrectomy, and were pseudophakic. Post-hoc detectable-effect calculations indicated that with 22 eyes, the study had 80% power to detect a mean difference of approximately 21.6 µm in SCL and 571.9 µm in SCA (α = 0.05), confirming that the observed postoperative changes are within the detectable range. Intra-observer repeatability was high for SCA (ICC = 0.998; CV = 1.22%) and good for SCL (ICC = 0.979; CV = 4.5%), reflecting low relative variability.

Discussion

In this study, we evaluated SC parameters using AS-OCT before and after DMEK surgery and found that the procedure significantly increased SCA and SCL (P = 0.001; 95% CI, 0.47–0.86 vs. P <0.001; 95% CI, 0.43–0.83; respectively). The observed postoperative differences remained statistically significant in both the SF6 and air groups. Furthermore, changes in SCA and SCL did not significantly differ between the air and SF₆ groups (P = 0.468 vs P = 0.974), indicating that intracameral SF₆ use does not adversely affect SC.

In a study, a decrease in SC parameters was observed in keratoconus patients who underwent PK and DALK compared to the nonsurgical group, with no significant difference between the PK and DALK groups.[17] Another study linked the high incidence of post-keratoplasty glaucoma in aphakic eyes to anterior chamber angle distortion and IOP elevation, identifying contributing factors such as tight and long sutures, thick peripheral cornea, and same-size donor–recipient trepanation.[22] In our study, this narrowing effect may not have been observed since patients who underwent DMEK were included for comparison, and no sutures were used, except for two corneal sutures at the side port and one for the main incision. In pseudophakic patients who underwent isolated DMEK, the absence of manipulation in the anterior chamber and iridocorneal angle may also have prevented the SC changes observed in PK and DALK. Although GAT tends to underestimate IOP in the presence of corneal edema, particularly in PBK or FECD,[23] it remains the gold standard in most clinical studies.[24] For this reason, we used GAT to measure IOP before and after DMEK. Despite a significant postoperative decrease in CCT (P < 0.001), no increase in IOP was detected (P = 1.00).

According to the available literature, preoperative and postoperative SC parameters in patients undergoing DMEK have not been previously evaluated. Additionally, although a statistically significant postoperative increase in SCA and SCL was observed in our study, no significant correlation was found between CCT and the changes in either parameter. Previously, it was reported that patients with sarcoidosis had a narrower SC, with inflammatory cell infiltration potentially being responsible.[25] This could explain to compression of the SC due to corneal edema as well as intense inflammation, leading to the subsequent narrowing of the SC channel. Such changes may be corrected through surgical intervention, resulting in reduced corneal edema and the expansion of SC parameters observed in our study. Supporting this finding, in eyes with Graves’ ophthalmopathy, where an increase in inflammation was anticipated, the SC parameters were found to be reduced compared to controls.[26] In addition, it was found that the SC parameters decreased with increasing age.[27]

According to a study, the mean SCL was significantly reduced and the mean SCA was significantly smaller in patients with PXG compared to controls.[15] Another study showed that a significant difference in the average SCA was found between the POAG and control groups, with a significant correlation to IOP. However, no such difference was observed for the SC diameter in Chinese patients.[16] Another research reported that the SCA, SC diameter, and perimeter were smaller in patients with primary angle-closure glaucoma compared to the healthy control group.[28] An 8% increase in SC cross-sectional area and expansion in SC volume were observed after selective laser trabeculoplasty (both P = 0.029), and a significant correlation was found between this change and IOP reduction (P = 0.023, R = 0.622).[29]

A previous study in the literature involving eyes that underwent keratoplasty reported a significant narrowing in SCA and SC diameter in eyes with keratoconus that underwent DALK and PK, compared to both keratoconic and healthy control eyes. However, no correlation was found between this narrowing and IOP.[17] Unlike that study, our research includes both pre- and postoperative data for DMEK cases, enabling a comparison of changes in IOP and SC parameters, and revealing a postoperative expansion in SC parameters. Additionally, when comparing different tamponade agents, no significant differences in these parameters were observed. Similar to the aforementioned study, we also did not find a significant correlation between the changes in IOP and SC parameters. Although previous studies have reported conflicting results,[153031] the lack of correlation in our study may be explained by the exclusion of glaucomatous patients and the relatively small sample size.

In our study, no significant effect was observed on SCA and SCL parameters between eyes treated with SF₆ and air (both P = 0.821). Similarly, the use of air or SF₆ tamponade during DMEK had no significant effect on postoperative IOP (P = 0.336). It has been previously demonstrated that SF6 gas can be safely used in DMEK to provide prolonged graft support without causing any acute toxicity.[32] In support of our findings, a previous study demonstrated that intracameral injection of SF6, even at high concentrations (100%), is safe and does not increase the risk of endothelial damage.[33] In our study, since 20% diluted SF₆ was used, no significant expansion would be expected, consistent with prior literature.[34] Another study also showed that intracameral use of 20% SF₆ during DMEK prolongs graft support, reduces complication rates, and does not result in a significant difference in endothelial cell loss.[35]

There are several limitations to our study. One of the limitations of the study is that, due the retrospective nature of the study, preoperative measurements of anterior chamber angle, volume, and depth were not available. Consequently, we could not assess potential correlations between SC values and surgery-induced changes in the anterior chamber, which should be considered in future prospective studies. By the third month after DMEK, corneal clarity is typically restored, allowing reliable postoperative IOP measurement using GAT. In our study, the postoperative IOP levels were 12 (6–20) mmHg, with no increase observed following surgery. These findings may indirectly suggest that preoperative IOP values were also within the normal range as any preoperative elevation would likely have resulted in a postoperative rise. Although GAT was the primary method used in this study, IOP measurements in eyes with PBK can also be obtained using alternative devices such as a Tono-Pen or iCare tonometer, which may be considered in future studies. In this study, images obtained from the nasal quadrant in edematous corneas were generally not of sufficient quality; therefore, cross-sections were acquired from the temporal quadrant. Previous studies have shown no significant difference in SC measurements between nasal and temporal quadrants.[3637] Due to the inherent difficulty of visualizing SC in edematous corneas, circumferential (360°) assessment could not be performed. Eyes in which SC could not be clearly visualized at either the preoperative or postoperative visit were excluded. As in many AS-OCT-based studies, reliance on adequate image quality may introduce selection bias since pre- and postoperative structural changes can compromise SC visibility. Nonetheless, all eligible eyes were imaged at both time points, and only SC measurements obtained from a reliably interpretable temporal-quadrant image were analyzed to ensure data accuracy. While restricting analysis to the temporal scans enhances measurement consistency, it may limit the generalizability of the findings. Although the choice of tamponade (air vs SF₆) was nonrandom and based on surgeon discretion—potentially introducing confounding by indication—baseline comparability between the groups was confirmed, with no significant differences in age, preoperative and postoperative CCT, or preoperative and postoperative IOP. Despite including only patients with PBK, providing a homogeneous population without glaucomatous eyes, the study was limited by its relatively small sample size and its single-center design. In addition, only 3-month postoperative data were analyzed, and no long-term follow-up was available. While intra-observer repeatability for SCA and SCL measurements was high to good, inter-observer repeatability was not evaluated, representing an additional limitation of the study.

Conclusion

To the best of our knowledge, evidence on pre- and postoperative SC parameters in eyes undergoing DMEK or other corneal transplant procedures remains limited. We believe it adds valuable insight to the literature. Future studies with larger samples and comparisons across surgical techniques in similar patient groups would be beneficial.

Neoadjuvant sintilimab and apatinib combined with perioperative FLOT chemotherapy for locally advanced gastric cancer: A prospective, single-arm, phase II study
Authors: Huinian Zhou, Bo Long, Zeyuan Yu, Junmin Zhu, Hanteng Yang, Changjiang Luo, Wenjuan Zhang, Chi Dong, Xiaoying Guan, Long Li, Gengyuan Zhang, Hongtai Cao, Shigong Chen, Linyan Zhou, Qichen He, Shiying Gan, Xiangyan Jiang, Qianlin Gu, Keshen Wang, Wengui Shi, Long Qin, Zuoyi Jiao
Published: 2024

For patients with locally advanced gastric cancer (LAGC), the effectiveness of perioperative chemotherapy can be significantly improved by adding programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitors during the neoadjuvant phase. However, a substantial percentage of patients with a programmed death-ligand 1 combined positive score (PD-L1 CPS) <1 or microsatellite stable (MSS) status may still not experience benefits. The antiangiogenic agent apatinib can promote vascular normalization, which is indicated by the ratio of neural/glial antigen 2 (NG2)-labeled pericytes to platelet endothelial cell adhesion molecule (CD31)-labeled endothelial cells,[1] and work synergistically with PD-1 inhibitors to improve the efficacy of both chemotherapy and immunotherapy. However, the predictive value of the baseline NG2+/CD31+ ratio for immunotherapy has not yet been established. Based on the positive outcomes from our pilot study [Supplementary Table 1, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151], a phase II trial, which aimed to enhance treatment efficacy without increasing the risk of toxicity compared with previous studies involving FLOT chemotherapy alone, was conducted using the combination of triple perioperative FLOT chemotherapy with sintilimab and apatinib in patients with cT3/4 LAGC. Furthermore, we also examined the potential of the baseline NG2+/CD31+ ratio as a predictive marker for this intensified treatment regimen.

This study was a prospective investigator-initiated, phase II, single-arm clinical trial, and registered in Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (www.chictr.org.cn, ChiCTR2200055269). Ethical approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Lanzhou University Second Hospital (2021A-606) in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and the Good Clinical Practice guidelines. All eligible patients provided written informed consent before enrollment.

Treatment-naïve patients aged 18 or older with HER-2-negative, locally advanced gastric adenocarcinoma (T3/4, Nany, M0) received a triple regimen as neoadjuvant treatment (NAT) every 3 weeks: sintilimab (200 mg) and FLOT regimen (docetaxel 50 mg/m2, oxaliplatin 85 mg/m2, 5-fluorouracil [5-FU 2600 mg/m2], and leucovorin 200 mg/m2) via intravenous infusion for four cycles, along with apatinib 250 mg orally once daily during cycles 1 to 3 before surgery. Following at least 21 days post-apatinib, radical D2 gastrectomy or extended lymph node dissection was performed. Post-surgery, four cycles of FLOT were administered as adjuvant therapy. The procedure and assessments were detailed in the supplementary file of study protocol, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151. Anti-CD31 antibodies and anti-NG2 antibodies were used to label vascular endothelial cells and vascular pericytes, respectively. The NG2+/CD31+ ratio was used to assess the vascular normalization status before and after NAT through multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF) analysis. The details of PD-L1 CPS, MSS, mIF, toxicities assessment, pathological analysis, and population settings are summarized in the supplementary material, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151.

The primary endpoint of this study was the pathologic complete response (pCR) rate. The secondary endpoints included the major pathological response (MPR) rate, objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), tumor down-staging rate, R0 resection rate, safety, disease-free survival (DFS), and overall survival (OS). All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp, Armonk, USA).

From January 6, 2022 to February 11, 2023, a total of 30 patients were initially screened. Baseline characteristics indicated a median age of 58 (range: 35–76) years, with a predominance of male patients (86.7%, 26/30) [Supplementary Tables 2 and 3, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151]. Half of the patients were at cT4 stage, and 90% (27/30) had regional lymph node metastasis. Additionally, 6.7% (2/30) of patients showed high microsatellite instability (MSI-H), and 43.3% (13/30) had a PD-L1 CPS ≥5. During the neoadjuvant treatment, 2 patients were excluded due to diarrhea or refusal to continue, resulting 28 patients in the full-analysis set (FAS); besides, one patient with type 2 diabetes died of diabetic ketoacidosis before receiving adjuvant treatment [Supplementary Figure 1, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151]

The radiological responses assessed in FAS indicated a range of tumor shrinkage from –100.0% to 1.1% [Figure 1A]. The ORR was 85.7% (95% confidence interval [CI]: 71.9%–99.5%) and the DCR was 100.0% [Supplementary Table 4, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151]. Responses to neoadjuvant treatment (NAT) varied, the duration of NAT was illustrated in Supplementary Figure 2A, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151. The pCR rate was 28.6% (95% CI: 10.7%–46.4%), and MPR rate was 53.6% (95% CI: 33.9%–73.3%) [Figure 1B and Supplementary Table 4, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151]. Notably, adjustments such as dose reductions and extended treatment intervals were made to manage toxicities. Despite these modifications, no significant differences in tumor pathological regression were noted between modified and standard treatments [Supplementary Figure 2B, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151]. Additionally, 28 patients who underwent surgery achieved a 100.0% R0 resection rate [Supplementary Table 5, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151], with a median of 17 (interquartile range [IQR]: 13–21) lymph nodes dissected. Furthermore, 25 (89.3%) patients experienced overall TNM downstaging [Supplementary Table 6, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151]. As of January 7, 2024, the median follow-up had not yet been reached. Survival analysis for the per-protocol set showed a 1-year DFS rate of 92.6% (95% CI: 73.9%–98.1%), and an 18-month cumulative DFS rate of 78.9% (95% CI: 51.0%–92.0%, Supplementary Figure 2C, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151). The 1-year OS rate was 96.4% (95% CI: 77.3%–99.5%), and the cumulative 2-year OS rate was 84.4% (95% CI: 57.9%–94.9%, Supplementary Figure 2D, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151). Subgroup analysis highlighted that patient with MSI-H status, a PD-L1 CPS ≥5, high tumor mutational burden (TMB-H), intestinal-type tumors, moderate differentiation, or cT3 stage tended to achieve favorable pathological regression [Supplementary Table 7, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151].

During the NAT period, all 30 patients experienced treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs), and most being mild (grades I–II), with 33.3% experiencing grade III or IV TRAEs, including nausea, vomiting, neutropenia, hypertension, and alopecia [Supplementary Table 8, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151]. Postoperative complications were limited, and 43.3% (12/28) of patients experienced mild [Supplementary Table 9, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151]. During adjuvant treatment, all 27 patients experienced any grade of TRAEs. More than half (51.9%) of these were mild (grade I-II), while 13 (48.1%) suffered from severe (grade III or IV) TRAEs including leucopenia, neutropenia, nausea, vomiting, and alopecia [Supplementary Table 10, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151].

Representative images of baseline vascular normalization status, pretreatment and preoperative radiological images of tumors, and postoperative pathology in patient 2 with MPR and patient 13 with Becker grade 3 pathological response are presented in Supplementary Figure 3A and B, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151. Significant differences in NG2+/CD31+ ratios were linked to partial pathological responses (PPR), with the ratio serving as a strong predictor for achieving PPR (area under the curve [AUC] = 0.99, P <0.001, Supplementary Figure 3C and D, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151) and MPR (AUC = 0.84, P <0.01, Supplementary Figure 3E and F, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151). Patients with baseline NG2+/CD31+ ratios ≥0.38 were more likely to achieve MPR, underscoring its prognostic value [Supplementary Table 11, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C151].

Neoadjuvant FLOT chemotherapy combined with sintilimab and apatinib significantly improved tumor regression in LAGC patients, achieving a 28.6% pCR rate, compared to 15.6% in the FLOT4-AIO study, which included a lower proportion of advanced stage patients.[2] Previous FLOT-only treatments showed only a 2.1% pCR in similar population. The Keynote-585 study showed that adding a PD-1 inhibitor increased the pCR rate to 17.0% from 7.0%.[3] Apatinib also improved chemotherapy efficacy, with a 9.7% pCR compared to 4.8% for FLOT alone. The Arise-FJ-G 005 study reported a 15.7% pCR and 33.3% MPR with a combination therapy, versus 5.7% and 17.0% with chemotherapy alone.[4] The recent Arise-FJ-G 005 study reported a 15.7% pCR and a 33.3% MPR rate with a combination of apatinib, PD-1 inhibitors, and chemotherapy, compared to 5.7% and 17.0% with chemotherapy alone.[5] While the survival data from this study are preliminary, the encouraging 2-year OS rate, 18-month DFS rate, and 100.0% R0 resection rate suggest potential long-term survival benefits for patients with LAGC. In addition, subgroup analysis revealed patients with MSI-H and PD-L1 CPS ≥5 might achieved favorable pathological response. Due to the limitations imposed by the small sample size, further research through larger, randomized trials is necessary to better understand the association between MSI status, PD-L1 CPS, pathological responses, and long-term prognosis.

Acknowledging the potential for increased toxicities with an increased number of drugs in the NAT phase, our study specifically enrolled LAGC patients with Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) 0-1 and administered FLOT chemotherapy every three weeks. We observed that 33.3% of patients experienced at least one grade III or IV TRAE during the NAT phase. This rate is slightly higher than the 25.0% reported in the FLOT-only group from the FLOT4-AIO study and is comparable to the 33.3% observed in the combination of neoadjuvant camrelizumab, apatinib, nab-paclitaxel, and S-1 from the NCT04195828 study. Dose reductions or extensions in treatment intervals substantially contribute to patient’s compliance instead of impairing the efficacy of this NAT. Notably, severe TRAEs (grades III-IV) occurred in 48.1% of patients during the adjuvant chemotherapy phase, which might be attributed to cumulative toxicities and decreased patients compliance. Given that over half of the patients achieved MPR, it is important to consider optimizing the postoperative FLOT regimen by reducing the dosage or switching to single or two-drug therapy regimens in future studies.

Our receiver operating characteristic analysis indicated that the baseline NG2+/CD31+ ratio might be a predictor of MPR. Pathological vessels can contribute to tumor progression and create an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. Antiangiogenic therapy can transform these pathological tumor vessels into normalized vessels, thereby enhancing the efficacy of both immunotherapy and chemotherapy. While PD-L1 CPS and MSI-H are well-established predictors of response to PD-1 inhibitors, the baseline NG2+/CD31+ ratio provides additional predictive value for the effectiveness of triple NAT, though further validation is needed.

In conclusion, the favorable antitumor effects and manageable toxicities associated with the triple neoadjuvant combination of FLOT, sintilimab, and apatinib in patients with LAGC offer primary evidence for further investigation. However, long-term survival benefits and the predictive value of the NG2+/CD31+ ratio require confirmation through larger cohort studies.

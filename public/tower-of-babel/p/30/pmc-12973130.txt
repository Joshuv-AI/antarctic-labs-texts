Implementing plasma p‐tau217 and cognitive testing for Alzheimer's screening in low‐education populations in central China

ABSTRACT
AbstractINTRODUCTIONWe evaluated a pragmatic screening strategy combining brief cognitive assessment with plasma phosphorylated tau217 (p‐tau217) in an elderly health screening cohort.METHODSParticipants completed the Memory and Executive Screening (MES) test and plasma biomarker assessment. Cognitive impairment was defined using individualized residual norms derived from an internal reference sample. The p‐tau217 thresholds were positive predictive value (PPV) ‐oriented and anchored to age‐ and cognitive status‐specific prior probabilities of amyloid‐β (Aβ) pathology.RESULTSMES‐defined cognitive impairment (≤−2 standard deviation [SD] deviation after adjustment for demographics) was identified in 12.4% of participants. In cognitively unimpaired (CU) individuals, age‐specific thresholds targeting a PPV of 0.7 yielded an overall p‐tau217 positivity rate of 11.8%, increasing from 3.5% (< 60 years) to 6.3% (60–70 years) and 25.9% (≥70 years). Among cognitively impaired (CI) participants, 26.6% were p‐tau217 positive.DISCUSSIONThis framework supports practical integration of plasma biomarkers into community‐based screening, with longitudinal follow‐up needed to further refine threshold selection.Highlights Regression‐based residual method reduced education bias, identifying 12.4% with cognitive impairment individuals.Fully automated chemiluminescent assay enabled practical, large‐scale biomarker implementation.Brief screening strategy supports early detection, clinical trial recruitment, and resource allocation in under‐resourced regions.

BACKGROUND

Over the past three decades, the incidence, prevalence, and mortality rates of Alzheimer's disease (AD) in China have steadily increased. According to the China AD Report 2025, approximately 16.99 million individuals were living with AD and related dementias (ADRD) in 2021. 1 This corresponds to an age‐standardized prevalence of 900.8 per 100,000 people and an age‐standardized incidence rate of 151.5 per 100,000 people. Thus, China accounts for nearly 30% of the global ADRD burden, far exceeding its share of the world population.

Henan Province, a populous central province with approximately 100 million residents, has one of the highest standardized ADRD prevalence rates in China (∼975.6 per 100,000). The province is characterized by a predominantly agrarian and aging population, lower educational attainment, and more limited healthcare resources compared to eastern regions. In this region, public awareness of AD remains low, leaving a large proportion of patients without appropriate clinical interventions. 2 Taken together, these factors highlight the urgent need for pragmatic, scalable screening strategies for older adults, which can enable earlier detection and timely intervention.

Recent advances in AD biomarker research have enabled the integration of biomarkers into population screening strategies, rather than relying solely on cognitive tests. Such integration facilitates etiologic delineation, risk stratification, and precision prevention. Although amyloid‐β (Aβ) positron emission tomography (PET) and CSF biomarkers remain the diagnostic gold standard, their high cost, invasiveness, and limited throughput restrict population‐level screening. Plasma biomarkers, particularly p‐tau217, offer a more scalable alternative. 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 However, large‐scale implementation is not feasible without low‐cost, fully automated assays. Given the low prevalence of Aβ pathology in the general population, the inherently low positive predictive value (PPV) of plasma‐based assessments poses a fundamental challenge. 7 , 8 , 9 Careful definition of biomarker thresholds based on prior probability and target PPVs is therefore essential to reduce unnecessary referrals and participant burden. 10

While biomarker testing provides insights into underlying AD pathology, cognitive assessments remain the fundamental screening tool, as cognitive status is strongly associated with the prior probability of Aβ pathology. Conventional paper‐and‐pencil tests and education‐sensitive scales (e.g., certain Montreal Cognitive Assessment [MoCA] versions) demonstrate reduced specificity in populations with limited formal education, potentially leading to overestimation of cognitive impairment. 11 For example, individuals from rural areas may be unfamiliar with specific test items, such as auditory verbal learning tasks, which can reduce engagement and consequently compromise the validity of cognitive assessments. In the Henan region, brief cognitive measures that are easy to administer and minimally dependent on formal education are therefore preferred. Additionally, regression‐based residual methods can adjust for demographic factors such as age and education, reducing demographic bias and enhancing the reliability and clinical utility of cognitive assessments. Based on these considerations, we evaluated, within a multicenter health screening cohort, a pragmatic workflow that integrates robust normed cognitive assessments with plasma p‐tau217 measurement.

The AD Screening and Prediction (ASAP) cohort is an ongoing, prospective, multicenter, observational brain health screening study conducted in Henan Province, central China. Given the province's large population, relatively low socioeconomic development, and lower average educational attainment, ASAP focuses on community‐based brain health check‐ups and aims to explore practical, scalable models of plasma biomarker‐based early AD screening and risk prediction in resource‐limited settings. From April to October 2025, 5,836 individuals aged 50 years and older who were undergoing health screenings were consecutively enrolled at ten tertiary hospitals across Henan Province. These hospitals included one provincial center (Henan Provincial People's Hospital, a National Core Cognitive Center) and nine regional medical centers located in Kaifeng, Nanyang, Zhumadian, Pingdingshan, Luoyang, Xinxiang, Anyang, Hebi, and Xuchang city. These sites were chosen to capture both the regional and population variability across the Henan region. After excluding 35 individuals with renal insufficiency (estimated glomerular filtration rate [eGFR] < 30 mL·min− 1·1.73 m− 2), 5,801 participants remained for analysis. The study protocol was approved by local ethics committees at all sites, and all participants provided written informed consent.

The Aβ‐positron emission tomography (PET) reference cohort (n = 566), sourced from ten ASAP research centers, served as the benchmark for deriving plasma p‐tau217 thresholds. It included 387 Aβ‐PET‐positive individuals (120 with AD dementia, 174 with AD‐mild cognitive impairment [MCI], and 93 cognitively normal) and 179 Aβ‐PET‐negative individuals (52 cognitively normal, 84 with MCI, and 43 with dementia).

Clinical data collection

At baseline, all participants completed a standardized paper questionnaire. Data collection was performed by staff who were uniformly trained in accordance with strict standard operating procedures. The following information was obtained. Demographics: age, sex, years of education, marital status. Medical history and comorbidities: hypertension, diabetes, stroke, traumatic brain injury, coronary heart disease, chronic kidney disease, tumor, insomnia, rapid eye movement (REM) sleep behavior disorder, depressive/anxiety symptoms, family history of dementia, smoking, and alcohol use.

Physical examination and laboratory measures: height, weight, body mass index (BMI), blood pressure, and routine biochemical tests (fasting glucose, lipid profile, liver, and kidney function).

Cognitive function was evaluated with the Memory and Executive Screening (MES) test, a tool suitable for populations with low educational attainment. 12 , 13 Developed in 2011 by Chinese researchers for detecting MCI, MES covers two key cognitive domains—memory and executive function—each contributing 50 points to a 100‐point total score. The scale includes tasks of sentence recall (immediate, short‐delay, and long‐delay), verbal fluency (naming kitchen items within 60 s), conflicting instructions (reverse tapping), gesture imitation, and inhibitory control (Go/No‐Go task). The assessment takes approximately 7 minutes, relies on verbal and demonstrative instructions, and requires no writing. In validation studies from Shanghai, a cutoff of 72 yielded an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.95 for multidomain amnestic MCI (sensitivity 87%, specificity 91%), while a cutoff of 62 distinguished AD from controls with an AUC of 0.99 (sensitivity 99.0%, specificity 97.8%).

All MES assessors from the ten centers received unified, standardized training, and each center had two quality‐control neurologists overseeing cognitive assessments to ensure protocol adherence. The MES scale was further administered in a semi‐electronic, audio/video‐assisted format to reduce inter‐center variability, with memory tests delivered via prerecorded standardized audio to ensure consistent speech rate and volume, and executive function tasks (e.g., Go/No‐Go and gesture imitation) presented through standardized video instructions. The entire MES administration process was audio‐recorded, and approximately 10% of assessments were randomly selected for secondary review to verify completeness and scoring accuracy.

Blood sample collection and biomarker assays

Five milliliters of fasting peripheral venous blood (with an ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid [EDTA] anticoagulant) were collected from each participant in the morning. Samples were centrifuged at 2,000 × g for 15 minutes. The plasma supernatant was then aliquoted into pre‐chilled 500 µL Eppendorf (EP) tubes and stored at −80°C. The interval from blood collection to plasma storage at −80°C was strictly controlled within 4 hours. All frozen plasma samples were transported on dry ice to the central laboratory at Henan Provincial People's Hospital. The interval between blood collection and biomarker assays was kept under 30 days.

Plasma biomarkers, including p‐tau217, p‐tau181, Aβ42, Aβ40, glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP), and neurofilament light chain (NfL), were quantified using the fully automated Vazyme® chemiluminescent immunoassay platform, which is approved by the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) of China (registration number: 20242400296). The detection system consists of antibody‐coated microspheres, buffers, and diluents. The limit of detection for p‐tau217 is 0.20 pg/mL, with a linear range of 0.50–50.00 pg/mL. In the development stage, 107 fresh plasma samples were compared head‐to‐head with the ALZpath Simoa p‐tau217 assay and the Fujirebio p‐tau217 assay, yielding correlation coefficients of 0.90 and 0.86, respectively. Further details regarding the Vazyme AD assays are provided in the Supplementary Materials.

In this study, laboratory personnel were blinded to all clinical information. Each assay batch contained 48 wells, of which four were allocated to within‐batch replicate measurements and 44 to study samples. Across 145 batches, a total of 6,368 participants were analyzed. Only two measurements exceeded the assay's upper limit of quantification (50.00 pg/mL) and were re‐assayed within the linear detection range. Approximately 10% of samples in each batch were randomly selected for duplicate measurements, yielding a within‐batch coefficient of variation (CV) of 8%, indicating good repeatability. Low‐, medium‐, and high‐level calibrators were run daily prior to sample measurement for system calibration, and between‐batch variability was consistently maintained below 15%.

Apolipoprotein E (APOE) genotype was determined using the TaqMan single‐nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) method. APOE ε4 carrier status was defined as the presence of at least one ε4 allele.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) with interquartile ranges (IQRs), while categorical variables are presented as counts and percentages. For comparisons among multiple groups, continuous variables with a normal distribution were analyzed using one‐way analysis of variance (ANOVA), whereas non‐normally distributed continuous variables were compared using the Kruskal–Wallis test. Chi‐squared (χ 2) test or Fisher's exact test was used for categorical variables. Restricted cubic spline (RCS) models were used to investigate the relationships between MES scores (dependent variable) and both demographic factors and plasma AD biomarkers. Biomarkers with skewed distributions and wide ranges (p‐tau217, NfL, and GFAP) were log‐transformed to approximate normality.

Cognitive status was assessed using a standardized residual approach based on internally derived norms. A cognitively normal reference sample was established from 203 regionally matched individuals with a Clinical Dementia Rating scale Sum of Boxes (CDR‐SB) score of 0 and preserved activities of daily living (ADL). Within this reference group, MES total scores were regressed on age, sex, and education, and the SD of the residuals was estimated. Cognitive impairment was defined as the deviation between observed and expected MES scores: participants with residuals ≤ −2 SD were classified as cognitively impaired (CI), while those with residuals > −2 SD were classified as cognitively unimpaired (CU).

To estimate age‐specific prior probabilities of Aβ pathology, plasma p‐tau217 concentrations were first analyzed using an unsupervised two‐component Gaussian mixture model (GMM) with unequal variances. Posterior probabilities of belonging to the positive component (P_post) were then calculated for each participant. Among CU individuals, a generalized additive model (GAM) was subsequently applied to characterize the nonlinear association between age and the prior probability of Aβ positivity.

The discriminative performance of six plasma AD biomarkers for Aβ pathology was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis with Aβ‐PET as the reference standard, and AUCs (95% confidence interval [CI]) were estimated via bootstrap resampling. To address demographic and clinical imbalances between the PET cohort and the community screening cohort—including age, sex, education, BMI, hypertension, diabetes, history of stroke, and eGFR, we applied inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW). This adjustment enhanced the applicability and generalizability of PET‐derived thresholds for community screening. Accordingly, the ROC performance of p‐tau217 in the IPTW‐weighted PET cohort served as the reference for determining thresholds in the community cohort.

For CU participants, thresholds were selected based on age‐specific prior prevalence (< 60, 60–70, ≥70 years) to achieve prespecified PPVs. For CI participants with higher Aβ prevalence, the PET‐derived optimal threshold (maximum Youden index) was directly applied. All participants were subsequently classified into four groups based on cognitive status and p‐tau217: (1) CU and p‐tau217 negative (cognitively healthy), (2) CU and p‐tau217 positive (preclinical AD), (3) CI and p‐tau217 negative (non‐AD cognitive impairment), and (4) CI and p‐tau217 positive (AD). This classification was used for descriptive analyses and between‐group comparisons.

A predictive model incorporating age, sex, education, plasma p‐tau217, and APOE genotype was developed using the full PET cohort. Internal validation was performed with 500 bootstrap resamples, and model performance was evaluated using the AUC, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA). Predicted probabilities from the PET‐based model were recalibrated using a Bayesian prior to account for the lower Aβ prevalence in the community cohort.

All statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.3), with two‐sided tests and P < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Participant characteristics

A total of 5,801 participants from ten tertiary hospitals were included in the ASAP cohort. The participants had a median age of 65.0 years, and 61.6% were female. The median educational attainment was 9.0 years, and the median MES score was 79 points. Comorbidities included hypertension (45.2%), diabetes (20.6%), and a history of stroke (23.3%) (Table 1). The APOE ε4 carrier frequency was 10.1% in the ASAP cohort and 38.9% in the PET cohort. The PET cohort included 387 Aβ‐positive individuals (120 AD‐dementia, 174 AD‐MCI, 93 cognitively normal) and 179 Aβ‐negative individuals (52 cognitively normal, 84 MCI, 43 dementia) (Table 1). RCS regression analyses were used to examine the relationships between MES scores and age, education, and plasma biomarkers, with biomarker analyses adjusted for age, sex, and education. MES scores declined progressively with increasing age, with an accelerated decrease after 70 years. Years of education were linearly and positively associated with MES scores. Determination of cognitive impairment

Among 203 cognitively normal individuals from the local population, we derived internal normative data using a multivariable linear regression model based on demographic variables, including age, sex, and years of education. The model was specified as: MES = 93.60 − 0.46 × age + 1.43 × years of education + 0.35 × sex (male). Individual residuals were calculated by subtracting each participant's expected MES score from their observed score. Standardized z‐scores were then obtained by dividing these residuals by the SD of the residuals from the regression model. Participants with z‐scores ≤ −2 were classified as CI, whereas those with z‐scores > −2 were considered CU. Based on this approach, 722 participants (12.45%) were classified as CI, and 5,079 (87.55%) as CU. By comparison, applying a fixed MES cutoff of 72 identified 1,953 participants (33.66%) as CI and 3,848 (66.33%) as CU, indicating that the fixed‐cutoff method overestimates the prevalence of cognitive impairment. Estimation of Aβ pathology prevalence in CU participants

Among the 5,801 participants in the ASAP cohort, the mean plasma p‐tau217 level was 2.29 pg/mL (SD = 2.40), the median was 1.73 pg/mL, and the interquartile range was 1.14–2.59 pg/mL. A two‐component GMM with unequal variances identified a lower‐mean “normal” component (mean = 1.58 pg/mL, SD = 0.68 pg/mL) comprising 80.9% of participants, and a higher‐mean, more variable “abnormal” component (mean = 4.53 pg/mL, SD = 2.57 pg/mL) comprising 19.1% (Figure 3(A)). The intersection of the two distributions occurred at 3.21 pg/mL, indicating that participants with p‐tau217 values above this threshold were more likely to belong to the presumptive Aβ‐positive group. In CU individuals, GAM analysis revealed a nonlinear increase in the estimated proportion of Aβ‐positive participants with age (Figure 3(B)). The predicted prevalence of Aβ pathology in CU individuals was approximately 10% at age 60, 20% at age 70, and 35% at age 80.

Age‐ and cognition‐stratified p‐tau217 screening thresholds in the ASAP cohort

In the unweighted PET cohort, plasma p‐tau217 demonstrated the highest discriminative performance for Aβ pathology with an AUC of 0.95 (95% CI: 0.93–0.97). Plasma p‐tau181 showed an AUC of 0.93 (95% CI: 0.90–0.95), and the p‐tau217/Aβ42 ratio had an AUC of 0.95 (95% CI: 0.93–0.97). Other biomarkers performed less well, with Aβ42/40 ratio achieving an AUC of 0.76 (95% CI: 0.72–0.81), GFAP an AUC of 0.84 (95% CI: 0.81–0.88), and NFL an AUC of 0.53 (95% CI: 0.48–0.59). After IPTW adjustment, the PET cohort was comparable to the ASAP cohort in terms of demographic and clinical characteristics (Table S1). ROC analysis in the IPTW‐weighted PET cohort showed that plasma p‐tau217 predicted Aβ‐PET positivity with an AUC of 0.95 (95% CI: 0.93–0.97). The optimal cutoff was 3.31 pg/mL, corresponding to a sensitivity of 89.9%, specificity of 89.4%, PPV of 95.4%, and negative predictive value (NPV) of 79.2%. The weighted ROC curve for p‐tau217 is shown in Figure S2. Given the higher prevalence of Aβ pathology in the CI group of the ASAP cohort, this cutoff was directly applied to define Aβ pathology among CI participants.

For the CU group, p‐tau217 screening thresholds were derived from ROC analyses in the IPTW‐weighted PET cohort. Age‐specific cutoffs were determined for three predefined age strata (< 60, 60–70, and ≥70 years), using GMM‐estimated prevalence rates of Aβ pathology (10%, 15%, and 25%, respectively) to achieve target PPVs (0.5, 0.6, 0.7, and 0.8) (Table 2). Corresponding sensitivity and specificity were also reported. Increasing age and higher underlying Aβ prevalence were associated with lower p‐tau217 thresholds for achieving a given PPV, whereas higher target PPVs uniformly required higher thresholds across age strata.

Joint stratification by cognitive status and p‐tau217

Participants in the ASAP cohort were stratified into four groups according to cognitive status and plasma p‐tau217 levels: CU/p‐tau217‐ (cognitively healthy), CU/p‐tau217+ (preclinical AD), CI/p‐tau217‐ (non‐AD cognitive impairment), and CI/p‐tau217+ (AD). In the CI group, 26.59% were p‐tau217 positive using the PET‐derived optimal threshold, with positivity rates of 9.71%, 19.87%, and 38.20% in individuals aged < 60, 60–70, and ≥70 years, respectively. In CU individuals, age‐specific thresholds were applied to achieve a fixed PPV of 0.7, resulting in an overall positivity rate of 11.79%, increasing from 3.47% in those under 60 to 6.28% in those aged 60–70, and 25.88% in those aged 70 and older. Multivariable prediction of Aβ pathology risk

Within the PET cohort, we developed a multivariable predictive model for Aβ pathology using age, sex, years of education, plasma p‐tau217 levels, and APOE ε4 genotype. The predictive model demonstrated excellent discrimination, with an AUC of 0.96 (95% CI, 0.94–0.97) and high overall accuracy of 0.91 (95% CI, 0.88–0.93). At an optimal cutoff of 0.613, the model achieved a sensitivity of 0.90, specificity of 0.92, PPV of 0.96, and NPV of 0.81, indicating strong and well‐balanced predictive performance. The ROC curve, calibration curve, DCA, and variable importance plot were shown in Figure S3. Applied to the ASAP cohort, the model generated individual predicted probabilities representing the raw risk of Aβ pathology. These probabilities were then recalibrated using a Bayesian prior probability adjustment based on community‐level Aβ prevalence, yielding adjusted risk for Aβ pathology. DISCUSSION

This study evaluated the utility of combining cognitive assessment with plasma biomarkers to detect cognitive impairment and stratify AD risk in a brain health screening cohort from central China, a population characterized by relatively low educational attainment. Two major refinements were implemented: (1) internally derived normative references to improve individualized detection of CI; and (2) age‐ and cognitive status‐specific plasma p‐tau217 thresholds. In this cohort, MES‐defined CI was present in 14.5% of participants, of whom 26.59% had abnormal p‐tau217 levels. Among the CU individuals, age‐specific thresholds corresponding to a fixed PPV of 0.7 yielded an overall p‐tau217 positivity rate of 11.79%, increasing with age from 3.47% (< 60 years) to 6.28% (60–70 years) and 25.88% (≥70 years). These findings provide population‐based evidence to inform community screening for early AD detection, facilitate recruitment for preclinical AD trials, and support public health resource planning.

In the ASAP cohort design, participants aged 50 years and older were selected, as plasma AD biomarkers may become abnormal more than a decade before the onset of clinical symptoms. 14 The MES scale was adopted for its favorable balance of sensitivity and specificity, together with its low educational dependence, cultural adaptability, simplicity, and practicality for rapid on‐site administration. 12 , 15 The MES assesses memory and executive function—domains key to both AD and vascular cognitive impairment—and exhibits minimal ceiling or floor effects. In this cohort, compliance and completion rates were excellent. Its cultural and contextual suitability further lies in features such as the absence of writing requirements and memory assessment through contextually meaningful sentence recall rather than unrelated word lists. As the MES was developed in a highly educated Shanghai cohort, the conventional 72‐point cutoff may not be appropriate for the present population. Accordingly, cognitive impairment was defined using a regression‐based residual method that accounts for demographic influences of age, sex, and education. Using this approach, approximately 15% of participants were classified as CI, consistent with prior community‐based prevalence estimates of MCI. 16 , 17 , 18 In contrast, using a fixed MES cutoff of 72 classified 33.66% of participants as CI, indicating substantial overestimation. This likely reflects the inability of fixed cutoffs to adjust for demographic differences, leading to higher false‐positive rates, especially among older adults and those with lower levels of education.

This study employed a novel fully automated AD plasma biomarker chemiluminescence platform, which is easily implementable in primary care settings with minimal training and low operational costs. In the PET reference cohort, p‐tau217 exhibited the highest AUC value among the examined biomarkers for discriminating Aβ pathology (AUC = 0.95). Therefore, p‐tau217 alone was selected as the primary biomarker in this study. While the Vazyme® assay maintains diagnostic performance comparable to established methods, it offers higher throughput and lower cost, making it more suitable for clinical use. A single analyzer can process up to 48 samples per hour. The price of plasma p‐tau217 testing is approximately USD 40, supporting its feasibility for large‐scale community screening and longitudinal follow‐up. Several studies have demonstrated that p‐tau217 can distinguish Aβ‐positive MCI from Aβ‐negative cognitive impairment with an AUC of 0.92, in line with our findings. 19 , 20 A recent multicenter study reported an AUC of 0.83 for discriminating Aβ pathology in cognitively normal individuals. 21 This likely reflects the progressive rise of p‐tau217 over the course of AD, which enhances its discriminatory capacity at later disease stages. To maintain sample size and improve the stability of derived thresholds, individuals with AD dementia were not excluded in the current analysis. Previous research in Chinese populations using the Lumipulse G platform has shown that both plasma p‐tau217 and the p‐tau217/Aβ42 ratio reliably identify Aβ‐positive status in clinical and community cohorts, with the ratio performing slightly better in community settings. 22 In this study, the p‐tau217/Aβ42 ratio was not used, as its performance in the PET cohort was comparable to that of p‐tau217 alone. Additionally, plasma Aβ42 and Aβ40 levels decline rapidly within hours of collection, whereas p‐tau217 remains relatively stable. 23 Immediate processing of samples for large‐scale community screening places heavy demands on personnel and medical resources, and delays can substantially compromise assay accuracy, limiting the feasibility and efficiency of population‐level implementation.

In community‐based AD screening, a key challenge with plasma p‐tau217 is translating absolute values into an interpretable risk of AD or Aβ pathology. To address this, we used a hierarchical screening strategy: cognitive stratification by the MES scale followed by p‐tau217‐based risk stratification. A recent study of the HUNT cohort from a Norwegian community population adopted a strategy similar to that used in the present study. 24 In that study, plasma p‐tau217 was measured using the ALZpath p‐tau217 assay (Quanterix), and two clinically used cutoffs (0.40 pg/mL and 0.63 pg/mL) were applied to classify participants as AD pathology‐negative, intermediate, or positive, without PET or CSF validation. AD pathology prevalence increased sharply with age, from 3.9% at 58–59 years to 65.2% at ≥90 years, with the risk nearly doubling every 10 years. This study prioritized PPV given the relatively low prevalence of Aβ pathology in the screening population. Beyond the intrinsic analytical performance of plasma biomarkers, PPV is directly influenced by the underlying prevalence of Aβ pathology; therefore, an abnormal p‐tau217 result does not necessarily indicate Aβ positivity. Low PPV may impose unnecessary psychological burden on screened individuals and increase the cost of subsequent confirmatory testing. In this study, the age‐related Aβ positivity rates estimated using GMM were comparable to those reported in previous studies. 7 , 8 We established age‐ and cognition‐stratified p‐tau217 thresholds corresponding to prespecified PPVs (Figure 6). For CU populations, a PPV above 70% seems acceptable, whereas in populations with Aβ pathology prevalence exceeding 30%, higher PPVs (> 80%) are readily achievable. When confirmatory resources are limited, higher thresholds can reduce false positives; when follow‐up is feasible, lower thresholds may be used to enhance AD monitoring and prevention. Overall, this screening approach requires transparent result disclosure, privacy protection, guidance on AD prevention, and equitable access to confirmatory evaluation. 25 The proposed framework is recommended for high‐risk populations, such as individuals over 70 years of age or those with a family history of AD.

Apart from the approach used in this study, alternative screening strategies for cognitive impairment include cognitive assessment alone, plasma biomarkers alone, or a two‐step strategy combining both. Cognitive assessment alone cannot determine underlying pathology and requires careful calibration in populations with low educational attainment, whereas plasma biomarkers alone can detect AD pathology but may not capture cognitive status. The two‐step approach, in which blood testing is performed only in individuals with cognitive impairment, increases the prior probability in low‐prevalence community settings, thereby improving PPV and reducing false positives, but miss preclinical AD. 24

A major strength of this study is the large‐scale, community‐based screening cohort and the adoption of a PPV‐oriented, stratified thresholding strategy. This approach accommodates different screening scenarios and requirements. This study has several limitations. First, p‐tau217 thresholds were derived from the Aβ‐PET cohort, which differs from community populations. Although IPTW was used to balance demographic characteristics and major comorbidities between the PET cohort and the ASAP cohort, the current thresholds are preliminary and require further optimization in the ASAP cohort using PET or CSF biomarkers as the reference standard. Second, higher thresholds were selected to increase PPV in low‐prevalence age groups, which improves specificity but may reduce sensitivity and underestimate Aβ pathology prevalence. Third, as a newly developed assay system, the Vazyme® platform requires further independent validation. Fourth, participants were voluntarily recruited rather than randomly sampled; therefore, the reported prevalence of cognitive impairment and Aβ pathology may not be generalizable to the general population.

In summary, this study explored a brain health screening model for older adults by integrating cognitive assessment with plasma biomarkers and defining age‐ and cognitive status‐specific thresholds. The proposed framework provides a practical approach to integrating plasma biomarkers into community‐based evaluations, with future efforts needed to optimize AD plasma biomarker thresholds through longitudinal follow‐up.

The efficacy of sequential radiotherapy after the combination of TACE with lenvatinib and anti-PD-1 antibodies for unresectable HCC
Authors: Xiangye Ou, Jinhai Li, Rongjian Pan, Deyi Liu, Jiayi Wu, Dong Liang, Yinan Li, Zhenxin Zeng, Han Li, Yuxuan Zhang, Junyi Wu, Maolin Yan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTriple therapy of transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE) combined with lenvatinib and anti-PD-1 antibodies has demonstrated excellent efficacy in unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (uHCC). However, tumor drug resistance is still a major problem and the rate of complete response in uHCC following triple therapy is relatively low. This study aimed to investigate the efficacy and safety of sequential radiotherapy after triple therapy in patients with uHCC to improve tumor response rate.MethodsThis retrospective study included 29 patients with uHCC who received sequential radiotherapy after achieving partial response (PR) or stable disease (SD) in tumor response per the modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (mRECIST) after triple therapy. The overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), and objective response rate (ORR) were analyzed to evaluate the efficacy of this regimen. Treatment-related adverse events were assessed to determine the safety profile.ResultsAmong the 29 patients, the median OS had not yet been reached, and the median PFS was 19.43 months. The 24-month OS and PFS rates were 72.8% and 46.5%, respectively. The lesions of 6 patients initially with tumor response of PR achieved complete response, and the lesions of 12 patients with SD achieved PR after sequential radiotherapy. All adverse events were manageable, and no treatment-related death occurred.ConclusionSequential radiotherapy after triple therapy enhanced tumor response and survival benefits for uHCC patients, with manageable adverse effects.

Triple therapy has demonstrated excellent efficacy in unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma. However, tumor drug resistance is still a major problem and complete necrosis of the lesions is not achieved in a proportion of individuals. Radiotherapy exhibits a favorable therapeutic impact on local lesions, and there may be a synergistic mechanism with targeted therapy and immunotherapy. Sequential radiotherapy after triple therapy may be able to control residual lesions, deepen tumor response, and achieve better therapeutic effects.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the most prevalent form of primary liver cancer, ranking as the sixth most common cancer and the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality globally.1 Moreover, due to the subtle early clinical signs, many patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage, precluding the possibility of curative surgery. The current first-line treatment regimens for this unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (uHCC) recommended in the NCCN guidelines include atezolizumab combined with bevacizumab, tremelimumab combined with durvalumab, sorafenib, lenvatinib, and other therapies.2 The median overall survival (OS) ranged from 10.7 to 19.2 months, and the objective response rate (ORR) was approximately 20.0%-27.3%.3–6

In recent years, numerous studies have documented that the combination of transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE) with lenvatinib and anti-PD-1 antibodies, known as triple therapy, has yielded superior outcomes in the management of advanced HCC, with an ORR of 26.1%-87.2%, median progression-free survival (PFS) of 6.3 to 22.5 months, and median OS of 15.7-29 months.7–10 Despite the impressive overall results of triple therapy, it is noteworthy that complete necrosis of the lesions is not observed in a proportion of individuals. as per the modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (mRECIST) post-treatment, with a complete response (CR) rate of 12.2%-16.7%.7,9,10 Studies have shown that patients with advanced HCC whose tumor response reaches CR after triple therapy have better survival benefits (2-year OS rate reached 90.1%).11 And patients whose lesions achieved major pathological response (MPR) or pathological complete response (pCR) after triple therapy had good long-term efficacy (1-year OS rate of 91.3%-97.1%).12,13 It could be seen that enhancing the extent of tumor response can result in significant long-term survival benefits. Consequently, managing the residual tumors to improve tumor response is pivotal to enhancing the efficacy of combined therapeutic strategies.

As precision therapy technology advances, local radiotherapy has been increasingly utilized in the treatment of advanced HCC. National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines recommend that patients with HCC who are not eligible for surgery, with tumor residual after interventional embolization, with macrovascular invasion, or with extrahepatic metastasis can be treated with external radiation therapy.2 Current researches indicated that radiotherapy offered substantial survival benefits for patients with advanced HCC, with a 1-year OS rate of 77.3% and a 3-year OS rate of 47.4%, and showing more obvious therapeutic effect for recurrent HCC particularly.14,15 In combination therapy, radiotherapy combined with targeted or immunotherapy has yielded promising outcomes, with median PFS ranging from 8.7 to 13.6 months and median OS from 19.0 to 20.1 months.16,17 These outcomes demonstrated the feasibility of radiotherapy for advanced HCC, and highlighted the potential of radiotherapy in enhancing the efficacy of combination therapy approaches.

In view of the fact that radiotherapy exhibits a favorable therapeutic impact on local lesions, and there may be a synergistic mechanism with targeted therapy and immunotherapy. Sequential radiotherapy may be able to control residual lesions, deepen tumor response, and achieve better therapeutic effects. Therefore, we designed this study to investigate the efficacy and safety of sequential radiotherapy in patients with uHCC, which were assessed as partial response (PR) or stable disease (SD) after triple therapy.

Materials and methods

This study protocol received ethical approval from the Institutional Review Board of Fuzhou University Affiliated Provincial Hospital(K2024-09-046). And it was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov(NCT07052448). The requirement of informed consent has been waived because of the retrospective nature of this study.

Patients

This study encompassed patients with uHCC who received triple therapy sequential radiotherapy between November 2020 and May 2023 at Fuzhou University Affiliated Provincial Hospital. Detailed radiological examinations, physical examination, liver function tests, Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer stage, best tumor response before radiotherapy and target of radiotherapy were collected at the initial visit. The diagnosis of HCC was based on histological examination of the tumor tissue or clinicoradiological criteria in accordance with the American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases criteria.18

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients diagnosed with HCC and assessed as unresectable by multidisciplinary team (MDT); (2) patients whose lesions achieved PR or SD after triple therapy according to the mRECIST and did not meet the criteria for radical surgical resection or could not tolerate surgical treatment; (3) patients who received sequential radiotherapy; (4) age between 18 and 75 years; (5) Child-Pugh class A or B; (6) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) score of 0-1; The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) previous acceptance of other antitumor treatments; (2) treatment in conjunction with other treatment regimens, such as radiofrequency ablation and chemotherapy; (3) history of other malignancies; and (4) incomplete data.

Procedure

Triple therapy

All patients received lenvatinib (8 mg for body weight < 60 kg or 12 mg for body weight≥ 60 kg) orally once daily, and anti-PD-1 antibodies (sintilimab 200 mg, tislelizumab 200 mg, camrelizumab 200 mg, toripalimab 240 mg, or pembrolizumab 200 mg) intravenously once every 3 weeks. Moreover, according to contrast-enhanced dynamic computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), TACE was performed every 4-6 weeks if there was an obvious hepatic arterial blood supply to the HCC. Lenvatinib and anti-PD-1 antibodies were stopped for 3 days before and after TACE. TACE was performed through the right femoral artery under local anesthesia. After the tumor-supplying artery was identified by selective liver angiography, the iodized oil was mixed with pirarubicin and injected into the selected tumor artery via a microcatheter. Subsequently, the supplying artery was selectively embolized with gelatin sponge particles until complete arterial flow arrest was observed.

Radiotherapy

Patients were recommended for initial evaluation of efficacy 2-6 months after receiving triple therapy, and those with tumor responses of PR or SD received sequential radiotherapy. The selection of radiotherapy modality is guided by discussions by an MDT and patients’ preferences. The radiotherapy regimen encompasses both stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) and conventional radiotherapy, with technical approaches including intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT) and volumetric modulated arc therapy (VMAT). Radiologists use a radiology planning system to delineate the clinical target volume (CTV) under CT guidance. The planned target volume (PTV) is defined as a 5-10 mm peripheral expansion based on CTV. At least 95% of PTV receives the prescribed dose. SBRT is defined as an extended hypofractionated stereotactic technique, with a total dose ranging from 24 to 60 Gy and a fractionation scheme consisting of 3 to 10 fractions. The dose limits for each organ are as follows: Intrahepatic lesion (maximum dose ≤60 Gy); Adrenal metastasis (maximum dose ≤42 Gy); Lymph node metastasis (maximum dose ≤45 Gy); Bone metastases (maximum dose ≤40 Gy); Macrovascular invasion (maximum dose ≤42 Gy). The dose limitations for organs at risk (OARs) are presented in Supplementary Tables S1 and S2.

Efficacy and safety evaluation

Tumor responses were assessed according to the mRECIST, including CR, PR, SD, progressive disease (PD), and ORR. ORR was defined as the sum of CR and PR rates. The survival benefit was evaluated by OS and PFS. OS was defined as the time interval from the start date of triple therapy to the date of death or the most recent follow-up. Meanwhile, PFS was defined as the time interval from triple therapy to the first radiologically confirmed PD, last follow-up, or death. Data were right censored at the last follow-up for living patients with no evidence of disease progression or death. For patients with simultaneous macrovascular invasion, lymph node metastasis, and extrahepatic lesions. CR was defined as all lesions achieving CR; PR was defined as intrahepatic lesions assessed as PR and the rest of the lesions achieving PR or CR, or intrahepatic lesions assessed as CR and the rest of the lesions achieving PR; SD was defined as intrahepatic lesions assessed as SD and the rest of the lesions achieving SD or PR or CR, or intrahepatic lesions assessed as CR or PR and the rest of the lesions achieving SD; and PD was defined as any lesion being assessed as PD or the appearance of a new lesion. During data collection, tumor response evaluation was performed by two fixed, experienced clinical physicians who independently reviewed, measured, and assessed the patients’ imaging data. In cases of discrepant opinions between the two clinicians, a third senior clinician was consulted for assessment to minimize errors arising from different evaluation criteria.

Treatment safety was evaluated continuously based on clinical symptoms and laboratory indicators. Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) were assessed according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events ver. 5.0. During the collection of treatment-related adverse event (AE) data, a well-experienced clinical physician reviewed the patients’ previous medical records and conducted at least one telephone follow-up and one outpatient visit. The physician inquired in detail about any adverse events experienced by the patients, performed thorough physical examinations, and recorded and graded the severity of the adverse events. Additionally, the evaluation results were reviewed by a senior clinician to minimize potential data bias.

Follow-up

The patients were followed up every 4-8 weeks. Clinical, laboratory, and radiological data were collected at each time point. After radiotherapy, patients continued the previous treatment regimen until PD, intolerable toxicity, or withdrawal of consent. Subsequent treatment, including radiofrequency ablation or systemic therapy, depended on the discussion by an MDT and patients’ preferences. All patients were followed up until death or the end date of September 2024.

Statistical analysis

Continuous data were expressed as mean (standard deviation) or median (range) according to the normality of the data, and categorical data were expressed as numbers (%). OS and PFS were calculated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. Statistical significance was defined as a two-tailed P-value < .05. Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics ver. 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY).

Results

Patient characteristics

Between November 2020 and June 2025, 29 patients who received triple therapy sequential radiotherapy at Fuzhou University Affiliated Provincial Hospital were included in this study (Figure 1). The baseline demographics and characteristics were listed in Table 1. The majority of the patients were under the age of 65 (72.4%); 26 patients (89.7%) were male; 21 patients (72.4%) had an ECOG PS score of 0; 13 patients (44.8%) had baseline AFP ≥ 400 ng/mL and 14 patients (48.3%) had baseline DCP ≥ 400 mAU/mL; 22 patients’ liver function (75.9%) was classified as Child-Pugh class A. Furthermore, 22 patients (75.9%) had maximum tumor size ≥ 5 cm, while 15 patients (51.7%) had multiple tumors. 23 patients (79.4%) were evaluated as BCLC stage C. Among these 29 patients, the lesions of 17 patients (58.6%) had tumor response of PR after triple therapy, while 12 (41.4%) achieved SD (Table 1).

Efficacy outcomes

As of the data cutoff date of December 2025, the median follow-up duration was 19.56 months (range, 7.77 to 62.30 months). Among the 29 patients, after sequential radiotherapy, the lesions of 6 patients initially with tumor response of PR achieved CR, and the lesions of 12 patients with SD achieved PR according to mRECIST criteria. The ORR reached 100%. Furthermore, treatment response was observed across multiple metastatic sites: significant retraction of lymph node metastases in 6 patients, complete disappearance in 1, inactivation of adrenal metastases in 2, shrinkage of bone metastases in 2, prominent retraction of vascular tumor thrombus in 5, and complete necrosis in 1 (Table 2). The median OS had not reached, and the median PFS was 19.43 months (95% confidence interval [CI], 9.580 to 29.287). The 6-, 12-, 18-, and 24-month OS rates were 100.0%, 96.3%, 78.4% and 72.8%, respectively. The 6-, 12-, 18-, and 24-month PFS rates were 96.6%, 85.9%, 55.8%, and 46.5%, respectively (Figures 2 and 3). Safety outcomes

Until December 2025, TRAEs were observed in 24 patients (82.8%), and 8 patients (27.9%) experienced grade 3/4 TRAEs (Table 3). Among these, the most frequent were abnormal liver function (58.6%), fatigue (54.5%), and hypertension (51.7%). The most frequent grade 3/4 TRAEs were abnormal liver function (13.8%), hypertension (6.9%) and thrombocytopenia (6.9%). During the follow-up period, 3 patients (10.3%) discontinued anti-PD-1 antibodies and lenvatinib, and 4 patients (13.8%) reduced the dose of lenvatinib owing to grade 3/4 TRAEs, respectively. These results are similar to those previously reported. There were no grade 5 TRAEs reported. All TRAEs were manageable throughout the follow-up period.

Discussion

This is the first study that assessed the efficacy of triple therapy sequential radiotherapy in uHCC, yielding a surprising result. During a median follow-up of 19.56 months, the median OS had not been reached, and the median PFS was 19.43 months. The 1- and 2-year OS rates were 96.3% and 72.8%, respectively. The 1- and 2-year PFS rates were 85.9% and 46.5%, respectively. The ORR reached 100% (CR: 20.7%, PR: 79.3%) according to mRECIST criteria. Regarding safety, TRAEs were similar to the previous reports and manageable.

Combining local therapy with targeted therapy and immunotherapy for advanced HCC has been a research focus point.19–21 Our team’s previous research has demonstrated that after receiving triple therapy, patients with advanced HCC have good survival benefits and the opportunity to receive salvage surgery. The overall survival of 1- and 2-year patients can reach 97.1% and 94.4%, MPR and PCR can reach 84.3% and 41.4%, respectively.12 For patients with HCC and PVTT, triple therapy also showed good efficacy, with a median OS over 24 months, median PFS of 12.53 months, an ORR of 69.8%, and a DCR of 84.0%, respectively.22 In addition, the tumor response after triple therapy is closely related to the survival prognosis of patients. Among patients who were assessed as having CR after triple therapy, and underwent salvage surgery, the 2-year OS achieved 90.1%.11 Yuan et al. demonstrated that HCC with PVTT treated with interventional therapy combined with targeted therapy and immunotherapy, with postoperative case results showing 31.6% of patients whose lesions achieved pCR. The 1-year and 2-year OS achieved 75.7% and 57.8%, respectively.23 It is evident that the greater the tumor response to combination therapy, the greater the survival benefit.

Although triple therapy has shown remarkable outcomes for advanced HCC, not all patients receive a full survival benefit. Some tumor lesions exhibit primary or secondary drug resistance, which contributes to low rates of CR. Enhancing the rates of CR or MPR can be instrumental in improving the overall survival prognosis for patients with advanced HCC. There is no standardized treatment protocol to improve tumor response after triple therapy. On this basis, radiotherapy could potentially offer a new therapeutic alternative.

Previously, radiation therapy for HCC was limited due to the radiosensitivity of normal liver parenchyma.24 However, with the development of precision radiotherapy techniques, increasingly literature has reported the application of radiotherapy in HCC. Two prospective studies of SBRT for the treatment of patients with HCC reported a favorable prognosis with 2-year local tumor control rate of 97% and 3-year OS rate of 78%.25,26 Another two controlled studies have shown that SBRT is no less effective than hepatectomy as well as radiofrequency ablation in the treatment of early-stage HCC, and that was more effective than radiofrequency ablation for HCC with residual or recurrent tumors after TACE treatment. Feasibility of visible radiotherapy in the treatment of HCC.27,28

Additionally, recent literature has also extensively debated the efficacy of radiotherapy in combination therapies for advanced HCC. A study in patients with TACE-refractory lesions showed that SRBT plus anti-PD-1 antibodies could significantly improve PFS and OS, with a median PFS of 19.6 months, an ORR of 71.0%, and the median OS was not reached.29 A retrospective cohort study enlisting 51 patients with HCC and PVTT suggested that hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy combined with radiotherapy may be an alternative treatment option for these patients. The median OS and PFS were 12.1 and 4.2 months, respectively. The ORR was 19.6%, with 13.7% in the primary tumor and 51.0% in the thrombus.30 Luo et al. indicated that patients with HCC with extrahepatic oligometastasis who receive IMRT in combination with TACE had an excellent survival benefit (median OS: 21 months, median PFS: 9.1 months, 1-year OS rate: 83%).31 These studies underscore the significant role of radiotherapy in combination therapy, particularly for patients with residual liver lesions post-first-line treatment, macrovascular invasion, and extrahepatic metastases.

Based on the above research, this study investigated the effects of triple therapy sequential radiotherapy, compared with the use of radiotherapy alone or direct combined radiotherapy, sequential radiotherapy is based on the good efficacy of triple therapy, the treatment for residual lesions, which is more targeted and less damaging to normal liver tissue. And the results of this study confirm that sequential radiotherapy can significantly enhance local tumor response, consequently extending patients’ survival times. The addition of radiotherapy to the treatment led to 6 patients with PR achieving CR, and 12 patients with SD achieving PR. Both extrahepatic metastases and macrovascular cancer thrombus exhibited marked regression. This may be due to a potential synergistic mechanism between radiotherapy and anti-PD-1 antibodies and lenvatinib. In addition to the tumor-killing effect of radiotherapy itself, prior evidence has demonstrated that radiotherapy can exert a pivotal immunomodulatory effect, inducing immunogenic cell death, releasing tumor antigens, and stimulating T cell-mediated immune responses.32–34 It can also trigger systemic anti-tumor immunity to manage metastatic lesions and elicit an ‘abscopal effect’.35 While the underlying mechanisms of this combined action require further investigation and clarification, the survival benefits derived from its integrated use are undeniable.

The present study has several limitations. Firstly, it is a single-center, retrospective study with a limited sample size and relatively short follow-up duration, which may lead to selection bias and relatively insufficient medical evidence. Secondly, the absence of a control group precludes the ability to quantify the extent to which sequential radiotherapy following triple therapy confers a survival benefit. Thirdly, The use of mRECIST and CTCAE in retrospective studies has its limitations.36 Therefore, in this study, at least two experienced clinical physicians independently assessed tumor response and treatment-related adverse events during data collection, in order to minimize potential data bias. Fourthly, the variability in the types of anti-PD-1 antibodies and radiotherapy modalities used across patients may have influenced the interpretation of treatment outcomes. Thus, further prospective, multicenter, and well-designed randomized controlled trials are needed to ascertain the benefits of triple therapy sequential radiotherapy in patients with advanced HCC.

In conclusion, sequential radiotherapy after the combination of TACE with lenvatinib and anti-PD-1 antibodies showed improving tumor response and survival benefits for uHCC patients with manageable adverse effects. This study presents a new, safe, and feasible treatment option; that is, patients in whom complete necrosis of the lesions has not been achieved after triple therapy may consider incorporating radiotherapy to enhance treatment efficacy.

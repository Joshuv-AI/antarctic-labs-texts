Bioinformatics analysis of PFKFB family in pan-cancer and preliminary exploration of PFKFB4 in bladder cancer
Authors: Yan Xu, Jian Xu, Chunyang Han, Qianlong Li, Hualong Lin, Zhiyong Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe phosphofructokinase-fructose bisphosphatases (PFKFBs) family is critical in tumors and the immune system. Nevertheless, the involvement of the PFKFB family genes in the immune microenvironment and prognosis of tumor patients has not been investigated using genomic and transcriptomic analyses.MethodsWe collected data from 33 cancers and comprehensively analyzed the PFKFB family genes for abnormal expression, genomic alterations, diagnostic and prognostic roles, subcellular localization, pathway enrichment, immune microenvironment, association with immune checkpoints, and drug sensitivity in the disease, as well as predicting patients’ immunotherapeutic response to ICI and targeting of small molecule drugs, and performed the above results by immunohistochemical staining, pathological sections, and experiments for validation. Finally, we experimentally confirmed the role of PFKFB4 in bladder cancer.ResultsWe found the differential expression and diagnostic biomarker value of PFKFBs family members in pan-cancer, and also found that they play a key role in pan-cancer in relation to the tumor microenvironment. PFKFBs are closely associated with common immune checkpoints, TMB and MSI, and PFKFBs family members can predict immunotherapy response in patients with a wide range of cancers. Finally, it was experimentally determined that knockdown of PFKFB4 gene expression inhibited proliferation and invasion of bladder cancer cells.ConclusionsOur study highlights the great potential of PFKFBs as prognostic and immunotherapeutic response biomarkers, which may pave the way for further investigation of the tumor infiltration mechanism and therapeutic potential of PFKFBs in cancer.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12935-026-04354-8.

Background

Since cancer is the primary cause of cancer-related deaths globally, it poses a serious threat to global public health [1]. Human genetics has undergone a revolution thanks to the advent of high-throughput sequencing and bioinformatics analysis tools, and genomic detection can help understand the function of human genomes in the course of cancer [2]. There is a lot of genetic variety within and across tumor types, but the majority of the genomic changes that interact with the tumor microenvironment and have a role in the immunotherapeutic response remain unidentified [3]. Thus, finding new biomarkers and treatment targets requires a knowledge of the multi-omics changes in various cancer types.

Phosphofructokinase-fructose bisphosphatases (PFKFBs), consisting of four members including PFKFB1, PFKFB2, PFKFB3, and PFKFB4, are regulatory glycolytic enzymes that control the formation and degradation of fructose 2,6-bisphosphate (F-2,6-BP) by modulating glycolysis [4]. According to reports, members of the PFKFB family regulate a number of different types of carcinogenesis and development [5]. For instance, by controlling the expression of PFKFB2, the exosome circCARM1 reprogrammes glycolysis in breast cancer [6]. The advancement of hepatocellular carcinoma can be influenced by PFKFB3’s ability to drive the metabolic reprogramming of glycolysis in these cells [7]. PFKFB4 phosphorylates NCOA3 to mediate the metabolic reprogramming of pentose phosphate (PPG). Its expression is notably increased in renal clear cell carcinoma compared to normal kidney tissue [4]. Current studies have shown that PFKFB4 is involved in the progression of a variety of tumors and can be used as a prognostic biomarker in a variety of tumors, but its systematic study in pan-cancer has not been elaborated [8–10]. PFKFBs are therefore important regulators in a range of human malignancies, and in order to get a better knowledge of PFKFBs’ involvement in cancer formation, the tumor microenvironment, and their potential for clinical therapy, comprehensive investigations of PFKFBs are both essential and helpful.

In this study, we explored the differential expression and diagnostic biomarker value of the PFKFBs family in pan-cancer. PFKFBs were also found to play a key role in pan-cancer in relation to the tumor microenvironment.PFKFBs are strongly associated with immunotherapy and sensitivity to common chemotherapeutic agents. Finally, it was experimentally determined that knockdown of PFKFB4 gene expression inhibited the proliferation and invasion of bladder cancer cells.

Materials and methods

Data sources

We downloaded data from 11,093 normal and tumor tissue samples for 33 different cancer types using The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/2023) and UCSC Xena public datasets (http://xena.ucsc.edu/2023) [11]. The data included transcript expression (HTSeq-FPKM), clinical information (phenotype and survival data), clinicopathological sections, and mutations. The HPA database (https://www.proteinatlas.org/2023) was utilized to determine the cellular localization of the PFKFB family members, and immunohistochemistry labeling was employed to confirm the protein expression levels of the PFKFB family members in tumors and normal tissues [12].

Mutation analysis

We used the Gene Set Cancer Analysis (GSCA) database (http://bioinfo.life.hust.edu.cn/GSCA/#/2023) [13] to conduct a thorough investigation of genomic abnormalities, including copy number alterations, somatic mutations, and methylation, in members of the PFKFB family. A thorough database for analyzing genetic changes in cancer is the GSCA database.

Analysis of diagnostic and prognostic value

The link between sensitivity and specificity may be seen in ROC graphs.Tests for diagnosis are frequently assessed using the area under the curve (AUC).The more diagnostic the variable is in predicting an event, the closer its AUC value is to 1.Using the mRNA expression data from TCGA, the diagnostic value of the PFKFB family genes was evaluated, and ROC curves were shown using a “pROC” tool.

The UCSC Xena database included prognostic data on individuals with pancreatic cancer, including overall survival (OS), disease-free survival (DFS), progression-free survival (PFS), and disease-specific survival (DSS). Next, one-way Cox regression and Kaplan-Meier curves were used to assess the predictive significance of PFKFBs in each malignancy. The threshold for the study was adj P value < 0.05. We positioned risk factors by analyzing results with HR > 1 and protective factors with 0 < HR < 1.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and immune infiltration analysis

We downloaded the gene sets of the GO, KEGG, and HALLMARK cancer-related pathways from the MSigDB database (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/index.jsp) in order to assess the expression of each PFKFB gene correlation with cancer-related pathways in order to look into the biological roles of PFKFB family members in different cancers.

The TISIDB database (http://cis.hku.hk/TISIDB/2023) [14] was used to download the correlation between the expression of PFKFB family genes and the degree of immune cell infiltration. The correlation between PFKFB genes and immune cell infiltration was then examined by calculating the Spearman correlation coefficients between PFKFB genes and immune cell-associated gene expression.

Immunotherapy response and drug sensitivity

Using Spearman correlation analysis, we determined the relationships between members of the PFKFB family and common immunotherapy biomarkers, such as tumor mutation burden (TMB), microsatellite instability (MSI), and common immune checkpoint genes. Using the CTRP and GDSC databases (https://www.cancerrxgene.org/2023, https://portals.broadinstitute.org/ctrp.v2.1/) [15], relationships between drug sensitivity and the expression of PFKFB family members were examined.

Cell culture

The Shanghai Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences provided the T24 and UM-UC3 human bladder cancer cell lines, which were then grown in a medium containing 5% fetal bovine serum in an incubator set at 37 °C with 5% CO2.

Cell counting kit-8 (CCK8) and EdU assay

Spread out in 96-well plates, T24 and UM-UC3 cells transfected with si-PFKFB4 were then injected into each well. CCK8 solution was then poured and mixed into each well at 0 h, 24 h, 48 h, 72 h, and 96 h, respectively, and the plates were then incubated at 37 °C without light. Using multimode zymography, the absorbance value of each well at 450 nm was calculated and tallied. After being transfected with si-PFKFB4, T24 and UM-UC3 cells were injected into 24-well plates and given an addition of EdU medium for incubation in each well. Cell fixative was added to each well to fix it at room temperature. Next, the glycine decolorization shaker was used to incubate the wells, and finally, slow shaking permeabilizing agent was added to the shaker. Following the addition of the staining reaction solution to each well under light avoidance treatment, fluorescence microscopy was used to analyze the pictures.

Wound healing and transwell experiments

After being digested and resuspended, T24 and UM-UC3 cells were counted, plated, vertically scored using a straightedge and a lance tip, placed to serum-free media, and examined under a microscope for 48 h. Trypsin-digested cells were resuspended in serum-free incomplete media. Then, the upper chamber of the transwell was filled with the cell suspension above, while the bottom chamber was filled with complete medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum. The transwell was removed 48 h later. Crystal violet staining was applied after the cells were fixed in paraformaldehyde. Images were captured using a microscope, and ImageJ was used to count the number of cells.

Statistical analysis

Overall survival (OS) was calculated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and survival curves were compared using the log-rank test. Correlation analysis by Pearson correlation was used to measure the linear relationship between the two variables, the data conformed to normal distribution with no outliers and were continuous. Spearman correlation analysis was used to measure the monotonic relationship between the two variables, X and Y. The independent and dependent variables were ordinal/hierarchical data.The threshold for the study was adj P value < 0.05. Image J and GraphPad software were used for experimental data processing and graphing. The FDR method is used when analyzing multiple datasets. R software is available on the official website in 2023.

Results

Expression of PFKFBs family genes in cancers

We examined the variations in PFKFBs mRNA expression in cancers and normal tissues in unpaired and paired samples using the TCGA database in order to further explore the involvement of PFKFBs family members in different tumor types. While PFKFB4 expression was considerably greater in most cancers with significantly elevated expression, PFKFB1, PFKFB2, and PFKFB3 expression was significantly lower in most tumors as compared to the corresponding normal tissues (Fig. 1A).

Dysregulated expression of PFKFBs family genes in cancer. (A) Differential gene expression of each PFKFBs family gene in various tumors. (B) Immunohistochemical staining of PFKFBs family genes. Darker IHC staining represents higher levels of protein expression

Then, using the HPA database as a guide, we examined the immunohistochemistry staining of PFKFBs in tumors to determine the extent of their protein expression. Comparing these cancers to their normal counterparts, we discovered that PFKFB4 expression was higher in endometrial cancer and decreased in breast cancer, ccRCC, and hepatocellular carcinoma, and that PFKFB3 expression was lower in squamous lung carcinoma, clear cell renal cell carcinoma, and hepatocellular carcinoma (Fig. 1B).

Gene alterations in the PFKFBs family

PFKFB1 exhibited the greatest frequency of harmful mutations in endometrial cancer (UCEC) among pan-cancers when we first examined single nucleotide variations (SNVs) (Figure S1A). When PFKFB family genes were analyzed for mutation frequency in pan-cancers, it was found that, out of all the family members, PFKFB1 had the greatest frequency of mutations (38%) (Figure S1B). The copy number variation (CNV) of the PFKFB family genes was then described. First, we examined the various forms of CNV (heterozygous amplification, pure amplification, heterozygous deletion, and pure deletion) of each family member in pan-cancer. We discovered that the percentage of PFKFB family members that were amplified was higher in BRCA, LIHC, UCEC, and UCS, while the percentage of deletions was higher in KICH, KIRC, HNSC, and GBM (Figure S1C). Additionally, we discovered a strong positive association in the majority of malignancies between the CNV of the PFKFB family genes and their levels of mRNA expression, with PFKFB2 showing the strongest correlation in pan-cancer (Figure S1D). The PFKFB family genes’ methylation status was subsequently investigated, and we discovered that it differed amongst malignancies, with PFKFB4 exhibiting a negative correlation in THCA and PFKFB3 exhibiting a substantial positive association in BRCA (Figure S1E). Furthermore, we discovered a strong negative correlation between the mRNA expression levels of the PFKFB family members and their methylation status in the setting of CNV (Figure S1F).

Diagnostic and prognostic value of PFKFB family genes in pancreatic cancer

ROC curves were used to assess the PFKFB family members’ potential as diagnostic biomarkers. Indicating that the PFKFBs had strong diagnostic value for these malignancies, the AUC values of PFKFB1 in CESC and CHOL, PFKFB3 in KICH, and PFKFB4 in BLCA, CHOL, PCPG, and UCEC, for instance, were all more than 0.95. Furthermore, in CHOL, PFKFB2’s AUC value was as high as 1. According to our findings, members of the PFKFB family with abnormal expression may be employed as cancer diagnostic biomarkers.The TCGA database was utilized to do uniCox regression and log-rank analysis on the PFKFB family genes in order to assess their predictive importance in various cancer types.

According to the findings, PFKFB1 was harmful in COAD but protective in ACC. It was also hazardous for disease-specific survival (DSS), overall survival (OS), and disease-free interval (DFI) in KIRC and SARC (Fig. 2B, Table S1). In CHOL, PFKFB2 was protective, but it was dangerous in KICH and LIHC, and in BLCA, LGG, and OV, it was hazardous for OS, DSS, and DFI (Fig. 2C, Table S1). In KIRC, PFKFB3 was protective, while in ACC, CESC, KIRP, SARC, and UVM, it was dangerous (Fig. 2D, Table S1). In CESC, KICH, LIHC, and MESO, PFKFB4 was dangerous (Fig. 2E, Table S1).

Subcellular localization and functional enrichment analysis of PFKFB family members

We used immunofluorescence pictures of PFKFB family members from the HPA database to better understand the subcellular localization of these proteins in order to gain a deeper understanding of the PFKFB family. The findings indicated that the nucleoplasm included the proteins PFKFB2, PFKFB3, and PFKFB4 (Fig. 3A).

To further understand the function of PFKFBs in the development of cancer, we examined the PFKFB family genes in relation to KEGG-related pathways. It was discovered that PFKFB1 low expression was enriched in MAPK SIGNALING PATHWAY and JAK STAT SIGNALING PATHWAY, whereas PFKFB1 high expression was enriched in PPAR SIGNALING PATHWAY.The JAK STAT SIGNALING PATHWAY, NOD LIKE RECEPTOR SIGNALING PATHWAY, and TOLL LIKE RECEPTOR SIGNALING PATHWAY were shown to have higher levels of PFKFB2 low expression. The MAPK SIGNALING PATHWAY was where PFKFB3 high expression was concentrated, while the REGULATION OF AUTOPHAGY was where PFKFB3 low expression was enriched.The CALCIUM SIGNALING PATHWAY has an enrichment of PFKFB4 low expression (Fig. 3B).

Association of PFKFB family genes with tumor microenvironment

PFKFB3 was considerably favorably connected with most immune cells, whereas PFKFB2 and PFKFB4 were negatively correlated with most immune cells, according to our association study of PFKFB family genes with different immune infiltrating cells in pan-cancer (Fig. 4A). We also used TCGA pathology sections to corroborate the aforesaid analytical results. For instance, patients with high PFKFB3 expression (TCGA-FD-A6TE) with bladder cancer had a greater amount of immune cell infiltration than patients with low PFKFB3 expression (TCGA-XF-A8HG). Patients with high PFKFB3 expression (TCGA-D7-6521) with gastric cancer had a higher amount of immune cell infiltration than patients with low PFKFB3 expression (TCGA-VQ-A8DV) (Fig. 4B).

Association between PFKFB family and immunotherapy and drug sensitivity

We also looked at the relationship between microsatellite instability (MSI) and tumor mutational burden (TMB) and PFKFB family expression. Each member of the PFKFB family and TMB in pan-cancer were correlated with TMB, and the results indicated that PFKFB1 correlated significantly positively with TMB in BLCA, LAML, LGG, LIHC, and PRAD, and significantly negatively with TMB in DLBC, LUAD, PAAD, THCA, and UCS. Significant positive correlations were seen between TMB and PFKFB2, ESCA, LAML, PRAD, STAD, and UCEC in BLCA.In ACC, CESC, COAD, SARC, SKCM, TGCT, and THYM, PFKFB3 had a strong positive association with TMB; in BLCA, BRCA, KIRP, LGG, LUSC, PRAD, and THCA, on the other hand, it demonstrated a substantial negative connection with TMB. In BLCA, BRCA, CESC, COAD, KICH, KIRC, LIHC, LUAD, SARC, SKCM, STAD, THCA, THYM, and UCEC, PFKFB4 was substantially positively correlated with TMB (Fig. 5A). PFKFB1 was substantially negatively connected with MSI in TGCT and favorably correlated with MSI in LUAD, LUSC, PRAD, and READ with regard to microsatellite instability in pan-cancer. In LUAD, LUSC, STAD, and UCEC, PFKFB2 had a positive correlation with MSI; in COAD, DLBC, HNSC, SARC, and THCA, however, there was a significant negative correlation. In CESC, COAD, and USC, PFKFB3 showed a positive correlation with MSI, but in RCA, DLBC, HNSC, SKCM, and STAD, there was a negative correlation. In CESC, COAD, HNSC, LUSC, PAAD, SARC, STAD, and UCEC, PFKFB4 and MSI had a positive correlation (Fig. 5B). We also looked at the relationship between the expression of shared immune checkpoints in pan-cancer and each gene in the PFKFB family. In the majority of malignancies, there was a substantial positive correlation between PFKFB3 and PD-1, PD-L1, CTLA4, and PD-L2 (Fig. 5C). This implies that the genes belonging to our PFKFB family could have significant effects on immunotherapy.

The immunotherapy data, which demonstrated that PFKFB1 expression in breast cancer (4T1) was considerably lower in the anti-PD-1 treatment non-responder group compared to baseline, was then used to assess the function of the PFKFB family in immunotherapy. Furthermore, melanoma (B16), colorectal cancer (CT26), lung cancer (LLC), head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (MOC22), gastric adenocarcinoma (YTN16), breast cancer (4T1, EMT6, P53-2225 L and T11), and gastric adenocarcinoma (YTN16) all showed significantly lower expression levels of PFKFB3 in comparison to baseline (Fig. 5D).

Using the GDSC and CTRP databases, we also examined the link between PFKFBs and drug sensitivity. A positive correlation indicates that a gene with increased expression is more resistant to the medication, and vice versa. For instance, there was a significant negative correlation between PFKFB2 expression and dexamethasone drug sensitivity, whereas PFKFB2 mRNA expression level demonstrated a significant positive correlation. As a result, tumors with upregulated or downregulated PFKFB2 expression were more drug-sensitive to dexamethasone (Figure S2).

PFKFB4 knockdown inhibits bladder cancer cell proliferation and migration

In T24 and UM-UC3 cells, we created two siRNAs (PFKFB4-1, PFKFB4-2) to reduce PFKFB4 expression in order to study the function of PFKFB4 in bladder cancer cells(Table S2). By using qPCR to confirm the reduction of PFKFB4 expression, the findings demonstrated that the two siRNAs mentioned above were capable of successfully reducing PFKFB4 expression (Figure S3A). After transfecting T24 and UM-UC3 cells with si-PFKFB4, we conducted CCK8, EdU, wound healing, and Transwell assays on them, respectively. The CCK8 results demonstrated that at 24, 48, 72, and 96 h, the proliferation ability of T24 and UM-UC3 cells in the NC group was significantly higher than that of the two si-PFKFB4 groups (Figure S3B). The EdU staining experiment findings demonstrated that T24 and UM-UC3 cells with the PFKFB4 gene knockdown had a much reduced potential for proliferation than the NC group (Fig. 6A-B). The transwell and wound healing experiment findings demonstrated a substantial reduction in the migratory capacity of T24 and UM-UC3 cells expressing low levels of PFKFB4 (Fig. 6C-F). The aforementioned findings suggested that bladder cancer cell migration and proliferation could be inhibited by PFKFB4 expression suppression.

Discussion

PFKFB family members are clinically valuable and have been shown to play significant roles in cancer by an increasing number of studies. Using extensive genomic and transcriptomic analyses pan-cancer data, we thoroughly examined the aberrant gene expression of the PFKFB family, genomic changes, diagnostic and prognostic roles, correlation with pathways linked to cancer, association with immune microenvironment and immune checkpoints, and prediction of patients’ immunotherapeutic response to immune checkpoint inhibitors and targeted small molecule drugs in cancers. We discovered that the levels of gene and protein expression for the PFKFB family are unique to different forms of cancer. For instance, there is a considerable difference in PFKFB3 between renal clear cell carcinoma (ccRCC), hepatocellular carcinoma, squamous cell lung cancer, and endometrial cancer. There are notable protein variations in PFKFB4 between endometrial, hepatocellular, breast, and ccRCC cancers. The expression of PFKFB genes in malignancies is influenced by DNA methylation and chromosomal changes. TMB and MSI are predictions of whether a patient will benefit from immunotherapy based on the level of tumor mutations detected in the patient prior to immunotherapy, and we found that PFKFB4 was significantly correlated with TMB and MSI in a wide range of tumors, suggesting that it may be closely associated with immunotherapy response. Furthermore, the majority of cancer patients may benefit from the use of PFKFB family genes as possible diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers, according to diagnostic and prognostic study conducted using the TCGA database. Our analysis of PFKFBs’ involvement in the development of cancer and the tumor microenvironment revealed that PFKFB3 strongly positively associated with the majority of immune cells, a finding that was corroborated by pathological sections.The PFKFB family may have substantial research value in the field of immunotherapy, as indicated by the strong link observed between the family and immunological checkpoints. In order to identify naturally occurring small molecules that can target the PFKFB1-3 protein, we lastly carried out molecular docking against the protein. Additionally, we discovered that bladder cancer cells’ capacity to proliferate and invade may be inhibited by PFKFB4 knockdown.

Significant advances in immunotherapy-based tumor therapy have opened up new avenues for treating tumors; nonetheless, because to the intricate nature of the tumor microenvironment, immunotherapy has resulted in tumor-specific reactions. Determining novel targets for immunotherapy that work better requires an understanding of the tumor microenvironment. According to research, PFKFB2-mediated glycolysis stimulates lactate-dependent persistent cytotoxicity in macrophages, and T-cell proliferation requires PFKFB3, the highest kinase in the PFKFB family of enzymes [16, 17]. Furthermore, the degradation of PFKFB3 causes macrophages to suppress their glycolytic activity, which in turn prevents macrophage activation and, eventually, lessens the severity of colitis in mice [18]. Pro-tumorigenic macrophages are induced by overexpression of PFKFB3 in colon cancer patient monocytes, but not in rectal cancer patient monocytes, indicating an increased probability of tumor recurrence [19].

According to a comprehensive analysis conducted in this work, PFKFB family members can serve as therapeutic targets, as well as diagnostic and prognostic indicators. However, our study does have certain shortcomings. Firstly, there are several biases, including the retrospective design, the limited number of patients and the mixed use of tumour specimens from patients who did and did not receive preoperative chemotherapy. To confirm the veracity of our findings, a large cohort of clinical cases must be gathered and evaluated. In this study, we used one-way Cox regression analysis to determine whether PFKFB family members were associated with survival in pan-cancer patients. However, one-way Cox regression analysis can only assess the effect of a single factor, and cannot consider the effects of multiple factors simultaneously. Furthermore, the use of multifactorial Cox regression analysis is limited by factors such as data availability or study design. Ultimately, subsequent research is required to confirm the role of PFKFB family member genes in cancer, and to identify the mechanism by which PFKFB family members operate as therapeutic targets and cancer biomarkers.

Conclusion

The PFKFBs family’s differential expression and diagnostic biomarker value in pan-cancer were examined in this work. Additionally, it was discovered that PFKFBs were crucial to pan-cancer in connection to the tumor microenvironment.Immunotherapy and common chemotherapeutic drug sensitivity are closely linked to PFKFBs. We found the cellular localization of PFKFB4 in cancer cells, its relationship with the immune microenvironment, as well as that it can be used as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker for bladder cancer by bioinformatics analysis, and we confirmed that knocking down the gene expression of PFKFB4 inhibited the proliferation and invasion of bladder cancer cells by in vitro experiments. This work used genomic and transcriptomic analyses to thoroughly examine the role and viability of PFKFB family members as therapeutic targets and biomarkers for prognosis and diagnosis, therefore identifying novel targets for cancer medication treatment in the future.

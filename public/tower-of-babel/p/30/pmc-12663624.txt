Effects of Chinese yam peels on immunity and gut microbiota in cyclophosphamide-induced chickens and optimization of extraction process
Authors: Qian Qu, Yimu Ma, Yiwen Huang, Xinglin Gao, Zhaoying Xuan, Xingyu Chen, Yaqi Tong, Shuhong Chai, Menghui Cao, Qi Dong, Weijie Lv, Shining Guo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractYam is famous for its nutritional and medicinal values and is widely distributed around the world, among which the Chinese yam (Dioscorea opposita thunb. cv. Tiegun) from Jiaozuo City, Henan Province is the most prominent. Chinese yam peel (CYP) is a waste product after processing, but it still retains abundant pharmacological compounds. In this study, the extraction process of CYP water extracts was optimized by single factor and response surface method according to the extraction rate and polysaccharide content of CYP extracts. The results showed that the optimal extraction process of CYP was solid-liquid ratio of 1:24, the extraction temperature of 75℃, and the extraction time of 4.4 h. The extraction rate of CYP extracts was 19.46 %, and the polysaccharide content was 100.53 mg/kg obtained by this extraction process. Seven-day-old chickens were given intramuscular injection of cyclophosphamide (CTX) for 3 days to establish immunosuppression models, supplemented with 0.5g/kg, 1g/kg and 2g/kg diet, respectively, and immunized with Combined Newcastle Disease and Infectious Bronchitis Live Vaccine. The results showed that different doses of CYP significantly inhibited CTX-induced decrease in body weight, thymus index, spleen index and bursa of Fabricius index. The levels of immunoglobulin IgG, IgM and Newcastle disease antibody in serum of chicken were also significantly reversed by CYP. Yam peel also repaired the intestinal barrier by up-regulating the relative mRNA expression of ZO-1, Claudin-1 and Occludin in jejunal, and increasing the ratio of villus height and crypt depth. In addition, CYP significantly improved the composition, richness and diversity of jejunal microbiota, and enriched the KEGG metabolic pathways related to amino acids, including histidine metabolism, cysteine and methionine metabolism, alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism. CYP also upregulated the abundance of beneficial bacteria Faecalibacterium, Subdoligranulum, and lachnospira, which were stronger positively correlated with the improvement effects of CTX-induced broiler, and downregulated the abundance of harmful bacteria Pseudomonas. The protective effect of CYP on CTX-induced broilers may be due to the regulation of gut microbiota by CYP, which in turn enhances immune function and repairs intestinal barrier.Keywords: Chinese yam peel, Immunosuppression, Gut microbiota, Immune function, Intestinal barrier

Introduction

Yam, known as “Shan Yao” in China, belongs to Dioscorea genus, which is mainly formed from the roots and tubers of the plant. Chinese yam is a highly valued medicinal plant. Among its varieties, the cultivar known as Dioscorea opposita Thunb. cv. Tiegun is particularly renowned; it is considered the premier variety of Huai Shan Yao from Jiaozuo City, Henan Province (An et al., 2024). It was recorded by Shennong's Materia Medica Classic for the treatment of tonic and beneficial qi (Khol et al., 2024). It is also a medicinal and food homologous plant, which is rich in nutrients such as protein, amino acids, starch, and fat, trace elements such as iron, calcium, phosphorus, and iodine, and active ingredients such as polysaccharides, alkaloids, and flavonoids (An et al., 2019; Kim et al., 2019). Modern pharmacological studies have shown that Chinese yam exhibits strong anti-inflammatory (Zeng et al., 2019), anti-oxidation (Hsu et al., 2006b), cholesterol-lowering (Wang et al., 2008), anti-obesity (Feng et al., 2024) and anti-cancer (Wang et al., 2021b) effects. The processing process of Chinese yam is washing, peeling and drying in turn, so the yam peel is abandoned, but there are still many medicinal active ingredients in the yam peel. Although Chinese yam peel (CYP) is not enough to be used in human medicine resources, it can be fully reused in livestock and poultry breeding. Some studies have reported that the addition of yam peel during carp feeding improves the intestinal barrier and regulates the intestinal microflora (Meng et al., 2019). However, the regulatory effects of CYP on livestock and poultry remain unclear.

Polysaccharides, characterized by complex carbohydrate polymerization by glycosidic bonds and more than 10 monosaccharides, are ubiquitous in nature (Li et al., 2024). Because of its rich content, polysaccharides are the main active ingredients of many traditional Chinese medicines (TCM) for disease prevention and treatment, including Cordyceps sinensis polysaccharide, Angelica polysaccharide, Ganoderma lucidum polysaccharide, etc. (Wang et al., 2024a). Polysaccharides in TCM are known for their immunomodulatory, anti-inflammatory, anti-tumor, and antioxidant activities, and are also known for their easy extraction, low toxicity, and minimal side effects (Li et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024). The extraction methods of polysaccharides include hot water extraction, alcohol precipitation, ultrasound-assisted and microwave-assisted extraction (Xue et al., 2024). Therefore, polysaccharides serve as a benchmark for optimizing the pharmacological activity of TCM in water extraction.

Immunosuppression is a common and ubiquitous disease caused by environment, stress, infection, etc., which leads to immune decline (Tian et al., 2024). Under intensive rearing conditions, the occurrence of immunosuppressive diseases in poultry is becoming more common, leading to increased susceptibility to other diseases as well as reduced vaccine immune effectiveness (Ma et al., 2023). Cyclophosphamide (CTX) is a well-established agent for inducing immunosuppression in experimental models. It primarily impairs the immune system by attenuating the specific immune response to antigens, reducing serum immunoglobulin levels and inducing atrophy of immune organs, particularly the spleen and thymus (Fan et al., 2013; Zhou et al., 2018)). These immunological deficits consequently lead to impairment of other functions, including gut microbiota dysbiosis and compromise of the intestinal mechanical barrier (Li et al., 2023). Long-term addition or abuse of antibiotics as therapeutic drugs leads to the inhibition of immune cell function (Jabs et al., 2018). Therefore, TCM has become one of the candidates for safe and effective anti-immunosuppressive drugs. Single herbs, compounds and active components of traditional Chinese medicine have been reported to be effective in the treatment of immunosuppression, including Lycium barbarum polysaccharide against immunosuppression through transcriptomics and metabolomics (Wang et al., 2024b), yam protein to alleviate intestinal immunosuppression through gut microbiota and its metabolites (Lu et al., 2024), kushen injection to alleviate macrophage-regulated immunosuppression by modifying the immune microenvironment (Yang et al., 2020), and so on. Gut microbiota, a group of microorganisms that live in the gastrointestinal tract of the host in a reciprocal manner, is often used to study the mechanism of TCM in disease prevention and treatment because of the integrity of its microecological balance (Feng et al., 2019). CYP has the potential to regulate immunosuppressive diseases through gut microbiota, which needs further exploration.

In this study, we optimized the extraction process of the hot water extract of CYP using single-factor and response methods, with polysaccharide content as the criterion. To evaluate the effects of CYP on immune function and intestinal health by constructing a chicken immunosuppression model with cyclophosphamide (CTX). Then, the changes of Gut microbiota were analyzed to preliminarily investigate the possible mechanism of CYP. Taken together, we provide evidence that human TCM wastes still retain significant properties against livestock and poultry diseases.

Materials and methods

Reagents

CYP (Dioscorea opposita thunb. cv. Tiegun) were provided by local farmers in Wen County, Jiaozuo City, Henan Province. Cyclophosphamide were purchased from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd (C849559). Combined Newcastle Disease and Infectious Bronchitis Vaccine, Live (Strain La Sota + Strain H120) were obtained from Pulike Biological Engineering, Inc. Methanol (Cas: 67-56-1), acetonitrile (Cas: 75-05-8), formic acid (Cas: 64-18-6) and isopropyl alcohol (Cas: 67-63-0) were purchased from ANPEL, and all solvents were LC-MS grade.

Single-factor experiment

According to the extraction process of the CYP extracts, we selected three extraction conditions for the single factor experiment: solid-liquid ratio (1:10, 1:15, 1:20, 1:25 and 1:30 g/mL), extraction time (1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 h) and extraction temperature (50, 60, 70, 80 and 85°C), and the optimized conditions were based on the extraction of polysaccharides as the standard. The extracted CYP supernatant was centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10 min, and then the polysaccharide levels were measured by the phenol-sulfuric acid method(Masuko et al., 2005).

Response surface test design

Referring to the results of single factor experiments, the Box-Behnken method in Design-Expert 8.0 was used to Design response surface experiments based on the independent variables (solid-liquid ratio (g/mL): A, temperature (°C): B, time (h): C) and levels (-1, 0, 1 represented the low, medium, and high levels of the independent variables) (Table S1). Box-Behnken results were then presented in Table S2 with the polysaccharide content (mg/g) of CYP extracts as the response value.

Preparation of CYP

CYP was dried to constant weight in an oven at 55°C, crushed, passed through a 60 meshes screen, and then extracted according to Box-Behnken results, centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10 min, concentrated with a rotary evaporator, and lyophilized for animal experiments.

The main components in the CYP

CYP (50 mg) were accurately weighed into the centrifuge tube, added 1 mL extraction solution (water-acetonitrile-isopropyl alcohol mixed solution, 1 : 1 : 1), extracted 30 min by low-temperature ultrasonic, centrifuged 10 min with 12000 rpm at 4 °C, set still for 1 hour to precipitate protein at -20 °C, then centrifuged 10 min with 12000 rpm at 4°C, the supernatant solution were dried in vacuum, added 0.2 mL 50 % acetonitrile solution, homogenized and centrifuged 15 min with 14000 rpm at 4 °C, the supernatant were taken for detection. UPLC conditions: The extracted sample were analyzed using an UPLC—Orbitrap-MS system (UPLC, Vanquish; MS, HFX) with HSS T3 column (100 × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μL, Waters, USA). Solvent system: phase A were Milli-Q water (0.1 % formic acid), phase B were acetonitrile (0.1 % formic acid). The column temperature was 40 °C, flow rate was 0.3 mL/min and injection volume were 2 μL. HRMS data were recorded on a Q Exactive HFX Hybrid Quadrupole Orbitrap mass spectrometer equipped with a heated ESI source (Thermo Fisher Scientific) utilizing the Full-msddMS2 acquisition methods. The ESI source parameters were set as follows: sheath gas pressure, 40 Arb; aux gas pressure, 10 Arb; temperature, 350 °C; and ion transport tube temperature, 320 °C.

Animal experiments

Two hundred 7-day-old White-Feathered Broiler (obtained from Guangdong Academy of Agricultural Sciences) were divided into 5 groups of 40 each: Control group, Cyclophosphamide group (CTX), 0.5 g/kg CYP group (CYP-L), 1 g/kg CYP group (CYP-M), 2 g/kg CYP group (CYP-H), and kept in clean environment of the animal experiment center of South China Agricultural University (approval number: SYXK 2022-0136) with suitable conditions. All procedures of animal experimental in this study have been approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the South China Agricultural University. The composition and nutritional levels of the basal diet were listed in Table S3.

Chicken immunosuppressive model induced by intramuscular injection of 80 mg/kg CTX for 3 consecutive days, and then their basal diets were supplemented with 0.5 g/kg, 1 g/kg, 2 g/kg CYP, respectively. Meanwhile, all the chickens were immunized with combined Newcastle Disease and Infectious Bronchitis Vaccine by eye spotting on the first day of the experiment. At days 7, 14, 21 and 28 of the experiment, body weight was recorded, blood was collected, and then the spleen, thymus and bursa of Fabricius were harvested and weighed, and the jejunum was placed in RNA tissue stabilization solution and 4 % paraformaldehyde for subsequent analysis.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

The detection of IgG (ml042771) and IgM (ml890233) in serum was performed using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay kit according to the manufacturer's instructions (Shagnhai Enzyme-linked Biotechnology Co. Ltd).

Quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR)

Tissues were extracted using Trizol reagent (Nanjing Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd., R401-01). The cDNA was synthesized by HiScript III RT SuperMix for qPCR (+gDNA wiper) (R323-01) and quantitated with the ChamQ universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Q312-2) according to the manufacturer's instructions (Nanjing Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd.). The samples were per-formed using qTOWER3 (Analytik Jena GmbhH) and the data was analyzed with the 2−△△CT method. The primer sequences are given in Table S4.

Histopathology

The jejunum tissues were set with formaldehyde, dehydrated, embedded, sliced, and then stained with H&E staining.

16S rRNA sequencing and gut microbiota analysis

Intestinal fecal DNA was extracted using an E.Z.N.A.® soil DNA Kit (Omega Bio-tek, R6825-01) according to the manufacturer’s protocols. The V3-V4 regions of the 16S rRNA gene were amplified with the primers 338F (5′ - ACTCCTACGGGAGGCAGCAG - 3′) and 806R (5′ - GGACTACHVGGGTWTCTAAT - 3′) by a GeneAmp 9700 PCR system (ABI). The PCR product was extracted from 2 % agarose gel and purified using an AxyPrep DNA Gel Extraction Kit (Axygen Biosciences), and quantified using Qubit 4 (Thermo Fisher). Purified amplicons were pooled in equimolar and paired-end sequenced on the Illumina MiSeq PE300 platform (Illumina). Sequencing reads were demultiplexed, quality controlled by fastp (version 0.21.0), and merged by FLASH (version 1.2.7). OTUs and feature tables were obtained by clustering the sequences by OTU based on 97 % similarity using Vsearch software (version 2.22.1). Taxonomic annotation of OTU sequences was performed using RDP classifier (version 2.13) with a confidence threshold of 0.7. The reference database was Silva 138.1 for bacteria and UNITE 9.0 for fungi. Statistical analysis and data visualization were performed in R. Beta diversity was calculated using Bray-curtis distance, and PCoA principal coordinate analysis and PERMANOVA permutation multivariate ANOVA were performed based on the above distance matrix. The nonparametric rank sum test was used to detect differences in microbial communities between groups. Correlations between specific species were analyzed by Spearman rank correlation. p values were corrected for multiple testing using the BH method, and the significance level was set at 0.05 by default.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 26.0 software was used for one-way analysis of variance (one-way ANOVA), and Duncan's test was used for multiple comparisons among the different groups. The results of data analysis were expressed as mean ± standard error, and P < 0.05 was considered as the significant level of difference. GraphPad Prism 8.0.2 software was used for plotting.

Results

Optimization of extraction conditions for polysaccharide content in CYP extracts

To explore the optimal conditions for extracting yam polysaccharides by water extraction, we first conducted a single-factor experiment. According to the extraction rate and polysaccharide content of CYP extracts, the results showed that the optimal solid-liquid ratio was 1:20 (Fig. 1A), the optimal extraction temperature was 70 °C (Fig. 1B), and the optimal extraction time was 3 hours and 4 hours (Fig. 1C), respectively.

CYP extraction conditions were optimized based on univariate and response surface analysis. The single factor method with different (A) solid-liquid ratio, (B) temperature and (C) time as variables was used to optimize the CYP extraction process based on extraction yield and polysaccharide content. Effects of (D) time and solid-liquid, (E) time and temperature, (F) and temperature and solid-liquid ratio on polysaccharide content in aqueous extract of CYP.

Based on the results of the single-factor experiment, a response surface analysis (RSM) with solid-liquid ratio (1:15, 1:20, 1:25), temperature (60 °C, 70 °C, 80 °C), time (3 h, 4 h, 5 h) as independent variables and polysaccharide content of CYP extracts as response value was performed (Table S1), and the results were shown in Table S2. Then the quadratic multinomial equation obtained after regression analysis of each factor by Design-Expert 8.0 was: Y = 98.32 + 7.04A + 2.09 + 2.35C + 0.41AB - 0.14AC - 0.73BC - 4.65A2 - 2.06B2 - 2.51C2. The results of variance analysis showed that the P value of the model was 0.0002, and the P value of lack of fit was 0.2236, indicating that the model could be used to predict the polysaccharide content of CYP extracts (Table S5). Meanwhile, the solid-liquid ratio, time and temperature had a significant effect on the polysaccharide content (solid-liquid ratio > time > temperature), while their interaction had little effect on the response value. The results of response surface diagram also showed that the influence degree of each factor was as follows: solid-liquid ratio > time > temperature, and the interaction effect on polysaccharide content was not significant (Fig. 1D, E, F). Taken together, the extraction process of CYP extracts was determined as the solid-liquid ratio was 1:24, the extraction temperature was 75 °C, and the extraction time was 4.4 h.

Analysis of major components in CYP extracts

According to the final extraction process, the polysaccharide content in the CYP extracts was 100.53 mg/g, and the yield of the extract was 19.46 % (Fig. 2E). To further investigate the pharmacological substances in CYP extracts, we performed UPLC-MS analysis (Fig. 2A, B). A total of 792 compounds were identified in CYP, of which 633 compounds were classified into 10 super classes, including alkaloids and derivatives, benzenoids, lignans, neolignans and related compounds, etc. (Fig. 2C). Among the 633 compounds, the top 3 compounds in the class classification were carbohydrates and carbohydrate conjugates with 48, amino acids, peptides, and analogues with 39, flavonoids with 38, followed by terpene lactones, cinnamic acids and derivatives, terpene glyeosides, and others (Fig. 2D). The results of representative compounds showed that fructose, raffinose, palmitic acid, l-glutamic acid, l-arginine, which have both nutritional and medicinal values, and many compounds, such as quercetin, apigenin, prunetin, loganetin, atractylenolide II, etc., which have been proved to have various biological effects such as regulating immunity and reducing inflammation, were contained in CYP (Table S6).

Supplementing CYP on body weight and organ index in CTX-induced chickens

To explore whether CYP still regulates immunity, which is the non-medicinal part of yam, CTX was used to induce immune suppression in chicken. Under the stimulation of CTX, the body weight of the chickens was consistently decreased significantly at 7, 14, 21 and 28 days, while different doses of CYP significantly restored the body weight of chickens (Table 1). Meanwhile, the best effect was observed in the low dose of CYP on days 7 and 14, and in the medium dose on days 21 and 28. CTX also resulted in a significant decrease in immune organ indices, including spleen, thymus, bursa of Fabricius (Table 2). Similarly, CYP significantly reversed the CTX-induced decrease in immune organ index, with the greatest effect in CYP-L group. However, there was no significant difference in spleen index between the Control group and the CTX group on days 21 and 28.

Supplementing CYP on immune level in CTX-induced chickens

In order to study the effects of CTX and CYP on immunity, immunoglobulin and valence of antibody against Newcastle disease were measured by ELISA and hemagglutination inhibition test, respectively. The results showed that CTX significantly inhibited the levels of IgG and IgM in serum of chicken on days 7, 14, 21, and 28, except for IgG at day 14 and IgM on day 28 (Fig. 3A, B). And different doses of CYP markedly up-regulated the levels of IgG and IgM in serum, and the CYP-L had the best effect on promoting the production of immunoglobulin. The results of valence of antibody against Newcastle disease demonstrated that CTX significantly reduced the immune effect of Newcastle disease vaccine in the early stage, while CYP significantly promoted the immune effect of Newcastle disease vaccine, and there was no significant difference in antibody level among the groups in the later stage (Fig. 3C). Collectively, CYP significantly enhanced the immune level of chickens.

Effects of CYP on jejunal barrier in CTX-induced chickens

The intestinal barrier is the first line of defense against pathogen invasion, and the repair effect of CYP on the intestinal barrier was also observed. The mRNA expression of intestinal barrier genes ZO-1, Claudin-1 and Occludin was significantly inhibited by CTX on days 7 (Fig. 4A), 14 (Fig. 4B) and 21 (Fig. 4C) in jejunal of chicken. The expression of intestinal barrier genes was reduced by CTX on day 28, but there was no significant difference (Fig. 4D). CYP supplementation significantly restored the relative expression levels of ZO-1, Claudin-1, Occludin genes on days 7 (Fig. 4A), 14 (Fig. 4B), 21 (Fig. 4C) and 28 (Fig. 4D), and there was no significant difference between the three CYP groups and CTX groups in Claudin-1 and Occludin mRNA expression on day 28 (Fig. 4). H&E analysis showed that CTX significantly reduced the villus height (Fig. 5A, D) and the ratio of villus height to crypt depth (Fig. 5C, D), but had no significant effect on crypt depth in jejunal of chicken (Fig. 5B, D). Although villus height and crypt depth were not significantly restored by CYP, the ratio of villus height to crypt depth was significantly upregulated (Fig. 5C, D).

Supplementing CYP on composition of gut microbiota in CTX-induced chickens

To further investigate the relationship between the efficacy of CYP alleviating CTX-induced immunosuppression and gut microbiota in chickens, the composition of gut microbiota in jejunal contents was analyzed. At the phylum level, a total of five phyla were identified, with Firmicutes, Bacteroidota and Proteobacteria as the top three dominant phyla (Fig. 6A). More importantly, CYP inhibited CTX induced changes in phylum taxa of jejunal. At the genus level, Lactobacillus and Bacteroides were the dominant genera, followed by Faecalibacterium and Escherichia-Shigella (Fig. 6B). The heatmap of genus analysis demonstrated that the composition of the taxa in the Control and CYP groups was more similar, which was consistent with the results at the phylum level (Fig. 6C). The results of Alpha diversity analysis, which reflected the abundance and diversity of microbial community, showed that CTX reduced the Chao1, Shannon and Observed species without statistical significance, but significantly reduced Pielou, while CYP significantly increased the four Alpha diversity indices (Fig 6D, E, F, G). These results indicated that CYP significantly enriched the abundance and diversity of microbial communities.

Differential gut microbiota altered by CYP in CTX-induced chickens

Beta diversity, commonly used to compare differences between different ecosystems, was also analyzed in this study. PCA scatter plot showed that the CYP group was highly similar to the control group, but significantly different from the CTX group on the microbial community of jejunal contents (Fig. 7A). PCoA analysis based on weighted unifrac distance demonstrated that the samples in the CYP group were the most clustered, and the clustering between the CYP group and the Control group was more obvious than that in the CTX group (Fig. 7B). The Venn diagram results showed that 300 OTUs were shared between CYP group and control group, 238 OTUs were shared between CYP and CTX group, and 232 OTUs were shared between these three groups (Fig. 7C). Meanwhile, 13 OTUs were unique to CYP, 5 OTUs were unique to CON, and 5 OTUs were unique to CTX. The results of LEfSe showed that at the genus level, Bifidobacterium was the differential biomarker in CTX group, Alistipes, DTUO89, Eubacterium-nodatum-group, Lachnospira and some norank genera were the differential biomarker in Control group, and Faecalibacterium, Shuttleworthia, Subdoligranulum were the differential biomarker in CYP group (Fig. 7D). Compared with CTX group, the Control group enriched pathway was sulfur metabolism, arginine and proline metabolism, plant-pathogen interaction (Fig. 7E), while CTX enriched pathway was fructose and mannose metabolism, and CYP group enriched pathway was histidine metabolism, cysteine and methionine metabolism, alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism, sulfur relay system, Cyanoamino acid metabolism, and Plant-pathogen interaction when compared between CTX and CYP groups through LEfSe difference analysis of metabolic pathways (Fig. 7F).

CYP altered key bacterial genera in CTX-induced chickens

At the genus level, the non-parametric factor Kruskal-Wallis was used to analyze the key genera among the three groups. The five clearly identified differential bacterial genera with the highest relative abundance were Faecalibacterium, Alistipes, Shuttleworthia, Subdoligranulum, and Incertae-Sedis, which were decreased markedly in CTX group and increased significantly after CYP supplementation (Fig. 8A). Caproiciproducens, Intestinimonas and Bifidobacterium were the genera with the low relative abundance but the most significant differences among different groups (Fig. 8A). Meanwhile, almost all of these differential genera were decreased by CTX and then increased by CYP, indicating that CYP significantly increased the abundance of gut microbiota. Based on the Spearman, we performed correlation analyses between differential key genera and CYP-enhanced immunity and intestinal barriers. The results demonstrated that key gut bacterial genera were obviously correlated to CYP-enhanced immunity and intestinal barriers (Fig. 8B). Among them, Faecalibacterium, Alistipes, Shuttleworthia, Subdoligranulum, Monoglobus, Lachnospira, and so on, were positively associated with CYP-enhanced immunity and intestinal barriers, while Pseudomonas and Bifidobacterium were negatively associated with CYP-enhanced immunity and intestinal barriers (Fig. 8B). More importantly, Subdoligranulum showed the most significant positive correlation and Bifidobacterium showed the most significant negative correlation with CYP-enhanced immunity and intestinal barrier.

Discussion

Yams are abundant in many places, but Chinese yam has been used as a functional food and traditional Chinese medicine in China, Japan and Korea for centuries (Wang et al., 2021a). A large number of literatures have shown that Chinese yam, especially Tiegun-yam, has the functions of enhancing immunity, anti-inflammation, anti-oxidation, and regulating intestinal flora (Hsu et al., 2006a; Wang et al., 2021a). In traditional Chinese medicine, its functions are tonifying spleen and stomach, nourishing lung, strengthening kidney and enhancing immunity (Li et al., 2023). CYP, the non-medicinal and edible part of yam, still retains many biological functions. Some studies have shown that peeling has little effect on the function of yam, but yam peel has the potential as an immune-stimulating feed additive to improve the immunity of carp by regulating intestinal flora and enhancing intestinal defense barrier (Feng et al., 2024; Meng et al., 2019). Therefore, it may also be used to enhance immune function in livestock and poultry breeding, but the precise effect needs to be further confirmed.

For the extraction solvent of CYP, we selected the most economical water extraction method, but in order to enhance the effect to a greater extent, we first optimized the extraction conditions of CYP water extract. Polysaccharides have been reported in literature as key active ingredients in many traditional Chinese medicines, responsible for their medicinal functions (Lv et al., 2022; Ren et al., 2023). In this study, the extraction process of yam peel was optimized based on the extraction rate of yam peel extract and the content of polysaccharide in it. Single factor combined with response surface analysis was the best method for condition optimization (Li et al., 2021), which was used to optimize the extraction process of yam peel in this study. The results showed that the solid-liquid ratio was 1:24, the temperature was 75 °C, and the extraction time was 4.4 h. Based on these conditions, the extraction rate of CYP extracts was 19.46 %, and the polysaccharide content was 100.53 mg/g. Inconsistent with this study, in order to obtain a higher purity of polysaccharide extract, most studies use water extraction and alcohol precipitation method (Yao et al., 2019), which will discard a lot of other medicinal components. CYP is the discarded material of Chinese yam, which should be fully utilized for its medicinal components to produce stronger functions for livestock and poultry.

We then conducted a comprehensive analysis of the ingredients in CYP extract based on untargeted metabolomics analysis by UPLC-MS. Our results revealed that CYP contains not only organooxygen, but also many other medicinal compounds, such as alkaloids, lignans, organic acids, etc. The most abundant in CYP is carbohydrates, which contain polysaccharides, indicating that polysaccharides in CYP are main medicinal compounds. Amino acids and flavonoids are also the main components in CYP. Many studies have reported that amino acids have nutritional value and important biological functions. l-arginine is dependent on T cells and synergize with anti-PD-L1 treatment to clear tumors (Canale et al., 2021). Glutamate promotes the proliferation and differentiation of intestinal stem cells through mTORC1 signaling and EGFR/ERK signaling to repair the intestinal barrier (Zhu et al., 2019, 2020). Flavonoids are recognized as the main active components of (TCM) (Qu et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2024b). Jiang et al. showed that apigenin inhibited inducible PD-L1 expression to enhance antitumor immunity in KRAS-mutant lung cancer (Jiang et al., 2021), and other studies have shown that apigenin has the potential to treat a variety of chronic diseases (Salehi et al., 2019). Quercetin has been reported to have a variety of beneficial health effects, including anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, anti-cancer, anti-cardiovascular activities, and so on (Kandemir et al., 2022). In addition, there are many other important medicinal substances, such as atractylenolide II (Wang et al., 2017), p-coumaric acid (Jaganathan et al., 2013), dihydrotanshinone I (Wen-wen et al., 2013) and curdione (Liu et al., 2020a). Therefore, CYP, even if it is a plant waste product, may have important medicinal value because of its abundance of medicinal compounds.

Bacterial invasion, viral infection, stress stimulation, and improper feeding and management all lead to immune dysfunction and induce immunosuppression (Martin et al., 2021; Tian et al., 2024). In this study, our results showed that CYP significantly reversed CTX-induced immunosuppression through the recovery of body weight and immune organ index. Although there is no study on CYP restoring body weight in immunosuppressed chickens, Lim et al. showed that CYP extract restored body weight in DSS-induced mice, which is consistent with our results (Lim et al., 2018). The recovery of immune organ index, the key site of immune cell generation and maturation (Zhou et al., 2024a), indicated that CYP enhanced the immune function of chickens. Immunoglobulin is also an important component of the immune system (Dixon et al., 2024) and was significantly upregulated by CYP in the current study. More importantly, we immunized CTX-induced chickens with live vaccine of Newcastle disease and infectious bronchitis, and demonstrated that CYP significantly enhanced the immune effect of the vaccine. Previous studies showed that peeled-Chinese yam had little effect on HFD-induced mice but had significant changes in TG, LDL-C, HDA-C and gut microbiota composition (Feng et al., 2024). It has been reported that ethanol-induced gastric ulcers in mice were significantly alleviated following the administration of CYP (Byeon et al., 2018). Vitro experiments demonstrated that CYP has significant antioxidant activity due to its bioactive substances (Yang et al., 2025). Furthermore, experiments conducted on fish revealed that CYP enhanced the intestinal barrier and the intestinal microbiota (Meng et al., 2019). These findings are consistent with the results of our study. Chinese Yam has been shown in many studies to enhance the activation of immune cells and induce the production of immune cell-related factors (Liu et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2022). These studies and data support that CYP, the non-medicinal part of medicinal plants, enhances the immunity of chicken.

Intestinal barrier is the first line of defense against pathogen invasion, and intestinal health, including intestinal flora, is the most important part of the overall health of the body (Pellegrini et al., 2023). In present study, our data demonstrated that CYP repaired the intestinal barrier by up-regulating the expression of intestinal barrier related genes and the ratio of villus height and crypt depth. This result is consistent with the results of another study, which showed that CYP improved gut barrier and promoted gut microvillus height in carp (Meng et al., 2019). Then the gut microbiota was also analyzed in our study, which was significantly modulated by CYP in other studies in carp (Meng et al., 2019). CYP significantly ameliorated CTX-induced upheaval in jejunal microbiota composition, increased the diversity and richness of microbiota, and led to the generation of more unique and different microbiota. These changes enriched CYP in different KEGG pathways, which were mainly enriched in amino acid metabolism pathways, including histidine metabolism, cysteine and methionine metabolism, alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism, etc. These suggested that CYP may enhance immune function and intestinal barrier by regulating amino acid metabolism, but further exploration and verification are needed.

In our study, CYP-regulated immunity and intestinal barrier were stronger positively correlated with the differential genera Faecalibacterium, Shuttleworthia, and Subdoligranulum, and obviously negatively correlated with the CTX-induced significant genera Bifidobacterium. Faecalibacterium is the main producer of butyric acid, which is a resource in the intestine to promote immunity and maintain intestinal barrier integrity (Faden, 2022; Liu et al., 2020b). Shuttleworthia was reported to may be involved in the activation of AMPK signaling pathway in fattening pigs(Wang et al., 2024c), but another study reported that Shuttleworthia was harmful in the regulation of gut microbiota by butyrate in broilers (Wan et al., 2022). Subdoligranulum, a producer of short-chain fatty acids, has a significant inverse association with inflammation and is a potential probiotic for IBD treatment (Cai et al., 2024; Van Immerseel et al., 2010; Xia et al., 2021). Bifidobacterium, recognized as the earliest gut beneficial bacteria (Derrien et al., 2022), was significantly reduced by CYP, suggesting that CYP immunomodulatory activity may be associated with significant improvement of other beneficial bacteria. In addition, CYP-regulated immunity and intestinal barrier were also markedly positively correlated with Monoglobus, Alistipes and Lachnospira, and negatively correlated with Pseudomonas. Monoglobus helps the active ingredient polysaccharide improve immunity (Wu et al., 2023), Alistipes is a growth enhancer for broilers and pigs because of its ability to degrade fibers, plant derived polysaccharides and produce short-chain fatty acids (Facimoto et al., 2025), Lachnospira is reduced in many diseases, contributing to recovery from inflammatory diseases (Manzano et al., 2022), and Pseudomonas is a common pathogen, these reports highlight the therapeutic potential of CYP for regulating immunity and repairing the intestinal barrier.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our results indicated that the optimal extraction process for water extraction of CYP was a solid-liquid ratio of 1:24, temperature 75°C, time 4.4 h, and the resulting CYP extracts with a polysaccharide content of 100.53 mg/g. CYP protected against CTX-induced immunosuppression by promoting immune function and repairing intestinal barrier in broilers. The protective effect of CYP on immunosuppressed broilers was significantly related to the regulation of gut microbiota, especially the enrichment of key differential bacterial genera and amino acid metabolism pathways. More importantly, the improvement effect of CYP on CTX-induced broilers was significantly positively correlated with the abundance of Faecalibacterium, Subdoligranulum, and Lachnospira, and negatively correlated with Pseudomonas, Bifidobacterium. Our findings provide evidence for the clinical application of food and medicinal Chinese medicinal wastes in livestock and poultry.

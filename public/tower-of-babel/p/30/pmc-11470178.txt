Induction of LARP1B under endoplasmic reticulum stress and its regulatory role in proliferation of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Xia Yang, Juntao Lu, Fangyu Su, Junhong Wu, Xinhao Wang, Zhaokun Hu, Zhaoyang Yan, Huanchen Xu, Xiaobin Shang, Wei Guo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Highlights•LARP1B is up-regulated in ESCC cells upon endoplasmic reticulum stress.•XBP1 stimulates the transcription of LARP1B in response to ER stress.•LARP1B is highly expressed in ESCC tissues and cells.•LARP1B promotes the proliferation of ESCC cells by maintaining CCND1 mRNA levels.Keywords: LARP1B, Endoplasmic reticulum stress, Proliferation

Introduction

Esophageal cancer ranks among the prevalent malignant tumors of the digestive system, posing a significant risk to human health due to its severe morbidity and mortality. It is ranked seventh in morbidity and sixth in mortality in the world [1]. Squamous cell carcinoma accounts for 90 % of the pathological type of esophageal cancer, while adenocarcinoma makes up 10 % [2]. Due to the inconspicuous symptoms in the initial stage of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), most patients are clinically diagnosed in the intermediate and terminal phases, resulting in an unfavorable prognosis [3]. In recent years, with the development of surgical techniques and the refinement of treatment approaches, the treatment effect of ESCC has been significantly enhanced. However, the pathogenesis of ESCC is still ambiguous. It is essential to elucidate the molecular underpinnings and identify potential biomarkers and molecular therapeutic targets, to enhance the prognosis of ESCC patients.

Endoplasmic reticulum stress (ER stress) is a pathological condition caused by the accumulation of unfolded or misfolded proteins in the ER lumen [4]. In response to ER stress, the unfolded protein response (UPR) is activated, which is coordinated by three major sensors across the ER membrane: PERK, ATF6, and ERN1, and these three sensors then separate from the ER chaperone GRP78 (Bip). PERK phosphorylates the translation initiation factor eIF2α at Ser51 to increase the translation of ATF4. ERN1 activates its RNase activity via autophosphorylation, resulting in the splicing of XBP1 into active XBP1s. ATF6 translocates to the Golgi apparatus, where it is cleaved by the proteases S1P and S2P, and releases its transcriptionally active amino acid termini [5]. These signaling pathways contributed to alleviating ER stress and maintaining cell viability by preventing protein synthesis and promoting the degradation of unfolded proteins [6]. Nevertheless, prolonged and intense ER stress activates apoptosis-related pathways [7,8]. Studies have demonstrated that ER stress can stimulate the occurrence and progression of tumors by regulating cell proliferation, apoptosis, and autophagy [9]. In ESCC, ER stress-related molecules XBP1 and ATF4 have been proved to participate in tumor progression by promoting angiogenesis, cell proliferation, and invasion [[10], [11], [12]]. However, the correlation between ER stress and cancer is a multifaceted field, and the role of ER stress in ESCC progression remains unclear and further investigations are necessitated.

To explore the genes involved in ER stress in ESCC, we constructed an in vitro cell model and observed a significant up-regulation of LARP1B. LARP1B, also known as LARP2 (located at 4q28), belongs to the La-associated proteins (LaRPs), an ancient superfamily of RNA-binding proteins, which are responsible for the regulation of RNA processing, maturation, and translation. Humans carry five LARP subfamilies: True La (LARP3), LARP1 (variants LARP1A and LARP1B), LARP4 (variants LARP4a and 4b), LARP6, and LARP7. Among them, LARP1B and LARP1A exhibit a specific level of structural resemblance [13]. LARP1A acted as a regulator of mRNA stability and translation, and played a cancer-promoting role in various cancers such as in cervical cancer, lung cancer, and ovarian cancer [14,15]. However, little attention has been paid to the role of LARP1B in occurrence and progression of cancers. In the present study, the expression level of LARP1B in ESCC tissues and cell lines was detected, and the main UPR signals which contributed to the transcription of LARP1B was investigated, and the oncogenic role and corresponding mechanisms were further identified.

Results

Endoplasmic reticulum stress induces LARP1B expression in esophageal cancer cells

To explore the biological role and molecular mechanism of ER stress in the progression of ESCC, we first treated the ESCC cell line TE-1 with two ER stress inducers, thapsigargin (TG) and tunicamycin (TM) [16]. The mRNA and protein expression levels of UPR markers in TG and TM treated cells were examined to ascertain the ER stress status of ESCC cells, and UPR markers peaked at 100 nM TG or 1 μg/ml TM treatment for 12 h (Fig. 1A-D). Then, the gene expression profiles of TE-1 cells treated with TG (100 nM) for 12 h were detected by RNA sequencing. Differentially expressed genes were screened according to the criteria of log2|foldchange| > 1 (Fig. 1E), among which LARP1B was identified as a significant up-regulated gene in response to ER stress. Analyses of mRNA and protein expression levels of LARP1B after induction of ESCC cells with TG and TM confirmed that LARP1B was significantly increased under ER stress condition (Fig. 1F-I).

ER stress induces LARP1B transcription via ERN1-XBP1 pathway

To ascertain the signaling pathway of the UPR accountable for the induction of LARP1B, ESCC cell lines TE-1 and KYSE150 were transfected with siRNAs targeting three ER transmembrane sensors: PERK, ATF6, and ERN1. As shown in Fig. 2A and B, suppression of PERK and ATF6 had no impact on the mRNA and protein expression levels of LARP1B, while suppression of ERN1 obviously diminished the expression of LARP1B (Fig. 2C). For further verification, TE-1 and KYSE150 cells were transfected with overexpression plasmids of ATF4, ATF6, and XBP1. Consistently, up-regulation of ATF4 and ATF6 did not increase the expression of LARP1B (Fig. 2D, E), while a notable elevation of LARP1B was observed in XBP1-overexpressed cells (Fig. 2F). Similar results were obtained in KYSE170 cells (Fig. S1). Consistent with the aforementioned findings, knockdown of ERN1 impaired mRNA and protein expression levels of LARP1B induced by TG in TE-1 cells (Fig. 2G). As a transcription factor, XBP1 plays an important role in transcriptional regulation of the genes in ER stress [17]. A binding element for XBP1 was found at positions −82 bp to −68 bp in the LARP1B promoter sequence by the online dataset JASPAR (Fig. 2H). Luciferase reporter assays further verified the transcriptional regulation of XBP1 on LARP1B in this region. The elevated luciferase activity was significantly decreased when the site (−82 bp∼−68 bp) was mutated. (Fig. 2I). These data indicate that transcription of LARP1B is induced by the ERN1 pathway through XBP1-dependent manner in response to ER stress.

LARP1B is up-regulated in ESCC tissues and cells

According to the Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis (GEPIA) database, high expression levels of LARP1B were observed in various cancers including esophageal cancer (Fig. 3A). Compared with normal esophageal epithelial cells, the mRNA expression levels of LARP1B in three ESCC cell lines also exhibited a remarkable increase (Fig. 3B). The relative mRNA expression levels of LARP1B in 76 primary ESCC tissues and adjacent normal tissues were further examined and a notable elevation of LARP1B in ESCC tissues was verified (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3C). The protein expression levels of LARP1B were additionally assessed through IHC staining assays. The positive protein expression frequency of LARP1B in ESCC tissues (59.2 %, 45/76) was significantly higher than that in corresponding normal tissues (38.2 %, 29/76) (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3D). Moreover, the expression of LARP1B at the mRNA and protein levels was visibly correlated with pathological differentiation and lymph node metastasis in ESCC tumor tissues (Fig. 3E and Supplementary Table 5).

LARP1B promotes proliferation of esophageal cancer cells, but does not affect migration and invasion abilities

To investigate whether LARP1B was involved in ESCC progression, cell function experiments were performed. TE-1 and KYSE150 cells were transfected with siRNA targeting LARP1B, and a significant decrease of LARP1B expression was detected in the transfected cells (Fig. 4A). Knockdown of LARP1B had an attenuated effect on both cell viability and colony formation rate detected by MTS and colony formation assays (Fig. 4B, C). However, knockdown of LARP1B didn't alter migration and invasion abilities of ESCC cells (Fig. 4D, E). The flow cytometry analysis further indicated that ESCC cells with depletion of LARP1B were arrested in the G1 phase (Fig. 4F). Similar results were obtained in siLARP1B-transfected KYSE170 cells (Fig. S2). These evidences indicate that LARP1B may play a crucial role in promoting proliferation of ESCC cells.

LARP1B affects the expression of CCND1

The mechanism through which LARP1B exerted its antitumor effect in ESCC was further investigated. Given the role of LARP1B in promoting proliferation of ESCC cells, we hypothesized whether LARP1B could mediate the expression of cell cycle regulators and focused on several cell cycle regulatory molecules including CCND1, CCNE1, ki67, MCM2, and PCNA [[18], [19], [20]]. Among these molecules, significant decreased mRNA and protein expression levels of CCND1 were detected in LARP1B-knockdown TE-1 and KYSE150 cells (Fig. 5A, B), while significant up-regulation of CCND1 was detected in LARP1B overexpressing TE-1 and KYSE150 cells (Fig. 5C). Similar results were obtained in LARP1B-knockdown or overexpressed KYSE170 cells (Fig. S3), suggesting the regulatory role of LARP1B on CCND1. To further confirm the regulatory effect of CCND1 by LARP1B under ER stress state, TE-1 cells with or without knockdown of LARP1B were treated with TG. As shown in Fig. 5D, knockdown of LARP1B partially abolished the TG induced up-regulation of CCND1 expression.

LARP1B binds to the mRNA of CCND1 and maintains its stability

The combination of LARP1B and mRNA of CCND1 was verified by RNA pull-down assay in TE-1 cells (Fig. 5E). In light of LARP1B's role as an RNA-binding protein, we further analyzed whether it can affect the stability of CCND1 mRNA. TE-1 and KYSE150 cells with or without knockdown of LARP1B were treated with actinomycin D. The mRNA stability of CCND1 was significantly reduced upon the knockdown of LARP1B (Fig. 5F). Taken together, LARP1B binds the mRNA of CCND1 and sustains its stability.

Inhibition of CCND1 partially reverses the promoting effect of LARP1B on the proliferation of ESCC cells

To further investigate the oncogenic effects of CCND1 induced by LARP1B in ESCC cells, LARP1B overexpression plasmid or blank plasmid was co-transfected with siCCND1 or siControl into TE-1 and KYSE150 cells. As shown in Fig. 6A, transfection of siCCND1 significantly decreased the expression levels of CCND1 in TE-1 and KYSE150 cells. Knockdown of CCND1 partially reversed the enhanced cell proliferation ability caused by overexpression of LARP1B in TE-1 and KYSE150 cells, as detected by MTS and colony formation assays (Fig. 6B, C). The cell cycle assays also confirmed that the inhibition of CCND1 attenuated the transition of G1-S phase induced by overexpression of LARP1B (Fig. 6D). Consequently, up-regulation of LARP1B regulated by ERN1-XBP1 pathway promotes ESCC cells proliferation by maintaining mRNA stability of CCND1 under ER stress condition (Fig. 6E).

Discussion

In recent studies, the role of ER stress in the occurrence and development of tumors has received extensive attention. As an adaptive process of cellular microenvironment dysregulation, ER stress participates in tumor genesis, development and drug resistance by inducing autophagy, invasion, and apoptosis of tumor cells [21,22]. ER stress can promote tumor cells invasion through various mechanisms including angiogenesis regulation, epithelial mesenchymal transformation (EMT) mediation, and degradation of extracellular matrix (ECM) [23]. The studies on the role of ER stress in ESCC development are relatively limited. UPR has been proved to activate downstream target genes to promote ESCC cells survival and tumor growth [24,25]. GRP78/PERK signaling pathway could encourage the malignant phenotype of ESCC cells and tumor angiogenesis [12,26]. ATF4 promoted multidrug resistance in ESCC cells through up-regulation of STAT3, and XBP1-induced UPR mediated the cross-talk between optimal acquired humoral immune response and innate immunity in ESCC, leading to higher tumor grade and poorer survival rates [27]. However, a multitude of mechanisms of ER stress are yet to be discovered in ESCC. In the present study, LARP1B was identified as a novel effector of ER stress in ESCC. LARP1B was transcriptionally induced by ERN1-XBP1 pathway under ER stress condition. LARPIB was upregulated in ESCC tissues and cells and acted as an oncogene to promote proliferation of ESCC cells by regulating expression of CCND1 through maintaining its stability.

LARP1B carries various domains associated with RNA recognition and binding, including La motif (LaM), RNA recognition motif-like (RRM-L) domain, unconventional Poly (A) binding protein (PABP) interaction motif 2 (PAM2), and highly conserved DM15 region [[28], [29], [30]]. The LaM and RRM constitute the La module, which can facilitate the specific selection of RNA substrates [31,32]. PAM2 is necessary for PABP binding [33,34]. The DM15 region is involved in regulating TOP mRNA [35]. In the LARP family, there is a certain similarity between LARP1A and LARP1B. LARP1A is a regulatory molecule of mRNA stability and translation, which may be an important functional link between mammalian rapamycin target (mTOR) pathway and TOP mRNAs regulation [36]. LARP1A promotes the malignant progression of cervical cancer, ovarian cancer, and non-small cell lung cancer [14,15]. However, up to now, there have been rare studies on the biological role of LARP1B, and the mechanisms of LARP1B in tumor progression remain to be further elucidated. In the present study, the oncogenic characteristic of LARP1B was innovatively verified in ESCC, which is mainly reflected in its ability to promote cell proliferation instead of migration and invasion. LARP1A has been proved to drive proliferation, migration, and invasion of tumor cells in vivo [15], suggesting that although both belong to the LARP1 subfamily, LARP1A and LARP1B share strong sequence similarities, they still have different functions in tumor progression, which may be partially attributed to their structural differences. Furthermore, the contributing effect of ER stress on proliferation, migration, and invasion of ESCC cells may be attributed to the combined action of multiple effector genes, rather than the effects of individual gene.

As an RNA binding protein, LARP1B was mainly combined with mRNA of CCND1 and maintained its stability, thereby further regulating ESCC cells proliferation. CCND1, belongs to the highly conserved cyclin family, is a crucial mediator of cell cycle. CCND1 facilitates the transition of cells from G1 to S phase by activating cyclin-dependent kinase 4 (CDK4) or CDK6 and phosphorylating the retinoblastoma protein [37,38]. Furthermore, CCND1 is also related to cell migration and metabolism [39]. The overexpression of CCND1 has been reported in various tumors such as in gastric cancer, breast cancer, and esophageal cancer [40]. Down-regulation of CCND1 promoted cell apoptosis and suppressed cell cycle entry, proliferation, migration, and invasion in epithelial ovarian carcinoma [41]. CCND1 functioned as a positive regulator of cell proliferation in ESCC [42], down-regulating CCND1 induced G1/S arrest [43]. In the present study, to analyze the mechanism of LARP1B regulating cell proliferation and the potential binding molecules, we screened some cell cycle regulatory factors. Among them, the expression of CCND1 could be significantly affected by LARP1B, and further analyses verified the binding effect of LARP1B with CCND1 and its role in maintaining CCND1 mRNA stability, suggesting that CCND1 may be a direct target gene of LARP1B to participate in the progression of ESCC and the RNA binding effect of LARP1B may possess strong specificity.

In summary, this study reveals that ERN1-XBP1s signaling pathway induces the expression of LARP1B under ER stress state in ESCC. LARP1B is highly expressed in ESCC tissues and is favorable for proliferation of ESCC cells through maintaining mRNA stability of CCND1. Targeting LARP1B may be a novel therapeutic strategy for treatment of ESCC patients.

Materials and methods

Clinical tissue specimens

The cancer tissue samples and the paired adjacent normal tissues were collected from 76 patients with ESCC. The patients were first diagnosed and didn't undergo any radiotherapy or chemotherapy before operation in the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University from 2015 to 2019. The clinical information was obtained from the hospital records. The surgically removed tissues were divided into two parallel portions: one was fixed in formalin and embedded in paraffin, while the other was placed in liquid nitrogen and stored at − 80 °C. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients, and the collection and use of these tissue specimens were approved by the Ethics Committee of the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University.

Cell culture and treatment

Human ESCC cell lines TE-1, KYSE150, and KYSE170 were obtained from the China Type Culture Collection (CCTCC, Wuhan, China). Cell lines were authenticated using short tandem repeat analysis as described in 2012 in ANSI Standard (ASN-0002) by the ATCC Standards Development Organization (SDO). Every cell underwent testing and was determined to be devoid of any mycoplasma contamination. Cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 (Gibco, Invitrogen, Life Technologies, Germany) medium supplemented with 10 % heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). The cells were incubated at 37 °C in a humidified incubator containing 5 % CO2. For thapsigargin (TG, (Sigma, St. Louis, Missouri, USA)) and tunicamycin (TM, (Sigma, St. Louis, Missouri, USA)) treatments, actively growing cells were incubated for the indicated times with TG or TM at the indicated concentrations. For mRNA stability assays, after various periods of treatment with 5 μg/mL actinomycin D (Sigma, St. Louis, Missouri, USA), cells were collected for subsequent investigations.

RNA extraction and quantitative RT-PCR assays

According to the manufacturer, total RNA was extracted from tissues and cells using triazole reagents (Solarbio, Beijing, China). The Transcription First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Roche, Basel, Switzerland) was utilized for the reverse transcription of RNA into cDNA. The qRT-PCR was performed using GoTap® qPCRMaster Mix (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) in a StepOnePlus real-time PCR system (Applied Biosystems). The relative mRNA expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method and normalized to GAPDH as endogenous control. All primers used for qRT-PCR were listed in Supplementary Table 1.

Western blot assay

Total protein was extracted using the RIPA lysis buffer and PMSF (Solarbio) according to the manufacturer's instructions. The BCA protein detection kit (Multi-Science, Hangzhou, China) was utilized to ascertain the protein concentration. Equivalent proteins were separated by 10 % polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transferred to PVDF membranes (Millipore, Sigma, Burlington, MA, USA). The membrane was sealed with 5 % skim milk and incubated overnight with a specific primary antibody at 4 °C, and then with a secondary antibody for one hour at room temperature. Afterwards, the membrane was visualized with an enhanced chemiluminescence assay (Multi Sciences), and protein bands were detected by Chemi XT 4 (Syngene). The main antibodies were listed in Supplementary Table 2.

RNA sequencing

After TE-1 cells were treated with TG (100 nM) for 12 h, RNA was extracted to construct a sequencing library for transcriptome sequencing to examine the differentially expressed genes between the experimental group and the control group by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China).

Cell transfection

The human full-length cDNA of ATF4 was subcloned into pcDNA3.1 vector and named pcDNA3.1-ATF4. The pcDNA3.1-ATF6, pcDNA3.1-XBP1, and pcDNA3.1-LARP1B were purchased from GenScript (Nanjing, China). The small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting human ERN1 (siERNA1), PERK (siPERK), ATF6 (siATF6), LARP1B (siLARP1B), CCND1 (siCCND1), and negative siRNA for control (siControl) were purchased from Genepharma (Shanghai, China). Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen) was used to transfect plasmids and siRNAs into cells according to the manufacturer's specifications. The sequences of the siRNAs were listed in Supplementary Table 3.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

The promoter regions of LARP1B with XBP1-binding elements were amplified and inserted into the pGL3-Basic vector (Promega). Synthesis of the point mutation of XBP1-binding site on LARP1B promoter was performed using a Q5 Site-Directed Mutagenesis Kit (New England Biolabs, Beijing, China). All constructs were verified by sequencing. Luciferase reporter constructs and pRL-TK Renilla were co-transfected into ESCC cells with pcDNA3.1 or pcDNA3.1-XBP1. The Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega) was employed to estimate the luciferase activity, which was normalized to Renilla luciferase activity. The primers for the promoter fragments of LARP1B were listed in Supplementary Table 4.

Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining assay

IHC was performed on histopathological sections from paraffin-embedded tumor sections and corresponding normal tissues according to standard protocols [44]. The primary antibody against LARP1B (Proteintech, Cat # 17277–1-AP) was applied to determine the protein expression of LARP1B. The extent of staining was analyzed by at least two pathologists blinded to the clinical data. The score for immunostaining consisted of two components. The staining intensity scores were calculated as follows: 0 (no staining), 1 (pale yellow), 2 (light yellow), and 3 (dark brown). The proportion of positively stained cells was scored as follows: 0 (0–10 %), 1 (11–25 %), 2 (26–50 %), 3 (51–75 %), and 4 (76–100 %). The final staining score was the sum of these two parts, and LARP1B expression was divided into a negative group (0–3) and a positive group (4–7) according to the final staining score.

Cell proliferation and colony formation assays

Cell proliferation was assessed using MTS and colony formation assays. For MTS assays, CellTiter96®AQueousOne Cell Proliferation Assay Kit (Promega) was used. Cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 1000 cells/well. After 0, 24, 48, 72, and 96 h of cell growth, 20 μl MTS solution (500 μg/ml) was added to each culture well and incubated for 2 h. The absorbance at 490 nm was measured using a microplate analyzer. For colony formation assays, transfected cells were seeded in six-well plates at a density of 3000 cells/well and grown for 7 days. Cell colonies were fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde and stained with 0.1 % crystal violet solution. The number of colonies (a colony consisting of at least 50 cells) was counted under a microscope.

Cell migration and invasion assays

Cell migration and invasion abilities were respectively measured by wound healing and transwell assays. For wound healing assays, transfected cells were seeded in six-well plates. When cells grew to 80 % confluence, straight scratches were scraped with a pipette and starved the cells overnight with a serum-free medium. The percentage of wound area was calculated as the ratio of scratch area at 12 h and 24 h compared to that at 0 h in each group. Matrigel-uncoated chambers or Matrigel-coated chambers (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA) were used for trans-well assays. Cells were seeded at a density of 1 × 105 cells per well in the upper compartment with 200 μl of serum-free medium, while 600 μl of medium containing 10 % FBS was added to the lower compartment. Cells located on the lower surface of the membrane after 24 h of incubation were fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde, and the number of migrated or invaded cells was counted after staining with crystal violet solution.

Flow cytometry analysis of cell cycle

The cell cycle distribution was measured using a cell cycle staining kit (Multi Science, Hangzhou, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions. The cells were washed twice with PBS 48 h after transfection and adjusted to a concentration of 1 × 106 cells per mL, stained with 1 ml propidium iodide (PI) for 30 min at room temperature in the dark. Cell cycle distribution was examined by flow cytometry (BD Bioscience).

RNA pull-down assay

Sense and antisense mRNA sequences of CCND1 were obtained using T7 RiboMAX™ Express Large Scale RNA Production System (Promega) according to the manufacturer's instructions. The obtained RNA sequences were biotin-labeled with the Pierce RNA 3′-Terminal Desthiolization Biotinylation Kit (Thermo). Protein lysates were extracted from ESCC cells. Subsequently, biotin-labeled RNA was incubated with streptavidin beads and protein lysates using the Pierce™ Magnetic RNA-Protein Pull-Down Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Proteins were separated and detected by Western blotting analysis.

Statistical analysis

For all in vitro experiments, three independent experiments were performed, and the results were displayed as the average ± standard deviation (SD). The two-tailed Student t-test was employed to compare the experimental and control groups, while the chi-square test and Fisher's exact test were utilized to assess the clinical data. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

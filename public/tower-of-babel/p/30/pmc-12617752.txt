PABPC1 expression in papillary renal cell carcinoma and its significance for survival outcomes☆
Authors: Cheng Huang, Aichun Long, Tianyun Li, Yuwei Shi, Hongfang Li, Cuiwei Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study examines the expression pattern and prognostic relevance of PABPC1 in papillary renal cell carcinoma (pRCC) to evaluate its potential as a clinical outcome biomarker.MethodsIn papillary renal cell carcinoma (pRCC), mRNA expression levels of PABPC1 were analyzed by comparing tumor (n = 290)and normal tissues (n = 32)using the GSCALite and UALCAN databases. Immune checkpoint expression in KIRP was evaluated with TCGA transcriptomic data.Survival analyses via GSCALite assessed the relationship between PABPC1 expression levels (144 high,145 low) and patient outcomes.Correlations between PABPC1 and clinical/pathological stages were examined for prognostic value.Validation involved HE and IHC staining on 7 clinical samples, with quantitative assessment of PABPC1 protein expression in papillary renal cell carcinoma (pRCC) tissues.ResultsAn upsurge in PABPC1 was detected in papillary renal cell carcinoma (pRCC) during the study.There is a significant association between high PABPC1 expression in patients and reduced overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), disease-specific survival (DSS), and disease-free interval (DFI) compared to low expression,with Log-rank P-values of 4.1e-05, 3.2e-06, 2.4e-07, and 0.00027, respectively, suggesting a poorer prognosis for KIRP patients.Moreover, the mRNA expression level of PABPC1 gradually increased with the progression of clinical and pathological stages.PD1/PDL1 inhibitors could enhance the prognosis of papillary renal cell carcinoma.ConclusionsPABPC1 could serve as a potential prognostic biomarker for individuals with KIRP.Keywords: Papillary renal cell carcinoma, Immune checkpoint analysis, PABPC1, PD-1, Biomarkers

Introduction

Papillary Renal Cell Carcinoma (pRCC), accounting for approximately 10–15 % of all renal cell carcinoma (RCC) cases, represents the second most prevalent histological subtype.1, 2 Unlike other RCC subtypes, pRCC is characterized by distinct features and unique molecular drivers. The management of pRCC poses significant challenges due to its heterogeneous response to treatment and the absence of reliable prognostic biomarkers undelying its progression and metastasis remained inadequately elucidated.3, 4, 5, 6

Poly(A)-binding protein C1 (PABPC1) is a critical regulator of mRNA stability and translation in eukaryotic cells.7 Aberrant expression of PABPC1 has been documented in various cancers, including lung, gastric, breast, bladder, esophageal, and nasopharyngeal carcinomas.8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 PABPC1 is essential for cell proliferation, stress responses, and apoptosis. Abnormal expression of PABPC1 has been linked to various cancer types, suggesting that it may be an important factor in tumorigenesis.15 Nevertheless, the role of PABPC1 in pRCC and its impact on patient prognosis remain unclear.

Within the cancer immune cycle, the binding of the immune checkpoint PD1 (PDCD1) with its ligand PDL1 (CD274) assists tumors in avoiding immune-triggered cell death and encourages tumor growth.16, 17 PD1/PDL1 inhibitors have demonstrated clinical effectiveness in treating various cancers. Specific antibodies that block PD1 or PDL1 can increase T-cell responses and aid in anti-tumor activity.18 PD-L1 inhibitors have become a hotspot in cancer immunotherapy in recent years. These can rejuvenate T-cell functionality, strengthen the immune response, and aid the immune system in detecting and destroying tumor cells.19

This investigation looks into the expression of PABPC1 in papillary renal cell carcinoma and its potential impact on survival outcomes.Using bioinformatics analysis and the GSCALite database, we examine the correlation between PABPC1 and clinical and pathological characteristics, and validate these findings through IHC. Our research seeks to identify PABPC1 as a potential biomarker for prognosis assessment in pRCC, thus aiding in the development of personalized treatment strategies for patients with this challenging malignancy.

Materials and methods

Data Sources

This research utilized the Express module from the GSCALite database (https://guolab.wchscu.cn/GSCA/) to carry out a pan-cancer differential analysis(n = 323). Genes that were upregulated in KIRP were selected, and PABPC1 was chosen as the target gene for further investigation. To enhance data reliability, PABPC1 expression data from both papillary renal cell carcinoma (KIRP,n = 290) and normal tissues(n = 32) were obtained from the Ualcan platform, followed by differential expression analysis to explore the potential role of PABPC1 in KIRP and its differences compared to normal tissues. The findings are illustraed using box plots.

Transcriptomic data was downloaded from the TCGA database, which includes gene expression levels across different samples. In the data processing stage, samples were grouped into high-expression (n = 144) and low-expression (n = 145) categories, using the median expression level of PABPC1 as the dividing line. We focused our analysis on the expression changes of three immune checkpoint genes—PDCD1 (PD-1), CD274 (PD-L1), and PDCD1LG2 (PD-L2)—among the groups. The data was consolidated using the gather function, and expression differences of immune checkpoint genes between groups were visualized with ggboxplot. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test assessed group differences, with statistical significance determined by p-values. Expression differences of each immune checkpoint gene between high and low expression groups are depicted in the box plots.

Using the GSCALite module, survival analysis was carried out to investigate how PABPC1 expression relates to survival in KIRP patients. The median mRNA value was used as a threshold to divide mRNA expression and clinical survival data into high and low expression groups. Survival times and statuses in both groups were fitted using the R package “survival”. Each gene associated with cancer was examined using the Cox proportional hazards model and the Log-rank test, with Kaplan-Meier survival curves comparing survival differences between groups of high and low expression.

Tissue sample collection

The Department of Pathology at the Affiliated Hospital of Southwest Medical University provided paraffin-embedded tissue blocks of papillary renal cell carcinoma. Eighteen cases of primary papillary renal cell carcinoma, comprising both cancerous and adjacent non-cancerous tissues, were sourced from the tissue sample bank. After excluding cases that did not meet the criteria, 7 cases were selected for HE and IHC staining. Based on the IHC staining results, the cancerous and adjacent non-cancerous tissue positive rates were statistically analyzed using the IHC plugin installed in ImageJ software. GraphPad Prism 9 software was used to analyze the positive rate results. The results are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (n = 5). An unpaired t-test was used to compare normally distributed data between two groups, with statistical significance set at a p-value less than 0.05.

Immunohistochemistry (Envision Method immunohistochemistry Procedure)

To deparaffinize paraffin-embedded tissue sections, they were immersed in xylene I for 10 min and then in xylene II for 8 min. Subsequently immersed in anhydrous ethanol, then 100 % ethanol, followed by 90 %, 80 %, and 70 % ethanol, each for a duration of 5 min. Sections were then rinsed with PBS buffer three times for 5 min each. Antigen retrieval was conducted using high-pressure heating in EDTA buffer (pH 9.0) for 6 min, citrate buffer (pH 6.0) for 3 min, or by natural cooling to room temperature. To block endogenous peroxidase activity, the sections were immersed in 3 % methanol-H2O2 for 10 min, then washed with PBS three times for three minutes each. Primary antibodies were applied and incubated at 27 °C for 60 min, followed by PBS washing (3 × 3 min).Afterward, secondary antibodies were applied and incubated at 27 °C for 30 min, with PBS washing performed three times for three minutes each. DAB chromogen was applied for color development at room temperature for 5 s to 1 min, observed microscopically, and stopped with tap water. The sections were rinsed under running water for 5 min, counterstained with hematoxylin for 2 min, differentiated using 0.1 % hydrochloric acid, and blued with saturated lithium carbonate. In the end, the sections were dehydrated using 95 % ethanol I for 1 min, 95 % ethanol II for 5 min, 100 % ethanol I for 5 min, and 100 % ethanol II for 5 min, then cleared in xylene for 2 min, and mounted with neutral resin.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analyses made use of the log-rank test and Cox proportional hazards regression, while an unpaired t-test was used to compare normally distributed data between two groups. A p-value less than 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Results

Differential expression analysis of PABPC1 and immune checkpoint Molecules in papillary renal cell carcinoma (KIRP)

In this study, we employed the Express module of the GSCALite database to conduct a pan-cancer differential expression analysis, which identified PABPC1 as significantly upregulated in papillary renal cell carcinoma (KIRP). PABPC1 was subsequently selected for further investigation, and its differential expression between KIRP and normal tissues was validated using the UALCAN platform.

Fig. 1A displays the expression levels of PABPC1 in different cancer types using TCGA data, indicating elevated expression in KIRP tissues relative to normal tissues. The bubble plot in Fig. 1B presents the results of differential expression analysis of PABPC1 between KIRP and normal samples, indicating a statistically significant difference (−Log10(FDR) = 2.39). To further assess PABPC1 expression in KIRP, we conducted a differential expression analysis using the UALCAN database.The investigation showed that PABPC1 expression was considerably elevated in tumor tissues as opposed to normal tissues (P = 6.97 × 10-4, Fig. 1C), hinting at its potential involvement in the pathogenesis and progression of KIRP. Furthermore, the impact of immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) on the expression of crucial immune checkpoint genes like PDCD1, CD274, and PDCD1LG2 was analyzed. In Fig. 1D, there is a marked upregulation of all three markers in the high-expression group, with respective p-values of < 0.001, < 0.01, and < 0.001. These findings indicate that ICB treatment may enhance the expression of critical immune markers, potentially boosting immune responses and improving antitumor immunity.

Evaluating the Connection between PABPC1 expression and survival prognosis in papillary renal cell carcinoma patients

This investigation focused on the association between PABPC1 expression and survival outcomes for patients diagnosed with papillary renal cell carcinoma (KIRP), including overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), cancer-specific survival (DSS), and disease-free interval (DFI).The analysis using Kaplan-Meier survival curves demonstrated a significant difference in overall survival (OS) between groups with high and low expression of PABPC1 (Log-rank P = 4.1e-05, Fig. 2A), with the high expression group exhibiting a markedly reduced survival probability. High PABPC1 expression may indicate poor prognosis in KIRP patients. The elevated expression of PABPC1 is likely linked to the malignancy of KIRP and overall survival prognosis. Furthermore, progression-free survival varied significantly between the high and low expression groups (Log-rank P = 3.2e-06, Fig. 2B). Elevated PABPC1 expression is associated with reduced progression-free survival, indicating its potential role in tumor progression and poorer prognosis. Additionally, we assessed the correlation between PABPC1 expression and cancer-specific survival (DSS) as well as the disease-free interval (DFI). The findings revealed that elevated PABPC1 expression correlates with a significantly reduced disease-specific survival probability compared to lower expression levels (Log-rank P = 2.4e-07, Fig. 2C), indicating an association with poor cancer-specific prognosis. A clear difference in disease-free interval (DFI) was noted between the high and low expression groups (Log-rank P = 0.00027, Fig. 2D), with the high expression group exhibiting a markedly reduced survival probability. The implication is that increased PABPC1 expression could facilitate tumor recurrence and lead to a poorer prognosis for disease-free survival. PABPC1 might act as a prognostic marker for KIRP patients. Based on the survival analysis results, we further evaluated the differential expression of PABPC1 mRNA across different clinical and pathological stages. PABPC1 expression was minimal at Stage I and significantly rose through to Stage IV, showing statistically significant differences (Fig. 2E). Stage III (p = 2.3e-05); comparison with Stage I. Stage IV (p = 1.4e-05). Similarly, PABPC1 expression showed a similar trend across different pathological stages, with significantly elevated levels as the pathological stage progressed, and the differences were statistically significant (Fig. 2F). This correlation underscores PABPC1′s potential involvement in KIRP progression and its viability as a biomarker for pathological staging. In summary, this study thoroughly examined PABPC1 expression in KIRP patients and its association with survival outcomes. Increased levels of PABPC1 are linked to tumor advancement, recurrence, and negative cancer-specific results, suggesting its potential as a prognostic indicator for poor outcomes in KIRP patients. PABPC1 expression significantly rises with advancing clinical and pathological stages in KIRP patients, suggesting a strong association with the malignancy and progression of papillary renal cell carcinoma. The data further endorse the use of PABPC1 as a potential biomarker.

Discussion

Prior investigations into PABPC1 expression in tumors were primarily concerned with analyzing gene copy numbers and protein expression in various tumor tissues or cells. Elevated PABPC1 expression correlates with unfavorable patient outcomes.11, 20, 21, 22 PABPC1 influences the expression of the transcriptome through its interaction with non-coding RNAs. influencing microRNA-mediated translation, and controlling particular oncogenic proteomes, which augments the plasticity of cancer cells and assists in therapeutic evasion throughout cancer progression.22, 23, 24 This study also assessed PABPC1 expression in pRCC and its prognostic implications using bioinformatics tools such as GSCALite, Ualcan, and TCGA. The findings reveal that The expression of PABPC1 is increased in pRCC patients and is strongly correlated with poor prognosis, highlighting its potential role as a prognostic biomarker.

Moreover, we assessed the association between PABPC1 expression and the stages of clinical and pathological development. The results show that as the disease progresses, the expression of PABPC1 gradually increases, with significantly higher expression in late-stage patients compared to early-stage patients. This is consistent with the role of PABPC1 in other tumor types reported in the literature, suggesting that PABPC1 may promote tumor malignancy by enhancing cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, thereby driving tumor progression and metastasis.9, 24, 25, 26 IHC analysis verified that PABPC1 expression is notably higher in pRCC tissues than in nearby tissues, suggesting its crucial role in pRCC progression. PABPC1 overexpression is related to poor outcomes in pRCC and may contribute to immune evasion in the tumor microenvironment by associating with immune checkpoint genes like PDCD1, CD274, and PDCD1LG2.18, 27, 28, 29, 30 The findings suggest that immune checkpoint gene expression is considerably higher in the group with high PABPC1 levels, pointing to a potential relationship with immune evasion mechanisms.

Although this study provides some valuable results, there are still certain limitations. First, the sample size in this study is relatively small, and it is based solely on bioinformatics analysis and IHC validation, lacking in-depth functional experiments to reveal the specific mechanisms of PABPC1. Future investigations should examine the role of PABPC1 in papillary renal cell carcinoma using larger clinical samples and laboratory research, emphasizing its influence on tumor cell proliferation, migration, and invasion.

Conclusion

Research findings showed that PABPC1 expression is upregulated in pRCC tissues and is significantly associated with poor prognosis in papillary renal cell carcinoma patients, reinforcing its potential as a prognostic biomarker.

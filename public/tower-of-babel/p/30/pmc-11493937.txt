Factors Influencing Treatment Preference in Patients with Diabetic Macular Edema: A Study Using Conjoint Analysis
Authors: Takao Hirano, Koji Tanabe, Toshinori Murata
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionDespite the wide range of treatment options available for diabetic macular edema (DME), adherence to treatment remains a barrier. Therefore, this study used conjoint analysis to examine the factors that patients with DME prioritize when choosing a course of treatment and investigated differences in quality of life and levels of disease self-management in patients with or without experience of anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) treatment.MethodsA cross-sectional study was conducted through an online survey in Japan between May 31, 2022, and June 30, 2022. Questionnaires were sent to 27,236 individuals registered in the diabetes panels, with experience of treatment for DME within the last 10 years. Conjoint analysis was employed to calculate the relative importance, i.e., degree of influence on patients' treatment choices, considering the trade-offs among five factors: cost per treatment, frequency of visits, anticipated post-treatment visual acuity, physician’s explanation about disease and treatment, and frequency of treatment-related side effects.ResultsA total of 237 responses were used to assess the relative importance of cost per treatment, frequency of visits, anticipated post-treatment visual acuity, physician’s explanation about the disease, treatment, and frequency of treatment-related side effects using conjoint analysis. The importance of each factor was anticipated post-treatment visual acuity at 30.0, frequency of treatment-related side effects at 25.5, treatment frequency at 17.7, cost per treatment at 16.5, and physician explanation about the disease and treatment at 10.4. The average EuroQoL 5 dimension 5 level index value in patients with and without anti-VEGF treatment experience was 0.785 and 0.825, respectively, with no major difference.ConclusionsAnticipated post-treatment visual acuity was identified as the most important factor in selecting a treatment regardless of the anti-vascular endothelial growth factor treatment experience demonstrating when patients with DME make treatment choices, anticipated post-treatment visual acuity is prioritized over treatment frequency and cost.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40123-024-01026-6.Keywords: Anti-VEGF, Conjoint analysis, Diabetes mellitus, DME, EuroQoL 5 dimension 5 level, Visual acuity

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study? Anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) treatment is now considered first-line therapy for the treatment of diabetic macular edema (DME). However, undertreatment still remains a major concern in patients with DME, due to lack of adherence.The study utilized conjoint analysis to assess the factors that influence treatment preference among patients diagnosed with DME. What was learned from the study? Patients with DME prioritize improvement in their post-treatment visual acuity when making decisions regarding different treatment options.It is important for a physician to effectively communicate and discuss the visual prognosis with the patient before initiating treatment for DME to ensure a good physician–patient relationship and to improve compliance.

Introduction

Diabetes mellitus (DM) refers to a group of metabolic disorders characterized by an increased concentration of glucose in the blood, and individuals living with diabetes have a higher risk of morbidity and mortality when compared with the general population [1]. The global prevalence of DM in adults has been increasing over recent decades and is predicted to rise from 8.8% in 2015 to 10.4% in 2040 [1]. Diabetic retinopathy is the most common microvascular complication of diabetes [2]. Diabetic macular edema (DME), which is an advanced complication of diabetic retinopathy, can occur as one of the leading causes of visual impairment in working-age patients with diabetes [3, 4].

A meta-analysis reported the global prevalence of DME to be 6.8% in people with diabetes aged 20–79 years [5]. The therapeutic options for DME include laser photocoagulation [6], vitrectomy [7], corticosteroid injections (intravitreal or sub-Tenon’s capsule) [8, 9], and intravitreal injections of anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) [10–13].

The use of anti-VEGF treatment is increasing worldwide and is now considered to be the first-line therapy for the treatment of DME [2, 11–16]. However, undertreatment is observed to be relatively common in patients with DME, owing to the need for multiple visits and the associated financial burden [16, 17]. Visual outcomes corroborate with the number of anti-VEGF injections, and adherence to the treatment schedule is the key to success of the treatment [18]. Adherence to treatment is lower in patients with DME than in those with neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD), putting these patients at a higher risk of vision loss [18, 19]. It has also been observed that patients in real-world practice receive fewer anti-VEGF injections than those in large randomized clinical trials, which may result in poorer visual outcomes [20–23]. The lack of compliance to treatment in patients with DME can be attributed to a poor understanding of the disease, the burden of associated comorbidities, or the benefits of treatment [24]. Clinical preferences and trends in the management of DME have been reported by Japanese physicians [25, 26]. However, it is difficult for physicians to understand patient perceptions of DME and its treatment due to a lack of studies on patient-reported outcomes (PROs). One of the most appropriate methods to study patient preferences is through the use of conjoint analysis. Conjoint analysis is an analytical method originally developed for market research, which has recently been applied to the fields of health care and medicine to evaluate the treatment preferences of patients [27–29]. The strength of conjoint analysis is that it allows the patients to openly express their preferences without the need for a justification [27]. In addition, this method helps in creating profiles with various attribute options, assessing their importance, and generating utility scores demonstrating the influence of each attribute on overall preferences [29]. The main aim of this study was to identify factors influencing treatment preference in patients with DME. This study also investigated the differences in quality of life (QOL) and levels of self-management of DME in patients with or without the experience of anti-VEGF treatment.

Methods

Study Design

A cross-sectional study in patients with DME was conducted via an online survey in Japan in collaboration with Social Survey Research Information Co., Ltd. (SSRI). The survey link was emailed to Rakuten Insight's pre-registered type 1 and type 2 DM panels from 2019 to 2021. Patients who registered for the panels had already been diagnosed with DM. Screening questions were utilized to choose respondents based on the inclusion/exclusion criteria. In this questionnaire, 3 primary PROs (patient treatment preference, patient activation measure [PAM-13], and EuroQoL 5 dimension 5 level [EQ-5D-5L]) were measured. The patients were informed of the study design and goals by SSRI, and an informed online written consent was sought from all patients prior to the study’s execution. Patients were provided with an online case report form to complete the questionnaire. After collection, all surveys were anonymously sent to an independent data center. The study was conducted from May 31, 2022, to June 30, 2022, and the data analysis was conducted in July 2022. The study was conducted in accordance with guidelines on good clinical practice and with the ethical principles established by the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethics approval for this study was obtained from the Medical Corporation Toukeikai Kitamachi Clinic Institutional Review Board (BGQ08442, September 15, 2021). This study followed Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guidelines.

Patient Population

The study population comprised male and female patients aged ≥ 20 years in Japan diagnosed with DME. For inclusion in the study, patients had to (1) agree to participate in the survey and provide informed consent; (2) have a diagnosis of DM and be undergoing treatment at a medical institution; (3) have a diagnosis of DME; and (4) have received treatment for DME in the last 10 years. Patients who were younger than 20 years, were welfare recipients, or failed to meet any of the above-listed inclusion criteria were excluded from this analysis.

Study Objectives and Endpoints

The primary objective was to identify factors influencing treatment preference in patients with DME. The primary endpoint was the relative importance of each attribute calculated by conjoint analysis. The secondary objectives included (1) differences in treatment preference between patients with and without anti-VEGF treatment experience (with anti-VEGF treatment refers to patients who received at least 1 anti-VEGF treatment); (2) differences in treatment preference between adherent and non-adherent patients (adherent patients: patients who have never missed a scheduled appointment); (3) QOL in patients with and without anti-VEGF treatment experience; (4) patient activation levels in patients with and without anti-VEGF treatment experience; (5) demographics and characteristics of adherent and non-adherent patients; and (6) differences in treatment preference by visual acuity.

In line with the secondary objectives, the secondary endpoints were the (1) relative impact on treatment selection by conjoint analysis in patients with and without anti-VEGF treatment experience; (2) relative impact on treatment selection by conjoint analysis in adherent and non-adherent patients; (3) mean and standard deviation (SD) of EQ-5D-5L index values in patients with and without anti-VEGF treatment experience; and (4) mean and SD of PAM-13 scores in patients with and without anti-VEGF treatment experience.

The exploratory objective was to identify treatment preference by visual acuity, and the exploratory endpoint was the relative impact on treatment selection by conjoint analysis in patients of each visual acuity group. This survey did not collect any data related to safety.

Study Assessments

Patient Treatment Preference: Conjoint Analysis

Conjoint analysis is one of the experimental design methods (local control, replication, randomization) developed primarily in the marketing field [30]. Since it is impossible to experiment and understand all the combinations, the method is to reduce the number of combinations as much as possible (orthogonal array) and try to estimate correctly. For conjoint analysis, characteristics related to treatment preference are often composed of 5–7 attributes and 2–3 levels for each attribute. In addition, treatment-related social and economic factors, as well as factors involving medical services, are established. In the current study, primary data collected from questionnaires filled out by patients were used. The questionnaire for conjoint analysis was assessed and validated internally by consultation with DME specialists and online patient interviews of seven patients with DME (who were diagnosed with DME after a diagnosis of DM). Of these seven patients, three were treated with anti-VEGF therapy for DME. The attributes and levels used for the conjoint analysis in this study are provided in Table S1 in the electronic supplementary material. A total of 18 profiles were created from the attributes and levels and were used for the survey; patients were asked to choose a favorable profile in nine questions randomly generated from the 18 profiles.

PAM-13

PAM-13, developed by Insignia Health, was used to assess the knowledge, skills, and confidence levels of patients to self-manage their health or chronic condition [31].

Patient QOL: EQ-5D-5L

QOL of patients was measured using the EQ-5D-5L, which comprises five dimensions: mobility, self-care, regular activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression. In addition to the five dimensions, a visual analog scale (VAS) score evaluated the patient’s experience with and without anti-VEGF treatment [32].

Patient Questionnaire

Patient information was collected using a questionnaire filled in by patients at the time of enrollment as listed in Table S2 in the electronic supplementary material.

Data Analysis

As the survey was conducted online, all data were collected and stored in digital form. All patients provided informed online written consent prior to their participation in the survey. The conjoint analysis was performed by creating conjoint cards using an orthogonal array based on attributes and levels listed in Table S1 in the electronic supplementary material. Two cards were randomly presented to a patient, and the patient was then asked to choose one preferred card. Questions were asked multiple times, and all conjoint cards were presented to one of the patients. A regression analysis was conducted with the obtained patient preference as the response variable and the conjoint card level as the explanatory variable.

EQ-5D-5L index values were calculated according to the EQ-5D-5L crosswalk index value calculator provided by EuroQoL and descriptively analyzed by SSRI. Data related to PAM-13 were analyzed by Insignia Health.

Statistical Analysis

All analyses were conducted by SSRI (except PAM-13) using Bell Curve for Excel and R as appropriate, and all details of the analysis were documented in the statistical analysis plan. All data were collected and stored in a digital form as this study was conducted online. Data analysis was descriptive. Quantitative indicators were summarized by the mean (SD and 95% CI), median (Q1, Q3), and minimum/maximum. Qualitative measurements were summarized by count and percentage.

Sample size estimation was calculated using the following formula:

n = number of respondents, t = number of task (in this study, it was expected to be nine times), a = number of alternatives per task (number of choices displayed, two in this study), c = number of analysis cells (maximum number of levels, three in this study).

In this study, the sample number was calculated as n *9*2/3 ≥ 500. According to this formula, the number of respondents needs to exceed 84 and the estimated number of responses for the primary outcomes is approximately 220–330. The actual sample size to be analyzed in this study was 237, which was consistent with the original estimation.

Results

Screening questionnaires were shared with 27,236 patients registered on the Rakuten Insight type 1 and 2 DM panels. A total of 237 patients who responded to the survey, passed the screening, gave informed consent, and completed their responses were included in the analysis (Fig. 1).

Flow of respondents. Responses from online questionnaires were assessed by SSRI. SSRI Social Survey Research Information Co., Ltd

The overall patient characteristics are provided in Table S3 in the electronic supplementary material (the majority of patients were in the age group of 20–59 years and with more male patients [85.7%] than female patients [14.3%]), and characteristics of patients in the visual acuity categories are provided in Table S4 in the electronic supplementary material. A total of 90 patients (38.0%) experienced anti-VEGF treatment, and 201 patients (84.8%) were adherent to treatment. A corrected visual acuity of 0.3 and under, 0.4 to 0.6, and 0.7 and over was reported by 88 (39.8%), 47 (21.3%), and 86 (38.9%) patients, respectively. Decimal visual acuity was used to express visual acuity in this study as it is commonly used in Japan. Decimal visual acuity of 0.3, 0.4, 0.6, and 0.7 approximately corresponds to Snellen visual acuity of 20/63, 20/50, 20/32, and 20/29, respectively [33]. Sixteen respondents answered that they were “not sure” about their current visual acuity. Henceforth, the conjoint analysis was conducted excluding these respondents. Patients were asked to choose a favorable profile in 9 questions that were randomly generated from the aforementioned 18 profiles (Fig. 2). Logistic regression was used to evaluate patient preferences toward a treatment option. The mean partial regression coefficient (weighted based on the number of patients as appropriate) was calculated, and the differences between the mean and each level were regarded as the part-worth utility value. For each of the 18 profiles presented in the conjoint question, the sum of the results obtained by multiplying the presence or absence (0/1) of each level by the part-worth utility value was calculated as the overall utility value. Based on the results of the patient survey, each profile was evaluated as a treatment option (Fig. 3A, B).

Overall Relative Importance of Attributes

The conjoint analysis reported that the relative importance of “amount paid per visit,” “treatment frequency,” “anticipated post-treatment visual acuity,” “explanation on disease and treatment,” and “incidence of treatment-related side effects” was 16.5, 17.7, 30.0, 10.4, and 25.5%, respectively (Fig. 4). “Anticipated post-treatment visual acuity” was the most important factor for selecting a treatment in patients with DME followed by “incidence of treatment-related side effects” and “treatment frequency.”

Relative Importance of Attributes in Patients With or Without Anti-VEGF Treatment

The relative importance of the attributes “amount paid per visit,” “treatment frequency,” “anticipated post-treatment visual acuity,” “explanation on disease and treatment,” and “incidence of treatment-related side effects” was 18.1, 17.4, 30.6, 8.0, and 25.9%, respectively, for patients with anti-VEGF treatment experience. For patients without anti-VEGF treatment experience, the relative importance of each attribute was 15.4, 18.0, 29.4, 12.1, and 25.1%, respectively (Fig. 5). Regardless of the anti-VEGF treatment experience, “anticipated post-treatment visual acuity” was identified as the most important factor for patients in selecting a treatment.

Relative Importance of Attributes in Adherent and Non-Adherent Patients

The relative importance of the attributes “amount paid per visit,” “treatment frequency,” “anticipated post-treatment visual acuity,” “explanation on disease and treatment,” and “incidence of treatment-related side effects” was 15.7, 16.7, 30.1, 10.8, and 26.8%, respectively, for adherent patients. For non-adherent patients, the relative importance of each attribute was 21.6, 24.3, 29.4, 8.1, and 16.6%, respectively (Fig. 6). Both adherent and non-adherent patients considered “anticipated post-treatment visual acuity” as the most important factor in choosing treatment. For non-adherent patients, the second key factor was “treatment frequency,” followed by “amount paid per visit.”

QOL and Patient Activation

The EQ-5D-5L index value, EQ-VAS, and PAM-13 scores in patients with or without anti-VEGF treatment experience were presented descriptively. The average EQ-5D-5L index value in patients with anti-VEGF treatment experience was 0.785 and without anti-VEGF treatment experience was 0.825 as listed in Table S5 in the electronic supplementary material. The average EQ-VAS score in patients with and without anti-VEGF treatment experience was 65.3 and 67.4, respectively as listed in Table S6 in the electronic supplementary material. Among 237 patients, only 230 were included in the analysis for PAM-13 and scores to avoid missing values. The average PAM-13 scores in patients with and without anti-VEGF treatment experience were 55.2 and 51.7, respectively as listed in Table S7 in the electronic supplementary material.

Relative Importance of Attributes Based on Anticipated Post-Treatment Visual Acuity

The relative importance of “amount paid per visit,” “treatment frequency,” “anticipated post-treatment visual acuity,” “explanation on disease and treatment,” and “incidence of treatment-related side effects” was 14.8, 17.4, 27.6, 13.0, and 27.2%, respectively, for patients with visual acuity of 0.3 and under. For patients with visual acuity of 0.4 to 0.6, the relative importance of each attribute was 26.1, 16.5, 31.1, 6.9, and 19.4%, respectively. For patients with visual acuity of 0.7 and over, the relative importance of each attribute was 13.3, 18.8, 32.7, 9.3, and 25.8%, respectively. Regardless of visual acuity, “anticipated post-treatment visual acuity” was identified as the principal factor in selecting a treatment (Fig. 7).

Discussion

In this study, an online survey and conjoint analysis were used to investigate treatment preferences in patients with DME. The results of the present study indicate that anticipated post-treatment visual acuity is the primary factor for patients in selecting the treatment for DME. The relative importance of “anticipated post-treatment visual acuity,” “incidence of treatment-related side effects,” “treatment frequency,” “amount paid per treatment,” and “explanation on disease and treatment” was 30.0, 25.5, 17.7, 16.5, and 10.4%, respectively. These results demonstrated that the key factor for patients with DME to make a decision regarding treatment was the visual acuity outcome after treatment. The results of this study are in congruence with previous analyses that have reported the importance of visual outcomes in patients receiving treatment for nAMD and DME [27, 34].

Conjoint analysis has been used in health care to evaluate patient preferences [27–29]. A previous survey conducted among physicians from Japan reported that financial cost was the main factor determining the choice of DME treatment and that the other important factors were treatment frequency and associated complications [26]. The results of a cohort study from Jordan reported that the patient’s perception of treatment outcomes plays an important role in influencing their commitment to treatment [35]. Conjoint analyses have also been used in the past to evaluate patient preferences for managing nAMD, glaucoma, and DME [27–29, 34].

The second most important factor in the overall population was the “incidence of treatment-related side effects,” suggesting the importance of prompt and appropriate treatment management to prevent adverse events. In a study evaluating the expectations and fears of patients with DME treated using intravitreal injections, 28.2% of patients were fearful of the possible adverse events associated with the treatment of DME [36].

As reported in a real-world study, the frequency of injections is directly associated with visual outcomes, and compliance as well as adherence to treatment is the key element for achieving successful treatment for DME [18]. In this study, the relative importance of treatment frequency was the third most important factor for patients for choosing treatment, and these findings were also noted in a similar study evaluating patient preferences in nAMD and DME, suggesting that patients can increase their visit frequency to achieve better visual acuity gain after treatment [34].

In real-world clinical practice, the frequency of anti-VEGF treatment for DME is low in comparison with large-scale randomized clinical trials [20–23]. In the current study, anticipated post-treatment visual acuity was the principal factor for patients in deciding treatment, followed by frequency and cost of treatment in the non-adherent group. It has been reported that non-adherence to treatment is more pronounced in patients with DME than in patients with other chorioretinal diseases such as AMD and branch retinal vein occlusion [19]. Due to the observed importance of the frequency of treatment and the costs associated with treatment in the non-adherent group, a treatment approach that maintains the effect of the treatment for a longer duration with a lower economic burden is desirable to prevent drop-off. It was observed that there was a lack of explanation on disease and treatment in the study groups. Around 10% of patients selected that explanation on disease and treatment was important in routine clinical practice to promote adherence and to prevent drop-off.

Anti-VEGF treatment has demonstrated excellent therapeutic benefits in patients with DME; however, it is associated with a high treatment burden and cost. Visual outcomes in patients with DME correspond with the number of injections of anti-VEGF treatment, which makes compliance a primary factor for successful treatment [18]. DME is a cause of progressive vision loss, especially in younger patients; hence, it is important to address factors that prevent the patient from adhering to treatment [35]. There were no notable differences in treatment preferences between patients with or without experience of anti-VEGF treatment in the current study, except for cost per treatment. For patients with anti-VEGF treatment experience, amount paid per visit was more important than treatment frequency. This is different from the preferences of the overall population and patients without anti-VEGF treatment experience. This data suggests that patients with anti-VEGF treatment experience give importance to amount paid per visit, however anticipated post-treatment visual acuity and incidence of treatment-related side effects were considered more important.

Recent conjoint analyses evaluating patient preferences for choosing treatment for nAMD and DME have reported that maintenance of vision and achieving good vision are the 2 key factors that help patients in deciding the treatment plan [27, 34]. Similar to previous reports, in the present study, anticipated post-treatment visual acuity was identified as the principal factor in selecting treatment in all visual acuity groups. The comparison of the relative importance of anticipated post-treatment visual acuity and incidence of treatment-related side effects (27.6% and 27.2% in the 0.3 and under group; 32.7% and 25.8% in the 0.7 and over group) suggests that patients with low visual acuity may have additional concerns about adverse events that may lead to vision loss.

The EQ-5D is commonly used for measuring health-related QOL for several diseases across diverse populations. The EQ-5D-5L was designed to overcome the shortcomings of the EQ-5D-3L designed earlier. The EQ-5D-5L has 5 levels of response options and has a VAS, which can help patients in recording the status of their health between the worst and best imaginable health [32]. A systematic review and meta-analysis assessing the properties of EQ-5D-5L in patients with type 2 DM demonstrated its validity and reliability, along with the occurrence of ceiling effects [37]. PAM scores are utilized to assess patient knowledge, skill, and confidence for self-management of a disease and help physicians in determining the appropriate care plan for a patient [31]. In the present study, the average EQ-5D-5L index values, EQ-VAS scores, and PAM-13 scores in patients with and without anti-VEGF treatment experience were 0.785 and 0.825, 65.3 and 67.4, and 55.2 and 51.7, respectively. Overall, EQ-5D-5L index values, EQ-VAS scores, and PAM-13 scores were similar between the groups with or without the experience of anti-VEGF treatment. On the other hand, according to an earlier study in patients with nAMD, the major reasons for discontinuing anti-VEGF treatment were either doctor’s advice or their own will (mean EQ-5D-5L, 0.87–0.92; mean EQ-VAS, 69.7–76.5; mean PAM-13 score, 46.7–51.8) [38]. Although it is difficult to compare the current study and the previous study evaluating the mean values and scores of EQ-5D-5L, EQ-VAS, and PAM-13 of patients with nAMD [38], index values of EQ-5D-5L and EQ-VAS scores in patients with DME in the present study demonstrated a lower trend than those of patients with AMD, whereas PAM-13 scores showed an opposite trend. Patients with DME have more opportunities than those with AMD to have their disease explained to them by not only ophthalmologists but also internal medicine physicians. This might be correlated with a higher trend of PAM-13 of patients with DME than that of patients with AMD. Regarding EQ-5D-5L, a study evaluating norms of the Japanese population in EQ-5D-5L (stratified by age and gender) reported an inversely proportional relationship of age and EQ-5D-5L index values, i.e., younger age was associated with better mean EQ-5D-5L scores (age 16–19 years: male, 0.978; female: 0.967) when compared to older age (age: 80–89 years; male: 0.82; female: 0.77), respectively [39]. These observations imply the possibility of a greater impact on EQ-5D-5L in DME than that of patients with nAMD.

The findings of this study highlight the potential gap between physicians and patients in deciding the treatment of DME. A comparison of the results of this study with large clinical studies suggests that there could be a gap in the recognition of DME treatment between physicians and patients, and that aggressive treatment may facilitate improvements in visual acuity and patient satisfaction. An earlier study evaluating the expectations and fears of patients associated with intravitreal injections suggested that patients expect to receive more information from their physicians about the possibility of improving their vision and availability of treatments and an explanation of the disease [36].

The limitations of this study lie with factors such as no access to medical records, clinical data, physicians’ opinions, and/or details about the severity of disease of the patients. Patients self-reported themselves as patients with DME and entered their visual acuity, in the survey; however, they were considered as “true DME” patients owing to the small financial incentive (< 0.5 USD) for completing the survey. Another limitation was selection bias toward younger patients who are more comfortable with online research and are willing to participate when compared with elderly patients who are less likely to participate. In addition, compared to previous reports, more male patients participated in this study, which may have had affected the results. Moreover, patients who participate in online surveys may be more active in managing their health, and this could be considered as a bias in selection of the study patients. Patients treated for DME within 10 years were included, so there is a possibility of recall bias. Future studies using samples from diverse sources and channels could help in validating the results of the current study and in recommending whether these results could be generalized. This study did not include visual acuity before anti-VEGF treatment, its treatment effect, or drug history, and these limitations should be addressed in future studies.

Conclusions

In conclusion, to our knowledge, this is the first study to quantitatively examine treatment preferences of Japanese patients with DME using conjoint analysis. The results suggest that patients place the highest importance on anticipated post-treatment visual acuity while choosing between the treatment options for DME. To build a good patient–physician relationship, it is important for the physician to explain the visual prognosis to the patient before initiating DME treatment.

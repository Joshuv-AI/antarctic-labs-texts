Aortic Dissection Complicated With Superior Mesenteric Artery Aneurysm
Authors: Hisato Takagi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTA case of acute type B aortic dissection (intramural hematoma) complicated by superior mesenteric artery aneurysm is herein reported. It is suggested that the coexistence of aortic dissection and superior mesenteric artery aneurysm should be based upon the shared etiology.

Contrast‐enhanced computed‐tomography scans for a 57‐year‐old untreated hypertensive man with sudden severe back pain revealed acute type B aortic dissection (intramural hematoma) (Figure 1A,B; arrows) and superior mesenteric artery aneurysm, 15 mm in maximum diameter (Figure 1C,D; arrows). No physical characteristics of connective tissue diseases, for example, Marfan, Ehlers‐Danlos, or Loeys‐Dietz syndrome were identified. The patient underwent optimal medical (i.e., antihypertensive and analgesic) treatment and uneventfully recovered. The superior mesenteric artery aneurysm would be followed with scheduled and repeated computed‐tomography scans.

Acute type B aortic dissection (intramural hematoma) (A, B; arrows) and superior mesenteric artery aneurysm, 15 mm in diameter (C, D; arrows).

Coexistence of aortic dissection and superior mesenteric artery aneurysm is extremely rare. Merely one case of localized aortic root dissection with superior mesenteric artery aneurysm has been reported up to date [1]. Cystic medial necrosis, fibromuscular dysplasia, and segmental arterial mediolysis included in the etiology of superior mesenteric artery aneurysm could induce aortic dissection irrespective of the abovementioned syndromes. It has been extensively recognized that sporadic, familial, and syndromic aortic dissection is classified as a degenerative disease featured by cystic medial necrosis [2]. Meanwhile, cystic medial necrosis is identified as pathological findings of visceral artery aneurysm including superior mesenteric artery aneurysm [3]. A number of cases with superior mesenteric artery aneurysm due to fibromuscular dysplasia have been also reported [4]. Although fibromuscular dysplasia generally affects medium‐sized arteries, especially the vertebral, extracranial carotid, and renal arteries, it could involve almost all arteries including the aorta, bringing about aortic dissection [5]. Furthermore, segmental arterial mediolysis involves the superior mesenteric artery (leading to dissection and aneurysm) the most frequently [6]. A case of aortic dissection associated with segmental arterial mediolysis has been also known [7]. The aforementioned findings suggest that the coexistence of aortic dissection and superior mesenteric artery aneurysm in the present case should be based upon the shared etiology. Because visceral artery aneurysm (including superior mesenteric artery aneurysm) is generally asymptomatic and could bring about fatal rupture [3], screening for it in cases with aortic dissection and follow‐up (when it is detected) are important.

DNA methylation-based estimator of blood telomere length (DNAmTL) outperforms qPCR-based telomere length in reflecting clinical and sociodemographic factors in people with HIV infection
Authors: Andrés Esteban-Cantos, Javier Rodríguez-Centeno, Rocío Montejano, Julen Cadiñanos, Rosa de Miguel-Buckley, Lucía Gutiérrez-García, Cristina Marcelo-Calvo, Patricia Martínez-Martín, Jose I. Bernardino, Alejandro de Gea-Grela, Alejandro Díez-Vidal, Carlos Oñoro, Berta Rodés, José R. Arribas
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTelomere length (TL) is a widely used biomarker of biological aging, and is shortened in people living with HIV (PWH) due to chronic inflammation and immune-activation. The DNA methylation-based estimator of TL (DNAmTL) has emerged as a promising alternative to classical TL measurements, but their comparative performance in PWH remains poorly characterized.MethodsWe conducted a cross-sectional study comparing DNAmTL and monochrome multiplex quantitative PCR-based TL (qPCR-TL) in two cohorts of PWH: 160 ART-naïve individuals and 143 long-term treated individuals with sustained viral suppression. DNAmTL was derived from blood DNA methylation profiles generated using the Infinium MethylationEPIC arrays, while qPCR-TL was measured using a standardized assay. We evaluated the agreement between methods using correlation, Bland-Altman analysis and Cohen’s Kappa coefficient, and their associations with demographic, clinical and immunological factors were assessed using multivariable linear regression.ResultsDNAmTL and qPCR-TL were moderately correlated in both ART-naïve (r = 0.54, p < 0.001) and ART-treated participants (r = 0.46, p < 0.001), showing reasonable to fair agreement across continuous and categorical assessments. Both measures were inversely correlated with chronological age, although associations were stronger for DNAmTL. Compared to qPCR-TL, DNAmTL demonstrated more robust and independent associations with female sex and ethnicity, immunovirological markers of HIV disease severity such as plasma HIV-RNA viral load and the CD4:CD8 ratio, as well as with comorbidities including type 2 diabetes and hepatitis C virus coinfection.ConclusionsDNAmTL provides a more biologically informative measure of aging and disease burden in PWH. Further research is warranted to validate these results and to determine its predictive value for clinical outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13148-026-02164-3.

Introduction

Telomeres are structures composed of tandem DNA repeats and proteins that cap the end of chromosomes and provide chromosomal stability [1]. Telomeres shorten with each cell division, leading to telomere attrition as part of the normal aging process [2]. Several studies show that short telomeres are associated with age-related conditions, such as cardiovascular disease, cancer, metabolic disorders, and increased risk of mortality [3, 4, 5], making telomere length (TL) a reliable biomarker of biological aging.

A variety of approaches are available for the measurement of TL [6, 7]. Telomere restriction fragment (TRF) analysis by Southern Blot is considered the gold standard method; however, it is a labor-intensive technique that requires large amounts of DNA, which limits its applicability in clinical and epidemiologic studies [8]. Measurement of TL by quantitative-polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) emerged as a feasible alternative, since it requires substantially less DNA and can be performed in high-throughput format [6, 7]. This method usually involves quantifying the abundance of telomeric DNA repeats (T) relative to a single-copy gene (S) using a standard curve of a reference sample (T/S ratio) [9]. Nevertheless, concerns remain regarding the precision of TL measurements by qPCR, as they may be affected by the DNA extraction method, DNA storage conditions and the type of qPCR master mix used [10, 11].

Machine learning analysis has been used to develop a novel DNA methylation-based estimator of TL, known as DNAmTL [12]. This epigenetic biomarker was generated by regressing TL measurements obtained by TRF analysis on blood DNA methylation at CpG dinucleotide sites using an elastic net regression model, ultimately producing an estimate that predicts blood TL based on the DNA methylation levels of a specific set of 140 CpG sites in the genome. Validation of DNAmTL in different cohorts has shown its association with mortality, health outcomes and lifestyle habits [12]. Although DNAmTL emerges as a promising biomarker for assessing the biological aging process, there is still limited data on its precision and robustness compared to other TL techniques, as well as its performance in minority populations.

In HIV infection, blood TL shortening occurs, in addition to aging, as a consequence of chronic inflammation and immune activation [13], and it does not fully recover despite long-term antiretroviral therapy (ART) [14]. Furthermore, the burden of age-related comorbidities is higher among people living with HIV (PWH) receiving ART [15, 16]. These findings have led to the consideration of HIV infection as a model of accentuated biological aging, highlighting the importance of biomarkers for monitoring the aging process and the risk of adverse health outcomes. Hence, this study aimed to evaluate the performance of the novel DNAmTL estimator and its relationship with clinical and sociodemographic variables in two independent cohorts of viremic and virologically suppressed PWH, compared to blood TL measured by qPCR.

Methods

Design and participants

This cross-sectional study compares qPCR and DNA methylation-based estimators of blood TL in two populations of PWH. We included 160 ART-naïve PWH selected from the telomere length sub-study of the NEAT001/ANRS143 clinical trial, a randomized and open-label trial that evaluated the efficacy of two ART regimens for the first-line treatment of HIV infection [17]. The inclusion and exclusion criteria in the parent clinical trial have been previously described [17]. We also included a total of 143 ART-treated PWH selected from a previously described prospective cohort of long-term virologically suppressed individuals at La Paz University Hospital (Madrid, Spain) [18]. Whole blood samples were collected from participants and stored at − 80 °C until analysis.

Data collection

For the ART-naïve population, we collected the following clinical and sociodemographic variables: age, sex, ethnicity, tobacco and alcohol use, CD4 count, CD4:CD8 ratio, plasma HIV RNA viral load, mode of HIV transmission, time since HIV diagnosis, and HIV CDC clinical stage. For the ART-treated population, we collected the following variables: age, sex, ethnicity, education level, tobacco use, CD4 count, mode of HIV transmission, time since HIV diagnosis, history of AIDS, and presence of comorbidities (including active and resolved hepatitis C virus coinfection, type 2 diabetes mellitus, hypertension and chronic kidney disease).

DNA methylation-based estimator of telomere length

Genomic DNA was extracted from whole blood samples using the MagPurix Blood DNA Extraction Kit 200 (Zinexts Life Science; New Taipei, Taiwan). A total of 900 ng of DNA was bisulfite converted using the EZ DNA Methylation Kit (Zymo Research; Irvine, CA, USA) following the manufacturer’s instructions. The converted DNA was then amplified, enzymatically fragmented and hybridized to Infinium MethylationEPIC BeadChip microarrays (Illumina; San Diego, CA, USA) following the standard Illumina protocol. The Illumina iScan SQ instrument was used to scan the hybridized microarrays and obtain raw image intensities. Samples were processed in batches and randomized across chips to minimize potential batch effects. The resulting raw data (IDAT files) were imported and processed in R (version 4.4.2) using the Noob method for background correction and dye-bias normalization implemented in the minfi Bioconductor package [19]. Sample-level quality control included a mean detection p-value below 0.01, concordance between predicted and declared sex, and median log intensities of methylated and unmethylated channels above the minfi default threshold. Methylation levels were then quantified as β-values, defined as the ratio of methylated probe intensity to the sum of methylated and unmethylated probe intensities. Processed data were uploaded to Horvath’s DNA Methylation Age Calculator software (https://dnamage.clockfoundation.org) to calculate the DNAmTL estimator, which predicts blood TL in kilobases (kb) using the precomputed coefficients reported by Lu et al. [12].

Monochrome multiplex quantitative PCR-based measurement of telomere length

Relative blood TL, expressed as the ratio of the telomere amplification product (T) to that of a single-copy gene (S) [T/S ratio], was determined using Cawthon’s monochrome multiplex quantitative polymerase chain reaction assay [9], with minor modifications as previously described [20]. Briefly, the assay amplifies, in a single reaction, telomeric repeats and a single-copy gene (albumin) using the PowerUp SYBR Green Master Mix (Applied Biosystems; Foster City, CA, USA). The sequences of the primers used are provided in Supplementary Table 1. Each reaction used 20 ng of genomic DNA in a final volume of 20 µl. Cycling conditions consisted of two initial incubation steps (2 min at 50 °C followed by 10 min at 95 °C), two pre-amplification cycles (15 s at 94 °C and 90 s at 49 °C), and 32 amplification cycles (15 s at 94 °C; 15 s at 62 °C; 15 s at 74 °C, with signal acquisition for the telomere amplicon; 15 s at 84 °C; and 15 s at 88 °C, with signal acquisition for the albumin amplicon). Amplicon specificity was verified by melting curve analysis from 72 °C to 95 °C, with 0.5 °C increments held for 5 s each. Analyses were performed on the CFX96 Touch real-time PCR detection system (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). For relative quantification of TL, a standard curve with genomic blood DNA from a pool of 3 healthy volunteers was prepared by 3-fold serial dilutions and included in each run. Each run also included a reference sample to allow inter-run normalization, and a no-template control. Samples were randomly distributed across plates and analyzed in triplicate. Those with a coefficient of variation of the T/S ratio greater than 10% were reanalyzed.

Statistical analysis

Participant characteristics were described using absolute and relative frequencies for categorical variables, and medians with interquartile ranges (IQR) for continuous variables. We used Pearson’s correlation coefficient to assess the linear association between DNAmTL and qPCR-based TL, as well as their associations with chronological age. The agreement between the two TL estimators was evaluated using the Bland-Altman method, where the limits of agreement are defined as the mean difference of the two measurements (bias) ± 1.96 times the standard deviation of the differences. Agreement between DNAmTL and qPCR-TL

We found moderate positive correlations between DNAmTL and qPCR-TL in both untreated (r = 0.54, p < 0.001) and long-term treated participants (r = 0.46, p < 0.001) (Fig. 1a and b). To further evaluate how both TL methods agree with each other, we performed Bland-Altman analysis using the standardized TL values (z-scores). In both HIV populations, less than 5% of participants fell outside the 95% limits of agreement (LoA), indicating reasonable agreement between the two methods with some individual variability observed across the range of TL measurements (Fig. 1c and d).

When we categorized participants as having long or short TL based on the median TL value, we found an agreement of 66.2% (Kappa coefficient = 0.32, p < 0.001) and 63.6% (Kappa coefficient = 0.27, p < 0.001) between DNAmTL and qPCR-TL in the ART-naïve population and the long-term treated population, respectively, denoting fair agreement between methods (Supplementary Fig. 1).

Association between TL estimators and sociodemographic characteristics

In both study populations, TL measurements demonstrated significant negative correlations with chronological age, with DNAmTL exhibiting stronger associations compared to qPCR-TL (Fig. 2). Univariate and multivariable regression models for each cohort confirmed the association between older age and shorter TL measurements (Figs. 3 and 4).

In univariate analyses, female sex was consistently associated with longer DNAmTL, whereas the association between female sex and longer qPCR-TL was observed only among ART-treated participants (Figs. 3 and 4). Additionally, we found a strong relationship between black ethnicity and longer DNAmTL in the ART-naïve group (p < 0.001) (Fig. 3a), as well as longer qPCR-TL in ART-treated participants who were current smokers (p = 0.010) (Fig. 4b). All these findings remained statistically significant in multivariable models adjusted for age and other TL-related variables. In contrast, current alcoholism and former tobacco use in ART-naïve participants were linked to both shorter DNAmTL and qPCR-TL in univariate analyses, but lost statistical significance after adjusting for other covariates (Fig. 3). We found no association between TL measurements and educational level.

Association between TL estimators and clinical and HIV-related variables

Among ART-naïve participants, shorter DNAmTL was independently associated with plasma HIV-RNA viral loads exceeding 100,000 copies/mL (p < 0.001), a lower CD4:CD8 ratio (p < 0.001), CD4+ T cell counts below 200 cells/µl (p = 0.001) and the HIV CDC-defined clinical stage C (p = 0.028) (Fig. 3a). After adjusting for age and other TL-related covariates, DNAmTL remained significantly associated with both CD4:CD8 ratio and HIV-RNA viral load (p < 0.001 and p = 0.001, respectively). Conversely, shorter qPCR-TL was independently associated with higher HIV viral load (p = 0.008), non-sexual HIV transmission (p = 0.031) and a lower CD4:CD8 ratio (p = 0.048); however, all these findings were no longer statistically significant in the multivariate analysis (Fig. 3b).

In the group of ART-treated participants with long-term virological suppression, shorter DNAmTL was associated with lower CD4+ T cell counts and previous history of AIDS (p = 0.012 and p = 0.038, respectively) (Fig. 4a), while shorter TL measured by qPCR was linked only to reduced CD4+ T cell counts (p = 0.006) (Fig. 4b). However, these associations lost significance when adjusted for age and other covariates. In this population, time since HIV diagnosis and the mode of HIV acquisition were not related to any of the TL measurements.

For the ART-treated population we also collected information on several comorbidities. We observed that participants with active hepatitis C virus (HCV) co-infection and type 2 diabetes mellitus had significantly shorter DNAmTL even after controlling for age and other TL-related covariates (p = 0.008 and p = 0.037, respectively) (Fig. 4a), while there was a trend towards significance between hypertension and shorter DNAmTL only in the univariate analysis (p = 0.051). By contrast, we found no associations between TL, as measured by qPCR, and the comorbidities included in this study (Fig. 4b). These findings were further supported by a complementary logistic regression analysis, in which participants were categorized into short and long TL groups using the median of DNAmTL and of qPCR-TL as the cut-off (Supplementary Table 4). Short DNAmTL was associated with active HCV co-infection both in the unadjusted analysis and after adjustment for age and sex (adjusted OR 7.00, 95% CI 1.62–30.36, p = 0.009). Short DNAmTL was also independently associated with type 2 diabetes mellitus, but this association was attenuated after adjustment for age and sex. Short qPCR-TL was not significantly associated with any of the comorbidities studied.

Discussion

This is the first study evaluating the performance of the DNAmTL estimator in adults with HIV infection, in comparison with qPCR-TL. By analyzing two independent populations of PWH receiving or not receiving ART, our results show only modest agreement between both methods, and indicate that DNAmTL outperforms blood qPCR-TL in detecting associations with clinical and sociodemographic factors.

The observed level of concordance between DNAmTL and qPCR-TL is consistent with previous reports in other HIV-uninfected populations, which have shown low-to-moderate correlations between the two measures (correlation coefficients ranging from 0.08 to 0.41) [12, 21, 22]. This modest correlation likely reflects both biological and technical differences between the two estimators. DNAmTL represents an epigenetic surrogate of TL trained on the gold standard TRF-based TL measurements and may capture broader aspects of cellular aging beyond the physical attrition of telomeres [12], whereas qPCR-based methods provide a direct measure that can be influenced by technical variability and assay reproducibility. Despite these differences, both DNAmTL and qPCR-TL showed significant negative correlations with chronological age in this study; however, DNAmTL exhibited stronger associations, aligning with observations in other populations where correlations with age ranged from − 0.5 to − 0.8 [12, 21]. A recent meta-analysis of 341 cross-sectional studies comprising more than 700,000 individuals has reported a pooled-corrected correlation between TL—as measured by qPCR, southern blot or quantitative fluorescence in situ hybridization (qFISH)—and chronological age of − 0.19 [23]. Taken together, these findings suggest that DNAmTL stands out as a more robust estimator of age-related changes.

Previous studies have reported that females and individuals of Black/African ancestry exhibit longer blood TL on average [24, 25]. In this work, we observed that blood TL was longer in females and individuals of African ancestry with HIV infection, and these associations were more clearly captured by DNAmTL. This supports that DNAmTL may better reflect biological variation linked to demographic factors. Regarding lifestyle factors potentially related to TL shortening, such as tobacco and alcohol use [26], we observed only weak associations with tobacco. In the ART-treated population, smokers exhibited significantly longer TL as measured by qPCR. However, this finding should be interpreted with caution as we did not have data to distinguish never-smokers from former smokers in the comparison group.

During untreated HIV infection, viral replication drives chronic inflammation and sustained immune activation that accelerate blood TL shortening [13, 27]. In our study, shorter TL was independently associated with higher plasma HIV viral load and lower CD4/CD8 ratios among ART-naïve participants, with DNAmTL showing significant associations even after adjustment for chronological age and other potential confounders. These findings support the notion that TL measurements, especially DNAmTL, may capture the underlying immunological impact of uncontrolled HIV infection. Following ART initiation and viral suppression, partial immune reconstitution occurs and is accompanied by increases in blood TL [14, 17]. Accordingly, in our ART-treated population, the associations between TL and immunological parameters were attenuated, suggesting that TL dynamics primarily reflect the cumulative burden of past HIV-related immune damage rather than the current disease stage.

The capacity of blood TL to serve as a biomarker of health and disease outcomes has been widely investigated, but findings remain inconclusive [28]. In the general population, several studies have found that DNAmTL has more robust associations with age-related comorbidities and adverse health outcomes than traditional TL measures [12, 29, 30], suggesting its potential as a biomarker of biological aging and disease risk. Among people living with HIV infection, previous studies have relied on TL measured by qPCR-based methods, and direct comparisons between DNAmTL and other TL measurements remain scarce. In this context, our study provides novel evidence that DNAmTL, but not qPCR-TL, shows significant associations with clinical outcomes such as type 2 diabetes and active HCV co-infection in participants under effective ART. Nevertheless, these findings should be interpreted with caution due to the limited scope of comorbidities included and the modest sample size. Consistent with our results, a previous report from the Veterans Aging Cohort Study (VACS) showed that DNAmTL is significantly associated with frailty, cancer, and the VACS index, a validated prognostic tool to estimate overall morbidity and mortality risk in people with HIV [31]. These findings suggest that DNAmTL could serve as a biomarker of health conditions, highlighting the need for future research, particularly longitudinal studies, to determine its predictive value. In this regard, our group has recently conducted the first study to evaluate the ability of DNAmTL and other epigenetic aging biomarkers to predict non-AIDS serious clinical events in a prospective cohort of well-controlled HIV-treated participants followed for 10 years; however, no significant predictive power for DNAmTL was observed [32].

Beyond these biological and clinical considerations, the two methods differ in DNA input, cost and turnaround time. The qPCR-TL assay requires only tens of nanograms of DNA per reaction, uses inexpensive reagents and delivers results within hours. The DNAmTL estimator, by contrast, requires a larger amount of DNA for the Infinium MethylationEPIC BeadChip analysis and a turnaround of several days. Additionally, consumable costs are several-fold higher than for qPCR-TL. Despite these higher operational costs, the EPIC array yields genome-wide methylation data from which multiple epigenetic biomarkers can be derived in parallel (DNAmTL, epigenetic clocks, estimated immune-cell composition, and others), substantially improving cost-efficiency when more than one biomarker is of interest. Together with the stronger reflection of clinical and sociodemographic factors observed in this study, these features support the use of DNAmTL as a more informative tool for research on biological aging in people with HIV.

This study has several limitations. First, the absence of a gold-standard method for TL assessment, such as TRF analysis, limited a more precise evaluation of DNAmTL performance, as qPCR provides a relative measurement of TL and is more susceptible to technical variability. Second, DNAmTL represents an epigenetic proxy for blood TL; therefore, associations observed with this biomarker should be interpreted in the context of biological aging rather than as a direct measure of physical TL. Third, the moderate sample size, and particularly the limited number of female and non-Caucasian participants, may have reduced the statistical power to detect associations between TL measurements and study variables, and limited the generalizability of our findings. Additionally, we cannot exclude residual confounding from unmeasured variables that might influence blood TL. Finally, given the cross-sectional design of the study, we were unable to assess the performance of TL measurements on telomere dynamics over time or to estimate hazard ratios for clinical events.

Conclusions

In summary, this study demonstrates that DNAmTL shows more consistent associations with demographic, clinical and immunological factors than qPCR-TL among adults living with HIV infection across different disease statuses. Additionally, our findings support the potential utility of DNAmTL as a biomarker of biological aging and underscore the need for further studies to validate its associations with clinical outcomes.

Allergic rhinitis activity and efficacy of 300 IR house dust mite sublingual immunotherapy tablet in pediatrics
Authors: Philippe Devillier, Mitsuhiro Okano, Giorgio Walter Canonica, Silvia Scurati, Josiane Cognet‐Sicé, Catherine Gentil, Kwok Yan, Julien Cottet, Eric Fromentin, Pascal Demoly, Oliver Pfaar, Yoshitaka Okamoto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHouse dust mite (HDM) allergic rhinitis (HDM‐AR) fluctuates over time with exposure conditions, making disease activity assessment essential when demonstrating treatment efficacy. We conducted post hoc tertile analyses to more accurately assess the efficacy of 300 IR HDM sublingual immunotherapy (SLIT) tablet in moderate‐to‐severe HDM‐AR pediatric patients during periods of increased disease activity.MethodsData from two Japanese randomized controlled trials of 300 IR HDM SLIT‐tablet were used to assess the average adjusted symptom score (AASS) and average combined symptom and medication score (ACSMS). To determine the impact of HDM‐AR activity, placebo group scores during the primary period (end of treatment) at each center were ranked from lowest to highest to establish three tertiles. Scores differences between SLIT and placebo were analyzed in each tertile using ANCOVA.ResultsThe first analysis included patients aged 5–16 years (300 IR = 193, placebo = 210). During the primary period, the effect of the SLIT‐tablet, estimated at −13.1% (AASS) and −12.9% (ACSMS) versus placebo overall, was more pronounced in the highest tertile (relative differences −27.3% and −28.5%, respectively), with similar results across age groups. The second analysis including a pool of adolescents (300 IR = 120, placebo = 135) showed an overall effect of −19.9% (AASS) and −21.2% (ACSMS) with 300 IR versus placebo, with again greater improvements in the highest tertile (−32.5% and −34.1%, respectively).ConclusionThe greatest improvements occurred in the tertile where pediatric patients exhibited higher disease activity. This underscores the true efficacy of 300 IR HDM SLIT‐tablet during periods when patients experience the most troublesome symptoms.

Key messageIn natural field studies of moderate‐to‐severe patients with house dust mite (HDM)‐related allergic rhinitis, where exposure conditions fluctuate and symptoms vary in intensity, tertile analyses can provide a valuable proxy measure of disease activity, offering a more accurate picture of treatment benefits. Applied here to clinical trials in pediatric patients, they showed that 300 IR HDM SLIT‐tablet has an even greater effect on those experiencing the highest symptom burden. INTRODUCTION

Allergic rhinitis (AR) is a type I allergic disease characterized by recurrent sneezing, rhinorrhea, nasal pruritus, and nasal blockage, and house dust mites (HDM) are common causative allergens. 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 AR significantly impacts the pediatric population and is a risk factor for the subsequent development of allergic airway diseases, including asthma. 3 , 4 , 5

Notably, there has been a marked increase in the diagnosed prevalence of AR over recent years, rising from 8.4% from 2012 and 2015 to 19.9% from 2016 to 2022. 6 The International Study of Asthma and Allergies in Childhood reported a global prevalence of rhinoconjunctivitis symptoms ranging from 4.2% to 12.7% and 45.1% in children aged 6–7 and 13–14 years, respectively. 7 Despite regional variations, the increasing prevalence of AR in children raises concerns regarding its potential impact on child development. 4 Furthermore, AR is frequently underdiagnosed in children due to insufficient reporting of symptoms to parents or healthcare providers, which is also hindered by suboptimal management and communication between healthcare professionals. 8 Although AR is not typically associated with severe morbidity, its symptomatology can significantly impair the quality of life in children, leading to sleep disturbances, emotional challenges, fatigue, irritability, and limitations in daily activities and social functioning. 9 , 10 , 11 , 12

The efficacy of allergen immunotherapy (AIT) in alleviating symptoms and reducing medication use in AR has been well established. AIT encompasses both subcutaneous immunotherapy (SCIT) and sublingual immunotherapy (SLIT), representing the only currently available disease‐modifying treatment for AR. 3 , 13 , 14 , 15 , 16 The 300 index of reactivity (IR) HDM SLIT‐tablet, which contains standardized allergen extracts from Dermatophagoides (D.) pteronyssinus and D. farinae in a total allergenic activity ratio of 1:1, was found effective and well tolerated in adults, adolescents, and children with HDM‐induced AR with or without controlled asthma, 17 , 18 , 19 , 20 , 21 , 22 , 23 with evidence for sustained post‐treatment benefits in adults. 17 Based on the positive results from the latter randomized controlled trial (RCT), the HDM SLIT tablet has been specifically recommended for long‐term benefits for AR. 3 The 300 IR HDM SLIT‐tablet has received regulatory approval in patients as young as 5 years in the Asia‐Pacific region and in adolescents and adults in Europe.

Allergy to HDM is expressed clinically in a very heterogeneous way, and the intensity of symptoms varies over time. 24 , 25 Although the mean effect size was quite consistent across the large, multicenter, RCTs with 300 IR HDM SLIT‐tablet performed under natural field conditions, 26 , 27 the difference in scores between the active treatment group and the placebo group did not reach the threshold for statistical significance in a 1‐year study because the patients were not symptomatic enough during the evaluation period to enable assessment of the efficacy versus placebo. 28 Indeed, several factors may reduce the effect size measured in these RCTs, such as (i) a high effect size in the placebo group, which limits the room for improvement in the active treatment, 29 (ii) a short‐term run‐in (baseline) period, even with placebo, before treatment allocation that does not recapitulate the natural history of the disease over the following year, and (iii) a low level of disease activity in a substantial proportion of the patient population during the short evaluation period that may not adequately capture the peak symptomology, during which the maximal treatment effect is likely to be observed. 30

In a recent publication, the study centers from three RCTs with 300 IR HDM SLIT‐tablet were grouped into tertiles according to the level of combined symptoms and medication scores in placebo patients at the end of treatment. These tertile analyses performed in studies involving a very large majority of adult patients have shown that treatment efficacy increased with the placebo score in each tertile, regardless of RCT and combined score, and that the 300 IR HDM SLIT‐tablet was associated with greater efficacy in the tertile showing the highest disease activity. 31

Subsequently, we applied the tertile approach using data from two Japanese RCTs of 300 IR HDM SLIT‐tablet, focusing on the pediatric population. A first analysis included children and adolescents from study JP‐D1732, 19 and a second included a pool of adolescents from both studies JP‐D1731 18 and JP‐D1732. The aim of this post hoc tertile‐based study was to more accurately assess the efficacy of the 300 IR HDM SLIT‐tablet in the pediatric population with moderate‐to‐severe HDM‐AR, particularly during periods of increased disease activity when patients are most in need.

METHODS

Clinical trials

The two post hoc analyses included data from the 1207D1731 (JP‐D1731, JapicCTI‐121917) 18 and 1501D1732 (JP‐D1732, Japic‐152981) 19 studies conducted in Japan (Table S1). Both were phase 3, randomized, multicenter, double‐blind, and placebo‐controlled trials. The first study enrolled patients aged 12–64, while the second focused on a pediatric population (5–16 years).

These two RCTs were conducted in compliance with local laws and regulations, the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, and good clinical practices established by the International Council for Harmonization. The protocols for the research projects were approved by the Ethics Committees and the Institutional Review Board of each study site. Parents or legal representatives provided written consent to participate after being fully informed about the study's objectives and procedures. Patient anonymity was preserved using methods approved by the Ethics Committees.

Participants and study treatment

The inclusion and exclusion criteria for both studies have been previously described. 18 , 19 The analyzed populations comprised patients with moderate‐to‐severe HDM‐AR, with or without controlled asthma, who were treated with the 300 IR HDM SLIT‐tablet or placebo for 1 year. The post hoc analyses focused on: (1) the pediatric patients (5–16 years) from the JP‐D1732 study and (2) a pooled cohort of adolescents (12–17 years) from both studies (JP‐D1731 and JP‐D1732).

Since 2015, the 300 IR SLIT tablet containing D. pteronyssinus and D. farinae allergen extracts (Actair®/Orylmyte®/Aitmyte®, Stallergenes Greer, Antony, France/Shionogi & Co. Ltd., Osaka, Japan; equivalent to 57,000 Japanese allergy units) has been approved for the treatment of adults and adolescents with HDM‐AR with or without conjunctivitis (HDM‐AR/C) in the Asia‐Pacific region and Europe, as well as for children aged 5–11 years in Japan, South Korea, Australia, and Macau. 22 , 32 Although both clinical trials evaluated the 300 IR dose, the study conducted in adults and adolescents also assessed a 500 IR dose. The present analyses included only data from patients treated with the 300 IR HDM SLIT‐tablet as approved.

Assessment of treatment efficacy by disease activity: Tertile approach and statistical analyses

As previously reported, the primary efficacy endpoint in both studies was the average adjusted symptom score (AASS) during the last 8 and 4 weeks of the 52‐week treatment period in JP‐D1731 and JP‐D1732, respectively. 18 , 19 The AASS is a rhinitis total symptom score (RTSS), calculated as the sum of scores for sneezing, rhinorrhea, nasal congestion, and nasal pruritus, adjusted for the use of rescue medication. 33 In both Japanese studies, the AASS ranged from 0 to 15. 18 , 19

Symptoms and medication use were recorded using slightly different scoring scales previously described 18 than those recommended by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the European Medicines Agency (symptom severity on a 0–3 scale: 0 = no symptoms, 1 = mild symptoms, 2 = moderate symptoms, 3 = severe symptoms, and rescue medication use on a 0–3 scale). Based on these recommendations, the European Academy of Allergy & Clinical Immunology (EAACI) issued a position paper in 2014 proposing a balanced scoring system, the average combined symptom and medication score (ACSMS), ranging from 0 to 6. 34

In our study, to derive a proxy combined symptom and medication score (CSMS) on a 0–6 scale, the following adjustments were applied. Nasal congestion, rhinorrhea, and sneezing (original range: 0–4) were proportionally transformed by multiplying by 3 and dividing by 4. Nasal pruritus (original range: 0–3) remained unchanged. Rescue medication use (original range: 0–2) was proportionally transformed by multiplying by 3 and dividing by 2.

For the tertile approach, the underlying hypothesis was that symptom scores in the placebo group reflect the disease activity of patients at specific centers, which share similar features, particularly with respect to HDM exposure. Consequently, treatment outcomes can be evaluated in relation to the intensity of the disease at a given location. For the analysis of the pediatric study JP‐D1732, study sites were ranked in ascending order according to the mean symptom score (AASS, scale 0–15, or ACSMS, scale 0–6) observed in the placebo group during the primary evaluation period. Sites were then grouped into low, medium, and high tertiles, each comprising approximately one‐third of the patients.

For the analysis involving adolescents from JP‐D1731 and JP‐D1732 studies, data from each treatment group were pooled. Tertiles were defined by: (1) assessing the AASS or ACSMS in the placebo group during the primary period at each center; (2) ranking centers from the lowest to the highest AASS or ACSMS; and (3) allocating centers into three tertiles (clusters), each including approximately one‐third of the patients. Thus, the low, medium, and high tertiles represented centers with low, medium, and high AASS or ACSMS in the placebo group during the primary period, which reflected a low, medium, or high intensity of symptoms in those areas.

All variables were summarized descriptively. For continuous variables, summary statistics included the mean, standard deviation (SD), 95% two‐sided confidence interval (CI) of the mean, median, lower quartile (Q1: 25th percentile), upper quartile (Q3: 75th percentile), and the minimum and maximum values. For categorical variables, absolute frequencies (n) and relative (%) frequencies were used. Unless otherwise specified, percentages were calculated within each treatment group or overall, using the number of non‐missing values as the denominator.

Differences between the 300 IR HDM SLIT‐tablet and placebo groups were evaluated using ANCOVA. As previously described, the primary efficacy analyses for both JP‐D1731 18 and JP‐D1732 19 used a mixed‐effects model for repeated measures (MMRM) approach. For the post hoc analyses, an ANCOVA model without tertile stratification included the treatment group as the main effect, and age, sensitization status, and baseline endpoint (AASS or ACSMS) as covariates. In the model incorporating tertiles, an interaction term between the treatment group and endpoint was added. Point estimates and 95% CIs for the absolute difference in adjusted least square (LS) means between the active and placebo groups were calculated, with relative differences (%) defined as 100 × (LS mean for active–LS mean for placebo)/LS mean for placebo. In the pediatric study JP‐D1732, analyses were stratified by age groups (5–11 years and 12–16 years).

Statistical analyses were performed by Stallergenes S.A. using SAS® for Windows version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, North Carolina, United States of America, USA).

RESULTS

Study population and baseline characteristics

A total of 438 patients aged 5–16 years participated in the JP‐D1732 study, of which 422 were included in the full analysis set, defined as all patients in the safety analysis population with evaluable efficacy data. Among these, 403 patients (193 in the 300 IR group and 210 in the placebo group) were evaluable for the first tertile analysis.

Demographics and baseline characteristics, including disease history and severity, were comparable between treatment groups and have been reported. 19 The baseline scores (AASS, ACSMS, ARTSS, and ARMS) describing symptomatology and rescue medication use are presented in Table S2.

Additionally, the JP‐D1731 and JP‐D1732 studies collectively enrolled 270 patients aged 12–17 years, of whom 255 patients (120 in the 300 IR group and 135 in the placebo group) were eligible for the second tertile analysis. The mean age of this cohort was 13.7 years (SD: 1.46), with a predominance of male participants (58.0%). Regarding disease duration, most participants (55.2%) had been affected for 5–9 years, while only 17.4% had been affected for less than 5 years, and 27.4% had experienced the disease for 10 years or more. Furthermore, more than three‐quarters of the adolescents (80.0%) were polysensitized. Demographics and baseline characteristics were comparable between the active treatment and placebo groups (Table 1). Baseline scores for AASS, ACSMS, and ARMS are presented in Table 1.

Treatment efficacy in pediatric patients: Overall and by tertiles (JP‐D1732 study)

In the pediatric population, the overall effect of the 300 IR HDM SLIT‐tablet during the primary period compared to placebo was significant: point estimates at −0.95, p = .0005 for the AASS and −0.20, p = .0050 for the ACSMS, with relative differences versus placebo of −13.1% and −12.9%, respectively.

For both scores, treatment effect was more pronounced with statistical significance in the highest tertile. For the low, medium, and high tertiles, the relative differences compared to placebo were +3.7%, −7.2%, and −27.3% (point estimate: −2.49 p < .0001), respectively, for the AASS and +5.6%, −4.9%, and −28.5% (point estimate: −0.57; p < .0001), respectively, for the ACSMS (Table 2 and Figure 1). Similar results for both AASS and ACSMS compared to placebo were observed across different age group subsets (Figure 2). Notably, absolute and relative differences versus placebo in the high tertile were quite similar in both 5–11‐ and 12–16‐year groups, that is, point estimates of −2.42 (−26.9%) and −2.70 (−29.0%), respectively for the AASS, and −0.53 (−26.3%) and −0.72 (−35.8%), respectively for the ACSMS.

Treatment efficacy in adolescents: Overall and by tertiles (pool JP‐D1731 and JP‐D1732 studies)

In the overall adolescent population, the treatment effect of the 300 IR HDM SLIT‐tablet during the primary period, compared to placebo, was significant, with point estimates at −1.42, p < .0001 for the AASS and −0.33, p < .0001 for the ACSMS, and relative differences of −19.9% and −21.2%, respectively.

The treatment effect was more pronounced with statistical significance in the highest tertile for both scores. For the AASS, the relative differences compared to placebo for the low, medium, and high tertiles were −4.0%, −14.0%, and −32.5% (point estimate: −2.97; p < .0001), respectively, and for the ACSMS, they were −1.9%, −17.4%, and −34.1% (point estimate: −0.68; p < .0001), respectively (Table 3 and Figure 3).

DISCUSSION

Because patients with HDM‐AR experience symptoms with diverse intensity over time, it is essential to evaluate treatment efficacy during periods of heightened disease activity. These post hoc tertile analyses of two Japanese RCTs 18 , 19 revealed that the 300 IR HDM SLIT‐tablet can be more effective than placebo in children and adolescents with moderate‐to‐severe HDM‐AR when the disease is particularly active. Indeed, both clinical scores analyzed showed the greatest significant differences versus placebo in the high tertile, where the placebo group exhibited higher AR activity during the primary evaluation period (i.e., at the end of treatment). These findings may provide better assessment of the efficacy of the 300 IR HDM SLIT‐tablet in the pediatric population with AR, consistent with results observed in adults. 17 , 18

Moreover, age‐stratified analyses showed that improvements in patients in the highest tertile of disease activity were consistently substantial in younger children (5–11 years) and in adolescents (5–16/17 years), aligning with outcomes demonstrated in adults, 31 that is, at least ca. 2.0–3.0 points improvement versus placebo.

Tertile analysis is commonly used in various therapeutic areas to enable a more detailed examination of trial data and to draw more precise conclusions regarding specific subgroups based on key variables. 35 , 36 , 37 In contrast to environmental exposure chamber studies, which feature controlled exposure to HDM allergens, the variable conditions inherent to RCTs can obscure the true differences between AIT and placebo responses. 38 Furthermore, since treatment impact in RCTs is assessed over a relatively short period compared to the overall trial duration, fluctuations in local, regional, and meteorological factors may result in some individuals experiencing few or no symptoms during the evaluation period, while others may exhibit more pronounced ones. This variability is particularly evident in our study's low tertiles, where sites with low clinical scores in the placebo group during the final weeks of treatment exhibited an apparent reduced effect of SLIT due to a floor effect. 31 Overall, in the context of HDM allergy, where allergen exposure is difficult to estimate accurately, the tertile approach employed here served as a useful proxy for disease activity, providing a clearer picture of the efficacy of 300 IR HDM SLIT tablet when AR symptoms are more marked.

Our integrated analysis of data from the JP‐D1731 and JP‐D1732 studies resulted in a cohort of 758 children and adolescents (5–17 years) with HDM AR. The two analyses conducted in this research strongly suggest that treatment efficacy may be greater when disease activity is higher (i.e., greater symptom severity), consistent with the findings reported by Devillier et al. 31 These authors performed tertile analyses using data from three RCTs conducted in adults and adolescents with HDM AR. 17 , 18 , 20 Their recent study concluded that applying the tertile approach to AIT research, where multiple interacting variables can influence outcomes, may yield more precise and clinically relevant measurements of efficacy and patient benefit, thereby better reflecting real‐world conditions.

Although beyond the scope of the present research, baseline scores from the two Japanese studies were analyzed to explore potential differences in disease burden between children and adolescents, which could account for variations in treatment effects observed in adolescents. However, no notable differences in baseline score levels were identified.

The large sample size and prospective design of the JP‐D1731 and JP‐D1732 trials represent key strengths of this analysis. Nevertheless, our study has some limitations. While post hoc analyses can generate valuable hypotheses, their retrospective nature limits the strength of comparisons both between and within tertiles. Not prespecified in the original study protocols, such tertile subgrouping may raise the risk of type I errors and overinterpretation. Also, it should be acknowledged that the grouping approach based on mean placebo scores at the center level may misclassify the disease activity experienced by individual patients. Therefore, it cannot be excluded that these findings may be overestimated and should be interpreted as exploratory. Another limitation of the results stems from the transformation of the symptom and medication scales initially used in the Japanese trials to derive a proxy CSMS. This transformation may add an additional bias but better reflects the EAACI‐balanced score enabling comparison with already published studies. Finally, because environmental HDM exposure, housing conditions, genetic background, and healthcare practices vary across regions and may affect disease expression and treatment response, our findings may not be fully generalizable beyond Japanese pediatric populations. Extrapolation should therefore be made cautiously, and studies in more diverse regional cohorts are needed to confirm and extend these results.

In conclusion, these subanalyses by disease activity highlight that the efficacy of the 300 IR HDM SLIT‐tablet may be even greater during the highest periods of AR activity in both younger children (5–11) and older adolescent populations (12–16/17). Such findings reinforce the relevance and benefits of this treatment all year long for pediatric patients with moderate‐to‐severe HDM AR and fluctuating symptom intensity.

Global burden of chronic liver disease due to NAFLD from 2012 to 2021: A trend, decomposition, and health inequality analysis
Authors: Yanrui Wu, Zongbiao Tan, Zhou Liu, Yupei Liu, Chuan Liu, Junhai Zhen, Jixiang Zhang, Weiguo Dong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNon-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is considered to be an important driver of the increasing burden of chronic liver disease (CLD) worldwide. It is necessary to analyze the burden of CLD due to NAFLD (CLD-NAFLD) systematically.MethodsData related to CLD-NAFLD burden from 2012 to 2021 were obtained from the Global Burden of Disease Study (GBD) 2021. The temporal trend of the incidence and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) was quantified by average annual percentage change (AAPC). The driving factors of the incidence/DALYs change were explored through decomposition analysis. Slope index and concentration index were employed to investigate cross-country health inequalities.ResultsDuring 2012–2021, the global age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR) of CLD-NAFLD increased from 551.52 to 592.78 (per 100,000 population), while the age-standardized DALY rate (ASDR) decreased from 31.92 to 30.90 (per 100,000 population). North Africa and Middle East had the highest age-standardized prevalence rate (ASPR), East Asia experienced the most rapid increase in ASIR, and Caribbean exhibited the most substantial increase in ASDR. Decomposition analysis showed that the main factors driving the increase in incident cases were population growth and epidemiologic changes, whereas population aging and population growth were the main driving factors for the increase of DALYs. There was cross-country health inequality in the DALYs, which showed a decreasing trend from 2012 to 2021. However, the health inequality in incidence was not significant.ConclusionsThe burden of CLD-NAFLD continues to increase. Health policy makers must develop corresponding strategies for the primary health care of metabolic diseases.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13098-025-01932-0.Keywords: Chronic liver disease, Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, Global burden of disease, Joinpoint regression, Decomposition analysis, Health inequality

Introduction

Chronic liver disease (CLD) is an important cause of global health loss, which can be classified into viral, alcoholic, metabolic, autoimmune, and others based on its etiology [1]. In the past 20 to 30 years, with the improvement of health conditions, the awareness of alcohol hazards, and the transformation of lifestyle, the etiological composition of CLD has undergone tremendous changes, especially the increasing proportion of CLD due to non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) [2]. Although the global prevalence of NAFLD is estimated at 30% [3], people’s awareness and attention to it remain inadequate. In fact, NAFLD is an umbrella term for a continuous group of clinicopathological manifestations, including simple steatosis (non-alcoholic fatty liver, NAFL) and non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) characterized by hepatocellular damage, in which NASH can further progress to advanced liver disease, namely cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) [4]. At present, NASH has become the second leading cause of liver transplantation in the United States [5]. If left unchecked, NAFLD is likely to become the most important cause of end-stage liver disease in the coming decades, resulting in a large number of disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) and liver and cardiovascular-related deaths, putting huge pressure on the health care system. Since the term “non-alcoholic” fails to accurately characterize the disease, multiple international hepatology societies issued a consensus in June 2023 to rename NAFLD as metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) [6], aiming to enhance awareness of metabolic risk factors and facilitate early diagnosis. Studies indicate that the vast majority of NAFLD patients meet the diagnostic criteria for MASLD [7], and therefore existing research on NAFLD retains significant reference value.

However, the prevalence of NAFLD varies in different regions or countries, which is determined by race, gene, lifestyle, economic level, medical conditions and many other factors [8, 9]. For instance, the patatin-like phospholipase domain-containing 3 (PNPLA3) rs738409 genotype, which is associated with NAFLD risk and disease severity, demonstrates significant geographical distribution differences [10, 11]. Thus, in order to facilitate regional cooperation organizations, government departments, and policymakers to fully understand the health challenges faced and provide guidance for the development of NAFLD prevention and control measures, it is necessary to analyze the burden of CLD due to NAFLD (CLD-NAFLD) at the global, regional, and national levels. In addition, as global aging accelerates and health systems must be reformed to meet the healthcare needs of older people, it is critical to quantify the impact of population aging on the burden of CLD-NAFLD.

Meta-analysis represents a traditional method for disease burden assessment, but its dependence on published literature constrains the generation of standardized and comprehensive estimates because existing research predominantly focuses on regions with abundant healthcare resources and exhibits substantial heterogeneity in diagnostic criteria and statistical models. For example, in the global NAFLD epidemiology meta-analysis by Younossi et al., prevalence could not be calculated for sub-Saharan Africa owing to insufficient primary data [3]. The Global Burden of Disease Study (GBD) is an authoritative database dedicated to disease burden [12], which has gradually attracted the attention of researchers. Therefore, this study aims to analyze the trends, decomposition, and health inequalities of CLD-NAFLD burden from 2012 to 2021 using the latest GBD 2021 dataset.

Methods

Data sources

GBD database makes use of important registration information, health administrative reports, disease surveillance and other resources, and has a specific evaluation system. More importantly, GBD is updated every year, including the inclusion of new datasets and the optimization of methodology. The present study was based on data obtained from GBD 2021 (published in 2024), which systematically estimated 288 causes of mortality, 371 diseases and injuries, and 88 risk factors across 204 countries and territories [12–14]. Detailed GBD methodologies have been published previously [12]. According to geographical proximity, the world was divided into 21 regions, such as Western Europe, East Asia, and Southern Latin America. Additionally, the 204 countries and territories were divided into five levels based on the socio-demographic index (SDI) quintile (high, high-middle, middle, low-middle, and low) [15]. The SDI is a composite indicator of development status and is highly correlated with health outcomes [15].

Although NAFLD has been officially renamed to MASLD, the naming of NAFLD is still used in this study because the nomenclature has not been changed in GBD database. The burden of CLD-NAFLD in this study included NAFL, NASH, compensated cirrhosis, and decompensated cirrhosis. Numbers, crude rates, and age-standardized rates (ASRs) of prevalence, incidence, and DALY related to CLD-NAFLD from 2012 to 2021 were downloaded from the Global Health Data Exchange (GHDx) website [16]. The data were publicly available and did not require informed patient consent or ethical approval.

Data analysis

ASR is an important index to effectively eliminate the influence of different age structures in different regions or different periods in the same region. In this study, age-standardized prevalence rate (ASPR), age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR), and age-standardized DALY rate (ASDR) were extracted from GBD 2021 to describe the distribution and trend of CLD-NAFLD burden. Each ASR was reported per 100,000 population and the corresponding 95% uncertainty interval (UI) was also provided. Moreover, the average annual percentage change (AAPC) of ASIR/ASDR was calculated by Joinpoint regression program (version 4.9.1.0) to quantify the temporal trend of CLD-NAFLD. The calculation process of AAPC was reported in the previous study [17]. The trend is considered to increase (or decrease) when the AAPC value is greater than 0 (or less than 0) and statistically significant.

To further explore the driving factors of the incidence/DALYs change of CLD-NAFLD from 2012 to 2021, we conducted the decomposition analysis method proposed by Das Gupta [18]. The total change was decomposed into three drivers: population aging, population growth, and epidemiologic changes. The detailed process of decomposition analysis is described in Supplementary Methods. Through decomposition analysis, the effect of each component on the total change could be quantified. In order to investigate the trends of cross-country health inequalities, slope index and concentration index, two complex measures of inequality recommended by the World Health Organization (WHO) were employed [19]. The calculation principles of these two indices are provided in Supplementary Methods.

All data processing, analysis, and graphics in this study were performed using R program (version 4.3.0). A two-sided P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Burden of CLD-NAFLD in 2021

Globally, the prevalent cases of CLD-NAFLD reached 1,267.82 (95% UI: 1,157.88-1,380.38) million in 2021, with an ASPR of 15,017.46 (95% UI: 13,755.84-16,360.80) per 100,000 population. At the regional level, North Africa and Middle East had the highest ASPR of CLD-NAFLD, up to 27,686.02 (95% UI: 25,586.19-29,913.98) per 100,000 population, followed by Central Latin America and Tropical Latin America. The lowest ASPR was observed in High-income Asia Pacific [8,884.50 (95% UI: 8,147.34- 9,665.43) per 100,000 population] (Table S1). Among 204 countries and territories, Kuwait, Egypt, and Qatar were the three countries with the highest ASPR of CLD-NAFLD, while Japan, Finland, and Canada were the three lowest (Fig. 1, Table S1).

In 2021, the global DALYs owing to CLD-NAFLD was 2.67 (95% UI: 1.90–3.60) million. The region with the highest ASDR of CLD-NAFLD was Andean Latin America [134.37 (95% UI: 90.34-191.39) per 100,000 population], followed by Central Latin America and Eastern Europe. East Asia exhibited the lowest ASDR [7.74 (95% UI: 5.10-11.02) per 100,000 population] (Table 1). At the national level, ASDR of CLD-NAFLD ranged from 4.19 to 194.58 per 100,000 population, with the highest in Mexico and the lowest in Singapore (Table S2).

Trends in the incidence and DALYs of CLD-NAFLD from 2012 to 2021

During 2012–2021, the global ASIR of CLD-NAFLD increased from 551.52 per 100,000 population to 592.78 per 100,000 population (AAPC = 0.81, 95% CI = 0.74–0.87). Among the 21 GBD regions, ASIR of CLD-NAFLD remained stable only in High-income Asia Pacific, and increased in all other regions. East Asia experienced the most rapid increase in ASIR, with an AAPC of 1.17 (95% CI: 1.03–1.31) (Table 2). In addition, ASIR of CLD-NAFLD in the five regions divided by SDI showed an increasing trend, among which AAPC of the high-middle SDI region was the largest (AAPC = 1.07, 95% CI = 0.98–1.17). For 204 countries and territories, a total of 195 (95.59%) experienced an increase in the ASIR of CLD-NAFLD from 2012 to 2021, while 7 (3.43%) remained stable and 2 (0.98%) demonstrated a decreasing trend. Notably, the most significant increase in ASIR was observed in China, with an AAPC of 1.20 (95% CI: 1.05–1.34), and the most remarkable decline was observed in the Republic of Korea, with an AAPC of -1.36 (95% CI: -1.85 to -0.87) (Fig. 2 and Table S3).

In terms of DALYs, the global ASDR of CLD-NAFLD decreased from 31.92 per 100,000 population to 30.90 per 100,000 population (AAPC=-0.37, 95% CI=-0.49 to -0.24) during 2012–2021. At the regional level, most regions showed a downward trend or remained stable. The most substantial increase in the ASDR of CLD-NAFLD was found in Caribbean, with an AAPC of 1.66 (95% CI: 1.32-2.00), and High-income Asia Pacific demonstrated the most rapid decline, with an AAPC of -2.78 (95% CI: -3.11 to -2.45). Among the five regions divided by SDI, only the middle SDI region showed an increasing trend (AAPC = 0.64, 95% CI = 0.53–0.74) (Table 1). At the national level, a total of 70 (34.31%) countries and territories experienced an increase in the ASDR of CLD-NAFLD from 2012 to 2021, while 47 (23.04%) remained stable and 87 (42.65%) demonstrated a decreasing trend. It is worth noting that the most significant increase in ASDR was observed in Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, with an AAPC of 4.59 (95% CI: 3.66–5.52), and the most remarkable decline was observed in Armenia, with an AAPC of -6.11 (95% CI: -9.53 to -2.57) (Table S2).

Sex and age patterns in the global burden of CLD-NAFLD

The incident cases, ASIR, DALYs, and ASDR of CLD-NAFLD in males were higher than those in females from 2012 to 2021. However, there was no significant gender difference in the temporal trends of ASIR and ASDR (Fig. 3A, B). After age stratification of the 2021 data, it was evident that the peak incidence rate was 20–24 years, and the incidence rate of females after the age of 45 was higher than that of males (Fig. 3C). In addition, it was noteworthy that the DALY rate of females after the age of 65 was higher than that of males (Fig. 3D).

Decomposition analysis of changes in incident cases and DALYs

From 2012 to 2021, the incident cases of CLD-NAFLD increased globally, especially in the middle SDI and low-middle SDI regions. And it increased in most of the 21 GBD regions, except for Eastern Europe and Central Europe. Globally, 68.72% of the incident cases’ increase was attributed to population growth, and 42.3% was attributed to epidemiologic changes. At the regional level, the increase in incident cases was also mainly driven by population growth and epidemiologic changes, but the proportion of epidemiologic changes was particularly prominent in East Asia (Fig. 4A and Table S4).

DALYs: disability-adjusted life years; CLD-NAFLD: chronic liver disease due to non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. Although the DALYs of CLD-NAFLD also increased globally, the contribution of epidemiologic changes to overall DALYs changes was negative (-19.02%), while population growth (68.32%) and population aging (50.70%) played a relatively significant role. Among the 21 GBD regions, the dominant factors in most regions with increased DALYs were population growth and population aging, whereas the main driver in regions with decreased DALYs was epidemiologic changes. However, epidemiologic changes were an important factor driving the increase of DALYs in Central Latin America, high-income North America, Central Europe, and the Caribbean. In addition, population aging played an absolutely dominant role in driving the growth of DALYs in East Asia and Eastern Europe (Fig. 4B and Table S5).

Cross-country health inequality analysis

In terms of incidence, whether in 2012 or 2021, the 95% CIs of the slope index and concentration index contained 0 (Fig. 5A, B), demonstrating that SDI-based, cross-country inequality in the incidence of CLD-NAFLD was not significant. As for DALYs, during 2012–2021, the slope index decreased from 36.55 (95% CI: 25.95–47.15) to 35.55 (95% CI: 24.22–46.88), and the concentration index decreased from 0.19 (95% CI: 0.11–0.27) to 0.17 (95% CI: 0.09–0.25) (Fig. 5C, D). Therefore, there was cross-country inequality in the DALYs of CLD-NAFLD, and the DALYs was more concentrated among high-SDI countries, but this health inequality had a decreasing trend.

Discussion

In the present study, we analyzed the global, regional, and national burden of CLD-NAFLD from 2012 to 2021 using GBD 2021 dataset.

The global ASPR of CLD-NAFLD increased from 13,926.36 per 100,000 population to 15,017.46 per 100,000 population in 10 years. In addition to the promotion of metabolic risk factors such as high-calorie diet and sedentary behavior, the increasing attention to NAFLD and the application of non-invasive diagnostic techniques (such as transient elastography) are also important reasons for the increase in the cases of CLD-NAFLD [20]. It is worth noting that the ASPR of CLD-NAFLD in North Africa and Middle East was much higher than that in other regions, and continued to grow, which is consistent with the high prevalence of obesity and type 2 diabetes in this region [21, 22]. As for the incidence of CLD-NAFLD, most countries or regions have shown an increasing trend, especially China in East Asia and India in South Asia, which are the two countries with the highest AAPC of ASIR. At present, India is in the throes of rapid economic and social changes. The nutritional transformation from traditional diet to modern diet and the reduction of physical activity brought about by urbanization are important factors leading to the rapid increase in the incidence of CLD-NAFLD [23]. In terms of DALYs, there was a decreasing trend in global ASDR of CLD-NAFLD from 2012 to 2021, which is closely related to the strengthening of NAFLD surveillance and management measures worldwide, especially the follow-up of NASH and cirrhosis. Among the 21 GBD regions, Andean Latin America and Central Latin America had the heaviest burden, rather than North Africa and Middle East, where ASPR was the highest. We consider that there are several reasons for the asynchrony between the highest prevalence and the highest DALYs. Firstly, given the disease duration and comorbidities of NAFLD, regions with longer life expectancy and severe population aging would correspondingly have a higher DALYs. Secondly, the proportion of PNPLA3 rs738409 genotype is higher in Andean Latin America and Central Latin America (such as Mexico) [9]. Thirdly, many countries in North Africa and Middle East lack large-scale epidemiological survey data of NAFLD, and the prevalence of viral hepatitis in this region is still high. Due to the limitations of GBD assessment methods, the estimation results may be biased [24].

After stratifying the burden of CLD-NAFLD by age and gender, it was found that the ASIR and ASDR were higher in men than in women, but the incidence rate and DALY rate for postmenopausal women surpassed those of men of the same age. Potential explanations for this gender difference are multifaceted but fall broadly into two categories: physiological and sociocultural factors [25]. Most studies have focused on the influence of physiological factors on gender differences in NAFLD, especially the potential protective effect of estrogen on NAFLD. The specific mechanisms are not fully understood, but estrogen has been observed to improve insulin sensitivity in adipose tissue and skeletal muscle [26], inhibit hepatic stellate cell activation and liver fibrosis [27]. In addition, there are some differences in the immune response and gut microbiota between males and females [28, 29]. From the perspective of disease progression, males have a higher risk of cardiovascular and liver complications and worse survival [30]. There are few studies on the gender differences caused by sociocultural factors. Based on existing literature, some researchers concluded that females have healthier eating habits than males, such as a higher proportion of vegetables and fruits [31]. In view of the above phenomenon, gender characteristics should be thoroughly considered in the development of preventive and health care strategies. For example, the screening of NAFLD and the management of comorbidities should be intensified in postmenopausal women.

Decomposition analysis showed that population aging and population growth were the main driving factors for the increase of DALYs in CLD-NAFLD. We are concerned that population aging has played a prominent role in promoting the growth of DALYs in East Asia, which is closely related to the severe aging situation in China [32]. In order to cope with the increasing burden of CLD-NAFLD and realize healthy aging, China should vigorously develop general medicine and geriatrics, optimize primary medical services, and achieve co-management of metabolic diseases. In health inequality analysis, we observed cross-country inequalities in the DALYs of CLD-NAFLD, which showed a decreasing trend from 2012 to 2021. But based on the analysis above, it is reasonable to speculate that this downward trend is caused by the rapid increase in the DALYs of CLD-NAFLD in countries with lower SDI, as the DALYs in those countries with higher SDI is also rising. Therefore, this falling inequality, it seems, is not good news. In terms of incidence, we did not find significant cross-country inequalities based on SDI, indicating that the prevention of NAFLD is still a thorny problem facing the world. At different SDI levels, there are some model countries with lower incidence or slower growth, and their experience in advocating a healthy lifestyle, creating a healthy environment, and maximizing the use of health resources is worth learning from other countries with the same SDI level.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first CLD-NAFLD burden study based on GBD 2021. Of course, there are some limitations of the present study. First of all, similar to other GBD studies, the reliability of the results depends on the accuracy of the original data, while economically underdeveloped regions (such as some countries in Africa) may suffer from serious misdiagnosis or missed diagnosis due to the limitation of health resources. Secondly, burden of cirrhosis was not reported separately in GBD 2021, so we were not able to further analyze the burden of cirrhosis caused by NAFLD. Finally, the burden of CLD varies by ethnicity, but the GBD database lacks race-related information. In the future, with the continuous strengthening of global monitoring of NAFLD and the updating and optimization of GBD database, the GBD research on CLD-NAFLD will be more meticulous and in-depth, so as to provide a more solid theoretical basis for the formulation of public health policies.

Conclusions

The burden of CLD-NAFLD continues to increase. Health policy makers must develop corresponding strategies for the primary health care of metabolic diseases.

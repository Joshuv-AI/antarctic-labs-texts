The Role of the Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor in Kidney Tubulointerstitial Fibrosis
Authors: Raymond C Harris, Ming-zhi Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryKidney fibrosis is a common cause of chronic kidney disease. Experimental studies have demonstrated a role for the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) signaling pathway in mediating the development and progression of kidney fibrosis. Deletion of Rhbdf2 (iRhom2), a member of the rhomboid family that regulates A disintegrin and metalloproteinase domain 17-mediated release of membrane-anchored proteins, including EGFR ligands, inhibited kidney interstitial fibrosis, which was accompanied by decreased EGFR activation in interstitial fibroblasts/myofibroblasts. In addition, overexpression of another EGFR ligand, heparin-binding epidermal growth factor-like growth factor, induced interstitial fibrosis in kidneys. Although EGFR activation did not induce myofibroblast transformation, it was necessary for the initial pericyte/fibroblast migration and proliferation prior to subsequent myofibroblast transformation by transforming growth factor beta or other profibrotic factors. Therefore, EGFR activation in kidney fibroblasts and pericytes serves as a specific initiator of interstitial fibrosis in response to kidney injury by stimulating pericyte/fibroblast migration and proliferation. These findings may also provide insight into the development of fibrosis in other organs and in other conditions.Keywords: Myofibroblast, pericyte, EGFR, HB-EGF, amphiregulin

Fibrosis is a common pathologic feature of many diseases in solid organs, including kidney diseases, and causes failure of organs as a result of scarring, thickening of tissue, and destruction of organ structure.1,2 A variety of clinical conditions can result in organ fibrosis. Recent studies indicate that following acute kidney injury (AKI), PDGFRα-/PDGFRβ+ (pericytes) and PDGFRα+/PDGFRβ+ (renal fibroblasts) interstitial cells migrate and proliferate before ultimately differentiating into myofibroblasts, which are the major source of increased extracellular matrix (ECM).3,4 Continued inflammatory injury can lead to persistent numbers of myofibroblasts that mediate development of tubulointerstitial fibrosis.5 It is known that transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) is a major factor inducing myofibroblast transformation, at least in vitro, but mechanisms that may serve to limit myofibroblast-induced tubulointerstitial fibrosis are less well understood. Currently, there are no specific treatments for tubulointerstitial fibrosis, so there is a need to understand the underlying mechanisms in the development of fibrosis in order to develop potential therapeutic strategies.

The epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) is a member of the erythroblastic oncogene B (ErbB) family of membrane-associated intrinsic tyrosine kinase receptors, which includes not only EGFR (ErbB1) but also ErbB2, ErbB3, and ErbB4. EGFR is activated by a number of ligands, including epidermal growth factor (EGF), transforming growth factor alpha (TGF-α), heparin-binding epidermal growth factor-like growth factor (HB-EGF), amphiregulin, betacellulin, epigen, and epiregulin, as well as non-ligand-mediated processes (Table 1).6 These EGFR ligands are expressed as membrane-associated proligands, and the soluble active ligands are released by metalloproteinase activity, most commonly A disintegrin and metalloproteinase domain 17/tumor necrosis factor alpha-converting enzyme (ADAM17/TACE). Ligand binding to EGFR initiates activation of its intrinsic kinase domain and subsequent phosphorylation of specific tyrosine residues within the cytoplasmic tail. These phosphorylated residues can serve as docking sites for signaling molecules and the activation of intracellular pathways controlling cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis.7 EGFR is widely expressed in kidney cells, including podocytes, glomerular endothelial cells, mesangial cells, proximal tubules, thick ascending limbs, distal convoluted tubules, and collecting duct cells.8 It is also expressed in cells in the interstitium (both cortical and medullary interstitial cells).

Although regulated EGFR activation in response to AKI has been shown to be important for effective epithelial cell regeneration, if there is incomplete recovery, persistent and dysregulated renal EGFR activation can lead to development of tubulointerstitial fibrosis.9–11 We reported an important role for kidney proximal tubule EGFR activation in the development of epithelial dedifferentiation and progressive tubulointerstitial injury in response to chronic angiotensin II (Ang II) administration.9 Persistently activated EGFR served as a scaffold for Shc/Grb2-mediated ERK activation. Inhibition of EGFR signaling with the specific EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor erlotinib or targeted proximal tubule deletion of EGFR expression significantly decreased both the extent of proximal tubule dedifferentiation and the tubulointerstitial fibrosis resulting from chronic Ang II administration.

There is increasing evidence that the renal tubules, and specifically the proximal tubules, are a target for diabetes and mediate the increased tubulointerstitial injury seen in diabetic nephropathy.12 Diabetes-induced increases in expression of both EGFR and its ligands and subsequent EGFR activation have previously been reported in both in vivo models and cultured renal proximal tubule epithelial cells.13,14 Proximal tubule hypertrophy is an early response seen with the onset of diabetes, and in a streptozotocin (STZ) model of type 1 diabetes, either erlotinib or selective proximal tubule deletion of EGFR expression reduced this early kidney hypertrophy.

The Hippo signaling pathway is a kinase cascade activated in mammals in response to different extracellular cues, such as apicobasal cell polarity, cell-cell interaction, NF2/Merlin, mechanotransduction, and activation of some G protein-coupled receptors.15 Activation of the Hippo signaling pathway causes mammalian sterile 20-like kinase 1/2 kinases to associate with their scaffolding partner SAV and phosphorylate large tumor suppressor kinase 1/2 and MOB1, resulting in increased LATS/MOB1 complex formation and large tumor suppressor kinase 1/2 activation, thereby leading to phosphorylation of downstream effectors Yes-associated protein (YAP) and WW domain-containing protein, the transcriptional coactivator with PDZ-binding motif (TAZ), at specific serine/threonine residues. This phosphorylation results in inactivation of YAP/TAZ by cytoplasmic sequestration and/or proteasome-mediated degradation. Reduction in serine phosphorylation by inactivation of the Hippo pathway or increased tyrosine phosphorylation by Src family kinases results in YAP/TAZ nuclear translocation and accumulation, thereby activating downstream target gene expression as a transcriptional coactivator by interacting with transcription factors such as transcriptional enhanced associate domain (TEAD) family members.16 Genome-wide analyses of YAP and TAZ transcriptional targets have identified many important target genes, such as connecting tissue growth factor (CTGF), amphiregulin, CYR61, ANKRD1, BIRC5, AXL, INHA, and COL8Al.15,17

YAP/TAZ activation has been implicated in lung, liver, skin, and solid cancer fibrogenesis,18 but it also promotes renal recovery following ischemic injury as well as cardiomyocyte regeneration and reduces cardiac fibrosis in the adult heart.19 In the kidney, YAP/TAZ activation in fibroblasts has been reported to mediate the effects of TGF-β-Smad during kidney fibrogenesis, and systemic inhibition of YAP/TAZ activation has been shown to attenuate interstitial fibrosis in mice with unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO).20 In addition, YAP activation by specific deletion of SAV1 in renal tubular epithelial cells has been reported to aggravate tubulointerstitial fibrosis in the UUO mouse model,21 but definitive mechanisms by which YAP activation in the kidney contributes to the development of kidney fibrosis have not been elucidated.

We found that YAP protein expression and phosphorylation were upregulated in diabetic mouse renal proximal tubule epithelial cells; this was abrogated in proximal tubule EGFR knockout mice (EGFRptKO) or with administration of the EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor erlotinib.22 Further studies demonstrated that EGFR/phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)/AKT signaling pathway activation mediated YAP gene expression, its nuclear translocation, and interaction with the TEAD transcription complex, thereby upregulating expression of the TEAD-dependent genes CTGF and amphiregulin.22 In a renal proximal tubule cell line, pharmacologic or genetic inhibition of EGFR or AKT blunted YAP expression in response to treatment with high amounts of glucose. In addition, knocking down YAP expression via specific small interfering RNA not only inhibited cell proliferation in response to exogenous EGF but also inhibited cell proliferation in response to treatment with high amounts of glucose. In this study, we observed decreased renal interstitial fibrosis in diabetic mice with YAP deletion specifically in the renal proximal tubule cells (YapPTiKO). Moreover, administration of verteporfin, a YAP inhibitor that acts by competitively binding with TEAD,23 effectively ameliorated diabetic kidney injury, as indicated by attenuation of both proteinuria and interstitial fibrosis. Further studies indicated that YAP activation-dependent expression of CTGF in renal proximal tubule cells and subsequent release activated fibroblast proliferation and transition to myofibroblasts, a potential mechanism for diabetic interstitial fibrogenesis.23 Our study also found that the beneficial effects of inhibition of RhoA/ROCK signaling on diabetic kidneys were mediated at least in part by inhibition of YAP activation.23 It is reported that both RhoA activation and AKT activation were downstream of EGFR activation, and they reciprocally interacted to regulate YAP activation in response to high glucose. YAP/TAZ has been implicated as a mechanosensor and a regulator of cell-cell and cell-ECM interactions.24,25 Activation of Rho/ROCK signaling is also implicated in sensing the adjacent matrix tension or forces by regulation of the actomyosin cytoskeleton.26 Excessive ECM production may enhance the stiffness of the peritubular matrix, which could further activate RhoA/ROCK signaling to form a vicious cycle of hyperglycemia-RhoA/ROCK-YAPECM-matrix stiffness, thereby driving the progression of fibrosis in diabetic nephropathy.

CTGF is a well-known target of YAP activation.27 Plasma and urinary CTGF levels are elevated in patients with diabetic nephropathy,28 and urinary and plasma CTGF levels have been suggested to be useful biomarkers of fibrogenesis in chronic kidney disease. Moreover, previous studies have suggested that genetic inhibition of CTGF expression protects against kidney injury in mouse models of both type 1 and type 2 diabetes and overexpression of CTGF worsens diabetic nephropathy.29 However, the source of plasma and urinary CTGF in diabetic patients remains unclear. Our study indicated that CTGF was strikingly upregulated in the hYAP1S127A-HEK cell and released into the conditioned medium, and silencing CTGF by small interfering RNA in the hYAP1S127A-HEK cell inhibited fibroblast proliferation and myofibroblast transition induced by the conditioned medium.23 These results suggest that YAP-dependent production and release of CTGF in diabetic renal proximal tubule cells may be a critical source of plasma and urinary CTGF in diabetic patients. However, CTGF is not the only driving force in diabetic kidney fibrosis, as inhibition of CTGF attenuates but does not halt the progression of diabetic nephropathy.29 In this regard, YAP activation upregulates other profibrotic and inflammatory factors besides CTGF, such as TGF-β2, CCL2, COL3A1, and THBS1, which may also drive diabetic interstitial fibrosis.

Our group recently determined that Dsk5 mice, which have constitutively active expression of EGFR, develop spontaneous glomerulosclerosis and tubulointerstitial nephritis.30 It is well recognized that there is sexual dimorphism in experimental animals with regard to the propensity for development of chronic renal injury. In addition, with the possible exception of diabetic nephropathy, premenopausal women have both a decreased incidence and a decreased rate of progression of chronic kidney disease.31 In experimental animals, the propensity for development of kidney injury in males has been shown to be due to the detrimental effects of testosterone rather than any protective effect. Furthermore, bodybuilders who abuse anabolic steroids have been found to be at increased risk of development of focal glomerulosclerosis and tubulointerstitial disease.32 The sex differences seen in kidney disease are undoubtedly multifactorial; however, our study study suggests that sex hormone-dependent differences in EGFR expression may play a significant role.30

Constitutively activated EGFR in male Dsk5 mice leads to progressive glomerulosclerosis and tubulointerstitial fibrosis, further confirming a detrimental role for aberrant and persistent increases in EGFR expression and activation seen in a variety of experimental models of progressive renal injury as well as in human chronic kidney disease. There is increasing evidence that continuous dysregulated EGFR activation is related to renal fibrogenesis.11,33–35 In models of tubulointerstitial fibrosis and diabetic nephropathy, administration of EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors or genetic deletion of EGFR markedly reduced kidney injury.36–38

In STZ-induced type 1 diabetes in endothelial nitric oxide synthase knockout (eNOS−/−) mice, EGFR inhibition with erlotinib attenuated the development of diabetic nephropathy at least in part by activating AMPK and inhibiting the mechanistic target of rapamycin pathway and subsequent increases in renal autophagy, as indicated by altered expression and activity of ATG12, beclin, p62, and LC3A II (hallmarks of autophagy) and decreases in endoplasmic reticulum stress (Fig. 1).36 In eNOS−/−:db/db mice, a model of accelerated type 2 diabetic nephropathy, pharmacologic (erlotinib) or genetic (waved-2, eNOS−/−:db/db, and waved-1, eNOS −/−:db/db) EGFR inhibition led to decreases in albuminuria, glomerulosclerosis, podocyte loss, renal profibrotic or fibrotic components, oxidative stress, innate and adaptive immune cell infiltration, and proinflammatory cytokines (Fig. 2).37 In addition, although selective podocyte EGFR deletion in eNOS−/−:db/db mice had no effect on body weight or fasting blood sugars, it led to relative podocyte preservation, marked reduction in albuminuria and glomerulosclerosis as well as renal proinflammatory cytokine/chemokine expression, and decreased profibrotic and fibrotic components in association with decreased podocyte expression of Rubicon and increased podocyte autophagy activity.39

Oophorectomy does not increase renal EGFR expression in wild-type mice.30 Similarly, female Dsk5 mice have decreased EGFR expression and are almost completely spared from progressive renal injury.30 By contrast, castration markedly decreases renal injury in male Dsk5 mice and testosterone increases EGFR expression and renal injury in female Dsk5 mice.30 There are no previous studies demonstrating a role for testosterone-mediated EGFR expression in the kidney, but there is a report that testosterone can increase EGFR expression in the liver.40

Aberrant EGFR activation has also been implicated in experimental models of rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis induced by anti-glomerular basement membrane serum and in puromycin aminonucleosidemediated nephropathy. EGFR was found to be activated in a model of hyperuricemic nephropathy that induced glomerulosclerosis and renal interstitial fibrosis, and injury could be ameliorated by an EGFR inhibitor.41 Renal fibrosis in an experimental model of chronic allograft nephropathy was also inhibited by EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibition.42

Recent studies have investigated signaling responses mediating the increased fibrosis mediated by EGFR. One study showed that integrin α1β1 prevented kidney fibrosis by reducing collagen production through inhibition of EGFR.43 In the absence of this interaction, EGFR activation leads to increased phosphorylation and nuclear translocation of the RNA-DNA binding protein fused in sarcoma, which induces collagen synthesis.44 Another study demonstrated an important role for forkhead box protein M1 (FOXM1) induction by EGFR in proliferation and repair of proximal tubules after acute ischemic injury.45 Although this study did not specifically examine effects in chronic kidney injury, FOXM1 inhibition has been shown to decrease UUO-induced kidney fibrosis, and persistent EGFR activation of FOXM1 in other organs leads to fibrosis.

The ligands for EGFR (EGF, TGF-α, HB-EGF, amphiregulin, betacellulin, and epiregulin) are produced as membrane-bound prohormones that are cleaved to their active, soluble form by proteolysis, predominantly by members of the ADAM family, especially ADAM17/TACE. Either pharmacologic or genetic inhibition of ADAM17/TACE has been shown to reduce renal tubulointerstitial fibrosis induced by Ang II infusion and UUO Inhibited release from proximal tubule of ADAM17-mediated EGFR ligands inhibited kidney fibrosis after severe ischemic injury.46 Furthermore, mice with selective deletion of amphiregulin in the proximal tubule develop less kidney fibrosis after kidney injury, whereas administration of soluble amphiregulin promotes tubulointerstitial fibrosis after either ischemic injury or UUO.47

We observed increased susceptibility to the development of renal injury in male mice in a model of tubulointerstitial fibrosis resulting from selective renal tubule overexpression of the EGFR ligand HB-EGF.48 We developed a novel mouse model that mitigates these variables, allowing for reproducible and spontaneous fibrosis, even on a “fibrosis-resistant” C57BL/6 background.49 Furthermore, mice with endothelial-specific HB-EGF deletion were found to have reduced Ang II-mediated endothelial dysfunction, renal fibrosis, and inflammation.50 However, these studies did not address how EGFR activation mediates increased interstitial fibrosis. Therefore, the goal of our most recent studies has been to determine the potential underlying mechanisms by which EGFR activation can mediate fibroblast activation in conditions leading to progressive kidney interstitial fibrosis.

Although TGF-β is recognized as a major driver of fibrosis, other signaling pathways responsible for initiating and maintaining chronic fibrosis are incompletely elucidated. We recently reported that EGFR activation is involved in the initiation, maintenance, and progression of renal fibrosis.49 hHB-EGFTg/Tg mice were backcrossed onto a C57BL/6 background, a strain relatively resistant to renal injury. Surprisingly, homozygous hH-BEGF expression specifically in the tubule epithelium of C57BL/6 mice was sufficient to induce fibrosis of the kidney. As early as 3 weeks, hHB-EGFTg/Tg mice had increased collagen deposition, which progressed as mice aged, up to 6 months. This spontaneous transgenic model recapitulated known hallmarks of renal fibrosis, including tubular dedifferentiation and dilation, interstitial expansion, accumulation of collagen and ECM molecules, and increased inflammation as well as myofibroblast activation, proliferation, and differentiation, providing a novel platform to investigate renal fibrosis progression. This increased fibrosis in the hHB-EGFTg/Tg mice was prevented by administration of the EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor erlotinib or by crossing with waved-2 mice, which have deficient EGFR tyrosine kinase activity (Fig. 3).49

The hHB-EGFTg/Tg model allows investigation of how renal tubule cells can directly impact the fibrotic response. Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) is a process in which epithelial cells lose their apical-basal polarity and acquire a migratory and invasive phenotype. However, the role of EMT in renal fibrosis is still debated. Transcriptional repressors of E-cadherin Snail and Slug were significantly increased in the tubule epithelium of hHB-EGFTg/Tg mice, consistent with an increase in the mesenchymal marker vimentin and a decrease in E-cadherin expression. Such findings suggest that epithelial cells undergo partial EMT without complete loss of cell-cell adhesion or the ability to gain a migratory or invasive phenotype. Collectively, these results suggest that partial EMT contributes to dedifferentiated epithelial cells that can directly relay signals to promote fibrotic progression without undergoing complete EMT and transitioning into the myofibroblast population.

Cell cycle arrest checkpoints ensure accurate DNA replication and cell division to maintain the genomic integrity of the cell. Severe or repeated insults to proximal tubule epithelial cells promote impaired or maladaptive repair responses that can alter their proliferative capacity and subsequent cell cycle profile. Phosphorylation of retinoblastoma (Rb) is necessary for cell cycle advancement. hHB-EGFTg/Tg mice had decreased p-Rb and increased activation of p53 and phosphorylation of histone H3Ser10, suggesting incomplete passage through the cell cycle. Proximal tubules of hHB-EGFTg/Tg mice had increased p-histone H3Ser10 expression compared with wild-type mice, further suggesting that epithelial cells had exited a quiescent state (G0) and progressed through the cell cycle, likely arresting at the G1 (p-p53, p-Rb, p21) or G2/M (p-p53, p-histone H3, p21) phase.

The hHB-EGFTg/Tg mice had an increase in interstitial cells positive for alpha smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), predominantly concentrated around the hHB-EGF-expressing tubules. Further, α-SMA+ myofibroblasts in hHB-EGFTg/Tg mice had increased Ki67, indicative of higher proliferative capacity, which was associated with expansion of the interstitial space and disruption of the tissue architecture. Dedifferentiated tubular epithelial cells have been demonstrated to release various chemokines and cytokines, including WNT ligands, sonic hedgehog ligands, CTGF, and TGF-β, all previously associated with fibrosis. Here we demonstrate that paracrine factors released from EGF-treated human tubular epithelial cells promote the proliferation of fibroblasts. These data reinforce the significant contribution of tubular injury to fibrosis and its role in driving fibrosis in neighboring cell types.

The rapid onset and progressive nature of renal fibrosis in the hHB-EGFTg/Tg model make it an attractive target for testing the efficacy of therapeutic agents. hHB-EGFTg/Tg mice had increased activation of EGFR and downstream signaling, such as ERK and AKT, and promoted activation of the TGF-β/Smad pathway in tubule epithelial cells.49 Previous studies demonstrated that pharmacologic or genetic inhibition of EGFR was sufficient to ameliorate angiotensin infusion-induced renal fibrosis. Further, Ang II promoted the activation of EGFR, which activated the TGF-β pathway. EGFR appears to be a central factor in the propagation of fibrotic signaling and is causal in various renal pathologies. Therefore, intervention with therapeutic approaches, including an EGFR tyrosine kinase or MEK inhibitor, attenuated fibrosis in the hHB-EGFTg/Tg mice, which have EGFR overactivation. Additionally, a genetic deficiency of EGFR tyrosine kinase activity in hHB-EGFTg/Tg mice prevented dedifferentiation and cell cycle arrest in epithelial cells. These data highlight the treatability of renal fibrosis in hHB-EGFTg/Tg mice. In summary, hHB-EGF expression in the proximal tubule cells provides a novel model for studying the mechanisms underlying renal fibrosis progression, highlights the significance of EGFR activation in the initiation and progression of renal fibrosis, and can serve as a platform to identify new targets and treatment strategies to combat fibrogenesis.45 Recently, we used this spontaneous renal fibrosis model to develop a noninvasive quantitative magnetic resonance imaging technique for quantitating kidney fibrosis.51,52

It has been suggested that initial increases in the interstitial fibroblast population are important for regeneration and recovery from AKI.53 However, incomplete recovery of kidney epithelium after acute injury and/or continued inflammatory injury can lead to persistent expression of myofibroblasts and development of tubulointerstitial fibrosis.54 The mediators of myofibroblast migration and proliferation are incompletely understood. In this regard, although TGF-β mediates myofibroblast transformation, it does not induce and may even inhibit fibroblast proliferation.1 Using genetically modified mice with selective deletion of EGFR in the pericyte/fibroblast population as well as in vitro cell culture studies and single-nucleus RNA sequencing (snRNA-seq), our studies provided evidence of a heretofore undescribed and important role for EGFR in kidney fibroblasts and pericytes as a specific initiator of interstitial fibrosis in response to kidney injury.55 These studies employed a variety of models of progressive kidney fibrosis, including UUO, ischemic kidney injury, folic acid nephropathy, and adenine nephropathy. As noted, previous studies indicated that global genetic EGFR deletion or pharmacologic blockade of kidney EGFR activation inhibited kidney fibrosis, whereas stimulating kidney EGFR activity promoted kidney fibrosis.9–11,30,46,49,56 However, these studies did not determine the mechanism by which EGFR mediated fibrosis, as EGFR activation did not directly stimulate a profibrotic program in fibroblasts or pericytes. However, we were able to determine that EGFR is expressed in kidney pericytes and resident fibroblasts, and we found significant inhibition of the number of interstitial fibroblasts/myofibroblasts and a subsequent decrease in interstitial fibrosis in response to a variety of experimental insults known to incite kidney fibrosis. Fibroblast/pericyte EGFR activation did not promote myofibroblast transformation, but instead mediated increased proliferation and migration to sites of injury. Because fibroblast EGFR activation did not itself induce myofibroblast differentiation, subsequent activation by TGF-β or other profibrotic agonists is necessary for fibroblast differentiation into myofibroblasts. However, without the crucial initial steps provided by EGFR activation, the ultimate development of tubulointerstitial fibrosis is severely impaired.

As noted, EGFR ligands exist as membrane-associated precursors that are cleaved to form their active ligands by metalloproteinases, especially ADAM17/TACE. Kefaloyianni et al46 reported that whole kidney ADAM17, EGFR, and EGFR ligands, including HB-EGF, TGF-α, amphiregulin, epigen, and epiregulin, increased following ischemic injury, consistent with the current results indicating similar progressive increases in response to UUO. ADAM17 hypomorphic mice had less kidney interstitial fibrosis after ischemic injury as well as in a UUO model. iRhoms, members of the rhomboid intramembrane protein family, are essential mediators of ADAM17 activation. In recent study, we found that deletion of iRhom2 inhibited tubulointerstitial fibrosis after ischemic injury.55

Although iRhom1 is ubiquitously expressed, iRhom2 was previously reported to be predominantly expressed in cells of myeloid lineage under basal conditions.57,58 However, we found that following kidney injury, total kidney messenger RNA expression of both iRhom1 and iRhom2 increased dramatically. We also detected iRhom2 protein expression colocalized with α-SMA in interstitial cells following UUO. In addition to association with ADAM17 at the plasma membrane, iRhom2 is necessary for the translocation of ADAM17 from the endoplasmic reticulum to the Golgi. Interestingly, in the α-SMA+ myofibroblasts, we detected the greatest iRhom2 immunofluorescence in the paranuclear region, consistent with localization to the Golgi and endoplasmic reticulum.

Strikingly, although there was no difference in immunoreactive EGFR colocalization with myofibroblasts between wild-type and iRhom2−/− mice, the colocalization of p-EGFR in myofibroblasts was significantly decreased in iRhom2−/− mice compared with wild-type mice 7 days after UUO. These findings suggested that EGFR activation in myofibroblasts was involved in the development of tubulointerstitial fibrosis in response to injury. However, because EGFR can be activated by a number of different ligands, it is likely that more than one ligand is involved. However, by taking an alternative approach to investigating the target cells, we were able to determine that direct activation of EGFR on fibroblasts mediated the observed effect of EGFR to promote tubulointerstitial fibrosis.

It is well known that EGFR activation can induce cell dedifferentiation and proliferation, and our results indicated that these properties mediate its essential function in the development of interstitial fibrosis in response to kidney injury.55 Selective deletion of EGFR in fibroblasts decreased markers of proliferation (Ki67 and EdU) in fibroblasts after UUO. In addition, the total number of myofibroblasts (α-SMA+ cells) decreased. EGFR activation increased both the proliferation and the migratory ability of cultured mouse fibroblasts and inhibited markers of fibrosis (α-SMA, collagen 1, and fibronectin). Of note, simultaneous administration of TGF-β was able to induce markers of myofibroblast differentiation, which were further increased with simultaneous administration of an EGFR inhibitor. Because TGF-β itself did not induce either fibroblast proliferation or migration, these results indicated that the initial step was activation of EGFR in PDGFRβ+ cells, leading to migration and proliferation, with subsequent inhibition of these processes and differentiation to myofibroblasts and ultimate development of tubulointerstitial fibrosis as profibrotic cytokine levels increased, the timing of which would be consistent with the time course of progressive expression of TGF-β messenger RNA observed after injury.

Selective deletion of EGFR in the PDGFRβ+ cell population inhibited fibrosis not only in the UUO model but also in response to ischemic or toxic (folic acid, adenine) injury.55 Of note, in both ischemic or toxic injury models, , the inhibition of interstitial fibrosis was not accompanied by any alteration in inflammatory cytokines, tubule injury markers, or functional recovery. These findings are consistent with previous studies by Kefaloyianni et al,46,47 which indicated that global or proximal tubule deletion of ADAM17 or proximal tubule deletion of amphiregulin did not alter functional recovery from ischemic kidney injury.

Seven distinct subclusters of fibroblasts and myofibroblasts were delineated in the UUO kidneys using snRNA-seq.55 On day 1 after UUO, the majority of these cells were still in subclusters in a relatively differentiated and quiescent state, as indicated by evidence of aerobic respiration and oxidative phosphorylation, and there were equivalent numbers of fibroblasts in the wild-type and PDGFRβ-Cre:EGFRf/f (FibEGFR−/−) mice in these subclusters. However, by day 3, the total number of fibroblasts increased markedly in wild-type kidneys but failed to increase in FibEGFR−/− kidneys, confirming the important role EGFR activation plays in the proliferation of fibroblasts as they transition to myofibroblasts. The snRNA-seq results confirmed that EGFR deletion in the PDGFRβ+ cells markedly decreased the migration and proliferation of fibroblasts but had a relatively minimal effect on the differentiation and maturation of myofibroblasts.

Gene expression of EGFR ligand family members in the PDGFRβ+ cell population was highest on day 1. Analysis of ligand-receptor interactions indicated that HB-EGF interacted with EGFR on day 3 in the subclusters associated with proliferation and migration. TGF-β gene family members had greatest enrichment in the early myofibroblast subcluster on day 1 but had persistent expression in subclusters that predominated on day 3, and they interacted with TGF-β receptors in all fibroblast and myofibroblast subtypes. While not ruling out paracrine activation, these findings suggested that HB-EGF and TGF-β may facilitate the migration, proliferation, and subsequent differentiation of fibroblasts/myofibroblasts in an autocrine manner.

In a variety of experimental models of tubulointerstitial fibrosis, these studies demonstrated the importance of EGFR activation in both kidney tubules and the PDGFRβ+ population of pericytes and fibroblasts in the development of kidney interstitial fibrosis. EGFR activation in pericytes/fibroblasts does not mediate myofibroblast transformation per se, but instead directs the initial pericyte/fibroblast migration and proliferation prior to subsequent myofibroblast transformation by TGF-β or other profibrotic factors (Fig. 4). Therefore, these studies provide new insights into our understanding of the mechanism underlying the development of interstitial fibrosis in response to kidney injury.

Ligand and receptor enrichment indicated that myofibroblasts had increased gene expression for ligands that can theoretically interact with receptors on kidney epithelium, endothelium, pericytes, and innate and adaptive immune cells. However, there was less evidence of ligands from intrinsic kidney cells interacting with receptors on the fibroblast or myofibroblast clusters on day 3 following UUO. These results help explain why EGFR deletion in pericytes/fibroblasts reduced the development of renal fibrosis but had no effect on tubular injury in response to different kidney injuries.

As a key member of the sex-determining region Y-related high-mobility group box (SOX) protein family, SOX9 is an important transcription factor with wide-spread roles in embryonic development and adult tissue homeostasis. SOX9 plays a pivotal role in control of branching morphogenesis during kidney development and is essential for the proper development of proximal tubules in mice.59 In adult mice, SOX9 expression in renal proximal epithelial cells is silenced, but it is markedly upregulated and peaks at 24–48 hours postischemic AKI. However, persistent elevation of SOX9 expression results in kidney fibrosis.60,61 The molecular mechanisms underlying SOX9 induction in response to kidney injury are not completely understood. Most recently, we reported the upregulation of SOX9 and EGFR expression in renal tubular epithelial cells of patients with acute tubular injury or early stages of chronic allograft nephropathy.62 The hHB-EGF overexpression in renal tubular epithelial cells led to increased SOX9 expression, which was abrogated when these mice were crossed with EGFR tyrosine kinase-deficient waved-2 mice. Genetic or pharmacologic inhibition of EGFR resulted in decreased SOX9 expression owing to inhibition of AKT-YAP/TAZ-TEAD signaling.

In addition to epithelial cells and interstitial fibroblasts, EGFR is expressed in macrophages.63 Macrophages, play important roles in both propagation and recovery of ischemic AKI and development of postischemia fibrosis.48 We show that myeloid deletion of EGFR promotes a pro-resolving, anti-inflammatory phenotype and increased efferocytotic capacity in macrophages in ischemic AKI. This leads to accelerated recovery in response to AKI and subsequent inhibited development of tubulointerstitial fibrosis. We further find that selective EGFR deletion in neutrophils accelerates recovery from ischemic kidney injury and reduces subsequent fibrosis. Deletion of EGFR expression in all murine myeloid cells or selectively in neutrophils decreases kidney neutrophil Mcl-1 expression and promotes neutrophil apoptosis, which is accompanied by accelerated recovery from organ injury and reduced subsequent fibrosis.64

Of interest, unlike the other ErbB family members, whose overexpression or constitutive activation mediates progression of many types of cancer, ErbB4 expression in cancer is often associated with growth suppression and improved prognosis. ErbB4 expression increases in chronic kidney disease, but in human kidney biopsy samples, ErbB4 expression levels were found to accelerate a model of murine polycystic kidney disease and inversely correlate with the degree of renal fibrosis.65 In both a UUO model and an AKI to chronic kidney disease model, deletion of ErbB4 promoted excessive renal fibrosis, which was accompanied by evidence of G2/M arrest and tubule dedifferentiation. It is also notable that there was increased YAP activation, suggesting that ErbB4 activation might serve as a counterregulator of the profibrotic actions of EGFR, although further studies will be necessary to investigate this possibility.

We found that deletion of ErbB4 promoted renal fibrosis in a mouse UUO model.65 There was a markedly earlier onset of tubulointerstitial fibrosis in ErbB4−/−ht + mice compared with wild-type mice, which was accompanied by evidence of G2/M arrest and tubule dedifferentiation and activation of YAP and p-Smad3, two putative mediators of tubulointerstitial fibrosis. Although ErbB4 deletion did not alter the increased activation of the ERK and p38 pathways following UUO, it did lead to a pronounced early decrease in activation of AKT, which is notable given the potential role of AKT in cytoprotection. Of note, the effect of ErbB4 appeared to be predominantly in nephron segments distal to the proximal tubules, which is consistent with the expression pattern of ErbB4 in the adult mouse kidney.66 ErbB4 is expressed in the adult kidney at a lower level compared with the fetal kidney, with highest expression in the loop of Henle, distal convoluted tubule, and collecting duct.66,67 Distal tubular injury, as indicated by H-FABP3 expression levels, was more severe in ErbB4 deleted obstructed kidneys compared with wild-type mice.

Prolonged tubular G2/M arrest has been shown to induce a profibrotic phenotype with upregulation of profibrogenic growth factors, including TGF-β1 and CTGF.68,69 Compared with wild-type mice, UUO kidneys with ErbB4 deletion had more tubule cells in the G2/M phase by day 3, as indicated by an increased ratio of p-histone H3 to Ki67, which may have contributed to the earlier accumulation of interstitial matrix and fibroblast activation. Intriguingly, EGFR activation also contributes to arrest of epithelial cells in the G2/M phase after chronic injury.69 Whether the absence of ErbB4 leads to more EGFR activation will be the subject of future investigation. Of note, we did not detect significantly increased cleaved caspase 3 levels in obstructed kidneys with ErbB4 deletion until day 6 after UUO, when renal fibrosis was already abundant. These results suggested that apoptosis of the tubular epithelium was not the proximate cause of fibrosis in this setting.

Elevated expression of ErbB4 can be seen in several types of chronic kidney disease, including polycystic kidney disease.70,71 Our previous studies found that ErbB4 expression was elevated in the cystic tubular epithelium of cpk mice, a murine autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease model, and ErbB4 deletion accelerated renal function decline in association with the development of pericystic fibrosis.71 More recently, it has been reported that neuregulin, an ErbB4 ligand, attenuated the development of nephropathy in a type 1 diabetes mouse model through activation of an ErbB4 signaling pathway, which prevented glomerular matrix formation and glomerulosclerosis.72 These studies are consistent with genome-wide association studies of type 1 diabetes-associated nephropathy that provided suggestive but not conclusive evidence (odds ratio, 0.66, P = 2.1 × 10−7) that an intronic single nucleotide polymorphism in ErbB4 might somehow be involved in the progression of diabetic nephropathy.73

The ErbB4 receptor can be proteolytically cleaved at a juxtamembrane segment, with release of intracellular domain of two isoforms (CYT-1 and CYT-2) and CYT-1 is degraded through ubiquitination (CYT-1) and CYT-2 is translocated to the nucleus and mediate transcriptional activity.74,75 ErbB4 CYT-1, which is expressed in the kidney,67 possesses a PI3K binding domain in its C-terminus. Activation of ErbB4 initiates a PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, phosphorylating YAP at serine 127, leading to its binding to 14-3-3 and staying in the cytoplasm as an inactive form. Furthermore, nuclear YAP and TAZ are transcription cofactors that promote TGF-β signaling via retention of activated Smad2/3 in the nucleus.20,66,67 In the present studies, ErbB4 deletion led to decreased AKT activation and increased nuclear YAP and p-Smad3 expression, suggesting that decreased AKT activation may have mediated increased YAP and TGF-β activation as well as promotion of tubulointerstitial fibrosis. However, it has also been reported that the PI3K/AKT pathway contributes to renal fibrogenesis through promotion of cell proliferation and interstitial ECM deposition,76,77 so further studies will be necessary to determine the role of AKT activation following UUO. Nevertheless, these studies make it clear that ErbB4 deletion accelerates the development and progression of renal fibrosis in obstructive nephropathy. The increased expression of ErbB4 in kidneys with chronic injury may actually reflect a compensatory effect to prevent the development of tubulointerstitial injury.

In summary, the ErbB receptor family have diverse effects on kidney pathophysiology. Although expression of ErbB1 (EGFR) is involved in renal epithelial recovery from acute injury, persistent activation can lead to the development of tubulointerstitial fibrosis. Conversely, expression of ErbB4, another member of this receptor family, appears to ameliorate the development of chronic kidney fibrosis.

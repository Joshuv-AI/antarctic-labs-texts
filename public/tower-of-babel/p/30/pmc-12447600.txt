Burden of bacterial antimicrobial resistance 1990–2021 in China: a systematic analysis for the global burden of disease study 2021
Authors: Yifei Chen, Jinxi Li, Yanhong Gong, Xiaoxv Yin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundChina is confronted with a severe burden of antimicrobial resistance (AMR), and to date, there has been no comprehensive estimate of the historical trend of the AMR burden in China.MethodsData on AMR were collected by the Global Antimicrobial Resistance Burden study 1990–2021. Logistic regression, polynomial models, stacked ensemble models, spatiotemporal Gaussian regression models, and mixed-effects meta-regression models are incorporated into the modeling framework to fit the burden of AMR and estimate the number of deaths and disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs) attributable to and associated with AMR in two counterfactual scenarios.ResultsIn 2021, it was estimated that 711,852 (95% uncertainty interval [UI] (586,447–837,256) deaths were associated with bacterial AMR in China, including 160,268 (132,375–188,160) deaths attributable to bacterial AMR. From 1990 to 2021, the number of deaths attributable to AMR among children under 5 years old declined by 95%, while that among the elderly aged 65 and above increased by 68%. Among the 12 infectious syndromes, the top three with the largest number of deaths attributed to AMR in 2021 were bloodstream infections (74,119), lower respiratory tract infections (60,839), and peritoneal and intra-abdominal infections (11,827). Among them, bloodstream infections were the infectious syndrome with the greatest increase in the number of deaths during the study period. In 2021, the pathogen with the highest number of deaths attributed to AMR was Staphylococcus aureus (39,643), and the one with the greatest increase in attributed deaths was Staphylococcus aureus, with an increase of 20,774 (16,880–24,668). In 2021, the pathogen–drug combination causing the highest number of AMR-attributed deaths was methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (26,023), and the pathogen with the highest annualized rate of change in attributed deaths over 31 years was third-generation cephalosporin-resistant Acinetobacter baumannii (17.5%).ConclusionsBacterial antimicrobial resistance in China was a serious threat to public health, especially for elderly people. Meanwhile, intervention measures such as popularizing vaccination, reducing the misuse of antibiotics, and developing new antibiotics should be combined to address this significant health threat.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12916-025-04333-2.

Background

“Combat drug resistance” is one of the 10 issues to track that World Health Organization (WHO) has identified in 2021 [1]. The Review on Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR) predicted that AMR could cause 10 million deaths by 2050 [2]. Moreover, a study indicated that there were 4.71 million deaths associated with AMR worldwide in 2021, including 1.14 million deaths attributable to AMR [3].

China has been one of the world’s largest consumers of antibiotics, with reported average usage levels significantly higher than those in high-income countries [4]. According to the latest research findings, the country also faces serious burden of AMR [5]. In response to the substantial burden of AMR, China has proactively engaged with the Global Action Plan on Antimicrobial Resistance adopted by WHO [6]. In 2022, China issued the National Action Plan on Antimicrobial Resistance (2022–2025) [7], which calls for comprehensive control of the AMR. This has led to a decline in both the clinical usage rates and intensity of antibiotic use in China, resulting in reduced AMR burden for certain pathogens [8]. For example, the detection rate of third-generation cephalosporin-resistant Escherichia coli decreased by 10.8% [8]. However, according to the China Antimicrobial Surveillance Network (CHINET) 2024 report, the detection rate of carbapenem-resistant gram-negative bacteria (CRGNB) remains high, and the detection rate of methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) has not shown significant decrease from 2022 to 2024, remaining around 30% [9]. This suggests that the burden of antimicrobial resistance in China remains severe and requires more comprehensive data-based solutions.

While AMR is widely recognized as a major public health threat in China, comprehensive and nationally representative studies which estimate its burden remain limited. The most recent and comprehensive assessment of the AMR burden was conducted in 2019 [5]. The study estimated that China had 602,561 (95% uncertainty interval [UI] 378,084–928,696) deaths associated with AMR and 145,465 (89,593–222,609) deaths attributable to AMR in 2019. However, this study evaluated only the cross-sectional burden, limiting the understanding of trends of AMR burden and the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on AMR. Understanding the trends of AMR is crucial for guiding the antimicrobial stewardship. Trend analysis helps identify new threats, evaluate past control measures, and predict future disease burdens, which is important for improving antibiotic use, infection control, and prioritizing drug and vaccine development. There have been several studies focusing on trends in AMR burden in China [10–12]. While these studies provided detailed insights into resistance trends for specific pathogens, the scope of pathogens and pathogen–drug combinations analyzed was limited, and they did not incorporate key metrics such as mortality rates, death counts, or disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs) to quantify the AMR burden. As a result, these studies were insufficient for assessing national-level trends in AMR burden. For example, a study by Qin et al. reported the prevalence of resistance and temporal trends for 7 main types of gram-negative bacilli and gram-positive cocci in China from 2005 to 2022 [10]. However, it did not incorporate key disease burden metrics such as mortality or DALYs and covered only a limited range of pathogens.

Given the growing threat of AMR to public health and healthcare systems in China, there is an urgent need for comprehensive and nationally representative estimates of AMR burden. We conducted the first nationwide assessment of the AMR burden in China from 1990 to 2021, covering 12 infection syndromes and 22 pathogens, using two counterfactual scenarios based on established methods from previous studies [3]. This study supports efforts to provide data-driven evidence to guide policy development, prioritize interventions, and optimize resource allocation for strengthening AMR surveillance and control strategies.

Methods

Overview and data sources

In this study, we used a subset of input data describing China from the Global Antimicrobial Resistance Burden (GARB) study to estimate the burden of infectious diseases and bacterial antimicrobial resistance [3]. The data input sources could be found at cloud files of Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME), and the website is https://cloud.ihme.washington.edu/s/Y72odgWKbjRMbLE [13]. The global overall input data are from 520 million sample size and 19,513 number of study-location-years. A total of 93 inputted data, including 8 administrative data, 1 epi surveillance, 82 scientific literatures, and 2 surveys, were available for mainland China (Additional file 1: Table S1). The input data can be categorized into the 10 types: multiple causes of death and vital registration, hospital discharge, microbial data with outcome, microbial data without outcome, literature studies, single drug–resistance profiles, pharmaceutical sales and antibiotic use, antibiotic use among children younger than 5 years with reported illness, mortality surveillance, hospital-linked cause of death records data (linkage). The data for this study were obtained from a publicly available database and did not require ethical review or informed consent. We used Python and R for data processing. The detailed code can be accessed at https://github.com/ihmeuw/AMR-Time-Series-and-Forecasts-2024.

Estimation steps

Detailed methods on the estimation process for infectious syndromes and AMR have been published previously [3], and Additional file 1 also includes a comprehensive description (Additional file 1: Estimation steps) [3, 14–35]. The estimation consisted of 5 steps.

We separately estimated the number and proportion of deaths from sepsis and other infection syndromes, case fatality rates and pathogen distributions for each pathogen, prevalence of antimicrobial resistance for each pathogen–drug combination, and the relative risk associated with resistant infections compared to drug-sensitive infections. Finally, we constructed two counterfactual scenarios and applied a multiplicative approach to estimate the AMR burden in China. The analysis focused on 12 infection syndromes, 22 pathogens, and 84 pathogen–drug combinations. The detailed list of infection syndromes and pathogens could be found at Additional file 1 (Additional file 1: Tables S2–S4).

Estimation step 3: prevalence of resistance by pathogen–drug combination

This study selected 84 pathogen–drug combinations to quantify the proportion of AMR for each pairing. A two-stage spatiotemporal modeling framework was employed to model the estimates. Firstly, we selected a range of covariates with biologically plausible associations to the prevalence of AMR and used a stacked ensemble model to fit the associations between selected covariates and data because of its ability to handle complex spatiotemporal data and improve prediction accuracy. Secondly, we applied these ensemble estimates to a spatiotemporal Gaussian process regression framework, which was tailored for each pathogen–drug combination to smooth them in time and space. Additionally, national antibiotic consumption metrics were integrated as explanatory variables in the stacked ensemble models for the prevalence of resistance. This study adopted the methodology of Annie J. Browne’s study which applied the regression trees (CART) method to the collected data in order to impute the missing values of antibiotic consumption for the period 1990–2021 in China [33].

Estimation step 4: relative risk of death for drug-resistant infection compared with drug-sensitive infections

This study employed a two-stage modeling framework combining mixed-effects binomial logistic regression and mixed-effects meta-regression to calculate the relative mortality risk for each pathogen–drug combination. To quantify the excess risk of non-fatal outcomes, a comparable approach was applied using a two-stage nested mixed-effects meta-regression model, which estimated the relative increase in extended hospital stays for resistant infections compared to sensitive infections. The first stage model is used to generate a priori information for the second stage model.

For pathogens with mutually exclusive resistance profiles, population attributable fractions (PAFs) were computed for each resistance spectrum, including cases where resistance occurred to at least one antimicrobial agent. This approach enabled the assessment of the AMR burden across diverse pathogen–drug combinations within a single species.

Estimation step 5: computing burden attributable to drug resistance and burden associated with drug-resistant infections

We calculated two counterfactuals to estimate drug resistance burden: (1) all drug-resistant infections are replaced by susceptible infections; (2) all drug-resistant infections are replaced by no infections. DALYs are the sum of years of life lost (YLLs) and years lived with disability (YLDs).

Uncertainty analysis

Uncertainty was propagated through integration of 100 draws for each combination of location, sex, age group, and year across all analytical stages. These samples were aggregated into final calculation of estimates of drug resistance–associated deaths/infections and resistance-attributable deaths/infections to propagate uncertainty throughout. To quantify uncertainty, 95% confidence intervals were calculated as a standard deviation of 1.96 below and above the mean value.

Results

Sepsis burden in 1990–2021

From 1990 to 2021, the sepsis burden shows a trend of initial decline followed by an increase, but with large differences by age group. In 1990, we estimated that there were 1,857,771 deaths with sepsis in China (95% UI 1,679,707–2,034,012), which decreased to 1,242,374 (1,129,696–1,354,998) deaths in 2012, and then increased to 1,348,429 (1,126,684–1,570,173) deaths in 2019 and 1,400,419 (1,156,101–1,644,737) deaths in 2021 (Fig. 1). In children under 5 years, sepsis deaths have decreased from 665,098 (557,043–773,153) in 1990 to 30,250 (24,036–36,463) in 2021, a decline of more than 95% over the past 31 years. In people aged 5 and over, however, deaths with sepsis increased by 15% over the study period, from 1,192,673 (1,047,303–1,338,043) in 1990 to 1,370,169 (1,124,951–1,615,387) in 2021 (Additional file 1: Table S5).Fig. 1China time trend of sepsis deaths (counts), by age, 1990–2021. AMR burden in 1990–2021

From 1990 to 2021, the change in the number of AMR deaths in China was modest, but the proportion of AMR deaths as a percentage of sepsis deaths increased. In 1990, it was estimated that 164,317 (95% UI 127,009–201,626) deaths were attributable to AMR in China, and 718,647 (583,250–854,044) deaths associated with AMR. In 2019, deaths attributable to AMR decreased to 159,390 (131,928–186,852), and AMR-associated deaths decreased to 692,245 (573,159–811,311). In 2021, 160,268 (132,375–188,160) deaths were attributable to AMR and 711,852 (586,447–837,256) deaths were associated with AMR in 2021, which increased slightly in both 2019 estimates. Additional file 1: Figures S1–S4 show the trends of AMR burden in China. In China, the all-age mortality rate attributable to AMR decreased from 14.0 deaths (10.8–17.1) per 100,000 in 1990 to 11.3 deaths (9.3–13.2) per 100,000 people in both 2019 and 2021 (Additional file 1: Table S1). However, the percentage of deaths with sepsis that are associated with AMR increased from 38.7% (34.7–42.0) in 1990 to 51.3% (50.9–51.7) in 2019 and then decreased to 50.8% (41.9–59.8) in 2021. Additionally, the percentage of deaths with sepsis that are attributable to AMR increased from 8.8% (7.6–9.9) in 1990 to 11.8% (11.7–11.9) in 2019 and decreased to 11.4% (9.5–13.4) in 2021 (Additional file 1: Table S5).

The number of DALYs attributable to AMR in China decreased from 7,776,301 (95% UI 5,904,912–9,647,690) in 1990 to 3,777,893 (3,119,612–4,436,173) in 2021. The number of DALYs associated with AMR decreased from 35,115,606 (27,921,168–42,310,043) in 1990 to 16,689,500 (13,814,904–19,564,097) in 2021 as well. Similarly, the all-age rate of DALYs attributable to AMR in China decreased from 661.0 (501.9–820.1) per 100,000 population in 2019 to 265.5 (219.3–311.8) per 100,000 population in 2021. Additional file 1: Table S6 provides estimates of the number and all-age rates of DALYs for two counterfactual scenarios in China in 1990, 2019, and 2021.

Burden of AMR by infectious syndromes

Between 1990 and 2021, there was a decline in deaths attributable to AMR for six infection syndromes and an increase for five infection syndromes in China (Table 1). The infection syndrome with the largest reduction in deaths is lower respiratory infections, from 377,310 (95% UI 300,478–454,142) associated AMR deaths and 86,699 (65,878–107,500) attributable AMR deaths in 1990 to 268,310 (217,179–319,441) associated deaths and 60,839 (49,407–72,272) attributable deaths in 2021. In 2021, only three infection syndromes had an attributable AMR burden of at least 10,000, in descending order: bloodstream infections, lower respiratory tract infections, and peritoneal and intra-abdominal infections. Burden of AMR by pathogens

From 1990 to 2021, China had a decline in the attributable AMR deaths for 13 pathogens and an increase for 8 pathogens (Additional file 1: Table S7). The pathogen with the largest decline was Streptococcus pneumoniae, with AMR-associated deaths declining by 58,874 (30,989–86,759) and AMR-attributed deaths declining by 17,120 (8004–26,236) over 31 years. The pathogen with the largest increase was Staphylococcus aureus, with AMR-associated deaths increasing by 83,401 (69,442–97,361) and deaths attributable to AMR increasing by 20,774 (16,880–24,668) over the same period. Only six pathogens had an attributable AMR burden of at least 10,000 in 2021, in descending order: Staphylococcus aureus, Streptococcus pneumoniae, Acinetobacter baumannii, Escherichia coli, Klebsiella pneumoniae, and Pseudomonas aeruginosa.

In China, among children under 5 years of age, the pathogens with the largest number of deaths attributable to AMR in 2021 were Streptococcus pneumoniae, Staphylococcus aureus, and Klebsiella pneumoniae. The pathogen with the largest reduction in AMR deaths was drug-resistant Streptococcus pneumoniae, with the AMR-attributable deaths declining from 24,961 (16,554–33,367) in 1990 to 961 (743–1178) in 2021, and the AMR-associated deaths declining from 117,098 (83,217–150,979) in 1990 to 5209 (4163–6283) in 2021. In contrast, AMR deaths from drug-resistant Streptococcus pneumoniae have increased among those aged 5 years and older, with the deaths attributable to AMR rising from 21,465 (14,577–28,352) in 1990 to 28,345 (22,986–33,703) in 2021, and the deaths associated with AMR rising from 100,704 (74,458–126,949) in 1990 to 153,718 (125,877–181,560) in 2021. In children aged 5 years and younger, deaths attributable to AMR declined from 1990 to 2021 for all pathogens; however, in people aged 5 years and older, AMR-attributable deaths rose for 15 drug-resistant pathogens in addition to six drug-resistant pathogens, namely Enterobacter spp., Serratia spp., Mycobacterium tuberculosis, non-typhoidal Salmonella, Salmonella enterica serovar Typhi, and Shigella spp. In people aged 5 years and older, the pathogens with more than 20,000 deaths attributable to AMR in 2021 were Staphylococcus aureus, Streptococcus pneumoniae, and Acinetobacter baumannii (Fig. 3A and B).

Burden of AMR by species and pathogen–drug combinations

In 2021, the pathogen–drug combination that posed the largest resistance burden in China is MRSA, responsible for 26,023 (17,580–34,465) deaths attributable to AMR. In addition to MRSA, two other pathogen–drug combinations caused more than 10,000 deaths attributable to AMR: carbapenem-resistant Streptococcus pneumoniae, which caused 11,182 (7377–14,987) attributable deaths, and carbapenem-resistant Acinetobacter baumannii, which caused 10,516 (7864–13,169) attributable deaths. To examine trends in the burden of AMR to different pathogen–drug combinations over time, we calculated the annualized rate of change in the deaths attributable to AMR from 1990 to 2021 separately. Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this study was the first comprehensive assessment of AMR burden trends in China. Compared with previous studies that focused on cross-sectional AMR burden at a single time point or within broader super-regions, our study adds substantial value by providing a comprehensive trend analysis of AMR-related mortality and morbidity in China from 1990 to 2021. While earlier work, such as the Global Burden of AMR 2019 study, provided crucial cross-sectional estimates, our analysis extends this by capturing longitudinal changes, including the early phase of the COVID-19 pandemic. Additionally, we uniquely analyze the burden trends associated with different pathogen–drug combinations, offering granular insights into resistance patterns across various pathogens and their corresponding treatments. This level of detail is essential for guiding targeted interventions and optimizing treatment protocols based on current resistance data.

In 2021, China accounted for 14% of global deaths attributable to AMR and 15% of deaths associated with AMR worldwide. In the two counterfactual scenarios presented in this study, if all drug-resistant infections were replaced by no infection, 711,852 deaths could have been prevented in 2021. Additionally, if all drug-resistant infections were replaced by drug-susceptible infections, 160,268 deaths could have been avoided. Notably, the study findings reveal a sustained upward trajectory in AMR-related mortality among individuals aged ≥ 5 years from 1990 to 2004, followed by a gradual decline thereafter, suggesting that targeted interventions may have initiated measurable impacts during this period. In 2004, the National Health Commission of China introduced the Guidelines for the Clinical Application of Antimicrobial Agents, marking the country’s first comprehensive framework to standardize antimicrobial usage [36]. These guidelines established evidence-based protocols for healthcare institutions, promoting rational antimicrobial prescribing practices while raising public awareness of antibiotic misuse. Subsequently, a national surveillance system for antimicrobial application and bacterial resistance trends was established in 2005 [37], enabling systematic data collection, trend analysis, and policy-relevant reporting. This infrastructure provided critical empirical foundations for refining and adapting antimicrobial stewardship policies over time.

Over the past three decades, the notable decline in AMR mortality in children under 5 years of age warrants special attention. Much of this decline may be attributable to the reduction in respiratory droplet-transmitted pathogens such as drug-resistant Streptococcus pneumoniae, drug-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae, and drug-resistant Pseudomonas aeruginosa. This trend is likely associated with the introduction and expansion of pneumococcal vaccination since 1998, as well as improvements in water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) in China [38, 39]. This finding also supports WHO’s recommendations for the prevention of AMR, which include preventing infections through frequent handwashing, maintaining environmental hygiene, and updating vaccinations [40]. Despite the substantial reduction in AMR-related deaths in children under 5, the proportion of deaths from sepsis, whether associated with or attributable to AMR, increased from 1990 to 2021. This suggests that infectious diseases in children are better controlled, while the burden of drug-resistant infections remains serious. Children with sepsis are facing infections that are increasingly difficult to treat due to limited effective antibiotic options.

The burden of AMR in adults increased from 1990 to 2021, with the greatest rise observed in individuals aged 65 years and older, where AMR-attributable deaths surged by 68% over the 31-year period. Several noteworthy factors may explain this trend. Firstly, China is experiencing an accelerating demographic transition and is recognized as one of the fastest-aging countries in the world. By 2040, the proportion of the population aged 60 years and older will increase from 12.4% in 2010 to 28% [41], implying an increasing base of drug-resistant infections. Secondly, the elderly usually have weakened autoimmune function. WHO reported that approximately 75% of elderly individuals (aged 60 and older) in China had chronic conditions such as cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and hypertension in 2019 [40]. Therefore, regardless of whether the causative pathogens are drug-sensitive or drug-resistant, the elderly are particularly vulnerable to infectious diseases. Thirdly, drug-resistant infections in the elderly are often linked to the inappropriate use of antibiotics in hospitals. A retrospective analysis by Xie et al. identified antimicrobial use, combination therapy, and prolonged treatment duration as risk factors for multidrug-resistant bacterial infections in elderly patients with hospital-acquired pneumonia [42].

In terms of infection syndromes, bloodstream infections in China exhibited the most obvious increase in AMR deaths from 1990 to 2021 and represented the most severe infection syndrome in terms of AMR burden in 2021. This may be attributed to the significant growth in elderly population in China and an increased frequency of invasive treatments [43]. According to a study of epidemiological data on bloodstream infections in China from 2010 to 2019, the proportion of drug-susceptible bacteria in bacteremia gradually decreased from 47.1 to 31.6%, and the proportion of multidrug-resistant bacteria gradually increased from 52.9 to 68.4% [44], corroborating the results of our study. The findings of this study indicate that despite a huge reduction in the burden of AMR from LRI over the past three decades, deaths attributed to AMR in 2021 still ranked second, suggesting the importance of preventing this infection syndrome in China. An analysis showed that the burden of LRI in children under 5 years in China is significantly higher than that in Europe and North America region, which may be due to the fact that the PCV vaccination rate in China is much lower than the global average [45]. In addition, there are obvious disparities in vaccine coverage between the eastern and western regions of China [46].

Although MRSA is no longer included in the latest WHO list of critical priority drug-resistant pathogens, our study shows that it still caused the greatest burden of AMR in China in 2021 and had increased steadily over the preceding 31 years. According to the latest report released by the China Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance System (CARSS), the detection rate of MRSA in China reached as high as 29.1% in 2023 [8]. This rate underscores that MRSA remains a significant pathogen in clinical infections. Furthermore, data from the CHINET indicate that MRSA exhibits resistance rates exceeding 50% to commonly used antimicrobial agents, including clindamycin, penicillin, oxacillin, and erythromycin [9]. These findings highlight the need for greater caution in empirical antibiotic prescribing and emphasize the importance of strengthening microbiological testing and antimicrobial susceptibility guidance to improve the precision of anti-infective therapy.

Carbapenem antibiotics are considered last-resort antibiotics for the treatment of severe bacterial infections. In the WHO’s update of the list of drug-resistant bacteria most threatening to human health, two of the four critical priority pathogens are carbapenem-resistant (carbapenem-resistant Acinetobacter baumannii, carbapenem-resistant Enterobacteriaceae) [47]. Our findings show that carbapenem-resistant gram-negative bacteria (CRGNB) also impose a substantial AMR burden in China. For example, data from CHINET show that carbapenem-resistant Acinetobacter baumannii in China exhibits resistance rates exceeding 50% to most antimicrobial agents, with susceptibility remaining only to polymyxins, colistin, and tigecycline [9]. The severe burden of CRGNB stems largely from extensive drug resistance or pan-resistance, so antimicrobial treatment options are limited. Furthermore, although some novel antibiotics have been approved for the treatment of CRGNB infections, their introduction in China has been relatively delayed [48]. Most CRGNB infections are hospital-acquired, and effective preventive measures are essential for controlling the infections. Relevant studies have proved that interventions such as active screening of specific populations for carbapenem-resistant Enterobacteriaceae in hospitals, single-room isolation of CRGNB-infected patients, and removal of handwashing from infected patient wards can all effectively reduce the incidence of CRGNB infections [49–51].

Our study found that the burden of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) in China decreased within 1 year after the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic (2020) and returned to pre-pandemic levels by 2021. The decrease might be associated with preventive measures such as environmental disinfection and isolation of infected individuals [52, 53]. Notably, although only about 8% of hospitalized COVID-19 patients worldwide had bacterial co-infections that warranted antibiotic use upon admission, approximately three-quarters of these patients still received antibiotics [54]. This suggests that antibiotics were often prescribed as a precautionary measure rather than based on confirmed bacterial infection [54]. At present, there are relatively few studies on the antibiotics use and drug-resistant infections during the COVID-19 pandemic throughout China. According to a meta-analysis, the proportion of antibiotic use among COVID-19 patients in the East Asia and Pacific region reached 68.6%, and the prevalence of multidrug-resistant infections was 38.6%. These figures indicate widespread inappropriate antibiotic prescribing and highlight the severity of AMR in this region [55]. Moreover, during the early phase of the pandemic, hospitals primarily admitted patients confirmed or suspected to have COVID-19, while non-urgent cases were often delayed until symptoms worsened [56]. This shift may have altered the composition of the hospitalized population. Specifically, there was an increased proportion of elderly individuals and those with compromised immune systems. These groups are at higher risk for severe infections and more likely to carry or acquire multidrug-resistant pathogens. Consequently, differences in the burden of multidrug-resistant bacteria between 2019 and the subsequent years of 2020 and 2021 may reflect changes in the characteristics of hospitalized patients. Moreover, as bacterial resistance development typically requires time before manifesting in clinical outcomes such as hospitalization or death, the observed rebound in antimicrobial resistance burden in China in 2021 could be linked to delayed effects of the pandemic. These findings suggest that continued monitoring of antimicrobial resistance trends over the next few years is essential to fully understand the long-term impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on antimicrobial resistance.

Our research has several inevitable limitations. Firstly, the input data mainly originates from hospital sampling data, which typically represents the most severe cases. Given that mortality is a primary concern in AMR studies, it is reasonable to focus on hospitalized patients, as these individuals are often at higher risk and may eventually require treatment in medical facilities. However, this approach may limit the representativeness of less severe or community-acquired infections. Secondly, the original data is in English, entailing potential biases. Thirdly, the COVID-19 pandemic has exerted an irresistibly negative impact on the monitoring of AMR, the operation of microbial laboratories, and the frequency of hospital outpatient visits, which would cause deviations in the input data after 2019. Fourthly, this study did not directly analyze the changes in the composition of hospitalized populations during the COVID-19 pandemic or their impact on the detection rates of multidrug-resistant bacteria. Future research could further incorporate epidemiological data to explore the potential effects of shifts in patient demographics during the pandemic on antimicrobial resistance patterns. Fifthly, our study also confronts unique challenges specific to the temporal trend analysis of AMR. For instance, there is a scarcity of data reporting antimicrobial resistance prior to 2000, which has been discussed in the study of global burden of AMR. Sixthly, although our study focuses on national-level trends due to limited subnational data, it provides a comprehensive overview of AMR across China. This approach adds value by focusing on China as a single entity rather than part of a larger super-region, highlighting unique national trends. However, significant regional disparities in drug-resistant bacteria detection rates suggest that future research should explore regional trends to inform more targeted interventions [8]. Seventhly, this study did not directly analyze changes in the structure of the hospitalized population and their impact on the detection rate of multidrug-resistant bacteria. Future studies could further incorporate epidemiological data to explore the potential impact of changes in patient composition on drug resistance patterns during the epidemic. Eighthly, due to the nature of the aggregated data used, we were unable to incorporate time-varying contextual factors related to the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic (e.g., lockdown measures, healthcare utilization changes) into our models. Ninthly, we were limited in our ability to conduct alternative counterfactual analyses (e.g., assuming stable resistance levels from 1990 or maintaining the highest observed susceptibility), as such scenario-based modeling requires more granular, longitudinal data at the pathogen level, which were not available in this dataset. Tenthly, our analysis covers the early phase of the COVID-19 pandemic; however, due to methodological limitations, we did not conduct a rigorous statistical assessment of its impact on AMR-related mortality and DALYs. These preliminary observations suggest a possible influence of the pandemic on healthcare-seeking behaviors, antibiotic use, and infection control practices, which may be worth further investigation.

Conclusions

Our study showed that there were an estimated 711,852 deaths associated with bacterial AMR in China, including 160,268 deaths attributable to bacterial AMR. From 1990 to 2021, the number of deaths attributable to AMR among children under 5 years old declined by 95%, while that among the elderly aged 65 and above increased by 68%. Bloodstream infections were the infectious syndrome with the greatest increase in the number of deaths during 31 years. The pathogen with the greatest increase in attributed deaths was Staphylococcus aureus. In 2021, the pathogen–drug combination causing the highest number of AMR-attributed deaths was methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus, and the one with the highest annualized rate of change in attributed deaths was third-generation cephalosporin-resistant Acinetobacter baumannii.

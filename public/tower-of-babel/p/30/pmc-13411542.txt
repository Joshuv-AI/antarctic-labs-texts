Predicting immune checkpoint inhibitor cardiotoxicity using machine learning: a systematic review of model performance and methodological quality
Authors: Vaibhav Roy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundImmune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) significantly improve cancer outcomes but can cause rare, potentially fatal cardiotoxicity, including myocarditis and major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE). Machine learning (ML) and artificial intelligence (AI) models have emerged as promising tools for early detection and risk prediction; however, their performance, methodological quality, and clinical applicability remain unclear.ObjectiveTo systematically evaluate ML-based prediction models developed to identify cardiotoxicity, myocarditis, or cardiac adverse events in patients receiving ICIs, and assess their methodological robustness using PROBAST.MethodsA systematic search of PubMed (18–20 November 2025) was performed using a multi-stage strategy (broad → refined → high-specificity). Seven studies met eligibility criteria. Data extraction covered population, predictors, outcomes, model types, validation, and performance metrics. Risk of bias was assessed using PROBAST. Supplementary files include full search strategies, extraction sheets, PRISMA flow diagram, and PROBAST tables.ResultsSeven ML models were identified, including XGBoost, multimodal deep learning, neural networks, ECG-based AI models, pharmacovigilance-driven ML, and two clinical nomograms. Sample sizes ranged from 23 to 4,282 patients. Reported AUC values varied: XGBoost, 0.92; Fusion AI, 0.88; FAERS ML, 0.83; ECG AI, 0.91; Time-series NN, predictive ranking only; Nomogram, 0.83–0.967. Troponin, ECG features, neutrophil–lymphocyte ratio, NT-proBNP, and clinical factors such as steroid dose and radiotherapy emerged as consistent predictors. PROBAST identified high risk of bias in three models (mainly due to small datasets, inadequate validation, and unclear handling of missing data). Two studies using independent validation cohorts achieved low risk of bias.ConclusionML-based models show promising discriminative ability for predicting ICI-related cardiotoxicity and myocarditis; however, most studies suffer from methodological limitations that restrict real-world clinical adoption. Larger multicenter cohorts, standardized definitions, multimodal integration, and adherence to TRIPOD-AI reporting guidelines are needed to advance clinically deployable models.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40959-026-00505-y.Keywords: Immune checkpoint inhibitors, Myocarditis, Cardiotoxicity, Machine learning, Artificial intelligence, Prediction models, Systematic review, PROBAST

Introduction

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have transformed the management of multiple solid tumors by improving long-term survival and achieving durable clinical responses [4]. Agents targeting programmed cell death protein-1 (PD-1), programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1), and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte–associated antigen-4 (CTLA-4) are now widely used across oncology, either as monotherapy or in combination with chemotherapy and radiotherapy [4]. Despite these therapeutic advances, ICIs can trigger immune-related adverse events (irAEs), among which cardiotoxicity—particularly myocarditis—remains one of the most severe and potentially fatal complications [5]. Although the reported incidence is low (0.3–1%) [6], ICI-related myocarditis carries a mortality rate approaching 40–50% in several cohorts [6], underscoring the need for improved early detection and risk stratification [8].

The clinical presentation of ICI-related cardiotoxicity is highly heterogeneous, ranging from mild biomarker elevation to fulminant myocarditis with malignant arrhythmias, cardiogenic shock, or sudden death [5]. Diagnosis often requires multimodal evaluation, including cardiac biomarkers, electrocardiography, echocardiography, and cardiac MRI [5]. However, timely identification remains challenging because symptoms overlap with non-cardiac irAEs, and conventional clinical predictors lack sensitivity [6]. Consequently, clinicians often face diagnostic uncertainty and delayed intervention, contributing to worse outcomes.

Over the past decade, machine learning (ML) and artificial intelligence (AI) have emerged as powerful tools in cardiovascular risk prediction [5]. Algorithms such as gradient boosting, neural networks, and multimodal fusion models can analyze high-dimensional clinical data, electrocardiograms, real-world pharmacovigilance databases, and imaging features to identify subtle patterns associated with cardiotoxicity. In cardio-oncology specifically, ML-based prediction models have shown the potential to enhance early recognition of cancer therapy–related cardiac dysfunction [5]. However, the evidence surrounding ML models applied specifically to ICI-induced cardiotoxicity remains fragmented, with substantial variability in study design, predictors used, outcome definitions, and reporting quality.

To date, no systematic review has synthesized the methodology, performance, and limitations of ML-based models developed to predict ICI-associated myocarditis, major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), or broader cardiotoxicity phenotypes. Existing reviews have either focused on general AI applications in oncology, non-ICI cardiotoxicity (e.g., anthracycline or trastuzumab) [7], or conceptual frameworks without evaluating model robustness or risk of bias. As a result, clinicians and researchers lack consolidated guidance on the reliability, generalizability, and methodological rigor of current ML prediction tools in this rapidly evolving field.

Therefore, the objective of this systematic review was to evaluate machine learning models developed to predict cardiotoxicity, myocarditis, or MACE in patients receiving immune checkpoint inhibitors. Specifically, we aimed to:

Methods

This systematic review followed PRISMA 2020 guidelines [2], and the search strategy adhered to PRISMA-S recommendations [9]. The protocol was designed a priori, and all methodological decisions were made before data extraction. No protocol for this systematic review was registered in a public registry (e.g., PROSPERO or OSF). Supplementary File 1 contains the complete reproducible search strategies for all iterations.

Search strategy

A systematic search of PubMed was conducted between 18–20 November 2025 using a multi-stage search approach (broad → refined → high-specificity). No restrictions were applied on publication date, language, or study design at the search stage.

Discussion

This systematic review synthesizes current evidence on machine learning–based prediction models for immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)–related cardiotoxicity, including myocarditis, MACE, and cardiac dysfunction [10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16]. Across seven included studies, we found increasing interest in applying artificial intelligence (AI) to detect or predict potentially fatal cardiac immune-related adverse events (irAEs) [8]. Despite this momentum, methodological heterogeneity, small sample sizes, and inconsistent reporting standards remain major limitations.

Summary of the discussion

Machine learning offers substantial promise for identifying patients at risk of ICI-related myocarditis and cardiotoxicity [10, 11, 14, 15, 16]. Current models perform well but remain limited by small datasets, retrospective designs, and inadequate reporting [1]. Fusion multimodal AI systems represent the most promising direction [11], but rigorous validation is essential before clinical integration [3].

Conclusion

This systematic review of seven ML-based prediction models for ICI-related cardiotoxicity reveals promising discriminative performance but significant methodological limitations restricting immediate clinical deployment [1, 3]. XGBoost, multimodal fusion AI, neural networks, and clinical nomograms consistently identify troponin, ECG abnormalities, NLR, and NT-proBNP as key predictors [ 10, 11, 14, 15, 16]. However, small sample sizes, inadequate external validation, and variable reporting quality remain critical barriers [1].

To advance clinically deployable AI tools in cardio-oncology, future research should prioritize: (1) prospective multicenter registries with standardized outcome definitions [1, 3]; (2) rigorous external validation across diverse populations; (3) adoption of TRIPOD-AI reporting standards; (4) integration of multimodal data (ECG, imaging, biomarkers, genomics); and (5) transparent model sharing and explainability assessment [3]. These initiatives will transform ML-based prediction into a reliable clinical decision-support tool for ICI-related myocarditis and cardiotoxicity risk stratification.

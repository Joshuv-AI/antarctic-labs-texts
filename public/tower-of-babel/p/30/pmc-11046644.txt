Symmetrical peripheral gangrene and Roth spots in a patient with infective endocarditis
Authors: Suraj Kumar, Maninder Kansal, Ankita Soni, Deepak Chaudhary, Bhupinder Singh
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe present a case of a young man with symmetrical peripheral gangrene (SPG) resulting from Streptococcus viridans-related infective endocarditis, an association which has not previously been reported. SPG is associated with up to 40% mortality and may necessitate amputation; early identification and treatment of the precipitating factors is very important.KEYWORDS: symmetrical peripheral gangrene, infective endocarditis

Case presentation

A 34-year man, who had previously been diagnosed with a bicuspid aortic valve, presented with complaints of fever over the previous 1 month and worsening dyspnea over the previous 1 week. Examination revealed tachypnea, tachycardia, blood pressure of 70/42 mmHg, early-diastolic murmur in the aortic area and bilateral basal rales in the chest. The patient was started on inotropic support (dopamine and nor-epinephrine injections) and decongestive therapy. Transthoracic echocardiography revealed vegetation on the aortic valve with severe aortic regurgitation. Antimicrobial therapy (injection vancomycin and gentamycin) was started. Blood culture yielded Streptococcus viridans. Fundus examination showed Roth spots (Fig 1a). The patient had a subtle improvement in his clinical parameters and became afebrile after 5 days of therapy. However, during the course of therapy, he started developing bluish discolorations of bilateral fingers and toes (Fig 1b, c) which gradually worsened to a gangrenous phase despite all appropriate measures. Peripheral arterial Doppler was not suggestive of any obstructive disease suggestive of symmetrical peripheral gangrene (SPG). After 4 weeks of antimicrobial therapy, he underwent successful aortic valve replacement. At 1 month of follow-up, the patient had persistent dry gangrene of bilateral fingers and toes. Amputation is planned.

Discussion

Symmetrical peripheral gangrene (SPG) is a rare entity in which the symmetrical gangrene of distal parts of fingers and toes occurs due to ischemia in the absence of the major arterial occlusive disease.1 The reason for SPG has been broadly divided into infectious or non-infectious. Etiopathogenesis of SPG is multifactorial. The possible pathogenesis for SPG includes vasoconstriction, prolonged hypotension, microvascular obstruction, inappropriate use of vasoconstrictor inotropes, disseminated intravascular coagulation, etc.2, 3 The association of SPG with S viridans-related infective endocarditis has not been described in the literature. SPG is associated with up to 40% mortality2 and patients may eventually need amputation of the gangrenous parts. Gangrene once established is irreversible; therefore it is very important to identify it early. Management usually involves the treatment of the underlying illness.

Minimally invasive adrenalectomy for the treatment of large pheochromocytoma: A single-centre experience
Authors: Sedat Çarkıt, Bahadır Öz, Mustafa Karaağaç, Mustafa Gök, Alper Akcan, Ahmet Öztürk
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction:Pheochromocytoma (PHEO) arises from the chromophil cells of the adrenal medulla. Surgical resection is the primary treatment for PHEO. Our work aimed to investigate the efficacy and safety of minimally invasive surgery in patients with large PHEO.Patients and Methods:This study included patients who were referred to our centre, General Surgery Outpatient Clinic, between January 2007 and December 2023, who were interviewed by a multidisciplinary endocrine board, who were diagnosed with PHEO by clinical and laboratory examination and who decided to undergo unilateral laparoscopic adrenalectomy.Results:A total of 275 patients had laparoscopic unilateral adrenalectomy during the research period. A total of 75 patients were operated on for PHEO. The PHEO group with a tumour size <6 cm consists of 46 patients, while the PHEO group with a tumour size >6 cm consists of 29 patients. The groups showed similar complications, conversion rates and hospital stays. We used multiple regression analysis to identify the independent predictors of prolonged operation time. The tumour size 6–8 cm (odds ratio [OR], 3.214; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.240–10.819; P < 0.021) and the tumour size ≥8 cm (OR, 6.343; 95% CI, 1.175–34.237; P < 0.031) were the significant predictors of prolonged operation time.Conclusion:Minimally invasive surgery can be considered the treatment of choice for <6 cm and ≥6 cm PHEO. Tumour size ≥8 cm and tumour size ≥6–8 cm for PHEO are predictive risk factors for prolonged operative time in LA.

INTRODUCTION

Pheochromocytoma (PHEO) arises from the chromophil cells of the adrenal medulla. Clinical symptoms and signs may vary considerably and may include headache, palpitations and hypertension.[12] Surgical resection is the primary treatment for PHEO.[3] Although pre-operative medical preparation, anaesthesia and surgical techniques have improved, mortality and morbidity are still high due to the fluctuation of catecholamine levels during surgery.[456] PHEOs over 6 cm are more difficult to manage because of haemodynamic instability during surgery compared with lesions under 6 cm. Nowadays, laparoscopic adrenalectomy (LA) has become a standard method for small and benign tumours.[7] However, this is still controversial in patients with PHEO over 6 cm. In reports of surgical treatment of PHEO tumours 6 cm and larger, significant associations have been found with longer operative time, higher intraoperative blood loss, higher conversion rates and a higher intraoperative hypertensive crisis.[45] However, other studies have not shown this relationship.[89]

Our work aimed to investigate the efficacy and safety of minimally invasive surgery in patients with large PHEO. The secondary objective is to identify prolonged operative time for LA.

PATIENTS AND METHODS

Study design, participants and pre-operative preparation

Patients diagnosed with clinical and laboratory PHEO who visited the general surgery outpatient clinic at our centre between January 2007 and December 2023 were included in this study. These patients also decided to undergo unilateral LA after being interviewed by a multidisciplinary endocrine council. To eliminate the possibility of multiple endocrine neoplasia, we obtained and evaluated the results of thyroid and neck ultrasonography and serum calcium and calcitonin levels in patients with PHEO. PHEO patients were assessed for their urine metanephrine, normetanephrine and vanillylmandelic acid levels throughout 24 h. The haemogram, coagulation profile and biochemical indicators were analysed in all patients, including serum electrolytes. The location of adrenal masses was evaluated using computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging [Figure 1a-c]. Scintigraphy using 131I-metaiodobenzylguanidine was conducted to detect any lesions outside the adrenal glands or on the other side of the body. Before the operation, all patients were given the standard pharmacologic therapy, which involved administering a selective alpha-adrenergic blocker (doxazosin) at least 15 days before the procedure. If the patient had a fast heart rate, they were additionally administered a beta-1 selective blocker.

Before surgery, all patients underwent evaluation for various factors, including age, gender, tumour diameter, body mass index (body mass index [BMI], kg/m2), the American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) score, comorbidities, clinical presentation, urinary metanephrine level, history of surgery, tumour side and size. We also assessed the operative time and the amount of bleeding during both the intraoperative and post-operative periods. The study classified PHEO into two categories based on size: those with a diameter of 6 cm or more (defined as large) and those with a diameter smaller than 6 cm. We compared the pre-operative and perioperative results between both groups. Patients diagnosed with PHEO were divided into two groups based on tumour size: those with tumours measuring 6–8 cm and those measuring 8 cm or larger. We used the Clavien–Dindo classification[10] to categorise problems occurring within 30 days. The study flow diagram is shown in Table 1.

Data collection

Data collection was from patients’ medical records. All patients were analysed preoperatively, including age, gender, body mass index (BMI, kg/m2), the ASA score, history of previous surgery, tumour side and size, operative time, conversion, dissection device, need for additional trocar and complications, hospital stay, pre-operative symptoms (sweating, headache and hypertension), intraoperative hypotensive and intraoperative hypertensive crisis, need for intraoperative vasopressors, increased urinary metanephrine or normetanephrine levels.

Outcomes

PHEO over 6 cm was defined as large PHEO. The objective of our work was to investigate the efficacy and safety of minimally invasive surgery in patients with PHEO ≥6 cm. The secondary objective is to identify prolonged operative time for LA. Intraoperative hypertensive crisis was defined as a systolic blood pressure (SBP) ≥180 mmHg and intraoperative hypotensive crisis was defined as an SBP ≤80 mmHg.

Peroperative and post-operative management

A lateral transperitoneal approach was used for all procedures under general anaesthesia. Volume expansion or norepinephrine infusion was used to treat intraoperative and post-operative hypotension as required. Invasive monitoring of arterial pressure was performed and used as a routine measure in cases of PHEO. Short-acting beta-blockers and nitroprusside were used to treat intraoperative hypertension. All patients were carefully monitored by the referring endocrinologist and underwent an endocrine study during the follow-up period.

Statistical analysis

The data analysis was conducted using JMP® software developed by SAS in Cary, NC. The data were analysed using descriptive statistical techniques, such as numerical values, percentages, mean and standard deviation estimates. Furthermore, the normality assumption was evaluated by doing the Shapiro–Wilk test. The data were presented in two formats: the mean ± standard deviation or the median with the interquartile range (25th–75th percentiles), depending on the distribution. The Chi-squared or Fisher’s exact test was performed to compare categorical variables. In contrast, quantitative and ordinal variables were compared using the t-test/Wilcoxon rank-sum test. If the variable had three or more levels, Kruskal–Wallis multiple comparisons were performed. Multiple logistic regression analyses were applied to identify the independent predictors of prolonged operative time for LA. The univariate analysis with P < 0.20 was included in the multiple model for the logistic regression analysis to obtain adjusted odds ratios (ORs). P < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant

Surgical technique

Under general anaesthesia, the adrenalectomies were performed with great attention to detail by skilled endocrine surgeons with a comparable learning curve. Every patient was administered preventive therapy consisting of antibiotics and antithrombotic medications. The adrenalectomies were conducted using employing four trocars on the right side and three on the left side [Figure 1d and e]. Patients were placed in either the left or right lateral decubitus position, with the side opposite to the tumour angled at about 45° for the right lateral approach and roughly 90° for the left lateral approach. The operative time was defined as the interval from the moment the skin was incised to the moment the wound was closed. The dissection was performed using either the L-hook monopolar cautery manufactured by Tyco in Boulder, Colorado, USA, or the Harmonic® ultrasonic scalpel produced by Ethicon Endo-Surgery, Inc. in Cincinnati, USA. Various tools were employed to ligate the adrenal vein, including the XL metal clip and the Hem-o-lok polymer ligation device. The XL Hem-o-lok clip was primarily applied for cases when the adrenal vein was of significant size [Figure 2a] or where the metal clip was unable to completely block the adrenal vein [Figure 2b]. The precise location where the inferior adrenal vein meets the adrenal vein is identified by examining the medial border. After insufflation, a 10-mm camera trocar was inserted on both sides of the body in the front armpit area, about 2–3 cm below the rib border, to carry out adrenalectomies on the right and left sides. Trocar diameters 10 mm and 5 mm were inserted on the right side, with the 10 mm trocar positioned along the midaxillary line and the 5 mm trocar placed along the midclavicular line. Trocars with diameters of 5 mm and 10 mm were inserted on the left side via the midaxillary and midclavicular lines, respectively. An additional trocar was introduced on the left side, as shown in Figure 1e.

A retractor was employed on the left side, in addition to extra trocars fitted with pancreatic and spleen retractors [Figure 2a]. In order to accomplish medial rotation of the spleen and tail of the pancreas, the splenophrenic, splenocolic and splenorenal ligaments are identified and then detached. To locate the upper limit of the left renal vein, an incision is made in the Gerota’s fascia. After finding the diaphragmatic vein, the region between the adrenals and the diaphragm is meticulously detached from the diaphragm towards the renal hilum. Dissection begins at the superior medial border of the adrenal glands. The adrenal vein is ligated utilising the previously mentioned technique. A retractor is employed to medially retract the liver [Figure 2c]. The triangular ligament is cut to detach it from the diaphragm. The inferior vena cava can be located by carefully dissecting the connective tissue between the medial border of the adrenal gland and the vena cava. The identification of the right adrenal vein was achieved by laterally retracting the adrenal gland by dissecting superiorly along the external border of the inferior vena cava. The adrenal vein was cut using a clipping or XL Hem-o-lok method. To avoid excessive stress on the liver, an extrahepatic vein was explored. The specimens were retrieved with an endo bag [Figures 2d and e]. The specimens were generally removed by enlarging the camera access site. Furthermore, some of them were removed through an 8–10-cm Pfannenstiel incision.

RESULTS

During the study period, laparoscopic unilateral adrenalectomy was performed in 275 patients. Of these, 75 were operated on for PHEO. A total of 75 patients were included in this study. The PHEO <6 cm group comprises 46 patients and the PHEO ≥6 cm group comprises 29 patients. The study flow diagram is shown in Table 1. Patient demographics and pre-operative data are listed in Table 2. There is no statistical difference between the groups regarding age, gender, ASA score, previous surgery and tumour side. The median tumour size was 44 (25–58) mm and 67 (60–150) mm for the PHEO <6 cm and PHEO ≥6 cm, respectively (P < 0.001). The operation times were 71.5 (44–143) and 94 (50–245) min for the PHEO <6 cm group and the FHEO ≥6 cm group, respectively (P < 0.001). The additional trocar was used in 6 patients (left, 6) in the PHEO ≥6 cm group and one (left, 1) in the PHEO ≥6 cm group (P < 0.007).

Intraoperative complications occurred in five patients in the PHEO <6 cm group, including three liver capsular lacerations and two bleeding due to organ injury, and three patients in the LT group, including one bleeding due to organ injury and two vascular injuries (P = 0.319). Post-operative complications occurred in six patients in the PHEO <6 cm group, including three atelectasis, one respiratory infection and two arrhythmias, and in five patients in the PHEO ≥6 cm group, including two atelectasis, one respiratory infection and one arrhythmia (P = 0.241). The reasons for conversion were uncontrolled bleeding (n = 1) and difficulty in dissection (n = 2). No patient experienced mortality or required a transfusion. The two groups had no statistical difference regarding hospital stay, follow-up times, conversion to open surgery and estimated blood loss. No evidence of benign tumour recurrence occurred in the groups. DISCUSSION

Our results demonstrate that minimally invasive surgery is a safe, effective and technically feasible procedure for treating PHEO ≥6 cm, as well as PHEO <6 cm. There was no statistically significant difference between the groups regarding conversion to open surgery, complications, mortality and hospital stay. Furthermore, the tumour’s size ≥8 cm and the tumour’s size ≥6–8 cm for PHEO are the predictive risk factors for the prolonged operative time in LA.

Several studies have demonstrated the advantages of LA in comparison to open adrenalectomy. The benefits include shorter hospitalisation length, lower post-operative pain, enhanced cosmetic results and decreased morbidity.[1112] Laparoscopic surgery for PHEO presents difficulties because of the considerable dimensions of the lesion, its high vascularity, its strong attachment to adjacent blood vessels and the requirement for minimum manipulation during the process.[13]

The median size of PHEO tumours was significantly greater than other adrenal tumours.[14] There has been a controversy about the safety and effectiveness of using LA for PHEOs that are larger than 6 cm in diameter. Several researchers have cautioned against conducting LA for these specific lesions.[141516] Recent investigations have shown that laparoscopy is more effective and safer than open surgery for treating large adrenal phaeochromocytomas.[1718] Several comparative studies have shown that LA is a safe and effective method for treating large PHEO in comparison to small PHEO. The duration of the operation, the rate of conversion, the occurrence of complications and the length of hospital stay are comparable in both groups.[5819] Similarly, while the operative time was longer in both the PHEO ≥6 and <8 cm and PHEO ≥8 cm groups, there were no significant differences between the two groups in terms of the number of conversions, blood transfusions needed, length of hospital stay or occurrence of complications in our study.

On the other hand, numerous investigations have associated large adrenal lesions, especially in PHEO, with increased conversion rates, blood loss, prolonged surgical durations and longer hospitalisation.[5202122] To the best of our knowledge, no studies have shown the factors determining the prolonged operative time for transperitoneal LA in PHEO; this has been reported in only a few studies within the scope of other functional adrenal tumours.[23] Our data show that tumour size between 6 and 8 cm (OR, 3.214; 95% CI, 4.923 [1.367–17.717]; P = 0.014) and tumour size of 8 cm or larger (OR, 16; 95% CI, 3.015–84.896; P = 0.001) were the main factors predicting prolonged operation time. Hasegawa et al.[23] demonstrated that transperitoneal LA resulted in a longer operation time for patients with PHEO and tumours that were 5 cm or larger, which aligns with the findings of our study.

The most commonly used laparoscopic approach is the lateral transabdominal approach.[24] The transperitoneal approach offers more manoeuvre, better visualisation and a more defined anatomical structure that facilitates the localisation of the adrenal gland.[25] A new meta-analysis and systematic review of the role of minimally invasive surgery in the management of large PHEO (>6 cm) found that transabdominal LA is better than retroperitoneal LA.[26] Conversely, a recent study showed that the retroperitoneal approach has advantages over the transabdominal approach regarding reduced operative time, less estimated blood loss and lower hospital costs for PHEOs >6 cm.[27] In our opinion, the best approach to LA is dependent on the surgeon’s most commonly used method.

In certain cases, an additional trocar may be necessary. Unlike the literature, we analysed the role of these parameters in the PHEO groups for transperitoneal LA. In our study, the additional trocar was used in the PHEO ≥6 cm group in 6 (20.6%) cases (left, 6) and in 1 (4.5%) case (left, 1) in the <6 cm PHEO group, which was statistically significant. We think that the spleen and pancreas can be retracted more efficiently with the use of additional trocar so that the improvement of dissection to the superior medial increases the success of laparoscopic surgery, especially in the PHEO >6 cm.

The complication rate of LA for PHEO >6 cm is 0%–34.8%.[52728] In our study, intraoperative and post-operative complications were 9% in the PHEO ≥6 cm group and 20.7% in the PHEO <6 cm group. Moreover, although operation time and blood loss were higher for PHEO ≥6 cm, conversion rates, hospital stay and complication rates were similar. Several studies have reported a conversion rate ranging from 4.8% to 37.5% for patients over 6 cm and 2.7% to 9.1% for those under 6 cm.[5827] Similarly, our conversion rates for the PHEO groups with a length >6 cm and <6 cm were 6% and 0.9%, respectively. In addition, we conducted a subgroup study of patients by comparing the perioperative outcomes based on tumour diameters. Our findings revealed that the group with tumour sizes ≥8 cm had a significantly more significant estimated blood loss and longer operational duration. The subgroups exhibited similar rates of complications, conversion and hospital stay.

The current study has some limitations because it is retrospective. The procedures were conducted by comprising two proficient endocrine surgeons with a comparable learning curve at a referral centre. We did not observe any effect on prolonged operative time, although in practice, we used energy devices in similar proportions. The similarity of patient demographics is an advantage of this study. In addition, we made a comparison within the cases of PHEO and did not include other endocrine diseases. We believe that this provides a more homogeneous study group in terms of a comparative analysis.

CONCLUSION

Minimally invasive surgery can be considered the treatment of choice in the <6 cm and ≥6 cm for PHEO. A multidisciplinary approach is of great importance in the peri- and post-operative period for LA. Tumour size ≥8 cm and tumour size ≥6–8 cm for PHEO are predictive risk factors for prolonged operative time in LA. Furthermore, the side of the tumour and the dissection method were not significant in predicting prolonged operative time. We need prospective controlled studies with larger patient numbers to confirm our data.

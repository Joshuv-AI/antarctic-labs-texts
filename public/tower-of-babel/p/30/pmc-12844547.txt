Impact of Anemia on Long-Term Outcomes and Mortality in ST-Segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction Patients: A SEMI-CI Cohort Study
Authors: Masoumeh Sadeghi, Fatereh Baharlouei Yancheshmeh, Azam Soleimani, Mohammad Hadi Mansouri, Hamidreza Roohafza, Razeiyeh Hasannezhad, Marjan Jamalian, Elham Ramezannezhad, Mohammad Hossein Paknahad
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:This study aimed to evaluate the effect of hemoglobin (Hb) levels on 3-year clinical outcomes and long-term cardiovascular-related mortality in MI patients.Methods:This study is a secondary study of the ST Elevation Myocardial Infarction Cohort Study (SEMI-CI) in Isfahan, Iran, that included 844 STEMI patients. The Hb level and other clinical and biochemical data during hospitalization were recorded at admission and also for 3 years annually. Cox regression analyses (univariate and multivariate) were performed to identify hazard ratios for CVD events.Results:The prevalence of anemia was 16.7% in our population. Anemia was associated with an increased risk of the composite event (HR: 2.38, 95% CI: 1.77–3.19), mortality (HR: 2.91, 95% CI: 2.03–4.15), and unstable angina (HR: 2.22, 95% CI: 1.07–4.58) during a 3-year follow-up. After adjusting factors including smoking, diabetes, hypercholesterolemia, atrial fibrillation, treated hypertension, and BMI, anemia was only associated with an increased risk of the composite event and mortality. The 3-year cardiovascular survival curve showed better results in the nonanemic group (P value < 0.0001).Conclusions:Anemia was associated with a higher prevalence of 3-year mortality in STEMI patients, especially in the first year. In addition, there were associations between anemia and some CVD events in long-term follow-ups such as composite CVD events and unstable angina in our population.

Introduction

Although the normal Hb distribution differs on several factors, such as sex, ethnicity, and physiological state, the World Health Organization (WHO) defines anemia as hemoglobin (Hb) concentration less than 12.0 g/dL in females and 13.0 g/dL in males.[1] Not only anemia is a common finding in patients hospitalized for coronary heart disease (CHD) but also associated with poor prognosis in coronary artery disease (CAD),[12] chronic heart failure (HF),[3] and acute coronary syndrome (ACS).[456] Acute ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) is the most dramatic manifestation of CAD.[7] The prevalence of anemia in STEMI patients varies from 6.4% to 45%,[68910] and it has been reported that it is associated with adverse outcomes in hospitalized patients.[61011]

Anemia potentially worsens myocardial ischemic insult due to two main reasons: decreasing the oxygen content of the myocardial blood supply[12] and increasing myocardial oxygen demand to necessitate a higher cardiac output to maintain sufficient systemic oxygen delivery.[13] Like other organs and tissues, the heart requires oxygen supply via blood. Myocardial perfusion includes approximately 4–6 percent of the cardiac output.[14] The amount of delivered oxygen in tissue depends on cardiac output and arterial oxygen content. Therefore, determinant causes of decreasing oxygen supply to tissues include reduced Hb concentration causing anemic hypoxia, decreased cardiac output causing static hypoxia, and lower Hb saturation which causes hypoxic hypoxia.[151617] Acute anemia and reduced venous return are common in myocardial infarction (MI); consequently, the heart has to work harder to increase oxygen consumption. Moreover, acute anemia reduces the myocardial blood supply by shortening the diastolic time in the cardiac cycle due to tachycardia[18] Mixed comorbidities in anemic patients may influence their short-term and long-term mortality and morbidity.[12]

The innovation of this study is the long-term 3-year follow-up period, which is rarely seen in other studies. Our society consists of Middle-East races so different from Europe and America in terms of the type of treatment and medicine used, and it examines its effect on mortality. Therefore, the present study aimed to evaluate the effect of baseline hemoglobin (Hb) levels on 3-year clinical outcomes and long-term cardiovascular mortality in STEMI Iranian patients.

Methods

Study population

This study is a prospective cohort study (SEMI-CI: ST Elevation Myocardial Infarction Cohort in Isfahan) that enrolled 772 patients with STEMI admitted to Chamran Hospital of Cardiology, Isfahan, Iran, in 2016 and followed up with them for over 3 years. This study is part of Acute Coronary Syndrome/Persian Registry of Cardiovascular Disease.[19] The inclusion criteria of our study were as follows: 20-year-old or older patients who were admitted within 24 hours from the onset of symptoms and diagnosis of STEMI according to the fourth universal definition of myocardial infarction and all patients were evaluated by a specialist.[2021] All patients were treated with PPI due to the use of dual-antiplatelet treatment (DAPT). The methodology of this study was published in detail before.[22]

Measurements

Demographic information and cardiovascular risk factors including, diabetes mellitus, treated hypertension, smoking, hypercholesterolemia, atrial fibrillation (AF), obesity (based on body mass index (BMI), and gender were recorded. In addition, we collected some other information such as the duration between the first medical contact (FMC) to the treatment given (hours) because MI mortality increases for every minute of delay, location of MI (based on ECG), treatment performed for the patients (primary PCI versus fibrinolytic therapy), number of epicardial territories with stenosis > 50%, and the last ejection fraction (EF) (based on EF in discharge visit). Above these, serum Hb was checked initially before any administration of fluids or IV medications at the time of admission. Anemia is defined as an Hb level < 12 g/dl for females and < 13 g/dl for males on yearly visits; they are questioned about their general health and any medical problems that may develop within a year, particularly major cardiovascular events (MACE).

Ethical issues

This study was approved by the Ethical Committee of the Isfahan University of Medical Science (Project number: 398508, Ethical code: IR.MUI.MED.REC.1398.383). Written informed consent was obtained at the beginning of enrollment in the cohort study after explaining the study protocol to patients.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as a mean ± standard deviation for continuous variables or as counts and percentages for categorical variables. Continuous variables were compared using an independent sample t-test or Mann–Whitney test. Categorical variables were compared using a Chi-square statistic. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed to identify hazard ratios for CVD events according to hemoglobin levels. In our analysis, we used 3 models; model 1 was adjusted for age and sex. Model 2 was adjusted for the duration between the first medical contact and treatment given, location of MI (based on ECG: anterior STEMI/others), treatment (primary PCI/thrombolysis/none), number of epicardial territories with stenosis > 50% and last ejection fraction. Finally, model 3 was adjusted for the current smoker, diabetes, hypercholesterolemia, atrial fibrillation, treated hypertension, and BMI. Each correlation between the variables is expressed as a hazard ratio with a 95% confidence interval (CI). A Kaplan–Meier curve was performed for 3-year cardiovascular survival in our population. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 24.0 (IBM. Corp., Armonk. NY). A P value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

The average age of the composite CVD event group was 65.35 ± 13.59 years, and 77.0% (167) of them were male. The average age of the noncomposite CVD event group was 58.99 ± 11.93 years with a contribution of 83.9% (526) of males. There was a significant difference in age between the two groups. Therefore, the mean age in composite CVD was higher. There were no significant differences between the two groups in terms of treated hypertension, positive history of smoking, diabetes mellitus, gender, hypercholesterolemia, and AF. Anterior STEMI was significantly higher in the composite CVD group. In terms of treatment, primary PCI was significantly higher in the composite CVD group, while thrombolytic therapy was higher in the group without composite CVD. Several anemic patients were significantly higher in the composite CVD group [Table 1].

Discussion

Iron deficiency anemia (IDA), which is the most common micronutrient deficiency, is a serious problem in both developed and underdeveloped countries. If anemia is not treated well, it leads to chronic consequences.[23] The prevalence of anemia was 16.7% in our population. Several previous reports showed a similar prevalence of anemia in STEMI patients[24]; however, it ranged from 6 to 43% in different studies.[68910] In our study, the mean age of the anemic group was significantly higher compared with the nonanemic group which is in line with previous reports that reported a higher prevalence of anemia in senescence.[25] According to our results, anemia was associated with a significantly increased prevalence of diabetes mellitus and even treated hypertension in STEMI patients similar to previously published reports.[262728] AF was found to be not linked with the anemia situation of subjects, while BMI, being male, positive history of smoking, and the number of epicardial territories with stenosis > 50% were significantly higher in a nonanemic group. Mortality was significantly more prevalent in the anemic group in our study; however, the prevalence of MI, stroke, and heart failure (MACE) was not significantly different between the anemic and nonanemic groups.

According to this study, women were more likely than men to develop anemia which is similar to numerous published reports.[28] Numerous studies have demonstrated the significant prevalence of anemia across female heart attack patients.[22930] According to this study, diabetic patients experience anemia at a considerably higher rate than nondiabetics which is in line with previous studies.[283132] Regardless of MI, diabetic patients often had a 2 to 3 times higher rate of anemia than nondiabetic patients.[33] Our results showed that the lower Hb level on admission was associated with long-term cardiovascular outcomes and a higher rate of mortality during 3 years of follow-up; however, some studies reported that anemia on admission is an independent predictor of mortality in patients with STEMI.[2293034] Rai et al.[28] found that anemia increases the risk of adverse hospital outcomes in STEMI patients.

A significant association was found in the present study between anemia and an increased risk of composite CVD event and mortality after adjusting for other factors. Previous studies demonstrated that AF is a common cardiac arrhythmia among STEMI patients, and it causes worsened in-hospital mortality perse; however, it is unclear that in anemic patients, it has an additive effect either.[353637] Similarly, Colombo et al.[38] discovered that both mild and moderate-to-severe anemia were related to a notable increase in all-cause mortality, which was decreased but still statistically significant after multivariate analysis. Another study found that although anemia may be a sign of higher mortality in people with ACS, it is unclear if it causes higher mortality.[39] Wu et al.[40] found an association between low hematocrit levels on admission and 30-day mortality (short-term mortality). Severe anemia is the lower survival in the elderly population. Sabatine et al.[6] reported that anemia was an independent strong predictor of major cardiovascular events (MACE) at the 30-day outcome in 40,000 patients with ACS. While these studies showed the prognostic impact of Hb in short-term follow-ups, the long-term effect of anemia is still unclear, with different results. Salisbury et al.[9] found an HR of 1.82 (95% CI: 1.11–2.98) for mortality in anemic patients with STEMI at 1 year. They reported higher 1-year mortality in moderate to severe hospital-acquired anemic patients. Another study found a significant association between anemia and mortality in 48 months.[8] On the contrary, Al Falluji et al.[2] concluded that there was no difference in survival between anemic and nonanemic patients. Our study also supported the association between anemia and mortality. Similar to previous studies, the higher mortality in patients with anemia was predominant in the first year.[41]

An important finding of our study was the significant association of anemia with an increased risk of composite CVD events and unstable angina in STEMI patients. Other cardiovascular events such as acute MI, ST, stroke, and heart failure were not reported enough in our population; thus, we could not estimate their HR among STEMI patients. However, the results of previous studies were conflicting. Liu et al.[42] showed that the risks of heart failure increased in patients with anemia compared with patients without anemia. Also, in a large sample study of 39922 patients diagnosed with ACS, there was a progressive increase in heart failure as the baseline hemoglobin dropped below 14 g/dL.[6] On the other hand, Lee et al.[12] reported a similar incidence of recurrent MI and stroke between anemic and nonanemic groups in STEMI patients during a 1-year follow-up. In addition, Dündar et al.[43] showed similar results about in-hospital incidence of stroke in STEMI patients with anemia compared with those without anemia. Further studies are needed to elucidate these probable associations, particularly in STEMI patients with anemia.

Study limitations

Several limitations should be mentioned in the present study. First, this study was conducted at a single center. There was a selection bias, and these defects would influence the results. Second, in this study, we only included the baseline hemoglobin on admission, without further recording about the discharge Hb or interval changes in Hb levels during hospitalization such as major or minor bleeding and the etiology of anemia.

Conclusion

It can be concluded that anemia was associated with a higher rate of 3-year mortality in STEMI patients especially in the first year. Also, there were associations between anemia and some CVD events in long-term follow-ups including composite CVD events and unstable angina in our population.

Electronic parking algorithm of new energy vehicle on slope based on FMPC
Authors: Biao Wang, Shangyue Zhang, Wujun Ji, Yun Gao
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Since the unique structure and transmission mode of new energy vehicles, the problem of parking prediction and control has always been the focus of its research. Therefore, to handle the predictive control problem of new energy vehicles in slope environments, the FMPC algorithm is applied to replace the core PID controller of new energy vehicles, and it is effectively validated using automotive simulation software on different slopes and vehicle masses. The experimental results indicate that new energy vehicles with FMPC algorithm can complete parking in about 6 s under different slopes and vehicle masses, while the PID algorithm may experience shaking and displacement. The FMPC algorithm proposed in the study achieves better results in electronic parking control of new energy vehicles on slopes, effectively improving the parking stability of new energy vehicles, which has practicality in practical slope parking of new energy vehicles.

Introduction

The continuous development of the automobile industry has also caused serious pollution to the natural environment, so it is urgent to reduce the use of fuel vehicles and promote the development of new energy vehicles [1,2]. Among them, the electronic control system of new energy vehicles is particularly essential. Its unique structure and control mode are not applicable to the traditional electric parking brake system [3,4]. Therefore, to avoid safety accidents and prevent new energy vehicles from sliding and shaking, it is required to redesign its electronic parking system [5,6]. The Model Predictive Control (MPC) algorithm has certain advantages in rolling optimization and feedback correction of automotive braking torque. It continuously predicts the vehicle speed that can be controlled by the calculated active torque and compares it with the reference trajectory, thus selecting the best control sequence for parking control of electronic vehicles. Huang et al. proposed a motion planning method for electric vehicles in dynamic environment by using sine resistance network to solve the emergency braking problem in new energy vehicle emergencies [7]. To effectively improve the system control of new energy vehicles and strengthen the energy recovery, Zhang and others constructed a new type of braking control strategy for predicting the regeneration of new energy vehicles on the basis of group intelligence [8]. Tang et al. proposed a layered brake control structure for new energy vehicles on the basis of in-depth reinforcement learning and multi-objective control strategy for the safety issues related to intelligent automatic driving of hybrid electric vehicles [9]. However, the control results of the traditional MPC algorithm are still inaccurate, and there may be sliding and shaking phenomena. Therefore, the study introduces fuzzy rules into the MPC algorithm and constructs the FMPC algorithm to replace the Proportional Integral Differential (PID) controller used in new energy vehicles. The electronic parking algorithm is applied to the slope parking control of new energy vehicles. The purpose of the experiment is to strengthen the electric control of new energy vehicles as well as solve the predictive control problem in the slope environment.

Related work

The electronic parking technology of new energy vehicles uses the characteristics of the wire control technology, which has a long-term braking function after parking and a temporary braking function during driving. Electronic parking technology is a new type of braking technology that applies automotive electronic control technology and computer microprocessors to automobile braking system [10]. However, as far as the braking system of new energy vehicles is concerned, the current relevant control theories have many limitations in the automatic control of this complex system [11]. Therefore, predictive control algorithm has become the focus of many scholars in the field of new energy vehicle slope electronic parking.

For example, the prediction model built according to the current theory can only be applied to represent the stable controlled object. In addition, it has model truncation errors that cannot be ignored. Meanwhile, its online identification is very inconvenient, and it cannot respond to the requirements of slow Time-variant system in a timely manner [[12], [13], [14]]. Machacek et al. constructed an online multi-level control structure based on Model predictive control algorithm for the energy management and braking problems of hybrid electric vehicles, which effectively optimized the energy control of electric vehicles and maximized the braking capacity [15]. Shi et al. put forward a Model predictive control algorithm to accurately track the brake pressure on the basis of the electro-hydraulic brake system related to fully automated driving vehicles, aiming at the problems related to the brake system of electric vehicles, thus effectively improving the braking capacity of electric vehicles and reducing the brake pressure of vehicles [16]. To effectively improve the response speed and control flexibility of electric vehicles, Chen et al. suggested a nonlinear predictive control algorithm using data-driven approach based on recursive high-order neural network modeling, which effectively improved the lateral control ability of new energy vehicles and improves the accuracy of brake prediction [17]. To enhance the overall stability of electric vehicles during rollover, Tian Y et al. proposed a force driven switching related path tracking predictive control algorithm, which effectively enhanced the control accuracy of electric vehicles and ensured vehicle rollover stability [12]. Manuel et al. proposed a predictive model for lateral motion of electric vehicles aiming at the issue of lateral predictive control of electric vehicles. This effectively strengthened the real-time correction and control of the vehicle steering system [18].

Lee et al. proposed a tracking predictive control method of integrated braking and steering path for electric vehicles based on the effective analysis of the road friction limit, aiming at the related problems of the lateral positioning control of electric vehicles. This effectively reduced vehicle collision [19]. To effectively reduce the collision probability of electric vehicles during steering, Li and others proposed a layered electric vehicle collision control method based on model predictive control, which effectively improved the performance of the electric vehicle collision avoidance control system [20]. To effectively enhance the stability of electric vehicles, Chen et al. proposed a switchable hydraulic interconnected suspension system based on the nonlinear predictive control model. Therefore, it effectively improved the anti-roll and pitch performance of electric vehicles [21]. To optimize the performance of voltage regulation of electric vehicles, Zhang et al. proposed an adaptive model predictive controller, which effectively strengthened vehicle energy management and enhanced vehicle linear control stability [22].

From the research of domestic and foreign scholars, the traditional control algorithms were mostly studied in the field of automobile flat pavement. Currently, the research on electronic parking of new energy vehicles on slopes is still relatively weak. In addition, the current research combining the model quality of the car body and the actual slope is also relatively small. Therefore, based on the traditional predictive control algorithm, this research carries out the joint estimation of the vehicle mass and road slope of new energy vehicles, and optimizes the PID controller using fuzzy rules. The FMPC algorithm proposed in the study is different from the traditional electronic parking algorithm, which can estimate the braking torque in advance to predict the vehicle speed. FMPC algorithm can control the new energy vehicle more accurately, so as to effectively prevent the vehicle from shaking and sliding. Its reliability and safety are superior to the traditional PID control.

Research on slope electronic parking algorithm for new energy vehicles

Research on predictive control algorithm and parking prediction model of new energy vehicles

Aiming at the predictive control problem of new energy vehicles in slope environment, the FMPC algorithm is proposed to replace the PID controller used on new energy vehicles based on fuzzy rules, and the electronic parking algorithm is applied to the slope parking control of new energy vehicles. Predictive control algorithm is a new type of computer control algorithm, which not only has good control effect, but also applies to various complex control systems. Therefore, it can be completely applied to the predictive control of the parking of new energy vehicles. MPC is a closed-loop optimal control method, whose main feature is that it can use the prediction model to predict the future system. Secondly, MPC can repeatedly optimize the calculation results and realize the control action in the system. Finally, MPC can continuously feedback to correct model errors [23].

Due to the consideration of vehicle weight, road slope and other factors in the study, it is impossible to obtain accurate braking torque at one time. Therefore, it is required to use MPC algorithm for rolling optimization and feedback correction. This study predicts the braking torque of the vehicle in real time and compares it with the reference track. In addition, the parking control of new energy vehicles is realized by selecting the best control sequence. Therefore, Fig. 1 illustrates the overall structure of the electronic parking prediction control system.Fig. 1Schematic diagram of the overall structure of the electronic parking prediction control system.Fig. 1

Schematic diagram of the overall structure of the electronic parking prediction control system.

As can be seen from Fig. 1, predictive control system is divided into three categories: predictive model, rolling optimization and feedback correction. Among them, the actual output of the internal PID controller of the vehicle is the predicted output value obtained by the prediction model, and then the actual output value is obtained after the feedback correction. It is compared with reference tracking, compensated, and finally adjusted by the controller. This process is a complete rolling optimization process. The traditional control algorithm is prone to slope sliding or jitter in the parking of new energy vehicles on slopes, and the three steps of MPC algorithm can effectively ensure the overall stability of the parking system. However, there is always some error between the prediction model and the actual system. Therefore, fuzzy rules are introduced into MPC algorithm and FMPC algorithm is proposed. The model integrates the advantages of MPC algorithm and fuzzy control algorithm, thus effectively improving the performance of dealing with external interference factors. Compared with traditional methods, predictive control models are more accurate and suitable for complex systems, maintaining the optimization characteristics of optimal control. Rolling optimization and feedback correction of new energy vehicle status

According to the process description in Fig. 2, it is found that PID control is essential in the rolling optimization of the state of new energy vehicles. Therefore, the research applies fuzzy rules to improve PID controller to replace the traditional PID controller. The fuzzy controller is mainly responsible for the fuzzification of input data, the fuzzy reasoning decision and the accuracy of decision. The design steps of fuzzy controller are shown in Fig. 4.Fig. 4Design steps of fuzzy controller.Fig. 4

Design steps of fuzzy controller.

It can be seen from Fig. 4 that the design of the fuzzy controller requires system analysis first. Secondly, the corresponding fuzzy method is selected and determined. Then the corresponding fuzzy control rule base is constructed. Then, anti-blur is output. Finally, the test correction is carried out. Generally speaking, fuzzy control has not only good dynamic response performance, but also good time-varying, nonlinear adaptive and robust. However, the fuzzy control system is difficult to achieve no static error in stable state. Due to the limited level of fuzzy rules, fuzzy control is subject to this constraint to a certain extent, resulting in errors. Therefore, the FMPC algorithm is organically combined with the fuzzy rule PID controller, and its braking torque is controlled by the fuzzy PID compound control, so that the actual performance of the PID controller is better improved. In the actual new energy vehicle, there are interference factors in its control system. Due to the influence of these uncertainties, the model of the system has greater prediction accuracy. To overcome the uncertainty of the system, the prediction of the model must be continuously revised through feedback correction, so that the prediction results are consistent with the actual output. The feedback correction of the FMPC control algorithm is to compare the output of the control target with the reference trajectory, and input the comparison result into the PI controller. Secondly, the rolling optimization is carried out repeatedly, and the predicted output is compared with the benchmark tracking. Then the PID controller for rolling optimization is input until the output of the prediction model is basically consistent with the reference trajectory. Then the control parameters into the control target are input. Finally, the control parameters are compared with the reference tracking. Repeating the above is a complete feedback correction process, which reduces the system error mainly through continuous comparison with the reference orbit. Thus, the system can be as close as possible to the reference orbit and achieve the control purpose.

For the parking of new energy vehicles on a slope, it is required to continuously obtain the real speed of new energy vehicles, and correct the real-time speed and the predicted speed output by the prediction model. The calculation expression is shown in Equation (15).(15)vcor′=vpre′+vout′2in Equation (15), vpre′ represents the predicted vehicle speed; vout′ indicates the true speed. It can be seen that feedback correction is the process of weighting the predicted speed of the vehicle and the actual speed of the vehicle, and then outputting the corrected speed to the reference track.

FMPC-based electronic parking experiment analysis of new energy vehicles on slopes

Parking simulation experiment of new energy vehicles on different slopes

To verify the superiority of FMPC algorithm in the new energy electronic ramp parking, the FMPC algorithm is compared with PID algorithm. CarSim is selected for the experimental simulation, which mainly defines the characteristic files and parameters of the entire vehicle. This section includes three databases, namely the vehicle model database, the database for defining driving conditions, and the external environment database. The vehicle model database contains the parameters and files of each subsystem within the vehicle, the driving condition database contains data parameters such as driving direction and speed, and the external environment database contains external environment parameters such as road slope and wind speed. In the experiment, the research is analyzed from the slope of the road and the mass of the whole vehicle (The reason is that the research mainly analyzes the slope control of electronic parking, so the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm can be judged by adjusting different slopes. Meanwhile, the quality of the entire vehicle have an impact on the parking of the car on the slope, so it is also analyzed). Park simulation experiments are conducted on different slopes to study the setting of the overall mass of new energy vehicles at 1500 kg. Conclusion

Aiming at the predictive control problem of new energy vehicles in the slope environment, this study proposes FMPC algorithm to replace the PID controller used in new energy vehicles based on fuzzy rules, and carries out experiments to analyze its effectiveness. The experimental results show that the new energy vehicles under the control of FMPC algorithm can park in about 6s under different slopes. When the gradient is 20°, FMPC controls the vehicle speed to fluctuate by-1 × 103 km/h∼1 × 103km/hwithin 6.82s–6.95s, the displacement and jitter amplitude are very small. The displacement of the vehicle in the PID algorithm is negative after 6s at different slopes. The displacement fluctuation at 20° is 35.2 m–36 m, and the torque fluctuation is 582Nm∼584Nm; The displacement fluctuation at 15° is 42 m–42.5 m. Torque fluctuation is between 544Nm and 545Nm. The maximum slope sliding speed of FMPC is (2e)−3 km/h, which is far lower than PID algorithm (0.12 km/h). New energy vehicles under the control of FMPC under different vehicle masses can also park on the slope in about 6s, and their jitter and displacement amplitude are very small. The speed and displacement of the PID algorithm under different mass have negative values. Under FMPC algorithm, when the mass is 1000 kg, the speed fluctuation is - 0.1 km/h∼0 km/h, and the torque fluctuation is 382∼385Nm; Torque fluctuation is 82Nm∼822Nm at 2000 kg mass. The distance of slope sliding after 5 s of FMPC algorithm is 3 × 10−3 m, far lower than PID (2 × 10−2 m). In general, the FMPC algorithm proposed in the study shows high stability under different slopes and different vehicle masses. Its performance of parking on slope is higher than PID algorithm, with higher reliability and safety. However, the parking on slopes studied and analyzed does not introduce complex conditions such as weather, so further research is needed in the future.

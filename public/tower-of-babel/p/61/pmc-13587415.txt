Can artificial intelligence applications be utilised in the management of dental trauma? A comparison of ChatGPT, Gemini, and DeepSeek
Authors: Dolu Kaya
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to evaluate the accuracy and reliability of the responses provided by the artificial intelligence applications (chatbots) ChatGPT 4o Plus, Google Gemini Pro, and DeepSeek V3 to questions regarding traumatic dental injuries.Methods14 open-ended questions were prepared to ask the chatbots regarding crown-root fractures, luxation injuries, and avulsion. Each question was asked to each application three times a day for three days. Responses were evaluated using the Global Quality Score (GQS) and modified DISCERN (mDISCERN) scales. Data were analysed using the SPSS programme. The Mann–Whitney U and Kruskal–Wallis tests were used in the evaluation. The significance level was set at p < 0.05.ResultsThe average GQS scores for DeepSeek, ChatGPT, and Google Gemini were 4.26, 4.36, and 4.43, respectively. The mean mDISCERN scores were 3.86, 4.01, and 3.85, respectively. A significant difference was found between the GQS and mDISCERN scores for the chatbots (p < 0.05). There was no significant difference between the GQS and mDISCERN scores for crown-root fractures (p > 0.05). While there was no significant difference in GQS scores for luxation injuries and avulsion (p > 0.05), a significant difference was found in mDISCERN scores (p < 0.05). Gemini received the highest score for most questions regarding GQS, while ChatGPT received the highest score regarding mDISCERN.ConclusionsThe findings of this study indicate that all three chatbots provide valuable support to dentists in the management of dental trauma. Although chatbots appear to facilitate emergency management in dental trauma, they are still under development, and additional information sources should also be used.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-026-08625-8.Keywords: Artificial intelligence, ChatGPT, Deepseek, Endodontics, Gemini, Large language models, Tooth injuries

Background

Artificial intelligence (AI) is a field of computer science that aims to develop intelligent systems capable of performing tasks that typically require human thought, such as learning, reasoning, and problem-solving [1]. Large language models (LLMs), a type of AI application, gained significant attention following OpenAI's release of ChatGPT in November 2022 [2]. LLMs are deep learning-based AI models that learn language structure by being trained on large text datasets and can generate human-like text. These models now enable computers to process and generate human language and engage in reasoning based on it [3]. They can also respond to multilingual questions.

OpenAI introduced ChatGPT 4.0 in March 2024 and the 4o version in May 2024. The developer claims that ChatGPT-4o shows a significant increase in processing speed and improvements in both text and code processing, particularly in languages other than English [4]. Similarly, Google launched its AI-powered chatbot Google Bard (Gemini) on 21 March 2023 and released its updated version, Gemini Advanced, on 8 February 2024 [5]. Gemini is an advanced chatbot that uses a 'local multimodal' model to efficiently analyse and adapt to various data formats such as text, audio, and video [6]. DeepSeek, which emphasises the principles of transparency and reproducibility, has rapidly spread worldwide with its chatbot launched in December 2024, thanks to its low cost and high download rates [7].

The characteristics of AI technologies have also popularised the use of AI models in scientific fields that seek solutions to complex health problems, such as medicine and dentistry, and require precision in the decision-making process [8]. As in all branches of dentistry, its use is rapidly increasing in endodontics. Numerous studies have contributed to the science of endodontics in many areas, including the diagnosis and treatment planning of endodontic disease, the facilitation of access cavity preparation of obliterated canals, and the safe biomechanical preparation of root canals with the support of AI [9, 10]. Studies conducted with AI applications in endodontics have shown high accuracy rates in many areas of endodontics, such as the diagnosis of periapical lesions, the diagnosis of vertical root fractures, the detection of complex root canal configurations, the determination of working length, and the prediction of the outcomes of endodontic retreatment and endodontic microsurgery [11]. In emergencies requiring immediate treatment, such as trauma, it has been reported that AI algorithms can improve prognosis by providing reliable, data-driven recommendations [12]. Nevertheless, studies on dental trauma remain limited [6, 12, 13]. Also, the questions in these studies were mostly multiple-choice or dichotomous (yes/no) questions [13–15]. Studies on the accuracy and reliability of information regarding dental trauma obtained from chatbots (ChatGPT, Gemini, DeepSeek), which have become increasingly common recently, are also limited [6, 16].

Rapid and accurate intervention is crucial in successfully treating TDIs, which are urgent and complex conditions. The variety of treatment options associated with dental trauma, which is the focus of endodontics, makes it important to investigate whether AI software can provide accurate and reliable answers to specific questions in this field [13]. The timing of treatment is as important as the treatment itself, as many adverse outcomes after trauma result from inadequate or inappropriate emergency interventions [17]. General dentists, in particular, may require emergency support in managing dental trauma. Dentists' ability to diagnose correctly and decide on the appropriate treatment option is critical in managing TDIs. General dentists may encounter and treat these cases and may need support from a specialist dentist [18]. However, in such situations, it may not always be possible for general dentists to reach the relevant specialist dentist immediately. Therefore, utilising AI applications can be beneficial. Unlike other AI studies, this research focuses on clinical scenarios in potential emergencies by posing questions to chatbots [14, 16]. Based on this, the study is designed to examine the responses of three AI applications to open-ended questions consisting of clinical scenarios related to TDI.

Methods

Ethical approval

Ethical approval for this cross-sectional study was obtained from the Nevşehir Hacı Bektaş Veli University Non-Interventional Clinical Research and Publication Ethics Committee (decision no: 2500041824).

Question design

The study assessed the knowledge of three LLM programmes regarding TDIs. For this purpose, a questionnaire consisting of 14 open-ended Turkish questions that dentists might ask in an emergency regarding TDIs was used (Table 1). The questions in the questionnaire were prepared according to the guidelines of the IADT [19, 20]. The clinical parameters used in the questions were determined according to the IADT clinical recommendations (The first 11 questions were prepared based on the information on diagnosis, clinical findings, radiographic assessment, treatment, and follow up in Tables 2–13 of the IADT guideline (supplementary file) [19], and the last three questions were prepared based on pages 334–337 [20]). Accordingly, the questions cover topics such as treatment options for TDI, treatment procedures, necessary radiological examinations, identification of the type of dental trauma, follow-up procedure for the traumatised tooth, splinting duration, and splint material properties. The opinions and suggestions of two specialists (an assistant professor in endodontics and a professor in pediatric dentistry) were taken into account in the preparation of the questions. The questions were initially prepared by the researcher and sent simultaneously to both experts. Based on their suggestions, the questions were finalised, and their approval was obtained.

Questions

Data collection

Questions on TDI were posed to ChatGPT 4o Plus (OpenAI, San Francisco, CA, USA), Gemini Pro (Google LLC., Mountain View, CA, USA), and DeepSeek-V3 (DeepSeek-AI, Hangzhou, China), among the most widely used LLM [21]. New accounts were created for this study on each chatbot. The questions were asked to each chatbot three times a day for three consecutive days, from July 24–26, 2025 [15]. The questions were asked at similar times each day to prevent time differences. This approach allowed us to examine the consistency of responses and whether responses changed over time. Thus, a total of 27 responses were obtained for each question, and a total of 378 responses were obtained for the three chatbots. To ensure consistency, the same researcher asked the questions using a single user account. All search history and cookies were cleared from the computer before the question–answer session. The questions were written in scientific language using terminology appropriate for chatbots. To minimise the influence of previous responses, each question was asked in a new chat window. Responses were recorded in a Microsoft Word document (Microsoft Office 365, Microsoft Inc., Redmond, WA, USA). Each question was asked once to three LLMs, with no follow-up questions or additional explanations.

Evaluation

In the study, nine endodontists (three assistant professors and six specialists in endodontics; each with at least 5 years of experience) evaluated the questions using the Global Quality Score (GQS) and modified DISCERN (mDISCERN) scales, according to IADT guidelines. The evaluated questions were sent without specifying any LLM name (e.g., chatbot 1, chatbot 2, etc.), ensuring that the reviewers did not know which LLM they were rating. Before the formal evaluation, all nine endodontists attended a calibration meeting. During this session, the definitions and scoring criteria for the GQS and mDISCERN scales were thoroughly explained. To harmonize the scoring approach, a pilot set of 10 randomly selected samples (not included in the final analysis) was evaluated collectively. Throughout the evaluation, each specialist received a digital copy of the latest International Association of Dental Traumatology (IADT) guidelines to ensure technical accuracy.

The modified GQS [22], derived from the Global Quality Score (GQS) [23], was developed to assess the quality of responses (‘context’ and ‘content’) (Fig. 1). Scores obtained from the GQS range from 1 to 5.

Global Quality Score (GQS) description

The mDISCERN scale [24, 25], adapted from the DISCERN scale, was developed to assess the quality and reliability of responses. The mDISCERN scale, consisting of 5 questions, evaluates the content of websites that provide health-related information based on three main factors: readability, source reliability, and information quality. Each answer is scored from 1 to 5 points. A score of 1 indicates the lowest quality and reliability, while a score of 5 indicates the highest quality and reliability (Fig. 2) [26]. Using these criteria, chatbot responses were evaluated based on the accuracy of the responses (context) and the completeness of the information provided (content). This allowed for the evaluation of the validity of the responses.

Statistical analysis

The data were analysed using SPSS 23.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). The mean (x ± ss), median, and quartile values (%25–75%) were used to analyze descriptive data. The Mann–Whitney U and Kruskal–Wallis tests were used to compare dependent and independent variables. Where significant differences were found, the Bonferroni-adjusted post-hoc test was applied for pairwise comparisons. The intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was utilized to examine inter-rater reliability in the GQS and mDISCERN tools. A significance level of p < 0.05 was accepted.

Results

Table 2 shows the total GQS and mDISCERN scores by chatbot type. The average GQS values for DeepSeek, ChatGPT, and Gemini were 4.26 ± 0.87, 4.36 ± 0.87, and 4.43 ± 0.65, respectively. For mDISCERN, the scores were 3.86 ± 0.54, 4.01 ± 0.57, and 3.85 ± 0.42, respectively. There was a statistically significant difference between GQS and mDISCERN scores for chatbots (p < 0.05). This difference in GQS is due to the difference in DeepSeek's score compared to other chatbots, while in mDISCERN, it's due to the difference in ChatGPT's score compared to other chatbots. The ICC analysis showed good interrater reliability for both overall GQS (ICC = 0.810; %95 Cl, 0.639–0.926) and mDISCERN (ICC = 0.795; %95 Cl, 0.618–0.919) scores (p < 0.001 for both).

Table 3 shows the total GQS and mDISCERN scores according to trauma types. For crown-root fractures, the average GQS values for DeepSeek, ChatGPT, and Gemini were 4.34 ± 0.58, 4.44 ± 0.76, and 4.50 ± 0.64, respectively, with no statistically significant difference between them (p > 0.05). There were also no significant differences in mDISCERN scores between the chatbots (p > 0.05). In terms of luxation injuries and avulsion, the mean GQS values for DeepSeek, ChatGPT, and Gemini were 4.19 ± 0.99, 4.32 ± 0.92, and 4.39 ± 0.66, respectively, with no statistically significant difference between them (p > 0.05). However, there was a significant difference between the chatbots regarding mDISCERN scores (p < 0.05). No significant difference was observed between the GQS scores for crown–root fractures and those for luxation injuries/avulsion for each chatbot (p > 0.05). When comparing mDISCERN scores, a significant difference was found only in ChatGPT (p < 0.05).

Discussion

TDIs are an important area of dentistry where complex endodontic approaches are applied [27]. Clinicians may find it challenging to diagnose and decide on a treatment method, particularly in complex dental trauma cases. To overcome this difficulty, interdisciplinary collaboration, long-term follow-up, and evidence-based approaches are adopted [28, 29]. As in many other fields in recent years, various studies have reported that AI can be utilised in this area. In these studies, the responses provided by chatbots to patients' questions and general questions related to dental trauma were examined [16, 26, 27]. These studies' questions were mostly multiple-choice or dichotomous (yes/no). Furthermore, it was observed that ChatGPT and Gemini versions were predominantly used in the studies, while DeepSeek was used to a limited extent. In this study, the responses to open-ended questions regarding different TDI scenarios asked to chatbots were examined in the context of professionally managing acute dental trauma cases encountered in the clinic. In addition to the advanced chatbot models ChatGPT 4o Plus and Gemini Pro, DeepSeek-V3 was also utilised in the examination of the questions.

Various factors, such as patient age, root development, the severity and timing of the trauma, make the diagnosis and management of TDIs complex and time-consuming. Due to this complexity, dentists often require additional guidance in managing TDIs. In this study, to address this need, AI systems—considered as potential information sources that could support clinicians—were evaluated using the GQS and mDISCERN scales. These scales evaluate the accuracy and quality of the information provided by AI systems regarding TDI and their scope and reliability. In this study, when examining the GQS scores that evaluated the quality and scope of questions related to TDI, Gemini ranked first. ChatGPT and DeepSeek, respectively, followed this. Furthermore, there was a significant difference between the GQS scores of Gemini and ChatGPT and the DeepSeek score (p < 0.05) (H01 rejected). When examining the mDISCERN scores, which evaluate the accuracy and reliability of the questions, ChatGPT, DeepSeek, and Gemini ranked highest to lowest, respectively. While no significant difference was found between Gemini and DeepSeek, ChatGPT differed significantly from both (H02 rejected). In the GQS scale evaluation, Gemini and ChatGPT provided more comprehensive answers than DeepSeek. In the mDISCERN evaluation, ChatGPT provided the most reliable answers, while Gemini and DeepSeek provided similarly less reliable answers. Although Gemini's answers were similar in accuracy to ChatGPT's, its mDISCERN score was lower. This can be explained as follows: ChatGPT provided references for most of the answers when asked a question. Gemini, on the other hand, answered the same question without providing references, and only provided references when asked for them. It is thought that Gemini's lower mDISCERN score may stem from this. Another study also evaluated the responses of ChatGPT, Gemini, DeepSeek and Perplexity to trauma-related questions [30]. The results were similar to those of the current study, reporting that ChatGPT and Gemini had high accuracy, but Gemini had lower reliability than other chatbots. In most studies evaluating the answers on TDI, endodontic clinic, regenerative endodontics, and oral pathology topics, ChatGPT obtained higher [16, 31, 32] or similar [14, 33] quality and/or reliability in parallel with the current study. In some cases, Gemini or DeepSeek scored higher [6, 34]. These differences may stem from the chatbots' ability to respond to the characteristics (accuracy, quality, scope, reliability, etc.) measured by GQS and mDISCERN. While these results show that no chatbot is reliable on its own, they suggest that ChatGPT is a step ahead, especially in terms of quality and reliability.

The clinical classification of TDIs is divided into three main categories: crown-root fractures, luxation injuries, and avulsion. Each of these categories requires the application of different management protocols due to the nature of the trauma [19, 20]. Therefore, access to up-to-date and accurate information on each protocol is critical to minimise potential complications and optimise prognosis. Consequently, this study also examined the information provided by chatbots according to the type of trauma. Gemini had the highest GQS score for crown-root fractures, while ChatGPT had the highest mDISCERN score. No significant difference was observed between the chatbots regarding either scale score. Similarly, the highest GQS score for luxation injuries and avulsion was found in Gemini, while the highest mDISCERN score was found in ChatGPT. While no significant difference was observed between chatbots regarding GQS scores, a significant difference was observed in mDISCERN scores (p < 0.05). Furthermore, there was a significant difference in mDISCERN scores according to trauma type in ChatGPT (p < 0.05). These results indicate that all three chatbots can be utilised, with Gemini being the primary choice, when seeking accurate and comprehensive information regarding types of trauma. Furthermore, it can be said that ChatGPT can be utilised primarily to obtain accurate and reliable information regarding luxation injuries and avulsion. In studies evaluating questions related to luxation injuries or avulsion, consistent with the current study, Gemini provided more accurate and comprehensive answers than ChatGPT 3.5. In contrast, ChatGPT 4.0 provided more detailed answers than Gemini [6, 15, 16]. In a study examining dental trauma, fluorosis, and tooth eruption/oral health in paediatric dentistry, dental trauma received the lowest GQS score [35]. In the limited number of chatbot studies examining types of trauma, it is seen that only one type of trauma is addressed and/or there are no comparisons between them [6, 14]. At this point, more studies are considered necessary.

This study reveals that AI-based chatbots have the potential to provide accurate information and optimize emergency management processes. However, it is necessary for clinicians to carefully evaluate the data obtained from these tools in clinical settings, and for clinical decision-making mechanisms to be subjected to critical scrutiny. Furthermore, the compliance of the presented outputs with ethical standards and clinical guidelines should be meticulously observed [36].

Limitation

A significant limitation of this study is the dynamic and rapidly evolving nature of Large Language Models (LLMs). While data collection was performed within a structured timeframe (between July 24–26, 2025) and measures such as cleaning chat histories and repeated alerts were taken to minimize temporal bias, the continuous update cycles of these models mean that small internal adjustments may have occurred even during the 3-day data collection period. Due to these frequent updates, the responses provided by LLMs are time-sensitive; therefore, future iterations of this study may yield different results as the models continue to evolve. Another limitation is that the questions are in Turkish. Therefore, the results may not be directly generalized to English or other languages. LLMs can produce misinformation called hallucinations. It is recommended that the information obtained be confirmed with primary sources. While clinicians possess essential critical thinking, interpretive skills, and emotional intelligence necessary for comprehensive patient care, the fact that LLMs do not fully possess these qualities is a limitation. In our study, each question was asked once to three separate LLMs, with no follow-up questions or additional explanations. However, in real clinical practice, a dentist might ask follow-up questions. Therefore, LLMs might also provide additional answers.

Conclusion

In this study, which examined chatbots' responses to questions related to TDIs, Gemini, ChatGPT, and DeepSeek received the highest GQS scores, respectively, while ChatGPT, DeepSeek, and Gemini ranked highest in terms of mDISCERN scores. When examined by trauma type, Gemini received the highest score in GQS for both trauma types, while ChatGPT received the highest score in mDISCERN. A significant difference was only found in the mDISCERN scores for luxation injuries and avulsion-type traumas. While all chatbots showed high average scores, ChatGPT demonstrated superior reliability in complex luxation and avulsion scenarios. Again, under similar conditions, when encountering a crown-root fracture case of the TDI type, it can be said that all three chatbots can be used. Although the study's results show that chatbots can be accurate and reliable sources of information for facilitating emergency management in dental trauma management, it should be remembered that these chatbots are still under development, and additional sources of information should also be used. Furthermore, based on the study results, developing AI-based tools containing information specific to endodontics that could facilitate trauma management under limited conditions may be recommended.

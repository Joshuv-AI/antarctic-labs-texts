Anterior lumbar interbody fusion for degenerative disc disease induced by retained gunshot projectile: A case report
Authors: Aecio Rubens Dias Pereira Filho, Alexandre Roberto Aprile, Allison Fernandes Roxo, Raul Pinheiro Medeiros, Érico Oliveira Honorato de Barros, Maria Paula Loureiro de Oliveira Pereir
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background: Degenerative disc disease (DDD) may emerge years after spinal trauma, particularly when ballistic fragments remain lodged within the disc space.Case Description: A 31-year-old male presented with a 1-year history of progressive low back pain and bilateral sciatica, 12 years after a conservatively managed lumbar gunshot injury with no initial neurological deficits. Imaging demonstrated a retained projectile within the L5–S1 disc space, associated with disc height loss, retrolisthesis, and Modic type 3 endplate changes. Anterior lumbar interbody fusion (ALIF) at L5–S1 was performed, permitting direct bullet removal and placement of an interbody cage. Early postoperative radiographs confirmed appropriate implant positioning with restoration of disc height and sagittal alignment, while clinical follow-up over 6 months documented sustained symptomatic improvement.Conclusion: In anatomically suitable scenarios, ALIF may represent a safe and effective option for the removal of anteriorly retained bullet fragments while addressing L5-S1 DDD.

INTRODUCTION

Degenerative disc disease (DDD) is a progressive condition typically associated with aging, mechanical stress, and genetic factors, yet prior spinal gunshot injuries may accelerate its course through direct damage and persistent inflammation.[2] Retained intra-discal projectiles create a sustained inflammatory milieu that promotes disc collapse and ultimately leads to delayed symptomatic DDD.[3,6] While protocols for acute management of spinal gunshot wounds are well established, evidence regarding long-term outcomes and optimal surgical strategies remains limited.[5] In such complex scenarios, anterior lumbar interbody fusion (ALIF) may provide specific advantages, notably direct access for projectile removal and the possibility of restoring disc height and sagittal alignment.[1,4,7]

CASE PRESENTATION

Twelve years after a conservatively managed lumbar gunshot injury with no initial neurological deficits [Table 1], a 31-year-old male developed a 1-year history of progressive low back pain and bilateral sciatica. The patient remained asymptomatic for almost a decade. Magnetic resonance imaging performed in 2019 revealed a retained bullet within the L5–S1 disc space, showing disc height reduction, mild retrolisthesis, bilateral foraminal stenosis, and Modic type 3 endplate changes [Figure 1]. Following multidisciplinary discussion, L5–S1 ALIF was selected to permit bullet removal and disc reconstruction while minimizing neural manipulation. The surgery, performed in August 2019, proceeded uneventfully. The procedure included projectile excision, interbody cage placement, and intraoperative confirmation of appropriate positioning [Figure 2]. The postoperative course was uneventful, with preserved neurological function. Early postoperative radiographs confirmed restoration of disc height and sagittal alignment [Figure 3], while 6-month follow-up documented progressive symptomatic improvement.

DISCUSSION

Retained ballistic fragments in the spine remain a subject of debate, particularly with respect to their long-term degenerative potential and the indications for their surgical removal. Although the acute management of spinal gunshot injuries is well established, evidence regarding long-term sequelae, including disc degeneration, remains limited.[5]

Chronic inflammation and structural changes induced by retained metallic fragments may accelerate DDD, which may culminate in delayed symptomatic presentations.[2,3,6] In the present case, clinically significant L5–S1 DDD manifested more than a decade after trauma, supporting the hypothesis that retained projectiles may act as a trigger for degeneration.

ALIF was selected for this patient, as it provided direct access to the disc space, facilitated complete discectomy, and allowed safe projectile removal without neural manipulation.[1,4,7] The approach further enabled restoration of disc height and sagittal alignment, findings that align with reports highlighting its biomechanical and clinical benefits. The favorable postoperative course – characterized by progressive symptomatic improvement and radiographic stability – suggests that ALIF may represent a valuable option in this rare but challenging scenario. Ethics statement

This case report describes a single patient. According to institutional and international guidelines, ethical approval was not required. Written informed consent for publication of the case details and accompanying images was obtained from the patient.[10]

CONCLUSION

ALIF may represent a safe and effective option for treating symptomatic DDD associated with retained intra-discal gunshot projectiles.

EGFR-TKI monotherapy versus combination therapy as first-line treatment for EGFR-mutant NSCLC with brain metastases: a real-world comparison analysis
Authors: Fei Qi, Yuzhu Chen, Yi Han, Peng Jiang, Siyu Lei, Xingyu Du, Yuan Gao, Fan Zhang, Juan Du, Hongxia Li, Tongmei Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOptimal first-line treatment for epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutant NSCLC with brain metastases (BMs) has not been fully defined. We aimed to compare different first-line tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) combination therapies and monotherapy for these patients in a real-world setting, especially in the era of 3rd generation TKIs.MethodsPatients with EGFR-mutant NSCLC and BMs receiving first-line EGFR-TKIs from 2019 to 2023 were retrospectively reviewed (n = 150): TKI alone (n = 73), TKI plus brain radiation (TKI + BRT, n = 25), TKI plus bevacizumab (TKI + BEV, n = 38) and TKI plus chemotherapy (TKI + CT, n = 14). Intracranial progression-free survival (iPFS), PFS, overall survival (OS), responses and adverse events of each group were compared and survival beneficial population were explored.ResultsAmong enrolled patients, majority (70.7%) received 3rd generation EGFR-TKIs and 29.3% received 1st /2nd generation drugs. The median iPFS of TKI alone group was significantly inferior than those in TKI + BRT, TKI + BEV and TKI + CT groups (22.1 vs. 32.9 months vs. not reached [NR] vs. NR, p = 0.001). The median PFS and OS were 11.1 and 40.1 months, 18.9 and 67.1 months, 16.5 and 38.8 months, 39.8 and 79.8 months, respectively, with TKI alone, TKI + BRT, TKI + BEV and TKI + CT (PFS, p = 0.014; OS, p = 0.015). The combination therapies brought iPFS and PFS benefits over TKI alone regardless of TKI generations. A linear correlation was demonstrated between iPFS and OS (R2 = 0.508, p < 0.001). Multivariate analysis revealed that the addition of BRT (HR = 0.375, 95%CI, 0.176–0.802, p = 0.011) or bevacizumab (HR = 0.294, 95%CI, 0.141–0.611, p = 0.001) significantly reduced intracranial progression risk. Whereas, the combinations of TKI and RT (HR = 0.326, 95%CI, 0.128–0.834, p = 0.019) or chemotherapy (HR = 0.083, 95%CI, 0.010–0.666, p = 0.019) were independent factors for favorable OS. No significant difference on survival or toxicity was observed among the three combination therapies.ConclusionEGFR-TKI combination therapies improved iPFS, PFS and OS than TKI monotherapy for patients with EGFR-mutant NSCLC and BMs irrespective of TKI generation or combination strategy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-026-16193-x.Keywords: NSCLC, Brain metastases, TKI, Combination, Intracranial control

Introduction

Lung cancer has emerged as the leading cause of cancer incidence and mortality worldwide with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) being the most common subtype [1]. The epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) represents one of the most common driver oncogenes of NSCLC [2]. Approximately half of Asian patients harbor EGFR gene mutations and 36.5%–40.3% of Chinese patients have EGFR mutations [3, 4]. The presence of EGFR mutations not only influences the prognosis and treatment strategies but also correlates with a higher propensity for brain metastases (BMs) [5, 6]. It was reported that 31.4%–64.7% of EGFR-mutant NSCLC patients developed BMs, whereas it accounted for only 19.5%–35.3% for EGFR wild-type cases [7–9]. Due to the limited effectiveness of systemic therapies in crossing the blood-brain barrier (BBB) and the complex brain microenvironment, patients with BMs usually have inferior survival and quality of life.

Targeted therapy has revolutionized the treatment landscape for NSCLC and also opened up new therapeutic avenues for patients with BMs [10–15]. Novel or 3rd generation EGFR-TKIs significantly reduced risk of central nervous system (CNS) progression versus 1st /2nd generation TKIs [12–14, 16]. In FLAURA study, the median intracranial progression-free survival (iPFS) was not reached (NR) with osimertinib and 13.9 months with 1st /2nd generation TKIs (HR, 0.48; 95%CI,0.26–0.86, p = 0.014) [16]. However, while targeted monotherapy has shown significant promise, its efficacy in treating BMs and managing acquired resistance remains a subject of ongoing investigation, highlighting the urgent need for more comprehensive treatment strategies for patients with BMs.

Combination strategies of EGFR-TKI and other treatment have been attempted in treating EGFR-mutant NSCLC with BMs [17–20]. Several studies reported improved iPFS of TKIs combined with brain radiation (BRT) than TKIs alone or BRT alone in patients with BMs [17–19, 21]. However, Jiang reported no survival benefit of adding brain whole brain radiotherapy (WBRT) to TKIs [22]. Besides, several prospective or retrospective studies reported the efficacy of dual inhibition of EGFR and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGR) in patients with BMs, though the conclusion remained controversial [23–27]. Treatment of EGFR-TKIs combined with systemic chemotherapy was another strategy. The GAP BRAIN study, a randomized multicenter phase 3 clinical trial, demonstrated gefitinib plus chemotherapy significantly improved iPFS (15.6 vs. 9.1 months, HR = 0.36, 95%CI, 0.25–0.53, p < 0.01), PFS (16.3 vs. 9.5 months, p = 0.002) and OS (35.0 vs. 28.9 months, HR = 0.65, 95%CI, 0.43–0.99, p = 0.04) compared with gefitinib alone for EGFR-mutant NSCLC with BMs. However, it is noteworthy that most of these studies were based on 1st /2ed generation TKIs. In the era of 3rd generation or novel TKIs, which present higher CNS or BBB permeability and better intracranial tumor control [11, 16, 28], whether combination therapy is still necessary is a question worth exploring.

Therefore, we conducted this real-world study, among EGFR-mutant NSCLC patients with BMs receiving TKIs as first-line treatment, in an effort to compare the efficacy and safety profile of TKI combination strategy versus TKI monotherapy in both 1st /2ed generation and 3rd generation TKI era.

Materials and methods

Patient eligibility

In this retrospective study, lung cancer patients with BMs who were diagnosed and treated at Beijing Chest Hospital between 2019 and 2023 were screened. Finally, a total of 150 eligible patients were enrolled (Fig. 1). The inclusion criteria were: (1) histologically/cytologically diagnosed NSCLC; (2) BM(s) confirmed by brain enhanced CT/MRI scan at diagnosis; (3) EGFR mutations verified by next generation sequencing or super-ARMS sequencing using tumor specimens; (4) EGFR-TKIs as first-line therapy; (5) complete treatment process, response assessment, and available follow-up data. Patient exclusion criteria included: (1) no baseline brain scan; (2) BM(s) during treatment process; (3) TKIs as non-first-line therapy; (4) incomplete information or lost follow-up.

Flowchart of patient selection. NSCLC = non-small cell lung cancer; SCLC=small cell lung cancer; EGFR= epidermal growth factor receptor; TKI= tyrosine kinase inhibitor; BRT=brain radiation; BEV=bevacizumab; CT=chemotherapy

Treatment

All enrolled patients received first-line EGFR-TKIs and were allocated into four groups according to different treatment regimens: TKI alone group, 73 patients who received EGFR-TKI monotherapy; TKI + BRT group, 25 patients receiving first-line EGFR-TKIs and brain radiotherapy; TKI + BEV group, 38 patients receiving EGFR-TKIs and bevacizumab; TKI + CT group, 14 patients undergoing EGFR-TKIs and systemic chemotherapy (Fig. 1).

A total of 44 patients had 1st /2nd generation EGFR-TKIs including icotinib (125 mg po tid; n = 28), gefitinib (250 mg po qd; n = 7), erlotinib (150 mg po qd; n = 1), afatinib (20-40 mg po qd; n = 7), and dacomitinib (30 mg po qd; n = 1). The remaining 106 patients received 3rd generation TKIs included osimertinib (80 mg po qd; n = 68), furmonertinib (80 mg po qd; n = 28) and amatinib (110 mg po qd; n = 10).

Either WBRT (3.0 Gy/f, 10 F) ± simultaneous integrated boost (SIB, 4.0 Gy/f, 10–15 F)or SRS (4–6 Gy/f, 5–10 F) was chosen according to patient’s condition. Radiotherapy schedule: VMAT 6MV-Xray P-CTV: 30 Gy/3Gy/10f, P-GTV: 40 Gy/4Gy/10 f. 5 treatments per week (Mon-Fri). The prescribed dose of SRS varies according to the longest BM diameter. Among the 25 patients in the TKI + BRT group, 7 patients had WBRT and 18 cases had SRS. For patients in TKI + BEV group, bevacizumab was administrated intravenously at a dose of 7.5 mg/kg, 21 days per cycle till the progression of disease or intolerable toxicity. For patients received chemotherapy, pemetrexed was given at a dose of 500mg/m2 on d1 and carboplatin (AUC = 5) on d1, 21 days per cycle, 4–6 total cycles followed by pemetrexed maintenance. Patients had dose adjusted when encountering adverse reactions according to doctors’ advice.

Data collection and response assessment

Patient clinicopathological, mutational information, treatment and prognostic data were collected and reviewed. Treatment responses were assessed one month after TKI initiation and every 2–3 months thereafter using blood routine, ultrasound examination, CT/MRI scan and nuclear medicine examination. Overall and intracranial responses of each patient were assessed according to Criteria (version 1.1) [29].

Statistical analysis

The primary endpoints were the median iPFS, PFS, OS; and the secondary endpoints were intracranial objective remission rate (iORR), intracranial disease control rate (iDCR) and treatment-related toxicity. PFS was defined as the time interval from EGFR-TKI initiation until disease progression or death from any reason. iPFS was calculated from the date of EGFR-TKI initiation to the date of intracranial progression or death from any reason, regardless of extracranial status. ePFS was calculated from the data of EGFR-TKI initiation to the date of extracranial progression or death from any reason, regardless of intracranial disease. iORR was defined as the proportion of patients classified as complete remission (CR) and partial remission (PR) of intracranial lesions. iDCR was defined as the proportion of patients who had intracranial CR + PR+stable disease (SD). Survival was analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared using a log-rank test. Univariate and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression was also performed. All statistical analyses were used SPSS 24.0 software. A two-tailed p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics of the enrolled patients

A total of 150 eligible patients were enrolled in this study. Patient baseline characteristics were summarized in Table 1. The median age at diagnosis was 58.5 ± 9.9 years and the male to female ratio was 1:2.19. Majority of patients were non-smokers (83.8%) without neurological symptoms (93.3%). EGFR exon 19 deletion (19DEL), 21 exon L858R mutation and other alternations were identified in 52.7%, 38.0% and 9.3% of patients, respectively. At diagnosis, 43.3% had one intracranial lesion, and 36% had 2–5 CNS metastases. Apart from BMs, more than half of patients had lung or bone metastases simultaneously. Amongst, 36, 8 and 106 cases received 1st, 2nd and 3rd generation TKIs as first-line therapy, respectively. Baseline characteristics were well balanced between the four treatment groups (Table 1).

Intracranial, extracranial and overall survival outcome

Till the last visit in November 2024, the median follow-up period was 28.8 (25.7–38.0) months. For the entire cohort of patients, the median iPFS, PFS and OS were 33.0 (27.4–38.6), 15.6 (11.2–20.0) and 46.7 (26.0-67.5) months. Patients who received EGFR-TKI combination treatment had superior intracranial tumor control than TKI monotherapy. The median iPFS were 22.1, 32.8 months, not reached (NR) and NR (p = 0.001; Fig. 2A), and the PFS were 11.1, 18.9, 16.5 and 39.8 months (p = 0.014, Fig. 2C) with TKI alone, TKI + BRT, TKI + BEV and TKI + CT, respectively. Patients who received TKI combination treatment had prolonged OS than those receiving TKI alone (67.1 vs. 38.8 vs. 79.8 vs. 40.1 months p = 0.015, Fig. 2D). However, no survival benefit on extracranial disease was observed between each group (Fig. 2B). There were no significant survival differences on iPFS, PFS and OS within the three combination groups (p > 0.05).

We then subsequently compared survivals of TKI combination versus TKI monotherapy stratified by TKI generation. Combination treatment significantly reduced the hazards of intracranial and systemic progression in both 1st /2nd generation (iPFS, HR = 0.605, 95%CI, 0.400-0.915, p = 0.004, Fig. 2E; PFS, HR = 0.569, 95%CI, 0.344–0.956, p = 0.036, Fig. 2G) and 3rd generation TKI era (iPFS, HR = 0.544, 95%CI, 0.367–0.806, p = 0.002, Fig. 2I; PFS, HR = 0.455, 95%CI, 0.238–0.867, p = 0.031, Fig. 2K). Besides, more favorable OS was achieved with 1st /2nd generation TKI combination than monotherapy (39.8 vs. 35.3 months, p = 0.021; Fig. 3H). There was a tendency of prolonged OS of combination therapy versus monotherapy with 3rd generation TKIs, though the difference did not reach statistical significance (NA vs. 43.8 months, p = 0.171; Fig. 3L). In 3rd generation era, TKI combination therapy yielded superior ePFS than monotherapy (33.8 vs. 16.9 months, p = 0.038; Fig. 2J). However, extracranial disease control was comparable in 1st /2nd generation era (19.7 vs. 15.5 months, p = 0.780; Fig. 2F).

Analysis of patient benefiting from TKI combination therapy than TKI alone

Subgroup analysis revealed that majority of patients had CNS benefit from EGFR-TKI combination therapy than TKI alone irrespective of gender, age and smoking status (Fig. 3). Patients with EGFR-19DEL or 21L858R rather than other alternations had significant iPFS benefits. Besides, patients with limitated number of CNS lesions achieved superior iPFS from TKI combination therapies than TKI alones (CNS lesion 1, HR = 0.419, 95%CI, 0.183–0.960, p = 0.040; CNS lesion 2–5, HR = 0.358, 95%CI, 0.154–0.833, p = 0.017; Fig. 3); whereas, for those with more than 5 CNS lesions, monodrug or combination had similar survival outcomes (Fig. 3). In terms of OS, age < 60 years (HR = 0.359, 95%CI, 0.171–0.755, p = 0.007), one CNS lesion (HR = 0.302, 95%CI, 0.116–0.790, p = 0.015) or EGFR-19DEL (HR = 0.273, 95%CI, 0.111–0.671, p = 0.005) was associated with favorable survival of EGFR-TKI combination therapy (Fig. 3).

Multivariate analysis of risk factors affecting iPFS, PFS and OS

We subsequently analyzed risk factors of iPFS, PFS and OS. Risk factors affecting survivals by univariate Cox regression (p < 0.1) were then included into multivariate analysis. The presence of CNS symptom was associated with unfavorable PFS (Table 2; Fig. 4A). However, OS was comparable regardless of CNS symptom (Table 2; Fig. 4B). Furthermore, results revealed that the combination of EGFR-TKI and BRT or chemotherapy were independent indicators for favorable iPFS, PFS and OS (Table 2). In addition, the combination of EGFR-TKI and bevacizumab was associated with improved iPFS (HR = 0.294, 95%CI, 0.141–0.611, p = 0.001) and PFS (HR = 0.566, 95%CI, 0.341–0.939, p = 0.028) but not OS (HR = 0.636, 95%CI, 0.309–1.312, p = 0.221). Patients with more CNS lesions were proved to had a tendency of inferior survivals than those with only one intracranial metastase (Table 2; Fig. 4C). Noteworthy, the combination therapies of EGFR-TKI and BRT, or chemotherapy achieved much more favorable OS among patients with limited CNS lesions (Fig. 4D-F).

Linear association between iPFS and OS

Regression analysis revealed linear correlations between median iPFS and OS (R2 = 0.508, p < 0.001; Fig. 4A), and iPFS rate and OS rate (R2 = 0.903, p < 0.001; Fig. 5B). Likewise, ePFS was positively correlated with iPFS of each patient as well (R2 = 0.476, p < 0.001; Fig. 5C). It was demonstrated that the iPFS improvement of TKI combination therapy over TKI monotherapy translated into OS benefits among patients in this study (Fig. 5D).

Response analysis

For the entire cohort of patients, the ORR, iORR and eORR were 79.3% (119/150),72.0% (108/150) and 66.7% (100/150), and the DCR, iDCR and eDCR were 96.0% (144/150), 98.7% (148/150) and 96.7% (145/150), respectively. We subsequently analyzed the responses in different treatment groups (Supplementary Table 1). TKI combination strategies achieved higher intracranial remission with iORR of 61.6% with TKI alone, and 88.0%, 81.6% and 71.4% of TKI + BRT, TKI + BEV and TKI + CT, respectively (p = 0.032). It was not surprising that patients receiving 3rd generation TKIs had favorable intracranial response over 1st /2nd generation TKIs (iORR, 79.2% vs. 54.4%, p = 0.005; iDCR, 100.0% vs. 95.5%, p = 0.085).

Treatment-related adverse events

In this study, patients were well-tolerated to treatment. Most of the treatment-related adverse events were mild including grade 1–2 rash (12.7%, 19/150), liver dysfunction (12.0%, 12/150), Paronychia (10.0%, 15/150), neutropenia (10.0%, 15/150) and diarrhea (6.7%, 10/150). Severe (grade 3–4) rash and neutropenia were documented in 2.7% (4/150) and 0.7% (1/150) of patients. The incidences of severe rash (14.3%) and myelosuppression (7.1%) were slightly higher in patients in the TKI + CT group; however, the difference did not reach any statistical significance (Supplementary Table 2).

Discussion

EGFR-TKI has been the gold standard treatment for patients with EGFR-mutant NSCLC [12–14]. Despite a good control rate of primary lesions, most traditional 1st /2nd generation EGFR-TKIs have limited CNS penetration resulting unsatisfied intracranial disease control. Combination strategies of traditional TKIs and BRT, antiangiogenic therapy or systemic chemotherapy have been attempted for EGFR-mutant NSCLC with BMs. However, in the era of 3rd generation EGFR-TKI, the definite role of combination strategy has not been fully explored in these patients yet. Besides, no study has compared the efficacy and safety of these combination strategies at present.

This study compared the efficacy and safety profile of EGFR-TKI combination treatment versus monotherapy in both 1st /2nd generation era and 3rd generation TKI era for EGFR-mutant NSCLC patients with BMs. It revealed that the first-line combination of TKIs and BRT, bevacizumab or chemotherapy improved iPFS, PFS and OS, regardless of TKI generations. Patients with limited number of CNS metastasis tended to benefit more from TKI combination therapies, indicating intensive treatment for these patients. Additionally, our study demonstrated the improvement of iPFS of combination therapy translated into OS benefit. Furthermore, no significant survival or safety difference was observed among the three combination strategies. Our results suggested that these combination strategies were effective and safety treatment options for first-line treatment of these patients.

It remains controversial of survival benefit of combination of BRT and TKIs [18, 19, 30–32]. Magnuson demonstrated that upfront SRS and WBRT followed by TKI achieved superior iPFS (23 vs. 24 vs.17.0 months, p = 0.025) and OS (46 vs. 30 vs. 25 months, p < 0.001) compared to TKIs [30]. However, several analyses demonstrated that the addition of BRT to TKIs, compared with TKIs alone, did not improve iPFS and led to similar or worse OS in NSCLC with EGFR mutation and BMs [22]. Differences in patient population, radiation plans, sequence of BRT and TKI and efficacy assessment might account for this controversy. In our study, patients in the TKI + BRT group had superior iPFS, PFS and OS than those in the TKI alone group, indicating that TKIs might be potent radiosensitizers with synergistic effect of combined therapy. It is recognized that BRT leads to early and delayed focal disruption of the BBB, from one week after radiation initiation to one month after radiation completion, which might account for the prolonged long-term survival of TKI + BRT [33].

NEJ026, CTONG1509 and BEVERLY studies have demonstrated reduced progression risk of bevacizumab plus erlotinib versus erlotinib alone in Japanese, Chinese and Italian populations (HR = 0.41–0.92) [23, 25, 26]. Small molecular anti-vascular agent apatinib was demonstrated to bring survival benefit in combination with gefitinib than gefitinib alone (PFS, 13.7 vs.10.2 months, HR = 0.71, p = 0.019) [34]. For patients with BMs, bevacizumab combined with traditional 1st /2nd generation TKIs was associated with improved iPFS, PFS and OS in a recent retrospective study of 172 untreated EGFR-mutant NSCLC with BMs [27]. However, WJOG9717L study failed to exhibit the efficacy of osimertinib plus bevacizumab for improving the PFS among patients with EGFR-mutant NSCLC as first-line treatment [24, 35]. In the present study of EGFR-mutant NSCLC with BMs, the combination of bevacizumab and TKIs achieved superior iPFS (HR = 0.294, 95%CI, 0.141–0.611, p = 0.001) and PFS (HR = 0.566, 95%CI, 0.341–0.939, p = 0.028) in comparison with TKIs alone, suggesting the synergistic effect of anti-angiogenic therapy and EGFR-TKIs on intracranial lesions.

Combination of TKIs with systemic chemotherapy have been designed in EGFR-mutant NSCLC based on the hypothesis that chemotherapy may largely eliminate TKI-resistant clones overcoming primary and secondary resistance [36–41]. In the NEJ009 Study, among patients with metastatic EGFR-mutant NSCLC, gefitinib plus pemetrexed and carboplatin yielded better ORR and PFS than gefitinib group (ORR, 84% vs. 67%, p < 0.001; PFS, 20.9 vs. 11.9 months, HR = 0.490, p < 0.001) [42]. The GAP-BRAIN Study further demonstrated the better intracranial ORR (85.0% vs. 63.0%, p = 0.002), iPFS (15.6 vs. 9.1 months, p < 0.001), PFS (16.3 vs. 9.5 months, p < 0.001) and OS (35.0 vs. 28.9 months, p = 0.04) in gefitinib plus chemotherapy over gefitinib alone for EGFR-mutant patients with BMs [41]. The survival benefit of adding chemotherapy to TKIs was also demonstrated in 3rd generation era [37, 43, 44]. CNS efficacy analysis of patients with BMs in FLAURA2 Study demonstrated improved intracranial disease control with HR for CNS progression or death of 0.58 (95%CI, 0.33–1.01) in comparison to osimertinib monotherapy [45]. Consistently, results in the present study also demonstrated the addition of chemotherapy to EGFR-TKIs improved iPFS and PFS, irrespective of TKI generations. Meanwhile, the incidences of grade 3–4 rash and neutropenia were slightly higher in TKIs plus chemotherapy than TKIs alone in the present study. However, this result should be interpreted cautiously since the sample size of TKI + CT group was quite small, and meanwhile patient selection bais of this retrospective study accounted for the low incidence of AEs of combination therapies.

EFGR-TKI is the backbone for the treatment of EGFR+ NSCLC with BMs. Given the limited efficacy of EGFR-TKI monotherapy for lung cancer patients with BMs, multiple studies have been conducted on dose-escalation of TKIs or the use of other novel agents in treating BM new lesions or progression. Osimertinib at 160 mg was demonstrated as a feasible therapeutic option for patients with isolated intracranial progression on the standard 80 mg dose [46]. Besides, Amivantamab and Lazertinib had shown satisfactory anti-tumor activity in patients with new or progressive CNS metastases after prior therapy with median PFS and OS of 5.8 and 14.7 months, respectively [47].

Conclusions

Our study demonstrated that the combinations of EGFR-TKIs and BRT, bevacizumab or chemotheray might be one of the effective treatment options for lung cancer patients with BMs. However, further large-scale prosepctive studies are warrant in the future to make a definative conclusions.

Limitations

Our study has several limitations. Patient selection bias is inevitable due to the retrospective nature of this disease. Patients who received EGFR-TKI alone were sightly elder than those in the combination groups. Besides, the sample size of patients in the TKI + CT group was comparably small and the conclusion of this study might not be solidly concrete. The exact role of TKI combination therapy needs to be explored in large-sample prospective studies.

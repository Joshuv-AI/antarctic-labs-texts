Characteristics and outcomes in atorvastatin therapy for chronic subdural hematoma: a national, observational real-world study in China, 2019–2024
Authors: Tao Liu, Zhihao Zhao, Jiao Wang, Xiaoying Chen, Jinhao Huang, Weiwei Jiang, Yunhu Yu, Xide Zhu, Kaijie Wang, Kun Lin, Hu Qin, Baixiang Peng, Guohe Zhang, Zhiyong Liu, Weiliang Chen, Jun Shen, Baozhi Chen, Shengjie Li, Mingqi Liu, Wanqiang Su, Wanhai Ding, Chunhua Hang, Wenhua Fang, Feilong Yang, Rui Zhu, Wai Sang Poon, Jianning Zhang, Rongcai Jiang, Craig S Anderson
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundThe incidence of chronic subdural hematoma (cSDH) is increasing in the aging population worldwide, where the success of conventional surgical decompression is complicated by high rates of complications and recurrence. The anti-inflammatory properties of atorvastatin make it an attractive, low-cost, and safe medical approach, but limited evidence exists regarding its clinical benefit in cSDH. We aim to evaluate temporal trends, disease characteristics, and clinical outcomes associated with atorvastatin treatment for cSDH in real-world settings in China.MethodsThis was a multicenter registry study of the Chronic Subdural Hematoma (MR-CSDH) in China. Adults received either 20 mg of atorvastatin or not daily for 8 weeks and were followed up for an additional 6 months at 61 medical centers between November 1, 2019 and October 30, 2024. The primary outcome was good functional outcome defined by modified Rankin scale scores of 0–3 at 6 months. Secondary outcomes included good clinical outcomes and recurrence at 3 months, mortality and recurrent SDH at 6 months, healthcare costs, suspected statin-related adverse events, and length of hospital stay. Logistic regression and propensity score matching (PSM) were used for analysis. The trial was registered with Chinese Clinical Trial Registry, identifier ChiCTR2200057179.FindingsA total of 2422 patients were included (mean [SD] age: 70.3 [14.5] years; 453 [18.7%] female), of whom 1777 (73.4%) received atorvastatin therapy. The proportion of cSDH patients receiving conservative treatment tripled, rising from 14.7% in 2019 to 53.4% in 2024 (P for trend <0.001), while the proportion undergoing surgical treatment significantly declined (P < 0.001). Moreover, the proportion of cSDH patients prescribed statin medication showed a steady temporal increase. Compared to patients who did not receive atorvastatin, those treated with atorvastatin had a higher likelihood of good functional outcome (odds ratio 1.93, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.29–2.89), lower risk of mortality (hazard ratio 0.24, 95% CI 0.06–0.92) at 6 months, and reduced length of hospital stay (mean difference [MD] −1.69, 95% CI [−3.01, −0.37], P = 0.012) and decreased hospitalization costs (MD −0.79, 95% CI [−1.31, −0.28], P = 0.003). However, the between-group differences in recurrent SDH (at 3 and 6 months) and adverse events were not significantly different.InterpretationThis study indicates a positive association between atorvastatin use and good clinical outcomes in patients with cSDH, and is increasingly being used in China.FundingThis study was funded by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (No. 82071390, 82001323, 82101434), the Science and Technology Project of Tianjin (No. 19YFZCSY00650), Tianjin Key Medical Discipline (Specialty) Construction Project, and the Zhao Yi-Cheng Medical Science Foundation.Keywords: Chronic subdural hematoma, Atorvastatin, Real-world study

Research in context

Evidence before this study

Chronic subdural hematoma (cSDH) primarily affects older adults, with patients in low- and middle-income countries often facing significant financial barriers to surgery. As a result, identifying effective non-surgical treatments is crucial, particularly for patients who are unsuitable candidates for surgery. This issue has become a key focus of global research. Atorvastatin therapy has shown potential in this patient population, and many hospitals, especially in China, have adopted it as a primary conservative treatment. However, there is a lack of large-scale, real-world clinical studies comparing the outcomes of atorvastatin treatment to no treatment in patients with cSDH.

Added value of this study

Between 2019 and 2024, the number of cSDH patients prescribed statin medications in China increased. This nationwide cohort study, involving 2422 patients with cSDH, found that atorvastatin treatment was significantly associated with better functional outcomes and lower mortality compared to patients who did not receive atorvastatin.

Implications of all the available evidence

These findings suggest that atorvastatin may be a viable therapeutic option for patients with cSDH, offering potential benefits in terms of improving functional outcomes and reducing mortality.

Introduction

Chronic subdural hematoma (cSDH) is one of the most common neurovascular conditions encountered in neurosurgery, and its incidence is particularly high in older people.1 The annual incidence of cSDH is estimated to reach 17.4 per 100,000 worldwide by 2030 due to the continued aging of populations and use of antithrombotic agents for cardiovascular prevention.2 Thus, cSDH will surpasses brain tumors as the most prevalent neurosurgical condition, with evidence to suggest it is increasing in young people from the widespread use of antiplatelet and anticoagulant drugs.3 However, limited data exist on the temporal trends, disease characteristics, and outcomes for cSDH in various population.

The standard treatment for cSDH involves surgical evacuation of the hematoma, but the outcomes are suboptimal,4, 5, 6 with at least one in five patients either dying or left with severe disability.7,8 Moreover, studies have shown that up to one-third of patients experience recurrent SDH after surgery, and approximately one-third of older patients die within a year.9, 10, 11, 12 In a fee-for-service healthcare system, surgical treatment can impose a heavy financial burden on patients and their families due to its high direct and indirect costs.3 In this context, there is an urgent need to identify efficient and cost-effective non-surgical treatment strategies.

Atorvastatin has the potential to alleviate inflammation, promote angiogenesis, and protect meningeal lymphatic drainage function.13, 14, 15 These effects may accelerate hematoma absorption and improve the prognosis for cSDH, making it a potentially viable, low-cost, non-surgical treatment option for cSDH. Several studies provide some insight into the potential benefits of atorvastatin in cSDH. In particular, a multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized trial involving 200 cSDH patients in China showed that patients treated with atorvastatin 20 mg daily for 8 weeks experienced a greater reduction in hematoma volume, better neurological outcomes, and a lower rate of delayed surgical intervention compared to the control no-atorvastatin group.16 Systematic reviews have also highlighted the potential for atorvastatin to improve outcomes and prevent recurrent SDH.17,18 The clinical need has driven interest and research on cSDH, which in turn has increased popularity in the use of atorvastatin to improve the recovery of patients in China. However, comprehensive data on the effectiveness and safety of atorvastatin in the real-world populations is limited.19 Herein, we present results of our multicenter, nationwide, prospective cohort study to determine temporal trends, disease characteristics, and clinical outcomes in the use of atorvastatin for patients with cSDH in China between 2019 and 2024.

Methods

Study design and patients

Although the non-surgical treatment of cSDH, including the use of atorvastatin, often forms part of an integrated treatment approach, there are no standardized protocols in practice and significant heterogeneity exists in the treatment schedules are being applied across patients. This study is part of the Multicenter Registry Study of the Chronic Subdural Hematoma (MR-CSDH), an ongoing multicenter registry study to better understand the clinical characteristics, treatment modalities, and outcomes for patients with cSDH in China.20 In brief, adult patients (age ≥18 years) with a diagnosis of cSDH and completeness of follow-up were included in this report. Patients missing essential baseline demographic, clinical, and imaging data; those unable to complete the trial due to inadequate adherence; and those in a critical condition with a prognosis of near end-of-life were excluded from the study. The study design and flow diagram of study cohort enrollment are provided in Fig. 1. A total of 61 medical centers in China participated in the study (eTable 1).

Flow diagram of patients into the study.

Atorvastatin therapy of 20 mg daily was initiated at admission after clinical assessment by the attending physician, with regular monitoring of hepatic and renal function during the treatment period. If any drug-related adverse events were identified, the medication was discontinued for observation. After discharge, patients extended atorvastatin therapy for up to eight weeks or longer. We conducted follow-up via telephone to confirm actual treatment duration. Adhering to the intention-to-treat (ITT) principle, we retained patients who discontinued the medication for various reasons such as serious adverse events or poor adherence.

The study was funded by the National Natural Science Foundation and the study protocol was centrally approved by the Ethics Committee of Tianjin Medical University General Hospital (IRB2022-YX-007-01). All participants provided written informed consent. The reporting is in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines.21

Outcomes

The baseline clinical variables that were collected included demographics, medical visits and history, medication history, clinical severity, physical measurements, and brain imaging features, as outlined in the assessment schedule (eTable 2). Additionally, details of all essential investigations and treatments were also recorded. The primary outcome was good functional recovery at 6 months, defined according to scores 0–3 on the modified Rankin scale (mRS). Secondary outcomes included good clinical outcomes (mRS 0–3) and recurrent SDH at Day 90, mRS score, worsening rate, all-cause mortality and recurrence at Day 180, health care costs, including any expense incurred during the provision of medical services to inpatients, suspected treatment-related adverse events (such as liver and kidney dysfunction, hyperglycemia, and infection) during the hospital stay, and length of hospital stay. The mRS score at Day 180 was analyzed as an ordinal outcome (shift across all categories). Worsening rate was defined as any increase on the mRS score at 6 months compared to the baseline mRS score. Recurrence was defined as reappearance of a symptomatic ipsilateral CSDH requiring reoperation, additional intervention, or hospital readmission during the study period. For patients with bilateral hematomas, recurrence on either side was considered a recurrence. Decisions over management and repeat brain imaging were at the discretion of the attending physicians. All data were collected by clinical staff with queries resolved through discussions with clinical monitors. All data were collected through a digital service platform system (https://login.medbit.cn) and uploaded and monitored according to the visit structure.

Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics of the study population by atorvastatin treatment were presented using the Student's t-test (for normally distributed variables), Mann–Whitney U test (for non-normally distributed variables), and chi-square tests (for categorical variables). Multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed to estimate the odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence interval (CI) for the association of statin therapy and binary outcomes. The model was adjusted for age, sex, body mass index (BMI), Glasgow coma scale (GCS) score, conservative treatment, use of glucocorticoids, hematoma volume, midline shift, separated cSDH, hypertension, heart disease, diabetes mellitus, medication history of antithrombotic drugs, and D-dimer level. For covariates measured multiple times, only the initial measurement at admission—prior to the initiation of atorvastatin therapy—was used in the analysis to avoid potential time-related bias. E-value was calculated to assess the robustness of results to unmeasured confounders.22 Cox proportional hazards regression was used to estimate the association by hazard ratios (HR) with 95% CI between statin group/control group and the all-cause mortality at 6 months, using the same covariates in the model. The Kaplan–Meier method was performed to plot cumulative hazard of all-cause mortality over 6 months according to groups with or without statin therapy. Multivariable linear regression was employed to estimate the mean difference (MD) and 95% CI for continuous outcomes with the same covariates. In line with the intention-to-treat principle, all patients who discontinued statin therapy were retained in their originally assigned group. Additionally, we performed a sensitivity analysis based on the per-protocol population, excluding patients who discontinued treatment due to adverse events.

To further minimize potential confounding between groups, propensity score matching (PSM) analysis was undertaken based on individual propensity scores estimated from logistic regression models, where patients were 1:1 matched with a 0.25 SD caliper for the logit of propensity scores without replacement (matched pairs were only formed if their logit scores differed by no more than 0.25 SD, and each treated individual was matched to one unique control without replacement).23 The standardized mean differences (SMD) for all covariates were used to assess the balance before and after PS matching, with an absolute value > 0.2 indicating a meaningful imbalance.24 PSM was conducted using known or suspected confounders associated with outcomes, as well as variables that exhibited significant baseline imbalances between the treatment groups. Variables incorporated in the PSM model included: age, sex, race Han, main occupation, residence classification, initial assessment location, current smoker, alcohol consumption, hypertension, heart disease, previous stroke, respiratory disease, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, medication history of antithrombotic drugs, hypoglycemic drugs, antihypertensive drug, and glucocorticoids, BMI, hematoma volume, hematoma density, midline shift, septated CSDH, GCS score, TC, LDL, HDL, D-Dimer, conservative treatment, glucocorticoids, and time interval. The R package “Matchit” was applied for PSM, and the package “cobalt” was used to conduct balance tests on matched variables.25

Stratified analyses were conducted to explore the influence of sex, age, BMI, low density lipoprotein (LDL) level, conservative treatment, previous stroke, hypertension, heart disease, diabetes mellitus, separated cSDH, hematoma volume, and GCS score in the association between statin and the primary outcome, with any interaction tested by adding interaction terms to the models. To avoid a substantial impact arising from missing values, the analyses were repeated using the missForest method26 and multiple imputation,27 with data considered missing at random (MAR) used covariates. For multiple imputation, 30 imputation datasets were generated using multivariate imputation by chained equations (MICE) algorithm with predictive mean matching (PMM).28 Finally, the imputed results were combined using the Rubin's rule.29 Given that the missForest method outperforms the MICE with PMM (MICE-PMM) approach in PS analysis when dealing with MAR and MAR nonlinear missing mechanisms,30 our PSM was performed on data filled by missForest. A standard significance level of two-sides P <0.05 was used. All analyses were undertaken using R 4.3.2.

Role of the funding source

The funders had no input into the design and conduct of the study, collection, management, analysis, and interpretation of the data, preparation, review, and approval of the manuscript, and the decision to submit the manuscript for publication.

Results

After excluding patients who were lost to follow-up or had missing clinical information, a total of 2422 patients were included (Fig. 1). Patients meeting the inclusion and exclusion criteria and having complete 6-month follow-up data were admitted to a hospital as early as November 2019.

Fig. 2A and B describe the patterns of treatment with statins for cSDH in China by province over time. From 2019 to 2024, the proportion of cSDH patients undergoing conservative treatment tripled, increasing from 14.7% in 2019 to 53.4% in 2024 (P for trend <0.001, Fig. 2C), with a significantly decline in the frequency of surgical treatment, from 85.3% in 2019 to 46.6% in 2024 (P < 0.001, Fig. 2C). Most noteworthy is that by 2024, the use of statin medications for either conservative treatment alone or as an adjunct to surgery in cSDH had reached 91.7% (Fig. 2C).

Based on factors such as economic development, infrastructure, population size, and social resources, Chinese cities are classified into five tiers. First-tier cities typically represent the most developed and competitive cities in China, while second-tier cities have rapidly emerged under certain economic and social conditions, with strong potential for further development. “Others” refers to cities whose economic and social development levels are slightly lower than those of first-tier and second-tier cities, but still possess a certain level of influence within their respective regions.

“Low density” refers to the hematoma presenting a lower X-ray absorption rate on the CT image, typically appearing as a darker area. “Isointense” means that the density of the hematoma is similar to that of the surrounding normal brain tissue, making it difficult to differentiate the hematoma from normal brain tissue on the CT image. “High density” generally refers to the hematoma showing a higher X-ray absorption rate on the CT image, appearing as a brighter area.

“Separated cSDH” refers to the presence of multiple small septa or partitions within the hematoma cavity in chronic subdural hematoma (CSDH).

Patients who received statin therapy had significantly better clinical outcome (mRS 0–3) at 6 months than statin non-users in both before and after PSM analysis (OR 3.74, 95% CI 2.20–6.35 for before PSM; OR 1.93, 95% CI 1.29–2.89 after PSM), as shown in Table 2. Discussion

In this large-scale, nationwide cohort study conducted in China, we observed a steady increase in the use of atorvastatin for the treatment of cSDH. Patients who received 20 mg of atorvastatin daily for at least 8 weeks after onset had a significantly higher likelihood of achieving a good functional outcome at 6 months, without a corresponding increase in adverse events. In addition, atorvastatin was associated with reduced healthcare costs and lower 6-month mortality, although it appeared to have little effect on recurrence. These findings were robust across multiple sensitivity analyses controlling for potential confounders and missing data. Collectively, the results indicate that atorvastatin, as a safe and effective therapeutic option, has been widely adopted in China within the Western Pacific region, conferring multiple benefits for patients with cSDH. This experience may merit further investigation on a global scale, with the potential to help alleviate the substantial worldwide burden of cSDH.

Statins are primarily known for their lipid-lowering properties, but there is growing evidence that their anti-inflammatory, endothelial-protective, and neuro-repairing abilities may influence the subdural environment.31, 32, 33 One randomized controlled trial undertaken in China has shown that atorvastatin can promote hematoma absorption and improve neurological function, according to the Markwalder grading scale, GCS scores, and the Barthel index measure of basic self-care activities of daily living at 24 weeks.16 A retrospective cohort study found that atorvastatin was associated with a lower rate of deterioration and the need for additional burr-hole drainage surgery in cSDH patients treated conservatively.34 Several meta-analyses suggest that statins can promote hematoma absorption and reduce SDH recurrence.17,35,36

Although these studies suggest a benefit of statin treatment in cSDH, their effect on improving long-term outcome in real-world clinical settings is unclear. In clinical practice, the management of cSDH is often multimodal, encompassing both pharmacological therapy and surgical intervention. While burr-hole drainage remains the standard surgical approach, it is associated with a notable risk of recurrence. Recent attempts to enhance this approach—such as intraoperative irrigation or adjunctive middle meningeal artery embolization (MMAE)—have demonstrated only modest additional benefits.37 A recent RCT evaluating surgical treatment for cSDH reported favorable outcomes (mRS score 0–3) in over 90% of patients across both intervention and control groups, thus exceeding the efficacy historically observed.38 Notably, more than 90% of patients in this trial also received statins therapy.

In our study, we found that the use of atorvastatin is steadily increasing for the treatment of cSDH patients in China. In addition, atorvastatin use (≥8 weeks) is associated with a good clinical outcome at 6 months and lower overall in-hospital costs and all-cause mortality after cSDH. These findings further indicate that atorvastatin could be a safe and cost-effective non-surgical treatment for cSDH.

Considering the aging demographic and clinical profile of cSDH patients, the benefits of statins may have broad and impactful benefits that extend beyond merely that of hematoma resorption. In exploratory analysis with multiple imputation and RF imputation, we observed that statin users had a significant reduction in renal related adverse events, suggesting that statins may have an additional potential to improve or protect renal function. A post-hoc analysis of RCTs covering over 30,000 patients found that, in cardiovascular high-risk populations without severe kidney disease, the use of atorvastatin was associated with a gradual improvement in renal function in a dose-dependent manner, with the highest dose (80 mg/day) yielding the greater effects.39 Moreover, a meta-analysis of 33 clinical trials that evaluated the impact of statins on renal function in patients with chronic kidney disease suggested that statins may delay the progression of chronic kidney disease through the outcomes of reduced urinary albumin and protein excretion or increasing creatinine clearance.40 Another study found that statins significantly reduced albuminuria and urinary albumin excretion, being most pronounced in patients with type 2 diabetic nephropathy.41 Studies have also shown that statin therapy is associated with low rates of contrast-induced nephropathy, regardless of baseline renal dysfunction.42 All this evidence suggest that statins may influence the prognosis of patients with cSDH through the ‘kidney–brain axis’, which lays a solid foundation for future pharmacological guidelines and clinical trials.

Additionally, our subgroup analysis found that atorvastatin might be more effective in separated cSDH patients or in those with large hematomas, which is near consistent with other studies.16 Separated cSDH, is a distinct subtype that involves a complex interplay of recurrent bleeding, inflammation, and angiogenesis within the subdural space, and often results in a poor prognosis and higher recurrence post-treatment.43 As in cases of large hematomas, separated cSDH is a predictor of unfavorable outcomes.44 Our findings therefore underscore the importance of individualized treatment for cSDH patients. Further research on the effects of atorvastatin across different subgroups within a broader cSDH cohort could guide more personalized treatment approaches.

With the increasing use of atorvastatin in cSDH patients, researchers have observed that a subset of patients remains unresponsive, regardless of the dose or treatment duration. Dosage customization, medication regimens, and combination therapies require further exploration. Consequently, strategies to enhance the efficacy of statins are being investigated, including the combination of atorvastatin and low-dose dexamethasone in cSDH.45 Further mechanistic research reveals that dexamethasone enhances anti-inflammatory and anti-angiogenic effects of atorvastatin by increasing the numbers and modulating function of macrophages in the hematoma.46 Studies have shown that cSDH hematomas can be absorbed through meningeal lymphatic vessels (mLV) and deep cervical lymph nodes (dCLNs). A prospective study showed that cSDH patients have impaired mLV function on the hematoma side, and that a higher postoperative improvement rate of mLV drainage function is associated with a lower hematoma recurrence.47 Another study found that cranio-cervical manual lymphatic drainage (cMLD) can promote hematoma absorption and improve the efficacy of atorvastatin-based conservative treatment in cSDH patients.48 However, due to the inability to quantitatively perform this technique and the lack of a clear, quantifiable execution process, it has not yet been widely adopted.

Strengths of this study include the population-based design, large sample size, comprehensive range of data, and various statistical approaches to control for the influence of confounding in an observational study. Nonetheless, there are several limitations that should be emphasized. First, as our study is an observation cohort, there is still the potential for measured and unmeasured confounding, indication bias, and impact of missing data despite the use of multiple statistical methods. However, we performed sensitivity analyses to evaluate the influence of unmeasured confounding. The E-values calculated for both primary and secondary outcomes indicated that the findings of our study for were robust. Second, we could not investigate the relationship between dosage, treatment duration, and prognosis as most patients received only 20 mg/day of atorvastatin for at least 8 weeks. Anecdotal evidence and review of the literature suggests this is the most common treatment regimen used in practice.16,19,49 However, we cannot exclude the possibility that different dosages or treatment durations of atorvastatin might yield different outcomes. At present, the use of atorvastatin in the treatment of cSDH is complex. It may be administered as a standalone therapy, in combination with other agents as part of conservative management, or as an adjunct to surgical intervention.38 In this study, we primarily considered atorvastatin as a foundational treatment and evaluated its effectiveness and safety in a real-world clinical context. Future studies focusing on the specific patterns and regimens of atorvastatin use are warranted to further validate our findings. Third, patients in a critical condition with a prognosis of near end-of-life were excluded from the study. Thus, all findings of this study are only applicable to adult CSDH patients who were not at immediate life-threatening risk. For patients in near end-of-life conditions, management decisions should be guided by clinical judgment and existing practice recommendations,3 as the evidence from our study does not provide sufficient basis for this population. Fourth, the low incidence of death in this study resulted in wide confidence intervals for the regression coefficients; therefore, caution should be taken in generalizing the death-related findings in this study. Fifth, in the process of hematoma characterization, most participating centers reported primarily whether the type of hematoma was separated, rather than applying the full classification system.44,50 Therefore, we were unable to conduct further analyses on the relationships between other hematoma subtypes and clinical outcomes including recurrence. We plan to incorporate more standardized imaging evaluation protocols in future prospective studies to address this limitation.

In summary, our real-world national multicenter study suggests there are beneficial effects of atorvastatin in the management of cSDH in China. These results suggest that integrating atorvastatin into standard treatment protocols for cSDH could enhance patient outcomes and reduce mortality rates without imposing additional adverse impacts. However, further research, ideally randomized, is warranted to confirm these findings, and for studies to define the underlying therapeutic mechanisms for the potential treatment effect. National and international cooperation on research will accelerate the optimization of treatment strategies for this increasing patient population, which will ultimately improve their prognosis and reduced healthcare costs in the future.

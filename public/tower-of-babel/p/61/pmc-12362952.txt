Genome-wide identification and characterization of the ubiquitin-specific protease (USP) gene family in cattle: primary analysis of muscle-specific USP genes and their influence on myogenesis
Authors: Yufei Zan, Junliang Li, Fengcheng Song, Yixuan Tang, Yuxuan Xin, Zhepei Zhang, Linjuan Wang, Lupei Zhang, Liang Du, Zhengrong Yuan, Xue Gao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe ubiquitin-proteasome system(UPS) is a critical biological pathway that regulates protein function and plays a pivotal role in muscle formation. Nevertheless, the current comprehension of the ubiquitin-specific protease (USP) family, an important component of the UPS, in relation to bovine myoblast development remains relatively limited. This study aims to characterize the bovine USPs and conduct a preliminary analysis of their function, to provide valuable insights for enhancing beef yield and quality.ResultsA comprehensive genome-wide analysis was conducted to explore the genetic characteristics of the USP family, which is categorized into 13 unique categories. The genetic homology of the USP family between cattle and other related species was discovered through collinearity analysis. Notably, cattle muscle tissue exhibits high expression levels of USP2, USP13, USP19, USP25, USP28, USP38, USP47, and USP53. Furthermore, the expression patterns of these genes during myogenic cell differentiation can be categorized into 4 distinct types. By designing small interfering RNA(siRNA) to interfere with the expression of USP2, USP28, USP38, and USP47 in the myogenic cell, the silencing of these genes reduced the proliferation capacity of the myogenic cell. Meanwhile, the silencing of USP2, USP28, and USP38 facilitated myogenic cell differentiation, while USP47 exerted an opposing effect.ConclusionsIn this study, a total of 53 members of USPs were successfully identified in Bos taurus. SiRNA interference experiments revealed that USP2, USP28, USP38, and USP47 are implicated in the regulation of myogenic cell proliferation and differentiation. These findings lay the foundation for further investigation into the precise roles of USPs in governing myoblast behavior and hold promise for enhancing beef quality.Keywords: USP, Muscle development, Deubiquitination, Myoblast proliferation and differentiation, Cattle

Background

The improvement of living standards and changing consumption patterns have led to an increasing preference for beef among individuals, indicating a rising demand [1]. Beef, a high-protein, low-fat, and low-cholesterol source of quality meat, not only offers a delectable taste but also provides essential nutrition with its abundance of various amino acids and trace elements required by the human body [2]. The ability of muscle development directly affects the weight gain, texture, and taste of beef. Muscle fibers serve as the fundamental unit of animal muscle tissue, and their quantity and type significantly impact the meat production capacity and quality of livestock [3]. The development of muscle fibers is a multifaceted biological process involving skeletal muscle cell proliferation, myotube fusion, and muscle fiber thickening, all of which are collectively regulated by various internal cellular mechanisms [4].

In particular, the ubiquitin-proteasome system(UPS) represents a crucial biological pathway that governs protein function. The UPS includes ubiquitin-activating enzyme (E1), ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme (E2), and ubiquitin ligase (E3). By conjugating ubiquitin (Ub) to target proteins, the UPS tags them for degradation via the 26S proteasome [5]. It is noteworthy that ubiquitination represents a reversible biochemical process. The deubiquitinase (DUB) can cut downstream protein-linked Ub and protect them from degradation by 26 S proteasome [6]. The DUB family is categorized into 5 subfamilies: USPs (ubiquitin-specific proteases), UCHs (ubiquitin carboxy-terminal hydrolases), OTUs (ovarian tumor proteases), MJDs (Machado-Joseph disease protein domain proteases or josephins) and JAMMs (JAB1/MPN/Mov34 metalloenzymes) [7]. The USPs constitute approximately 60% of all DUBs and form the largest and most diverse subfamily within the DUB family [8]. The USPs play a significant role in various biological processes in vivo, including cell proliferation, differentiation, and biological activities. USPs can regulate the process of cancer, inflammation, and other diseases by deubiquitination, regulating autophagy, cell cycle, and other biological activities [9]. For instance, USP7 can stabilize the downstream substrate p53 through the deubiquitination reaction, thereby impeding tumor cell proliferation and triggering apoptosis [10]. USP8 directly interacted with and deubiquitinated SQSTM1 (sequestosome 1) to regulate the autophagy activity of cells [11]. Meanwhile, USP1 can facilitate hepatocellular carcinoma progression by modulating mitochondrial fission and metabolic reprogramming via cyclin-dependent kinase 5 stabilization [12]. In addition, USPs also have a certain function in the regulation of inflammation [13]. USP11 can prevent the interpretation of LPA1 (lysophosphatidic acid receptor 1), and enhance the pro-inflammatory effect of LPA1, thus aggravating pneumonia. Recent studies have demonstrated that knocking down or drug inhibition of USP11 mitigated LPS(lipopolysaccharide)-induced lung damage in mice [14]. USPs also play an indispensable role in the regulation of obesity, and both USP15 [15] and USP19 [16] serve as key factors in adipogenesis. It is worth noting that USP21 is recognized as a significant candidate gene affecting subcutaneous fat deposition in Holstein cattle [17]. Therefore, conducting a comprehensive genome-wide analysis of the bovine USP family is crucial due to its significant role in various biological activities.

Relevant evidence strongly supports the pivotal role of the UPS in muscle development. For instance, E3 ubiquitin ligases MuRF1 (Muscle RING finger 1) and MAFbx (muscle atrophy F-box) are widely recognized as valuable markers in studies related to skeletal muscle atrophy [18]. Especially, the absence of MuRF1 can lead to an increase in the thickness of muscle fibers in Duroc pigs, thereby enhancing the lean meat ratio without impacting their growth performance [19]. In addition, The FBXO40(FBXO protein 40) is classified as the key component of ubiquitin ligase, capable of inducing insulin receptor substrate 1(IRS1) ubiquitination in skeletal muscle and thereby restricting IGF1(Insulin-like growth factor 1) signaling to regulate the proliferation of skeletal muscle cells [20]. FBLX22( F-box and leucine-rich protein 22), a member of the F-Box family, has also been identified as an E3 ubiquitin ligase and facilitates muscle atrophy [21]. Although DUBs and E3 ubiquitin ligase have antagonistic effects, their role in muscle development has been limited.

Current research has explored the role of USPs in skeletal muscle development and injury repair, accumulation of 26 S proteasomes, and expression changes in various USPs have been observed in age-induced sarcopenia [22]. Notably, USP10, USP14, USP19, and USP45 have been implicated in regulating muscle fiber type transformation in Chinese perch [23]. Furthermore, several USPs including USP2, USP13, USP18, USP19, USP21, USP28, and USP47 play significant regulatory roles during muscle development [24–30]. Specifically, USP2 and USP21 can influence mitochondrial activity and ATP(Adenosine Triphosphate) synthesis while controlling energy metabolism to regulate skeletal muscle cell proliferation and differentiation [24, 28]. Moreover, USP19 and USP21 also impact mouse muscle development through insulin signaling regulation [26, 28]. Collectively, this body of evidence underscores the indispensable role of the deubiquitination process in governing muscle proliferation and differentiation.

Although the role of E3 ubiquitin ligase in regulating muscle development has been partially validated in cattle, there is a lack of reports on the functions of USPs [31]. Therefore, a comprehensive genome-wide analysis was conducted to investigate multiple USPs across diverse cattle breeds. The study provides valuable insights into the USPs that exert influence on myogenic cell proliferation and differentiation, thereby contributing to future research endeavors aimed at enhancing muscle development in beef cattle within the field of cattle breeding.

Materials and methods

Materials

Sequence information and transcriptome data were obtained from:

UniProt website (https://www.uniprot.org/);

NCBI website (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/);

Ensembl website (http://useast.ensembl.org/info/data/ftp/index.html);

GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/).

Tissue and cell sources: This experiment selected the Pingliang Red cattle herd, which was raised in Pingliang City, Gansu Province, China, and was allowed to graze and drink freely. 3 castrated bulls with similar slaughtering ages (30 months) and weights (750 kg) were selected as the experimental samples. The cattle were slaughtered according to GB/T 19,477 − 2018, “Operating procedure of livestock and poultry slaughtering—Cattle”. After the animals were slaughtered, the heart, liver, spleen, lung, kidney, longissimus muscle, and adipose tissue were collected and immediately flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen-filled tubes for subsequent total RNA extraction. The myogenic cells were derived from a cell line that had been cryopreserved in the laboratory at -80 ℃ [32].

Genome-wide identification of USPs

51 USP proteins from Homo sapiens and 50 USP proteins from Mus musculus were downloaded from the UniProt. The genome information of Bison bison bison, Bos indicus, Bos mutus, Bos taurus, and Bubalus bubalis were downloaded from the NCBI. The genome information of Bos grunniens, Hybrid Bos taurus, and Hybrid Bos indicus was downloaded from the Ensembl. All USP protein sequences of the 8 bovine subfamilies were identified by the Basic Local Alignment Search Tool (BLAST) function of NCBI, and the incorrect sequences were manually compared and removed.

The physicochemical properties (including molecular weight, isoelectric point, etc.) of the 53 USP proteins in Bos taurus were analyzed using the ProtParam website of ExPASy (https://web.expasy.org/protparam/) [33]. The subcellular localization of 53 USP proteins in Bos taurus was simulated using the CELLO v.2.5 (http://cello.life.nctu.edu.tw/) [34].

Phylogenetic analysis

The sequences of all USPs in 10 species were compared using MEGA(v11.0), and a phylogenetic tree was constructed using the Neighbor-Joining method. The bootstrap repetition number was set to 1000, and the other parameters were set to default values. The resulting phylogenetic tree was adjusted and beautified using Evolview(v2.0).

Structural features analysis

The conserved structural domains of USP proteins were predicted using the Conserved Domain Search(CD-search) function of the NCBI-CDD(v3.21) database and visualized using TBtools(v2.119). The conserved motifs of 53 USP proteins in Bos tarurs were detected in MEME 5.0 and visualized using TBtools(v2.119) [35, 36]. The minimum and maximum number of amino acids in the predicted motifs were set to 6 and 50, respectively, and the number of motifs was limited to 10. The number and location of introns, exons, and Untranslated Regions(UTRs) in USPs were quantified using Generic Feature Format(gff) files and visualized using TBtools(v2.119).

Chromosomal distribution and collinearity analysis

The gene chromosomal distribution map of USP family members in Bubalus bubalis, Bos taurus, Bos indicus, Bos mutus, Hybrid Bos taurus, and Hybrid Bos indicus were marked using TBtools(v2.119). Using the MCScanX toolkit, the collinearity analysis of homologous genes in 6 species of bovine was predicted, and the result was visualized using TBtools(v2.119).

Gene expression analysis by RNA-seq

The expression matrix for each sample in the Bos taurus dataset was downloaded from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158412). The dataset contains expression data for 8 tissues (adipose tissue, cerebellum, cerebral cortex, hypothalamus, liver, lung, skeletal muscle, and spleen). The expression of USPs in different tissues was analyzed using this dataset [37]. The results were visualized and clustered using the heatmap tool in OmicStudio (https://www.omicstudio.cn/tool).

Culture and induction differentiation of myogenic cells

The myogenic cells from our laboratory were thawed and cultured in a controlled environment at a temperature of 37℃ with a carbon dioxide concentration of 5%. The culture medium formula is DMEM/F12 (Gibco, USA), 20% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, USA), and 1% penicillin/streptomycin. The myogenic cells were cultured until they reached 80% confluence, following which were transitioned into a differentiation culture medium. The formula for the differentiation culture medium is DMEM/F12 (Gibco, USA) supplemented with 2% bovine serum (Gibco, USA).

RNA extraction and quantitative real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

The expression characteristics of the USPs family were detected by quantitative real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) in 7 different tissues (heart, liver, spleen, lung, kidney, muscle, and fat) and at 6 different stages of cell differentiation (0, 24, 48, 72, 96, and 120 h) using primers (Table 1).

Quantitative real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) primers

RNA extraction was performed every 24 h from different tissues and cells of beef cattle at various stages of differentiation using TriZol reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) and chloroform. The extracted RNA was then precipitated with isopropanol, resuspended in RNase-free H2O, electrophoresed, quantified, reverse transcribed using the All-in-One First-Strand Synthesis MasterMix kit (Lanbioed Technology, China), and stored at -20℃.

The RT-qPCR reaction mixture was 20µL: Taq SYBR® Green qPCR Premix 10µL, upstream and downstream primers (10µmol/L) 1µL, cDNA (100 ng/µL) 2µL, RNase-free H2O 6µL. The RT-qPCR reaction program was: 95℃ pre-denaturation for 30 s; 95℃ denaturation for 15 s; annealing for 15 s; and 72℃ extension for 60 s, for a total of 40 cycles. β-actin was used as an internal reference gene, and the experiment was repeated 3 times.

Small interfering RNA(siRNA) interference

USP2, USP28, USP38, and USP47 were silenced in myogenic cells by transfection with small interfering RNA (siRNA), and the same non-specific siRNA (Si-NC) served as a control (Table 2). 3 siRNA sequences were designed for USP2, USP28, USP38, and USP47, respectively, and synthesized by the Biological Bioengineering Company (Shanghai, China). Myogenic cells were transfected with small interfering RNA(siRNA) at an initial density of about 40%~50% and were transfected for 8 h with 75 pmol siRNA, 1.5 µL Lipomaster 3000 reagent (Vazyme, China), and 250 µL Opti-MEM (sigma, America) to myogenic cells cultured in 6-well plates. The siRNA with the highest interference efficiency was screened for downstream experiments. After 48 h (80%~90% cell density), RNA was extracted and siRNA interference efficiency and differential expression of proliferation and differentiation marker genes were measured by RT-qPCR.

Cell-Counting-Kit-8(CCK8) assay

The cell suspension was seeded (30,000 cells per well), culture plates were pre-incubated in an incubator for 12 h, 10µL of Cell Counting Kit solution was added to each well, plates were incubated in the incubator for 2 h, and absorbance at 450 nm was measured with a microplate reader.

Statistical analysis

All RT-qPCR results were determined using the 2−ΔΔCt method. 3 independent technical repetitions were performed for each test. Statistical significance was determined using GraphPad Prism 7.0 software.

Results

Identification of USPs

By retrieving genomic data for 8 bovine subgenomes from NCBI and Ensembl databases, and using human and mouse USPs as query sequences, a total of 50 Bos mutus USP genes, 50 Bos indicus USP genes, 50 Bos grunniens USP genes, 53 Bos taurus USP genes, 53 Bubalus bubalis USP genes, 50 Bison bison bison USP genes, 48 Hybrid Bos taurus USP genes, and 48 Hybrid Bos indicus USP genes were identified.

After predicting the physicochemical properties of 53 USP proteins in Bos taurus, it was found that the smallest USP protein (BOSTA-USP3) consisted of only 303 amino acids, while the largest (BOSTA-USP34) consisted of 3,579 amino acids. The molecular weight of the proteins ranged from 34,254.89 (BOSTA-USP3) to 40,757,95.7 (BOSTA-USP34). Of the 53 USP proteins in Bos taurus, 32 were forecasted to be acidic proteins, 4 were estimated to be neutral proteins, and 17 were predicted to be basic proteins. The subcellular localization of most USPs was simulated to be in the nucleus, while BOSTA-USP3, BOSTA-USP18, and BOSTA-USP50 were estimated to be located in the extracellular space, and BOSTA-USP13, BOSTA-USP25, and BOSTA-USP35 were predicted to localize in the cytoplasm (Table 3).

Structural characteristics of USPs in cattle

Each of these USPs contains the conserved Ubiquitin carboxyl-terminal hydrolase(UCH) domain characteristic. Furthermore, multiple evolutionary branches exhibited the zfn zinc finger structure domain, ubiquitin associated(UBA) domain, and Meprin and TRAF homology(MATH) domain that were associated with functional diversification during evolution. The analysis of conserved motifs showed that all USP proteins shared 5 conserved motifs (motif 1, motif 2, motif 3, motif 4, and motif 7), with most possessing a complete set of 10 motifs. Additionally, the gene structure diagram for the USP family illustrated significant variations in introns, exons, coding DNA sequences (CDSs) and Un-translated regions(UTRs) (Fig. 1).

The chromosomal distribution and collinearity of USP genes in 6 cattle species were analyzed. Of the 53 USP genes identified in cattle, all are distributed across 23 chromosomes. Tissue-specific analysis of USPs

To further elucidate the distinct physiological functions of USP genes, this study analyzes the differential expression of USP genes across 8 bovine tissues and conducts a clustering analysis. Notably, multiple USP genes demonstrated elevated expression levels in the cerebellum, while USP2, USP13, USP19, USP25, USP28, USP38, USP47, and USP53 exhibited high expression in muscle tissue. Furthermore, specific expressions of USP4, USP13, USP18, USP36, US52, and USP53 were observed in adipose tissue, whereas specific expressions of USP26 and USP43 were found in the hypothalamus (Fig. 5a). To validate the findings, tissue samples (including heart, liver, spleen, lung, kidney, muscle, and adipose tissue) from Pingliang Red Cattle were collected. The specificity of the selected muscle-specific USP genes was subsequently confirmed through replication experiments, yielding results consistent with the anticipated outcomes (Fig. 5b).

Discussion

Cattle is recognized as a crucial domesticated animal species that plays a pivotal role in providing essential daily nutrition for human consumption. Investigating the regulatory mechanisms underlying bovine muscle development holds significant scientific importance, as it directly impacts both the quantity and quality of beef production. Extensive research has been conducted on the UPS’s involvement in muscle differentiation, particularly focusing on E3 ubiquitin ligase. However, limited attention has been given to exploring the role of USP genes in myogenic cell proliferation and differentiation, especially within Bovids. This study aimed to contribute valuable insights into understanding the evolutionary trajectory and functional significance of USP genes in cattle while promoting advancements in genetic breeding research.

Structural features of bovine USP family proteins and genes

In this study, differences in the number of family members may be related to the replication process of the whole genome of different species. The UCH domain is the most conserved domain of the DUB family and all of the cattle USPs contain this domain. USPs of different branches have various specific domains, such as the MATH domain, UBA domain, and zfn zinc finger domain [38–40]. These domains, when combined with specific protein substrates, perform distinct functional roles. For instance, it has been observed that the acetylation level of the UBA domain modulates its affinity towards the downstream p62 [41]. Motif analysis showed that most of the USP proteins contained 5 conserved motifs, namely motif 1, motif 2, motif 3, motif 4, and motif 7. Most of these USP proteins contain most of the motifs, which indicates a high degree of homology between the USP subfamilies. However, it is worth noting that in the last clade, which contains only 3 genes, USP53, USP54a, and USP54b, which only retain 5 most conserved motifs, may indicate some functional specificity of this clade. These results show that the USPs in the same evolutionary clade tend to show similar gene structure and conserved patterns in motifs.

Phylogenetic Relationship and Collinearity Analysis of USP Family Proteins

The biological functions of evolutionary proteins are more similar, as indicated by the 2 model organisms, human and mouse, and the joint construction of evolutionary trees can better indicate the function of USPs in the bovine subfamily. Phylogenetic analysis divided them into 13 branches, with possibly functional similarities among the enriched USP proteins in each branch. USP1 was clustered into a distinct phylogeneteic branch, suggesting that USP1 functions may be highly overlapping among species. Some studies show that USP1 in mice directly acts on the PI3K-Akt-FOXO-mTOR pathway, and indirectly affects the corresponding mouse muscular dystrophy [42]. Therefore, the USP1 regulation mechanism may also exist in bovine subfamilies. From the analysis of chromosome localization and gene collinearity, the chromosomes among the 6 cattle subfamilies are basically in one-to-one correspondence, especially between yaks and beef cattle and between Hybrid Bos taurus and Hybrid Bos indicus. Although the number of chromosomes (2 N = 50) is different from other species (2 N = 60), there is also a large chromosomal homology between them. This conserved evolution also proves the important role of the USP gene family in life activities.

USPs regulate the development of myogenic cells

DUBs are proteins that recognize and hydrolyze the bonds linking Ub to substrate proteins or other Ub molecules, thereby executing diverse biological functions in living organisms. The dysregulation of DUBs has been implicated in a myriad of human diseases, including the promotion and dissemination of cancer, the onset of inflammatory responses, and the development of muscular disorders. In addition, they regulate biological processes such as embryonic development and muscle production. It is expressed in different developmental stages, including unfertilized eggs, gastrula, neurula, and late tailbud stages. Multiple signaling pathways, such as Hippo pathway effector YAP(Yes-associated protein), e-cadherin, SNAIL, SATB1(special AT-enriched binding protein 1), MAPK(mitogen-activated protein kinases), transcription extension factor A3 (TCEA3), and β-TRCP protein, are regulated by stabilizing their substrate proteins to maintain homeostasis in adult tissues [43]. Hiroshi Kitamura’s research found that USP2 is also associated with defects in myocyte proliferation and differentiation and mitochondrial protection [41]. USP47 plays an important role in different stages of embryonic development and maintenance of homeostasis in adult tissues [44]. USP38 can significantly increase the stability of c-Myc by antagonizing F-Box and WD repeat domain containing 7(FBW7α) and promoting cell proliferation [45].

In cattle, USP16 has been identified as a key regulator of daily milk production and milk fat percentage [46]. This finding is highly significant due to the essential role of precise regulation in the complex process of milk fat production, involving receptor-ligand binding, transporters, enzymes, and dynamic ubiquitination and deubiquitination for activation and degradation [47]. Furthermore, the findings from single-cell RNA-seq and single-cell bisulfite sequencing have revealed that USP7 plays a crucial role in bovine embryo development, with its acetylation modification level being of utmost importance for embryonic development. This parallels the function of the UBA domain following acetylation modification, as p62 also serves as a pivotal factor in embryonic development [48]. Several other studies conducted in cattle have successfully validated the functionality of deubiquitination enzymes across different species, thereby indicating their evolutionary conservation and suggesting a potentially significant role in bovine muscle development.

At least 12 genes associated with the UPS have been demonstrated to regulate alterations in muscle mass or fiber size. Notably, nearly all of these genes encode ubiquitin ligases. Furthermore, DUBs can modulate protein ubiquitination and act antagonistically to ubiquitin ligases. However, the precise role of this enzyme in regulating muscle mass under normal or catabolic conditions remains largely unexplored. The tissue expression patterns of the genes are indicative of the specificity of their function to some extent. 8 muscle-specific genes were identified, USP2, USP13, USP19, USP25, USP28, USP38, USP47, and USP53 through the online public database, and verified the results of the database. At the same time, USP38 and USP47 also have certain expression levels in fat, and this difference may be due to species differences. It has been shown that USP2 and USP21 can affect ATP synthesis in mitochondria and control cellular energy metabolism to regulate the development of skeletal muscle cells [24, 27]. USP19 can regulate the differentiation and regenerative of mouse muscle by affecting the unfolded protein response (UPR) in the endoplasmic reticulum [49]. MiR-145-5p targets USP13, which subsequently inhibits the differentiation and apoptotic of goat primary myogenic cells (GPMs) [25]. Meanwhile, miR-92b-3p increased the cell proliferation of pulmonary artery smooth muscle by inhibiting the expression of USP28 [29]. Moreover, USP47 was able to regulate the apoptotic of rat cardiomyocytes after ischemic injury by regulating the stability of NF-κB, and USP47 was also expressed in an amount in the validation of tissue-specific expression in the heart [43]. USP47 plays an important role in different stages of embryonic development and maintenance of homeostasis in adult tissues as well [40]. It is expressed in different developmental stages, including unfertilized eggs, gastrula, neurula, and late tailbud stages. By designing siRNA to interfere with the expression of USP2, USP28, USP38, and USP47 in myogenic cells to determine the regulatory role of these 4 genes for cell proliferation and cell differentiation of myogenic cells, silencing of these genes all reduced the proliferation capacity of myogenic cells, and silencing of USP2, USP28, and USP38 promoted myoblast differentiation, while USP47 strained this process. At present, Mayuko Hashimoto’s research has confirmed that the knockout of the USP2 causes a defect in the proliferation rate of mouse C2C12 myogenic cells, which is consistent with our findings [24].

Collectively, these findings enhance our comprehension of the physical properties and molecular expression patterns of bovine USPs, thereby contributing to a more profound understanding of the field. However, further investigations are warranted to elucidate the precise biological functions of these genes and their potential regulatory mechanisms in diverse physiological developmental processes, particularly on skeletal muscle growth and development.

Conclusion

A comprehensive genome-wide analysis of the bovis USP family was performed. A total of 503 USPs were identified and divided into 13 distinct branches. Collinearity analysis showed that the USP family was homologous between cattle and other species. By designing siRNA to interfere with the expression of USP2, USP28, USP38, and USP47 in myogenic cells, silencing these genes reduced the proliferation capacity of myogenic cells. In addition, the silencing of USP2, USP28, and USP38 promotes differentiation of myogenic cells. However, USP47 appears to inhibit this process. Therefore, further investigations are warranted to elucidate the specific biological functions of these genes as well as their potential regulatory mechanisms in various physiological developmental processes, particularly skeletal muscle growth and development. The findings of this study lay the foundation for further research on controlling the development of myoblasts, and are expected to help improve the quality and yield of beef.

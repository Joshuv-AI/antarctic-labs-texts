Key indicators for evaluating Iran’s health insurance performance
Authors: Alireza Olyaeemanesh, Amirhossein Takian, Mohammad Effatpanah, Mohammad Mehdi Nasehi, Zahra Shahali, Sahar Kargar, Mohammadreza Mobinizadeh, Efat Mohamadi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPerformance evaluation is a critical aspect of managing organizations, especially in the healthcare sector, where financial and resource management have direct implications on public health. The Iran Health Insurance Organization (IHIO), responsible for covering over half of the Iranian population, faces challenges due to rising healthcare costs and international sanctions. This study focuses on identifying performance evaluation indicators for the IHIO, aiming to provide a systematic framework to assess the organization’s efficiency and accountability, aligning with the national health policies of Iran.MethodsThis study employed a multi-method approach, including content analysis, scoping review, and expert consensus, and was conducted in four phases: (1) content analysis of policies, (2) scoping review of the literature, (3) pooling and screening of indicators, and (4) development of indicator metadata. The content analysis involved examining legal documents and policies governing IHIO from 1994 to 2024. A scoping review was conducted using keywords related to health insurance evaluation, and 40 relevant studies were selected from international databases. The indicators were screened in three round through expert panels and the Delphi method, focusing on technical competence, feasibility, and coherence. The indicators were prioritized and detailed metadata profiles were created for each.ResultsA total of 544 performance evaluation indicators were initially identified. After the first screening, 339 indicators were excluded, leaving 205 indicators classified into 12 thematic areas. Subsequent screening phases further refined this list to 78 indicators, of which 26 were ultimately prioritized based on their relevance to the IHIO’s performance. Among these, the “Percentage of the Population Covered by the Health Insurance Organization” emerged as the highest-priority indicator, demonstrating exceptional technical competence and coherence. These findings underscore the critical role of transparent reporting and strategic resource management in improving performance and promoting equity in Iran’s health insurance coverage.ConclusionThe study successfully identified 26 key performance indicators for evaluating IHIO, highlighting the importance of insurance coverage and financial management. Strengthening collaboration with healthcare providers and improving transparency will enhance public trust and improve the organization’s performance and productivity. The findings emphasize the need for strategic collaboration between IHIO and healthcare providers and suggest improving resource allocation and policy implementation for better healthcare outcomes in Iran.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13561-025-00666-z.

Background

One of the most crucial factors organizations consistently prioritize is performance evaluation. At its core, performance refers to the completion of tasks that an organization is committed to [1]; in management, it signifies the extent to which an organization meets its objectives. Performance evaluation is an integral part of the performance management process, providing a retrospective report on the current state of affairs. These reports, typically tied to specific time periods, are vital for managers and decision-makers, as they must determine the necessary executive and corrective actions based on the current situation, performance evidence, and the data presented [1–3].

The performance evaluation process begins with defining indicators that align with the organization’s objectives, ensuring these indicators accurately reflect progress toward those goals. Identifying these performance indicators is crucial [4]; establishing Specific, Measurable, Assignable, Realistic, and Time-bound (SMART)s criteria makes it easier for managers and policymakers to evaluate the current situation and compare it against organizational goals and standards [5]. To identify suitable indicators, managers should consult job descriptions, strategic programs, field research, interviews, and existing lists of indicators from previous studies. Collaboration with specialists and experts can help standardize these indicators across organizations. In the subsequent stage, data relevant to each metric is collected and analyzed. This information is then organized within a structured framework to facilitate the examination, analysis, and planning of necessary executive and corrective actions [6].

Considering the aforementioned, performance evaluation is a critical component of performance management for both governmental and non-governmental organizations, facilitating their progress toward defined goals [7]. This is especially vital in social sectors like healthcare, where financial resources significantly influence public health and daily life [8, 9]. In Iran, the healthcare financing system is universal, drawing from multiple sources, including social insurance organizations, government support, private insurance, and individual payments, alongside contributions from various industries [8, 10].

The Iran Health Insurance Organization (IHIO) is a key entity responsible for providing social health insurance for more than half of the population [11]. Rising healthcare costs, compounded by international sanctions, have intensified the need for efficient resource management and accountability [12, 13]. Macro health policies emphasize equity and performance transparency, necessitating a robust evaluation framework for IHIO.

Despite the IHIO’s annual performance reports and the existence of studies on health insurance financing and indicators, significant gaps remain. Existing studies have primarily focused on health insurance financing models, cost-sharing policies, and individual performance indicators [14, 15], but few have systematically assessed IHIO’s overall performance against its objectives. For instance, while some studies have looked at the financial sustainability of health insurance schemes in Iran [16, 17], or the impact of sanctions on health outcomes [18, 19], there is limited research dedicated to creating a comprehensive framework for evaluating the IHIO’s performance in terms of its broader goals, such as equity in healthcare access and the transparency of healthcare delivery. As highlighted by a study [20], evaluating performance across a range of indicators, rather than focusing solely on financial aspects, is crucial for addressing the gaps in resource allocation and service quality. Without a systematic and holistic approach to performance evaluation, it is difficult to ensure that the IHIO is aligned with national health priorities, particularly under the constraints posed by sanctions.

This study seeks to address these gaps by developing a comprehensive framework to evaluate the IHIO’s performance. The first aim is to identify and prioritize key performance indicators (KPIs) that capture the multifaceted roles of the organization. These indicators will encompass not only financial and resource efficiency metrics but also factors related to equity in service delivery, patient satisfaction, and transparency. The second aim is to create a robust evaluation framework that can be used to assess how well IHIO meets its objectives, as outlined in national health policies and legal frameworks. This framework will allow policymakers to identify areas for improvement and enhance accountability within the organization. The ultimate goal is to ensure that the IHIO not only delivers healthcare efficiently but also contributes to improving health equity and accountability in the broader healthcare system of Iran.

Methods

This study employed a multi-method approach, integrating qualitative and quantitative methods. Content analysis was conducted to extract relevant data from policies and legal documents, while a scoping review synthesized findings from existing literature. Subsequently, expert panels and the Delphi method were used to refine and prioritize indicators, with quantitative techniques applied for scoring and decision-making (Fig. 1). In this section, details of the methodology for each phase are presented.

The first phase: content analysis of policies

This section of the study utilized qualitative content analysis of documents related to the IHIO for 2024, encompassing policies from 1994 to 2024, including Article 29 of the Constitution. This approach was selected for its significance in comprehensively analyzing legislative and policy documents, thereby identifying the objectives and operational frameworks necessary for assessing the performance of the IHIO. By using this method, we ensure that our evaluation is consistent with national health policies and legal requirements. The research aimed to identify objectives, programs, and activities for monitoring IHIO’s performance by examining and compiling relevant legal documents, regulations, and monitoring processes. All documents were prepared, classified, and reviewed, with key phrases extracted. The analysis identified objectives concerning inputs, processes, and outputs, and summarized the findings in a worksheet detailing document titles, sources, and implementation levels.

Data analysis

To analyze the textual data of documents and policies, qualitative content analysis and a holistic analytical approach were utilized. The steps undertaken for this were carried out in eight stages, which included data collection, familiarization with the data, preparation for coding, coding the data, data reduction, data display, data comparison and conclusion and report presentation (Table 1).

The second phase: scoping review

This stage involved a scoping review [21] of the literature conducted in an international database using specific keywords related to the study’s objectives. This method facilitates a comprehensive synthesis of existing literature and global best practices. It was essential to incorporate international databases, to ensure thorough coverage and capture a wide range of indicators utilized worldwide, thereby enriching the process of indicator selection. Recognizing that relevant policies and indicators might be found in gray literature, searches were also conducted on official government websites and reports related to health insurance performance evaluation. Validity of study: To ensure the validity of the process for adding and removing articles, two members of the research team reviewed the studies. After an initial screening to eliminate duplicates and irrelevant articles, these researchers assessed the remaining articles for their relevance and extractable information. This process was completed through two members of the research team (EM and AO) conducted the information extraction. Both individuals have extensive qualitative research experience, including conducting systematic reviews and policy analyses. Their backgrounds ensured the reliability and accuracy of the data extraction process.

Data Analysis: The texts of the selected articles and studies were analyzed using narrative or thematic analysis. Included studies were thoroughly examined; general information was recorded in a table, followed by a synthesis of the content focusing on the study’s findings. Relevant information was extracted and noted based on the study’s objectives and the criteria outlined for inclusion.

The third phase: indicator selection process

The third phase of this study focused on the systematic selection of performance evaluation indicators for the IHIO. To achieve this, the study implemented a multi-step process involving expert panels and the Delphi method. The utilization of expert panels and Delphi rounds aimed to leverage specialized knowledge and achieve consensus on the relevance and feasibility of the indicators. This approach enhanced the robustness of our evaluation by incorporating multidisciplinary perspectives and facilitating iterative scoring.

Pooling of indicators

In this stage, all identified indicators (extracted from document analysis and the scoping review) were consolidated to create a comprehensive list of performance evaluation indicators for the IHIO. It was important to note that this list may have contained overlaps, as certain indicators could have been repeated due to various policies and multiple studies. For this reason, it was necessary to conduct an initial screening, as a “First round of indicator screening”.

First round of indicator screening

Following the extraction and listing of identified indicators, the first screening was conducted by two researchers based on specific criteria: redundancy (removing repeated indicators), ambiguity (eliminating unclear indicators), and relevance to the IHIO (discarding indicators incompatible with Iran’s system). The screening also involved consolidating some indicators and making editorial revisions. Ultimately, the indicators were classified hierarchically based on thematic similarity, resulting in a structured thematic categorization.

Second round of indicator screening

In the second round of screening, the indicators were evaluated based on the criterion of “Importance and Usefulness” To facilitate this screening, the indicators were presented in a checklist format to experts (Table 3). A checklist was presented to 26 experts with diverse backgrounds in health insurance, policymaking, and health economics. The experts were selected based on their academic qualifications and professional experience. The majority of the experts held advanced degrees, including PhDs, MDs, or MScs in health policy, healthcare management, or related fields. On average, the experts had over 5 years of professional experience in health insurance systems, health policy analysis, and management.

A total of 70 experts participated, including policymakers, academic researchers, and practitioners specializing in health insurance, health policy, and healthcare management. Their expertise ensured a comprehensive and accurate assessment of the indicators.

Multi-criteria decision-making methods were used for data analysis [22]. A decision matrix was created from averaged expert opinions, and the importance of each criterion was determined using the Shannon entropy weighting method. Indicators were then prioritized through a simple weighting method, which involved scaling the decision matrix and calculating scores for each option. This process included forming the decision matrix based on the number of criteria and indicators evaluated.

The weights for the criteria were calculated using the Shannon entropy method, which objectively determines the importance of each criterion based on the variability of scores assigned by experts in previous evaluation rounds. This approach minimizes subjective bias and ensures consistency in the weighting process [23, 24]. The calculated weights for the criteria ranged between 0 and 1, with higher weights indicating greater importance. These weights were normalized to ensure their sum equals 1, allowing for direct comparisons among criteria.

Results

The identification of documents and policies related to the monitoring of the performance of the IHIO led to the recovery of 20 relevant policies. After evaluating these policies, a total of nine were ultimately included in the study (Appendix 1). The number of indicators identified from the relevant documents and macro policies was 256 (Appendix 2).

In the scoping review phase, a total of 205 articles were initially retrieved from the selected databases. After removing 25 duplicates, 180 articles remained for screening. Based on title and abstract screening, 72 articles were excluded due to irrelevance. Finally, 108 full-text articles were assessed, and 40 studies [10, 26–65], were included for data extraction and synthesis according to the inclusion criteria. The study selection process for the scoping review phase is illustrated in the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) flow diagram (Appendix 3). 288 indicators were identified through deep analysis and synthesis during the scoping review phase (Appendix 4); these indicators were extracted from 40 studies included in the scoping review. The selected studies, published between 2005 and 2023, represented a variety of research designs and originated from both high-income and middle-income countries. Most focused on performance evaluation frameworks in health insurance systems, particularly financial efficiency, service coverage, equity, and policy implementation.

In total, 544 indicators were identified from the consolidation of the study’s data sources.

During the initial screening phase, 339 were excluded in the first screening phase based on predefined criteria: duplication, lack of clarity or specificity, and irrelevance to the IHIO’s operational context.

The remaining 205 indicators were categorized into 12 thematic areas (Fig. 2). Based on this classification, the majority of indicators pertained to the topic of “Technical efficiency” (14.6%), while the least were related to “Research and Development” (3.4%) (Fig. 2).

Priority indicators for evaluating performance of IHIO

Based on the study’s findings, an overall assessment using the three criteria applied in the third round of screening showed that 88.4% of the 78 indicators scored above 8 (out of 10) (SD = 1.8), indicating their priority and significance in evaluating the organization’s performance. The thematic frequency of the prioritized indicators (26 indicators) included: Burden of cost and number of visits (5 indicators), Technical efficiency (4 indicators), Financial resources (4 indicators), National health accounts (3 indicators), Health services covered (3 indicators), Population covered (2 indicators), Contracted health care facilities (2 indicators), and one indicator each for Research, and development, Demographic indicators, and others. The highest score was attributed to the indicator “Percentage of the Population Covered by the Health Insurance Organization” (Mean: 9.328, SD: 1.02), while the lowest score was for the indicator “Percentage of Clinics (categorized by public, private, charitable, and others) contracted with the Health Insurance Organization” (Mean: 7.06, SD: 2.39) (Table 6; Appendix 6).

Discussion

This study identified and prioritized performance evaluation indicators for the IHIO, emphasizing the organization’s efficiency and equity. The findings highlight the critical role of insurance coverage, as evidenced by the highest-priority indicator, Percentage of the Population Covered by the Health Insurance Organization. This underscores the importance of ensuring comprehensive coverage to enhance accessibility and organizational effectiveness. In contrast, the relatively low ranking of the Percentage of Contracted Clinics indicates a need to strengthen collaborations with private and charitable healthcare facilities to optimize service delivery. To enhance clarity, the key findings are subsequently discussed under thematic categories.

Equity and access to health care

This study applied Shannon entropy to prioritize performance evaluation indicators, demonstrating its usefulness in complex health system decision-making. A 2018 study using Shannon entropy and Grey Relational Analysis to assess health financing in Eastern Mediterranean Region (EMR) countries, including Iran, similarly highlighted the value of quantitative methods in prioritizing financial and operational metrics [66]. The consistency between our findings and that study reinforces the strength of entropy-based approaches and underscores the central role of financial indicators in improving efficiency and promoting equitable resource allocation in health systems. The emphasis on indicators related to cost burden and service utilization reflects the IHIO’s urgent need for strategic financial planning, particularly under resource constraints. This aligns with international evidence showing that insurance coverage and financial efficiency are closely linked to organizational performance and health equity [67]. Studies from countries such as Turkey, Ethiopia, the UK, and Germany highlight the value of efficient resource use and strategic partnerships with diverse healthcare providers to enhance equity, access and service quality [68, 69].

Financial performance and resource allocation

The emphasis on financial indicators, resonates with global trends in healthcare performance evaluation. Studies in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) highlight that strategic financial management is critical for ensuring sustainability and achieving Universal Health Coverage (UHC) [70, 71]. IHIO’s focus on these indicators underscores its efforts to balance equity with efficiency in a resource-constrained context.

National health accounts are another key dimension in evaluating the performance of health insurance organizations. The government’s share in total health expenditures significantly affects the financial burden on insurance funds like the IHIO. For example, U.S. data from 2021 show that the federal government financed 34% of healthcare costs, while private businesses, state and local governments, and other private sources accounted for smaller shares. Private insurance covered 28% and out-of-pocket payments made up 10% of total spending [72]. However, the relationship between public financing and insurance costs is complex, influenced by coverage levels, plan types, and population characteristics. Insurance policies can also significantly reduce household health spending, especially among low-income groups. For instance, adequate coverage may lower health-related expenses from 45 to 25% of total income for poor households [73]. These insights highlight the importance of including national health account indicators in evaluating insurance performance, particularly for systems with social and governmental mandates.

Demographic trends

Demographic indicators and population coverage are essential for evaluating the IHIO’s performance, as they provide a detailed understanding of the insured population’s characteristics, i.e. age, gender, income, education, occupation, and marital status. Analyzing these variables helps align services with beneficiaries’ needs and supports evidence-based policy formulation. The impact of demographic burden is heterogeneous, with population aging emerging as a major challenge, affecting economic dynamics, disease prevalence, and healthcare utilization. Reduced insurance coverage among aging populations has been linked to significant drops in emergency visits (40%) and hospital admissions (61%) due to unmet needs [74]. Without addressing the health and social care demands of older adults, achieving UHC will remain out of reach. As such, performance evaluation criteria and their weighting must be adapted to the demographic structure of each insurance fund [75]. Insurance policies should account for the specific characteristics of urban and rural populations to reduce disparities in access to and use of health services. Evaluating these differences has become a priority in countries with large, diverse populations [76]. In Iran, this issue has also been formally addressed through the 2004 Social Insurance Regulation for Rural and Nomadic Populations1.

Strategic purchasing and provider relations

Indicators related to contracted centers, regulatory metrics, and operational efficiency measures of the organization were other indices identified in this study. The number, diversity, and type of contracts with these centers regarding the services purchased and the population they cover influence the organization’s performance. The issue of who provides the services is a crucial aspect of strategic purchasing [77]. Regulatory indicators are also reflective of the implementation of existing laws and policies within the organization and its provincial departments; these indicators demonstrate how regulations are monitored and the system’s accountability concerning current policies, ultimately leading to a form of continuous performance control of the organization.

International evidence shows that health service coverage and the extent of population benefit are critical indicators in evaluating insurance performance. Expanding coverage generally improves access, financial protection, and health outcomes. Understanding the drivers of variation in insurance reform outcomes is essential for informing future publicly funded schemes aimed at achieving UHC [70]. Coverage levels for medications, inpatient, para clinical, preventive, and primary care services should be aligned with population needs and prioritized to address the most pressing demands while avoiding waste. Additionally, fair financial contribution across different insurance funds and between urban and rural populations is a key performance criterion in assessing both current performance and future reform options [78, 79].

While international experiences offer valuable insights, their direct applicability to Iran requires cautious interpretation. Structural differences, i.e. the impact of economic sanctions, centralized decision-making, and varying levels of resource availability [80], may limit full contextual transfer. Therefore, adapting international best practices to Iran’s unique environment requires local validation and flexible implementation strategies.

Technical and allocative efficiency

One of the most important dimensions of an organization’s performance is its efficiency. The indicators related to the operational efficiency of IHIO aim to provide metrics for assessing the organization’s efficiency and implementing appropriate interventions when necessary. The share of administrative costs in the purchase of health services, given the characteristics of the IHIO, constitutes about 5–20% of the organization’s total expenses; this is a significant portion for large insurance companies [81]. In the United States, administrative costs account for approximately 15 to 25% of total healthcare costs, amounting to around 600 billion to 1 trillion dollars annually in total public health expenditures in 2019. Billing and coding expenses, physician administrative activities, and insurance administrative costs are primary contributors to these expenses, which should be considered when assessing the efficiency of the organization [82].

In evaluating the impact of insurance company characteristics on the profitability and financial productivity of public insurance companies in Turkey, Ethiopia, the United Kingdom, and Tunisia over a specific period, it was determined that the financial performance of insurance institutions has a positive correlation with the size of the insurance company and the growth rate of premiums [67–69, 77, 83]. These studies demonstrate that in developing countries, more than 50% of health expenditures are devoted to pharmaceutical products. Therefore, leveraging strategic procurement of health services within insurance organizations can significantly impact the targeted coverage and purchasing of these organizations and enhance their performance [69, 71].

Health outcomes and system impact

The ultimate goal of the health system and its dependent subsystems, including the health insurance organizations, is to equitably enhance health levels. In the health outcome indicators, metrics such as the mortality rate of children under five, maternal mortality, and mortality due to non-communicable diseases can reflect the performance of the health system as well as the insurance organizations [84]. The analyzed documents in this study also highlighted this important issue, with a key objective of the analyzed policies being the improvement of public health. Consequently, 11 indicators from the identified set were dedicated to health outcome indicators, although these indicators did not rank high or prioritized during the screening process.

Underrepresented areas: research and long-term capacity building

Despite the methodological robustness of the study, indicators related to education, research, and development were underrepresented in the final prioritized list. This reflects a broader trend in resource-constrained settings, where immediate operational concerns often overshadow long-term innovation. However, experiences from countries like South Korea and Sweden [84, 85] show that investment in research and development yields long-term improvements in healthcare outcomes. This suggests that IHIO should consider gradually integrating more Research and Development related indicators into future evaluation cycles.

Strengths and weaknesses

This study has several strengths, including the relevance of its topic, given the crucial role of health insurance in improving community health. By identifying key indicators, the study aligns with the needs of both the health system and the population, offering valuable insights for enhancing health policies and programs. Its comprehensive methodology—based on document analysis, literature review, and expert consensus—is another notable strength.

However, the limited number of studies on performance indicators, particularly for mandatory social insurance schemes, represents a key limitation. To address this gap, the analysis also included studies on private health insurance. Other limitations include the variable quality of available data and inconsistencies in indicator measurement.

The study acknowledges the impact of external factors i.e. economic constraints and sanctions on IHIO’s performance. Future research should explore these effects, develop resilience-focused indicators, and incorporate end-user perspectives. Priorities also include operationalizing indicators through clear implementation plans, addressing rural health disparities, and leveraging technology, artificial intelligence, and data science to enhance performance measurement and monitoring in Iran’s health insurance system.

Conclusion

This study aimed to identify performance evaluation indicators of IHIO and successfully identified 544 indicators from various sources. Following a screening and prioritization process, 26 indicators were designated as key metrics for assessing the organization’s performance. Among these, the “Percentage of the Population Covered by the Health Insurance Organization” emerged as the most critical indicator, underscoring the importance of comprehensive insurance coverage in achieving equity and accessibility. The overall findings underscore the importance of the breadth of insurance coverage; furthermore, the results indicated that the organization’s financial performance and technical efficiency play significant roles in various areas, such as cost management and optimal resource utilization. These findings highlight the necessity for strengthening collaboration between the organization and health service providers, as well as giving greater attention to educational and research sectors. Additionally, financial management, especially in resource-constrained conditions, requires careful attention and planning.

Utilizing indicators related to demographic and economic characteristics is essential for improving the performance of health insurance organizations and advancing public health goals. The identified and prioritized indicators in this study provide a roadmap to identify strengths, address weaknesses, and enhance transparency and public trust in IHIO. Presenting clear and accessible performance reports strengthens accountability and client relationships. To operationalize these indicators, IHIO should incorporate them into digital dashboards, establish feedback loops, and link key metrics to performance-based contracts, requiring investment in data systems, staff capacity, and alignment with national health priorities.

Recent advances in Artificial Intelligence (AI), data science, and digital technologies offer new opportunities to enhance the performance evaluation of health insurance systems. These tools can improve data analysis, fraud detection, service utilization monitoring, and forecasting. Future efforts should consider how such technologies can be integrated into IHIO’s evaluation processes to support more timely and informed decision-making. Future studies should focus on developing actionable strategies for implementing these indicators, addressing infrastructure and process requirements to ensure successful integration into the IHIO’s performance evaluation system.

To translate these indicators into practice, IHIO should integrate them into digital dashboards, establish feedback loops for decision-making, and link select indicators to performance-based contracts. This will require investment in data systems, staff training, and alignment with national health priorities to ensure consistent and effective use. Future studies should focus on developing actionable strategies for implementing these indicators and should also explore the infrastructural, regulatory, and ethical requirements for integrating AI and data science methods into IHIO’s performance evaluation system. These efforts should also involve key stakeholders, i.e. patients, healthcare providers, and local administrators, to ensure the indicators are relevant, actionable, and aligned with real-world needs.

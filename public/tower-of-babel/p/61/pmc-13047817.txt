Clinical and translational advances in regulatory T cell-based strategies in allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation: highlights from ASH 2025
Authors: Somayeh Yazdanparast, Saeid Abroun, Tahereh Rostami, Saeed Mohammadi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRegulatory T cells (Tregs), winners of the 2025 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, emerged as an unsung giant of peripheral immune tolerance after allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (allo-HSCT). These cells exert influence across four interconnected immunological axes encompassing graft-versus-leukemia (GVL), graft-versus-host disease (GVHD), relapse, and viral infection. This correspondence highlights key advancements in Treg-based interventions in allo-HSCT, ranging from basic research to clinical applications, as presented at the ASH 2025 Annual Meeting. Accordingly, Tregs are used as GVHD prophylaxis via the Orca-T platform and as a therapeutic strategy for steroid-refractory (SR)-GVHD patients. Experimental studies further pave the way to address existing limitations toward next-generation Treg-based cell therapy in allo-HSCT.Keywords: Regulatory T cell, Allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, Cell therapy, Immune reconstitution, Graft-versus-host disease

To the Editor,

Timely immune reconstitution after allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (allo-HSCT) is essential for long-term survival, as it dictates the control of graft-versus-host disease (GVHD), infections, and relapse. In this context, regulatory T cells (Tregs) move beyond conventional immunosuppressive therapy in balancing immune system. This correspondence summarizes recent updates from the 2025 ASH Annual Meeting on Tregs in allo-HSCT, focusing on advancements, limitations, and future directions.

Prophylactic application

Tregs accelerate immune reconstitution by biologically programmed immune tolerance, in contrast to standard pharmacological suppression. The Orca-T, a precision-engineered allograft containing donor Tregs, enables transition from general to precision immunosuppression. Orca-T combined tacrolimus demonstrated safety, feasibility, and effective GVHD prevention while preserving graft-versus-leukemia (GVL) effects, yielding low relapse rate, favorable survival, and reduced non-relapse mortality (NRM) [1]. Compared with the standard allo-graft GVHD prophylaxis regimen (tacrolimus+methotrexate), Orca-T is associated with higher survival and lower NRM [2], and significantly improves quality of life and reduces hospitalization [3]. Orca-T+tacrolimus used prophylactically compared with post-transplant cyclophosphamide (PTCy), was also associated with clinical benefits [4]. Despite these promising findings, Orca-T’s challenges as discussed below, remain before matching the broad applicability and practicality of PTCy.

Therapeutic application

Tregs emerged as a potential cell-based therapy for managing steroid-dependent/steroid-refractory/(SD/SR)-GVHD. Donor-derived Tregs are safe, with minimal or no significant adverse events and infection complications, effectively controlling SR-GVHD and supporting immune reconstitution in haploidentical transplantation [5]. The ASH 2025 moves beyond conventional Treg-based therapy by combining Tregs with memory T cells, which is beneficial for managing viral reactivation and an essential clinical complication [5]. Furthermore, arming Tregs with a CD6 chimeric antigen receptor (CD6-CAR) is a promising targeted therapy for cGVHD without significant cytokine release syndrome (CRS) or immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS) [6].

Limitations

First, manufacturing hurdles encompass the need for centralized/high-precision cell-sorting of Orca-T, scalability and cost-effectivity, donors availability, and complexities in ex vivo isolation and expansion, CAR-directed antigen selection, and exhaustion. Second, regulatory hurdles including compliance with good manufacturing practice (GMP) standards, safety, feasibility, and efficacy, and ethical considerations. Third, safety cancers include over-immunosuppressive activity of Tregs, off-target effect, compromising Tregs identity by immunosuppressive drug and precise trafficking resultant GVL impairment and relapse. Finally, limited clinical data for certain clinical decision-making necessitates further investigations.

To address limitations observed in current clinical Treg platforms, recent preclinical studies have provided context-dependent insights to optimize Treg induction, trafficking, functional stability, and tissue-specific targeting to strengthen persistence-precision-efficacy-safety axis (Table 2).

Induction

The IFNγ-primed, synchronized mesenchymal stem/stromal cells (γSMCs)/γSMC-derived extracellular vehicles and gingerol, exert their immunoregulatory effects in preclinical aGVHD models by promoting FOXP3+Treg differentiation and restoring the Treg/Th17 balance, respectively [7, 8].

Homing

The CXCR4 plays a vital role in anchoring Tregs within the bone marrow (BM) and peripheral niches with enhanced regulatory functions, as BM-specific/BM-like Tregs CXCR4high demonstrate elevated FOXP3 expression [9]. AS well, manipulation of Tregs to enhance CD103 expression improves their gut homing [10], and chemical modification coupled with nanoparticles enable organ-specific delivery [11].

Functional restoration

TIM3 is overexpressed in total and active Tregs in early relapse patients after allo-HSCT. Therefore, TIM-3 blockade with sabatolimab, may restore Tregs dysfunction [12].

Limitations

Experimental studies hurdles encompassing manufacturing, regulatory and ethical considerations required for clinical translation, cost-effectiveness, incomplete understanding of safety, efficacy, and genetic modification, uncertainty in tissue-targeting precision, and unknown potential off-target effect. Finally, next-generation Orca-T or CAR-Treg​ development (arming with CXCR4, TIM-3 blocking, etc.) remains a critical area of investigation.

Concluding remarks and future directions

The data presented at ASH 2025 showed that Tregs may be the old guardians of the graft, but still remain gold. However, the forces for overcoming remaining bottlenecks should be joined by focusing on combination therapy (low-dose IL-2, epigenetic modulator drug, and immunosuppressive agents), genetically modified Treg (CAR-Treg, TCR-Treg, and selective gene knockouts Treg), in-situ Treg manipulation or creating suppurative niche (providing cytokines signals, metabolic reprogramming, and microbiota intervention), and third-party Treg as an off-the-shelf source (like induced-pluripotent stem cell-derived Treg). Finally, what’s next? Are Treg-directed therapies ready for immune-precision and personalized medicine in allo-HSCT recipients? That’s a key question guide future investigations.

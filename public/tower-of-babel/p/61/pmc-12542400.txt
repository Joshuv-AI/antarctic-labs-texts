Efficacy of non-invasive ventilation in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease management: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials
Authors: Jiukang Li, Liping Ran
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundChronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) poses significant challenges in respiratory care, with exacerbations and impaired gas exchange being major concerns. Non-invasive ventilation (NIV) has emerged as a valuable therapeutic approach, mitigating respiratory distress and hospitalization risks without the complications associated with invasive ventilation. This systematic review and meta-analysis aim to consolidate evidence from randomized controlled trials (RCTs) to elucidate the therapeutic benefits of NIV across various stages of COPD, providing insights into its potential to improve patient outcomes.MethodsWe conducted a comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs using PubMed, Embase, Scopus and Cochrane databases that compared NIV with standard care (including oxygen therapy, medications, and nasal cannula oxygen delivery) in patients with COPD at various stages, including stable and post-exacerbation. The primary outcomes of interest were mortality outcomes (MO), intubation and exacerbation outcomes (IAEO), and arterial blood gas outcomes (ABGO). Odds ratios (ORs) and risk ratios (RRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated as summary statistics. Heterogeneity was assessed using the I² statistic.ResultsOur meta-analysis of 16 RCTs comprising 1,369 patients demonstrated significant benefits of non-invasive ventilation (NIV) in both post-exacerbation chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (PECOPD) and stable COPD patients. The studies demonstrated a low risk of bias and exhibited low heterogeneity (I² statistic) due to consistent interventions, comparators, and patient characteristics. Pooled analysis revealed statistically significant reductions in MO [PECOPD: OR 0.52 (0.43–0.64), p < 0.001; stable COPD: OR 0.56 (0.44–0.71), p < 0.001], intubation frequency [PECOPD: OR 0.64 (0.54–0.76), p < 0.001; stable COPD: OR 0.43 (0.31–0.60), p < 0.001], exacerbation frequency [PECOPD: OR 0.47 (0.38–0.59), p < 0.001; stable COPD: OR 0.37 (0.25–0.53), p < 0.001], and PaCO2 levels [PECOPD: RR 0.55 (0.45–0.67), p < 0.001; stable COPD: RR 0.56 (0.42–0.74), p < 0.001], as well as significant increases in PaO2 levels [PECOPD: RR 0.52 (0.43–0.64), p < 0.001; stable COPD: RR 0.61 (0.48–0.77), p < 0.001].ConclusionsOur meta-analysis demonstrates that NIV significantly improves clinical outcomes in patients with COPD, including reduced mortality, intubation frequency, exacerbation frequency and PaCO2 levels and significant increase in PaO2 levels. These findings support the use of NIV as a valuable therapeutic strategy in managing patients with COPD. However, further studies are warranted to refine patient selection and optimize NIV protocols, thereby maximizing therapeutic benefits.

Introduction

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a significant public health concern, affecting millions of individuals worldwide and imposing a substantial burden on healthcare systems [1]. The diagnosis of COPD involves a comprehensive assessment of symptoms, lung function tests (spirometry), and physical examination. The Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) provides guidelines for diagnosis and staging, which inform treatment decisions. According to GOLD criteria, a post-bronchodilator forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV1)/forced vital capacity (FVC) ratio < 0.7 is diagnostic for COPD [2, 3]. Managing COPD requires a multifaceted approach, including pharmacological interventions (such as steroids, bronchodilators, and antibiotics), pulmonary rehabilitation, and ventilator support [4, 5]. Stable COPD is characterized by a period of time when the patient’s symptoms are stable and not experiencing an acute exacerbation [6]. During this period, patients may experience symptoms such as dyspnea, wheezing, and coughing, but these symptoms are typically manageable with medication and other treatments. Conversely, post-exacerbation COPD (PECOPD) patients are those who have recently experienced an acute exacerbation of COPD and are in the recovery phase [7]. These patients are at increased risk of recurrent exacerbations, hospital readmissions, and mortality, and require careful management to prevent further decline and improve outcomes [8].

Non-invasive ventilation (NIV) has been explored as a potential therapeutic approach to improve outcomes in stable COPD and PECOPD patients [9, 10]. NIV’s benefits include reduced morbidity, mortality, and healthcare utilization, as well as improved patient comfort and reduced risk of complications associated with invasive mechanical ventilation [11, 12]. NIV serves as an alternative to invasive ventilation methods, delivering ventilator support (pressure-supported airflow) via a non-invasive interface, such as a nasal, oronasal, or full-face mask, to the respiratory muscles [13, 14]. By avoiding sedation and endotracheal intubation, NIV mitigates problems such as ventilator-associated pneumonia, sinusitis, and local tissue damage, thereby reducing the duration of hospital and ICU admissions [15, 16]. The benefits of NIV in stable COPD and PECOPD patients are thought to be due to several mechanisms, including improved lung function, reduced respiratory muscle fatigue, and increased oxygenation [17]. NIV may also reduce the risk of respiratory failure and hospital readmissions by providing ventilator support during periods of increased respiratory demand [18]. Several studies have investigated the benefits of NIV in stable COPD and PECOPD patients, with promising results [19, 20]. For instance, a randomized controlled trial (RCT) published in the European Respiratory Journal found that NIV improved lung function and exercise tolerance in stable COPD patients, with a significant increase in FEV1 of 120 mL [21]. The study also found that NIV improved exercise tolerance, as measured by the 6-minute walk test.

Another RCT published in the American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine found that NIV improved quality of life and reduced hospital readmissions in PECOPD patients, with a significant improvement in St. George’s Respiratory Questionnaire score (−4.2 points) [22]. The study also found that NIV reduced hospital readmissions by 40% compared to the control group. A meta-analysis published in the Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews found that NIV improved lung function and reduced hospital readmissions in PECOPD patients, with a significant improvement in FEV1 (mean difference: 100 mL) [23]. The meta-analysis also found that NIV reduced hospital readmissions (RR: 0.72, 95% CI: 0.55–0.94). A study published in the Journal of COPD found that NIV improved sleep quality and reduced daytime sleepiness in stable COPD patients, with a significant improvement in Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index score (−2.1 points) [24]. The study also found that NIV reduced daytime sleepiness, as measured by the Epworth Sleepiness Scale. Previous meta-analyses have yielded inconsistent results regarding the benefits of NIV in stable COPD patients, typically defined as those without exacerbations in the past 4 weeks. While long-term or domiciliary NIV use has been associated with reduced mortality and improved quality of life, outcomes such as hospital admissions and gas exchange have been variable [25]. In post-exacerbation COPD patients, NIV has been shown to decrease exacerbation frequency without significant differences in mortality rates or arterial gases [26].

The heterogeneous effects of NIV across different COPD stages and outcomes limit its applicability. Therefore, this systematic review and meta-analysis aim to synthesize evidence from pertinent RCTS [27–42] selected as per the predefined inclusion exclusion criteria to clarify the therapeutic benefits of NIV in various COPD stages, providing insights into its potential to enhance patient outcomes.

Materials and methods

Study selection or inclusion/exclusion criteria

Our analysis encompassed RCTs that compared various NIV devices, including BiPAP and nocturnal ventilation, delivered via different interfaces (full-face, oronasal, or nasal masks), with standard care therapies. These standard care therapies included oxygen supplementation, long-term oxygen therapy (LTOT), pharmacological interventions (such as antibiotics, bronchodilators, steroids, theophylline, and mucolytic agents), or sham NIV. We focused on adult patients (aged 18 years and above) across different COPD phases, including stable COPD, and post-exacerbation COPD (PECOPD). Eligible studies adhered to the Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) criteria for COPD diagnosis, defined by a post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio below 0.7. We excluded non-randomized studies, as well as prospective and retrospective study designs, to ensure a rigorous evaluation of NIV’s therapeutic benefits.

Information sources

A comprehensive systematic search was performed across multiple databases, including PubMed, Embase, Scopus, and Cochrane, spanning publications from 2000 to March 2025. The search was limited to peer-reviewed articles, excluding conference abstracts, and theses.

Search strategy

Our search strategy was multifaceted and comprehensive, incorporating a range of relevant terms to ensure that all pertinent studies were identified. We combined keywords such as non-invasive ventilation, NIV, non-invasive positive pressure ventilation (NPPV), bilevel positive airway pressure (BiPAP), and variable positive airway pressure (VPAP) with terms related to chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, including COPD, pulmonary disease, and pulmonary emphysema. Additionally, we utilized Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms to further enhance the sensitivity of our search. To identify potential studies for inclusion, two reviewers (LZ and CL) conducted an independent evaluation of article titles, abstracts, and full texts. This rigorous process allowed us to carefully assess the relevance and eligibility of each study. In instances where discrepancies arose between the two reviewers, a senior researcher (JY) was engaged to facilitate resolution through discussion and consensus. Furthermore, to ensure that our search was exhaustive, we manually searched the reference lists of relevant studies to identify additional articles that may have been missed through our database search. This supplementary search strategy enabled us to capture a broader range of studies and increase the comprehensiveness of our review. Data extraction process

Following the identification of articles meeting the inclusion criteria, we systematically extracted relevant data using a standardized data extraction form, capturing key study characteristics and outcomes, including study details (author, year, design), intervention and comparator specifics (NIV intervention and control group details), follow-up duration, COPD phase (stable or post-exacerbation), participant demographics (age), exacerbation information (rates, frequency, severity), and physiological measures (PaCO2 levels), ensuring consistency and accuracy in data extraction to facilitate systematic analysis of included studies.

Data items

We analyzed key outcomes, including mortality, exacerbation rates, intubation rates, and arterial blood gas parameters (PaCO2 and PaO2 levels), comparing the NIV intervention group with the control group. To ensure accuracy and reliability, two researchers (LZ and CL) independently extracted relevant data, including patient demographics and event data, from the selected randomized controlled trials (RCTs) using a predefined data extraction form, thereby minimizing errors and ensuring consistency in data collection and analysis.

Risk of bias assessment

To evaluate the methodological quality of the included studies, we employed the Cochrane Collaboration’s risk of bias tool, a widely accepted and validated instrument [43]. This tool assesses several key domains, including randomization, allocation concealment, blinding, and completeness of follow-up, which are critical to ensuring the internal validity of randomized controlled trials. For each domain, we rated the risk of bias as high, low, or unclear, providing a comprehensive assessment of the potential biases that may have influenced the results of each study.

Quantitative data synthesis

We employed a contrast-based analysis to evaluate binary outcomes, including mortality, exacerbation, and intubation rates, as well as continuous outcomes such as PaCO2 and PaO2 levels. For binary outcomes, log odds ratio differences and standard errors were estimated from reported odds ratios and corresponding 95% confidence intervals [44, 45]. Risk ratios with 95% CIs [46] were calculated for continuous outcomes based on extracted event data. Heterogeneity across studies was assessed using the I2 statistic [47], with values indicating low (< 25%), moderate (≈ 50%), or high (> 75%) heterogeneity. Depending on the level of heterogeneity, we used either a fixed-effects model (Mantel-Haenszel method) or a random-effects model (DerSimonian-Laird method) to account for inter-study variability [48]. Publication bias was evaluated using funnel plots and Egger’s test, where the log odds ratio for each study was plotted against its standard error for the mortality outcome [49, 50]. This assessment helped identify potential biases in study reporting. Statistical analyses were conducted using R software (Version 4.0.4) and STATA (Version 15.0), with a significance threshold set at p < 0.05 [51, 52]. RevMan version 5.4 [53] was used to generate risk of bias summaries and diagrams. Binary outcomes were reported as odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals. Subgroup analyses were performed according to COPD stage (stable COPD, post-exacerbation COPD), allowing exploration of potential differences in treatment effects across patient populations.

Results

Identification of studies

A total of 321 records were retrieved through database searching, out of which 243 underwent title and abstract screening. After eliminating duplicates and irrelevant records (n = 65), 178 randomized controlled trials (RCTs) were evaluated for eligibility. Nevertheless, 162 RCTs were disqualified due to factors like unsuitable comparator, intervention, condition, population, absence of necessary outcome, and redundant data. Ultimately, 16 RCTs were incorporated into this meta-analysis. The selection process is depicted in Fig. 1.

Study characteristics

A total of 16 RCTs encompassing 1369 participants met the inclusion criteria, comprising stable COPD (n = 478 participants, 6 RCTs) and post-exacerbation COPD (PECOPD) (n = 891 participants, 10 RCTs). These trials compared nocturnal or home-based NIV to various COPD treatments, including long-term oxygen therapy (LTOT), supplemental oxygen, medications, nasal high-flow therapy, nasal cannula, and sham treatment. The studies featured both male and female participants with varying COPD severity, baseline PaCO2 levels, hypercapnia, recent exacerbations, and differing follow-up durations and NIV usage. Table 2 outlines the specifics of the interventions and controls employed in the included studies. Most trials utilized the BiPAP ventilator system as the NIV device, delivered via nasal, full-face, or oronasal masks.

Characteristics of participants

The studies included patients with stable COPD (n = 6 studies) and patients with PECOPD (n = 10 studies). The average age of patients across all studies exceeded 60 years, with baseline partial pressure of carbon dioxide (PaCO2) levels greater than 6 kPa in most studies, indicating that most patients experienced hypercapnia, a condition characterized by elevated levels of carbon dioxide in the blood.

Bias assessment

A thorough risk of bias assessment was conducted to evaluate the overall quality of the included studies, using a pre-defined questionnaire to examine the methodological rigor of the 16 RCTs in the meta-analysis. The traffic light plot (Fig. 2) and summary plot (Fig. 3) provide a visual representation of the risk of bias, indicating an overall low risk of bias for the meta-analysis. Specifically, 13 out of 16 RCTs showed a low risk of bias, suggesting high methodological quality. However, two RCTs (Braunlich et al. [27] and Cheung et al. [29].) had a moderate risk of bias due to issues with randomization and selective reporting. In contrast, one RCT (Hedsund et al. [32]) had a high risk of bias, primarily due to flaws in outcome measurement. With p > 0.05, Egger’s test rejects the existence of funnel plot asymmetry (Fig. 4) and reveals no overall publication bias.

Evidence synthesis

The results of the statistical analysis of the primary and secondary clinical outcomes are as follows:

Mortality outcome

This meta-analysis provides evidence that NIV significantly reduces mortality rates, with a pooled odds ratio (OR) of 0.52 [95% CI: 0.43–0.64, I² 0.0%, p < 0.001] for PECOPD and a pooled OR of 0.56 [95% CI: 0.44–0.71, I² 0.0%, p < 0.001] for stable COPD, as illustrated in Fig. 5A. Furthermore, Egger’s test (p > 0.05) indicates no significant funnel plot asymmetry (Fig. 5B), suggesting the absence of publication bias.

Intubation frequency

This meta-analysis shows that NIV significantly reduces intubation frequency in the intervention group compared to the control group. The pooled OR for PECOPD was 0.64 [95% CI: 0.54–0.76, I² 15.8%, p < 0.001], and for stable COPD, the OR was 0.43 [95% CI: 0.31–0.60, I² 0.0%, p < 0.001], as shown in Fig. 6A. Furthermore, Egger’s test (Fig. 6B) yielded a p-value above the significance threshold of 0.05, indicating no evidence of funnel plot asymmetry and suggesting the absence of publication bias.

Exacerbation frequency

Our findings demonstrate that NIV substantially decreases exacerbation frequency in the intervention group compared to the control group. Specifically, the OR for PECOPD was 0.47 [95% CI: 0.38–0.59, I² 0.0%, p < 0.001], while for stable COPD, the OR was 0.37 [95% CI: 0.25–0.53, I² 0.0%, p < 0.001], as depicted in Fig. 7A. Moreover, Egger’s test (Fig. 7B) showed a p-value exceeding the significance level of 0.05, indicating no significant funnel plot asymmetry and implying the absence of publication bias.

Discussion

The findings of our meta-analysis underscore the therapeutic benefits of NIV in patients with COPD, particularly in reducing mortality, intubation frequency, exacerbation frequency, and improving arterial blood gas outcomes, which is consistent with previous studies highlighting the efficacy of NIV in managing COPD patients. The reduction in mortality observed in our study aligns with existing literature, such as a meta-analysis by Wilson et al. [54] and a Cochrane review by Osadnik et al. [55], demonstrating that NIV significantly reduced mortality in COPD patients with acute respiratory failure. Our study’s findings on intubation frequency are also supported by previous research, including a randomized controlled trial by Brochard et al., which demonstrated that NIV significantly reduced intubation rates in COPD patients with acute respiratory failure. The decrease in exacerbation frequency observed in our study is consistent with existing evidence, including a meta-analysis by Struik et al. [56] and a study by Köhnlein et al. [57], which found that NIV reduced exacerbation rates and improved survival in patients with stable COPD. The improvement in arterial blood gas outcomes, including reduced PaCO2 levels and increased PaO2 levels, is also supported by previous research, such as studies by Plant et al. [58] and Díaz et al. [59], which demonstrated that NIV improved gas exchange and reduced respiratory acidosis in COPD patients.

The benefits of NIV in COPD patients can be attributed to several mechanisms, including reducing respiratory muscle fatigue, improving alveolar ventilation, and enhancing gas exchange [60]. Despite the benefits of NIV, there are challenges associated with its use, such as mask leaks, device discomfort, and severe hypoxia, which may limit its effectiveness [61]. Furthermore, patient selection and NIV protocol optimization are crucial to maximizing therapeutic benefits. Our study’s findings have important implications for clinical practice, suggesting that NIV should be considered a valuable therapeutic strategy in managing COPD patients, particularly those with acute exacerbations or stable disease. Clinicians should be aware of the benefits and challenges associated with NIV and optimize its use accordingly. Overall, our meta-analysis demonstrates that NIV significantly improves clinical outcomes in patients with COPD, supporting its use as a valuable therapeutic strategy in managing these patients, and further studies are warranted to refine patient selection and optimize NIV protocols.

Our meta-analysis of 16 randomized controlled trials (RCTs) comprising 1,369 patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) demonstrated significant benefits of non-invasive ventilation (NIV) in reducing mortality, intubation frequency, exacerbation frequency, and improving arterial blood gas outcomes, which is consistent with previous studies highlighting the efficacy of NIV in managing COPD patients, particularly in post-exacerbation COPD (PECOPD) and stable COPD patients. The pooled analysis revealed statistically significant reductions in mortality outcomes (MO) for both PECOPD (OR 0.52, 95% CI 0.43–0.64, p < 0.001) and stable COPD (OR 0.56, 95% CI 0.44–0.71, p < 0.001), indicating that NIV is effective in reducing mortality in both patient populations. Similarly, NIV significantly reduced intubation frequency in both PECOPD (OR 0.64, 95% CI 0.54–0.76, p < 0.001) and stable COPD (OR 0.43, 95% CI 0.31–0.60, p < 0.001) patients, highlighting its potential to minimize the need for invasive mechanical ventilation. Furthermore, NIV significantly reduced exacerbation frequency in both PECOPD (OR 0.47, 95% CI 0.38–0.59, p < 0.001) and stable COPD (OR 0.37, 95% CI 0.25–0.53, p < 0.001) patients, which is consistent with existing evidence suggesting that NIV can reduce the frequency of exacerbations and improve quality of life in COPD patients. Additionally, NIV significantly improved arterial blood gas outcomes, including reduced PaCO2 levels in both PECOPD (RR 0.55, 95% CI 0.45–0.67, p < 0.001) and stable COPD (RR 0.56, 95% CI 0.42–0.74, p < 0.001) patients, and increased PaO2 levels in both PECOPD (RR 0.52, 95% CI 0.43–0.64, p < 0.001) and stable COPD (RR 0.61, 95% CI 0.48–0.77, p < 0.001) patients. These findings suggest that NIV is effective in improving gas exchange and reducing respiratory acidosis in COPD patients, which can help to improve patient outcomes and reduce the risk of complications.

Our meta-analysis confirms that NIV is a valuable treatment approach for managing COPD patients, particularly those with post-exacerbation COPD (PECOPD) and stable COPD. NIV significantly improves clinical outcomes, including reduced mortality, intubation frequency, exacerbation frequency, and improved arterial blood gas outcomes. These findings have significant implications for clinical practice, suggesting that NIV should be considered a key component of COPD management, particularly for patients with acute exacerbations or stable disease. By incorporating NIV into treatment plans, clinicians can improve patient outcomes, reduce complications, and enhance quality of life. Further research is needed to refine patient selection, optimize NIV protocols, and investigate long-term benefits. By doing so, we can improve patient outcomes and reduce the burden of COPD on individuals and society.

Strength and limitations

This meta-analysis features several key strengths, including the comprehensive retrieval of data from multiple electronic databases and clinical trial registries, which encompassed a broad range of studies beyond published journal articles. The inclusion of exclusively high-quality RCTs with meticulous documentation of patient follow-up and exit, including reasons for withdrawal, further enhances the study’s rigor. Moreover, the low heterogeneity observed in most meta-analyses suggests that the results are predominantly robust. However, several limitations warrant consideration. Firstly, the potential for publication bias cannot be entirely ruled out, as the search strategy may not have captured all relevant studies, particularly those presented in conference proceedings or abstract books. Furthermore, despite diligent efforts to identify all relevant data and retrieve unpublished information, some data remained inaccessible, which may have impacted the results. Additionally, the limited sample sizes in some trials may have diminished the study’s statistical power to detect certain outcomes. Finally, inherent variability in methodological approaches, measurements, and outcome definitions among studies may have contributed to heterogeneity and impacted the results, despite attempts to standardize definitions. These limitations notwithstanding, our study provides valuable insights into the benefits and challenges of NIV in COPD patients. The findings have important implications for clinical practice and highlight the need for further research to optimize NIV protocols, refine patient selection, and investigate long-term outcomes. By acknowledging these limitations and building on the strengths of our study, future research can further advance our understanding of NIV in COPD management and improve patient outcomes.

Conclusion

Our meta-analysis provides strong evidence that NIV is a valuable therapeutic strategy for managing COPD, yielding significant improvements in clinical outcomes. Specifically, NIV was associated with reduced mortality, decreased intubation frequency, lower exacerbation frequency, and improved gas exchange, as evidenced by decreased PaCO2 levels and increased PaO2 levels. These findings suggest that NIV can be a highly effective treatment approach for COPD patients, particularly those with acute exacerbations or stable disease. By reducing respiratory muscle fatigue, improving alveolar ventilation, and enhancing gas exchange, NIV can help to alleviate symptoms, slow disease progression, and improve quality of life. Furthermore, by reducing the need for invasive mechanical ventilation, NIV can also lower the risk of complications and improve patient outcomes. Despite the promising findings, our meta-analysis highlights the need for further research to refine patient selection and optimize NIV protocols to identify the patient subgroups that derive the most benefit from NIV, determine the optimal duration and intensity of NIV therapy.

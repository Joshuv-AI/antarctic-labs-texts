GhSCAMP13 Negatively Modulates Salt Tolerance Through GhNHX7b‐Dependent Na+ Homeostasis in Cotton
Authors: Haohong Tang, Zhaojie He, Cong Sun, Haonan Shi, Zhan Gao, Yanlong Jin, Wenting Ji, Linfu Feng, Ning Wang, Zunzhe Tian, Guanjing Hu, Jie Sun, Xianpeng Xiong
Published: 2026

Soil salinization severely disrupts plant osmotic regulation, ion homeostasis and metabolic pathways, causing significant yield losses (Chawhan and Chintapalli 2025; Panuccio et al. 2014). As the world's dominant natural fibre crop, cotton exhibits moderate salt tolerance but remains threatened by high salinity (Abdelraheem et al. 2019). Secretory carrier membrane proteins (SCAMPs) are a highly conserved family of tetraspanins regulating vesicle trafficking (Law et al. 2012), with emerging links to plant responses to abiotic stress (He et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2023). However, their precise molecular mechanisms remain unclear and warrant further investigation.

Following the evidence that the GhSCAMP gene family plays a significant role in the cotton salt‐stress response (He et al. 2024), we cloned GhSCAMP13, a salt‐responsive member of this family, from the ‘TM‐1’ cultivar to further characterize its functional role under salt stress. Structural analysis showed that GhSCAMP13, encoded by a 954‐bp CDS, contains the conserved DUF3552 and SCAMP domains (Figure S1A). GhSCAMP13 was preferentially expressed in roots (Figure S1B) and was significantly induced by 200 mM NaCl treatment (Figure S1C). Furthermore, in salt‐tolerant cultivars, GhSCAMP13 expression was down‐regulated after 6 h of salt stress (Figure S1D), implying a potential role as a negative regulator of the salt stress response. Subcellular localization assays confirmed that the GhSCAMP13‐GFP fusion protein primarily localizes to the plasma membrane (Figure S1E).

To evaluate the function of GhSCAMP13 under salt stress, we employed virus‐induced gene silencing (VIGS). Upon 200 mM NaCl treatment, GhSCAMP13‐silenced plants (TRV: GhSCAMP13) exhibited alleviated leaf wilting and chlorosis compared to the control (TRV: 00), alongside higher antioxidant enzyme activities (CAT, POD and SOD) and lower malondialdehyde (MDA) levels (Figures S2 and S3). Three independent CRISPR/Cas9 knockout lines (Ghscamp13‐1/2/3) in the G. hirsutum cv. Jin668 background showed reduced plant height but normal leaf area (Figures S4 and S5). Consistent with the VIGS results, all mutants displayed enhanced salt tolerance (Figure 1A,B), characterized by higher leaf relative water content and dry weight biomass (Figure S6). Conversely, heterologous overexpression (OE) of GhSCAMP13 in Arabidopsis resulted in salt hypersensitivity, including reduced germination rates, shorter primary roots and intensified oxidative damage (Figure 1C, Figures S7 and S8). GhSCAMP13 deficiency attenuated H2O2 and O2− accumulation, whereas its overexpression intensified oxidative stress (Figure S9). Ion content analysis revealed that Ghscamp13 roots accumulated less Na+, maintained higher K+ levels, and possessed a superior K+/Na+ ratio compared to WT, whereas GhSCAMP13‐OE lines displayed opposite trends (Figures S10 and S11). Non‐invasive micro‐test (NMT) further demonstrated that Ghscamp13 roots exhibited significantly higher Na+ efflux and K+ influx than WT (Figure 1D, Figure S12), indicating that GhSCAMP13 negatively regulates salt tolerance by inhibiting Na+ efflux.

Functional analysis of GhSCAMP13 under salt stress in cotton (details provided in Appendix S1).

Given that both GhSCAMP13 and the Na+/H+ antiporter GhNHX7b localize to the PM and regulate sodium homeostasis (Chen et al. 2017; Lin et al. 2005), we hypothesized that they physically interact to modulate salt tolerance. This interaction was validated through LCI, Co‐IP and BiFC assays, confirming their co‐localization at the PM (Figure 1E, Figure S13). To investigate their genetic relationship, we analysed the morphology and ion fluxes of VIGS‐silenced cotton lines under salinity (Figure S14). Under control conditions, all genotypes grew normally; however, salt stress triggered severe leaf wilting and chlorosis in all lines except TRV: 00/Ghscamp13 (Figure S15). This tolerance was completely lost upon silencing GhNHX7b in the Ghscamp13 background (TRV: GhNHX7b/Ghscamp13), which displayed severe necrosis similar to TRV: GhNHX7b/WT. Mechanistically, TRV: 00/Ghscamp13 roots showed significantly higher net Na+ efflux and K+ influx than TRV: 00/WT, reflecting enhanced ion detoxification (Figure S16). Silencing GhNHX7b alone compromised these ion‐flux capacities, and co‐silencing GhNHX7b in the Ghscamp13 background reduced them to levels near those of TRV: GhNHX7b/WT. These findings indicate that GhSCAMP13 negatively regulates salt tolerance by modulating Na+/K+ homeostasis in a GhNHX7b‐dependent manner.

Haplotype analysis based on cotton 3K‐TCG SNP dataset (He et al. 2021) revealed two distinct haplotypes of GhSCAMP13 (Figure 1F). Specifically, all wild accessions (26) carried Haplotype 1 (Hap‐1), whereas 2562 of 2598 cultivated accessions possessed Hap‐2. Landraces exhibited an intermediate distribution, with 173 and 135 accessions carrying Hap‐1 and Hap‐2, respectively (Figure S17). High pairwise Fst values revealed marked differentiation between wild and cultivated accessions, suggesting strong directional selection during domestication (Figure 1F). Transcriptomic and qPCR data show that upon salt stress, GhSCAMP13 expression was induced to a significantly higher amplitude in cultivars (Hap‐2, e.g., TM‐1 and J668) than in wild or landraces accessions (Hap‐1, e.g., G. mustelinum, TX1925 and TX2090) (Figure S18). To test the physiological impact of these haplotype variations, J668, TX1925 and TX2090 were subjected to salt stress. Under salinity, Hap‐1 accessions (TX1925 and TX2090) maintained greener shoots and robust roots, exhibiting significantly higher net Na+ efflux and K+influx compared to Jin668 (Hap‐2), which suffered severe leaf drop, apical damage and root inhibition (Figures S19 and S20). Collectively, these findings indicate that GhSCAMP13 experienced strong selection during domestication and that the Hap‐2 allele fixed in modern cultivars is associated with high salt‐induced expression and reduced salt tolerance.

In summary, our study identifies GhSCAMP13 as a negative regulator of root salt tolerance, likely by constraining GhNHX7b‐dependent plasma‐membrane Na+ extrusion or transporter function. The physical association between GhSCAMP13 and GhNHX7b, together with genetic epistasis, supports a model in which GhSCAMP13 modulates GhNHX7b activity or membrane behaviour rather than its steady‐state accumulation. Loss of GhSCAMP13 releases this constraint, enhancing Na+ exclusion, improving K+/Na+ homeostasis, and reducing oxidative injury. As GhSCAMP13 also appears to have undergone strong directional selection during cotton domestication, its higher expression in cultivated germplasm may reflect selection on a pleiotropic or linked trait, potentially at the expense of salt tolerance. Although heterologous expression in Arabidopsis provided rapid functional validation, stable overexpression of GhSCAMP13 in cotton will be required to confirm its role in the native genetic background. Future field‐based analyses will also be essential to assess the agronomic consequences of manipulating GhSCAMP13, particularly with respect to fibre yield and quality under salinity.

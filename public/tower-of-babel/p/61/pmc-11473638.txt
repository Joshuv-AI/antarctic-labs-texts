AHP VIKOR framework for selecting wind turbine materials with a focus on corrosion and efficiency
Authors: Sekar Kidambi Raju, Saravanan Natesan, Amal H Alharbi, Subhash Kannan, Doaa Sami Khafaga, Muthusamy Periyasamy, Marwa M Eid, El-Sayed M El-kenawy
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe research objective in the context of the study relates to the major concern of corrosion affecting the wind turbines in operation to find materials with high durability in relation to environmental conditions of operation, strength, and cost. A method is an integration of the Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) and VIKOR Multi-Criteria Decision Making (MCDM) techniques that will assess seven different material options on sixteen criteria that comprise corrosion resistance, mechanical properties, cost, and a negative environmental impact. From this result, the AHP method calculated the weights for the indicators and chose potential materials, and finally, the VIKOR method used these materials and compared and ranked them to obtain a compromise solution. The research novelty integrates the AHP and VIKOR MCDM methods to address corrosion in wind turbines. By evaluating seven materials against challenging sixteen criteria—including corrosion resistance, mechanical properties, cost, and toxicity, AHP ranks and weights the criteria, while VIKOR identifies the optimal material choice. This dual approach enhances the selection process, ensuring the chosen material improves turbine performance and durability, offering a significant advancement in the sustainable development of wind energy technology. In conclusion, by integrating AHP and VIKOR, it comprehensively evaluates multiple material options based on corrosion resistance, mechanical properties, cost, and environmental impact. This methodology effectively identifies materials that enhance wind turbine performance and extend their lifespan, addressing a critical industry challenge. The alternative exhibits a similarity to the positive ideal solution (Si) of 0.3704 and a relative closeness to the ideal solution (Ri) of 0.0750. Additionally, its priority ranking (Qi) is 0.001, placing it in the first rank for Carbon Fiber Reinforced Polymers (CFRP) within the selection methodology.Keywords: Wind turbine material selection, Corrosion resistance, Analytical Hierarchy process, VIKOR multi-criteria decision making, Performance optimizationSubject terms: Climate sciences, Environmental sciences, Ocean sciences, Engineering, Materials science

Introduction

The durability and lifetime of wind turbines are matters of convulsion because of corrosion. Therefore, corrosion is one of the major issues which should be addressed in the wind energy sector. To solve this problem, this research proposed a hybrid technique combining the AHP and VIKOR multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) methods for selecting corrosion-resistant materials in the wind turbine building process. This approach utilizes a sensitive technique that holistically examines factors that affect material selection. The alternative materials are identified as Nickel-Based Superalloys (A1A2): Carbon Fiber Reinforced Polymers (CFRP) (A2), Stainless Steel (A3), Titanium Alloys (A4), Inconel (A5), Ceramic Matrix Composites (CMCs) (A6), Single Crystal Alloys (A7), and they are evaluated against sixteen challenging indicators, among the factors of corrosion resistance, mechanical property, economic effectiveness, and environmental impact. These factors make a difference for designers who require high-yield and strong wind turbines. For clarity purposes, this information is further processed through the AHP, which makes it possible to provide weights for the indicators. Thus facilitating a structured decision-making process. In the next step, the VIKOR method is employed, and as a result, a comparison analysis is done in this way, giving a chance to consider the perspective of the compromise decision. Systemically approaching this ensures that the chosen material not only serves for the technical construction of wind turbines but is also reasonable and environmentally conscious. Adopting such an approach, we look at getting greater efficiency and sustainability in developing wind energy systems as we attempt to enhance the decision-making process in terms of producing the optimum wind turbine materials. Novelty of the research work

The research work is distinctive from prior work due to the unified use of the AHP and VIKOR MCDM methods to resolve the significant problem of corrosion in wind turbines. Through such assessment, this study considered the seven material options with respect to sixteen criteria, namely, corrosion, static mechanical properties, fatigue properties, cost, recyclability, weight, availability and health hazards or toxicity of the metals and alloys. The AHP method ranks and assigns weights to the criteria and routinely looks for potential materials, while the VIKOR method ranks the feasible choices to arrive at the optimal compromise solution. This sort of dual-methodology approach not only improves the selection procedure for durable material selection but also guarantees that the chosen material will help in the performance enhancement and durability of wind turbines, which resolves an important industry problem innovatively.

Why the integration of the proposed model

Integration of AHP with VIKOR is critical in this regard to assess the diverse characteristics of materials and rank them according to the preferences for wind turbines. AHP is applied to work out the overall importance value of the criteria such as corrosion resistance, mechanical properties, cost and environmental effect relative to one another by comparing them in pairs to decide their associated weights. This eliminates the probability of discrepancies while prioritizing criteria, thus establishing a proper hierarchy. In this regard, the weights are utilized by VIKOR for the purpose of ranking all the materials and this tool is more concerned with the selection of a compromised solution with regard to all criteria. Its use relies on the strengths of both methods to present a sound and rigorous procedure for identifying efficient and durable materials for improving wind turbine performance in light of the crucial problem of corrosion prevalent in use environments. It combines the traditional VIKOR technique with fuzzy logic to handle the inherent uncertainty and vagueness in decision-making. By incorporating fuzzy logic, it allows for more nuanced evaluations of criteria such as corrosion resistance, mechanical properties, cost, and environmental impact. This method provides a more flexible and realistic assessment by capturing expert opinions and preferences in a fuzzy framework.

Motivation of the research work

The motivation for this research arises from the imperative need to tackle corrosion problems in wind turbines due to the high effects they have on the power system. Technical features such as durability, strength, and cost indication of the material selecting required for improving these renewable energy systems’ performance. This research adopts the AHP Model and the VIKOR MCDM to ensure that the evaluation of the seven material options is systematic, and for this purpose, the sixteen criteria such as corrosion resistance, mechanical properties, cost and impact on the environment are considered. To increase the reliability and the lifetime of wind turbines in various climates, this methodology aims to give a comprehensive assessment of the materials that prevent corrosion.

Advantages

Various aspects that form the basis of the efficiency of utilizing modern techniques and methodologies in the development of offshore wind energy include the following benefits. An action plan using domain knowledge and various factors improves subjective assessments of offshore wind farm options. Through linguistic Pythagorean fuzzy sets, the risk assessment methods include the gray areas and imprecision prevailing in offshore wind project risk assessment. It highlights material choice since the environmentally friendly materials used in the construction of new wind turbine blades have been pinpointed. Scholars have hybridized SWARA and WASPAS, and the findings are that these methods efficiently identify suitable offshore wind farm locations. Optimization algorithms of thermal energy and wind power in desalination plants demonstrate economic and environmental returns. Fuzzy AHP for categorizing issues related to the roadblocks in offshore wind energy development provides better approaches for strategic decisions. Automated systems and new, advanced approaches to corrosion control and material specifications link to enhanced turbine effectiveness and durability. Studies on the use of renewable materials for the strengthening of aluminum composites and superhydrophobic coatings for ice prohibition provide an increase in the effectiveness of turbines. Strategies of diagnosis and monitoring of corrosion allow efficient control and prevention, and GIS as a selection method is the criteria for selecting the best location for the wind farm. All of this development can collectively be said to enhance offshore wind energy systems efficiency, sustainability and reliability.

Disadvantages

The studies identify several disadvantage points in the question of offshore wind energy development. The objectivity of evaluation may be compromised even when applying scientific and academic judgment and adopting multiple criteria evaluation; therefore, the alternatives may be evaluated subjectively. Some of the RA techniques, like the linguistic Pythagorean fuzzy sets, may embrace imprecision, but they may not adequately handle offshore wind projects’ uncertainties and complexities. Choosing green materials for the construction of turbine blades may cause certain problems connected with the interaction between performance and ecological characteristics. Innovative techniques for selecting locations of offshore wind farms might be problematic owing to the combination of many factors and the use of fuzzy logic in the choice of hybrid decision-making. Optimization algorithms developed for integrated thermal energy and wind power for desalination plants might be an issue of practicality and economic feasibility. When classifying development roadblocks via fuzzy analytic hierarchy processes, the barriers may be reduced to a simpler form than what they exist in, resulting in a rudimentary solution. Improvements in protection against corrosion and improvements in materials standards while extending the turbine’s useful life may, in the meantime, lead to higher initial costs and added complexity. It is possible to face some practical and scalability issues while reinforcing aluminum composites with sustainable materials or using superhydrophobic coatings for ice prevention. Some of these techniques, such as enhanced diagnosis and corrosion monitoring through enhanced maintenance, can be costly and demanding technical skills. Despite the extensive information included in the GIS-based site selection methods for wind farms, the accessibility of accurate data and geographical applicability of the methods might have certain drawbacks. All these difficulties indicate that there are still profound developments in technical, economic and environmental issues for offshore wind energy systems in the future.

Research gap

The current literature on corrosion protection systems for offshore wind turbines, rehabilitation of composite material and the utilization of green hydrogen generation is informative, but it depicts research gaps in the following ways. Thus, numerous investigations are dedicated to researching durable coatings, other types of composite materials, and mechanical characteristics of wind electricity generating equipment and its components, but only some works are concerned with the problem of their long-term sustainability and influence on the environment. Moreover, there needs to be a more adequate failure analysis of materials and structures and the causes of problems such as SCC cracking. The other factor needed is the assessment of recovery strategies for the given difficulties. Also, different decision-making tools and multi-criteria methods have been developed for the adaptive site selection and design of wind farms, but the coordinated relationship between wind power and hydrogen generation has yet to be covered systematically. Lastly, while some pieces of research review the economic sustainability and risk of offshore wind to hydrogen conversion, a larger number of investigations are needed to assess the applicability and effectiveness of these systems in various geographical and climatic conditions.

State of art

The understanding of the concurrent corrosion protection systems and global perspectives, as well as the reinforcement of composites and green hydrogen for offshore wind turbines, is already very mature and intricate. It can be noted that the authors have advanced many achievements in the creation of long-stressed coatings and new composites based on natural binders, for example, the use of coconut rice and shells in the enhancement of the mechanical as well as chemical properties of wind turbine parts. Also, failure analyses have been conducted at Component_ scales regarded as nominal, and potential issues, for instance, SCC cracking in slip-ring connectors, have been found with recommendations for the recovery approaches able to enhance the components’ reliability and durability. Despite the ability of decision-making models and multiple criteria techniques in facilitating successful wind farm siting and design, wind-integrated hydrogen production still lacks extensive exploration. Moreover, there are still primary economic studies of wind-to-hydrogen conversion; however, there is a lack of studies of these systems and their application in different geographical and climatic conditions. Scientists are also using AI & big data for the pre-diagnosis and preventive care of turbine breakdowns that help boost wind energy efficiency & longevity.

Methods and materials

AHP stands for Analytical Hierarchy Process, and this tool is created to break a complex choice into easier-to-manage units to examine. It serves decision-makers in different sectors like management, engineering and the wind turbine in enhancing their ability to match alternatives and systemic appraisal purposes. AHP here brings to the table the function of ranking the criteria against their alternatives based on pairwise comparison. AHP assigns value to each criterion, and it significantly helps the decision-maker stay on the same track, and it guards against being emotionally influenced, thereby making the decision-maker make deliberate selections. This is what makes AHP critical in wind turbines since it attaches a number of challenging indicators a weightage. Such indicators, which are diverse and could be cited as examples of honesty, modernity, and effectiveness, among others, are fundamental in assessing the suitability and feasibility of wind turbine technologies. Through the division of the decision-making process into distinct, understandable sections, AHP allows major stakeholders to show greater efficiency when remodeling priorities and deciding the most proper course of action for wind turbine projects.

The VIKOR model is the flag that is waved over the MCDM methodologies, bringing much flexibility to the struggle among plenty of different criteria. While the classical approaches base their efforts on identifying the optimum solution, VIKOR aims at discovering the interim solutions which could be considered as unifying the conflicting values. Taking into account both the utility and “maximum individual guarantee,” VIKOR followed the three criteria route that enabled it to offer a compromise solution that would be able to manage the complex environment of multiple criteria. Facing the complex question of wind turbine allocation, VIKOR delivers the necessary light to guide the way. It ends up doing this even very nicely by giving weight to conflicting goals so that only the best scheme that appears to be the most beneficial on every front is selected. Different factors, from engineering considerations to economic viability and environmental impact, are also considered by the tool, which is a great innovative tool to help us achieve eventual goals that are multifaceted. The variety sharpens its application in different areas, which reinstates its effectiveness in pointing out a structure that is to be used to cut across the complicated dynamics of wind turbines.

Assessment of classroom design for physical education using COCOSO algorithm and modified Sugeno Weber aggregation operators
Authors: Qiaoli Wei, Junwei Yao, Weitao Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPhysical education and traditional sports are gaining increasing importance for comprehensive development, making the systemic design of classrooms necessary. Well-designed physical education rooms encourage students to participate in both curricular and extracurricular activities. With a well-structured classroom, opportunities for various teaching methods are available, and everyone is encouraged to participate. The research presents a novel approach to applying the Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) within multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) to design physical education classroom environments. The MCDM approach facilitates the successful evaluation of superior options among multiple alternatives. Through AHP processes, decision-makers can evaluate the importance of criteria by making pairwise comparisons until they identify the most suitable choice. The q-rung ortho pair fuzzy set (q-ROFS) framework extends fuzzy sets with a more sophisticated system that shows both the degree of membership (DoM) and degree of non-membership (DoNM) throughout the interval with a large range. The Sugeno-Weber t-norm (TNM) and t-conorm (TCNM) play a significant role in modeling flexible fuzzy systems, offering more control over how fuzzy sets interact. Due to their adjustable parameters, they can easily aggregate complex information. We aimed to develop aggregation operators (AOs) based on the AHP, combined with Sugeno-Weber TNM and TCNM, known as q-rung orthopair fuzzy Sugeno-Weber weighted averaging (q-ROFSWWA) and q-rung orthopair fuzzy Sugeno-Weber weighted geometric (q-ROFSWWG) operators, to address current needs. Additionally, the paper describes an improved combined compromise solution (CCS) methodology to determine the precise ranking of alternatives. Then, we presented the MCDM algorithms of the developed q-ROFSWWA and COCOSO approach under AHP and q-ROFS information. Through practical examples, we rank the four different classes for physical education, including versatile equipment, integration of outdoor spaces, adaptable and versatile environments, and safety and comfort features. We apply our proposed theory to investigate four alternatives, and the obtained result is “integration of outdoor spaces.” To demonstrate the importance of the proposed approach, we conduct a comparative analysis to investigate authenticity and validation. Then, we provide a conclusion. Fuzzy set theory

Crisp sets are effective when applied when things are clear, but do not work well when circumstances are less certain. Zadeh1 created the fuzzy set theory using a DoM scale that ranged from zero to one. It improves the process of finding facts and processing scientific data by helping organizations deal with fuzzy and unclear information. When fuzzy sets were invented, they greatly improved how scientists approach decision making. Fuzzy sets represent the essential idea that drives researchers to use this theory because it solves real-world problems in social systems. Ardil1 developed a ranking analysis methodology using fuzzy sets (FS) to facilitate the identification of the best combat aircraft. The evaluation of disposal locations used FS, according to Kahraman2. In their work, Sahu et al.3 investigated the robustness of web-based applications by applying the Fault Tolerance (FT) technique.

The development of decision-making sciences and DoNM theories resulted in difficulties for FS theory in addressing intricate situations. Atanassov4 developed IFS to resolve this problem through the condition which allows DoM and DoNM to exist simultaneously between the interval . This new framework has driven several data scientists and mathematicians to use its concepts in solving challenging, realistic problems. The research by Yager5 presented q-Rung ortho pair fuzzy sets (q-ROFS) as a more sophisticated version of both IFS and PyFS. Through this approach, researchers have introduced an improved analytical structure that constrains the square of membership and non-membership value pairs between and . Many academic journals have researched the implementation of this framework in their studies.

The importance of TNM and TCNM in fuzzy set theory

Triangular norms and triangular conorm play a crucial role in the field of fuzzy set theory. Their importance stems from the difficulties encountered by numerous fuzzy set models in effectively processing information or conforming to the core definition of fuzzy sets, which permits results to exceed the boundaries of the unit interval . To address this issue, the development of novel mathematical frameworks has become essential. Manger et al.6 were among the first to introduce the concepts of TNM and TCNM, which prompted numerous researchers to identify various TNMs and TCNMs tailored to specific FS models. For instance, Khan et al.7 explored the operational principles of Archimedean TNM and TCNM, while Yahya et al.8 formulated Frank TNM and TCNM operations. Additionally, Huang9 introduced Hamacher TNM and TCNM rules aimed at data aggregation.

Novelty of proposed theory

In the past era, there were many mathematicians proposed several methods for aggregation of fuzzy information under the FS, IFS and PyFS based environments. All the discussed methodologies are suitable tools for investigating imprecise information. However, none of these discussed the allocation of weightage to attributes. Numerous scholars in the field of mathematics have explored various AHP models in connection with FS and q-ROFS theories, as evidenced by the existing literature. The theory q-ROFS gives a more general and expressive framework to (deal with) higher degrees of uncertainty than using typical fuzzy sets, IFS, or PyFS, and the Sugeno-Weber operational laws also play the role in enriching the model and modeling nonlinear interactions and interdependencies between criteria. The Sugeno-Weber operation is novel compared to the usual aggregation principles because it offers a parameter that can be varied to determine the level of compensation amongst inputs to produce a more even-handed and responsive result of the decision outcome. This integration would render the given strategy distinctive and potent, and would provide enhanced sensitivity, reporting data protection, and stability on account of intricate decision-making conditions.

AHP for q-ROFS-based information

The MCDM frameworks exist to help identify and select options from multiple alternative sets with conflicting criteria. The decision sciences field requires the MCDM framework as it provides structured solutions for complex problems involving multiple-factor assessment. Organizations gain enhanced decision-making abilities through this method because it enables alternative assessments and determines the weights of vital criteria, thereby resulting in informed choices. The AHP serves as a fundamental tool within MCDM, providing a structured approach for assessing and ranking the various factors that influence complex decisions. By decomposing the decision-making process into a hierarchy of smaller, more manageable sub-problems, AHP enables decision-makers to allocate weights to each criterion based on their relative importance through pairwise comparisons. Furthermore, AHP incorporates a consistency check, which aids in minimizing bias and enhancing the precision of the decision-making process.

AHP enhances transparency, supports logical decision-making, and showcases adaptability across multiple domains by integrating both qualitative and quantitative data. Thus, it functions as a powerful tool for tackling complex MCDM challenges. Numerous mathematicians have applied AHP in various fuzzy environments. Figure 3 illustrates the AHP.

At the third level, alternatives are methodically compared, and their corresponding weights are evaluated, enabling a comprehensive analysis of the data represented as q-ROFNs. Proposed MCDM theory

Yazdani et al.30 were the first to propose the CoCoSo method. By combining the actual weight in addition (SAW) and a product that is exponentially weighted (EWP), three aggregation methods were used in the decision-making process, which can be seen as a feasible compromise. The CoCoSo approach has been effectively applied to logistic provider selection31. Some real-world issues will arise during the decision-making process. Several ambiguous situations have been addressed by the CoCoSo approach when working with complex preference data. According to Karasan and Bolturk32, the CoCoSo technique was expanded during this time. Wen et al.33 explained that the CoCoSo technique was tested in hesitant fuzzy language. Together with aggregation strategies, it incorporates the SAW34, WASPAS (weighted aggregated sum product assessment)35, and MEW (multiplicative exponential weighting) methods36. This approach provides decision-makers with a multifaceted compromise solution that aligns with the results of other MCDM techniques, including the MOORA (multi-objective optimization based on ratio analysis)37 and VIKOR38 methods. The idea of q-ROFS and entropy-based AOs for assessing decision-making problems, as proposed by Khan et al.30, and the Shannon entropy and MARCOS method for MCDM models under q-ROFS, presented by Li et al.39. The effective q-ROFS-based Aos for MCDM problems, discussed by Rahim et al.32 and Abbas et al.33, propose an integrated MCDM approach under the q-ROFS data.

In the COSOSO method, we have explored in more detail the models typically used for evaluating classroom designs, such as TOPSIS, VIKOR, and fuzzy-based approaches. While they provide helpful information, these techniques often struggle to manage decision situations that are uncertain and difficult to define. Therefore, our study suggests applying both the AHP technique for selecting the most important criteria and the COCOSO approach for ranking the alternatives under consideration. Applying this idea enhances the accuracy of decisions by leveraging the structured approach of AHP and the compromise-based flexibility of the COCOSO method.

Physical education classroom design requires creating an educational setting that supports better learning outcomes for physical education principles and activities instruction. Physical education space design combines area organization with equipment selection to effectively utilize instructional resources while creating an environment that meets safety standards and motivates learners toward the educational goals of physical education. Classroom design for physical education creates essential conditions that directly influence three primary student-related outcomes: safety measures, engagement levels, and academic quality. Beyond creating usable areas, the effective classroom design for physical education promotes an environment that positively affects students’ physical, mental, and emotional health outcomes. An adequately designed classroom space meets the diverse needs of students by supporting active participation teamwork activities, and promoting sustainable wellness. By incorporating safety protocols, technological resources, and inclusive methodologies, the design enhances the overall educational experience, ensuring that physical education is both stimulating and meaningful. Result and discussions

In the modern age, the use of MCDM is highly significant in selecting classroom designs for physical education, as it enables a structured and objective evaluation of various design alternatives based on multiple factors. These factors may include space efficiency, ventilation, flooring safety, adaptability for different activities, and overall cost. By applying MCDM methods, decision-makers can prioritize design elements that best support physical movement, student engagement, and instructional flexibility. This approach ensures that the selected design not only meets functional and safety standards but also enhances the quality of the teaching and learning environment. For the precise assessment of MCDM models, many mathematicians have proposed several theories based on different concepts of fuzzy sets. However, the thought of q-ROFS is one of the best tools for investigating fuzzy information. AHP is vital because it converts subjective thoughts into numbers, allowing for the comparison of various criteria with confidence. It organizes complicated choices into a series and makes paired comparisons to determine the importance of each one. After that, all the priorities are combined to determine the ranking of the alternatives based on their weighted scores. The concepts of Sugen-Weber TNM and TCNM operations are reliable for enhancing the functionality of q-ROFS and expanding the capacity of the data aggregation procedure. By applying the idea of q-ROFS, AHP, and Sugeno-Weber operations, a new family of AOs, referred to as q-ROFSWWA and q-ROFSWWG operators, has been established. Additionally, we have investigated some fundamental axioms of AOs, including monotonicity, boundedness, and idempotency. Besides these AOs, we have also proposed the COCOSO method for q-ROFS to rank the alternatives under the MCDM approach. Then, we have established the MCDM algorithms for the proposed COCOSO method and family of AOs. To check the applicability of the proposed theories, we solve a real-life numerical example. In this regard, we rank the four different classes for physical education, including versatile equipment, integration of outdoor spaces, adaptable and versatile environments, and safety and comfort features. We apply our proposed theory to investigate four alternatives, and the obtained result is “integration of outdoor spaces.” Finally, for the investigation of authenticity and validation of the developed theories, we have compared them with some existing MCDM models, such as the q-ROFEWA and q-ROFEWG operators developed by Riaz et al.41, as well as the q-ROFDWA and q-ROFDWG operators introduced by Du42. The concept of q-ROFAAWA and q-ROFAAWG operators was proposed by Farid and Riaz43. The idea of q-ROFWA and q-ROFWG operators, developed by Riaz et al. [47], is presented in Table 9.

Conclusion

In the age of a technological environment, choosing the most suitable option from a variety of choices is a considerable challenge for decision-makers. In the field of decision-making sciences, MCDM is an essential tool for integrating complex information and determining the optimal selection based on multiple sets of criteria. Various mathematical models have been established within the field of FS theory. However, some of these models have not effectively aggregated information when dealing with the concept of a large range of DoM and DoNM. This article presents new AOs derived from Sugeno-Weber TNM and TCNM operations. The AHP is recognized as a precise and widely adopted method for calculating attribute weights. Building on these foundational aspects, we have developed AHP-based q-ROFSWWA and q-ROFSWWG operators and introduced a new concept of COCOSO methods within the q-ROFS environment. We developed an algorithm for the AHP to compute the weights of attributes and proposed the q-ROFSWWA, q-ROFSWWG, and COCOSO methods. This approach has enabled us to provide solutions to practical numerical examples that rank the best classroom design selection for physical education. Finally, to evaluate the validity and effectiveness of our proposed method, we extensively compared it with existing techniques.

The COCOSO method is highly effective in MCDM, but it also has some significant drawbacks. Because results depend on the weights, any changes in the weights can make the model unpredictable. Lacking a robust method for handling the lack of clarity or accuracy in input data, the technique has limited real-life applications. Additionally, the addition or removal of alternatives in COCOSO can lead to inconsistencies in the outcome of decisions.

In the future, we aim to broaden our theoretical framework to include various fuzzy structures, along with TNM and TCNM operations, such as interval value q-rung orthopair fuzzy sets, as defined by Joshi et al.45 and complex interval-valued q-rung orthopair fuzzy Hammy mean operators, presented by Ali et al.46.

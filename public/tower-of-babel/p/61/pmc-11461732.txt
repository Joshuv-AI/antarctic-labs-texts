CircTSN promotes the proliferation and metastasis of gastric cancer through the miR-1825/SLC38A2 signaling axis
Authors: Xuqiang Dong, Tianyu Cheng, Lijun Zhang, Liqun Song, Chao Shi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundComprehensive treatment of gastric cancer (GC) is progressing, but the rapid proliferation and metastasis of GC remains a cause of high recurrence and mortality rates. In this study we investigated GC-associated circRNA tending to yield more insight into the mechanisms of gastric cancer development.MethodsWe detected the expression levels of circTSN in GC tissues and cell lines using qRT-PCR. The circular structure of circTSN was confirmed by Sanger sequencing, agarose gel electrophoresis and RNase R. A series of cell functional experiments were employed to investigate the implication of circTSN aberrant expression on the proliferation and metastasis of GC cells. The predicted binding domain between circTSN and miR-1825 was analyzed by luciferase reporter gene analysis. Meanwhile, subcutaneous tumor xenografts in nude mice were used to validate the role of circTSN in vivo.ResultsIt was found that RNA levels of circTSN were significantly elevated in GC tissues and cell lines, which was also confirmed to contain a closed-loop structure. CCK8, clone formation, EdU, transwell and in vivo experiments indicated that the highly expressed circTSN was involved in the proliferation and metastasis process of GC. In addition, circTSN modulates the expression of SLC38A2 by sequence-specific binding to miR-1825.ConclusionThis study identified that circTSN, which is highly expressed in GC, was able to contribute to the proliferation and metastasis of GC cell through miR-1825/SLC38A2 axis and this might provide a new candidate target for the precision treatment of GC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01407-0.

Background

Gastric cancer (GC) has been the fifth most prevalent malignant tumor morbidity and the fourth most prevalent mortality in the world [1, 2]. It is still one of the major causes of human deaths despite the fact that the treatment of GC has gradually evolved from the traditional surgery combined with chemotherapy to the integrated treatment modality of surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, targeted therapy and immunotherapy [3, 4]. The pathogenesis of GC is intricately related to a variety of factors such as environment, genetics, and dietary habits [5–7]. Therefore, it is of great significance to fully explore the potential molecular mechanisms of GC proliferation and metastasis, so as to find molecular markers for early diagnosis and prognosisand to exploit effective and efficient targeted drugs [8].

Since the discovery of circRNAs, it has always been considered as a by-product of mis-splicing or precursor mRNA processing and has not received sufficient attention [9]. Over the last few years, due to the development and extensive application of high-throughput sequencing technology, an increasing number of circRNAs have been detected. Studies have demonstrated that circRNAs are widely involved in a variety of pathophysiological pathways such as cellular genetics, development, apoptosis, inflammation, stemness and immunity and are involved in the genesis of multiple malignant tumors [10–15]. CircRNA is a single-stranded covalently closed loop structure that is devoid of a 5' cap end structure and a 3' polyadenylate tail, and has the advantage of being less susceptible to exonuclease degradation than conventional linear RNA molecules [16, 17]. CircRNA can exert regulatory effects through multiple pathways: (1) acting as a competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) and indirectly regulating downstream gene expression by competitively binding to microRNAs [18–20]; (2) blocking the mediating role of RNA-binding proteins (RBPs) [21, 22]; and (3) translating into polypeptides [23, 24]. The most extensive and in-depth doctrine of the ceRNA mechanism is also the most academically recognized. As a sponge for miR-127-3p, circLGMN suppressed LGMN mRNA degradation mediated by miR-127-3p in Glioblastoma (GBM). Elevated LGMN protein levels contributed to the progression of GBM and were associated with poor prognosis [25]. Fang et al. found that highly expressed circTRAF3 abolished the suppressive role over the oncogene AKT3 by binding miR-203a-3p in nasopharyngeal carcinoma, leading to enhanced cell proliferation and invasion of nasopharyngeal carcinoma cells in vitro and in vivo [26].

In this study, we identified a circular RNA named circTSN, which enhanced the protein level of SLC38A2 as an oncogene in GC by binding miR-1825 and inhibiting its function. We hope that our findings could be of benefit to the treatment and prognosis of GC and that the circTSN/miR-1825/SLC38A2 pathway could be a candidate for therapeutic target for advanced GC.

Materials and methods

Cell line culture

The GC cell lines (HGC27, MGC803, MKN45, MKN28, SFC7901 and AGS) and gastric mucosal epithelial cells (GES-1) used in this study were obtained from the Shanghai Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). These cells were incubated in 1640 medium (Hyclone, South Logan, USA) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibico, Grand Island, USA) and 100 U/ml penicillin and streptomycin (Gibico, Grand Island, USA) in an atmosphere of 5% Carbon Dioxide and 37 °C.

Tissue specimens

All GC tissues involved in this study were obtained from patients who were diagnosed with GC and underwent surgical procedures at Department of Gastrointestinal Surgery of our institute. The approval of the Medical Ethics Committee of Yixing People's Hospital was obtained in advance and all methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. The gastric cancer and paracancerous tissues involved in this study were obtained from patients with T1-T3a gastric cancer who underwent surgical treatment at our gastrointestinal surgery department from 2017 to 2020. All patients had not received preoperative chemotherapy or immunotherapy and had signed an informed consent form before surgery.

Cell transfection

To explore the function and mechanism, we attempted to regulate the transcriptional level of nucleic acids. We commissioned Genepharma (Shanghai, China) to design and synthesize small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) and miRNA mimics. All cell transfection were performed using the Lipofectamine 3000 (Thermo, Waltham, USA).

Reverse transcription-quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) assay

Total RNA of both GC tissues and cells was obtained with the help of Trizol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, USA) according to the protocol provided by the manufacturer. After that, cDNA was generated with miRNAFirst Strand cDNA Synthesis (Stem-loop Method, Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China) or PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit (Takara, Tokyo, Japan). qRT-PCR was performed with QuantiNova SYBR Green PCR Kit (QIAGEN, Duesseldorf, Germany) in CFX96 Touch Real-Time PCR Detection System (Bio-Rad, Hercules, USA). During this study, GAPDH and U6 were taken as internal reference. In addition, GAPDH and U6 were taken as internal references and their relative expression levels were compared using 2−ΔΔCt method, respectively.

RNA fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)

Cells were first fixed by 4% paraformaldehyde (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, USA) and then permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, USA). Subsequently, cells were subjected to an overnight hybridization reaction at 37 °C with the Fam-labeled circTSN and Cy3-labeled miR-1825 probes and the nuclei were stained with DAPI. The final images were obtained using a Leica system (Wetzlar, Germany).

Cell counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay

HGC27 and MKN45 cells were previously inoculated into 96-well plates overnight and incubated at 37 °C. After adding 10 μL CCK-8 (Dojindo, Kumamoto, Japan) to each well and incubating for another 2 h, the optical density (OD) values at 450 nm of the 96-well plates were measured using an enzyme labeler (Thermo, Waltham, USA).

Clone formation assay

Transfected cells were seeded in 12-well plates and cultured. Two weeks later, the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, USA) for 15 min at room temperature and then stained with crystal violet (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, USA) for 15 min.

5-Ethynyl-2′-deoxyuridine (EdU)

Cells in logarithmic growth phase were placed 4 × 103/well in a 96-well plate and cultured to normal growth phase. After that, we conducted the follow-up procedures based on an EdU Cell Proliferation Detection Kit (RiboBio, Guangzhou, China).

Transwell

Transwell inserts (Corning, Corning, USA) were placed in 24-well plates. 250 µl of serum-free medium was added to the upper layer and 750 µl of complete medium in the lower layer. 20,000 cells were seeded onto the upper layer. After 12–24 h of incubation, the medium was discarded and the cells were fixed with formaldehyde and stained with crystal violet.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

The pmirGLO plasmid was constructed to contain the predicted binding sites between circTSN and miR1825 and a luciferase reporter gene, along with its corresponding mutant plasmid (GenePharma, Shanghai, China). Transfection was performed using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, USA). Firefly luciferase activity was measured 48 h after co-transfection of pmirGLO plasmids and mimics using the Dual Luciferase Reporter Assay Kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China).

Western blot assay

Total protein was extracted using RIPA buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors (CST, Danvers, USA) at 4 °C for 30 min. The supernatant was centrifuged at 12,000 g, mixed with loading buffer (CST, Danvers, USA) and denatured. Proteins were separated by electrophoresis on an SDS-PAGE membrane and transferred to a PVDF membrane (Merck Millipore, Billerica, USA). We cropped the PVDF membranes according to the size of the target proteins [27], then incubated them to label the proteins with primary and secondary antibodies, and finally detected the proteins using ECL (Merck Millipore, Billerica, USA).

Xenograft mouse model

Six-week-old BALB/c nude mice were purchased from GemPharmatech (Shanghai, China) and randomly clustered. Transfected gastric cancer cells were implanted into the axillary subcutaneous tissue of nude mice. The size of the subcutaneous neoplastic tumors was measured weekly (Statement: the diameters were all less than the maximum tumor size of 20 mm allowed by the Medical Ethics Committee of Yixing People’s Hospital). 4 weeks later, mice were anesthetized by intraperitoneal injection of sodium phenobarbital and then executed by cervical dislocation. The subcutaneous neoplastic tumors were weighed. All animal experiments were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of our institution.

Statistical analysis

Each experiment in this study was independently replicated for three times. GraphPad Prism 7.0 was used for statistical analysis. P < 0.05 was considered significant.

Results

Identification of circTSN characteristics and expression levels

Based on The Human Reference Genome, we found that circTSN was shaped from TSN mRNA due to reverse splicing of Exon 2, Exon 3, Exon 4 and Exon 5 into a loop a loop of 387nt length during the mature formation process (Fig. 1A). We re-characterized the above findings with Sanger sequencing and the result was as expected (Fig. 1B). Subsequently, we detected the expression level of circTSN in 40 pairs of GC tissues from Department of Gastrointestinal Surgery of our institute using qRT-PCR, and the results showed that circTSN was highly expressed in GC tissues compared with paracancerous tissues (Fig. 1C). As well, circTSN was expressed at significantly higher levels in GC cell lines (HGC27, MGC803, MKN45, MKN28, SGC7901 and AGS) than GES-1 (Fig. 1D). Although some circRNAs were reported to accumulate in the nucleus, most were efficiently transported to the cytoplasm to function [28]. The FISH analysis conducted in our study also clarified that the majority of circTSN was distributed in the cytoplasm (Fig. 1E). We then experimentally verified the cyclization state of circTSN. Specific convergent and divergent primers were employed to amplify complementary DNA (cDNA) and genomic DNA (gDNA), respectively. Subsequent analysis of the amplification products by agarose gel electrophoresis showed that circTSN could only be amplified by divergent primers (Fig. 1F). RNase R was able to cleave and degrade almost all linear RNA from the 3'-5' direction, but did not facilitate the digestion of circular RNA [29]. We found that circTSN, but not TSN mRNA, could be stably and consistently expressed after treatment with RNase R (Fig. 1G). The interference with circTSN suppressed the proliferation and metastasis of GC cells

To investigate the effect of aberrant expression of circTSN on the biological behavior of gastric cancer cells, we transfected si-circ and si-NC into MKN45 and HGC27 GC cells and verified its efficiency and reliability (Fig. 2A). In colony formation assays and CCK8 analysis, silencing circTSN significantly inhibited the proliferation and colony number of MKN45 and HGC27 cells (Fig. 2B–D). After small interferences inhibited the expression level of circTSN, we detected an effective attenuation of the fluorescence signal of EdU in MKN45 and HGC27 (Fig. 2E–F). The experiments described above confirmed that circTSN boosted the proliferation of GC cells and then we proceeded to conduct Transwell to verify the function of circTSN on cell migration. CircTSN sponged to miR1825 through specific binding domain

As we described in the introduction section, the ceRNA mechanism had been the most extensively studied and acceptable mechanism of effect regarding circRNA. We searched the databases miRanda (https://microrna.org/microrna.home.do), RNAhybird (http://bibiserv.techfak.uni-bielefeld.de/rnahybrid/) and CircInteractome (https://circinteractome.nia.nih.gov/index.html) and explored miRNAs that may bind specifically to circTSN (Fig. 3A), which showed that miR-1825 and miR-1200 may be the targets of circTSN. We purchased specific circTSN probe for RNA pull down analysis and verified qualified pull-down efficiency by qRT-PCR in MKN45 and HGC27 (Fig. 3B). After pulling down circTSN by the specific probe, we examined the RNA levels of the two candidate miRNA in the pull-down product and demonstrated that miR-1825 presented a higher abundance in MKN45 and HGC27 (Fig. 3C). We obtained the specific sequence for binding between circTSN and miR-1825 from CircInteractome (Fig. 3D). Based on the predicted binding sequence between circTSN and miR-1825, we designed the circTSN plasmid containing the luciferase reporter gene and the corresponding binding region mutant plasmid. The results of the dual luciferase reporter system showed a decrease in luciferase signal due to miR-1825 binding in the wild group, while the signal was normal in the mutant group in MKN45 and HGC27 (Fig. 3E). On the other hand, the biotin-labeled miR-1825 probe was able to obtain a considerable abundance of circTSN, whereas the mutant group obtained almost none (Fig. 3F). CircTSN/miR1825/SLC38A2 axis facilitated GC growth in vivo

In addition, we validated in vivo the promotion of circTSN on GC and circTSN/miR-1825/SLC38A2 axis. We implanted transfected GC cells under the axillary skin of nude mice and measured the size of the subcutaneous tumors weekly (Fig. 6C). The mice were sacrificed at week four (Fig. 6A) and the neoplastic tumors were weighed (Fig. 6B). We witnessed a significant increase in the volume and weight of tumor tissues after overexpression of circTSN, while silencing SLC38A2 inhibited the promoting effect of circTSN on GC. Discussion

Despite many advances in the diagnosis and treatment of GC, the 5-year overall survival rate of GC remains relatively poor worldwide owing to its recurrence and metastasis [30]. Much has been explored in recent years about the role of non-coding RNAs in GC progression [31], and circNAs are particularly notable due to their innate advantages and unique modes of regulation [32, 33]. In this study, we identified for the first time in GC a circ RNA named circTSN, which is expressed at elevated levels in GC and could promote GC proliferation and metastasis through the miR-1825/SLC38A axis.

CircRNAs are a novel class of non-coding RNAs that form closed continuous loops through covalently linked 3' and 5' ends, which are more resistant and stable compared to linear RNAs. Since its unique structure, circRNA can be stably existed in blood, lymph fluid, urine, etc. and has potential to be a biomarker for the diagnosis and prognosis of GC. Luo et al. revealed that overexpression of circPOFUT1 enhanced cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and autophagy-associated chemotherapeutic resistance in GC, which could be inhibited by overexpression of miR-488-3p. CircPOFUT1 decreased miR-488-3p in GC by a sponge effect to activate PLAG1 and ATG12 expression thereby enhancing malignant phenotypes and autophagy-associated chemotherapeutic resistance in GC [34]. CircIPO7 could directly bind to caprin-1, blocking the interaction between caprin-1 and G3BP1 and dissociating it from the ribosome, which resulted in repression of caprin-1 translation and downstream inactivation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway [35]. Moreover, Liu et al. identified for the first time by immunoprecipitation and mass spectrometry that CM-248aa, a protein encoded by circMTHFD2L, was significantly down-regulated and was involved in the proliferation and metastasis in GC, which correlated with the lymph node metastasis and pathological staging of advanced GC [5].

During our study, we reported for the first time a circular RNA termed circTSN in GC and identified its head-to-tail circular structure of the new molecule by Sanger sequencing, agarose gel electrophoresis, subcellular localization and RNA stability tests, which is crucial for the study of circRNAs. We conducted a variety of functional experiments involving GC cells, such as CCK8, cell colony formation, Tanswell and EdU, demonstrating that high expression of circTSN could contribute to the proliferation and metastasis of GC. We predicted and analyzed the regulatory axis of circTSN through multiple online bioinformatics databases and validated it in subsequent experiments. Dual luciferase assay and RIP experiment adequately authenticated the binding sequence between circTSN and miR-1825. After interfering with the expression of miR-1825, we noticed that circTSN was able to regulate the RNA level of the oncogene SLC38A2 through sponging miR-1825 by qRT-PCR, Western blot and animal experiments. In conclusion, our findings proposed a circTSN/miR-1825/SLC38A2 regulatory axis that facilitated GC proliferation and metastasis. Undeniably, this study had some limitations, such as the mechanism of circTSN/miR-1825/SLC38A2 axis had not been deeply investigated. In addition, the regulation of metastasis of GC by circTSN was not explored in vivo.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we found that circTSN was highly expressed in both GC tissues and cell lines. CircTSN was able to enhance the proliferation and metastasis of GC in vivo and in vitro via the miR-1825/SLC38A2 axis (Fig. 6D). Our report may provide a new promising strategy and target for targeted therapy in GC.

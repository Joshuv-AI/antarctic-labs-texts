The Australian and New Zealand Intensive Care Research Centre and Rinaldo Bellomo
Authors: D Jamie Cooper
Published: 2025

The Australian and New Zealand Intensive Care Research Centre (ANZIC-RC) (https://www.monash.edu/medicine/sphpm/anzicrc) was formed after a successful NHMRC) enabling Grant in 2006 to establish a centre for designing, managing, and teaching pivotal randomised trials in intensive care medicine. The grant was led by four Chief Investigators–the first four Chairs of the ANZICS Clinical Trials Group–Simon Finfer and John Myburgh based in Sydney, and Rinaldo Bellomo and Jamie Cooper in Melbourne.

Prof John McNeil, Head of the School of Public Health and Preventive Medicine at Monash University, realised the potential for such a Centre, and at short notice negotiated the long-term University support which led to the ANZIC-RC being located at Monash, on the Alfred campus. This support was critical to the consistent successes of the ANZIC-RC over the next 20 years. The School included high-level expertise in biostatistics, project management of clinical trials, and many clinical registries. It proved to be an ideal location to grow an intensive care research methods centre.

Following his passion to advance critical care medicine globally, Rinaldo worked with many teams–with ANZIC-RC being just one string to his formidable bow. From inception, he was Co-Director with Jamie Cooper. He was always on the ANZIC-RC Board and shared the Chair role with Jamie. This 20-year partnership led to Jamie and Rinaldo being jointly awarded the Research Australia GSK Award for Research Excellence (ARE) in 2021 in recognition of their global leadership and innovative research in critical care medicine that helped transform approaches to the treatment of critically ill patients worldwide. The GSK ARE is one of the most prestigious awards available to the broad Australian medical research community.

Initially, the ANZIC-RC developed and ran trials in traumatic brain injury (DECRA PMID: 21434843; EPO-TBI PMID: 26452709; POLAR PMID: 30357266), and ARDS (PHARLAP PMID: 31356105; TEAM PMID: 36286256), then expanded to blood transfusion (TRANSFUSE PMID: 28952891), and early goal directed therapy for sepsis (ARISE PMID: 25272316 + PRISM PMID: 28320242). By 2025 there were nine separate programs of work, each led by academic clinicians–Acute Brain Injury, Extracorporeal Membrane Oxygenation (ECMO), Health Economics, Nutrition, Mechanical Ventilation, ICU Recovery, Renal/Metabolic, Sepsis, and Consumer Engagement. Rinaldo believed that the best research programs integrated all aspects of initial research, trial design, management and biostatistics, and that only within these programs could pivotal collaborative national and international network-supported phase III randomized controlled trials (RCTs) be generated and executed at the highest level.

ANZIC-RC grew from 5 publications in the first year (2006), to an average of 270 p.a. in the last 5 years to July 2025. In 2025, the ANZIC-RC had a total of >2,700 publications, with >$134M in grant funding, 80% of which were Cat 1 (NHMRC/MRFF).

Since 2006, one third of Rinaldo’s extraordinary 62 papers in the highest impact journals (NEJM/JAMA/Lancet) were from ANZIC-RC. These concerned building the initial influenza pandemic program (PMID: 19815860), which then led to the prolific REMAP-CAP Covid-19 platform; revised sepsis definitions (PMID: 24638143) and new sepsis therapies (PMID: 31950979); sedation research aiming to decrease delirium (PMID: 31112380); TBI (PMID: 26452709) therapy with EPO, which then expanded to all trauma patients; optimising cardiac arrest management (PMID: 37318140), and refining mobilisation strategies for ventilated patients (PMID: 36286256).

Rinaldo’s role in many ANZIC-RC programs, which were often not his primary research foci, was to encourage and mentor early and mid-level academics, to suggest research directions, and to guide NHMRC grant applications and top journal publication strategies. He also attracted many international academics and PhD students–some of whom were hosted at ANZIC-RC.

When initiating a new program, Rinaldo loved to find a newly popular clinical therapy based on inadequate early clinical studies, and then write grants to fund definitive phase III RCTs that later confirmed the new trendy concept as ineffective–and enabled clinicians to relax and go back to their established clinical practice. The Australasian Resuscitation in Sepsis Evaluation (ARISE) trial (PMID: 25272316) of early goal directed therapy is a good example.

Rinaldo’s interests closest to his heart in renal medicine, sepsis and fluid therapies, led to innumerable grants and trials mostly led from other sites, in particular Austin Hospital Melbourne, The George Institute Sydney, Medical Research Institute New Zealand, and Royal Melbourne Hospital.

Not only was Rinaldo an exemplary clinician researcher, he was our dear friend and colleague–always enormous fun, overflowing with ideas and inspiration, and infectious enthusiasm for new projects. He had a special knack for knowing which current topics would generate journal interest and citations.

Rinaldo was superb at understanding people, what inspired them, and how to encourage them to greater heights. He truly played a major role in training and mentoring the next generation of intensive care academic clinicians. He worked seamlessly with the ANZICS Clinical Trials Group (ANZICS-CTG) collaboration of intensive care academics nationally, and was a key collaborative link between universities, institutes, states, and countries, which enabled many of our largest and best-known trials in intensive care medicine to be successful.

Patient blood management for patients undergoing cardiac surgery in Middle Eastern countries: Multicenter survey
Authors: Ahmed Abdalwahab, Ahmed Abuzaid, Hossam Walley, Doaa Fawzy Abdelfattah, Sarah A. Elmetwally, Ahmed Elsherbeny
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:The purpose of this survey was to delineate and compare patient blood management (PBM) approaches in cardiac surgery across nine Middle Eastern countries while identifying the main challenges against the implementation of the PBM program in cardiac surgery as reported by the surveyed centers.Design:An online survey was established to assess current PBM practices in surveyed countries, including risk factors for bleeding or transfusion, management of preoperative anemia in elective cases, and antifibrinolytic use.Setting:This questionnaire was conducted among cardiac anesthesiologists in Middle Eastern countries in 2024.Participants:Only doctors participated voluntarily in this survey.Interventions:No intervention.Measurements:We assessed the extent of adoption of PBM practices in surveyed countries for patients who underwent cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass.Main Results:Of 40 survey responses, 26 (60%) were eligible for analysis. Most respondents were cardiac anesthesiologists. Key risk factors of bleeding or transfusion identified by over 70% of respondents included redo cardiac surgery, preoperative anemia, recent clopidogrel use, thrombocytopenia <100 × 109/L, and oral anticoagulants. More than half of the centers would correct preoperative anemia using iron. Tranexamic acid was universally used, though administration regimens varied. Autologous priming and normothermia were the most common bypass strategies that would be used by more than 50% of respondents. Viscoelastic testing was available in more than 70% of centers and used by more than half of respondents in case of clinical bleeding.Conclusion:PBM practices in Middle Eastern cardiac centers are heterogeneous, reflecting inconsistent adoption of guidelines. Enhanced training, institutional support, and homogenized national protocols are needed to standardize PBM in the region.

Introduction

J. Isbister, a hematologist in 2005, introduced the term “patient blood management” (PBM) to advocate for a paradigm shift that prioritizes the patient and their needs within a multidisciplinary framework, aimed at optimizing outcomes.[12] Since 2010, the World Health Organization (WHO) and various international societies have endorsed PBM as a standard of care, with reference to the subsequent three-pillar concept: (a) preoperative identification of patients at elevated risk of hemorrhage and optimization of anemia, (b) intraoperative management of homeostasis and reduction of blood loss, and (c) management of postoperative microvascular hemorrhage and improving patient recovery.[3456]

Cardiac surgery represents a major surgical field with high transfusion rates, making it a critical target for PBM. PBM guidelines and programs have been implemented at both the national and international levels.[78]

PBM programs have been promoted in Middle Eastern countries, but the degree of implementation and adherence to guidelines remains unclear. Assessing the current status of PBM practices in this region is crucial for identifying gaps and areas for improvement.

This study aimed to delineate and compare PBM practices in cardiac surgery across multiple centers in various Middle Eastern nations. We hypothesized that, despite the availability of evidence-based PBM guidelines, there would be variability in their implementation and adherence among the surveyed centers.

Methods

Survey design and development

The questionnaire

The primary author drafted a preliminary version of the questionnaire informed by clinical experience and an analysis of existing national and international guidelines and laws in the target countries. The draft was reviewed and validated by co-authors representing each participating country to ensure relevance and cultural appropriateness.

The final questionnaire consisted of 19 questions comprising single-choice, multiple-choice, and open-ended formats and was divided into three main sections:

Center and Respondent Characteristics: included questions on participants specialties, country of practice, the annual volume of cardiac surgeries involving (CPB), the percentage of patients requiring blood transfusions during cardiac procedures with CPB, presence of hospital PBM guidelines, and the existence of PBM coordinator.PBM Practices: assessed recognition of transfusion risk (e.g., redo surgery, anemia, and anticoagulants use), management of preoperative anemia in elective surgery (use of iron, erythropoietin, and Red Blood Cells (RBC) transfusion), use of antifibrinolytics, Cardiopulmonary bypass (CBP) strategies (normothermia, autologous priming, and others), anticoagulants practices, access to and use of viscoelastic tests, and the blood products commonly ordered.Barriers to Implementation: participants were asked to identify key challenges in implementing PBM in their centers.

Survey development

The online survey was created and distributed using the web platform Survey Monkey® (Palo Alto, CA, USA) and was accessible between October 1 and December 31, 2024. The survey link was shared through professional networks, e-mail invitations, and social media by the study authors. Responses were anonymous.

Response rate

The response rate was defined as the number of centers that completed the survey divided by the total number of centers performing cardiac surgery with CPB in each respective country.

Statistical analysis

Data were processed using Microsoft Excel (Microsoft Corp, CA, USA).

Continuous variables were presented as medians and interquartile ranges. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Results were illustrated using bar charts where appropriate.

Ethical considerations

This study did not involve patients or confidential health data. Participation was voluntary and anonymous, and no incentives were offered. Completion of the survey implied informed consent. Ethical approval was waived based on local institutional guidelines for anonymous surveys among healthcare professionals.

Results

Response rate

Out of 40 total responses collected between October 1 and December 31, 2024, twenty-six fulfilled the inclusion criteria and were analyzed, yielding an overall response rate of 60%. The highest national response rate was observed in Saudi Arabia (KSA), with 92% of eligible centers participating. Risk predictors for bleeding or transfusion

Across all surveyed countries, over 70% of respondents declared the following as high-risk factors for bleeding or transfusion during cardiac surgery with CBP: redo cardiac surgery, preoperative anemia, clopidogrel 5 days or less before surgery, thrombocytopenia <100 × 109/L, and oral anticoagulants (warfarin).

Preoperative anemia was recognized as a significant predictor of high risk across all centers. In KSA, over 90% of respondents identified oral anticoagulants and preoperative anemia as the most significant risk predictors [Figure 2].

PBM practices in non-emergent surgery

Preoperative anemia management

Over 70% of surveyed centers reported optimizing the patient‘s hemoglobin level before elective surgery [Table 2]. Iron supplementation was the most commonly used intervention for this (almost 50% of the centers). Only 19.2% of all respondents and 27.2% in KSA centers used preoperative RBC transfusion.

Antifibrinolytic use

All surveyed centers reported routine use of tranexamic acid. Dosage regimens varied; however, most centers used a 1–2 g bolus followed by continuous infusion [Table 2]. Over 70% of centers used tranexamic acid for all cardiac procedures, with variable procedure-specific [Figure 3].

Bypass strategies

Normothermia and autologous priming were each used by more than 54% of centers. Use of cell salvage was very limited (3.8% overall; 9.3% in KSA) [Figure 4].

Different perioperative techniques and bypass strategies

Anticoagulation

Heparin was universally used in the Middle East and KSA centers, but dosing practices for heparin and protamine varied across centers [Table 2].

Viscoelastic testing

Available in 70% of centers overall and 100% of KSA centers. In case of clinical bleeding after bypass, 50% of all respondents and more than 90% of KSA centers used viscoelastic testing to assess coagulation [Table 2].

Blood products and factor concentrates for bleeding

Fresh frozen plasma was the most frequently used product in bleeding scenarios, utilized by 73.1% of all centers and 81.9% in KSA [Figure 5]. There is increased use of factor concentrates (e.g., fibrinogen concentrate, prothrombin complex concentrate, and FVII alpha).

Challenges against implementation of PBM program in cardiac surgery

42.3% of all surveyed centers cited lack of knowledge as the primary barrier, while 45.5% in KSA centers reported lack of funding as the main obstacle to PBM implementation [Table 2].

Discussion

This multicenter survey offers an overview of current PBM practices in cardiac surgery across nine Middle Eastern countries. Our findings reveal considerable variability in the implementation of PBM measures despite the widespread availability of PBM guidelines across the region. While encouraging trends were noted, such as the near-universal use of tranexamic acid, overall adherence to guideline-recommended PBM strategies remains inconsistent (maybe due to divergent guidelines).

A notable strength of this study is its broad geographical scope, covering cardiac surgery centers from KSA, Egypt, UAE, Oman, Kuwait, Lebanon, Bahrain, Morocco, and Libya. Saudi Arabia, which had the highest response rate (92%), was analyzed separately to provide deeper national insights.

Our total response rate (60%) was similar to those generally obtained in the same type of online studies.[9101112]

Key areas of variability

Although the guidelines recommend the use of oral or intravenous iron, with or without erythropoietin, for management of preoperative anemia (Class IIb/A grade of evidence),[8] there was a discrepancy in the use of them in clinical practice between centers as revealed by the study [Table 2].Our study found a low rate of cell salvage utilization in all studied centers including, KSA centers, despite guidelines recommending the routine use of cell salvage to avoid transfusions (Class IIa/B level of evidence).[8]While tranexamic acid use was universal, dosage and administration protocols varied significantly, consistent with the absence of specific regimen recommendations in current guidelines.[8]The application of mini-bypass systems varies across countries; despite that, they are typically regarded as more efficient than conventional CPB in minimizing hemodilution and requirements for blood transfusion.[131415] The beneficial effects of normothermia and autologous priming on hemodilution, which prevent the negative consequences of low levels of hematocrit, such as impaired hemostasis, end-organ function, and cognitive outcomes, have also been demonstrated.[1617]

The differences in practice likely reflect disparities in institutional resources, access to equipment, staff training, and institutional commitment to PBM. Some centers reported not having a dedicated PBM coordinator or formal institutional guidelines, which may further contribute to inconsistency in clinical decision-making.

Our results also highlight important challenges to PBM implementation. While lack of knowledge was the most common regional barrier, lack of funding emerged as the main issue in KSA. This suggests that tailored national strategies—ranging from educational initiatives to resource allocation—are needed to promote guideline adherence.

Internationally, similar challenges have been noted in PBM adoption. European studies have also reported heterogeneous practices despite the existence of robust guidelines.[18] In contrast, some high-income countries have successfully implemented structured PBM programs supported by national policies, centralized training, and funding. Our findings underscore the need for Middle Eastern centers to learn from these models by adopting institutional PBM protocols, conducting staff training, and investing in cost-effective technologies such as viscoelastic testing and cell salvage systems.

Future research should aim to correlate variations in PBM practices with patient outcomes and transfusion rates. High-performing centers may also serve as regional models for best practices in PBM implementation.

Conclusion

This survey highlights significant variability in the adoption of PBM practices across cardiac surgery centers in the Middle East despite the availability of international guidelines. Key PBM elements—such as anemia management and blood conservation strategies—are inconsistently applied. To address these gaps, increased efforts are needed to improve clinician education, secure institutional funding, and implement standardized, evidence-based PBM protocols. Regional collaboration and national initiatives can play a critical role in promoting consistent, high-quality blood management across the region.

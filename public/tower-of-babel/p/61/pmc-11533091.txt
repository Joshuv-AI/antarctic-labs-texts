Electronic nicotine delivery system flavors, devices, and brands used by adults in the United States who smoke and formerly smoked in 2022: Findings from the United States International Tobacco Control Four Country Smoking and Vaping Survey
Authors: Shannon Gravely, Tracy T. Smith, Benjamin A. Toll, David Ashley, Pete Driezen, David T. Levy, Anne C.K. Quah, Geoffrey T. Fong, K. Michael Cummings
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Highlights•One in five adults in the United States who smoked or quit smoking were vaping nicotine in 2022.•Fruit was the most commonly used category of ENDS flavoring, and tanks were the most commonly used device.•Disposables were more often used by younger adults, and tanks by older adults.•A majority of adults who were vaping in 2022—and smoked or formerly smoked—were using a variety of flavors, devices, and brands.•Research is needed to determine whether enforcement actions that would remove unauthorized ENDS products may have unintended consequences.

Introduction

In the United States (US), the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) Center for Tobacco Products (CTP) regulates electronic nicotine delivery systems (ENDS) as tobacco products (FDA, 2023). To legally sell ENDS, companies must receive authorization via the Premarket Tobacco Product Application (PMTA) process, demonstrating that commercial sale of the product is appropriate for the protection of public health (FDA, 2024a). Tobacco products that were on the market as of Aug. 8, 2016 were required to submit an application to the FDA by Sept. 9, 2020. In 2021, FDA finalized the PMTA rule that described the required content, format, and review of PMTAs (FDA, 2021). Products that received authorization from the FDA by September 9, 2021 could remain on the market legally beyond that date. Any product for which a PMTA was submitted by Sept 9, 2020, and for which a final decision has not been made, is not a legally marketed product, but FDA has chosen to use enforcement discretion and not enforce the law for these products until a final decision is made, thus many of these ENDS products remain available. Any products reviewed by FDA that were issued a marketing denial order cannot be marketed legally. However, some denial orders have been rescinded and are back under review, including JUUL (Reuters, 2024).

By the end of 2022 (the completion of survey fieldwork for our study), three brands of ENDS devices had received new product authorization (Vuse, NJOY, Logic) in tank or pod/cartridge-based systems and only in tobacco flavor (FDA, 2024a). Newer disposable brands have not been authorized, although they have flooded the US ENDS market in the last two years (Perrone, 2023, Yin, 2023; WSPM Group, 2024). A study by Crespi et al. (Crespi et al., 2024) found that between 2020 and 2023, there was an increase in the number of unique devices and e-liquids, notably disposables and sweet flavors, used by US adults (ages 21+) who frequently vaped (≥5 days/week). Among all respondents, only 1.3 % of adults reported using PMTA-authorized devices. The study used a convenience sample of adults across all 50 US states who vaped daily/near daily, but did not specifically include adults who were smoking or who had quit smoking (Hardesty, 2023), which is the target population for harm-reduction purposes (FDA, 2024b).

Building on this previous work, we estimated the prevalence of vaping in 2022 and examined which flavors, devices, and brands were used most often among a nationally representative sample of US adults who smoked cigarettes or formerly smoked in 2022. Our study sample was comprised of individuals who could benefit from using ENDS to quit smoking and remain abstinent from smoking after quitting, including those ages 18–20 who may be able to switch more easily than older adults even though they are not legally allowed to buy tobacco products. Furthermore, as there is little information on differences in flavor and device preferences for this key population, we characterized adults using ENDS by device type (tanks, cartridges/pods, disposables) and flavor (tobacco flavor only, fruit flavor only, and all other flavors except tobacco and fruit) by sociodemographics, smoking status, vaping frequency, satisfaction with vaping versus (vs.) smoking, and reasons for vaping (likeable flavors, to reduce/quit smoking, or to stay quit from smoking). We also tested whether the flavor-device combinations differed among those who smoked on plans to quit smoking or recent attempts to quit smoking.

Methods

Study design and data collection

We used data from the Wave 4 (August–December 2022) US arm of the International Tobacco Control Four Country Smoking and Vaping Survey (ITC4CV) Survey, which is an on-going longitudinal study of nicotine product use. Adults (≥18 years) who smoke cigarettes (≥monthly), formerly smoked, and/or were using another nicotine product (≥weekly, e.g., ENDS, heated tobacco products, nicotine pouches) were recruited from the Ipsos KnowledgePanel (Ipsos, 2016–2024), which is a probability-based online panel that provides a statistically valid representation of the US population. Eligible respondents who smoked or formerly smoked must have reported smoking at least 100 cigarettes in their lifetime. Weights (calibrated using the 2022 National Health Interview Survey) were applied to make the sample as representative as possible of adults who smoke, quit smoking, and/or vape ENDS. The survey protocols and all materials received ethics approval. Complete details about the ITC4CV Survey are presented in the technical report (ITC Project, 2023).

The current study included respondents who smoked cigarettes at least monthly (n = 1400) or had quit smoking (n = 616) and estimated the prevalence of at least monthly ENDS use. We excluded 35 respondents who vaped less than monthly (only those who vape ≥ monthly are asked about their vaping behaviors). Among the subset of respondents who were vaping at least monthly (n = 554), we examined ENDS devices, flavors, and brands used most often, as well as their characteristics and behaviors related to vaping. Supplemental Fig. 1 presents a study flow diagram and Supplemental Table 1 describes the study sample.

Measures

The following variables were included in the analyses: age, sex, smoking status, and device type (four main types), flavor (10 flavor categories), and brand used most often. We also categorized respondents into nine groups by combinations of device type (tanks; cartridges/pods; disposables) and flavor (tobacco flavor only; fruit flavor only; and all other flavors except tobacco and fruit) to characterize respondents using one of the combinations by age, sex, income, smoking status, vaping frequency, satisfaction with vaping vs. smoking, and reasons for vaping (likeable flavors, to reduce/quit smoking, or to stay quit from smoking). Additionally, respondents who smoked were asked about their plans to quit smoking and whether they tried to quit in the past year. We previously published two studies that examined several reasons for vaping by cigarette smoking status (Gravely, 2022a) and by device (Gravely, 2023); however, this study focused on flavors and vaping to aid/maintain smoking cessation. Supplemental Table 2 provides details on the study measures included herein.

Statistical analyses

A weighted logistic regression was used to estimate prevalence of at least monthly vaping. Prevalence was estimated using conditional marginal estimates from the logistic model, adjusting for age, sex, and smoking status. Multinomial logistic models were used to: (1) examine ENDS flavors and devices used most among the subset of adults who were vaping, overall and by smoking status and age group; and (2) identify characteristics of those who used one of the nine flavor-device combinations (11 models). For all analyses, we defined age as ‘younger adults’ (18–39) vs. ‘older adults’ (40+) due to small sample sizes in some groups, particularly when the respondents were stratified by flavor and then into the 9 flavor-device subgroups. Finally, we used unweighted frequency data to identify all unique brands reported and the top 15 brands used most often. All analyses were conducted using SAS Version 9.4. Statistical significance was computed at the 95 % confidence level.

ENDS flavor category and device type use most often

Tanks were the most commonly used devices (34.7 %), followed by disposables (27.4 %), pre-filled cartridges/pods (23.0 %), and re-fillable cartridges/pods (14.9 %). Adults who smoked and who were younger were significantly more likely to use disposables, and significantly less likely to use a tank. The five most commonly used flavors were: fruit (33.9 %), tobacco (20.1 %), menthol (12.2 %), candy/sweets (10.8 %), and mixed ice flavors (10.0 %). Adults who formerly smoked were significantly more likely than adults who were smoking to use mint and candy/sweet flavors. Younger adults were significantly more likely than older adults to use fruit and mixed ice flavors, and less likely to use tobacco flavor (Table 1).

Flavor-device combinations

The results for characteristics by flavor-device combinations are presented in Table 2. Among all respondents who smoked or had quit smoking, flavor-device combinations differed significantly by age, smoking status, and vaping frequency, but did not differ by sex or income. We found differences across flavor-device combinations for the reason for vaping ‘because they like the flavors’ (highest: fruit in tanks: 93.1 %; lowest: tobacco-flavored disposables: 35.8 %) and for ‘vaping is at least as satisfying as smoking’ (highest: fruit in tanks: 93.3 %; lowest: tobacco-flavored disposables: 22.4 %).

Among those who smoked and endorsed vaping to help reduce smoking, a higher proportion used tobacco flavor in pod/cartridge devices, followed by fruit in tanks, and fruit in pod/cartridge devices. The lowest proportion used tobacco-flavored disposables. There were no significant differences by flavor-device combination for plans to quit smoking, having made a recent attempt to quit smoking, or vaping to help quit smoking.

Among adults who formerly smoked, the majority of all adults vaped to remain abstinent from smoking, with tobacco flavor in tanks, fruit flavor in tanks, and tobacco-flavored disposables with the highest proportion endorsing this reason for vaping. The lowest proportion who endorsed this reason for vaping were using fruit-flavored disposables (62.6 %) and fruit in pod/cartridge devices (77.3 %).

Discussion

While it is difficult to distinguish between ENDS that are currently under review by CTP versus those being sold illegally outside enforcement discretion, the majority of adults who were vaping and smoking or formerly smoked in 2022 were using a variety of flavors and devices that go beyond the products that had been authorized for sale by CTP. Fruit was the most commonly used flavor; however, the use of fruit flavors may have been underestimated. For example, the choice of ‘fruit only’ was the most commonly used characterizing flavor category, but mixed fruit flavors (e.g., blueberry ice, strawberry shortcake) would not have been counted as ‘fruit only’. Disposables were more often used by younger adults, and tanks by older adults. Respondents reported using about 100 unique ENDS brands, with JUUL being the most common. Although some Vuse tobacco flavor e-liquids have been authorized for sale by the FDA, and was the 3rd most popular ENDS brand reported by respondents, more than half were using non-tobacco flavors. Our findings were similar to Crespi et al. where Smok, Geekvape, and Vuse were in the top five ENDS brands used most in 2022. Elf Bar has surpassed JUUL in more recent years (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), 2023, Crespi et al., 2024, Greenfield, 2022, Initiative, 2023).

ENDS use behavior rapidly changes in response to changes in the availability of products on the market, along with the introduction of newer devices. Previous ITC studies found that among US adults who smoked or formerly smoked, tanks (refillable box-shaped and pen devices) were the most commonly used devices and disposables were least popular in 2016 (O’Connor, 2019) and 2018 (Felicione et al., 2022). Gravely et al. (Gravely et al., 2022b) found that among US adults who vaped and smoked or formerly smoked in 2018 and 2020, 62.8 % and 51.5 % used refillable tanks respectively; 29.1 % and 39.6 % used pre-filled cartridge/pod devices, and fewer than 10 % used disposables in both years. According to our findings in 2022, there has been a shift in the opposite direction, with tanks decreasing in popularity (34.7 % used tanks most often) and a large increase in disposable ENDS use (27.4 % used disposables most often). Our 2022 data also show that disposables were more commonly used than pre-filled pod/cartridge devices (23.0 %).

One possibility for changes in device type preferences in recent years may be partly attributed to the FDA’s priority enforcement against flavored prefilled cartridges/pods in 2020 (with the exception of tobacco and menthol), which shifted the market to devices that were exempt from the enforcement priority (Kasza et al., 2023). Notably, the significant increase in the use of disposables–which were exempt from the 2020 policy–occurred within a year following the enforcement priority on cartridge devices, with brands such as ‘Puff Bar’ and then ‘Elf Bar’ (now known as EBDESIGN), driving the recent market changes (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), 2023, Crespi et al., 2024, Initiative, 2023, Yin, 2023). Another reason for the massive increase in disposables is that they are easy to use, have larger nicotine reserves, come in a multitude of flavors, contain high concentrations of nicotine, have high marketing exposure (particularly on social media), and are often less expensive than non-disposable brands. We anticipate that this trend may continue if the flurry of disposable brands from China continue to be available for sale in the US market (Kirkham et al., 2023, Tobaccoreporter., 2024; WSPM Group, 2024).

The use of cartridge/pod devices appears to have somewhat remained stable between 2020 (Gravely et al., 2022b) and 2022; however, Kasza et al. (Kasza et al., 2023) found that after the 2020 priority enforcement, the use of fruit-flavored cartridge ENDS use decreased, whereas fruit-flavored disposable ENDS use increased. The present data also show that cartridges/pods were the only device type where tobacco flavor was more prevalent than fruit, thus it appears that adults who used cartridge/pod devices in flavors that were prioritized to be removed from the market, switched to devices that offer fruit/sweet flavors, or continued to use pods in tobacco or menthol flavors after the 2020 enforcement policy came into effect (Gravely et al., 2022b, Kasza et al., 2023).

The findings of our study demonstrate that a variety of devices and flavors are used by adults who smoke or formerly smoked cigarettes. Thus, careful consideration should be given to (a) providing a variety of flavors and devices that will increase the appeal, acceptability, and satisfaction for using ENDS by adults who smoke and want to quit smoking, while simultaneously limiting appeal to youth and young adults who do not smoke, and (b) the effects of enforcement actions that remove products with a PMTA denial order from the market on rates of cigarette smoking for adults who are using ENDS to quit smoking or as a means to stay abstinent from cigarettes.

Causal Effect of Blood Cell Perturbation Phenotypes on Multiple Sclerosis via Immune Mediation: A Mendelian Randomization Study
Authors: Jia-Jia Yun, Jin-Qiu Wang, Jia-Hui Wang, Zhen Wang, Ya-Lei Li, Yu Yang, Fang-Min Chen, Chao Ren
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Multiple sclerosis (MS) is an inflammatory disease that affects the central nervous system, characterized by myelin damage caused by immune dysfunction and genetic factors. Nevertheless, the role of peripheral blood and immune cells in the development of MS remains poorly defined. We employed a two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) approach, analyzing data from 91 blood cell perturbation phenotypes and 731 immune cell traits. Causal inference was conducted using multiple robust MR techniques, including inverse variance weighting, with mediation analysis and sensitivity tests (Cochran’s Q, MR-Egger intercept, and leave-one-out analysis) performed to validate the results.The present study identified significant associations between 9 blood cell perturbation phenotypes and 34 immune cell traits with MS risk. The effect of neutrophil disturbances on MS was partially mediated by HLA-DR expression on B cells, with a mediation proportion of approximately 16.38%. Moreover, sensitivity analyses confirmed the robustness of these findings.This study suggests that specific blood cell perturbations may increase MS risk and reveals the mediating role of immune cells between blood and nervous system disturbances. In addition, we provide genetic evidence for understanding MS immune mechanisms, which could help guide the development of targeted immunotherapies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10571-025-01617-2.

Introduction

Multiple sclerosis (MS) is an autoimmune disease affecting the central nervous system (CNS), characterized by myelin breakdown and gradual nerve deterioration. It affects approximately 2.8 million people worldwide, most of whom are between 18 and 40 years of age (Jakimovski et al. 2024).Individuals with MS may experience motor weakness, sensory disturbances, and cognitive difficulties, including problems with thinking and memory (Polman et al. 2011). The progression of MS can lead to severe and permanent disability, as well as substantial financial burdens, with annual care costs estimated at €22,800 for mild cases and €57,500 for severe cases (Kobelt et al. 2017). Although genetic factors are linked to MS, environmental elements are believed to disrupt the immune system, triggering harmful immune responses that play a key role in MS development (Liu et al. 2025). Notably, activation of CD4⁺ T cells enables their entry into the CNS, where they release GM-CSF and IL-17, attracting other immune cells and causing damage to oligodendrocytes and myelin (Jamann et al. 2024; Li et al. 2024). Reduced Nrg-1β1 levels in MS patients may weaken its regulatory influence on microglia, leading these cells to produce more IL-1β and IL-6. In animal models, restoring Nrg-1β1 has been shown to reduce neuroinflammation and demyelination (Kataria et al. 2021). Current therapies aim to eliminate B cells and reduce lymphocyte numbers in the brain; however, this may disrupt immune balance and overlook key immune cell targets (Roodselaar et al. 2021). Therefore, identifying and understanding the main immunological pathways in MS is essential for developing more precise and effective therapies.

Recent advances in testing blood cell features (Homilius et al. 2024) now allow researchers to study diseases by replicating their conditions in the laboratory. This approach enables the examination of how cells respond to stress, offering insights into vulnerabilities within the hematopoietic system. While a few previous studies have explored how environmental factors influence immune cell activity through epigenetic changes (Tavassolifar et al. 2020), most have focused on stable immune traits. It is crucial to use genetic approaches to understand how blood cells respond to environmental factors, how these responses influence MS risk, and the role of different immune cells in exacerbating the disease.

Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) are the primary method for establishing clinical causality. Nonetheless, for complex diseases, conducting RCTs can be challenging due to the large sample sizes required, lengthy follow-up periods, ethical constraints, and high costs. Mendelian randomization (MR) uses natural genetic variations in a manner analogous to the random assignments in RCTs.(Emdin et al. 2017) This approach helps minimize confounding factors and overcomes many of the limitations in determining causality inherent to observational studies.

MR techniques enable the investigation of whether blood cell features contribute to increased MS risk. To our knowledge, this is the first application of the two-sample MR method to explore this question. Together, these findings may help guide the development of novel treatment strategies for MS.

Materials and Methods

Study Design

This study was reported following the STROBE-MR reporting guidelines (Skrivankova et al. 2021). We conducted a two-sample, two-step Mendelian randomization (TS-MR) analysis to investigate the role of immune cells in mediating the effects of blood cell phenotype perturbations on MS. Publicly available genome-wide association study (GWAS) data were used to identify single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) strongly associated with our exposure variables, which served as instrumental variables (IVs). The analysis was considered reliable because the selected IVs met three key Mendelian randomization assumptions (Sleiman and Grant 2010; Burgess and Thompson 2013): (1) IVs are strongly associated with the exposure factors; (2) IVs are not influenced by confounding factors that could affect the exposure–outcome relationship; and (3) IVs affect the outcome only through the specific exposure and not via any unrelated biological pathways. TS-MR analysis was used to examine how changes in blood cell features, such as their characteristics or energy demands in MS, may increase the risk of developing the disease. The present study then assessed how alterations in blood cell perturbation phenotypes influenced previously identified immune cell traits and quantified the extent to which these effects contributed to MS risk. Sources of Data

The GWAS summary data of 91 blood cell perturbation phenotypes that this study used come from the study by Homilius M et al.(Homilius et al. 2024). A Lab experiment on 2,600 individuals of European descent was carried out, various stimulants were used, and different measurement times were chosen to study how blood cells respond to environmental changes. Specific information can be seen in Supplementary material 1. In this paper, there were 731 immune cell traits, fully displaying quantitative information about multiple immune cell subsets in the periphery (Orrù et al. 2020). Four types of measurements were taken: absolute cell (AC) counts (n = 118), relative cell (RC) counts (n = 192), median fluorescence intensities (MFIs) (n = 389), from surface antigen, and morphological parameters (MPs) (n = 32). The panels defined by the MFI, AC, and RC include B cells, CDCs, mature T-cell stages, monocytes, myeloid cells, TBNK (T, B, NK), and Tregs. The MP feature consists solely of CDC and TBNK panels. The IEU OpenGWAS database provided the data(URL: https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/). See supplementary material 2 for specific information. MS’s GWAS summary statistics came from Release 12 of the FinnGen Consortium (FGC). This dataset included 498,857 individuals from European ancestry: 2,926 cases and 495,931 controls. FGC is a large genomics project which analyzed more than 500 K Finnish biobank samples, linked with genetic with health data to understand the disease mechanism and susceptibility. Note that the GWAS data were from different consortia or organizations. In theory, there was no sample overlap among the groups (more details are given in supplementary Table 1).

Selection of IVs Using SNPs

MR analysis for this study was performed using the R package TwoSampleMR (version 0.6.8) and adhered to all three key MR assumptions. First, we selected SNPs associated with changes in blood cell perturbation phenotypes and immune cell traits using a stringent significance threshold (P < 5 × 10⁻⁸) to satisfy the relevance criterion. If this threshold yielded too few instrumental variables (IVs), we increased it to P < 1 × 10⁻5 as described by previous studies (Wang et al. 2023) so as to include additional relevant genetic variants while balancing the number and strength of IVs. The clump data function from the TwoSampleMR package was also used to group identified SNPs by applying a stringent linkage disequilibrium (LD) threshold (r2 < 0.001) to avoid strong LD within each exposure factor (Wang et al. 2021). During data preparation, the harmonize data function was employed to ensure alignment of effect alleles and effect size directions between exposure and outcome datasets, while removing ambiguous or inconsistent SNPs to maintain consistency. The F-statistics were calculated for each SNP to assess instrument strength, retaining only those with values greater than 10 as final IVs (Burgess and Thompson 2011). All summary data used in this analysis underwent quality control during the original studies. These rigorous checks ensured that the final set of IVs was reliable, independent, and met the fundamental assumptions of MR analysis, supporting a robust conclusion on causality. Further details are provided in Supplementary material 3.

Two-Sample MR Analysis

All research data were made publicly available and underwent summary-level de-identification; therefore, no additional ethical approval was required. All MR analyses were performed using the TwoSampleMR package (version 0.6.8) in R. The primary analysis employed the inverse variance weighted (IVW) method (Burgess et al. 2013; Bowden et al. 2017) which calculates a weighted average of the effects of the IVs on the outcome. IVW provides precise estimates by accounting for the contribution of each genetic variant and the variability in their associations. To determine whether to apply a fixed-effect or random-effect IVW model, heterogeneity tests were conducted.

The robustness of the results was further assessed using the weighted median method (Bowden et al. 2016), MR-Egger intercept (Burgess and Thompson 2017; Verbanck et al. 2018), weighted mode, and simple mode approaches. The MR-Egger intercept evaluates the extent to which SNPs have pleiotropic effects on both the exposure and outcome by examining the intercept term. This provides an estimate of the true causal effect after accounting for bias from confounding factors. In essence, the MR-Egger intercept offers a corrected causal estimate that adjusts for potential violations of MR assumptions due to horizontal pleiotropy. The weighted median method calculates the median effect across all IVs, providing a valid causal estimate even if up to half of the IVs are invalid, provided the median-weighted SNP is valid. The weighted mode identifies the most common effect estimate among IVs and remains accurate as long as the largest group consists of valid IVs. Similarly, the simple mode determines the most frequent effect estimate directly, but without applying weights.

We applied mediation analysis within the TS-MR framework to assess the mediating role of immune cell traits. First, we used the IVW method to estimate the total effect (β_all) of blood cell perturbation phenotypes on MS. Next, we calculated the mediating effect by multiplying the impact of blood cell perturbation phenotypes on immune cell traits (β₁) by the effect of those immune cell traits on MS (β₂). The proportion of mediation was then quantified using the mediation ratio, defined as (β₁ × β₂)/β_all.

Sensitivity Analysis

We conducted several sensitivity analyses to evaluate the robustness of MR results and assess whether any assumptions were violated. The MR-Egger intercept test was used to detect significant horizontal pleiotropy; an intercept significantly different from zero (P < 0.05) suggests the presence of pleiotropic bias. We applied the MR-PRESSO tool to identify and remove potentially problematic SNPs exhibiting horizontal pleiotropy, ensuring the validity of our findings after correcting for outliers. The Cochran’s Q test (Kulinskaya et al. 2011) was used to assess heterogeneity among causal effect estimates from different IVs; a significant result indicates variability in effect estimates. To determine if any single SNP disproportionately influenced the overall causal estimate, we performed a leave-one-out analysis (Cheng et al. 2017). Moreover, funnel plots were used to detect any evidence of bias. Together, these comprehensive sensitivity analyses support a thorough assessment of the reliability and robustness of our MR study results.

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted utilizing standard R packages (version 4.4.1), comprising TwoSample MR (version 0.6.8), MR-PRESSO (version 1.0), foreach (version 1.5.2), ggplot2 (version 3.5.1), forestploter (version 1.1.2), and grid (version 4.4.1).

Mediation Analysis of Immune Cell Traits

To investigate how immune cell traits might influence MS risk in response to altered blood cell perturbation phenotypes, we conducted extensive MR analyses. First, using the IVW method, we examined associations between 731 immune traits and MS risk, identifying 34 immune cell traits significantly predicting MS risk (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3 and supplementary Table 3). Next, we applied the IVW method to assess the relationships between these 34 immune cell traits, 9 blood cell perturbation phenotypes, and MS risk. Additional tests, including the MR-Egger intercept, were performed to validate the results. We also conducted sensitivity analyses using MR-Egger, weighted median, weighted mode, and simple mode methods to confirm the robustness of our findings. Discussion

Causal Associations of Neutrophil Perturbations with MS Risk

MS is an autoimmune disease characterized by chronic inflammation and demyelination of the central nervous system, with complex pathophysiological mechanisms closely associated with immune system dysfunction(Walton et al. 2020). While previous MR studies have revealed the role of immune cells in MS, most have focused on static immune markers(He et al. 2022), often overlooking the dynamic mechanisms underlying immune responses to stress. Khan et al.(Khan et al. 2023) previously noted that current MS diagnosis largely depends on static biomarkers, such as oligoclonal bands and neurofilament proteins, which, despite their diagnostic value, do not fully capture functional changes in the immune system.

The present study combines the TS-MR analysis framework with blood cell perturbation phenotype data to systematically assess the potential causal relationship between immune cell characteristics under stress conditions and MS risk. In total, we identified nine blood cell perturbation phenotypes significantly associated with MS risk. Mediation analysis further revealed that HLA-DR molecules on the surface of B cells may mediate the effect of neutrophil perturbations, specifically, the side fluorescence coefficient of variation of neutrophil 4 in response to colchicine treatment measured by WDF dye, on MS risk, with a mediation effect accounting for 16.38%. Previous studies have shown that HLA-DR15 carriers exhibit elevated HLA-DRB1 expression in MS brain tissue and are linked to more severe cortical lesions(Enz et al. 2020), supporting our hypothesis that dynamic HLA-DR expression plays a key role in MS pathogenesis.

This perturbation phenotype uses the microtubule inhibitor colchicine to simulate cellular stress, combined with flow cytometry WDF channel fluorescence signals, to quantify the response heterogeneity of a specific neutrophil subpopulation (NE4) under stress. In CNS-related pathological states, neutrophil subpopulations with NE4-like characteristics may exacerbate neuroinflammation through multiple pathways. For instance, they release reactive oxygen species (ROS) and pro-inflammatory mediators such as IL-1β and IL-6, which directly damage the blood–brain barrier (BBB). They also form neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) containing components like histones and myeloperoxidase, which not only induce vascular endothelial damage and promote immune thrombosis but also expose self-antigens, such as myelin proteins, thereby triggering or amplifying autoimmune responses(Tang et al. 2024; Sun et al. 2023; Dowey et al. 2021). Additionally, ROS can inhibit neutrophil apoptosis by activating the STAT6 signaling pathway, prolonging their pro-inflammatory effects and NET release(Huang et al. 2024). Collectively, these processes form key steps in the vicious cycle of BBB leakage, microglial activation, neuronal damage, and neuroinflammation.

Mediating Role of HLA-DR in MS Pathogenesis

The present study further investigated the potential biological mechanisms linking neutrophil dysfunction to MS risk through mediation analysis. The results indicated that HLA-DR molecules on B cells may partially mediate the effect of altered neutrophil characteristics on MS risk, with an estimated mediation proportion of 16.38%. As the primary MHC class II molecule, HLA- DR’s key role on B cells is to activate CD4⁺ T cells via antigen presentation (Kotsias et al. 2019; Guermonprez et al. 2002). However, HLA-DR functions extend beyond antigen presentation, playing a role in regulating complex immune cell interaction networks (Shmakova et al. 2024; Ye et al. 2024), For example, studies have shown that specific pathogen components, such as the HIV Tat protein, can modulate B cell HLA-DR and its co-stimulatory molecule expression by influencing the NF-κB signaling pathway, thereby affecting immune responses (Maas-Bauer et al. 2024). This indicates that HLA-DR expression is tightly regulated and crucial for B cell function. In the context of neuroinflammation, HLA-DR-expressing B cells and infiltrating neutrophils may engage in mutual regulation: factors secreted by B cells can recruit and activate neutrophils (Vazquez et al. 2015; Duddy et al. 2007; Ahn et al. 2021), while neutrophil-derived proteases may in turn affect B cell HLA-DR expression and antigen-presenting capacity (Tang et al. 2024). This potential positive feedback loop may amplify the release of neurotoxic mediators, worsening neural damage. Notably, HLA-DR plays a distinctive role in MS pathogenesis. The HLA-DR15 haplotype is one of the strongest genetic susceptibility factors for MS, with its encoded DR2a and DR2b molecules effectively presenting myelin-associated autoantigens, such as citrullinated MBP peptides, and exogenous trigger antigens like Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) proteins to CD4⁺ T cells on B cell surfaces. This process drives the activation and expansion of autoreactive T-cell clones, ultimately promoting autoimmune attacks against the CNS (Wang et al. 2020). Furthermore, our findings connect the dynamic response of neutrophils to the critical antigen-presenting and immune-regulatory functions of B cells, suggesting that abnormal neutrophil activation may, directly or indirectly, influence HLA-DR expression on B cells or alter the range of antigens they present, thereby promoting MS onset. Regarding targeted interventions, a small-molecule inhibitor of HLA-DR2b, PV-267, has been developed; it blocks the presentation of myelin antigens in vitro, inhibits self-reactive T-cell activation, and shows promising preventive and therapeutic effects in an experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis model (Ji et al. 2013). Besides, recent studies indicate that histones in NETs promote Th17 cell differentiation via TLR2, exacerbating autoimmune inflammation. Histone neutralizers, such as mCBS, may disrupt this positive feedback loop(Wilson et al. 2022), offering novel avenues for targeted MS therapy.

Strengths, Limitations, and Future Directions

Importantly, our study combines the TS-MR method with large-scale blood cell perturbation phenotype data to explore the potential role of immune system plasticity in MS pathogenesis. Compared to traditional observational studies, it reduces the impact of confounding bias and reverse causality, while shifting the focus from static immune cell counts to dynamic immune responses. This approach offers new insights into how immune adaptability influences MS risk. Nevertheless, this study has several limitations that should be taken into account. Although the selected IVs meet the core assumptions of MR analysis and multiple sensitivity analyses were conducted to mitigate the effects of pleiotropy, it is impossible to completely rule out that these genetic instruments may influence MS risk through unknown pathways, which is an inherent limitation of the MR method. Moreover, this study primarily relied on GWAS data from European populations. Given genetic and environmental differences across populations, the generalizability of these findings to other ethnic groups requires caution (Adam et al. 2021), and future studies should aim to replicate these results in more diverse cohorts. This study selected FinnGen R12 as the source of MS outcome data due to its high-quality genotype data, clear and consistent phenotype definitions, and relatively homogeneous European population composition, which effectively controls for population structure-related biases. Although its sample size is slightly smaller than that of larger multi-center datasets like IMSGC and its population representativeness is somewhat limited, FinnGen R12 offers strong adaptability and data consistency for causal inference at the mechanistic level, providing a solid foundation for this study. Future research will integrate larger and more diverse datasets, such as IMSGC, to improve the robustness and generalizability of findings. Furthermore, while MR analysis suggests that specific altered blood cell traits may causally influence MS risk via certain immune cell phenotypes, the underlying biological mechanisms remain unclear and require validation through longitudinal cohort studies and experimental cellular and animal models. In addition, translating these findings into clinical interventions, particularly for MS prevention and treatment, poses significant challenges, including determining optimal dosages, addressing genetic heterogeneity among individuals, and conducting long-term safety assessments. While the potential therapeutic targets identified by MR studies are promising, developing targeted interventions requires overcoming technical hurdles related to target specificity, safety, and efficacy. Future research should prioritize integrating multi-omics data and conducting RCTs to further validate these genetic insights and advance their clinical translation into precision medicine strategies.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the present study innovatively applied causal inference to investigate dynamic immune phenotypes, highlighting the critical need to shift focus from static cellular composition to dynamic functional regulation in understanding MS immunopathology. Moreover, our findings offer new insights and genetic evidence to guide the development of future therapeutic strategies targeting immune system functional plasticity.

A commentary on ‘Impact of virtual reality education on disease-specific knowledge and anxiety for hepatocellular carcinoma patient scheduled for liver resection: a randomized controlled study’
Authors: Guangwei Qing, Guang Yang
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

The rising mortality rate associated with liver cancer, particularly hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), poses a significant public health concern1. While radical hepatectomy offers a primary treatment avenue, many patients harbor anxiety about the procedure due to limited understanding. With the burgeoning use of virtual reality (VR) technology in clinical settings, patients’ comprehension of treatment plans, particularly surgical interventions, can be enhanced, potentially reducing anxiety. A recent randomized controlled trial by Jaehun Yang et al.2, published in the International Journal of Surgery, sheds light on this matter through a comprehensive analysis involving 88 participants. We offer recommendations aimed at improving the manuscript’s quality.

Initially, while the study aimed to comprehensively consider the representativeness and quality of the sample, we believe that the exclusion criteria set by Jaehun Yang et al. may not be sufficiently stringent, potentially introducing some degree of bias into the study results. We suggest refining the inclusion and exclusion criteria to enhance the robustness of the study and minimize potential biases. Therefore, we recommend augmenting the exclusion criteria as follows: encompassing a broader range of relevant mental disorders, including but not limited to anxiety disorders, panic disorders, somatic symptom disorders, etc.; clearly defining individuals currently undergoing treatment with antidepressant or antianxiety medications; and specifying individuals engaged in alcohol and psychoactive substance abuse.

Moreover, while the authors utilized relevant assessment tools such as the Korean versions of State-Trait Anxiety Inventory-X (STAI-X)-1 and STAI-X-2, alongside the visual analog scale, for anxiety assessment, we suggest incorporating the globally recognized Self-Rating Anxiety Scale (SAS) for a more thorough evaluation3. Additionally, we propose adopting a multimodal approach to assess mental health, which integrates various assessment modalities such as self-report scales, clinical interviews, and medical record reviews. This multifaceted strategy enhances both the comprehensiveness and accuracy of anxiety evaluations, overcoming the limitations associated with relying solely on one assessment tool and facilitating a more holistic comprehension of patients’ mental well-being.

Additionally, to mitigate the impact of extraneous variables on the results, this study meticulously adjusted for several significant confounders, such as age, sex, level of education, prior psychiatric history, etiology of HCC, previous medical interventions, and the planned surgical procedure. Nevertheless, we propose broadening the scope of analysis to encompass additional potential confounding factors, thereby enhancing the study’s robustness and consistency. These factors may encompass income, intraoperative antibiotic administration, employment status, marital status, socioeconomic status, level of physical activity, presence of diabetes or hypertension, history of alcohol consumption, and smoking habits4.

Lastly, the manuscript would benefit from a more detailed exploration of the origin of anxiety, whether stemming from HCC itself or anticipation of surgery. Further information through surveys or clinical observations could elucidate psychological reactions and coping strategies, enhancing understanding of anxiety sources.

In summary, the research conducted by Jaehun Yang et al. represents a significant step forward in our comprehension of the efficacy of VR education in alleviating preoperative anxiety among individuals undergoing liver resection. Their study offers a thorough examination of the impact of VR-based interventions on preoperative anxiety management, providing fresh and valuable insights into the potential of immersive technology to improve patient experience and surgical outcomes. This investigation establishes a crucial foundation for tailoring personalized care strategies aimed at enhancing both postoperative recovery and the psychological well-being of patients. We appreciate the opportunity to provide feedback on this study and anticipate further exploration in this domain to continually enhance patient care standards.

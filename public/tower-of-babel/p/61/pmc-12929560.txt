DBH-Chain: a decentralized blockchain-enabled healthcare framework for end-to-end delay optimization
Authors: Mansoor Ali, Ramesh Kumar, Muhammad Zahid Tunio, Abdullah Lakhan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe enormous growth in the development of smart healthcare services has increased the need for protective, secure, low-latency, and scalable infrastructure capabilities to support heterogeneous devices and continuous clinical processes. In order to provide smooth communication between mobile, wireless, fog, and cloud nodes, this article offers DBH-Chain, a lightweight, decentralized blockchain-enabled healthcare system that mainly uses a client-to-fog and fog-to-cloud socket architecture. To address the hybrid intercommunication and processing delays among the nodes of the heterogeneous layers, the research creates a queuing-based Mobile Measurement Partner (MMP) hierarchy in conjunction with a Knapsack-driven adaptive scheduling algorithm and Markov Modulated Poisson Stream (MMPS). Strict latency limits and effective task execution are made possible by this design technique. For creating security and trustworthiness, in order to address lightweight computation, this research propose a Proof of Validation (PoV) consensus that guarantees authenticated interactions among the participating distributed nodes. In comparison to other cutting-edge healthcare architectures, the results of the simulation show that the proposed DBH-Chain achieves up to 99.32% scheduling efficiency, a reduction in hybrid delay down to 41% to 57% and maintains the data validation integrity in the ledger up to 98.87%. However, the report of comprehensive analysis confirmed that the proposed solution significantly reduces end-to-end latency, strengthens data security, and improves workload distributions. In short, it provides a robust and practical foundation for futuristic smart healthcare systems.Keywords: Blockchain, Distributed ledger technology (DLT), Healthcare system, Markov modulated poisson stream (MMPS), Knapsack, Adoptive schedulingSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

These days, the global shift towards intelligent and connected healthcare ecosystems has expedited the effective adoption and deployment of wireless sensor networks, mobile devices, fog, and cloud infrastructure that can continue to support patient monitoring1,2. Emergency response and real-time monitoring of medical data interchange are also included. Nevertheless, despite their remarkable potential, existing healthcare systems remain heavily dependent on server-based architectures that suffer from significant challenges, including a single point of failure, high intercommunication delays, increased vulnerabilities to cyberthreats, and limited scalability2–4, as shown in Fig. 1. As dynamic healthcare applications continue to grow, particularly in critical domains such as remote diagnoses, telemedicine, and Internet of Medical Things (IoMT)-enabled clinical monitoring. Achieving secure, protected, transparent, low-latency, and efficient data processing has become a critical technological priority4,5, as shown in Fig. 1.

In the meantime, a number of technical constraints, namely network problems and a lack of interoperability, are impeding the current end-to-end optimization in healthcare4–6. In addition, there are organizational issues such as disjointed systems and a lack of workers. Thus, opposition to change and legislative obstacles, particularly those related to data privacy, exist. These highlighted factors become the direction of this paper; the research proposes a novel and secure lightweight decentralized BDLT-based healthcare Distributed Application (DApp) designed specifically for hybrid delay optimization in a heterogeneous network environment. This DApp employs a client-fog with cloud socket architecture to streamline the flow of data across nodes with fluctuating capabilities. In order to effectively reduce hybrid delay, this paper deploys a Markovian MMPS queuing hierarchy with knapsack-enabled adoptive scheduling algorithm. Motivation and research questions

In recent times, the advanced healthcare systems increasingly rely on continuous data acquisition from the initial stage of the lifecycle to the end. In this whole scenario, the collection of data from heterogeneous environments, like mobile devices, wireless sensors of IoMT, fog-enabled gateways, and from the cloud, enables clinical repositories retrieval. While evaluating this, this paper also examines the rising volume and velocity of healthcare data management, especially in the centralized server-based architecture, which is still unable to efficiently process and deliver time-sensitive medical records. Due to this, the research found that some of the centralized bottlenecks, like latency issues, reduce reliability and expose critical threats while preserving privacy in healthcare information, which mainly compromises patient safety in a real-time environment. Along with that, hybrid delay is one of the rising research prospects that emerged when data travels across multiple communication layers, especially the mobile-to-cloud layer, wireless sensor-to-fog/edge layer, and fog-to-cloud layer, as shown in Fig. 3. It has become one of the most persistent limitations affecting the dynamic management of healthcare applications.

The proposal of the proposed design.

While investigating, this paper analyzes that the successful adaptation of BDLT in healthcare platforms is not performing well enough in terms of imposing high computational costs. It is due to the integration of heavy consensus protocols like Proof of Work (PoW) and Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT), as shown in Fig. 3. Such consensus is unsuitable for healthcare DApp, especially for those platforms that belong to heterogeneous healthcare, where mobile devices and fog nodes have limited processing power. These factors raise a clear need for a lightweight, protected, transparent, secure, and delay-aware BDLT DApp that can seamlessly operate across resource-allocated nodes without affecting the performance, as shown in Fig. 3.

In addition, the healthcare data streams exhibit high variability and unpredictability, requiring real-time health task scheduling in order to ensure that latency-sensitive tasks are prioritized. However, the current approaches lack automated protocols to coordinate workloads across the client-fog solution, which mainly leads to inefficient resource usage and increased end-to-end delay. Due to this issue, the paper highlights the critical gap that becomes the importance of designing a hybrid scheduling algorithm that can adaptively allocate tasks based on available resources, urgency levels, management, and queue dynamics maintenance.

The implementation of these critical factors is the major motivation for this paper, which aims to introduce a decentralized architecture for healthcare data management and security using BDLT. It addresses the major parameters of hybrid delays, medical task scheduling and management, and data protection. By incorporating an MMPS queuing system with adaptive scheduling and PoV protocol, the proposed DApp meets the dual requirements of data security and real-time performance in terms of lightweight task handling. Related work

In the recent environment, DApp using BDLT for healthcare service management have been largely adopted, recognized for their transparency, integrity, immutability, provenance, and decentralized trustworthiness. Early literature23,24 focused on using BDLT for the secure and protected storage of medical records and patient identity management, which not only rely heavily on traditional consensus protocols, such as PoW and PBFT, also known as IBFT, but also maintain hashing throughout for transactional record management. Nevertheless, such protocols required high-computing resource consumption and long confirmation delays, which mainly render the scheduled transactions unsuitable for real-time healthcare applications25. Existing solutions25–27 have shifted towards the lightweight BDLT infrastructures and permissioned private network environment in order to reduce the latency and support efficient resource management, especially for the node-based block transactions, like mobile with fog devices connectivity and management.

In this whole scenario, various research articles28,29 highlighted that fog and cloud computing technology are considered cost-efficient and effective enablers for reducing intercommunication delay by processing data closer to the sources. Throughout this, fog-enabling technology-assisted healthcare applications have shown significant enhancement in handling dynamic IoMT data streams and related management28–30. Whereas most current cloud architectures lack integrated support for secure data validation and decentralized trust, resulting in vulnerability to forgery and unauthorized access. In addition, such applications typically overlook hybrid delay limitations that pop up when healthcare service-enabled data traverses multiple communication layers.

On the other end, queuing theory has been applied to manage healthcare tasks and reduce congestion in permissioned private network. Markovian queuing algorithms, like MMP, have been used to estimate healthcare services times and system throughput, but they fail to consider multi-layered platforms and heterogonous healthcare tasks31,32. Currently, advanced queueing algorithms like Markov Modulated Poisson Stream (MMPS) have gained attention for modeling dynamic traffic patterns. Nevertheless, their integration into decentralized BDLT based healthcare architectures, which still remains limited.

Task scheduling plays a significant role in minimizing delay and enhancing resource consumption, especially in a multi-layer healthcare network. Algorithms like priority scheduling, round-robin, and metaheuristic-based algorithms have been proposed, but their suitability diminishes when maintaining heterogeneous task types across mobile-fog with a cloud environment. Optimization-enabled algorithms, including genetic algorithms, linear programming, and Knapsack, have demonstrated improved performance. Most of the previous studies33–35 combine like algorithms with BDLT-assistant healthcare architectures, which validate their impact on hybrid delay minimization.

As the adoption of BDLT grows drastically, technological experts emphasize the need for lightweight consensus protocols suitable for healthcare platforms with limited computational capabilities. Some of the novel protocols, like Proof of Authority (PoA) and other trust-based validation algorithms, have been proposed to reduce computational load. However, they still pose limitations related to validator centralization, trust assumptions, and scalability. The literature35–37 illustrates a lack of validation mechanisms specifically designed for healthcare nodes running across mobile-enabled cloud layers, which is still a gap that needs to be addressed by one of the lightweight mechanisms in recent decentralized architectures.

Open research direction

While the proposed DBH-Chain, a decentralized BDLT-enabled healthcare DApp, offers many advantages, there are still some unresolved research issues that need to be addressed in order to fully achieve technological scalability, security, provenance, trustworthiness, and efficiency. The list of open research issues with possible solutions is mentioned as follows:

Scalability in BDLT network design

As the number of healthcare nodes and devices increases, the maintenance of the BDLT network and its performance is critical while keeping latency low, becoming one of the challenging issues41–43. Currently, the classical consensus protocols may not be capable in terms of scale efficiency across thousands of mobile devices, cloud nodes, and middleware fog connectivity. Creating lightweight and adaptive consensus protocols is becoming one of the needs while developing the DApp. The design of PoV is created, where it requires further optimization to handle a large amount of IoMT traffic without compromising security. On the other side, the designed MMPS with Knapsack-enabled scheduling algorithm reduces hybrid delay; however, varying and adjusting network conditions, unpredictable workload arrivals, and high-density platforms can still cause occasional delays. Dynamic adaptive scheduling strategies that can respond instantly to permissioned private network congestion remain open research challenges.

Resource management and interoperability

Numerous fog and mobile nodes have little memory, battery, and computational power43–45. It guarantees that BDLT operations, PoV validation, and task executions continue to be lightweight and energy-efficient, which is still a significant constraint, particularly in IoMT platforms with limited resources. However, the private healthcare network of today frequently comprises multiple stakeholders using outdated standards. The integration of cloud platforms with the BDLT framework is necessary to ensure smooth interoperability and secure data sharing and exchange; this is still an ongoing research subject.

Data privacy, fault tolerance, and resilience

A decentralized BDLT-enabled DApp must adhere to local data protection laws and healthcare standards like HIPAA in order to secure sensitive patient data46,44,45. Although the integrity of the BDLT network is guaranteed, an ongoing restriction is the need to incorporate privacy-preserving techniques like Zero-Knowledge Proofs without causing a substantial delay. On the other hand, a major problem is the technology that makes sure the DApp can withstand malicious attacks, network consumption, and node failures without compromising overall compute speed. However, there is an ongoing research topic regarding the lightweight technique for distributed recovery and real-time reconfiguration in hybrid platforms.

Conclusion and future direction

This paper highlighted the critical issues, challenges, and limitations emerged in the centralized healthcare environment, especially the consumption of network bandwidth, importantly latency, throughput, and transactional delay. However, this paper offers DBH-Chain, a lightweight, BDLT-based healthcare DApp deployed that mainly uses a client-to-fog and fog-to-cloud socket architecture in order to provide smooth communication between mobile, wireless, fog, and cloud nodes. While addressing the hybrid intercommunication and processing delays among the nodes of the heterogeneous cloud-layers, the research creates a MMP hierarchy with MMPS based on queuing and a Knapsack-enabled adaptive scheduling algorithm. This design style enables efficient task execution and strict latency restrictions. On the other side, this paper presents a PoV consensus that addressed lightweight computing and guarantees authenticated interactions among the participating distributed nodes in order to create security and reliability. In comparison to other cutting-edge healthcare architectures, the simulation results show that the proposed DBH-Chain achieves up to 99.32% scheduling efficiency, a reduction in hybrid delay down to 41% to 57% and maintains the data validation integrity in the ledger up to 98.87%.

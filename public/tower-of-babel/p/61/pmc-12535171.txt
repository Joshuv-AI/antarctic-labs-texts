Genome-wide characterization of NHX gene family in Medicago sativa under abiotic stress
Authors: Yuqi Zhang, Xinyue Ma, Hao Liu, Li Zhao, Fei He, Mingna Li, Xue Wang, Ruicai Long, Junmei Kang, Qingchuan Yang, Lin Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSoil salinization is one of the major abiotic stresses limiting global crop production. As key regulators of plant salt tolerance, the NHX gene family remains systematically uncharacterized in alfalfa (Medicago sativa). In this study, genome-wide analysis identified 35 NHX family genes (MsNHXs) in alfalfa, phylogenetically classified into three subfamilies. Subcellular localization prediction revealed that while six genes localized to the vacuole, the majority were distributed on plasma membranes, suggesting their primary contribution to salt tolerance through membrane-associated mechanisms. Tissue-specific expression analysis identified four genes (MsNHX7, MsNHX12, MsNHX21, and MsNHX24) with distinct tissue expression preferences. Analysis of abiotic stress responses demonstrated that MsNHX1 and MsNHX10 responded to salt, drought, and cold stress, while MsNHX1 and MsNHX19 were specifically induced by drought. cis-acting element analysis further revealed promoter enrichment in defense/stress-responsive and phytohormone-related elements, consistent with their roles in environmental adaptation. This study provides a theoretical foundation for elucidating salt tolerance mechanisms in alfalfa and supports its genetic improvement.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-025-07458-x.Keywords: Medicago sativa L., MsNHX, Expression analysis, Abiotic stress

Introduction

Soil salinization, defined as the accumulation of water-soluble salts in the soil surface layer, is a pervasive environmental stressor that causes progressive soil degradation [1]. Globally, approximately 1.1 billion hectares (Gha) of land is affected by salinization [2], representing nearly 7% of the Earth’s terrestrial surface [3]. This process is further aggravated by anthropogenic activities, including improper wastewater management, excessive fertilizer application, and ecosystem degradation [4, 5]. As a degraded form of the primary medium for plant growth, saline soils have become a severe constraint on global agricultural productivity. Recent estimates indicate significant salinization across regions such as the Middle East and North Africa, Central Asia, Australia, Southern Europe, the southwestern United States, and parts of South America [6]. Therefore, identifying key salt-tolerance genes and elucidating their molecular mechanisms are crucial for developing salt-tolerant crops and ensuring sustainable agriculture in affected regions.

Salt stress triggers three major physiological challenges in plants: ionic stress, osmotic stress, and oxidative stress [7]. Na⁺ accumulation is the primary driver of ionic stress, with both the salt overly sensitive (SOS) pathway and Na⁺/H⁺ antiporters (NHX) playing central regulatory roles. The SOS signaling pathway comprises three core components: SOS1 (a plasma membrane-localized Na⁺/H⁺ antiporter that maintains cellular pH and ion homeostasis) [8], SOS2 (a serine/threonine protein kinase that activates SOS1) [9], and SOS3 (a calcium sensor that perceives salt-induced Ca²⁺ signals) [10, 11]. Functionally, SOS3 interacts with SOS2 to form a calcium-responsive complex that phosphorylates and activates SOS1, thereby mediating plant adaptation to ionic stress under saline conditions.

Concurrently, NHX proteins critically regulate ionic homeostasis by catalyzing electroneutral Na⁺ (or K⁺)/H⁺ exchange, a process driven by H⁺-electrochemical gradients from H⁺-ATPase and H⁺-PPase proton pumps. This exchange operates at a 1:1 stoichiometry with zero net charge movement (ΔΨ = 0), preserving membrane potential stability. In Arabidopsis, AtSOS1 [12] (synonym: AtNHX7) exemplifies functional-phylogenetic duality—identified as AtSOS1 via mutant screening yet phylogenetically classified as AtNHX7. The NHX family exhibits functional divergence across its eight members: plasma membrane-localized AtNHX7/SOS1 mediates Na⁺ extrusion to reduce cytoplasmic toxicity, while tonoplast-localized AtNHX1–AtNHX6 [13] sequester Na⁺ into vacuoles, lowering cytosolic concentrations and utilizing ions for osmotic adjustment. In contrast, AtNHX8 [14] (plasma membrane-localized) functions independently of the SOS pathway, primarily transporting K+ to maintain pH and ion homeostasis. Although vacuolar NHXs lack direct SOS regulation, their salt-inducible expression complements SOS1 activity, forming a synergistic network of extracellular exclusion and vacuolar sequestration for comprehensive Na+ detoxification.

The NHX gene family has been functionally characterized in multiple plant species, including Arabidopsis thaliana (initial identification) [12–14], Glycine max [15], Medicago truncatula [16], Solanum lycopersicum [17], Gossypium spp. [18, 19], and Solanum tuberosum [20]. In soybean, NHX genes demonstrated the longest response duration to KCl and NaCl stress, persisting for up to 48 h. In Medicago truncatula, MtNHX3, MtNHX6, and MtNHX7 showed significant upregulation after salt treatment in the roots. Similarly, in cotton, the NHX family exhibited differential expression under salt stress, with relative gene expression levels varying according to the duration of salt treatment. Silencing GhNHX1 in cotton significantly reduced salt tolerance, indicating that GhNHX1 acts as a positive regulator of salt stress resistance in cotton. However, research on NHX genes in alfalfa (Medicago sativa) remains limited, with only MsNHX1 functionally validated in heterologous systems (e.g., Arabidopsis). This gap is particularly significant given alfalfa’s value as a high-protein, high-yield perennial forage legume widely cultivated in China [21]. Alfalfa production faces severe constraints from limited arable land and abiotic stresses (e.g., salinity, drought), which critically reduce yield and utilization potential [22]. Consequently, identifying and characterizing salt-tolerance genes in alfalfa is essential to improve crop resilience, expand cultivation areas [23, 24], elucidate molecular mechanisms of abiotic stress tolerance, and enable targeted breeding of salt-tolerant germplasm.

This study systematically characterized the NHX gene family in alfalfa, including comprehensive analyses of gene identity, structural features, phylogenetic relationships, and promoter cis-regulatory elements. To elucidate their functional roles in stress responses, we investigated the expression profiles of NHX genes under three major abiotic stresses: salinity, drought, and low-temperature conditions. Transcriptomic findings were experimentally validated through qRT-PCR assays, ensuring data reliability. Our results demonstrate that the NHX family members not only serve as central regulators of salt tolerance but also exhibit significant involvement in drought and cold stress adaptation mechanisms.

Materials and methods

Identification and characterization of the NHX genes

The complete genomic resources for alfalfa (Medicago sativa L.), including protein sequences, gene annotation files (GFF format), and genomic DNA sequences, were obtained from the publicly available whole-genome sequencing project (https://fgshare.com/projects/whole_genome_sequencing_and_assembly_of_Medicago_sativa/66380) [25]. Initially, BLASTP homology searches were performed against the alfalfa proteome using Arabidopsis thaliana AtNHX1-AtNHX8 protein sequences as queries, with a stringent E-value cutoff of 1E−5. Concurrently, the hidden Markov model (HMM) of the NHX family conserved domain (PF00999) was retrieved from the Pfam database (http://pfam.xfam.org/) [26]. Using HMMER 3.0 with default parameters (E-value ≤ 1e−5, bit score ≥ 25), the alfalfa proteome was scanned to identify putative NHX homologs. The MsNHX gene family members were ultimately determined by integrating results from both BLASTP and HMMER screening.Subsequently, physicochemical properties of the identified MsNHX proteins - including isoelectric point (pI), molecular weight (MW), and instability index - were analyzed using the ProtParam tool on the ExPASy online platform (https://web.expasy.org/protparam/). Finally, subcellular localization predictions for MsNHX family members were conducted using the WoLF PSORT online prediction system (https://wolfpsort.hgc.jp/).

Chromosomal localization, evolutionary tree and collinearity analysis of the MsNHX gene in alfalfa

Based on the genome annotation data of Medicago sativa, we determined the physical locations of the MsNHX genes on the chromosomes and visualized their chromosomal distribution using TBtools. Subsequently, the MEGA11 [27] software was employed to perform multiple sequence alignment of the MsNHX protein sequences, with the following parameters: gap opening penalty of 10 and gap extension penalty of 0.2. A neighbor-joining (NJ) phylogenetic tree was constructed based on the alignment results, with branch support assessed by 1000 bootstrap replicates. Furthermore, the gene structure of MsNHX was analyzed according to the GFF annotation file, while synteny analysis was conducted using MCScanX [28] (E-value cutoff: 1E−5). The results were visualized using Advanced Circos.

Gene structure and conserved motif analysis of NHX Genes in Medicago sativa

The NHX gene sequences of Medicago sativa were obtained from the genome annotation file, and gene structure analysis including exon-intron organization was performed based on the GFF3 annotation file. To investigate the protein characteristics, conserved motifs in MsNHX proteins were identified using the MEME suite [29] (Multiple Em for Motif Elicitation) with default parameters, which predicted a total of 10 conserved motifs. Both the gene structure and motif distribution across MsNHX proteins were visualized using TBtools [30].

Cis-acting element analysis of the alfalfa MsNHX genes

To investigate the potential regulatory elements in the promoter regions of MsNHX genes, a 2.0 kb DNA sequence upstream of the coding sequence (CDS) of each MsNHX gene was extracted from the Medicago sativa genome. These sequences were then submitted to the PlantCARE database (http://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/plantcare/html/) for comprehensive prediction of cis-acting elements. The cis-acting elements were visualized and further screened.

Expression profiling of alfalfa MsNHX genes in different tissues

To characterize the expression patterns of MsNHX genes across various tissues, RNA-seq datasets encompassing six distinct tissue types (roots, elongated stems, pre-elongated stems, leaves, flowers, and nodules) were retrieved from the NCBI database. Additionally, transcriptome data from alfalfa subjected to salt stress (SRR7091780-SRR7091794), drought stress, and cold stress (SRR7160313-SRR7160357) under accession SRP055547 were analyzed to examine stress-responsive expression profiles [31, 32]. The obtained RNA-seq data were systematically processed to evaluate tissue-specific and stress-induced expression patterns of the MsNHX gene family.

Plant materials, growth conditions, stress treatments, and qRT-PCR analysis

The experiment utilized Zhongmu No. 1 alfalfa cultivar obtained from the Institute of Animal Science, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences. Seeds were treated at 4°C for 3 days before being transferred to greenhouse conditions with a 16/8 h photoperiod, 70–80% relative humidity, and day/night temperatures of 24 °C/20°C for 2 weeks of growth. For stress treatments, plants were subjected to three abiotic stress conditions: salt stress using 250 mM NaCl solution with root tip samples collected at 0 (control), 0.5, 1, 3, 6, 12, and 24 h post-treatment; drought stress using 400 mM mannitol with root tip samples collected at 1, 3, 6, 12, and 24 h; and cold stress at 4 °C with leaf samples collected at 0 (control), 2, 6, 24, and 48 h. For each treatment, three biological replicates were established, with each replicate consisting of five pooled seedlings randomly distributed in growth chambers to counter spatial effects. Control plants were grown under normal conditions (16 h of light/24°C, 8 h of darkness/20°C, and 70–80% humidity). For qRT-PCR analysis, gene-specific primers were designed using Primer 5.0 software (Table S8). Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent and reverse transcribed to cDNA using the EasyScript First-Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit with random primers (N9). Quantitative PCR was performed using SYBR Premix Ex Taq (TaKaRa, Japan) on a 7500 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems) with three technical replicates per biological replicate. The alfalfa actin gene served as the internal control, and relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method [33].

Statistical analysis of differential expression

Normalized gene expression values were obtained using the Median of Ratios method in DESeq2 [34]. Differential expression analysis was performed with the Wald test, and the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure [35] was applied to control the false discovery rate (FDR) at 5% (q < 0.05). Genes with absolute log₂ fold change ≥ 1 and q < 0.05 were defined as significantly differentially expressed.

Results

Genome-wide identification and chromosomal localization of NHX family members in the Medicago sativa genome

Through comprehensive genome-wide analysis combining BLAST comparisons with Arabidopsis NHX homologs, hidden Markov model (HMM) profiling, and protein structure validation, we identified 35 MsNHX genes in the autotetraploid alfalfa cultivar ‘Xinjiangdaye’ (Medicago sativa). As summarized in Table 1, the encoded proteins range from 269 to 564 amino acids in length, with predicted isoelectric points (pI) between 5.2 and 9.67. Approximately 77% of the genes exhibited instability coefficients lower than 40, suggesting that the majority encode relatively stable proteins. Subcellular localization predictions indicated that MsNHX genes are primarily localized to vacuoles and the plasma membrane, with 26 genes predicted to localize to the plasma membrane. The MsNHX genes were unevenly distributed across chromosomes, with only 19 out of the 32 chromosomes harboring these genes (Fig. 1). The highest density was observed on chr1.1–chr1.4 and chr7.4, collectively accounting for approximately 57% of the total MsNHX genes.

Chromosomal distribution of NHX genes in Medicago sativa

Phylogenetic and collinearity analyses of MsNHXs

To investigate the phylogenetic relationships of NHX proteins among Arabidopsis thaliana, Medicago truncatula, and Medicago sativa, we constructed an evolutionary tree using the Neighbor-Joining (NJ) method. The results revealed that the 49 NHX proteins analyzed included 8 NHX proteins from Arabidopsis thaliana, 6 NHX proteins from Medicago truncatula, and 35 NHX proteins from Medicago sativa. The smallest branch of the tree consisted of 8 NHX proteins, including only one MtNHX2 gene, while the remaining members were MsNHX proteins (Fig. 2). Phylogenetic analysis indicated that the NHX genes of Medicago sativa are more closely related to those of Medicago truncatula than to those of Arabidopsis thaliana.

Phylogenetic tree of NHX genes in Medicago sativa, Arabidopsis thaliana and Medicago truncatula

To further support this finding, we analyzed their syntenic relationships. The results showed 17 collinear pairs between MsNHX and AtNHX (Arabidopsis thaliana) genes, and 28 collinear pairs between MsNHX and MtNHX (Medicago truncatula) genes (Fig. 3). Among these, five MsNHX genes exhibited collinearity with AtNHX1. In contrast, in Medicago truncatula, the MtNHX2 gene showed the most significant collinear relationships, with six MsNHX genes being syntenic to it. In summary, the NHX gene families of Medicago truncatula, Arabidopsis thaliana, and Medicago sativa exhibit conserved phylogenetic relationships.

Gene structure and conserved motifs of MsNHX gene family

To elucidate the structure-function relationship of MsNHX proteins, we systematically analyzed their gene architectures and conserved motifs. Through MEME suite analysis, ten distinct motifs were identified across 35 MsNHX proteins (Fig. 4), with sequences exhibiting group-specific conservation patterns. Phylogenetic tree-based clustering classified these motifs into three major groups (Fig. 5), wherein intra-group motifs displayed significant sequence homology. In addition to motif conservation, exon-intron structural organization analysis revealed that all MsNHX genes contain multiple exons flanked by UTRs (Fig. 5), indicating strong structural conservation within this gene family.

Gene duplication events and collinearity analysis of MsNHX genes in Medicago sativa

To investigate the role of gene duplication events in the evolution of the MsNHX gene family, we identified tandem and segmental duplication events across chromosomes. A total of seven tandem duplication events were identified, involving the following gene pairs: MsNHX3/MsNHX4, MsNHX5/MsNHX6, MsNHX6/MsNHX7, MsNHX10/MsNHX11, MsNHX12/MsNHX13, MsNHX15/MsNHX16, and MsNHX30/MsNHX31 (Table S1). These tandem duplication events are predominantly located on chromosomes chr1.1–chr1.4 (Fig. 1), a region characterized by high MsNHX gene density. Given that gene duplication is a major driver of plant evolution, we hypothesize that the evolution of NHX genes in Medicago sativa largely occurred on chr1.1–chr1.4; genes in this region may therefore play significant functional roles.

In the MsNHX gene family, we identified 25 segmental duplication events unevenly distributed across chromosomes. As shown in Fig. 6, no tandem duplication events were detected on chr4.1–4.4, chr5.1–5.4, or chr6.1–6.4. In contrast, segmental duplication is the predominant evolutionary driver (Table S2), occurring at significantly higher frequencies than tandem duplication. Specifically, 10 segmental duplication events (40% of total events) localized to chr1.1 (Table S2). Distribution pattern analysis revealed that chr1.1–chr1.4 exhibits the highest gene duplication density, supporting our hypothesis that genes enriched in this region critically drive MsNHX gene family evolution in plants.

Prediction of cis-elements in the promoter sequences of MsNHX genes

To elucidate the regulatory potential of MsNHX genes, we analyzed cis-acting elements within their 2,000 bp promoter regions using PlantCARE. Our investigation revealed an intricate network of regulatory motifs associated with diverse biological processes (Table S3), including stress-responsive elements such as TC-rich repeats (defense), LTRs (low-temperature), and ARE (oxidative stress), phytohormone elements such as ABRE (ABA), CGTCA/TGACG-motifs (JA), TCA-element (SA), AuxRE-core (auxin), and P-box (GA). Integrated analysis (Fig. 7) identified ABRE as the most prevalent element (present in 100% of promoters), indicating dominant ABA signaling across the family. Notably, 17 genes contain LTRs (low-temperature), 13 genes harbor TC-rich repeats (defense). These findings demonstrate that MsNHX cis-regulatory functions are primarily associated with phytohormone signaling, multi-stress adaptation, and developmental regulation, with light response (Box 4/G-Box/GT1) being a secondary component.

Tissue-specific gene expression of MsNHXs

To characterize the biological roles of MsNHX genes in alfalfa (Medicago sativa), we examined their expression profiles across six distinct tissues: elongated stems, flowers, leaves, nodules, pre-elongated stems and roots (Table S4). Among the 35 putative MsNHX genes, only 19 showed detectable expression levels (Fig. 8). Strikingly, 16 genes exhibited no detectable expression in any analyzed tissue, suggesting they may require specific inductive conditions. Four genes demonstrated strict tissue-specific expression patterns: MsNHX7 and MsNHX12 were exclusively expressed in flowers; MsNHX21 was root-specific; and MsNHX24 showed nodule-specific expression. The remaining 15 expressed genes displayed multi-tissue expression profiles, with MsNHX9 and MsNHX13 showing particularly high transcript abundance across all six examined tissues. This expression pattern implies that these two genes may play important roles in fundamental physiological processes in alfalfa.

Expression analysis of NHX genes Medicaogo sativa leaves under abiotic stress

To elucidate the functional divergence of MsNHX genes under abiotic stress (Tables S5–S7), we analyzed RNA-seq data to characterize their expression profiles under drought, salt, and cold stress (Fig. 9). The analysis revealed that MsNHX genes exhibited differential responses to all three stressors. Two genes, MsNHX1 and MsNHX10, showed statistically significant (P < 0.05) upregulation across all stress treatments, with MsNHX10 displaying particularly strong induction (~ 42-fold increase under both drought and salt stress). Although both genes were co-upregulated during drought, their divergent expression patterns under salt and cold stress implied functional specialization in distinct stress-response pathways.

qRT-PCR-based profiling of MsNHX gene expression in Medicago sativa under abiotic stress

The NHX pathway plays a crucial role in maintaining cytoplasmic ion homeostasis. To validate the transcriptome data and investigate expression pattern variations, we selected three genes (MsNHX1, MsNHX10, and MsNHX18) that showed differential expression under stress conditions for qRT-PCR analysis. Our results confirmed the transcriptome data (Table S9) and revealed distinct temporal expression patterns. Under drought stress, all three genes exhibited similar expression trends, with upregulated expression at the M3 stage, although with varying peak expression levels.

Notably, MsNHX1 showed an early-response to salt (S2), and cold (C1) stress, with expression peaked at the initial stages followed by gradual decline. In contrast, MsNHX10 displayed a late-phase response, with expression peaked at later stages of salt (S5) and cold (C3) stress before decreasing. The most complex expression profile was observed for MsNHX18, which exhibited multiple expression peaks with significant fluctuations under salt stress, while maintaining a relatively stable expression pattern under cold stress. The complex expression pattern of MsNHX18 under salt stress may result from dynamic fluctuations caused by sequential expression waves of genes involved in localized plant stress responses [36] (Fig. 10).

Discussions

Soil salinization poses a significant global environmental challenge, particularly in northwestern, northern, and northeastern regions of China [37]. This phenomenon leads to the deterioration of soil structure through multiple mechanisms, including reduced water retention capacity and permeability, diminished soil fertility [38, 39] and in severe cases, soil compaction that prevents vegetation growth and disrupts ecosystem integrity [40]. As such, soil salinization represents a critical environmental issue for both agricultural and pastoral systems. Current research indicates that alfalfa (Medicago sativa) contributes to soil remediation by improving soil porosity and modifying microbial communities [41]. Owing to its vigorous growth characteristics, high biomass yield, and broad adaptability, alfalfa has become a globally important forage crop, with cultivation exceeding 40 million hectares worldwide [42]. Consequently, investigating the molecular mechanisms of salt tolerance in alfalfa and developing salt-tolerant cultivars [43] are crucial for sustainable agriculture in saline-affected regions [44, 45].

Plants have evolved sophisticated salt-tolerance mechanisms [46, 47]. Among these, the NHX (Na⁺/H⁺ antiporter) family plays a central role in maintaining ionic homeostasis by regulating the transmembrane transport of Na⁺, K⁺, and H⁺, thereby mitigating osmotic imbalance and pH fluctuations under salt stress. Although this mechanism is well-characterized in many plant species, research on NHX proteins in alfalfa (Medicago sativa) remains limited. To date, only one study, published in 2009, reported the transformation of the MsNHX1 gene without subsequent functional validation [48]. In this study, through comprehensive genome-wide analysis, we identified 35 NHX homologs in the autotetraploid alfalfa genome, systematically designated MsNHX1–MsNHX35 according to Arabidopsis nomenclature standards. Collinearity analyses revealed notable genomic clustering, with approximately 60% of MsNHX genes localized to five chromosomal regions (chr1.1–chr1.4 and chr7.4). These regions showed significant enrichment of both tandem and segmental duplication events. Comparative collinearity further demonstrated that MsNHX genes exhibit preferential syntenic conservation with Arabidopsis loci on chromosomes 1 and 5, while displaying a more uniform genomic distribution in the close relative Medicago truncatula. These findings not only support a closer phylogenetic relationship within Medicago species, but also suggest conserved evolutionary trajectories underlying their adaptation to environmental stresses. The observed duplication hotspots are likely evolutionarily significant genomic regions harboring key regulatory genes involved in stress responses [49, 50].

Analysis of conserved motifs and gene structures within the MsNHX family revealed high intra-clade similarity, consistent with previous reports [51–53], suggesting that specific intron-exon architectures and motif variations are correlated with functional specialization. To further explore the functional potential of these genes, we systematically analyzed promoter cis-regulatory elements, which are crucial for transcriptional regulation and allow meaningful cross-species functional comparisons due to their conserved yet specialized nature [54, 55].

Our cis-element analysis identified four major categories: defense/stress response elements, low-temperature responsive elements, light-responsive elements, and plant growth regulator-related elements. As illustrated in Fig. 7, stress-responsive elements were predominant, with 13 MsNHX genes containing TC-rich repeats (associated with stress resistance) and 17 possessing LTR elements (low-temperature responsive), strongly supporting the family’s role in abiotic stress adaptation. Notably, all MsNHX promoters contained elements related to major phytohormonal pathways, including gibberellin-responsive elements (GARE-motif, P-box), jasmonate-responsive elements (TGACG-motif, CGTCA-motif), salicylic acid-related elements (TCA-element), and abscisic acid (ABRE) and auxin (TGA) responsive elements. The substantial number of growth regulator-related elements, illustrated in the stacked plot (Fig. 7), implies that the NHX gene family is involved not only in direct stress responses but also in growth regulation that indirectly enhances stress tolerance. These findings collectively highlight the integral role of the NHX gene family in mediating both stress adaptation and fundamental growth processes in alfalfa. Through comprehensive analysis of published transcriptomic data, we systematically characterized the expression patterns of MsNHX genes under various abiotic stresses. Four genes (MsNHX1, MsNHX10, MsNHX18, and MsNHX19) showed significant differential expression under drought stress. qRT-PCR validation confirmed the drought-responsive expression of MsNHX1, MsNHX10, and MsNHX18. Of these, MsNHX1 and MsNHX10 exhibited pronounced transcriptional changes under all three stress conditions tested (salt, drought, and cold), albeit with distinct expression patterns.

Evolutionary analyses revealed syntenic conservation between MsNHX1/MsNHX10 and their orthologs AtNHX1 (Arabidopsis thaliana) and MtNHX1 (Medicago truncatula), supporting conserved roles in stress adaptation. These paralogs, however, exhibit divergent cis-regulatory architectures that likely underlie functional specialization. The multi-stress responsive genes MsNHX1 and MsNHX10 possess combinatorial regulatory modules including ABRE (ABA response) [56] and LTR (low-temperature responsiveness) [57], facilitating crosstalk between ABA-dependent and ABA-independent pathways (e.g., DREB-CBF cascades) [58] and enabling coordinated responses to salt, cold, and osmotic stress. In contrast, the drought-specialized MsNHX18 and MsNHX19 display unique regulatory signatures characterized by jasmonic acid-responsive motifs (TGACG/CGTCA) and ABA-activated ABRE elements. The co-localization of ABRE and jasmonate motifs suggests a feed-forward regulatory loop wherein drought-induced ABA primes JA biosynthesis, amplifying downstream defense responses—a mechanism consistent with recent models of ABA-JA crosstalk in root ion homeostasis [59].

Similar cis-element combinations and multi-stress responsiveness have been documented in other species such as Gossypium [60], Triticum aestivum L. [61], and Cucurbita L. [62], indicating evolutionary conservation of regulatory mechanisms. Moreover, overexpression of TaNHX2 from wheat enhances salt stress tolerance in diverse species including soybean, alfalfa, and eggplant, further underscoring the functional conservation of NHX genes. These regulatory features represent adaptations to niche-specific stresses, while combinatorial cis-element configurations enable precise spatiotemporal regulation of NHX gene expression.

Conclusions

This study identified 35 MsNHX genes in alfalfa (Medicago sativa) through genome-wide analysis and classified them into three subfamilies based on phylogenetic relationships. The results demonstrate that both tandem and segmental duplication events played significant roles in the evolutionary expansion of the MsNHX gene family. Notably, we characterized three key stress-responsive genes MsNHX1,MsNHX10, and MsNHX18 that are closely associated with abiotic stress responses. qRT-PCR analysis revealed that the expression levels of MsNHX1 and MsNHX10 were significantly upregulated (P < 0.05) under salt, drought, and cold stress conditions compared to the control (CK). Through comprehensive analysis of cis-regulatory elements and duplication event frequencies, we found that these three genes likely participate in alfalfa’s abiotic stress responses through distinct molecular mechanisms. Evolutionary patterns of MsNHX1 and MsNHX10 suggest functional diversification, with MsNHX10 likely originating from MsNHX1 via a duplication event. Critically, their divergent expression profiles under various biotic stresses indicate that these genes are not functionally redundant. In contrast, MsNHX18 and MsNHX19 exhibited specific induction under drought stress, likely acting through integrated phytohormone signaling pathways. These findings provide important insights into the molecular basis of stress tolerance in alfalfa and illustrate how gene duplication contributes to the functional specialization of stress-responsive gene families in plants. However, this conclusion still lacks functional validation using overexpression and knockout lines to further validate these findings. The study establishes a foundation for further investigation of abiotic stress resistance mechanisms in this important forage crop.

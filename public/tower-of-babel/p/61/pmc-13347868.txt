Rationale and design of Low-Frequency REpetitive TRanscranial Magnetic Stimulation Combined with Endovascular Treatment in ACute Ischaemic StrokE (RETRACE II): a randomised double-blind controlled multicentre phase II pilot study
Authors: Lingling Ding, Wenjie Wang, Tingyu Yi, Gaocai Zhang, Xinsheng Han, Wenhuo Chen, Hao Wang, Yongjun Wang, Zixiao Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWhile endovascular therapy (EVT) remains the primary treatment for acute ischaemic stroke (AIS) management, persistent functional deficits in patients with successful recanalisation underscore the necessity for complementary neuroprotective strategies.AimTo investigate the safety and efficacy of low-frequency repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (LF-rTMS) as a potential adjunctive neuroprotective intervention following EVT in AIS patients.DesignThe Low-Frequency REpetitive TRanscranial Magnetic Stimulation Combined with Endovascular Treatment in ACute Ischaemic StrokE (RETRACE-II) trial is a phase II, multicentre, prospective, randomised, double-blind, sham-controlled pilot study. 60 successfully recanalised AIS patients with anterior circulation occlusion were equally randomised (1:1) to active LF-rTMS or sham intervention. The intervention involved administration of 1200-pulse 1-Hz LF-rTMS sessions (two times per day for 3 consecutive days) targeting the ipsilesional primary motor cortex (M1), initiated within 24 hours of symptom onset. Sham procedures maintained equivalent positioning with deactivated magnetic output. Standardised protocol assessments were conducted at 3-day (postintervention), 7-day and 90-day follow-ups.Study outcomesThe primary efficacy endpoint was the proportion of early neurological recovery (defined as a reduction of ≥4 points on the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) or achieving a score of 0–1) at 3 days. Secondary outcomes included ischaemic penumbral salvage volume ratio, final infarct volume measured by brain MRI at 7 days, and modified Rankin Scale score at 90 days. Safety outcomes encompassed symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage, neurological deterioration (≥4-point increase in NIHSS score) and all-cause mortality through 90-day follow-up.DiscussionRETRACE-II establishes methodological rigour for evaluating neuromodulation therapies during the hyperacute stroke phase, with findings expected to inform future trials and advance combination therapy paradigms in cerebrovascular neuroprotection.Trial registration numberNCT06064747.Keywords: Cerebral Infarction, Stroke, Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation

Introduction

Cerebrovascular diseases are the leading causes of mortality and disability among adults in China, with approximately 80% attributed to ischaemic stroke.1 The primary objectives in managing acute ischaemic stroke (AIS) events are recanalisation of occluded vessels, restoration of blood perfusion and salvage of hypoperfused regions. Endovascular therapy (EVT), whether used independently or following prior administration of intravenous thrombolysis (IVT), is the optimal therapeutic strategy to accomplish these objectives.2 However, even achieving successful recanalisation after EVT, only 20%–25% of patients experience favourable outcomes.3 4 Advances in EVT therapy have significantly enhanced recanalisation success rates; thus, mere refinements in devices or procedural methods may not substantially improve clinical outcomes, while effective neuroprotective strategies could potentially overcome this limitation.5

Stroke researchers worldwide eagerly anticipate the advent of a new era combining EVT therapy with neuroprotection approaches to benefit more AIS patients. Current neuroprotective research prioritises the preservation of the ischaemic penumbra, with nerinetide (NA-1) being the most intensively studied agent. NA-1 mitigates stroke progression by suppressing neuron excitotoxicity. However, a recent clinical trial demonstrates no additional benefits from NA-1 administration in AIS patients undergoing EVT therapy.6 A recent meta-analysis showed that NA-1 may exert neuroprotective effects in patients selected for reperfusion therapy within 3 hours of stroke onset, highlighting the critical importance of intervention timing.7

Beyond neuronal excitotoxicity, secondary pathological cascades following cerebral ischaemia, including oxidative stress, neuronal injury repair, apoptosis and microcirculatory dysfunction, critically drive the transition of ischaemic penumbra to infarct core. These processes not only hinder favourable outcomes in EVT therapy but also serve as potential therapeutic targets for neuroprotection.8,10 Recently, neuromodulation has been proposed as a possible neuroprotective strategy for AIS patients. Low-frequency repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (LF-rTMS) is a safe, non-invasive and painless neuromodulation technique that shows significant potential for promoting neurological recovery following stroke, potentially targeting spreading depolarisation and inverting the haemodynamic response.11,13 Preclinical studies demonstrate that early administration of LF-rTMS following ischaemic reperfusion could reduce infarction volume and enhance motor function.14 The neuroprotective effects were potentially related to inhibiting excitotoxicity, anti-apoptosis, improving cerebral blood flow and enhancing neural plasticity.15 16

LF-rTMS may be a potential adjuvant intervention to ameliorate neurological deficits following EVT therapy. However, there is currently a lack of clinical evidence supporting the use of LF-rTMS in combination with EVT treatment. High-quality randomised controlled trials are urgently needed to validate the efficacy and safety of LF-rTMS in the early treatment of AIS patients, providing evidence for new therapeutic strategies during the acute phase of AIS. This study aims to investigate the safety and efficacy of combined EVT treatment and LF-rTMS in patients with AIS.

Method

Study organisation and design

Low-Frequency REpetitive TRanscranial Magnetic Stimulation Combined with Endovascular Treatment in ACute Ischaemic StrokE (RETRACE-II) study is a phase II, multicentre, prospective, randomised, double-blind, sham-controlled pilot clinical trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of LF-rTMS in AIS patients undergoing endovascular treatment (IVT combined with endovascular treatment or direct endovascular treatment). Enrolled patients will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either LF-rTMS or sham rTMS. Figure 1 presents the screening, allocation and follow-up procedures of the study participants.

Sample sizes

As a pilot exploratory study of LF-rTMS in AIS patients undergoing EVT, with no prior data in this context, formal power calculation was infeasible. This pilot study will enrol 60 AIS patients undergoing EVT (30 per group). Based on comparable pilot trials17 18 and guidelines recommending 12–30 per group.19 20 This size provides robust preliminary efficacy and variability data while remaining feasible, directly informing the design and sample size calculation for a subsequent phase III trial.

Participants

Adult AIS patients aged 18–80 who presented within 24 hours of symptom onset, with anterior circulation infarction due to acute occlusion of the internal carotid artery or middle cerebral artery M1 segment occlusion, and underwent endovascular treatment, were consecutively enrolled in this trial. If the exact onset time was unknown, the last known normal time was used instead. Figure 2 shows details of the inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria. ICA, internal carotid artery; MCA, middle cerebral artery; rTMS, repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation.

Baseline data collection

Demographic information, medical and medication history, pre-stroke modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score, ECG results and laboratory tests were collected from patients before randomisation. Physical examinations, as well as the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score at admission, were assessed by experienced neurologists. Multimodal CT/MRI and CT perfusion (CTP)/perfusion-weighted imaging (PWI) were employed to evaluate the infarction core, hypoperfused regions and the responsible vascular territories (figure 3).

Data and blood samples collection. AIS, acute ischaemic stroke; EVT, endovascular therapy; LF-rTMS, low-frequency repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation.

Randomisation

60 smart cards were randomised into active and sham stimulation cards on a 1:1 ratio, using an interface module (IFM) (YingChi, Shenzhen, China). There is no visual difference between the IFM cards. The experiment designer entrusts the operator to assign the stimulation card randomly. Subjects and operators are unaware of whether the assigned card provides active or sham stimulation, thereby preventing bias. This card will be the sole identifier for the corresponding subject’s subsequent intervention operations. Once a card is activated, any patient who has been randomised and subsequently withdraws or is removed from the clinical trial for any reason will retain their stimulation card.

Blinding

This trial adheres to a double-blind design. Both participants and experimenters will remain blinded to the treatment allocation. Regardless of whether participants are in the LF-rTMS or sham group, the unmarked side of the coil will be placed close to the patient’s scalp. The sham stimulation system built into the rTMS device integrates true and sham stimulation coil configurations within the same coil. To ensure the success of blinding, the researchers responsible for assessing clinical signs and clinical outcomes are not involved in the LF-rTMS stimulation. During follow-up visits, patients will also be asked about their guesses regarding whether they received real or sham stimulation.

Researchers could only request emergency unblinding after communicating with the Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) in case of a serious adverse event (SAE) that affects the patient’s safety and requires identification of the type of intervention.

Intervention

Before the stimulation begins, the resting motor threshold (RMT) will be determined for each participant by identifying the minimum stimulation intensity over hot spots to evoke a motor-evoked potential larger than 50 uV in 5 of 10 trials over the contralesional first dorsal interosseous muscle.

LF-rTMS group

Using an ‘8’-shaped coil, 1-Hz rTMS will be applied to the M1 region of the affected hemisphere, with a stimulation intensity set at 100% RMT. Each session will consist of 1200 pulses, with two sessions (2400 pulses) administered per day, separated by an interval of at least 2 hours, each lasting approximately 24 min. rTMS will be initiated on the day of randomisation, following reperfusion therapy and within 24 hours of onset, and will be administered continuously for 3 days, comprising a total of six sessions, amounting to 7200 pulses (figure 4).

Sham control group

A sham stimulation coil will be used to stimulate the M1 region of the affected hemisphere, producing the same stimulation sounds as the LF-rTMS group but without delivering any effective stimulation. Coil placement, sound volume and session timing are identical across arms. The stimulation frequency will match that of the LF-rTMS group, with two sessions per day (with an interval of at least 2 hours between sessions), each lasting approximately 24 min. This will commence on the day of randomisation and will continue for a total duration of 3 days.

All rTMS operators across participating sites will be trained using a centrally developed Standard Operating Procedure manual. Quarterly audits will verify protocol adherence, and any deviations will prompt immediate refresher sessions to ensure ongoing consistency throughout the study. The stimulation parameters were set by the principal investigators and stored in the LF-rTMS operating system, with no authorisation granted to other operators to modify these parameters. Before stimulation, patients were fitted with a head cap customised to their head morphology, which was marked with the locations of bilateral M1 regions. The lightweight and thin design of the cap did not interfere with the penetration depth of the LF-rTMS pulses.

In addition to the study interventions, both groups of participants will receive standard clinical care according to American Heart Association/American Stroke Association guideline recommendations.21 This includes comprehensive post-EVT management protocols such as monitoring in a stroke unit, secondary stroke prevention measures (eg, antiplatelet therapy and statin therapy) and standardised rehabilitation strategies. Rehabilitation therapy will commence as soon as possible after the patient’s condition stabilises, following current guidelines. It will include task-special training, each lasting about 45 min, administered 5 days per week. Rehabilitation interventions will be individualised based on the patient’s clinical needs. The intensity and duration will be regularly reassessed and adjusted according to the patient’s progress and tolerance.22 23

Follow-up information

The study’s visit points include the day of randomisation, 3±1 days post-randomisation, 7±2 days post-randomisation or at discharge and 90±7 days. A specialised physician will conduct the mRS score and NIHSS score. In cases of death at any time during follow-up, the participant is assigned an mRS score of 6. The NIHSS score and mRS were assessed by specialised physicians trained and certified. To maintain consistency across sites, the same structured interview format was used, and raters were blinded to treatment allocation.

Imaging data collection

The multimodal CT includes non-contrast CT (NCCT), CT angiography (CTA) and CTP were collected. NCCT is used to rule out intracranial haemorrhage, while CTA is employed to identify the site of vascular occlusion. The multimodal MRI sequences include T1-weighted imaging, T2-weighted imaging, diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC), fluid-attenuated inversion recovery and PWI. Preprocedural CTP/PWI images were processed using the image analysis software iStroke.24 The ischaemic core was defined as tissue with regional cerebral blood flow <30% on CTP or areas with ADC<620×10⁻⁶ mm²/s on MRI. Hypoperfused tissue was defined as regions with time-to-maximum (Tmax)≥6 s on both CTP and PWI. All automated segmentation results were manually reviewed and verified by independent, experienced clinicians to ensure accuracy and consistency across all cases.

On 3±1 days, brain CT or MRI examination will be conducted based on baseline imaging modalities to determine the rate of penumbral salvage and infarct volume growth. On 7±2 days or at discharge, brain MRI will be performed to ascertain the final infarction volume. In addition, patients in the imaging subgroup will undergo CT or MR perfusion imaging (CTP or PWI) to compare changes in hypoperfused volume, collateral circulation and cerebral blood flow before and after the stimulation.

Blood sample collection

Fasting blood samples were collected in PAXgene Blood RNA Tube and EDTA anticoagulation blood collection tube at baseline, 3 days and 7 days after randomisation. The serum and white cells were extracted immediately. All the blood samples were frozen in cryotubes in a −80 ℃ refrigerator. The samples were transported through the cold chain to the centre laboratory in Beijing Tiantan Hospital, where all blood samples were stored at −80℃ until testing was performed. RNA sequencing and quantitative proteomics will be used to characterise molecular changes induced by LF-rTMS, with a focus on pathways related to neural injury and repair. Differentially expressed genes and proteins will be identified using |log₂ fold change|≥1 and Benjamini-Hochberg‐adjusted p<0.05 [False Discovery Rate (FDR)<5%].

Adverse events and Data Safety Monitoring Board

During the study and follow-up period, any AEs or SAEs that occur will be promptly assessed. In this study, the primary SAE is sICH, defined according to the Heidelberg bleeding classification.25 The study established an independent event adjudication committee composed of multidisciplinary experts to systematically evaluate and review AEs and SAEs during the trial. A DSMB, operating under a pre-approved charter, will conduct periodic reviews of enrolment, AEs and protocol adherence. During the course of this study, if AE/SAEs, laboratory abnormalities, other medical conditions, or if an evaluation deems that suspending/discontinuing the study intervention is in the participant’s best interest, DSMB may recommend modifications to the study protocol or early termination of the trial.

Data management

All data will be systematically collected by trained research personnel. Case report forms will be meticulously completed at each visit point following the protocol requirements. The relevant documentation will be securely stored and managed by designated staff at each research centre. Imaging data, including head MRI and CT, will be archived in Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine format. On completion of subject enrolment, all corresponding data should be transmitted to Beijing Tiantan Hospital, where dedicated data supervision and management personnel will conduct quality control and oversight of the research data, ensuring its accuracy, security and confidentiality.

Statistical analysis

The study used appropriate statistical methods to evaluate the efficacy and safety of LF-rTMS in AIS patients. All statistical analyses will be processed by SAS V.9.4. Continuous variables will be summarised as mean±SD or median (IQR), as appropriate; categorical variables will be presented as counts and percentages. We will perform analyses under the intention-to-treat principle. The primary outcome will be analysed using a logistic regression model, adjusting for baseline NIHSS, age, time from onset to randomisation and prespecified baseline covariates exhibiting imbalance between treatment groups (p<0.10 in univariate analysis). Adjusted ORs with 95% CIs will be reported. Secondary and safety endpoints will be assessed using general linear models for continuous imaging measures; proportional-odds logistic regression for ordinal outcomes; logistic regression for binary outcomes (all adjusted for baseline NIHSS, age and other prespecified prognostic factors); and Cox proportional hazards regression for time-to-event outcomes (eg, mortality, sAEs), HRs with 95% CIs will be reported. Multivariate regression models will further adjust for confounders identified through univariate screening and clinical rationale. Missing primary outcome data will be handled by multiple imputation under a missing-at-random assumption. Two-sided p<0.05 will be considered significant. Prespecified subgroup analyses will be conducted by age (≤65, >65), sex (male, female) and baseline NIHSS score (6–15, 16–25).

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, the RETRACE-II study is the first randomised clinical trial to use LF-rTMS in AIS patients during the hyperacute phase who have undergone endovascular treatment and achieved vascular recanalisation. This study aims to provide initial insights into the safety of LF-rTMS in the hyperacute phase of stroke and its efficacy in patients receiving EVT therapy with successful vascular recanalisation.

Reperfusion therapy is the preferred treatment for AIS patients, with EVT demonstrating superior clinical outcomes compared with IVT.2 However, previous studies have shown that among stroke patients with large vessel occlusion, only 20%–25% achieve a good outcome even after vascular recanalisation.3 4 Notably, for every 1% of the preserved penumbra, the likelihood of achieving a good clinical outcome at 3 months increases by 7.4%.26 Preservation of the ischaemic penumbra represents a critical target for neuroprotective strategies.

Recent extensive research focuses on developing novel adjunctive neuroprotective therapies (such as cathodal transcranial direct current stimulation (C-tDCS) and high-flow oxygen) following EVT to improve outcomes in AIS patients. Some clinical studies have not achieved anticipated results, potentially due to the predominant focus of neuroprotective trials on single-target pathophysiological mechanisms of stroke.27 28 Due to complex ischaemic cascades after stroke, single-target neuroprotective approaches have demonstrated limited efficacy, underscoring the necessity of multitargeted interventions with synergistic mechanisms. Therefore, exploring new strategies to salvage the ischaemic penumbra while achieving vascular recanalisation is an urgent and important area that needs further investigation.

Neuromodulation is a promising adjunctive strategy for improving functional outcomes after reperfusion therapy. Clinical and preclinical studies have demonstrated that LF-rTMS is safe and well-tolerated, with no SAEs reported. Existing literature supports the application of LF-rTMS in patients with acute and subacute strokes, demonstrating its efficacy in enhancing motor function and improving clinical outcomes.16 Recent studies reveal that the neuroprotective mechanisms of LF-rTMS are not only linked to the restoration of interhemispheric excitability balance and neural plasticity but also encompass synergistic interactions across multiple mechanistic dimensions, including enhancing cerebral perfusion, suppressing excitotoxicity, attenuating oxidative stress, promoting neuroplasticity and inhibiting apoptosis.29,32 These pathways constitute critical determinants of ischaemic penumbra salvage. The guidelines recommend that for subacute and chronic stroke patients, applying LF-rTMS to the contralesional hemisphere to suppress neuronal hyperexcitability while enhancing ipsilesional excitability could synergistically promote functional recovery.30 However, for the hyperacute phase of stroke, suppressing rather than enhancing neuronal excitability in the ipsilesional hemisphere may be more beneficial for improving outcomes.33

A recent preclinical study indicates that LF-rTMS applied to both contralesional and ipsilesional hemispheres in rats with ischaemia-reperfusion injury could achieve penumbral salvage, reduce infarct volume and enhance motor functional recovery.14 The underlying mechanism may be related to the expression of apoptotic factors such as B-cell lymphoma 2, cysteine-aspartic protease 3 and matrix metalloproteinase-9, with reduced expression of these factors aiding in the preservation of the ischaemic penumbra.34 In this study, we will use imaging such as multimodal CTP or PWI to assess the impact of LF-rTMS on penumbra salvage.

The research also found that earlier LF-rTMS intervention was associated with smaller infarct volumes and more significant improvements in motor function.14 Previous research has demonstrated that within the first 24 hours following stroke onset, pathophysiological processes such as apoptosis and glutamate-mediated excitotoxicity predominantly mediate neural injury and repair mechanisms.35,38 Considering LF-rTMS documents anti-apoptotic and anti-excitotoxic effects by enhancing GABAergic levels, we elected to initiate stimulation in the ipsilesional hemisphere within 24 hours of symptom onset. Additionally, blood samples would be collected to quantify biomarkers associated with neurorepair and angiogenesis, aiming to elucidate the neuroprotective mechanisms of LF-rTMS.

Another non-invasive neuromodulation technique, C-tDCS, shares mechanistic similarities with LF-rTMS in suppressing neuronal hyperexcitability. A clinical study suggested that C-tDCS applied to the ipsilesional hemisphere in reperfusion-treated patients with large vessel occlusion or moderate-to-severe stroke demonstrated a trend toward reduced infarct volume,18 likely mediated by regulating neuronal excitability and inhibiting spreading depolarisation. Researchers demonstrated that applying C-tDCS to the ipsilesional hemisphere within 24 hours post-stroke could improve blood flow in the affected hemisphere, promote collateral circulation and achieve a median ischaemic penumbra salvage rate of 66%.39 Similarly, a recent study has shown that pulsed electromagnetic fields applied to the ipsilesional hemisphere in AIS patients undergoing reperfusion therapy could reduce infarct volume, improve functional independence and exhibit no reported AEs.17 The neuroprotective mechanisms may involve suppression of neuronal hyperexcitability, attenuation of oxidative stress and excitotoxicity.

In conclusion, given the complex pathophysiological cascades post-ischaemic stroke, LF-rTMS targeting the lesioned hemisphere instead of the contralesional hemisphere within 24 hours may yield greater therapeutic benefits for AIS patients in the hyperacute phase. RETRACE-II aims to assess the neuroprotective effects and safety of LF-rTMS in AIS patients undergoing EVT therapy. We hypothesise that administering LF-rTMS during the hyperacute stroke to the ipsilesional hemisphere could provide neuroprotective effects by preserving the ischaemic penumbra, thereby reducing functional disabilities. This research will provide high-quality clinical evidence for the application of LF-rTMS in the hyperacute phase of AIS, laying a theoretical foundation for future clinical trials.

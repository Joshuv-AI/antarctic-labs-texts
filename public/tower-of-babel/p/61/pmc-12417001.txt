A Therapeutic Tightrope: Managing Coexisting Crohn Disease and Systemic Lupus Erythematosus With Risankizumab
Authors: Hamdan AlAwadhi, Muna Almutawa, Sara El Ouali, Zaher Koutoubi, Esat Memisoglu, Ahmad AlDuaij, Rajaie Namas
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe coexistence of systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) and Crohn disease (CD) is rare and presents significant diagnostic and therapeutic challenges due to overlapping clinical, radiologic, and histologic features. We present a 24-year-old woman with SLE, chronic immune thrombocytopenic purpura, and newly diagnosed CD treated with risankizumab, an interleukin-23 inhibitor approved for CD. She achieved complete clinical and radiologic remission of CD without SLE flares over 12 months. To the best of our knowledge, this represents the first documented case of risankizumab successfully used in a patient with SLE and CD. Further research is needed to assess the long-term safety and wider applicability of interleukin-23 inhibitors for this underserved patient population.

INTRODUCTION

The coexistence of systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), and immune thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP) presents substantial diagnostic and therapeutic challenges. SLE is a multifaceted autoimmune disease marked by widespread inflammation and tissue injury, driven by the presence of autoantibodies and immune complexes.1 IBD is a chronic inflammatory disorder of the intestine, linked to gut microbiota dysregulation.2 Chronic ITP further complicates management, requiring therapies that control autoimmunity while minimizing hematologic risks.3

Although rare, the overlap between SLE and Crohn disease (CD) is particularly challenging due to shared features such as fatigue, arthralgia, and abdominal pain. Both diseases activate similar immune pathways involving interleukin-12 (IL-12), interleukin-23 (IL-23), and T-helper 17 (Th17) cells, making it difficult to determine the primary driver of symptoms.4,5 Therefore, a comprehensive multidisciplinary evaluation is essential.

We present a 24-year-old woman with chronic ITP who subsequently developed SLE, secondary Sjogren syndrome, and perianal CD, successfully managed with risankizumab. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first reported use of risankizumab in a patient with concurrent SLE and CD.

CASE REPORT

A 24-year-old woman with a history of chronic ITP presented to the rheumatology clinic at Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi in 2017 with bleeding gums, fatigue, and easy bruisability. She also reported a malar rash, photosensitivity, oral ulcers, a rash over the joints, and diffuse frontal hair thinning.

Laboratory evaluation revealed a platelet count of 20,000/µL, a positive ANA (1:640, speckled), and positive antichromatin, anti-Smith, anti-Sjögren’s syndrome type A, anti-Sjögren’s syndrome type B, and anti-ribonucleoprotein antibodies, with normal complement levels and anti–double-stranded DNA. Platelet autoantibodies were positive, von Willebrand factor was normal, and Factor VIII was elevated.

She was diagnosed with steroid-responsive chronic ITP, SLE, and secondary Sjögren syndrome. Treatment began with prednisone 50 mg daily, tapered to 10 mg, followed by hydroxychloroquine 200 mg twice daily and azathioprine 100 mg daily as steroid-sparing agents. She achieved hematologic and clinical remission and discontinued prednisone in April 2019.

The patient was lost to follow-up in February 2021 and self-discontinued hydroxychloroquine and azathioprine later that year. In 2022, she developed worsening perianal pain and rectal bleeding, which was treated with laser ablation for hemorrhoids. A colonoscopy performed at that time was unremarkable. Two months later, she experienced bruising and gum bleeding, although her platelet count remained stable at 150,000/µL. Given her preference for intravenous treatment to improve compliance, rituximab was initiated in September 2022.

By March 2023, she reported recurrent abdominal pain associated with rectal bleeding. Fecal calprotectin was elevated at 776 μg/g (normal <50 μg/g). Serologic testing revealed elevated anti-Saccharomyces cerevisiae antibodies (ASCA IgG 104 U), positive chitobioside carbohydrate antibodies (ACCA 100 U), and elevated laminaribioside carbohydrate antibodies (ALCA 67 U). Mannobioside antibodies (AMCA) were negative, and perinuclear antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies was also negative.

Repeat colonoscopy showed perianal inflammation and serpiginous superficial ulcerations in the rectum, sigmoid, and descending colon (Figure 1). Duodenal biopsy revealed intraepithelial lymphocytosis with partial villous atrophy, and gastric biopsy showed chronic gastritis. Colonic biopsies from the descending and sigmoid colon demonstrated active chronic colitis. Histologic evaluation of the rectal biopsies revealed lamina propria edema, mild crypt architectural distortion, mixed inflammatory infiltrates with focal neutrophilic infiltration, and small, loosely formed granulomas (Figure 2).

Magnetic resonance enterography confirmed a 15-cm segment of sigmoid colitis. Coronal T1-weighted postcontrast images demonstrated proximal dilatation of the sigmoid colon (Figure 3) and segmental luminal narrowing with mural thickening and intense enhancement, consistent with active inflammation (Figure 3). Axial HASTE imaging confirmed circumferential wall thickening and narrowing of the sigmoid lumen (Figure 4). Collectively, these endoscopic, histologic, and radiologic findings were consistent with CD.

A multidisciplinary team including specialists from gastroenterology, rheumatology, pathology, radiology, and hospital medicine reviewed the case. The team concluded that anti-tumor necrosis factor (TNF) therapy was contraindicated due to her history of SLE. Risankizumab was selected as a safer alternative and initiated in early 2023.

Over the following 12 months, the patient remained asymptomatic, experienced no lupus flares, and demonstrated complete radiologic resolution of colonic inflammation on repeat magnetic resonance enterography in March 2024.

DISCUSSION

Managing overlapping autoimmune diseases requires an individualized approach, especially when treatment for one condition may exacerbate another. In this case, the primary challenge was selecting therapy without provoking a lupus flare. Differentiating CD from lupus enteritis was essential, as both conditions can present with abdominal pain and bleeding but have distinct features. CD typically involves segmental, transmural inflammation and perianal disease, whereas lupus enteritis causes diffuse bowel wall thickening and mesenteric edema, often steroid-responsive.6 Our patient's endoscopic ulcerations, chronic colitis on histology, and segmental sigmoid involvement on imaging supported a diagnosis of CD.

Serologic testing further reinforced this diagnosis. Elevated ASCA IgG, along with increased antiglycan antibodies such as ACCA and ALCA, markers specific to CD7 and infrequently seen in SLE8 provided additional support. However, these antibodies are not diagnostic and must be interpreted within the broader clinical, endoscopic, and histopathologic context.9

Although anti-TNF agents are standard therapy for moderate-to-severe CD, especially with perianal involvement,9 their use is generally contraindicated in SLE due to the risk of drug-induced lupus erythematosus.10 Therefore, risankizumab, a selective IL-23 p19 inhibitor, was chosen. By targeting the IL-23/Th17 axis, it avoids TNF blockade, a key consideration in SLE. IL-23 is increasingly recognized as a contributor to lupus pathogenesis, particularly in cutaneous manifestations and lupus nephritis, suggesting potential therapeutic benefits from IL-23 inhibitors such as risankizumab.11,12 The ADVANCE, MOTIVATE, and FORTIFY trials have demonstrated risankizumab's efficacy and safety in inducing and maintaining remission in moderate-to-severe CD, including in patients refractory to anti-TNF therapy.13,14 Other biologics, including vedolizumab and ustekinumab, were considered. Vedolizumab has a slower onset and limited efficacy in perianal disease.15 Ustekinumab, which targets both IL-12 and IL-23, raises theoretical concerns due to IL-12's role in immune defense and regulation, although phase II data in SLE show no increase in serious infections or malignancy during short-term use.16

Beyond SLE, managing CD in patients with overlapping autoimmune conditions requires balancing efficacy and safety. For instance, in those with coexistent psoriatic arthritis, treatment options include TNF inhibitors, Janus kinases inhibitors, or IL-23 inhibitors. However, IL-17 inhibitors such as secukinumab and ixekizumab have been associated with new-onset or exacerbation of IBD and should be used with caution in overlap syndromes.17,18

Although risankizumab is not approved for SLE, our patient tolerated it well, with no lupus flares. Only one prior report has described new-onset SLE in a patient treated with risankizumab for psoriasis19; however, causality remains unclear and may reflect coincidental disease onset rather than a drug-induced process.

Our experience highlights the potential utility of IL-23 inhibition in this complex overlap population. Further research is needed to evaluate its long-term safety, efficacy, and broader applicability for patients with multiple autoimmune conditions.

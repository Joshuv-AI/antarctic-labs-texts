In‐hospital course of patients with heart failure with improved ejection fraction in the DELIVER trial
Authors: Maria A. Pabon, Muthiah Vaduganathan, Brian L. Claggett, Safia Chatur, Sara Siqueira, Pablo Marti‐Castellote, Rudolf A. de Boer, Adrian F. Hernandez, Silvio E. Inzucchi, Mikhail N. Kosiborod, Carolyn S.P. Lam, Felipe Martinez, Sanjiv J. Shah, Akshay S. Desai, Pardeep S. Jhund, John J.V. McMurray, Scott D. Solomon, Orly Vardeny
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AimsPatients with heart failure (HF) with improved ejection fraction (HFimpEF) may face residual risks of clinical events that are comparable to those experienced by patients with HF whose left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) has consistently been above 40%. However, little is known about the clinical course of patients with HFimpEF during hospitalization for HF.Methods and resultsDELIVER randomized patients with HF and LVEF >40% to dapagliflozin or placebo, including HFimpEF (LVEF previously ≤40%). We evaluated all HF hospitalizations adjudicated by the clinical endpoints committee with available data for determination of in‐hospital course. Complicated hospitalization was defined as any hospitalization requiring intensive care unit stay, intravenous vasopressors/inotropes/vasodilators, invasive or non‐invasive ventilation, mechanical fluid removal, ultrafiltration, or mechanical circulatory support. LVEF changes were extracted using a validated GPT‐3.5, a large language model, via a secure private endpoint. Of the 6263 patients enrolled in DELIVER, 1151 (18%) had HFimpEF. During a median follow‐up of 2.3 years, there were 224 total HF hospitalizations in 144 patients with HFimpEF and 985 in 603 patients with LVEF consistently >40%. Patients with HFimpEF experienced higher rates of complicated HF hospitalization as compared with patients with LVEF consistently >40% (39% vs. 27%; p < 0.001). Among those who experienced a first HF hospitalization, there was no significant difference in length of stay or in‐hospital mortality between patients with HFimpEF versus LVEF consistently >40%. In a subset of participants who had at least one LVEF measurement available during HF hospitalization, 66% of those with HFimpEF and 29% of patients with LVEF consistently >40% experienced a reduction in their LVEF to ≤40% from the time of enrolment (p < 0.001). In the entire DELIVER cohort, dapagliflozin reduced total uncomplicated and complicated HF hospitalizations, irrespective of HFimpEF status (p interaction ≥0.30).ConclusionsAmong patients hospitalized for HF in DELIVER, those with HFimpEF experienced a more adverse in‐hospital clinical course, necessitating higher resource utilization beyond standard diuretic therapy compared with patients with HF and LVEF consistently >40%, but had similar in‐hospital mortality. Treatment benefits of dapagliflozin were not modified by hospitalization type.

Introduction

Heart failure (HF) hospitalizations often signal poor prognosis and are associated with significant healthcare expenses. 1 Patients with HF with improved ejection fraction (HFimpEF) have a lower risk of HF hospitalizations as compared to patients with HF with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF), 2 but remain at risk for adverse clinical outcomes. 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 We have previously shown that about 30% of participants in DELIVER (Dapagliflozin Evaluation to Improve the Lives of Patients with Preserved Ejection Fraction Heart Failure) and over 40% of participants in DAPA‐HF (Dapagliflozin and Prevention of Adverse Outcomes in Heart Failure) required escalation of therapeutic management beyond intravenous diuretic use alone during a HF hospitalization event. 8 Whether patients with improved left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) who are hospitalized behave differently from those with LVEF consistently >40% remains unknown. As such, in this post hoc analysis of the DELIVER trial we aimed to describe the characteristics, severity and outcomes of patients with HFimpEF during a HF hospitalization event, including response to sodium–glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitor therapy.

Methods

The study design and primary results of the DELIVER trial have been previously reported. 9 Briefly, DELIVER was a randomized, double‐blind, placebo‐controlled trial in patients with HF and mildly reduced (HFmrEF) or preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) conducted at 353 sites in 20 countries and included adults (≥40 years) with symptomatic HF with New York Heart Association (NYHA) class II–IV functional limitations, LVEF >40%, structural heart disease (left atrial enlargement or left ventricular hypertrophy), and elevated natriuretic peptide levels. Participants were allocated to receive dapagliflozin 10 mg daily or placebo, in addition to other recommended therapies. Unlike prior trials in HFmrEF/HFpEF, patients were eligible if they had a history of LVEF ≤40%, if they otherwise fulfilled study entry criteria. The protocol was approved by the local ethics committees or institutional review boards at each site and an independent monitoring committee reviewed the trial.

Exposure and outcomes

We evaluated total HF hospitalizations (first and recurrent) adjudicated by the clinical endpoints committee. Complicated hospitalization was defined based on investigator‐reported data as those requiring intensive therapies, which included intensive care unit stay, intravenous vasoactive therapies (including vasopressor, inotrope, or vasodilator), invasive or non‐invasive ventilation, mechanical fluid removal, ultrafiltration or mechanical circulatory support. We excluded six HF hospitalizations in DELIVER given insufficient data on complexity parameters. Uncomplicated hospitalizations were defined as those not requiring intensive in‐hospital interventions.

Changes in left ventricular ejection fraction during follow‐up

The LVEF data during a HF hospitalization were primarily extracted using GPT‐3.5, a large language model, via a secure private endpoint. The model's effectiveness was assessed by a validation cohort of 200 patients, wherein every LVEF value the model extracted was verified as correct. However, in this same cohort, when the GPT model indicated no LVEF data were found, further manual review identified LVEF data in approximately 25% of these cases. Thus, while the model performed with 100% accuracy for data it found, it had a capture completeness of 75%, necessitating manual review for the rest of the cohort in those instances where it did not report LVEF data. The manual review ensured comprehensive data capture by verifying cases of non‐detection by the model. If LVEF was reported as a range, we conservatively recorded the lower end of the range to maintain consistency.

Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics and presenting clinical symptoms/signs of worsening HF among those patients who experienced at least one HF hospitalization by HFimpEF status were compared using counts and percentages for categorical variables, means and standard deviations for normally distributed continuous variables, and medians and interquartile ranges for non‐normally distributed continuous variables. To compare differences between groups, Pearson's χ2 tests, two‐sample t‐tests, and Wilcoxon rank‐sum tests were used, respectively. Logistic regression was used to calculate the odds ratio (OR) for complicated hospitalization, decrease in LVEF to 40% or less and decrease in LVEF by 10% or greater, adjusting for sex and LVEF at enrolment. Predictors of complicated hospitalization were assessed using multivariable logistic regression models using a stepwise forward selection adding variables with a p < 0.05, and interaction testing was performed to assess differences by HFimpEF status. Multivariable linear regression was used to calculate the mean changes in LVEF by subgroup adjusted by LVEF at enrolment. Treatment effects on total HF hospitalizations were analysed using the semiparametric proportional rates method of Lin et al. 10 Statistical analyses were performed using STATA 16.1 (College Station, TX, USA).

Results

Baseline characteristics and presenting signs and symptoms

A total of 6263 patients were randomized to either dapagliflozin or placebo. Of these, 1151 patients (18.0%) had HFimpEF. Overall, 144 patients with HFimpEF (13%) and 603 patients with LVEF consistently >40% (12%%) experienced at least one HF hospitalization. Among those hospitalized patients, participants with HFimpEF tended to be younger (70 ± 11 vs. 73 ± 9 years, p = 0.002), less likely female (30% vs. 42%, p = 0.006), less likely to have a history of atrial fibrillation (52% vs. 67%, p = 0.001) but more likely to have a history of coronary artery disease (39% vs. 30%, p = 0.044), cardiac resynchronization therapy/defibrillator implantation (9% vs. 3%, p < 0.001), and lower LVEF (50% vs. 55%, p < 0.001) compared with those hospitalized with LVEF consistently >40%. Patients with HFimpEF tended to have lower Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire total symptom score (75 [52–85] vs. 67 [48–83], p = 0.05) than those with LVEF consistently >40%, although both groups had similar NYHA class, natriuretic peptides (1400 [745–2695] vs. 1507 [850–2666], p = 0.48) (Table 1 ). Upon presentation for a first HF hospitalization, there were relatively few differences in signs and symptoms of HF between groups, with the exception of patients with HFimpEF reporting less fatigue (46% vs. 59%) (online supplementary Table S1 ).

In‐hospital clinical course of patients hospitalized for heart failure by HFimpEF status

During a median follow‐up of 2.3 years, there were 224 total HF hospitalizations in 144 patients with HFimpEF, and 985 hospitalizations in 603 patients with LVEF consistently >40%. The incidence rate for HF hospitalization was 5.9 and 5.7 per 100 patient‐years for HFimpEF and patients with LVEF consistently >40%, respectively. Patients with HFimpEF experienced higher rates of complicated HF hospitalization as compared with patients with LVEF consistently >40% (39% vs. 27%, p < 0.001) (online supplementary Table S2 ). This finding was consistent after adjusting for sex and baseline LVEF at enrolment (OR 1.61, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.18–2.20, p = 0.003). There were no significant differences in intensive care unit stay (17% vs. 13%, p = 0.11), invasive and non‐invasive mechanical ventilation, mechanical fluid removal or mechanical circulatory support (49% vs. 34%, p = 0.11), but patients with HFimpEF received more vasoactive medications during a HF hospitalization (28% vs. 16%, p < 0.001) (Figure 1 ). Among the entire DELIVER population, predictors for complicated HF hospitalization included age, Latin America and Asia regions of enrolment. There was no effect modification for predictors of complicated HF hospitalization by HFimpEF status except for geographic region, such that patients with HFimpEF had a particularly high propensity for complicated hospitalizations in Asia compared with other regions (p interaction = 0.01) (online supplementary Table S3 ). There was no significant difference in length of stay between HFimpEF participants versus patients with LVEF consistently >40% (7 days [4–12] vs. 7 days [4–10]) or in‐hospital mortality (8% vs. 6%, p = 0.40).

Left ventricular ejection fraction changes during study follow‐up

Of the total 747 patients enrolled in DELIVER who experienced at least one HF hospitalization, 66% had LVEF data available during a hospitalization. Those with LVEF data available were more likely to have a history of coronary artery disease and myocardial infarction, lower LVEF and systolic blood pressure at baseline, and were more likely treated with multi‐drug guideline‐directed medical therapy (online supplementary Table Appendix S1 ). Among the 144 patients with HFimpEF who experienced at least one HF hospitalization, 65 (45%) participants had at least one LVEF measurement available during one of the HF hospitalization events. Of those, 43 (67%) experienced a reduction in their LVEF to ≤40% during a HF hospitalization. In contrast, among the 603 participants with LVEF consistently >40% that had at least one HF hospitalization, 428 (71%) patients had available LVEF data. Of those, 125 (29%) experienced a reduction in their LVEF to ≤40% during a HF hospitalization (p < 0.001) (Figure 2A ). After adjusting for baseline LVEF, HFimpEF patients had an OR of 3.74 (95% CI 2.07–6.78) for a reduction in LVEF to ≤40% during hospitalization (p < 0.001).

Change in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) from randomization to heart failure (HF) hospitalization in participants with HF with improved ejection fraction (HFimpEF) and HF and LVEF consistently >40%. EF, ejection fraction.

The mean change during a HF hospitalization from LVEF at enrolment was −11 ± 11 in participants with HFimpEF and − 7 ± 11 in participants with LVEF consistently >40% (p = 0.003). This difference remained significant after adjusting for baseline LVEF at enrolment with participants with HFimpEF experiencing an adjusted mean decrease in LVEF change of −6 units (95% CI −9 to −3) compared to participants with LVEF consistently >40% (p < 0.001). After adjusting for LVEF at enrolment, HFimpEF participants had a higher risk of dropping their LVEF by 10% or more than participants with LVEF consistently >40% (OR 2.65, 95% CI 1.53–4.60, p = 0.001). The distribution of changes in LVEF measured during a HF hospitalization relative to baseline values is depicted in Figure 2B .

Treatment effect of dapagliflozin on complicated heart failure hospitalization by HFimpEF status

As previously reported, 8 dapagliflozin reduced total HF uncomplicated and complicated HF hospitalizations across the entire DELIVER cohort (uncomplicated: rate ratio [RR] 0.67, 95% CI 0.55–0.82; complicated: RR 0.82, 95% CI 0.63–1.06; p for heterogeneity = 0.20). There was no effect modification by HFimpEF status (p interaction = 0.30 for complicated hospitalization, p interaction = 0.69 for uncomplicated hospitalization).

Discussion

In this post hoc analysis of the DELIVER trial, patients with HFimpEF experienced a more adverse in‐hospital clinical course, necessitating higher resource utilization beyond standard diuretic therapy compared with patients with HF and LVEF consistently >40%, but had similar in‐hospital mortality. The treatment benefits of dapagliflozin in reducing the risk of hospitalizations remained consistent regardless of hospitalization type or HFimpEF status (Graphical Abstract).

Despite improvement in LVEF, HFimpEF patients continue to face a significant risk of adverse clinical events. Previous studies indicate that approximately 20% of HFimpEF patients will suffer cardiovascular death or require advanced HF therapies. 3 , 4 , 11 Furthermore, findings from the DELIVER trial suggest that the risk of HF hospitalization in HFimpEF patients is comparable to that of patients with LVEF consistently >40%. 3 However, these findings may be related to the DELIVER trial's enrolment criteria, which selected a high‐risk cohort of HFimpEF patients characterized by persistent symptoms and elevated natriuretic peptide levels.

We had previously reported that patients with HFrEF enrolled in DAPA‐HF were more likely to encounter complicated HF hospitalizations than those enrolled in DELIVER. 8 Extending on this, here we report that patients with HFimpEF experience a more adverse course of HF hospitalizations, resembling that of patients with HFrEF in DAPA‐HF. The reasons behind this are less clear. One possibility is that HFimpEF patients may continue to exhibit characteristics similar to those with HFrEF, including interval LVEF declines during hospitalization.

Despite higher complexity of HF hospitalizations, patients with HFimpEF who presented with a first HF hospitalization, reported less fatigue than patients with LVEF consistently >40%. It is possible that reverse cardiac remodelling in HFimpEF patients may lead to improved physical conditioning and quality of life, contributing to reduced fatigue. Also, prior studies have shown that patients with LVEF consistently >40% exhibit severe exercise intolerance and the most rapid rates of high‐energy phosphate depletion, impaired maximal oxidative capacity, and abnormally low peripheral oxygen extraction during exercise as compared to patients with HFrEF. 1 , 2

Left ventricular ejection fraction trajectories in HF patients are notably dynamic, with documented fluctuations over time. 12 , 13 Previous research indicates that up to half of HFrEF patients who initially exhibit improvement in ejection fraction will experience a decline within approximately 3.5 years post‐recovery. 14

This could explain why patients with HFimpEF in the DELIVER trial exhibited a higher incidence of complex hospitalizations compared to patients with LVEF consistently >40%, as they might have declines in LVEF during HF hospitalization, potentially increasing their need for vasoactive medications.

Indeed, in our cohort, we noted an LVEF decrease from enrolment to hospitalization in two‐thirds of patients with HFimpEF with at least one HF hospitalization and available data. This contrasts the patients with LVEF consistently >40%, in which only ~30% experienced a drop in LVEF to ≤40%. However, due to the small sample size and incomplete data, we cannot draw definitive conclusions. When feasible, future clinical trials enrolling patients with HFimpEF should consider evaluation of baseline LVEF by an imaging core laboratory to ensure accurate and consistent classification of HFimpEF as well as an imaging arm to study LVEF fluctuations in this patient population.

Interestingly, we observed that in‐hospital mortality did not differ significantly between patients with HFimpEF and those with LVEF consistently >40%. This finding could be attributed to several factors. One consideration is the potential lack of statistical power to detect differences in‐hospital mortality based on HFimpEF status. Additionally, variability in the completeness of HF hospitalization records across different clinical sites may have led to inconsistent data capture. Lastly, some of the care delivery elements were likely subjective and variable across sites, which might, in turn, introduce noise in the association with in‐hospital mortality.

Our study has some limitations. This study was a post hoc analysis and patients with HFimpEF only represented 18% of the participants of DELIVER; as such, these findings should be interpreted as hypothesis‐generating. Complicated hospitalizations were not formally adjudicated, and the components used to define them have not been prospectively validated and may not be generalizable to all regions and care settings. Also, not all the participants had LVEF measured during a HF hospitalization which introduces the potential for ascertainment bias, and the sample with recorded LVEF changes may not represent the entire hospitalized population. Clinicians might have been more inclined to assess LVEF in patients where a change was suspected based on clinical presentation, leading to overestimating the prevalence and magnitude of LVEF changes during hospitalization. Furthermore, since baseline LVEF were investigator‐reported without the use of an imaging core laboratory, we cannot exclude the possibility of measurement errors and misclassification of patients as HFimpEF at enrolment.

An additional important limitation is the lack of information on post‐discharge LVEF changes. This constrains our insight into the fluctuating patterns of LVEF in patients with HF. A decrease in LVEF observed during a hospitalization may be transient and does not necessarily establish a new permanent baseline. Previous research indicates that patients with HFrEF, as well as those with HFmrEF, can exhibit LVEF rebound after discharge. 15 The inability to ascertain the post‐discharge LVEF trajectory in our study poses a constraint on the long‐term clinical implications of in‐hospital LVEF decline.

In summary, among patients hospitalized during follow‐up in the DELIVER trial, patients with HFimpEF were more likely to have a complicated hospitalization requiring greater resource utilization compared with participants with LVEF consistently >40%. Increased in‐hospital complexity in patients with HFimpEF might be related to interval declines in LVEF during hospitalization. Despite these differences, in‐hospital mortality and length of hospital stay were similar across groups. The treatment benefits of dapagliflozin in reducing the risk of HF hospitalizations did not appear to differ by hospitalization complexity or HFimpEF status.

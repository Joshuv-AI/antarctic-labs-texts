Influence of the use of an intraoperative image-guided alignment system on the refractive outcomes in subjects with preoperative astigmatism: a large comparative retrospective study
Authors: Luke Rebenitsch, Inder Paul Singh, Julie M. Schallhorn
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe aim of this study is to compare the refractive outcomes in a large cohort with preoperative corneal astigmatism who underwent cataract surgery with toric intraocular lens (IOL) implantation assisted (study group) or no (control group) with an intraoperative image-guided cataract planning system (Callisto, Carl Zeiss Meditec, Jena, Germany).MethodsIn this retrospective comparative database study, mean and centroid postoperative cylinder, mean absolute error (MAE) and the proportion of eyes with a prediction error (PE) within ± 0.25 D, ± 0.50 D and ± 1.00 D (both in cylinder and spherical equivalent (SE)) were compared between both groups. These endpoints were also compared depending on the type of preoperative astigmatism, i.e. against the rule (ATR), oblique or with-the-rule (WTR) astigmatism.ResultsIn total, 31,176 cases were analyzed in this study. Mean (± standard deviation (SD)) postoperative cylinder was found to be significantly lower in the study group (0.419 ± 0.432 D) compared with the control group (0.443 ± 0.453 D, p < 0.001). This statistical difference was observed in eyes with ATR and oblique astigmatism when looking at subgroups. Postoperative centroid cylinder (mean @angle ± SD) was 0.042 @97° ± 0.600 D in the study group and 0.043 @100° ± 0.633 D in the control group. MAE in cylinder was significantly lower in the study group compared to the control group. A slight but significantly higher percentages (up to 3%) of eyes with a PE in cylinder within ± 0.25 D, ± 0.50 D and 1.00 D were observed in the study group compared to the control group.ConclusionsResults of this study showed that the use of CALLISTO surgical planning system can improve refractive outcomes after cataract surgery and toric IOL implantation in patients with preoperative corneal astigmatism, especially in terms of cylinder for ATR and oblique astigmatism.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12886-026-04918-2.

Background

The prevalence of pre-existing anterior corneal astigmatism has been estimated to be 40–46% in patients with cataract [1–6]. Implantation of toric intraocular lenses (IOLs) during cataract represents a well-known procedure to effectively correct this refractive error [7]. However, implantation of such IOLs also presents several challenges for the surgeons including not only the accurate measurement of astigmatism axis and magnitude, but also the IOL power calculation and IOL alignment [8]. The different parameters require the surgeon to handle multiple documents and data points from various sources such as optical biometer, corneal topography, optical coherence tomography (OCT), medical record, etc., which can increase the risk of error [9].

The Callisto cataract planning system (Carl Zeiss Meditec, Jena, Germany) is an image-guided, markerless system designed to assist cataract surgeons. This system provides surgery documentation and an interface for the integration and control of surgical instruments in the operating room. A previous study has shown that this system can help to precisely positioning the toric IOL [10]. In this study, a mean angle of 2.71 ± 1.64° was found between the intended and the postoperative axes [10]. Another study also found that a mean 4-weeks postoperative uncorrected visual acuity (VA) of 0.07 ± 0.12 logMAR after toric IOL implantation using the Callisto system [11]. However, to the best of our knowledge, no study has compared the refractive outcomes with and without this system.

The aim of this study was to compare the refractive outcomes in subjects undergoing cataract surgery with toric IOL implantation with or without assistance of the Callisto cataract planning system.

Materials and methods

A web-based cataract surgery planning system that includes postoperative outcomes (Veracity, Carl Zeiss Meditec) was searched to identify eyes that had undergone cataract surgery from December 2018 to January 2025 with a toric IOL. This study was exempt from full review by the institutional review board of the University of California, San Francisco, because it involved a retrospective analysis of de-identified data and thus was deemed not to be human research, therefore, informed consent was not required. The study adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Eyes were included if they had no prior history of corneal refractive surgery or keratoconus, the planning had been conducted using the Barrett Toric formula, they had an axial length between 15 and 30 mm and a preoperative cylinder of at least 0.5 D. Eyes were then divided into two groups, those that used the Callisto alignment system for intraoperative alignment and those that used a different marking system.

Subgroup analysis were also performed depending on the type of astigmatism, i.e. against-the-rule (ATR), oblique, and with-the-rule (WTR).

Baseline characteristics as well as postoperative actual and predicted cylinder and spherical equivalent (SE) and surgically-induced astigmatism (SIA) were extracted from VERACITY database. Postoperative centroid cylinder values were calculated by converting Cartesian space (X, Y) to polar coordinates (cylinder, angle), and reported in the following format: mean @ angle ± standard deviation. Double-angle plots of postoperative cylinder values were also displayed.

Mean absolute errors and prediction errors were calculated. The percentage of eyes that had a postoperative PE within ± 0.25 D, ± 0.50 D and ± 1.00 D were also determined.

Both eligible eyes were included in the analysis. To account for inter-eye correlation among eyes from the same patient, adjusted analyses were performed using generalized estimating equations with patient-level clustering, robust standard errors, and an exchangeable working correlation structure. Continuous outcomes were analyzed using Gaussian GEE (Generalized Estimating Equations) models with identity link, and binary accuracy outcomes were analyzed using logistic GEE models. Models were adjusted for age at surgery, sex, axial length, mean keratometry, preoperative cylinder, postoperative follow-up time, and calendar time of surgery. For each outcome, descriptive statistics were calculated using the same complete-case analytic population as the corresponding adjusted GEE model. For continuous outcomes, model-adjusted marginal means were estimated for each group by standardizing predictions to the same covariate distribution. The reported adjusted estimate difference (AED) corresponds to the estimate obtained for the study group minus the one obtained for the control group. For binary outcomes, model-adjusted marginal probabilities were estimated for each group, and the reported odds ratio (OR) corresponds to the estimate obtained for the study group versus the one obtained for the control group. For continuous error-magnitude outcomes, negative AEDs indicate lower adjusted error in the study group. For binary accuracy outcomes, adjusted ORs greater than 1 indicate higher adjusted odds of achieving the specified refractive accuracy threshold in the study group.

Subgroup analysis were performed based on the type of astigmatism, i.e. against-the-rule (ATR, preoperative axis of the principal meridian < 30° or > 150°), with-the-rule (WTR, < 120° and > 60°) or oblique (≥ 30° and ≤ 60°).

Results

In total, 31,176 eyes were included; 13,9 28 cases (44.7%) in the group that used intraoperative image guidance (performed by 267 surgeons) and 17,248 cases (55.3%) in the control group (performed by 181 surgeons). Among them, 19,690 cases (63.2%) were included in the ATR subgroup (8,775 (28.2%) in the study group and 10,915 (35.0%) in the control group), 4,671 (15.0%) in the oblique subgroup (2,076 (6.7%) in the study group and 2,595 (8.3%) in the control group) and 6,815 (21.9%) in the WTR subgroup (3,077 (9.9%) in the study group and 3,738 (12.0%) in the control group). Subject demographics and baseline characteristics are presented in Table 1. Despite most p values were statistically significant between both groups, these differences in demographics and baseline characteristics were not clinically meaningful. It is interesting to note that mean axial length was slightly shorter in the ATR subgroup compared to the oblique or WTR subgroups. Moreover, mean preoperative cylinder was found to be the highest in the WTR subgroup and the lowest in the oblique subgroup.

Postoperative cylinder

Mean (± standard deviation (SD)) postoperative cylinder was found to be significantly better in the study group (0.419 ± 0.432 D) compared with the control group (0.443 ± 0.453 D, p < 0.001, 95%CI [-0.04;-0.02], AED=-0.01). A statistical difference in favor of the study group was also found for patients with ATR (0.408 ± 0.414 D vs. 0.437 ± 0.452 D, p < 0.001, 95% CI [-0.04;-0.018], AED=-0.03) and oblique (0.420 ± 0.460 D vs. 0.445 ± 0.437, p = 0.048, 95% CI [-0.05;-0.00], AED=-0.03) preoperative astigmatism but not for patients with WTR astigmatism (0.451 ± 0.459 D vs. 0.460 ± 0.467, p = 0.56, 95% CI [-0.03;0.02], AED=-0.01) (Table 2).

Postoperative centroid cylinder (mean @angle ± SD) was 0.042 @97° ± 0.600 D in the study group and 0.043 @100° ± 0.633 D compared to the control group (Fig. 1). A lower mean centroid value was found in the study group for patients with ATR astigmatism, and a similar centroid angle was found between both groups for patients with oblique astigmatism. However, postoperative cylinder centroid was found to be better in the control group for patients with WTR astigmatism. Overall, standard deviations of centroid cylinder were found to be systematically lower in the study group compared to the control group, regardless the type of astigmatism (Fig. 1). No statistical difference was found between both groups in terms of centroid (based on the Cartesian coordinates) (Fig. 1).

MAE between postoperative and predicted values (cylinder and spherical equivalent)

The MAE in cylinder was found to be significantly lower in the study group (0.350 ± 0.347 D) compared to the control group (0.365 ± 0.364 D, p < 0.001, 95%CI [-0.03;-0.01], AED=). This statistical difference was found for patients with preoperative ATR (0.341 ± 0.328 D vs. 0.358 ± 0.361 D, p < 0.001, 95% CI [-0.03;-0.01], AED=-0.02) but not for the 2 other subgroups (Table 3). However, MAE in spherical equivalent was not found to be significantly different between the study and the control group (0.314 ± 0.372 D vs. 0.322 ± 0.275 D, p = 0.07, 95% CI [-0.01;0,00], AED=-0.01) (Table 3). Standard deviations were generally found to be lower for the study group compared to the control group, while proportions of outliers were found to be similar between both groups, regardless of the subgroup (Table 3).

Proportion of eyes with a PE between postoperative and predicted values (cylinder and in spherical equivalent) within ± 0.25 D, ± 0.50 D and ± 1.00 D

A slight but significantly higher proportions of eyes with a PE in cylinder within ± 0.25 D, ± 0.50 D and 1.00 D were observed in the study group (50%, 79% and 96%, respectively) compared to the control group (47%, 77% and 95%, respectively, p < 0.05, ORs > 1.10) (Table 4). Statistically significant differences were only observed in the ATR and oblique subgroups for the proportion of eyes with a PE in cylinder within ± 0.25 D and ± 0.50 D, and also within ± 1.00D in the oblique subgroups. However, proportions of eyes with a PE in spherical equivalent within ± 0.25 D, ± 0.50 D and 1.00 D were not found to be significantly different between groups (Table 4).

Discussion

In this large database study, the refractive outcomes (including cylinder, MAE and PE) were compared between cataract surgery involving toric IOL implantation performed with or without the assistance of the Callisto planning system. Results showed that a significant improvement of ~ 0.02 D in mean postoperative cylinder and in MAE in cylinder when Callisto was used compared to when the system is not used. Moreover, percentages of eyes with a PE in cylinder within ± 0.25 D, ± 0.50 D and 1.00 D improved up to 3% when Callisto is used compared to when it was not. Subgroup analysis showed that mean postoperative cylinder were also found significantly better for Callisto in patients with ATR and oblique but not WTR astigmatism. MAE in cylinder was found significantly better for Callisto in patients with ATR astigmatism while MAE in SE was not found to be significantly better for Callisto in any of the subgroups. In ATR and oblique astigmatism subgroups, a slight improvement (up to 5%) in proportions of eyes with a PE in cylinder within ± 0.25 D, ± 0.50 D and 1.00 D was observed in Callisto group. Importantly, the adjusted mean difference and odd ratios as well as the 95% CI reported in this study allowed us to assess statistical uncertainty and clinical relevance of the reported results and confirm the statistical differences found in favor of the Callisto group in this study in terms of postoperative cylinder and MAE in cylinder.

Standard deviations reported for MAE in cylinder and SE were high, which was expected due to the “real-world” aspect of the cataract cases included in this study. However, it is worth noting that standard deviations were found to be generally slightly lower in the study group compared to the control group (for all patients as wells as for all subgroups), suggesting that the use of Callisto planning system can reduce variability between prediction and actual postoperative outcomes. However, similar rates of outliers were found when Callisto was used or not.

The improvement provided by the use of Callisto in terms of refractive outcomes can seem low at first sight. However, there is a current emphasis in the field of cataract surgery, which is maximizing refractive outcomes of patients with preoperative astigmatism. For this reason, even small improvements remain clinically meaningful in the context of reducing residual astigmatism, because this can only positively affect patient expectations in terms of overall satisfaction, spectacle independence, and perceived quality of vision [12]. Moreover, ATR astigmatism can induce increased vision loss compared to WTR astigmatism. In this study, we found more statistically-significant improvements in refractive outcomes for patients with ATR and oblique, suggesting that the Callisto system can be particularly beneficial for these patients.

In a prospective comparative study recently published, similar manifest refractive spherical error were reported between Callisto system and another surgical planning system ORA (Alcon, Fort Worth, TX) [13]. However, a reduced surgery time (about 6 min faster) was observed when Callisto was used compared to ORA [13]. Overall, these results and the ones from this present study suggest that Callisto represents a valuable tool for surgeons who desire to improve workflow efficiency while maximizing refractive outcomes to their patients.

However, the retrospective design of this study represents a limitation and prospective studies should be conducted to confirm these findings. Another limitation of this study is that the method for marking the eye during cataract surgery (manual or digital) was not reported in the Veracity database, which could have influence the results. Moreover, Veracity database does not provide information regarding surgeon’s experience, type of centers or case complexity, so we were not able to demonstrate if both groups were similar regarding these aspects, which may lead to some biases. Another limitation of this study is that the distribution times between groups were significantly different between the study and the control group (Supplementary file 1). Finally, the impact of the potential improvement in refractive outcomes brought by the use of Callisto has not been assessed in this study. More studies are needed to assess if the improvement observed in this study is significantly impactful to address increasing patient expectations.

Conclusion

Results of this study showed that the use of Callisto surgical planning system can improve refractive outcomes after cataract surgery and toric IOL implantation in patients with preoperative corneal astigmatism.

A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 study to assess efficacy and safety of ropeginterferon alfa-2b in patients with early/lower-risk primary myelofibrosis
Authors: Ghaith Abu-Zeinah, Albert Qin, Harinder Gill, Norio Komatsu, John Mascarenhas, Weichung Joe Shih, Oleh Zagrijtschuk, Toshiaki Sato, Kazuya Shimoda, Richard T. Silver, Ruben Mesa
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Primary myelofibrosis (PMF) is the most aggressive of the myeloproliferative neoplasms and patients require greater attention and likely require earlier therapeutic intervention. Currently approved treatment options are limited in their selective suppression of clonal proliferation resulting from driver- and coexisting gene mutations. Janus kinase inhibitors are approved for symptomatic patients with higher-risk PMF. Additionally, most ongoing clinical studies focus on patients with higher-risk disease and/or high rates of transfusion dependency. Optimal treatment of early/lower-risk PMF remains to be identified and needs randomized clinical trial evaluations. Pegylated interferon alfa is recommended for symptomatic lower-risk PMF patients based on phase 2 non-randomized studies and expert opinion. Ropeginterferon alfa-2b (ropeg) is a new-generation pegylated interferon-based therapy with favorable pharmacokinetics and safety profiles, requiring less frequent injections than prior formulations. This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial will assess its efficacy and safety in patients with “early/lower-risk PMF”, defined as pre-fibrotic PMF or PMF at low or intermediate-1 risk according to Dynamic International Prognostic Scoring System-plus. Co-primary endpoints include clinically relevant complete hematologic response and symptom endpoint. Secondary endpoints include progression- or event-free survival, molecular response in driver or relevant coexisting gene mutations, bone marrow response, and safety. Disease progression and events are defined based on the International Working Group criteria and well-published reports. 150 eligible patients will be randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive either ropeg or placebo. Blinded sample size re-estimation is designed. Ropeg will be administered subcutaneously with a tolerable, higher starting-dose regimen. The study will provide important data for the treatment of early/lower-risk PMF for which an anti-clonal, disease-modifying agent is highly needed.

Introduction

Primary myelofibrosis (PMF), polycythemia vera (PV), and essential thrombocythemia (ET) are BCR-ABL-negative myeloproliferative neoplasms (MPNs), resulting from the clonal proliferation of bone marrow hematopoietic stem or progenitor cells [1, 2]. Most patients with PMF harbor mutually exclusive driver mutations in genes encoding Janus kinase 2 (JAK2), calreticulin (CALR), or myeloproliferative leukemia virus oncogene (MPL). Patients with ET or PV may develop secondary myelofibrosis (MF), i.e., post-ET or post-PV MF, which have a similar clinical manifestation and treatments as PMF [3]. Thrombohemorrhagic complications and leukemic transformation are part of the clinical course of MPNs [4, 5]. Patients with PMF carry a risk of thrombosis and bleeding [6, 7]. The risk and cardiovascular complications are probably significantly underestimated in PMF [4, 6] and are often overshadowed by disease progression and transformation to blast phase MPN. Of the three major MPNs, PMF has the worst overall morbidity and mortality, and is characterized by progressive anemia, bone marrow reticulin fibrosis and collagenosis, systemic symptoms, and hepto-splenomegaly [7, 8]. Multiple scoring systems have been developed for prognostication in PMF, including the International Prognostic Scoring System (IPSS), Dynamic IPSS (DIPSS), and DIPSS-plus [9]. Current clinical trials focus on higher-risk overt PMF, generally intermediate to high risk DIPSS-plus score, but there is an ongoing need for clinical trials of treatment to address symptom burden and disease risks in pre-fibrotic and lower-risk PMF.

Rationale for the study

In 2016, WHO revised and clarified diagnostic criteria for PMF to include pre-fibrotic PMF and overt PMF [1]. Following the revision, extensive research has been done to validate the diagnostic accuracy and evaluate its impact [10, 11]. The criteria for the diagnosis of pre-fibrotic/early PMF requires at least one of the four minor clinical criteria: anemia, leukocytosis, elevated lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) and splenomegaly. Pre-fibrotic/early PMF was found to be associated with a high frequency of thrombohemorrhagic complications, not previously well recognized [4]. Leukocytosis is a significant risk factor for overall thrombosis and arterial thrombosis and is a predictor of survival in patients with pre-fibrotic/early PMF [12, 13]. Thrombocytosis, defined as platelet count ≥ 450 × 109/L, was present at diagnosis in approximately 84% of the patients with pre-fibrotic/early PMF [12]. Persistently elevated white blood cell (WBC) and platelet counts, specifically > 11 × 109/L and > 400 × 109/L respectively, are associated with increased thromboembolic (TE) risk [14, 15].

Currently, most of the approved drugs and clinical studies for PMF focus on higher-risk disease with high rates of transfusion dependency. JAK inhibitors such as ruxolitinib, pacritinib and momelotinib are approved for patients with intermediate or high-risk MF [16]. JAK inhibitor fedratinib is approved for intermediate-2 or high-risk MF [17]. Patients diagnosed with pre-fibrotic/early PMF may not be eligible for or benefit from treatment with JAK inhibitors. Patients with lower-risk PMF, i.e., low or intermediate-1 risk without splenomegaly are reasonably not suited for receiving JAK inhibitors given their approved requirements and potential side-effects including infections, cytopenias (neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and anemia), gastrointestinal issues, and non-melanoma skin cancer [18]. The treatment options for patients with early/lower-risk PMF, i.e., pre-fibrotic or low to intermediate-1 PMF, are thus very limited and often involve an unsatisfying “watch-and-wait” management approach. Greater attention is needed for developing novel and effective therapies that could deliver clinically relevant, progression-free responses. Current standards of care are usually observation or participation in a clinical trial. Compared to patients with ET, patients with early/lower-risk PMF often present similarly with thrombocytosis, but have a higher leukocyte count, lower hemoglobin level, higher LDH level, frequent palpable splenomegaly which can progress rapidly, and are often symptomatic. Additional adverse mutations may appear, besides the three driver mutations JAK2V617F, CALR and MPL [2, 12, 19]. Therefore, there is a need for a validated treatment for this patient population. In this respect, a disease modifying agent to selectively deplete clonal proliferation will mitigate the TE risk and associated clinical and laboratory (lab) abnormalities already discussed.

Rationale for assessing ropeginterferon alfa-2b (ropeg) in patients with early/lower-risk PMF

Commonly used hydroxyurea (HU) is a nonspecific chemotherapeutic drug which induces cytoreduction for reducing the TE risk in MPNs [20–23]. However, it does not appear to produce durable complete hematologic response (CHR) or possess disease modifying capabilities such as providing progression-free or event-free survival [24, 25]. In addition, its use in patients with early/lower-risk PMF is limited due to its risk of inducing anemia, potential leukemogenic transformation and non-melanoma skin cancer [24, 26].

Interferon alfa (IFN-α) therapy has demonstrated meaningful clinical activity in patients with early PMF or low or intermediate-1 risk MF, inducing bone marrow response and preventing the development of marked splenomegaly, anemia and advanced MF [27–31]. Recombinant IFN-α was also suggested to improve bone marrow morphology associated with successful clinical response in MF patients including patients with overt PMF [27, 29–31]. These were small, non-randomized phase 2 studies. Based on this evidence, it is recommended by the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines and used as an off-label treatment for symptomatic patients with low-risk PMF [32]. However, it is highly under-utilized in clinical practice and there are no well-run, prospective randomized controlled clinical trials demonstrating the safety and efficacy of an IFN-based therapy in patients with early/lower-risk PMF.

Ropeginterferon alfa-2b (ropeg) is a novel polyethylene glycol-conjugated (PEGylated) IFN-α-based therapy with a favorable pharmacokinetic (PK) profile [33, 34]. Its injection is approved for the treatment of adult patients with PV regardless of prior therapies in Europe, the United States, and many other countries or regions [35, 36]. It is currently under development for other indications including ET [37–39]. It does not appear to cause clinically significant anemia as demonstrated in its treatment of patients with PV [40]. In PV, its treatment induces durable CHR and deep molecular response, and prolongs event-free survival [24, 41, 42]. Its mechanism of action is at least an effect of the IFN-α signaling in activating downstream tumor suppressors or their related proteins and inhibiting relevant proto-oncogenes involved in cell cycle progression and neoplastic phenotype of the MPN malignant cells [43]. Ropeg has been shown to be well-tolerated in various clinical studies [24, 41, 42, 44, 45]. Furthermore, ropeg is being evaluated in patients with pre-fibrotic PMF, DIPSS low/intermediate-1 risk PMF or secondary MF in a single arm phase 2 clinical study and was found to be well-tolerated with durable hematologic responses and evidence of disease modification [46].

Therefore, ropeg represents a new-generation IFN-α-based therapy which may provide clinical efficacy with tolerability in patients with early/lower-risk PMF. This randomized, double blind, placebo-controlled, multi-center phase 3 clinical study will assess the efficacy and safety of ropeg in patients with early/lower-risk PMF. The trial is aimed at assessing the clinically relevant complete hematologic response (Cr-CHR), MF-associated symptoms, TE risk, anemia status, molecular response, time to progression, bone marrow response, and progression-free survival (PFS) or event-free survival (EFS).

Methods

Patient population

This phase 3 clinical study is designed to assess the efficacy and safety of ropeg in patients with pre-fibrotic/early PMF or overt PMF at low or intermediate-1 risk categorized according to DIPSS-plus. The patient population is referred to as “early/lower-risk PMF” for abbreviation. The pre-fibrotic/early PMF or overt PMF diagnosis is according to World Health Organization (WHO) 2016 or 2022 classification [1].

Overall study design

It is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multi-center phase 3 clinical study. The primary objective of the study is to measure the efficacy and safety of patients with early/lower-risk PMF. Eligible patients will be enrolled and randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive ropeg treatment or placebo control in a core study consisting of a 4-week screening, 56-week treatment, and 4-week safety follow-up (Fig. 1). Patients will be randomized centrally by region through interactive response technology (IRT). Ropeg or placebo will be given subcutaneously once every two weeks (biweekly) in a double blinded manner during the whole 56-week treatment period. The double-blinded treatment of ropeg and placebo in the study will be un-blinded after the end of the 56-week treatment (EoT) for analyses of primary and secondary endpoints. Patients of both groups who have completed the safety follow-up for the core study will enter an extended 56-week treatment. Ropeg and placebo will not be given during the 4-week safety follow-up period. Patients who are entering the extension will receive the treatment at the Week 60 visit. The extension is an open-label study, patients will be observed and no longer receive placebo treatment in the control group. The study duration for each patient will be approximately 29 months including a 4-week screening, 56-week treatment, and 4-week safety follow-up for the core study (15 months) and a 60-week extension phase consisting a 56-week treatment and 4-week safety follow-up (14 months).

Study drug

Patients will be subcutaneously administered with ropeg or placebo at a starting dose of 250 µg at Week 0, followed by an intra-patient dose titration to 350 µg at Week 2, and then 500 µg every two weeks from Week 4 onwards. Dose could be adjusted according to tolerability and safety. The dosing regimen provides a treatment option that has a higher starting dose of 250 µg and simpler dose titrations to the maximum recommended dose of 500 µg compared to the approved dosing regimen. This dosing regimen has been tested in several clinical studies in patients with PV or with pre-fibrotic/early PMF or DIPSS low or intermediate-1 risk MF and found to be well-tolerated [41, 46–48]. Self-injection is allowed after intra-patient dose escalation for patients who demonstrate the ability of self-administration after proper training. The dosing schedule modification, i.e., from biweekly to monthly, based on response is allowed in the extension phase.

Dose reduction or interruption is driven by safety and tolerability. Dose changes must be recorded in the electronic case report form (eCRF). If intolerance persists after dose reduction or interruption, treatment should be discontinued. Treatment re-initiation must begin at the prior lower dose than the dose that caused the toxicity. For example, if a grade 3 AE occurs at 500 µg, treatment re-initiation will occur with 350 µg. If there is an absence of response at this reduced dose and the AE is recovered, the dose will escalate to the next higher dose level. Study assessments

After a written informed consent is obtained and eligibility is determined, site visits will be scheduled for biweekly before Week 4 and monthly thereafter until Week 56 (EoT) for safety and efficacy assessment. Clinical and hematologic tests including platelet and WBC counts, and Hgb at Week 0/Day 1, Weeks 16, 28, 40, and 56 will be assessed for response. A bone marrow biopsy will be performed at screening, unless suitable biopsy samples collected within 24 weeks prior to screening are available for the assessment. A safety follow-up visit will be at 28 days after EoT visit.

Co-primary endpoints including clinically relevant CHR (CrCHR) and no progression on clinical symptoms based on the MFSAF TSS v4.0 will be measured at 56 weeks of treatment. Molecular response with regards to driver or relevant non-driver mutations will be assessed by a central lab at 56 weeks, same time as bone marrow evaluation, and anti-ropeg antibody determinations.

Safety evaluations will be assessed by vital signs, lab tests, physical examinations, electrocardiogram (ECG), echocardiography (ECHO), chest X-ray, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status, ocular examination, and AEs. An independent Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) will provide oversight and monitoring of the conduct of clinical trials to ensure the safety of participants and the validity and integrity of study data.

Approximately 20 patients will be invited to participate in the serial PK sub-study during the core study phase. Serial PK blood samples (n = 4) will be collected at the first dose and after the 9th dose (steady state). PK analysis will be conducted by central lab. Sponsor, investigator, and patient will be blinded to the PK results until the unblinding of the treatment after the EoT in the core study.

Outcomes

The study has two co-primary endpoints, including CrCHR and symptom endpoint. The CrCHR is defined according to the CHR criteria of International Working Group-Myeloproliferative Neoplasms Research and Treatment (IWG-MRT) and requirement of no TE events: specifically, platelet ≤ 400 × 109/L, and WBC ≤ 10 × 109/L, and Hgb ≥ 10.0 g/dL, and absence of major thrombotic events, and no progression to secondary acute myeloid leukemia (AML). The symptom endpoint is no progression on clinical symptoms based on the Myelofibrosis Symptom Assessment Form vs. 4 (MFSAF vs.4) Total Symptom Score (TSS). No progression on symptoms is defined as: if baseline MFSAF TSS score ≤ 10, TSS score stays ≤ 10; or if baseline TSS score > 10, no increase > 50% in TSS score.

The secondary endpoints include the rate of hematological response as defined in the primary endpoint at 56 weeks; changes in mutant allele frequencies (MAF) of JAK2V617F, other JAK2 mutations, CALR, and MPL mutations, as well as non-driver mutations from baseline to Week 16, 28, 40, and 56; percentage of patients with palpable splenomegaly ≥ 5 cm at Week 56; time to CrCHR; time to disease progression (TTP); PFS; EFS; bone marrow response including changes in bone marrow morphology (cellularity, erythroid, myeloid, megakaryocyte) and fibrosis (by reticulin stain) at week 56 and end of the extension study; changes in cytokine profile at Week 56 or EoT; PK and population PK (PPK) analysis; immunogenicity of ropeg using anti-drug antibody (ADA).

For EFS, events include death from any causes, major thrombotic events [51], and [4] disease progression, whichever occurs first.

Safety endpoints include incidence of AE and serious adverse events (SAEs), clinical lab parameters, physical examination, vital signs and ECG abnormalities, incidence of AEs of special interest, and AEs leading to dose reduction or discontinuation of the treatment.

Sample size

The placebo is not expected to cause clinically appreciable effects regarding the two co-primary endpoints. Previously, it was shown that patients receiving placebo experienced significant worsening symptoms or only 5.3% of patients achieved a ≥50% reduction in MFSAF TSS v2.0 at week 24 of the treatment in MF patients [52, 53]. For CrCHR, we conservatively assumed a 10% response rate for the placebo considering potential fluctuations in the lab values for hematologic parameters, which is likely to represent a maximal value. At the higher-starting dose regimen, i.e., the 250-350-500 μg dosing schema, ropeg achieved a CHR rate of 61.2% at 24 weeks and 71.4% at 52 weeks in patients with PV [41, 45]. In patients with PV, ropeg did not cause clinically significant anemia such as at the ≥grade 3 level or at the grade 2 level that is persistent and unmanageable [40]. However, approximately 6.1% of the patients showed reversible reduction of Hgb at the grade 2 level, i.e., at the 8-10 g/dL range [40]. Anemia status is an important parameter for PMF and the Hgb response of ≥10.0 g/dL is required for the CrCHR. Therefore, we conservatively estimated a CrCHR of 50% for the ropeg arm assuming no major thrombotic event and progression to AML or advanced PMF. Ropeg is currently being evaluated in a phase 2 study in patients with pre-fibrotic/early PMF or DIPSS low/intermediate-1 risk MF [46]. Responses of this study in Hgb, WBC and platelet were 75%, 82% and 74% respectively at 24 weeks, and 82%, 80% and 71% respectively at 48 weeks [46], which is also consistent with a hematologic response of approximately 50%. Therefore, 135 patients (2:1 randomization ratio, 90 for the ropeg arm and 45 for the placebo group) are required to have at least a 90% power of detecting a superiority given the estimated CrCHR of approximately 50% for the ropeg arm and 10% for the placebo, as significant at the 3% level (2-sided binomial test). A 10% drop-off rate is considered given that less than 10% discontinuation rate was observed in the previous study in patients with PV when treated at the higher starting dose regimen. Therefore, a total sample size of approximately 150 patients is planned for detecting the superiority.

For co-primary symptom endpoint, the aim is to show that the effect of ropeg is greater than that of the placebo in controlling symptom progression. Subject to further discussions with regulatory agencies, the hypothesis for this aim will be tested initially at the 0.02 alpha-level (2-sided). With 150 patients (2:1 randomization ratio) and at least 80% power, the detectable difference between the treatment groups in the symptom endpoint is at least 30%.

The family-wise type-1 error rate (overall alpha level) is controlled at the 0.05 level using the Bonferroni-Holm method with local alpha planned to be allocated as 0.03 for the CrCHR endpoint and 0.02 for the symptom endpoint. When any one of the co-primary endpoints passes at its local alpha, the local alpha will then propagate to the other co-primary endpoint; the next co-primary endpoint will then be tested at the 0.05 level (2-sided).

Blinded interim sample size re-estimation

Because of uncertainty in the assumptions on which the calculation of the sample size is based, an interim analysis for possibly adjusting the sample size is considered. For CrCHR rate at Week 56, the uncertainties are the incidences of peripheral blood Hgb level, thrombotic events, and progression to AML. The Hgb level can represent a greater uncertainty as it could be influenced by clinical course of early/lower-risk PMF and a drug effect. An interim analysis blinded to the sponsor will be conducted for sample size re-estimation (SSR) for preserving the power to minimize the above clinical uncertainty at half of the trial. That is, when approximately 50% of patients have completed 56 weeks of treatment or withdrawn from the study prior to 56 weeks. The sample size will be recalculated by a DSMB statistician using the conditional power (CP) approach based on the interim data without revealing any information of the CP to the sponsor. The sample size will be adjusted upward only when the CP of reaching significance at the final analysis with the original sample size (150 randomized patients) is > 50% for the CrCHR rate. No early stopping rule for claiming efficacy is planned. The target CP with the adjusted sample size is at least 80%, but the adjusted sample size will be capped at a maximum number that is a recommended integer ending in ten, e.g. 20, 30, 40, or 50 for minimizing the risk of revealing any CP information. When the CP with original sample size is < 50%, the study may continue without adjusting sample size. The maximum sample size increment will be agreed upon between the Sponsor and the DSMB and will be documented in the DSMB minutes prior to the interim analysis. It has been shown that increasing sample size when the interim result is “promising” (meaning CP at least 50% with the original sample size) will not inflate the type I error rate [54, 55]. Only the revised sample size, if adjusted, will be recommended to the Sponsor by the DSMB without any other specific information to avoid any possibility of back calculation regarding the effect size. Additional interim analysis may be performed for a longer-term assessment of the symptom endpoint when necessary.

Statistical analysis methods

Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel (CMH) test will be used for analyzing the two co-primary efficacy endpoints. The regions or pre-defined pooled regions will be the blocks for the CMH test. General statistical summaries are applied to primary and secondary endpoints. For categorical endpoints, frequencies and percentage will be presented with a 95% confidence interval. For continuous variables, number of patients, mean, standard deviation (SD), median, lower quartile (Q1), upper quartile (Q3), minimum, maximum, and 95% CI will be presented if appropriate. For endpoints with measurements over time, the statistics will also be presented by timepoint. For time to event variables, such as time to CrCHR, TTP, PFS, and EFS, the Kaplan-Meier method will be used to estimate the accumulative distribution as well as associated statistics, such as median time and confidence interval.

AEs will be described according to the System Organ Classes and Preferred Term of the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) and will be graded according to Common Terminology Criteria for AEs (CTCAE) v5.0. Descriptive statistics will be made for the number of occurrences, number of patients and incidence of various AEs (all AEs, treatment-emergent adverse events [TEAEs], SAEs, drug-related TEAEs, TEAEs of grade 3 and above, TEAEs leading to discontinuation of the trial, TEAEs leading to drug suspension in each arm.

Immunogenicity analysis including the proportions of patients with positive or negative anti-ropeg antibody and the status of neutralizing antibody in ADA-positive patients will be summarized.

Discussion

MF, primary or secondary, carries the most aggressive of phenotypes of the MPNs and requires disease-modifying treatment. Currently approved treatment options for MF, mainly JAK inhibitors, suppress hematopoietic and inflammatory cytokine signaling to exert its clinical effects of symptom improvement and spleen volume reduction but appear to be limited in their selective antitumor or anti-clonal effects that are read out by meaningful and durable reduction in driver mutation allele frequency and survival outcomes. Despite marked effect on spleen size reduction and significant symptom relief that benefit patients, therapeutic inhibition of JAKs in MF has not been shown to significantly reverse bone marrow fibrosis or induce complete cytogenetic or molecular remissions [3]. Although a minor fibrosis reversion frequency was observed with the JAK inhibition in patients with MF, it was not observed to be associated with survival advantage [56]. JAK inhibitors are approved for treatment of symptomatic patients with higher risk MF and the optimal treatment for patients with early/lower-risk PMF or MF remains to be identified. The eligibility criteria of most of the clinical studies in MF focused on intermediate and high-risk disease with high rates of transfusion dependency. These studies aim to evaluate spleen volume reduction and symptom response as primary study endpoints that are relevant to advanced MF. Patients with pre-fibrotic/early PMF and lower risk MF were usually excluded from the clinical studies with either monotherapy or combination therapy with JAK inhibitors. Similarly, endpoints relevant to patients with early/lower-risk PMF or MF, e.g., normalization of peripheral blood counts to reduce the TE risk, CrCHR and no worsening in symptoms, are not often used in the studies for late-stage disease. Approved labels of medications to treat MF do not include pre-fibrotic PMF or low-risk MF.

In a large retrospective study of 470 patients with PV, longer-term recombinant IFN-α therapy was associated with a lower risk of MF and mortality, suggesting that its treatment could prevent MF and prolong survival [57]. Ropeg, a new generation of IFN-α-based therapies, has been shown to induce CHR, deep molecular response, and prolong EFS [24, 41, 42, 58, 59], IFN-based treatment was shown to reduce bone marrow fibrosis, improve bone marrow morphology and retard disease progression in patients with early MF [27, 29, 30]. IFN-α, like its same type cytokine IFN-beta (β), is involved in the cell cycle-based, selective anti-cancer mechanism which renders tumor cells to enter senescence and lose tumorigenicity [60, 61]. These data suggest that ropeg may be a suitable treatment for the proposed patient population in this study. Disease-modifying, clinically relevant hematologic response or no symptom worsening without disease progression to advanced PMF and AML is anticipated.

Treatment guidelines including the NCCN recommend use of PEGylated IFN-α for patients with lower-risk MF, together with other experimental therapeutic options. The recommendation of IFN-α to treat MF was based on roughly ten studies with a total of 141 patients treated with various IFN-α formulations and dosing schedules [62]. It was concluded that patients with lower-risk MF and a pre-fibrotic hypercellular disease phase appear to benefit more from the IFN treatment than patients in the overt disease phase with extensive bone marrow fibrosis and advanced bone marrow hypercellularity [62].

Recently, ropeg is being evaluated in patients with pre-fibrotic/early PMF (pre-PMF), low/intermediate-1 risk PMF or secondary MF according to DIPSS in a single arm phase 2 clinical study and the efficacy and safety data were presented during the American Society of Hematology (ASH) meetings 2022 and 2023 [46]. 37 men and 29 women with a median age of 59 (range: 30–86) years were enrolled. The diagnoses were pre-PMF (N = 46; 69.6%), overt PMF (N = 6; 9.1%), post-PV MF (N = 5; 7.5%), and post-ET MF (N = 9; 13.6%). Responses in Hgb, WBC and platelet were 75%, 82% and 74% respectively at 24 weeks, and 82%, 80% and 71% respectively at 48 weeks. Reduction of MAF at 24 and 48 weeks was achieved for JAK2V617F in 50% (19/38) of patients and 79% (22/28) respectively; and for CALR in 44% (7/16) of patients and 50% (5/10) of patients respectively. Forty-six patients (69.6%) had bone marrow biopsies serially performed, with 42 patients (91%) having stable/improved marrow fibrosis [36]. Eight patients (17.4%) had resolution of marrow fibrosis by 48 weeks. Ropeg was well-tolerated [46]. The study showed encouraging efficacy and excellent safety of ropeg in patients with pre-PMF and low or intermediate-1 risk MF.

In summary, existing data support the clinical development of ropeg in the treatment of patients with pre-fibrotic/early PMF or PMF at low or intermediate-1 risk according to DIPSS-plus, i.e., the early/lower-risk PMF patient population, in the phase 3 setting. The randomized, placebo-controlled, multicenter phase 3 study is designed to provide valuable data of ropeg regarding achievement and durability of CrCHR; molecular response in driver or relevant non-driver mutations; management of symptom progression; maintenance of no thrombohemorrhagic complications or progression to advanced PMF or AML (progression-free and event-free survivals); and safety in this patient population.

Pre-existing infiltration with T cells and CD14+ myeloid cells is associated with treatment response to imiquimod in primary and recurrent vulvar high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesions
Authors: Caroline L P Muntinga, Peggy J de Vos van Steenwijk, Loes F S Kooreman, Sigrid Regauer, Ziena Abdulrahman, Ruud L M Bekkers, Sjoerd H van der Burg, Gerda Trutnovsky, Edith M G van Esch
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractImiquimod is a standard therapy option for vulvar high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesions (vHSIL). In a retrospective study, the pre-existing composition of the immune cell infiltrate was associated with clinical outcome after imiquimod treatment. To validate these findings, an in-depth analysis of the tumor microenvironment was performed on a vHSIL cohort treated with imiquimod in the prospective PITVIN randomized controlled trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT01861535). Pretreatment biopsies of per-protocol patients participating in the PITVIN trial allocated to the imiquimod arm were included (n=38). These were analyzed by multispectral immunofluorescence using two seven-color staining panels for T cell and myeloid cell composition. Samples were scanned with the Vectra imaging microscope. Cell phenotyping was conducted using semi-supervised machine learning. Quick complete response (qCR) (n=27) was defined as absence of clinical lesions at 16 weeks; slow complete response (n=4) as delayed clearance requiring treatment up to 6 months; and non-response (n=7) as persistent histological vHSIL after 6 months, necessitating extended treatment or surgery. Analysis of the tumor immune microenvironment revealed interpatient variability in immune cell infiltration. Immune infiltrate composition in primary and recurrent vHSIL was comparable and this was also reflected by their clinical responsiveness to imiquimod. Higher numbers of intraepithelial CD3+CD8-(CD4+) T helper cells CD4+FoxP3+ regulatory T cells and CD14+ inflammatory myeloid cells and lower numbers of intraepithelial CD33+ immature cells were detected in qCR when compared with other response groups. Importantly, the lesions of complete responders displayed a positive correlation between the numbers of CD4+, CD8+ T cells and CD14+ inflammatory myeloid cells infiltrating the stroma and epithelium, indicative of a coordinated immune response. Slow complete responders displayed increased intraepithelial infiltration of recently activated CD4+PD1+ T cells (p<0.05), but displayed a lower CD14+ and CD68+ myeloid cell infiltration when compared with qCR. The presence of a pre-existing coordinated infiltration of vHSIL lesions with CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells and CD14+ inflammatory myeloid cells in patients displaying a CR after imiquimod treatment confirms our previous findings and suggests their use as biomarkers to predict responsiveness, but may also function as biomarkers to reduce or extend treatment duration of imiquimod.Keywords: Immunotherapy, Tumor microenvironment - TME, Biomarker

Background

Vulvar high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesions (vHSIL), previously referred to as usual vulvar intraepithelial neoplasia, is a premalignancy caused by a persistent infection with high-risk human papillomavirus (hrHPV). Several effective surgical and topical treatment modalities are available, for example, CO2 laser ablation, surgical excision, and imiquimod cream. Surgical options are associated with changes in anatomy and concomitant psychosexual distress.1 Moreover, vHSIL recurs in up to 50% of treated patients.2 3 Imiquimod has been proven to be a safe and effective non-invasive alternative to surgery for treatment of vHSIL, with complete response rates of 60–80% and recurrence rates of 9% after complete response.3,5 Imiquimod is an immunomodulating cream, acting as a Toll-like receptor-7 (TLR-7) agonist with antiviral and antitumoral effects.6

To show non-inferiority of imiquimod to surgery, a multicenter, randomized, non-inferiority trial (PITVIN; ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT01861535) was performed in women with vHSIL.4 The randomization was stratified for unifocal or multifocal disease. Both primary and recurrent vHSIL were included. Non-inferiority of imiquimod was demonstrated, with 80% of patients assigned to the imiquimod arm achieving a complete response. In this study imiquimod was self-applied for a minimum of 16 weeks using a slowly escalating dosage scheme. All patients who did not have complete clinical response at 4 months were asked to continue imiquimod treatment until complete clinical response, with a maximum duration of 6 months. A complete response was defined as histological regression to no dysplasia or vulvar low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesions (vLSIL) at 6 months. In patients with persistent disease at 6 months, either surgical excision took place or imiquimod treatment was continued, based on patient preferences. At 6 months, 37 of 46 patients (80%) had reached complete response.4

The local immune microenvironment is suggested to play a crucial role in the clinical course of the disease, irrespective of the primary or recurrent status of the lesion.7 8 In a retrospective study, the composition and coordination of immune infiltrates was found to differ between complete responders and non-responders to imiquimod treatment. Complete response to imiquimod therapy was associated with the presence of type 1 CD4+ and CD8+ T cells and CD14+ inflammatory myeloid cells.9 10 Systemically, a pre-existing proliferative T cell response to HPV 16 L2/E6/E7 was observed, suggesting that a pre-existing HPV-specific T cell response is associated with a complete clinical response.11

To validate previously identified immune profiles correlating with the therapeutic response to imiquimod in vHSIL and to study differences between quick and slow responders, we performed in-depth analyses of the lesions of patients treated with imiquimod in the PITVIN trial.4 We confirmed previous findings and found that intraepithelial infiltration with higher counts of CD4+ T cells and CD14+ inflammatory myeloid cells, together with lower infiltration of CD33+ immature cells is associated with quick complete response (qCR).

Methods

Patient samples

Pretreatment formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded biopsies of per-protocol patients participating in the PITVIN trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT01861535) with histologically confirmed vHSIL and randomized to treatment with imiquimod were included in this study for analysis. In this study, all pretreatment biopsies were revised by an independent pathologist. Only samples from patients who completed the PITVIN trial per protocol were included.

Multispectral immunofluorescence staining

After pathologic revision, two distinct seven-color multispectral immunofluorescence panels were used based on the panel previously described by Abdulrahman et al (online supplemental table 1).10 The first panel was specifically designed for the assessment of T cells and consisted of CD3, CD8, FoxP3, TIM3, Tbet, PD-1 and 4',6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI). The second panel, tailored for myeloid cells, featured CD14, CD33, CD68, CD11c, CD163, HLADR, and DAPI. DAPI was used as a nuclear counterstain. Tonsil slides were used as positive controls.

Quantification of immune cells in the tumor microenvironment

Whole slide scans were acquired with the Vectra V.3.0.5 multispectral imaging microscope (PerkinElmer) at 20× magnification, as previously described by Abdulrahman et al.10 Spectral unmixing was carried out using inForm V.2.6 image analysis software (PerkinElmer-Akoya Biosciences). Automated phenotyping and quantification of immune cells within the tumor microenvironment were performed with Qupath V.0.3.2 image analysis software.12 Dysplastic regions as delineated by the pathologist were used for analysis. Epithelium, stroma and non-analyzable regions, such as glands, blood vessels, and tissue and staining artifacts were manually annotated. Qupath automatically segmented DAPI+nucleated cells. Then, a classifier was trained to assign a phenotype to each cell, which was visually inspected. Errors were addressed by refining the training until all discrepancies were resolved. The results contained the final density for each phenotype in positive cells/mm2.

Statistical analysis

qCR was defined as no clinical vulvar lesion at 16 weeks, thus stopping imiquimod treatment. Slow complete response (sCR) was defined as no complete clinical response at 16 weeks, thus continuing imiquimod treatment until 6 months. Non-response (NR) was defined as persistent histological vHSIL after 6 months of imiquimod use, requiring further prolongation of imiquimod treatment or surgical excision. Median immune counts of qCR, sCR and NR were compared with the Kruskal-Wallis test. A threshold of median cell count ≥10 cells/mm2 in at least one response group was considered biologically common and was included in the analysis.10 To compare baseline characteristics, one-way analysis of variance and Fisher-Freeman-Halton exact tests were used. To analyze trends in non-parametric data, the Jonckheere-Terpstra test was used. Statistical analysis and data visualization was performed using GraphPad Prism (V.9, GraphPad Software, 2021) and IBM SPSS Statistics (V.28, IBM, 2021). A two-sided p value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Of the 38 patients studied (figure 1), 27 patients had a complete clinical response after 16 weeks of imiquimod treatment. At 6 months the clinical response was confirmed histologically by regression to vLSIL or no dysplasia, defining them as qCR. 11 patients continued treatment with imiquimod until 6 months of which 4 patients had a complete clinical and histological response at 6 months, defining them as sCR. The remaining seven patients had persistent disease at 6 months, and were classified as NR. After 6 months of imiquimod treatment, patients with persistent disease could choose whether they preferred continuing imiquimod treatment or surgical excision. There were no significant differences in baseline clinical characteristics and lesion characteristics between response groups (table 1, online supplemental table 2).

Primary and recurrent vHSIL have similar immune infiltrates and respond well to imiquimod treatment

The vulva is a highly immune-active anatomical location.10 However, there is a high interpatient variability in the number of immune cells infiltrating the epithelium and stroma of vHSIL (online supplemental figure 1). Multispectral immunofluorescence and analysis of the immune cells present showed six distinct phenotypes in the lymphoid compartment and seven phenotypes in the myeloid compartment (figures2 3). The identified CD3+CD8−FoxP3− cells were classified as CD4+ T cells, consistent with earlier studies that revealed an average of 99% CD4+ T cells among the population of CD3+CD8−FoxP3− cells.13

In this cohort, 77.8% of the primary and 90.9% of recurrent vHSIL responded to imiquimod treatment. Comparison of primary (n=27) and recurrent (n=11) vHSIL reveals no differences in focality or smoking (online supplemental table 3). Examination of the lesions’ immune microenvironment revealed no distinct differences in the T cell and myeloid cell compartment between primary and recurrent vHSIL (figure 4, online supplemental figure 2 and 3). This is in line with previous literature7 8 and the fact that both types of lesions were able to respond well to imiquimod treatment (online supplemental table 3).

The importance of CD4+ and CD14+ intraepithelial infiltration in relation to therapy responses

To assess if the numbers and composition of the immune infiltrate differed per response group, we divided the patients into three groups: qCR, sCR and NR. In the epithelium, the absolute number of T cell infiltration decreased gradually from qCR to sCR and NR (figure 2A). In qCR, CD4+ T helper cell infiltration is increased compared with sCR and NR, with a median of 83 cells/mm2 in qCR, 50 cells/mm2 in sCR and 39 cells/mm2 in NR (online supplemental figure 4A). The same trend was observed for intraepithelial CD4+FoxP3+ regulatory T cell infiltration (figure 2). Notably, a higher infiltration of CD4+FoxP3−PD1+TIM3− T cells, was observed in sCR compared with qCR and NR (figure 2, online supplemental figure 4A). In relation to the other subpopulations of T cells, those expressing Tbet were relatively low, and no significant differences were observed in their infiltration between sCR and qCR (online supplemental figure 4). Despite higher overall immune cell infiltration in the stroma, the composition of the T cells in this compartment did not differ between response groups (figure 2, online supplemental figure 4).

The absolute infiltration of myeloid cells in the epithelium was lower compared with the T cell infiltration. CD11c+ dendritic cells (DCs) infiltrated the epithelium of all response groups, but were not detected in the stroma (online supplemental figure 5). The epithelium of qCR was more infiltrated with CD14+ inflammatory myeloid cells relative to sCR and NR (figure 2B). No differences were observed with respect to CD68+ macrophages. Clearly, overall CD14+ and CD68+ immune cell infiltration was lower in both the epithelial and stromal compartment of sCR when compared with qCR. In addition, substantial infiltration with CD33+ immature myeloid cells was particularly observed in the epithelium of sCR and NR groups, and in the stroma of sCR (figure 2B, online supplemental figure 5).

Coordination of the immune response separates quick from slow responders

To assess differences in the coordination of immune infiltration, we compared the correlations between the numbers of the identified cell subtypes between and in each of the two compartments. The lesions of CR display a coordinated response of CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells and CD14+ expressing myeloid cells both in and between epithelial and stromal compartments, as reflected in the positive correlations between the number of infiltrated cells of these subtypes (figure 5, online supplemental figure 6). In contrast, NR expresses a negative correlation between CD4+ and CD8+ T cell infiltration and the CD14+ myeloid cells across both compartments.

When the complete responders are further divided into qCR and sCR as described above, the qCR retains the positive correlation between CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells and CD14+ inflammatory myeloid cells in the epithelial and stromal compartment (figure 5), while sCR displays a strong negative correlation between the T cell and myeloid cell compartment, in line with the overall lower influx with CD14+ myeloid cells in this group (figure 2, online supplemental figure 5).

Altogether these data suggest that the clinical response of sCR may be delayed due to insufficient mature myeloid cell presence and consequently a lack of a coordinated T cell and myeloid cell immune response.

Discussion and conclusions

An extensive in-depth analysis of the immunological composition of the vHSIL lesions from patients treated with imiquimod in the large randomized and prospective PITVIN trial,4 revealed a positive correlation between CD14+ myeloid cell, CD4+ T cell and CD8+ T cell infiltrates across epithelial and stromal compartments in complete responders to imiquimod treatment at 6 months. As vHSIL is by definition confined to the epithelium,14 the immune infiltration in epithelial and stromal compartments represents different tissue microenvironments. In line with findings from other (pre)malignant epithelial diseases, immune cell composition and counts often vary between these compartments.15 16

Our data not only align with new insights on the collaboration between T cells and myeloid cells in their effort to control malignancies,17 but also corroborate our previous retrospective study suggesting the role of these three cell subtypes in complete vHSIL clearance after imiquimod treatment and after therapeutic vaccination.10 13 This notion is also supported by data showing a positive impact of CD4+ T cells and CD14+ myeloid cells as a prognostic marker in vulvar squamous cell carcinoma.18 19

Notably, the positive correlation between T cells and CD14+ myeloid cells in this and our previous studies10 13 not only included inflammatory M1-like macrophages but also CD163+ M2 like macrophages, known to suppress local immunity17 and potentially explaining why these lesions display relatively low numbers of Tbet-expressing T cells and do not resolve spontaneously despite T cell infiltration.

CD14 is a pattern-recognition receptor that promotes type I interferon expression and favors optimal NF-kB-dependent cytokine production.20 As a co-receptor for TLR signaling to initiate intracellular signaling, CD14 plays an essential role in the pretreatment immune microenvironment.21 Given that imiquimod functions by activating TLR-7 and initiating a Th1 immune response, the observed variation across response groups may influence treatment efficacy.6

In a previous study, the fraction of CD33+ epithelial infiltration declined per response group, from being the highest in non-responders, to lowest infiltration in complete responders and healthy vulvar epithelium.10 This sustains our findings, as we discovered low infiltration of CD33+ monocytes in qCR and higher infiltration in NR. These CD33+ monocytes often exhibit immunosuppressive myeloid-derived suppressor cell (MDSC)-like features.22 23 Increased pre-treatment CD33+ infiltration with MDSC-like function could contribute to a local immunosuppressive state and persistence of vHSIL by suppressing T cell proliferation and function. Since imiquimod acts via TLR-7 activation to promote a Th1 response, a high pre-existing burden of CD33+ MDSCs may impair treatment efficacy.6 24

In vHSIL, no biomarker has yet been identified to aid clinicians and patients in treatment decision and contribute to individualization of treatment options. Recently, a biomarker, named “cervical immune biomarker for imiquimod” (CIBI), was identified to predict the response of cervical high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesions to imiquimod.25 CIBI was based on the pretreatment immune infiltrate of CD4+ T cells, CD4+FoxP3+ regulatory T cells, CD68+CD163− M1-like macrophages and CD11c+ DCs.25 Since the cell types associated with clinical response to imiquimod differ between hrHPV-induced cervical and vulvar lesions, a predictive biomarker for vHSIL treatment needs to be developed using the immune cells found here. This biomarker could assist clinicians not only in response, but also in guiding treatment duration or combination therapy, as patients achieving qCR may respond before 16 weeks, whereas sCR may benefit from prolonged imiquimod therapy. Based on the higher infiltration with CD4+PD1+ in sCR one could, in theory, co-treat this group with local PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors to crank up local T cell activity. However, since the efficacy of imiquimod in vHSIL is 80%, the potential adverse events related to checkpoint therapy may preclude its combined use in the clinic for these patients.

It is not surprising that the cell types associated with response to imiquimod differ between hrHPV-induced lesions in these two locations, with cHSIL being mucosal dysplasias, and vHSIL being cutaneous lesions, as also the composition of the tumor immune microenvironment in HPV16+ tumors at different anatomical locations is substantially different.26 Similar differences are seen with respect to immune infiltration and checkpoint blockade therapy response of cutaneous melanoma and mucosal melanoma.27 28

The findings in this study are subject to three major limitations. First, the small number of patients with multifocal lesions, even though multifocal vHSIL is described between 49% and 83% of vHSIL cases.29 30 This may have caused a bias in our findings as multifocal vHSIL has a higher risk of recurrence and progression.3 A second limitation of the study was the small group of non-responders. Although this was inevitable due to the real-world setting of the study, it negatively influenced the statistical strength of our findings. Third, there is substantial overlap in the infiltration with specific immune cells between patients in one and the other response groups. This makes it difficult to identify a simple biomarker capable of predicting response to imiquimod treatment with high sensitivity and specificity.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our findings demonstrate that both primary and recurrent vHSIL displaying a coordinated T cell and CD14+ immune infiltrate contributes to lesion regression after imiquimod therapy. Given that treatment of vHSIL involves either mutilating surgery or imiquimod therapy, our results may support the development of a biomarker to identify patients eligible for the less invasive and equally effective imiquimod treatment. Additionally, this biomarker may help distinguish between patients who would benefit from prolonged therapy and those for whom early discontinuation is appropriate, enabling a more tailored and optimized treatment approach.

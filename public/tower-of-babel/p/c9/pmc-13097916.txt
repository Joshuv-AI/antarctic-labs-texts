Introduction

Robotic-assisted surgery represents one of the most significant technological advancements in modern cardiothoracic surgery. Over the past two decades, robotic platforms have expanded the scope of minimally invasive cardiac and thoracic procedures by providing enhanced dexterity, three-dimensional visualization, tremor filtration, and improved surgeon ergonomics [1–5]. These advantages are particularly valuable in cardiothoracic surgery, where operative precision within confined anatomical spaces is essential and directly influences procedural safety and patient outcomes [6, 7].

The adoption of robotic systems has enabled increasingly complex procedures such as coronary artery bypass grafting, mitral valve repair, and pulmonary lobectomy to be performed using minimally invasive approaches [8–13]. Compared with conventional open or laparoscopic techniques, robotic-assisted surgery has been associated with reduced postoperative pain, shorter hospital stays, faster recovery, and improved functional outcomes, reinforcing its growing role in contemporary surgical practice [1, 14–21]. As a result, robotic cardiothoracic surgery has evolved from an experimental innovation to an established component of care in selected centers worldwide [22, 23].

This expansion mirrors broader trends in healthcare toward precision medicine, personalized treatment strategies, and technology-driven clinical decision-making [24–27]. The global burden of cardiovascular and thoracic diseases continues to rise, further accelerating interest in surgical approaches that maximize clinical effectiveness while minimizing invasiveness and resource utilization [28–30]. Alongside these clinical drivers, rapid advancements in robotic technology, imaging, and data analytics have contributed to a steady increase in scientific output related to robotic-assisted cardiothoracic surgery.

However, the growth of this literature has been heterogeneous, spanning diverse procedures, technologies, and clinical objectives. Early research primarily focused on feasibility and safety, while more recent studies emphasize oncologic outcomes, learning curves, procedural standardization, and the integration of emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence [31–35]. This diversification has produced a large and complex body of research, making it increasingly difficult to identify overarching trends, influential contributors, and evolving research priorities.

Bibliometric analysis offers a systematic and quantitative approach to addressing this challenge. Unlike traditional narrative reviews or meta-analyses, bibliometric methods enable the mapping of scientific knowledge at a macro level by analyzing publication trends, citation networks, keyword evolution, and collaborative structures across countries, institutions, and authors [36–38]. Such analyses are particularly valuable in rapidly evolving, technology-driven fields, where understanding the structure and trajectory of research activity can inform future investigation, training strategies, and policy decisions [39, 40].

Although prior reviews have examined specific robotic cardiothoracic procedures or clinical outcomes, a comprehensive bibliometric evaluation integrating both cardiac and thoracic robotic surgery has been lacking. Consequently, the intellectual landscape of robotic-assisted cardiothoracic surgery, its developmental phases, dominant themes, and emerging directions remains insufficiently characterized. Therefore, the aim of this study is to conduct a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of the global literature on robotic-assisted cardiothoracic surgery. By examining publication trends, citation impact, geographic and institutional contributions, authorship networks, and thematic evolution, this study seeks to clarify how the field has developed, why current research focuses have emerged, and where future efforts may be most impactful.

Methods

Data Collection

Data on published documents were retrieved on November 25, 2024, from the Web of Science Core Collection, a database recognized for its comprehensive and reliable collection of over 12,000 articles [41–43] (see Supplementary Table S1 for the complete WoS search strategy). The search strategy was developed collaboratively with a subject matter expert and a librarian experienced in systematic reviews and bibliometric studies. To enhance search efficacy, a range of terms was incorporated into the search strategy, including “thoracic surgical procedures,” “thoracic surgery,” “heart surgical procedure,” “cardiac surgical procedure,” “cardiothoracic surgery,” “cardiac surgery,” “lung surgery,” “esophageal surgery,” “chest surgery,” as well as terms related to robotics, such as “robots,” “robotic systems,” “robotic technology,” and “remote operations (robotics).” The detailed search formula, including the use of Boolean operators to connect these keywords, is provided in Table S1.

Eligibility criteria and study selection

The eligibility criteria for inclusion were defined as follows: peer-reviewed articles written in English and published in journals indexed by the Web of Science Core Collection. The focus was limited to original research articles and reviews related to robotic-assisted cardiothoracic surgery. To maintain dataset quality, articles with irrelevant study designs were excluded, including conference proceedings, editorials, book chapters, letters, early access articles, meeting abstracts, preprints, news items, and retracted papers. Non-English publications were also excluded to ensure consistency, as the bibliometric tools used in this study are optimized for English text.

Original research and review articles were selected for their rigorous peer-review process, which supports the dataset’s scientific reliability. Other types of literature, including conference proceedings, editorials, and books, were excluded as they generally lack comparable peer review and indexing, which could disrupt citation trends. Retracted papers were also removed to preserve data integrity. Non-English publications were excluded, as the bibliometric software used is optimized for English text, ensuring consistent analysis across articles.

The study selection process was conducted by two independent researchers. Articles were screened in three stages: title screening, abstract screening, and full-text screening. Any discrepancies between the two researchers during the selection process were resolved by a third reviewer to ensure consensus and maintain the reliability of the selection process. During the title screening stage, articles with clearly irrelevant topics were excluded. Abstracts of the remaining articles were then reviewed to assess their relevance to the research question. Finally, the full text of potentially relevant articles was evaluated. No automation tools were used during this process.

A total of 506 records were initially retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection database based on the defined search strategy. In the first phase of screening, 87 records were excluded for not meeting basic eligibility criteria. These excluded items included 12 book chapters, 27 proceedings, 11 editorials, 3 letters, 3 early access articles, 3 meeting abstracts, 2 preprints, 1 news item, 1 retracted article, and 24 non-English papers. After this initial filtering, 419 records remained and were subjected to title and abstract screening.

In the second screening stage, 80 articles were excluded for being irrelevant to the study’s focus on robotic-assisted cardiothoracic surgery. These exclusions were based on the following reasons: 1: several studies investigated robotic applications in non-cardiothoracic specialties, such as urology (e.g., robotic prostatectomy), gynecology (e.g., robotic hysterectomy), and general surgery (e.g., robotic colorectal resections) (n = 38); 2: some records focused on robotic assistance in neurosurgery or orthopedic interventions, which fell outside the cardiothoracic domain (n = 29); 3: a number of articles dealt with robotic support systems in intensive care units, nursing, or rehabilitation settings rather than surgical procedures (n = 6); and 4: several engineering-oriented studies discussed robotic hardware design, sensor development, or artificial intelligence algorithms without clinical implementation or relevance to thoracic or cardiac surgery (n = 7). After applying all screening criteria, a total of 339 peer-reviewed articles were included in the final bibliometric analysis (Fig. 1).

Data Analysis

We utilized Biblioshiny, VOSviewer, and CiteSpace to evaluate the documents retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection. The data was subsequently transformed into CSV and plain text formats for analysis.

VOSviewer, developed by the Center for Science and Technology Studies at Leiden University, Netherlands, is a powerful tool for scientometric network analysis. It excels in generating maps from network data and providing visually appealing graphics for academic publications. VOSviewer is particularly effective in creating network diagrams that illustrate co-citation, co-occurrence, citation, and bibliographic coupling among publications, journals, authors, research institutions, countries, and keywords [44–46].

CiteSpace was another critical tool used in this study. This Java-based application combines scientometric methods with data visualization to analyze citation patterns. It provides researchers with insights into the dynamics, structure, and distribution of scientific knowledge by generating detailed maps through advanced data mining algorithms and comprehensive information analysis. CiteSpace supports the identification of key studies, significant advancements, and emerging areas of interest in academic research through visual representations of citation networks. CiteSpace also provides betweenness centrality, which measures how often a node (such as a country, institution, or author) lies on the shortest path between other nodes in the network. This metric identifies elements that act as bridges or connectors across research clusters, indicating their structural importance within the scientific collaboration network. In addition, CiteSpace is highly effective in performing cluster analysis, which organizes related studies based on patterns of co-citation to uncover links between various research domains. It uses log-likelihood ratio (LLR) tests to assign informative labels to each cluster, drawing from significant terms found in the clustered articles. The quality of clustering is evaluated using modularity and silhouette scores: modularity measures the clarity of the network’s internal divisions—with values closer to 1 indicating well-separated, loosely linked sub-networks—while silhouette scores reflect how consistent and coherent the groupings are, with higher scores indicating better-defined clusters. Additionally, CiteSpace employs time-slicing to examine how key concepts evolve over designated time intervals. Its burst detection algorithm, grounded in Kleinberg’s approach, pinpoints sudden spikes in citation frequency, helping to identify emerging trends or notable research developments [47].

We also employed Biblioshiny, a web application interface for the Bibliometrix R package, to simplify bibliometric analysis. Biblioshiny facilitates detailed analyses, including network analysis, descriptive statistical evaluations, and bibliometric network visualization, in a user-friendly format. Biblioshiny also utilizes several community detection algorithms—such as Louvain, Walktrap, and Multidimensional Scaling (MDS)—to uncover clusters within networks. These algorithms help highlight related research areas and collaborative relationships by identifying patterns and structures in the data [48, 49].

Discussion

This bibliometric analysis provides an overview of how robotic-assisted cardiothoracic surgery research has evolved over time, reflecting the field’s transition from early feasibility studies to more specialized, outcome-driven, and technology-integrated investigations. The analysis of publication trends in robotic-assisted cardiothoracic surgery from 1998 to 2024 revealed a consistent upward trajectory in research activity. Despite some fluctuations in annual publication numbers, the overall trend demonstrates sustained growth, with the number of publications peaking in 2023 at 29 articles. By 2024, the cumulative number had reached 339, reflecting an expanding scholarly interest in this field. While variations in publication counts may be influenced by factors such as funding changes, delays, or shifts in research focus (e.g., the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic), the overall upward trend signals an enduring commitment to advancing robotic-assisted cardiothoracic surgery [53–56].

Our study revealed substantial international collaboration, which has been crucial in advancing the field. Cooperation across institutions has facilitated the exchange of knowledge, resources, and expertise, accelerating progress. These partnerships have enabled large-scale clinical trials and studies bridging multiple disciplines, driving significant advancements in the field [57–61].

The citation analysis identified the most cited study as the one by J. Bodner et al. This study highlighted promising experiences with robotic surgery, demonstrating its capability to perform complex procedures such as thymectomy, fundoplication, and esophageal dissection with high precision and safety [62]. Most patients recovered without significant complications, with discharge occurring within 3 to 8 days. However, challenges, such as the lack of specialized instruments for lung surgeries, hindered more complex operations. Despite these challenges, the study concluded that robotic-assisted thoracic surgeries are feasible and effective, though advancements in instrumentation are needed to improve outcomes in more demanding cases.

The second most cited study, by Hao-Xian et al., provided valuable insights into various surgical procedures, including robotic surgery [63]. It compared long-term survival and disease-free survival outcomes across three approaches: robotic surgery, video-assisted thoracic surgery (VATS), and thoracotomy in patients with stage I lung cancer. No significant differences in overall survival were observed, but the robotic approach involved sampling more lymph nodes, potentially enhancing cancer staging accuracy. Additionally, robotic and VATS techniques resulted in shorter hospital stays and fewer postoperative complications.

The third most cited study by Louie et al. focused on early robotic lung resections versus established VATS cases [64]. It found comparable clinical outcomes between the two techniques, but patients undergoing robotic surgery experienced less postoperative pain and faster recovery. The study suggested that while robotic surgery holds potential benefits, further research is needed to clarify its advantages over VATS.

Keyword analysis revealed that in robotic cardiothoracic surgery, frequently used terms such as “experience” and “outcomes” dominate the literature. The term “experience” underscores the importance of sharing clinical knowledge gained from robotic surgeries [65–67], while “outcomes” highlights the focus on evaluating the effectiveness and success rates of various surgical approaches [68, 69]. Additionally, the frequent use of keywords like “lobectomy” and “resection” underscores their central importance.

“Lobectomy,” which refers to the removal of a lung lobe, is primarily associated with lung cancer treatment, while “resection” denotes the excision of diseased tissue, often tumors. These terms emphasize the ongoing refinement of robotic techniques for minimally invasive cancer surgeries, aiming to improve precision, reduce recovery time, and minimize incision size [70–73].

Since 2019, there has been an increasing focus on sharing experiences in robotic cardiothoracic surgery. These experiences emphasize the outcomes of procedures such as lobectomies, resections, and cancer treatments. The growing use of terms like “experiences” reflects the importance of documenting procedural insights, surgical effectiveness, and patient outcomes. This shift towards transparency and data-driven approaches aims to enhance surgical techniques and patient care. For instance, Soliman and colleagues demonstrated significant improvements in surgical outcomes using the da Vinci Xi robot, based on their extensive experience with robotic-assisted lobectomy [74]. The introduction of the robotic platform significantly reduced post-operative complications, hospital stays, and readmission rates compared to traditional VATS procedures. The need for conversion to open surgery during lobectomy was also greatly reduced, demonstrating the system’s precision. As Soliman’s team gained experience with the Xi system, they observed fewer complications and improved efficiency, particularly in oncological cases, with more lymph nodes retrieved. Soliman emphasized that proficiency with the robotic system consistently improved outcomes, underscoring its growing importance in complex thoracic surgeries [74]. Additionally, in the study by Jiao and colleagues, robotic bronchial sleeve lobectomy for central lung tumors was evaluated, focusing on surgeons’ experience with the da Vinci robotic system [75]. The results showed high complete resection rates, no conversions to thoracotomy, reduced surgical duration, minimal blood loss, and a low morbidity rate of 20.9%. As surgeon experience increased, operative time decreased, and complication rates dropped from 33.3% to 10.8%, highlighting the importance of surgical experience in improving outcomes and reducing complications in complex robotic procedures [75]. These studies highlighted the importance of sharing experiences in robotic cardiothoracic surgery, showing that accumulated expertise leads to significantly improved surgical outcomes.

Overlay analysis revealed a shift in robotic cardiothoracic surgery research, with an increasing focus on keywords such as “lung,” “lobectomy,” “cancer,” and “outcomes.” This shift reflects a growing interest in refining surgical techniques and improving patient outcomes, particularly in the field of oncology [76–78]. Several studies have demonstrated the application of robotic surgery in lobectomy. For instance, the study by Asbjornsson et al. explored the transition from thoracotomy to VATS lobectomy for non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) in a low-volume center [79]. They highlighted the adoption of minimally invasive techniques like VATS, which showed positive impacts on patient outcomes. Similarly, the VIOLET study underscored the shift toward minimally invasive approaches, including VATS and robotic surgery, for lung cancer lobectomy. This study reported improved outcomes, such as shorter hospital stays and better physical function, compared to open surgery [80].

The bibliometric analysis highlighted the evolving clinical themes within the field of robotic surgery. Initially, the focus was on beating-heart surgery, controlled catheter systems, and esophageal surgery, reflecting the early advancements and exploration in minimally invasive procedures. Over time, the field shifted its emphasis toward robotic lobectomy, which has emerged as a dominant research and clinical focus, indicating its growing adoption in thoracic surgery.

Integrating the clusters identified in our bibliometric analysis with their clinical implications revealed significant advancements in robotic-assisted cardiothoracic surgery. For example, Robotic mitral valve repair (Cluste #8) offers numerous advantages over traditional surgical methods. As a minimally invasive technique, it leads to decreased postoperative ventilation requirements, shorter intensive care unit stays, and faster recovery times compared to conventional sternotomy. These benefits significantly enhance patient outcomes and satisfaction [81, 82]. In addition to mitral valve repair, robotic-assisted valve surgery has demonstrated efficacy in other valvular pathologies. For instance, a recent study by Jagadeesan et al. highlighted the advantages of robotic-assisted aortic valve replacement (RAVR) over transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR). RAVR was associated with lower rates of pacemaker and vascular complications, reduced 1-year paravalvular leak, and decreased mortality [83].

Robotic esophageal surgery (Cluste #7) offers several advantages over conventional surgical approaches, providing improved outcomes and significant benefits to patients. Robotic platforms enhance precision and visualization during procedures, offering three-dimensional imaging and articulated instruments that improve accuracy in challenging areas such as the esophagus. This precision enables meticulous lymph node dissection and safer tumor resections, which are particularly critical in oncological cases [84, 85].

The integration of robotics in the treatment of congenital heart diseases (cluster #4) marks a revolutionary step in pediatric and adult cardiac surgery [86]. Robotic systems, such as the da Vinci surgical platform, have enabled minimally invasive techniques that offer significant benefits over traditional open-heart surgery. These systems provide enhanced precision, stability, and control through 3D imaging and articulated instruments, which mimic the dexterity of the human hand in challenging and confined surgical environments. As a result, robotic approaches are increasingly utilized in repairing atrial and ventricular septal defects, mitral valve abnormalities, and other congenital heart conditions, particularly in cases where traditional methods are deemed more invasive [87, 88].

Robotic lobectomy (cluster #0) is an advanced minimally invasive surgical approach for the treatment of lung cancer, particularly NSCLC. It offers several advantages over traditional open surgery and VATS [89, 90]. Utilizing robotic platforms such as the da Vinci system, this technique provides surgeons with enhanced dexterity, precision, and three-dimensional visualization, enabling more complex and accurate surgical procedures [5, 91–93].

The identification of “Robotic Lobectomy” as the most prominent cluster reflects the growing adoption of robotic approaches in thoracic oncology [94–96]. While numerous feasibility studies and retrospective case series have demonstrated promising perioperative outcomes, there remains a critical need for large-scale prospective randomized trials comparing robotic lobectomy with VATS. These trials should assess not only oncologic outcomes such as margin status and lymph node retrieval, but also patient-reported quality of life and cost-effectiveness, which are underrepresented in the current literature. Additionally, standardized procedural protocols and training pathways are needed to support widespread implementation across centers.

The emergence of AI as a distinct thematic cluster underscores the increasing integration of intelligent algorithms into robotic-assisted surgery [35, 97]. However, the majority of current publications focus on model development and retrospective analysis. There is an unmet need for external validation of these AI tools in prospective clinical settings, along with rigorous assessment of their impact on surgical outcomes, workflow efficiency, and decision-making. Furthermore, the regulatory pathways—including FDA clearance or CE marking—for AI applications in robotic surgery remain undefined and require attention from both researchers and policymakers.

Articles in the “Beating-Heart Surgery” cluster primarily represent single-center experiences and small retrospective cohorts [98–100]. While off-pump robotic CABG is technically feasible and potentially beneficial in selected patients, its adoption remains limited due to a lack of robust comparative data. There is a pressing need for multicenter registries, prospective cohorts, or ideally, randomized controlled trials that compare robotic off-pump CABG with conventional off-pump or on-pump approaches to determine reproducibility, safety, and long-term outcomes.

One of the primary benefits of robotic lobectomy is its minimally invasive nature, which leads to reduced postoperative pain, shorter hospital stays, and faster recovery times compared to open lobectomy. Studies show that robotic lobectomy patients experience fewer complications, such as prolonged air leaks and pneumonia, and lower rates of conversion to open surgery compared to VATS and open approaches [101, 102].

The integration of AI into robotic-assisted surgeries is poised to revolutionize clinical practice by enhancing decision-making, precision, and personalized care [103, 104]. AI algorithms can assist in preoperative planning and intraoperative navigation, leading to more accurate and efficient procedures [105, 106]. Clinically, this could result in improved surgical outcomes, reduced operative times, and lower complication rates.

Although robotic-assisted techniques offer clear technical advantages, their adoption across cardiothoracic subspecialties remains uneven, a pattern that is also reflected in the bibliometric findings of this study. While thoracic applications have become well established, cardiac and congenital procedures continue to represent a smaller and more specialized portion of the literature. This disparity likely reflects several practical and clinical limitations, including high procedural costs, limited availability of specialized training, and the need for highly coordinated multidisciplinary teams [3, 107, 108]. In cardiac surgery specifically, the absence of large randomized trials, concerns regarding myocardial protection and cardiopulmonary bypass integration, and the continued effectiveness of established minimally invasive alternatives have constrained widespread adoption. From a bibliometric perspective, the relatively lower volume and centrality of cardiac robotic publications suggest that research activity mirrors real-world clinical selectivity, where robotic approaches are applied in carefully chosen cases rather than as routine practice. These findings underscore that growth in publication volume should not be interpreted solely as clinical superiority, but rather as an indicator of evolving interest, feasibility, and contextual suitability within different domains of cardiothoracic surgery.

Limitations and future suggestions

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, it relied exclusively on data from the Web of Science Core Collection (Science Citation Index Expanded and Emerging Sources Citation Index), which, while reputable, may not fully capture the breadth of global research on robotic-assisted cardiothoracic surgery. Many relevant studies published in Scopus, PubMed, or Embase, including those in regional or emerging journals—particularly from East Asia, Latin America, or open-access platforms—may have been excluded. This could lead to the underrepresentation of contributions from certain regions or institutions, and may affect the completeness of the international collaboration and productivity analyses. Future bibliometric investigations should consider integrating multiple databases to ensure a more comprehensive and inclusive assessment.

Second, we included only English-language publications, largely due to limitations in bibliometric software and the requirement for standardized keyword mapping. As a result, 24 non-English studies were excluded, which may have led to the omission of important contributions from major robotic surgery centers, particularly in countries like Japan, South Korea, and China, where early-stage research may be published in native-language journals. This exclusion introduces language bias and may distort global representation. Future studies should explore multilingual approaches or utilize translation tools to reduce this gap.

Third, bibliometric tools like VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and Biblioshiny rely heavily on citation-based metrics, which naturally tend to highlight older or widely known publications. While this can help identify influential work, it may also cause newer, innovative, or underrecognized studies to be overlooked simply because they haven’t yet had time to accumulate citations. Moreover, authors and institutions with established reputations may benefit from what’s known as “prestige bias”—where their work is cited more frequently not necessarily because of superior quality, but because of their visibility or name recognition. This can unintentionally overshadow valuable contributions from emerging researchers or smaller institutions. Other citation-related factors—like self-citations, citation networks among collaborators, or the influence of journal impact factors—can also skew the way influence and importance are perceived in the analysis. In addition to citation-related and database-related limitations, it is important to acknowledge that a substantial portion of highly cited literature in robotic-assisted cardiothoracic surgery originates from early adopters and pioneering centers with strong expertise and institutional commitment to robotic platforms. Studies introducing new technologies or novel surgical applications are inherently susceptible to enthusiasm bias, selection bias, and limited external validity, as patient populations are often highly selected and outcomes reflect specialized settings rather than routine practice. Consequently, bibliometric prominence may be influenced not only by scientific merit, but also by novelty, institutional visibility, and author advocacy. This dynamic may further amplify citation counts for early innovation-focused studies while underrepresenting neutral, negative, or pragmatic assessments of robotic-assisted surgery. Recognizing this bias is essential when interpreting bibliometric influence, particularly in areas such as cardiac and congenital surgery where adoption remains selective and evidence from large randomized or multicenter studies is limited.

Fourth, bibliometric analyses are not designed to assess the clinical quality, methodological rigor, or patient-centered outcomes of individual studies. Instead, they provide a macro-level overview of research trends, thematic evolution, and knowledge structure. As such, bibliometric findings should ideally be interpreted alongside systematic reviews or meta-analyses to provide a more comprehensive evaluation of clinical effectiveness and outcomes.

Finally, given the rapid pace of innovation in this field, recent technological developments—such as the integration of artificial intelligence, augmented reality, or modular robotic systems—may not yet be fully captured due to publication and indexing delays. Regular bibliometric updates and longitudinal trend tracking will be essential to monitor the ongoing evolution of robotic cardiothoracic surgery.

As robotic-assisted surgery continues to evolve, this study highlights several promising avenues for future research and clinical advancements in cardiothoracic surgery and beyond. A key area is the integration of AI, which could transform robotic surgery by offering real-time analytics, enhanced imaging, and predictive modeling to improve accuracy and efficiency [103, 109]. Developing AI algorithms to assist with intraoperative decision-making, refine surgical techniques via machine learning, and tailor approaches based on patient-specific data should be prioritized [110–112]. Additionally, expanding research on AI’s role in surgical training could also standardize skills and assess performance, ensuring consistency in complex procedures. Further investigation is needed into robotic surgery’s adaptability across various surgical specialties, including general, orthopedic, urologic, and neurosurgical fields. Research should focus on creating modular robotic platforms suited to diverse anatomical and procedural needs [113–117]. Standardizing protocols to optimize outcomes will also be crucial for broader adoption [118, 119]. Moreover, addressing the high costs of robotic systems is another priority. Research into cost-reduction strategies, such as modular designs and regional training hubs, could improve accessibility, especially in low-resource settings [120, 121]. Finally, Longitudinal studies are essential to assess long-term outcomes of robotic surgery, including quality of life, recurrence rates, and survival, alongside research on human-robot interaction and AI-driven training to enhance effectiveness and accessibility.

In clinical practice, the implementation of advanced robotics and AI has the potential to address several ongoing challenges. One critical area for future investigation is the reduction of healthcare costs associated with robotic-assisted surgery, as these technologies currently require substantial investment in both equipment and training [122, 123]. Studies should evaluate cost-effective strategies, including regional robotic training centers and streamlined maintenance protocols, which could make robotic systems more accessible to diverse healthcare facilities. Furthermore, a greater emphasis on developing portable and flexible robotic platforms may support wider use in low-resource settings, allowing more patients to benefit from minimally invasive surgical techniques globally.

Emerging technologies such as augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR) are also likely to impact the field of robotic-assisted surgery. Future research could examine how AR and VR might be integrated with robotic systems to provide surgeons with enhanced visualization and real-time guidance, particularly for complex anatomical regions [124, 125]. Such innovations could enhance surgical precision and further reduce recovery times by minimizing tissue disruption.

Conclusion

This bibliometric study highlights the significant and growing interest in robotic-assisted cardiothoracic surgery, revealing a trend towards increasingly complex and specialized procedures, particularly in oncology. The findings indicate a strong focus on enhancing surgical precision and improving patient outcomes through minimally invasive techniques, such as robotic lobectomies for lung cancer. Additionally, keyword and cluster analyses demonstrate key research themes like robotic lobectomy, recent trends, beating-heart surgery, and AI, reflecting the field’s evolving nature and its integration of advanced technologies to refine surgical practices. This bibliometric study, while not directly evaluating clinical outcomes, offers practical insights into robotic-assisted cardiothoracic surgery. By mapping the intellectual landscape, it identifies research gaps and emphasizes the need for future studies on standardizing procedures, assessing long-term outcomes, and addressing challenges such as training and accessibility. In particular, multicenter prospective trials are essential to validate clinical effectiveness and generalizability, and the increasing role of AI underscores the need for robust regulatory evaluation and external validation before widespread clinical adoption. These efforts are crucial to ensuring that technological advancements translate into broader clinical and systemic benefits.

Extensive multifocal extranodal diffuse large B-cell lymphoma involving parotid, breast, gastrointestinal tract, and dorsal spine: a case report
Authors: Syed Hameed-Ul-Hassan Shah, Syed Ibraheem Ul Hassan Ramzi, Hooria Ahmad Qureshi, Jeevan Gyawali, Waseem Sattar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Introduction:Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is the most common type of non-Hodgkin lymphoma, with extranodal involvement in up to 40% of cases. However, simultaneous involvement of multiple extranodal sites without dominant nodal disease remains very rare and diagnostically challenging.Case presentation:We report a case of a 61-year-old female with a history of hypertension and recurrent anemia, who presented with progressive swelling in the left breast and parotid area, and systemic symptoms. Evaluation with imaging and biopsy confirmed multifocal extranodal DLBCL involving the bilateral parotid glands, left breast, stomach, and thoracic spine. Immunohistochemistry showed CD20 and BCL2 positivity and a high Ki-67 index (70%–80%), which was suggestive of an aggressive non-germinal center B-cell phenotype. The patient was given R-CHOP chemotherapy and intrathecal methotrexate with a favorable early response.Discussion:This case illustrates the difficulty in diagnosing multifocal extranodal lymphoma in the context of the absence of dominant nodal disease. Extranodal DLBCLs can mimic primary organ cancers and lead to misdiagnosis or delayed therapy. With the poor prognosis of widespread extranodal disease and potential molecular high-risk features, multidisciplinary management and diligent long-term surveillance are essential. Limited access to molecular analyses (e.g. MYC/BCL2/BCL6) in resource-limited settings remains a significant challenge.Conclusion:Multifocal extranodal DLBCL with parotid gland, breast, gastrointestinal tract, and spine involvement is extremely rare and requires a very high index of suspicion. Image-guided biopsy and immunophenotyping at an early stage are crucial to avoid misdiagnosis and guide appropriate therapy. Individualized and risk-adapted approaches are crucial in maximizing the outcome in such aggressive and rare presentations.

Introduction

Among non-Hodgkin lymphomas, diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is the most widespread kind. Extranodal DLBCL, defined as illness outside of the lymph nodes and lymphoid tissue, can occur in up to one-third of individuals[1]. Extranodal DLBCLs can arise as primary events or in more advanced stages of nodal illness. The location of these aggressive tumors and their high relapse rates present substantial clinical challenges[2]. Despite the relative prevalence of extranodal presentations, there is little and frequently conflicting information available about their occurrence, as well as the majority of the distinct types and sites. This is mainly because these tumors, which are numerous when considered collectively, are so extensively dispersed throughout the body that assembling a sufficient series at any particular site is difficult[3].

Although extranodal illness can affect almost every organ system, it most frequently manifests in the gastrointestinal tract (GI), breast, thyroid, skin, and testes[1]. In contrast, the involvement of the parotid and breast is extremely rare. Only 2%–5% of salivary gland tumors and 5% of extranodal lymphomas detected in the parotid gland are primary malignant lymphomas of the salivary gland[4], while primary breast lymphoma accounts for only 0.5% of breast malignancies and 2% of non-Hodgkin lymphoma[5]. Therefore, it is very uncommon for DLBCL to arise in both the breast and parotid gland at the same time. Such presentations could make diagnosis extremely difficult since clinical and radiographic characteristics cannot differentiate lymphoma from more prevalent benign and malignant tumors, as clinicians rarely suspect lymphoma as the initial diagnosis for a parotid mass[6]. Similarly, the clinical and imaging findings of breast lymphoma can mimic those of breast carcinoma[7]. These features contribute to the delay in diagnosis or misdiagnosis.

We present a case of extranodal DLBCL presenting with extensive organ involvement, including the parotid gland, breast, stomach, and spine.

Case presentation

We present a 61-year-old female with a history of hypertension and recurrent anemia, who presented to the medicine department with a gradually enlarging painless lump in the parotid area over the past year. Recently, she developed a palpable mass in the left breast with associated symptoms including fatigue, generalized musculoskeletal pain, dizziness, dysphagia, epigastric discomfort, along with unintentional weight loss and decreased appetite.On physical examination, there was bilateral post-auricular swelling, a palpable left breast lump, and suspicious bilateral cervical and axillary lymphadenopathy. The breast mass was hard with no nipple retraction but mild overlying skin thickening of the breast.

Multiple enlarged lymph nodes were noted along the bilateral upper carotid jugular chain, the largest on the left side measuring 1.3 cm and on the right side measuring 1.2 cm. Immunohistochemical and morphological characteristics support the diagnosis of DLBCL. Biopsy and imaging were consistent with a stage IV high-grade non-germinal center B-cell lymphoma with extensive extranodal involvement.

Following diagnosis, the patient was started on R-CHOP chemotherapy. Rituximab 600 mg was administered in 600 ml of normal saline over 4 hours, and doxorubicin 80 mg was diluted in 100 ml of normal saline and infused over 30 minutes. Vincristine 2 mg was given in 100 ml of normal saline over 30 minutes, and cyclophosphamide (endoxan) 1300 mg was administered in 500 ml of normal saline. The patient also received 12 mg intrathecal methotrexate for Central Nervous System (CNS) prophylaxis due to extensive extranodal involvement. She tolerated the first three cycles of dose-adjusted R-CHOP treatment well, with radiographic clearance of disease regions and observable clinical improvement. She completed a total of six cycles. After the third cycle, the patient received a 450 ml blood transfusion and subsequently underwent seven additional transfusions of 450 ml each until the completion of chemotherapy. A CT scan of the chest, neck, and abdomen was performed following three cycles. There was no discrete evidence of cervical lymphadenopathy on the CT neck, and neither extra-thoracic nor axillary lymphadenopathy was seen on the CT chest.

The patient remains on active surveillance after achieving a response with first-line chemotherapy, with close monitoring of labs and physical examination. Regular monitoring is required to check for cardiac complications and secondary cancers as a result of anthracycline-based therapy.

Discussion

The multifocal extranodal pattern in this patient posed unique diagnostic and therapeutic challenges. Concurrent involvement of four non-contiguous organs, the breast, parotid gland, stomach, and dorsal spine, without dominant nodal disease, represents one of the rarest clinical forms of DLBCL, occurring in <1% of patients[8]. Such presentations obscure conventional clinical boundaries, as each lesion mimicked far more common organ-specific pathologies, delaying diagnosis until tissue confirmation and immunohistochemistry were obtained.

At presentation, the differential diagnoses were extensive. The breast mass could reasonably be mistaken for carcinoma, fibroadenoma, or even metastatic disease[9,10]. Similarly, the parotid lesion raised strong suspicion for pleomorphic adenoma, mucoepidermoid carcinoma, or Warthin’s tumor, which are far more common than lymphoma at this site[11,12]. Gastric involvement suggested adenocarcinoma or gastrointestinal stromal tumor[13], while the destructive dorsal vertebral lesion more typically fits metastatic carcinoma, myeloma, or infectious spondylitis[14,15]. In our setting, where tuberculosis (TB) remains prevalent, disseminated TB is often the leading clinical suspicion in cases with constitutional symptoms and multifocal organ disease, further complicating early recognition. This diagnostic overlap illustrates the importance of considering lymphoma in rapidly progressive or atypical lesions, even in organs where it is rarely encountered.

Breast and parotid involvement remain particularly rare in DLBCL, and their coexistence in the same patient is seen less often. Breast lymphomas account for <2% of extranodal lymphomas and only 0.5% of breast malignancies[9,10], while parotid lymphomas represent 2%–5% of salivary gland tumors and are more often MALT-type than DLBCL, implying more aggressive biology in this case[11,12]. Spinal involvement further complicated the diagnosis and added another layer of complexity, as primary vertebral lymphoma is distinctly uncommon. Isolated dorsal lesions are usually attributed to metastasis, myeloma, or infection, with lymphoma rarely considered unless biopsy is obtained[14,15]. In our case, the dorsal spine lesion confirmed the multifocality of disease and advanced Ann Arbor stage IV status, underscoring the need to keep lymphoma in the differential of destructive spinal masses when systemic features are present.Reports of multifocal extranodal DLBCL remain scarce. A recent case by Kim et al[16] described extensive organ involvement without dominant nodal disease, resembling our constellation of sites. Li et al[17] reported a patient with seven extranodal sites and CNS progression despite R-CHOP, highlighting the aggressive disease biology of such presentations. Likewise, a Hellenic Cooperative Oncology Group study found multifocal extranodal disease strongly correlated with high IPI risk and poor outcomes[18]. These studies place our case among the most exceptional presentations of DLBCL documented, while reinforcing the need for early recognition and comprehensive workup.

Radiologically, our patient exhibited a solid breast mass with skin thickening and an enhancing parotid lesion, both initially suggestive of epithelial malignancies. Histology and immunophenotyping confirmed non-germinal center subtype, which is associated with inferior prognosis compared with germinal center DLBCL[19]. Recurrent anemia prompted endoscopic evaluation, which revealed gastric polyps infiltrated by DLBCL. Anemia has been reported in up to 50% of patients with advanced DLBCL and often correlates with poorer outcomes[20]. Together, these findings indicated aggressive systemic disease. Treatment was initiated with R-CHOP chemotherapy, which is the standard first-line treatment.

Given the involvement of four extranodal sites, our case would traditionally be categorized as high risk according to both the Ann Arbor staging system and the International Prognostic Index. Despite these unfavorable prognostic markers, the patient demonstrated an excellent response to R-CHOP, underscoring the shortcomings of existing staging systems in accurately predicting outcomes for multifocal extranodal DLBCL[21]. Intrathecal methotrexate was added for CNS prophylaxis due to extensive extranodal involvement. Recent data, however, question whether intrathecal prophylaxis is adequate for vertebral disease given poor drug penetration across the bone–CNS interface[22].

An important limitation in our case was the unavailability of molecular profiling for MYC, BCL2, and BCL6 rearrangements. This gap has significant implications: double-hit and triple-expressor lymphomas are known to carry substantially worse outcomes with standard R-CHOP[23,24]. Failure to identify such subtypes risks undertreatment, especially in resource-limited settings where intensified regimens or novel agents may not be considered without confirmatory testing. This highlights the urgent need to expand access to molecular diagnostics, as treatment decisions increasingly rely on such data to tailor risk-adapted therapy.

Despite adverse features multifocal extranodal disease, non-germinal subtype, anemia, and potential undetected double-hit status our patient responded well to R-CHOP and entered remission after three cycles. Nonetheless, the prognosis in such cases remains guarded, as heavy extranodal burden and molecular risk factors strongly predict relapse[25]. Vigilant long-term surveillance and multidisciplinary care are essential.

Conclusion

In summary, this case highlights a rare, deceptively presenting multifocal extranodal DLBCL involving breast, parotid, stomach, and dorsal spine that can mimic site-specific malignancy or infection. Early image-guided biopsy with immunohistochemistry is essential in atypical or rapidly progressive masses. Where available, molecular profiling (MYC/BCL2/BCL6) should guide risk-adapted therapy; absence of this testing risks undertreatment and hence poorer outcomes. Given the heavy extranodal burden and relapse risk, patients require close follow-up and multidisciplinary care; improving access to molecular diagnostics in resource-limited settings is a priority.

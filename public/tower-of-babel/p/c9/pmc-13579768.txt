Retrospective study of treatment effectiveness and remission predictors of obinutuzumab in primary membranous nephropathy
Authors: Zhenbin Jiang, Sijia Zhou, Wenhan Bao, Song Wang, Yue Wang, Danxia Zheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRituximab is recommended as the first-line treatment for moderate- to high-risk primary membranous nephropathy (PMN). Obinutuzumab, a highly humanized anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody, has been shown to exhibit enhanced efficacy in laboratory studies. This study aimed to investigate the efficacy, side effects, and potential remission predictors of obinutuzumab in PMN treatment.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study included PMN patients who presented with nephrotic syndrome and who were treated with obinutuzumab (1 g on day 1 and day 28 in the majority of patients) between March 2022 and December 2023. Logistic regression, Kaplan–Meier curve analysis, and the log-rank test were used to assess treatment effectiveness and predictors of remission. Safety profiles were also recorded and analyzed.ResultsA total of 66 PMN patients, with a mean age of 50 ± 13 years, were enrolled, and 48 (72.7%) were male. The average follow-up duration was 18.1 ± 8.0 months. During the follow-up period, 60 patients (90.9%) achieved clinical remission, including 31 (47.0%) who achieved complete remission. The median time to the first recorded partial response (PR) was 4 (IQR 2 ~ 8) months. The anti-phospholipase A2 receptor antibody (aPLA2Rab) decrease rate during the first month of treatment was 88.3% (IQR 68.0%~95.7%). In total, 47/52 (90.4%) patients in the low aPLA2Rab group (aPLA2Rab < 150 RU/ml) and 13/14 (92.9%) in the high aPLA2Rab group (aPLA2Rab ≥ 150 RU/ml) achieved clinical remission. The remission rate of the low aPLA2Rab group did not significantly differ from that of the high aPLA2Rab group (HR = 1.656, 95% CI 0.895 ~ 3.065; P = 0.108). Decrease in aPLA2Rab at month-1 was associated with complete remission (OR = 1.033, 95% CI 1.003 ~ 1.063; P = 0.033) and had a predictive value for complete remission (AUC = 0.734, 95% CI 0.606 ~ 0.862; P = 0.002), and patients were more likely to achieve complete remission when the decrease rate exceeded 90.8%, with a sensitivity of 65.5% and a specificity of 75.8%. No serious adverse events were observed; the most common were mild infusion-related reactions (48.5%).ConclusionObinutuzumab rapidly and persistently reduces serum aPLA2Rab levels in PMN patients, leading to a favorable clinical remission rate. Decrease in aPLA2Rab at month-1 after treatment can predict clinical remission of PMN.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12882-026-05161-z.

Primary membranous nephropathy (PMN) is a leading cause of nephrotic syndrome in adults. Previous studies have shown that approximately 35%~40% of patients who received conservative treatment developed kidney failure within 10 years. In contrast, only 8%~11% of patients treated with an alkylating agent/corticosteroid combination experienced the same outcome [1]. Persistent and massive proteinuria is a major risk factor for progression to end-stage renal disease (ESRD). In PMN patients, alleviating or completely suppressing proteinuria can significantly delay the onset of ESRD.

Rituximab, a human/murine chimeric type I anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody, can induce the killing of CD20 + cells through multiple mechanisms and is highly recommended as a first-line treatment for moderate- to high-risk PMN [2]. The remission rate of rituximab in PMN patients ranges from 60%~80% [3]. However, 20%~40% of patients still fail to achieve clinical remission after they use rituximab, necessitating alternative treatment regimens. Recently, different studies identified multiple factors contributing to rituximab resistance, such as urinary rituximab loss [4], reduced bioavailability (lower doses of rituximab) [5], the development of anti-rituximab antibodies [6], high immunological activity (high anti-PLA2R titer), and chronic kidney damage. Some of these resistance mechanisms can be overcome by adopting different immunosuppressive drugs.

Obinutuzumab, a humanized type II anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody with optimized immunoglobulin G1 Fc, enhances antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), induces direct cell death (DCD), and reduces complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) [7]. Obinutuzumab has demonstrated more complete B-cell depletion and it is recommended as a first-line treatment for large B-cell lymphoma. In PMN, obinutuzumab has been reported to be effective in treating refractory MN, even in patients whose response to rituximab has failed [8, 9]. Considering its stronger B-cell depletion effect [10] and relatively lower cost, we have used obinutuzumab to treat not only refractory PMN but also newly diagnosed PMN since March 2022, and we even observed that obinutuzumab achieves favorable efficacy in inducing and maintaining remission among patients with MCD [11]. This retrospective study reports the efficacy, side effects, and potential remission predictors of obinutuzumab in PMN patients with nephrotic syndrome in our hospital.

Methods

Participants and data collection

PMN patients admitted to Peking University Third Hospital from March 2022 to December 2023 were recruited for this study. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (a) PMN confirmed by renal biopsy and/or positive serum anti-phospholipase A2 receptor antibody (aPLA2Rab); (b) nephrotic syndrome; (c) patients received at least 1 g of obinutuzumab; and (d) patients were followed up for at least 6 months. Some patients presented with hypotension, hyperkalemia, severe nephrotic syndrome or personal contraindications, and thus had not received ACEi/ARBs for up to 3 months prior to obinutuzumab treatment, but all patients received optimized supportive treatment tailored to their individual conditions. The exclusion criteria were (a) renal biopsy-confirmed presence of other important glomerular diseases in addition to MN that cause proteinuria in patients, such as severe diabetic nephropathy; (b) nonnephrotic range proteinuria or an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) < 15 ml/min/1.73 m2 (ESRD) at the time of obinutuzumab treatment; and (c) concurrent use of other immunosuppressants with obinutuzumab.

Demographic data and previous immunosuppressive regimens were recorded. Laboratory parameters, including aPLA2Rab, 24-h urinary protein, serum albumin, serum creatinine, eGFR (calculated by the CKD-EPI formula [12]), serum immunoglobulin G (IgG), and CD19 + B-cell count, were recorded at baseline (within 7 days before the first dose of obinutuzumab) and during follow-up. The serum aPLA2Rab titer was assessed using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kit purchased from Euroimmune (Lubeck, Germany). A titer ≥ 20 RU/ml was defined as positive according to the manufacturer’s protocol, and a titer < 2 RU/mL was defined as negative according to the literature [13].

Protocol for administration of infusion therapy

All study participants underwent a standardized preinfusion pretreatment protocol: (a) Prophylactic medication protocol: 80 mg of methylprednisolone was administered via intravenous infusion 1 h before obinutuzumab administration. Acetaminophen (oral) and diphenhydramine (intramuscular injection) were given 30 min before obinutuzumab administration. (b) Control infusion parameters: The initial infusion rate of obinutuzumab was 50 mg/h. In the absence of infusion-related reactions (IRRs), the infusion rate was increased stepwise. The rate was increased by 25 mg/h every 60 min, with a maximum infusion rate of 100 ~ 200 mg/h. All patients received an initial dose of 1 g of obinutuzumab at baseline, followed by a second dose of 1 g at the 2nd–12th weeks (The decision on whether to administer the second dose and when to give it depends on the physician discretion, as well as the patient’s tolerance, financial situation, or infections). If proteinuria did not achieve partial remission at the 6th and 12th months of follow-up, additional 1 g injections were given on the basis of the patient’s consent.

Outcomes and endpoints

The remission definitions were as follows: partial remission (PR), 24-h urinary protein < 3.5 g and a ≥ 50% reduction from baseline, serum albumin ≥ 30 g/L, and stable renal function; complete response (CR), 24-h urinary protein < 0.3 g, albumin ≥ 35 g/L, and stable renal function. Clinical remission was defined as either CR or PR. Immunological remission, aPLA2Rab < 2 RU/ml after treatment. B-cell depletion, CD19 + B-cell count < 5 cells/µL. Relapse was defined as a urine protein concentration ≥ 3.5 g/d reappearing in patients who achieved CR or PR. For relapsed patients who initially achieved PR, in addition to a urine protein concentration ≥ 3.5 g/d, the value increased by ≥ 50% compared with the minimum value. The end-point of the study was follow-up of patients until August 31, 2025, the development of ESRD, or loss to follow-up.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using SPSS 26 statistical software and were plotted with GraphPad Prism 9. Normally distributed data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD), and differences between two groups were compared using the t test. Nonnormally distributed data are presented as medians and interquartile ranges (IQRs), and differences were compared using the nonparametric Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Kaplan–Meier curves and the log-rank test were used to analyze the outcomes of different subgroups. Logistic regression was performed to identify factors influencing the treatment effectiveness of obinutuzumab in patients with PMN, variables with clinical relevance and univariate P < 0.2 were included in the final multivariate logistic regression model. ROC curve analysis was used to evaluate the predictors of remission. P < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance, and P < 0.01 was considered highly significant.

Results

General information

As shown in Table 1, a total of 66 patients, including 48 males (72.7%), were enrolled, with a mean age of 50 ± 13 years. Twenty-seven patients (40.9%) received obinutuzumab as initial therapy, and 39 patients (59.1%) received it as second-line therapy, of whom 21 patients (31.8%) had a prior history of rituximab treatment. The mean follow-up time was 18.1 ± 8.0 months, ranging from 6 ~ 37 months. Patients were divided into a low aPLA2Rab group (aPLA2Rab < 150 RU/ml) and a high aPLA2Rab group (aPLA2Rab ≥ 150 RU/ml). The low aPLA2Rab group had a greater percentage of male patients (78.8% vs. 50.0%, P = 0.031), higher serum albumin levels (25.3 ± 3.7 vs. 23.0 ± 3.7 g/L, P = 0.037), more patients who received only 1 g of obinutuzumab (26.9% vs. 0%, P = 0.030), and fewer patients who received more than 2 g of obinutuzumab (9.6% vs. 28.6%, P = 0.087).

aPLA2Rab and B-cells

The aPLA2Rab concentration decreased significantly from 55.4 (IQR 18.4 ~ 134.7) RU/ml at baseline to 6.5 (IQR 1.3 ~ 16.4) RU/ml one month after treatment (Table S1, P < 0.001). The rate of decrease in the aPLA2Rab concentration during the first month of treatment (hereafter referred to as the month-1 aPLA2Rab decrease) was 88.3% (IQR 68.0%~95.7%) (Fig. 1D). In the low aPLA2Rab group, aPLA2Rab decreased from 28.7 (IQR 7.4 ~ 87.8) RU/ml to 3.9 (IQR 0.9 ~ 10.0) RU/ml at month-1, a decrease of 88.3% (IQR 66.2%~96.3%). In the high aPLA2Rab group, aPLA2Rab decreased from 353.2 (IQR 227.5 ~ 647.8) RU/ml to 49.6 (IQR 13.3 ~ 179.5) RU/ml at month-1, a decrease of 87.3% (IQR 68.0%~93.5%). There was no significant difference in the aPLA2Rab decrease rate between the two groups (Fig. 1A, P = 0.686).

B-cell depletion was observed, with B-cell counts decreasing to 0/µL one month after the first dose. After two consecutive doses, B-cell depletion was maintained for approximately 9 (7, 11) months (Figure S1A).

24-h urinary protein

The changes in 24-h urinary protein during treatment are presented in Fig. 1B and Table S1. Notably, the 24-h urinary protein level decreased significantly during the first month of treatment, from 6.0 (IQR 4.6–9.5) g/d to 4.7 (IQR 3.2–7.4) g/d (P = 0.002), a decrease of 36.3% (IQR 7.9%–57.5%) (Fig. 1D). In the low aPLA2Rab group, the 24-h urinary protein concentration decreased from 6.0 (IQR 4.6–9.7) g/d at baseline to 4.6 (IQR 2.9–7.4) g/d at month-1 (P = 0.003). In the high aPLA2Rab group, no significant improvement was observed within the first 3 months, from 6.4 (IQR 4.7–9.6) g/d to 6.9 (IQR 5.1–9.6) g/d (P = 0.759), but the 24-h urinary protein significantly decreased from baseline to the 6th month, from 6.4 (IQR 4.7–9.6) g/d to 3.5 (IQR 1.8–5.1) g/d (P = 0.007).

Serum albumin, IgG, and renal function

The serum albumin concentration significantly increased as early as the first month of treatment, from a baseline of 24.8 ± 3.8 g/L to 27.5 ± 4.3 g/L (Fig. 1C, P = 0.001), with a median increase of 13.6% (IQR 8.5%~22.4%) (Fig. 1D). Serum IgG levels markedly increased from a baseline of 5.99 ± 2.47 g/L to 7.93 ± 3.17 g/L after 2 months of treatment (Figure S1B and Table S1; P = 0.011). Both the serum creatinine level and eGFR remained stable, with no significant fluctuations during the follow-up period (Figure S1C ~ D and Table S1; P > 0.05).

Obinutuzumab as initial therapy

A total of 26 patients (39.4%) in our cohort were naive to other immunosuppressive therapies. The level of aPLA2Rab was significantly higher in the initial therapy group than in the second-line therapy group (94 vs. 27 RU/ml, P = 0.022), while no significant differences were observed in other baseline characteristics (Table 2). At 1 month after treatment initiation, serum aPLA2Rab levels decreased markedly in both groups, with a more pronounced reduction in the initial group (87 ± 11% vs. 72 ± 27%, P = 0.09). The trends of urinary protein reduction, serum albumin elevation and renal function changes were generally comparable between the two groups (Figure S2).

Clinical outcomes

Among the 66 patients, 60 (90.9%) achieved clinical remission. This included 31 patients (47.0%) who achieved CR, with a median CR duration of 11 (IQR 8 ~ 16) months, and 29 patients (43.9%) who achieved PR, with a median PR duration of 4 (IQR 2 ~ 8) months (Fig. 2). The information of patients who did not achieve remission is presented in Table S2. Subgroup analysis revealed clinical remission rates of 47/52 (90.4%) in the low aPLA2Rab group and 13/14 (92.9%) in the high aPLA2Rab group. Kaplan–Meier analysis revealed that the remission rate of the low aPLA2Rab group was not significantly better than that of the high aPLA2Rab group (HR = 1.656; 95% CI 0.895 ~ 3.065; P = 0.108; Fig. 3A). Due to the insufficient sample size and limited statistical power, a Type II error may exist.

Among patients who received obinutuzumab as initial therapy, 25/26 (96.2%) achieved clinical remission, whereas 35/40 (87.5%) in the second-line therapy group achieved clinical remission. The administration of obinutuzumab as initial therapy did not significantly affect clinical remission for patients with MN (HR = 1.431, 95% CI 0.837 ~ 2.449; P = 0.131; Fig. 3B). Among patients who received prior rituximab treatment, 19/21 (90.5%) achieved clinical remission, whereas among rituximab-naive patients, 41/45 (91.1%) achieved clinical remission. Prior rituximab exposure had no significant effect on the efficacy of obinutuzumab for MN treatment (HR = 0.663, 95% CI 0.370 ~ 1.189; P = 0.168; Fig. 3C).

During follow-up, 56 patients were monitored for aPLA2Rab. Among them, 50 (89.3%) achieved immunological remission, with a median remission time of 3 (IQR 2 ~ 6) months. 45 of the 50(90%) patients who achieved immunological remission subsequently achieved clinical remission, among them, 27 (54%) achieved CR. 5 of the 6(83.3%) who did not achieve immunological remission still achieved clinical remission but without CR. Kaplan–Meier analysis revealed that patients who achieved immunological remission had a significantly greater likelihood of clinical remission (HR = 2.164, 95% CI 1.007 ~ 4.652; P = 0.048; Fig. 3D).

Independent factors affecting the therapeutic effect

Patients were classified into a remission group and a nonremission group on the basis of their clinical remission status (the remission group consisted of those with PR and CR). No statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups in terms of demographic characteristics, baseline laboratory results, pathological staging, prior treatment history, follow-up duration, or posttreatment aPLA2Rab changes (Table 3). When stratified by the achievement of CR, compared with the non-CR group, the CR group was younger (46 ± 14 vs. 54 ± 12 years, P = 0.010), had a higher baseline eGFR (102 ± 20 vs. 85 ± 30 mL/min/1.73 m², P = 0.008), and had a greater decrease in aPLA2Rab at month-1 (95.0% (IQR 75.3 ~ 97.3%) vs. 78.5% (IQR 60.0 ~ 90.0%), P = 0.002) (Table 3).

Predictors of disease remission

The results of the ROC curve analysis (Fig. 4) revealed that a greater decrease in aPLA2Rab at month-1 resulted in a greater likelihood that the patient would achieve complete remission (AUC = 0.734, 95% CI 0.606 ~ 0.862; P = 0.002). When the aPLA2Rab decrease rate exceeded 90.8%, the probability of the patient entering complete remission was greater, with a sensitivity of 65.5% and a specificity of 75.8%.

Relapse

A total of 6 people relapsed during the follow-up period (Table S3). After treatment, the aPLA2Rab concentration of these patients decreased to a minimum of less than 2 RU/ml, and the number of B-cells decreased to 0/µL. The time to achieve PR was between 1 and 9 months, among which 1 person achieved CR. The duration of remission maintenance ranged from 6 to 18 months. Among these patients, aPLA2Rab increased again in 4 patients, but none reached the onset level. The B-cells in all 6 patients returned to the pretreatment level.

Among the above-mentioned patients with relapse, 3 (case 3,10,21) achieved remission again after another 1 g injection of obinutuzumab. One patient (case 16) received 2 g of obinutuzumab but showed no remission after 12 months of follow-up. One patient (case 17) was lost to follow-up after another 1 g of obinutuzumab injection. Another patient (case 63) was switched to CTX regimen and after 9 months of follow-up with a cumulative CTX dose of 7 g, remission has still not been achieved.

Safety

In this study, a total of 36 patients reported 44 adverse events (Table 5), with infusion reactions being the most common (48.5%). The most prevalent infusion-related reaction was chills/fever (25.8%), and patients could tolerate these reactions. Among the other adverse reactions, infection was the most common (12.1%), including upper respiratory tract infection (9.1%) and pneumonia (3.0%). Pneumonia improved after hospitalization for anti-infective treatment. The second most common event was elevated transaminases (4.5%), which were mildly elevated in all cases and did not exceed twice the upper limit of normal. After statins discontinued and liver-protective treatment, the condition improved.

Discussion

This study retrospectively analyzed the efficacy of obinutuzumab in treating MN and its influencing factors. We discovered that one month after obinutuzumab was injected, the level of aPLA2Rab decreased by 88.3% compared with that at baseline. During the follow-up period of 18.1 ± 8.0 months, the use of obinutuzumab alone led to clinical remission in 90.9% of the MN patients, among whom 47.0% achieved complete remission. The decrease in the rate of aPLA2Rab at month-1 serves as a predictive indicator for complete remission of PMN. When the decrease in aPLA2Rab at month-1 exceeds 90.8%, it indicates a high probability that the patient will enter a state of complete remission.

Obinutuzumab can reduce the level of aPLA2Rab rapidly and persistently. In this study, the level of aPLA2Rab decreased from 55.4 RU/ml at baseline to 6.5 RU/ml during the first month of treatment, a decrease of 88.3%. It further decreased to 2.0 RU/ml in the third month (decrease rate of 94.5%). Our results are consistent with those of the following two studies. Su, X et al. [14] reported that the level of aPLA2Rab decreased from 89.8 RU/ml to 29.9 RU/ml (decreased by 66.7%) at the 1st month and to 2.6 RU/ml (decreased by 97.1%) at the 4th month after obinutuzumab treatment. Hao, J et al. [15] reported that the level of aPLA2Rab decreased by 93.6% at the 3rd month (from 65.7 to 4.2 RU/ml) after obinutuzumab treatment. However, the results of the rituximab-associated cohort revealed that at the 6th month of rituximab treatment, the level of aPLA2Rab decreased from a baseline of 102.5 RU/ml to 8.3 RU/ml in the GEMRITUX cohort [16] and from 165 RU/ml to 0 RU/ml in the NICE cohort [17]. Both our study and studies by other scholars have shown that obinutuzumab is superior to conventional-dose rituximab (375 mg/m²×2) and not inferior to high-dose rituximab (1 g×2) in reducing aPLA2Rab levels.

Obinutuzumab has a marked effect on reducing urinary protein. In this study, the urinary protein level decreased from a baseline of 6.0 g/d to 2.0 g/d at the 6th month and further to 0.9 g/d at the 12th month. The results of this study are essentially consistent with those of previous studies as follows. Hao, J et al. [15] reported that the urinary protein level decreased from 6.7 g/d at baseline to 1.9 g/d at the 6th month, and Hu, X et al. [18] reported that the urinary protein decreased from 8.4 g/d at baseline to 0.5 g/d at 12 months after obinutuzumab treatment. However, the results of the relevant studies on rituximab, namely, the GEMRITUX cohort and the NICE cohort, revealed that at the 6th month of treatment, the urinary protein concentration in the rituximab group decreased from 8.4 g/gCr to 3.7 g/gCr and from 5.9 g/gCr to 2.0 g/gCr, respectively [17]. In the study by Hu, X et al. [18], for patients in the rituximab group (375 mg/m²×4), the urinary protein level decreased from 7.9 g/d at baseline to 1.2 g/d at the 12th month. The above research results suggest that obinutuzumab is superior to rituximab in reducing urinary protein levels.

The remission rate and remission time of obinutuzumab in treating MN in this study were superior to those of most studies on the use of rituximab for treating MN. The clinical remission rate of urinary protein in this study was 90.9%, and the median time to PR was 4 months (the median time to the last follow-up was 18 months), which was higher than the rate of 64.9% reported in the GEMRITUX trial (the median follow-up and remission times were 17 months and 9 months, respectively), although the dose of rituximab in the GEMRITUX trial was lower (375 mg/m²×2) [16]. This rate is also higher than the clinical remission rate of 60% (at 24 months) reported in the MENTOR study (rituximab 1 g×2) [19] and the 65% remission rate reported by Ruggenenti et al. (with a median follow-up of 29 months and a remission time of 7.1 months) [20]. The results of this study are comparable to the remission rate of 85% in the RI-CYCLO study (rituximab 1 g×2, at 24 months) [21] and the remission rate of 86% in the NICE cohort (rituximab 1 g×2, with a median follow-up of 15 months and a remission time of 3 months) [17]. However, this study is retrospective, with some missing data, and its reliability is lower than that of the abovementioned studies on rituximab.

In our cohort, the obinutuzumab dose was individualized according to patients’ baseline clinical status, financial situation and treatment response. Most patients achieved clinical remission at a dose of 2 g. In the low aPLA2Rab group, 26.9% (14/52) of patients still obtained satisfactory clinical remission with 1 g obinutuzumab. By comparison, all patients in the high aPLA2Rab group required a dose of ≥ 2 g, and 28.6% (4/14) of them needed 3 ~ 4 g, which was higher than the 9.6% in the low aPLA2Rab group. The study by Su et al. [14] reported that a larger proportion of patients in the second-line therapy group received 3 ~ 4 g of the drug compared with the initial treatment group (38.4% vs. 10%). In the studies by Li H et al. [22] and Li L et al. [23] on obinutuzumab for the treatment of MN, the mean dosages were 2.6 g and 2.3 g respectively, and both cohorts enrolled patients with an inadequate response to initial therapy. Trillini et al. [24] prospective evaluated the response to Obinutuzumab (1000 mg at Weeks 0, 2 and 4) rescue therapy in rituximab-resistant, and/or intolerant primary MN patients, 17 patients (85%) had progressed to the combined end point (PR or CR) over a median of 9 (6–12) months. Nevertheless, our study suggests that prior initial therapy has no significant impact on drug dosage selection, which is predominantly correlated with the baseline aPLA2Rab level at the time of treatment.

We summarized 18 published retrospective analyses regarding obinutuzumab in the treatment of MN, and the number of cases in each study was ≥ 10, involving a total of 435 patients (Table 6). The overall clinical remission rate (CR + PR) was 83.2% (361/434), the median remission time ranged from 2.7 to 9 months, and the complete remission rate was 27.2% (118/434). Among the studies, Su et al. [14] and Cheng et al. [25] reported a 59-case cohort with a remission rate of 85% (the median follow-up was 9.4 months and the remission time was 4.9 months) and a 55-case cohort with a remission rate of 84% (the median follow-up was 13 months and the remission time was 9 months). The remission rates and remission times reported in our study were similar to those reported in the abovementioned studies.

The reasons for the superior efficacy of obinutuzumab compared with that of rituximab in treating MN are multifaceted: (1) The structure of obinutuzumab has an additional modification in the hinge region, causing changes in the spatial conformation of the antibody and alterations in the binding pattern [10]. (2) Obinutuzumab has been modified by glyco-engineering, causing the Fc segment to have a stronger affinity for FcγRIIIa [10], manifested as greater ADCC, less CDC, and additional induced DCD compared with those of type I Fab molecules [7, 10, 26]. (3) Laboratory studies have shown that the CD20 dimer binds two type I Fab molecules but only one type II Fab molecule, which manifests as twice the binding of the CD20 dimer [27]. (4) Obinutuzumab treatment results in rapid loss of B-cell subsets in peripheral blood and tissue [28]. Therefore, the clinical effect of an equal dose of obinutuzumab is significantly stronger than that of rituximab. In conclusion, the effect of obinutuzumab ultimately manifests as more complete B-cell depletion [29]. Patients who are not satisfied with the therapeutic effect of rituximab can still achieve a remissive effect after switching to obinutuzumab. This may be because although both are anti-CD20 monoclonal antibodies, their precise binding sites are different; thus, they do not interfere with each other [30, 31]. Obinutuzumab has good safety, which in our hospital is also related to the pretreatment process and the infusion rate. Slow and prolonged infusion has reduced the occurrence of infusion reactions, and patients have shown good tolerance. Moreover, for patients with significantly low immunoglobulin levels, we administered intravenous immunoglobulin to reduce the occurrence of opportunistic infections.

Infusion-related reactions were the most common adverse event in our cohort, occurring at an incidence of 48.5%, which was markedly higher than the rates reported by Cheng et al. [25](11/55, 20.0%) and Su et al. [32](9/59, 15.3%). This discrepancy may be related to drug batch variation, more than half of the infusion reactions in our patients occurred within a concentrated period of two months. Nevertheless, all patients were well tolerant and successfully completed the therapeutic course. Among long-term complications, infection was the most prevalent, with an incidence of 12.1% in our cohort. This figure was lower than that reported by Cheng et al. [25] (13/55, 23.6%) and comparable to the finding of Su et al. [32] (7/59, 13.9%). Hepatic function impairment ranked second, with an incidence of 4.5%, which was consistent with previously reported rates of 1.7%~3.6%. Accumulated evidence suggests that liver function abnormalities may be associated with concurrent medications, particularly statins. Discontinuation of the suspected drugs combined with symptomatic hepatoprotective treatment could restore liver function to normal in all affected patients. Regular monitoring of liver function is required during obinutuzumab treatment.

Our study has several limitations. First, despite the relatively large sample size of this study, it remains a single-center retrospective analysis. The lack of data from multicenter studies and randomized controlled trials (RCTs) is a significant drawback. Our study provides real-world supplementary data to existing prospective and retrospective evidence. Second, only 43 of 66 patients had received the maximum tolerable dose of renin-angiotensin system inhibitors (RASI) for more than three months prior to initiating rituximab therapy. Given the spontaneous remission tendency of membranous nephropathy, there is a risk of overestimating the efficacy of obinutuzumab. Third, considering the limited sample size and non-standardized retrospective dosing, we abandoned structured dose–response or time-to-event analysis, meanwhile, we strongly emphasized the urgent need for future prospective studies to clarify the optimal obinutuzumab dosage in PMN. Fourth, the number of patients without clinical remission was small (n = 6), so the statistical power was insufficient to identify robust independent predictors of treatment failure. Negative findings regarding non-response predictors should be interpreted with great caution. Finally, no comparisons were made between the efficacy and safety of obinutuzumab and those of rituximab and other immunosuppressants used within our center.

In conclusion, obinutuzumab can rapidly decrease the levels of aPLA2Rab in MN patients as early as the first month of treatment, followed by a reduction in urinary protein levels and the induction of disease remission. Obinutuzumab demonstrates favorable efficacy and safety in real-world PMN patients, and may serve as a promising alternative therapy. Notably, the rate of decrease in aPLA2Rab at month-1 during the first month of treatment can predict complete remission of urinary protein, making it a valuable biomarker for assessing the therapeutic efficacy of obinutuzumab.

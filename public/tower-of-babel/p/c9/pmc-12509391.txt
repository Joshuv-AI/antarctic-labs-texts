Serum galectin-3 level increase correlates with poor prognosis in acute coronary syndrome
Authors: Xinchen Wang, Ge Song, Chen Wei, Jingyi Liu, Yuewen Qi, Weichao Shan, Ying Zhang, Lixian Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionLevels of galectin-3 (GAL-3), a pro-inflammation biomarker, are closely associated with a diagnosis of acute coronary syndrome (ACS). However, the predictive ability of GAL-3 for long-term adverse prognosis in ACS has not been well-investigated. This prospective cohort study aimed to evaluate the predictive value of GAL-3 using classification and regression tree (CART) analysis.ResultsA total of 134 patients with ACS at The Affiliated Hospital of Chengde Medical University were consecutively enrolled between January 2016 and May 2017. All included patients (102 [76.1%] male, median age, 58.18 years) completed follow-up (median, 6.5 years). The primary end-point was major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs), including all-cause death, need for rehospitalization due to severe angina, and revascularization (re-percutaneous coronary intervention). CART results showed that GAL-3 had better prognostic ability than the other four cytokines and that the optimal GAL-3 cut-off value was 1.778 ng/mL. Kaplan–Meier analysis showed that the cumulative survival rate in the GAL-3 ≥ 1.778 ng/mL group was significantly lower than that in the GAL-3 < 1.778 ng/mL group (log-rank test, all p = 0.010). Cox regression analysis identified GAL-3 ≥ 1.778 ng/mL as an independent risk factor of long-term adverse prognosis in ACS.ConclusionSerum level of the pro-inflammation cytokine GAL-3 ≥ 1.778 ng/mL was independently associated with a high risk of MACEs in patients with ACS.

Introduction

Ischemic heart disease is the leading cause of mortality worldwide. Acute coronary syndrome (ACS) is characterized by especially high mortality rates and poor prognosis and has serious deleterious effects on the quality of life and lifespan of patients [1, 2]. Recently, several large-scale studies have focused on exploring new methods to predict patient prognostic risk in ACS in attempts to improve patient outcomes and reduce the corresponding economic burden on society [3, 4]. In case of advanced coronary atherosclerosis, the formation of small hematomas and thrombi within a lesion significantly contributes to the progressive narrowing of the arterial lumen. In certain situations, these blood clots may increase in size over time, eventually obstructing the coronary artery within a short period of hours or days, resulting in the onset of ACS [5].Inflammation is regarded to have a critical role in the occurrence and development of ACS [6–8], and numerous studies have shown that serum inflammatory cytokine levels are closely correlated with ACS. Adipose tissue acts as an energy storage and endocrine organ that can secrete various bioactive molecules, collectively known as adipokines, that can have both anti- and pro-inflammatory properties and are closely related to inflammation and atherosclerosis.

Galectin-3 (GAL-3) is a 26-kDa galactosidase-binding protein belonging to the galectin family [9]. This family consists of three subtypes—prototype, chimera, and tandem. GAL-3 is a pro-inflammation biomarker belonging to the chimera subtype that is secreted by activated macrophages and plays crucial roles in several physiological and pathological processes [9]. Its level is known to be correlated with many cardiovascular conditions, including heart failure, cardiovascular death, and all-cause mortality, and it promotes macrophage activation and monocyte attraction in atherosclerosis [10, 11]. However, the relationship between GAL-3 and long-term clinical prognosis in ACS remains unclear. This study aimed to explore novel cytokines that can be used for effective long-term prognosis prediction and risk classification in ACS using classification and regression tree (CART) analysis.

Methods

Study design and population

A total of 168 patients with ACS—including unstable angina (UA), non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI), and ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI)— at The Affiliated Hospital of Chengde Medical University were consecutively enrolled in the present study between January 2016 and May 2017. All participants underwent coronary angiography performed by a team of experienced cardiologists. The inclusion criteria were (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) ACS clinical type diagnosis (including STEMI, NSTEMI, and UA) according to the 2013 ACCF/AHA Guideline for the Management of ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction and the 2014 AHA/ACC Guideline for the Management of Patients with Non-ST-Elevation Acute Coronary Syndromes; and (3) at least 50% stenosis of the lumen in at least one major coronary artery branch after coronary angiography. The exclusion criteria were severe inflammatory infectious disease; connective tissue disease; secondary coronary vasculitis; other heart diseases causing angina pectoris, such as hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and myocarditis; severe valvular heart disease; severe liver and kidney disease, etc.

This study was conducted according to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants provided informed consent.

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics

Data on all demographic and clinical characteristics of enrolled patients were collected by a cardiovascular research team. Hypertension was defined as a systolic blood pressure ≥ 140 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥ 90 mmHg at rest or a previous diagnosis of hypertension during antihypertensive therapy [12]. Type 2 diabetes mellitus (DM) was defined as diabetes symptoms and either random blood glucose ≥ 11.1 mmol/L, fasting plasma glucose ≥ 7.0 mmol/L, or a 2-h oral glucose tolerance test result ≥ 11.1 mmol/L or no diabetes symptoms but meeting at least twice blood glucose meets the above criteria [13]. Dyslipidemia was defined as serum total cholesterol ≥ 5.18 mmol/L, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) ≤ 1.04 mmol/L, LDL-C ≥ 3.37 mmol/L, or triglyceride ≥ 1.7 mmol/L or a previous diagnosis of dyslipidemia on medication [14].

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays and measurement of inflammatory markers

Blood was sampled from the radial artery of each patient after overnight fasting and from the antecubital vein with patients in the supine position before coronary angiography was performed. The blood samples were centrifuged at 3000 × g for 10 min and then stored at − 80 °C. The serum levels of GAL-3, netrin-1 (NTN1), plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1), retinol-binding protein-4 (RBP4), and interleukin-37 (IL-37) were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits (Jiangsu Meimian Industrial Co., Ltd, China). All laboratory assistants and doctors involved in blood sample collection received standard procedural education and training. All procedures were conducted according to the manufacturer’s instruction manuals.

Follow-up and end-points

All patients were evaluated and prescribed aspirin, clopidogrel, ticagrelor, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor/angiotensin receptor blocker, statins, and/or β-blocker based on the guidelines mentioned above. Follow-up examinations were conducted by cardiovascular physicians in accordance with the principles of standardization to control bias. Follow-up data were collected at the 1, 3, 6, and 12 month clinical visits and annually thereafter through telephone interviews based on medical records. For consistency, the fixed date for termination of follow-up was June 23, 2023, and vital status was censored at this time point. The primary end-point was defined as major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs) including all-cause death, rehospitalization with severe angina, and revascularization. All-cause death was defined as death due to any reason. Revascularization was defined as undergoing a re-percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI).

Modeling via CART

To determine the most appropriate cytokines predictive for ACS outcome, we performed CART analysis, which is an empirical statistical technique based on recursive partitioning of the data space to predict response [15]. A model is obtained by binary segmentation of the data using the values of predictive factors, and segmentation variables and points are automatically selected from possible candidate predictive factor values to achieve the best fit. Then, one or two “child nodes” are recursively divided into two regions, and the process continues until some stopping rules are applied. Finally, the result of this process is represented as a binary decision tree. For this analysis, each model included data on cytokine levels, sex, age, MI, dyslipidemia, stroke, ventricular tachycardia and ventricular fibrillation, heart failure, hypertension, DM, history of coronary artery disease (CAD), and left ventricular ejection fraction (< 50% or ≥ 50%). The root mean square error (RMSE) for each model was calculated and used to choose the best predictive biomarker. The same method was used to determine the optimal cut-off cytokine level.

Statistical analysis

Normally and non-normally distributed variables are presented as the mean ± standard deviation and median and interquartile range values, respectively. Differences between non-normally distributed continuous variables were analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables are presented as numbers (%), and the χ2 test was used to examine differences between two groups. Survival was estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared between groups using the log-rank test. Multivariate Cox proportional hazards modeling was used to assess the ability of predicting prognostic risk. SPSS version 26 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA), GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software Inc, La Jolla, CA, USA), and Python 3.9.0 were used for all statistical analyses. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

CART analysis revealed GAL-3 as the best predictive cytokine among the assessed factors

Models derived using the methods described above showed that the RMSE, which represents predictive ability, was the lowest for GAL-3 (0.4472) among those of all five biomarkers assessed in this study (Table 1), indicating that GAL-3 has good prognostic ability. Patient characteristics

A total of 168 patients with ACS were consecutively enrolled from January 2016 to May 2017 at The Affiliated Hospital of Chengde Medical University. Six patients were excluded from the study: one patient had an infectious disease, two had hematological diseases, three had malignant tumors, and 28 were lost to follow-up. Finally, 134 patients with ACS (102 [76.1%] male patients; median age, 58.18 years) were completely followed-up and included in the final analysis (median follow-up time: 2381 days, 6.5 years). MACEs occurred in 48 patients (35.8%), 11 patients died, and 37 patients were rehospitalized. Of these 37 patients, 13 underwent re-PCI (Fig. 2). Kaplan–Meier survival analysis

Kaplan–Meier curves were plotted to observe the differences between GAL-3 level subgroups after GAL-3 was converted into a second categorical variable based on cut-off value. The Kaplan- Meier survival curves and the log-rank test showed that the cumulative survival rate in the GAL-3 ≥ 1.778 ng/mL group was significantly lower than that in the control group (log-rank test, p = 0.010; Fig. 3).

Cox proportional hazards modeling

Serum GAL-3 level was also considered as a second categorical variable based on cut-off value through CART analysis, as mentioned above, before Cox proportional hazards model.

Model 1 considered only GAL-3 level, whereas Model 2 was adjusted for age (≥ 65 or < 65 years), hypertension (with or without), family history of CAD (with or without), dyslipidemia (with or without), DM (with or without), and sex (male or female). All variables were converted into second categorical variables in the multivariate Cox regression models (Table 4). The results showed that the HR for GAL-3 ≥ 1.778 ng/mL was 3.179 (95% CI: 1.255–8.052, p = 0.015) in Model 1 and 3.438 (95% CI: 1.325–8.921, p = 0.011) after adjustment in Model 2.

P for interaction subgroup analysis

In order to analyze whether the ability to predict prognosis was distinct in different subgroups, we conducted a p for interaction analysis for five subgroups based on sex (male or female), age (≥ 65 or < 65 years), AMI (with or without), dyslipidemia (with or without), hypertension (with or without), DM (with or without), family history of CAD (with or without), and LVEDD ≥ 50 mm (with or without)(Fig. 6). The p for interaction values for all subgroup were greater than 0.05; thus, the predictive ability of GAL-3 was not influenced by any of these factors. Same results were also been seen when predicting rehospitalization risk (including re-PCI and rehospitalization with severe angina) (Fig. 7).

Discussion

In the present study, we analyzed the correlation between serum GAL-3 level and long-term prognosis in patients with ACS. The main finding of this study was that an increased level of the pro-inflammation cytokine GAL-3 was independently associated with a higher risk of MACEs in patients with ACS. Moreover, the predictive ability of GAL-3 was not found to be influenced by sex, age, dyslipidemia, hypertension, or DM, and a higher GAL-3 level was correlated with a higher risk of rehospitalization. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to explore the correlation between serum GAL-3 level and long-term outcome in patients with ACS.

The relationship between GAL-3 and atherosclerosis has been widely researched. Atherosclerosis is considered closely associated with inflammation [16] and GAL-3, as a pro-inflammation cytokine, is a known amplifier of inflammation in atherosclerotic plaque progression through macrophage activation and monocyte chemoattraction and concentration in unstable regions of human carotid atheromas [17]. The functions of GAL-3 in atherosclerosis could be related to atherosclerotic plaque formation and stability by regulation of endothelial dysfunction, inflammation, oxidative stress, and lipid endocytosis [18, 19]; additionally, GAL-3 down-regulation inhibits atherosclerotic progression and inflammation [20]. Thus, targeting GAL-3 could be a new effective treatment for atherosclerosis. Serum GAL-3 level has been found to be an independent risk factor in patients with CAD with persistent inflammation [21]; moreover, intrapericardial infusion of GAL-3 in healthy and hypertensive rats was found to lead to myocardial macrophage and mast cell infiltration, increased collagen I deposition, and impaired diastolic and systolic ventricular function [22, 23]. Our study found that serum GAL-3 level correlated with the prognosis of patients with ACS, which could be due to related to GAL-3 being a myocardial damage biomarker. GAL-3 has shown a notably better ability to predict heart failure risk in patients with myocardial injury, and has even been used to treat heart failure in some studies [22–26]. Increased serum GAL-3 levels were also observed in patients with heart failure, and it was associated with heightened fibrosis and inflammation, which promote heart failure through inflammatory cell infiltration, fibroblast proliferation, cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, and collagen synthesis [27].

A previous clinical study had also reported a higher serum level of gal-3 as an independent predictor of all-cause mortality, cardiovascular death, and heart failure in patients with CAD, which is similar to the findings of our present study [28]. GAL-3 could have a significant impact on the entire body as well as the cardiovascular system, especially in patients with coronary atherosclerotic plaque. Healing after MI involves tissue repair with progressive extracellular collagen matrix deposition and scar formation [29]. Meanwhile, a defect in the healing process or an increase in infarct size are major determinants of increased risk of poor prognosis. As GAL-3 stimulates inflammation and fibrosis, it plays an important role in healing after MI22.

Finally, newer statistical methods including CART and p for interaction were used in the present study. The use of CART to determine the optimal cut-off value strengthened validity. In order to analyze the impact of different subgroups on predictive ability, the p for interaction method was used to examine the possible effects of sex, age (≥ 65 and < 65 years), dyslipidemia, hypertension, and DM. In all subgroups, the above conclusion still held true, which provides multiple perspectives for clinical applications. Finally, increased GAL-3 level was found to be independently associated with a higher risk of MACEs in patients with ACS. Moreover, the predictive ability of GAL-3 was not influenced by sex, age, dyslipidemia, hypertension, or DM. Therefore, the follow-up should be strengthened for high risk of ACS in the clinical practice.

However, this study had several limitations. First, it was a single-center study with a relatively small sample size. Second, despite various adjustments in the modeling, residual or unassessed confounding factors could influence the conclusions. Third, we drew relatively conservative conclusions in this study, as further investigations in this area of research are still needed.

Conclusions

In conclusion, an increase in the level of the pro-inflammation cytokine GAL-3 was independently associated with a higher risk of MACEs in patients with ACS. Moreover, the predictive ability of GAL-3 was not influenced by sex, age, dyslipidemia, hypertension, or DM.

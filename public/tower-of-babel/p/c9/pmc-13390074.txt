3-dimensional analysis of the triangle of Koch in typical atrioventricular nodal reentrant tachycardia: Insights using peak frequency, activation vector, and wave speed
Authors: Yusuke Sakamoto, Hiroyuki Osanai, Yuichiro Sakai, Yoshiki Sogo, Eiji Yoshida
Published: 2026

Catheter ablation, an established treatment of typical atrioventricular nodal reentrant tachycardia (AVNRT), targets the slow pathway, which corresponds to the right inferior extension (RIE). Electrogram-based markers are used to identify this region,1 and recent advances in 3-dimensional mapping allow detailed visualization of the slow pathway and optimal ablation targeting.2,3

In this prospective study, 15 consecutive patients with typical slow-fast AVNRT underwent high-resolution mapping with a EnSite X system (Abbott Laboratories, Chicago, IL) during sinus rhythm (SR) and AVNRT, followed by ablation with a TactiFlex catheter (Abbott Laboratories). We aimed to characterize slow pathway electrophysiology using peak frequency (PF), fractionation, activation vector, and wave speed and examine their relationship to the anatomical course and function of the RIE. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Tosei General Hospital and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients.

Figure 1A shows the spatial distribution of PF within the triangle of Koch. In all patients, relatively high-PF regions were consistently observed in the posterior septum during SR and AVNRT. A paired t test was used for within-patient comparisons. Catheter ablation at these sites eliminated AVNRT. In contrast, areas showing electrogram fractionation during AVNRT (cutoff 7) demonstrated lower PF values. PF distribution varied with distance from the His bundle recording site and reflected the anatomical course of the RIE. In regions where the RIE ran deeper within the myocardium, near-field components and high-frequency signals were attenuated. Figure 1B illustrates pivot points identified during SR and AVNRT. During SR, pivot points were observed in 60% of patients, with a mean distance of 10.3 ± 4.1 mm from the successful ablation site. During AVNRT, pivot points were observed in 47% of patients; the distance to the earliest activation site was 18.8 ± 8.8 mm.

Figure 1C shows the relationship between activation vectors and local electrograms during SR. In all patients, vector convergence was consistently observed in the high-PF area corresponding to the successful ablation site (green circle). Superior to this region, toward the His bundle, ascending and descending vectors were observed. Local electrogram analysis revealed the following:(a)His bundle potential.(b)Reduced His bundle potential corresponding to the nodal–His transition zone.4 On the basis of this, the RIE was presumed to be inferior to this level.(c)High-PF site where vectors converged. Jackman potentials were observed in all cases, and annotation at this potential yielded high frequencies, suggesting that Jackman potentials reflect electrograms related to the RIE entrance.(d)A site at an anatomical level similar to (c) but outside the low-voltage bridge showed sharper electrograms despite comparable anatomical height.(e)A descending vector near region (b) with sharp atrial potentials, considered bystander atrial activation.

Descending bystander vectors were more frequently observed in cases without an identifiable pivot point, suggesting that pivot point formation depends on the depth and anatomical course of the RIE.

Figure 1D shows the distribution of relative wave speed calculated from phase differences between adjacent electrograms during SR. The wave speed map did not apply a predefined cutoff; instead, in each case, the highest relative wave speed corresponded to the high-PF area in the posterior septum, suggesting faster propagation near the RIE entrance. Comparison of wave speed between the right atrium and the His bundle–RIE region showed higher values in the His bundle–RIE region in 73% of cases and comparable values in 27%. Because this region may include bystander atrial activation, wave speed assessment may be affected by this propagation.

In typical AVNRT, the distribution of PF and pivot points, with integrated activation vector and wave speed analyses, enables the characterization of the functional and anatomical properties of the RIE. This integrated assessment clarifies substrates within the triangle of Koch and may improve precision in slow pathway ablation.

Genetic association between SORBS1 rs2274490 and INSR rs12971499 polymorphisms with polycystic ovary syndrome: A case–control study
Authors: Amir Hossein Ebrahimi, Pegah Shakib, Fereshteh Yavari, Zeinab Sharafi, Abbas Morovvati, Mohammad Reza Zarei, Ashkan Dirbaziyan, Tahereh Komeili Movahhed
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is one of the most endocrine metabolic disorders affecting women of reproductive age. PCOS is a multifactorial disorder that results from genetic and environmental factors. Several potential genetic variants are suggested to be associated with PCOS susceptibility. Insulin signaling genes and their single nucleotide polymorphism (SNPs) have been targeted to evaluate their association with PCOS. We aimed to investigate the association between INSR rs12971499 C/T and SORBS1 rs2274490 A/G polymorphisms and PCOS in Iranian women.MATERIALS AND METHODS:This case–control study included 50 PCOS participants and 50 age-matched control women in an Iranian population from Royan Research Institute in Qom, Iran, from October 2022 to November 2023. PCOS was diagnosed according to the Rotterdam criteria. Exclusion criteria included hypothyroidism, hyperprolactinemia, Cushing’s syndrome, adrenal hyperplasia, tumors androgen secreting, and ovarian tumors. The age, BMI, and serum levels of LH, FSH, LH/FSH, FBS, estradiol, prolactin, AMH, and testosterone were determined. Tetra-primer ARMS-PCR was used for SNP genotyping.RESULTS:We found that SNP rs2274490 A/G of SORBS1 was associated with a significantly increased risk of PCOS. The allelic and genotype frequencies of SORBS1 rs2274490 A/G significantly differed between control and PCOS subjects. The dominant and recessive genetic models were shown to be significantly associated with PCOS. There was no association between SNP rs12971499 C/T of INSR and susceptibility to PCOS.CONCLUSION:SORBS1 rs2274490 A/G is a susceptibility locus associated with increased PCOS risk in Iranian women, but there is no association between INSR rs12971499 C/T and PCOS risk.

Introduction

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is one of the common diseases of women in Iran and other countries, despite the significant progress made in its treatment, the etiology and genetics of the disease are not well known. PCOS is associated with a variety of clinical signs such as obesity, impaired glucose tolerance, irregular or no menstrual cycle, hirsutism, and hyperandrogenism. Studies have shown that PCOS increases the risk of infertility, abnormal uterine bleeding, metabolic syndrome, type 2 diabetes, and cardiovascular diseases.[12] There is also evidence of an increased risk of depression, nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), and some cancers. Numerous clinical studies have established that individuals with PCOS have higher insulin levels and insulin resistance. Several single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) associated with PCOS have been reported, including the target genes of glucose uptake and insulin resistance. INSR is considered to be associated with PCOS and insulin resistance. However, the association between PCOS and INSR gene variants is still controversial.[34] Insulin receptor (INSR) and sorbin and SH3 domain containing 1 (SORBS1) gene, which are known as components of insulin signaling, play an important role in women suffering from polycystic ovary syndrome. The INSR gene is located in the short arm of chromosome 19p13.2 and includes 22 exons. The INSR gene is one of the most important sites related to PCOS.[56]

This gene encodes a member of the receptor tyrosine kinase family of proteins, the encoded protein draft is proteolytically processed to produce alpha and beta subunits that form a heterotrimeric receptor. The binding of insulin or other ligands to this receptor activates the insulin signaling pathway, which regulates the uptake and release of glucose as well as the synthesis and storage of carbohydrates, lipids, and proteins. Mutations in this gene underlie severe insulin resistance syndromes, including type A insulin resistance syndrome, Donohue syndrome, and Rabson–Mendenhall syndrome.[7]

Examples of studied genes in insulin signaling pathways include IRS1, IRS2, AKT2, INSR, GSK3B, PPP1R3, PTP1B, and SORBS1.[8] Therefore, some of the first genes considered for PCOS were genes involved in the insulin signaling pathway.

The SORBS1 gene is located on the long arm of chromosome 10q24.1 and contains 39 exons. CAP/Ponsin protein, also known as Sorbin containing SH3 (src homology 3) domain, is a protein encoded by the SORBS1 gene. It is a small part of a family of adapter proteins that regulate cell adhesion, signaling, growth factor, and cytoskeleton formation. In macrophages, SORBS1 deficiency protects against high-fat diet-induced insulin resistance and reduces inflammation. The suppression of SORBS1 in HT29 cells using siRNA inhibited cell proliferation and cell migration in comparison with the control group. Taken together, it was hypothesized that SORBS1 could serve a key role in cancer cell growth and migration.[9] By binding Casitas B-lineage Lymphoma (CBL) to its receptor, insulin, required for insulin-stimulated glucose transport in the formation of actin stress fibers and focal adhesions, SORBS1 is involved in the tyrosine phosphorylation of CBL. Insulin stimulates the phosphorylation of Casitas B-lineage Lymphoma (C-CBL), which leads to translocation to the lipid raft region. The plasma membrane becomes the insulin receptor through the C-CBL-CAP complex, and as a result, the vesicles are transported by the glucose transporter 4.[10]

Therefore, SORBS1 represents a very important adapter protein in the signaling pathway of glucose uptake stimulated by insulin resistance and obesity. SORBS1 polymorphisms are associated with type 2 diabetes and obesity.[10] rs2274490 in the SORBS1 gene and rs12971499 in the INSR gene have been identified as genetic variants of PCOS.

Although SORBS1 rs2274490 and INSR rs12971499 polymorphisms can be a risk factor for people with PCOS, no study investigates the role of these genes as a screening tool in PCOS patients. Several polymorphisms have been reported so far in PCOS that have been associated with the incidence of PCOS. However, single nucleotide changes of 12971499rs in the INSR gene and 2274490rs in the SORBS1 gene have not been investigated in PCOS subjects in Iran. Therefore, the purpose of this study was a genetic association study of SORBS1 rs2274490 and INSR rs12971499 polymorphisms with PCOS by the Tetra-primer amplification refractory mutation system-polymerase chain reaction (ARMS-PCR) method.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

This case–control study was conducted on patients referred to Royan Research Institute in Qom, Iran, from October 2022 to November 2023.

Study participants and sampling

For the present study, a total of 100 women with PCOS and age-matched non-PCOS women (as control) were included. Based on the previous studies, the control group included healthy women with regular menstrual cycles, without endocrine dysfunction, and without taking oral contraceptives for 3 months [Figure 1].

PCOS participants were diagnosed according to the Rotterdam criteria which are based on the presence of at least two of PCOS characteristics including clinical or biochemical signs of hyperandrogenism, oligomenorrhea or amenorrhea, and polycystic ovaries on ultrasound. Patients with other associated disorders such as hypothyroidism, Cushing syndrome, hyperprolactinemia, adrenal hyperplasia, androgen-secreting tumors, and ovarian tumors were excluded.[10]

All required information, including age, symptoms, and disease status, was recorded confidentially.

According to the previous study,[11] when minor allele frequency is 0.4 (P = 0.41), for an 85% confidence level (Z = 1.440), with an absolute precision of 10% (d = 0.1), the required sample size using the following formula was calculated.

Data collection tool and technique

The minimum sample size is 98 people after the confirmation of PCOS participants by a gynecologist. Finally, 50 PCOS and 50 healthy individuals were recruited. The demographic information was obtained, weight and height were recorded, and BMI (weight (kg)/height in m2) was calculated. Fasting blood were taken from all participants on the morning of the second or third day of menstruation and subsequently centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10 min at 4°C. Then, the serum was aliquoted and stored at -80°C for batch analysis.

The serum levels of FSH, LH, AMH, prolactin, estradiol, and testosterone were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) commercial kits (Demeditec Diagnostics GmbH, Germany), in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. The serum glucose level was determined using the colorimetric method (pars azmun, Iran). The DNA of blood samples was extracted based on DNA kit protocol (DNPTM Kit, Sinaclon, Iran), where 400 µl of genomic lysis buffer was added to 100 µl of blood sample and mixed completely by vertexing for 15–20 sec. Then, 500 µl of precipitation solution was added and mixed by vertexing for 5 seconds, centrifuged at 12,000g for 10 min and the supernatant was discarded. 1 ml of wash buffer was added to the pellet, vortexed for 3–5 seconds, and centrifuged at 12,000 g for 5 minutes; then, the supernatant was discarded (this step was repeated once more). The pellet was placed at 65°C for 5 minutes. 50 µl of solvent buffer was added to pellet and incubated for 2–5 min at room temperature. Concentration and purity of DNA were evaluated with nanodrop spectrophotometer at 260/280 nm, and DNA samples were stored at -20°C. The tetra-primer ARMS PCR technique was used to determine the genotype of single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNP). In this technique, an awkward base pair strategy is used so that the PCR reaction shows the amplification of a specific allele. To design the primers, first, the genomic sequences of SORBS1 and INSR genes, along with the sequence around the desired polymorphic region, were extracted from the NCBI website. Then, with the help of primer design software and information service that was introduced in 2012 and is available on the website http: 11 primer1.soton.ac.uk/primer1.html PCR ARMS. The sequence of primers is shown in Table 1. For each PCR reaction, a total volume of 25 µL was prepared, consisting of 5 µL of template DNA, 12.5 µl of PCR master mix (2X), 0.5 µL of each tetra primer (forward and reverse), and 5.5 µL of RNase-DNase free water. Evaluation of SORBS1 rs2274490 and INSR rs12971499 genes was performed under the following conditions: initial denaturation at 95°C for 5 minutes, followed by 35 cycles of denaturation at 94°C for 30 seconds, annealing at 70°C to 30 s duration and extension at 72°C for 30 s, with a final extension at 72°C for 5 min.

Agarose gel electrophoresis

Then, to evaluate the polymorphism of SORBS1 rs2274490 and INSR rs12971499, the Tetra-primer ARMS PCR products were analyzed using 2.0% agarose gel electrophoresis, in 0.5X TBE buffer, containing DNA-safe stain.

Ethical consideration

This research was agreed upon by the Ethics Committee of the Islamic Azad University of Qom, Iran (IR.IAU.QOM.REC.1401.088). An informed consent form was obtained from all participants.

Statistical analysis

In the present study, all data were analyzed with the SPSS software version 26 and with Chi-Square, Logistic regression, and Mann–Whitney tests. Analyses were performed considering the value of P < 0.05 and calculating the OR ratio with a 95% confidence interval (CI).

Discussion

PCOS is a common endocrine disorder in reproductive aged women with a global prevalence of 5–18%, which depends on the diagnostic criteria used. Almost 50% of women are not diagnosed or are late diagnosed.[12] PCOS is a multifactorial disorder caused by genetic susceptibility, hypothalamus–pituitary–ovary axis imbalance, hyperandrogenism, hyperinsulinemia, and obesity-related conditions and characterized by anovulatory infertility, menstrual irregularities, type 2 diabetes, hirsutism, weight gain, and late sequences including cardiovascular diseases, metabolic syndrome, endometrial, breast and ovarian cancers.[1213] PCOS is a complex and highly heterogeneous disease. The exact molecular mechanism and cellular pathways of this disorder are not yet known. Etiological research showed the importance of genetic susceptibility for the pathogenesis of this disease.

Familial aggregation provides evidence in support of the genetic basis for PCOS, but due to the remarkable heterogeneity and pathophysiological complexity of this syndrome, single gene mutations within families have not been reported as causative genes. Considering that PCOS results from genetic and environmental factors and the complex interaction between these factors, identification of potential loci/variants that contribute to PCOS is important to understand the pathophysiology of this syndrome, which in turn facilitates treatment development.

Studies have shown that several genes instead of a single gene, such as insulin (INS), insulin receptor (INSR), insulin receptor substrate1 (ISR1), Thyroid adenoma-associated (THADA), luteinizing hormone receptor (LHR), the luteinizing hormone/human chorionic gonadotrophin receptor (LHCGR), follicle-stimulating hormone B (FSHB), and follicle-stimulating hormone receptor (FSHR) may be involved in the development of PCOS.[1415161718]

Insulin resistance and hyperinsulinemia are common in polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). The use of insulin-sensitizing medications to improve PCOS symptoms suggests that insulin resistance plays a major role in the pathophysiologic factor.

Therefore, most candidate loci or their variations, which have been targeted to assess their association with PCOS, are related to insulin signaling (such as INSR, IRS1, IRS2, and SORBS1). Since PCOS participants have insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and obesity as common characteristics, targeting genomic variants related to insulin and metabolic syndrome reveals a role in the pathogenesis of PCOS.[1419] In PCOS, impaired insulin sensitivity is determined by a range of genetic factors. The INSR (insulin receptor) gene is considered one of the most promising candidate genes for PCOS. INSR has an important role in insulin signaling, and polymorphisms of this gene might be associated with PCOS.[2021] Analysis of SNPs of INSR among different population groups has shown that some SNPs in some racial/ethnic groups are associated with PCOS but not in other racial/ethnic groups. The binding of insulin to the extracellular domain of the insulin receptor leads to autophosphorylation and phosphorylation of intracellular protein substrates, and as a result, these pathways are activated. The PI3K-AKT/PKB pathway is responsible for most of the metabolic actions of insulin and stimulation of GLUT-4 translocation and glucose uptake. Ras/mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway, in which the Grb2/Sos pathway leads to activation of Ras signaling, affecting cell proliferation and differentiation. Casitas B-lineage Lymphoma (Cbl)-associated protein (CAP)/Cbl pathway.

INSR exerts metabolic effects such as stimulation of glucose uptake through two main pathways: the phosphatidyl kinase3 (PI3K) and the Cbl/CAP pathway. c-Cbl is one of the downstream signaling proteins, which is involved in glucose uptake and interacts with CAP. After insulin stimulation and Cbl phosphorylation, CAP-Cbl complexes are transferred from the insulin receptor to lipid rafts and produces an intracellular signal for the translocation of GLUT-4 from the cytosol to the cell membrane and stimulates glucose uptake.[2223] We therefore hypothesized that dysregulation of the insulin receptor and its associated downstream signaling proteins, such as SORSB1, may play an important role in PCOS pathogenesis.

A case–control study (400 PCOS and 480 control women) in a Chinese population that investigated two INSR SNPs (rs2059807 and rs1799817) revealed that rs2059807 was significantly associated with PCOS risk in Chinese women, but rs1799817 was not associated with PCOS risk.[24] A similar study conducted among the Indian population, including 253 PCOS and 308 control women, indicated that both SNPs are significantly associated with PCOS.[21] Another study performed in Saudi Arabia with 126 PCOS and 118 control women revealed that rs1799817 was a susceptibility locus associated with PCOS in Saudi women.[6] These studies have thus shown that susceptibility loci associated with PCOS vary among ethnic groups.

In a genome-wide association study (GWAS), fifteen independent SNPs were genotyped in a total of 2,082 Han Chinese women. This genotype–phenotype study identified INSR, TOX3, DENND1A, and THADA as susceptibility loci in Chinese Han subjects.[14]

In the case–control study that recruited 66 infertile women with PCOS history found variation in THADA associated with PCOS.[25] In another study in a sample of Colombian women with PCOS, THADA gene SNPs were not associated with the risk of PCOS.[26] Therefore, while there are common susceptibility loci in all PCOS populations, it seems some potential loci or their variants depend on ethnic groups, so to understand better the potential loci associated with PCOS, studying diverse ethnic groups is essential.

In our study, the allelic frequency of INSR rs12971499 C/T was not significantly different between control and case subjects (OR = 1.603, CI = 0.900-2.855, P = 0.109). The frequency of the T allele was 32% in the control and 43% in the case subjects. That is contrary to a study of Caucasian women in the USA in which T allele frequency of INSR rs12971499 C/T was 54.3% for the control and 63.5% for the case and significantly different (P = 0.009) and, therefore, was considered as an SNP with allelic association with PCOS in the discovery study (273 cases, 173 control), but in replication study (526 cases, 3585 control), the significant association was not observed (P = 0.24).[11]

In the present study, genotype frequencies of INSR rs12971499 between case and control subjects were not significantly different (P = 0.230). Besides, analysis of the recessive, dominant, and codominant models showed no significant difference in the case and control. These data imply that INSR rs12971499 C/T is not associated with PCOS. In other words, it does not increase the risk of PCOS in Iranian women. In the present study, the allelic frequency of SORBS1 rs2274490 A/G was significantly different between control and case subjects (OR = 3.781, CI = 2.003-7.137, P < 0.001). The frequency of the A allele was significantly higher in the case than in the control (81% vs. 53%). In the Goodarzi et al.[11] study, which recruited from the Caucasian population, SORBS1 rs2274490 T/C was studied in the discovery cohort, and the frequency of an associated allele (C) among the case (0.430) and control (0.334) was significantly different (P = 0.006). However, this SNP was not selected to be evaluated in the replication cohort, so its association with PCOS was not revealed.

A study, which included 233 PCOS participants and 105 controls from the Korean population, investigated the association of T228A polymorphism of the SORBS1 with PCOS and found the frequency of minor allele G was higher in the PCOS group than in the control group (P = 0.039) and therefore was considered to be associated with PCOS in the Korean population. The frequency of the AG genotype was 23.2% in PCOS and 12.4% in controls. There was no significant difference in the frequencies of AA and GG genotypes between cases and controls.

The recessive (AA vs. AG+GG) and codominant (AA or AG or GG) genetic models were shown to be significantly the risk allele for PCOS.[24] In our study, the genotype frequencies of SORBS1 rs2274490 A/G differed significantly between the cases and controls. In the case group, the GG genotype (14%) was significantly lower than the control group (42%), and the AA genotype in the case (76%) was significantly higher than the control group (48%) (P = 0.006). Moreover, the dominant (AA+GA vs. GG) and recessive (AA vs. GG+GA) genetic models were shown to be significantly associated with PCOS risk (dominant, (OR = 4.448, CI = 1.675-11.811, P = 0.003) (recessive, OR = 3.431, CI = 1.461-8.057, P = 0.005). These results suggest an increased risk of PCOS for those who have the A allele. In other words, carriers of the A allele exhibit an increasing trend for PCOS.

PCOS patients demonstrate changes in anthropometric measurements such as BMI, waist and hip circumferences, waist/hip ratio, and also abnormalities in hormonal and biochemical features.

It is believed that obesity is one of the most important risk factors for PCOS. Obesity increases the possibility of insulin resistance, associated with a lack of ovulation and increased androgen secretion.[27] Factors, such as disturbances of the hypothalamic–pituitary–ovarian axis and hyperinsulinemia, influence the pathophysiology of PCOS. A high level of circulating LH and LH/FSH ratio is found in PCOS patients. Insulin resistance leads to compensatory hyperinsulinemia, which increases the amplitude and frequency of GnRH and LH pulse secretion, thereby significantly increasing ovarian androgen synthesis. Insulin increases the expression of enzymes such as P450c17 that are involved in androgen synthesis and acts synergistically with LH on theca cells to stimulate ovarian androgen production. Conversely, hyperinsulinemia suppresses SHBG production in the liver, which increases the amount of free testosterone.[2829]

In our study, the PCOS group demonstrated significantly higher levels of LH, FSH, LH/FSH, FBS, estradiol, prolactin, AMH, testosterone, and elevated BMI than the control group. The age was comparable in both groups. In a study, which included 883 PCOS and 927 control subjects, the BMI and testosterone levels of the PCOS group were significantly higher than those of the control group. In contrast, the PCOS group was significantly younger than the control group, and fasting glucose was the same in both groups.[30]

In a study that recruited 421 PCOS patients and 322 non-PCOS in Punjab, the BMI of PCOS subjects was significantly higher compared with healthy women. The LH and total testosterone were significantly higher in PCOS compared with non-PCOS subjects, while FSH levels were quite similar among PCOS patients and controls.[31] Another study recruited 66 PCOS and 44 control women and reported that the age was similar in both groups. The BMI, LH, LH/FSH, AMH, FBS, estradiol, and prolactin levels were significantly higher in PCOS compared with normal women, while the FSH level was significantly higher in normal than in PCOS women.[25] In a case–control study, 150 PCOS patients and 150 controls were enrolled. PCOS patients have significantly larger values of BMI, LH, LH/FSH, testosterone, FBS, and insulin compared with controls. There were no significant differences between PCOS and control group in prolactin and estradiol.[32]

The controversial results in the clinical features of studies could be explained by variations in the age range, sample size of studies, dietary status, BMI (underweight, normal, overweight, lean), ethnicity, and therapeutic regimes of both PCOS and non-PCOS subjects. Besides, the duration of the subject’s menstrual cycle in which the clinical samples were taken, and technique types for measuring biochemical markers also play a role in hormonal and biochemical amounts in PCOS and non-PCOS women. Because clinical features of subjects such as sex hormones, metabolic manifestations, and anthropometric measurements are closely related to each other, a variation in a single factor can influence others. In a study by examining visceral obesity index (VAI) and lipid accumulation product (LAP) levels in nonobese PCOS patients, it was found that even in nonobese PCOS patients, VAI and LAP play a significant role in assessing cardiometabolic risk.[2] In the study of Singh et al.,[33] by examining the menstrual pattern and anthropometric characteristics of women with infertility, it was found that women with primary and secondary infertility tend to be overweight. Compared with the control group, there was no statistical difference in the menstrual pattern of women with infertility. However a significant difference was reported in weight, BMI, hip circumference, WC, and neck circumference (NC) of women with primary infertility compared with the control group

In summary, we identified a significant association between increased PCOS risk and the rs2274490 of the SORBS1 gene in some Iranian populations.

Limitation and recommendation

There are limitations in the current research. First, the sample size was relatively small, and further research is needed to identify the possible association of INSR and SORBS1 genes with PCOS. Second, in this study, only two SNPs were selected for investigation, while several genes in different molecular pathways are associated with polycystic syndrome.

In spite of its limitations, this study gains importance because there are no data available to date on the association of the SORBS1 rs2274490 and INSR rs12971499 polymorphism and PCOS in the Iranian population.

It is suggested that in future studies, other polymorphisms affecting the occurrence of PCOS should be investigated. Also, family correlation studies will be very useful to confirm the association of these polymorphisms with PCOS because there are not the limitations mentioned in population studies. In fact, by identifying the genes involved in PCOS, more efficient methods for diagnosis, prevention, and treatment of this disease will be provided.

Conclusion

Our data suggest that the INSR rs12971499 C/T polymorphism is not associated with overall PCOS risk in the Iranian population, but SORBS1 rs2274490 A/G might be associated with PCOS.

It seems that ethnic background and geographical factors may play an important role in genetic polymorphism. However, large-scale studies that involve more SNPs in a more ethnically diverse population need to be conducted to elucidate the genetic association of INSR and SORBS1 gene polymorphisms with susceptibility to PCOS in Iranian women reliably.

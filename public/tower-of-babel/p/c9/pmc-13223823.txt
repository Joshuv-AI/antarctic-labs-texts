Diagnosis of intermittent hydronephrosis on multiphase CT intravenous pyelogram and [99mTc]Tc-MAG3 study–Homsy sign
Authors: Tahleesa Cuda, Chetan Thapa, David A. Pattison, William Fong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A 40-year-old female was investigated for intermittent right flank pain and management of large uterine fibroids. A multiphase CT intravenous pyelogram showed the development of mild right hydronephrosis and hydroureter between the non-contrast and delayed pyelogram phases of imaging, secondary to large uterine fibroids compressing the pelvic course of the right ureter. The MAG3 renography demonstrated an initial downsloping renogram followed by rapid upsloping and increased renal cortical uptake during forced diuresis, accompanied by clinical symptoms. These findings indicate intermittent obstruction, also known as the Homsy sign.

Introduction

Intermittent obstruction of the renal tract, known as intermittent hydronephrosis, is often an elusive diagnosis, as it can present with renal angle pain despite a structurally normal or near normal appearing renal collecting system on imaging [1]. It is usually caused by extrinsic obstruction, most commonly at the level of the pelvi-ureteric junction [[1], [2], [3]]. Forced diuresis increases the urinary flow rate in the renal collecting system, which can surpass its drainage capacity and lead to distension of the renal collecting system. Renal scintigraphy may initially appear normal but during forced diuresis, a double-peak pattern may emerge on the renogram [1,4].

Case report

A 40-year-old female presented for investigation of intermittent right flank pain during workup for the management of large uterine fibroids. An initial CT intravenous pyelogram was unremarkable on non-contrast imaging, with the delayed pyelogram showing the interval development of mild right hydronephrosis and hydroureter with abrupt tapering at the level of the large subserosal uterine fibroids within the right hemipelvis. Following urological review, 99mTc-MAG3 diuretic renography was requested to assess the significance of the suspected renal tract obstruction and investigate the cause of the patient’s pain before definitive management of the uterine fibroids.

Following the administration of 40mg of intravenous frusemide at T0, MAG3 dynamic planar imaging initially showed normal right renal perfusion, cortical uptake, parenchymal transit, excretion and emptying of the right renal collecting system. At approximately T+20 minutes, cortical radiotracer activity and right renal collecting system activity began rapidly increasing with an upsloping renogram, with the appearance of a delayed double peak. At this point, the patient also developed right renal angle pain and discomfort, limiting her ability to remain still on the gamma camera bed. The contour of the bladder on pre-void imaging appeared asymmetric due to the mass effect of the right-sided uterine fibroids. The scintigraphic features, combined with the onset of clinical symptoms, were indicative of a right-sided intermittent obstruction, known as a Homsy sign. Obstructive findings on scintigraphy and the patient’s symptoms resolved on post-void imaging.

Discussion

Intermittent renal tract obstruction is often difficult to diagnose because of the mild hydronephrosis caused [1]. It usually results from extrinsic compression, such as abnormal vessels, fibrotic bands, adhesions and kinks, most frequently at the pelvi-ureteric junction [[1], [2], [3]]. This condition often leads to Dietl’s crisis, a distinctive clinical syndrome characterized by intense episodic abdominal pain with or without nausea and vomiting, which is caused by rapid distention of the renal pelvis and stretching of the renal capsule [3].

Frusemide induces diuretic stress by increasing urine flow, which causes pressure that surpasses the drainage capacity of the renal collecting system [5]. In cases of intermittent obstruction, stress diuretic renography shows a delayed double-peak pattern that appears 10-15 minutes after administering the diuretic, leading to self-obstruction, known as a Homsy sign [1,2]. The pattern of obstructive uropathy on diuretic renography varies from non-obstructive causes such as vesico-ureteric reflux, congenital malformations or a non-compliant bladder. For example, vesico-ureteric reflux demonstrates continued cortical radiotracer excretion with diuretic effect, despite radiotracer reflux into the collecting system [6].

This case illustrates intermittent obstruction of the right renal tract, initially noted on multiphase CT intravenous pyelogram, and subsequently caused by forced diuresis during diuretic renography, accompanied by clinical symptoms consistent with a Dietl crisis. It is unique in that the obstruction involves the pelvic part of the right ureter due to extrinsic compression from right-sided uterine fibroids, rather than at the more common pelvi-ureteric junction.

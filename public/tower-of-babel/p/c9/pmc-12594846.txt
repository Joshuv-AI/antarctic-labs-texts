Regional distribution and isotope ratios of radiocesium from the Fukushima Daiichi nuclear power station and global fallout in Tokai-mura
Authors: Asako Shimada, Takehiko Tsukahara, Masao Nomura, Taro Shimada, Seiji Takeda, Hiroaki Takahashi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Due to the accident at the Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Station (FDNPS), radiocesium such as 134Cs, 135Cs, and 137Cs was dispersed over a wide area of eastern Japan and mixed with radiocesium from global fallout. The depth profiles of 137Cs for samples taken in 2003 before the FDNPS accident and in 2017 after the FDNPS accident in Tokai-mura (about 115 km NE of Tokyo) were both described by exponential equations from the surface up to a depth of 15 cm. Systematic grid sampling of surface soil at a depth of 5 cm was conducted at 3 sites in Tokai-mura in 2019, and distributions of the 137Cs concentration, 134Cs/137Cs radioactivity ratio, and 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio were measured. It was found that the 137Cs concentration varied among sites and within individual sites, while the 134Cs/137Cs radioactivity ratio was constant for all samples collected at 3 sites, 1.01 ± 0.04 (2σ). The 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio for the two sites was constant and comparable to that obtained for soil sampled near FDNPS. On the other hand, the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio for the other site varied and showed higher values (0.355–0.446), suggesting the influence of global fallout. Based on the results, the mixture percentages of radiocesium originating from global fallout and the FDNPS accident were estimated.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-25911-7.

Introduction

When a nuclear facility is decommissioned and its completion is confirmed, the radionuclide activity concentrations on the site must comply with the criteria for site release. As an example of site release criteria, the use of clearance levels was established in Japan, and the level for 137Cs is 0.1 Bq/g12. However, 137Cs concentration in eastern Japan increased because of the accident at the Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Station (FDNPS). For example, 137Cs concentration in soil near and within Tokai-mura, which is approximately 114 km south of FDNPS, increased approximately 101−103 times compared with levels before the accident3–10. In this way, when the site was obviously contaminated by radionuclides not originating from the decommissioned facilities, such as the FDNPS accident, global fallout, etc., these activity concentrations are treated as background and excluded from the evaluation of activity concentration from the decommissioned facilities1. We proposed two methods for setting background concentrations11,12: (1) setting the reference area outside of the target site and radioactivity of the target site being compared with that of the reference area. (2) calculation using the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio and 137Cs concentration from the FDNPS accident, decommissioned facility, and target site.

The isotope ratio serves as a fingerprint to identify the origin of isotopes. The 134Cs/137Cs activity ratios for stagnant water sampled at Units 1, 2, and 3 of FDNPS are 0.91, 1.00, and 1.01 (decay corrected to March 11, 2011), respectively13. The ratio for drain water containing radionuclides released by vent operation of Unit 1 was 0.8614. These results indicate that the 134Cs/137Cs activity ratio originating from Unit 1 is lower than those originating from Units 2 and 3. Using the 134Cs/137Cs activity ratio as an index, it was suggested that the Oshika Peninsula13 and areas located within 3 km to the north-northwest of FDNPS15,16 were primarily contaminated by radiocesium released from Unit 1. In this regard, the 134Cs/137Cs activity ratio decay corrected to March 11, 2011, is almost constant at 1 across most of the eastern Japan following the FDNPS accident17,18.

As just described, the 134Cs/137Cs activity ratio is useful to estimate the origin of radiocesium. However, detection of 134Cs will be difficult in the future because of radioactive decay (half-life of 134Cs is 2.065 y). As an alternative, the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio is useful because of longer half-lives (1.33 × 106 y for 135Cs, and 30.04 y for 137Cs). The 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratios for Units 1, 2, and 3 of FDNPS were calculated as 0.390, 0.336, and 0.345, respectively (using ORIGEN22UPJ simulation results19. By contrast, reported 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratios for environmental samples varied widely, ranging from 0.315 to 0.72920–31. In addition, the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratios would reflect concentrations from radiocesium not only from the FDNPS accident but also from global fallout. The 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio originating from global fallout was considered to be 2.62 ± 0.4831–34. By focusing on soil data in which 137Cs concentration is high enough to be considered only from the FDNPS accident, excluding the data from soil predominately contaminated by Unit 1, and measured by thermal ionization mass spectrometry (TIMS), the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratios was ranging between 0.362 ± 0.001 and 0.366 ± 0.00321,22,25,26. This suggests that 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratios originating from the FDNPS accident can be treated as a constant, similar to the 134Cs/137Cs activity ratio.

The constant 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio in the global fallout background is useful for calculation of 137Cs concentrations from a decommissioned facility using the isotope ratio. In other words, radioactivity from the FDNPS accident should be high enough that global fallout can be considered negligible. Otherwise, the calculation becomes more complex and requires more information, such as the 137Cs concentration and 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio from global fallout. In this study, the true state of radiocesium contamination in Tokai-mura was surveyed, and the applicable range for identifying the origin of radiocesium using the 134Cs/137Cs activity ratio and the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio was estimated.

Methods

Soil sampling

Figure 1 shows the sampling sites (Tokai-mura) and points with a map and pictures12. The 5-cm sample layers from 0 to 20 cm depth were collected on December 17, 2003, prior to the FDNPS accident at site A, to investigate the depth profile of 36Cl35. These samples were used for 137Cs measurements, not for 135Cs measurements. Four boring core samples were collected on December 20, 2017 at site B, as indicated by orange circle in Fig. 1. The cores were sliced into 2 cm layers up to 10 cm depth and 5 cm layers from 10 cm to 15 cm depth. The same depth layers were mixed and sifted through a 2-mm mesh. These samples were used for 134Cs and 137Cs measurements.

Soil sampling sites and locations. The blue circles indicate sampling points using the U-8 container, and the green and orange circles indicate sampling points using the boring core. Four U-8 samples in the red box were mixed. The photograph was referred from reference12.

Five-cm depth surface soil samples were collected at three sites assigned B, C, and D in April 2019. In Fig. 1, the intersection points indicated by blue circle with a number represent sampling points of the 5-cm depth surface soil samples, all sides are 1 m wide, and N indicates the north. Site B is a hotspot, where the 137Cs concentration is specifically higher than that of the other area in Tokai-mura, within a bush; site C is adjacent to a forest, and site D is in an urban area. Twenty of the surface soil samples were systematically collected at sites C and D in a 3 m × 4 m tetragon area, and eight samples at site B. The sampling method followed an International Atomic Energy Agency guideline36. The soil was collected by inserting an U-8 container37 into the surface soil layer up to a depth of 5 cm, and these samples were designated as “U-8 samples”. Boring core samples with depths of 0–5, 0–10, and 0–15 cm were collected at sites B and C. The images of the sampling points are indicated by green circles in Fig. 1. The air dose rates of the sampling points at 1 cm and 1 m height were also measured using a portable dose rate meter (PCS-172, ALOKA Co., Ltd.).

Sample treatment

After measuring flux of γ-rays of the U-8 samples as they were collected to determine the activities of 134Cs and 137Cs, four samples in each red box in Fig. 1 were mixed. Plant matter and stones were removed using a sieve with a mesh size of 2 mm. These samples were designated as “mixed samples”. Each mixed sample was packed into an U-8 container to measure flux of γ-rays. The U-8 samples taken at site B were not mixed because the amount of radioactive cesium necessary for accurate isotope ratio analysis can be obtained from the sample amount in one U-8 container. The U-8 sample from site B was also similarly sieved through a 2-mm mesh. Boring core samples were sieved and packed in an U-8 container to measure the flux of γ-rays, and designated as “boring core sample”.

After sieving, mixed samples and boring core samples taken in April 2019 were used for the measurement of the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio. The dissolution method is described in our previous studies26,27. The mixed sample was heated to 723 K for 3 h to burn off organic material. Approximately 20 g of the soil sample was placed into a polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) crucible, and 50 ml of HNO3 (61%) was added. The PTFE crucible was sealed in a pressure-resistant stainless-steel container and heated at 473 K for 4 h. After cooling, the HNO3 solution was filtered through a PTFE membrane filter with a pore size of 0.1 μm. The filtrate was then evaporated to near dryness, and the residue was dissolved in 3 mol/L HNO3 to produce a soil sample solution, which was subsequently used for chemical separation as described in our previous study27. 0.015 mol/L calix[4]arene-bis(t-octylbenzo-crown-6) (BOBCalixC6) in 1-octanol, pre-washed with 1 mmol/L HNO3 solution and equilibrated with 3 mol/L HNO3 solution, was contacted with an equal volume of the soil sample solution to extract Cs. When the total volume of the soil sample solution was large, continuous batch extraction was repeated five times. After phase separation, the Cs-extracted BOBCalixC6 solution was scrubbed with 1 mol/L HNO3 solution to remove extracted undesirable elements. The extracted Cs was recovered by shaking with three volumes of 1 mmol/L HNO3 solution at 323 K in a constant-temperature oven for 30 min. In some cases, ammonium molybdophosphate (AMP) or ammonium phosphotangstate (APT) was added to the soil sample solution to adsorb Cs. After filtration, the AMP or APT precipitate was dissolved in 1.5 mol/L NH4OH solution and loaded onto an anion exchange resin to remove Mo or W. The eluate from the anion exchange resin was then loaded onto a cation exchange resin to separate Cs. The Cs recovery solution was evaporated to dryness. If a large amount of residue remained, the purification using BOBCalixC6 described above was applied. Although different separation methods were employed, the equivalence of the results has been confirmed.

Measurement of 134Cs and 137Cs

The activity of 134Cs and 137Cs in soil samples was measured using a high-purity germanium (HPGe) semiconductor detector of 30% relative efficiency (GC3020, Mirion Technologies, Canberra) placed in lead shield. Samples were packed into an U-8 container as mentioned above with the height of 1, 3, or 5 cm. Certified reference materials (CRM) of soil sample for 134Cs, 137Cs, and 40K packed into U-8 containers with the height of 1, 3, and 5 cm (JSAC 0471, 0472, 0473) were purchased from the Japan Society for Analytical Chemistry. The efficiencies of γ-ray measurements for 134Cs and 137Cs were obtained from the measurement of the CRM. The activity of 134Cs and 137Cs was calculated using Spectrum Explorer software (Mirion Technologies, Canberra). The net count of the 605 keV peak of 134Cs exceeded 1000 counts, except for 3 U-8 samples and one mixed sample collected at site D. The uncertainty of the 134Cs/137Cs activity ratio was calculated using the error propagation formula.

Measurement of 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio

After purification of Cs from the soil material, the sample was applied to a Re filament with a TaO activator and carbon powder, as previously reported26,27. 135Cs and 137Cs were measured using TIMS (Triton XT, Thermo Fisher Scientific) equipped with a retarding potential quadrupole lens filter. The filament current was set in the range of 1.28 to 1.42 A. It was confirmed that the 138Ba/133Cs isotope ratio was less than 2.4 × 10− 8 for the sample containing 1 µg of Cs and 1 µg of Ba when the filament current was 1.42 A. Since ionization energy of Ba (5.2 eV) is higher than that of Cs (3.9 eV), Ba is more difficult to ionize under these filament current conditions. Therefore, isobaric interferences from 135Ba and 137Ba were negligible for 135Cs/137Cs measurements.

Results and discussion

Depth profile of 137Cs concentration

Figure 2 shows the depth profile of 137Cs concentrations collected in 2003 before the FDNPS accident at site A and 2017 after the FDNPS accident at site B. The 137Cs concentrations were decay-corrected to March 11, 2011.

Fig. 2Depth profile of 137Cs and 134Cs concentrations. The soil samples (a) were taken at site A in 2003 and site B in 2017 (b). The solid curve serves as a visual guide for exponential decay.

Depth profile of 137Cs and 134Cs concentrations. The soil samples (a) were taken at site A in 2003 and site B in 2017 (b). The solid curve serves as a visual guide for exponential decay.

It is considered that the primary source of 137Cs in the 2003 sample was the global fallout from the atmospheric nuclear weapons tests. The 137Cs concentration decreased exponentially to a depth of 15 cm. The 137Cs inventories in 0 − 5, 5 − 10, and 10–15 cm layers to the total 137Cs inventory in the 0–15 cm layer were 81%, 18%, and 1%, respectively. Despite the sampling being conducted 40 years after the ban on atmospheric nuclear weapon tests, most 137Cs existed in the 0–5 cm layer.

Also, for the 2017 sample, most 137Cs was concentrated in the upper layers, and the 137Cs concentration decreased exponentially with depth. The 137Cs inventories in 0 − 2, 2 − 4, 4 − 6, 6 − 8, 8 − 10, 10–15 cm layers of the total 137Cs inventory were 63%, 22%, 6.8%, 6.0%, 1.3%, and 0.3%, respectively. Both depth profiles indicated that over 80% of 137Cs was present within the top 5 cm, and 99% of 137Cs was present within the top 10 cm. Most 137Cs existed within the 0–10 cm layer in line with the reported depth profile of 137Cs in undisturbed land before and after the FDNPS accident38–40. The 137Cs inventory in the 0–10 cm layer collected in 2017 (1580 ± 73 Bq), was 1000 times higher than that collected in 2003 (1.6 ± 0.1 Bq). The 134Cs concentration decay-corrected to March 11, 2011 was comparable to the 137Cs concentration in all layers

Regional distribution of radiocesium

Figure 3 shows the 137Cs concentration (Bq/g) distribution measured for the U-8 samples. The 137Cs concentrations varied from 1.52 to 8.78 Bq/g, 0.73 to 1.60 Bq/g, and 0.04 to 0.85 Bq/g for sites B, C, and D, respectively. Among sites and within the same site, the 137Cs concentrations varied; for example, the maximum difference was 21 times for site D, indicating that the 137Cs contamination lacks uniformity.

Figure 3 also shows the distribution of the air dose rates at 1 cm and 1 m heights for sites B, C, and D. The 137Cs concentration reflected the high and low states of the air dose rate at a 1 cm height. A minor difference might result from natural radionuclides, such as 40K and uranium series in the soil. The air dose rates at 1 m height appeared to reflect 137Cs concentration of soil in a wider area than those at 1 cm height. Because γ-rays from 137Cs are poorly shielded by air but are attenuated by soil, the 1 cm measurement at ground level primarily detects radiation directly below the measurement point. However, at 1 m height, the γ-rays from 137Cs in the surrounding soil are also measured. For example, at site B, the average air dose rate at 1 cm was 0.56 ± 0.18 µSv/h and that at 1 m was 0.47 ± 0.05 µSv/h. According to aircraft monitoring results (decay corrected to November 16, 2017), the dose rate corrected to 1 m height is less than 0.1 µSv/h across the Tokai-mura area because it was measured at a 300 m height and averaged41. The dose rates at site D were comparable to those of aircraft monitoring, while those at sites B and C were higher than those of aircraft monitoring.

For the soil samples collected at sites C and D, four U-8 samples were mixed to homogenize them, and then sieved to remove stone and vegetation. The weighted average of 137Cs concentration was calculated based on the weight and the 137Cs concentration of the U-8 samples and compared with the 137Cs concentration determined for the mixed samples. Table 1 summarizes these results. The 137Cs concentrations of the mixed samples and the weighted average 137Cs concentrations were comparable for site C, where stone was scarce, and approximately 1.1% of vegetation was present. However, the 137Cs concentrations for the mixed samples were higher than those for the weighted average 137Cs concentrations for site D because the U-8 samples for site D contained a higher percentage of stones (19–35%), and the 137Cs concentration in stone is lower than in soil. The maximum difference in the 137Cs concentration was 21 times for U-8 samples and decreased 3.0 times (weighted average) and 3.2 times (mixed samples) for site D.

134Cs/137Cs activity ratios

Figure 4 shows the relationship between 134Cs and 137Cs radioactivity in the U-8 samples, where the activity values were corrected to March 11, 2011, and two datasets in which the 134Cs activity was below the detection limit were omitted. Although the activity varied widely, the relationship was linear, indicating that the 134Cs/137Cs activity ratio was constant. This result correlated with the results reported by Mikami et al.17, indicating that the primary 134Cs and 137Cs contamination source at these sites was the FDNPS accident.

Fig. 4Relationship between 134Cs and 137Cs activity in U-8 samples (the activity was decay corrected to March 11, 2011).

Relationship between 134Cs and 137Cs activity in U-8 samples (the activity was decay corrected to March 11, 2011).

The 134Cs/137Cs activity ratio (decay-corrected to March 11, 2011) for the U-8, mixed soil, and boring core samples (65 results) was constant for all samples in this study. The average was 1.01 ± 0.04 (2σ), correlating with the reported values for environmental samples taken in the eastern Japan after the accident, except for the northwest part of Futaba Town and Oshika Peninsula. This result indicates that this area was primarily contaminated by radiocesium from Units 2 and 3 of FDNPS13,15–18,21,22,26. The depth of the U-8 and mixed soil samples was 0–5 cm, and those of the boring core samples were 0 − 5, 0 − 10, and 0–15 cm. No differences in the 134Cs/137Cs activity ratios based on depth were observed.

It was considered that the 137Cs concentration comprised FDNPS and global fallout, and that the 134Cs concentration originated only from the FDNPS accident13,42. If global fallout impacted the 134Cs/137Cs radioactivity ratio, the ratio would decrease due to 137Cs from global fallout and would be more sensitive for areas with lower levels of contamination from the FDNPS accident. Since the relative SD for the observed 134Cs/137Cs radioactivity ratios was 4% (2σ), the 137Cs concentrations that make 4% decrease in the 134Cs/137Cs radioactivity ratio were calculated for the mixed soil samples. The lowest radiocesium case (134Cs: 0.266 ± 0.006 Bq/g, 137Cs: 0.260 ± 0.005 Bq/g) yielded the 137Cs concentration of 0.011 Bq/g, and the highest case (134Cs: 1.30 ± 0.04 Bq/g, 137Cs: 1.30 ± 0.05 Bq/g) yielded that of 0.054 Bq/g. According to the environmental monitoring report before the FDNPS accident, the 137Cs concentrations in the soil collected inside or near Tokai-mura in November 2010 were 0.00084 − 0.056 Bq/g, avg. 0.022 ± 0.020 Bq/g6. It is considered that accuracy is insufficient to distinguish 137Cs originating from global fallout and the FDNPS accident, as the effect of global fallout was almost within 2σ.

Site D is an urban area and open land, and the observed 137Cs concentration was relatively low. The average 137Cs concentration of the mixed soil samples was 0.59 ± 0.21 Bq/g. Environmental monitoring results of the 137Cs concentration in the soil before November 11, 2010 and after May 24, 2012 for the same district of site D (Funaishikawa − Tokai-mura, though not at the same location), were 0.0058 and 0.088 Bq/g, respectively43,44 (decay-corrected to March 11, 2011). The 134Cs concentration was reported as 0.056 Bq/g on May 24, 2012, and when decay-corrected to March 11, 2011, it was 0.084 Bq/g. The 134Cs/137Cs radioactivity ratio is 0.95, noticeably lower than that in our experiments. Here, if the 137Cs concentration before the FDNPS accident (0.0058) was subtracted from that after the accident (0.088), the 137Cs concentration and the 134Cs/137Cs radioactivity ratio from the 1 F accident would be 0.082 Bq/g and 1.02, respectively. The 134Cs/137Cs radioactivity ratio was consistent with our experiments, which were obtained for soil with higher 137Cs concentrations. Thus, the 134Cs/137Cs radioactivity ratio might be impacted by global fallout when the 137Cs concentration is less than 0.1 Bq/g.

Site B is located inside a bush near the coast and is the starting point of hill facing the sea breeze. It exhibited the highest 137Cs concentration in this study (avg. 5.55 ± 2.76 Bq/g). According to the simulation study, a plume from FDNPS migrated to this area from the Pacific Ocean during the morning of March 20, 2011, resulting in deposit contamination15. One of the environmental monitoring points is close to site B, and the reported 137Cs concentration of the 0–5 cm layer before the FDNPS accident was 0. 81 ± 0.17 mBq/g3–5. This concentration is 1/6852 of the post-accident concentration of the site, indicating that global fallout was probably too low to affect the 137Cs concentration and the 134Cs/137Cs activity ratio at site B.

135Cs/137Cs isotopic ratios

Table 2 summarizes the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratios, 137Cs concentrations, and 134Cs/137Cs activity ratios. High precision is required to evaluate the origin of 135Cs and 137Cs. Generally, TIMS measurement has the advantage of precision for isotope ratio measurement. However, the relative SDs of the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio measurements in this study were relatively high. This is due to the relatively low 135Cs and 137Cs concentrations in the soil samples, which results in a higher proportion of the naturally occurring 133Cs (137Cs/133Cs: 10− 7–10− 8). Therefore, the 135Cs peak overlapped with the peak tailing of 133Cs. The 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratios for sites B and D correlated within the margin of uncertainty (avg. 0.366 ± 0.004). Although the depths of the core boring samples collected at site B were 0–5 cm and 0–15 cm, no difference in the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio depending on the depth was observed. This value is also comparable to our previous results obtained for the soil samples collected just outside the fence of FDNPS (0.3625 ± 0.0013) [26]. This result indicates that the major source of 135Cs and 137Cs was also the FDNPS accident at these sites, and that the influence of global fallout on the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratios was too small to be detected.

The 137Cs concentration in the 5-cm deep soil before the FDNPS accident at the environmental monitoring site near site B was 0. 81 ± 0. 17 mBq/g ((2.5 ± 0.5) ×10− 16 g/g)3–5. It was reported that the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio originating from global fallout during the atmospheric testing of nuclear weapons was 2.62 ± 0.48 (decay corrected to March 11, 2011)31–33. These values were used to calculate the 135Cs concentration before the FDNPS accident as (6.6 ± 1.8) × 10− 16 g/g. When the 135Cs and 137Cs concentrations before the FDNPS accident were 6.6 × 10− 16 g/g and 2.5 × 10− 16, respectively, and these values were subtracted from the measured 135Cs and 137Cs concentrations, the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio from the 1 F accident at site B was 0.363–0.366, and the difference from the measured values was less than 0.3%. Similarly, using the same 137Cs concentration and 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio from global fallout, the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio from the FDNPS accident at site D was 0.357–0.366, a difference of less than 2%. This result agrees with the results that the measured 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratios were constant, and the influence of global fallout is too small to be detected. It is presumed that the radiocesium concentrations before the FDNPS accident at sites B and D are comparable to those at the environmental monitoring site near site B.

The 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratios for site C were noticeably higher than those for sites B and D, and the values varied. Figure 3 shows that the radiocesium contamination is not uniform, indicating that the mixture fraction of radiocesium from the FDNPS accident and global fallout may vary according to the location. Since the 134Cs/137Cs activity ratios primarily from the 1 F accident were approximately constant, it can be inferred that the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio from the FDNPS accident is also constant. Assuming that the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratios from global fallout and the FDNPS accident were 2.62 and 0.365, respectively, the 137Cs and 135Cs concentrations from the FDNPS accident and global fallout as well as the percentages of 135Cs and 137Cs from global fallout relative to the total were calculated for samples collected at site C (Table 3). The estimated 137Cs activity concentrations from global fallout were 0.0046–0.0126 Bq/g (avg. 0.007 ± 0.004 Bq/g). These 137Cs concentrations fall within the reported 137Cs concentrations before the FDNPS accident inside or near Tokai-mura (0.00052 Bq/g − 0.067 Bq/g) and did not influence the 134Cs/137Cs radioactivity ratio after the FDNPS accident, as mentioned above. The percentages of 137Cs from global fallout to the total were 0.4%−3.6% (avg. 1.0%). However, determining a few percentage of differences through radioactivity measurement is challenging and falls within the measurement uncertainty. The percentages of 135Cs from global fallout to total 135Cs were 3.0%−21.2% (avg. 7.5%), and it increased with depth. Since the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio from global fallout is relatively high (2.62), the effect of global fallout on the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio is measurable.

The average 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio and 3 times SD for samples collected sites B and D is 0.366 ± 0.010. The calculated 137Cs concentrations from global fallout to make the isotope ratio 0.376 are 0.00045, 0.0045, and 0.045 Bq/g for 0.1, 1, and 10 Bq/g of 137Cs in soil samples, respectively. According to the monitoring results provided by Ibaraki Prefecture, 137Cs concentrations inside or near Tokai-mura before the FDNPS accident were 0.010 ± 0.009 Bq/g43. This implies that global fallout is negligible when the 137Cs concentration is more than 2.2 Bq/g. However, some monitoring points indicated more than 0.01 Bq/g of 137Cs concentration before the FDNPS accidents, and the influence of global fallout should be therefore considered. The 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio was constant at site D, even though the 137Cs concentration was less than 2.2 Bq/g (avg.; 0.58 ± 0.21 Bq/g). Therefore, if monitoring results before the FDNPA accident show that the 137Cs concentration from global fallout is low, it does not affect the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio, even for soil samples with less than 2.2 Bq/g of 137Cs.

Conclusions

Systematic grid sampling of surface soil was carried out at three sites in Tokai-mura to assess the true state of radiocesium contamination in Tokai-mura. Although the 137Cs concentrations were non-uniform among and within sites (average 137Cs concentration of sites B, C, and D with standard deviation (2σ) are 5.55 ± 5.5, 1.06 ± 0.36, and 0.58 ± 0.42 Bq/g), the 134Cs/137Cs radioactivity ratio was constant (1.01 ± 0.04, decay corrected to March 11, 2011). This indicates that the 134Cs and 137Cs in Tokai-mura primarily originated from the FDNPS accident, and the content of 137Cs from global fallout was less than 4%. If a comparable amount of 137Cs to the site release standard, 0.1 Bq/g, originating from a decommissioned facility was contained in the soil, the 134Cs/137Cs activity ratio would decrease by more than 5% for < 2 Bq/g of soil. Therefore, the 134Cs/137Cs activity ratio is useful for identifying the origin entirely of Tokai-mura excluding hot spots. However, the estimated usable period is approximately10 years after the FDNPS accident.

The 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratios were approximately constant at sites B and D (0.366 ± 0.004) and were comparable to those determined near the FDNPS site. The 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratios at site C showed higher values (0.387 ± 0.025). The 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratios reported from global fallout as 2.62 ± 0.48 (decay-corrected to March 11, 2011)31–33 is higher than those from the FDNPS accident, and therefore, influenced the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio. In this study, the 137Cs concentrations in the soil samples were 0.26− 8.78 Bq/g. According to the monitoring results near and within Tokai-mura from 2007 to 2010 (before the FDNPS accident), 137Cs concentrations in soil varied from 5.2 × 10− 4 Bq/g to 6.7 × 10− 2 Bq/g, depending on the site. If the 137Cs concentration from global fallout was 5.2 × 10− 4 Bq/g, technically, these very low concentration values of the global fallout would not affect the measured isotope ratios. By contrast, 6.7 × 10− 2 Bq/g of 137Cs originating from global fallout could have increased the measured 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio. For the minimum and maximum 137Cs concentration observed in this study (0.26 Bq/g and 8.78 Bq/g), with the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio of 0.366 for FDNPS and 2.62 for the global fallout, and 137Cs concentration from global fallout of 6.7 × 10− 2 Bq/g, the calculated 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratios are 0.946 and 0.382, respectively.

The 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratios at site C were higher than that derived from FDNPS, indicating the presence of global fallout derived radiocesium before the 1 F accident. Furthermore, the observed increase in the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio with depth aligns with the expectation that radiocesium originating from global fallout has penetrated to deeper soil layers. Because the maximum observed 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio in this study was 0.446 ± 0.006, the maximum of the global fallout derived 137Cs at site C was 0.0126 Bq/g. There are no data of 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio originating from global fallout before the FDNPS accident for soil samples collected in Japan, therefore such measurements should be conducted.

In order to distinguish between 137Cs originating from a decommissioned facility and that from the background, the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio originating from the background should be treated as a constant. If the contribution from global fallout to the current 137Cs concentration in soil is less than 0.6%, the 135Cs/137Cs isotope ratio can be used to distinguish the origin of 137Cs.

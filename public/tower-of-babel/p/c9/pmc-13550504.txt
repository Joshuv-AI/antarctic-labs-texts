Correlations of PSEN1, PSEN2, and APP missense variants on the production of Aβ peptides against variant effect prediction
Authors: Jiyeon Song, Serina Yan, Sophia Liu, Adhvaith Sridhar, Kijung Sung, Joshua Pillai, Chengbiao Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractINTRODUCTIONEarly‐onset Alzheimer's disease (EOAD) is mostly caused by mutations in presenilin‐1/2 (PSEN1, PSEN2) and amyloid precursor protein (APP) genes. Both genetic and experimental evidence have supported the PSEN‐APP amyloid hypothesis as the leading pathogenesis of Alzheimer's disease (AD). Thus far, 276 missense variants from PSEN1, 2, and APP have been identified. Herein, we report the abilities of variant effect predictors (VEPs) for identifying the degree of pathogenicity among alleles linked with EOAD.METHODSWe performed pairwise correlations between 276 in vitro functional assays of missense variants from PSEN1, PSEN2, and APP and clinical data among EOAD patients against 37 VEPs. Furthermore, we used the predicted biophysical data of all three proteins to refine our understanding of the molecular basis of missense variants in dominant negative (DN) or loss‐of‐function (LoF) effects.RESULTSWe found that VEPs were consistently correlated with age at onset (AAO) among EOAD patients and the amyloid‐β (Aβ) 42/Aβ40 ratio biomarker. Regarding the ratio, we observed discrepancies in the predictor variables influencing the Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio, with conflicting evidence as to whether the elevated ratio stems from diminished Aβ40 levels or augmented Aβ42 production. We also identified that with increased predicted pathogenicity, there were decreased Aβ38 levels. Conversely, this study found no direct correlations with Aβ37 and Aβ43. Lastly, structural studies show characteristics of both DN and LoF mechanisms, as there are variants positioned in buried hydrophobic domains and charged surfaces.DISCUSSIONCurrently, a major clinical challenge in the field is the lack of reliable approaches for predicting the pathogenicity of variants in EOAD. Our study also illuminates the need for experimental studies to identify the predictor variable causing the increased Aβ42/Aβ40 biomarker.Highlights We performed correlations of 37 VEPs against in vitro data of EOAD variants.VEPs demonstrate potential as diagnostic tools for early onset Alzheimer's disease.Pathogenic missense variants show characteristics of DN and LoF effects.

BACKGROUND

Presenilin (PSEN) genes have been identified as major genetic linkages with early onset familial Alzheimer's disease (EOAD). 1 They encode the catalytic subunit of γ‐secretase, which cleaves type I transmembrane proteins such as amyloid precursor protein (APP). While APP is essential for synaptic plasticity, its cleavage releases amyloid‐β (Aβ) peptides. 2 Pathogenic accumulation of Aβ peptides is mediated by the sequential cleavage of APP, a transmembrane protein encoded on chromosome 21. 3 This process is driven by the γ‐secretase complex and its catalytic subunits, presenilin‐1 (PSEN1) and presenilin‐2 (PSEN2). Although most EOAD cases arise from mutations in PSEN1, APP alleles are often located near secretase cleavage sites and directly leading to Aβ production of more aggregable isoforms. 4 Similarly, PSEN2 variants, although rarer and more associated with more variable ages of onset, offer unique biochemical insights into the disease. 5 Along with APP and PSEN1, these genes are the foundation of the amyloid cascade hypothesis. 6 While the wild‐type function of these genes is essential to neuronal survival, the pathogenic impact of missense variants follows a distinct structural and functional dichotomy. Loss‐of‐function (LoF) effects are generally driven by mutations in buried, hydrophobic residues that destabilize the protein core, leading to misfolding and degradation. 7 Conversely, dominant‐negative (DN) effects are frequently associated with exposed, charged residues on the protein surface. These surface variants typically preserve overall structural integrity but disrupt critical protein‐protein or protein–ligand interactions, allowing the mutant protein to interfere with the wild‐type complex. 8

In the landmark reporting from Sun et al., 9 there was a lack of statistical evidence between the Aβ42/Aβ40 levels and age at onset (AAO) among AD patients. In Szaruga et al., 10 a unifying model was postulated where mutations led to destabilization of the γ‐secretase in addition to other environmental factors to form amyloidogenic Aβ. Likewise, this model has received experimental support from Petit et al., 11 identifying trends among the entire Aβ spectrum. Petit et al. 11 suggest that the negative data from Sun et al. 9 are due to the experimental conditions of the cells in vitro. Importantly, their study discovered a linear relationship between the Aβ (37 + 38 + 40) / (42 + 43) ratio and AAO. Based on our current understanding, we still have not identified the minimum Aβ profile changes required to impact AAO, thereby impeding direct usage in therapeutic settings and genetic counseling.

Over the past decade, missense variant effect predictors (VEPs) have been developed to predict missense mutations pathogenicity based on sequential patterns, evolutionary conservation, and recently secondary structure. These in silico approaches have broad applicability in precision medicine applications, and have been used for numerous Mendelian disorders. 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 , 16 In particular, AlphaMissense (AM) has shown correlation with in vitro functional assays, the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics classification, and other genetic factors. 17 Previously, we performed brief pairwise correlations between CADD, AM, ESM‐1b, and EVE with the 114 variants of unknown significance (VUS) of PSEN1, PSEN2, and APP, and found that increased Aβ42/Aβ40 levels were driven by decreased Aβ40 levels, but lacked statistical significance for individual Aβ levels. 18 The main limitation was the limited sample size of variants, predictors, and biochemical/clinical data used for analysis.

Herein, we conducted extensive correlational analyses of 37 VEPs (Table 1) with in vitro functional assays of Aβ42, 40, 38, and 37 along with AAO clinical data from EOAD patients. We also performed pairwise correlations with biophysical data of these proteins against clinical and biochemical levels. This cross‐sectional study is critical to providing validation of the translational capabilities of VEPs for AD, and refine our understanding of the presenilin hypothesis.

METHODS

In vitro functional assays, patient data, and variant effect predictors

All in vitro functional data for PSEN1, PSEN2, and APP variants were based on published studies. We aggregated assays for 138 PSEN1 variants from Sun et al. 9 and 36 additional missense variants from Petit et al. 11 We further included 56 PSEN1, 33 PSEN2, and 25 APP variants from our previous work, derived from Hsu et al. 19 and Marsh et al. 20 While the combined PSEN1 datasets across these sources provided 218 total entries, filtering the redundancy yielded 177 unique PSEN1 missense variants. After consolidating all sources and removing duplicates across all three genes (276 variants), the final analytical dataset is comprised of 235 unique missense variants listed. Clinical datasets were plotted against in vitro experimental values using stratified analysis to assess the predictive value of the cellular model. The AAO among patients with PSEN1 variants were obtained from Sun et al. 9 Lastly, data for all VEPs were obtained from dbNSFP, 21 specifically “rankscore” columns. Even though there are duplicate variants reported from previously published studies, owing to differing experimental conditions and AD patients, we did not remove these variants from our correlational analyses.

Biophysical structures

Since there are no full‐length structures of all three amyloidogenic proteins reported from the Protein Data Bank, we used AlphaFold 3 (AF3) via the AlphaFold Server to create high‐accuracy prediction templates. 22 We then superimposed Cryo‐EM structures of PSEN1 at 2.40 Å (PDB: 8KCS) and PSEN2 at 3.00 Å (PDB: 7Y5X) for validation and refinement of AF3 models. The DSSP library calculated relative solvent accessibilities (RSA) of residues. 23

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses performed in this study used GraphPad Prism (version 10.6.1). All data are shown as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Unless stated otherwise, α = 0.05 and p‐values are indicated in the corresponding figures and tables. Spearman's correlation was calculated in the scipy library. For unbalanced sample sizes, a Kruskal–Wallis test followed by Dunn's post‐hoc test was used for all pairwise comparisons. The source data for the RSA and clinical data are provided in the Supplemental data (GitHub).

RESULTS

Variant effect predictors correlate strongly with age of onset and Aβ42/Aβ40 levels, but not with individual Aβ levels among amyloidogenic variants

Using the aggregated clinical and functional assay data for PSEN1, PSEN2, and APP missense variants, we performed pairwise correlations against all 37 VEPs. Within each heatmap, we have provided the Spearman's correlation value for each individual predictor. In Figure S1–5, we correlated each individual predictor against all outcomes, including biochemical Aβ levels and clinical data as heatmaps. Throughout the main text, we have displayed the visualizations for the exemplary correlations of AM to summarize the broad associations for each gene. We chose to display AM as it is the most accurate VEP to date.

Beginning with the PSEN1 variants from Sun et al., 9 we have identified multiple correlations in predictive pathogenicity and outcomes among each gene. AAO served as the primary predictor variable, showing a significant negative correlation across the majority of VEPs (32/37; 86.48%) (Figure 1A). Specifically, AM scores were significantly correlated with AAO; mutations with rank scores approaching 1.0, which are classified as ‘pathogenic’, were consistently associated with an earlier age of disease onset. For the critical Aβ42/Aβ40 levels in PSEN1 variants, the majority of algorithms had a significantly positive correlation (29/37; 78.37%) (Figure 1B). Our analyses sought to further refine the cause of increased Aβ42/Aβ40 levels with individual Aβ42 and Aβ40 levels (Figure 1C–D). In Aβ42, more than 80% of VEPs failed to detect significant reductions with increased predicted pathogenicity (6/37; 16.22%). Similarly, most techniques lacked significance with Aβ40 levels (18/37; 48.65%). However, we identify stronger negative associations with Aβ40 (Figure S3), supporting our previous hypothesis from Pillai et al. 18 that the increased Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio is driven by a marked decrease in Aβ40 levels in PSEN1.

Our analyses continued with those PSEN1 variants from Petit et al. 11 with emphasis on correlations with Aβ37, Aβ38, and Aβ43 (Figure 2A–C). We identified no significant correlations between the VEPs and Aβ37 levels (5/37; 13.51%). More than half of all algorithms detected a significant negative correlation with Aβ38 levels (23/37; 62.16%). Likewise, only a minority of variants demonstrated positive correlations with Aβ43 levels (7/37; 18.92%). Overall, there is generally weak significance among VEPs and Aβ37, Aβ38, and Aβ43 levels among PSEN1 variants. Next, our correlational analyses sought to validate the data from Sun et al. 9 Similarly to Aβ37 levels, we consistently showed no significant reductions in Aβ40 levels (2/37; 5.41%) (Figure 1D). Even more astonishing is that more than half of all VEPs had positive correlations with Aβ42 levels (20/37; 54.05%) (Figure 2D). Overall, there appears to be major discrepancies observed in the Aβ40 and Aβ42 levels between the variant samples of Petit et al. 11 and Sun et al. 9 for the underlying mechanism for increased Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio in PSEN1 variants and upholds the postulate proposed from Petit et al. 11

Lastly, we sought to resolve this controversy for the Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio using the sample from our previous study, Pillai et al., 18 shown in Figure S2. As expected, the majority of predictors were significantly positively correlated with Aβ42/Aβ40 levels (34/37; 91.89%). From individual Aβ40 levels, we detected a strongly negative correlation in most correlations (33/37; 89.19%) that is likely to increase the ratio. Likewise, Aβ42 levels were not significantly correlated for most VEPs (2/37; 5.41%).

Our analyses continued to APP and PSEN2 variants from Pillai et al. 18 For APP, we found that the Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio was significant (20/37; 54.05%) (Figure S5). Unfortunately, we did not detect significant trends in the Aβ42 (4/37; 10.81%) and Aβ40 levels (1/37; 2.70%) that led to this strong correlation. This is likely owing to the limited sample size of APP variants (n = 25). In contrast, the PSEN2 variants showed Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio that was significantly positively correlated (18/37; 48.65%), and was led by marked increases in Aβ42 (24/37; 64.86%) and insignificant Aβ40 levels (3/37; 8.11%) (Figure S4). These PSEN2 correlations align with the mechanistic conclusions previously reported by Petit et al. 11 and Fernandez et al. 24 who utilized similar predictors of dysfunction across autosomal dominant AD (ADAD) causal genes.

Biophysical analyses reveal missense variants associated with EOAD support both loss‐of‐function and dominant negative mechanism

In Figure 3A, we display biophysical structures of all three proteins superimposed to Cryo‐EM structures, indicating near perfect similarity. We calculated the RSA of the wild‐type residue for which mutations occur. We first identified that AAO is not correlated with surface exposure in PSEN1 (ρ = 0.1278; p = 0.1735) (Figure 3B). Conversely, as surface exposure increased, the total activity of the PSEN1 increased significantly (ρ = 0.2148; p = 0.0157) (Figure 3C). In Table 2, we report the summary of pairwise correlations between surface exposure (RSA) of missense variants and Aβ levels. While we identified significant positive correlation with individual Aβ42, Aβ40 levels in the Sun et al. 9 dataset, the Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio failed to reach significance. Conversely, all variables from Petit et al. 11 were insignificant when correlated with RSA. These mixed findings suggested that RSA does not appear to be a strong predictor of functional effects among EOAD mutations.

Finally, we sought to stratify the EOAD‐associated missense variants based on side‐chain properties of the wild‐type residue to further contextualize mutational effects. We aggregated all variants by gene, accounting for duplicate variants from independent samples, and showed the total count by RSA in Figure 3D for PSEN1 and Figure S6–7 for APP and PSEN2. We have set a threshold at 0.25 to indicate whether the residue type is buried or surface exposed. For PSEN1, we observe that mutations occurring on positively (Arg, His) and negatively (Asp, Glu) charged residues were generally exposed on the surface of the protein. By the same token, alleles occurring in hydrophobic residues were more buried in PSEN1, with the exception of the Trp residues.

In silico, in vitro metrics align with Alzforum clinically classified AD‐causing alleles

Lastly, we expanded our validation studies by determining the pairwise comparison of clinically verified alleles from the Alzforum, which include benign, pathogenic, and VUS, with a VEP, AM, and the in vitro metric, Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio.

Computational AM rankscores demonstrated a consistent ability to distinguish pathogenic mutations from benign controls (Figure 4A–C). For PSEN1 and APP, pathogenic variants clustered were predicted as pathogenic that was significantly greater than benign alleles. Similar correlations were observed for PSEN2, where pathogenic variants maintained higher scoring despite smaller sample sizes. Similar to the rankscore trend, we observed a similar alignment between biochemical profiles and clinical outcomes across all three genes (Figure 4D–F). Pathogenic variants exhibited significantly elevated Aβ42/Aβ40 ratios compared to benign alleles that remained at baseline, respectively.

DISCUSSION

In this study, we performed extensive pairwise correlations of in vitro assays and patient data for pathogenic variants of PSEN1, PSEN2, and APP with VEPs and biophysical models. This study was motivated by the lack of diagnostic approaches to characterize variants in EOAD, leading to extensive analyses of VEPs. We make an important contribution to translating the landmark findings of Sun et al. 9 and Petit et al. 11 to identify broad clinical correlations for EOAD. We show consistently that a more detrimental VEP score is correlated with increased Aβ42/Aβ40 levels. However, the cause for this correlation is debated among individual Aβ42 and Aβ40 levels. Our data also indicate that VEPs are well‐correlated with AAO, a crucial predictor variable in precision medicine approaches for EOAD. Lastly, using predicted biophysical structures of each amyloidogenic gene, we show that missense variants display hallmarks of both DN and LoF effects to the complex. Therefore, this study offers wide‐spread validation of VEPs and biophysical techniques for precision medicine applications for EOAD.

The potential cause(s) for increased Aβ42/Aβ40 levels are of particular controversy. Our previous study 18 and Sun et al. 9 both agree that decreased Aβ40 levels caused the increased ratio levels, while Petit et al. 11 identified increased Aβ42 levels as the driving factor. Surprisingly, the PSEN2 data from Pillai et al. (2025) were in agreement with the conclusion from Sun et al. (2016), as we observed a negative correlation for Aβ42 in these variants. Petit et al. (2022) argued that the findings from Sun et al. (2016) were inconclusive owing to experimental conditions, such as detergent extraction, which may have masked absolute Aβ42 production. While our overall pairwise correlations appear in agreement with Petit et al. (2022) regarding the ratio's importance, our individual peptide data for PSEN2 suggest a more complex mechanism, potentially reflecting the limitations of static biophysical models in representing dynamic enzymatic cleavage. Further experimental studies are necessary to gain an understanding of whether decreased Aβ40 or increased Aβ42 levels cause this important biomarker of EOAD.

Our study also shows the potential of VEPs to aid in clinical diagnostics among EOAD variants. Nevertheless, it remains to be determined whether VEPs have the ability to decipher mutation pathogenicity among variants with varying levels of pathogenic effects. In our data, though there is a negative logarithmic relation between predictors and AAO, it is important to note that a few variants are predicted as benign and occur at both earlier and late onset (∼40s, ∼60s) despite being pathogenic from Sun et al. (2016). Therefore, further studies are necessary to correlate VEPs and AAO. Additionally, we performed a pairwise comparison of AM rankscores, Aβ42/Aβ40, sourced from Sun et al. (2016), Petit et al. (2022), and Pillai et al. (2025), and clinically validated pathogenicity data from Alzforum. We observed a significant alignment between these metrics; specifically, benign variants exhibited lower Aβ ratios and rank scores, while pathogenic variants showed a marked increase in both. This observed parallel between in vitro, in silico, and clinical results suggests that integrating computational predictions with clinical phenotypes can significantly enhance the translational value of AD research. We also suggest pairwise correlations with longer Aβ strands, as suggested by Petit et al. (2022), which may be beneficial for molecular diagnostic approaches.

Lastly, we attempted to address the crucial question of the presenilin hypothesis: “Do pathogenic variants exhibit DN effects or a LoF mechanism?” Structurally, there are pathogenic variants occurring on buried hydrophobic residues, indicative of LoF effects. However, there are a minority of variants located on the surface, specifically those occurring on arginine. It is possible that these surface variants alter signaling, demonstrating DN effects. Taken together, we argue that future studies should emphasize deciphering mechanistic heterogeneity for EOAD.

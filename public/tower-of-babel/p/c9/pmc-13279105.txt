Prognostic Value of Angiographic Microcirculatory Resistance After Coronary Intervention: Insights From the FAVOR III China Trial
Authors: Hongliang Zhang, Wence Shi, Zhenyan Zhao, Guannan Niu, Zhengrong Liu, Changdong Guan, Lihua Xie, Tongqiang Zou, Xiang Cheng, Ning Guo, Fei Ye, Liansheng Wang, Jie Mi, Shengxian Tu, Junbo Ge, Lei Song, Yongjian Wu, the FAVOR III China Study Group
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundA novel computational angiographic microcirculatory resistance (AMR) derived from a single angiographic view presents a feasible alternative to the pressure wire‐based index of microcirculatory resistance. However, its prognostic significance in patients undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) remains insufficiently established.MethodsThis is a post hoc analysis of 3404 patients undergoing PCI from the FAVOR III China (Comparison of Quantitative Flow Ratio Guided and Angiography Guided Percutaneous Intervention in Patients With Coronary Artery Disease) trial. Pre‐ and post‐PCI AMR were measured in target vessels, with percentage change in AMR before and after PCI calculated as (100×[post‐PCI AMR−pre‐PCI AMR]/pre‐PCI AMR). The primary model used was the log‐rank test, and the proportional hazards model was also used to assess the association between AMR and the 3‐year risk of major adverse cardiac events, defined as a composite of all‐cause death, myocardial infarction, or ischemia‐driven revascularization.ResultsPatients with percentage change in AMR before and after PCI ≥85 (23.7%) versus <85 (76.3%) had comparable baseline characteristics but received more and longer stents per patient. Overall major adverse cardiac events risk was similar between groups (14.8% versus 12.4%; hazard ratio [HR], 1.18 [0.95–1.45]; log‐rank P=0.064). However, in patients with post‐PCI AMR ≥250, percentage change in AMR before and after PCI ≥85 showed a significant increase in the major adverse cardiac events risk (16.3% versus 10.8%; HR, 1.52 [1.14–2.04]), contrasting with no difference when post‐PCI AMR <250 (12.3% versus 13.4%; HR, 0.89 [0.63–1.25]; P interaction=0.019).ConclusionsIn patients undergoing PCI from the FAVOR III China population, significant AMR elevation (percentage change in AMR before and after PCI ≥85) in target vessels alone did not predict outcomes, but in the subgroup with post‐PCI AMR ≥250 it identified patients at increased 3‐year cardiovascular risk.REGISTRATION https://www.clinicaltrials.gov; Unique identifier: NCT03656848.

The coronary microcirculation network, which is a network of vessels <400 μm in diameter, governs ≈80% of myocardial vascular resistance and critically regulates blood flow distribution to meet metabolic demands. 1 , 2 Although percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) effectively addresses epicardial stenosis and improves clinical outcomes, 3 procedural instrumentation inevitably releases particulate debris and vasoactive mediators that may obstruct the coronary microcirculation. 4 , 5 This iatrogenic coronary microvascular dysfunction not only impairs immediate perfusion but predicts long‐term adverse outcomes, 6 as elevated postprocedural microcirculatory resistance consistently correlates with increased cardiovascular risk. 7 , 8 Therefore, accurate assessment of coronary microcirculatory function has become a critical focus for clinicians.

Although the index of microcirculatory resistance remains the gold standard for microvascular assessment, its requirement for invasive pressure‐wire measurements under hyperemia limits routine clinical application. 9 , 10 Emerging angiographic solutions now enable microcirculatory evaluation without adjunct devices. Building on validated correlations between quantitative flow ratio (QFR) and wire‐based fractional flow reserve, 11 , 12 we developed the measurement of angiographic microcirculatory resistance (AMR), a novel metric derived solely from standard angiography that eliminates the need for pressure–temperature sensor wires or hyperemic agents. 13 Initial validations demonstrate strong AMR‐index of microcirculatory resistance correlations across coronary disease phenotypes and suggest prognostic usefulness in exploratory studies. 14 This operator‐friendly, nonhyperemic approach thus presents an attractive alternative warranting large‐scale clinical validation.

The recently published FAVOR III China (Comparison of Quantitative Flow Ratio Guided and Angiography Guided Percutaneous Intervention in Patients With Coronary Artery Disease) trial established that QFR‐guided PCI significantly improves outcomes compared with angiography‐alone strategies. 15 , 16 Leveraging this well‐characterized cohort, we conducted the first large‐scale analysis to determine whether AMR measurements before and after PCI could stratify long‐term cardiovascular risk in contemporary clinical practice.

METHODS

Study Design

This post hoc analysis used data from the FAVOR III China trial (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT03656848; date of trial registration: August 31, 2018), an investigator‐initiated, multicenter, randomized sham‐controlled study comparing QFR‐guided versus angiography‐guided PCI strategies. The design of the FAVOR III China trial has been published previously, and the original trial protocol is available as a Supplementary File accompanying the primary results publication. 15 , 17 The protocol was approved by the ethics committee at each participating site and was performed in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines of the China National Medical Products Administration. Written informed consent was secured from every participant before randomization. Access to individual participant data from this randomized controlled trial is restricted to protect participant confidentiality in accordance with the informed consent agreements and ethical approvals. A standalone statistical analysis plan was developed for the main trial but is not publicly available. Requests for further details on the statistical analysis plan or the original trial data may be directed to the corresponding authors or the trial steering committee. Researchers interested in accessing the data may submit a proposal to the corresponding authors, which will require approval from the trial’s data access committee and a signed data use agreement.

Patient Population

This study enrolled adult patients (aged ≥18 years) scheduled to undergo PCI based on standard coronary angiography findings. Key inclusion criteria included stable or unstable angina, or myocardial infarction occurring at least 72 hours before screening. Patients were required to have at least 1 coronary lesion with a diameter stenosis of 50% to 90% and a reference vessel diameter of ≥2.5 mm, as determined by visual assessment. Exclusion criteria included moderate to severe chronic kidney disease (defined as creatinine >150 μmol/L or estimated glomerular filtration rate <45 mL/kg per 1.73 m2), severe heart failure, allergy to contrast agents or study medications, pregnancy, or an expected survival of <1 year. Angiographic exclusion criteria encompassed severe vessel tortuosity, overlap in the stenosed segment, or suboptimal imaging quality that would preclude accurate computation of QFR. In the present study, 199 patients were excluded due to the determination of no requirement for PCI based on QFR assessment, and an additional 222 patients were excluded due to the unavailability of target vessel angiographic images. Consequently, a total of 3404 patients were included in the final analysis (Figure 1).

Randomization and Procedures

In the FAVOR III China trial, all angiographic assessments were performed following standardized protocols to ensure rigor and reproducibility. Before randomization, operators identified target vessels and lesions intended for PCI based on angiographic findings. Eligible patients were then randomized in a 1:1 ratio to either QFR‐guided or standard angiography‐guided PCI groups. Randomization was stratified by key clinical and anatomical factors, including diabetes, multivessel disease, the presence of high‐grade stenosis (>90% diameter stenosis with TIMI [Thrombolysis In Myocardial Infarction] flow grade <3), and study center.

Angiographic and QFR data were analyzed offline by an independent core laboratory (cardiovascular interventional diagnosis and treatment image analysis core laboratory platform, Fuwai Hospital, Beijing, China) for all randomized participants, using the AngioPlus software (version 3, Pulse Medical). For the QFR‐guided group, 2 angiographic projections with at least a 25° difference in angle were analyzed for QFR computation in real time. PCI was performed in lesions with a QFR ≤0.80 or a diameter stenosis ≥90%. If no lesion met these criteria, PCI was deferred. In the angiography‐guided group, PCI decisions were based on visual assessment according to the standard practice at each participating center.

To ensure blinding and eliminate procedural bias, all patients wore music‐playing headphones during catheterization, and a 10‐minute delay was incorporated for either true or sham QFR measurements. Patients, postprocedural health care providers, and outcome assessors remained blinded to treatment allocation throughout follow‐up. Optimal medical therapy was uniformly prescribed in both groups during the study period.

Follow‐up evaluations were performed at 1 and 6 months after discharge, and annually up to 3 years postrandomization.

AMR Computation

AMR computation was systematically performed for all enrolled patients undergoing PCI. In cases with single treated lesions (n=2584, 75.9%), the corresponding vessel underwent angiographic analysis. For multilesion interventions (n=820, 24.1%), vessel selection prioritized the left anterior descending artery, followed by left circumflex and right coronary arteries. Pre‐ and post‐PCI AMR values were obtained by analyzing angiographic images of the target vessel taken from the same projection angle before and immediately after PCI. Percentage change in AMR before and after PCI (AMRΔ%), calculated as 100×(post‐PCI AMR−pre‐PCI AMR)/pre‐PCI AMR, was used to quantify the direction and magnitude of PCI‐induced microcirculatory alterations.

All AMR computations used the AngioPlus Core software (version 3; Pulse Medical) based on single‐view μQFR analysis. Certified analysts at an independent core laboratory (Fuwai Hospital, Beijing, China) conducted blinded assessments following standardized protocols. The methodological details of AMR computation have been previously reported in the literature. 13 Briefly, based on the pressure drop, the distal coronary pressure can be obtained as the product of the mean aortic pressure and μQFR, which is then divided by the hyperemic coronary flow velocity to compute AMR. First, the optimal angiographic view with minimal vessel overlap is selected, and the system automatically delineates the target vessel's contour. The contrast flow velocity is derived by dividing the vessel centerline length by the contrast filling time, which is then converted into hyperemic flow velocity. 18 Subsequently, a frame with optimal contrast filling is selected as the key frame, and the single‐view μQFR is calculated based on the Murray bifurcation fractal law. 19 , 20 Finally, in line with previous studies, we used 86 mm Hg as the aortic pressure value under maximal hyperemia. 21 Figure 2 showed a representative example of target vessel AMR assessment.

Clinical End Points

The primary end point was the 3‐year rate of major adverse cardiac events (MACE), defined as a composite of all‐cause death, myocardial infarction, and ischemia‐driven revascularization. To account for the potential influence of invasive procedures and stenting on clinical outcomes, a secondary end point was defined as the 3‐year rate of MACE excluding periprocedural myocardial infarction related to the index or planned staged procedures. Other secondary end points included the individual components of MACE and stent thrombosis. Specific definitions of each of the clinical end points have been previously reported. 15 All clinical events were adjudicated by an independent clinical events committee comprising experts blinded to treatment allocation.

Safety Event Ascertainment

The clinical events comprising the primary end point of this analysis, all‐cause death, myocardial infarction, and ischemia‐driven revascularization, were adjudicated safety outcomes from the parent FAVOR III China trial. In the original trial, all potential safety events were systematically reported by the investigative sites and subsequently adjudicated by an independent, blinded clinical events committee using prespecified standardized definitions detailed in the trial protocol. For the present post hoc analysis, we used the final, clinical events committee‐adjudicated event data from the locked trial database. No additional event adjudication was performed specifically for this analysis. The assessment of the association between angiographic microcirculatory resistance parameters and these adjudicated events was performed as described in the Statistical Analysis section.

Statistical Analysis

All analyses were conducted in the intention‐to‐treat population. Continuous variables were summarized as mean±SD for normally distributed data or median with interquartile range for nonnormally distributed data. Normality of the data was assessed using the Shapiro‐Wilk test. Comparisons of continuous variables between groups were performed using independent‐samples t tests for normally distributed data or the Mann‐Whitney U test for nonnormally distributed data, as appropriate. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages and were compared using the χ2 test or Fisher exact test in cases where the expected frequencies in contingency tables were ≤5. Receiver operating characteristic curves and areas under curve values were used for primary end point prediction, with the optimal cutoff determined to maximize sensitivity and specificity.

Time‐to‐event data were analyzed using the primary outcome model, the Kaplan‐Meier method, with comparisons between groups conducted using the log‐rank test. Hazard ratios (HRs) with corresponding 95% CIs were estimated using multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression models. Sensitivity analyses of the primary and major secondary end points were done by multivariable Cox regression: model 1 included study center as a random effect, model 2 included the 3 stratification factors (diabetes, multivessel disease, and the presence of any vessel with diameter stenosis >90% with TIMI flow <3) as fixed effects and study center as a random effect, and model 3 included the 3 stratification factors (diabetes, multivessel disease, and the presence of any vessel with diameter stenosis >90% with TIMI flow <3) and additional baseline covariates (age, sex, hypertension, hypercholesterolemia, previous myocardial infarction, clinical presentation, and Synergy Between PCI With Taxus and Cardiac Surgery score) as fixed effects and study center as a random effect. The analyses of the 3 models were conducted in accordance with the prespecified standard analytical framework of the FAVOR III China trial.

All statistical tests were 2‐tailed, and a P value <0.05 was considered indicative of statistical significance unless otherwise specified. All statistical analyses were performed using R software, version 4.2.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

RESULTS

Baseline Characteristics and Grouping

Between December 25, 2018 and January 19, 2020, a total of 5881 patients were screened for participation in the study, with 3847 successfully enrolled. The details of the screen failure reasons have been previously reported. Of the enrolled participants, 22 withdrew consent, leaving 3825 patients randomized into the intention‐to‐treat population. For the present analysis, 199 patients without PCI procedure were excluded, and another 222 patients were excluded because of nonlegible angiography (Figure 1). The mean pre‐PCI and post‐PCI target vessel AMRs for all patients were 194.1 (IQR, 104.7–283.5) and 247.9 (IQR, 176.9–318.0), respectively, whereas the mean AMRΔ% was 53.4 (IQR, −22.4 to 129.2). All 3 variables exhibited a nonnormal distribution. Figure S1 shows the distributions of pre‐PCI AMR, post‐PCI AMR, and AMRΔ% in all patients as assessed by the core laboratory.

The cutoff values for post‐PCI AMR and AMRΔ% were initially determined as 248.0 and 84.1, respectively, based on receiver operating characteristic curve analysis. (Figure S2). To better align with clinical practice and former conclusion, we adopted rounded thresholds of 250 for post‐PCI AMR and 85 for AMRΔ%. Using these cut points, 1530 patients (44.9%) had a post‐PCI AMR ≥250, whereas 1874 patients (55.1%) had a post‐PCI AMR <250. Similarly, 806 patients (23.7%) had an AMRΔ% ≥85, whereas 2598 patients (76.3%) had an AMRΔ% <85. Baseline characteristics were not significantly different between patients with high and low post‐PCI AMR, as well as between those with high and low AMRΔ% (Table 1). Furthermore, no significant differences in baseline characteristics were observed between the subgroups stratified by either post‐PCI AMR or AMRΔ% status across the randomization groups. Procedures

The procedural details of patients with different levels of post‐PCI AMR or AMRΔ% are presented in Table 2. Compared with patients with AMR <250, those with AMR ≥250 had slightly shorter and larger stents and were more likely to achieve complete functional revascularization as indicated by residual functional Synergy Between PCI With Taxus and Cardiac Surgery score (residual functional Synergy Between PCI With Taxus and Cardiac Surgery score of 0, as assessed by core laboratory offline QFR measurements in both groups). Although among patients with AMRΔ% ≥85, they had a lower proportion of transradial approach, had more and longer stents implanted, and overall required a greater amount of contrast agent and longer procedural time than patients with AMRΔ% <85. However, there was no difference between the 2 groups in achieving complete functional revascularization.

Clinical Outcomes

The 3‐year follow‐up was available in 97.2% patients with a median follow‐up of 1021 days (Q1: 1125 days, Q3: 1125 days). There were no significant differences in MACE (Figure 3A), all‐cause death, myocardial infarction, or ischemia‐driven revascularization (Table S5) between the high and low post‐PCI AMR groups. Similarly, no differences were observed between the 2 groups in the major secondary outcome (Figure 3B). In regard to the AMRΔ% groups, patients with AMRΔ% ≥85 exhibited a trend toward a higher risk of events across all end points, with no statistical significance (Figure 3C,D and Table S6).

DISCUSSION

Principal Findings

This study is the first to validate the clinical value of AMR in a large population. Although elevated post‐PCI AMR (≥250) showed no independent association with 3‐year MACE, a critical interaction emerged. Patients with both post‐PCI AMR ≥250 and significant AMR elevation (Δ% ≥85) demonstrated substantially increased cardiovascular risk (16.3% versus 10.8%; HR, 1.52 [95% CI, 1.14–2.04]). This threshold effect underscores the clinical relevance of procedural microembolization quantified through AMR dynamics.

Divergence From Prior Evidence

Contrary to previous reports linking elevated microcirculatory resistance with adverse outcomes, 7 , 8 our findings derive from a predominantly stable coronary artery disease population (94.5% non‐acute coronary syndrome) with lower baseline risk. Earlier studies focused on acute coronary syndrome cohorts where plaque vulnerability heightens embolic risk. 22 The FAVOR III China cohort's unique profile, characterized by elective procedures and stabilized plaques, likely attenuates the absolute prognostic signal of post‐PCI AMR alone. This population heterogeneity necessitates reinterpretation of microcirculatory indices' clinical usefulness.

Threshold Paradox and Hemodynamic Insights

The observed threshold paradox, where elevated post‐PCI AMR values (300 or 350) were unexpectedly associated with better outcomes (see Figure S3), highlights the complex nature of microcirculatory physiology. This phenomenon may result from multiple hemodynamic factors: after the resolution of epicardial stenosis, the QFR typically improves significantly, whereas hyperemic flow velocity increases to a lesser extent. This discrepancy can mathematically inflate the AMR value, although overall myocardial perfusion has objectively improved. Moreover, patients with successful restoration of epicardial blood flow may develop compensatory mechanisms that allow them to tolerate elevated microvascular resistance without experiencing adverse events.

Therefore, the relationship between AMR and clinical risk is not simply linear. An elevated post‐PCI AMR does not necessarily indicate a worse prognosis. Its clinical interpretation should consider procedural success, baseline microvascular function, and individual hemodynamic adaptability. Static AMR thresholds may carry different implications depending on the clinical context and thus require careful interpretation.

Pathophysiological Significance of AMRΔ%

The prognostic advantage of AMRΔ% lies in its unique ability to isolate acute procedural microembolization while eliminating interference from baseline chronic coronary microvascular dysfunction, a key limitation of static post‐PCI AMR measurements. By quantifying the relative change from preprocedural baselines, AMRΔ% effectively filters out chronic endothelial pathologies (eg, diabetes‐related endothelial impairment), 2 , 23 specifically capturing the acute embolic burden introduced during PCI. Our study finds that AMRΔ% ≥85 serves as a sensitive threshold for clinically significant microembolization, which is closely associated with patient prognosis, 24 whereas post‐PCI AMR alone conflates preexisting microvascular disease with procedure‐induced damage. This mechanistic distinction is particularly important in elective PCI populations, where static postprocedure resistance measurements often misclassify chronic microvascular remodeling as acute injury. In contrast, AMRΔ% ≥85 accurately identifies patients who have sustained significant procedural embolization.

Furthermore, interaction analyses demonstrated that the prognostic impact of AMRΔ% ≥85 on MACE is highly dependent on the patient's underlying post‐PCI microvascular status. Among patients with post‐PCI AMR ≥250, indicating severe microvascular dysfunction, AMRΔ% ≥85 emerged as a potent risk determinant, conferring a 52% increase in MACE risk. These results strongly support our central hypothesis: in patients with already compromised microvascular reserve, acute microembolization during PCI (reflected by elevated AMRΔ%) exerts a second‐hit effect, ultimately translating into substantially worse clinical outcomes. In contrast, in patients with post‐PCI AMR <250, suggesting preserved or only mildly impaired baseline microvascular function, AMRΔ% ≥85 showed no significant association with subsequent MACE risk. This finding carries important mechanistic and clinical implications; it suggests that when microvascular integrity remains largely intact, the coronary microcirculation may retain sufficient functional reserve and reparative capacity to withstand acute embolic insults during PCI, thereby preventing the translation of procedural injury into long‐term adverse clinical events. Although the interaction between post‐PCI AMR and AMRΔ% was the only statistically significant finding across all prespecified subgroup analyses and carries clear clinical relevance, it should be interpreted with caution due to the potential for multiple comparisons inherent to exploratory analyses. Therefore, this observation should be considered hypothesis‐generating, and its validity warrants confirmation in future prospective studies.

Procedural Correlates and Prevention Implications

The observed association between AMRΔ% ≥85 and procedural complexity was substantiated by distinct technical parameters. Patients with significant microcirculatory deterioration exhibited greater femoral access use (3.0% versus 1.5%, P=0.01), higher stent burden (1.82 versus 1.56 stents and 48.02 versus 41.10 mm total length, both P<0.001), and prolonged intervention duration (evidenced by elevated contrast volume/radiation exposure). Notably, although lesion complexity metrics were comparable between groups, the correlation between extended stent coverage and microembolic risk aligns with established evidence linking longer lesion treatment to distal embolization. 25 , 26

This mechanistic understanding carries direct therapeutic implications. For patients exhibiting post‐PCI microcirculatory dysfunction with AMRΔ% ≥85, where acute embolization dominates over chronic coronary microvascular dysfunction, prophylactic intracoronary vasodilator administration (eg, adenosine 100–200 μg, nicorandil 2 mg) during stent deployment may mitigate no‐reflow phenomena. Particularly in high‐risk scenarios involving diffuse lesions (>30 mm) or preexisting microvascular disease, such preventive strategies could disrupt the embolization‐inflammation cycle, preserving microvascular perfusion. 22 , 24 Future trials should evaluate protocolized vasodilator use in AMRΔ% ≥85 patients, potentially integrating real‐time AMR monitoring to guide therapeutic intensification during complex interventions.

CONCLUSIONS

This large‐scale post hoc analysis of the FAVOR III China trial reveals critical limitations in AMR for long‐term risk stratification following PCI. Although neither post‐PCI AMR nor AMRΔ% alone demonstrated sufficient prognostic discrimination at 3 years, their synergistic application uncovered a high‐risk phenotype. Patients with combined post‐PCI AMR ≥250 and AMRΔ% ≥85 exhibited a 52% increased cardiovascular event rate (HR, 1.52 [95% CI, 1.14–2.04]), underscoring the clinical relevance of procedural microembolic burden quantified through dynamic AMR changes.

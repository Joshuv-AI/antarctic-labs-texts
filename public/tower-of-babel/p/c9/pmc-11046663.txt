Understanding the mechanistic basis of the pro-resolving effect of efferocyte-derived extracellular vesicles – a future cardiovascular therapeutic potential?
Authors: Karran Kiran Bhagat
Published: 2024

Introduction

Macrophages clear dead and dying cells through a process called efferocytosis. Efferocytosis is therefore crucial in the resolution of inflammation and restoration of homeostasis. When this process is impaired, it is associated with the progression of multiple chronic inflammatory disease such as atherosclerosis. When macrophages undergo efferocytosis, they release certain soluble mediators, as well as extra-cellular vesicles (EVs), which potentially aid in inflammation resolution. Our working hypothesis is that when macrophages release EVs, this contributes to overall efferocytosis efficiency of surrounding macrophages. Our lab had demonstrated the upregulation of various proteins, which were upregulated on efferocyte-derived EVs and I focused on prosaposin (PSAP) in particular. The two main objectives of this work were to independently validate whether PSAP was enriched on EVs while testing its role in efferocytosis efficiency.

Materials and methods

Fluorescently labelled apoptotic cells were fed to macrophages. Percentage of apoptotic cell engulfment was considered as positive efferocytosis.

Results

Through the use nano flow cytometry we confirmed the increased expression of PSAP on efferocyte-derived EVs. Following this, we demonstrated that when PSAP action is blocked with either a neutralising antibody or antagonism of its cognate receptor GPR37, the EV-induced increase in efferocytosis efficiency is blocked. Conclusion

We validated that PSAP expression is increased on efferocyte-derived EVs and that when this action is blocked, there is an overall decrease in efferocytosis efficiency. We concluded that PSAP acts via GPR37, thus raising the question of a potential therapeutic direction for the treatment of atherosclerosis.

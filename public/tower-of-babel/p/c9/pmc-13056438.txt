Systemic Bevacizumab Therapy in Recurrent Respiratory Papillomatosis: First Preliminary Study From Azerbaijan
Authors: Ramil Hashimli, Aynur Aliyeva
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjectiveTo report the first clinical experience from Azerbaijan using systemic bevacizumab therapy for the treatment of recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (RRP) and to evaluate its efficacy in reducing recurrence and surgical burden in both pediatric and adult patients.MethodsA retrospective case series of five patients (four pediatric, one adult) with confirmed RRP treated at a single institution. All patients had a history of multiple surgical interventions and were considered candidates for adjuvant systemic therapy. A single intravenous dose of bevacizumab (5–10 mg/kg) was administered, and patients were followed clinically and endoscopically to assess remission duration and recurrence.ResultsAll patients achieved prolonged remission following a single intravenous dose of bevacizumab. Pediatric patients experienced remission periods ranging from 8 to 24 months, with one child remaining recurrence‐free for over 2 years. The adult patient, with severe tracheobronchial involvement and a history of 112 surgeries, achieved a 6‐month remission—the longest interval between interventions in over a decade. No adverse drug‐related events were reported. In this small series, a single bevacizumab dose was associated with prolonged remission intervals; however, these findings should be interpreted cautiously and viewed as hypothesis‐generating.ConclusionSystemic bevacizumab may be a promising adjuvant option for selected patients with severe RRP, as it was associated with longer interventional‐free intervals in this retrospective patient series. Definitive conclusions regarding efficacy and surgical burden reduction require prospective studies with standardized severity measures and comparator groups. This first reported clinical experience from Azerbaijan contributes valuable real‐world data to the emerging global evidence base supporting anti‐VEGF therapy in RRP. Larger prospective studies are needed to establish optimal dosing regimens and long‐term outcomes.Keywords: airway papilloma, anti‐VEGF therapy, Azerbaijan, pediatric RRP, recurrent respiratory papillomatosis, systemic bevacizumab

Introduction

Recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (RRP) is a rare, benign neoplastic disease of the respiratory tract caused by infection with human papillomavirus (HPV), most commonly Types 6 and 11. It is characterized by the development of exophytic papillomas that can affect the larynx, trachea, and even lower airways, leading to frequent recurrences, airway obstruction, voice changes, and significant morbidity. Traditional management has primarily relied on repeated surgical debulking to maintain airway patency, which may be associated with high procedural burden, significant iatrogenic injury, and variable long‐term outcomes [1].

In recent years, systemic bevacizumab, a recombinant humanized monoclonal antibody directed against vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), has emerged as a promising adjuvant treatment for RRP [2, 3]. Originally developed for oncologic indications, bevacizumab exerts its therapeutic effect by inhibiting VEGF‐mediated angiogenesis, a process increasingly recognized as central to the vascularity and proliferative behavior of papillomatous lesions. Elevated VEGF expression has been demonstrated in RRP and is thought to contribute to lesion growth, persistence, and recurrence. By neutralizing circulating VEGF, systemic bevacizumab suppresses neovascularization, thereby disrupting the vascular support required for continued papilloma proliferation. Angiogenesis attenuation is associated with reduced lesion growth and diminished airway obstruction, resulting in improved airway stability and longer intervals between surgical interventions. When administered following surgical debulking, anti‐VEGF therapy may further suppress residual microscopic disease, delay regrowth, and reduce overall disease burden. Clinical observations suggest that activation of this anti‐angiogenic mechanism may occur even after a single systemic dose, resulting in meaningful extension of disease‐free intervals in both pediatric and adult patients [4, 5, 6, 7, 8].

Although the literature on systemic bevacizumab in RRP remains limited, case series and retrospective reviews have suggested meaningful clinical benefits. Several reports indicate that systemic administration can significantly reduce disease burden, extend intersurgical intervals, and, in some cases, lead to prolonged remission [3]. Moreover, a recent scoping review documented that systemic bevacizumab led to a complete response in a substantial proportion of patients and reduced the need for surgical intervention [9]. Despite these encouraging findings, long‐term follow‐up data and prospective studies are still needed to better define optimal dosing, treatment duration, and safety profiles [1].

To date, most published data consist of small case series and retrospective analyses, with no universally accepted standardized protocols for systemic bevacizumab use in RRP. However, a recent expert consensus statement has begun to outline clinical guidance regarding patient selection, dosing considerations, and monitoring strategies, reflecting growing clinical integration of this therapeutic approach [10].

In this study, we report the first clinical experience with systemic bevacizumab in Azerbaijan for the treatment of RRP. We describe five patients treated at our institution—including both pediatric and adult cases—and report on treatment responses, remission durations, and observed outcomes. This national case series contributes novel real‐world data to the evolving global evidence base for systemic bevacizumab as an adjuvant therapy in RRP.

Methods

This retrospective case series includes five patients (four pediatric and one adult) diagnosed with RRP who were treated with systemic bevacizumab at our institution in Azerbaijan between 2021 and 2025. Diagnosis was confirmed in all cases by clinical and histopathologic evaluation following surgical debulking. Ethical approval for this study was obtained from the Azerbaijan State Advanced Training Institute for Doctors named after A. Aliyev (Approval no. LAR.4 [10.01.2026], February 1, 2026), and all procedures were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants (or from a parent/legal guardian for minors) before inclusion in the study.

Patient Selection

Patients were eligible for systemic bevacizumab therapy if they had clinically severe or rapidly progressing RRP requiring multiple surgical interventions per year. Systemic therapy was considered when disease recurrence resulted in a substantial clinical burden, including compromised airway patency, voice disturbance, reduced quality of life, or frequent need for surgical debulking.

Severe or rapidly recurrent disease was defined by one or more of the following criteria: requirement for repeated surgical interventions within a single year, evidence of progressive, or multilevel airway involvement on endoscopic evaluation, or clinically significant recurrence affecting airway patency or voice quality despite prior surgical management. Disease severity was assessed descriptively based on surgical frequency, the anatomical extent of papillomatous involvement observed during endoscopic examination, and the overall clinical impact on the airway and voice, rather than using a standardized disease severity scoring system. No validated RRP severity instrument (e.g., Derkay staging) was prospectively recorded; therefore, objective pretreatment versus posttreatment severity scoring could not be performed in this retrospective review. Accordingly, changes in disease burden are presented descriptively and should not be interpreted as a standardized quantification of severity reduction.

Patients were excluded from systemic therapy if they had limited, nonprogressive disease adequately controlled with infrequent surgical interventions, contraindications to anti‐VEGF therapy, or incomplete clinical follow‐up that precluded meaningful assessment of treatment response.

Treatment Protocol

Systemic bevacizumab was administered intravenously as a single cycle at either 5 or 10 mg/kg, depending on clinical severity and body weight. All patients received one full dose while under a medical oncologist's supervision in the hospital. Pediatric patients received a 5 mg/kg dose, except for one case (Patient 4), who received 10 mg/kg. The adult patient received 10 mg/kg. Systemic bevacizumab was administered only after adequate postoperative healing was achieved, ensuring airway stability and minimizing the confounding effects of residual macroscopic disease.

All patients underwent surgical debulking before systemic bevacizumab administration. Bevacizumab was administered after surgical removal of visible papillomatous lesions, once postoperative healing was clinically stable. This approach was chosen to minimize airway obstruction, reduce the active disease burden, and enable clearer assessment of systemic therapy during follow‐up. No intralesional bevacizumab injections were used in any case.

No maintenance cycles or repeat doses were given. All patients were followed clinically and endoscopically at regular intervals (every 3–6 months) to monitor recurrence, voice quality, and airway patency. HPV vaccination (Gardasil) was administered to the adult patient in parallel with systemic therapy.

Disease Assessment and Outcome Definition

Clinical outcomes were evaluated using endoscopic findings during follow‐up, recurrence requiring surgical intervention, and changes in the interval between surgical procedures. Recurrence was defined as endoscopically detected papillomatous regrowth that was clinically significant, operationalized as regrowth leading to a decision for repeat surgical debulking; endoscopic regrowth not requiring intervention was classified as mild recurrence. Remission duration (surgery‐free interval) was calculated from the date of single‐dose intravenous bevacizumab administration following surgical debulking to the date of clinically significant recurrence requiring surgery or to the last endoscopic follow‐up.

Safety Monitoring

Patients were monitored clinically for bevacizumab‐associated adverse effects during follow‐up visits (every 3–6 months), with attention to blood pressure, bleeding complications, thromboembolic symptoms, and wound‐healing status. Blood pressure measurements and any available laboratory results documented in the medical record during follow‐up were reviewed to screen for bevacizumab‐associated toxicity (e.g., hypertension and proteinuria). Follow‐up after bevacizumab administration ranged from 6 to 24 months across patients.

Results

Patient Characteristics and Baseline Disease Burden

Five patients with RRP were treated with single‐dose systemic bevacizumab. Four patients (80%) were pediatric (ages 5–12 years), and one patient (20%) was an adult aged 35 years. Three patients were male (60%), and two were female (40%).

The age at diagnosis ranged from 1.5 to 6 years, reflecting predominantly juvenile‐onset disease. Before bevacizumab therapy, all patients had a documented history of multiple surgical debulking procedures, with a total number of surgeries ranging from 3 to 112 per patient (median: 6 surgeries). The adult patient alone accounted for 112 procedures, highlighting extreme disease severity and long‐standing surgical dependence. Before systemic therapy, recurrence intervals ranged from approximately 2 to 4 months in the more aggressive cases to yearly recurrence in less severe pediatric disease (Table 1). Treatment Exposure

All patients received a single intravenous dose of systemic bevacizumab following surgical debulking. Bevacizumab dosing ranged from 5 to 10 mg/kg. Three pediatric patients (60%) received 5 mg/kg, one pediatric patient (20%) received 10 mg/kg, and the adult patient (20%) received 10 mg/kg. No patient received repeat systemic dosing or maintenance therapy during follow‐up. No intralesional bevacizumab injections were administered.

Clinical Response and Remission Outcomes

All five patients (100%) achieved prolonged disease remission after systemic bevacizumab administration, compared with their pretreatment recurrence patterns. Remission duration ranged from 6 to 24 months (median: 9 months). Four of five patients (80%) achieved remission for ≥ 9 months, and two pediatric patients (40%) remained disease‐free for 24 months at the last follow‐up. Only one patient (20%) experienced a mild recurrence during follow‐up; it was endoscopically detected and did not require surgical intervention.

When comparing pretreatment and posttreatment recurrence patterns, patients with prior recurrences every 2–4 months experienced a twofold to threefold increase in the disease‐free interval. The adult patient, who previously required surgery every 2–3 months, achieved a 6‐month remission, representing the longest surgery‐free interval in over a decade.

Patient 1

A 5‐year‐old boy was diagnosed with juvenile‐onset RRP at age 3. He underwent seven surgical debulkings at intervals of 2–3 months due to progressive laryngeal involvement. Following a single intravenous dose of bevacizumab at 5 mg/kg, the patient experienced an 8‐month remission period. A mild recurrence was noted thereafter, but it was clinically insignificant and did not require surgical intervention.

Patient 2

A 6‐year‐old boy, diagnosed at age 4, had previously undergone three surgical procedures approximately every 4 months. He received a single dose of bevacizumab at 5 mg/kg. The patient has since remained in remission for 24 months, with no recurrence of symptoms or requirement for surgery.

Patient 3

A 7‐year‐old girl was diagnosed with RRP at age 4 and had undergone six surgeries at intervals of 3–4 months. After receiving 5 mg/kg of systemic bevacizumab, she has remained disease‐free for 9 months at the time of this report.

Patient 4

A 12‐year‐old girl with a 6‐year history of RRP had undergone four surgeries, typically once per year. Given the moderate disease burden, she received a single dose of bevacizumab at 10 mg/kg. At 2 years posttreatment, she remains in clinical remission with no evidence of recurrence.

Patient 5

A 35‐year‐old man was diagnosed with aggressive juvenile‐onset RRP at 18 months of age. Over the course of his disease, he underwent 112 surgical procedures due to extensive papillomatosis involving the larynx, trachea, and bronchi. He received a single dose of systemic bevacizumab at 10 mg/kg in combination with HPV vaccination (Gardasil). Following treatment, the patient experienced a 6‐month remission at the time of publication—the longest interval between surgeries in over a decade—compared with previous recurrences occurring every 2–3 months (Figure 1). In the adult patient, HPV vaccination (Gardasil) was administered contemporaneously with systemic bevacizumab and should be considered a potential confounding factor when interpreting the observed prolongation of the surgery‐free interval. Although the therapeutic impact of vaccination in established adult RRP remains uncertain, the temporal overlap limits causal attribution to bevacizumab alone in this case.

Pediatric vs. Adult Outcomes

Pediatric patients demonstrated particularly durable responses. The median remission duration among pediatric cases was 16.5 months, compared with 6 months in adults with extensive tracheobronchial involvement. Despite receiving only a single systemic dose, pediatric patients showed sustained suppression of clinically significant disease, with no requirement for repeat surgical intervention during follow‐up (Figure 2).

Illustrates the therapeutic progression and clinical outcomes observed across the patient cohort.

Safety and Tolerability

No clinically significant adverse events related to systemic bevacizumab were observed in any patient during follow‐up. Specifically, no episodes of hypertension, bleeding, delayed wound healing, or clinically evident systemic toxicity were documented. No laboratory‐defined toxicity or clinically significant adverse events were observed during the follow‐up period. A trend toward longer remission duration was observed among patients with lower baseline surgical burden and pediatric age, whereas the shortest remission was observed in an adult patient with extensive lower airway involvement. Overall, systemic bevacizumab led to a clinically meaningful reduction in the need for surgery in 100% of patients, with 80% remaining surgery‐free throughout follow‐up. No clear dose–response relationship could be established in this small cohort, as prolonged remission was observed in both 5 and 10 mg/kg dosing groups.

Discussion

In this study, systemic bevacizumab demonstrated clinically meaningful benefit in patients with RRP, significantly reducing disease burden and prolonging remission intervals compared with prior patterns of frequent surgical interventions. All patients experienced extended disease‐free periods after a single systemic dose of bevacizumab (5–10 mg/kg), ranging from 6 months to 2 years—a notable improvement compared to their pretreatment recurrence intervals (often every 2–4 months) and surgical burden.

These findings align well with previously published literature suggesting that systemic bevacizumab can reduce papilloma growth and prolong intervals between surgical procedures in severe or refractory RRP. In several case reports and retrospective series, systemic bevacizumab has been associated with a prolonged surgical interval and reduced disease severity in both pediatric and adult patients. In a systematic review of RRP patients treated with systemic bevacizumab, a significant proportion (≈95%) exhibited markedly increased post‐bevacizumab surgical intervals compared with their pretreatment histories, and more than half required no additional surgery during a mean follow‐up of nearly 2 years [1].

Similarly, larger retrospective cohorts from international centers have reported that systemic bevacizumab reduces recurrence and the need for surgery, often with repeated and sustained dosing. In one multi‐institutional review, systemic bevacizumab was associated with significantly fewer surgeries per year and clinical improvement in most patients, with a relatively favorable safety profile [11, 12].

The mechanism of bevacizumab in RRP is believed to stem from its anti‐angiogenic activity by targeting VEGF, which contributes to papilloma growth and vascularity. Reducing angiogenesis appears to diminish papilloma proliferation and may reduce obstructive lesions, thereby translating into longer remission intervals and improved airway outcomes [6, 13].

Our pediatric cohort (four children aged 5–12 years) demonstrates that even a single dose of bevacizumab can achieve prolonged remission, particularly in patients with significant surgical histories. Notably, two children have been in remission for 2 years posttreatment, which is among the longest post‐bevacizumab intervals reported with a single dose in similar series. The adult patient with aggressive and disseminated RRP also experienced a marked extension of his surgical interval from every 2 to 3 to 6 months after bevacizumab, even in the context of extensive tracheobronchial involvement.

The outcomes in this series, although limited in sample size, add to the growing real‐world evidence that systemic bevacizumab is a viable adjuvant for refractory RRP across age groups. While systemic therapy protocols in literature often involve repeated dosing cycles (e.g., 5–10 mg/kg at 2‐ to –4‐week intervals) to sustain disease control [12], our findings suggest that even a single dose may produce durable clinical responses in select patients—a hypothesis that warrants further prospective study.

From a safety perspective, systemic bevacizumab has demonstrated a generally acceptable profile in RRP patients in prior reports, although adverse events such as hypertension, proteinuria, and rare severe complications have been described and warrant monitoring in clinical practice [6, 9, 14]. In our series, no significant drug‐related complications were observed, though systematic adverse event analysis was beyond the scope of this report. Overall, our experience supports the emerging consensus that systemic bevacizumab may be one of the most effective adjuvant therapies currently available for severe RRP and offers a therapeutic option for patients with high surgical burden or disseminated disease [1].

In our series, systemic bevacizumab was consistently administered after surgical debulking, reflecting common clinical practice in patients with significant airway involvement. Surgical removal of macroscopic disease likely contributed to immediate airway stabilization, while bevacizumab may have helped suppress residual microscopic disease and delay regrowth. This combined approach may partly explain the prolonged remission intervals observed in our patients, even after a single systemic dose.

The strengths of this study include detailed clinical follow‐up, inclusion of both pediatric and adult patients with varying disease severity, and assessment of outcomes following a single systemic dose of bevacizumab without maintenance therapy, reflecting real‐world clinical practice. Importantly, this series represents the first reported national experience with systemic bevacizumab therapy for RRP in Azerbaijan, contributing novel regional data to the existing literature. Although intralesional bevacizumab has been reported as an option with lower systemic exposure, systemic administration was selected in this series as an adjuvant strategy to reduce recurrence in patients with clinically significant or recurrent disease patterns, including an adult with extensive lower‐airway involvement. For the pediatric patient with infrequent annual recurrence, the indication for systemic anti‐VEGF therapy is less straightforward and should be individualized, weighing potential benefit against systemic risk and available local alternatives [15, 16].

Nevertheless, several limitations must be acknowledged, including the retrospective design, small sample size, absence of a prospectively applied standardized disease severity scoring system, variable follow‐up duration, and lack of a control group, all of which limit definitive conclusions regarding efficacy, optimal dosing, and long‐term safety. Although several patients experienced prolonged surgery‐free intervals after a single systemic bevacizumab dose, this observation should be interpreted cautiously, given that most published systemic protocols employ repeated dosing schedules. Our findings do not supersede established multidose regimens and instead support the need for prospective evaluation in larger cohorts using standardized severity and outcome measures. Future prospective investigations with larger cohorts, standardized outcome measures, and extended follow‐up are required to better define patient selection criteria, dosing strategies, and the long‐term safety profile of systemic bevacizumab in both pediatric and adult RRP populations.

Conclusion

This case series presents the first documented use of systemic bevacizumab therapy for RRP in Azerbaijan. In all five patients, including four children and one adult with extensive disease history, a single systemic dose of bevacizumab (5–10 mg/kg) was associated with prolonged remission intervals and reduced need for surgical intervention. These results reinforce findings from prior international reports and suggest that systemic bevacizumab, even when administered as a single dose, may offer significant clinical benefit in selected RRP patients. Our experience supports the growing consideration of systemic anti‐VEGF therapy as a viable adjuvant in managing RRP, especially in cases with high disease burden or frequent recurrences. Given the limited sample size and retrospective nature of this series, larger prospective studies are warranted to validate these findings, determine optimal dosing strategies, and further assess long‐term safety and efficacy in both pediatric and adult populations.

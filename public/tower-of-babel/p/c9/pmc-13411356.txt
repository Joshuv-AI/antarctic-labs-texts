68Ga-FAPI PET/CT features of thoracic sarcoidosis: a prospective observational study
Authors: Ruxuan Chen, Silu Liu, Zhiyi Li, Yujie Shi, Chi Shao, Xinyu Song, Yaping Luo, Zuojun Xu, Hui Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSarcoidosis is a multisystem granulomatous disease that most commonly affects the lungs. 18F-2-fluoro-2-deoxyglucose (18F-FDG) PET/CT is a sensitive imaging modality for assessing of sarcoidosis. However, fasting before 18F-FDG PET/CT is generally required to improve image quality and diagnostic accuracy. In this context, our study aims to explore the utility of 68Ga-fibroblast activation protein inhibitors (68Ga-FAPI) PET/CT in sarcoidosis, with particular emphasis on lesion uptake patterns and intensity, as well as the correlation with disease activity.MethodsThis is a prospective, single-center, observational study conducted at the Peking Union Medical College Hospital. Eight patients with newly diagnosed thoracic sarcoidosis were consecutively recruited for 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT between January 2024 and December 2024. The follow-up 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT imaging was scheduled 6–12 months after standard treatment. In addition, we retrospectively analyzed 18F-FDG PET/CT scans from 16 sarcoidosis patients. For both imaging groups, the maximum and mean standardized uptake values (SUVmax and SUVmean, respectively) and corrected lesion-to-background ratio (LBR) were calculated.Results68Ga-FAPI PET/CT imaging detected FAPI-avid lesions in all 8 patients, including enlarged lymph nodes (8 patients, SUVmax = 7.78 ± 3.98, LBR = 7.4 ± 5.9), pulmonary infiltrates (6 patients, SUVmax = 5.35 ± 1.15, LBR = 10.4 ± 3.8), spleen nodules (2 patients) and hepatic nodules (1 patient). Following treatment, the follow-up 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT scans showed lesion shrinkage and decreased uptake parameters. However, some pulmonary lesions showed non-significant changes of SUV or LBR. Addtionally, the average SUVmax, SUVmean, and LBR for 68Ga-FAPI of lymph nodes tended to be lower than 18F-FDG uptake parameters in the retrospective cohort.Conclusions68Ga-FAPI PET/CT can be useful for evaluating both active and chronic granulomatous inflammation. It is also potentially valuable for determining disease extent, detecting occult disease, and monitoring treatment response in sarcoidosis.Keywords: Sarcoidosis, Fibroblast activation protein inhibitor, Positron emission tomography/computed tomography

Background

Sarcoidosis is a multisystem inflammatory disease characterized by lymphadenopathy and sterile granulomas [1]. More than 90% of patients with sarcoidosis have lung or intrathoracic lymph node involvement [2]. The clinical course of sarcoidosis is highly heterogeneous, ranging from asymptomatic radiographic abnormalities to progressive pulmonary fibrosis and extrapulmonary organ damage. Imaging plays a crucial role in the clinical management of patients with sarcoidosis, encompassing diagnosis, biopsy guidance, staging, assessment of disease activity, and monitoring therapeutic response [3]. Conventional imaging modalities include chest radiography and high-resolution computed tomography. Typical findings such as bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy with perilymphatic, micronodular pulmonary infiltrates are highly specific for sarcoidosis.

Nuclear medicine imaging techniques can provide additional functional information beyond conventional anatomical imaging. Specifically, the recommended indications for 18F-2-fluoro-2-deoxyglucose (18F-FDG) positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) in sarcoidosis include suspected clinical diagnosis in cases of equivocal prior investigations, assessment of disease extent, assessment of pulmonary disease activity, and assessment of cardiac sarcoidosis in certain scenarios [4]. 18F-FDG PET/CT is a sensitive imaging modality for assessing occult lesions of sarcoidosis. However, fasting before 18F-FDG PET/CT is generally required to improve image quality and diagnostic accuracy. Fibroblast activation protein is a transmembrane proteinase overexpressed by activated fibroblasts in fibrotic and inflammatory tissues [5, 6]. Radiolabeled fibroblast activation protein inhibitors (FAPI) enable PET imaging of fibroblast activation and have demonstrated utility across malignancies, pulmonary fibrosis, and several inflammatory conditions [7–11]. The histological hallmark of sarcoidosis is well-formed, nonnecrotizing granulomas composed of epithelioid histiocytes and multinucleated giant cells surrounded by lymphocytes, plasma cells, and fibroblasts in the periphery [2]. Activated fibroblasts play important roles in sarcoidosis granulomas, including structural support, promotion of tissue healing, and mediation of fibrotic organ damage across different disease courses [12]. To explore the utility of FAPI PET/CT in sarcoidosis, this exploratory, prospective, single-center study evaluates thoracic 68Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT in patients with newly diagnosed sarcoidosis, with particular emphasis on lesion uptake patterns and intensity, as well as the correlation with disease activity. Additionally, we compared these preliminary findings with 18F-FDG PET/CT imaging in a retrospective cohort of sarcoidosis patients. Overall, this study provides preliminary but valuable prospective evidence for the evolving paradigm of imaging-based assessment in sarcoidosis.

Methods

Study design and participants

The 68Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT study is a prospective, single-center, observational study conducted at the Peking Union Medical College Hospital, a tertiary referral center for sarcoidosis and diffuse parenchymal lung diseases in China. Patients with newly diagnosed thoracic sarcoidosis [13] were consecutively recruited for 68Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT from the outpatient clinic of the Department of Pulmonary and Critical Care Medicine between January 2024 and December 2024. The inclusion criteria included (a) participants had a compatible clinical presentation, the finding of non-necrotizing granulomatous inflammation in one or more tissue samples, and the exclusion of alternative causes of granulomatous disease [13]; (b) participants had to be ≥ 18 years of age at the time of enrollment; and (c) participants agreed to undergo 68Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT examinations. Exclusion criteria encompassed pulmonary infections, malignancy, pregnancy, breastfeeding, or an inability to provide informed consent. After enrollment, all patients received guideline-concordant standard therapy [14]. The follow-up 68Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT imaging was scheduled during the stable disease phase. The time interval between the two 68Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT scans was 6–12 months. In addition, we retrospectively enrolled matched thoracic sarcoidosis patients who had undergone an 18F-FDG PET/CT imaging before steroid treatment and retrieved their data (demographics, diagnoses, medical history, laboratory and radiographic examinations) from the electronic medical record system.

The PET/CT study in patients with sarcoidosis was approved by the institutional ethical review board (IRB) of the Peking Union Medical College Hospital (approval number: K4307; approval date: 2023-08-08) and subsequently registered on the Chinese medical research registry (https://www.medicalresearch.org.cn) under ID MR-11-23-041540. All of the prospectively enrolled patients provided written informed consent. Written informed consent from patients in the retrospective 18F-FDG PET/CT cohort was waived because we used anonymized health care data, which met the IRB’s minimal risk waiver criteria.

PET/CT imaging

FAPI-04 labeled with gallium-68 (68Ga-FAPI-04) was manually radiolabeled immediately before injection. Briefly, to adjust the pH to 3.5–4.0, 92 µL of sodium acetate (1.25 M) were added to 1 mL of 68GaCl3 eluent (68Ga3+ in 1.0 M HCI) obtained from a 68Ge/68Ga generator (ITG). After the addition of an aliquot of 20 µL (1 µg/µL) of FAPI-04 (CA94025; CSBio Co.), the mixture was heated to 100 °C for 10 min. The reaction solution was diluted to 5 mL and passed through a preconditioned Sep-Pak C18 Plus Light cartridge (Waters), and the cartridge was eluted with 0.5 mL of 75% ethanol to obtain the final product. The radiochemical purity of the product was analyzed by thin-layer chromatography. The 68Ga-FAPI-04 injections were filtered through a 0.22-µm Millex-LG filter (EMD Millipore) before clinical use. 18F-FDG was synthesized in-house with an 11-MeV cyclotron (CTI RDS 111; Siemens). The PET scans were performed on dedicated PET/CT scanners (Panorama [United Imaging] or Polestar m660 [SinoUnion]). For 18F-FDG PET/CT, participants fasted for longer than 6 h, and blood glucose levels were monitored (4.9–7.9 mmol/L) before an injection of 18F-FDG (5.55 MBq per kilogram of body weight). The PET/CT images (2 min per bed) were acquired with an uptake time of 80.6 ± 25.7 min.

For the baseline 68Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT (8 patients), imaging was performed (2 min per bed) with an uptake time of 57.3 ± 13.2 min after an injection of 77.7 ± 21.8 MBq 68Ga-FAPI-04. For the re-examination of 68Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT (6 patients), imaging was performed (2 min per bed) with an uptake time of 44.0 ± 16.9 min after an injection of 66.2 ± 27.0 MBq 68Ga-FAPI-04. PET/CT was performed from the apex of the lungs to the upper abdomen. All patients underwent unenhanced low-dose CT (120 kV, 30–50 mAs) for attenuation correction and anatomic reference. The acquired data were reconstructed using the ordered subset expectation maximization method.

Image interpretation

18F-FDG and 68Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT scans were reviewed on the Syngo multimodality workplace (Siemens) and assessed by 2 experienced nuclear medicine physicians, with consensus on image interpretation of lesion involvement. The SUVmax of the lesion exhibiting the greatest uptake in each involved organ and the blood pool background (measured in the descending aorta, 1 cm in diameter) and the lung background (normal lung tissue away from the lesion, 1 cm in diameter) were measured by the same nuclear medicine physician using the volume-of-interest method with a unified standard. Each involved organ was also assessed using the corrected lesion-to-background ratio (LBR), defined as the SUVmax of the lesion with the greatest uptake divided by SUVmax of the background (we used normal lung tissue area as background for lung lesions, and blood pool for other organs).

To measure the SUVmean of all lesions within the scanning range, total active volume (TAV, defined as the sum of the active uptake volumes of all involved lesions) and total lesion uptake (TLU, defined as the sum of an individual TAV multiplied by its SUVmean) were semiautomatically measured with the MIM workstation (version 6.6.11; MIM Software). Volumes of interest were drawn to encompass all lesions involved, as previously identified by the 2 readers on the PET/CT images. Subsequently, the contours were semiautomatically segmented with an SUV cutoff of 2.5, and then checked and manually adjusted. Afterward, volumetric parameters of TLU and TAV were automatically obtained from the statistics generated with the final volumetric extraction, and the SUVmean of all involved lesions was calculated as the quotient of TLU and TAV (SUVmean = TLU/TAV).

Hepatic and splenic lesions were identified based on abnormal FAPI uptake (visually higher than the normal liver/spleen background; no cutoff value was applied) and/or CT morphological features (hypodense nodules). Image interpretation was performed primarily by visual assessment from two experienced nuclear medicine physicians, with confirmation by semi-quantitative SUVmax analysis.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS (Statistical Product, Service Solutions) Statistics 26.0 and GraphPad Prism 9.5.1 software. Categorical variables are presented as percentages. Continuous variables are presented as the mean (± standard deviation) or median (with interquartile range [IQR] or range) values. The Chi-square or Fisher’s exact test, and Student’s t-test or Mann–Whitney U test were applied for categorical and continuous variables as appropriate. Paired t-tests were used to compare variables at baseline and follow-up. A two-tailed P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Participants

From January 2024 to December 2024, 8 participants with newly diagnosed thoracic sarcoidosis (5 men and 3 women; median age: 52 years [IQR: 49–60 years]) were prospectively enrolled. All patients had histological confirmation of non-necrotizing granulomatous inflammation in mediastinal lymph node biopsy samples. Three patients (37.5%) had a smoking history. No other environmental or occupational exposure history was noted. The median time interval between disease onset and sarcoidosis diagnosis was 4.5 months (IQR 2.5–30.0 months). All 8 patients underwent baseline 68Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT scans after enrollment and then received guideline-concordant standard therapy. All patients presented with bilateral hilar and mediastinal lymphadenopathy. The other manifestations of sarcoidosis in this cohort included pulmonary infiltrates (6 cases), spleen nodules (2 cases) and hepatic nodules (1 case). All patients were treated with oral prednisone (initial dose: 20–40 mg daily; tapered after 2–6 weeks). Among them, 2 patients also received oral methotrexate (one for extensive pulmonary lesions, and the other one for hepatic nodules with abnormal liver function tests). After 6–12 months of treatment, 6 patients underwent the second 68Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT scan, and 2 patients declined to repeat the test.

Comparison of 68Ga-FAPI-04 and 18F-FDG PET/CT in thoracic sarcoidosis

To compare the imaging characteristics of 68Ga-FAPI-04 and 18F-FDG PET/CT in sarcoidosis, we retrospectively enrolled 16 matched thoracic sarcoidosis patients who had undergone an 18F-FDG PET/CT imaging before steroid treatment between January 2023 and December 2025. The comparison of uptake parameters is presented in Table 2. The background FDG uptake values were 1.67 ± 0.46 in the blood pool and 0.48 ± 0.22 in the normal lung tissue area. The average SUVmax, SUVmean, and LBR for 18F-FDG of lymph nodes were all numerically higher than the uptake parameters of 68Ga-FAPI-04. The uptake parameters of pulmonary lesions for 18F-FDG also tended to be higher than those for 68Ga-FAPI-04, but the differences were not statistically significant.

Discussion

In this exploratory prospective observational study, we described features of 68Ga-FAPI-04 in different thoracic sarcoidosis lesions using semiquantitative parameters (SUVmax, SUVmean, and LBR). We compared these parameters between the baseline PET/CT scan and the follow-up scan after 6–12 months of anti-inflammatory treatment. We found that lesions of sarcoidosis in various organs (lungs, lymph nodes, liver, and spleen) were all FAPI-avid. The uptake of 68Ga-FAPI-04 in lymph nodes, liver and spleen significantly decreased after treatment, but some lesions in the lung remained FAPI-avid. Additionally, we compared these preliminary 68Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT findings with a retrospective cohort of 18F-FDG PET/CT imaging in sarcoidosis, which showed numerically higher SUVmax, SUVmean and LBR of enlarged lymph nodes on 18F-FDG PET/CT.

In 2018, 68Ga-FAPI was reported as a promising PET/CT tracer for diagnostic imaging of malignant tumors with a high content of activated fibroblasts [15]. Multiple studies have been conducted since then across various types of malignancies [6, 7, 16]. With the increasing number of FAPI PET/CT scans performed, a growing number of nonmalignant FAPI-avid lesion types have been published [5, 6], including inflammatory and fibrotic diseases. Siebermair et al. first reported the detection of FAPI uptake in the cardiac sarcoidosis-involved area, in which the FDG uptake had decreased after 6 months of anti-inflammatory treatment [17]. Based on this hint of ongoing cardiac remodeling, immunosuppressive therapy was continued. Their findings suggested a potential role of FAPI-PET/CT in guiding immunomodulatory therapy. Subsequently, cases of FAPI-PET/CT imaging in hepatic sarcoidosis and pulmonary sarcoidosis were reported [18, 19]. In the case report of pulmonary sarcoidosis, mediastinal lymphadenopathy without significant FAPI uptake was noted [19].

Since respiratory system is the most commonly involved organ system in sarcoidosis, we conducted this prospective study to explore the thoracic 68Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT findings in sarcoidosis. In contrast to the findings of O’Brien et al. [19], our study showed active FAPI uptake in all of the enlarged lymph nodes of sarcoidosis patients, which suggests that fibroblasts are activated within the involved lymph nodes. In the previous studies about 68Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT imaging in IgG4-related disease, enlarged inflammatory lymph nodes with pathologically proven progressive transformation of germinal centers exhibited minimal FAPI uptake [8, 20]. Notably, the mean SUVmax of 68Ga-FAPI-04 in involved lymph nodes has been reported as 2.03 ± 1.60 in IgG4-related disease [20], markedly lower than the average observed in this sarcoidosis cohort (7.78 ± 3.98). In a case of tuberculosis, 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT imaging revealed intense 68Ga-FAPI uptake in enlarged lymph nodes (SUVmax = 20.1) [21]. These findings suggest that FAPI PET/CT may serve as a sensitive imaging modality for the differential diagnosis of granulomatous versus other inflammatory lymphadenopathies.

After effective treatment, previous FAPI-avid lesions in the lymph nodes, spleen and liver shrank or completely resolved. The pulmonary lesions in some cases also showed significant improvement (as demonstrated in Fig. 1), but the overall decrease in uptake parameters of pulmonary lesions was not significant in this cohort. One patient showed increased SUVmax in some lung lesions on follow up 68Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT. Based on the favorable treatment response observed after an additional 3 months of therapy (as demonstrated in Fig. 3), we speculate that the increased SUVmax in these lesions may reflect an active tissue remodeling process. Since fibroblast activation can also be present in lung tissue healing and progressive fibrotic pulmonary lesions [10, 22], it may be challenging for FAPI PET/CT to accurately distinguish between inflammatory and ongoing fibrotic pulmonary lesions; however, persistent FAPI uptake following treatment may suggest a continued need for maintenance therapy.

Although PET/CT is not routinely used in the assessment of sarcoidosis, FDG PET/CT has proven to be particularly valuable for evaluating reversible granulomatous inflammation, monitoring treatment response, determining disease extent, detecting occult disease, assessing cardiac or osseous involvement, and identifying optimal biopsy sites via a single scan [23]. In cases of suspected pulmonary sarcoidosis, FDG PET/CT demonstrated good diagnostic performance and correlated with pulmonary functional test parameters [24]. Fasting before FDG PET/CT is routinely required to lower blood glucose levels, ensure optimal tracer accumulation in target tissues, while reducing physiological uptake in muscles, myocardium, liver, and other organs, thereby improving image quality and diagnostic accuracy [25]. FAPI PET/CT obviates the necessity for strict dietary modifications before imaging, giving it a distinct advantage over FDG PET/CT. Additionally, physiologic FAPI uptake in the liver and spleen is generally low and lower than that of FDG, even in the fasting state [26]. This characteristic confers a distinct advantage on FAPI PET/CT for visualizing lesions within the liver or spleen.

Qiao et al. [27] compared the uptake parameters of FAPI PET/CT and FDG PET/CT in various inflammatory lung lesions and found that FAPI uptake in blood pool was lower than FDG uptake, consistent with previous studies [26]. Therefore, a higher LBR is achieved compared with that for FDG uptake, facilitating more accurate identification of inflammatory lesions. However, in our study, the background uptake values of FAPI and FDG were similar in both the blood pool and the normal lung area. FDG PET/CT showed numerically higher uptake parameters in lymph nodes compared with FAPI PET/CT. Larger scale studies are needed to validate the findings.

Since normal fibroblasts minimally express FAP, FAP expression levels are very low in normal adult human tissues. Increased FAPI uptake suggests fibroblast activation, as occurs in tissue damage, remodeling, inflammation, or neoplasia. In inflammatory diseases such as sarcoidosis, FAPI PET/CT is potentially valuable for detecting occult disease, determining disease extent, and monitoring treatment response. More specifically, FAPI PET/CT may be helpful in differentiating active inflammation from permanent fibrosis, thereby identifying the critical therapeutic window and guiding adjustments to treatment intensity and duration.

The present study has several limitations, including its small sample size and selection bias, due to its single-center study design. Due to concerns about accumulated radiation exposure, we did not perform both FAPI PET/CT and FDG PET/CT in the same patients in this cohort. The FAPI and FDG PET/CT imaging data came from different patient cohorts rather than the same group of patients, and there are clinically noticeable differences in male/female ratio and disease course duration between the FAPI PET/CT and FDG PET/CT groups. Such baseline imbalances may influence the results. Therefore, the comparison results of uptake parameters between FAPI PET/CT and FDG PET/CT should be interpreted cautiously. A well-matched prospective concurrent control study is essential to validate these findings. Despite these limitations, the sarcoidosis patients included in both groups exhibited typical imaging patterns of thoracic sarcoidosis, which lends potential clinical value to our findings. A larger-scale multicenter investigation is warranted in the future.

Conclusions

68Ga-FAPI-04 PET/CT is a promising tool for evaluating both active and chronic granulomatous inflammation in sarcoidosis. The lack of strict dietary restrictions before imaging, together with low physiologic FAPI uptake in the liver, spleen, and other organs, confers distinct advantages on FAPI PET/CT over FDG PET/CT.

Bioactive potential of Rhododendron lowndesii: Extraction, antioxidant analysis, antimicrobial assay, and Liquid Chromatography-mass Spectrometry profiling
Authors: Anup Luitel, Rajan Shrestha, Lalit Mohan Pant, Ashwinee Kumar Shrestha, Niru Thapa, Manika Shrestha, Ronak Shrestha, Ganesh Datt Joshi, Kirankumar Hullatti, Janardan Lamichhane
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Rhododendron lowndesii Davidian is an endemic medicinal plant of Nepal found in an altitude range of 3000–4100 m. This study investigates the phytochemical composition and bioactivities of its methanolic extract. The aerial part of the plant was extracted through methanol maceration to achieve 31.50% ± 0.92% yield. Qualitative and quantitative phytochemical analysis of the extract revealed the presence of alkaloids, flavonoids, phenols, steroids, terpenoids, coumarins, and carbohydrates, with a total phenolic content of 175.63 ± 1.37 mg GAE/g and total flavonoid content (TFC) of 1090.36 ± 2.29 mgQE/g. Antioxidant activity assay of the extract suggested its strong radical scavenging activity through DPPH (IC50:19.46 ± 1.85 µg/mL) and ABST is 2,2'-azino-bis(3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid) assays (IC50: 13.4 ± 0.20 µg/mL). Antibacterial activity as determined by the disc diffusion method, showed significant effect against Bacillus subtilis, Enterococcus faecalis, and Staphylococcus aureus (minimum inhibitory concentration: 312.5–1250 µg/mL) but limited efficacy against Salmonella enterica. High-resolution liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry identified pharmacologically significant compounds, including zapotin and cepharanthine. These findings suggest that R. lowndesii has rich phytochemical profile and bioactivity; this supports its potential as a medicinal plant and need of its pharmacological research.

INTRODUCTION

Rhododendron lowndesii is an endemic medicinal plant of Nepal found in high-elevation (3000–4100 m) meadows and rocky cliffs of areas such as Mustang, Manang, and Dolpa.[1] Morphologically, it is characterized by short height (often 15–20 cm in height), hairy leaf margins, and distinctive yellowish to creamy-yellow flowers with red petioles that differentiate it from other Rhododendron species such as R. lepidotum or Rhododendron cowanianum, which bear purplish-pink or pink flowers and exhibit more upright growth.[2]

Other Rhododendron species such as Rhododendron arboreum, Rhododendron anthopogon, and Rhododendron campanulatum, have been extensively studied for their antioxidant, antimicrobial, anticancer, and hepatoprotective properties.[3] R. lowndesii has promising bioactivity, but the bioactivity of the high-altitude species, R. cowanianum, has not been reported. Considering that plants growing in high-altitude and harsh climates frequently accumulate elevated levels of secondary metabolites such as flavonoids, terpenoids, and phenolic acid compounds, evaluation of pharmacological potential of R. lowndesii is important. This study aims to explore the phytochemical constituents, antioxidant potential, and antimicrobial potential of the methanolic extract of R. lowndesii and unravel the high-resolution liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (HR-LCMS)-based compound profiling of the species to provide insights into its therapeutic potential.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Sample collection and morphological identification

A whole plant samples of R. lowndesii were collected from Mustang, Nepal. An herbarium specimen of the samples was authenticated from and deposited at the National Herbarium and Plant Laboratories (NHPLs), Godawari, Nepal.

Herbarium Data compilation and analysis

Herbarium specimens of R. lowndesii archived at the NHPL from 1964 to 2024 were reviewed. Information, including collection location, altitude, name of collector, and date of collection, was reviewed. The geographical distribution of the collection sites was mapped.

Phytochemical Extraction of Rhododendron lowndesii

The whole plant was extracted through maceration containing sample and methanol in 1:3 (w/v) ratio, at 32°C, for 48 h in an orbital incubator shaker. The crude extract was filtered using Whatmann No. 1 filter paper and stored in an air tight container at 4°C till further analysis.

Assessment of total phenolic content and total flavonoid content

The total phenolic content (TPC) of plant extract was measured using the Folin Ciocalteu method.[5] Similarly, the estimation of total flavonoid content (TFC) of plant extract was done through Aluminum chloride colorimetric assay.[6]

Determination of Antioxidant activity

Antioxidant activity of the extract was determined as described previously by DPPH is 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl[7] and ABTS method[8] using ascorbic acid as the positive control.

Determination of Antibacterial activity

Bacterial strain

Pathogenic bacterial strains such as Gram-positive (Bacillus subtilis [ATCC 6633], Enterococcus faecalis [ATCC 19433]), Gram-negative (Salmonella enterica [ATCC 14028]), Methicillin susceptible Staphylococcus aureus (ATCC 0027), and Methicillin-resistant S. aureus (MRSA; CCARM 3090) were used for the test.

Antibacterial activity

Agar disk-diffusion method was used to determine the antibacterial potential of R. lowndesii extract[9] using 6-mm sterile filter paper discs impregnated with 10 µL of plant extract (30 mg/mL in DPPH is 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl) and nitrofurantoin (300 µg/disc) and norfloxacin (10 µg/disc) discs as positive controls.

Determination of minimum inhibitory and bactericidal concentrations

The minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) for plant extract was determined using the macro dilution method. For this, 300 µL of extract at concentrations of 2500 µg/mL, 1250 µg/mL, 625 µg/mL, 312.5 µg/mL, 156.25 µg/mL, and 78.125 µg/mL were treated with 50 µL of bacterial suspension with density 5 × 105 CFU/mL[10] in 96 well plate, except the negative control. Positive controls contained only MHB and extract without bacteria. The 96-well plate was then incubated at 37°C for 24 h, and bacterial growth was evaluated by measuring optical density at 600 nm (OD600) using a microplate reader both before and after incubation. Following the determination of MIC, wells exhibiting no sign of growth were transferred to MHA plates and incubated for 24 h at 37°C to assess minimum bactericidal concentration (MBC),[11] the MBC was then determined to be the lowest extract concentration showing no growth.

High-resolution liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry profiling

The methanol extract underwent chemical profiling through high-resolution liquid chromatography coupled with mass spectrometry. The mass spectrometry analysis utilized the 6200 series TOF/6500 series QTOF system, controlled by Acquisition Software Version B.05.01(B5125.3). Conducted in the positive ionization mode (ESI+), the study employed a targeted metabolite detection approach (metabolite_ ESI_ +VE_ M). For the assessment, a 5 µL sample aliquot was injected into the system.

Phytochemical Analysis of Rhododendron lowndesii

The methanolic extract of the plant contained alkaloid, flavonoids, tannins, phenol, steroids, terpenoids, coumarins, and carbohydrates [Table 1] and absence of lignans, anthocyanins, and saponins. The extract showed TPC of 175.63 ± 1.37 mg GAE/g and TFC of 1090.36 ± 2.29 mgQE/g.

Bioactivity evaluation of Rhododendron lowndesii

Antioxidant activity

The methanolic extract of the plant demonstrated higher IC50 value for antioxidant activity, i. e., 19.41 ± 1.85 and 13.44 ± 0.20 µg/mL which was significantly higher (7.55 ± 0.51) µg/mL (P < 0.01) and 5.31 ± 0.54 µg/mL (P < 0.01) than that of ascorbic acid as determined by DPPH assay and ABTS methods, respectively [Figure 3].

IC50 values of Rhododendron lowndesii extract as shown by DPPH and ABTS assay

Antibacterial assessment

The methanolic extract of R. lowndesii exhibited antibacterial activity against gram + ve bacteria, E. faecalis and B. subtilis with ZOI of 20.67 ± 1.7 mm (20 ± 0 mm for nitrofurantoin) and 18.5 ± 0.41 mm (20 ± 1 for nitrofurantoin), respectively, MIC of 312.5 µg/mL and MBC of 625 µg/mL. The activity was lower against S. aureus with ZOI of 14.5 ± 0.41 mm (18 ± 1 for nitrofurantoin), MIC of 312.5 and MBC of 1250 µg/mL and least against gram − ve bacteria S. enterica with ZOI of 7.33 ± 0.94 mm (28 ± 1 for norfloxacin), MIC of 625.0 and MBC of 1250 µg/mL [Figures 4 and 5a and b]. The negative control DMSO did not show any ZOI.

DISCUSSION

The geographic restriction of R. lowndesii and the consistency in its distribution, altitudinal range, and repeated collection over decades indicates a stable but limited natural habitat in Nepal, and strongly supports it as an endemic species. The extraction yield (31.50% ± 0.92%), in methanol suggest an abundance of methanol-soluble phytochemicals in the plant. This finding correlates with results of Shootha et al.[7] and Baral et al.[29] in other species of Rhododendron. However, the absence of lignans, anthocyanins, and saponins in R. lowndesii could indicate species-specific phytochemical traits or limited solubility in methanol. Further studies on different solvent systems and extraction techniques could provide insights into the full phytochemical profile of R. lowndesii.

The findings from TPC and TFC indicate a potent polyphenolic profile of R. lowndesii that is comparable to several other Rhododendron species. For instance, R. arboreum exhibited TPC ranging from 19.23 ± 0.13 to 135.10 ± 0.12 mg GAE/g and TFC from 31.53 ± 0.06 to 67.70 ± 0.10 mg QE/g in various extracts.[30] The exceptional TPC and TFC content may be linked to its ecological and genetic properties, similar high TFC has been reported in Chromolaena odorata Linn leaf extract.[31] Endemic to highly elevated regions, R. lowndesii thrives in extreme environments such as UV radiation, low temperature, and oxidative stress. Such substantial amounts of phenolic acids, flavonoids, flavones, and other polyphenols highlight the different pharmacological potential including anti-inflammatory, anti-allergic, hepatoprotective, and antimicrobial and anticancer activities.[32]

The low IC50 values of the methanolic extract as shown in Figure 3 indicate that plant extract may be rich in bioactive compounds capable of donating electrons or hydrogen atoms to neutralize free radicals, thereby reducing oxidative stress. Phenolic and flavonoids compounds can serve as nucleophiles that can scavenge free radicals.[33] The significant antioxidant potential observed aligns with the high phenolic and flavonoid content of the extract, in consistent with reports for related Rhododendron species, such as Rhododendron maddenii (IC50 value of 22.76 4 µg/mL for DPPH) and Rhododendron dalhousiae (IC50 value of 8.52 µg/mL for ABTS assay).[7] Variations in antioxidant activity may depend on the extraction solvent, extraction method, environmental factors affecting plant composition, and the interaction of various bioactive compounds within the extract.

The MIC and MBC against the pathogens supported the significant antibacterial activity of the extract. The enhanced activity, particularly against gram +ve pathogens compared to gram − ve bacteria, may be attributed to the peptidoglycan layer in the bacterial cell wall. However, the observed inhibition still suggests some degree of antibacterial potential against resistant strains. Similar findings were observed with Rhododendron tomentosum.[34]

The secondary metabolites as reveled by HR-LCMS, given in Table 3 are all well known for the pharmacological significance. Rhododendron lepidotum, Rhododendron decorumn shares some overlapping compounds such as Euphornin and Koenigicine. Similarly, R. lowndesii uniquely contained Lucidenic acid A and Ganoderic acid F, the two triterpenoids with established anticancer and hepatoprotective potential.[3536] This shows the pharmacological potential of R. lowndesii.

CONCLUSION

This study shows that R. lowndesii, an endemic species of Nepal, is a valuable plant with important pharmacological potential. Phytochemical and HR-LCMS screening revealed a diverse array of secondary metabolites of the plant that has potent antioxidant and antibacterial properties. Identification of the specific bioactive compounds, their mode of action, as well as assessment of their potency in vivo are needed in future studies. As the plant is confined in a specific ecological region, conservation actions are recommended toward the sustainable use of this valuable species.

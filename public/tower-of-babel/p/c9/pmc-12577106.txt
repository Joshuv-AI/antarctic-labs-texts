Analysis of the clinical characteristics and nursing strategies for 11 cases of Candida auris bloodstream infection
Authors: Yan Duan, Chuangchuang Mei, Xiaojing Hong, Donghao Cai, Xiaojun Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo analyse the clinical characteristics, antifungal drug resistance, and prognostic factors of patients with Candida auris bloodstream infection. Explore targeted nursing strategies, and provide reference for clinical diagnosis, treatment, and nursing.MethodsClinical data were collected from 11 patients with C.auris bloodstream infections admitted to Guangdong Provincial Second Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine between January 2023 and June 2025. The data included underlying diseases, history of invasive procedures, antibiotic use, treatment regimens, laboratory indicators, etc. Use a self-made form to collect patient care measures and track their condition. The clinical characteristics of the patients and factors affecting their prognosis were analysed.ResultsAmong the 11 patients, males accounted for 90.9%, with a mean age of 62 years; They were primarily distributed across the Acupuncture Rehabilitation Department and the Intensive Care Unit (ICU); 8 patients had pneumonia, 10 patients had peripherally inserted central venous catheter(PICC), and 9 patients had used antibiotics before infection; 7 patients received targeted treatment, including 6 patients who used caspofungin. Antimicrobial susceptibility testing revealed a fluconazole resistance rate of 81.8%. In terms of prognosis, 6 cases were in the good prognosis group and 5 cases were in the poor prognosis group; the neutrophil-to-albumin ratio (NPAR) was significantly lower in the poor prognosis group than in the good prognosis group (P = 0.006). In terms of nursing measures, there was no statistically significant difference in targeted nursing measures between patients with different prognoses. However, differentiated nursing care can be provided based on patients’ underlying conditions, NPAR values, and other factors.ConclusionPatients with C.auris bloodstream infections are predominantly males with underlying medical conditions, who have undergone invasive procedures and been exposed to antibiotics. NPAR can serve as a prognostic assessment indicator. Enhanced care during invasive procedures, infection control measures, and targeted disease monitoring are effective strategies for preventing healthcare-associated infections and improving patient outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-025-11931-1.Keywords: Candida auris, Bloodstream infection, Clinical characteristics, Resistance, Prognosis, Nursing strategies

Background

Since its first isolation and reporting in 2009 [1], C.auris has rapidly spread globally, with over 50 countries reporting cases of C.auris infections [2, 3]. C.auris exhibits high resistance to commonly used antifungal drugs such as fluconazole. Studies have shown that in some regions, the resistance rate of clinically isolated C.auris strains to fluconazole exceeds 90%,and up to 30% resistance to amphotericin B [4, 5], which limit clinical treatment options. Additionally, the multidrug resistance of C.auris facilitates its spread, leading to outbreaks of infection. In recent years, outbreaks have been reported in the United States, Europe, and other regions, making it one of the ‘super fungi’ posing a serious threat to public health safety [6, 7].

Bloodstream infections are one of the leading causes of death among hospitalised patients [8], and Candida bloodstream infections are typically highly insidious. Once Candida invades the bloodstream, it can trigger a systemic inflammatory response, leading to multiple organ dysfunction syndrome and posing a serious threat to the patient’s life and health C.auris due to factors such as high resistance to antifungal drugs, can result in an overall mortality rate of up to 45% once bloodstream infection occurs [9]. However, if intervention is initiated promptly during the early stages of infection, it can significantly improve patient outcomes [10, 11].

Due to the low incidence of C.auris, there are currently few studies reporting C.auris bloodstream infections. Given the high mortality rate of C.auris bloodstream infections and the urgent need for clinical prevention and control, this study aims to help clinicians identify high-risk patients early and take targeted preventive and therapeutic measures in a timely manner through a retrospective analysis of the clinical data of 11 patients with C.auris bloodstream infections. Through effective nursing interventions and management, optimising hospital environmental cleaning and disinfection measures, and reducing the spread of C.auris within healthcare facilities, public health risks can be mitigated, and patient safety enhanced.

Methods

Patient selection

Data on patients with C.auris bloodstream infections admitted to Guangdong Provincial Second Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine from January 2023 to June 2025 were collected retrospectively. The inclusion criteria were as follows:

The bacteria isolated from blood culture were C.auris.

Meeting the diagnostic criteria for bloodstream infection(The diagnostic criteria for bloodstream infections refer to the standards published by the US Centres for Disease Control and Prevention/Healthcare Safety Network (CDC/NHSN) in 2008) [12]: ①Positive blood culture results for one or more organisms; ②At least one of the following symptoms: fever (>38 °C), chills, or hypotension; ③The patient’s symptoms, signs, and positive laboratory results are unrelated to infections in other body sites; ④Exclusion of false-positive results due to sample contamination.

Meeting the diagnostic criteria for bloodstream infection(The diagnostic criteria for bloodstream infections refer to the standards published by the US Centres for Disease Control and Prevention/Healthcare Safety Network (CDC/NHSN) in 2008) [12]: ①Positive blood culture results for one or more organisms; ②At least one of the following symptoms: fever (>38 °C), chills, or hypotension; ③The patient’s symptoms, signs, and positive laboratory results are unrelated to infections in other body sites; ④Exclusion of false-positive results due to sample contamination.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Remove duplicate cases. When the same patient is diagnosed with C.auris bloodstream infection multiple times, use the data from the first positive blood culture.

(2) Exclude patients with incomplete data. Exclude cases with missing data on demographic information, laboratory parameters, and Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation (APACHE II) scores.

The management protocol following C.auris detection is as follows: Upon identification by the laboratory, critical value notification is issued to the ward.The ward then issues isolation orders. The attending nurse completes the‘Candida auris Infection Patient Case Management Registration Form’ designed by Guangdong Provincial Second Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine. Isolation and disinfection measures are implemented for the patient. Targeted nursing interventions are formulated and evaluated based on the patient’s condition. Post-discharge or post-isolation follow-up is conducted to monitor prognosis.Within the form, tailored nursing interventions are determined based on factors such as invasive procedures and underlying conditions, encompassing: Invasive procedure nursing addresses patients with urinary catheters, PICC, or mechanical ventilation. Airway nursing targets those with pneumonia or tracheostomy tubes requiring suctioning to maintain airway patency. Rehabilitation nursing supports bedridden patients, those with mobility impairments, or individuals requiring post-cerebral infarction rehabilitation training.

Based on the discharge status recorded on the first page of the medical record at the time of discharge (including discharge by medical order, transfer by medical order, and transfer to a community health service institution/ township health centres, in-hospital death and non-medical discharge indicate poor patient outcomes [13]). Cases involving in-hospital death and discharges against medical advice were classified into the poor prognosis group, while cases involving medical discharge, medical transfer, and medical transfer to community health service institutions/township health centres were classified into the good prognosis group. Compare the impact of demographic information, different nursing measures, hospitalisation information, invasive procedures, underlying disease information, inflammatory markers, etc. on prognosis between different prognosis groups. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Guangdong Provincial Second Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Ethics Review Opinion Number: Z202404-002-01). Microbiological tests

Blood cultures were performed using the BACTEC FX blood culture system (BD, USA). Positive cultures were identified using the Huiji EXS3000 mass spectrometer (Zhongyuan, China). Antimicrobial susceptibility testing was conducted using the ATB FUNFUS 3 kit (Bio-Merieux SA, France). Antimicrobial susceptibility breakpoints were referenced from the US CDC and the European Committee on Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing: fluconazole ≥ 32 µg/mL, amphotericin B ≥ 2 µg/mL [14], 5-flucytosine ≥ 0.5 µg/mL [3, 14].

Data collection

Patient data including gender, age, underlying conditions, department, APACHE II scores at admission and at diagnosis, and whether patients had used urinary catheter(CAU), peripherally inserted central venous catheter (PICC) mechanical ventilation(MV), or broad-spectrum antibiotic use prior to infection, as well as prophylactic and targeted treatment status, were collected through the Hospital Information System. Based on the time of infection, collect blood routine indicators within 24 h of the positive blood culture test in the Laboratory Information System, including white blood cell count, absolute neutrophil count, absolute lymphocyte count, platelet count, C-reactive protein, and serum albumin levels. Statistical analysis

Count data are expressed as N(%). Intergroup comparisons were analysed using the chi-square test or the FISH exact probability method. Normally distributed continuous data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Non-normally distributed continuous data are presented as [M (P25, P75)], and intergroup comparisons were performed using the Mann-Whitney U test. The above statistical analyses were performed using R software(version 4.3.1), and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Mixed infection in other sites refers to the isolation of C.auris in other sites prior to the diagnosis of C.auris bloodstream infection [16].

Results

Hospitalisation information

Among the 11 patients with C.auris bloodstream infections, 1 died and 10 survived within 30 days, resulting in a mortality rate of 9.1% (1/11). The deceased patient was Case 5, a male who developed fever on the fifth day of hospitalisation. Blood culture results were positive, with C.auris isolated. The patient died on the 29th day of infection, with the cause of death being severe infection and heart failure. Among the 11 patients, 10 were male and 1 was female; ages ranged from 42 to 93 years, with an average age of 62 ± 18 years; they were distributed across four different departments: 4 cases in the Acupuncture and Rehabilitation Department, 4 cases in the ICU, 2 cases in the Respiratory Medicine Department, and 1 case in the Endocrinology Department. In terms of the distribution of infection times among cases, two cases were identified in 2023, two cases in 2024, and seven cases in 2025. The median length of hospital stay was 29(13,32) days, and the median time from admission to infection was 5(3,12) days. The average body temperature at the time of infection was 38.4 ± 0.9 °C. Eleven patients mainly receivedInvasive operation nursing, airway nursing, rehabilitation nursing. Among the 11 patients, five were in the poor prognosis group, comprising four cases of discharges against medical advice and one death. All four patients discharged against medical advice were discharged due to lack of improvement following infection, with families opting to discontinue treatment or pursue palliative care. Discussion

This study conducted a retrospective analysis of the clinical data of 11 patients with C.auris bloodstream infections to systematically investigate their clinical characteristics, antifungal drug resistance, and prognostic factors. The results showed that patients with this type of infection exhibited significant population and departmental distribution characteristics, with a prominent pattern of drug resistance. The NPAR can serve as a prognostic assessment indicator.

In this study, C.auris bloodstream infection patients were predominantly male (10/11, 90.9%), with an average age of (62 ± 18) years. Previous reports indicate that C.auris patients are more commonly older males. An analysis of C.auris infections from 2009 to 2020 showed that among 827 patients, 61.4% were males, and 47.7% were aged ≥ 60 years [17]. In terms of C.auris bloodstream infections, this trend is even more pronounced. Vinayagamoorthy et al. [18] reported that the average age of C.auris bloodstream infection patients among COVID-19 patients was 65.3 years, with males accounting for 80.0%(28/35); Mulet et al. [19] found through a four-year follow-up study that the median age of 86 C.auris bloodstream infection patients was 65 years, with males accounting for 80.2%(69/86). The findings of this study are consistent with the reported data, suggesting that middle-aged and elderly males may be at higher risk due to impaired immune function and a higher burden of underlying conditions.In terms of departmental distribution, the highest proportions were observed in the Acupuncture and Rehabilitation Department (4 cases, 36.4%) and the ICU (4 cases, 36.4%). Previous reports have indicated that the distribution of C.auris and infection outbreaks primarily occur in ICUs [20–23], with 94% of C.auris isolates from certain regions having previously been admitted to an ICU. Research has also highlighted that reusable medical devices, including thermometers, blood pressure cuffs, and infusion pumps, are significant contributors to C.auris infections and transmission [21, 23]. Patients in the acupuncture rehabilitation department are primarily bedridden due to conditions such as cerebral infarction or haemorrhage, requiring long-term rehabilitation treatment and nursing care, which necessitates the use of shared medical equipment. Additionally, they often require PICC (100%, 4/4 cases).Patients in the Acupuncture and Rehabilitation Department were primarily bedridden due to conditions such as cerebral infarction or cerebral haemorrhage, requiring long-term indwelling CAU (100%, 4/4 cases) or PICC(100%, 4/4 cases). Invasive procedures are a significant pathway for C.auris to breach mucosal barriers and enter the bloodstream [24–26]. Pneumonia (8 cases) was the most common complication among the 11 patients, and 9 patients had received broad-spectrum antibiotics prior to infection, further impairing their ability to resist infection. This aligns with the findings of Benedict et al. [26], who reported that C.auris bloodstream infection patients often have underlying conditions such as diabetes and pneumonia.The antimicrobial susceptibility test results of this study showed that C.auris exhibited a high resistance rate to fluconazole, reaching 81.8%(9/11), which is highly consistent with the global resistance patterns of C.auris [4, 5]. This phenomenon is primarily associated with mutations in the ERG11 gene, which lead to structural changes in the target enzyme of azole drugs, 14α-demethylase of ergosterol [27, 28], suggesting that fluconazole should not be used as an empirical treatment for C.auris bloodstream infections. In the treatment regimen, six patients received caspofungin (an echinocandin), which acts by inhibiting the synthesis of β-1,3-glucan in fungal cell walls, resulting in a relatively low resistance rate. Among the cured cases, the highest proportion of patients received caspofungin, consistent with guidelines recommending echinocandins as first-line agents for C.auris infections [2, 29, 30]. No patients in this study received prophylactic antifungal therapy, and 7 patients received targeted treatment, suggesting that clinical awareness of early intervention for high-risk patients still needs to be strengthened.

In this study, two patients (Cases 2 and 8) did not receive antifungal therapy but had good outcomes. Case 2 and Case 8 were both diagnosed with catheter-related bloodstream infections. C.auris was isolated from both the catheter tips submitted for testing and the blood cultures. Following the subsequent blood culture alarm indicating a positive result, both patients had already returned to normal body temperature; consequently, antifungal therapy was not administered. This scenario represents a common challenge encountered in our clinical practice. On the one hand, the average time to positive fungal blood culture was 1.98 days, more than three times longer than the 0.57 days for Enterobacteriaceae [31], and the time required for antimicrobial susceptibility testing after a positive result was also at least two days. In clinical practice, by the time we provide antimicrobial susceptibility testing results, patients often no longer exhibit infection symptoms. At this point, initiating antifungal therapy may result in side effects outweighing the benefits [32]. On the other hand, there is currently a lack of randomised controlled clinical trials globally to evaluate the treatment of C.auris infections. Targeted treatment should be based on the severity of the infection, immune status, and C.auris antimicrobial susceptibility test results, with appropriate antifungal drugs selected comprehensively for treatment [11, 33].For patients who have been on long-term antibiotics or undergone invasive procedures, antifungal therapy should be initiated in conjunction with blood culture results when necessary.

Based on the characteristics of the patients in this study, targeted nursing measures include: (1) standardised nursing care for invasive procedures to block the core routes of infection; among the 11 patients, 10 received PICC and 9 used CAU. Invasive medical procedures can introduce C.auris directly from the skin into deep tissues or the bloodstream [24–26]. In daily clinical nursing practice, the use of urinary catheters must strictly adhere to the principle of ‘aseptic technique-meticulous maintenance-timely removal’; Additionally, for patients in the acupuncture rehabilitation department, due to prolonged bed rest, special attention should be paid to catheter fixation to avoid repeated movement of the catheter causing mucosal damage. Mucosal damage is the primary route for C.auris to enter the bloodstream following invasive procedures. (2) Targeted care for high-risk populations with underlying conditions: Enhancing infection resistance. The majority of patients are middle-aged and elderly males (90.9%), with many having comorbidities such as pneumonia (8 cases) and hypertension (6 cases). Nursing care should focus on nutritional support, monitoring serum albumin levels, and providing protein supplementation via enteral nutrition for those with albumin levels below 30 g/L. If necessary, albumin infusion may be administered as per medical advice to strengthen the body’s immune barrier and reduce the risk of infection. For patients at high risk, skin decontamination should be performed twice daily using a 2% chlorhexidine gluconate solution to wipe areas such as the axillae and inguinal regions to reduce the risk of C.auris colonisation [34, 35]. (3) Precision monitoring of antimicrobial use: Nine patients had used cephalosporin or carbapenem antibiotics prior to infection. The overuse of broad-spectrum antibiotics can disrupt the microbial balance and promote the proliferation of C.auris [36]. Establish an antibiotic usage log to record the type, dosage, and duration of treatment, with particular attention to the duration of carbapenem use. Collaborate with pharmacists to assess criteria for discontinuation or de-escalation of treatment. Additionally, observe patients for oral mucosal leukoplakia or diarrhoea to promptly identify signs of fungal infection. (4) Isolation measures and environmental management: C.auris is transmitted through contact and can survive in the environment for up to 14 days [37]. Confirmed patients should be placed in single-room isolation or centralized isolation, and implement infection control measures such as hand hygiene and environmental cleaning and disinfection. Research indicates [38] that promptly implementing contact precautions and appropriate environmental cleaning from the identification of the first positive case is critical to preventing C.auris-related outbreaks. (5) Disease monitoring and prognosis assessment: Dynamic optimisation of interventions. NPAR is a key prognostic indicator. This study found that the NPAR level in the poor prognosis group was significantly lower than that in the good prognosis group (P = 0.006). NPAR is the ratio of absolute neutrophil count to serum albumin, and changes in this indicator may more comprehensively reflect the severity of infection.Nursing care should combine laboratory indicators with clinical symptoms for comprehensive monitoring and indicator tracking. Daily monitoring of blood counts and serum albumin should be conducted, with NPAR calculated dynamically.When NPAR < 0.2, this indicates an increased risk of poor prognosis, and the physician should be notified promptly to adjust the treatment plan.

In summary, C.auris bloodstream infections are characterised by low incidence and high mortality rates, and currently lack large-scale epidemiological data. This study systematically collected complete clinical data from 11 confirmed cases, focusing on the severe type of bloodstream infection, and conducted a comprehensive analysis of its clinical characteristics, antimicrobial resistance, and prognostic factors. The findings revealed that patients in the acupuncture rehabilitation department, who are often bedridden and require long-term catheterisation, constitute a high-risk group for C.auris bloodstream infections. Additionally, preliminary findings suggest that NPAR may have prognostic value for C.auris bloodstream infections. In terms of nursing care, based on the finding that invasive procedures (10 cases of central venous catheterisation and 9 cases of urinary catheterisation) are the primary routes of infection, a standardised management protocol of ‘aseptic technique-meticulous maintenance-timely catheter removal’ is proposed, along with targeted measures such as nutritional support for patients with underlying conditions and environmental disinfection, providing practical guidelines for clinical infection control.

However, at the same time, we must recognise that this research has certain limitations.This study is a single-centre retrospective study with a small sample size, which may introduce selection bias. Additionally, genetic sequencing of C.auris was not performed, precluding the establishment of a clear association between resistance genes and clinical phenotypes. Furthermore, the prognostic value of NPAR requires validation through multicentre, large-sample studies. Next, we shall collaborate with other medical institutions to gather additional data for further analysis. Furthermore, we plan to conduct follow-up observations in conjunction with the ward on NPAR data from patients with C.auris bloodstream infections, identifying suitable candidates for timely intervention to further clarify the prognostic value of NPAR.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the 11 patients with C.auris bloodstream infections in this study were predominantly male, most had underlying conditions such as pneumonia and hypertension, had undergone invasive procedures, and had been exposed to antibiotics, with caspofungin being the most commonly used treatment. The resistance rate of C.auris to fluconazole reached 81.8%. Clinicians can use NPAR as an assessment criterion to strengthen monitoring of high-risk populations, standardise antifungal treatment, and implement targeted nursing measures to reduce infection risks and improve patient outcomes.

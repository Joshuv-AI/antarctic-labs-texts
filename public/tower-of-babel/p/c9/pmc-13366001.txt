IFPHPOS0026 Mental and Physical Health Outcomes of Informal Caregivers: A Systematic Review
Authors: Danielle Varley, Rebecca Jones, Anne-Marie Bagnall
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionInformal caregiving involves providing short- or long-term support for a family member, partner, friend, or neighbour with a disability, illness or injury. While frameworks have been developed to support informal caregivers, informal caregivers are typically unaware of the support available and are not trained or adequately equipped to undertake strenuous tasks, leading to negative physical and mental health outcomes. This systematic review aimed to synthesise the literature to investigate the impact of informal caregiving on physical and mental health.MethodsUsing a developed strategy, six electronic databases were searched and retrieved records screened by two independent reviewers. A data extraction table was used to assist in sifting, sorting and organising relevant information. Quality assessments were completed for each included record.ResultsOf the 5932 retrieved articles, 29 studies met the inclusion criteria. Evidence suggests caregivers are likely to suffer from pain, anxiety, depression and burnout, as well as diminished quality of life, impaired wellbeing and difficulty sleeping. Caregiver age, gender and socioeconomic status were found to be associated with impaired mental health.DiscussionWhile there are a small number of protective individual factors, informal caregivers are more likely to experience poorer physical and mental health than the general population. As the number of informal caregivers is likely to increase due to an ageing population, health and social care systems must prioritise how they can best support them. However, it must be acknowledged that training caregivers or providing extensive support may be difficult due to austerity measures and staff shortages.

In vitro Comparison of Soft-Tissue Grafting Materials for Periodontal Surgery: A Systematic Review
Authors: Ojas Anand Gajbhiye, Meghna Chauhan, Mohanasaranya Sethu Muniyasamy, Debarghya Pal, Shanvi Ray, Manish Rathi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThis systematic review was done to examine in vitro research that evaluates the features of different soft tissue grafting materials in periodontal surgery. The search was conducted using the terms “soft tissue grafts,” “periodontal surgery,” “efficacy,” “growth factor,” and “cell viability”. Out of the 590 publications analyzed from 2010 to 2024, only 5 were chosen and included in the calculations based on their publication year. All things considered, in periodontal surgery acellular dermal matrix (ADM), platelet derivatives, and collagen matrices offer efficient substitutes for autogenous grafts that minimize donor-site morbidity and improve patient comfort. Though in some uses like root covering or soft tissue augmentation these materials produce similar outcomes, their usefulness varies depending on the clinical setting.

INTRODUCTION

When it comes to periodontal treatment, periodontal soft tissue grafting is a crucial component for reducing gingival recession, improving aesthetics, and enhancing functional outcomes. Throughout medical history, autogenous grafts, which include subepithelial connective tissue grafts (SCTG) and free gingival grafts (FGG), have been considered to be the gold standard.[12] The purpose of this systematic review is to examine in vitro research that evaluates the features of different grafting materials, with a particular focus on cellular response, extracellular matrix production, and long-term durability.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Using databases including PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science, a thorough literature search was undertaken to find in vitro studies comparing several soft tissue grafting materials for periodontal surgery. Research that had been published in publications that had been subjected to peer review and evaluated biocompatibility, fibroblast adhesion, collagen production, and inflammatory reactions was included that met the inclusion criteria.

DISCUSSION

ADM is a biological scaffold made from human or animal tissue that retains collagen, elastin, and other extracellular matrix proteins but discards cells.[8] Revascularization and cellular infiltration help this material integrate into the receiving tissue, supporting regeneration.

Autologous platelet derivatives like platelet rich plasma (PRP) and platelet rich fibrin (PRF) are rich in growth factors that aid tissue regeneration and wound healing.[9] In intrabony defects and mucogingival surgeries, PRF improves bone fill and soft tissue repair. PRF outperforms open flap debridement in regeneration, according to research. Though less effective than connective tissue grafts (CTG) or enamel matrix derivatives (EMD) in root covering procedures.[10]

Periodontal surgery also uses collagen matrices from swine or bovine tissues. Comprehensive processing reduces immunogenicity and provides a biocompatible foundation for cellular adhesion and tissue regeneration.[11]

Due to their predictability and regeneration capabilities, autogenous grafts are the best alternative for many periodontal procedures. CTG uses circulatory linkages between the graft and recipient to improve root coverage and soft tissue augmentation.[12]

CONCLUSION

In summary, ADM, platelet derivatives, and collagen matrices present effective alternatives to autogenous grafts in periodontal surgery.

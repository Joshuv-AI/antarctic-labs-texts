A protocol for real-time tracking of transposon products and host proteins in Drosophila ovaries
Authors: Yaqian Xu, Kun Dou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryDrosophila ovary is an excellent model for studying material transportation and dynamics. Here, we present a protocol for live imaging of transposon products and host proteins in Drosophila ovaries. We describe steps from ovary dissection, ovariole isolation, and mounting to live tracking of fluorescently labeled proteins with single-cell resolution. Furthermore, we detail procedures of image processing for jitter correction and kymograph analysis. This protocol can be applied to studies on the dynamics of other fluorescently tagged proteins in Drosophila ovaries.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Shen et al.1Subject areas: cell Biology, developmental biology, Genetics, Microscopy, model Organisms

Before you begin

The protocol below describes detailed steps for real-time tracking of transposon RNPs together with host Staufen particles in live Drosophila egg chambers of the ovary. The sample preparation process is modified from the procedures in the relevant chapter of “Drosophila: Methods and Protocols”.2 We further include the procedures of image processing for jitter correction and kymograph generation. This protocol uses female adult Drosophila of the following genotype: nos-Gal4/αtub67C-RFP-Staufen; UASp-aub sh, 3S18-GFP/ubi-RFP-Pav. nos-Gal4 drives the transcription of UASp-aub short hairpin sequence in germ cells, to induce Aub depletion. αtub67C-RFP-Staufen indicates the Staufen particles.1,3 3S18-GFP labels the integrase of the 3S18 retrotransposon.1 ubi-RFP-Pav marks the cell-cell bridges, ring canals.1,3 This protocol can be applied to studies on dynamics of other fluorescently labeled proteins.

Innovation

This protocol combines the live imaging protocol with the image jitter correction method, providing a detailed hands-on procedure from sample preparation to image acquisition and data processing. The ovariole sample preparation method is modified from “Drosophila: Methods and Protocols”,2 and is further applied to simultaneous detection of the dynamics of transposon products and host particles. Through real-time imaging with multi-channel fluorescence detection, the impact of transposon activation on host material transportation is clearly observed. Furthermore, we include jitter correction of two fluorescent channels and kymograph analysis procedures in this workflow.

Procedures of jitter collection using plugins of Fiji

Expected outcomes

This protocol describes the steps for dissecting Drosophila ovaries, separating individual ovarioles, and mounting them for real-time tracking the dynamics of fluorescence-labeled proteins with single cell resolution (Methods Video S1), as well as subsequent procedures for image jitter correction and kymograph generation. A successful live imaging enables clear observation of target particle transportation through the ring canals. Subsequent image processing using Fiji plugin can correct sample drift in the xy plane, resulting in jitter-free time-lapse recordings. Furthermore, the Fiji plugin KymographBuilder allows generation of kymographs, which indicate the spatial-temporal dynamics of the targets. The protocol described here has been validated for real-time tracking and analysis of the 3S18 transposon RNPs, host Staufen RNPs (Figure 4), lipid droplets, and mitochondria.1

Limitations

The medium in this protocol provides sufficient nutrients to maintain egg chamber viability for up to two hours. Therefore, it is unsuitable for imaging beyond two hours, for example, to observe the egg chamber growth. The addition of LMA helps to prevent the sample drift, but it is difficult to replenish new nutrients for the egg chambers. In our hands, this protocol has been validated for live-imaging of 40 min.

Although egg chambers are placed near the glass bottom of the imaging dish to minimize the working distance for the microscope objective, the imaging depth is still limited. We have validated this protocol for imaging stage 4–7 egg chambers. For larger egg chambers, for example stage 9 egg chamber with a diameter of approximately 40–50 μm,4 good resolution may only be achieved for the proximal one third of the egg chamber.

The laser power, exposure time, and intervals between captures should be adjusted based on specific characteristics of the fluorescent proteins. For particles with very weak fluorescence signals, the signal-noise ratio may be low, thus may not be suitable for live-imaging. Additionally, images using multiple fluorescence channels need to be sequentially acquired due to the single-camera configuration of the microscope. Given that multiple Z-sections are needed for each channel, it is challenging to capture more than two channels within a short time. This issue could potentially be resolved by implementing dual detection cameras to capture two channels simultaneously.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

The ovarioles shift during live imaging.

Potential solution

•Ovariole shifts are often caused by insufficient concentration of low melting agarose. Melt the 2.5% LMA for at least half an hour at 65°C to ensure complete dissolution. Gently pipette a sufficient amount of 2.5% LMA into the live imaging medium that has been preheated to 37°C to dilute it to 0.5% to 0.8%. If the ovarioles still move obviously, increase the LMA concentration appropriately. Note that the final concentration should not exceed 0.8%, as higher concentration of LMA will exert considerable pressure on the ovarioles after solidification (step 3).

Problem 5

Potential solution

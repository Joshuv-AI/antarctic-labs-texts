Hybrid golden jackal and golden sine optimizer for tuning PID controllers
Authors: Kailong Mou, Ming Yang, Mengjian Zhang, Deguang Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In the domain of control engineering, effectively tuning the parameters of proportional-integral-derivative (PID) controllers has persistently posed a challenge. This study proposes a hybrid algorithm (HGJGSO) that combines golden jackal optimization (GJO) and golden sine algorithm (Gold-SA) for tuning PID controllers. To accelerate the convergence of GJO, a nonlinear parameter adaptation strategy is incorporated. The improved GJO is combined with Gold-SA, capitalizing on the expedited convergence speed offered by the improved GJO, coupled with the global optimization and precise search capabilities of Gold-SA. HGJGSO maximizes the strengths of two algorithms, facilitating a comprehensive and balanced exploration and exploitation. The effectiveness of HGJGSO is assessed through tuning the PID controllers for three typical systems. The results indicate that HGJGSO surpasses the comparison tuning methods. To evaluate the applicability of HGJGSO, it is used to tune the cascade PID controllers for trajectory tracking in a quadrotor UAV. The results demonstrate the superiority of HGJGSO in addressing practical challenges.

Introduction

PID controllers have a long-established history in process control applications within industries, dating back to the 1890s1–3. Despite their long history, PID controllers continue to dominate the field as the most widely used controllers in process industries. The research on PID controllers reports that approximately 90% of process industries rely on PID as their preferred control mechanism4. This widespread utilization is attributed to the PID controller’s robustness, simplicity, independence of the system model, and ease of retuning control parameters. However, PID controllers face certain shortcomings. For instance, being a linear controller, attempting to control a non-linear system requires linearizing the model, which diminishes control accuracy. Additionally, the correct setting of PID controller parameters is crucial in different control systems. Incorrect settings will result in undesired speed overshoot, sluggish response, or even loss of control. Therefore, this study delves into the problem of optimizing PID controller parameters.

Brief literature review

Tuning PID controller parameters has long been recognized as a challenging and significant issue, making it a hot research topic in both academic and industrial settings. The traditional methods (TM) used to tune PID controller parameters include Ziegler-Nichols (Z-N) method5, Chien-Hrones-Reswick (C-H-R) method6, and Cohen-Coon (C-C) method7. However, the effectiveness of tuning PID controller parameters using these methods heavily depends on observation and experience. Additionally, these methods necessitate continuous parameter adjustments and calculations throughout the regulation process, which are time-consuming and energy-intensive. Furthermore, it fails to achieve precise regulation8–10.

In recent years, various methods based on intelligent algorithms have emerged to optimize PID controller parameters for regulating closed-loop control systems and achieving objectives related to stability, applicability, and robustness. These methods include swarm intelligence algorithms (SIAs)11, expert systems (ESs)12, neural networks (NNs)13–15, and fuzzy control (FC)16–18. As a category of random search algorithms, SIAs are inspired by biological intelligence or natural phenomena. Generally, they simulate foraging and reproduction behaviors observed in nature. By abstracting these behaviors into measurable key indicators and establishing mathematical models, SIAs have been widely applied to address various optimization problems19–24. Classical SIAs include particle swarm optimization (PSO)25, ant colony optimization (ACO)26, gray wolf optimizer (GWO)27, sparrow search algorithm (SSA)28, duck swarm algorithm (DSA)29. Kashyap et al.30 utilize PSO to adjust the PID gait controller of a humanoid robot, reducing stabilization time and eliminating overshoot. Mughees et al.31 design a magnetic levitation system and use ACO to adjust fractional order PID controller parameters. Rana et al.32 employ GWO to tune the PID controller, achieving efficient maximum power point tracking for PEM fuel cells. As science and technology advance, the complexity of optimization problems continues to increase. Many of these problems are characterized by multi-factor dependencies, large-scale problems, and high difficulty. Although original SIAs have significant dvantages, such as easy to implement and understand and capable of quickly generating solutions, they may struggle with the intricate relationships and large-scale nature of modern problems and not adapt well to the diverse and evolving requirements of complex problems. To address these challenges, researchers and practitioners are increasingly turning to advanced technique33–35. These advancements focus on enhancing convergence speed, optimization precision, and the capability to avoid local optima. Feng et al.36 propose an improved PSO to optimize the parameters of a PID controller with the goal of enhancing the tracking accuracy of a robotic excavator electro-hydraulic position servo system. Liang et al.37 introduce an improved GA specifically designed to optimize the nonlinear fuzzy control rules in a fuzzy PID controller.

Hybrid algorithms (HAs) are a key area of research due to their potential to combine the strengths of different algorithms and address their individual limitations38. By integrating two or more algorithms, HAs can leverage the unique advantages of each component algorithm and tackle a wide range of complex optimization problems that may be challenging for a single algorithm to handle effectively. Izci et al.39 present an arithmetic optimization and Nelder-Mead algorithm (AOA-NM) and apply it to a PID controller-based automotive cruise control system. Izci et al.40 develop a hybrid atom search particle swarm optimization (H-ASPSO) algorithm, which is then used for an automatic voltage regulator. Izci et al.41 combine atom search optimization with Nelder-Mead algorithm and apply it to a doubly fed induction generator-based wind turbine system. These studies illustrate how HA can leverage the strengths of multiple optimization techniques to solve complex problems effectively, achieving robust and generalizable solutions in various applications. Eker et al.42 introduce HASO-SA algorithm, which combines atom search optimization (ASO) with simulated annealing (SA). This hybrid approach is designed to improve optimization results for both nonlinear and linear problems. Mishra et al.43 employ PSO-GWO to fine-tune the parameters of the FOPID controller for reactive power and harmonic compensation of power filters under different conditions. Although HAs often enhance performance by combining strengths of different methods, they still face challenges, such as slow convergence, high computational complexity, and poor maneuverability. Addressing these issues often involves developing more advanced HAs that enhance the balance between exploration and exploitation, improve convergence speed, and reduce computational complexity while maintaining versatility and adaptability. Xu et al.44 develop HQPSO-VNS, which integrates quantum particle swarm optimization (QPSO) and variable neighborhood search (VNS), providing faster convergence, better stability, and wider applicability. Zhang et al.45 design a new HA, which leverages the advantages of both GWO and PSO to address complex optimization challenges. Although HAs can offer superior performance and versatility in some cases, these disadvantages and limitations must be carefully considered during their design and implementation.

Golden jackal optimization (GJO) emulates the cooperative hunting behavior of golden jackals, leveraging their collaborative group mechanism to achieve optimization objectives46. The hunting mechanism has demonstrated effectiveness in balancing exploration and exploitation, exhibiting notable performance in convergence speed and solution accuracy. Despite the widespread adoption of GJO in various domains, it exhibits two notable shortcomings. First, the linear change in energy variation during prey evasion in GJO results in slow convergence during the iteration process. Second, with an increasing number of iterations, individuals in the population gradually converge towards male and female golden jackals with better fitness. If superior jackals cannot escape from local optima, the entire population becomes susceptible to getting stuck in local optima. Wang et al.47 apply GJO based on reinforcement learning for segmenting CT images to facilitate COVID-19 diagnosis. Lou et al.48 improve GJO for mobile robot path planning, incorporating a pre-decrement slow non-linear energy attenuation strategy and a roulette selection mechanism. Elseify et al.49 propose a novel placement technique based on improved GJO for single-objective and multi-objective problems. Rezaie et al.50 combine PSO and GJO to predict the efficiency and output power of a fuel cell. Houssein et al.51 present an improved GJO based on a dyadic learning mechanism. Tuning PID controller parameters using the proposed HGJGSO

This section proposes a hybrid golden jackal and golden sine optimizer (HGJGSO). First, the fundamental principles of golden jackal optimization (GJO) and golden sine algorithm (Gold-SA) are presented. Subsequently, a nonlinear parameter adaptation strategy is used to improve the performance of the original GJO. Finally, HGJGSO is developed by integrating Gold-SA with the enhanced GJO and applied to tune PID controller parameters.

Golden jackal optimization

GJO53 is a novel swarm intelligence algorithm inspired by cooperative foraging and hunting mechanism of jackals. In GJO, jackals run parallel to their prey and surpass it. There are three primary stages in the hunting process of jackals: (1) searching for advancing toward the prey; (2) encircling and disturbing the prey until it halts; (3) attacking the prey with a pounce. The mathematical formulation of GJO is summarized as follows.

Exploration phase

The nature of jackals allows them to perceive and track the prey. however, occasional escapes or difficulty in capturing the prey may occur. Consequently, jackals patiently wait and seek new prey. In this phase, the hunting process is led by the male jackal, followed by the female jackal. Hybrid algorithm based on improved GJO and Gold-SA

Both GJO and Gold-SA exhibit distinctive characteristics. Each algorithm incorporates unique search mechanisms. GJO, guided by the male jackal with support from the female jackal for position update, can rapidly and effectively identify optimal solutions in the search space. In contrast, Gold-SA, guided by the four parameters \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${r_1}$$\end{document}r1, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${r_2}$$\end{document}r2, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${c _1}$$\end{document}c1, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${c _2}$$\end{document}c2, achieves global optimization in complex problems with multiple local optimal solutions. Consequently, GJO excels in global search but falls short in local search. Gold-SA, remarkably efficient in global optimization and precise search, compensates for GJO’s deficiency in local search, enhancing the precision and quality of results. On the other hand, despite Gold-SA’s capability to deliver high-accuracy results, it may experience extended convergence times due to the extensive calculations required during iterations, thus reducing efficiency. The search strategy of GJO, known for efficient optimization and quick convergence, can offset this drawback of Gold-SA.

The performance metrics include overshoot, settling time, peak time, and ITAE value. Overshoot represents the percentage by which the peak of the system response curve exceeds the desired value. It indicates the extent of deviation from the target value before settling. Settling time is the time required for the system to remain within a certain error band around the final value. It measures how quickly the system stabilizes after a disturbance or change in input. Peak time is the time that it takes for the system to reach its first peak value in the response curve. It reflects how quickly the system initially reacts to a change. ITAE measures that considers the magnitude of error over time, with lower values indicating better performance. It assesses how well the system minimizes errors throughout its response. These performance metrics provide a comprehensive view of how each algorithm affects the control results of the systems, allowing for a detailed comparison of their effectiveness in managing system dynamics.

Frequency response analysis

Frequency response analysis is a fundamental tool in control systems used to assess system stability by analyzing how the system responds to different frequencies of input signals. This technique involves examining a system’s response to sinusoidal inputs at various frequencies, which helps in understanding the system’s behavior in the frequency domain. It is crucial for designing controllers that meet specific performance criteria and for ensuring system stability. Magnitude plot shows how the amplitude of the output changes with frequency and phase plot depicts how the phase of the output changes with frequency. HGJGSO-based trajectory tracking for quadrotor UAV

Quadrotor UAVs are multi-input, strongly coupled, multivariate, and underactuated systems, and they are applied in various fields, such as aerial photography, archaeology, and border patrol55,56. To validate the applicability of HGJGSO, this section employs it to optimize the PID parameters of a quadrotor UAV for trajectory tracking.

Quadrotor UAV controller design

As depicted in Fig. 14, cascade PID control expands on single-loop PID control by integrating an additional PID controller, establishing inner and outer loops for dual-loop control. Unlike single-loop PID control, where only one parameter is adjusted, cascade PID control enables joint regulation by the inner and outer loops, aligning positional and velocity changes. This configuration ensures that during quadrotor UAV flight, intense variations do not induce drastic oscillations in the trajectory, which enhances resilience against external disturbances. Conclusion and future work

This study addresses the challenge of parameter tuning in PID controllers using a hybrid golden jackal and gold sine optimizer algorithm (HGJGSO). A nonlinear parameter adaptation strategy using cosine function transforms the linear decay of prey energy into a nonlinear form, which expedites the convergence of GJO. The effectiveness and practicality of HGJGSO in tuning PID controllers are validated by two experiments. In the first experiment, HGJGSO is used to tune PID controller parameters for stable, large overshoot, and unstable systems. HGJGSO is compared with six classical intelligent algorithms and Z-N method. Performance indicators, including overshoot, settling time, and peak time, collectively show that HGJGSO outperforms the comparison algorithms. In the second experiment, HGJGSO is applied to tune the cascade PID controller parameters for a quadrotor UAV, which achieve the precise trajectory tracking. In comparison to GJO, HGJGSO exhibits superior precision and closely adheres to the desired motion trajectory. Future work will delve into the implementation of HGJGSO in addressing real PID control systems, especially in intricate or dynamic environments. In addition, future research will also focus on discovering novel combination mechanisms, improving adaptability, and incorporating machine learning and artificial intelligence into the algorithm design.

An Unresponsive Male and an Indoor Generator
Authors: Emma Alley, Kristen Kish
Published: 2025

Case Presentation

The patient was a 21-year-old male with no significant past medical history who presented to the emergency department (ED) after being found unresponsive in his home. A storm had caused a power outage, and he had started a generator in his home to restore power. The patient regained consciousness after removal from the home and application of 15 L nonrebreather but remained confused with a Glasgow coma score of 14. He was tachycardic on arrival to the ED with carbon monoxide (co) levels of 15.3% total hemoglobin (coHb), lactate level of 5.4 mmol/L, and initial troponin level of 92 ng/L. Diagnosis: Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Leading to ST-Elevations

The patient was diagnosed with co poisoning leading to ST-elevations. co poisoning results in approximately 40,000 ED visits per year. Common presenting symptoms may include headache, nausea, blurred vision, confusion, shortness of breath, syncope, and chest pain.1,2 ST-elevations are an uncommon complication of co poisoning. In rare cases, co poisoning can lead to ST-elevation myocardial infarction, even in patients without underlying coronary artery disease.1,3 Current guidelines for use of hyperbaric oxygen for treatment of co poisoning include coHb >25% or >15% in pregnancy, loss of consciousness, pH < 7.25, and evidence of end-organ damage, such as altered mental status, EKG changes, or elevated cardiac biomarkers. Patients without these findings may be observed for 4 to 6 hours on 15 L nonrebreathers.2 Our patient ultimately received hyperbaric oxygen therapy with improvement of his cardiac enzymes and mental status. He had a normal echocardiogram after hyperbaric oxygen therapy.

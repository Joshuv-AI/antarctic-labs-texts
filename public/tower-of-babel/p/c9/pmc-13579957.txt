Adjuvant systemic therapy versus active surveillance after resection following conversion therapy for hepatocellular carcinoma: a propensity score matched study
Authors: Benyi He, Zehao Zheng, Junyu Gan, Zhijun Zuo, Min Deng, Jiahua Wen, Jie Mei, Wenmin Liao, Longzhou Xu, Rongce Zhao, Rongping Guo, Lianghe Lu, Wei Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe role of adjuvant systemic therapy after resection following conversion therapy in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) rendered resectable by transarterial therapy combined with systemic therapy remains unclear. This study aimed to evaluate the impact of adjuvant systemic therapy on survival outcomes in real-world practice.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed patients with initially unresectable HCC who underwent curative-intent resection after successful first-line transarterial therapy combined with systemic therapy between January 2018 and December 2023. Postoperative management included either adjuvant systemic therapy or active surveillance. Propensity score matching (1:2) was applied to balance baseline characteristics. Overall survival (OS) and recurrence-free survival (RFS) were evaluated using Kaplan-Meier methods and compared by log-rank tests. Subgroup and recurrence pattern analyses were also performed.ResultsAmong 496 eligible patients, 120 received adjuvant systemic therapy and 376 underwent active surveillance. After matching, 326 patients were included (116 vs. 210). Adjuvant systemic therapy was associated with significantly improved OS (HR 0.58, 95% CI 0.38–0.89; p = 0.01) and RFS (HR 0.64, 95% CI 0.48–0.84; p = 0.002). Survival benefit of adjuvant systemic therapy was observed in patients with BCLC stage C disease (OS: HR 0.53, 95% CI 0.31–0.90, p = 0.02; RFS: HR 0.61, 95% CI 0.43–0.87, p = 0.007) and in those with BCLC stage A or B disease exceeding the up-to-seven criteria (OS: HR 0.46, 95% CI 0.22–0.95, p = 0.03; RFS: HR 0.56, 95% CI 0.34–0.91, p = 0.02). Multivariable analysis identified adjuvant systemic therapy as an independent predictor of improved OS and RFS.ConclusionsIn patients undergoing resection following conversion therapy for initially unresectable HCC, adjuvant systemic therapy was associated with improved survival, particularly among those with high-risk disease features.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-026-05029-x.

Background

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the predominant histological subtype of primary liver cancer and a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [1]. Approximately 70% of patients present with intermediate or advanced-stage disease and are not candidates for upfront surgical resection at diagnosis [2, 3]. With the advent of combination therapeutic strategies, particularly first-line transarterial and systemic treatments, a proportion of initially unresectable tumors can be rendered resectable, enabling subsequent hepatectomy [4–8]. However, despite curative-intent resection, postoperative recurrence remains common, raising a critical clinical dilemma regarding the role of adjuvant therapy.

Although multiple systemic agents have demonstrated clear clinical benefit in advanced-stage HCC, their role as adjuvant therapy after curative-intent resection remains poorly defined. Randomized trials evaluating adjuvant systemic therapy in resected HCC have largely yielded negative or inconclusive results. Notably, the phase III STORM trial failed to demonstrate a recurrence-free survival (RFS) benefit with adjuvant sorafenib compared with placebo (HR 0.94, 95% CI 0.78–1.13) [9]. Similarly, in the updated analysis of the IMbrave050 trial, the RFS benefit of adjuvant atezolizumab plus bevacizumab over active surveillance was not sustained (HR 0.90, 95% CI 0.72–1.12) [10]. In contrast, a randomized phase II trial demonstrated that adjuvant sintilimab significantly prolonged RFS compared with active surveillance in patients with HCC and microvascular invasion (HR 0.53, 95% CI 0.36–0.79), with a manageable safety profile [11], highlighting the potential importance of patient selection in the postoperative setting.

Given the lack of consensus regarding adjuvant systemic therapy after resection for HCC, postoperative management remains highly variable in clinical practice, particularly among patients who undergo surgery following successful first-line transarterial therapy combined with systemic therapy as conversion therapy [12, 13]. In this specific population, whether the administration of adjuvant systemic therapy confers additional survival benefit compared with active surveillance has not been adequately investigated. Most available evidence has focused on unselected resected populations or perioperative treatment strategies, leaving the postoperative adjuvant setting after resection following conversion therapy largely unexplored.

To address this knowledge gap, we conducted a real-world propensity score matched study to compare the clinical outcomes of adjuvant systemic therapy versus active surveillance after curative-intent resection in patients with HCC who achieved resectability following first-line transarterial therapy combined with systemic therapy.

Methods

Study population

This retrospective cohort study included consecutive patients with HCC who underwent curative-intent hepatic resection following conversion therapy at Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center between January 2018 and December 2023. All patients were initially deemed unresectable at diagnosis and subsequently received first-line transarterial therapy combined with systemic therapy, after which surgical resection became feasible.

Patients were eligible for inclusion if they met the following criteria: (i) age ≥ 18 years; (ii) histologically or clinically confirmed HCC, as defined by the diagnostic criteria of the European Association for the Study of the Liver and the American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases [14, 15]; (iii) receipt of first-line transarterial therapy combined with systemic therapy resulting in conversion to resectable disease; (iv) R0 resection with curative intent; and (v) availability of complete baseline clinical data and at least 4 months of follow-up from the index date. Patients were excluded if they had evidence of Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG-PS) other than 0–1, Child-Pugh score > 7, evidence of extrahepatic metastasis at the time of surgery. According to postoperative management, patients were classified into two groups: those who received adjuvant systemic therapy and those managed with active surveillance after resection.

The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board and Institutional Ethics Committee of Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center (Approval No. B2025-237-01). The requirement for informed consent was exempted by the ethics committee due to the retrospective design and use of anonymized data. The study was conducted in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Conversion therapy and resection

Conversion therapy is applied in selected patients with HCC who are not candidates for upfront resection because of technical constraints or unfavorable oncological characteristics. Details of conversion therapy administration are provided in the Additional file 1: Supplementary Methods [16, 17]. Resectability after conversion therapy was assessed by a multidisciplinary team based on tumor burden, liver function, and technical feasibility of complete resection. According to Chinese guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of primary liver cancer [18], criteria for resectability after conversion therapy included: (i) adequate future liver remnant volume (> 30%, or > 40% in patients with compromised liver function); (ii) feasibility of R0 resection; (iii) Child-Pugh class A or B; (iv) ECOG-PS 0–1; (v) absence of extrahepatic disease; and (vi) no other surgical contraindications.

Hepatic resection was performed using ultrasonic dissection, with meticulous hemostasis and selective placement of surgical drains, and R0 status was confirmed by postoperative pathological assessment.

Adjuvant systemic therapy administration

Decision of administer adjuvant systemic therapy after curative-intent resection was made by a multidisciplinary team based on a comprehensive assessment of both preoperative and postoperative characteristics. These considerations included baseline tumor burden and biological features, radiological response to conversion therapy, postoperative pathological findings, postoperative performance status and liver function, as well as patient preference and treatment affordability. Dosing schedules and the categories of adjuvant systemic regimens are detailed in Additional file 1: Table S1 and Additional file 1: Table S2. Adjuvant treatment was generally initiated within 4 to 8 weeks after surgery and continued until disease recurrence, unacceptable toxicity, or completion of the planned course, in accordance with institutional practice.

Follow-up and outcomes

Patients underwent an initial postoperative assessment within 4–8 weeks after resection, followed by scheduled follow-up visits every 2 to 3 months thereafter. Postoperative status and laboratory parameters were assessed at the time of the initial postoperative evaluation. At each visit, assessments included physical examination, blood tests (including serum tumor markers and liver function tests), and contrast-enhanced abdominal computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Tumor response before surgery and postoperative recurrence were evaluated radiologically according to the modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.0 (mRECIST v1.0) [19]. Patients achieving complete or partial response after conversion therapy were classified as responders, whereas those with stable or progressive disease were categorized as non-responders. The last follow-up was conducted on March 1, 2025.

The primary outcome was OS (Defined as the time from the date of surgery to death from any cause. Patients without events were censored at the date of last follow-up.) and RFS (Defined as the interval from the date of resection to the first documented radiological recurrence or death from any cause, whichever occurred first.)

Statistics analysis

Categorical variables were summarized as numbers and percentages and compared using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. To reduce potential confounding between the adjuvant systemic therapy and active surveillance groups, propensity score matching (PSM) was performed using a logistic regression model. The PSM model included clinically relevant baseline variables potentially associated with postoperative treatment selection. Patients were matched at a 1:2 ratio using the nearest-neighbor method with a caliper width of 0.2. Covariate balance after matching was assessed using standardized mean differences (SMD), with an SMD < 0.1 considered indicative of adequate balance. In addition, inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) based on propensity scores was performed as a sensitivity analysis. Covariate balance after weighting was similarly evaluated using SMD, with values < 0.1 indicating adequate balance. Landmark analysis was conducted as a sensitivity analysis. Survival analyses were restricted to patients without recurrence or death within 2 month after resection, and follow-up started from the 2-month landmark time point.

OS and RFS were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared between groups with the log-rank test. Cox proportional hazards regression models were applied to identify factors associated with survival outcomes. Variables with a p value < 0.05 in univariable analyses were subsequently entered into multivariable models. Hazard ratios (HRs) and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported.

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.2.1). GraphPad Prism 8.0 was used for data visualization. All statistical tests were two-sided, and a p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

Among 610 patients with hepatocellular carcinoma who underwent resection after first-line systemic therapy during the study period, 496 patients received curative-intent resection following successful conversion therapy and were included in the final analysis. Of these, 120 patients received adjuvant systemic therapy, whereas 376 patients were managed with active surveillance after surgery (Fig. 1).

After 1:2 propensity score matching, 326 patients were included in the matched cohort, comprising 116 patients in the adjuvant systemic therapy group and 210 patients in the active surveillance group. Following matching, baseline demographic, clinical, and tumor-related characteristics were well balanced between the two groups, with all standardized mean differences below 0.1 (Table 1).

In the matched cohort, patient age, sex, ECOG performance status, hepatitis B virus infection status, Child-Pugh class, and the presence of cirrhosis were comparable between groups. Tumor burden-related variables, including serum AFP and PIVKAⅡ levels, maximum tumor diameter, tumor number, and macrovascular invasion, were also well balanced. In addition, no significant differences were observed between the two groups with respect to conversion therapy characteristics (including transarterial modality, systemic regimen, and radiological response) or postoperative pathological findings, including tumor differentiation, MVI, and pathological complete response. Postoperative recovery status was comparable between the two groups, with no significant differences in postoperative Child-Pugh class, hospital-stay, or laboratory parameters (Additional file 1: Table S3).

Survival outcomes associated with adjuvant systemic therapy

The median follow-up duration for the entire cohort was 31.0 months (interquartile range [IQR], 22.6–40.1). Among patients receiving adjuvant systemic therapy, the median treatment duration was 7.2 months (IQR, 4.0–11.7). In the overall cohort before propensity score matching, patients who received adjuvant systemic therapy demonstrated significantly improved OS and RFS compared with those managed with active surveillance (OS: HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.37–0.92, p = 0.02; RFS: HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.51–0.89, p = 0.005; Fig. 2A-B). The 1-, 2-, and 5-year OS rates were 97.5%, 86.9%, and 67.1% in the adjuvant group versus 95.7%, 82.3%, and 54.3% in the surveillance group, while the 1-, 2-, and 5-year RFS rates were 64.9%, 48.5%, and 34.5% versus 50.8%, 37.6%, and 24.9%, respectively. After 1:2 propensity score matching, the survival benefit of adjuvant systemic therapy remained consistent. In the matched cohort, adjuvant systemic therapy was associated with significantly prolonged OS (HR 0.58, 95% CI 0.38–0.89; p = 0.01) and RFS (HR 0.64, 95% CI 0.48–0.84; p = 0.002) (Fig. 2C-D). The 1-, 2-, and 5-year OS rates were 97.4%, 86.4%, and 66.4% in the adjuvant group compared with 94.1%, 80.1%, and 50.9% in the surveillance group, and the corresponding RFS rates were 65.4%, 48.4%, and 34.3% versus 49.9%, 36.3%, and 12.0%, respectively. The median RFS was 8.7 months longer in the adjuvant systemic therapy group (20.80 months; 95% CI, 15.97 months - NR) than the active surveillance group (12.10 months; 95% CI, 8.07–16.43 months). In sensitivity analyses using a landmark approach, the survival advantage associated with adjuvant systemic therapy remained consistent in the matched cohort (Additional file 1: Figure S1). Similarly, IPTW-adjusted analyses demonstrated improved covariate balance after weighting (Additional file 1: Figure S2) and further supported the association between adjuvant systemic therapy and favorable OS and RFS outcomes (Additional file 1: Figure S3).

BCLC stage–specific survival benefit of adjuvant systemic therapy

Stratified analyses according to Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) stage further demonstrated a differential treatment effect. Among patients with BCLC stage A or B disease, adjuvant systemic therapy was associated with an improvement in OS and RFS compared with active surveillance. However, these differences did not reach statistical significance (OS: HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.33–1.40, p = 0.30; RFS: HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.43–1.07, p = 0.09; Fig. 3A-B). In contrast, among patients with BCLC stage C disease, adjuvant systemic therapy conferred significantly improved OS and RFS (OS: HR 0.53, 95% CI 0.31–0.90, p = 0.02; RFS: HR 0.61, 95% CI 0.43–0.87, p = 0.007; Fig. 3C-D). Consistent findings were observed in the unmatched cohort, where adjuvant systemic therapy was associated with improved OS and RFS in patients with BCLC stage C disease, but not in those with BCLC stage A or B disease (Additional file 1: Figure S4).

Given the heterogeneity within early- and intermediate-stage disease, further subgroup analyses were performed among patients with BCLC stage A or B tumors exceeding the up-to-seven criteria and milan criteria [20, 21]. In the unmatched patients beyond up-to-seven criteria, adjuvant systemic therapy was associated with significantly improved OS and RFS compared with active surveillance (OS: HR 0.51, 95% CI 0.27–0.97, p = 0.04; RFS: HR 0.60, 95% CI 0.39–0.90, p = 0.01; Fig. 4A-B). These associations remained consistent after propensity score matching (OS: HR 0.46, 95% CI 0.22–0.95, p = 0.03; RFS: HR 0.56, 95% CI 0.34–0.91, p = 0.02; Fig. 4C-D). However, analyses based on the milan criteria showed no significant OS or RFS benefit of adjuvant systemic therapy among patients with BCLC stage A or B disease beyond the milan criteria, either before or after propensity score matching (Additional file 1: Figure S5).

Risk factors associated with OS and RFS

Univariate analysis identified low tumor differentiation, MVI, absence of pathological complete response, non-response to conversion therapy, and active surveillance as significant risk factors for OS. And elevated PIVKA-II levels, multiple tumors, low differentiation, MVI, absence of pathological complete response, non-response to conversion therapy, and active surveillance were significantly associated with inferior RFS (Table 2).

Subgroup analyses of survival outcomes

Given that adjuvant systemic therapy was identified as an independent prognostic factor for both OS and RFS in the multivariable analysis, further subgroup analyses were performed in the propensity score matched cohort to explore the consistency of treatment effects across clinically relevant subgroups (Fig. 5).

In the OS analysis (Fig. 5A), adjuvant systemic therapy was consistently associated with improved survival compared with active surveillance across multiple patient subgroups. Survival benefit was observed among patients aged < 55 years, those with hepatitis B virus infection, and patients with preserved liver function (Child-Pugh class A). Notably, adjuvant systemic therapy conferred pronounced OS benefit in subgroups characterized by high tumor burden, including patients with AFP ≥ 400 ng/ml, PIVKAⅡ ≥40 mAU/ml, maximum tumor diameter ≥ 100 mm, and multiple tumors. Consistent benefits were also observed among patients who treated with HAIC and ICIs-based systemic regimens as conversion therapy, and patients who achieved responders to conversion therapy. With respect to postoperative pathological features, adjuvant systemic therapy was associated with improved OS in patients with low tumor differentiation, absence of MVI, and absence of pathological complete response.

In the RFS analysis (Fig. 5B), adjuvant systemic therapy demonstrated a broadly consistent benefit over active surveillance across most subgroups. Improved RFS was observed in patients aged < 55 years, male patients, those with ECOG-PS 0, hepatitis B virus infection, Child-Pugh class A liver function, and absence of cirrhosis. Stratification by tumor-related characteristics similarly showed that patients with high tumor burden derived significant RFS benefit from adjuvant systemic therapy, including those with AFP ≥ 400 ng/ml, PIVKAⅡ ≥40 mAU/ml, tumor diameter ≥ 100 mm, multiple tumors, and macrovascular invasion. Additionally, adjuvant systemic therapy was associated with prolonged RFS among patients receiving HAIC and ICIs-based conversion regimens, as well as among radiological responders to conversion therapy. Across postoperative pathological subgroups, adjuvant systemic therapy was consistently associated with improved RFS irrespective of tumor differentiation status, with particularly favorable outcomes observed in patients without microscopic vascular invasion and those without pathological complete response. Additional sensitivity analyses restricted to patients without pathological complete response yielded generally consistent results before and after propensity score matching (Additional file 1: Figure S6).

Relationship between preoperative and postoperative systemic therapies

Distribution of postoperative adjuvant systemic regimens according to preoperative conversion treatment strategies is shown in Additional file 1: Figure S7. Among patients receiving preoperative TKIs monotherapy, 4/5 (80.0%) maintained TKIs monotherapy, whereas 1/5 (20.0%) transitioned to ICIs-based combination therapy. Among those treated with preoperative ICIs-based therapy, 85/115 (73.9%) received postoperative ICIs-based combination regimens, while 28/115 (24.4%) and 2/115 (1.7%) received ICIs monotherapy and TKIs monotherapy, respectively. Reasons for postoperative regimen modification included limited response to conversion therapy, financial considerations, concern regarding complications, and patient preference. No significant differences in OS or RFS were observed between patients who maintained the same therapeutic class postoperatively and those who underwent regimen modification following surgery (Additional file 1: Figure S8).

Patterns of recurrence and post-recurrence management

Patterns of disease recurrence and subsequent management are summarized in Additional file 1: Table S5. Both before and after propensity score matching, patients receiving adjuvant systemic therapy exhibited a significantly lower 1-year recurrence rate compared with those managed with active surveillance (before PSM: 34.2% vs. 48.1%, p = 0.007; after PSM: 33.6% vs. 49.0%, p = 0.007). Among patients who experienced recurrence, the proportion receiving subsequent anticancer treatment was comparable between groups. Furthermore, the use of locoregional therapies such as transarterial treatment, ablation, resection, and radiotherapy as well as systemic therapy after recurrence did not differ significantly according to postoperative management strategy in either cohort.

Discussion

In this propensity score matched study, we assessed the effect of adjuvant systemic therapy versus active surveillance after resection following conversion therapy in patients with HCC rendered resectable by first-line transarterial therapy combined with systemic therapy. Adjuvant systemic therapy was associated with significantly improved OS and RFS, with the benefit predominantly observed in patients with high-risk disease, including those with BCLC stage C tumors, BCLC stage A or B disease beyond the up-to-seven criteria, and adverse clinical and pathological features. These results address an important unmet need in postoperative management after conversion resection and support a risk-adapted role for adjuvant systemic therapy in this emerging patient population.

Adjuvant therapy in HCC has long represented an area of substantial unmet clinical need, as most trials have failed to demonstrate a clear survival benefit following curative-intent resection. Although TKIs and ICIs-based regimens have established efficacy in advanced HCC as first-line treatment [22–25], their role in postoperative adjuvant setting remains uncertain. In the phase III IMbrave 050 trial, the initial analysis showed a significantly improvement in RFS with atezolizumab plus bevacizumab compared to the control group [26]. However, this benefit was not sustained in subsequent interim analyses [10]. Similarly, the phase III STORM trial failed to demonstrate an RFS benefit with adjuvant sorafenib after resection [9]. Importantly, these landmark adjuvant trials enrolled broadly defined resected populations and were not designed to specifically address patients who were initially unresectable and subsequently underwent resection following successful conversion therapy. In contrast, the present study focused on this distinct clinical subgroup and demonstrated significant improvements in both OS and RFS with adjuvant systemic therapy, particularly among patients with high-risk clinical and pathological features. Patients undergoing resection after conversion therapy differ biologically and clinically from those treated with upfront surgery, as they typically present with advanced disease, substantial tumor burden, and more aggressive tumor biology at baseline, translating into a markedly elevated risk of postoperative recurrence [27]. Notebly, the postoperative survival outcomes observed in our matched cohort were highly consistent with those reported in recent retrospective studies, which evaluated patients undergoing hepatectomy after conversion therapy [28, 29]. In this context, concentrating postoperative systemic therapy on a cohort enriched for recurrence risk may facilitate the detection of a treatment effect that is difficult to discern in more heterogeneous resected populations.

The survival benefit associated with adjuvant systemic therapy was broadly consistent across multiple subgroups, supporting the robustness of our findings. Within the heterogeneous BCLC stage A or B population, improved survival was observed in patients exceeding the up-to-seven criteria, whereas no significant benefit was detected when stratified by the milan criteria. This difference likely reflects the distinct ability of these criteria to capture tumor burden and biological aggressiveness. The up-to-seven criteria incorporate both tumor size and number and are more closely associated with recurrence risk [21]. Patients beyond this threshold may therefore represent a higher-risk subgroup in whom postoperative systemic therapy is more likely to confer benefit by suppressing occult disease. In contrast, the milan criteria were primarily developed for transplant selection and may be less sensitive for risk stratification in the postoperative setting, potentially explaining the absence of a detectable benefit under milan-based stratification.

In stratified analyses, adjuvant systemic therapy was associated with improved OS and RFS in patients with features indicative of high tumor burden, including elevated AFP and PIVKA-II levels, large tumor size, multiple lesions, and macrovascular invasion. These factors are well recognized markers of aggressive tumor biology and are associated with a high likelihood of microscopic residual disease after resection [30, 31]. In such settings, postoperative recurrence is more likely driven by occult intrahepatic or extrahepatic micrometastases rather than de novo tumorigenesis, rendering these patients biologically more susceptible to systemic therapeutic intervention [32, 33]. Interestingly, we found that patients who responded to conversion therapy appeared to represent a subgroup with inherent sensitivity to systemic treatment, in whom continuation of postoperative systemic therapy may help maintain the disease control achieved preoperatively, regardless of whether the adjuvant regimen was identical to the conversion strategy. By contrast, among patients who achieved pathological complete response, no significant survival difference was observed between adjuvant systemic therapy and active surveillance, consistent with a tumor-free with drug-free strategy proposed in a previous study [12]. However, the present study differed from the previous pCR-focused cohort by specifically focusing on first-line transarterial combined systemic conversion strategies and inclusion of radiological non-responders. Given the distinct radiological response framework in transarterial combined systemic conversion, patients with pCR were retained in the primary analysis to better reflect real-world postoperative outcomes. Notably, sensitivity analyses restricted to patients without pathological complete response yielded generally consistent findings. Taken together, these observations provide a biologically plausible explanation for why adjuvant systemic therapy may preferentially benefit patients with high-risk features after resection, whereas its effect appears attenuated in lower-risk populations.

In the present cohort, most patients received postoperative adjuvant regimens that were broadly consistent with their preoperative conversion strategies, particularly among those treated with ICIs-based therapy. This perioperative IO-based treatment paradigm has recently been supported by the CARES-009 trial [34]. Our findings provide complementary real-world evidence supporting the feasibility and potential clinical benefit of maintaining systemic therapeutic strategies across the perioperative setting in patients undergoing resection after conversion therapy. Notably, no significant survival differences were observed between patients who maintained the same therapeutic class and those who underwent postoperative regimen modification. However, these exploratory findings were limited by the small sample size and warrant further prospective validation.

Analysis of recurrence patterns and subsequent management provides further context for the observed survival benefit associated with adjuvant systemic therapy. Although recurrence patterns were largely comparable between groups, patients receiving adjuvant systemic therapy experienced a significantly lower rate of early recurrence, particularly within the first year after resection (Additional file 1: Table S5), with comparable post-recurrence treatment strategies. This finding suggests that the survival advantage associated with adjuvant systemic therapy is primarily attributable to a reduction in early postoperative relapse rather than differences in salvage treatments after recurrence. Given that early recurrence after resection is strongly associated with poor long-term outcomes [35], delaying or preventing such events may translate into meaningful improvements in both RFS and OS.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective design may introduce residual selection bias, despite the use of propensity score matching to balance baseline characteristics. In addition, unmeasured confounders related to pre- and postoperative status may still exist. Second, this was a single-center study, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other clinical settings. Third, the predominance of hepatitis B virus–related HCC in the study cohort may restrict the extrapolation of our results to populations with different underlying disease etiologies. In addition, heterogeneity in preoperative and postoperative systemic treatment strategies may limit the interpretability and external validity of our findings. Prospective, multicenter studies with larger cohorts and predefined adjuvant regimens are warranted to validate these findings.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study support the role for adjuvant systemic therapy in selected patients undergoing resection after conversion therapy and provide a rationale for prospective studies in this evolving treatment paradigm.

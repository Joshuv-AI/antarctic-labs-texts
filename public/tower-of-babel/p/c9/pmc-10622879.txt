Selenomethionine alleviates environmental heat stress induced hepatic lipid accumulation and glycogen infiltration of broilers via maintaining mitochondrial and endoplasmic reticulum homeostasis
Authors: Jinzhong Jing, Huijin Zeng, Quanjun Shao, Jiayong Tang, Longqiong Wang, Gang Jia, Guangmang Liu, Xiaoling Chen, Gang Tian, Jingyi Cai, Bo Kang, Lianqiang Che, Hua Zhao
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the increasing of global mean surface air temperature, heat stress (HS) induced by extreme high temperature has become a key factor restricting the poultry industry. Liver is the main metabolic organ of broilers, HS induces liver damage and metabolic disorders, which impairs the health of broilers and affects food safety. As an essential trace element for animals, selenium (Se) involves in the formation of antioxidant system, and its biological functions are generally mediated by selenoproteins. However, the mechanism of Se against HS induced liver damage and metabolic disorders in broilers is inadequate. Therefore, we developed the chronic heat stress (CHS) broiler model and investigated the potential protection mechanism of organic Se (selenomethionine, SeMet) on CHS induced liver damage and metabolic disorders. In present study, CHS caused liver oxidative damage, and induced hepatic lipid accumulation and glycogen infiltration of broilers, which are accompanied by mitochondrial dysfunction, abnormal mitochondrial tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress. Dietary SeMet supplementation increased the hepatic Se concentration and exhibited protective effects via promoting the expression of selenotranscriptome and several key selenoproteins (GPX4, TXNRD2, SELENOK, SELENOM, SELENOS, SELENOT, GPX1, DIO1, SELENOH, SELENOU and SELENOW). These key selenoproteins synergistically improved the antioxidant capacity, and mitigated the mitochondrial dysfunction, abnormal mitochondrial TCA cycle and ER stress, thus recovered the hepatic triglyceride and glycogen concentration. What's more, SeMet supplementation suppressed lipid and glycogen biosynthesis and promoted lipid and glycogen breakdown in liver of broilers exposed to CHS though regulating the AMPK signals. Overall, our present study reveals a potential mechanism that Se alleviates environment HS induced liver damage and glycogen and lipid metabolism disorders in broilers, which provides a preventive and/or treatment measure for environment HS-dependent hepatic metabolic disorders in poultry industry.Key worlds: Heat stress, Broilers, Hepatic metabolic disorder, ER stress, Mitochondrial dysfunction, Selenoprotein

Introduction

For the past few years, with the increasing of global mean surface air temperature, extreme heat events occur frequently [1]. Environmental heat stress (HS) has become an important factor that impairs the global livestock industry. Among the livestock products, broiler is the most efficient source of protein. According to the United Nations Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), in 2021, poultry bird meat production accounts for more than 38% of the global meat production [2]. Due to the advanced breeding technology, broilers show the characteristics of rapid growth rate, high metabolic rate, great heat production and poor heat resistance [3,4]. Therefore, broilers are sensitive to environmental HS, and HS causes severe economic losses in poultry industry each year [5]. HS causes poor growth performance and health status of broilers, which is mainly accompanied by the liver injury and metabolic disorders [6,7]. Liver is the key metabolic organ that carries the glucose and lipid metabolism of the broiler. Current evidences suggest that HS causes the lipid accumulation and glycogen infiltration in liver of broilers [8,9]. While, the main factor of liver metabolic disorder caused by HS is oxidative damage. HS causes mitochondrial stress, impairs the mitochondrial respiration, increases the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), and ultimately causes liver oxidative damage [10]. Excessive ROS also causes cell death with associated pathological states [11,12]. Mitochondrion is the ultimate metabolic center for lipid and glucose, mitochondrial dysfunction generally inhibits the hepatic lipid catabolism and causes lipid accumulation [13]. Therefore, it is reasonable to expect that environment HS induced hepatic lipid accumulation and glycogen infiltration in broilers is closely related to the mitochondrial dysfunction.

The smooth endoplasmic reticulum (ER) is the key organelle that mediates lipid biosynthesis and transportation, and ER homeostasis is indispensable for lipid metabolism [14]. Accumulating evidences indicate that environment HS causes ER stress in cells and animals [[15], [16], [17]]. Under stress condition, excessive unfolded or misfolded proteins accumulated in the ER lumen can effectually active the ER unfolded protein response (ER-UPR) to recover ER homeostasis, thus the ER-UPR is the representative behavior of ER under stress [18]. The activation of ER-UPR is generally mediated by three ER transmembrane receptors: activating transcription factor 6 (ATF6), inositol-requiring protein 1 (IRE1) and protein kinase-like endoplasmic reticulum kinase (PERK) [19]. However, activation of ER-UPR related signals promotes lipid and glucose biosynthesis in the liver [20,21]. Hence, it is reasonable to expect that alleviation of mitochondrial dysfunction and ER stress may contribute to relieving environmental HS induced hepatic glucose and lipid metabolism disorder of broilers.

Selenium (Se) is an essential trace element for animals that involves in the composition of antioxidant system and plays a unique role in ROS removing [22]. Our previous studies suggest that dietary Se supplementation contributes to maintaining mitochondria and ER homeostasis [23,24]. Se exerts its biological functions mainly though selenoproteins, and 24 selenoproteins have been identified in broilers [25]. Among them, GPX4, SELENOO and TXNRD2 are considered as the mitochondrial selenoproteins, DIO2, SELENOF, SELENOK, SELENOM, SELENON, SELENOS and SELENOT are ER resident selenoproteins [26]. These selenoproteins play an irreplaceable role in mitochondrial and ER homeostasis maintenance. Besides, other free selenoproteins such as GPX1, GPX2 and GPX3 could clear excessive ROS and recover redox homeostasis [23]. Generally, dietary organic Se supplementation can linearly increase the Se concentration and effectively promote the expression of selenoproteins in liver [24,27]. Therefore, our present study aims to explore whether environmental HS causes hepatic glycogen and lipid metabolism disorders of broilers, and the potential protect mechanism of organic Se in form of selenomethionine (SeMet). Our results may provide new insight for the prevention and/or treatment of environmental HS-dependent hepatic metabolic disorders.

Materials and methods

Animal, diet and experimental design

The Animal Care and Use Committee of the Laboratory Animal Center at Sichuan Agricultural University approved this animal trial, and the ethics approval code is as followed: SCAUAC202107-1. In present study, 400 Arbor Acres male broilers (aged 21 d, average body weight was 680 ± 70 g) were randomly allotted into 5 treatment groups with 8 replicates of 10 broilers per replicate. Broilers in each replicate were housed in a cage with the same size (length × width × height, 100 cm × 80 cm × 60 cm). During the experiment, broilers in control group (CON) were raised in a thermoneutral environment (22 ± 2 °C) and fed on the basal diet (formulated in accordance with National Research Council: Nutrient Requirements of Poultry [NRC 1994], details in Supplementary Table 1) with no additional Se, and the relative humidity was kept at 50% ± 5%. Broilers in the other four treatment groups were kept in a separate house and exposed to hyperthermal condition (33 ± 2 °C) every day and fed on basal diet supplied with 0.0 (Chronic heat stress group, CHS), 0.2 (CHS + 0.2 Se), 0.4 (CHS + 0.4 Se) and 0.6 (CHS + 0.6 Se) mg Se/kg SeMet (the determined dietary Se concentration was shown in Supplementary Fig. 1), and the relative humidity was kept at 65% ± 5%. Except for temperature and relative humidity, other feeding conditions were consistent between control group and heat treatment groups. The poultry farm monitoring system (ROTEM AC-2000 PLUS, AgroLogic Ltd, Israel) was used to control the temperature and relative humidity. The extra heat for heat treatment groups was provided by an electric hot air blower (50 KW, Shandong Machinery Co., Ltd, Qingzhou, China). The extra relative humidity for heat treatment groups was regulated by a fog machine (PC-2804, Taizhou Paichi Machinery Co., Ltd, Taizhou, China). Broilers in each treatment group were free access to diet and water, the experiment lasted for 21 days.

Sample collection and preparation

After 21 days, 30 broilers from 5 treatment groups (6 broilers in each group with a body weight closed to the average body weight) were selected. After an overnight fasting, these broilers were weighed and recorded as broiler live weight, then the blood samples were collected from the internal jugular vein using a sterile vacuum tube and maintained at room temperature for 1 h, centrifuged (3000×g) for 10 min, the serum from each blood samples were collected and pipetted into sterile centrifuge tubes and stored at −20 °C for further analyses. After broilers were slaughtered, all livers were weighed and recorded as liver weight, and the liver relative weight were calculated: Liver relative weight = liver weight/broiler live weight × 100%. Liver samples were collected from the left lobe of liver and placed in a sterile tube, then snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C for further analyses. The right lobe of liver was carefully cut and one portion of liver tissue was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde solution for Oil red O staining and Periodic Acid Schiff (PAS) staining.

Oil Red O staining

Oil Red O staining was performed to determine the lipid concentration in liver tissue of broilers. The liver tissues were trimmed into a length of 2 mm and embedded with optimal cutting temperature compound (OCT). After solidification, the OCT embedded liver tissue samples were cut into slices of 5–6 μm thickness by using the thermostatic frozen microtome (LS-2900, Shenyang Longshou Electronic Instrument Co., Ltd, Shenyang, China). Slices were stained with Oil Red O for 10 min and differentiated with 60% isopropanol, then washed with distilled water and re-stained with hematoxylin. After washed with distilled water, the stained sections were sealed with glycerin. The stained sections were visualized with digital microscope camera (BA210Digital, Motic China Group Co., Ltd, Xiamen, China), and 3 representative areas were selected to collect 400 × images. The lipid droplet area was determined using Image-Pro Plus 6.0 image analysis system (Media Cybernetics, Inc., Rockville, MD, USA). The relative lipid droplet area was calculated: Relative lipid droplet area (%) = lipid droplet area/tissue area × 100%.

Periodic Acid Schiff staining

PAS staining was performed to determine the glycogen concentration in liver tissue of broilers. The liver tissues were dehydrated by automatic dehydrator (JT-12 S, Wuhan Junjie Electronics Co., Ltd, Wuhan, China), then these liver tissues were cut into slices by using a rotary microtome (Leica-2016, Leica Biosystems, Wetzlar, Germany). After routine deparaffinization procedures, slices were oxidized in periodic acid for 5 min, stained in Schiff Reagent for 15 min and rinsed with running water for 10 min, then counterstained with hematoxylin for 2 min. After differentiated with acid differentiation solution for 2–5 s, sections were returned to blue with Scott blue solution and washed with distilled water for 3 min, dehydrated and sealed with neutral gum. The stained sections were visualized with digital microscope camera (BA210Digital, Motic China Group Co., Ltd, Xiamen, China), and 3 representative areas were selected to collect 400 × images. The Integrated Optical Density (IOD) and area of PAS-stained specimens was quantitatively determined using Image-Pro Plus 6.0 image analysis system (Media Cybernetics, Inc., Rockville, MD, USA). The relative glycogen area was calculated: Relative glycogen area (%) = glycogen area/tissue area × 100%.

Serum biochemical analyses

The activity of alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and alkaline phosphatase (ALP), and the concentration of glucose (GLU), non-esterified fatty acid (NEFA), total bile acid (TBA), total cholesterol (TC), triacylglycerol (TG), high-density lipoprotein cholesterin (HDL-C) and the low-density lipoprotein cholesterin (LDL-C) in serum of broilers were measured using the automatic biochemistry analyzer (3100, HITACHI, Japan).

Selenium concentration in liver

The concentration of Se in liver of broilers were determined by using the hybrid generation-atomic fluorescence spectrometer (AFS-230E, Beijing Haiguang instrument, China) against the standard Se reference [GB 5009.93–2010, National Research Centre for Certified Reference Materials, Beijing, China]. The sample pretreatment method was reported in the previous study [28].

Liver biochemical analyses

The concentration of TG, glycogen, pyruvate, mitochondrial citric acid (CA), and adenosine triphosphate (ATP) in liver were determined by using the corresponding assay kits (no. A110-1-1, A043-1-1, A081-1-1, A128-1-1, A095-1-1, Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China). The activity of lipid and glycogen metabolism related enzymes [acetyl-coa carboxylase (ACC), fatty acid synthase (FAS), adipose triglyceride lipase (ATGL), hepatic lipase (HL), glycogen phosphorylase (GP), glycogen synthase (GS)] and tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle related enzymes [citroyl synthetase (CS), isocitrate dehydrogenase (ICD), α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase (α-KGDHC)] in liver were measured by using the commercial enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits (no. MM-3430401, MM-150401, MM-6019701, MM-202701, MM-6069402, MM-6068902, MM-6069702, MM-6070102, MM-137702, Jiangsu Meimian Industrial Co., Ltd, Jiangsu, China). The activity of TCA cycle related enzymes [succinodehydrogenase (SDH), malic dehydrogenase (MDH)] in liver were determined by using the corresponding assay kits (no. A022-1-1, A021-2-1, Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China). The concentration of mitochondrial acetyl-CoA and ROS in liver were determined by using the ELISA kits (no. MM-6065001, MM-6012001, Jiangsu Meimian Industrial Co., Ltd, Jiangsu, China). The activity of antioxidant indicators [glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px), total antioxidant capability (T-AOC), total superoxide dismutase (T-SOD)] and the concentration of malondialdehyde (MDA) in liver were determined by using the corresponding assay kits (no. A005-1-2, A001-1-1, A015–1, A003-1, Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China). The total proteins in liver samples were determined with the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) method by using a commercial assay kit (no. A045-3, Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China). The sample pretreatment methods and determination methods were according to the manufacturer's instructions.

RT-PCR analyses of mRNA expression in liver

Briefly, the total RNA in each liver sample was extracted by using RNAiso Plus (no. 9109, Takara, Dalian, China). After inspected by nucleic acid electrophoresis, 1000 ng of total RNA were used for cDNA synthesis with the PrimeScript RT reagent kit (no. RR047A, Takara, Dalian, China). The qPCR was performed in a final volume of 10 μL by using the SYBR Premix Ex TaqTM Ⅱ kit (no. RR820A, Takara, Dalian, China) on the QuantStudio 5 Flex system (Applied Biosystems, CA, USA). The relative mRNA expression was normalized to the expression of β-Actin and calculated by using the 2−ΔΔCt method as the previous described [29]. The primers for 16 lipid metabolism related genes, 4 glycogen metabolism related genes, 12 TCA cycle related genes, 8 ER stress biomarkers, 23 selenogenes, AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), sterol regulatory element binding transcription factor 1 (SREBP1) and β-Actin in present study were designed by using the Primer Express 3.0 (Applied Biosystems, CA, USA), and listed in Supplementary Table 2.

Western blot analyses of protein abundance in liver

The liver samples from all the treatment groups were homogenized in ice-cold radio immunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) buffer with phenylmethanesulfonyl fluoride (PMSF) protease inhibitor buffer (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) and centrifuged (12,000×g) for 30 min at 4 °C. The supernatant of each sample was collected in a sterile tube, and the total protein concentration in each sample was measured using the BCA kit (no. A045-3, Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China). The total protein concentration of each sample was adjusted to 8 μg/μL by using the RIPA buffer with PMSF protease, then 400 μL of the above protein samples were mixed with 100 μL SDS-PAGE sample loading buffer (5 × ) (no. P0015L, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) and denatured in a water bath (95 °C) for 10 min for western blotting process. Western blotting process was performed as the previous description [27]. The primary antibodies used in present study were shown in Supplementary Table 3, and the secondary antibodies were anti-rabbit or anti-mouse IgG (dilution ratio: 1:5000, Proteintech Group, IL, USA). The bands were visualized by using an enhanced chemiluminescence system (Bio-Rad, CA, USA), and the densitometric of Western blot bands were analyzed using the Image Lab™ software system (Bio-Rad, CA, USA).

Statistical analysis

In present study, we performed the one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey's multiple range tests in SPSS 27.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, USA) to confirm whether there were differences between each group, ANOVA p-values of less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Data are expressed as means ± standard errors. Principal component analysis of selenotranscriptome in liver was accomplished by SPSS 27.0 (SPSS, Inc., Chicago, USA). The correlation analysis between the key selenogenes and other indicators was accomplished by Origin 2021 (OriginLab, Northampton, MA, USA). Results were plotted using GraphPad Prism Version 8 software (Graphpad software, LLC, San Diego, USA).

SeMet alleviated heat stress induced liver damage and hepatic triglyceride accumulation and glycogen infiltration

We evaluated the negative effect of environmental HS on liver of broilers and the protective effects of SeMet. In present study, CHS decreased the liver relative weight of broilers (Fig. 1A) and increased the protein abundance of heat shock protein 70 (HSP70) in liver (Fig. 1C). Oil Red O staining and PAS staining showed that CHS increased the relative lipid droplet area and glycogen area in liver of broilers (Fig. 1D, E and 1F). Further measurements showed that CHS increased the concentration of TG and glycogen in liver of broilers (Fig. 1G and H). Dietary different levels of SeMet linearly increased the Se concentration in liver (Fig. 1B), and exhibited protective effects. 0.4 and 0.6 mg/kg Se supplementation numerically increased the liver index of broilers exposed to CHS (Fig. 1A). Besides, broilers received SeMet showed the lower protein abundance of HSP70 (Fig. 1C), the lower relative lipid droplet area and glycogen area (Fig. 1D–F), and the lower concentration of TG and glycogen in liver (Fig. 1G and H).

SeMet alleviated heat stress induced hepatic lipid and glycogen metabolism disorders though regulating AMPK signal

We further investigated the potential mechanism that SeMet alleviates heat stress induced hepatic lipid accumulation and glycogen infiltration. For lipid metabolism, CHS generally up-regulated the mRNA expression of lipid synthesis related genes, especially Acetyl-CoA carboxylase alpha (ACACA), fatty acid synthase (FASN), monoacylglycerol O-acyltransferase 1 and 2 (MOGAT1 and MOGAT2) (Fig. 2A). On the contrary, CHS down-regulated the mRNA expression of lipidolysis related genes, especially acyl-CoA synthetase long-chain family member 1 (ACSL1), ATGL and carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1α (CPT1α) (Fig. 2A). Broilers exposed to CHS also showed the higher activity of lipid synthesis related enzyme ACC (Fig. 2B) and the lower activity of lipidolysis related enzymes ATGL and HL (Fig. 2D and E). Different levels of Se supplementation especially 0.6 mg/kg decreased the mRNA expression of ACACA, FASN and MOGAT2, while generally increased the mRNA expression of acetyl-CoA acyltransferase 1 (ACAA1), acetyl-CoA acyltransferase 2 (ACAA2), acyl-CoA dehydrogenase, long chain (ACADL), ACSL1, CPT1α, enoyl-CoA hydratase, short chain 1 (ECHS1), diacylglycerol lipase (LIPE) and monoglyceride lipase (MGLL) (Fig. 2A) in liver of broilers exposed to CHS. Besides, broilers received Se showed the lower activity of ACC (Fig. 2B) and the higher activity of ATGL and HL (Fig. 2D and E). For glycogen metabolism, CHS up-regulated the glycogen synthesis related genes glycogen synthase kinase 3 alpha (GSK3α), GS and glucan branching enzyme 1 (GBE1) (Fig. 2F). CHS also increased the activity of GS (Fig. 2H). Broilers received Se showed the higher mRNA expression of liver glycogen phosphorylase (PYGL) and the lower levels of GSK3α, GS and GBE1 (Fig. 2F). Besides, dietary Se supplementation increased the activity of GP (Fig. 2G), and decreased the activity of GS (Fig. 2H). Further measurements showed that CHS increased the mRNA expression of AMPK and SREBP1 (Fig. 2I), while decreased the protein abundance of P-AMPKα (Fig. 2J). Dietary Se supplementation decreased the mRNA expression of SREBP1, 0.2 and 0.4 mg/kg Se tended to increase the protein abundance of P-AMPKα.

SeMet alleviated heat stress induced abnormal tricarboxylic acid circulation in liver

Tricarboxylic acid cycle is the main pathway that mediates catabolism of glucose and lipids. In present study, CHS exhibited no impact on the level of pyruvate (Fig. 3C), while decreased the levels of mitochondrial acetyl-CoA and citric acid by 21.3% and 14.7% (Fig. 3B, D and 3E), respectively. Further measurements found that broilers exposed to CHS showed the lower mRNA expression of isocitrate dehydrogenase 1 (IDH1), fumarate hydratase (FH) and malate dehydrogenase 2 (MDH2) (Fig. 3A). Besides, the activity of TCA cycle related enzymes CS, ICD, α-KGDHC, SDH and MDH in liver of broilers exposed to CHS were reduced by 14.4%, 21%, 4.8%, 38.9% and 37.4% (Fig. 3B, 3F-3J), respectively. Dietary SeMet supplementation exhibited protective effects, 0.6 mg Se/kg increased the mRNA expression of CS and oxoglutarate dehydrogenase (OGDH), 0.4 mg Se/kg increased the levels of IDH1, 0.4 and 0.6 mg Se/kg increased the expression of FH, three levels of Se increased the expression of MDH2 (Fig. 3A). Additional Se also increased the levels of pyruvate, mitochondrial acetyl-CoA and citric acid (Fig. 3C–E). Besides, Se supplementation recovered or increased the activity of CS, ICD, SDH and MDH in liver of broilers exposed to CHS (Fig. 3F–J).

SeMet improved the antioxidant capacity and contributed to the maintenance of mitochondrial homeostasis in liver under heat stress

We evaluated whether CHS causes oxidative damage and mitochondrial stress in liver and the protective effect of SeMet. In present study, broilers exposed to CHS exhibited the higher level of MDA (Fig. 4B) and the lower activity of T-AOC (Fig. 4C) in liver. CHS also decreased the concentration of ATP (Fig. 4E), while increased the concentration of ROS (Fig. 4F) in liver of broilers. Besides, broilers exposed to CHS showed the higher protein abundance of heat shock protein 60 (HSP60) and caseinolytic mitochondrial matrix peptidase chaperone subunit X (CLPX) in liver (Fig. 4G and H). Dietary SeMet supplementation showed the mitigating effects, which increased or recovered the activity of GSH-Px (Fig. 4A) and T-AOC (Fig. 4C), while decreased the concentration of MDA (Fig. 4B). Additional Se supplementation also increased the concentration of ATP (Fig. 4E) and decreased the concentration of ROS (Fig. 4F) in liver of broilers exposed to CHS. Besides, broilers received Se under CHS showed the lower protein levels of HSP60 and CLPX (Fig. 4G and H), while the higher protein abundance of NAD-dependent protein deacetylase sirtuin 3 (SIRT3) (Fig. 4I).

SeMet alleviated heat stress induced endoplasmic reticulum stress in liver

Endoplasmic reticulum is the key organelle for lipid synthesis and transportation. In present study, the ER stress biomarkers in liver were confirmed. Broilers exposed to CHS showed the higher mRNA expression of PERK, activating transcription factor 4 (ATF4), eukaryotic translation initiation factor 2 alpha (eIF2α), C/EBP homologous protein (CHOP), IRE1, X-box-binding protein 1 (XBP-1) and glucose-regulated protein 78 (GRP78) (Fig. 5A, B, 5C, 5D, 5F, 5H). Further measurements showed that CHS increased the protein abundance of XBP-1 (Fig. 5I). Dietary SeMet supplementation exhibited protective effects, which decreased the mRNA expression of PERK, ATF4, CHOP and XBP-1. Besides, Se supplementation decreased the protein abundance of XBP-1 in liver of broilers exposed to CHS.

SeMet promoted the expression of selenotranscriptome and key selenoproteins in liver

Selenium exerts its biological functions mainly through selenoproteins. Here, the expression of selenotranscriptome and key selenoproteins in liver were confirmed. In present study, CHS alone exhibited limited impact on the expression of selenotranscriptome (Fig. 6A). Dietary SeMet supplementation globally up-regulated the selenotranscriptome in liver of broilers exposed to CHS. To distinguish the key selenogens affected by SeMet, the principal component analysis was performed, results showed that 11 selenogenes (GPX4, TXNRD2, SELENOK, SELENOM, SELENOS, SELENOT, GPX1, DIO1, SELENOH, SELENOU and SELENOW) were observed at relatively distant positions in three-dimensional space (Fig. 6B), these genes were the key selenogens. Among these key selenogenes, GPX4 and TXNRD2 are considered as the mitochondrial selenoproteins, SELENOK, SELENOM, SELENOS and SELENOT are located in the ER, and GPX1, DIO1, SELENOH, SELENOU and SELENOW are free selenoproteins. The present results showed that SeMet supplementation under CHS generally up-regulated the expression of these key selenogenes, except SELENOS (Fig. 6C). We further validated 3 key selenogenes (GPX1, GPX4 and SELENOS) at the protein level. Results showed that SeMet supplementation under CHS generally increased the protein abundance of these 3 key selenoproteins in liver (Fig. 6D–F).

Discussion

Environmental HS impairs the health of broilers, which restrict the poultry industry. In our study, compared to control group, CHS significantly suppressed the growth of broilers (final body weight: 2266.8 g vs 1598.9 g). Liver is the key metabolic organ that carries the glucose and lipid metabolism of broilers. Current evidences posit that HS causes liver oxidative damage and impairs the hepatic metabolism in broilers [7,8]. In present study, broilers exposed to CHS showed the higher activity of ALT, and the higher concentration of GLU, TBA and TG in serum. Serum ALT activity and TBA level are used to assess liver damage, and the elevated activity of ALT and TBA concentration are generally accompanied by hepatic metabolism disorder [30,31]. The increase of serum GLU is usually mediated by abnormal glucose metabolism, such as increased liver glycogen output [32]. Serum TG level is usually used to evaluate the lipid metabolism status, and the increased serum TG level is generally accompanied by fatty liver [33]. The increased serum ALT activity, GLU, TBA and TG concentration of broilers suggest that environmental HS causes liver injury and mediates glucose and lipid metabolism disorder. Dietary supplementation with antioxidants is an effective way to prevent liver damage and liver steatosis [34,35]. Selenium is an excellent antioxidant and our previous study indicates that additional Se supplementation alleviates liver damage under stress condition [24]. In present study, dietary SeMet supplementation decreased the activity of ALT, and the concentration of TBA and TG in serum of broilers exposed to CHS. Therefore, it is reasonable to expect that dietary SeMet may improve environmental HS caused glucose and lipid metabolism disorder of broilers by alleviating liver injury.

We further tested whether heat stress causes liver damage and the protective effects of SeMet. Slaughter test showed that CHS decreased the liver relative weight, which suggest that CHS causes liver damage and suppressed the hepatic development. As an important molecular chaperone, HSP70 is considered as a cellular thermometer and generally used to evaluate HS damage in animals [36]. In present study, broilers exposed to CHS showed the increased protein level of HSP70 in liver, which further validates the fact that environment HS induces liver damage. Previous studies indicate that dietary Se supplementation can effectively increase the hepatic Se concentration and relieve liver damage [24,27]. Similarly, our present results showed that additional SeMet supplementation linearly increased the Se concentration in liver and decreased the protein levels of HSP70, indicating the anti-heat stress ability of SeMet.

Oil Red O staining and PAS staining of tissue sections can directly observe and quantitatively analyse the lipid and glycogen in tissues [37,38]. In present study, the liver tissue sections were stained with Oil Red O and PAS, and CHS increased the relative lipid droplet area and glycogen area in liver. The further determination with commercial kits showed that CHS increased the TG and glycogen levels in liver. However, dietary SeMet supplementation decreased the relative lipid droplet area and glycogen area, as well as the TG and glycogen levels in liver of broilers exposed to CHS. These results further confirm that HS causes liver damage and mediates hepatic glycogen and lipid metabolism disorders. While, dietary SeMet supplementation effectively relieved these negative effects caused by HS in liver of broilers.

To investigate the potential mechanism that environment HS causes hepatic lipid accumulation and glycogen infiltration and the mitigation effects of SeMet. We determined the expression of lipid and glycogen metabolism related genes and enzymes, as well as the AMPK signal that mediates carbohydrate metabolism in liver. For lipid metabolism, CHS generally increased the expression of lipid synthesis related genes (ACACA, FASN, MOGAT1, MOGAT2), and increased the activity of ACC. While, CHS decreased the expression of lipidolysis related genes ACSL1, ATGL and CPT1α, and decreased the activity of ATGL and HL. ACC encoded by ACACA is the key enzyme that mediates de novo synthesis of fatty acids [39]. FAS encoded by FASN is a critical rate-limiting enzyme in the lipid synthesis process, which regulates the process of fatty acid chain extension [40]. MOGAT is located in the ER and promotes the biosynthesis of TG, the increased expression of MOGAT causes lipid accumulation in liver [41,42]. ACSL1 binds to scaffold protein TANK (TRAF family member associated NFκB activator)-binding kinase 1 and localizes to the mitochondria, thus promotes the fatty acids β-oxidation [43]. ATGL encoded by ATGL gene is a rate-limiting enzyme that specifically hydrolyzes the first ester bond of TG and promotes TG hydrolysis [44]. HL is the liver-specific lipid hydrolase that promotes the hydrolysis of TG in very low-density lipoprotein, thus prevents the hepatic lipid accumulation [45]. The changes of these lipid metabolism related genes and enzymes indicate that environment HS promotes lipid biosynthesis and suppresses lipidolysis, which are consistent with the increased relative lipid droplet area and TG concentration in liver. Dietary SeMet supplementation generally decreased the expression of lipid synthesis related genes, as well as the activity of ACC. While dietary SeMet inclusion globally increased the expression of these lipidolysis related genes, as well as the activities of ATGL and HL. These results confirmed that SeMet can relieve hepatic lipid metabolism disorder of broilers exposed to environment HS.

For glycogen metabolism, CHS increased the expression of glycogen synthesis related genes (GSK3α, GS and GBE1) and increased the activity of GS. GSK3α is a multifunctional serine/threonine protein kinase that involved in multiple signaling pathways, non-phosphorylated GSK3 activates GS to promote glycogen synthesis [46]. Current evidence suggests that HS activates the GSK3α [47]. GBE1 catalyzes the formation of 1, 6-glycosidic bonds to achieve the branching of sugar chains, thus promotes glycogen synthesis. However, GBE deficiency results in the intracellular aggregation of linear glycogen molecules, which significantly impairs the efficiency of glucose release from the abnormal glycogen [48]. The changes of these glycogen metabolism related genes and enzymes indicate that environmental HS promotes glycogen synthesis, which are consistent with the increased relative glycogen area and glycogen concentration in liver. Besides, the increased expression of GBE1 promotes glucose release from glycogen, thus causes high GLU levels in serum. Dietary SeMet supplementation generally decreased the expression of these glycogen synthesis related genes and the activity of GS. SeMet also increased the expression of PYGL and linearly accelerated the activity of GP. PYGL is a liver type glycogen phosphorylase, which promotes the breakdown of liver glycogen [49]. Our present results confirmed that SeMet relieves hepatic glycogen metabolism disorder of broilers exposed to environment HS.

AMPK is an energy sensor that maintains cell energy homeostasis, activates of AMPK promotes catabolism and inhibits anabolism [50]. SREBP1 is the membrane-bound protein anchored to the ER and nuclear envelope that regulate the expression of ACC and FAS, thus promotes the lipid biosynthesis [51,52]. However, the activation of SREBP-1 is generally regulated by AMPK. Besides, the beta subunit of AMPK can bind to glycogen synthase, and AMPK phosphorylation inhibits glycogen biosynthesis [53]. In present study, CHS suppressed the protein expression of P-AMPK and increased the expression of SREBP1, which were consistent with the increased activity of ACC and GS, and the increased TG and glycogen concentration in liver of broilers. Previous study suggests that Se improves the hepatic metabolism though regulating the AMPK signals [27]. Our study showed that SeMet supplementation linearly decreased the expression of SREBP1, and 0.2 and 0.4 mg/kg SeMet tended to increase the protein expression of P-AMPK, indicating that SeMet may alleviate HS induced hepatic lipid accumulation and glycogen infiltration of broilers though regulating AMPK signals.

The ultimate place for the complete oxidative breakdown of carbohydrates is mitochondria. Here, as the intermediate product of glycolipid metabolism, acetyl-CoA enters the tricarboxylic acid cycle and effectually oxidized to produce NADH and FADH2, and a variety of enzymes such as CS, ICD, α-KGDHC, SDH and MDH mediate the above processes [54] In present study, environment HS decreased the mRNA expression of IDH1, and suppressed the activity of CS, ICD, SDH and MDH. Besides, CHS decreased the concentration of mitochondrial acetyl-CoA and citric acid, which confirmed that HS suppresses the reaction of TCA cycle. These results indicate that HS impairs the glucose and lipid breakdown though suppressing the reaction of TCA cycle. While, the missed acetyl-CoA in CHS group may involve in de novo fatty acid synthesis [55], thus causes the high hepatic TG concentration. Dietary SeMet supplementation abolished the negative effects of HS on the reaction of TCA cycle, indicates that SeMet promotes the hepatic glucose and lipid catabolism of broilers exposed to HS though maintaining the normal operation of the mitochondrial TCA cycle.

The maintenance of mitochondrial homeostasis is indispensable to catabolism. Environment HS caused liver oxidative damage in broilers is generally associate with mitochondrial dysfunction [56]. Mitochondrial dysfunction is common in metabolic disorder associated fatty liver disease, which is generally accompanied by increased production of ROS [57,58]. Besides, evidence shows that prevention of mitochondrial dysfunction is relevant to attenuate hepatic lipid accumulation [35]. Here, we determined the antioxidant capacity and the mitochondrial stress biomarkers in liver of broilers. CHS increased the concentration of MDA and ROS, while decreased the activity of T-AOC and the levels of ATP. Besides, CHS also increased the protein abundance of HSP60 and CLPX in liver. ATP is the main source of energy for glycogen and lipid biosynthesis [59], the increased hepatic TG and glycogen suggest that the missed ATP in CHS group may be used for glycogen and lipid synthesis. HSP60 and CLPX are representative biomarkers of mitochondrial stress. As a molecular chaperone localized in mitochondria, HSP60 maintains mitochondrial homeostasis [60]. CLPX involves in the construction of respiratory chain complex II subunit succinate dehydrogenase B (SDHB) in mitochondria, loss of CLPX induces misfold of SDHB, thus impairs oxidative phosphorylation [61]. Our present results suggest that environmental HS suppresses lipids and sugars breakdown though impairing mitochondrial homeostasis. The latest evidences indicate that Se deficiency impairs redox homeostasis and mitochondrial function, while additional Se supplementation contributes to the recovery of mitochondrial function under stress condition [23,62]. In present study, SeMet supplementation improved the antioxidant capacity, increased the ATP concentration, decreased the ROS levels, decreased the protein abundance of HSP60 and CLPX, while increased the protein levels of SIRT3. SIRT3 is only found in mitochondria, which eliminates excessive ROS and inhibits apoptosis, thus maintaining cellular homeostasis [63]. Based on the above results, it is reasonable to think that SeMet contributes to maintaining the hepatic catabolism of broilers exposed to HS though recovering the mitochondrial homeostasis.

Endoplasmic reticulum is the key organelle that mediates the lipid biosynthesis and transportation, and ER homeostasis is important for lipid metabolism [14]. In liver, TG are synthesized in the smooth ER and assembled into very-low-density lipoprotein (VLDL) for transport to the blood. Previous studies show that HS induces ER stress and causes lipid accumulation in liver of broilers [8,17]. ER-UPR is the representative behavior of ER under stress [18]. Under stress, the ability of the ER to fold proteins is impaired, which causes unfolded proteins accumulation. These excessive unfolded proteins facilitate the activation of ER-UPR through three ER transmembrane sensors: PERK, IRE1 and ATF6 [[64], [65], [66]]. The activation of these sensors promotes the restoration of ER homeostasis though regulating multiple downstream signaling molecules such as eIF2α, ATF4, CHOP and XBP-1. GRP78 is an ER localized molecular chaperone that senses ER stress and promotes proper protein folding [67]. Here, these ER stress biomarkers in liver of broilers were determined. CHS increased the mRNA expression of PERK, ATF4, eIF2α, CHOP, XBP-1 and GRP78, and increased the protein abundance of XBP-1. Previous study shows that Se deficiency causes liver ER stress and damage of broilers [68]. Dietary Se supplementation recovers the hepatic ER homeostasis under oxidative stress [24]. In this study, SeMet supplementation decreased the mRNA expression of PERK, ATF4, CHOP and XBP-1, and decreased the protein abundance of XBP-1. These results suggest that SeMet contributes to maintaining the ER homeostasis, thus reducing the lipid synthesis in liver of broilers exposed to CHS.

Accumulating evidences suggest that Se performs its biological function mainly through the synergy of multiple selenoproteins [23,27,28]. The expression of selenoproteins in liver of broilers are effectively regulated by dietary Se levels [69]. Here, we explored the mRNA expression of the selenotranscriptome in liver of broilers. Interestingly, though CHS alone exhibited limited impact on the expression of selenotranscriptome, SeMet supplementation generally increased the expression of the selenotranscriptome in liver of broilers exposed to CHS. Principal component analysis suggests that 11 selenogenes play key roles in combating environment HS. Among them, GPX4 and TXNRD2 are considered as the mitochondrial selenoproteins, SELENOK, SELENOM, SELENOS and SELENOT are ER resident selenoproteins, the other 5 (GPX1, DIO1, SELENOH, SELENOU, SELENOW) are free selenoproteins [26]. GPX4 can effectively scavenge lipid hydroperoxides and contributes to the maintenance of mitochondrial homeostasis [70]. TXNRD2 protects against disulfide damage and regulates redox homeostasis, knockout of the mitochondrial TXNRD2 induces metabolic dysfunction [71]. For these ER resident selenoproteins, SELENOK controls the ER homeostasis though scavenging excessive ROS, SELENOK deficiency causes ER stress and induces apoptosis [72]. SELENOM is an oxidoreductase bearing structural similarity to thioredoxin and yet to be fully characterized. However, the latest study shows that SELENOM knockdown induces apoptosis and ER stress [73]. SELENOS participates in retro-translocation channel building that promotes misfolded peptide chains degradation and maintains ER homeostasis [74]. SELENOS can be used as a marker that reflect ER stress, and SELENOS deficiency causes hepatic ER stress and lipid accumulation [75,76]. For these free selenoproteins, GPX1 belongs to the glutathione peroxidase family that can effectively clear excessive ROS and maintains cell metabolic homeostasis [77]. DIO1 is a hepatic enzyme that converts T4 to the bioactive T3, the increased expression of DIO1 suppresses the TG synthesis [78]. SELENOH interactions with other selenoproteins and regulates the redox homeostasis, while it is also a key regulator for cell cycle progression [79,80]. SELENOU is a newly identified protein in broilers, however its function is unknown, and the latest study shows that SELENOU knockdown triggers autophagy in broilers [81]. SELENOW synergistically works with TXNRD2 and TXNRD3 that regulates the cellular redox [82]. Dietary SeMet supplementation increased the mRNA expression of these selenogenes except SELENOS, and increased the protein abundance of GPX1, GPX4 and SELENOS. These results indicate that the protective effect of SeMet is mediated by the increased expression of selenotranscriptome and these key selenoproteins. The increased expression of these selenoproteins contributes to maintaining the mitochondria and ER homeostasis, thus recover the hepatic metabolic homeostasis. The above results were further confirmed by correlation analysis. In present study, these key selenogenes were generally negatively correlated with these mitochondrial stress and ER stress biomarkers, and generally negatively correlated with the concentration of TG and glycogen. Furthermore, almost all the key selenogenes were positively correlated with each other, which validates that Se performs its protective effects through the synergy of multiple selenoproteins.

Conclusion

The present study reveals that environment HS caused liver damage of broilers is accompanied by mitochondrial dysfunction and ER stress, which lowers the antioxidant capacity, promotes lipid and glycogen biosynthesis, suppresses the operation of the mitochondrial TCA cycle and the breakdown of lipids and glycogen, thus induces hepatic lipid accumulation and glycogen infiltration. Dietary SeMet supplementation can linearly increase the hepatic Se concentration, which contribute to increase the expression of selenotranscriptome and 11 key selenoproteins, thus mitigate the negative effects of CHS. The increased expression of these key selenoproteins synergistically improve the antioxidant capacity, maintain the mitochondria and ER homeostasis, promote lipid and glycogen breakdown, promote the operation of the mitochondrial TCA cycle and suppress the biosynthesis of lipids and glycogen, thus recover the lipid and glycogen metabolic homeostasis in liver of broilers exposed to environmental HS (Fig. 8). Our present study suggests that increased dietary organic Se supplementation in form of SeMet can relieve environmental HS induced hepatic metabolic disorders in broilers.

High energy and durable PANI, CuO and ZnO nanocomposites for aqueous/organic supercapacitors and their H+/NH+4 ions co-intercalation
Authors: Aranganathan Viswanathan, Akshay Manohar, Vanchiappan Aravindan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTernary composite of PANI, CuO and ZnO in its standardized weight composition of PANI54.55%:CuO36.36%:ZnO9.09% (PCZ9) was synthesized by in-situ single-step chemical method to study the supercapacitance of it and its binary composites. An aqueous supercapacitor cell of PCZ9 provided specific energy (E) higher than that of vanadium flow batteries and as close as to that of Pb-acid batteries, which is 27 W h kg−1 with the specific power (P) of 0.6 kW kg−1 at 1 A g−1, mainly inflicted by H+/ ions co-intercalation/de-intercalation. While, an organic asymmetric supercapacitor cell made up of an binary composite of PCZ9, which is CuO80%:ZnO20% (CZ) provided E higher than that of Pb-acid batteries, Ni–Cd batteries, Ni-MH batteries, Na-metal chloride batteries and Na-S batteries, which is 71 W h kg−1 with the P of 0.232 kW kg−1 at 0.1 A g−1. Most importantly, the organic asymmetric supercapacitor continued to provide higher E up to the current density of 2 A g−1, as a trait of its excellent rate capability. In addition, both the aqueous symmetric and organic asymmetric supercapacitors exhibited promising durability by retaining 58.11 and ~ 75% of their initial energy up to 5000 cycles and 2500 cycles , respectively, when subjected to durability test at 0.4 V s−1 and 1 A g−1 (at different temperatures), respectively. The high E of the aqueous symmetric supercapacitor cell of PCZ9 was demonstrated by making it to exhibit its power backup ability, when, the PCZ9 symmetrice cell provided 1 min. and 30 sec. of power backup for 1 min. of charging using the 1.5 V AA battery of Duracell, establishing its high energy character.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-45954-8.Keywords: Copper oxide, Zinc oxide, Polyaniline, Supercapacitor, Aqueous electrolyte, Green electrolyte, Organic electrolyteSubject terms: Chemistry, Energy science and technology, Materials science

Introduction

The ongoing attempts to increase the specific energy of supercapacitors to the level of batteries worldwide are still in the making, as the objective has not yet been achieved. In order to have a clear aim at the goal, it is imperative to learn about the goal. In order to look at the batteries in terms of their energy storage parameters, energy storage mechanisms and their different kinds, the following descriptions are essential. Generally, batteries are capable of providing higher specific energy (E), low specific power (P) and moderate durability of a few thousand cycles1. The cost associated with batteries implicitly depends on the abundance of their key elements, like electrodes, separators, current collectors, electrolytes, casing materials, and the method of fabrication. Of these, the electrodes are the vital ones as they involve in the energy storage reactions in association with the electrolyte used, when a suitable potential is applied. The electrodes involved are determining their energy storage mechanisms. The two kinds of electrodes are faradaic electrodes and non-faradic electrodes. The faradic electrodes include metal oxides and hydroxides like CuO, ZnO, VO2, SnO2, SnO, NiO, Ni2O3, V2O5, Ni(OH)2, Al(OH)3, etc., and conducting polymers like polyaniline (PANI), polypyrrole (PPy), etc. The non-faradaic electrodes include the carbonaceous materials like activated carbon (AC), reduced graphene oxide (rGO), graphene, etc. Of these, the ones with abundance, easy processability and capable of storing energy in combinations of mechanisms, among the energy storage mechanisms available such as electrical double layer formation, surface redox reactions, diffusion controlled intercalation reactions, reversible doping and dedoping of protons on their structure, diffusion controlled bulk electrode processes would be highly beneficial in engineering an electrode that is capable of storing and delivering higher energy. Similarly, the role of electrolytes in any energy storage systems is indispensable, as they provide electrical conductivity to the entire system and involve in the energy storage mechanisms as integral parts. Depending on the kinds of electrodes and electrolytes used, the batteries are classified into different categories. A few types are, Pb-acid batteries, use Pb and PbO as electrodes, and 30% Conc. H2SO4 as electrolyte1, Ni–Cd batteries use NiOOH and Cd as electrodes; and KOH as electrolyte1, Ni-metal hydride batteries use NiOOH and hydrogen-absorbing alloy of lanthanum, cerium, neodymium, and/or praseodymium; or nickel, cobalt, aluminum, and/or manganese as electrodes; and KOH as electrolytes1, Na-metal chloride batteries use molten sodium and solid metal halides (NiCl2, FeCl2, CuCl2, ZnCl2, etc.) as electrodes; and molten salts like sodium tetrachloroaluminate (NaAlCl4), as electrolytes2, Na-S batteries use molten sodium and molten sulfur as electrodes; and Na-ion-conductive β-alumina ceramic as electrolytes1, and Li-ion batteries (LIBs) use verities of electrode like LiFePO4, LiCoO2, Li2MnO3, LiMPO4 (olivines, M = Fe, Mn Co & Ni), etc. vs. graphite or Li4Ti5O12 3; and lithium ion containing electrolytes3. Of these, the ones with the superior E are LIBs, and they provide higher operational potential (0–5 V vs. Li) too. Here, in this research on supercapacitors, elevating the E from the level of capacitors (5–15 Wh Kg−1)1 to the level of batteries is the aim. Among the different category of batteries, the ones that exhibit relatively lower E are vanadium redox flow batteries (VRFB), Pb-acid batteries, Ni–Cd batteries and particularly their E are in the presence of aqueous electrolytes. Therefore, it is wise to approach their E considering the environmental, toxicity, and cost factors. Of these three, the one that was chosen to attain in this study is that of Pb-acid batteries as they are still extensively used in verities of appliances like automobiles, uninterrupted power supplies, etc. The E and P that can be attained from Pb-acid is in the range of 30–40 and 0.06–0.11 kW kg−1, respectively4. Battery is a device that is made of combining elemental cells in either of series or parallel connections in order to obtain the desired voltage and power. As seen in Table S1, from different sizes and voltages of Pb-acid batteries, the energy (in Wh) obtained is in the range of 27 to 394.8 Wh, and when they are normalized to their respective weights of the devices, then the E (in Wh kg−1) are in the range of 11.25 to 49.97 Wh kg−1. These are of the devices constituted by different numbers of individual cells. When the E (in Wh kg−1) is calculated per cell, then they are in the range of 1.875–14.285 Wh kg−1, as each cell or each assembly of cells that are connected in parallel fashion in the assembly provides 2 V. In addition, as seen in Table S1, the Pb-acid batteries are heavy in weight and larger in sizes. At the same time, it is not be forgotten that the overall weight is of the device, an energy storage system, therefore it involves the other passive materials like the plastic casing, current collectors, separators, and the electrolyte solution; and they add up to the total weight, thus leading to the underestimation of the true energy storage ability of the electrodes. However, they are also the integral parts of the device. Hence, the numbers printed on the devices normalized to the weight of the entire device not of electrodes and therefore it is better to assume that the energy of such devices, considerably higher than calculated, and so accordingly adjust the aim when their performance is tried to be achieved from the other kinds of energy storage devices like supercpacitors. Therefore, achieving supercapacitor electrodes which are light weight and capable of providing higher E than that of the devices that are already available in markets is wise, particularly of aqueous type, could be a milestone in the research of supercapacitors. As to achieve this mile stone this research was carried out.

Of a plethora of energy storage materials available, the materials that are chosen for the present study are CuO, ZnO and PANI, in the form of their binary and ternary composites. These materials were chosen for the study considering the following merits that they possess commonly, which are their abundance, easy synthesis, non-toxicity, easy processability, their high theoretical specific capacities, which are 6745, 9786, and 294 mAh g−1 (in LIBs)7 for CuO, ZnO and PANI, respectively, their faradaic mechanisms of energy storage, and in expensiveness. It is important to consider that the ZnO was generally synthesized by high temperature calcination of Zn(OH)2, but it is proved from our earlier studies that it could be synthesized at lower temperature of 80 °C, by chemical reduction method when synthesized along with the synthesis of PANI and other metal oxides like VO28, and SnO29. The PANI is one of the extensively explored conducting polymers due to its aforementioned advantages and its tunable conductivity by the use of appropriate proton-containing dopants. It stores energy by reversible proton doping and dedoping on its structural backbone, during which a series of faradaic reactions between three of its redox forms, namely, leucoemeraldine, emeraldine and perinigraniline, and between each of their salt and base forms take place, and these faradaic reactions occur depending on the state in which the PANI is obtained during the synthesis. These more than one kind of the faradaic reactions of PANI, make it a reliable source of high E and P10. In addition, the PANI allows its synthesis compatibility with other metal oxides, thereby forming the composites with constituents having the least aggregation. The synergy between CuO and ZnO is well known, as studies have already been performed with their composites for a variety of applications, including energy storage in both supercapacitors11–14 and batteries 15,16, as well as catalysis17. In addition, the combination of CuO and ZnO is further supported by the facts that these are p-type and n-type semiconductors, respectively17. The combination of them creates a kind of PN-junction in the composite to create an additional interface to store energy. Designing the electrode materials with PN-junctions is beneficial as it increases overall energy storage performances than their constituents of p-and n- type materials. Which is due to the following reasons;

The n-type materials act as an electron conductor as well as a window layer due to their high electron mobility, wide band gap, and substantial exciton binding energy at room-temperature. Therefore, they are highly utilized in various energy storage applications including supercapacitors. The transition metal oxides used in pseudocapacitors have drawbacks, such as low electrical conductivity and limited surface area that limit their electrochemical performance. To overcome these limitations, designing hybrid electrode materials of p- and n-kind that integrate the physical and chemical properties of these two kind of materials, enabling multiple redox reactions, reducing the charge transfer resistance, optimizing the power and energy density, and extending the potential window range, and thereby enhancing energy storage capacity has been reported to be the wise notion 18. In such hetero structures, integration of p- and n- types of components leads to electron movement across the interface due to differences in band structures, and Fermi levels, which affect the electron/ion states in adjacent areas. Theoretically, it is reported that the redistribution of charges within faradaic composite materials can enhance the tendency of electron donation/acceptance or alter the oxidation states of metal elements, thereby enhancing faradaic activity. In addition, the redistribution of charge spontaneously generates an internal electric field, accelerating electron/ion diffusion capabilities, enhancing charge transfer in electrode materials 19.

Already there are reports that revealing the electrodes of PN-junction like Bi2O3-TiO2 (the synergy has been reported to reduce the band gap and increase the operational potential window18), ZnO/NiO (the synergy has reported for the accumulation and discharge of more charges during the charging and discharging20), NiCo2O4/NiCoAl-layered double hydroxide (the synergy has reported for the increase of the faradaic activity and increase of conductivity19), NiO/phosphorus-doped MnO2 (the synergy has reported for the improved charge transport at the interface and prolonged electrochemical reactions21) and MnOx/MnS2 (the synergy has reported for the increase of columbic force to boost the NH4+ diffusion kinetics, in turn increase of energy storage22). In addition, in ion supercapacitors, the ion has the tendency to form H-bond with the host materials, which in combination with the anisotropy caused from the tetrahedral structure of ions limits its diffusion ability in host materials, and thus resulting in the less energy storage but it is reported that use of ions in the PN-junction type of electrodes enhances its diffusion kinetics due to the increased columbic force towards the diffusion kinetics of ions, as exemplified as ion storage in MnOx/MnS2, a PN-junction type material in 22.

Driven by these facts, a study on the ternary and binary composites of PANI, CuO and ZnO was designed to standardize the best compositions of them, to study their supercapacitive behavior in both symmetric and asymmetric cell configurations using different aqueous electrolytes and an organic electrolyte. The aqueous electrolytes involving supercapactive studies are with symmetric supercapcitor cells and the aqueous electrolytes used are 1 M H2SO4 and the liquid by-product obtained after the synthesis of PANI (SLP) as it proved to ameliorate the energy storage by 4023, 2224 and 57%9 in comparison with that obtained in the presence (ITP) of 1 M H2SO4. The organic electrolyte involving supercapacitive studies is with asymmetric/or hybrid supercapacitor cells, and the electrolyte used is LIPASTE (1 M LiPF6 in ethylene carbonate and dimethyl carbonate). The studies have produced very interesting findings and results. Most importantly the E on par with Pb-acid batteries, Ni–Cd batteries, Ni-metal hydride batteries, Na-metal chloride batteries and Na-S batteries were obtained.

Here in the present study, the fundamental energy storage and its enhancement using the SLP is pivot around the role of H+ and ions and their co-intercalation/de-intercalation mechanism. The source of which is naturally synthesized as SLP during the course of synthesis of PANI. The use of ion in hybrid supercapacitors is the new emerging trend in energy storage field due to the unique electrolytic characters that the ion provides. The ions are the abundance, inexpensive, widespread availability, non-toxicity, and low molar mass (18 g·moL−1) of ion. This lower molar mass of ion not only makes it as the lightest among metal ions but even lighter than hydronium ions (H3O+). This character of ion enables it to provide high energy density, rapid diffusion rates, and favorable cyclic performances compared to other electrolytic ions. The ionic radius of ion is 1.48 Å and it is relatively larger; however, its hydrated ionic size is 3.31 Å, which is relatively smaller. This unique combination of being lightweight and having a small hydrated ionic size facilitates its kinetics for ion intercalation/de-intercalation and rapid diffusion into the electrodes. In addition, compared to proton or hydronium ions, the hydrolysis of ammonium salts provide moderately acidic to neutral electrolyte atmosphere which reduces the detrimental side reactions such as hydrogen evolution and reduces corrosion of metallic parts of energy storage devices. This reduction in the hydrogen evolution helps in increasing working potential window of the ion containing energy storage devices and this higher working potential window is associated with the lower Gibbs free energy of charging-discharging processes 25.

The ion possess a tetrahedral shape and which is different from the spherical shape of metallic ions. This shape of ion facilitates the insertion processes of it in comparison with that of spherical metallic or nonmetallic charge carriers. The ion forms H-bonds with the host electrode materials during the intercalation and de-intercalation processes, and as these H-bonds are flexible and provide greater topotactic intercalation chemistry, therefore the ion provides higher energy storage. In addition, these H-bonds act as the bridge for charge transfer in comparison to metallic charge carriers and thus reducing the resistance associated with the charge transfer during the energy storage processes. During the ion intercalation and de-intercalation process, the ion participate in the charge transfer with the electrode by the formation and destruction of H-bonds, which results in improved energy storage in comparison with metal ions-based intercalations /de-intercalations26. The ions are weakly acidic to neutral, making them safer, non-toxic, and environmentally friendly in comparison with H+ ions. Analogues to H3O+ ions, the ion also dissociate highly in aqueous solution and thus providing high ionic conductivity and in turn high rate capability 25. The order of radius of hydrated forms of the following ions is Li+ > Na+ > K+ > > H+ and therefore, the and the H+ ions have the high ionic mobility, in turn, rapid ion transport throughout the pores, enhanced ion adsorption/intercalation and eventually improves energy storage 25. In addition, the ion is less reactive and which facilitates safety by reducing the thermal runaway. Therefore, the facilitated intercalation chemistry, wider operation potential and high ionic conductivity of ions help in achieving the higher energy storage. The SLP combines these aforementioned properties of with those of H+ to provide H+/ ions co-intercalation and de-intercalation for the higher energy storage. Experimental

Synthesis of polyaniline (PANI)/copper oxide/zinc oxide (PCZ) nanocomposites

In the synthesis of PCZ composite, 300 µL of aniline was mixed in 100 mL of distilled water (DW) by stirring, then 1.2 g of ammonium persulphate dissolved in a minimum quantity of DW was added, followed by 5 mL of 3 M methane sulphonic acid, and stirred for 5 h. After which, 40 mL of 0.1 M CuSO4. 5 H2O was added, and the pH was increased to 12 using 6 M NaOH. Then, the reduction reaction was conducted by adding an adequate amount of hydrazine hydrate for 2 h at 80 °C. After the 2 h of reduction, 6.15 mL of 0.1 M ZnSO4. 7 H2O was added, and the stirring was continued for 30 min at 50 °C. Then the obtained PCZ composite is purified and stored for analyses. The PCZ composites were synthesized by maintaining the quantities of aniline, CuSO4. 5 H2O, constant and by varying the quantities of ZnSO4. 7 H2O. The synthesized composites, their composition and respective nomenclatures are given in Table 1. The described synthesis is of PCZ9. The binary composites corresponding to the composition of PCZ9 were synthesized by just avoiding one of the precursors at a time, following the same synthetic method of PCZ9, as it provided better energy storage performance.

Electrochemical characterizations

When the energy storage capability of the PCZ composites were experimented, the one with the least quantity of ZnO, that is, PCZ9, exhibited better performance, while with an increase in ZnO content, its energy storage capability reduced. Then the binary composites of composition corresponding to that of PCZ9 were synthesized and experimented to substantiate that the ternary composite exhibits superior performance than that of its binary composites. All these aforementioned studies were conducted by fabricating the PCZ and their binary composites as symmetric supercapacitor cells using 1 M H2SO4 as electrolyte. Since the PCZ9 has exhibited better performance, and the use of liquid by-product of synthesis of PANI (SLP) as electrolyte is reported to be a fruitful way to increase energy storage. The supercapacitance of PCZ9 was studied using SLP, and it exhibited performance higher than that obtained ITP of 1 M H2SO4. In addition, one of the binary composites synthesized is CZ (composite of CuO/ZnO), which is a battery type material therefore whose energy storage in the form of a asymmetric supercapacitor using LIPASTE as electrolyte (both half-cell vs. Li (0.005 to 3 V) and full cell vs. activated carbon (AC) (0.7 to 3.7 V)) was conducted (fabrication details are provided in section S1) and in turn the supercapacitance of CZ using SLP also was conducted. The interesting findings and the related discussion are as follow.

Symmetric cells of PCZ nanocomposites using 1 M H2SO4

The CV (Fig. 4a) and CD (Fig. 4b) plots of PCZ composites of different compositions are with definite faradaic peaks and plateaus, indicating their larger faradaic mechanisms of storing energy. These faradaic reactions include the surface redox reactions of CuO, ZnO, protonation, deprotonation on PANI’s backbone and proton intercalations/ de-intercalations. The b-values (Eqn. S4) (the significance of b-value is given in Eqn. S4) corresponding to the peak that is stable up to the higher scan rates are 0.54 (R2 – 0.9841), 0.38 (R2 – 0.9855) and 0.37 (R2 – 0.9865) for PCZ9, PCZ16 and PCZ23, respectively (Fig. S7a). The peak positions that were considered for the deduction is the one at ~ 0.39 V in the forward scan. As these values indicate, the maximum amount of bulk diffusion-controlled intercalation reaction occurs in the PCZ9, and thus it exhibits a higher faradaic area and, in turn, stores higher energy in comparison with other PCZ composites. The PCZ9 possesses 9% of ZnO in it. As the percentage of ZnO increases, the intrinsic resistance increases; in turn, the conductivity and the energy storage decrease. The diffusion co-efficient (Do) (Eqn. S5) of PCZ9, PCZ16 and PCZ23 are found to be 0.015 (R2 – 0.9904), 0.004 (R2 – 0.9803) and 0.003 cm2 s−1 (R2 – 0.9768), respectively (Fig. S7b). This decrease in Do indicates that the increase in the amount of ZnO in PCZ composite disallows the effective diffusion of electrolyte into the bulk, thus increasing the intrinsic resistance, reducing conductivity and thus causing low energy storage in relation to PCZ9. It is attributed to the phenomenon of an increase in particle sizes due to aggregation of metal oxides on the PANI surface, leading to the formation of aggregates and in turn, high intrinsic resistance and low energy storage42. The extent of occurrence of surface process (electrical double layer formation and surface redox reactions) of energy storage and bulk diffusion-controlled intercalation reactions (Eqn. S6 and Eqn. S7) are 8.10 and 91.90% (R2 – 0.6490); 3.47 and 96.53% (R2 – 0.9277); and 3.68 and 96.32% (R2 – 0.9621) for PCZ9, PCZ16 and PCZ23, respectively (Fig. S7c). As these values indicate, the predominant process of energy storage is intercalation and de-intercalation processes, and the percentage of surface processes of energy storage is decreasing with an increase in ZnO content. Which further substantiates the point that the addition of low-conducting ZnO (band gap of CuO—1 eV, Cu2O—2.2 eV43 and ZnO—3.37 eV17) beyond 9%, decreases the energy storage as it decreases the percentage of surface phenomena and increases the intrinsic resistance by aggregation. It is perceived that in PCZ9, the constituents are dispersed in such a way that minimum aggregation and maximum interaction with the electrolyte leading to ample diffusion, is manifested. As the concordance with the CV results, the CD plots show the longer discharge time (td) for PCZ9 (Fig. S7d), indicating its maximum energy storage, and with an increase in the content of ZnO, the td decreases, indicating the decrease in energy storage. The PCZ9 capable enough to exhibit its faradaic signs of peak and plateaus even up to the scan rate of 0.5 V s−1 (Fig. 4c) and 25 A g−1 (Fig. 4d). Which indicates the effective interaction between the electrode and electrolyte even at high scan rates and current densities due to effective diffusion of electrolyte as it is, at lower scan and current rates. This phenomenon brings about better rate capability in PCZ9 in relation to other PCZ composites. The specific capacity (Q) (Eqn. S8), specific energy (E) (Eqn. S9), specific power (P) (Eqn. S10) and coulombic efficiency (η) (Eqn. S11) of PCZ composites (Table 3). The maximum current density that the PCZ9, PCZ16 and PCZ23 could reach without losing their energy storage nature completely are 25, 14 and 11 A g−1, respectively. The high current sustainability of PCZ9 signifies its robustness in relation to other PCZ composites. As seen, the CD curves at 25 and 50 °C exhibit longer td, indicating that 25 and 50 °C are more conducive for higher energy storage, which is due to the common fact that at higher temperatures the mobility of electrolytic ions increases, thus the conductivity increases and in turn higher energy storage is obtained. At lower temperatures, the mobility of electrolytic ions are affected, the diffusion of them into the electrode is affected, thus lower energy storage and in turn lower rate capability (Fig. 13f). In addition, the temperature plays a critical role in governing the electrolyte transport properties and interfacial reaction kinetics of the hybrid capacitor. With increasing temperature, the electrolyte viscosity decreases, facilitating easier ion transport in accordance with the Stokes–Einstein relationship51. The resulting enhancement in ionic mobility leads to higher ionic conductivity and a corresponding reduction in the ohmic resistance of the electrolyte. Simultaneously, the charge-transfer resistance at the electrode–electrolyte interface decreases due to thermally activated interfacial reactions. Collectively, these effects reduce polarization, accelerate ion transport, and improve the electrochemical utilization of the electrodes, resulting in enhanced device performance52, as reflected by the higher energy and power densities observed in Fig. 13f at elevated temperatures. However, operating at higher temperatures for an extended period also intensifies parasitic side reactions, which adversely affect long-term cycling stability53. These side reactions can irreversibly consume active lithium, leading to lithium inventory loss and a gradual decline in Q during prolonged cycling, as evidenced in Fig. 13g.

Just the names of PCZ9 and CZ indicate the ones whose electrochemical studies were performed ITP of 1 M H2SO4 as defined earlier.

The faradaic area and td obtained for PCZ9-SLP (Fig. 14b) and CZ-SLP (Fig. 14c) are higher than that of the PCZ9 and CZ, which indicates that the relatively higher energy is stored ITP of SLP. The Q and E of PCZ9-SLP and CZ-SLP are 166 C g−1 and 27.67 Wh kg−1; and 119 C g−1 and 19.9 Wh kg−1, respectively, at 1 A g−1, which are 72.37 and 221.54% higher than that obtained ITP of PCZ9 and CZ, respectively. The P obtained is 0.6 kW kg−1 for both PCZ9-SLP and CZ-SLP. The η are 81.77 and 74.18% (Table S8) for PCZ9-SLP and CZ-SLP, respectively, which are lower than those obtained from PCZ9 and CZ. These lower η indicate the relatively less diffusion kinetics of ions of SLP and higher occurrence of diffusion-controlled H+/ ions co-intercalation processes. The Do of PCZ9-SLP and CZ-SLP are 0.013 (R2 – 0.9975) and 0.002 cm2 s−1 (R2 – 0.9869), respectively (Fig. S19a), and the corresponding b-values are 0.606 (R2 – 0.9960) and 0.2 (R2 – 0.9922) (Fig. S19b). These Do are lower than those obtained for PCZ9 and CZ, which is attributed to the combination of different ions of different sizes moving at different rates, as the SLP contains (NH4)+ ions, which are less mobile than H+. The b-value of PCZ9-SLP is slightly higher than that of PCZ9, which indicates the relatively higher occurrence of surface capacitive processes in PCZ9-SLP in comparison with PCZ9. The b-value of CZ-SLP is lower than that of CZ, which indicates the larger occurrence of the H+/ ions co-intercalation process and the least of surface processes. The percentage contribution of surface capacitive and bulk H+/ ions co-intercalation processes are 4.29 and 95.71% (R2 – 0.7501), and 6.30 and 93.70% (R2 – 0.9083) (Fig. S20c) for PCZ9-SLP and CZ-SLP, respectively. The percentage occurrence of diffusion-controlled bulk process in CZ-SLP is substantially higher than that in CZ (83.40%), which is why the lower η is observed in the case of CZ-SLP. Because of these lower diffusions and higher occurrence of H+/ ions co-intercalation processes, the rate capability of PCZ9-SLP and CZ-SLP is reduced in comparison with PCZ9 and CZ, respectively.

The PCZ9-SLP retains its capacitive behavior up to 0.2 V s−1 (Fig. 14d) and 12 A g−1 (Fig. 14e), which are inferior to PCZ9. And the CZ-SLP retains its capacitive behavior up to 0.5 V s−1 (Fig. S20a) and 7 A g−1 (Fig. S20b), which are again inferior to CZ. The percentage retention of initial Q by PCZ9-SLP at 12 A g−1 is 28.62% and that by CZ-SLP at 7 A g−1 is 3.04% (Fig. 15a), which are lesser than the respective retention of Q by PCZ9 and CZ at the same current density, which are 32.14 and 57.29%, respectively. These inferior rate capability of PCZ9-SLP and CZ-SLP are due to the point that at higher current densities the electrolytic ions of different sizes and mobility of SLP do not get ample time to diffuse through the bulk of the electrode materials and establish the effective interactions to create high energy storage and this point is further supported by the point that the intercalation processes are generally sluggish than the surface capacitive phenomena. The E exhibited by the PCZ9-SLP are higher than the VRFB and closer to Pb-acid batteries; and that of CZ-SLP at lower current densities are on par with VRFB (Fig. 15b). The high E of aqueous symmetric supercapacitor cell of PCZ9-SLP was demonstrated by making it to exhibit its power backup ability, when, for 1 min of charging using the 1.2 V AA battery of Duracell, it provides 1 min and 30 s. of power backup establishing its high energy character (Video S1). Since the Durocell 1.5 V primary battery was used for charging, the cut of potential was 1.5 V and the rate at which the current flew through the circuit was ~ 150 mA. This high current and voltage rate endurance proves the energy capability of the composite.The η of both PCZ9-SLP and CZ-SLP (Fig. 15c) increase with increase in current density and reaches closer to 95% at higher current densities which is due to the point the with the increase in current density the relative proportion of intercalation process decreases and surface capacitive processes increases, which is seen in the form of decrease in the faradaic peak intensities at high scan rates in Fig. 14d and Fig. S20d (The corresponding CD plots provided in Fig. S20e). The PCZ-SLP and CZ-SLP retain 39.10% and 64.83% of their initial Q at the end of 5000 cycles (Fig. 15d), respectively. The better stability of CZ-SLP is attributed to its structural uniformity, as it has similar morphology in the entire bulk of the material, which is confirmed by the particle size distribution analysis as shown in Fig. S20f. and the single peak in it indicate the presence of similar sized particels which is ~ 980 nm and this obtained value is in agreement with the results of FE-SEM images as shown in below Fig. S14. While in the case of PCZ9-SLP, the PANI present in it undergoes large volumetric changes during charging and discharging with electrolyte ions of different sizes, this aspect of PCZ9 makes it to undergo deterioration faster than the CZ-SLP.

The obtained energy storage performance of PCZ9 and CZ is compared with recent similar materials, and it is seen that the obtained performances are perceived to be comparable and better than the reported ones (Table 7). The salient features of the present study are 1) the ZnO was synthesized in a single step at the temperature as low as 80 °C in chemical reduction method, in addition, this synthesis of ZnO at low temperature is possible only when synthesized as a composite with PANI as one of the constituents in the chemical oxidation–reduction method. This method of ZnO containing composite synthesis could be carried out without the use of any sophisticated instruments in a single step, unlike the conventional methods of using sophisticated instruments (as explained below) at higher temperatures for long time in more than one step. 2) The use of the liquid by-product of the synthesis of PANI as electrolyte, which is generally discarded as waste. This approach not only reduces the use of other aqueous electrolytes and expenses associated with it but also provides better performance than the 1 M H2SO4 and expected to provide comparable performance with other aqueous electrolyte. In addition this approach embodies the waste to wealth approach and create ecosystem for H+/ ion co-intercalation/de-intercaltion, which effectively increases the energy storage. And 3) this composite provided energy comparable with that of lead acid batteries, and exhibiting its potential to be the light weight, inexpensive, less risky, durable, high power and environmental friendly energy storage as an alternative to the heavy weighing, toxic heavy metal containing, non-environmental friendly, low power, risk posing (as it uses 30% Conc. H2SO4 as electrolyte), and expensive lead acid batteries.

The primary difference between the 1 M H2SO4 and SLP is the presence of (NH4)2SO4, and whose presence makes the entire study an H+/ ion supercapacitors, inadvertently, and the ion storage is one of the emerging trends in the field of energy storage, recently. In addition, since the electrolytes are synthesized in situ along with the PANI 23or with the PANI containing electrolytes as reported in our early works24,48, their integration occurred in such a way that saturation effect of (NH4)2SO4 in the electrolyte is effectively minimal in comparison with the electrolyte that is prepared with the (NH4)2SO4 at higher concentrations with other electrolyte like H2SO4. This effective minimization of common ion effect particularly in the mixture of H2SO4 and (NH4)2SO4 (SLP) increases abundance of ions in the electrolyte, which in turn increases the overall ionic conductivity and diffusion of electrolyte into the electrode materials.

In addition, the SLP used in this study was prepared by following the chemical stoichiometry as presented in 69 and the obtained SLP was the red-brown color clear liquid while the electrolyte mixture containing 3 M H2SO4 and 5 M (NH4)2SO4, that prepared manually exhibited the solubility issue as the complete solubility of (NH4)2SO4 did not take place, despite that when the top clear liquid was used in the supercapacitor the recrystallization of (NH4)2SO4 occurred to disallow its usage in the supercapacitors as shown in Fig. S23. Therefore, the SLP and its composition is unique in its properties and performance as electrolyte. The diffusion of ions of (NH4)2SO4 in addition to those of H2SO4 synergistically forming the H+/ ion co-intercalation/de-intercalation, increases the energy storage performance of the PANI/metal oxide nanocomposite electrode materials particularly, as ion increases diffusion controlled the intercalation/de-intercalation in the metal oxides as detailed following paragraphs. Therefore, the use of SLP as the electrolyte of H+/ ions instead of pristine ion containing electrolytes that is prepared manually using the salts of ion at different concentration would be highly beneficial for higher energy storage. The use of SLP as electrolyte for PANI/metal oxide composites and for metal oxides is an unique indirect route of ion storage technique.

The energy storage of copper oxide ITP of 1 M H2SO4 (CuO-SA) and SLP (CuO-SLP), were studied to establish its greater role in imparting the energy storage. The CV curves (Fig. S24a and b), CD curves (Fig. S24c and d), plots of current density vs. Q (Fig. S25a), E vs. P (Fig. S25b), current density vs. η (Fig. S25c) and EIS plots (Fig. S26) of CuO in the presence of 1 M H2SO4 (CuO-SA) and SLP (CuO-SLP) are displaying the enhanced performance of CuO-SLP. As it is seen from CV and CD plots that the CuO stores higher energy ITP of SLP as they exhibit higher faradaic area and td ITP of SLP. The mechanism that store the energy in CuO-SLP is H+/ ions co-intercalation and de-intercalation as confirmed from their b-values. The b-value of CuO-SA and CuO-SLP are 0.574 and 0.374, respectively (Fig. S26a), and it is important to note that these b-values are pivoted around 0.5, which is the indication of bulk diffusion controlled intercalation and de-intercalation processes. The CuO-SA possessing the b-value of 0.574 and which is higher than 0.5 indicated the minimal occurrence of surface capacitive reactions along with intercalation processes, while the b-value of 0.374 of CuO-SLP indicates the complete intercalation and de-intercalation processes. The percentage occurrence of surface capacitance and diffusion controlled H+/ ions co-intercalation and de-intercalation in CuO-SA and CuO-SLP are 11.22 and 88.78 (R2 – 0.7744); and 3.73% and 96.27% (R2 – 0.8476), respectively (Fig. S26b). It is because of this predominant bulk processes occurring ITP of SLP, CuO-SLP store higher energy in comparison with CuO-SA. However, since the SLP is made up of ions of different sizes its diffusion rate becomes sluggish and therefore CuO-SLP exhibits poor rate capability than CuO-SA. The Q retention up to 5 A g−1 of CuO-SLP is 3.92% while that of CuO-SA is 41.60% at the same current density. The Q, E, P and η of CuO-SA and CuO-SLP are 52.64, 8.77, 0.6 and 72.52%; and 58.6 C g−1 , 9.76 W h kg−1, 0.6 kW kg−1 and 63.24% at 1 A g−1, respectively. The low η of CuO-SLP in comparison with CuO-SA is attributed to the high extent of sluggish H+/ ions Co-intercalation/de-intercalation processes in it. In Nyquist plots of CuO-SA and CuO SLP (Fig. S26c), at higher frequency, the solution resistance of CuO-SA and CuO-SLP are 0.2633 and 0.7907 Ω, respectively, indicating the higher conductivity of SA than SLP in the system, however at mid frequency the higher diameter of CuO-SA than CuO-SLP indicates the lower charge transfer resistance (Rct) of CuO-SLP and which facilitate the higher energy storage in it, thus higher energy stored in CuO-SLP. This lower Rct of CuO-SLP is attributed to the H—bond formation of ions with CuO forming a bridge for easy charge transport during the charge storage process25. The longer Warburg line and the resistance associated with it of CuO-SLP at lower frequency indicates the slower diffusion of ions of SLP in CuO-SLP and therefore its poor rate capability in comparison with CuO-SA. Correspondingly, the Bode impedance plots (Fig. S26d) and plots of capacitance vs. log frequency (Fig. S26e) show the lower impedance and higher capacitance of CuO-SLP at mid frequency in comparison with the CuO-SA. In addition, this 3 M MSA doped PANI 70stores energy by the combination of proton intercalation and surface redox reactions that occur between its redox forms such as leucoemaraldine, emaraldine and peringraniline ITP of 1 M H2SO4 as indicated by the b-value of 0.677 (R2 – 0.9974) (Fig. S26f.) and the percentage contribution of surface and bulk intercalation/de-intercalation processes are 8.77% and 91.23% (R2 – 0.8987) (Fig. S25g). The use of SLP in PANI increase the extent of intercalation processes as already proved in our early studies on effect of SLP in energy storage of PANI 23. Since the ZnO could not by synthesized as a single entity at lower temperature its energy storage was not studied. Therefore the dominant species of specific energy is CuO and that of specific power is PANI .

Conclusions

The notion of improving the specific energy (E) of supercapacitors to the realm of batteries is substanially progressed by the ternary composite of PANI, CuO and ZnO in its standardized weight composition of PANI54.55%:CuO36.36%:ZnO9.09% (PCZ9), that was synthesized by in-situ single-step chemical method. Even one of its binary composites, CuO80%:ZnO20% (CZ) also exhibited its high E on par with batteries in the presence of organic electrolyte LIPASTE. The aqueous supercapacitor cell of PCZ9 provided E higher than that of vanadium flow batteries and as close as to that of Pb-acid batteries, which is 27 W h kg−1 with the specific power (P) of 0.6 kW kg−1 at 1 A g−1 such a high E was inflicted by H+/ ions co-intercalation/de-intercalation. While, an organic asymmetric supercapacitor cell of CZ provided E, which is higher than that of Pb-acid batteries, Ni–Cd batteries, Ni-MH batteries, Na-metal chloride batteries and Na-S batteries, which is 71 W h kg−1 with the P of 0.232 kW kg−1 at 0.1 A g−1. Most importantly, the organic asymmetric supercapacitor continued to provide higher E up to the current density of 2 A g−1, as a trait of its excellent rate capability. In addition, both the aqueous symmetric and organic asymmetric supercapacitors exhibited promising durability by retaining 58.11 and ~ 75% of their initial energy up to 5000 cycles and 2500 cycles, respectively, when subjected to durability test at 0.4 V s−1 and 1 A g−1 (at different temperatures), respectively. The high E of the aqueous symmetric supercapacitor cell of PCZ9 was demonstrated by making it to exhibit its power backup ability, when 1 min of charging using the 1.5 V AA battery of Duracell, enabled the PCZ9 supercapacitor cell to provide 1 min and 30 s. of power backup, establishing its high energy character. Therefore, these composites would be immensely useful in fabricating high energy and durable supercapacitors.

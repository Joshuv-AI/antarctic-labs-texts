Research note: Phosphoproteomic analysis of macrophage immunity stimulated by egg white glycopeptide
Authors: Hong He, Jinghui Wang, Zhihong Cai, Mei Wang, Di Wu, Xinhui Wang, Jinqiu Wang, Yi He, Wei Wang, Putri Widyanti Harlina, Sihai Gao, Fang Geng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractEgg white glycopeptides (EWG) are dietary peptides with immune regulatory activities; however, the underlying immune regulation mechanisms of EWG-mediated have not been fully elucidated. Phosphorylation represents a crucial protein modification in immune cell signaling. In this study, phosphoproteomic technology was employed to investigate alterations in macrophage phosphoprotein profiles in response to EWG stimulation (EWG group) using lipopolysaccharide stimulation as a positive control (LPS group), and bioinformatics approaches were used to analyze the associated signaling pathways. The untreated macrophages served as the control group (CK group). Compared with the CK group, 625 differentially phosphorylated proteins (DPPs) were identified in the EWG group, involving 774 differential phosphorylation sites. Among them, the number of up-regulated DPPs was less than that of down-regulated DPPs, whereas the LPS/CK group displayed the opposite trend. This finding indicated that the mechanisms underlying EWG- and LPS-activated macrophage immunity were related to protein dephosphorylation, but the regulatory patterns of protein phosphorylation differed between EWG and LPS. In the EWG/CK group, 60.75% of DPPs were located in the nucleus and annotated to immune signaling pathways such as leukocyte transendothelial migration and Fc gamma R-mediated phagocytosis. Similar to LPS, the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB pathway was identified to be crucial in the immune regulation of EWG-activated macrophages. This study elucidated the changes in the phosphorylated protein profiles underlying the EWG-mediated activation of macrophage immunity, providing a theoretical basis for developing EWG as an immunomodulatory food bioactive compound.Keywords: Egg white glycopeptide, Food bioactive compound, Phosphoproteomic, Immune regulation

Introduction

Protein phosphorylation, an important and extensively studied post-translational modification, is a highly dynamic signal transduction mechanism that serves as a link between extracellular signals and various physiological processes. Research has suggested that protein kinases and phosphatases are closely related to the addition and removal of inorganic phosphate groups (i.e., protein phosphorylation and dephosphorylation), thereby ensuring the precise regulation of signal transduction within the cell (Tanzer, et al., 2021).

In recent years, several studies have focused on utilizing food-derived glycoproteins to improve immunity. Chicken eggs are an important protein source worldwide, with egg whites being abundant in various glycoproteins (e.g., ovalbumin, ovomucin, etc.). During gastrointestinal digestion, these glycoproteins break down and release egg white glycopeptides (EWG). Previous studies have found that EWG might regulate the macrophage immunity by potentially activating relevant signaling pathways, such as NF-κB, C-type lectin receptor, and TNF signaling pathways (He, et al., 2024a; He, et al., 2024b). Thus, EWG might serve as a potential immunomodulatory active food ingredient. However, it is unclear whether EWG activates macrophage immunity by impacting the expression of phosphorylated proteins in macrophages. Phosphoproteomics has been demonstrated to be an effective strategy for studying kinase-mediated signal transduction regulation. For instance, Sjoelund, et al. (2014) found that treatment of macrophages with toll-like receptor (TLR) 2, TLR4, and TLR7 ligands markedly elevated the protein phosphorylation in multiple signaling pathways, such as those involving cytoskeleton components and phospholipase C. Moreover, protein phosphorylation associated with phagocytosis occurred exclusively in the reaction to TLR2 and TLR4, especially the latter. In another study, 405 differentially phosphorylated proteins (DPPs) were identified in lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-stimulated macrophages, with 141 up-regulated DPPs and 264 down-regulated DPPs (Luo, et al., 2020). These findings indicated that phosphoproteomic analysis is imperative for investigating the molecular mechanisms of macrophage immunoregulation. Therefore, it is necessary to elucidate the mechanisms underlying EWG-mediated activation of macrophage immunity using phosphoproteomics techniques.

This work aimed to investigate the alterations in the expression profiles of phosphoproteins in mouse macrophages stimulated by EWG using phosphoproteomics analysis. Further, the signaling pathways involved in EWG-activated macrophage immunity were summarized, and the mechanisms underlying macrophage immunity were revealed by assessing the protein phosphorylation levels. Our findings provide theoretical guidance for developing EWG as an active immunomodulatory food compound.

Materials and methods

Materials

EWG was prepared according to the method described by He, et al. (2024b) in a previous study. Mouse macrophages (RAW264.7 cells) were purchased from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China) and cultured in a controlled incubator at 37°C and under 5% CO2. Bicinchoninic acid (BCA) kit, LPS, and phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) were obtained from Beyotime (Shanghai, China). Tetraethylammonium bromide (TEAB), dithiothreitol, trifluoroacetic acid (TFA), iodoacetamide, formic acid (FA), acetonitrile, and lysis buffer were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, USA). Tandem mass tag (TMT) labeling reagent was attained from ThermoFisher Scientific (Waltham, MA, USA).

Cell stimulation

RAW264.7 cells were cultured in a culture dish at a density of 2 × 107 cells/mL. Then, the cells were treated for 8 h with either EWG (125 μg/mL, EWG group) or LPS (25 ng/mL, LPS group). Cells treated with PBS for 8 h were used as the control group (CK group). Then, the dishes were washed thrice with PBS at 4°C. Finally, the cell pellets were obtained via scraping for subsequent protein extraction.

Protein extraction and trypsin digestion

RAW264.7 cells were lysed with lysis buffer and centrifuged at 12000 × g for 10 min, and the supernatant was obtained. A BCA kit was used to determine the protein concentration. Subsequently, an equal amount of protein solution was taken from each group, and standard protein was added to the protein solution to obtain samples of equal volume. Thereafter, pre-cooled acetone was added to the samples, and the mixtures were vortexed, mixed, incubated at -20 °C for 2 h, and then filtered. The precipitate was washed and dispersed in a 200 mmol/L TEAB. Then, trypsin (1:50 g/g) was added to the solution to digest the proteins. Finally, dithiothreitol (5 mmol/L final concentration) was added to the digest solution, and the mixture was incubated at 56 °C for 30 min. Next, iodoacetamide (11 mmol/L final concentration) was added to the mixture, followed by incubation for 15 min to obtain peptides. The peptides were desalted and vacuum freeze-dried to obtain lyophilized powder.

TMT labeling and high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) fractionation

The peptides were solubilized in 0.5 mol/L TEAB and subsequently labeled with TMT labeling reagents. The labeled peptides were subjected to desalting and then fractionation using high-pH reversed-phase HPLC, equipped with an Agilent 300Extend C18 column (4.6 mm × 250 mm). Sixty fractions were acquired under 8%-32% acetonitrile gradient treatment. These fractions were randomly combined into four fractions and then freeze-dried.

Modification enrichment of peptides

The peptides were solubilized in an enrichment buffer solution, transferred to immobilized metal ion affinity chromatography material, and incubated on a rotary shaker. The material was washed thrice with 50% acetonitrile/0.5% acetic acid solution and then thrice with 30% acetonitrile/0.1% TFA solution. The phosphopeptides were then eluted using 10% ammonia, and eluates were collected, desalted, and freeze-dried for subsequent analysis.

Liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) analysis

The phosphopeptides were solubilized in LC mobile phase A, containing 0.1% FA and 2% acetonitrile, and then separated through ultra-high performance LC (EASY-nLC 1200) under a flow rate of 500 nL/min. The mobile phase B comprised 0.1% FA and 90% acetonitrile. The separation gradient was as follows: 0-40 min (4%-20% B), 40-52 min (20%-32% B), 52-56 min (32%-80% B), and 56-60 min (80%-80% B). The separated peptide was injected into a nanospray ion source for ionization, and subsequently analyzed using the Q Exactive™ HF-X mass spectrometer. MS spectra were acquired in the 350-1400 m/z range at a scanning resolution of 60000, and MS/MS spectra were acquired at a fixed start of 100 m/z and a scanning resolution of 30000. The signal threshold was 8.3 × 104 ions/s, automatic gain control was 5 × 104, and dynamic exclusion time for MS/MS scans was 15 s.

Database search and bioinformatics analysis

MaxQuant software (v1.6.15.0) was utilized to analyze the MS/MS data, with the Mus_musculus_10090_SP_20220107.fasta database selected. Minimum peptide length and maximum number of peptide modifications were set to seven amino acid residues and five, respectively. The cleavage enzyme selected for this study was trypsin/P, and two sites could be missed. The fixed modification was carbamidomethyl, and variable modifications included deamidation, methionine oxidation, protein N-terminal acetylation, and phosphorylation of serine, threonine, and tyrosine. The quantification method employed was TMT-10plex, with a false discovery rate of 1% for the identification of proteins and peptide-spectrum matches.

The mean biological quantification ratio of phosphorylation sites between the groups was expressed as fold change (FC). At p < 0.05, FC > 1.3 and FC < 1/1.3 were indicated to reflect up-regulated and down-regulated phosphorylation sites, respectively. The enrichment of DPPs was assessed by implementing the two-tailed Fisher’s exact test. Functional annotation and pathway enrichment analysis were performed using gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses, respectively, and p-adjust < 0.05 indicated statistical significance.

Results and discussion

Identification of phosphorylated proteins and phosphorylation sites

Protein phosphorylation has been identified as a pivotal cellular regulatory mechanism that activates or inhibits multiple signaling pathways (Tanzer, et al., 2021). In the present study, a total of 10,721 phosphorylation sites on 10,149 phosphorylated peptides belonging to 3,497 phosphorylated proteins were identified from the CK, LPS, and EWG groups. Among these sites and proteins, 6,834 phosphorylation sites and 2,613 phosphorylated proteins were quantifiable. Ser (S), Thr (T), and Tyr (Y) residues were the main phosphorylation sites in the cells, accounting for 89.94%, 9.44%, and 0.62% of the phosphorylation modifications in macrophages, respectively (Fig. 1A). Motif score analysis demonstrated high scores (>45) for base sequences xxxxxx_S_DSDxxx, xxxxxx_S_DxExEx, xxxxxx_S_DEDxxx, xxxxxxD_S_ExExxxx, and xxxxxx_S_DEExxx (Fig. 1B).

Different lowercase letters indicate statistically significant differences (p < 0.05) among groups.

Screening of DPPs and differential phosphorylation sites

As shown in Fig. 1C, the CK, LPS, and EWG groups, particularly the latter two, exhibited clustering on the distribution plot, and significant separation among the three groups (principal component (PC) 1: 36.4%; PC2: 16.5%), suggesting strong intragroup biological replication within the sample groups while maintaining intergroup variability. Changes in the phosphorylation modification level were primarily related to the variations in protein expression and phosphorylation-responsive regulatory mechanisms. A total of 625 DPPs (242 up-regulated and 383 down-regulated) and 774 differentially phosphorylated sites were identified in the EWG/CK group, and 862 DPPs and 1096 differentially phosphorylated sites were identified in the LPS/CK group (Fig. 1D). Notably, the EWG/CK group exhibited fewer up-regulated DPPs than down-regulated ones, whereas the LPS/CK group displayed the opposite trend, indicating distinct regulatory phosphorylation patterns in macrophages induced by EWG and LPS.

Analysis of major DPPs

We further analyzed major DPPs to elucidate the effects of EWG on the phosphorylated proteome of macrophages (Fig. 1E). Notably, rho-associated protein kinase 1 (Rock1) was significantly up-regulated in the EWG and LPS groups (3.163- and 2.897-fold, respectively; p < 0.05) compared to the CK group. Rock1, a physiological regulator, is an efficacious therapeutic target for regulating the functions of neutrophils and macrophages in inflammatory diseases. Moreover, compared to the CK group, homer protein homolog 3 (Homer3), basigin (Bsg), cyclic AMP-dependent transcription factor ATF-3 (Atf3), nuclear factor NF-kappa-B p100 subunit (Nfkb2), NF-kappa-B inhibitor zeta (Nfkbiz), and CD44 antigen (Cd44) were significantly up-regulated (p < 0.05) in the EWG and LPS groups. No significant differences were observed in the expressions of Bsg, Nfkbiz, and Cd44 between the EWG and LPS groups (p > 0.05). These phosphorylated proteins are closely related to immune regulation (Liu, et al., 2023). Typically, LPS can trigger an inflammatory response by activating TLR4 on the cell membrane of macrophages, an important component of the innate immune system. Therefore, these results suggested that, similar to LPS, EWG might activate macrophage immunity by regulating these phosphorylated proteins.

The phosphorylation cascades and dephosphorylation events are important biological features regulating innate immune cells (Tanzer, et al., 2021). Compared to the CK group, B-cell linker protein (Blnk), cell division cycle protein 20 homolog (Cdc20), TNF receptor-associated factor 1 (Traf1), tumor necrosis factor (Tnf), fos-related antigen 1 (Fosl1), telomere-associated protein RIF1 (Rif1), and osteopontin (Spp1) were significantly down-regulated in the EWG and LPS groups (p < 0.05). However, their expressions were comparable between the EWG and LPS groups (p > 0.05). Among these proteins, Blnk was down-regulated by 0.273- and 0.25-fold in the EWG/CK and LPS/CK groups, respectively. Furthermore, the expression of two, six, and seven sites of Fosl1, Rif1, and Spp1 in the EWG group was significantly down-regulated compared to the CK group (p < 0.05). These proteins, particularly Spp1, are involved in immune responses. Spp1, a highly phosphorylated glycoprotein, mediates immune and inflammatory responses via CD44 binding (Wang and Niu, 2024). Previous studies have shown that LPS treatment induces acute inflammation in RAW264.7 cells and leads to the abnormal expression of 405 phosphorylated proteins (141 up-regulated and 264 down-regulated) (Luo, et al., 2020). These results suggested that EWG, similar to LPS, played a key role in regulating macrophage immunoreactivity via protein dephosphorylation processes.

Functional annotation of DPPs

Subcellular localization revealed that the DPPs were primarily distributed in the cell nucleus (60.75%), with 140 up-regulated DPPs and 251 down-regulated DPPs (Fig. 2A). This finding might be attributed to significant EWG-induced alterations in the physiological activity within the nuclei of macrophages. DPPs in the EWG/CK group were primarily annotated to cellular process, biological regulation, and metabolic process in the biological process category; cell and intracellular in the cellular component category; and binding and catalytic activity in the molecular function category (Fig. 2B). In addition, the DPPs were widely annotated to signaling and immune system process, which are closely related to immune regulation. The basic functions of the innate immune system are largely regulated by protein phosphorylation (Tanzer, et al., 2021). The addition of phosphate groups to proteins can only be catalyzed by specialized kinases. Furthermore, the EWG/CK group exhibited more down-regulated DPPs and fewer up-regulated DPPs. Consequently, dephosphorylation events were also important in the regulation of innate immune cells.

KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of DPPs

We conducted KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of DPPs to elucidate the mechanisms underlying EWG-mediated macrophage immunoregulation. The up-regulated DPPs identified in the EWG/CK group were mainly involved in significant enrichment pathway of mitophagy-animal, focal adhesion, leukocyte transendothelial migration, and proteasome. In contrast, the down-regulated DPPs were primarily associated with the choline metabolism in cancer, Fc gamma R-mediated phagocytosis, lysine degradation and regulation of actin cytoskeleton (Fig. 2C). This finding indicated that EWG primarily regulated the physiological metabolic processes of macrophages through the above-mentioned signaling pathways. Leukocyte transendothelial migration is regulated by multiple factors, such as chemokines and adhesion molecules (Worthylake and Burridge, 2001). We found an up-regulation of six phosphorylated proteins involved in leukocyte transendothelial migration, suggesting that EWG might exert immunomodulatory effects by activating this signaling pathway. Moreover, Fc-gamma receptor (FcγR), a classical receptor for the Fc region of IgG, participates in innate and adaptive immune responses (Park, et al., 2020). EWG treatment resulted in a down-regulation of nine phosphorylated proteins in the Fc gamma R-mediated phagocytosis pathway, which might be closely associated to maintaining intracellular immune homeostasis by macrophages. These results indicated that EWG exerted immunomodulatory effects by regulating phosphorylation-related signaling pathways in macrophages.

The key role of EWG in immune regulation

Immune cell signaling is dependent on the regulation of protein phosphorylation. Thus, a comprehensive analysis of protein phosphorylation profiles is essential for understanding the complex signaling in EWG-treated macrophages. The TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB signaling pathway is pivotal in immune and inflammatory mechanisms. Previous proteomic results have suggested that EWG stimulates RAW264.7 cells and exerts immune effects via the NF-κB signaling pathway, the TLR signaling pathway, and so on (He, et al., 2024b). Therefore, the present study further analyzed the phosphorylated proteome on the EWG/CK and LPS/CK groups in the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB pathway (Fig. 2D).

TLRs, pattern recognition receptors located on the cell membrane, have been demonstrated to play a pivotal role in innate and adaptive immunity (Sjoelund, et al., 2014). Upon recognition of LPS or EWG by TLR4, adaptor proteins containing Toll/IL-1 receptor domains are recruited, initiating signal transduction pathways and ultimately activating NF-κB and MAPK kinases, which, in turn, regulate the immune responses of macrophages. The NF-κB kinases comprise five distinct proteins, namely RelA (p65), RelB, c-Rel, p50 (Nfkb1), and p52, which can form homodimers and heterodimers. In unstimulated macrophages, NF-κB dimers are restricted to the cytoplasm by the NF-κB inhibitor protein. However, upon external stimulation, the NF-κB dimers kinase (IKK) complex is activated, which phosphorylates the NF-κB inhibitor protein and leads to its degradation by the proteasome. Following the shedding of the NF-κB inhibitor protein, the released NF-κB dimer is phosphorylated and subsequently translocates to the nucleus, activating target gene transcription (Iacobazzi, et al., 2023). Furthermore, MyD88 serves as an essential adapter protein in intrinsic immune signaling, linking upstream TLRs and downstream NF-κB signaling pathways.

Interleukin-1 receptor-associated kinase 4 (IRAK4) is critical in the systemic inflammatory response as a key factor downstream of the interleukin-1 receptor (IL-1R) and TLR signaling pathways. Previous proteomic studies have shown that EWG stimulation induces a significant IRAK4 upregulation in macrophages (He, et al., 2024b). However, no significant changes in IRAK4 were observed at the phosphorylated protein level, indicating that EWG stimulation primarily activated IRAK4 at the protein level, triggering other downstream molecules. In the NF-κB pathway, expression of tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and E3 ubiquitin/ISG15 ligase TRIM25 (TRIM25) was down-regulated at the phosphorylated protein level in the EWG/CK group. These DPPs further stimulated TAK1, which, in turn, led to a 1.311-fold up-regulation of p50 at the protein level (He, et al., 2024b). Similarly, p50 was up-regulated by 1.335-fold at the protein level in LPS-stimulated macrophages (He, et al., 2024b). Therefore, similar to LPS, EWG can activate the TLR4 signaling pathway, subsequently activating the downstream NF-κB signaling pathway.

Furthermore, following the activation of the NF-κB signaling pathway, NF-κB enters the cell nucleus and induces the production of pro-inflammatory mediators and molecules, such as cyclooxygenase-2, interleukin-1β, interleukin-6, and TNF-α, further stimulating the inflammatory response (Iacobazzi, et al., 2023). The current study found that EWG stimulation led to a significant up-regulation of Nfkb2 and a significant down-regulation of tumor necrosis factor receptor-associated factor 1/2 (TRAF1/2) at the phosphorylated protein level. These findings indicated that EWG triggered macrophages to exert immunomodulatory effects primarily via the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB signaling pathway. Notably, TRAF1/2 and TNF-α were significantly down-regulated at the phosphorylated protein level, which further suggested that EWG-mediated regulation of macrophage immunity was closely linked to dephosphorylation. In conclusion, this study elucidated the alterations in macrophage phosphoprotein profiles induced by EWG stimulation and its associated signaling pathways, especially the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB signaling pathway. These findings revealed a prospective signaling pathway for exploring the mechanisms underlying EWG-mediated immune activity in vivo, and provided a theoretical foundation for the further development of EWG as an immuno-functional food bioactive compound.

A novel computational study of reverse degree topological indices and entropies for nanostar dendrimers
Authors: Ashfaq Ahmed Qummer, Muhammad Saqib, Shahbaz Ali, Mamo Abebe Ashebo, Sardor Jumaniyazov
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper is a topological study of nanostar dendrimers in the context of topological indices based on reverse degree. Nanostar dendrimers with highly branched structures and potential use in drug delivery and nanotechnology offer a challenge to structural analysis in terms of their complexity. The main aim of the work is to calculate and discuss some topological indices in reverse form, which can be used to obtain the necessary information concerning the molecular structure, the pattern of branches, and the connectivity. The indices are a good source of quantitative information on the hidden topology of nanostar dendrimers and their effects on chemical and physical characteristics. The findings indicate that reverse degree-based indices are very useful in defining the complex topological distributions of dendrimers, hence providing a strong mathematical framework, which contributes to subsequent developments in nanomaterials studies and their potential applications. Entropy-based topological measurements of polypropylenimine octaamine dendrimers and fullerene dendrimers have great advantages to chemists as a quantitative characterization of the molecular complexity, branching behaviors, and structural anomalies. Entropy measures based on degree-based edge partitions give the distribution of atomic connectivity and provide a rigorous method of comparing highly branched dendritic networks and cage-like carbon structures. We compute molecular entropies for polypropylenimine octaamine dendrimers and fullerene dendrimer networks.Keywords: Nanostar dendrimers, Reverse degree indices, Molecular topology, Nanomaterials science, Nanobiotechnology, EntropiesSubject terms: Chemistry, Materials science, Mathematics and computing, Nanoscience and technology

Introduction

The recent, progressive branch of nanobiotechnology is an amalgamation of the concepts of biology and nanotechnology to design nanoscale instruments and machines that may be used to investigate and engineer biological mechanisms with relevant precision as never before. With advanced nanofabrication technology, scientists can create some of the most innovative devices that can respond molecularly to cells, proteins, and other biomolecules, in addition to drug delivery systems, molecular imaging devices, and nanosensors, to name a few, through nanofabrication technology1. Biology and nanotechnology can totally change the process of therapeutics, healthcare, and diagnostics, as they offer new insights into the complexity of biological processes and diseases2.

Using the concepts of nanofabrication, the creative and quickly developing discipline of nanobiotechnology creates state-of-the-art tools for researching and engaging with biosystems at the molecular level3. Concepts from biology, chemistry, materials science, biophysics, and nanotechnology are all combined in the interdisciplinary field of nanobiotechnology. Its goal is to develop nanoscale functional devices that enable in-depth molecular research of biological systems4. Because nanoscale production techniques offer such fine control, researchers can engineer materials and structures with remarkable precision. A major area of interest for nanobiotechnology is dendrimers. They are artificial macromolecules made by a methodical process called nanoscale fabrication, which results in a highly organised and complex architecture to Combinatorial Chemistry High Throughput Screening.

Dendrimers are similar due to their regular and uniform structure branches of trees, which branch out of a main central core. Because dendrimers are extremely proud of their great qualities and powers diversifiable and demanded in several applications because of their distinct structure5.

One of the features that distinguishes the dendrimers is their monodispersity compared to that of the conventional polymeric systems. The uniform size, shape, and chemical structure of dendrimers distinguish them from standard. polymers. Conversely, regular polymers can exhibit a range of sizes and topologies. Their behavior and characteristics may be more accurately controlled because of this trait, which makes them valuable in a range of scientific and technological domains. There are many applications for dendrimers, including pharmaceutical delivery systems6, gene therapy vectors7, 8, biomaterials9, nanosensors, catalysis, and imaging agents. Analyses of some structural properties on a class of Hierarchical scale-free networks and statistical analyses of a class of random pentagonal chain networks with respect to several topological properties are discussed in Refs.10–15.

With the careful functionalization of dendrimers with desirable molecules or patterns, researchers can develop customized systems that are superior. characteristics, including improved solubility, biocompatibility, or targeted. responses to particular cell types or tissues. The adaptability of dendrimers and the multidisciplinary essence of nanobiotechnology. offer tremendous possibilities for discoveries in a multitude of areas and sectors, such as biotechnology, materials science, medicine, and pharmaceuticals. The earlier research16–19 offers more thorough insights into the various uses and possibilities of dendrimers, clarifying the current findings and research in this fascinating field of scientific inquiry.

The structural characteristics of medicinal drugs are represented mathematically in medicine; the structure is represented as an undirected graph. In this graph, each vertex stands for an atom, and the edges represent the chemical bonds that bind these atoms together. Lets look at a straightforward graph that represents the organisation of medicine and is designated as G. In G, the edges are denoted by E(G) and the set of vertices by V(G). If two atoms have a chemical link, they are regarded as neighbouring. The number of chemical bonds that surround an atom in the undirected medicinal structure is known as its degree. The degree of an atom x is denoted by . Over the past 40 years, scientists have created a variety of crucial indices to assess and measure the properties of therapeutic substances. These indexes consist of the eccentric index, Zagreb index, PI index, and Wiener index. Every index is a real valued function that converts a medicine structure into a certain range of real numbers so that its characteristics can be described. Hakeem et al. computed the degree-based indices of Y-graphene and Zigzag graphene nanoribbons20, Huilgol et al. examined the structure activity connections, and Gao et al. investigated the use of topological indices in pharmaceuticals21.calculated the fullerenes topological indices22. Prior research has also examined a variety of dendrimer topological indices23, 24. Degree-based entropies were used by Hui et al. to predict the physicochemical characteristics of antiemetic medications25, and Hayat and Imran talked about the topological indices of specific networks26. Entropies for polypropylenimine octaamine dendrimers and fullerene dendrimer networks

The research on entropy-based topological measurements of Polypropylenimine Octaamine Dendrimers and Fullerene Dendrimer has great advantages to chemists as a quantitative characterisation of the molecular complexity, branching behaviours, and structural anomalies. Entropy measures based on degree-based edge partitions give the distribution of atomic connectivity and provide a rigorous method of comparing highly branched dendritic networks and cage-like carbon structures. To chemists, these entropy values are of great assistance in the determination of physicochemical properties, including stability, reactivity, surface activity, and molecular compactness. In addition, the entropy-based indices have been useful in structure-property relationship (SPR) and quantitative structure-activity relationship (QSAR) investigations to convert the complex molecular structure into numerical values that can be computed. This allows the efficient screening, comparison, and optimization of dendrimeric materials for applications in drug delivery, nanotechnology, catalysis, and materials science. Therefore, the entropy analysis not only adds to the theoretical knowledge about molecular topology, but it also assists in making a valuable decision when designing and producing chemicals. Conclusion

The Reverse Degree Topological Index (RDTI) for two distinct classes of nanostar dendrimers has been precisely calculated in this work. The head-to-core, core-to-tail, and overall architectures of these intricate macromolecules are revealed in a number of new ways by this study. The structure-property link for nanostar dendrimers starts to take shape as the RDTI values start to appear. Our understanding of dendrimer designs is improved by these RDTI computations, which also provide a theoretical foundation for additional real-world dendrimer applications in materials science, medicine, and nanobiotechnology. This research offers a significant prospect for expansion. It may be possible to look into even more topological indices in order to comprehend dendrimer structures and their functions, both in terms of their comprehensibility and WWII comprehensibility. Our understanding of the connection between topological characteristics and physicochemical properties may be greatly improved by this investigation, which may also advance predictive modelling skills for dendrimer-based applications. It may be possible to validate and enhance our theoretical models by collaborating with experimental researchers to create and analyse dendrimers that have the characteristics we anticipate. Additionally, it would guarantee the dependability of these models in real-world scenarios. This theoretical study serves as a springboard for upcoming empirical research in the rapidly expanding field of nanobiotechnology. Our goal is to close the gap between applications of nanobiotechnology and topological theory. Therefore, the synthesis and functionalisation of dendrimers nanosized polymers having a tree-like branched structure as vehicles for targeted drug delivery, are the focus of our initial set of studies. Background and theory are discussed in detail, and all our computational results are presented with the required accuracy and precision.

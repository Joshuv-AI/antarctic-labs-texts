Diagnostic performance and risk assessment of combined copeptin and high-sensitivity cardiac troponin I in Chinese emergency department
Authors: Juan Sun, Xingtong Chen, Yitong Huang, Yongcai Tang, Yan Du, Xiaofan Gu, Jun Liao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRapid and early identification of acute myocardial infarction (AMI) is crucial for initiating timely treatment and assessing post-discharge risks. Copeptin’s unique release profile may help detect early myocardial damage, particularly when high-sensitivity cardiac troponin I (hs-cTnI) is undetectable. However, there is a limited understanding of copeptin’s application in the Chinese population, highlighting the need for further research.MethodsA prospective cohort study was conducted, enrolling 414 consecutive patients presenting to the emergency department (ED) within 6 h of chest pain onset. Baseline clinical characteristics were recorded, and copeptin and hs-cTnI levels were measured from the initial blood draw. Patients were followed for 180 days to determine the occurrence of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE). The final diagnosis was independently adjudicated by a panel of cardiologists blinded to copeptin results.ResultsOf 414 Chinese patients, 112 (27.05 %) had AMI. In hs-cTnI-undetectable patients, median copeptin was 290 pmol/L (IQR: 211–547) in STEMI and 130 pmol/L (IQR: 47.8–184) in NSTEMI. DBRS (copeptin/hs-cTnI dual-negative biomarker rule-out strategy) had higher AUC than single biomarkers (P < 0.05), with 100 % NPV (95 % CI: 95.86–100) for chest pain (3/6 h). Elevated copeptin (Hazard Ratio (HR) 3.65, 95 % CI: 2.35–5.67) and hs-cTnI (HR 1.69, 95 % CI: 1.36–2.10) independently predicted 180-day MACE (P < 0.001).ConclusionsIn Chinese patients, DBRS provided a safe and effective strategy for ruling out AMI. In addition, Combined negative results predicted safer 180-day post-discharge outcomes.Keywords: hs-cTnI, Copeptin, Acute myocardial infarction, Major adverse cardiovascular events, Chinese population

Introduction

Ruling out acute myocardial infarction (AMI) remains a major challenge in emergency department (ED). Despite the high prevalence of suspected acute coronary syndrome (ACS) presentations, only approximately 10–20 % are confirmed as AMI (STEMI or NSTEMI), with over 60 % attributed to non-cardiac etiologies [1,2]. To ensure patient safety and prevent missed diagnoses of evolving myocardial infarction, a common practice is to subject patients to 0–1 h or 0–3 h of observation in chest pain units (CPUs), resulting in excellent prognoses but at a high cost to healthcare systems [[3], [4], [5]].

Copeptin, the C-terminal fragment of arginine vasopressin (AVP), is a sensitive biomarker of acute hemodynamic stress and is rapidly elevated in patients presenting with AMI. The synergistic relationship between copeptin and high-sensitivity cTnI/T (hs-cTnI/T), has been demonstrated in numerous studies to provide excellent rule-out capabilities for AMI, with negative predictive values (NPVs) reaching up to 99 % [[6], [7], [8], [9], [10]]. However, the reproducibility of these high NPVs has been challenged by some studies, and the incremental benefit of copeptin over hs-cTnI remains debated [[11], [12], [13]]. Although extensive data exists from studies on European and North American populations [7,8,11], the scant representation of the Chinese population in current research necessitates additional investigation into the clinical utility of copeptin in this demographic, which may have unique cardiovascular risk profiles and distinct patterns of healthcare access.

In this study, we conducted a prospective study to evaluate the safety in patients form Southern China for early discharge following the rule-out of AMI using a single combined assay of hs-cTnI and copeptin at the time of ED presentation. Furthermore, we assessed the prognostic value of initial copeptin and hs-cTnI levels for 180-day MACE in patients with chest pain.

Investigational cardiac biomarker assays

The first blood sample collected within 6 h of the most recent symptom onset upon patient admission was obtained. Sodium heparin-anticoagulated plasma was used for the measurement of copeptin and hs-cTnI. After centrifugation, samples were stored at −80 °C until a sufficient number of samples were collected for batch analysis, which was performed in a blinded manner. Specifically, hs-cTnI was measured using the UniCel DxI 800 analyzer (Beckman Coulter) in the clinical laboratory of Guangzhou First People’s Hospital. The hs-cTnI assay reagent used was with lot number #440340. The limit of detection for this assay was 0.002 ng/mL. The analytical performance of the Access hs-cTnI assay was characterized by a CV of ≤10 % at hs-cTnI levels ≥0.0115 ng/mL and an intra-laboratory SD of ≤0.00115 ng/mL at levels <0.0115 ng/mL. The 99th percentile upper reference limit (URL) was 0.0175 ng/mL. Copeptin concentrations in heparin-anticoagulated plasma were measured using the Thermo Fisher Scientific BRAHMS Copeptin proAVP KRYPTOR assay on the Thermo Fisher Scientific BRAHMS KRYPTOR analyzer. The limit of detection for this assay was 0.7 pmol/L. The 95th percentile URL in a normal reference population was 10 pmol/L [14].

Endpoint 1: Gold standard/adjudicated diagnosis

Every patient experiencing chest pain ultimately received a diagnosis from a panel of at least three cardiologists. The clinical diagnosis was based on criteria established by the European Society of Cardiology [4]. All final diagnoses were categorized into the following five groups: 1) ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI); 2) non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI); 3) unstable angina (UA); 4) cardiovascular disease but not due to acute coronary syndrome (ACS) (cardiovascular non-ACS); and 5) non-cardiac chest pain. Patients with a final diagnosis of possible ACS that could not be definitively determined, as well as patients highly suspected of ACS who did not undergo in-hospital confirmation, were excluded.

Endpoint 2: 180-Day prognostic outcomes

Major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) were defined as the occurrence of cardiac death, recurrent AMI requiring percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), unplanned PCI, readmission for recurrent ACS, significant cardiac arrhythmia requiring intervention, or severe heart failure. Patient follow-up for 180 days was conducted via telephone, email, and review of medical records.

Data collection

Data regarding initial hospitalization and follow-up were retrieved from the clinical physician’s Hospital Information System (HIS) and the laboratory information system (LIS) of Guangzhou First People’s Hospital. Both systems provided access to patients’ electronic medical records. The Global Registry of Acute Coronary Events (GRACE) risk score was collected upon patient admission to facilitate comprehensive risk stratification for both in-hospital and long-term mortality.

All potential major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) were adjudicated by a panel of at least three board-certified cardiologists blinded to treatment group assignment, using all available clinical information. All data were compiled into spreadsheets and independently verified by two reviewers blinded to group assignment to ensure accuracy. Missing data were recorded as “NA” and excluded from analysis.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed data or median and interquartile range (IQR) for non-normally distributed data. Categorical variables were presented as counts and percentages. Comparisons between continuous variables were performed using Student’s t-test or the Kruskal-Wallis test, as appropriate. Categorical variables were compared using the Pearson chi-square test. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and a P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Biomarker data were log-transformed when necessary. Cox proportional hazards regression was used to analyze the impact of risk factors on MACE. The proportional hazards assumption for all variables was tested using scaled Schoenfeld residuals, and no significant deviations were found. Copeptin and hs-cTnI were log-transformed and evaluated in the Cox regression models to determine their association with MACE. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis were performed to illustrate the lower incidence of cardiac death within 180 days in patients with double-negative copeptin and hs-cTnI results compared to double-positive patients. Statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.2.1 and SPSS version 20.0.

Copeptin elevation in STEMI and NSTEMI with non-diagnostic high-sensitivity cardiac troponin I

Median hs-cTnI concentrations at presentation were significantly higher in STEMI (0.839 ng/mL, IQR: 0.05–4.92) and NSTEMI (0.448 ng/mL, IQR: 0.139–1.53) compared to UA (0.0085 ng/mL, IQR: 0.004–0.014), cardiovascular non-ACS (0.008 ng/mL, IQR: 0.003–0.025), and non-cardiac chest (0.003 ng/mL, IQR: 0.002–0.006) (P < 0.001, Kruskal-Wallis). Similarly, median copeptin levels were elevated in STEMI (62.8 pmol/L, IQR: 15.3–261) and NSTEMI (24.8 pmol/L, IQR: 9.05–79.3) relative to UA (8.25 pmol/L, IQR: 3.10–16.5), cardiovascular non-ACS (7.24 pmol/L, IQR: 3.63–23.6), and non-cardiac chest (4.84 pmol/L, IQR: 3.35–9.31). (P < 0.001, Kruskal-Wallis) (Table 1). Copeptin levels were elevated in STEMI and NSTEMI patients who had normal hs-cTnI values at the time of presentation (hs-cTnI ≤ 0.0175 ng/mL, 99th percentile): median copeptin concentration in initial hs-cTnI-negative STEMI patients was 290 pmol/L (IQR: 211–547; n = 13); in NSTEMI, it was 130 pmol/L (IQR: 47.8–184; n = 5). For other diagnoses, in UA, it was 7.81 pmol/L (IQR: 3.05–12.1; n = 17); in cardiovascular non-ACS, it was 5.80 pmol/L (IQR: 3.47–13.8; n = 86); in non-cardiac chest, it was 4.76 pmol/L (IQR: 3.19––8.78; n = 139), (P < 0.001, Kruskal-Wallis). Analysis for diagnostic performance of cardiac Markers

In AMI patients with a chest pain onset of ≤3 h, the ROC AUC at ED arrival was 0.883 (95 % confidence interval [CI]: 0.814–0.951) for hs-cTnI, 0.924 (95 % CI: 0.867–0.980) for copeptin and 0.97 (95 % CI: 0.934–1) for hs-cTnI and copeptin. In AMI patients with a chest pain onset of < 6 h, the corresponding values were 0.907 (95 % CI: 0.858–0.956),0.902 (95 % CI: 0.849–0.954) and 0.973 (95 % CI: 0.948–0.997), respectively. Following chest pain onset, hs-TnI diagnostic performance increases with time, while copeptin diagnostic performance decreases. The copeptin/hs-cTnI dual-negative biomarker rule-out strategy (DBRS) using copeptin and hs-cTnI is superior to using hs-cTnI or copeptin alone in AMI and STEMI (P < 0.05 in DBRS vs (hs-cTnI or copeptin). For NSTEMI patients, there was no significant difference in diagnostic performance between DBRS and using hs-cTnI or copeptin alone (P > 0.05) (Fig. 3).

For AMI patients with initially normal hs-cTnI (<99th percentile ≤ 0.0175 ng/mL), and negative copeptin (<10 pmol/L) (n = 180, [43.48 %]), the negative predictive power (NPV) was 100 % (95 % CI: 95.86–100). Negative copeptin at presentation in combination with a negative hs-cTnI ruled out 43.4 % of all patients without need for a second blood draw. These findings suggest that incorporating copeptin into the AMI diagnostic algorithm may facilitate the early rule-out of AMI.

Post-discharge safety issues using combined copeptin and hs-cTnI

Although the biomarker strategy of an incorporation copeptin-hs-cTnI rule-out of AMI has been analyzed in a number of retrospective observational biomarker studies and our prospective studies results [[6], [7], [8], [9], [10], [11], [12], [13]], rigorous validation is required to ensure patient safety and establish its clinical utility beyond safety. We observed 39 (21.59 %, n = 184) of all patients developed a MACE during the 180-day follow-up (FU) period. Univariate Cox regression analysis demonstrated that elevated copeptin (hazard ratio [HR] 3.65, 95 % CI: 2.35–5.67, P < 0.001, Wald test) and hs-cTnI (HR 1.69, 95 % CI: 1.36–2.10, P < 0.001, Wald test) emerged as the strongest independent risk factor of post-discharge MACE in patients with suspected ACS (Fig. 4). Meanwhile, Table 3 presents a comprehensive MACE information in hs-cTnI-group and copeptin-hs-cTnI group. During the 180-day follow-up period, 1 patient (1.4 %) experienced significant arrhythmia and 2 patients (2.7 %) underwent unplanned PCI in baseline copeptin and hs-cTnI group (41.1 %, n = 74). For either elevated hs-cTnI or copeptin patients (47.0 %, n = 110), 7 patients (6.4 %) experienced cardiac death, 1 patient (0.9 %) had severe arrhythmia,6 patients (5.5 %) had recurrent AMI requiring PCI, 11 patients (10.0 %) underwent unplanned PCI, 4 patients (3.6 %) were readmitted for ACS, and 7 patients (6.4 %) developed severe heart failure. (P < 0.001, Fisher’s exact). Elevated hs-cTnI levels correlate positively with myocardial injury and are strongly associated with MACE [[15], [16], [17], [18]]. In our 180-day follow-up, 10 patients (9.35 %, n = 107) with hs-cTnI levels below the 99th percentile experienced a higher incidence of MACE compared to those with combined negative copeptin and hs-cTnI results. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis showed that the 180-day follow-up revealed no cardiac mortality events in patients with dual-negative copeptin and hs-cTnI results, suggesting a favorable long-term safety profile for this patient cohort (P = 0.008) (Fig. S1).

Discussion

Accurate and timely diagnosis of acute chest pain remains a cornerstone of emergency medicine. Only approximately 8 % of patients presenting with acute chest pain are diagnosed with AMI, while over 60 % are attributed to non-cardiac etiologies [1,2]. Meanwhile, Boeddinghaus, et al. reported that only 6–22 % of patients presenting at an ED with suggestive AMI had mild TnT/I elevation at presentation [19]. Consequently, the final diagnosis require serial measurements and prolonged ECG monitoring. A lot of studies in Netherlands, Europe, United States, South Korea have shown that DBRS can effectively exclude AMI, achieving an NPV exceeding 99.0 % [[6], [7], [8], [9], [10]]. Furthermore, compared to the conventional 0–3 h hs-cTnI diagnostic strategy, the addition of copeptin has been shown to significantly reduce hospital length of stay by 0.9 h (95 % CI: 0.7–1.1), while maintaining non-inferiority in MACE [8]. Our findings confirm and add further evidence to the efficiency and safety of the DBRS seen in these previous studies. Furthermore and in line with previous studies, our results confirm that copeptin is efficient and safe in rule-out of AMI in combination with hs-cTnI in Chinese people [8,10]. We further observed that DRBS exhibited superior diagnostic performance compared to either hs-cTnI or copeptin alone. Notably, the diagnostic efficacy of DBRS was particularly pronounced in STEMI compared to NSTEMI, and its performance was further enhanced within the first 3 h of symptom onset.

Copeptin, the C-terminal fragment of arginine vasopressin (AVP), is secreted in equimolar concentrations with AVP in response to both endogenous and hemodynamic stress. Notably, its predictive utility in various clinical conditions, including the development of heart failure post-myocardial infarction, is well-documented [[20], [21], [22], [23], [24]]. Additionally, the magnitude of troponin elevation at presentation and its dynamic changes reflect the extent of myocardial injury and are associated with patient prognosis [15,16]. For example, recent studies have identified a postoperative increase in troponin I (ΔcTnI) exceeding 40 % of baseline, coupled with an absolute postoperative value exceeding five times the 99th percentile upper reference limit, as the optimal threshold for diagnosing prognostically significant perioperative myocardial injury (PMI) in patients undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) for NSTEMI [15]. Furthermore, prior research has indicated that a copeptin and troponin dual-negative result may facilitate safe early discharge in low-to-intermediate risk patients with suspected ACS [25,26]. Our study demonstrated the both copeptin (HR 3.65, 95 % CI: 2.35–5.67, P < 0.001) and hs-cTnI (HR 1.69, 95 % CI: 1.36–2.10, P < 0.001) were risk factor of 180-day MACE. During follow-up, patients with DBRS results (41.1 %, n = 74) experienced a low incidence of MACE. In contrast, patients with either elevated copeptin or hs-cTnI (47.0 %, n = 110) experienced a significantly higher incidence of MACE, including 7 cardiac deaths (6.4 %), 1 severe arrhythmia (0.9 %), 6 recurrent AMIs requiring PCI (5.5 %), 11 unplanned PCIs (10.0 %), 4 readmissions for ACS (3.6 %), and 7 cases of severe heart failure (6.4 %) (P < 0.001, Fisher’s exact test). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated no cardiac deaths in the DBRS during the 180-day follow-up, whereas 9 deaths occurred in the positive group (P = 0.0084), highlighting the long-term safety of DBRS.

These findings suggest that the initial copeptin and hs-cTnI results are valuable for risk stratification in patients presenting with suspected ACS. DBRS identifies a low-risk population with a favorable prognosis, potentially reducing unnecessary interventions and hospitalizations. Conversely, elevated copeptin or hs-cTnI levels are associated with an increased risk of MACE and cardiac death, underscoring their utility in identifying high-risk patients who may benefit from aggressive management strategies.

Limitations

Generalizability is limited by the single-center design and restricted sample size, necessitating multi-center validation. Moreover, the 180-day follow-up may not fully capture long-term prognostic implications, warranting extended follow-up in future studies. The absence of a cohort with non-diagnostic ECGs and low hs-cTnI, coupled with limited NSTEMI/STEMI sample sizes, restricts the scope of our findings. Future trials should incorporate these patient populations and increase overall sample size to enhance statistical power.

Conclusions

Combined copeptin and hs-cTnI testing safely excludes AMI (100 % NPV) in a Southern Chinese cohort, while elevated levels independently predict MACE and cardiac death. DBRS conferred lower 180-day event risk, supporting the clinical utility of this biomarker strategy for AMI management.

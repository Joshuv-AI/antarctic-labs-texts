Image-based obstacle detection methods for the safe navigation of industrial unmanned aerial vehicles
Authors: Liang Wang, Yin Shen, Haichao Li, Qunhong Shi, Songming Liu, Senyang Chen, Zeyu Chen, Ezzeddine Touti
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractComputer vision is becoming increasingly important for industrial unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) to do real-time object and obstacle recognition while they are engaged in autonomous navigation. Variable texture features of objects, on the other hand, frequently lead to feature disappearance, which in turn reduces the accuracy of detection. This work proposed a novel Texture-variant Obstacle Object Classification Model (TOOCM) for feature extraction and dynamic texture representation. By capturing small texture fluctuations in real-time situations, the primary goal is to enhance the accuracy of both obstacle detection and object classification. The TOOCM model incorporates an N-layer ResNet architecture designed to address the difficulties of disappearing features in an adaptable Manner through dynamic layer augmentation based on variations in texture and size. Reconstruction of each convolutional layer in the ResNet is performed on an as-needed basis, taking into consideration the amount of texture concentration in incoming picture regions. The emergence of identifiable texture regions will result in the identification of new objects, which will then trigger adaptive categorization and layer restructuring activities. TOOCM, in contrast to traditional fixed-layer deep models, offers layer-wise learning updates during the navigation of UAV, which guarantees constant performance in complicated settings. The results of the experiments show that TOOCM achieves a greater detection accuracy of 14.09%, enhanced precision of 14.53%, and a loss reduction of 14.17%, particularly in high-density obstacle settings. These results demonstrate that the suggested adaptive feature learning approach is effective.Keywords: Object detection, Obstacle classification, ResNet, Texture variant, Unmanned aerial vehiclesSubject terms: Engineering, Electrical and electronic engineering, Mathematics and computing, Computational science, Computer science, Information technology, Scientific data, Software

Introduction

Background

Following the advent of industrial unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) technology, there has been a significant improvement in the technological foundation of unmanned vehicle (UV) obstacle detection. Industrial UAVs of today play a crucial role in various fields, including agriculture, surveillance, and disaster response1. These autonomous platforms utilize advanced approaches to detect obstacles, ensuring safe flight and maximizing operational efficiency in unpredictable and complex environments. The detection of obstacles by industrial drones is primarily achieved through the use of computer vision techniques, LiDAR, radar, and object classification systems based on deep learning2. Various lighting conditions, occlusion, interference from the surrounding environment, and a large number of obstructions — both stationary and moving — are the factors that complicate situations like these. The reduction of these complications is necessary to improve the overall performance of the drone, as well as to enhance navigation security and reduce the risk of collisions3. Techniques that have been traditionally used, such as edge detection, template matching, and histogram-based segmentation, have not been able to address the challenges present in industrial settings. Recent developments in deep learning frameworks, such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), You Only Look Once (YOLO), Faster Region-based CNN (Faster R-CNN), and Single Shot MultiBox Detector (SSD), have significantly improved the accuracy of real-time obstacle detection and classification in UAVs4,5. When it comes to obstacle recognition using UAVs, one of the most critical challenges is the vanishing texture feature problem. This difficulty arises when some textures become indistinguishable due to variations in illumination, occlusion, or other factors6. N-Layer ResNet Learning, which incorporates adaptive layer reconstruction methods for updating convolutional layers dynamically in terms of feature concentration and texture variation7,8, is one of the more recent algorithms that has been developed to solve this issue, and this makes it possible for UAVs to detect obstacles more accurately and allows for proper detection even in adverse situations.

With the current state of research, multimodal sensor fusion is gaining increasing popularity. This technique involves combining signals from LiDAR, cameras, and radar to provide greater detection robustness9,11. Deep learning architecture also utilizes attention mechanisms and transformer networks to enhance contextual awareness and differentiate between various types of barriers12–18. Industrial UAV obstacle detection systems are being propelled forward by emerging advancements in deep learning architecture and real-time data processing13. These technologies promise to improve the safety and efficiency of autonomous flight. Object identification in autonomous vehicles has been significantly improved thanks to deep learning algorithms, which have demonstrated increased accuracy and robustness in complex environments. CNN and transformer-based models can extract complex information from sensor input, enabling vehicles to detect impediments in a more effective manner10,15. It is possible to identify the intended obstacle from UAVs based on their structure and texture; nevertheless, the object sensing and texture differentiation processes are becoming increasingly complicated as a result of the displacements of UAVs16. By using these methods, reliance on handcrafted features, which are frequently affected by changes in lighting, weather, and occlusions, is reduced. YOLO and Faster R-CNN were two of the earliest deep learning-based object detection models. These models offered quick inference but struggled to detect items that were small or obstructed17. Combining attention processes with transformer-based networks was used to enhance contextual awareness and detection accuracy18, addressing the issues presented. Hybrid methods that combine recurrent neural networks and graph neural networks can enhance spatiotemporal reasoning, thereby enabling more adaptable obstacle identification19. These advancements lead to continuous improvements in autonomous vehicle navigation, resulting in increased safety and operational efficiency for vehicles. Recalling the discussions thus far, the vanishing point problem is addressed to enhance the accuracy of obstacle identification in industrial UAVs.

Problem statement

Existing models often struggle to properly manage the disappearing feature problem, which arises from fluctuations in object textures, sizes, and shapes encountered during UAV navigation. Although substantial gains have been made in deep learning for object detection, traditional fixed-layer convolutional networks remain incapable of adapting to dynamic visual environments due to their limited flexibility. As a result, these networks are not suitable for reconstructing layers in response to varying feature concentrations. Due to this limitation, the accuracy of obstacle detection is reduced, and the rate of erroneous classifications increases, particularly in visually complex or cluttered scenarios. The research presented here presents a Texture-variant Obstacle Object Classification Model (TOOCM) that incorporates an adaptive N-layer ResNet architecture. This model is designed to address the challenge presented. The model’s structure is designed to be dynamically adjusted in response to real-time texture concentration and feature mapping, enabling it to recognize and categorize new or partially visible objects, even when feature information is restricted or degraded. TOOCM enhances object recognition accuracy, reduces classification errors, and ensures more robust performance in dynamic and unexpected UAV operation contexts. This is achieved through on-the-fly architectural modification.

UAVs, have become indispensable in industrial and surveillance applications, particularly in settings where human operation is restricted or hazardous. The use of real-time computer vision, which enables precise identification of objects and obstacles, is one of the most important factors that makes autonomous UAV navigation possible. YOLO-based models

YOLO-based models and techniques are used in unmanned surface vehicle (USV) object detection processes. Yu et al.20 designed a deep learning (DL) based maritime object detection method to address objects in the images. It is a lightweight detection method that detects objects or things from low-resolution pixel inputs. The technique also addresses unresolved problems that arise during object detection processes. To improve the driving efficiency of industrial UAVs, Han et al.21 introduced an obstacle avoidance method via YOLO and path planning algorithms. The planning algorithm combines the detected obstacle information to get sufficient data for further processes. The technique utilizes multi-lane scenes as input, thereby expanding the accuracy range of the detection systems. The avoidance method predicts the severity of the obstacle and provides a feasible solution to eliminate the barriers during driving. Alahdal et al.22 proposed the YOLO-based object detection method for autonomous vehicles (AV). The proposed method analyzes datasets tailored from AV. Preprocessing techniques are employed to extract the features from high-quality images. The method utilizes the YOLO model to enhance the accuracy and robustness of object prediction on roads. It also elevates the precision and recall ratio of object detection services for AV.

To address challenges in autonomous driving, Cao et al.23 developed the YOLO-based method for small object detection. It is an improved method that incorporates a down-sampling module to analyze the channel features from the datasets. The detection method identifies the small objects from dense or dark input images. The method eliminates computational complexity in object detection, thereby reducing the error rate. The detection method achieves high precision in small object detection. For real-time object detection, Li et al.24 developed a lightweight algorithm for rotational object detection. The algorithm utilizes dense scenes as input, producing significant data for object detection. The detection algorithm uses a feature enhancement attention module (FEAM) to utilize relevant information from the datasets. The algorithm significantly improves the efficiency and feasibility level of the detection systems.

Obstacle detection

Obstacle detection is a complicated task to perform in AV and industrial UAVs. To enhance the safety level of users, Dodge and Yilmaz25 proposed convex vision-based negative obstacle detection for AV. The framework employs a unique stereo-perception approach to assess the intricate nature of dense scenes. The perception approach identifies the key values and components that produce optimal information for the obstacle detection process. The useful information is extracted from the dataset that minimizes the computational latency rate of the detection process. An improved version of25 was developed by Abd Alhattab et al.26 for USV. MOOS-IvP is utilized here to identify the maritime obstacles on the pathways. The developed model employs a real-time fusion technique to integrate data from stereovision systems. The model expands the range of efficacy and effectiveness in obstacle detection for USV. A dual-view colour fusion-based coastal obstacle detection framework is designed by He et al.27 for USV. The framework is used to distinguish the difference between the waves for USV. The hidden factors from the background are identified using the dual-view technique. The designed framework achieves high accuracy in the obstacle detection process.

To enhance the system’s efficiency, Jin et al.28 developed a wide baseline stereovision-based method for obstacle detection. The method utilizes a single-shot multi-box detector (SSD) to detect obstacles for the USV. The method also utilizes DL technology to analyze and identify obstacles based on given inputs. The developed method enlarges the accuracy, quality, and feasibility range of the detection systems. A U-V disparity and residual optical flow-based obstacle detection method is introduced by Yuan et al.29 for autonomous driving systems. U-V disparity images are used here as input, producing relevant information and features for obstacle detection. Autonomous driving systems detect obstacles that can cause severe damage and traffic congestion. The introduced method improves the accuracy, precision, recall, and energy efficiency level of the detection process.

Yang et al.30 proposed a Deepfake detection network for superior generalization. A channel spatial triplet attention network (CSTAN) is implemented in the network to categorize the difference between real and fake features from the images. The detection network extracts local information from the images, producing significant data for the deepfake detection process. The network enlarges the overall accuracy and reliability range of the systems.

A perception module is used here to collect and analyze the abundant features for object detection. The module gathers environmental status and factors to identify objects in surface areas. The developed model improves the precision of the object detection process. The introduced model increases the accuracy, adaptation, and lightweight capability of the detection systems.

Object detection methods are used to ease navigation services for autonomous vehicles. Aulia et al.33 designed a CNN-based object detection model for autonomous mobile robots (AMRs). The model is used to detect and classify objects from real-world datasets. CNN uses a feature extraction technique to extract the essential features from the datasets. The extracted features reduce the computational cost and latency rate of the systems. The designed model improves the accuracy of object detection and classification services.

A feature fusion-based thermal object detection framework is developed by Eltahan and Elsayed34 for autonomous driving systems. The detection framework addresses the issues caused by objects on the surface. The framework fuses the thermal weights to get sufficient data for object detection. It also identifies the objects from low-resolution RGB images. Experimental results show that the developed framework increases the accuracy range of the detection process. An effective relative localization and object detection network (AeroNet) for aerial-ground unmanned vehicles is proposed by Shen et al.35. The model utilizes the DL algorithm to detect objects located on the surface. The model also improves the real-time performance of the vehicles as needed. The proposed model reduces the computational time and energy consumption of the detection systems.

Detection precision

Precision avoids false positives, ensuring that only actual obstacles are classified as such. The proposed method achieves higher precision, reducing incorrect obstacle classifications and avoiding unnecessary detections that could lead to erroneous navigation decisions. Conventional models have lower precision because they rely on static feature extraction, which makes them susceptible to false detections. The proposed method utilizes the N-Layer ResNet Learning model, which leverages feature concentration with texture region analysis and vanishing texture detection. This process enables the system to distinguish between genuine obstacles and background noise. The proposed method refines classification decisions to monitor well-defined textures that contribute to object recognition. The process of updating and reconstructing layers analyzes texture changes and ensures high precision, even in complex environments (Fig. 9).

Assessment of loss

The presence of errors in obstacle classification leads to a loss, with a lower loss indicating a more reliable and optimized model. The proposed method achieves a lower loss when compared to existing models. Traditional obstacle detection models rely on fixed-layer networks and struggle with texture variations, resulting in higher classification errors when the system cannot update its layers during the occurrence of vanishing texture. This leads to an increase in both false positive and false negative rates, which affects the overall model performance. However, the proposed N-Layer ResNet model minimizes loss by updating its convolutional layers and reclassifying the textures based on the output of the previous layers. The lower loss in the proposed method ensures its superior learning capability, which makes it robust in handling diverse obstacles for industrial UAV navigation, even in changing lighting conditions and occlusions (Fig. 10).

To better assess the proposed object detection framework, additional evaluation metrics were considered alongside accuracy, precision, and loss. Specifically, the F1-score and Mean Average Precision (mAP) were calculated across the range of 0.5 to 0.95. These measures are customary in the field of object detection to identify objects and safeguard against false positives.

Recall captures the detection of true positives among all ground-truth objects, illustrating the model’s thoroughness in detection. The F1 score synthesizes precision and recall, thus offering a single statistic capturing both false and true positives and negatives. The evaluation metric mAP@0.5:0.95 calculates the average precision over all valid detections and measures precision at various levels of strictness, focusing on the model’s ability to identify objects accurately. Apart from metric expansion, the listed approaches — YOLOv836, Faster R-CNN37, Swin Transformer38, and CenterNet39, which are considered strong baseline object detection methods — were evaluated alongside the new method under the same experimental conditions. The findings from the ablation studies reveal that all modules have a positive impact on the functionality of TOOCM. Among all the modules, feature density computation has the most significant impact on detection precision, perhaps by focusing on pertinent object areas. Vanishing feature recovery helps retain recall and memory for partially blocked or dimly lit objects. The proposed TOOCM framework encompasses multi-stage computations, including feature density computation, region classification, texture variation estimation, and feature recovery. All of these modules add extra decoupling computation, which May consume resources and hinder real-time UAV implementations. This is especially true in UAV maneuvers within cluttered and dynamic environments. In other scenarios, TOOCM would need to be optimized for real-time operations, including pruning, quantization, or even detection performance.

Although the proposed TOOCM framework generally works well, there are instances where it does not perform as expected. TOOCM struggles especially with very small or densely crowded objects because distinguishing between separate instances in heavily overlapping features with low spatial resolution is not possible. The accuracy of detection often decreases in very low illumination or when there is heavy motion blur, as model features are less representative of the actual scene. Another possible limitation concerns scenes that are very cluttered or overly dynamic, such as aerial views of a UAV flying at different altitudes, which can produce a significant amount of background noise that may lead to either false detection or missed detection of objects. These highlighted failure cases provide insights into areas where additional work is still possible, such as incorporating multi-scale feature aggregation, attention mechanisms, or extreme scenario data augmentation, to enhance robustness.

Conclusion

The proposed Texture-variant Obstacle Object Classification Model presented a novel method for obstacle classification during industrial UAV navigation. The model dynamically builds layers from feature concentration and texture differences by utilizing N-Layer ResNet Learning and mitigating the vanishing texture problem. The feature concentration and its corresponding textures are Mapped to identify the vanishing point problem. The N-layers of the ResNet architecture are adjustable based on the Maximum region detection and texture classification to prevent loss in feature estimation. TOOCM differs from traditional models by providing proper obstacle detection and classification as the vehicle navigates. This model not only enhances navigation safety but also advances vision-based autonomous systems. Further optimization of techniques for layer reconstruction and integration is pursued through multi-sensor fusion for improved obstacle detection in diverse environments. With these functions, the proposed model achieves 14.52% high obstacle detection accuracy, 14.19% high precision, and a 14.33% low loss ratio for the Maximum iteration. The assessment in this study focuses on the image subset of the Lyft 3D Object Detection dataset. While this dataset is large and contains many high-quality annotations, it lacks UAV-specific contextual features, such as views from above, varying altitudes, and challenging weather conditions. To further confirm the generalizability of the proposed approach, future work should also analyze UAV-centric datasets. Such datasets include VisDrone, UAVDT, or even bespoke aerial images obtained from actual UAV operations.

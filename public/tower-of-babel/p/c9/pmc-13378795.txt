Comparison of virtual reality and conventional teaching in dental education: A randomized study
Authors: V. C. Santhosh, Vidhya Baskaran, Chetna Arora, Mushir Mulla, Munaz Mulla, Naina Pattnaik, M. Nazargi Mahabob, Akhil Shetty, Alok Dubey, Kumar Anand
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Dental education is being revolutionized by inclusion of novel technologies such as virtual reality (VR). This technology is gradually becoming part of training program for dental professionals as well as students. The present study aimed to compare and evaluate the effectiveness of VR based education and conventional teaching methods in dental education.MATERIALS AND METHODS:This was a randomized, interventional, and comparative study conducted on 200 dental undergraduate students studying in Dental teaching Hospital and college. The study purpose was explained to all students in English and/or their native language. Determination of sample size was done by using the “Stata” software (version 12) at 95% confidence level and 80% power. The students were enrolled using census method and the selected study participants were randomly categorized in 2 groups comprising of 100 students each (a) Group A students trained with VR and (b) Group B students trained using traditional training method. Study investigator responsible for allocation of sample was blinded by coding. The data collection was performed in two phases wherein 1st phase included designing of an environment based on VR while 2nd phase comprised of implementation and collection of data. “Multi-view” video capture method was used for implementing VR based training system. The observed data analysis was done by Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS v. 19.0; SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) software.RESULTS:Mean ± standard deviation (SD) age of students was observed as 22.92 ± 0.64 years. Mean ± SD student satisfaction score from virtual training method (Group A) was found to be 91.02 ± 7.8 whereas in group B (traditional teaching program), it was found to be 65.02 ± 0.35 which was statistically significant (P = 0.01). No statistically significant difference was obtained on comparing understanding and level of comprehension between both the study groups (P = 0.23).CONCLUSION:VR based dental teaching and clinical training program demonstrated higher satisfaction in comparison to traditional teaching method.

Introduction

Dental health education equips students working in an independent manner as dental professionals by integrating knowledge, clinical skills, and behavior by providing training towards attaining manual dexterity developed during undergraduate program in simulation training laboratories such as phantom head model exercises.[1] Despite various advancements, academic institutions find it difficult to meet advancements in academic syllabi, facing challenges such as ethical concerns, financial constraints, and the need for substantial newer investments and latest innovation.[23] Teaching in dental sciences require use of human samples for example, extracted teeth and bone for the study of anatomical structures however, these involve both ethical as well as logistical issues.[4] Virtual reality (VR) provides simulations which enable students in refining their clinical skills in actual clinical settings.[5] This technology provides assistance to the dentists for planning of surgical procedures in a precise manner thus, providing improvement in patients’ outcomes.[6] VR can enhance patient’s satisfaction by creation of relaxing environments during dental procedures.[7]

VR based systems are currently being used in dental curriculum for enhancement of learning skills. Systems like SIM to CARE Dente and 360° VR videos have received positive feedback for enhancing overall student’s satisfaction on comparison with traditionally available 2-dimensional videos.[89] Virtual platforms dealing with surgical procedures such as 3D Max as well as Unity 3D have demonstrated their effectiveness in training for dental implants and have higher predictability along with operability.[10] Haptic devices such as Freeform can also be used for providing training on dental implants.[11] Voxel-Man simulator can also be used as an important tool for providing dental education.[12] The Denex image guided implant placement has demonstrated faster rate of completion of student assignments.[13] VR can be used in dental practice for addressing anxiety, management of pain, and encouraging cooperation during dental treatments.

VR can revolutionize dental science by raising the level of patient care, academics, and clinical training. Dentists and the educators must be trained for using VR. VR can also be very helpful to the researchers for multi-institutional collaborative purposes while being situated indifferent locations thus promoting large scale and longitudinal studies. This technology can also be used for educating general public regarding oral health and create awareness regarding oral health among children and in specially abled individuals. Therefore, VR should prioritize training and development of essential infrastructure for its implementation in dentistry.[14]

However, traditional teaching methods are still being supported for pre-clinical teaching and training.[14] On comparing traditional learning with virtual learning, feedback can be obtained in faster manner with virtual learning. In traditional method of learning, it is difficult for students to understand their errors and means of correcting them.[15] VR provides real time feedback on parameters such as the accuracy of the procedure, force used during instrumentation, how tools were held (e.g., angle of use) and completion time. This quick feedback loop is critical for learning, as it enables students to know what mistakes they are making and how they can improve their performance in the next trials. For example, if a student applied too much pressure while drilling a cavity in one of the VR modules, the system instantly showed them where they were going wrong and offered instructions on how to drill correctly. This provides a real time corrective mechanism for the student and helps to learn faster which is more beneficial in achieving proficiency as well.

Hence, based upon literature evidence, this study was designed with an aim to compare the effectiveness of VR based education and conventional teaching methods in dental education. Figure 1 showing the theoretical framework.

Materials and Methods

Study design and settings

This was a randomized, interventional, and comparative study conducted on total 200 dental undergraduate students studying in first to final year in a Dental teaching Hospital and college. The study purpose was explained to the students in English and their native language.

Study participants and sampling

The study population included dental undergraduate students (n = 200) studying at place of study. The size of study sample was obtained using the Stata software (version 12) at 95% confidence level and 80% power which came at 100.

Statistical formula used for sample size calculation:

N = z2 X pX (1-p)/e2

N = sample size

Z = z score for confidence level (95%)

P = population proportion (0.5)

E = margin of error

Hence, for increasing the statistical power, sample size was then increased up to 200 students. The students were enrolled in the present study using the census method. The selected study participants were then randomly categorized in two groups a) Group A (Students trained with VR) (n = 100) and b) Group B (Students trained using traditional training method (n = 100). Permuted block randomization method was used for randomly allocating the subjects to respective groups. Students were sampled in a random manner. One of the most important features in interventional studies is blinding. One of the study investigators (allocator) responsible for allocating sample was blinded by means of coding the students. This helped in increasing validity of study results and reduction in bias.

Data collection tools and techniques

Data collection was performed in two phases. First phase included designing of an environment based on VR while the second phase comprised of implementation and collection of data. “Multi-view” video capture method was used for implementing the VR based training system. This system filmed any training procedure at 5 EKEN 4KUHD 60 frames/second in various views. All filmed video demonstrations were arranged using green screen technology and there after fed in a Unity game engine in 3D environment. This enabled the user watching training procedure at different angles in a virtual space in an android system.

Inclusion criteria included: (1) Students pursuing dentistry, (2) those who provided written consent for participating in the study and (3) those students who had an Android smartphone. Exclusion criteria included: (1) unwillingness for participating in the study and (2) those who did not want to undergo training using VR.

Practical skills which were acquired by selected students were assessed in both the study groups i.e. conventional teaching and VR technology using a pre-validated 9-itemed checklist with Cronbach α score of 0.9.

Ethical consideration

Institutional ethical clearance was obtained before initiation of the study from the required Ethics committee (IEC-7824NM).

Statistical analysis

The collected data were analyzed using the Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS v. 19.0; SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) software. Descriptive (frequency, percentage, mean and standard deviation) and inferential (Mann–Whitney U-test) statistical tools were used for drawing inference. P < 0.05 was considered as significant. Average effect size (d = 0.4) indicative of moderate effect was obtained.

Results

Age and gender distribution

Of the 200 study participants in this study, 53% were males and 47% belonged to female gender. The mean ± standard deviation (SD) age (in years) of students was observed as 22.92 ± 0.64.

Comparisons between traditional or conventional teaching and VR based teaching program:

The survey results showed that in Group A, 77% students had complete satisfaction with VR technology. In total, 99% reported high comprehensibility with the study training materials made using VR technology. Total mean ± SD student satisfaction score from virtual training method was found to be 91.0 ± 7.85. On the other hand, in group B (traditional teaching program), total mean ± SD student satisfaction score was found to be 65.0 ± 0.35. 89% responded with understanding and complete comprehension with this mode of teaching. Hence, higher mean ± SD scores were obtained in group of students who were trained with virtual technology when compared with group B students trained with traditional mode of teaching. Statistically significant difference (P = 0.01) was obtained on comparing the student satisfaction scores whereas no statistically significant difference on comparing understanding and level of comprehension (P = 0.2). 92.1 ± 0.45 students could easily understand content through virtual training while 87.2 ± 1.23 undergraduate students found it easy to understand content through traditional learning method however, on statistical comparison, no statistically significant difference (P = 0.3) was obtained. 96.3 ± 1.67 undergraduate students found virtual training a reliable technique in dental learning whereas 92.4 ± 0.23 students found traditional method more reliable which was found to have no statistical significance (P = 0.3). 98.5 ± 0.28 reported virtual training to have more accurateness while 96.1 ± 0.09 students reported it to be more accurate which was found to have no statistical significance (P = 1.0). On comparing comprehensibility, 99.1 ± 0.03 students in group A found the technique comprehensible compared to 98.1 ± 0.07 who found traditional teaching more comprehensible and was found to be statistically non-significant. In total, 96.3 ± 0.18 undergraduate dental students found time management better with virtual method while 87.1 ± 0.56 undergraduate dental students found that traditional teaching managed time better with no statistically significant difference (P = 0.1). 98.1 ± 1.19 dental undergraduates found learning more enjoyable with virtual learning method while 97.7 ± 0.17 found conventional learning more enjoyable. However, no statistically significant difference (P = 0.1) was found between both the groups. In total, 99.1 ± 1.09 undergraduate students found that they could achieve course objectives better with virtual training while 98.1 ± 1.12 found that traditional teaching met course objectives more easily. This was found to have no statistically significant difference (P = 1.0). 92.0 ± 1.09 dental undergraduates found virtual training easy to understand when compared with 92.1 ± 0.19 dental undergraduates who found conventional learning easier to understand. Although, no statistically significant difference (P = 1.0) was found between both the study groups [Table 1 and Graph 1].

Discussion

VR based technology uses simulations in dental education allowing self-assessment, knowledge gain, and clinical skills by students which significantly reduces risks by increasing the safety margin that can have a negative impact on patient if not properly performed.[16] These develop high level of satisfaction amongst students.[17] The VR technology has extremely high potential in providing realistic level of training.[16] Acquisition of clinical knowledge along with practical skills along side an ability towards replicating these acquired skills in a real time clinical environment in dental office settings compose important constituents of VR based clinical training and transfer of knowledge. Various studies have demonstrated positive impact of VR over student knowledge, diagnosis, and practical skills.[181920] Also, while getting trained, a student can simultaneously receive feedback in preclinical settings that enables one to improve their skills.[20]

In the present study, comparisons were made between training programs provided with VR platform versus conventional teaching for dental undergraduate students. On comparing via a “9-itemed” questionnaire, no statistically significant differences were obtained between both the training programs. However, higher satisfaction was noted for training program based on VR in comparison with conventional teaching program. High satisfaction despite no statistically proven superiority of VR technology over conventional teaching can be explained by small sample size and lack of clinical evaluation. Our findings are in contrast to the observations made by Liebermann et al. and Choi et al. who reported significant improvement in clinical skills such as tooth extraction and academic knowledge after imparting training using VR.[515]

Our results demonstrate that the studied population though accepted training with VR, there is no significant difference in acceptance of this technology and traditional mode of teaching. This may be attributed to lack of integration of VR in routine dentistry and educational curriculum. An integral teaching and training program will result in increased interactions resulting in alleviation of patient’s anxiety as well as enhancement of learning process along with improved outcome. Before incorporation of this technology in training and academic curriculum, dental professionals and educators should receive training on VR technologies which will enable them to completely exploit potential advantages of this technique in various clinical fields.[2122]

Koolivand et al. observed that adding haptic technology to VR can improve students practical skills, hand skills, theoretical knowledge, self-confidence, and learning environment.[23]

Cheng et al. investigated the impact of VR simulation training and jawbone models on dental student’s proficiency in periodontal skills and concluded that dental scaling and root planning teaching should adopt a combination of VR and the traditional model.[24]

Li et al. compared the effectiveness of two widely used VR simulators (Unidental and Simodont) against traditional phantom heads for veneer tooth preparation training and found no significant differences in training outcomes between VR simulators and traditional phantom heads for veneer preparation, suggesting that VR technology may serve as a viable alternative or supplementary tool in dental education.[25]

A systematic review in dentistry reported that using VR in prosthodontic education might improve student’s learning outcomes.[26]

This study novelty is based upon the implementation of a multi-view unity-based VR software using green screen capture method which is a technological innovation. However, since technology is not freely available, it is not cost-effective for institutes lacking adequate resources for conducting such programs. Data derived from similar studies conducted in various institutions or different geographic locations could help in comparing outcomes from VR based training in comparison with conventional teaching method.

Limitations

Major limitation of the current study is its small sample size and single institutional study. Incorporation of multiple institutional through collaborations can be done to diversify study participants, minimize study bias, and validation of study findings. These confounding factors can be overcome by involving multiple investigators who are trained in providing education through VR as well as traditional teaching method.

Future directions

Use of artificial intelligence-supported assessment which is algorithm based can be incorporated in these training and educational programs. This will help in providing real time conclusions from such training programs and can pave path for incorporating VR in non-medical and non-dental fields as well.

Conclusion

VR can be used for spreading oral health literacy and awareness among general public apart from training and teaching. However, it has not been included in teaching curriculum of most health sciences. Hence, introduction of health policy which makes incorporation of VR in routine teaching and evaluation programs is required.

Risk factors for venous thromboembolism in newly diagnosed multiple myeloma patients based on thrombosis prophylaxis
Authors: Sen Zhao, Yini Wang, Jing Jia, Qiling Bu, Wenming Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to explore risk factors for symptomatic venous thromboembolism (VTE) in newly diagnosed multiple myeloma (NDMM) patients, with particular focus on the moderating effect of age. A retrospective case-control study was conducted, and clinical and laboratory data from 309 NDMM patients were analyzed. Multivariable logistic regression and interaction analysis were employed to identify independent risk factors and assess age as an effect modifier. Age (OR = 1.060, 95% CI: 1.019–1.102), immobilization ≥ 72 h (OR = 2.835, 95% CI: 1.207–6.662), elevated D-dimer (> 0.55 mg/L) (OR = 2.294, 95% CI: 1.161–4.532), estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) < 60 ml/min/1.73m2 (OR = 2.088, 95% CI: 1.065–4.095), doxorubicin (OR = 4.760, 95% CI: 1.642–13.792), and dexamethasone 160 mg/cyc combined with immunomodulators (IMiDs) (OR = 2.758, 95% CI: 1.197–6.355) were independent risk factors for VTE (P < 0.05). Baseline anticoagulation (OR = 0.209, 95% CI: 0.049–0.896) and antiplatelet therapy (OR = 0.260, 95% CI: 0.132–0.511) served as protective factors (P < 0.05). Interaction analysis revealed that the effects of moderate-to-severe renal insufficiency and 160 mg/cyc dexamethasone-IMiDs combination were significantly modified by age (P for interaction < 0.05). The risk associated with renal insufficiency was amplified in older patients. The 160 mg/cyc dexamethasone-IMiDs combination increased VTE risk in younger patients but showed no significant association with VTE risk in older patients. Age significantly modifies the risk profile for VTE in NDMM. Notably, renal insufficiency poses a greater threat in older patients. Introduction

VTE is one of the most common complications in patients with NDMM, with an incidence of 10–30% [1], usually occurring within the first year after MM diagnosis or at the time of disease recurrence, and is associated with high tumor load, release of inflammatory factors following treatment and the use of chemotherapeutic agents [2–4]. Currently, several risk assessment models, including the International Myeloma Working Group (IMWG) recommendations, the SAVED and IMPEDE score, have been proposed in the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines for VTE prophylaxis in MM patients [5]. In 2022, Chinese experts proposed the VTE risk stratification system for MM patients in China [6], but the clinical applicability needs to be confirmed. The purpose of this study is to identify the risk factors for symptomatic VTE in NDMM population.

Patients and methods

Data source and study population

This study was a single-center retrospective case-control study. Patients with NDMM who received chemotherapy at Beijing Chaoyang Hospital between September 2017 and September 2022 and developed VTE within one year since the start of chemotherapy were included as the case group, while patients with NDMM who did not develop VTE during the same period were randomly assigned to the control group at a 1:3 ratio. Inclusion criteria were newly diagnosed multiple myeloma patients who received systemic therapy for at least one cycle. Exclusion criteria were incomplete data, patients lost to follow-up, or death within one year not caused by thrombosis. The diagnosis of NDMM was confirmed by medical record review according to the IMWG criteria [7]. VTE episodes were confirmed by imaging or angiography. Pulmonary embolism (PE) was diagnosed based on CT pulmonary angiography (CTPA) or high-probability ventilation-perfusion scan showing arterial obstruction, filling defects, or thrombus. Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) was diagnosed by vascular ultrasound and/or computed tomography angiography (CTA) (Fig. 1).

Variable definition and data collection

Baseline data: Included age, gender, body mass index (BMI), smoking, comorbidities (hypertension, coronary heart disease, diabetes, stroke, heart failure, etc.) and physical status (immobilization ≥ 72 h, surgeries within 30 days prior to chemotherapy, pelvic/femoral/hip fractures within 30 days prior to chemotherapy).

Disease characteristics: DS staging of MM, ISS staging, M-protein typing, presence of extramedullary plasmacytoma, and amyloidosis.

Laboratory tests: Fasting venous blood samples were collected in the morning upon admission for complete blood count (CBC), biochemical analysis and coagulation function tests before initiation of any treatment.

Treatment factors: Erythropoietin (EPO), central venous catheterization (CVC), chemotherapy regimens. To evaluate the independently impact of chemotherapeutic agents, treatment regimens were categorized as follows: (1) Doxorubicin-containing regiments; (2) Doxorubicin-free regimens were further classified as: (a) Dexamethasone 160 mg/cyc combined with IMiDs (b) Dexamethasone 120–160 mg/cyc combined with IMiDs (c) Dexamethasone 160 mg/cyc without IMiDs (d) Dexamethasone 120–160 mg/cyc without IMiDs (e) IMiDs without dexamethasone (≥ 120 mg/cyc).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 27.0 and R version 4.5.1. Continuous variables following normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation, with independent samples t-tests for intergroup comparisons. Otherwise, they were represented as M (Q1, Q3), with Mann-Whitney U test for intergroup analysis. Categorical variables were presented as frequency (%) with χ2 tests for comparison, and Fisher’s exact probability method was used for small frequency values.

Candidate variables (univariate P < 0.1) were entered into a multivariable logistic regression to identify independent VTE risk factors. Interaction was evaluated via product terms and quantified on an additive scale using relative excess risk due to interaction (RERI), attributable proportion (AP), and synergy index (SI). Nonlinear age-VTE association was examined by restricted cubic spline (RCS) (3–5 knots, median reference). To confirm this finding, 1:1 propensity score matching (PSM) (caliper value = 0.02) was performed, requiring all SMD < 0.1, followed by conditional logistic regression.

Ethical review

This study was based on existing clinical data and involved no additional interventions or risks to participants. Patient information was anonymized prior to analysis. The requirement for informed consent was waived by the Institutional Review Board (approval No. 2022-ke-493), and the study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Results

Study population and baseline characteristics

A total of 309 NDMM patients were included, with 72 developing VTE during follow-up and 237 serving as controls. The overall cohort had a mean age of 61.2 ± 9.4 years and was predominantly male (62.1%). As expected in an NDMM population, most patients presented with advanced disease (ISS stage III: 51.8%) and received IMiDs-based regimens (74.4%). Prior to chemotherapy, 185 (59.9%) patients received antiplatelet therapy, and 16 (5.18%) underwent anticoagulation therapy. Baseline characteristics of NDMM patients

Compared with controls, patients who developed VTE were significantly older and more frequently had immobilization, fracture, and acute infection at baseline (all P < 0.01). They also exhibited higher baseline D‑dimer levels and lower eGFR. In terms of treatment, the VTE group more often received doxorubicin but less often received antiplatelet therapy at baseline.

Risk factors for VTE in NDMM patients

Univariable analysis showed that age, acute infection, immobilization ≥ 72 h, elevated D‑dimer (> 0.55 mg/L), eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m², and doxorubicin were associated with increased VTE risk in NDMM patients (all P < 0.05). Antiplatelet therapy was associated with reduced risk (P < 0.01). Multivariable logistic regression identified age, immobilization ≥ 72 h, D‑dimer > 0.55 mg/L, eGFR < 60 ml/min/1.73m2, doxorubicin, and therapeutic regimen② (dexamethasone 160 mg/cyc with IMiDs) as independent risk factors for VTE (all P < 0.05), whereas anticoagulation and antiplatelet therapy were independent protective factors (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2). Subgroup analysis and effect modification by age

Formal interaction tests identified two factors whose effects on VTE risk were significantly modified by age (Fig. 3). A positive additive interaction was observed between age ≥ 65 years and eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m² (RERI = 3.21, 95% CI: 0.24–6.19, P = 0.017; AP = 0.74). Neither factor alone was associated with a significantly increased risk (OR = 0.66 and 1.46, respectively; both P > 0.05), but their coexistence increased VTE risk 4.34‑fold (95% CI: 1.99–9.45, P < 0.001).

A negative multiplicative interaction was found between age and the dexamethasone‑IMiDs combination (160 mg/cycle) (multiplicative interaction term = 0.22, P = 0.023). This regimen was a strong predictor of VTE in patients aged < 65 years (OR = 3.80, P = 0.011), but showed no significant association in those ≥ 65 years (OR = 0.83, P = 0.743). Propensity score matching analysis

We performed a propensity score matching analysis, matching patients on key potential confounders: renal function (eGFR) and exposure to the dexamethasone (160 mg/cycle) + IMiDs regimen. After matching 41 case-control pairs, the previously significant association between continuous age and VTE was attenuated and no longer statistically significant (before matching: OR = 1.060, 95% CI: 1.019–1.102, P = 0.004 ; after matching: OR = 1.013, 95% CI: 0.986–1.042, P = 0.350).

Comparison with an established risk score

The predictive performance of our exploratory model (incorporating age-specific interaction terms) was compared with that of the validated IMPEDE score. In our cohort, our model achieved an AUC of 0.773 (95% CI: 0.708–0.837), while the IMPEDE score had an AUC of 0.613 (95% CI: 0.530–0.696). The NRI was 0.358 (95% CI: 0.195–0.521, P < 0.001), and the IDI was 0.155 (95% CI: 0.106–0.203, P < 0.001) (Fig. 5).

Discussion

Venous thromboembolism (VTE) remains a major complication in multiple myeloma (MM), yet existing risk scores are derived predominantly from Western cohorts and their applicability in Asian populations remains underexplored. In this Chinese NDMM cohort, we not only validated well‑established risk factors [8, 9]-such as age, doxorubicin-but also identified age as a critical effect modifier, revealing distinct risk profiles for older (≥ 65 years) and younger patients. The selection of 65 years as a cut-off was further supported by a restricted cubic spline analysis (see Supplementary Materials).

Age is closely associated with the occurrence of VTE, with over 60% of VTE events occuring in individuals aged ≥ 65 years [10, 11]. In multiple myeloma patients, age is incorporated as a risk factor in various VTE risk models. However, limited studies have examined VTE risk stratified by age. The most salient finding of this study is that age significantly modifies the risk associated with two key factors: renal insufficiency and immunomodulatory drug-based regimens.

Patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) are at increased risk of developing VTE. A cohort study by Folsom et al. revealed that patients with stage 3–4 CKD had a 1.6 to 1.7 times higher risk of VTE than the control group [12]. Another study found that, after adjusting for age, the incidence of PE in patients on maintenance dialysis was higher than that in the general population [13]. Consistent with previous findings, this study identified renal insufficiency as an independent risk factor for VTE in NDMM patients. More importantly, this study demonstrated that age significantly alters the impact of renal insufficiency on VTE, with a greater increase in risk observed in elderly MM patients than in younger patients.

Clinical evidence confirms a strong correlation between IMiDs and VTE in NDMM patients, especially when high-dose dexamethasone or doxorubicin is administered, with VTE rates increasing from 1% to 5% with IMiDs monotherapy to 10%-28% with combination therapy [14, 15]. However, it is not clear whether the treatment-related risks vary with age. This study found that the combination of dexamethasone 160 mg/cyc with IMiDs increased VTE risk in younger patients but not in older patients, a reversal that may be partly explained by more frequent IMiDs dose reductions in elderly patients with renal impairment. Further analysis reveals that the risk of combined treatment was independent of cumulative dose (see Supplementary Materials).

Our findings align with global literature on the pro-thrombotic role of inflammation and tissue injury [16–18], as reflected by D-dimer levels. Antiplatelet and anticoagulant prophylaxis were confirmed as effective protective measures.

In an exploratory analysis, a model incorporating these age-specific interactions showed comparable discrimination (AUC = 0.773) to the IMPEDE score (AUC = 0.613) in our cohort. This proof‑of‑concept analysis suggests that age‑stratified risk assessment may enhance predictive accuracy in Asian MM patients. However, this finding is preliminary and requires external validation before clinical implementation.

This study has several limitations. Its single‑center, retrospective design renders it susceptible to unmeasured confounding. VTE events were symptom‑driven, potentially underestimating the true incidence of asymptomatic thrombosis. The exploratory interaction model was not pre‑specified and awaits validation in independent, prospective cohorts.

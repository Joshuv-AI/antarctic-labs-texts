Post-Operative Outcomes at One Year of STREAMLINE Microinvasive Glaucoma Surgery Combining Micro-Goniotomy and Focal Ab-Interno Canaloplasty
Authors: Meghan Sharma, Catherine Johnson, Cameron Michael Carpenter, Blessing S Ofori-Atta, Ben J Brintz, Ashley Polski, Austin S Nakatsuka
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeThe STREAMLINE Surgical System performs microinvasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS) by creating micro-goniotomy incisions in the trabecular meshwork with focal ab-interno canaloplasty of Schlemm’s canal. This retrospective review examines the procedure’s effect on intraocular pressure (IOP) and number of glaucoma medications throughout one post-operative year.MethodsAll cases at the John A. Moran Eye Center with Current Procedural Terminology codes 65820 and 66174 were searched for STREAMLINE cases from October 2021 to May 2024. Eyes were excluded if the case was combined with another procedure other than phacoemulsification, did not include both goniotomy and ab-interno canaloplasty, or was a standalone STREAMLINE procedure without phacoemulsification. Demographic data and baseline number of medications and IOP were recorded. IOP measurements were recorded on post-operative day 0, week 1, month 1, month 3, month 6, month 9, and month 12. Number of medications was recorded post-operatively at 6 months and 12 months. Linear mixed effects models were fit to estimate IOP at each follow-up time while accounting for within-eye correlation.ResultsThirty-nine eyes that underwent the procedure were included from 29 patients of ages 18–86. The average IOP decrease from baseline IOP was 0.68 mmHg on post-operative day 0 (p=0.52), 1.49 mmHg at post-operative month 1 (p=0.16), 1.67 mmHg at post-operative month 3 (p=0.17), 1.62 mmHg at post-operative month 6 (p=0.18), 1.05 mmHg at post-operative month 9 (p=0.36), and 1.87 mmHg at post-operative month 12 (p=0.13). There was a post-operative IOP increase of 0.18 mmHg at post-operative week 1 (p=0.88). The average number of glaucoma medications was reduced by 47% at 6 months post-operatively (p<0.001) and 48% at 12 months (p=0.003).ConclusionIn a retrospective review of 39 eyes at one institution, STREAMLINE goniotomy and ab-interno canaloplasty significantly decreased the number of glaucoma medications one year after surgery.

Introduction

Glaucoma is an ocular disease that can lead to irreversible vision loss.1 Reducing intraocular pressure (IOP) is the only evidence-based approach for glaucoma management.2 This can be achieved by increasing outflow of aqueous humor through the trabecular meshwork (TM) into the Schlemm’s canal (SC) and ultimately into the aqueous veins using medical or surgical management.2–4 Microinvasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS) utilizes less invasive, more physiological, and lower-risk approaches to enhance outflow of aqueous humor, thereby reducing IOP.5 MIGS may include various surgical techniques, such as goniotomy and ab-interno canaloplasty with viscodilation. Goniotomy involves the excision of some or all of the TM to allow for direct outflow of aqueous humor into the SC.4,6 Ab-interno canaloplasty with viscodilation involves inserting a viscoelastic material into the SC that applies tension to the inner wall of the SC and produces distention within the TM, increasing aqueous outflow.7

The STREAMLINE Surgical System (New World Medical, Rancho Cucamonga, CA, USA) is an FDA-cleared instrument that was approved in 2021 for use in mild-to-moderate open angle glaucoma.8 This MIGS procedure involves creating micro-goniotomy incisions in the TM with focal ab-interno canaloplasty of the SC by delivering small volumes of ophthalmic viscoelastic device (OVD) during ocular surgery.9,10 The instrument is single-use, disposable, and can be used as a standalone procedure or in conjunction with phacoemulsification. STREAMLINE Surgical System consists of a polymer handset tipped with a surgical grade stainless-steel cannula that accesses the TM through a corneal incision. The device has a button that can be depressed to retract a polymer outer sleeve to facilitate positioning of the inner cannula into the SC and deliver approximately 7 µL of ophthalmic viscoelastic fluid. Eight deliveries of OVD may be made with a single device.10 The STREAMLINE procedure is novel, and the literature on it is primarily prospective studies that are limited to 6 months follow-up or only one demographic group (for example, only Hispanics in Lazcano-Gomez et al).9–12 We present one of the first retrospective studies on STREAMLINE ab-interno canaloplasty and goniotomy in a heterogenous patient population and assess the procedure’s effect on IOP and number of glaucoma medications throughout one post-operative year.

Methods

All methods were approved by the University of Utah Institutional Review Board and adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki. Because of the retrospective nature of the study, informed consent was not required by the University of Utah Institutional Review Board. The study was HIPAA compliant, and patient data confidentiality was maintained. A retrospective review of all cases under Current Procedural Terminology codes 65820 for goniotomy and 66174 for transluminal dilation of aqueous outflow canal without retention of device or stent at the John A. Moran Eye Center from October 2021 to May 2024 was performed.13 All cases of a STREAMLINE goniotomy and ab-interno canaloplasty procedure combined with phacoemulsification were included. Eyes were excluded if the case was combined with another procedure other than phacoemulsification, did not include both goniotomy and ab-interno canaloplasty, or was a standalone STREAMLINE procedure without phacoemulsification. Demographic data and baseline number of medications and IOP were recorded. IOP measurements were recorded on post-operative day 0, week 1, month 1, month 3, month 6, month 9, and month 12. Patients were examined on post-operative day 0 because the primary surgeon routinely performs same-day post-operative exams for routine phacoemulsification and did not feel that the STREAMLINE procedure added additional risk to necessitate an additional post-operative day 1 exam. Number of medications was recorded post-operatively at 6 months and 12 months.

Patient characteristics were summarized as counts (%) for categorical variables and as means and standard deviations (SD) for continuous variables. To estimate the effect of time on IOP, we fitted a linear mixed-effects model with fixed effects for all time periods and a random intercept for each patient’s eye to account for within-eye correlation. The effect of time following surgery on the number of glaucoma medications was examined using a generalized linear Poisson mixed-effects model with a logarithmic link, including fixed effects for time and the same random intercept structure. In both analyses, each post-surgical time point was compared to baseline. Results were reported as means and ratios, respectively, with 95% confidence intervals (CIs), along with P values. Statistical significance was assessed at the 0.05 threshold, and all statistical tests were two-tailed. All analyses were performed with R version 4.3.0.14

Discussion

We present a retrospective review of all STREAMLINE goniotomy and ab-interno canaloplasty cases combined with phacoemulsification over one post-operative year at one institution from October 2021 to May 2024, which shows a significant reduction in the number of medications at post-operative months 6 and 12. No significant adverse events such as IOP spikes, vision loss, or hyphema were observed, and none of the patients required additional ocular surgery during the post-operative follow-up period. STREAMLINE is a procedure that allows the surgeon to create micro-goniotomies with focal ab-interno canaloplasty while simultaneously delivering a volume of OVD that is similar to or greater than that used by other commercial ab-interno canaloplasty devices.12,15,16 Additionally, STREAMLINE ab-interno canaloplasty does not involve permanent implantation of a device, as some implantable devices have been associated with corneal endothelial cell loss (ECL).12,17 Multiple studies have shown that combining STREAMLINE with phacoemulsification confers no significant increase in the risk of ECL compared with phacoemulsification alone.10,12,18 The absence of additional ECL is a strength of the STREAMLINE ab-interno canaloplasty procedure.

There is limited data on the efficacy of STREAMLINE goniotomy and ab-interno canaloplasty since FDA approval of the device in 2021. Prospective studies by Lazcano-Gomez et al evaluated the STREAMLINE ab-interno canaloplasty and goniotomy in Hispanic patients.10,12 Glaucoma is a leading cause of blindness in Hispanics, with its prevalence approximately twice as high in Hispanics compared with whites.19,20 At 6 months follow-up with 20 subjects who had mild-to-severe primary open angle glaucoma (POAG), they reported a mean IOP reduction of ≥20% from baseline in 89.5% of eyes.9 A subsequent study by Lazcano-Gomez et al included 45 eyes from 45 Hispanic adult patients with mild-to-moderate glaucoma and concluded that STREAMLINE provided significant reduction in IOP and IOP-lowering medications over 12 months post-operatively.12 While these studies concluded that STREAMLINE ab-interno canaloplasty combined with phacoemulsification safely reduced IOP and number of IOP-lowering medications, the results were limited to Hispanic patients, limiting generalizability.10,12 The 2024 VENICE trial was the first randomized control trial to evaluate STREAMLINE, comparing STREAMLINE ab-interno canaloplasty with iStent W (Glaukos, Laguna Hills, CA, USA). Interim findings of 6 months of post-operative data demonstrated comparable IOP and medication reduction between the two procedures when combined with phacoemulsification, but the study examined only the canaloplasty function of STREAMLINE and not goniotomy.11

The present study is one of the first retrospective studies on STREAMLINE ab-interno canaloplasty and goniotomy over one post-operative year and noted significant reductions in IOP-lowering medications at 6 months and 12 months from baseline. A 2025 study by Stanke and Nguyen is one of the only other retrospective STREAMLINE studies to our knowledge. They found significant reductions in mean IOP at 1 post-operative month and 6 post-operative months, but not at 12 post-operative months. They also only assessed patients with mild, moderate, and severe POAG.21 Our study, on the other hand, included a more heterogenous patient population with a variety of glaucoma diagnoses. Since no notable decreases in IOP were noted in our study, this may suggest limited effectiveness of Streamline in other types of glaucoma, though further research is required to reach this conclusion.

A retrospective study, like the present study and the study by Stanke and Nguyen, has strengths, including its long post-operative follow-up period and ability to assess the long-term effects of a procedure in a normal, uncontrolled clinic setting; however, this study design also poses some limitations. For instance, not all individuals included in our sample had data available for each time point, and 18 eyes were lost-to-follow-up by post-operative month 12 (Table 2). Eyes were often lost-to-follow-up because the patients did not come to their scheduled follow-up appointments or came at a time much later than their scheduled appointment at the appropriate time point (for example, a patient came in at 16 post-operative months instead of 12 post-operative months). This study was also limited to cases at one institution, with all the cases performed by the same surgeon, which may limit generalizability. Due to convergence issues with a more complex nested model and limited data from both eyes (only 10 of 29 patients), we used a reduced model that accounts for correlation among repeated measurements from the same eye but does not model potential correlation between eyes from the same patient. Our study population was also primarily White (75%), which also may limit the external validity of our findings. Additionally, some patients had other factors in their treatment that may have affected IOP. One patient, for instance, was given the option to stop using latanoprost since intraocular pressures were decreasing but opted to stay on the medication. Another patient was receiving intravitreal injections during the study follow-up period, which may have increased IOP.22 Additionally, phacoemulsification itself can lead to reductions in IOP.23 Finally, our study did not include a washout period. Studies that have included a washout period, such as the VENICE trial, were able to detect a larger IOP decrease in the STREAMLINE group than in our study. Lack of a washout period is a limitation of our retrospective study and may have contributed to the lack of significant IOP reduction in this study.11 Our study also found a post-operative increase in IOP at week 1; however, this is likely due to other factors following cataract surgery which can elevate IOP, such as inflammation or steroid use.24,25

To date, most studies on STREAMLINE have reported postsurgical outcomes of up to 12 months. Research examining outcomes beyond 12 months is needed to estimate the duration of STREAMLINE’s IOP-lowering effect. Future studies comparing postoperative outcomes between STREAMLINE and other MIGS procedures in open angle glaucoma patients are needed. While a randomized controlled trial is currently underway comparing STREAMLINE vs iStent W, interim data have shown no difference between the two procedures.11 Further research through retrospective studies, prospective case series, and randomized controlled trials may help determine whether STREAMLINE ab-interno canaloplasty is an effective MIGS procedure in reducing IOP and number of IOP-lowering medications.

Conclusion

STREAMLINE Surgical System performs a microinvasive glaucoma surgery that combines micro-goniotomy and focal ab-interno canaloplasty. In a retrospective review of cases of STREAMLINE ab-interno canaloplasty combined with phacoemulsification at one institution over one post-operative year, significant decreases in number of medications at post-operative months 6 and 12 were observed, but no significant decreases in IOP were noted. No significant adverse events were noted in any patients, suggesting that STREAMLINE goniotomy and canaloplasty may be a safe procedure that reduces the number of post-operative medications after one year of post-operative follow-up in glaucoma patients. This study was retrospective in nature, leading to several limitations including loss-to-follow-up and lack of a washout period. Randomized control trials are necessary to further assess the safety and effectiveness of the procedure.

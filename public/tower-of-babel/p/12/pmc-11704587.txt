The relationship of sex and aortic diameter at the time of acute type A aortic dissection
Authors: Lamia Harik, Mario Gaudino, Mohammed Rahouma, Arnaldo Dimagli, Roberto Perezgrovas-Olaria, Kevin R An, Talal Alzghari, Giovanni Soletti, Jordan Leith, Gianmarco Cancelli, Charles Mack, Leonard N Girardi, Christopher Lau
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveEvaluate sex differences in patients undergoing repair of acute type A aortic dissection (ATAAD).MethodsSex-stratified, single-center cohort study of patients undergoing ATAAD repair from 1997 to 2022. The primary outcome was aortic diameter at time of presentation with ATAAD. Secondary outcomes were mortality, myocardial infraction, stroke, hemodialysis, tracheostomy, re-exploration for bleeding, a composite of major adverse events, and long-term survival.ResultsIn 390 consecutive patients (150 women), men were younger than women (61.0 years; interquartile range [IQR], 50-70 years vs 70.5 years; IQR, 59-78 years; P < .001), had higher body mass index (28.6; IQR, 25.1-32.3 vs 25.4; IQR, 21.9-29.2; P < .001), more frequent peripheral vascular disease (11.7% vs 4.7%; P = .03), renal insufficiency (36.7% vs 22%; P = .003), malperfusion (34.2% vs 18.7%; P = .007), and smoking history (65% vs 44%; P < .001). There was no sex difference in median aortic diameter at the time of ATAAD (men: 5.3 cm; IQR, 4.9-6.1 cm and women: 5.2 cm; IQR, 4.6-5.9 cm; P = .12) even when adjusted for body mass index (men: 5.7 cm; IQR, 5.4-6.1 cm and women: 5.4 cm; IQR, 5.4-6.1 cm; P = .19). On multivariable analysis, there was no interaction between aneurysm size and sex (interaction P = .62). Sex was not associated with major adverse events (odds ratio, 0.75; 95% CI, 0.07-7.39; P = .81).ConclusionsThere was no sex difference in aneurysm size at the time of presentation of ATAAD, even after adjustment for body mass index, and no interaction between aneurysm size and sex, suggesting that aortic diameter remains a reasonable criterion for intervention irrespective of sex.Key Words: acute type A aortic dissection, acute type A aortic dissection in women, sex differences in acute aortic dissection, acute aortic syndrome

Central Message

There was no sex difference in aortic diameter at time of presentation with acute type A aortic dissection, even after adjustment for body size, and there was no interaction of sex and aneurysm size.

Perspective

Aortic diameter alone remains a reasonable indication for aortic aneurysm repair, irrespective of the sex of the patient, unless the individual patient is at the extremes of body size.

Acute type A aortic dissection (ATAAD) is the most common form of acute aortic syndrome, and carries a high potential for morbidity and mortality.1 The range of possible sequelae include aortic rupture, cardiac tamponade, acute aortic insufficiency resulting in heart failure, and end-organ malperfusion leading to ischemic complications including myocardial ischemia, stroke, and other ischemic sequelae.1 Without operative treatment, mortality is up to 65%,2, 3, 4 whereas surgical mortality has been 17% to 19% in recent large database studies, such as the International Registry of Acute Aortic Dissection (IRAD),4 Society of Thoracic Surgeons database,5 and German Registry for Acute Aortic Dissection Type.3

Diameter-based measurements remain the only guideline-recommended method for deciding on when to operate on intact aortic aneurysms to prevent an acute aortic syndrome.2 Although other parameters, such as aortic size index, cross-sectional area, aortic length, and aortic tortuosity, have been proposed as potential indications for surgery, none has supplanted aortic diameter.6 However, it remains unclear whether women, who have generally smaller body size than men, experience ATAAD at smaller aortic diameters. In this study, we aim to evaluate if there is a sex difference in aortic diameter at presentation with ATAAD and assessed operative and long-term outcomes.

Methods

We conducted a single-center cohort study of patients with ATAAD. This study was approved by the Weill Cornell Medicine Institutional Review Board (#1607017424; January 9, 2022), and the need for individual patient consent was waived. The Aortic Database was retrospectively queried for all consecutive patients undergoing surgery for ATAAD between May 1997 and September 2022. All patients underwent cross-sectional imaging before surgery, and the maximum aortic diameter was derived from the radiology report on this imaging.

Patient information is entered prospectively into the database at presentation; intraoperative and postoperative data are prospectively entered during the hospitalization. Postdischarge, clinical and imaging evaluations are performed every year or sooner if clinical status requires it. Long-term survival data were obtained by medical record review and/or linkage to the National Death Index (updated November 1, 2023).

The cohort was stratified by sex. The primary outcome was aortic diameter at time of presentation. The secondary outcomes were mortality, myocardial infraction, stroke, new hemodialysis requirement, need for tracheostomy, re-exploration for bleeding, a composite of these of major adverse events (MAEs), and long-term survival.

Surgical Technique

Repairs were performed as previously described.7 Arch repairs were performed using deep hypothermic circulatory arrest with retrograde cerebral perfusion and systemic temperature of 18 °C. Arch reconstruction was performed using hemiarch technique or total arch replacement with island reimplantation or debranching.

Statistical Analysis Plan

Categorical variables were summarized as frequency and percentages and compared across groups using χ2 or Fisher tests as appropriate. Continuous variables were tested for normality using the Shapiro-Wilks test and then summarized as mean and standard deviation if normally distributed, and then compared using the Student t test. If continuous variables were nonnormally distributed, they were summarized as median and interquartile range (IQR) and compared using the Mann-Whitney test.

The association between aneurysm size and clinically relevant baseline variables was explored by a multivariable linear regression model, which included age, sex, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease defined as a prior diagnosis of chronic bronchitis or emphysema), diabetes, hypertension, preoperative renal insufficiency (defined as preoperative creatinine level 1.5-3.0 mg/dL or preoperative dialysis requirement), and left ventricular ejection fraction. These estimates were presented as regression coefficient (beta) and its 95% CI.

The association between the composite outcome of major adverse events and clinically relevant variables, including age, sex, diabetes, nonelective procedure status, New York Heart Association functional class III or IV heart failure, and procedure year was tested using a multivariable logistic model, which also included an interaction term for sex and aneurysm size. These estimates were presented as odds ratio (OR) and 95% CI.

Postoperative survival was assessed with the Kaplan-Meier method. Men and women were compared via log-rank test. Competing risk of reoperation, with death as a competing risk, was calculated using the Fine-Gray method. Data were stored using the Microsoft Access software suite, and statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.2.1 within RStudio (R Foundation for Statistical Computing).

Results

Preoperative Patient Characteristics

From May 1997 until September 2022, 390 patients underwent surgery for ATAAD, including 240 men (64%) and 150 women (36%). Baseline patient characteristics are presented in Table 1. Men were younger than women (61.0 years; IQR, 50-70 years vs 70.5 years; IQR, 59-78 years; P < .001), had higher body mass index (BMI) (28.6; IQR, 25.1-32.3 vs 25.4; IQR, 21.9-29.2; P < .001), were more likely to have a history of smoking (65% vs 44%; P < .001), peripheral vascular disease (11.7% vs 4.7%; P = .03), renal insufficiency (36.7% vs 22%; P = .003), and malperfusion of at least 1 vascular distribution (34.2% vs 18.7%; P = .007). There was no difference in the frequency of nonelective (urgent/emergency) case status (95.0% [228 out of 240] vs 95.3%; P = .99), presence of bicuspid aortopathy (4.7% vs 7.1%; P = .61) or other connective tissue disorders (8% vs 5.4%; P = .42).

Discussion

In the present single-center cohort study, including 390 patients (150 women) with ATAAD, there was no difference in aneurysm size at the time of presentation with ATAAD between men and women. There was no interaction between aneurysm size and sex. Despite differences in age and clinical risk factors between the sexes, there was no difference in operative mortality or MAEs. There was also no difference in 10-year survival between the sexes.

Older studies of the natural history of aortic aneurysms found that the incidence of aortic events increased sharply at an aortic diameter of 6.0 cm.8 However, more contemporary IRAD data have shown that the majority of patients with ATAAD present with aortic diameters <5.5 cm.9 Based on these and other data, professional society guidelines now recommend that at high-volume, experienced centers with multidisciplinary aortic teams, repair should be considered at an aortic diameter of 5.0 cm.2

The use of an aortic size index, or aortic diameter relative to the body size of the patient, rather than aortic diameter alone as the indication for surgical repair has been suggested due to the concern that in smaller patients with smaller aortic diameter at baseline, relative aortic growth is greater, which may place such patients at higher risk for acute aortic events at smaller aortic diameters.10, 11, 12 Others have advocated specifically for women, who generally have smaller body size than men, to have a distinct threshold for repair of aortic aneurysms based on aortic size index.13,14 Yet there are limited data on whether a sex difference in aortic diameter at the time of presentation with ATAAD exists because several recent studies on sex differences in patients with ATAAD have neglected to comment on aortic diameter.15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21 In a study of more than 1000 patients (346 women) with acute aortic dissection from the IRAD, Nienaber and colleagues15 found that women had a higher incidence of periaortic hematoma (27.6% vs 21.4%; P = .03) and complete false lumen thrombosis (17.0% vs 10.7%; P = .01), but not intramural hematoma (11.7% vs 10.2%; P = .49), when compared with men; the authors did not report aortic diameter at time of presentation. In a 2022 retrospective study of the Interventional Cohort of the IRAD, including 2832 patients (969 women), Huckaby and colleagues22 found that women were more likely than men to present with intramural hematoma (19.4% vs 13.2%; P = .011), periaortic hematoma (24.8% vs 18.6%; P = .044), and a complete false lumen thrombosis (17.2% vs 10.2%; P = .032). This study did find a sex difference in median aortic diameter, but only at the level of the sinotubular junction (women: 4.0; IQR, 3.5-4.7 cm vs men: 3.7; IQR, 3.1-4 cm; P = .007). Two recent meta-analyses on sex differences in ATAAD found that women with ATAAD were older, had lower BMI, lower body surface area, and lower creatinine than men, all of which are consistent with the findings in the present analysis, but did not comment on diameter at the time of presentation.20,21

Although a sex difference has previously been identified in outcomes of various types of cardiac surgery, including coronary artery bypass grafting23,24 and aortic arch repair,25 the existing data on a sex difference in ATAAD repair outcomes are mixed. Nienaber and colleagues15 found that of the patients with ATAAD who underwent surgical intervention, women had higher operative mortality than men (31.9% vs 21.9%; P = .013). Huckaby and colleagues22 found that there was no difference in the age-adjusted risk of operative mortality between the sexes (OR, 1.40; 95% CI, 1.00-1.98; P = .53). In a 2015 retrospective analysis of 504 patients with ATAAD (245 women), Fukui and colleagues17 found no difference in operative mortality between sexes (women: 5.8% vs men: 4.5%; P = .646), and on multivariable logistic regression, they found that sex was not a predictor of operative mortality (OR, 0.70; 95% CI, 0.28-1.75; P = .45). The present analysis found no sex difference in operative mortality or MAEs. Long-term survival was also similar between sexes in our cohort, despite women presenting at an older age.

Limitations

Our results must be interpreted considering their context and limitations. This was an observational, retrospective study, and thus selection bias is present. All included patients underwent surgical repair, and therefore the study cannot comment on aortic size or clinical outcomes in those patients with ATAAD who did not receive surgical intervention. Although the role of patient height in aortic size indexing has been debated, height was not captured in our institutional database, and so its role could not be assessed. Baseline patient heterogeneity is particularly difficult to account for when comparing between sexes because unmeasured confounders between sexes persist. The sample size of our cohort (390 patients) may have been insufficient to capture a difference in aortic diameter between sexes. Lastly, these data are from a single, high-volume center and interventions were performed by high-volume, experienced aortic surgeons. Given the well-described relationship between cardiac and aortic surgery outcomes generally,26,27 and ATAAD repair specifically,28,29 our results may not be generalizable.

Conclusions

In this single-center cohort study of 390 patients who underwent surgery for ATAAD, there was no difference in aneurysm size at the time of presentation of ATAAD between men and women. After adjustment for BMI, there was still no difference in aneurysm size at time of presentation, and there was no interaction between aneurysm size and sex. Our findings suggest that despite smaller body size in women, recommendations for aortic aneurysm repair that are based on aortic diameter alone may not require adjustment for sex unless the individual patient is at the extremes of body size.

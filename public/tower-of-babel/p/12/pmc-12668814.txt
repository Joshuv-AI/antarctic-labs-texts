Prediction of obstetric outcome in vaginal breech birth using ultrasound pelvimetry in nulliparous women—A feasibility study
Authors: Massimiliano Lia, Anne Dathan‐Stumpf, Julia Franke, Beatrice Vogt, Noura Kabbani, Holger Stepan, Anne Tauscher
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionMagnetic resonance (MR) pelvimetry is widely used in planning vaginal breech birth and may support women's informed decision‐making regarding their preferred mode of birth. This feasibility study aimed to assess whether transperineal ultrasound (TPU) could measure the maternal pelvis as accurately as MR pelvimetry and thus predict the outcome of vaginal breech birth.Material and MethodsIn this prospective cohort study, nulliparous women with a singleton fetus in breech presentation received TPU for the measurement of the anteroposterior mid‐pelvic diameter (AMD). These measurements were compared with those in MR pelvimetry to assess agreement and reliability. In women choosing to attempt vaginal breech birth, we additionally examined the association between the AMD (adjusted for possible confounders) and intrapartum cesarean section. The predictive performance of the AMD and traditional pelvic diameters (i.e., obstetric conjugate, interspinous, and intertuberous distance) was compared by means of the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC).ResultsOverall, 67 nulliparous women with breech presentation received both TPU and MR pelvimetry, of which 47 chose a vaginal breech birth (30 successful vaginal births and 17 intrapartum cesarean sections). The repeatability coefficients and intraclass correlation coefficient for the AMD were 0.38 cm and 0.97 (95% CI 0.96–0.98), respectively. Bland–Altman analysis between the AMD measured in TPU and MR pelvimetry yielded a mean difference of −0.0052 cm (95% CI −0.066 to 0.056 cm) with upper and lower limits of agreement of 0.48 cm (95% CI 0.38–0.59 cm) and −0.49 cm (95% CI −0.6 cm to −0.39 cm), respectively. In the subgroup of women who attempted vaginal breech birth, AMD was significantly associated with intrapartum cesarean section (adjusted odds ratio 0.25; 95% CI 0.06–0.81; AUC 0.77), while the obstetric conjugate, interspinous, and intertuberous distances were not.ConclusionsTPU can accurately and reliably measure the AMD, a novel pelvic diameter in breech presentation. Importantly, a smaller AMD was associated with an increased risk of intrapartum cesarean section if vaginal breech birth was attempted. Consequently, TPU could represent an alternative to MR pelvimetry and support women in deciding their preferred mode of birth in breech presentation.Keywords: anteroposterior mid‐pelvic diameter, breech presentation, breech progression angle, cesarean section, obstetric conjugate, transperineal ultrasound

Key message

Transperineal ultrasound showed high agreement with magnetic resonance pelvimetry in measuring the anteroposterior mid‐pelvic diameter. This novel, sonographically measured pelvic diameter predicts the risk for intrapartum cesarean section in vaginal breech birth.

INTRODUCTION

Women with a fetus in breech presentation require thorough consultation about risks, benefits, and possible birth outcomes before choosing between a trial of vaginal birth and elective cesarean section. 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5

While there is evidence that magnetic resonance (MR) pelvimetry can predict various obstetric outcomes of vaginal breech birth, other studies could not support its clinical value. 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 As a result, there is no unanimity among guidelines in regard to whether MR pelvimetry should be performed prior to vaginal breech birth, let alone which pelvic diameters to measure and which cutoff to use. 1 , 2 , 3 , 5 Nevertheless, it is plausible that pelvimetry can support women's decision‐making and could thus avoid elective cesarean sections for breech presentation. 4 In fact, one study observed that MR pelvimetry was the second most important factor women relied on when choosing their preferred birth mode. 13

However, MR pelvimetry is not universally available for birth planning in breech presentation, and an alternative method to perform pelvimetry would be desirable. Previous studies have shown that measurement of the obstetric conjugate (OC) by transabdominal ultrasound is feasible 14 , 15 , 16 , 17 and can predict intrapartum cesarean section in cephalic presentation. 18 However, the OC may have limited clinical value in vaginal breech birth, as it only predicted intrapartum cesarean section in newborns with a birthweight above 3800 g. 6 Consequently, a pelvic diameter that captures more information (e.g., sacral curvature and pelvic cavity) could be more useful and, theoretically, measurable with transperineal ultrasound (TPU).

The objectives of this study were to: (1) assess the feasibility of measuring the hereby proposed anteroposterior mid‐pelvic diameter (AMD) by TPU before the onset of labor and (2) examine whether these measurements were associated with the outcome of vaginal breech birth.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

Study design

This was a monocentric, prospective, observational cohort study conducted between January 2024 and March 2025 at the University Hospital in Leipzig. At this institution, vaginal breech birth is offered as an alternative to elective cesarean section, and MR pelvimetry is performed routinely in nulliparous women.

Women were invited to participate in this study if they (1) were pregnant with a singleton non‐anomalous fetus in breech presentation, (2) desired a trial of vaginal breech birth, and (3) had no contraindications for vaginal birth or MRI.

Multiparous women were not eligible for this study, as per our hospital‐specific protocol, MR pelvimetry is not required in these cases. Furthermore, women with successful external cephalic version were excluded from this study.

At their first visit at our clinic, all potential participants were informed about the study and included after giving their informed consent. Participants were excluded from the analysis if either TPU or MR pelvimetry was not performed before the onset of labor.

MR pelvimetry was used as the gold standard to examine the accuracy of TPU in measuring the maternal pelvis. Furthermore, intrapartum cesarean section was the primary outcome to examine the clinical value of TPU when vaginal breech birth is attempted. Additional maternal, birth, and neonatal data required for the analysis were obtained from medical records.

The study protocol was approved by the ethics committee of the University Hospital in Leipzig (406/23‐ek, December 19, 2023) and adherent to the ethical principles for medical research involving human subjects established in the Declaration of Helsinki.

Transperineal ultrasound

For the purpose of this study, TPU was performed at inclusion and always before the onset of labor. TPU was performed in a supine position with an empty bladder. A convex transducer was covered with a sterile glove and placed between the labia majora. The midsagittal plane was localized by visualizing the pubic symphysis, the urethra, and the cervix, and the BPA was measured as described previously. 19

Subsequently, the probe frequency was reduced to increase ultrasound penetration and visualize the anterior edge of the sacral bone. The symphysis was oriented horizontally or slightly obliquely in the ultrasound image, and a line was drawn through its center and perpendicular to its long axis. The distance between the points where this line meets the inner edge of the symphysis and the inner edge of the sacral bone was defined as the AMD (Figure 1).

The AMD was measured three times in three different images by the same investigator during the same examination. If measurements were not possible (e.g., due to acoustic shadowing), the TPU was repeated at a later visit.

All examinations were performed by one of the investigators with at least 3 years of experience in TPU (M.L., J.F., and B.V.), who were blinded to the results of MR pelvimetry.

Examinations were performed using a Voluson SWIFT E8 or E10 (GE Healthcare, Zipf, Austria).

MR pelvimetry and birth management

At our institution, nulliparous women who desire a vaginal breech birth routinely receive MR pelvimetry. The examination is usually performed in the 37th week of gestation using a 1.5‐Tesla MRI system (Symphony, Siemens Healthcare, Erlangen, Germany). Two investigators experienced in the interpretation of MR pelvimetry (A.T. and A.D.‐S.) measured the OC (the sagittal distance between the promontory and the dorsal surface of the symphysis), the interspinous distance (ISD, distance between the sciatic spines), the intertuberous distance (ITD, distance between the inner edges of the sciatic tuberosities), and the AMD (equivalent method as in TPU; Figure 1) using the MR images. Both examiners were blinded to the measurements performed with TPU, the characteristics of the women, and to each other's measurements. For the analysis, the average values of the measurements of the two examiners were used.

According to our hospital‐specific protocol, a trial of vaginal breech birth is offered as an alternative to elective cesarean section if the OC is equal to or greater than 12.0 cm and estimated fetal weight (EFW) is between 2500 and 4000 g. 3 , 14 To ensure a fetal weight within these limits, ultrasound is performed every 2 weeks and EFW is calculated with the Hadlock formula 20 and converted to Z‐scores. 21 Of note, pelvic diameters other than the OC were not taken into account when deciding the birth mode. All women are informed about the risks and benefits of elective cesarean section and vaginal breech birth. If the latter is chosen as the preferred mode of delivery, labor is always attended by an experienced board‐certified obstetrician, who was blinded to the TPU measurements. The decision to perform intrapartum cesarean section is at the discretion of the attending obstetrician and based on current guidelines on the interpretation of cardiotocography 22 and the diagnosis of labor arrest. 23 Specifically, arrest in the first stage was defined as failure to progress despite 4 hours of regular contractions, while arrest in the second stage was diagnosed if vaginal birth was not imminent after 2 hours in full dilation (3 hours when epidural analgesia was used).

Statistical analysis

In a first step, all women who received both TPU and MR pelvimetry were included in the analysis to examine the repeatability and reliability of TPU measurements and their agreement with MRI measurements.

Repeatability (i.e., the variation in repeated measurements made under the same conditions) is described by the repeatability coefficient, which represents the range by which two measurements on the same subject can differ on 95% of occasions. 24 Reliability relates the magnitude of the measurement error to the inherent variability between subjects 24 and was assessed by calculating the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) as previously proposed for when different study subjects have different raters (i.e., two‐way mixed model). 25 ICC values of 0.75–0.9 and >0.9 indicated good and excellent reliability, respectively. 26 The repeatability coefficient and the ICC were calculated by using the three measurements of the AMD.

Bland–Altman analysis was used to quantify the agreement between AMD measured by TPU and by MR pelvimetry (i.e., the closeness of the measurements made on the same subject by the two techniques). 24 , 27 To this end, the AMD values of the three TPU measurements and those of the two MRI measurements were averaged. The bias (i.e., mean of differences), limits of agreement, and their 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) were reported numerically and presented graphically.

Acceptable limits of agreement were established a priori at ±1 cm and were based on previously reported results on the sonographic measurement of the OC. 14 , 15 , 17

In a second step, multivariable logistic regression was used to find independent predictors for intrapartum cesarean section in the subgroup of women who chose vaginal breech birth. All variables with a possible association (i.e., p value ≤0.25) to this adverse outcome were included in this multivariable regression.

Additionally, the predictive performance of the AMD and MR pelvimetry (OC, ISD, and ITD) for intrapartum cesarean section was determined by computing the area under the receiver operating characteristics (ROC) curves (AUC). ROC curves of different parameters were compared by bootstrap resampling as previously described. 28

The sample size calculation was based on the requirements of the Bland–Altman agreement analysis and performed with a previously described method. 29 We estimated the mean difference of measurements to be 0.05 cm (standard deviation ±0.35 cm) with the pre‐defined clinical agreement limits at ±1 cm based on results of a similar Bland–Altman analysis. 15 Under these circumstances, a total of 56 cases would provide a statistical power of 90% (alpha level at 0.05) to detect a clinically acceptable agreement between AMD measurements by TPU and MR pelvimetry.

p values <0.05 were considered statistically significant.

The R Statistical Software (version 4.4.1) 30 was used for data analysis, regression analysis (rms‐package), ICC estimation (psych‐package), ROC‐curve analysis (pROC), Bland–Altman agreement analysis (blandr‐package), and graphics drafting (ggplot2‐package).

This study was reported in adherence to the STROBE guidelines. 31

RESULTS

Overall, 67 nulliparous women with a fetus in breech presentation were included in this study and received both TPU and MR pelvimetry before the onset of labor. The AMD could be measured in all cases (in three cases, a second attempt was necessary). A total of 47 women attempted vaginal breech birth. Of these, 30 (64%) gave birth vaginally (17 requiring obstetric maneuvers, 13 without manual assistance) and 17 (36%) needed intrapartum cesarean section (13 due to arrest of labor, three due to non‐reassuring fetal heart rate, and one due to HELLP syndrome diagnosed during labor). Sonographic measurement of the anteroposterior mid‐pelvic diameter

The median AMD measured by TPU was 12.9 cm (IQR 12.5–13.4 cm) and significantly associated with maternal height (p = 0.0022) and the OC (p < 0.0001); however, not with pre‐pregnancy BMI (p = 0.45), the ISD (p = 0.75), or the ITD (p = 0.61).

The repeatability coefficient and ICC of the AMD measured by TPU were 0.38 cm and 0.97 (95% CI 0.96–0.98), respectively.

The Bland–Altman agreement analysis between the AMD measured by TPU and its equivalent measured in MRI yielded a bias of −0.0052 cm (95% CI −0.066 to 0.056 cm) with limits of agreement ranging between 0.48 cm (95% CI 0.38–0.59 cm) and −0.49 cm (95% CI −0.6 cm to −0.39 cm). Visual inspection of the plotted differences showed no heteroscedasticity (Figure 3). The correlation between AMD values measured by TPU and MR pelvimetry is shown in Figure 4.

Prediction of intrapartum cesarean section

In the subgroup of women attempting vaginal breech birth, a wider AMD was independently associated with a lower risk for the need of intrapartum cesarean section (adjusted odds ratio per 1 cm increase: 0.25; 95% CI 0.06–0.81; p = 0.034; Table 2). In particular, we observed that the rate of successful vaginal breech births was 83.3% when the AMD ≥13.5 cm, while it was 25% when the AMD was <12.5 cm (Figure 5). In contrast, the BPA (p = 0.79), the OC (p = 0.25), the ISD (p = 0.87), the ITD (p = 0.53), and other maternal factors were not associated with intrapartum cesarean section (Table 2).

Of the parameters measured in TPU and MR pelvimetry, the AMD showed the highest predictive performance for intrapartum cesarean section in women attempting vaginal breech birth (Figure 6). When comparing ROC curves, the AMD (AUC 0.77; 95% CI, 0.62–0.92) predicted intrapartum cesarean section significantly better than the OC (AUC 0.6; 95% CI 0.43–0.77; p = 0.04), the ISD (AUC 0.48; 95% CI 0.32–0.66; p = 0.01), and the ITD (AUC 0.56; 95% CI 0.39–0.72; p = 0.03).

DISCUSSION

There are three main findings in this study. First, TPU before the onset of labor can accurately and reliably measure the AMD (a sagittal mid‐pelvic diameter) in breech presentation. Second, this sonographically measured AMD was associated with intrapartum cesarean section in nulliparous women attempting vaginal breech birth. Third, the AMD had a significantly higher AUC for the prediction of intrapartum cesarean section compared with traditional pelvic diameters (i.e., OC, ISD, and ITD) assessed by MR pelvimetry.

The AMD is a novel, sonographically assessed pelvic diameter, which can be measured with excellent reliability and precision. With an ICC of 0.97, its reliability is equivalent to that of the measurement of OC by transabdominal ultrasound (ICC: 0.97) 15 or by MR pelvimetry (ICC: 0.97). 10 In our view, the AMD is also measurable with high precision using TPU, as repeated measurements fell within a 0.38 cm range with a 95% probability (i.e., the repeatability coefficient).

Additionally, we show that there is sufficient agreement between the AMD measured in TPU and its anatomical equivalent measured in MRI, which represents the gold standard of pelvimetry. The limits of agreement (including their 95% CI) calculated by our Bland–Altman analysis were clearly within the acceptable limits of ±1 cm, which were set a priori for this study (Figure 3). Furthermore, agreement parameters yielded by our analysis were better than those reported for the measurement of the OC using transabdominal ultrasound, 14 , 15 , 17 suggesting that TPU may be more accurate in evaluating the female pelvis. Altogether, this study shows that the measurement of the AMD by TPU before the onset of labor is feasible and highly reliable in breech presentation.

Importantly, we also show that the AMD was significantly associated with the need for intrapartum cesarean section in nulliparous women attempting vaginal breech birth. In fact, the AMD, measured by TPU (AUC 0.77; 95% CI, 0.62–0.92), was superior to both the OC (AUC 0.6; 95% CI 0.43–0.77), the ISD (AUC 0.48; 95% CI 0.32–0.66), and the ITD (AUC 0.56; 95% CI 0.39–0.72) in predicting this adverse obstetric outcome (Figure 6). Although previous research has shown promising results for the predictive ability of the OC 8 , 9 and the ISD, 10 several studies could not replicate any association between these two diameters and obstetric interventions in vaginal breech birth. 7 , 10 , 11 , 12 Additionally, a previous study at our institution argued that OC only predicts the need for intrapartum cesarean section if the newborn weighs more than 3800 g. 6

Thorough counseling is essential to support women in choosing their preferred mode of breech birth. Crucially, there is evidence that MR pelvimetry plays a major role in the process of women's informed decision‐making. 13 However, this examination is neither universally accessible nor recommended by national guidelines on the management of breech presentation. 1 , 2 , 3 , 5 TPU may represent an accessible, low‐cost alternative to MR pelvimetry, as we demonstrated that this ultrasound technique can reliably and accurately measure the maternal pelvis in breech presentation. This technique can easily be incorporated in the ultrasound examination for fetal weight estimation before labor, while any other form of pelvimetry inevitably causes additional costs. Importantly, TPU could be more informative than MR pelvimetry when planning a vaginal breech birth, as the AMD (measured sonographically) showed a significantly higher AUC than the OC, the ISD, and the ITD in predicting intrapartum cesarean section. Nevertheless, further research is needed to confirm these results and establish how TPU could be implemented in the management of breech presentation. As it has been proposed for cephalic presentation, 18 a combination of pelvic diameters and fetometric parameters could support women in deciding their preferred mode of breech birth. Additionally, chances for successful vaginal breech birth could be increased with early induction of labor in some cases, and TPU could support how women with a high risk of intrapartum cesarean section are identified.

The limitations of this study must be addressed. First, a cohort of 47 women is too small to analyze uncommon outcomes (e.g., serious neonatal morbidity) or to integrate the AMD into a validated multivariable prediction model, which would be desirable for its clinical application. Additionally, the reported odds ratios must be interpreted with caution, as the limited number of cases can lead to overestimation of the effect size that the AMD has on the risk of intrapartum cesarean section. 32 Second, this study was conducted at a single site, and TPU measurements were performed by three investigators. Furthermore, multiparous women and those with successful external cephalic version were excluded from this study. This limits the conclusions that this study can draw on the generalizability of TPU measurements and their predictive value in vaginal breech birth. Third, this study did not compare measurements between different raters. However, we compared sonographic measurements to the gold standard of pelvimetry, which is more relevant to assess the accuracy of a novel technique of measurement. Moreover, the ICC formula used in this analysis (i.e., two‐way mixed model) accounts for the situation where different subjects are measured by different investigators. 25 Fourth, the comparison between the AMD and the OC should be interpreted with particular caution, as the OC itself heavily influenced the decision to attempt a vaginal breech birth. Lastly, we observed that visualization of the sacral bone's inner edge was not possible in three cases (4.5% of the study cohort), possibly due to acoustic shadowing of either the fetus or the rectum content. Although measurements could be performed at a later visit in all three cases, this may represent a limitation of TPU in the evaluation of the female pelvis.

Nonetheless, this study deserves merit for several reasons. First, it is a prospective study in which investigators performing TPU and those evaluating MR pelvimetries were blinded to each other's measurements. Furthermore, the obstetric team in the labor ward was unaware of the measurements performed for the purpose of this study (i.e., the AMD). Second, all participants received an MR pelvimetry in addition to TPU. To the best of our knowledge, this makes this study the largest to examine the accuracy of sonographic pelvimetry by comparing it to an established gold standard (i.e., MR or X‐ray pelvimetry). 14 , 16 , 17 Thirdly, the robust study design in which acceptable limits of agreement and necessary cohort size were established a priori is considered to be essential in method comparison studies, but regrettably is often neglected. 33

CONCLUSION

In summary, the uniqueness of this study is highlighted by the introduction of TPU as a method to evaluate the female pelvis in breech presentation. This is accomplished by assessing the AMD, which can be measured reliably and accurately through this technique before the onset of labor. Importantly, we provide evidence that the AMD (measured by TPU) could be a potential predictor for intrapartum cesarean section when vaginal breech birth is attempted. As a result, TPU has the potential to become an accessible and low‐cost alternative to MR pelvimetry and could support the consultation of women with a fetus in breech presentation at term.

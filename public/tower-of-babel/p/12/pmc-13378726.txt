The complete chloroplast genome sequence of Mentha sachalinensis (Briq.) Kudô (Lamiaceae)
Authors: Yuan Yuan Zhu, Yifan Yu, Jingqiong Wan, Xiaolan Li, Cai Zhang, Runting Yin, Zhen Ouyang, Yuan Wei, Zhongyi Hua
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMentha sachalinensis (Briq.) Kudô is a potentially valuable aromatic plant in the Lamiaceae. Here, we assembled and characterized its complete chloroplast genome. The genome is 152,159 bp long and has a typical quadripartite structure, comprising a 83,293 bp large single-copy (LSC) region, a 17,668 bp small single-copy (SSC) region , and two 25,599 bp inverted repeats (IRs). The overall GC content is 36.99%. A total of 130 genes were annotated, including 85 protein-coding genes, 37 tRNA genes, and eight rRNA genes. This genome constitutes a valuable genomic resource for species identification, phylogenetic reconstruction, and evolutionary analyses within the genus Mentha.Keywords: Lamiaceae, Mentha sachalinensis, aromatic plants, phylogenetic analysis

Introduction

The genus Mentha is taxonomically complex because extensive hybridization, polyploidization, and discordance between molecular and morphological patterns complicate species delimitation. Recent plastome analyses have improved phylogenetic resolution and identified hypervariable regions potentially useful for species discrimination (Li et al. 2020; Soorni and Golchini 2025). Integrating morphometric analyses, chloroplast phylogeny, and whole-genome data from over 300 museum specimens, Tyler et al. (2026) recognized 13 species, three subspecies, and 15 hybrid combinations.

However, these studies did not include Mentha sachalinensis (Briq.) Kudô 1921. M. sachalinensis is a perennial herb, naturally distributed across China, the Russian Far East, and northern Japan. It typically inhabits riversides, lakesides, and moist meadows at 170–1100 m (Li and Hedge 1994). Rich in volatile oils (Rohloff 2002), M. sachalinensis has previously been studied for chemotypic variation (Chou et al. 1995) and foliar glandular hair morphology (Yu et al. 1997). Although no difference in foliar glandular hairs was observed between M. sachalinensis and Mentha canadensis L. 1753 (Yu et al. 1997), they differ in diagnostic macromorphological traits: M. sachalinensis has exserted stamens and leaves commonly exceeding 5 cm, whereas M. canadensis has included stamens and leaves no longer than 5 cm (Li and Hedge 1994). Nevertheless, molecular information on M. sachalinensis remains limited, and its complete chloroplast genome has not been reported.

To clarify its phylogenetic position, we assembled and characterized the complete chloroplast genome of M. sachalinensis and compared it with those of other Mentha species.

Materials and methods

A mature individual of M. sachalinensis was collected from Huludao, Liaoning Province, China (40.57°N, 119.76°E) (Figure 1), and subsequently transplanted to the herbal garden at Jiangsu University. The specimen was identified based on diagnostic morphological traits, including leaves exceeding 5 cm in length (Figure 1A) and stamens clearly exserted from the corolla (Figure 1B). Fresh leaves were harvested from this individual for genomic analysis. A voucher specimen was deposited at the Herbarium of Medicinal Plants, School of Pharmacy, Jiangsu University (voucher no. JSU-2025-MSH009; contact: Z.Y. Hua, hzy@ujs.edu.cn).

Plant morphological characteristics of M. sachalinensis. (A) Plant collected in Huludao, China. (B) Flowers produced after transplantation, showing exserted stamens. Photographs by Zhongyi Hua.

Total genomic DNA was extracted from 100 mg of fresh leaf tissue using the cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) method (Doyle and Doyle 1987). Paired-end libraries with an average insert size of 350 bp were prepared using the NEBNext® Ultra™ DNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina® (NEB, USA), following the manufacturer’s protocol. High-throughput sequencing was carried out on the Illumina NovaSeq X Plus platform, generating approximately 11.75 Gb of 150 bp paired-end reads.

Raw sequence data were quality-filtered using fastp, yielding 11.65 Gb of clean reads for subsequent assembly (Chen et al. 2018). The chloroplast genome was de novo assembled using GetOrganelle v1.7.7.1 (Jin et al. 2020). To assess genome coverage, paired-end reads were mapped to the assembled chloroplast genome using BWA-MEM, and per-site depth was calculated using ‘samtools depth-a’. Genome annotation was performed with both CPGAVAS2 (Shi et al. 2019) and PGA (Qu et al. 2019). The circular genome map was generated using OGDRAW (Greiner et al. 2019).

To infer the phylogenetic position of M. sachalinensis, the complete chloroplast genomes of eight other species within the genus Mentha were retrieved from NCBI. Clinopodium abyssinicum and Monarda didyma were selected as outgroup taxa. PhyloSuite (Zhao et al. 2025) was used to identify and extract 80 protein-coding genes shared by all 11 chloroplast genomes. Each gene was aligned separately using MACSE (Ranwez et al. 2018), and the resulting alignments were concatenated. A maximum-likelihood (ML) phylogenetic tree was constructed using IQ-TREE (Nguyen et al. 2015) with 1,000 bootstrap replicates and the TPM3u + F + I substitution model as determined by ModelFinder (Kalyaanamoorthy et al. 2017).

Results

The chloroplast genome of M. sachalinensis was assembled with a total length of 152,159 bp (Figure 2), with an average sequencing depth of 2,784.32× and 100% genome coverage (Figure S1). The M. sachalinensis chloroplast genome displays a conserved quadripartite architecture, comprising a pair of inverted repeats (IRs) of 25,599 bp each, which separate the large single-copy (LSC, 83,293 bp) and small single-copy (SSC, 17,668 bp) regions. The overall GC content was 36.99%, with regional GC contents of 43.02% in the IRs, 35.86% in the LSC, and 31.91% in the SSC.

Circular map of the M. sachalinensis chloroplast genome. Genes are shown around the outer circle and color-coded by functional category. The inner circle indicates the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions, and the gray plot represents GC content.

Genome annotation identified 130 genes in the plastome, comprising 85 protein-coding genes, 37 tRNA genes, and eight rRNA genes, with 17 genes duplicated in the IR regions, including six protein-coding genes (ndhB, rpl2, rpl23, rps7, rps12, ycf15), seven tRNA genes (trnA-UGC, trnE-UUC, trnL-CAA, trnM-CAU, trnN-GUU, trnR-ACG, trnV-GAC), and four rRNA genes (rrn5, rrn16, rrn4.5, rrn23). With respect to intron organization, ten protein-coding genes (ndhA, ndhB, petB, petD, atpF, rpl2, rpl16, rps16, rpoC1, ycf3) and six tRNA genes (trnA-UGC, trnE-UUC, trnK-UUU, trnL-UAA, trnT-CGU, trnV-UAC) each harbor a single intron (Figure S2). In addition, clpP and rps12 each contain two introns, with rps12 being trans-spliced across the LSC and IR regions to produce two transcripts (Figure S3). Overall, the gene content and intron organization of the M. sachalinensis chloroplast are similar to other Mentha chloroplasts, such as M. canadensis (Li et al. 2020), M. longifolia (Vining et al. 2017), and M. × verticillata (Zubair Filimban et al. 2022).

Phylogenetic analysis based on 80 protein-coding genes shared across all sampled chloroplasts placed M. sachalinensis as sister to M. canadensis with 100% bootstrap support (Figure 3). The remaining Mentha taxa formed a distinct clade, with M. longifolia placed separately and three well-supported sister pairs resolved: M. pulegium-M. suaveolens, M. spicata-M. × verticillata, and M. × piperita-M. × villosa. These results indicate the close evolutionary relationship between M. sachalinensis and M. canadensis and clarify the phylogenetic relationships among the examined Mentha taxa.

Discussion and conclusions

In this study, we assembled the complete chloroplast genome of M. sachalinensis. Phylogenetic analysis recovered a well-supported sister relationship between M. sachalinensis and M. canadensis. This separation is consistent with their diagnostic morphology: M. sachalinensis has stamens clearly exserted from the corolla and leaves commonly exceed 5 cm, whereas M. canadensis has included stamens and leaves no longer than 5 cm (Li and Hedge 1994). Together, phylogenetic and morphological evidence supports their recognition as distinct species.

The overall GC content of M. sachalinensis (36.99%) represents the lowest value reported to date among Mentha chloroplast genomes, slightly below the core range (37.8%–38.0%) of other species in the genus (Vining et al. 2017; Li et al. 2020). Nevertheless, this value remains within the broader variation range observed across Lamiaceae, suggesting that it reflects minor interspecific variation rather than an anomalous departure from the conserved nucleotide composition of the genus. The M. sachalinensis chloroplast characterized in this study contains 130 genes (85 protein-coding, 37 tRNA, and eight rRNA), a composition broadly consistent with that reported for other congeners such as Mentha × verticillata (133 genes) (Zubair Filimban et al. 2022) and M. canadensis (134 genes) (Li et al. 2020). The minor differences in gene number likely reflect variation in gene duplication at the IR-SC boundary regions rather than genuine gene loss or gain.

These findings provide a genomic resource for Mentha. Nuclear genomic data, population-level sampling, and morphometric analyses are needed to further clarify species boundaries within this reticulate genus.

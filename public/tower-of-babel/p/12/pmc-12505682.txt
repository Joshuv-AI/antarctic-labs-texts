Digital pathology enabling lean management of HER2/neu testing in breast Cancer
Authors: Aishwarya Sharma, Prarthna Shah, Manali Ranade, Trupti Pai, Ayushi Sahay, Asawari Patil, Tanuja Shet, Heena Gupta, Devika Chauhan, Puneet Somal, Sankalp Sancheti, Sangeeta Desai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionInvasive breast carcinomas with an equivocal result of HER2/neu on immunohistochemistry (IHC) are reflex tested by fluorescent in situ hybridisation (FISH). Molecular testing is often not available in rural laboratories, from where it is routinely outsourced to central laboratories. Histopathology review (HPR) and IHC analysis of the tissue sample is thus repeated at the central laboratory before molecular testing which increases the turnaround time (TAT) and cost incurred by both the patient and the hospital. We aimed to assess the reduction in TAT and the cost effectiveness after introducing Digital Pathology (DP).MethodsThe tumours with equivocal HER2/neu results were outsourced for FISH from HBCH, Sangrur (rural laboratory) to Molecular Pathology Laboratory, TMH, Mumbai (central laboratory). The Haematoxylin-Eosin (HE) and IHC slides of 47 cases were virtually shared after scanning by Philips SG 60 digital slide scanner. Paraffin blocks of these cases were sent for FISH testing only. TAT of these prospectively shared cases were compared with a retrospective cohort in which virtual slides were not available. The cost benefits were also assessed.ResultsWith the availability of DP, we were able to obviate repeat IHC testing. We were able to achieve a 43.9 % reduction in the TAT (15.65 days to 8.775 days). We also achieved a 30 % reduction in cost.ConclusionThis is a prototype study highlighting the utility of DP in the lean management of HER2/neu testing. The integration of DP in the referral process reduces the TAT and expenditure optimizing resource utilisation.Keywords: Digital pathology, Molecular pathology, Turnaround time

Introduction

Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2/neu) is an important prognostic and predictive biomarker in primary and metastatic breast cancer. HER2/neu overexpression is seen in 15 % of breast carcinomas and is associated with poor prognosis.1 The protein expression is tested by immunohistochemistry (IHC) and gene amplification by in-situ hybridization. IHC is the screening test to evaluate HER2/neu protein overexpression, whereas fluorescent in situ hybridisation (FISH) is used to confirm the HER2/neu gene amplification status in cases with equivocal IHC results (Score 2+) as per the ASCO-CAP guidelines 2018 for invasive breast carcinoma (IBC).2 The IHC assessment can be constrained by pre-analytical factors like fixation that are frequently unpredictable in referred specimens.3 Tumour tissue with an equivocal expression of HER2/neu on IHC (Score 2+) is reflex tested for gene amplification.4 Assessment of gene amplification by ISH is often not feasible in a rural laboratory. Establishing a molecular laboratory in a remote, rural location poses significant challenges. It is resource-intensive, necessitating specialized personnel, incurring higher reagent expenditures, and carries a heightened risk of test failure. FISH is considered to be the gold standard for testing HER2/neu due to its high sensitivity and specificity. However, it is a cumbersome technique with a steep learning curve and involves the use of expensive equipment including the fluorescent microscope.5,6 Due to these factors, molecular tests are often outsourced from rural laboratories to the larger reference laboratories.

In our routine practice, molecular tests which are not performed at the sister institutions (spokes) are performed by Molecular Pathology Laboratory, Tata Memorial Hospital (TMH), Mumbai (hub). Paraffin blocks are sent from the sister institutions for histopathological review (HPR), IHC and FISH. HPR and IHC analysis of the tissue sample is thus repeated before molecular testing which further increases the turnaround time (TAT) and cost incurred by both the patient and the hospital.

It is now well established that virtual slides shared between multiple pathologists help obtain second opinions and can be effectively utilized to obviate additional testing. Utility of whole slide imaging (WSI) in extending quality diagnostic services to remote/underserved regions, second opinion, seeking expert consultations in subspecialties, peer review, tumour boards, education, research and quality assurance is proven beyond contention. Adoption of digital pathology (DP) into routine diagnostics promises improved turnaround time (TAT) with efficient surgical pathology workflow, easy archival and retrieval of diagnostic material.7, 8, 9

We planned to circumvent excess time and cost incurred by employing DP. By sharing virtual slides, we planned to forgo the need for repeat IHC testing, allowing the cases to be directly forwarded for molecular evaluation. Before establishing such a workflow, it was essential to test the robustness of the IHC setup in the rural laboratory. Our rural laboratory's IHC results were consistently reliable, as demonstrated through participation in External Quality Assurance Schemes – National Cancer Grid EQAS (NCGEQAS).10,11 We also tested the concordance of HER2 IHC results in our retrospective cohort and compared the TAT of this cohort with our prospective cohort in which DP was deployed. We hypothesized that the implementation of DP would significantly reduce TAT compared to the conventional method and would also result in financial benefit.

Thus, we aimed to utilise DP to reduce the TAT and expenditure of the molecular cases that were routinely sent from Homi Bhabha Cancer Hospital (HBCH), Sangrur (the spoke, rural laboratory), to the central laboratory (TMH, Mumbai) for further molecular analysis.

Material and methods

This study was conducted as an observational study comparing TAT between two cohorts of breast carcinoma cases with equivocal HER2/neu IHC expression: a retrospective cohort and a prospectively collected cohort in which DP was implemented. The project was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee.

At our rural laboratory, the breast carcinoma cases are routinely evaluated by.

using anti-HER-2/neu (4B5) Rabbit Monoclonal antibody on a Ventana autoimmunostainer (Ventana Medical systems, AZ, USA) in HBCH, Sangrur. The tumours with an equivocal HER2/neu expression were outsourced for molecular analysis to Molecular Pathology Laboratory, TMH, Mumbai. Paraffin blocks and requisition forms of these cases were sent through a courier service that took 3–4 days. Prior to the deployment of DP, these cases were initially sent to the Histopathology laboratory and after confirming their equivocal nature by IHC, the cases were taken up for molecular testing. Study sample

Retrospective cohort

A retrospective analysis of the previously sent cases for molecular testing from 1st January 2022 to 31st December 2022 was undertaken. The TAT of these cases, defined as the point of case accession at TMH to the time of reported diagnosis, was calculated. HER2/neu IHC concordance between our rural laboratory and TMH, Mumbai was also assessed.

Prospective cohort

Digital pathology has been integrated into the workflow of our rural tertiary care oncology hospital for the procurement of expert second opinions, educational program, and research activities. For the present study, the haematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and IHC slides of 47 HER2 cases were digitized between July 2023 and January 2024.

The criteria for patient selection included:

1. Cases with HER2/neu equivocal staining (score 2+) defined by weak to moderate complete membrane staining in >10 % cells2.

2. Cases with adequate tissue for molecular testing.

Whole slide imaging (WSI)

Philips SG60 digital slide scanner (Best, Netherlands) was used for scanning. Our SOP recommends a tissue thickness of 3 μm, placed centrally on a clean glass slide.

The pre-scan quality assessment was done by the trained laboratory personnel. The scanning speed varied depending on the size of the tissue. Out-of-focus images and incomplete images were re-scanned.

Following WSI, the paraffin blocks were immediately sent for molecular analysis without generating requests for pathology review or additional IHC staining.

The WSI of H&E and IHC slides were assigned to the pathologist at TMH. The molecular pathology team was also intimated about the digitally scanned slides through an online messenger. In the molecular laboratory, a single H&E slide was prepared to confirm the suitability of tissue for FISH testing. Repeat IHC analysis was not performed. TAT for this cohort was assessed. The details of this workflow is summarized are Fig. 1B.

Statistical tests

In the retrospective cohort, the concordance between the HER2/neu IHC results from rural laboratory and TMH Mumbai was assessed using both Cohen's Kappa and Gwet's AC1 test. To evaluate the difference in the mean TAT between the retrospective and the prospective cohorts, a sample t-test with Welch's correction was performed. All analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS 24.0 (Armonk, NY).

Results

IHC concordance before digital project initiation

There were 60 HER2/neu equivocal (Score 2+) cases that were sent from our rural laboratory to the reference laboratory from 1st January 2022 to 31st December 2022 for FISH testing. The IHC assessment of HER2/neu expression demonstrated concordance in 59 out of 60 cases (98.3 %), with one case being scored as 1+ in the reference laboratory.

The high concordance indicated that the HER2/neu scoring between the two centres was very consistent. Due to the highly skewed distribution of HER2 scores, Cohen's kappa was not an informative measure of agreement, yielding a value of 0.00 despite a 98.3 % overall concordance. We also calculated the Gwet's AC1, which is less affected by imbalanced marginal distribution, which indicated almost perfect agreement. This corroborates the high reliability of HER2/neu scoring between the two centres. Thus, the IHC stained slides could be reliably shared between the two centres and repeat testing could be obviated.

Cases assessed digitally: Implications on time and human resources

The mean TAT of the cases in the retrospective cohort where repeat IHC testing was.

performed was 15.65 days. Retesting also led to increase in the TAT and utilized the limited, overworked skilled workforce. After introducing virtual slides, the mean TAT was reduced to 8.775 days (standard TAT at our hospital for HPR and IHC: 8 days; standard TAT for molecular testing for FISH: 15 days). Thus, we were able to achieve a 43.9 % reduction in TAT. These results have been summarized in Table 1. A two-sample t-test with Welch's correction was also used to compare the TAT between the two cohorts. The results revealed a statistically significant difference (t = −7.49; p = 6 × 10−11), indicating that digital pathology significantly reduced the TAT.

Costing of cases assessed digitally

Routinely, an amount of INR 7200 (∼70$) would have been spent by the patient for seeking an HPR and IHC for HER2/neu review in the absence of the digitally scanned slides. The cost of a FISH test for HER2/neu was ∼INR 16800. The total cost of HPR and IHC review along with molecular testing per patient was thus ∼ INR 24000 as opposed to that of INR 16800 with the help of virtual slides. Thus, overall this digital project saved approximately 30 % of monetary resources thus proving to be much economical.

Discussion

Introducing DP in a traditional surgical pathology laboratory is associated with multiple challenges, including upfront huge investment in procurement of WSI system and supporting software, total storage capacity, network bandwidth, installation of medical grade display monitors and most importantly, the intradepartmental culture barriers in transitioning to a digital platform from the conventional bright field microscope. Despite the hurdles, the long term benefits of digital pathology are remarkable.12

DP has revolutionised traditional pathology practices in this era, leading to improved efficiency, accuracy and accessibility while reducing costs. It facilitates rapid teleconsultations and second opinions which is especially useful in remote and rural areas with limited resources.7 In our study, WSI and enabling remote access to pathology material especially the HER2/neu IHC slide streamlined the review process of breast cancer cases.

The integration of DP in the referral process from spoke centres to centralised molecular pathology laboratories significantly enhances efficiency and cost effectiveness. It reduces the cost incurred by duplication of tests and optimizes resource utilisation. In our study, the cost duplication of repeat HPR and HER2/neu IHC was saved (∼30 %) and the cost incurred by the patient at the central laboratory was only for the FISH testing.

In the study by Ho et al.,13 the economy impact model was based on two benefits offered by implementation of digital pathology system, namely productivity and efficiency improvements, and improved outcomes derived from a reduced rate of surgical pathology interpretive errors. In surgical pathology, the transition to a WSI system resulted in an increased efficiency, with an improvement rate of 13 % for individual pathologist when compared with a manual-based system utilizing conventional microscopes and glass slides.14 Implementation of an enterprise-wide DP system also enabled a greater degree of centralization of histopathology labs throughout the organization, promoting economies of scale in laboratory operation. The cost savings offered by increasing the surgical pathology workflow efficiency were the main component of the potential savings. The 5-year cost savings due to improved productivity were estimated at $12.38 million, composing 70 % of total savings offered by implementation of a digital pathology system. Hanna et al15 projected a 1.3 million$ saving during the five year period 2019–2023 secondary to personnel restructuring and decreased vendor services due to decreased need for glass slide transport and physical storage of glass slides due to ready availability of WSI.

The other major advantage of DP is the reduction of TAT for diagnosis and additional ancillary testing. In the study by Hanna et al.,15 surgical specimens with prior WSIs were reported 25 % (1 day) sooner due to decrease in glass slide retrieval of prior patient material. Our study showed approximately 43 % reduction in the time taken for testing at the central laboratory. It also reduced the burden on already stretched, overworked skilled workforce of Molecular Pathology Laboratory, TMH, and helped better utilisation of skilled human resource. Faster delivery of test reports resulted in reduction in waiting period for further management of breast cancer patients in prototype rural area. This improvement streamlined the treatment process, allowing patients to receive timely care and interventions, which is crucial for better outcomes.

The ability to share digital images obviates the need for physical transportation of slides, minimising the risk of damage during the transit. It also enables collaboration among pathologists regardless of their geographical locations, reduces professional isolation, enhancing interdisciplinary consultations and diagnostic accuracy. This rapid transmission of information facilitates faster decision making, patient management and treatment planning.

The strength of the study was that it was the first study from India to study the importance and utility of DP in obviating ancillary testing for referral cases from the sister institution in prototype rural area. The limitations were that this study had a small patient cohort which precluded assessment of the long term patient and hospital outcomes. There are also certain constraints of implementing this workflow at other rural centres. The prerequisite for robust IHC setup at the rural laboratory which is consistently replicable is a must. In addition, establishment of DP workflow as well as training manpower in rural areas remains a challenge.

Conclusion

Overall, DP in the referral process provides a solution to decrease cost, human effort and the TAT by obviating repeat tests. Adoption of this technology optimizes resource utilisation and improves patient care making it a priceless asset in today's era of modern Pathology.

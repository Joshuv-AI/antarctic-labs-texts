Efficacy of eco-friendly synthesized silver nanoparticles in ameliorating tartrazine induced reproductive toxicity in Japanese quails
Authors: Fiza Abbas, Shabana Naz, Hanan Al-Khalaifah, Samra, Maryam Fatima, Ulfat Zahra, Hafsa Saeed, Sania Satti, Abdul Hafeez Abdul Razzaq, Rifat Ullah Khan, Ala Abudabos, Raed M Al-Atiyat, Ihtesham Ul Haq, Ibrhaim A Alhidary
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTartrazine, a synthetic azo dye that imparts a lemon-yellow color, is widely used in food products. Tartrazine has raised health concerns due to its potential to induce physiological and biochemical disruptions in different species. This study aimed to evaluate the protective effect of AgNPs synthesized from Azadirachta indica against tartrazine-induced reproductive toxicity using Japanese quail as an animal model. For this purpose, a total of 672, 14-day-old Japanese quails were purchased and, after a 10-day acclimatization period, were equally divided into 7 treatment groups (96 birds per group) with six replicates and a sex ratio of 1 male to 3 females. Group 1 served as the control and received a basal diet only. Groups 2 and 3 were administered tartrazine at doses of 10 and 20 mg/kg body weight, respectively. Groups 4 and 5 received tartrazine (10 mg/kg body weight) in combination with AgNPs at doses of 4 and 8 mg/kg body weight, respectively. Groups 6 and 7 received tartrazine (20 mg/kg body weight) combined with AgNPs at doses of 4 and 8 mg/kg body weight, respectively. The trial spanned 11 weeks. Reproductive toxicity was assessed by evaluating hen-day egg production, fertility, hatchability, embryo mortality, gonadal weight, and hormone levels. Tartrazine exposure significantly altered reproductive parameters in Japanese quails, as evidenced by reduced hen-day egg production, fertility, hatchability, gonadal weight, and disrupted hormone levels (p < 0.05). The highest reproductive toxicity was observed in quails administered Tz at 20 mg/kg body weight. However, AgNPs administration effectively restored reproductive parameters toward control levels, with the Tz (10 mg/kg) and AgNPs (8 mg/kg) co-treated group exhibiting the greatest improvement. In conclusion, tartrazine exposure induced reproductive aberrations in Japanese quails. However, green synthesized AgNPs proved effective in ameliorating tartrazine-induced toxicity, as their administration significantly improved reproductive health.Keywords: Tartrazine, Silver nanoparticles, Azadirachta indica, Japanese quail, Reproductive toxicity

Introduction

Food colorants are widely used to enhance the visual appeal of food products, as an attractive appearance plays an important role in influencing consumer preferences and purchasing behavior (Ara et al., 2022). Both natural and synthetic colorants have been utilized in the food industry to improve product appearance and acceptability (Gupta et al., 2019). Among synthetic colorants, tartrazine—also known as Food Yellow 4, Acid Yellow 23, or Yellow 5—is a commonly used azo dye derived from coal tar that imparts a lemon-yellow color to various food and pharmaceutical products (Ara et al., 2022; Qasim and Lafta, 2024). Tartrazine is frequently present in beverages, flavored snacks, frozen desserts, condiments, and confectionery products. In addition to food applications, it is also used in several non-food products such as cosmetics, shampoos, soaps, vitamins, and certain pharmaceutical formulations (Uppada et al., 2024).

After oral ingestion, tartrazine undergoes metabolic reduction primarily by intestinal microbiota through azoreductase enzymes, resulting in the formation of aromatic amines such as aminopyrazolone and sulfanilic acid (Ameur et al., 2020). In avian species, the digestive system possesses distinct physiological characteristics, including a relatively short gastrointestinal transit time and a unique gut microbial community, which may influence the metabolic transformation of xenobiotics such as azo dyes. These metabolites can be absorbed through the intestinal epithelium and transported to metabolically active organs, including the liver and reproductive tissues. Previous studies have reported that exposure to tartrazine may induce neurobehavioral alterations, genotoxicity, cytotoxicity, immunotoxicity, and mutagenic effects (Albasher et al., 2020; Dos et al., 2022).

The toxic effects of tartrazine have largely been attributed to oxidative stress mechanisms. The metabolic by-products of tartrazine can enhance the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), promote lipid peroxidation, and suppress the activity of antioxidant enzymes, ultimately leading to cellular damage and apoptosis (Albasher et al., 2020; Essawy et al., 2023; Baysal et al., 2025). Reproductive tissues are particularly sensitive to oxidative stress because of their high metabolic activity and rapid cellular proliferation. In avian species, reproductive functions are tightly regulated by the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal (HPG) axis, which controls steroid hormone secretion, follicular development in females, and spermatogenesis in males. Disruption of these physiological processes may impair ovarian follicular maturation, ovulation, and reproductive behavior, thereby negatively affecting reproductive performance (Essawy et al., 2024; Baysa et al., 2025).

The Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee on Food Additives has established an acceptable daily intake (ADI) for tartrazine of 0–7.5 mg/kg body weight per day (EFSA, 2009). Tartrazine has also been approved for use as a colorant in the feed of certain animal species, including small rodents, cats, dogs, ornamental fish, and grain-consuming ornamental birds (EFSA, 2016). Nevertheless, concerns persist regarding the unregulated use of tartrazine in various consumer products without appropriate labeling, which may increase the risk of exposure (Ara et al., 2022). Consequently, identifying effective strategies to mitigate the potential toxic effects of tartrazine has become an important area of research.

Recent advances in veterinary and poultry sciences have highlighted the potential of plant-derived bioactive compounds and nanotechnology-based approaches to enhance growth performance, improve health status, and alleviate toxicological stress (Al-Gheffari et al., 2024; Iqbal et al., 2024; Haryati et al., 2025). Among these approaches, silver nanoparticles (AgNPs) have attracted considerable attention due to their antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, and anticancer properties (Ahsan et al., 2024; Rasool et al., 2024; Al-Khalaifah et al., 2025a; Sadan et al., 2025; Usman et al., 2025). Green-synthesized AgNPs have been shown to scavenge free radicals such as hydroxyl, DPPH, and superoxide radicals, thereby reducing oxidative stress at the cellular level (Pandey et al., 2022; Almudayni et al., 2025). In addition, these nanoparticles can penetrate biological barriers and reach therapeutic concentrations in tissues while potentially minimizing adverse effects on surrounding normal cells (Al-Gheffari et al., 2024).

Despite their promising biological properties, concerns remain regarding the safety of AgNPs, particularly following prolonged exposure or administration at high doses. The biological effects of AgNPs can vary depending on factors such as particle size, morphology, surface coating, and route of administration (Laib et al., 2024). Therefore, considerable research has focused on improving the biocompatibility of silver nanoparticles and reducing their potential toxicity. One effective approach is the green synthesis of nanoparticles using plant extracts, which provides an environmentally friendly and biologically compatible method for nanoparticle production (Tareq et al., 2022; Bashir et al., 2024). Plant-mediated synthesis incorporates bioactive phytochemicals that act as natural reducing and stabilizing agents, thereby enhancing antioxidant activity and improving nanoparticle biocompatibility (Muthu et al., 2025).

Azadirachta indica (Neem) is a well-known medicinal plant widely cultivated throughout the Indian subcontinent and recognized for its diverse pharmacological properties (Dutt et al., 2023). Leaves of A. indica are particularly rich in alkaloids, flavonoids, phenolics, triterpenoids, carotenoids, ketones, and steroids (Huma et al., 2024). These phytoconstituents possess strong antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory activities that can inhibit microbial growth, reduce inflammation, and protect tissues from oxidative damage (Barik et al., 2024). Furthermore, compounds present in Azadirachta indica leaf extracts can act as natural hydrogen donors, redox agents, and oxygen quenchers, thereby neutralizing free radicals and enhancing endogenous antioxidant defense systems (Lakkim et al., 2023). When incorporated into the synthesis of silver nanoparticles, these phytochemicals not only stabilize the nanoparticles but also facilitate the reduction and detoxification of harmful aromatic amines produced from tartrazine metabolism, enhancing the overall protective and antioxidant effects of AgNPs in biological systems (Pandey et al., 2022).

The Japanese quail (Coturnix coturnix japonica) is widely used as an experimental avian model due to its rapid embryonic development (approximately 16 days), high egg production, and short maturation period, which allow efficient evaluation of reproductive and toxicological responses within a relatively short time (Al-Khalaifah et al., 2025a). In addition, Japanese quails share several physiological and reproductive characteristics with other avian species, making them a valuable model for studies related to reproductive biology and toxicology (Hosseini et al., 2017; Al-Khalaifah et al., 2025b).

Therefore, the present study aimed to evaluate the toxic effects of tartrazine on the reproductive system and to investigate the protective potential of silver nanoparticles synthesized from Azadirachta indica leaves against tartrazine-induced reproductive toxicity in Japanese quails.

Materials and methods

Chemicals

Tartrazine was purchased from Food Net Chemical Company, Pakistan. Additional chemicals were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich Company.

Synthesis and characterization of silver nanoparticles

Fresh Azadirachta indica (Neem) leaves were collected from various parts of Faisalabad. The leaves were first rinsed with running tap water to eliminate dirt and other contaminants, followed by thorough wash using double distilled water. The leaves were then air dried at room temperature and cut into smaller pieces. Approximately 15 g of cut leaves were accurately weighed and placed in 50 mL Erlenmeyer flask containing 50 mL of deionized water. The mixture was boiled in a water bath for 25 minutes at 60°C, After heating, the extract was allowed to cool at room temperature and subsequently filtered with Whatman filter paper no. 42 to obtain leaf extract. For the synthesis of AgNPs, a 100 mL solution of 1 mM silver nitrate (AgNO3) was prepared in an Erlenmeyer flask. Then, 50 mL of this AgNO3 solution was mixed with 10 mL of Azadirachta indica leaf extract under continuous stirring at room temperature for 20 minutes. At ambient temperature, the mixture was incubated in a dark to prevent the photo-activation of silver nitrate. The formation of AgNPs was initially verified by color shift from reddish to dark brown, confirming the reduction of silver ions. The formation was further validated using UV- visible spectroscopy. After 24 hours, the AgNPs solution was centrifuged at 2000 rpm for 15 minutes, and the resulting pellets were dried at 100°C in an oven for 24 hours (Kumari et al., 2022).

The synthesized AgNPs were characterized using UV-Visible spectroscopy, Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) and X-ray diffraction (XRD) techniques. UV-Visible spectrophotometer (Hitachi U-2800) was used to record the absorption spectrum of AgNPs in the 200-800 nm in wavelength range. Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy (PerkinElmer) was employed to determine the functional groups present in the neem and responsible for stabilizing the Ag-NPs. An X'Pert Pro diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation was used for X-ray diffraction (Single Crystal System D8 Advance, Bruker) investigation in order to ascertain the nanoparticles structure and size distribution with a scanning speed of 10°/min (Khalid et al., 2025).

Experimental birds and doses

This experiment was carried out in accordance with the rules for the management and care of laboratory animals established by Government College University, Faisalabad. A total of 672, 14-day-old Japanese quails with average body weights (49 g ± 12) were purchased from the Avian Research and Training Center (ART center), located at the University of Veterinary and Animal Sciences (UVAS), Lahore. After cautious transportation, these chicks were transported to GCUF's Animal House. They were kept in wire cages under standard hygienic conditions and similar housing. The quails were acclimatized for 10 days. Environmental conditions such as temperature (22-25°C) and humidity (65-70%) were maintained in accordance with the standard approach for handling Japanese quails. All birds were fed with commercial basal diet and were given unlimited access to water and food. They were also maintained under a photoperiod of 16 hours of light and 8 hours of darkness. During the trial, all groups received the same management practices, i.e., lighting, feeding, and watering. Following the acclimatization period, the quails were randomly allocated to 7 different treatment groups. Each group comprised 96 birds, organized into 6 replicates (16 quails in each replicate) with a sex ratio of 1 male to 3 females (4 males and 12 females per replicate). The 1st group was given only the basal diet; the 2nd group received the basal diet along with tartrazine at a dose of 10 mg/kg body weight; the 3rd group received the basal diet along with tartrazine at a dose of 20 mg/kg body weight, 4th group received the basal diet along with tartrazine at a dose of 10 mg/kg body weight and AgNPs at a dose of 4 mg/kg body weight, 5th group received the basal diet along with tartrazine at a dose of 10 mg/kg body weight and AgNPs at a dose of 8 mg/kg body weight, 6th group received the basal diet along with tartrazine at a dose of 20 mg/kg body weight and AgNPs at a dose of 4 mg/kg body weight and 7th group received the basal diet along with tartrazine at a dose of 20 mg/kg body weight and AgNPs at a dose of 8 mg/kg body weight. A tartrazine stock solution was prepared using distilled water, and each experimental bird received an equal dose of 10 mg/kg body weight. The treatments were administered once daily via oral gavage needle throughout the trial period. The trial spanned 11 weeks, consisting of 1-week acclimation phase followed by a 10-week study phase. Estimation of reproductive toxicity

Reproductive toxicity was assessed by evaluating the effects of tartrazine alone and in combination with AgNPs on the following parameters: hen-day egg production, fertility, hatchability, embryo mortality, gonadal weight, and hormone levels.

Hen day egg production

Every day, eggs were collected, and the total production was calculated using the hen day egg production (HDEP) formula (Al-Khalaifah et al., 2025b).

Fertility, hatchability and embryo mortality

Egg collection started at the 9th week of age during the experiment to achieve a more steady egg production rate. To reduce contamination during the research period, the eggs were collected twice daily, in the morning and late afternoon. Every egg was marked with the replicate number and date. After thorough examination for cracks, eggs were kept in a refrigerator for a brief period to preserve freshness and stop early embryo development. At the conclusion of each collection week (9th, 11th, and 13th of age), all the eggs were collected from each group and placed in an automated incubator. Constant temperature of 37.5°C with 60% relative humidity and 36.7°C with 70% relative humidity were maintained during the setter and hatcher periods, respectively. After eighteen days, all eggs were broken to determine fertility. Fertility, hatchability, and embryo mortality percentages were calculated using the following equations (Al-Khalaifah et al., 2025b).

Sample collection

At the end of the experiment, all quails were weighed, and five birds per replicate were randomly picked from each group for blood collection. A blood sample of approximately 2-3 mL was drawn from the brachial vein between 9:00 and 11:00 a.m. and placed in heparinized tubes. The blood was centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 15 min to separate the plasma. The plasma was then stored at −20°C for hormonal analysis. Following the collection of blood samples, all the quails were weighed, and then humanely euthanized by cervical dislocation. The quails were subsequently dissected to obtain the ovaries and testes. The reproductive organs were weighed using a digital balance. After weighing, the organs were washed with deionized water to eliminate any blood and stored on ice until further analysis. The gonadosomatic index was calculated using the following formula (Ugrin et al., 2023).

Hormonal assay

Plasma samples from male and female Japanese quails were used to quantify estradiol, progesterone, testosterone, luteinizing hormone (LH), and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) using commercially available enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits, following the manufacturers’ instructions.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 21. Reproductive traits measured over time (hen-day egg production, fertility, hatchability, and embryo mortality) were analyzed using two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with treatment and week as fixed factors, including their interaction (treatment × week). Parameters measured at a single time point (body weight, gonadal weight, gonadosomatic indices, and hormone concentrations) were analyzed using one-way ANOVA with treatment as the main effect. When significant differences were detected, means were separated using Tukey’s post hoc test. Differences were considered statistically significant at p < 0.05. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation, and the experimental unit was the replicate pen.

Results

Characterization of Ag-NPs

The UV-Visible spectroscopy is employed to analyze the optical characteristics of Ag-NPs, particularly to assess their absorption spectrum and the peak of Surface Plasmon Resonance (SPR). An SPR peak for silver nanoparticles was observed at 430 nm in the UV-Vis spectra, which is characteristic of silver nanoparticles. The FTIR is used to assess the functional groups present on the AgNPs surface. The spectrum of the produced silver nanoparticles shows significant absorption peaks at 3278 cm⁻¹, 1638 cm⁻¹, and 1045 cm⁻¹. The broad peak at 3278 cm⁻¹ is associated with O–H stretching vibrations of hydroxyl groups, indicating the involvement of polyphenolic compounds. The peak at 1638 cm⁻¹ may suggest the presence of C = C stretching of conjugated alkene or aromatic rings, which are the characteristics of phytochemicals. The band at 1045 cm⁻¹ correlates with C–O stretching of ether linkages. Thus FTIR findings verified the presence of many phytochemicals from A. indica which participate in the reduction Ag+ ions to Ago atoms and in the stabilization of AgNPs as capping agents. X-ray diffraction (XRD) technique was employed to analyze the structural analysis. At an angle of 2θ, XRD spectra showed a very broad set of diffraction peaks in the 15–35o range. The diffraction peak indicates that the synthesized AgNPs are amorphous in nature and very small in size. The extra peaks observed in the X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis were believed to indicate the presence of other compounds within the silver nanoparticles solution derived from the plant extract

Impacts of Tz and AgNPs supplementation on reproductive performance of Japanese quails

The impacts of Tz exposure and the potential protective effects of AgNPs on fertility (%), hatchability (%), and embryo mortality (%) in quails are illustrated in Table 3. The groups receiving Tz, particularly at 20 mg/kg, demonstrated markedly reduced fertility (65.88%) and hatchability (64.44%) compared with the control and other groups. Birds supplemented with Tz 10 mg/kg along with AgNPs 8 mg/kg improved fertility (75.36%) and hatchability (73.08%). Discussion

Research has focused on the synthesis and application of nanoparticles—such as silver, zinc oxide, and gold nanoparticles—for antimicrobial, anticancer, antioxidant, and targeted drug delivery purposes, highlighting their potential in disease mitigation and animal health (Al-Suwailem et al., 2024; Alahmadi, 2025; Akram et al., 2025). In the present study, a week-wise evaluation was conducted to assess the effects of tartrazine at two doses (10 and 20 mg/kg) and green-synthesized AgNPs (4 and 8 mg/kg) on the reproductive system of Japanese quails, including hen-day egg production, fertility, hatchability, embryo mortality, gonadal weight, and hormone levels.

Exposure to both low and high doses of tartrazine resulted in a significant decrease in hen-day egg production and fertility compared with the control group, with the high dose (20 mg/kg) showing the greatest decline. This reduction is directly linked to alterations in reproductive hormone levels, as LH and FSH levels were significantly lower in tartrazine-treated groups. Luteinizing hormone (LH) contributes to ovulation, whereas follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) promotes ovum growth and maturation. Low levels of LH and FSH, as observed in our study, likely impaired follicular development, leading to decreased egg production. Gonadal dysfunction, indicated by reduced gonadal weight, supports the notion that tartrazine disrupts ovarian follicle maturation during the pre-ovulatory phase (Prastiya et al., 2022; Asser-sohn et al., 2021). In males, testosterone is a key androgen influencing reproductive function and secondary sexual traits (Yuneldi et al., 2025).

Tartrazine exposure induced oxidative stress in reproductive tissues, evidenced by the decrease in antioxidant defense, increased ROS, and lipid peroxidation observed in previous studies (Baysal et al., 2025; Meghapriya and Kishori, 2019). These mechanisms likely underlie the reduction in spermatogenesis, folliculogenesis, and gonadal weight seen in our quail model. Furthermore, as an endocrine disruptor, tartrazine alters hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis function, affecting gonadotropins (LH, FSH) and sex steroids (testosterone, estradiol), which may result in infertility (Bhattacharjee et al., 2023; Ara et al., 2022).

Embryo mortality and decreased hatchability were also observed, particularly in the high-dose tartrazine group (20 mg/kg), indicating dose-dependent reproductive toxicity. These findings align with prior studies in rodents showing reduced fetal body weight and increased mortality upon tartrazine exposure (Bhattacharjee et al., 2023; Hashem et al., 2019). One of the most sensitive indicators of reproductive toxicity, gonadal weight, was significantly reduced in tartrazine-exposed groups. This decrease is consistent with oxidative damage to ovarian and testicular tissues caused by ROS, resulting in structural and biochemical lesions (Bassit and Esmail, 2025).

Importantly, co-administration of green-synthesized AgNPs with tartrazine significantly improved reproductive parameters, restoring values close to control levels. The 8 mg/kg AgNP dose showed greater efficacy than the 4 mg/kg dose, demonstrating a dose-dependent protective effect. This restoration is likely due to the antioxidant properties of AgNPs, which scavenge free radicals, reduce oxidative stress, and protect reproductive tissues. Incorporation of A. indica phytochemicals into AgNPs enhances their free radical scavenging capacity and stability, providing both antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects in vivo. These phytochemicals act as natural hydrogen donors, redox agents, and oxygen quenchers, neutralizing free radicals and mitigating the toxic aromatic amines produced from tartrazine metabolism (Pandey et al., 2022; Lakkim et al., 2023). Encapsulation of AgNPs with plant-derived compounds also reduces potential oxidative stress from silver itself, enhancing the therapeutic potential of the nanoparticles (Tareq et al., 2022).

Our findings are supported by previous studies demonstrating that biosynthesized AgNPs can restore hormonal balance and improve fertility in rodent models exposed to oxidative stressors (Hassan et al., 2024; Alwan and Al-Saeed, 2021; Al-Ameri and Ali, 2024). Similarly, the present study shows that green-synthesized AgNPs effectively mitigate tartrazine-induced reproductive toxicity in Japanese quails, highlighting their potential for protecting against oxidative stress-mediated reproductive damage.

Overall, this study provides direct evidence that tartrazine induces dose-dependent reproductive toxicity via oxidative stress and hormonal disruption, and that green-synthesized AgNPs, particularly at higher doses, effectively ameliorate these adverse effects. These results strengthen the mechanistic link between oxidative stress and reproductive impairment and demonstrate the therapeutic potential of plant-mediated nanoparticle formulations (Fig. 1).

Conclusions

The present study successfully demonstrated that tartrazine exposure induced reproductive aberrations in Japanese quails. However, green-synthesized AgNPs proved effective in ameliorating tartrazine-induced toxicity, as their administration significantly improved reproductive health. Future studies should investigate the molecular mechanisms through which AgNPs mitigate tartrazine induced reproductive toxicity. Additionally, future research should examine optimal dosing and explore the use of other nanoparticles in place of AgNPs to reduce tartrazine-induced toxicity.

Evidence-based nursing practice of radiation-induced oral mucositis in patients with head and neck cancer
Authors: Li Tian, Zimo Zhang, Songbo Wu, Jun Ma, Suqing Tian, Panfeng Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo integrate the best available evidence with the clinical practice for nursing care of radiation-induced oral mucositis (RIOM) in patients with head and neck cancer, develop an evidence-based practice protocol, and evaluate its effectiveness in clinical application.MethodsGuided by the Knowledge-to-Action (KTA) framework, this study summarized the best available evidence for nursing care of RIOM in patients with head and neck cancer and adapted it to the clinical scenario based on expert opinions. An evidence-based practice protocol was developed by integrating the results of a baseline review and an analysis of barriers to implementation. The protocol was implemented in clinical settings, and its effectiveness was evaluated by assessing changes in outcomes before and after implementation, including nurses’ implementation rate of review indicators, the incidence of severe RIOM, oral pain scores, and the onset time of different RIOM grades.ResultsAfter the clinical application of the evidence-based practice, the implementation rate of the 9 review indicators of nurses was significantly improved (P ​< ​0.05). The incidence of severe RIOM at the end of radiotherapy decreased from 43.64% to 24.07%. The oral pain score was significantly reduced from 5.53 ​± ​1.62 to 4.44 ​± ​1.54 (P ​< ​0.05). Additionally, the onset of grade I RIOM was significantly delayed during radiotherapy (P ​< ​0.05), while no significant changes were observed in the onset time of grade II and III RIOM (P ​> ​0.05).ConclusionsThe implementation of an evidence-based practice protocol for the nursing care of RIOM in patients with head and neck cancer could enhance nurses’ clinical performance, reduce the incidence of severe RIOM at the end of radiotherapy, alleviate oral pain, and delay the onset of mucositis. It could bridge the gap between evidence and clinical practice and enhance the quality of nursing care.Keywords: Head and neck cancer, Radiation-induced oral mucositis, Clinical nursing, Evidence-based practice

Introduction

Head and neck cancer (HNC) refers to a group of malignant tumors originating in the head and neck region, with squamous cell carcinoma being the most common histological type, accounting for more than 90% of cases.1 Due to the high radiosensitivity of HNC, radiotherapy often becomes the preferred treatment option when complete surgical resection is not feasible.2 However, while radiotherapy targets malignant cells, it can also damage healthy tissues. Among its complications, radiation-induced oral mucositis (RIOM) is one of the most frequent and severe in patients with HNC.3 RIOM can lead to several complications, including oral ulceration, infection, and gastrointestinal toxicities, which may necessitate interruptions in radiation therapy.4,5 Such treatment disruptions can adversely affect patient prognosis and significantly diminish quality of life.6 The incidence of RIOM in patients undergoing conventional radiotherapy for HNC ranges from 80% to 100%, with severe RIOM occurring in approximately 28%–80% of cases.7 Nearly all patients with oral, oropharyngeal, and nasopharyngeal cancers, as well as about two-thirds of those with hypopharyngeal and laryngeal cancers, develop RIOM.8

Evidence-based practice (EBP) is a decision-making and clinical practice approach grounded in scientific evidence.9 It requires nurses to prudently and judiciously integrate the best available evidence with clinical expertise and patient preferences, thereby supporting clinical nursing decisions and promoting the professionalization and scientific advancement of nursing practice.10 Although there is an increasing body of high-quality evidence regarding the nursing care of RIOM in HNC patients, a gap remains between evidence and clinical practice. The incidence of severe RIOM in clinical settings remains relatively high, and the translation of evidence into practice continues to face multiple challenges.11

The Knowledge-to-Action (KTA) framework, a widely recognized model for EBP,12 divides the process into two key components: knowledge creation and knowledge application. Guided by the KTA framework, this study aims to integrate the best available evidence and clinical practice of RIOM care for patients with HNC. By developing and implementing an EBP protocol, this study seeks to promote the effective translation and application of evidence, standardize clinical nursing behaviors, improve patient outcomes, and ultimately enhance the quality of RIOM care for patients with HNC in a systematic, standardized, and scientific manner.

Methods

Participants

The clinical setting for evidence application was the Department of Radiation Oncology at a tertiary hospital in Beijing. The study population comprised all nurses in the department and all patients undergoing radiotherapy for HNC.

Inclusion criteria for nurses were: (1) employment in the radiation oncology department for at least one year; and (2) informed of the study and willing to participate. Exclusion criteria were inability to comply with study requirements due to personal reasons during the study period.

Inclusion criteria for patients were: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) confirmed diagnosis of HNC and undergoing radiotherapy; and (3) clear consciousness, adequate cognitive function, and provision of informed consent. Exclusion criteria were: (1) presence of oral mucositis prior to radiotherapy; (2) inability to cooperate due to severe disease conditions; or (3) concomitant communication or cognitive impairments.

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the participating institution. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment.

Knowledge creation

Establishment of an evidence-based practice team

An EBP team was established for this study, consisting of 8 members: one deputy director of the nursing department, one expert in evidence-based methodology, one associate chief physician from the department of radiation oncology, two head nurses, two staff nurses from the same department, and one postgraduate nursing student. All team members had received systematic training in EBP.

Identify evidence-based issues

The PIPOST structured model13 was used to determine the evidence-based questions for this study: Population (P): HNC patients undergoing radiotherapy; Intervention (I): RIOM nursing related measures, including oral assessment, basic oral care, nutritional support, and multidisciplinary collaborative management; Professional (P): Medical and nursing staff in the department of radiation oncology; Outcome (O): Nurses’ implementation rate of clinical review indicators, incidence of severe RIOM at the end of radiotherapy, oral pain scores, and onset time of different grades of RIOM; Setting (S): oncology radiotherapy department of a tertiary hospital in Beijing; Type of evidence (T): clinical practice guidelines, evidence summaries, expert consensus, and systematic reviews.

Evidence summary

Based on the “6S” evidence model,14 this study searched relevant clinical practice guidelines, evidence summaries, expert consensus, and systematic reviews. Databases searched included BMJ Best Practice, Up to Date, National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE), Guidelines International Network (GIN), World Health Organization (WHO), National Health Commission of China, Chinese Nursing Association, National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN), Multinational Association of Supportive Care in Cancer (MASCC), International Society of Oral Oncology (ISOO), American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO), European Society For Medical Oncology (ESMO), Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO), China Anti-Cancer Association (CACA), Cochrane Library, JBI Evidence-based Health Care Center Library, PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, SinoMed, CNKI, and Wanfang database. The search time was from the establishment of the database to January 31, 2024. The retrieved literature was systematically screened and evaluated, and relevant evidence was extracted and summarized. A total of 10 articles were included, including 5 clinical practice guidelines,15, 16, 17, 18, 19 1 evidence summary,20 2 expert consensus21,22 and 2 systematic reviews.23,24 A total of 17 pieces of evidence were extracted and summarized from the included literature, including 5 aspects, covering multidisciplinary collaborative management, oral assessment, basic oral care, nutritional support, and honey application.

Knowledge application

Evidence integration and development of review indicators

An expert panel was convened to evaluate the synthesized evidence using the FAME criteria,25 which assess Feasibility, Appropriateness, Meaningfulness, and Effectiveness. A total of 9 experts from the clinical setting were invited to participate. Each piece of evidence related to the nursing care of RIOM in patients with HNC was carefully reviewed for its applicability to current clinical practice. Based on expert feedback, the researchers deleted the evidence of “honey application” as experts considered that the current clinical application of honey was complex and limited. Additionally, two pieces under “oral assessment” and one piece under “basic oral care” were merged with other evidence entries. The remaining evidence was refined and incorporated accordingly.

Ultimately, the experts recommended a total of 4 aspects and 13 pieces of evidence suitable for the introduction of this clinical practice scenario: multidisciplinary collaborative management (2 pieces), oral assessment (4 pieces), basic oral care (4 pieces), and dietary/nutritional support (3 pieces). Then, combined with clinical scenarios, the above evidence was split and integrated into 9 measurable review indicators. The finalized evidence content and corresponding review indicators are detailed in Table 1.

Conducting a clinical baseline review

According to the inclusion and exclusion criteria of research objects, the research team conducted a baseline review of 21 nurses and patients with HNC undergoing radiotherapy in the department from March to June 2024 to investigate the gap between evidence and clinical practice.

Analysis of barriers and facilitators to evidence implementation

Following the baseline review, the researchers carried out semi-structured interviews with seven health care professionals from the department. Based on the combined findings from the review and interviews, potential barriers and facilitators influencing evidence implementation were identified. The barriers included: (1) insufficiently developed standardized workflows within the department; (2) inadequate methods for utilizing oral assessment tools; (3) incomplete patient education materials; (4) insufficient knowledge and skills among nurses. Facilitators included: (1) strong leadership and managerial support for clinical change; (2) a collaborative and supportive environment among medical and nursing staff, favoring EBP; (3) positive nurse-patient relationships within the department, facilitating clinical application of evidence.

Development and application of the evidence-based practice protocol

Guided by the synthesized evidence and informed by the identified gaps and barriers from the baseline review, the research team drafted a preliminary EBP protocol for RIOM nursing care in patients with HNC. Subsequently, relevant experts were consulted to revise and refine the draft. The final protocol was established based on expert feedback.

According to the inclusion and exclusion criteria, all patients who met the criteria from September to December 2024 and 21 nurses who were the same as the baseline review were selected to conduct the review after the application of the EBP. The review indicators and methods were consistent with the baseline. The specific components of the protocol implementation are as follows.

(1)Refinement of the standardized assessment process

Refinement of the standardized assessment process

To standardize nurses' assessment practices, the research team refined the department's standardized evaluation process for patients with HNC undergoing radiotherapy. The process is intended to guide nurses in conducting systematic oral and nutritional assessments, including a baseline evaluation before radiotherapy and regular follow-up assessments during treatment. Oral assessment before radiotherapy included patients' oral hygiene, dental conditions and daily cleaning methods. If patients had oral diseases before radiotherapy, including dental caries and mucositis, they were recommended to go to the stomatology department for treatment. Nutritional assessment involves the use of the Nutritional Risk Screening tool (NRS 2002)26 by nurses to identify patients at risk of malnutrition. If patients had nutritional risk, they would be evaluated and given nutritional support by nutritionists. During radiotherapy, oral mucositis assessment tools should be used for oral assessment. Nurses need to timely record the changes of patients' oral symptoms and nutritional status, so that radiation oncologists and dentists can adjust treatment measures according to patients' conditions, and nutritionists can provide individualized nutritional support for patients.

(2)Optimization of the nursing management process for RIOM

Optimization of the nursing management process for RIOM

To standardize nursing interventions before and during radiotherapy, the research team refined the departmental nursing management process for RIOM in patients with HNC. Before radiotherapy, nurses provided face-to-face health education to patients and their families and distributed education booklet. During radiotherapy, nurses need to timely record the severity of RIOM symptoms, oral pain and the onset time of different RIOM grades, and dynamically adjust the corresponding nursing interventions according to the severity of RIOM. Following the initiation of radiotherapy, nurses implemented preventive nursing measures against RIOM, including basic oral care, oral functional exercises, and dietary guidance. Once RIOM developed, enhanced monitoring of patients’ oral and nutritional status was required, along with reinforced nursing interventions focusing on oral hygiene, rinsing with mouthwash, and dietary nutrition. In this study, Kangfuxin solution, a traditional Chinese medicine preparation, was used as the mouthwash. When RIOM symptoms worsened, nurses were expected to communicate with radiation oncologists and dentists in a timely manner to initiate pharmacological treatments such as analgesics and anti-infective agents. If nutritional risk was identified, nutritionists were consulted promptly to provide nutrition-related support.

(3)Standardization of oral mucositis assessment tool utilization by nurses and patients

Standardization of oral mucositis assessment tool utilization by nurses and patients

To standardize the use of oral mucositis assessment tools by both nurses and patients, the research team compared several domestic and international tools to evaluate their strengths and limitations. Based on the findings, the WHO Oral Mucositis Grading Scale27 was selected for nurse assessments, and the Oral Mucositis Daily Questionnaire (OMDQ), translated into Chinese by Gu Yanhong et al.,28 was adopted for patient self-assessment. This approach facilitated health care professionals in monitoring changes in patients’ oral symptoms, enabling the timely implementation of appropriate treatment and nursing interventions to effectively manage symptom progression.

(4)Development of patient education materials for RIOM

Development of patient education materials for RIOM

To assist nurses in providing more comprehensive and systematic health education and to enhance the effectiveness of education as well as patient adherence, the research team developed a health education booklet focused on RIOM care for HNC patients based on literature review and evidence synthesis. Nurses distributed the booklet to patients before radiotherapy and encouraged patients and their family members to actively participate in the symptom management of RIOM.

(5)Conducting nurse education and training

Conducting nurse education and training

Before implementing the EBP protocol, the research team provided targeted educational sessions to all nurses in the department to enhance their knowledge and skills. The training first focused on specialized instruction regarding RIOM, covering its pathogenesis, epidemiological characteristics, influencing factors, and the progression of clinical symptoms, in order to strengthen nurses' understanding and awareness of RIOM. Following this knowledge training, the researchers introduced the specific components of the protocol, including the standardized assessment and nursing management procedures, as well as the application of relevant oral assessment tools. This aimed to improve nurses’ clinical competence and adherence in executing the EBP protocol.

Evaluation indicators

Implementation rate of review indicators by nurses

The implementation of various review indicators of nurses was observed and recorded using the clinical review record form designed by the research team. “√” was used to indicate that the index was implemented, and “ ​× ​” was used to indicate that the index was not implemented. The implementation rate for each review index was calculated as a percentage, where a higher percentage indicated greater implementation.

Incidence of severe RIOM

Incidence of severe RIOM (Grade III/IV) ​= ​number of patients with severe RIOM/total number of patients in the statistical period ​× ​100%. At the end of radiotherapy, the severity of RIOM in patients was evaluated according to the WHO oral mucositis grading standard.27 The clinical manifestations were graded as follows: Grade 0: No oral mucositis; Grade I: Oral soreness, erythema; Grade II: Oral erythema, ulcers, can eat solids; Grade III: Oral ulcers, requires a liquid diet only; Grade IV: Oral alimentation not possible. Grades I and II indicate mild to moderate RIOM, whereas grades III and IV indicate severe RIOM.

Oral pain score of patients

At the end of radiotherapy, patients’ oral pain was assessed using the Numeric Rating Scale (NRS), which ranged from 0 to 10 to reflect varying pain intensities. A score of 0 indicated no pain; 1–3 indicated mild pain; 4–6 indicated moderate pain; and ≥ 7 indicated severe pain. The score was positively correlated with pain severity.

Time to onset of different grades of RIOM

During radiotherapy, the severity of RIOM was graded using the WHO oral mucositis standard.27 Each patient with HNC was monitored for the occurrence of RIOM, and the time of onset for each grade of RIOM was recorded.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 27.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Categorical variables were presented as frequencies (n) and percentages (%), and comparisons between groups were performed using the χ2 test. Continuous variables with a non-normal distribution were expressed as medians and interquartile ranges (IQR), and group differences were assessed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Normally distributed continuous variables were presented as mean ​± ​standard (SD) deviation and compared between groups using independent samples t-tests. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

Evidence-based practice can improve the implementation rate of review indicators of nurses

After the implementation of the EBP, nurses showed a significant improvement in implementation with each review indicator for RIOM care in patients with HNC (P ​< ​0.05). Among them, the implementation rate of the review index 3 “nurses perform oral assessment at least once a week after the first radiotherapy of patients” increased from 0 at baseline to 90.74% after the application of the practice. This improvement may be attributed to the fact that during the baseline review, nurses did not perform regular weekly oral assessments starting from the initiation of radiotherapy. The department lacked standardized procedures for the assessment and care of RIOM in patients with HNC, and nurses had insufficient awareness of the need for early evaluation and intervention, leading to low implementation rates.

In response, the developed protocol included standardized procedures for assessment and nursing management. It specified that radiotherapy nurses should conduct regular evaluations and provide nursing interventions before and during treatment. Before implementation, comprehensive training was provided to all nurses on RIOM-related knowledge and the content of the practice protocol. This training offered scientific guidance for clinical behavior, enhanced nurses’ initiative, and improved their ability to execute the protocol effectively.

In addition, this study addressed the barrier of inadequate patient education materials by developing a health education booklet grounded in evidence and expert opinions. The booklet helped nurses reduce their workload while enhancing the effectiveness of patient education and care guidance. Consequently, it improved nursing efficiency and enhanced the execution of care practices, thereby facilitating the implementation of evidence in clinical settings and bridging the gap between evidence and practice.

Evidence-based practice can reduce the incidence of severe RIOM and oral pain in patients

Patients with severe RIOM (Grade III/IV) often experience severe pain and difficulties in eating, which significantly affect their quality of life and hinder the process of radiotherapy. This is a major reason for interruptions or even termination of radiotherapy plans. Oral pain level is one of the key evaluation criteria for grading RIOM symptoms—higher pain levels indicate greater severity. Reducing oral pain helps improve patients’ tolerance to radiotherapy and is essential for ensuring the successful completion of treatment.

The results of this study showed that after implementing the EBP, the incidence of severe RIOM in HNC patients at the end of radiotherapy decreased from 43.64% during the baseline review to 24.07%, with no cases of grade IV RIOM. The oral pain score also dropped from (5.53 ​± ​1.62) to (4.44 ​± ​1.54) (P ​< ​0.05), indicating a significant reduction in RIOM severity. Previous study29 has also shown that implementing EBP could effectively reduce the incidence of RIOM and the level of oral pain in patients with nasopharyngeal carcinoma, which was consistent with the findings of this study. This confirmed that EBP, as a scientific decision-making model, could significantly improve clinical outcomes for RIOM by integrating the best available evidence with clinical experience. Research has indicated that symptom management of oral mucositis should focus on monitoring symptoms, controlling pain, and maintaining oral hygiene, which were the most effective strategies to prevent or reduce progression.30 Furthermore, early intervention could reduce pain and improve nutritional status.31

In the EBP protocol developed in this study, a series of nursing interventions were implemented collaboratively by radiotherapy nurses together with radiation oncologists, dentists, and nutritionists. The protocol systematically integrated oral assessment and care, nutritional assessment and support, and multi-channel health education, thereby ensuring that the nursing management of RIOM was proactive, standardized, continuous, and anticipatory throughout the entire radiotherapy process. This enabled nurses to monitor patients’ RIOM symptoms in a timely manner and to provide early nursing interventions aimed at reducing the severity of RIOM.

Evidence-based practice can delay the onset time of RIOM in patients

Delaying the onset of RIOM allows patients to maintain relatively normal oral function for a longer duration, which contributes to enhanced tolerance to radiotherapy and ensures smooth implementation of radiotherapy. In this study, after the application of the EBP, the onset time of grade I RIOM was significantly later than that at baseline review (P ​< ​0.05). Previous research32 has shown that evidence-based oral health behavior care programs can significantly delay the onset time of mild RIOM in patients with HNC, which was consistent with the results of this study.

The analysis indicates that, within the EBP protocol developed in this study, radiation oncology nurses collaborated with radiation oncologists, dentists, and nutritionists in a multidisciplinary manner to provide patients with professional and systematic interventions, such as oral hygiene guidance and nutritional support. In addition, nurses conducted regular symptom monitoring and documentation during radiotherapy and guided patients in implementing early preventive interventions. Multidisciplinary collaborative management before and during radiotherapy can improve the tolerance of patients to the adverse effects of radiotherapy and delay the onset and progression of RIOM symptoms to a certain extent.

In this study, there was no significant difference in the onset time of grade Ⅱ and Ⅲ RIOM before and after the application of the practice (P ​> ​0.05). This may be attributed to the fact that the severity of mucosal injury is positively correlated with the progression of radiotherapy. In the later stages of radiotherapy, the cumulative dose of ionizing radiation to mucosal cells increases, accelerating the severity of RIOM symptoms.33 At this stage, individualized treatment and care provided by health care professionals are essential for preventing the progression of severe symptoms. Therefore, future research should focus on enriching the content of EBP to better address this issue.

Limitations

Due to constraints in manpower, material resources, and limited time, the EBP in this study was only reviewed and applied in the clinic for a short period of time, and the sustainability of the EBP in clinical application needs to be tracked. Follow-up studies need to dynamically optimize the EBP protocol and pay attention to the long-term effects of application. In the process of EBP, new evidence should be introduced in time to promote the continuous improvement of nursing quality and the routinization of clinical practice reform.

Conclusions

Guided by the KTA framework, this study implemented an EBP intervention for the care of patients with RIOM in HNC. The effectiveness of the intervention was evaluated before and after its implementation. The results demonstrated an improvement in nurses' clinical execution of RIOM care, a reduction in the incidence of severe RIOM, and a decrease in patients’ oral pain levels, as well as a delay in the onset of RIOM. These findings confirmed the feasibility and effectiveness of the intervention protocol. The study achieved successful knowledge translation from evidence to practice and facilitated clinical practice change, promoting the integration of evidence into nursing care.

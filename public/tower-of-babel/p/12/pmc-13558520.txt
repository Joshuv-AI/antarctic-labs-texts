Characteristics of Dupilumab-Treated Patients with Atopic Dermatitis Over Time: A Cross-Sectional Assessment of Potential Channeling Bias upon Pediatric Label Extension
Authors: Julie Barberio, Venkatesh Harikrishnan, Shiyao Gao, Andrea Marcus, Sarah-Jo Sinnott
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionSelectively prescribing new medications to patients with severe disease can lead to channeling bias in observational studies. Potential for channeling upon label extension has not been described. The objective of this study was to describe dupilumab-treated patients with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (AD) at initial United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval for adults (March 2017) and at label extensions to adolescents (March 2019), children (May 2020), and young children (June 2022).MethodsA cross-sectional study was conducted using Optum’s de-identified Clinformatics® Data Mart Database to describe baseline characteristics of patients with AD in the US who initiated dupilumab within 6 months of FDA approval or label extension.ResultsDupilumab-treated patients with AD at initial approval, versus the most recent label update, had a higher prevalence of comorbid Type 2 inflammatory diseases (e.g., asthma: 33% versus 22%), a larger proportion of prior systemic immunosuppressant use (31% versus 5%), and more frequent dermatologist visits in the previous year (mean 6.1 versus 3.1 visits). Newly licensed adolescents and children had a greater burden of atopic comorbidities and a different pattern of prior AD treatment than adults.ConclusionPatients initiating dupilumab in March 2017 had markers of high disease burden, consistent with channeling. As the label extended, the population shifted toward the indicated moderate-to-severe phenotype. Channeling trends were difficult to assess given the well-established differences in comorbidities and treatment patterns in pediatric AD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s13555-026-01859-y.

Introduction

Newly marketed medications are more likely than existing therapies to be prescribed to patients with more severe or poorly controlled disease [1–3]. Over time, as evidence accumulates supporting real-world safety and effectiveness, uptake increases among the general indicated patient population [1, 4]. The user population in the early marketing phase is therefore expected to have a worse baseline risk of adverse outcomes than the general indicated population, which can lead to confounding, referred to as channeling bias, in observational safety studies [5].

Although channeling bias and methods to mitigate it are well recognized [1–3], the potential for its sequential occurrence during label extensions (e.g., from adult to pediatric populations) has not been described. Traditionally, products are first authorized by the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for adults, with supplementary approvals for pediatric patients occurring an average of 8–10 years later [6–8]. During this lag time, prescribing experience and perceived benefit for adults commonly lead to “off-label” prescribing to children [9, 10]. It is therefore unclear whether patients exposed to a medication following pediatric label extensions are affected by channeling bias in the same way as the initial population.

Here, we use dupilumab as an example of a therapy for which channeling bias could exist among new-user populations upon label extension. FDA initially approved dupilumab for the treatment of moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (AD) in adults (March 2017), then expanded to adolescents ≥ 12 years (March 2019), children 6–11 years (May 2020), and young children 6 months–5 years (June 2022) (Table 1). Our objective was to examine the characteristics of the evolving population of dupilumab-treated patients with AD to determine whether channeling bias exists during pediatric label extension. Understanding the presence and magnitude of such bias is critical for the appropriate interpretation of real-world safety and effectiveness data for dupilumab and other therapies with sequential label extensions. Methods

Data Source

We used Optum’s de-identified Clinformatics® Data Mart Database (CDM), which contains administrative claims for over 65 million US commercial and Medicare Advantage health plan members.

Study Population

All patients with their first dupilumab dispensing between 28 March 2017 and 30 November 2023 were identified. Those included were aged ≥ 1 year and had ≥ 365 days of prior continuous enrollment with ≥ 1 AD diagnosis (International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision [ICD-10] L20.x). Although dupilumab is licensed for patients with AD aged ≥ 6 months of age, the database’s lack of birth month information prevented precise identification of infants < 1 year.

Statistical Analyses

All analyses were descriptive and considered patient characteristics prior to dupilumab initiation. Diagnoses, procedures, and pharmacy dispensings were derived from billing codes, with absence of claims indicating absence of conditions or services. We examined demographics, comorbidities, prior AD medications, and healthcare utilization (see Tables S1 and S2 in the electronic supplementary material) in a 365-day baseline period prior to dupilumab initiation. Other diseases typically arising from Type 2 inflammation—including asthma, allergic rhinitis, and food allergies—often occur as comorbidities of AD and contribute to the complexity of therapeutic management [15]. These characteristics are useful proxies for disease burden and have been shown to correlate with the severity of AD [16].

To examine potential channeling over time, mutually exclusive subgroups were created based on whether dupilumab initiation occurred within the 6 months following initial FDA approval or subsequent label extension (Table 1). This timeframe captured early uptake while achieving sufficient sample sizes [1, 4]. Baseline characteristics were examined for evidence of channeling based on disease burden. As a sensitivity analysis, the cohort was restricted to adults (≥ 18 years).

Statement of Ethics

In the US, Institutional Review Board review is not required for studies that do not meet the definitions of human subject research or clinical investigation, such as this one. Informed consent is not required for this type of study.

Results

A total of 42,373 new dupilumab-treated patients aged ≥ 1 year between 28 March 2017 and 30 November 2023 were identified, among which 22,926 (54%) had an AD diagnosis. The final analytic cohort included 15,320 patients meeting continuous enrollment requirements.

There were 362 patients with AD newly treated with dupilumab in the 6 months following initial FDA approval. The label update subgroups included 997 (first update), 1233 (second update), and 1747 (third update) patients, respectively (see Table S3 in the electronic supplementary material). Adults (≥ 18 years) made up 99% of the treated population at initial approval but only 80–83% at the three label updates. Those in the March 2017 subgroup, compared with the label update subgroups, were more likely to be male (56% versus 43–46%). A higher prevalence of comorbid Type 2 inflammatory diseases was evident in the March 2017 subgroup compared with the label update subgroups (e.g., asthma: 33% versus 22–28%; allergic rhinitis: 25% versus 19–25%). Non-atopic comorbidities were generally consistent across subgroups. The March 2017 subgroup, compared with the label update subgroups, demonstrated a larger proportion of prior systemic immunosuppressant use (31% versus 5–13%), prior systemic corticosteroid use (76% versus 60–70%), and prior high potency topical corticosteroid use (22% versus 12–21%) (Fig. 1A). Visits to a dermatologist (mean 6.1 versus range of mean 3.1–3.7 visits) or allergist/immunologist (mean 2.9 versus range of mean 1.6–2.0 visits) in the year prior to dupilumab initiation were more frequent in the March 2017 subgroup than in the label update subgroups. The frequencies of inpatient hospitalizations, emergency room visits, and outpatient visits were generally consistent across subgroups (Fig. 1B). Finally, characteristics of the label update subgroups were examined comparing the newly approved age groups with the entire licensed populations. At the first label update (March 2019), 16.2% (n = 162) of dupilumab-treated patients with AD in the entire licensed population (patients aged ≥ 12 years) belonged to the newly approved age group (adolescents aged ≥ 12 to < 18 years) (See Table S5 in the electronic supplementary material). Compared with the entire licensed population, the newly licensed adolescents had a higher prevalence of atopic comorbidities including asthma (49% versus 28%), allergic rhinitis (45% versus 25%), and food allergy (20% versus 8%). The newly licensed adolescents were less likely than the entire licensed population in March 2019 to have previously used systemic corticosteroids (50% versus 69%) or very-high-potency topical corticosteroids (22% versus 31%) yet were more likely to have used a phosphodiesterase-4 inhibitor (24% versus 16%) in the year prior to dupilumab initiation. No meaningful differences were observed in prior use of systemic immunosuppressants (15% versus 13%) (Fig. 2A). At the second label update (May 2020), 6.8% (n = 84) dupilumab-treated patients with AD in the entire licensed population (patients aged ≥ 6 years) belonged to the newly approved age group (children aged ≥ 6 to < 12 years) (see Table S5 in the electronic supplementary material). Compared with the entire licensed population, the newly licensed children had a higher prevalence of atopic comorbidities including asthma (46% versus 27%), allergic rhinitis (50% versus 22%), and food allergy (14% versus 5%). The newly licensed children were less likely than the entire licensed population in May 2020 to have previously used systemic corticosteroids (57% versus 66%) or very-high-potency topical corticosteroids (18% versus 31%) in the year before dupilumab initiation. Prior uses of medium (80% versus 62%) and low (38% versus 22%) potency topical corticosteroids, topical calcineurin inhibitors (35% versus 27%), phosphodiesterase-4 inhibitors (23% versus 12%), and oral antihistamines (36% versus 25%) were more common in the newly licensed children than the entire licensed population (Fig. 2A). Visits to an allergist/immunologist in the year prior to dupilumab initiation were more frequent in the newly licensed children (mean 5.3 versus 1.8 visits), but the frequencies of visits to a dermatologist, emergency room, outpatient setting, or inpatient setting were generally consistent (Fig. 2B).

At the third label update (June 2022), 7.1% (n = 124) dupilumab-treated patients with AD in the entire licensed population (patients age ≥ 1 year) belonged to the newly approved age group (young children aged < 6 years) (see Table S5 in the electronic supplementary material). Compared with the entire licensed population, the newly licensed young children had a higher prevalence of some atopic comorbidities, including allergic rhinitis (29% versus 19%) and food allergy (31% versus 6%); however, the prevalence of asthma was not meaningfully different (19% versus 22%). The newly licensed young children were less likely than the entire licensed population in June 2022 to have previously used systemic corticosteroids (44% versus 60%) or very-high-potency topical corticosteroids (20% versus 35%) in the year prior to dupilumab initiation. Prior uses of medium (81% versus 63%) and low (59% versus 27%) potency topical corticosteroids, topical calcineurin inhibitors (43% versus 29%), phosphodiesterase-4 inhibitors (23% versus 9%), and oral antihistamines (35% versus 24%) were more common in the newly licensed young children compared with the entire licensed population (Fig. 2A). The frequencies of visits to an allergist/immunologist, dermatologist, emergency room, outpatient setting, or inpatient setting in the year prior to dupilumab initiation were not meaningfully different in the newly licensed young children compared with the overall licensed population (Fig. 2B).

Discussion

We used nationwide administrative claims data from the US to describe the characteristics of real-world dupilumab users with AD over time. New dupilumab-treated patients with AD at initial FDA approval, compared with three subsequent label updates, had a higher prevalence of comorbid Type 2 inflammatory diseases, a larger proportion of prior use of systemic immunosuppressants and systemic corticosteroids, and more frequent visits to dermatologists and allergists/immunologists in the previous year. Males comprised 56% of initial dupilumab users but only 43–46% at label updates, possibly reflecting associations between male sex and AD severity[20–22] or treatment allocation bias [23–25]. These findings suggest higher disease burden in the initially treated population [16, 18, 19]. The prevalence of disease burden markers in the treated population decreased over time; dupilumab-treated patients at the label updates had profiles of atopic comorbidities, prior AD medications, and healthcare utilization reflective of a moderate-to-severe AD phenotype. This observed trend is consistent with expectations of initial channeling to patients with worse baseline risk profiles, followed by a greater uptake in the general indicated population [1, 4]. The shift in characteristics over time to include patients across the full spectrum of the indicated moderate-to-severe population may be driven by many factors, including broader clinical experience and confidence with the medication as well as evolving reimbursement criteria. These findings highlight that early real-world studies of dupilumab may have enrolled a population with higher baseline disease burden, which could influence the interpretation of safety and effectiveness outcomes. Researchers conducting observational studies should account for the time period of dupilumab initiation relative to approval dates and consider time-stratified analyses to mitigate the impact of channeling bias.

Age-stratified analyses revealed patterns of characteristics masked in overall analyses. The newly approved pediatric groups showed higher rates of allergic rhinitis and food allergies compared with the entire licensed population. Asthma was more prevalent in newly approved adolescents and children, but not young children, likely reflecting diagnostic challenges in this age group [26]. Increased allergist/immunologist visits among the newly licensed pediatric groups aligned with their higher prevalence of atopic comorbidities [27, 28]. Such evolution in comorbidity profiles is likely a reflection of the well-established atopic march during infancy and childhood [29, 30]. The newly licensed age groups also showed distinct treatment patterns, with less use of systemic and high-potency topical corticosteroids but greater use of low-/medium-potency topical corticosteroids and calcineurin inhibitors. This pattern reflects established pediatric AD treatment guidelines, which recommend less potent topical therapies and advise against oral corticosteroids in children, rather than indicating differences in disease severity [31, 32].

Assessing channeling in newly approved pediatric groups was challenging because of their distinct comorbidity and treatment profiles. Clinical measures of severity, had they been available in this data source, would likely have been more useful to address this question. Regardless, understanding the evolving characteristics of dupilumab users in terms of comorbidity profiles and prior history of AD treatments remains important for contextualizing real-world medication safety. For example, atopic diseases are risk factors for anaphylaxis and hypersensitivity reactions [33, 34] as well as organ-specific complications (e.g., lungs, intestinal tract).[35]. At the time of label extension, increased prescribing to pediatric patients, who have a higher prevalence of comorbid atopic conditions than adult patients, could theoretically result in a higher observed rate of such adverse events, which could be flagged in routine pharmacovigilance processes. Understanding how new dupilumab users at different points in time may have diverse baseline profiles of atopic comorbidities and risks of adverse events could therefore help inform pharmacovigilance activities. Researchers conducting real-world observational studies of dupilumab safety and effectiveness should therefore consider the time period of treatment initiation relative to approval dates as a potential source of confounding, and time-stratified analyses may help mitigate the impact of channeling bias.

This analysis is not without limitations. Validated measures of AD severity were not available for this population from physician-rated AD assessments (e.g., Eczema Area and Severity Index, Overall Disease Severity score, and body surface area affected by AD) or patient-reported outcomes (e.g., Patient-Oriented Eczema Measure). Therefore, burden of disease was proxied based on atopic comorbidities, systemic treatments received, and measures of healthcare utilization, as has been done in prior real-world studies [18, 19, 36, 37]. Additionally, findings may not generalize beyond US insured populations because of different dupilumab prescribing practices and reimbursement criteria.

Conclusion

When dupilumab was newly marketed in March 2017, patients exhibited markers of higher disease burden, consistent with expected channeling patterns. Over time, the treated population shifted toward the indicated moderate-to-severe AD phenotype. Label extension to pediatric patients introduced populations with different atopic comorbidities and treatment profiles, consistent with well-established patterns. These analyses demonstrate how newly licensed group characteristics become masked in overall analyses as the treated population expands, which should be considered in future real-world medication safety and effectiveness studies.

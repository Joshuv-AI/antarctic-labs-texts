Wired for care: a cross-sectional survey protocol of health information technology, resilience and performance in Canadian long-term care homes
Authors: Mirou Jaana, Danielle Cruise, Tarek Khalil, Guy Paré, Haitham Tamim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionCanadian long-term care (LTC) homes have faced long-standing challenges with quality of care, staffing and resource limitations. In Ontario, specifically, there has been increased focus on resilience and performance in LTC homes given the burden that the COVID-19 pandemic presented (eg, highest number of resident deaths due to COVID-19 across Canada, staffing crisis). Nevertheless, little is known about the current practices and operational state of LTC homes in the province and limited data are collected on Ontario’s LTC homes at the organisational level and on the factors that may contribute to their resilience and improved performance. We propose a research protocol that aims to: (1) assess the organisational characteristics and resources in LTC homes in Ontario; and (2) support an in-depth analysis of the relationship between organisational characteristics and health information technology (HIT) resources, resilience and performance for preparedness and system strengthening.Methods and analysisAll LTC homes in Ontario will be invited to participate in a cross-sectional survey to assess their organisational characteristics and HIT resources and investigate their resilience and performance. In the absence of a complete and detailed list of LTC homes in Ontario, our research team extracted publicly available data from multiple sources to develop a comprehensive list of all LTC homes in Ontario with relevant administrative contact information. This consolidated list will be used in the data collection stage. HIT indices will also be developed for electronic medical records and assistive technology to assess LTC homes’ digital capabilities and level of information technology maturity. Exploratory factor analysis will be conducted to identify the underlying factor structure of the measured variables and develop composite measures of digital capabilities/HIT maturity, of resilience and performance. Multivariate analysis will be conducted to examine the relationships between organisational characteristics/HIT use and organisational performance, while controlling for resilience.Ethics and disseminationThis study received ethics approval from the University of Ottawa Research Ethics Board (S-06-25-11678). The results of this research will be submitted to relevant peer-reviewed journals and presented at professional and academic conferences. A one-page executive summary of the results will also be shared with the study participants and the two LTC associations in Ontario (ie, Ontario Long-Term Care Association and AdvantAge).Keywords: Surveys and Questionnaires, Information technology, Health policy, Quality in health care, Health Services for the Aged, HEALTH SERVICES ADMINISTRATION & MANAGEMENT

Background

In Canada, an ageing population with higher life expectancy presents increased risk of older adults developing chronic disease, leading to greater demand for long-term care (LTC).1 Canadian LTC homes provide 24-hour nursing and personal care for those who are unable to live independently (eg, older adults with cognitive impairment).2 Across Canada, there are 2076 LTC homes (ie, 54% privately owned and 46% publicly owned).3 As LTC is not covered under the Canada Health Act (ie, federal legislation that governs Canada’s public healthcare funding4), each province is responsible for their own legislation and health administration, which has created inconsistencies in the quality of care and services offered.5 In Ontario (ie, province with the largest number of older adults and LTC homes3 and the largest healthcare jurisdiction6 7), there has been a greater focus on resilience and performance improvement in LTC homes.8 This is the result of a pressing need to address systemic underfunding and human resource challenges (eg, understaffing, burnout, high turnover, inadequate pay), along with other challenges that were exacerbated by the latest COVID-19 pandemic (eg, poor residents’ quality of life, inadequate personal protective equipment).5 9 10 Recent research suggests that the challenges faced by LTC homes during the pandemic were both predictable and preventable; however, failure to adequately support the LTC workforce, integrate the LTC system with other healthcare areas and address the complexity of residents’ needs contributed to the poor performance of Canadian LTC homes.8 Currently, Ontario lacks a gold-standard for assessing the performance of LTC homes, with only some homes measuring residents’ quality of care using deficit-oriented approaches (eg, percentage of residents experiencing pain) and limited data collected and available for research at the organisational level (eg, staffing levels).5

Prior studies have considered performance in LTC from the lens of quality of care and residents’ health outcomes. For example, a recent systematic review that aimed to identify the health-related quality indicators that are publicly reported internationally presented 99 clinical quality indicators in LTC homes (eg, pressure ulcers, weight loss).11 Other studies conducted in Canada discussed LTC home characteristics that were associated with lower quality of care outcomes, including size and staffing levels (ie, smaller homes having lower staffing levels and showing lower quality of care outcomes).12 13 Nevertheless, we have limited understanding of the staffing practices in LTC homes, the characteristics of these organisations and their resources, among other domains that are commonly used to monitor and assess organisations.14,16 Thus, scholars have called for more research into performance management in LTC homes to accelerate improvements in this area.5

While performance concerns were commonly discussed in the Canadian LTC sector,5 9 17 some LTC homes were more successful than others in adapting during times of crisis.18 For example, data published by the Government of Ontario on the COVID-19 deaths in LTC homes indicates that some LTC homes had no/very few resident deaths due to COVID-19, while others had a high COVID-19 infection and death rates among residents.19 Despite the success of some LTC homes in adapting during the pandemic, it is unclear what factors contributed to their response (eg, beyond the availability of resources, such as personal protective equipment, human resources).20 In this context, resilience emerges as an important organisational capability. It refers to the process by which a LTC home ‘builds and uses its capability endowments to interact with the environment in a way that positively adjusts and maintains functioning prior to, during, and following adversity’.21 In LTC, we have limited knowledge on the types and extent of resilience, with most health services research on resilience focusing on primary care.22

Studies on health information technologies (HIT) use in LTC homes have discussed the importance of these tools for maintaining the services provided to residents in times of crises (eg, therapeutic arts and recreation services through video conferencing platforms, such as Skype and Zoom).23 Specific HIT resources include assistive technologies (AT) and electronic medical records (EMRs), given their potential to support the care provided by healthcare professionals and benefit older adults through improved activity, social engagement, disease management and safety. HIT innovations and tools (eg, Teams, Zoom, telehealth) can play an important role in transforming healthcare organisations’ practices and improving performance.24 Yet, despite their relevance in supporting organisational resilience and performance in other healthcare organisations,24 25 HIT innovations have not been thoroughly studied in relation to performance in LTC homes.

This research contributes to this understudied area by proposing a cross-sectional survey in Ontario that aims to: (1) Assess organisational characteristics and HIT use in LTC homes; and (2) Examine the relationship between organisational characteristics and HIT use, resilience and performance in LTC homes. Therefore, this study will present baseline information on LTC homes in Ontario, the largest province accounting for around 40% of the Canadian population,26 and enable a better understanding of the state of LTC and the role of HIT and resilience in shaping organisational performance. To ensure quality, transparency and avoid duplication of work,27 we present below the details of our survey protocol.

Methods

Research design

The protocol involves a cross-sectional survey of all 615 LTC homes across Ontario,28 representing a wide geographical area (ie, most populous province in Canada with the largest number of LTC homes3) covering a population of over 76 000 older adults living in LTC homes.28

Sample size considerations

A comprehensive sampling approach will be employed using a sampling frame comprising 615 LTC homes; with each LTC home serving as a sampling unit. Based on response rates reported in prior studies (20–30%),29 30 we anticipate obtaining between 123 and 185 responses. This estimate is consistent with the minimum sample size threshold of 189, as calculated using Cochrane’s formula for categorical data,31 assuming a 90% confidence level, 5% margin of error and p=0.5.

Participants

To satisfy the eligibility criteria for inclusion within the study, potential respondents should meet the following criteria. Under the Long-Term Care Homes Act, there are three specific staffing requirements for all LTC homes, including a home administrator (ie, responsible for the overall management of the LTC home), a director of nursing and personal care (ie, a registered nurse who is responsible for supervising and directing all aspects of nursing and personal care, as well as providing direct care to residents when needed) and a medical director (ie, a physician who is responsible for evaluating, addressing and providing all aspects of medical practice and clinical procedures within the LTC home).32 Based on the scope of their roles, the home administrator, director of nursing and personal care and medical director of each LTC home would be the most knowledgeable and best positioned to provide information on the organisational characteristics, resilience and performance of their respective LTC home. As Ontario does not have a consolidated list of administrators, directors of nursing and personal care and medical directors, the research team developed a list of the detailed contact information of all administrators/directors in the LTC home in Ontario using publicly available information (eg, LTC homes’ websites, Ministry of Long-Term Care website).33 To ensure that potential respondents meet this requirement and eligibility criteria to be able to respond to the survey, it will be exclusively sent to the administrators/directors. Specifically, one response per LTC home is sought from a member of the senior leadership team (ie, chief executive officer/home administrator, director of care or equivalent or medical director), with only one completed survey included per LTC home.

Data collection and management

Figure 1 illustrates the data collection process. Three concurrent recruitment strategies will be used. First, a paper copy of the survey, along with a stamped return envelope, will be mailed to each LTC home in Ontario using publicly available contact information for the home administrator, director of nursing or medical director. Second, an electronic version of the survey (via a SurveyMonkey link) will be emailed to the same contact person at each LTC home. Participants may choose to complete either the paper-based or electronic version of the survey, depending on their preferred mode of participation. These recruitment and response options will be implemented concurrently to maximise accessibility and response rates. Third, we will also contact the two associations that represent the for-profit and not-for-profit LTC homes in Ontario (ie, Ontario Long-Term Care Association and AdvantAge, respectively), to inform them about the current survey and solicit their support by sharing this information with their members. Interested members, who may have mislocated the mailed or emailed survey, may then contact the research team to obtain a new copy. Respondents will be provided with a period of 4 weeks to respond to the survey; reminders will be sent halfway during this period.

Survey instrument

Recommendations and best practices for survey design in the literature guided the development of the questionnaire,34,36 which covers five sections related to: organisational characteristics; HIT use (EMRs and AT); facilitating conditions; resilience; and performance. The specific questions in each section were informed by a thorough literature review and anchored in the definitions of established constructs (table 1).37,44 Completing the questionnaire is expected to take around 20 min in duration. Each of the survey sections is described in greater detail below and examples of survey questions are presented in online supplemental material 1.

Section 1: LTC home characteristics

In an effort to reduce the length of the questionnaire, we extracted secondary data on some of the organisational characteristics of Ontario’s LTC homes that are publicly available (ie, profit status, geographical location, number of beds, bed classifications and waitlists for LTC placement).45 This led to the inclusion of only seven closed-ended survey questions that assess LTC homes’ organisational characteristics on which we are unable to find available secondary data related to human resources, the resident population and HIT capacities of the organisation.

Section 2: HIT use

Older adults in LTC homes have complex physical/mental health needs, and often live with frailty and cognitive impairment, thus requiring a high level of assistance to complete activities of daily living5 and extra work from caregivers/HC professionals.46 In addition to the high care needs of older adults in this environment, the prevalence of loneliness and social isolation is almost double that observed among home-dwelling older adults.47 Thus, AT presents promising solutions for addressing these challenges, by supporting HC professionals,48 improving communication and safety and reducing loneliness.49 Specifically, AT is an umbrella term that refers to the products and systems used to ‘help maintain or improve an individual’s functioning related to cognition, communication, hearing, mobility, self-care and vision, thus enabling their health, well-being, inclusion and participation’.50 The definition of AT and what they encompass may vary considerably. For example, assistive products (eg, handrails, chairs for shower), assistive devices (eg, wheelchairs, hearing aids), AT devices (eg, touchscreens, sensors) are all considered types of AT.49 For this research, we focus on AT devices, given their potential to address the challenges faced in LTC and present benefits to older adults’ activity, social engagement, disease management and safety.51 These include ambient living technologies (eg, sensors and devices for completing tasks); social interaction technologies (eg, touchscreen tablets, video conferencing); and robots (eg, pet robots/companions, assistive robots in physiotherapy).52,56 Thus, in this study, we assess LTC homes’ adoption and use of AT and the perceived impacts on residents’ care across 26 questions. First, 2 questions are general and capture familiarity with AT and the AT adoption profile of the LTC home. Second, 12 closed-ended questions dive into more details on the types of AT, their use and the proportion of residents benefitting from them (measured on 1–5 Likert scale). Last, 11 questions measure the perceived impacts of various AT (along the three categories specified above) on residents’ care (1–5 Likert scale).

EMRs have been shown to be an essential resource for improving and supporting quality of care, residents’ quality of life, clinical decision-making and overall home performance.57 58 In Ontario, PointClickCare has the largest market share with their EMRs software implemented in 367 LTC homes, representing around 60% of the homes in Ontario.59 Across LTC homes, the use of this software varies, as each home must purchase specific modules (ie, additional features and components that enable the software to serve different functions) based on their individual needs.59 Beyond the homes that use PointClickCare, it is unclear what software is being used (if any), as limited evidence exists on HIT implementation and maturity in LTC homes in the province.60 Given the potential for EMRs to impact the performance of a LTC home (eg, through providing access to relevant resident data in a timely manner, increasing patient safety and improving physician efficiency),61 62 and the variation in use across LTC homes in Ontario, the survey includes a section (14 closed-ended and Likert scale questions) that assesses the LTC home EMRs implementation state and the functions being used. Last, two HIT open-ended questions are included at the end of the survey to allow participants to express their own opinions,27 and capture these leaders’ perspectives on the technology priority areas of investment.

Section 3: Facilitating conditions

Facilitating conditions refer to resources and infrastructure available to support the use of HIT solutions.63 This survey includes six questions (1–5 Likert scale) related to staff training on digital technologies, technological infrastructure and tools and available support.

Section 4: Resilience

Different types of resilience have been discussed in the literature including: absorptive resilience (ie, the ability of the organisation to take in challenges with the goal of maintaining organisational functioning and service delivery64); adaptive resilience (ie, the organisations’ ability to continue providing services while making changes at the organisational level based on the availability of resources65); and transformative resilience (ie, innovative changes in the functions and structures of organisations that allow them to ‘bounce forward’ and improve their processes and systems65,67) capacities. We will assess resilience in our survey across this classification using 10 questions measured on a 1–5 Likert scale.

Section 5: Performance

We will extract secondary publicly available data on the performance of LTC homes from Health Quality Ontario’s website,68 which capture quality of care measures (ie, experiences of pain, worsening pain, falls in the last 30 days, improved physical functioning, inappropriate use of antipsychotics, restraint use, worsened depressive mood, worsened physical functioning and worsened pressure injuries). Additionally, participants will be asked to complete seven questions (closed-ended and 1–5 Likert scale) that complement these measures and assess the LTC homes’ organisational performance in relation to resources (eg, funding, utilisation), staff turnover and digital system outages. Demographic questions are included at the end of the survey to capture the respondents’ characteristics.

Table 1 presents a summary of the survey content. The questionnaire was pretested with six managers in one LTC home to ensure that the questions were clearly articulated and that the response options were relevant,69 resulting in minor changes/edits (eg, excluding convalescent care beds from the average length of stay for residents, adding additional examples of ambient living technologies, adding a space to capture the number of occupational and physiotherapists that are employed in the LTC home). Additional details regarding the instrument can be obtained from the first author on request.

Data analysis

Double data entry and verification procedures will be used to create a consolidated dataset integrating both online and mailed survey responses. The primary outcome of interest is organisational performance, assessed through secondary indicators of quality of care as well as survey-based measures of organisational performance. Resilience is conceptualised as a potential confounding variable that may influence organisational capacity, HIT capacity and LTC home performance.

Descriptive analysis will first be used to characterise the participating LTC homes and summarise the domains under investigation. Continuous variables will be reported using means, medians, SD and ranges, whereas categorical variables will be summarised using frequencies and percentages. Exploratory factor analysis (EFA) will then be conducted to identify the underlying factor structure of the measured variables and support construct validity.70 71 The EFA results will inform the development of composite measures representing the key dimensions of digital capabilities/HIT maturity, resilience and performance. In parallel, HIT indices will be developed for EMRs and AT to assess LTC homes’ digital capabilities and level of IT maturity.

Using these composite measures and HIT indices, multivariate analyses will be performed to examine the relationships between organisational characteristics, HIT use and organisational performance while controlling for resilience. No prior bivariate screening will be done to minimise the risk of model misspecification and exclusion of important predictors,72 73 and of overlooking confounding and interactive effects between variables.74 The specification of the regression models will depend on the distributional properties of the outcome variable.75 Model assumptions will be assessed prior to analysis, and findings will be reported using appropriate effect estimates and CIs. All analyses will be conducted using SPSS V.30 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, New York, USA), with statistical significance set at p<0.05.

Ethics and dissemination

This study received ethics approvals from the University of Ottawa Research Ethics Board (S-06-25-11678). The results of this research will be submitted to relevant peer-reviewed journals and presented at professional and academic conferences. A one-page executive summary of the results will be sent to participating LTC homes once the data analysis is complete. The summary of the findings will also be shared with AdvantAge and the Ontario Long-Term Care Association for potential dissemination to their members.

Patient and public involvement

Knowledge users (ie, LTC management and clinicians) were involved in pretesting the survey to ensure its relevance and clarity. Issues identified in the pretesting stage were addressed and the survey was revised accordingly prior to sending to all LTC homes in Ontario. As explained earlier, we will also engage the two associations that represent the LTC homes in Ontario to solicit their support in diffusing the information about the survey and their assistance with knowledge dissemination.

Discussion

Performance, resilience and HIT use are closely interconnected in the health systems literature,76 which suggests that measures of resilience be embedded into performance management systems, given the important role that resilience plays in shaping organisational performance.76 For example, health systems with limited human resources may have a reduced capacity to respond efficiently to emerging threats, which compromises resilience and can affect their quality of care.76 HIT solutions can help improve healthcare professionals’ experience (eg, efficiency and communication with patients)77 and quality of care provided to residents (eg, reducing medical errors, improving the patient experience),78 79 and thus play an important role in shaping organisational resilience and performance. Yet, to the best of our knowledge, research examining organisational characteristics and performance in Canadian LTC homes remains limited,14,16 and it is unclear how HIT use relates to resilience and their impact on how these organisations perform.

This survey protocol aims to fill this gap and provides a methodological foundation to support the rigour, reproducibility and future implementation of the study. It presents the foundation for one of the first province-wide comprehensive datasets on LTC homes allowing the investigation of the relationship between organisational characteristics, HIT capacity, resilience and performance in Ontario LTC homes. It establishes a baseline for future longitudinal research and can serve as a benchmark for national and international comparisons/comparative research and guide preparedness efforts. Once implemented, the survey is expected to inform policy, planning and resource allocation decisions aimed at strengthening the LTC sector and system capacity and preparedness in the face of future crises.

This research contributes to an understudied area by proposing and empirically testing a theoretical model that examines the relationship between organisational characteristics, HIT use, resilience and performance in LTC homes. In light of the literature from the USA on the relationship between organisational characteristics and technologies in LTC homes (eg, LTC homes with more resources and EMRs perform significantly better than LTC homes with fewer resources),79 it is important to investigate whether similar relationships exist in the Canadian context and identify resources that are critical to organisational success. This will provide insights for policymakers and enable planning and inform provincial initiatives.

It is important to note some limitations that may present, which we will attempt to mitigate. Non-response bias is a significant concern associated with survey studies.80 To increase the response rate, we aim to use personalised emails, use two personalised reminder emails/telephone calls and pay attention to the survey length and response choices to minimise the burden on respondents.80 We also pretested the instrument with six managers and ensured that their feedback is incorporated in the revised version of the survey. Other research has acknowledged a lack of transparency and replicability as limitations of survey research.81 To overcome this, the Enhancing Quality and Transparency of Health Research (EQUATOR) Network developed the Consensus-Based Checklist for Reporting of Survey Studies (CROSS) checklist.82 The checklist contains 40 items that are critical for researchers to report when conducting survey research in healthcare,82 which are used in this research. Last, common method bias (CMB) may occur in surveys as a result of social desirability bias (eg, participants that respond to the questions in a manner that is more favourable or acceptable based on societal views, rather than their own perspectives), item ambiguity, use of the same scales across all items contained in the questionnaire and assessment of the predictor and criterion variables at the same point in time, among others.83 Procedural remedies that we will adopt to help overcome possible CMB include ensuring anonymity of the survey responses, reporting the data only at the aggregate level and ensuring the clarity of survey items. Furthermore, when feasible, we will use extracted secondary data published through Health Quality Ontario (eg, on quality of care) and the Ministry of Long-Term Care (eg, on organisational characteristics) to overcome challenges related to the predictor and criterion variables being assessed at the same point in time.

In summary, this study addresses an important gap in the literature on LTC organisations. LTC homes are complex organisations operating in highly dynamic and resource-constrained environments characterised by factors such as unstable funding and persistent workforce shortages, all of which can affect organisational performance.5 Understanding how organisational structures, processes and resources influence performance in these settings is therefore of critical importance. This will generate baseline evidence on organisational characteristics, HIT use, resilience and performance among LTC homes in Ontario. The findings will provide a valuable foundation for future research, benchmarking initiatives, and evidence-informed planning and policy development.

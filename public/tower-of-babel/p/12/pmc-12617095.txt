OA20223. Parents’ attitudes toward childhood vaccination in the pre-pandemic era: Post-pandemic reflections
Authors: A Argyroudi, A Gardikiotis
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe present study investigated how parents are informed about childhood vaccination and how their attitudes toward vaccination influence the information sources they rely on (e.g., pediatricians, public scientific authorities, the internet).MethodsThe study was conducted during the pre-pandemic period (November–December 2018), employing a qualitative research design. A total of 18 parents of children aged 6 months to 14 years participated in semi-structured individual interviews. Participants were asked about their understanding of their child’s health, their perceived severity of childhood diseases, the perceived benefits and risks of vaccination, and how they search for vaccination-related information. Interviews were transcribed and analyzed using thematic analysis.ResultsAccording to the findings, participants categorized as unquestioning acceptors tended to search less frequently for vaccination related information online and expressed strong trust in medical and public health authorities. In contrast, cautious acceptors reported actively seeking information but consulted pediatricians to confirm their findings and address specific concerns. Hesitant participants relied more extensively on the internet and based their trust in pediatricians primarily on personal attitudes—such as whether the pediatrician had vaccinated their own children—rather than on perceived expertise. Therefore, concerns about vaccine safety and side effects were already present before the COVID-19 pandemic. However, recent post-pandemic research highlights growing distrust in vaccine related information, an expansion in the range of information sources (e.g., chatbots, diverse social media platforms), and increased polarization in parental attitudes toward vaccination.ConclusionsFuture research in the current context may confirm or even intensify pre-pandemic patterns, underlining the necessity for further studies to support the design of targeted and effective communication strategies regarding vaccination.Key messages• The present study highlights how parents’ attitudes toward childhood vaccination in the pre-pandemic era influenced their information-seeking behavior and how these patterns are expected to evolve in the post-pandemic context.• The findings underscore the need for further research to inform the development of childhood vaccination campaigns that account for different levels of trust in scientific knowledge and aim to strengthen confidence in credible and official sources.TopicChildhood vaccination, vaccine hesitancy, pandemics.

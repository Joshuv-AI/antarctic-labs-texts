Effect of Ticagrelor Versus Clopidogrel After Off‐Pump Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting on Postoperative Atrial Fibrillation: A Cohort Study
Authors: Qin Jiang, Keli Huang, Lixue Yin, Hong Kong, Zhenglin Yang, Shengshou Hu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study aimed to explore the effect of a P2Y12 inhibitor regimen on the occurrence of postoperative atrial fibrillation (POAF) after off‐pump coronary artery bypass graft surgery in carriers with the cytochrome P450 family 2 subfamily C member19 loss‐of‐function allele.Methods and ResultsFrom May 2019 to November 2023, patients containing the cytochrome P450 family 2 subfamily C member19*2 or *3 allele undergoing elective first‐time off‐pump coronary artery bypass graft surgery including aspirin 100 mg/d and ticagrelor 180 mg/d (AT group; n=95) versus clopidogrel 75 mg/d (aspirin and clopidogrel group; n=95) were prospectively followed. The primary end point was the cumulative incidence of POAF in a week. The secondary end points were POAF burden, platelet aggregability, systemic immune‐inflammation index and heart rate variability. The incidence of POAF was 21.1% in the AT group versus 41.1% in the aspirin and clopidogrel group (hazard ratio, 0.46 [95% CI, 0.27–0.76]; P=0.003). POAF burden, ADP‐induced platelet aggregation and systemic immune‐inflammation index was notably lower in the AT group than the aspirin and clopidogrel group. Heart rate variability data showed an increase in both high‐frequency and SD of normal‐to‐normal RR intervals in the AT group with a decreased low‐frequency/high‐frequency ratio, suggesting that the sympathetic/parasympathetic activation was balanced.ConclusionsIn patients carrying the cytochrome P450 family 2 subfamily C member19 loss‐of‐function allele, an AT regimen after off‐pump coronary artery bypass grafting was associated with a lower incidence of POAF, paralleled by lower atrial fibrillation burden, ADP‐induced platelet aggregation, lower systemic immune‐inflammation index reaction, and a balanced automatic nerve system compared with an aspirin and clopidogrel regimen. Inhibiting the systemic immune‐inflammation response and sustaining automatic nerve balance may underlie the therapeutic effect of POAF by a potent antiplatelet combination.

Postoperative atrial fibrillation (POAF) is a common complication of cardiac surgical procedures, which typically happens during the first week after surgery and is associated with increased long‐term risks such as late atrial fibrillation (AF), stroke, and death. 1 The underlying mechanism of POAF remains incompletely understood. 2 Multiple factors such as the systemic inflammation response and sympathetic nerve excitation are partly responsible for the pathogenesis of POAF, but the data regarding the underlying role of platelet aggregation and platelet‐associated systemic immune inflammation on POAF after coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) needs to be investigated.

Pharmacological dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) with the combination of aspirin and a P2Y12 inhibitor is an established therapy for patients undergoing off‐pump coronary artery bypass grafting (OPCABG). Continuous DAPT is required for patients during the first year after OPCABG to prevent a prothrombotic state and graft stenosis. 3 The commonly used P2Y12 inhibitors have distinct antiplatelet efficacy and result in a significant decrease in cardiovascular adverse outcomes. A study showed that using an aspirin and clopidogrel (AC) regimen as DAPT in patients undergoing CABG is associated with higher all‐cause and cardiovascular death than those with aspirin and ticagrelor (AT). 4 Whether the lower cardiovascular benefits of AC treatment versus AT therapy are due to the diminished antiplatelet effect in the population of loss‐of‐function (LOF) allele at the gene cytochrome P450 family 2 subfamily C member19 (CYP2C19) remains to be determined. Similarly, the effect on POAF after OPCABG using 2 distinct DAPT regimens with differentiated antiplatelet efficacy on the hereditary background of LOF genotypes had not been investigated in clinical practice.

Due to the higher percentage of CYP2C19 LOF in an Asian population, 5 choosing the suitable antiplatelet regimen in these patients after OPCABG to prevent POAF is of clinical significance. Whether potent antiplatelet efficacy in the population of CYP2C19 LOF was associated with a lower incidence rate of POAF and downregulated the systemic immune‐inflammation index (SII), 6 which defined as the product of platelet and neutrophil–lymphocyte ratio, remains unknown. It is well known that there is more pronounced antiplatelet effect of ticagrelor compared with clopidogrel. We conducted this observational study to validate the hypothesis that AT therapy contributed to a lower incidence rate of POAF after OPCABG than the AC regimen on the basis of more potent antiplatelet efficacy, decreased systemic immune inflammation, and modulation of the activated automatic nerve system.

Methods

Data Availability

The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Study Protocol

The prospective, open‐label, observational study was approved by the Institutional Ethical Review Board of Sichuan Provincial People's Hospital (approval number: 2019042, approval date: March 4, 2019) and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its amendments. Informed consent was obtained from all patients on publication of the data.

Sample Size Calculation

A 40% incidence rate of POAF in the AC population was assumed. According to the sample size calculation equation, at least 90 patients in each group were enrolled to provide a power of 0.8 to detect a 50% reduction in the POAF incidence with a 2‐sided type I error rate of 0.05. Assuming a dropout rate of 10 patients during the follow‐up, we determined that the total enrollment was 200 patients.

Patients

From May 2019 to November 2023, adult Chinese Han patients with CYP2C19 LOF genotype in 1 institution hospitalized for elective first‐time OPCABG were consecutively selected to be enrolled in this study. The eligible subjects were grouped into an AC group and an AT group at the discretion of the operating surgeon. A flowchart of included and excluded patients is shown in Figure 1. The exclusion criteria were (1) preoperative AF or those patients taking antiarrhythmic agents other than β blockers and uncontrolled hyperthyroidism; (2) concomitant valve or other cardiac disease such as ventricular aneurysm, or an urgent bypass procedure; (3) lung, hepatic, renal dysfunction, stroke, or preoperative bleeding diathesis; and (4) those patients switched to another antiplatelet regimen or cessation of target antiplatelet therapy. The primary outcome was the incidence of POAF 1 week after the procedure. Secondary end points were AF burden, plasma arachidonic acid and ADP‐induced platelet aggregation, and the SII.

Surgical Strategy and Antiplatelet Regimens

The surgery was performed via OPCABG by a single doctor. 7 The left internal mammary artery was anastomosed to the left anterior descending artery and great saphenous vein, or radial artery grafts were used to bypass other target vessels with aortocoronary or sequential bypass. Preoperative administration of aspirin was continued until the day of surgery, P2Y12 receptor inhibitors were withdrawn for at least 5 to 7 days before surgery. The DAPT regimen after CABG was as follows: The AT group received aspirin 100 mg/d and ticagrelor 2×90 mg/d; the AC group received aspirin 100 mg/d and clopidogrel 75 mg/d. DAPT was administrated after chest tube drainage was ≤30 mL/h for 2 hours.

Postoperative AF

POAF was defined as the occurrence of AF confirmed using continuous electrocardiography telemetry until hospital discharge and 7‐d Holter ECG monitoring after surgery. The presence of electrocardiographic characteristics of AF, lasting for at least 30 seconds, was documented as POAF. 8 AF burden was defined as the total duration of AF divided by the total duration of the 7‐day recording time following OPCABG with an interpretable ECG signal. 9 Heart rate variability (HRV) was analyzed from the individuals consistently remaining in sinus rhythm with frequency domain methods (high frequency [HF]; low frequency [LF]; and LF/HF ratio) and time domain methods (SD of normal‐to‐normal RR intervals, SD of the average NN intervals for each 5‐minute segment of a 24‐hour HRV recording, SD of the average NN intervals, and root mean square of successive differences).

CYP2C19 Genotyping and Platelet Function Testing

LOF genotypes were determined by CYP2C19 genotyping using the polymerase chain reaction restriction fragment length polymorphism technique. The primer sequences for CYP2C19*2 (681 G>A) and CYP2C19*3 (636 G>A) were used to detect the specific genomic sequence. 10 Multiple‐electrode aggregometry (Roche Diagnostics, Switzerland) was performed using whole hirudin anticoagulated blood solution, and changes in electrical impedance were detected when platelets aggregated on the electrodes. To quantify aspirin and P2Y12 inhibitor's effect, whole blood was stimulated with 5 mmol/L plasma arachidonic acid and 3 μm/L ADP within the manufacturer's recommended time frame, respectively. 11 Serum samples for platelet aggregation were obtained by venipuncture 1 day before OPCABG and 1 and 5 days after the procedure during the morning hours in a fasting state.

Systemic Immune‐Inflammation Index

The plasma level of white cell count including neutrophils, lymphocytes, and platelets in addition to high‐sensitivity C‐reactive protein were measured before the procedure and every day after OPCABG. Neutrophil–lymphocyte ratio (NLR) was determined as the ratio of absolute number of neutrophils to that of lymphocytes from a complete blood cell count. 12 SII is the product of the platelet count and NLR, which is designed as a surrogate marker of SII inflammation from a complete blood cell count. 13

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using the SPSS software version 25.0 (IBM, Chicago, IL) or Prism 10 (GraphPad Software), Boston, MA. Continuous variables with normal or nonnormal distribution are expressed as mean±SD or median (first quartile–third quartile), which was determined using the F test. Categorical variables were presented as the counted numbers (percentages). The risk factors for POAF, including treatment regimen, antiplatelet initiation time, sex, age, body mass index, left main coronary disease, triple‐vessel lesions, smoking status, obstructive sleep apnea syndrome, hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and severe left ventricular dysfunction (left ventricular ejection fraction <35%) were determined using binary logistic regression analysis. Kaplan–Meier analysis curves were used to compare the time‐dependent incidence of POAF in the 2 groups. The hazard ratio (HR) between the 2 groups was compared using the log‐rank test for cumulative risk. Statistical analysis for baseline data and secondary outcomes was performed using unpaired 2‐sample t test for normally distributed continuous variables and the Mann–Whitney U nonparametric test, Pearson's χ2 test or Fisher exact test if n≤5 for categorical variables. Receiver operating characteristic curve was used to analyze the probability of SII biomarker to predict the incidence of POAF. Differences were considered statistically significant at P<0.05.

Results

Patients Enrollment

A total of 200 consecutive patients undergoing elective CABG were enrolled, 95 patients in the AT group and 95 in the AC group were included for the final analysis. Among those excluded participants, 4 patients in the AT group switched to the AC group due to severe dyspnea, 4 patients allocated in the AC group switched to the AT group due to low‐quality target vessels. The DAPT regimen was suspended in 1 patient in the AC group because of the need for reexploration due to bleeding. One patient in the AT group died on the second day due to irreversible hemodynamic failure (Figure 1). No differences in age, sex, body mass index, left main stenosis, triple‐vessel lesions, smoking status, comorbidities, and left ventricular ejection fraction <35% were found between the groups (Table 1). Multivariate logistic analysis showed that antiplatelet regimen grouping and antiplatelet initiation time were the only 2 independent predictors of POAF after adjusting for baseline parameters (odds ratios, 0.23 [95% CI, 0.11–0.50]; and 1.15 [95% CI, 1.06–1.24]). In addition, no difference in graft characteristics (including distal anastomosis number, second arterial conduit usage) and intraoperative transit‐time flow measurement findings were found between the AT and AC groups (Table 2).

Primary Outcome and POAF‐Related Measurements

All 190 patients in the final analysis completed the 7‐day follow‐up; there were no missing data. Twenty patients in the AT group and 39 patients in the AC group developed POAF during the first 7 days after CABG, respectively (21.1% versus 41.1%, HR, 0.46 [95% CI, 0.27–0.76]; P=0.003) (Figure 2; the CYP2C19 genotyping was well distributed between the 2 groups). The median of the first episode of POAF occurred at 56.4 hours (34.8–84.0) after surgery in the AT group and 62.4 hours (33.6–86.4) in the AC group (P=0.25). AF burden, defined as the percentage of time spent in AF during the 7‐day monitoring period following CABG, was significantly lower in the AT group (Table 3). The duration of POAF was significantly lower in the AT group than that in the AC group (6.3 hours [1.1–12.4] versus 14.0 hours [5.5–28.0]; P=0.01). In the 59 patients developing AF, the mean burden was 8% (13.9 hours). When the AF burden was categorized according to higher (>8%) and lower (<8%) burden, there were significantly fewer patients in the higher burden in the AT group (P=0.02). The number of AF episodes in each patient were significantly lower in the AT group than in the AC group (5.5 [1–7] versus 8.0 [4–11]; P<0.01). One patient in the AT group and 11 patients in the AC group developed POAF lasting >24 hours (P=0.04). At the time of discharge, no patients remained in AF in both groups.

POAF Treatment and HRV

Among the patients developing POAF, spontaneous conversion to sinus rhythm occurred in 6 patients, 16 of 20 patients in the AT group and 37 of 39 patients in the AC group received amiodarone therapy (Table 3). When divided according to the dose of amiodarone (1200 mg), significantly fewer patients in the AT required >1200 mg of amiodarone (P=0.03). The use of esmolol for ventricular rate control was less frequent in the AT group (P=0.02). No patient in either group underwent electrical cardioversion. Between the end of the 7‐day monitoring and discharge, 2 patients in the AC group and no patients in the AT group had recurrent AF; reoccurrence of POAF was not included in the final analysis for the primary outcome. No new POAF occurred between 7 days after surgery and hospital discharge in both groups.

In the HRV analysis for remaining in sinus rhythm, both the LF power and HF power were significantly higher in the AT group compared with those in AC group, but the LF/HF ratio, which reflected sympathetic/parasympathetic balance, was significantly lower in the AT group compared with those in the AC group 2 days after the procedure. In all, the parameters reflecting parasympathetic activity such as the SD of normal‐to‐normal RR intervals, SD of the average NN intervals, and root mean square of successive differences were significantly higher in the AT group than that in the AC group (Figure 3).

Secondary Outcomes

The time to initiation of a DAPT regimen after OPCABG was similar in both groups (18.7±5.1 hours versus 18.1±6.4 hours; P=0.49). Platelet counts and arachidonic acid–induced platelet aggregation were not statistically different between the 2 groups before surgery, 1 day and 5 days after the operation. However, ADP‐stimulated platelet aggregation was lower in the AT group than in the AC group 1 day after OPCABG (50.0%±27.1% versus 79.2%±9.0%; P<0.01) and remained at a lower level 5 days after the procedure (14.7%±5.1% versus 30.0%±5.7%; P<0.01). The postoperative inflammation response indexes such as NLR, high‐sensitivity C‐reactive protein, and SII were also lower in the AT group than those in the AC group. There was a notably lower SII in the AT group compared with that in the AC group at 1 day (4668.3±938.5 versus 5064.2±822.0; P<0.01) and 5 days after procedure (1217.1±265.9 versus 1452.9±444.1; P<0.01; Figure 4).

Discussion

Main Findings

The main new finding of this observational cohort study is that in a population with CYP2C19 LOF allele, an AT regimen reduced the incidence of POAF by 48.7% compared with an AC regimen. AT also contributed to a lower AF burden, ADP‐induced platelet aggregation, SII response, and balanced sympathetic/parasympathetic system compared with an AC regimen after isolated OPCABG procedures.

POAF Duration Coincident With DAPT Initiation

New‐onset AF after cardiac surgery is mainly occurs in the first week and peaked on day 2. 14 POAF is a highly prevalent complication with increased long‐term risk. 15 The pathophysiology of POAF is multifactorial. Studies suggests that an excessive, persistent, and expanded proinflammatory response and activated sympathetic nerve system following OPCABG may precipitate POAF. 2 Increased platelet activation after elective primary CABG is thought to increase with the occurrence of POAF. 16 Adjunctive therapy such as remote ischemic preconditioning 17 has shown that platelet aggregation plays a role in AF induction and maintenance via reducing platelet activation and reactivity during catheter procedures for AF. 18

Clopidogrel and ticagrelor, the classic platelet P2Y12 inhibitors, are the most used agents in DAPT after CABG. Clopidogrel is a prodrug that is activated by the hepatic CYP2C19. Ticagrelor, which does not require conversion from prodrug to active drug in the liver, directly and reversibly binds to a site on the P2Y12 receptor preventing ADP downstream signaling. ADP triggers platelet aggregation via platelet degranulation, thromboxane production, and eventual activation of the glycoprotein IIb/IIIa receptor. Consequently, ticagrelor inhibits platelet aggregation 0.5 to 2 hours after ingestion compared with clopidogrel, which takes 2 to 6 hours before the onset of action. 19 In patients with the CYP2C19 LOF allele, clopidogrel resistance during treatment indicates high platelet reactivity. 20 A previous comparative study among different P2Y12 inhibitors in patients with acute coronary syndrome undergoing coronary artery stenting has shown that stronger inhibition of platelet aggregation in response to ADP is associated with a low inflammatory response. 21 Ticagrelor has pleiotropic effects in preclinical studies including attenuation of ischemia–reperfusion injury, mitigating adverse inflammation, cardiac remodeling, and atherosclerosis via an adenosine‐mediated pathway against the release of catecholamines and platelet and leukocyte activation. 22

Platelets and Inflammation

Platelets contribute to inflammatory responses through two fundamental mechanisms: release of inflammatory mediators and platelet‐leukocyte interaction, both of which could be suppressed by treatment with a P2Y12 inhibitor. 23 Platelet reactivity in patients after percutaneous coronary intervention for acute myocardial infarction was reversely linked to left ventricular remodeling, and the proposed mechanism is related to activated systemic cardiac inflammatory responses. 24 Mendelian randomization analysis shows that high neutrophil counts were associated with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, suggesting that high blood neutrophil count is a causal risk factor for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. 25 The NLR, reflecting systemic inflammation and physiological stress via 2 opposite immune pathways, independently predicts cardiovascular risk and all‐cause death and is reduced by interleukin‐1β blockade independent of lipid‐lowering agents. 26 SII is an extended marker of systemic inflammation. SII constitutes a parsimonious and reproducible parameter demonstrating the potential for delineating the patients vulnerable to poor outcomes after OPCABG given the combined contribution of proinflammatory and prothrombotic corpuscular lines. 6 Preoperative SII is a useful prognostic biomarker for POAF in patients undergoing cardiac surgery on cardiopulmonary bypass. 13 Platelet activity is higher in the early postoperative period in patients undergoing OPCABG compared with those on cardiopulmonary bypass. 27 In our study, the patients in the AC group had a greater postoperative SII value than that in the AT group despite similar preoperative values, which also corresponded with a higher platelet aggregation and increased incidence of POAF. In our analysis of the prognostic value of inflammatory parameters for POAF, SII had a higher area under the receiver operating characteristic curve than other indexes such as NLR and high‐sensitivity C‐reactive protein.

POAF and the Autonomic Nerve System

The AF burden is an estimate of the total time in AF, which is calculated by total time in AF divided by the total monitored time and expressed as a percentage. 28 It is more sensitive than AF‐related symptoms or occurrence times in reflecting the severity of atrial arrhythmias. Consistent with a lower incidence of POAF, the AT group had a lower AF burden with shorter duration of hospitalization and medication burden. 29 It is well known that neuromodulation of the autonomic nerve system on the heart plays a role in the pathophysiology of atrial arrhythmias. 30 Autonomic function reaches the lowest values 3 to 6 days after CABG and returns to presurgery values after 60 days. 31 CABG is able to induce an increase in cardiac vagal modulation in comparison with presurgery values in healthy individuals after 90 days. 32 In our study, HF and SD of normal‐to‐normal RR intervals values were higher in the AT group than in the AC group, indicating better preservation of parasympathetic function. A cohort study demonstrated that HRV indexed by SD of normal‐to‐normal RR intervals and SD of the average NN intervals was negatively associated with increased inflammation response indexes such as C‐reactive protein and white blood cell count in healthy middle‐aged and older patients. 33 Thus, an autonomic imbalance in favor of the parasympathetic system in the AT group along with weak inflammatory processes play an important role in mitigating the initiation and maintenance of POAF.

Limitations

Our study has several limitations. First, this was a single‐center clinical study with a relatively small number of patients, which reduces the generalizability of our study to other populations. Second, different aspirin dosages in clinical practice have been used worldwide, which may influence the efficacy of P2Y12 inhibitors and result in potential heterogeneity on platelet aggregation. Third, no dynamic biological samples were available from the atrium for establishing the relationships between pathological evidence and serum inflammation indexes 34 in addition to the analysis of HRV. 35 This clinical study collected only patients with LOF, whether these 2 kinds of medications presented a similarly distinct effect on POAF in patients with weight issues remains unknown. 36 Additional prospective studies including larger numbers of multiethnic patients and longer follow‐up periods to determine the incidence of POAF and bleeding events are needed to confirm the findings of this study.

Conclusions

This study demonstrated that AT compared with AC contributed to a lower incidence of POAF and an immune‐inflammatory response in patients who had CYCP19 LOF genotype after OPCABG. The role of antiplatelet therapy in the initiation and maintenance of POAF following OPCABG needs to be further investigated in clinical studies.

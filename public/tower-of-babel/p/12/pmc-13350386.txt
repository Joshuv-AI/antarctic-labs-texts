Safety and Efficacy of a Novel Rotational Atherectomy System in Coronary Calcifications
Authors: Changling Li, Mian Wang, Jun Jiang, Xiang Ma, Haifeng Yu, Peng Dong, Xiaomin Chen, Lefeng Wang, Lang Hong, Ping Xie, Qing Yang, Genshan Ma, Yiming Zhong, Bin Lin, Ruifen Cao, Tingting Wu, Jessie Shen, Bin Yue, Jian’an Wang, the CORECT Investigators

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCalcified coronary lesions present challenges during percutaneous coronary intervention, often impeding successful stent delivery and expansion. Rotational atherectomy (RA) facilitates plaque modification in such cases. However, clinical performance of a novel RA system remains unclear.ObjectivesThis noninferiority study evaluated the safety and efficacy of the novel system.MethodsThis trial included 2 prospective phases, a first-in-human (FIH) study and a pivotal randomized controlled trial. In the FIH study, 15 patients underwent percutaneous coronary intervention with the novel RA system, and clinical success was assessed. In pivotal trial, 224 patients with calcified lesions were randomized to receive RA using either the novel (n = 111) or established (n = 113) system, followed by stenting. The primary endpoint was freedom from major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs), a composite of cardiac death, myocardial infarction, and target vessel revascularization, at 30 days. Secondary endpoints included MACEs at 12 months and quantitative imaging analyses.ResultsAll FIH patients achieved clinical success. In pivotal study, the median follow-up time was 349.5 (IQR: 343, 359) days. Freedom from MACEs at 30 days was 97.3% (108 of 111 vs 110 of 113; 95% CI: 92.3%-99.4% vs 92.4%-99.5%) in both groups (P for noninferiority <0.0001). At 12 months, MACEs remained low in both groups (2.7%; 95% CI: 0.9%-8.1% vs 2.7%; 95% CI: 0.9%-8.0%; P = 1.0000). Postprocedural minimal stent area was similar on intravascular ultrasound between the 2 groups (5.29 ± 1.20 vs 5.29 ± 1.47 mm2, P = 0.9921).ConclusionsThe novel RA system demonstrated noninferior safety and efficacy to established system, offering an effective alternative for the treatment of complex calcified coronary lesions. Coronary artery calcification (CAC) results from calcium salts deposition within hardened atherosclerotic plaques in advanced coronary artery disease. Percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) is the primary treatment for CAC; however, severe intimal calcification, the predominant CAC type, substantially increases PCI complexity and risks.1,2 Studies demonstrated that PCI in calcified lesions is associated with higher rates of periprocedural complications, unfavorable long-term clinical outcomes, and an increased need for revascularization.3, 4, 5 Moreover, research indicates that patients with extensive calcification are at a greater risk for coronary adverse events.6

Several interventional strategies exist for CAC management, including balloon angioplasty, rotational atherectomy (RA), orbital atherectomy (OA), etc. Conventional balloon angioplasty has a low success rate in dilating moderately to severely calcified lesions and is accompanied by a high rate of complications.7 Cutting balloons, although potentially effective, risk blade entrapment at high pressure and are unsuitable for severe stenosis due to their large profile.8 Conversely, coronary atherectomy is essential in the management of complex calcified lesions. By selectively modifying calcified plaques through differential cutting while preserving adjacent healthy vascular tissue,9 atherectomy enhances balloon dilatation, facilitates successful stent delivery, and ensures optimal stent expansion.10,11

Previous studies have demonstrated the feasibility, safety, and effectiveness of atherectomy. A single-center registry found that RA followed by drug-eluting stent (DES) implantation resulted in high procedural success and low long-term revascularization and major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs) in patients with complex calcified lesions.12 A randomized trial showed that stenting with RA achieved better strategy success than without RA.13 The Evaluate the Safety and Efficacy of Orbital Atherectomy System in Treating Severely Calcified Coronary Lesions (ORBIT) series trials confirmed the efficacy of OA for severely calcified lesions.14,15

The established Rotablator RA system (Boston Scientific), has been a key tool in interventional cardiology; however, it has certain limitations, including slow flow or no reflow after RA and frequent burr replacement. The FireRaptor (Microport Rotapace) is a novel RA system, designed to address these drawbacks. It has won the 2024 Red Dot and IF Design Awards and has been approved by the US Food and Drug Administration and the China’s National Medical Products Administration. This study is the first head-to-head, noninferiority trial comparing the novel with the established RA system. The primary objective is to evaluate the safety and efficacy of the novel RA system in treating calcified coronary lesions.

Methods

Patients and study design

The CORECT (CorOnary atheRectomy system in patiEnts with Coronary arTery calcification; NCT05447585) trial includes 2 phases, a first-in-human study (FIH) and a pivotal study. The FIH study was a prospective feasibility study enrolling a total of 15 patients who underwent PCI with the novel RA system and was conducted solely at the coordinating center (The Second Affiliated Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine). The pivotal study is a prospective, multicenter, randomized controlled, noninferiority trial conducted at 24 sites in patients with calcified coronary lesions (see Supplemental Methods for list of participating sites). Before the initiation of the pivotal study, each operator from noncoordinating centers was required to treat 2 roll-in patients to account for the learning curve associated with the novel devices.

Eligible patients were aged 18 to 80 years, scheduled for PCI with DES implantation, and had a single de novo calcified coronary lesion with ≥70% stenosis by visual estimate or 50% to 70% stenosis accompanied by ischemic symptoms in an artery with a reference vessel diameter (RVD) of 2.5 to 4.0 mm and a lesion length ≤40 mm. Exclusion criteria included acute myocardial infarction (MI) within 30 days, unstable hemodynamics, left ventricular ejection fraction <30%, and intracardiac thrombus. Detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria are provided in the Supplemental Methods.

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The protocol was approved by the institutional ethics committee at each participating center. All patients provided written informed consent.

Randomization and procedures

All eligible patients were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive coronary atherectomy using either the novel (treatment group) or the established (control group) RA followed by DES (Firebird2, Microport). An interactive web response system was employed to ensure the allocation concealment.

Standard PCI techniques were employed in all procedures. The use of temporary pacing leads, hemodynamic support devices, or intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) (manufactured by Boston Scientific or Philips) for lesion assessment and stent expansion was determined at the discretion of the operator. DES were deployed unless contraindications to prolonged dual antiplatelet therapy were present.

Patients received aspirin (300 mg loading dose 24 hours before PCI, followed by 100 mg daily; no loading dose if taken >72 hours prior) and P2Y12 inhibitors (ticagrelor was recommended with a loading dose of 180 mg 6 hours before PCI, followed by 90 mg twice daily; no loading dose if 180 mg ticagrelor or 75 mg clopidogrel was taken >72 hours prior). Intravenous heparin was administrated intraoperatively to maintain an activated clotting time of >250 seconds. Alternative anticoagulants (eg, tirofiban) were used at the operator’s discretion.

RA was performed strictly following the standard protocols. The burr of the control group was tested and primed at 150,000 to 170,000 rpm. The burr of the novel system was tested and primed at low, medium, and high speeds starting at 120,000 rpm with a 1.25-mm burr. For RVD ≥3 mm, subsequent passes used medium- or high-speed settings or a 1.50-mm burr. Each pass was limited to ≤20 seconds (details of the device are provided in the Supplemental Methods and Supplemental Figure 1). The flushing cocktail for the treatment group, consisting of 500 mL normal saline, 5,000 U heparin, and 5 mg nitroglycerin, was continuously infused during the RA. After RA, DES was implanted, with multiple stents permitted for complete revascularization. Post-dilatation was performed at the operator’s discretion.

Follow-up and endpoints

After PCI, dual antiplatelet therapy for at least 12 months was recommended. Patients were prescribed aspirin (100 mg daily) and P2Y12 inhibitors (preferably ticagrelor 90 mg twice daily; or clopidogrel 75 mg daily). Alternative antiplatelet or anticoagulation therapy was left to the physician’s discretion according to clinical guidelines and patients’ condition. A 12-lead electrocardiogram was performed 6 to 48 hours post procedure. In addition, creatine kinase, CK-MB, and troponin T levels were measured within the same time window. After discharge, patients were followed up through 3 scheduled visits (either outpatient or telephone) at 30 days, 6 months, and 12 months. Throughout the study period, any adverse events and concomitant medication use were recorded.

The endpoint for the FIH phase was clinical success, defined as successful completion of RA and stent implantation with residual stenosis <50%, Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction (TIMI) flow grade 3, and no periprocedural complications or in-hospital MACEs up to 7 days post procedure.

The primary endpoint of the study was freedom from MACEs within 30 days post procedure in the pivotal phase, defined as the composite of cardiac death, MI, and target vessel revascularization (TVR). Secondary endpoints included MACEs at 6 and 12 months, device success, angiographic success, clinical success, procedure-related complications, patient-oriented composite endpoint (PoCE), target lesion failure, target vessel failure, target lesion revascularization, TVR, death, MI, any coronary revascularization, and stent thrombosis at 30 days and 6 and 12 months. Detailed definitions for endpoints are provided in the Supplemental Methods. All clinical events were confirmed by an independent clinical event committee. Angiographic and IVUS parameters were also evaluated as secondary endpoints, including minimal lumen diameter (MLD), acute gain, diameter stenosis percentage (%DS), minimum lumen area (MLA), area stenosis (%), stent area, stent expansion index, and acute gain in lumen area. Details of quantitative angiographic and IVUS analysis are provided in the Supplemental Methods.

Statistical analysis

The pivotal study was powered to test noninferiority for the primary endpoint. Based on previous studies and expert consensus,14,16,17 the expected freedom from MACEs within 30 days post procedure was assumed to be 94% in both groups. A noninferiority margin of −10% was selected. To achieve 80% power at a 1-sided α level of 0.025, and accounting for a 20% attrition rate, a total of 224 patients was required.

All analyses were performed in the intention-to-treat population, with the per-protocol population used for a secondary analysis of the primary endpoint. Continuous variables were presented as mean ± SD and compared using the Student’s t-test or Wilcoxon rank sum test, as appropriate. Categorical measures were expressed as counts and percentages and compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. For noninferiority testing of the primary endpoint, rate difference between groups and its 95% CI were calculated using the Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test, adjusted for center effects. Noninferiority was established if the lower bound of the 95% CI exceeded −10%. Subgroup analyses were performed based on demographics and lesion characteristics that are clinically considered to potentially affect the primary endpoint. Event rates for each group were estimated using Kaplan-Meier methods. Univariate Cox proportional hazards model was also employed to estimate the HR and the corresponding 95% CI. The proportional hazards assumption was tested using Schoenfeld residual tests and met for all models (P > 0.05). All analyses were performed using SAS 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc); a P value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

The study flow is depicted in Figure 1. A total of 224 patients were enrolled in the pivotal study, RA and PCI were successfully performed in all patients. The mean age was 68 ± 7.4 years, and 64% (144 of 224) were men; 82% (183 of 224) of patients had hypertension and 29% (66 of 224) had hyperlipemia. Angiographic and procedural characteristics

Supplemental Table 2 presents the angiographic and procedural characteristics, patients were comparable with regard to lesion location and morphology. Most of the lesions are located in the left anterior descending artery. The mean %DS of target lesions was high in both groups (86.05% ± 6.78% in treatment group vs 85.64% ± 6.94% in the control group, P = 0.6569) and more than 95% (95.5% [106 of 111] vs 97.3% [108 of 111]) of lesions were categorized as type B2/C. The proportion of balloon predilatation before RA was similar in both groups (67.6% [75 of 111] vs 67.3% [76 of 113], P = 0.9604). Notably, the mean burr diameter in the treatment group was significantly smaller than that in the control group (1.40 ± 0.12 vs 1.52 ± 0.18 mm, P < 0.0001) and the mean maximum rotational speed was also significantly lower in the treatment group (139,153 ± 20,287 vs 150,482 ± 20,827 rpm, P < 0.0001) (Supplemental Figure 2), whereas the total rotational time was similar (44.16 ± 40.01 vs 46.25 ± 40.38 seconds, P = 0.6985). In addition, the number of stents implanted and the size of stents were comparable in both groups.

Clinical outcomes

In the FIH study, clinical success was achieved in all 15 patients with no procedure-related complications (Supplemental Table 3).

In the pivotal study, the primary endpoint, freedom from MACEs within 30 days post procedure, were 97.3% (108 of 111, 95% CI: 92.3%-99.4%) vs 97.3% (110 of 113, 95% CI: 92.4%-99.5%) in 2 groups. The lower bound of the 95% CI for the difference was −4.2%, exceeding the predefined noninferiority margin of −10%, thereby confirming noninferiority (P < 0.0001). Similar findings were observed in the per-protocol population. Supplemental Table 4 demonstrates periprocedural clinical outcomes, with no significant differences observed between the 2 groups. Quantitative angiographic and IVUS analysis

IVUS analysis (Table 3) showed comparable MLA, MLD, and area stenosis at baseline in 2 groups. The mean baseline IVUS calcification arcs were 308.6 ± 62.7 and 302.4 ± 64.6 for the treatment and control groups, respectively, with no significant difference observed. Discussion

The current study is the first multicenter, randomized control study comparing the novel RA system with the established RA system. It demonstrated that, in patients with moderate to severe coronary calcified lesions, the novel RA system was noninferior to the established RA for the primary endpoint of freedom from MACEs at 30 days post procedure (Central Illustration). The study also finds that 1) both devices achieved similarly high rates of device success, angiographic success, and clinical success; 2) 30-day clinical adverse events, including PoCE, cardiac death, target lesion failure, revascularization, and stent thrombosis, were rare and comparable between the 2 RA systems; and 3) the low clinical event rates observed during the 12-month follow-up suggest that both systems offer long-term safety and efficacy.Central IllustrationSafety and Efficacy of a Novel Rotational Atherectomy System in Coronary CalcificationsThe novel RA system (treatment) was noninferior to the established (control) RA system for 30-day MACEs in calcified coronary lesions. Safety and Efficacy of a Novel Rotational Atherectomy System in Coronary Calcifications

The novel RA system (treatment) was noninferior to the established (control) RA system for 30-day MACEs in calcified coronary lesions. DES = drug-eluting stent; IVUS = intravascular ultrasound; MACEs = major adverse cardiovascular events; MI = myocardial infarction; PCI = percutaneous coronary intervention; RA = rotational atherectomy; TVR = target vessel revascularization.

Previous studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of RA in modifying heavily calcified lesions before DES implantation, with low rates of clinical adverse events, findings that align with our study.12,18, 19, 20 In the FIH phase of this study, clinical success was achieved in all patients, with no long-term target lesion revascularization or death, supporting the safety and feasibility of the novel RA system for treating calcified lesions. The pivotal randomized controlled trial further confirmed its efficacy in comparison with an established RA system. The angiographic success using the novel RA system observed in our study (96.3%) was comparable to that reported in the ROTAXUS (Rotational Atherectomy Prior to TAXUS Stent Treatment for Complex Native Coronary Artery Disease) trial (96.7%).13 Moreover, compared with the OA system used in the ORBIT II trial,15 the primary endpoint of freedom from 30-day MACEs was modestly higher with the novel RA system (97.3% vs 89.6%), indicating that the novel one provides a new, safe, and effective option for calcified lesion preparation. Notably, the significantly smaller size of the burr of the novel RA system compared with the control device enhances its flexibility in treating complex lesions. The mean burr diameter reported in other studies was mildly larger, and the novel RA system’s maximum rotational speed was also at least 10,000 rpm lower than that of similar devices,13,21 which may contribute to efficient lesion modification while potentially reducing vessel trauma and complications such as perforation.

One of the key findings of our study was the quantitative coronary angiographic and IVUS data obtained immediately after RA and before stenting. Previous studies have largely lacked angiographic or IVUS assessments at this specific time point. The ORBIT II trial reported the final MLD of 2.9 ± 0.0 mm, reflecting the combined effect of OA and stenting.15 Similarly, the ROTAXUS trial reported a postprocedural in-stent MLD of 2.57 ± 0.38 mm and an acute gain of 1.56 ± 0.43 mm in patients treated with RA followed by DES.13 Another study also observed a slightly larger in-stent MLD after PCI with RA but did not report data immediately after RA.21 In contrast, our study conducted angiographic and IVUS evaluations at 3 time points: baseline, post-RA, and post stent. The mean MLD significantly increased from 1.68 ± 0.17 mm at baseline to 2.01 ± 0.20 immediately after RA (P < 0.0001), demonstrating that the treatment device effectively modified the calcified lesion and facilitated subsequent stent delivery and expansion. These data from 3 timepoints highlighted the impact of RA before stent implantation, providing novel insights into the lesion modification process.

On the other hand, the novel RA system features an eccentric burr design, in which the center of mass is offset from its rotational center. This allows rotational speed adjustments to control centrifugal force, thereby regulating grinding diameter and minimizing the need for instrument exchanges during the procedure. In addition, the eccentric burr intermittently contacts calcified plaques, helping to maintain blood flow and prevent complete obstruction. The novel RA system is compatible with a 6-F guiding catheter in all cases. Moreover, its diamond-coated burr enables bidirectional rotational ablation, reducing the risk of burr entrapment. These design optimizations enhance portability, procedural simplicity, and clinical efficacy, making this novel RA system a valuable addition to the RA armamentarium.

Our study has several strengths. First, we conducted a noninferiority randomized controlled trial comparing 2 RA-based strategies, which remains the gold standard for evaluating safety and efficacy of new medical devices. Second, we performed quantitative angiographic and IVUS assessments immediately after RA, providing direct evidence of lesion modification effectiveness using RA. Third, despite the open-label design, our data were adjudicated by an independent imaging core laboratory and a clinical event committee, ensuring unbiased and rigorous evaluation.

Study limitations

First, we did not include a planned long-term angiographic evaluation. Instead, we scheduled long-term clinical follow-ups to assess patient outcomes. Although RA serves as a preparatory step for treating heavily calcified lesions, the long-term angiographic results are likely influenced more by stents than by RA itself. Second, the pivotal phase of the trial was not powered to detect differences in clinical success, an endpoint crucial for evaluating lesion preparation feasibility and strategy effectiveness. However, the FIH phase demonstrated a 100% clinical success rate, and results in the pivotal trial were consistently high and comparable between the 2 RA systems. Furthermore, our clinical success rate significantly exceeded the performance goal (82%) set for the primary efficacy endpoint in the ORBIT II trial, further supporting the effectiveness of this novel RA system. Third, it was also not powered for safety endpoints like vessel perforation. Given the low incidences of these complications, larger post-market studies are warranted for long-term safety assessment.

Conclusions

The novel RA system was noninferior to the established RA system for the primary endpoint of freedom from MACEs at 30 days in patients with coronary calcified lesions. Both systems showed comparable periprocedural, 30-day, and long-term clinical outcomes. These findings support the novel RA system as a safe and effective alternative for calcified lesion preparation prior to stenting.

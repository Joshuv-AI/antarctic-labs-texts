3D X-ray microscope acts as an accurate and effective equipment of pathological diagnosis in craniofacial imaging
Authors: Qianhui Ren, Zhao Yang, Yilei Lu, Jing Pan, Yangyang Li, Ying Guo, Mengning Bi, Yucong Zhou, Huiquan Yang, Linjie Zhou, Fang Ji
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractCraniofacial structure and dental hard tissue used to be researched on by traditional imaging tools such as light microscope, electron microscope and micro-CT. Due to the limitations of imaging principle, resolution and 3D rendering reconstruction technique, traditional imaging tools are constrained for presenting fine structure and precise measurements. Here a brand-new imaging equipment–3D X-ray microscope is introduced to realize a more efficient scanning by demonstrating the comparison of the craniofacial structures and dental hard tissue of diabetes and normal DBA mouse. To explore a higher resolution, more efficient imaging measurement and 3D reconstruction method on craniofacial structure and dental hard tissue. The study included 12 DBA mice which were divided into two groups (control group and diabetes group). The heads were separated and scanned by 3D X-ray microscope, after which regions of interest were selected, followed by measurement and 3D reconstruction based on microscope attached software Dragonfly pro©. Hemi-mandibles were collected for enamel mineral density assessment supported by QRM-MicroCT-HA phantom. Data was submitted to paired t-tests at a 95% confidence level. The automatic assessed enamel thickness of diabetes mice decreased on average, whereas the rest of manual measurements and automatic assessed density showed no statistical difference. We constructed HA phantom assisted enamel density procedure in Dragonfly software. Craniofacial structure and dental hard tissue were well-presented both in 2D slide and 3D reconstruction viewport by 3D X-ray microscope which can be routinely used as craniofacial structure and dental hard tissue imaging tool.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-74139-4.Keywords: 3D X-ray microscope, 3D rendering reconstruction, Dental hard tissue, Craniofacial structure, Enamel measurement, Enamel densitySubject terms: Biological techniques, Computational biology and bioinformatics

Introduction

The craniofacial structure are three-dimensionally arranged through 14 facial and 8 cranial bones that provide essential support and projection for the overlying soft tissues and organs, forming the cranium, sensory organs, mandible, temporomandibular joint (TMJ), palate, and other structures1. The complexity of craniofacial structure was firstly proposed as “morphologie” by Johann Wolfgang von Goethe in 18th century2. Reconstructing and measuring this three-dimensional(3D) complex are one of the greatest challenges, especially for diagnosis and bioengineering of the craniofacial defects both in clinic and lab3. Craniofacial phenotypic study has correlated genetic and epigenetic factors to improve our understanding of growth and development in health and disease, in which 3D imaging has played a key role in advancing craniofacial phenomics by facilitating highly sensitive and specific characterizations of craniofacial and dental morphology4.

Teeth are complicated structures composed of several different mineralized tissues, namely enamel, dentine, and cementum, which enveloped a soft tissue, the dental pulp. The dental root anchors to the surrounding alveolar bone via short, tendon-like fibers called the periodontal ligament, which insert both into root cementum and bone5. Mature enamel is the hardest, most mineralized tissue in the human body, comprising > 95% by weight crystals of substituted calcium hydroxyapatite (HA; Ca10[PO4]6[OH]2)6, which thickness is 2–2.5 mm in average7. The phenotype of enamel, like thickness and density can be affected by nature versus nurture factors. Enamel lacks any capacity for cellular repair and once formed, must function over a lifetime8. Thus, dental hard tissue measurements are vital for the observation of etiology and pathophysiology of amelogenesis imperfecta, such as the density, volume and thickness of enamel and dentine, enamel surface area, the number of defects and the overall morphology9.

At present, the main imaging methods for evaluating mineralized tissues include light microscopy, scanning electron microscopy (SEM), transmission electron microscopy (TEM), X-ray two-dimensional planar imaging and X-ray three-dimensional imaging. These methods have their own advantages, but the resolution is limited due to diffraction, even though the most widely used micro-CT, let al.one light microscope and electron microscope, the entry criteria for sample and particularity of sample preparation confine their popularity in laboratory10,11. Almost all hard tissues and structures can be investigated using micro-CT or µ-CT, including root canal morphology, enamel thickness, enamel abrasion, and the amounts of pre-dentin or reparative dentin12. With the advantage of penetrability of X-ray, 3D X-ray microscope (3D XRM) has demonstrated its power in exploring the structure and function of cells at the nanometer resolution, routinely achieving 0.6 μm pixel resolution (PR)13, by exploiting the short wavelength, long penetration depth and elemental specificity of X-rays in biological, medical and material fields14. 3D XRM is distinguished by two-stage magnification technology from traditional micro-CT, whose scintillator converts X-rays into visible light at the 2nd stage, and then amplified optically13. This feature reduces the dependence on geometric amplification at 1st stage after passing through samples and thus can maintain submicron high resolution at large working distance, enabling the user to select magnification by changing objectives or by changing the geometric magnification. The 3D data set is reconstructed using Feldkamp-Davis-Kress (FDK) filtering back projection algorithm15, use the combination of advanced reconstruction engine OptiRecon and DeepRecon in subsequent processing to improve the contrast noise ratio of samples and maintain or even improve image quality while shortening scanning time.

The use of 3D dental and craniofacial imaging in dentistry experiment requires validate image software platform for follow-up analysis process. Numbers of software for image analysis allow users to separate target area, segment volumetric models, 3D analysis and quantification. 3D XRM image processing software Dragonfly pro©(v.2022.1, ORS Inc., Québec, Canada)16 provides different image segmentation shapes, rendering states, so that the output of results can be more efficient and higher precision. The image filter system reduces the image artifact and improves the image sharpness greatly. In subsequent analysis, the combination of image processing and statistical algorithms can be directly implemented by accessing Dragonfly’s Python script17.

Diabetes mellitus (DM), a common metabolic disorder of carbohydrate and lipid metabolism, characterized by hyperglycemia, inflammation, and high oxidative stress, leads to systemic complications, i.e., peripheral neuropathy, retinal degeneration, renal insufficiency, atherosclerosis, and microangiopathy18. According to International Diabetes Federation, till 2019, 1 in 11 adults (20–79 years) have diabetes (463 million people)19. Recently, studies on the correlation between DM and oral complication are increasing gradually. Dental Caries Previous clinical research suggested that much higher caries prevalence in diabetic children may be due to alteration in the salivary gland functions that causes decreased salivary flow, or increased salivary glucose levels which may have increased permeability of the parotid gland basement membrane to the elevated blood glucose20. Similar findings were found in mice that increased chewing friction caused by mountainous food intake, decreased saliva volume, teeth mineralization ability, and inhibition of odontogenesis in high sugar environment all lead to significantly increased dental caries rate in diabetic mice21. Periodontal Disease The development of periodontitis is attributed to the hyperglycemia22, in which periodontium and alveolar bone are targets of diabetic damage23. Chronic immune system activation with increased levels of circulating leukocytes and pro-inflammatory markers (increased interleukin, acute-phase inflammatory marker, C-reactive protein, etc24). has been reported in people with inadequately controlled diabetes25,26. Dental Dysplasia Type1 DM (T1DM) may disturb the development of enamel and dentine27, leading to reduction of the rate of dentine mineral apposition and formation28, decreased micro-hardness from dentin-enamel junction, and increasing in the amount of calcium and phosphorus in outer mature enamel29. High glucose can enhance the odonto-/osteogenic capacity of stem cells from apical papilla (SCAPs) via NF-κB pathway30. Inflammation of Dental Pulp DM mainly exert their damaging effect on endothelial31, moreover, the accumulation of atheromatous deposits in the intimal tissues of the blood vessels lumen and a thickened basement membrane, which impairs leukotactic response, damaged blood vessels and decreased in polymorphonuclear leucocytes (PMNs) killing ability. Because the dental pulp has limited or no collateral circulation, it is more prone to be at risk for bacterial or opportunistic infections comparing to non-DM dental pulp32. Pathologic calcifications such as diffuse calcification and pulp stones always appear in dental pulp tissues of diabetic patients. Calcifications in this pulp study were found in both diabetics and non-diabetics. In diabetes of long duration, late diabetic vascular changes may occur in the dental pulp, and in these DM patients there is a specific type of calcification33.

Therefore, this research dug into the craniofacial and dental measurement of 3D XRM in diabetes and normal DBA mouse group, and explored auto-calculation to minimize manual variation during observation. With the help of bone density phantom, we establish the enamel density assessment procedure for 3D XRM. The function of 3D reconstruction enabled a clearer comparison of different group in a much higher resolution. This research may contribute to a new laboratory-diagnostic paradigm for the study of craniofacial structure and dental hard tissue and help to explore a higher resolution, more efficient imaging measurement and 3D reconstruction method on craniofacial structure and dental hard tissue.

Methods and materials

Ethical statement

All mice used in this project were maintained in a specific pathogen-free facility of the Ninth People Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai Jiao tong University School of Medicine with standard conditions (12-h light/dark cycle, ~20–24 °C, ~40–60% humidity). The gender of the mice does not affect the conclusion of this study. Only male mice aged 8 weeks were used. All animal experiments were in full compliance with the ethical regulations for animal testing and research and were approved by the Animal Ethical and Welfare Committee of Ninth People Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai Jiao tong University School of Medicine (SH9H-2021-A862-SB). In our experiments, all mice were randomized to each experimental group. Data collection and analysis were not performed blind to the conditions of the experiments. No animal was excluded from the study. This study is reported in accordance with ARRIVE guidelines and all methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations.

Sample preparation

Eight-week-old male DBA mice (n = 12), weighing 23 ± 2g, were randomly assigned to two groups, including diabetes and control group. Streptozocin (STZ) were used to establish a T1DM mouse model (50mg/kg, 5 days). The control group was injected i.p. with a single dose of citrate buffer. The animals were fed Purina Chow No.1 and water ad libitum. Blood samples were obtained via vein puncture of a tail vein 2, 10, 20 and 30 days after Day 0, and blood glucose levels were determined using a glucometer to test the establishment of animal model. A blood glucose level greater than 200mg/dl was considered diabetic. The mice were housed in stainless-steel cages on 12h light/dark cycles at 21–24°C. After 22 weeks, the animals in control and diabetes group (Control: n = 6, Diabetes: n = 6) were selected randomly, and were killed by decapitation with CO2 euthanasia induction. Animal carcasses were handled harmlessly by the animal center. The skulls were separated and stored in 10% Formalin. After overall scanning, we separated the mandibles into 2 pieces, the left part and the right part.

Overall and partial 3D XRM scanning

After patting dry, the sample was sealed in Parafilm to prevent desiccation, supported by absorbent ball, and put into 50ml Centrifuge Tube (Corning, USA), which was secured by a metal stub (7 mm outer diameter, Scanco Medical, Switzerland) from the bottom. Imaging was performed on 3D XRM scanner (Versa 510, Carl Zeiss X-ray Microscopy, USA) using the settings of 80 KV, 7 W power. The isotropic voxel resolution for overall craniofacial structure was 14.697μm and the scans were acquired using an objective of 0.4×, a 0.03 mm glass filter (LE1), a field-of-view (FOV) of 15,050 μm2. Five-frame averaging was used to collect a total of 1014 images with an exposure time of 1 s, resulting in an overall scan time of approximately 1 h. The resolution for partial imaging was 3.322 μm and the scans were acquired using an objective of 4×, a field-of-view (FOV) of 3361.7 μm. Five-frame averaging was used to collect a total of 1014 images with an exposure time of 2 s, resulting in an overall scan time of approximately 2 h.

Scanning with HA phantom

The mandibular soft tissue of the sample was removed as much as possible. Then the mandibles were sealed into centrifuge tube as before, and the HA phantom (MHA-442, QRM-MicroCT-HA D32, Moehrendorf, Germany) was fasten in Parafilm outside the tube. In that way, the tooth sample was fixed in front of HA phantom so that the CT data of HA phantom and tooth enamel can be scanned simultaneously. The base material of the phantom is a proprietary epoxy resin. The inserts are the same base material but enriched with calcium hydroxyapatite (HA) Ca5(OH)(PO)3 of appropriate grain size. The whole series of HA phantoms compose of specified HA named HA0, HA50, HA200, HA800, HA1200, which density are 1.13 ± 0.5%, 1.16 ± 0.5%, 1.26 ± %, 1.65 ± 0.5%, 1.91 ± 0.5% g/cm3 with HA propotion of 0.00%, 4.28%, 15.86%, 48.80%, 63.49%, and base epoxy resin proportion of 100.00%, 95.72%, 84.14%, 51.20%, 36.51%. Calibrated HA of HA0, HA50, HA200, HA800, HA1200 phantoms are 0.0, 49.6, 199.3, 802.0, 1207.1 mgHA/cm3 ± 0.5%. The settings of imaging were 80 KV, 7 W power. The capability of determining mineral density of hard tissue was available only at a specific resolution scanning. The resolution for HA phantom-medicated imaging was 18.24 μm and the scans were acquired using an objective of 0.4×, a field-of-view (FOV) of 18,673 μm2.

2D slides and superimpositions observation

Dragonfly pro integrated data format extends as TXM (.txm) from 3D X-ray microscopes, which is available for Dragonfly Pro only the following visualization and image analysis. We obtained 2D, 1000 × 1000-pixel slides and selected regions of interest (ROIs) for the following measurement in fused views or across multiple scene views. At the same time, real-time automated superimposition of 2D slides was presented, just like 2D cephalometric images. The software synchronized 2D and superimpositions views in the workspace to facilitate side-by-side comparisons of different datasets.

3D rendering reconstruction

The 3D data set can be automatically reconstructed once the scanning finished, using FDK filtering back projection algorithm and the combination of advanced reconstruction engine OptiRecon and DeepRecon. For the reconstruction parameters, ring artifact correction and smoothing were fixed at zero, and the beam artifact correction was set at 40%. The ROIs were selected and rendered in Dragonfly pro software to present 3D volumetric visualization image. Surface area and volume of the measurement can be acquired at once.

Enamel measurements

ROIs were drawn by Dragonfly pro software to include enamel. In 2D interface, the enamel thickness (μm) used to be measured by randomly choosing 4 sites in every surface (buccal/lingual/mesial/distal) on the same plane (occlusal plane as the baseline of superimposition), while in Dragonfly-supported 3D XRM, enamel thickness, surface area (μm2) and volume (μm3) were measured automatically after 3D reconstruction. Occlusal plane was defined as the midpoint of tips of molar cusps. Classic method of enamel density measurement: Raw ROIs of total enamel were selected and whole enamel greyscale was normalized into density by enamel grayscale from the same site. Phantom method of enamel measurement: HA phantom contents with 5 different density value were analytically determined. We imported the test data into dragonfly, adjusted the gray values, and found CT images of the HA phantom and tooth enamel. Then, we imported 5 density value with “mg HA/cm3” and “mg/cm3” two units of bone density. Circular probe was used to input the CT mean values of HA phantom or air into Raw Indensity, and two randomly-chosen density figures were selected for CT normalization. We used the formula to calculate enamel density (gHAp/cm3) by Dragonfly Python. Specific testing parameters were shown in Supplementary Table 1.

Statistical analysis

Data regarding enamel parameters (enamel thickness, surface area, volume, mineral density) underwent statistical analysis with the Graphpad prism Software (version 8.0.2, Boston, USA). A paired t test determined if the observed changes were significant with a 95% confidence level. The level of significance was considered to be 5% (p ≤ 0.05). At same time, previous ROIs were saved for second measurements in the entire 3D volumes for strict standardization of the calculated areas. The examiner was strictly blinded while evaluating images from different groups. The mean of the two measurements was taken as the final data for further comparisons.

Result

2D slides and 3D reconstruction in different voxels

Similar to traditional scanning equipment micro-CT, 3D XRM firstly scanned to generate 2D slices of different voxels (2D overall slide from coronal/sagittal/transverse view: Fig. 1A, 2D overall superimposition from coronal/sagittal/transverse view: Fig. 1B,C). Sagittal sections were cut along the plumb line past the midpoint of skull (based on the location of mid-palatal suture), coronal sections: the plumb line past the occlusal point of first upper and lower molars (midpoint point of mesio-buccal cusps of first upper and lower molars), and cross-sectional sections: the crown plane of mandibular molars. The processing system Dragonfly rendered images colorfully according to their different ROIs, and consequently we can better distinguish details (Fig. 1C). Then 3D overall real-time reconstruction images were performed in interactive interface with X-Y-Z axes (Fig. 1D). We first associating the plane with one of the axes, Dragonfly enabled reproducible loading of tooth and mandible with the overlapping of axes. With the orientation of overall scanning, partial imaging focused on molars, generating sagittal, coronal and transverse 2D slides, 2D superimposition images (Fig. 2A,B). Following 3D overall real-time reconstruction images were presented as imaging and reconstruction methods used before (Fig. 2C). By adjusting objective lens, we can reconstruct fine structure of enamel surface to easily observe enamel decay (Fig. 2D). According to 3D rendering method, Dragonfly presented colorful images from different perspective (Fig. 2E). There showed a decayed lingual surface of lower molar in all three work views.

Enamel measurement---thickness, surface area, and volume

In 3D reconstruction interface, we firstly selected enamel ROIs of every single sample. Then Dragonfly automatically calculated enamel thickness (Fig. 3A). There showed a trend that diabetes caused a thinning of enamel (Fig. 3B). The interface presented mean, maximum, minimum and standard deviation of calculation (Fig. 3C). Here, we represented mean as enamel thickness. Meanwhile, the heat map was vividly reconstructed to demonstrate the thickness status of the whole enamel cap (Fig. 3D). Dragonfly software also automatically generated the average surface area (Fig. 3E,F) and volume (Fig. 3G,H) of the entire enamel. There were no significant differences between two groups in surface area and volume (Fig. 3F,H). Surface area and volume measurement can be acquired from Properties of ROIs interface accordingly (Fig. 3I).

Enamel density: CT value or HA phantom absolute value converting to mineral density?

Traditionally, scholars converted CT value into relative mineral density value (gHAp/cm3) by using a linear calibration curve based on the grey scale values obtained from the mineral reference (linear regression, R2 > 0.9995). We firstly extracted ROIs of enamel, measured CT values of maximum, minimum and mean (Fig. 4A), plotted histogram of CT values in descending order (Fig. 4B), and chose one of sample (DM4) as reference to normalize into relative density for comparison (Fig. 4C). The results showed that HA in WT mouse enamel surpassed DM mouse. Inevitably, different scanning batches was prone to increase intra-group error and inter-group error due to the stability of machine condition. The scanning chamber of 3D XRM may have unstable enough environment to narrow down the difference of CT value between every imaging. In order to further eliminate the system error and under circumstance of requiring absolute density, HA phantom manifests irreplaceable characteristics. We imported the test data into Dragonfly, and found CT images of HA phantom and enamel. Then, we imported density data of HA phantom provided by supplier into Dimension Unit Manager menu (Fig. 4D), producing density units of mg A/cm3 and mg/cm3. Circular probe was used to input the CT mean values of HA phantom or air into Raw Indensity (Fig. 4E), and density data were selected for CT normalization. Enamel data calibrated with the HA phantom can be obtained by circular probe. There was no significant difference in enamel density between groups, but the difference was greater in DM group and smaller in WT group (Fig. 4F). To erase measurement error and assure the accuracy of automated calculation of Dragonfly software, observer measured 5 sites for enamel density and then calculated mean value. Discussion

In this study, we validated 3D XRM techniques as a reliable tool to present craniofacial structure and dental hard tissue by imaging on diabetic model mice. The large demand of experimental animals, and complexity of craniofacial structure require an efficient and high-resolution imaging machine. 3D XRM, a tomographic technique, interrogates the interiors of optically-opaque samples by X-ray illumination34, which enables various measurements with no magnification and detailed assessments in axial, coronal and sagittal slices and in 3D reconstruction work view. 3D XRM routinely achieves down to < 0.7 μm True Spatial Resolution™ reveals osteocyte lacunae (Ocy.Lc) in reasonable detail, which is well-suited for hard tissue investigation. Scanning machines with processing software (Amira35, Dragonfly, et al.) helps a lot in large-sampled experiment.

Scholars have different views on the reference plane in clinical imagery36–38, as well as experimental analysis39,40. The inappropriate selection of reference plane leads to large system errors that cannot be corrected by statistics process. Digital orientation of samples is the first step toward reproducible results, but the shape of mandible and maxilla are too complex to position41. The morphology of molar teeth of many mammalian species, including humans and mice, is such that the long axis of the crown is not continuous with the long axis of the roots. An angle of approximate 25° toward the buccal side exists between the two axes in the mouse molar42. The subtle slant in orientation causes the difference of enamel Slice Plane in each sample, in which enamel thickness varies. Dragonfly Visual Plane tool interactively clips image data, regions of interest, multi-ROIs, and meshes in any arbitrary plane. The processing system is designed such that the perpendicular position of mandible and the long axis of the crown will be continuous between different samples. 3D XRM images are acquired as a volume and can be manipulated at the workstation using DICOM-viewing software to create the corrected image reformations in the sagittal oblique and coronal oblique planes43. Craniofacial structure, especially for TMJ or bimaxillary developmental observation that requires axially corrected images along and perpendicular to the long axis of the mandibular condyle, 3D XRM is recommended for its relative coordinate location module. Images can be manipulated at the workstation software Amira for further processing43. Furthermore, a customized loading system Dragonfly inside the microscope provides advanced reconstruction engine which is conducive to the observation of more subtle hypoplasia and caries on the surface of enamel both in 2D slides and 3D reconstruction work view. Computer Vision has provided immense support to medical diagnostics and experimental researches over the past two decades, especially for 3D imaging. 3 dimension modeling in medical imaging has been pursued using surface rendering, volume rendering and regularization based methods44. Volume rendering is a visualization technique whereby acquired CT volumetric data are processed in their entirety, resulting in a 3D image that can be manipulated and customized in real time to display anatomic details from different perspectives. As a light ray passes through the volume, the volume contributions are accumulated and calculated on specific points along this ray by the process of sampling. Classification is the mechanism by which each sample point is given a color and an opacity value via transfer functions. This step allows the formation of a color representation from gray-scale sections. Compositing is the process by which the color and opacity values from each line of sample points are accumulated by means of a mathematic formula to generate the final projection on the corresponding pixel45. Cinematic rendering, a new 3D reconstruction technique, provides a lifelike representation of imaging data and may have potential for enhancing diagnostic utility compared with volume rendering, particularly in terms of a more natural and physically accurate image with improved shape and depth perception.

Fragmentation index (Fr.I.)46determines the connectivity of enamel, which is calculated in 3D reconstruction version, by comparing the volume and surface of binary solid before and after treatment. In following experiment and exploration, we will focus on further enamel index.

(P and A are the volume and surface and the subscript numbers 1 and 2 indicate before and after treatment.)

Apart from measurements we mentioned in our research, 3D XRM saddled with processing software also characterizes osteocyte lacunar properties13, processes optimization and failure analysis for semiconductor, images low Z materials for materials science, reconstructs virtual histology for atherosclerotic plaque. The versatility inherent on the platform advances studies of soft and hard tissue, both stained and unstained, from microstructure to nanostructure, for applications such as quantification of osteocyte lacunar properties (volume, density, etc.) in bone morphology and investigation of unstained soft cartilage tissue. Higher-resolution have improved the definition of the soft tissue and bone structures of the craniofacial anatomy and its malformations47.

Dragonfly has a user-friendly interface, does not require extensive training and previous segmentation. Our team has worked industriously on developing computerized algorithm and modules that allow auto-calculation and auto-measurement of 3D XRM scans by inserting artificial Intelligence system48. In addition, one of our goals of machine learning development is to limit the human errors in image analysis, in an attempt to automate the processes of calculation and measurement.

However, appropriate scanning duration and sizable chambers of micro-CT is superior to that of 3D XRM, when scanning small narcose animals. Especially for prolonged 3D XRM scanning time, duration and depth of anesthesia in 3D XRM are challenged, which may cause inevitable systematic errors in the whole experiment. Additionally, in-vitro specimen needs extra preparation to fit objectives and geometric magnification13.

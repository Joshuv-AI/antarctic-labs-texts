Safety and efficacy of electro-thumbtack needle for acute mountain sickness patients: a protocol of a randomized, single-blinded, and placebo-controlled study
Authors: Xin Wang, GuangJun Wang, ShuYong Jia, Labasangzhu Labasangzhu, Zirong Wang, Jia Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAcute mountain sickness (AMS) is considered the most common altitude sickness. It can be detrimental to the health of tourists who rapidly ascend high mountains, and can also impair the performance of individuals who move to the plateau for work or education. Acupuncture has been shown to improve AMS as a simple, safe, and effective nonpharmacological method, in case electro-thumbtack needle (ETN) is a more convenient form of acupuncture for both doctor and patient. There are no studies validating the effectiveness of electro-thumbtack needle in improving symptoms in participants with AMS. In this study, we will conduct a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the clinical efficacy and safety of electro-thumbtack needle in participants with AMS. Our hypothesis is that electro-thumbtack needle is safe and effective in treating participants with AMS.MethodsThis study is a single-center, randomized, single-blinded, and placebo-controlled study involving at least 114 participants who were diagnosed with AMS. The participants randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to the electro-thumbtack needle group and the sham acupuncture group. The treatment protocol involved stimulation of seven predefined acupuncture points, including Zhong Wan (RN12), bilateral Nei Guan (PC6), bilateral He Gu (LI4), and bilateral Tai Yang (EX-HN5), for approximately one minute each, with continuous application over 48 h. The primary outcome was improvement in 2018 Lake Louise score (LLS) after 48 h of treatment. Secondary outcome indicators included the incidence of participants with moderate-to-severe AMS (AMS > 5)and AMS, the LLS, visual analogue scale of headache, clinical functioning scores, the Groningen Sleep Quality Survey, the Stanford Somnolence Scale, blood pressure, oxygen saturation, and heart rate, in addition to treatment-related adverse events were also captured.DiscussionThis trial aims to ascertain the therapeutic benefits of ETN in mitigating AMS symptoms, thereby contributing to the evidence base for traditional medical practices, particularly acupuncture, in high-altitude medicine.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trials Registry: ChiCTR2300073882. Registered on 24 July 2023.

Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

Acute mountain sickness consists of a range of non-specific symptoms, the onset of which mainly depends on the altitude and the rate of ascent [1]. Individual differences, environmental factors, and underlying diseases also influence its incidence [2, 3]. The prevalence typically ranges from 10 to 85% [4–7]. As a self-limiting disease, it usually appears within 24 h after entering the plateau and can resolve within a week in mild cases. However, in severe cases, it may progress to high-altitude cerebral edema (HACE) or high-altitude pulmonary edema (HAPE), and can even lead to death [8]. With the economic development and the increasing demand for living, working, or traveling at high altitudes, this disease can no longer be ignored.Descent is usually the most direct and effective way in the presence of severe symptoms, but it is often impractical. Acetazolamide is an effective medication recommended by the guidelines [9], but many studies have involved different altitudes and rates of ascent [10–13], which creates difficulties in the combined interpretation of results, and together with considerations of minimizing its adverse effects, the optimal dose of acetazolamide has also been difficult to determine, and further research is needed to develop new means of control.

Studies have indicated that acupuncture can alleviate altitude sickness symptoms such as altitude hypoxia-related headaches and insomnia [14, 15], Simultaneously, the clinical experience of our team’s acupuncturists in high-altitude areas has also confirmed the efficacy of acupuncture in treating acute mountain sickness. Nevertheless, the application of acupuncture is often constrained by patients’ fear and resistance towards needle-based treatments [16, 17]. Thumbtack needle or called intradermal needle presents a more convenient alternative and effective [18], benefiting both practitioners and patients. The electro-thumbtack needle (ETN) device comprises a 2 mm long thumbtack needle and a 10 mm diameter conductive medical tape. Compared to traditional acupuncture, ETN offers milder stimulation, longer treatment durations, and minimal interference with daily activities. Conductive tape and thumbtack needle can form an overall electrochemical effect, so when it covers the epidermis to fix the thumbtack needle and bury the needles, the ETN current generating a stronger stimulation. However, no prior research has explored the potential of ETN in improving AMS. Therefore, the present study aims to conduct a randomized controlled trials to assess the clinical efficacy and safety of ETN in AMS patients.

Objectives {7}

The purpose of this study was to conduct a randomized, single-blind, placebo-controlled trial to examine the therapeutic effect of ETN on improving symptoms in patients with AMS.

Trial design {8}

This study was a single-center, parallel-group design with a randomized, single-blind and placebo-controlled method. It is a superiority test. Participants with a diagnosis of AMS will be recruited and randomly assigned to the ETN group and the sham acupuncture (SA) group, maintaining a 1:1 ratio. The study consists of a five days follow-up period.

Methods: participants, interventions and outcomes

Study setting {9}

This study was set up as a recruitment site, Tibet University, which is located in Lhasa, China, at an altitude of 3650 m. Participants are treated primarily by licensed acupuncturists.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

Participants diagnosed with AMS will be randomly assigned to the ETN group and the SA group in a 1:1 ratio. The ETN device comprises a 2 mm long thumbtack needle and a 10 mm diameter conductive medical tape. Conductive tape and thumbtack needle can form an overall electrochemical effect, so when it covers the epidermis to fix the thumbtack needle and bury the needles, the ETN current generating a stronger stimulation. Placebo needles have the same appearance as ETN, except that they are blunt-tipped and do not pierce the skin. This ensures that participants are blinded to the intervention.

Intervention description {11a}

Treatments will be performed by a professional licensed acupuncturist. The ETN program entails the selection of a total of 7 acupuncture points, as per the guidelines specified in the National Standard of the People’s Republic of China 2021 (GB/T 12346 − 2021) “Names and Positioning of Meridian Points”. These acupuncture points include Zhong Wan (RN12), bilateral Nei Guan (PC6), bilateral He Gu (LI4), and bilateral Tai Yang (EX-HN5).

The procedure involves routine disinfection of the skin prior to administration. Following the initial application, the healthcare practitioner applies the ETN to stimulate the targeted acupuncture points, aiming for the patient to experience a sensation of tingling sensation, distension, numbness, or acidity in the respective areas. Each acupuncture point should be stimulated for approximately one minutes, and it is recommended for participants to self-administer ETN once a day following the provided schedule: in the morning, midday, evening, and before bedtime.

To ensure safety and sterility, a single-use sterile ETN bearing the specifications 0.22*2 mm and the registration number 20,172,200,965 is to be employed. Regarding the frequency, duration, and timing of the intervention, the ETN should be left in place for 48 h before removal. The SA group was treated with the same protocol as the ETN groups.

If the patient’s AMS symptoms are intolerable, emergency management should be instituted, which may include remaining at the current altitude and symptomatic management (e.g., non-opioid analgesics for headache, antiemetic medications for nausea and vomiting). If symptoms worsen or do not improve after 1–2 days of appropriate intervention, or if the participant develops evidence of HAPE (defined as severe cough, dyspnea at rest, cyanosis, and rales) or HACE (defined as ataxia, altered consciousness, or both), the condition may be diagnosed by a licensed clinician and then transported to a hospital for immediate treatment.

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions {11b}

Participants may withdraw from this study at any time during the course of the study, and participants may also be terminated from continuing this study when the participant encounters circumstances that make continuation of the study unsuitable, including severe deterioration, serious adverse events, poor compliance, and other problems.

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

The period of this study was one week, our visit sites were relatively intensive, and each participant had a regular follower to monitor health conditions to enhance compliance, and it was only when multiple contacts failed that the researcher chose to remove the participant and record the reasons.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

Throughout the study period, participants were treated with a standard treatment program. If the patient’s self-perceived condition during treatment is intolerable, emergency medication options are available with notification to the follow-up investigator, including Rhodiola rosea capsules, four capsules three times a day, ibuprofen, 600 mg every 8 h, Compound Danshen Drops, ten pills three times a day, and Gaoyuanan capsules, three capsules three times a day.

Provisions for post-trial care {30}

Not applicable, the disease is acute and self-limiting. General symptoms resolve within one week, and participants who develop severe symptoms or progress to HACE and HAPE will be transported to a hospital for standard treatment and medication procedures.

Outcomes {12}

Both the ETN group and the SA group were visited before and 6 h, 12 h, 24 h, 48 h, 72 h, 5d, 7d after treatment. The primary outcome was improvement in 2018 LLS after 48 h of treatment. Secondary outcome indicators included the incidence of participants with moderate-to-severe AMS (AMS > 5 and AMS, visual analogue scale(VAS) of headache, clinical functioning scores(CFS), the Groningen Sleep Quality Survey (GSQS), the Stanford Somnolence Scale (SSS), blood pressure(Bp), oxygen saturation(SO2), and heart rate(HR), LLS, CFS, VAS, GSQS, SSS, BP, HR, and SO2 were measured before and 48 h after treatment, LLS, CFS, VAS were measured 6 h ,12 h and 72 h after treatment, and LLS, CFS, VAS, GSQS, and SSS were measured 24 h after treatment. LLS and CFS were measured on days 5 and 7 after treatment. Calculation of the rate was based on the last collected data accordingly. The rate was calculated accordingly to the last data collected. in addition to treatment-related adverse events were also captured.

Sample size {14}

Post-treatment LLS will be used as the primary outcome for sample size estimation; this study is the first randomized controlled trial to validate the efficacy of ETN therapy for AMS, so there is no reference study to calculate sample size. The team’s pre-small sample pre-trial showed 2.22 ± 1.30 in the LLS treatment group and 3.90 ± 1.20 in the control group after 6 h of treatment. (We did not obtain more data due to conditions). We performed calculations with this data, setting the superiority value to -1 and α to 0.05, so that the final sample size of a group of 45 cases could have a power of 80%, and taking into account the 20% dropout rate, we further increased the sample size to a group of 57.

Recruitment {15}

Participants diagnosed with AMS will be recruited. The study aims to collect at least 114 participants with AMS from 2023.08.01-2025.04.01. Participants will be screened primarily at the University of Tibet, and since AMS occurs every year from the plains into the plateau, we anticipate that there will be sufficient enrollment to ensure our sample size.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

This study was simply randomized using a team-developed central randomization system, and participants were assigned to the ETN group and the SA group in a 1:1 ratio using a computerized central randomization procedure after enrollment. As recruitment was completed, participants were only given a Subject-Specific ID (SSID) without knowing the intervention grouping used.

Concealment mechanism {16b}

This experiment used a single-blind design in which participants were blinded throughout the experiment, and the central randomization system grouped them according to random numbers, so that participants and trial staff could not distinguish visually which intervention was used. After informed consent is obtained, randomization codes and group assignments are made by specialized researchers through the central randomization system, and administrators independent of the study intervention and evaluation will assign medical appliances in advance based on grouping. Since the treatment is performed by a specialized acupuncturist, it is difficult to blind the treating practitioner, but the symptom assessor, recorder, and follow-up staff are fully blinded.

Implementation {16c}

Information about the trial is disseminated through posters, and research assistants explain our program in detail to willing participants and recruit them. Participants who met the inclusion criteria and signed an informed consent form to participate in the study were assigned a randomization code and group through a centralized randomization system by a specialized researcher. A professional acupuncturist will select the medical appliances corresponding to the group and treat the participants according to the group.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {17a}

With the exception of the treating acupuncturist and the researcher who distributed the randomized groups (both of whom were not involved in the rest of the study), the participants, the symptom-recording researcher, and the followers who supervised the symptoms and adverse events were unaware of the randomization status.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

Unless the patient develops a serious adverse event or a serious deterioration in clinical condition, blinding is always maintained, and in the event of urgent unblinding the investigator records the date, reason, and should be documented on the case report form (CRF).

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {18a}

Clinical data, including general patient profile information, baseline data, will be collected directly through the patient, the CRF will be initially filled in on paper and then entered into the centralized randomization system by the relevant personnel, and finally the data will be exported to Microsoft Excel in a uniform manner, and the follow up period will last until the 7th day after the treatment.

Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up {18b}

Each participant had a dedicated follow-up appointment via WeChat for symptomatic follow-up at 6 h, 12 h, 24 h, 48 h, 72 h, 5d, and 7d post-treatment, and certain participant allowances were given to enhance adherence.

Data management {19}

Patient data is initially obtained from paper CRFs, then transcribed into the system’s electronic CRFs and securely stored on the computer in Excel format through uniform export to Microsoft Excel. Specialized data monitors are in place to randomly check the CRFs completed by investigators during both paper CRF completion and electronic CRF entry, and electronic CRFs are completed with character type restrictions and logical controls for quality control to minimize potential human error.

Confidentiality {27}

The original case report form and informed consent form will be placed in a locked file cabinet at the Institute of Clinical Basic Medicine of Traditional Chinese Medicine, China Academy of Chinese Medical Sciences. Patient identification details will not be disclosed in any publications. After publication of the article, trial data can be accessed by contacting the authors to facilitate international academic exchange.

Plans for collection, laboratory evaluation and storage of biological specimens for genetic or molecular analysis in this trial/future use {33}

Participant saliva sample collection was performed before and after treatment. Sample saliva is stored in a -80 °C refrigerator and all samples collected during the trial are labeled with the patient’s unique SSID. Upon completion of the study, the samples will be destroyed upon request.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

For descriptive analyses, normally distributed continuous variables were reported using mean and standard deviation, and non-normally distributed using interquartile spacing and median. For categorical variables, the main report was percentage.

Comparisons between groups of measures that conformed to a normal distribution through the normality test were made using the t-test; comparisons between groups of measures that were not normally distributed were made using the rank sum test. The chi-square test or Fisher’s exact probability method was chosen for count data depending on whether they conformed to normal distribution. Unless otherwise specified, the overall comparison test level between groups α = 0.05, P < 0.05 will be considered as statistically significant differences. Analysis of repeated measures data: Repeated-measures data were analyzed by between-group repeated-measures modeling (comparison of trends in score improvement at different time points between two groups). Ggeneralized estimating equation (GEE) model was selected for analysis to compare the differences between two groups in LLS, VAS of headache, CFS. Survival analysis: Survival curves were plotted using the Kaplan-Meier method; survival differences were assessed using the Log-Rank test; multivariate analyses were performed using the Cox proportional risk model, and landmark analyses were used if the survival curves crossed to assess the effect of intervening factors on the rate of recurrence.

Interim analyses {21b}

An interim analysis will be conducted when approximately 50% of our sample has completed the follow-up assessment.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g. subgroup analyses) {20b}

Subgroups were analyzed according to sex, BMI, Bp, time of arrival at the plateau, presence of AMS, SO2, mode of transportation, and prior therapeutic drug use history.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c}

For each outcome, we typically analyze at the participant level using intention-to-treat (ITT), where all participants who record an outcome are included in the analysis according to the intervention group to which they were randomly assigned. For important outcome indicators, where the missingness rate is small (10–15%), the fill method will be used as the main analysis method for missing data, taking the last observation and filling in each missing value. Sensitivity analyses were conducted using the findings from the different filling methods in the meantime.

Plans to give access to the full protocol, participant level-data and statistical code {31c}

The protocol has been uploaded to https://www.chictr.org.cn/ (ID: ChiCTR2300073882). Datasets for the current study are also available from the principal investigator upon reasonable request.

Composition of the data monitoring committee, its role and reporting structure {21a}

AMS is an acute, self-limiting disease, and acupuncture intervention is a low-risk intervention; therefore, this study is a short-term study and one that generally does not have significant safety concerns, and therefore a dedicated data monitoring committee was not considered.

Adverse event reporting and harms {22}

Any adverse events occurring during the study, whether or not related to the study, will be evaluated and recorded in the Adverse Reaction Report Form. Serious adverse events will be reported to the Ethics Committee of the Institute of Clinical Basic Medicine of Traditional Chinese Medicine, China Academy of Chinese Medical Sciences within 24 h of the event. The Principal Investigator will determine whether the adverse event or serious adverse event is related to the study intervention. If the reported event is related to the intervention, the intervention will be stopped immediately.

Frequency and plans for auditing trial conduct {23}

Frequency of project tracking review is 12 months, as requested by the Institute.

Plans for communicating important protocol amendments to relevant parties (e.g. trial participants, ethical committees) {25}

When major modifications to the trial occur, any amendments to the protocol will be submitted by the Principal Investigator to the Medical Ethics Committee of the Institute of Clinical Basic Medicine of Traditional Chinese Medicine, China Academy of Traditional Chinese Medicine for approval. If the revision involves trial participants, they will also be notified.

Dissemination plans {31a}

The results of the study will be published in international peer-reviewed journals and disseminated to relevant healthcare professionals, the public and other interested groups after the results are published.

Discussion

Traditional medicine has potential for development in specific environmental diseases, but evidence is scarce [19, 20]. AMS is a group of syndromes consisting of nonspecific symptoms. Acupuncture has been shown to be effective for headaches, dizziness, nausea and vomiting at low altitudes. Thus, acupuncture therapy, as a non-pharmacological treatment with fewer side effects [21], may be an effective and safer alternative for treating AMS.

Currently acetazolamide is the most recognized effective and appropriate AMS prophylactic drug recommended by guidelines, however its adverse effects limit its use [22], and different altitudes and rates of climbing respond differently to drugs. Thumbtack-needle is a special form of acupuncture, with “shallow, safe, painless, convenient and long-lasting” as the main characteristics. Electro thumbtack needle further adopts conductive tape to cover and fix thumbtack-needles in the body, which can greatly increase the spontaneous electrochemical current in the covered area, and is a combination of mechanical stimulation and biological and chemical stimulation at the same time. It is a unique acupuncture technique that combines mechanical stimulation and biological and chemical stimulation to enhance the therapeutic effect.

One of the key techniques in this study is the selection of acupoints, which were selected based on the expert consensus of the preferred acupoints. Acupoint Nei Guan (PC6) stimulation seemed to counteract the effects of acute normobaric hypoxia on end-diastolic velocity in middle cerebral artery [23], especially after acupuncture, and significantly reduced systolic and diastolic blood pressure. He Gu (LI4) relieves anxiety [24], relief pain such as pain during labor [25] relief nausea and vomiting [26], and Tai Yang (EX-HN5) can superiorly improve migraine [15], and significantly reduced systolic and diastolic blood, Zhong Wan (RN12) is often used to treat digestive disorders. The second of the key techniques is the implementation of the acupuncture blinding method. The current study is the first innovation in conducting blinding of portable acupuncture equipment: blunt thumbtack needle, we purposely searched for blunt-tipped thumbtack needle as a control, which do not pierce the skin and do not produce electrical stimulation. It to be used for the successful implementation of thumbtack needle blinding. The third key technique is the selection of outcome indicators, which is selected from the current common and recognized indicators for AMS. And the safety of acupuncture was taken as an important investigation index such as tingling sensation, hematoma, bleeding, and skin sensitization. Also, multiple time points were used for observation to evaluate short-term efficacy and long-term efficacy.

There are certainly some challenges to our research. Due to the unique nature of the school, the research environment and time constraints make it potentially challenging to recruit a sufficient number of qualified participants and ensure their engagement and compliance during the study. We are actively preparing for this issue, for example, joint school publicity. Meanwhile, extending the preliminary findings to a wider range of application scenarios and populations may require additional research.

In summary, this study will provide information to establish the safety and potential efficacy of ETN in a broader population of people with AMS to reduce disease progression and promote symptomatic recovery.

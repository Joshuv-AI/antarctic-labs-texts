Development and validation of a disease-syndrome integrated assessment model for AECOPD severity: protocol for a nationwide multicentre cross-sectional study
Authors: Hai-Long Zhang, Jia-Min Liu, Ya Li, Long-Yu Wang, Pei-Lin Jia, Zhao-Xu Yao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAcute exacerbations of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (AECOPD) accelerate disease progression and increase mortality. Accurate severity assessment is essential for standardised clinical management. Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) shows efficacy in treating AECOPD, but no practical TCM-specific severity assessment tool exists. This study integrates multidimensional Western and TCM variables to develop an AECOPD severity assessment model, aiming to support clinical evaluation, improve treatment efficacy and prognosis and offer methodological insights for related research.Methods and analysisThe overall study design comprises four sequential steps. First, factors influencing AECOPD severity will be preliminarily screened through literature review and the first round of expert questionnaire surveys. Second, a multicentre cross-sectional study will be performed to establish a clinical information database; multiple statistical methods, combined with a second expert questionnaire survey, will be employed to identify the relevant variables associated with disease severity. Third, based on the established clinical database and identified variables, a disease-syndrome integrated AECOPD severity assessment model will be developed using both Classification and Regression Trees and Backpropagation Neural Networks. Fourth, the models will undergo comprehensive evaluation and validation to determine an accurate, practical, reproducible and externally valid disease severity assessment tool.Ethics and disseminationThe study protocol and all required documents have been submitted for review and approval to the Independent Ethics Committees of all the participating sites. All participants will provide their written informed consent on study entry, and all the recorded data will be treated as confidential. Ethical approval for this study was granted by the Institutional Review Board of the First Affiliated Hospital of Henan University of Chinese Medicine (Approval No. 2025HL-342)Trial registration numberNCT06918353.Keywords: COPD Exacerbations, Machine Learning

Introduction

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a prevalent chronic disease with high morbidity and mortality worldwide, severely endangering human health.1,3 It is estimated that over 400 million people are affected by COPD globally.4 According to the WHO, COPD consistently ranks as the third leading cause of death worldwide, responsible for approximately 3.23 million deaths in 2019.4 The Global Burden of Disease study shows that although age-standardised prevalence rates have declined, the absolute number of COPD cases has increased by 112.23% since 1990 due to population growth and ageing.5 Globally, the prevalence of COPD among people aged 40 years and above ranges from approximately 10% to 15%, with significant geographical variations, and the highest burden is observed in East Asia, South Asia and sub-Saharan Africa.6 In China, the prevalence rate among people aged 40 years and above is as high as 13.7%, and approximately 27% among those aged 60 years and above.7 Acute exacerbations of COPD (AECOPD) are critical events in the disease’s natural history, closely associated with accelerated health deterioration, reduced quality of life, loss of work productivity, accelerated lung function decline, increased healthcare costs and heightened mortality risk. Worldwide, patients with COPD experience approximately 0.5 to 3.5 acute exacerbations annually.8 Regarding economic burden, hospitalisation and medication costs associated with AECOPD account for 68% to 83% of total direct medical costs for COPD.8 Data from the United States indicate that annual direct medical costs attributable to COPD were estimated at US$31.3 billion in 2019, projected to increase to US$60.5 billion by 2029.9 Studies from European countries also report substantial economic burdens, with average annual healthcare costs for uncontrolled patients with COPD in France reaching approximately €12 000.10

Accurate assessment of AECOPD is crucial for standardising the formulation of clinical strategies, thereby improving patient prognosis and optimising the allocation of healthcare resources. Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) demonstrates good efficacy and advantages in treating AECOPD. Multiple randomised controlled trials and systematic reviews/meta-analyses have provided evidence-based support for the effectiveness of TCM in treating AECOPD. For instance, a recently published meta-analysis including 40 randomised controlled trials involving 3475 patients with AECOPD showed that adding Qingjin Huatan decoction to conventional Western medicine significantly improved lung function, reduced inflammatory factor levels, without increasing the risk of adverse reactions.11 Another multicentre randomised double-blind placebo-controlled trial focusing on the ‘AECOPD risk window’ included 336 patients and confirmed that TCM treatment based on syndrome differentiation significantly reduced the risk of acute exacerbations, prolonged the time to first acute exacerbation and improved clinical symptoms.12 Furthermore, acupuncture, as an important TCM therapeutic modality, has been evaluated in a meta-analysis of adjunctive treatment for AECOPD, showing that adding acupuncture to conventional Western medicine improved clinical effectiveness and enhanced lung function and blood gas parameters.13 Chinese herbal formulas such as Jianpi Huatan Tongfu granule have also been shown to alleviate inflammatory responses and improve clinical symptoms in patients with AECOPD.14 Mechanistic studies of formulas like Qingjin Huatan decoction have further revealed the molecular basis of their anti-inflammatory effects through regulation of the miR-340-5p/MAP3K2 pathway.15 16 Currently, several tools are used to assess AECOPD severity, including the Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) grading, the Chinese expert consensus criteria, the EXACT-PRO and the Rome proposal. However, these tools have notable limitations: they rely primarily on physiological parameters and clinical judgement, lack integration of patient-reported outcomes and TCM syndromes and exhibit moderate inter-rater reliability. Moreover, none of them incorporate TCM syndrome differentiation which is essential for guiding TCM treatment. Despite the established efficacy of TCM in AECOPD, there is currently no universally accepted severity classification standard that incorporates TCM syndrome differentiation. Furthermore, no accurate, practical and TCM-feature-specific disease assessment tools are available for clinical use. Existing tools, such as the GOLD grading system, rely solely on Western medical parameters and do not capture TCM syndrome information, limiting their utility in guiding integrated TCM-Western medicine treatment. There is an inherent logical connection between the efficacy of TCM treatment and the development of TCM-featured assessment tools: the core of TCM treatment lies in ‘syndrome differentiation and treatment determination’, formulating individualised treatment plans based on the patient’s current syndrome type. Therefore, accurate TCM syndrome identification is the prerequisite and foundation for guiding TCM treatment. However, current severity assessment of AECOPD is primarily based on Western medical indicators (such as GOLD grading) without adequately integrating TCM syndrome information, resulting in the lack of a unified assessment framework for integrated Chinese and Western medicine clinical decision-making. Given the accumulating evidence that TCM can improve outcomes in AECOPD, there is an urgent need to develop a severity assessment tool that integrates both Western medical indicators and TCM syndrome elements to support personalised, integrated treatment decisions. This study aims to fill this gap by constructing a disease-syndrome integrated model using machine learning.

The research hypothesis of this study is: by integrating multidimensional Western medical indicators and quantified TCM syndrome elements, a ‘disease-syndrome integrated’ AECOPD severity assessment model will be constructed. This model will not only reflect the patient’s Western medical disease severity but also capture their TCM syndrome characteristics, thereby providing more precise decision support for formulating integrated treatment plans (including TCM treatment). In other words, the innovation of this study lies not in developing new treatment methods but in developing an assessment tool that can better guide treatment decisions (including TCM treatment).

The rapid development of big data technology and artificial intelligence enables clinicians to use clinical prediction models for more precise screening and assessment, facilitating more scientific clinical decision-making, better disease prevention and management and rational allocation and optimisation of medical resources.17 18 Machine learning algorithms, as a core technology of artificial intelligence, are increasingly applied in disease prediction modelling19; however, there is still a lack of multistrategy modelling research for the assessment of AECOPD severity. Therefore, drawing on the concepts of machine learning algorithms and adopting multistrategy modelling methods to construct an accurate, practical, reproducible and externally validated disease-syndrome integrated AECOPD severity assessment model is of great significance for accurately assessing disease condition, guiding the formulation of clinical strategies and standardising clinical diagnosis and treatment. It can also provide ideas and methods for research on disease assessment models in related fields.

Methods and analysis

Study design and setting

The overall research design comprises four sequential modules: variable screening, database establishment and variable determination, model construction and model evaluation. Specific procedures include: (1) preliminary screening of factors influencing AECOPD severity through literature review and the first-round expert questionnaire survey; (2) establishment of a clinical information database via multicentre cross-sectional investigation, with identification of severity-associated variables using multiple statistical methods combined with a second-round expert questionnaire; (3) development of a disease-syndrome integrated AECOPD severity assessment model employing both Classification and Regression Trees (CART) and Backpropagation Neural Networks (BPNN) based on the established database and identified variables and (4) model evaluation and validation to determine an accurate, practical, reproducible and externally validated severity assessment tool. Variable selection

Literature research

Literature search was conducted across CNKI, WANFANG Data, VIP Chinese Journal Service Platform, SinoMed, PubMed, Cochrane Library and Embase to identify studies related to AECOPD severity assessment. Database-specific search strategies were developed with retrieval periods spanning from inception to 30 June 2025. Two researchers independently screened records according to inclusion/exclusion criteria, cross-checked results and consulted a third reviewer to resolve discrepancies. Inclusion criteria: (1) Study population: AECOPD-diagnosed patients; (2) Study design: randomised controlled trials, cross-sectional studies, cohort studies, case-control studies or retrospective analyses; (3) Explicit reporting of severity-associated variables (personal history, laboratory tests, pulmonary function, chest CT, quality of life, exercise tolerance, TCM syndromes). Exclusion criteria: (1) case reports or systematic reviews and (2) conference abstracts or unavailable full-text articles.

Using SPSS V.25.0, extracted data from eligible studies—including basic information (title, first author, journal, publication year) and target variables (personal history, laboratory tests, pulmonary function, chest CT, quality of life, exercise tolerance, TCM syndromes)—were compiled into the AECOPD Severity Assessment Variable Database. Frequency analysis for each extracted variable and association tests (eg, χ² tests for categorical variables, t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests for continuous variables) between potential predictors and AECOPD severity outcomes were performed in SPSS V.25.0. Visualisation analysis was implemented in CiteSpace V.5.8.

The first round of expert consultation

Based on variables screened through literature review, an expert consultation questionnaire was developed assessing three domains: personal characteristics, disease-related indicators and objective laboratory/imaging parameters. The questionnaire employed a 5-point Likert scale. Surveys were distributed via electronic forms and postal correspondence. Thirty influential respiratory specialists meeting the following criteria were recruited: (a) associate senior title or higher and (b) ≥10 years of clinical experience in TCM, Western medicine or integrated respiratory practice.

In China, many respiratory physicians receive training in both Western medicine and TCM, and integrated practice is common in tertiary hospitals. Our expert panel was deliberately composed to include specialists with diverse backgrounds: approximately one-third with predominant TCM experience, one-third with Western medicine experience, and one-third with integrated experience. All experts met the criterion of ≥10 years of clinical experience in their respective fields, ensuring a balanced perspective on AECOPD severity assessment.

Returned questionnaires underwent data verification with exclusion of ineligible responses. SPSS V.25.0 was used for data entry and database construction. Delphi method calculations were performed to analyse the expert responses. Variables with high importance scores (based on percentile weighting) were preliminarily identified as candidate severity-associated factors for further investigation.

Database establishment and variable determination

Participant-clinical investigation

A multicentre cross-sectional investigation enrolled patients with AECOPD from outpatient or inpatient settings at eight hospitals nationwide (The First Affiliated Hospital of Henan University of Chinese Medicine (Central China), Peking University Third Hospital (North China), Jiangsu Province Hospital of Chinese Medicine (East China), Hospital of Chengdu University of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Southwest China), Shenzhen Hospital of Southern Medical University (South China), Qingdao Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Qingdao Hiser Hospital) (East China), Xiangyang Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Central China), Affiliated Hospital of Liaoning University of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Northeast China)). These eight hospitals are all integrated traditional Chinese and Western medicine respiratory disease diagnosis and treatment centres in their respective regions, with extensive experience in AECOPD diagnosis and treatment and strong research capabilities, ensuring high-quality data collection. Through this site selection strategy covering six major geographical regions (North China, Northeast China, East China, Central China, South China, Southwest China), the samples collected in this study can better represent the clinical characteristics and TCM syndrome distribution of patients with AECOPD across different regions of China. Furthermore, unified training and data collection standards will be implemented across all hospitals to ensure consistency and comparability of multicentre data. Diagnostic criteria referenced the 2024 GOLD and the Chinese Expert Consensus on Diagnosis and Management of AECOPD (2023 Revision).

Severity grading will be based on the severity classification criteria for AECOPD outlined in the 2024 GOLD report, which represents the current internationally recognised clinical practice guideline. The specific grades are: Mild: (1) Dyspnoea Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) score <5; (2) respiratory rate (RR) <24 breaths/min; (3) heart rate (HR) <95 bpm; (4) oxygen saturation (SaO2≥92%) while resting and breathing ambient air (or the patient’s usual prescribed oxygen) with a change ≤3% (if prior data before exacerbation is available) and (5) C reactive protein (CRP) <10 mg/L (if obtained). Moderate (meeting at least three of the following five criteria*): (1) Dyspnoea VAS score ≥5; (2) RR≥24 breaths/min; (3) HR≥95 bpm; (4) SaO2<92% while resting and breathing ambient air (or the patient’s usual prescribed oxygen) with a change >3% (if prior data before exacerbation is available); and (5) CRP≥10 mg/L. *If ABG is obtained, it may show hypoxaemia (PaO2≤60 mm Hg) and/or hypercapnia (PaCO2>45 mm Hg), but without acidosis. Severe: (1) Dyspnoea, RR, HR, SaO2 and CRP are the same as in the moderate category and (2) ABG shows new or worsening hypercapnia and acidosis (PaCO2>45 mm Hg and pH<7.35). This grading will be determined by two independent study physicians based on the patient’s initial clinical presentation and treatment decisions on admission. Any discrepancies will be resolved through discussion or adjudication by a third senior physician. This preliminary GOLD-based grading will then be further evaluated in conjunction with patients' clinical outcomes (e.g., in-hospital death, transfer to ICU, ventilation use, or 30-day readmission). If the clinical outcomes align with the GOLD grade, the classification will be finalized; if inconsistencies arise, the final severity grade will be determined by expert panel consensus.

We employ this widely accepted clinical standard as the ‘ground truth’ labels for model training based on the following rationale: (1) Clinical relevance and interpretability: The GOLD criteria are directly linked to specific levels of clinical intervention. A model based on these criteria yields predictions that are easily understood and adopted by clinicians, facilitating direct translation into treatment decisions. (2) Consensus and comparability: Using a consensus standard ensures that our findings are comparable to the vast body of existing literature and clinical practice. ③ Core innovation of the study: The core innovation of this study lies in developing a model that can accurately and objectively predict or classify AECOPD severity by leveraging multidimensional indicators. We hypothesise that by incorporating dimensions not covered by the GOLD criteria, such as TCM syndrome differentiation information, this model can provide richer, more personalised pathophysiological insights than the application of GOLD classification alone, and may identify patient subgroups with distinct risk profiles within a given GOLD grade.

TCM syndrome differentiation adhered to the Diagnostic Criteria for TCM Syndromes of COPD (2011) issued by the Pulmonary Disease Committee of Internal Medicine Branch, China Association of Chinese Medicine. Inclusion criteria: (1) Meeting AECOPD diagnostic criteria and (2) Voluntary participation with signed informed consent. Exclusion criteria: Comorbid major psychiatric disorders or cognitive impairment precluding effective communication.

Sample size estimation

The sample size was calculated considering two primary factors: (1) the requirements for developing a stable machine learning model and (2) ensuring sufficient representation of the ‘Severe’ class (the anticipated minority class based on clinical outcomes). Based on preliminary data from participating hospitals, the proportion of patients meeting the ‘Severe’ criteria (composite endpoint) is estimated to be approximately 15%. A common rule of thumb for classification tasks is to have at least 50–100 events (samples in the minority class) per candidate feature in the final model. After preliminary screening and expert review, we anticipate approximately 30–50 key variables will enter the final modelling phase. To be conservative and ensure robust model development, we aim for at least 100 events in the ‘Severe’ class. Therefore, the total required sample size is estimated to be: 100/0.15=667. To account for potential data incompleteness, multicentre variation, and to provide ample data for training complex models (like BPNN) and internal validation, we will enrol ≥1000 patients in the cross-sectional study. This sample size also comfortably exceeds the common heuristic of 10–20 samples per final variable, even at the upper end of our variable estimate (20 * 50=1000).

Additionally, external validation will independently recruit a prospective cohort of 500 patients with AECOPD. Therefore, the total sample size for this study is at least 1500 patients (1000 for model development+500 for external validation).

Investigation content and quality control

A clinical survey form was developed based on literature review, expert questionnaire results and AECOPD disease characteristics, comprising: (a) Patient demographics: name, age, sex, smoking history; (b) Disease-related indicators: disease duration, prior-year exacerbation frequency, comorbidities, clinical symptoms/signs, TCM syndromes; (c) Objective parameters: complete blood count, hepatic/renal function, electrocardiography, chest CT, pulmonary function. Inpatients completed the clinical survey form (as described above) within 24 hours of admission. Quality control measures included: (1) Standardised clinical forms and informed consent documents; (2) Uniform training for multicentre researchers with inter-rater reliability testing ensuring accuracy and authenticity and (3) Quality-control team conducting periodic random audits.

Regarding the determination of TCM syndrome diagnosis: To ensure consistency across multiple centres, TCM syndrome differentiation will not rely on a single qualitative diagnosis. Instead, it will be operationalised using a standardised ‘TCM Syndrome Element Assessment Form’. This form deconstructs the syndrome into quantifiable elements (eg, for ‘Phlegm-Heat Obstructing the Lung syndrome’: cough with thick, yellow sputum (frequency/colour score), fever (body temperature/severity score), thirst, tongue coating, pulse condition). Each element is assessed using a Likert scale or predefined categories. All participating TCM physicians will receive centralised training on this form, and inter-rater reliability will be assessed using a subset of cases to ensure consistency (target Cohen’s kappa >0.6).

Establishment of clinical database and variable screening

Following collection, verification and consolidation of surveys from all centres (forms with >20% missing items were omitted), two independent data administrators used EpiData V.3.1 to establish a clinical information database. Statistical analysis in R software employed Lasso regression, elastic net and ridge regression to identify variables significant in ≥2 methods (p<0.05). Notably, TCM syndrome elements (as collected through the above-mentioned form) will be included as candidate variables in the screening process alongside Western medical indicators, ensuring that TCM-related information is fully considered from the early stages of model development.

Second round of expert consultation

An expert questionnaire was developed based on variables screened from the clinical investigation for the second-round consultation. Expert selection and questionnaire evaluation criteria were identical to the first round. Relevant variables influencing AECOPD severity were determined according to survey results.

Construction of disease assessment model

Data processing and grouping

A clinical information database was established from 1000 patients with AECOPD surveyed. The database comprises two components: feature sets and classification labels. Feature sets include identified severity-associated variables, including quantified TCM syndrome elements; classification labels have three severity levels (mild, moderate, severe). Data underwent imputation, clustering, dimensionality reduction, cleaning and normalisation. After preprocessing, the dataset was randomly divided into 10 subgroups for 10-fold cross-validation: nine subgroups were used as the training set, and the remaining subgroup as the testing set, with the process iterated 10 times to ensure robust internal validation.

Machine learning algorithms and model construction

CART constitute a binary decision tree comprising CART. Classification trees employ the Gini index for feature selection, while regression trees use the least squared error minimisation principle, ultimately forming a binary tree structure. The CART pruning algorithm involves: (1) generating subtree sequences and (2) cross-validating subtrees. To mitigate overfitting, the CART algorithm will employ cost-complexity pruning. The full tree will first be grown, and then a sequence of nested subtrees will be generated by increasing the cost-complexity parameter (α). The optimal subtree will be selected via 10-fold cross-validation on the training set, choosing the model with the smallest cross-validated error. These procedures were implemented in R software to construct the final CART model. BPNN represent multilayer feedforward neural networks based on error backpropagation, structured with input, hidden and output layers. BPNN learning comprises four processes: (1) Forward propagation: Data transmission from input to output layers via hidden layers; (2) Error backpropagation: Error signals (difference between expected/actual outputs) propagate backward to adjust connection weights; (3) Iterative training: Alternating forward propagation and backpropagation for memory consolidation; (4) Convergence: Global error minimisation towards network stabilisation. R software was used to execute BPNN model construction.

Evaluation and validation of models

Internal validation

Internal validation was performed using 10-fold cross-validation during model construction. Model discrimination and calibration were assessed across the full dataset, with predicted probabilities as the independent variable and AECOPD severity as the categorical outcome. Discrimination was evaluated by plotting receiver operating characteristic curves in R software and calculating the area under the curve (AUC). Calibration was verified through calibration curves and the Brier score.

External validation

To ensure temporal and geographical independence from the model development data, external validation will be performed using a prospectively enrolled cohort of 500 patients with AECOPD recruited after the completion of the cross-sectional study and the finalisation of the prediction model. Recruitment will take place at two new centres not involved in the initial cross-sectional survey. Subject eligibility, diagnostic criteria, inclusion/exclusion criteria, data collection protocols (including the standardised TCM Syndrome Element Form), and quality control measures will be identical to those used in the model development phase. This prospective cohort will constitute the independent validation set. Discrimination and calibration of the finalised prediction model will be evaluated in this set. The optimal model will be determined based on its performance across both internal and external validation metrics, alongside clinical applicability assessments.

Comparison with existing standards in predicting clinical outcomes

Although this study employs the 2024 GOLD classification as the ‘ground truth’ labels for model training, the ultimate clinical value of the model lies in whether it can more accurately reflect the patient’s true disease burden than existing standards. To this end, we will conduct a prespecified subanalysis in the external validation cohort: compare the ability of our integrated model versus the sole use of the 2024 GOLD severity classification in predicting key clinical outcomes. We will calculate the predictive performance (eg, AUC, sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, negative predictive value) of both the model’s predicted probabilities and the GOLD grades for the final selected key outcome indicators, and compare the AUCs using the DeLong test. This analysis will directly test whether our model can better identify high-risk patients than the current GOLD standard, thereby demonstrating its superiority in reflecting disease burden.

Model interpretation and comparison

Model interpretability is crucial for clinical adoption. For the final optimal model, SHapley Additive exPlanations values will be calculated to quantify the contribution of each input variable (including TCM elements) to the predicted severity for individual patients and globally. This will provide intuitive insights into which factors drive the model’s decisions.

To justify our choice of CART and BPNN, we will also train and compare the performance of two additional benchmark algorithms: Logistic Regression (as a simple, interpretable baseline) and Random Forest (as a robust ensemble method). Model selection will be based on a comprehensive evaluation of discrimination (AUC), calibration (Brier score, calibration curve), clinical utility (decision curve analysis) and interpretability.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public were not directly involved in this protocol due to its technical focus on clinician-oriented model development (CART/BPNN algorithms) and standardised multicentre data collection requirements. Research questions and outcomes were informed by expert consensus and literature evidence reflecting patient priorities (eg, quality of life impacts). Patients participate only through routine clinical assessments with written informed consent. Participation burden was minimised through ethics-approved clinical routines without additional interventions. The results of the trial will be presented at national and international meetings and will be published in peer-reviewed journals. It is important to note that the expert panel involved in the Delphi consultations was independent from the investigators conducting the multicentre cross-sectional data collection. This separation was deliberately designed to minimise potential bias and ensure that the variable selection process reflects expert opinion rather than being influenced by the data collectors.

Discussion

AECOPD accelerates COPD progression, causing lung function decline exceeding 25%.20 Globally, patients with AECOPD exhibit high hospitalisation rates with rapid clinical deterioration and significant in-hospital mortality. Data from the USA indicate that the average cost of a COPD-related hospitalisation exceeds US$10 000,21 while studies from Denmark show that the annual direct and indirect economic burden of patients with COPD is significantly higher than that of the general population.10 Domestic studies report average hospitalisation costs of RMB11 598 per AECOPD admission,22 while Beijing data demonstrate per-capita costs rising from RMB19 760 in 2009 to RMB20 119 in 2017, accompanied by 5-year survival rates under 50%. 20 23 24 As a pivotal clinical milestone in COPD progression, prompt and accurate AECOPD assessment is critical for early intervention to mitigate adverse outcomes. Syndrome differentiation constitutes the foundation of TCM diagnosis. The disease-syndrome integrated approach represents a cornerstone of contemporary TCM and integrated medical practice.25 26 Incorporating TCM syndromes as latent variables into severity assessment models will enhance clinical evaluation precision by capturing holistic pathophysiological information not reflected in conventional Western biomarkers. TCM syndromes integrate symptoms, signs, and constitutional factors that may indicate early deterioration or specific response patterns to treatment. By including these variables, the model can identify patient subgroups with distinct risk profiles, leading to more personalised and accurate severity stratification.

AECOPD severity is influenced by comorbidities and baseline conditions. The current severity classification standards for AECOPD primarily include those proposed by the Global Strategy for Diagnosis, Management and Prevention of COPD (GOLD),27 the Chinese Expert Consensus on AECOPD Management (2017 Revision)28 and the Chinese COPD Guidelines (2021 Revision)29: mild (worsening of respiratory symptoms that can be controlled by increasing bronchodilators); moderate (requiring systemic corticosteroids and/or antibiotics) and severe (requiring hospitalisation or emergency department visits). Although this grading system is relatively well-recognised internationally, it focuses primarily on treatment strategies following acute exacerbations. Its judgement relies on patients’ clinical symptoms and signs, failing to achieve quantitative disease assessment and requires improvement in early identification, prevention and mitigation of negative impacts from acute exacerbations. Moreover, as it is primarily based on actual healthcare resource utilisation, significant variability exists in reporting AECOPD outcomes across countries and regions due to differences in available treatment resources and local customs affecting healthcare-seeking and hospitalisation criteria. Third, the Exacerbations of Chronic Pulmonary Disease Tool (EXACT-PRO)30 comprises 14 items, each scored 0–5, with a total score range of 0–100; higher scores indicate greater severity. This tool is primarily used to assess the frequency, severity and duration of COPD acute exacerbations. However, it is mainly based on symptom assessment, is highly subjective, contains numerous and relatively complex items, and is inconvenient for clinical use. Fourth, the Rome Proposal criteria31 established AECOPD severity grading criteria based on six objective clinical indicators: Dyspnoea VAS score, SaO2, RR, HR, serum CRP and arterial blood gas analysis (when necessary). Although this represents a relatively objective grading system that can provide reference for disease severity assessment to some extent, the variable thresholds are largely based on literature and retrospective studies. There is a lack of large-scale prospective studies to demonstrate its utility in clinical practice and research, and its level of evidence requires improvement. Although the aforementioned studies have actively explored AECOPD severity assessment, all have certain limitations. Currently, there remains a lack of accurate, practical and quantitative tools for AECOPD severity assessment, with no ideal classification standard that simultaneously satisfies the requirements of being objective, reproducible, easy to use in clinical practice, and capable of integrating both Western and TCM perspectives to guide personalised treatment. Therefore, this prospective study integrates TCM syndromes with modern medical indicators to establish a disease-syndrome integrated AECOPD assessment tool and severity classification. This approach addresses limitations of existing tools while enabling rapid severity evaluation, optimised clinical decision-making and improved prognoses. The model will provide an instant severity score based on routinely available clinical data, allowing clinicians to quickly identify high-risk patients who may need intensive monitoring or specific interventions. By integrating TCM syndromes, the model can also suggest tailored treatment options (eg, specific herbal formulas), potentially improving outcomes through personalised medicine. External validation will confirm its predictive accuracy for key outcomes, supporting its use in clinical practice to enhance patient management.

The rapid advancement of machine learning algorithms and artificial intelligence enables more scientific, efficient and accurate extraction and processing of medical data. Precise disease assessment and prediction of pathology probability through machine learning algorithms, which integrate domain expertise with computational data analysis, are crucial for optimising diagnostic outcomes and advancing disease prevention, diagnosis and research.32,36 As an enabling technology, AI provides novel perspectives for clinical assessment model development,37 facilitating the transition from empirical to evidence-based and precision medicine. While machine learning algorithms are increasingly applied to disease prediction modelling,9 most focus on exacerbation risk prediction in patients with COPD,38,40 with limited multistrategy modelling for AECOPD severity assessment. Decision trees and artificial neural networks represent key branches of machine learning for classification tasks. Prior studies used CART algorithms and backpropagation (BP) neural networks for disease diagnosis, classification and prediction.41,45 Thus, constructing a disease-syndrome integrated AECOPD severity assessment model based on CART and BP neural networks, followed by comparative performance evaluation, will critically guide standardised AECOPD management.

The innovation of this disease-syndrome integrated assessment model lies not only in the fusion of TCM syndrome elements and Western medical indicators, but also in the construction, via machine learning algorithms, of a tool that may reflect patient disease burden more precisely than the existing GOLD classification. Through the subsequent comparison with the GOLD standard in predicting hard clinical outcomes, we expect to confirm that this model can provide prognostic information beyond current guidelines, offering stronger support for clinical decision-making.

In summary, this study integrates literature review and multicentre cross-sectional investigation. It employs three statistical methods: Lasso regression, elastic net and ridge regression, combined with expert questionnaires to identify key variables. Leveraging the clinical database and established variables, CART and BP neural networks innovatively synthesise multidimensional indicators from both Western and Chinese medicine. We will develop a severity assessment model using patient characteristics as input parameters and severity levels as output. Model performance will be comprehensively compared across three dimensions: data sensitivity, prediction accuracy and stability. This process will determine an accurate, practical, reproducible and externally validated disease-syndrome integrated AECOPD assessment tool with preliminary severity classification criteria, providing methodological frameworks for disease assessment modelling in related fields.

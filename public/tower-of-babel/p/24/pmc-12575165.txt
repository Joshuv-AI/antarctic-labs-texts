Health behaviors in women conceiving by medically assisted reproduction vs natural conception: A cross‐sectional study of 23,334 nulliparous women
Authors: Ann H Hansen, Lærke Priskorn, Niels Jørgensen, Nina O Nathan, Anja Pinborg, Anders Juul, Hanne K Hegaard
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionWomen conceiving by medically assisted reproduction (MAR) have a planned pregnancy, easing adherence to preconception recommendations, which they are expected to be highly motivated to comply with. However, little is known about the actual adherence among these women. Thus, we investigate to what extent women conceiving by MAR treatment adhere to recommendations from the Danish Health Authority regarding preconception health behavior and whether the adherence differs from that observed in women conceiving naturally. Secondly, we elucidate differences in health behavior in early pregnancy by mode of conception.Material and MethodsA cross‐sectional study using patient‐reported questionnaire data from 23,443 nulliparous women collected in connection with their first‐trimester nuchal translucency scan from 2012 to 2022 at Copenhagen University Hospital in Denmark. The women answered an online questionnaire including information on whether they conceived by MAR, which type of treatment, and their health behaviors before and during pregnancy. This included alcohol consumption, smoking, exercise, and intake of dietary supplements. Differences in health behaviors by mode of conception were analyzed using multiple logistic regression with adjustments for age and educational level.ResultsOverall, 91% of women answered the questionnaire. Women who conceived by MAR (15%) had healthier preconception behaviors with significantly higher odds of taking folic acid supplements (adjusted odds ratio [aOR] 11.40, 95% confidence interval [CI]:9.95;13.04), stopping smoking due to planning of pregnancy (aOR 1.72, 95% CI:1.49;1.98), avoiding smoking (aOR 4.67, 95% CI:3.88;5.62), and avoiding alcohol (aOR 2.40 95% CI: 2.19;2.64) compared to women conceiving naturally. Adherence to recommendations among women who conceived by MAR was 30.6% for alcohol avoidance preconceptionally. In early pregnancy, women conceiving by MAR still had significantly healthier behaviors overall, except for exercise, which was lower (aOR 0.86, 95% CI: 0.80; 0.93), with 46.3% meeting the recommended weekly hours of exercise.ConclusionsWomen conceiving by MAR generally exhibit healthy behaviors with high adherence to recommendations from the Danish Health Authority both preconceptionally and in early pregnancy, though only one third adhered to recommendations on avoiding alcohol preconceptionally, and half of the women adhered to recommendations on exercise in early pregnancy. Overall, women conceiving by MAR had healthier behaviors than women conceiving naturally.Keywords: alcohol drinking, early pregnancy, exercise, health behavior, medically assisted reproduction, preconception

Key message

Overall, women conceiving by medically assisted reproduction have healthier behaviors than women conceiving naturally before and during pregnancy. However, they showed poor adherence to recommendations on avoiding alcohol and engaging in exercise, which should be focus areas for preventive counseling.

INTRODUCTION

Preconception health plays a pivotal role in the subsequent health of mother and infant. Lifestyle factors such as exercise, smoking, alcohol consumption, and dietary supplements are all important. 1 Women having healthy behaviors in the preconception period have a reduced risk of adverse maternal outcomes, such as preeclampsia, gestational diabetes, and preterm birth. 1 , 2 , 3 Also, infants born to mothers with healthy preconception behaviors have a reduced risk of low birthweight, neural tube defects, and congenital heart defects. 2 , 4

Infertility is an emerging problem worldwide 5 , 6 and specifically in Denmark, almost 20% of reproductive‐aged couples suffer from infertility, making it a large public health problem in that age group. 7 Currently, one in eight children in Denmark is born after fertility treatment. 8 Consequently, the number of women conceiving by medically assisted reproduction (MAR) is becoming a large patient group in the healthcare system. Unlike women who conceive naturally, those undergoing MAR are always in contact with the healthcare system prior to pregnancy. Therefore, these women are an important target group for specific counseling on preconception behavior, particularly because health behavior before pregnancy has been shown to carry into early pregnancy. 9 , 10

In Denmark, women planning to conceive are recommended by the Danish Health Authority to avoid smoking, passive smoking, and intake of alcohol; engage in 3.5 h of exercise per week (as are all others); and supplement with 400 μg folic acid per day until gestational week 12. 11 Even though the group of women conceiving by MAR is likely to be highly motivated to adhere to these recommendations in the preconception period to optimize their chances of achieving a healthy pregnancy and infant, little is known about their actual behavior and whether they adhere to the recommendations. Further, current studies investigating health behavior in women conceiving by MAR are limited and lack inclusion of Scandinavian populations. 12 , 13 , 14 , 15

Therefore, the study aims to investigate to what extent women conceiving by MAR treatment adhere to the recommendations from the Danish Health Authority and whether their preconception health behavior differs from that observed in women conceiving naturally, to determine whether specific health and lifestyle factors should be taken into consideration in clinical counseling. Also, a secondary aim was to investigate the difference in health behavior in early pregnancy between the two groups.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

Study population

The study is based on data from the Copenhagen Pregnancy cohort, which comprises a large population of pregnant women planning to give birth at Copenhagen University Hospital—Rigshospitalet in the capital region of Denmark. A detailed description of the cohort has been published previously. 9 In short, pregnant women respond to an online questionnaire when booking an appointment for a first‐trimester nuchal translucency scan between gestational weeks 11 and 14. The scan is a part of the national prenatal screening program and has a high national attendance of approximately 90% of all pregnant women in Denmark. 16 The questionnaire includes clinical patient‐reported outcomes, which are transferred to the women's medical records and used for individual antenatal consultations. Approximately 91% of the women responded to the questionnaire. Pregnant women with alcohol use disorder or substance abuse are referred to a special care unit at another hospital and are not included in the cohort.

From September 2012 to December 2022, data from 40,857 pregnancies were included in the cohort. Data from nulliparous women were included in the present study, resulting in data from 15,358 pregnancies being excluded. Also, 2,056 women were excluded due to missing data on parity (n = 1665) and whether they received MAR treatment (n = 391), resulting in a total study population of 23,443 nulliparous pregnant women.

Questionnaire data

Mode of conception

The women were categorized as conceiving by MAR if they had answered “yes” to having been treated for infertility up to the current pregnancy. Women answering “no” were categorized as conceiving naturally. Women with information about the specific type of fertility treatment (15%) were further categorized as conceiving via intrauterine insemination (IUI), in vitro fertilization (IVF), or intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) (Table 1).

Outcomes

Alcohol consumption

Total units of weekly alcohol consumption before pregnancy were categorized as no alcohol, >0–7 units/week, 8–14 units/week, and >14 units/week. The answer to weekly alcohol consumption during pregnancy was from 2012 to 2019 provided in units per week, and from 2019 to 2022, in the categories “One unit of alcohol or more per week,” “Less than one unit of alcohol per week,” and “No alcohol.” Results from the two time periods were merged and dichotomized into <1 unit/week and ≥1 unit/week. Categorizations were based on alcohol consumption recommendations from the Danish Health Authority. 11 Further, the women were asked about binge drinking with emphasis on that the women should include the whole pregnancy, also the period before the pregnancy was recognized. The number of episodes was categorized as one or more episodes or none (Table 1).

Smoking

The women were asked about their smoking status before and during pregnancy. Women who did not smoke were further asked whether they had stopped smoking because of planning to become pregnant. To assess passive smoking before and during pregnancy, the women were asked whether their partner smoked at both time periods (Table 1).

Exercise

Exercise engagement was defined as any engagement in exercise in the last 3 months before pregnancy and in early pregnancy. If the women provided a positive answer, they were further asked about 11 predefined types of exercise and the corresponding hours spent on each per week, both before and during pregnancy. The group of women who stopped exercising after becoming pregnant was categorized from those who engaged in exercise before pregnancy but not during. Also, the total amount of exercise per week was calculated before and during pregnancy, respectively, and dichotomized with a cut‐off value of 3.5 h/week, corresponding to 30 min/day as recommended by the Danish Health Authority (Table 1). 11

Dietary supplements

The women were asked about folic acid, vitamin D, and pregnancy multivitamin supplements at the time of conception and in early pregnancy. In Denmark, pregnancy multivitamin entails the recommended amounts of folic acid (400 μg) and vitamin D (10 μg); therefore, if women were taking either folic acid supplements or pregnancy multivitamin supplements, they were categorized as taking folic acid. The same was the case for vitamin D. During pregnancy, the women were additionally asked about daily iron (40–50 mg) and calcium intake (dairy products) (Table 1).

Covariates

Self‐reported information on maternal characteristics was categorized as shown in Table 2 and includes information on age, height, weight, body mass index (BMI), self‐rated health, chronic diseases, cohabiting with a partner/spouse, planned pregnancy, highest obtained educational level, and occupational status.

Statistical analyses

Medians (5–95 percentiles) and percentages of the questionnaire data were calculated for a basic description of women conceiving by MAR and women conceiving naturally, and the Mann–Whitney U‐test and the Pearson's Chi‐square test were used to test between‐group differences. The outcome variables related to health behavior were described by percentages, and between‐group differences were tested using Pearson's Chi‐square test.

To assess the association between mode of conception and health behavior, logistic regression analyses were used to calculate the odds ratio (OR) of engaging in various health behaviors among nulliparous pregnant women who conceived by MAR compared to those who conceived naturally. Analyses were conducted both unadjusted and adjusted for age and educational level, and adjusted results were illustrated in forest plots. Covariates were selected a priori as potential confounders based on findings from previous studies. 17 , 18 , 19 , 20 Women with missing data were omitted from the analyses.

All results are presented as unadjusted OR and adjusted OR (aOR) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CI). Statistical tests were two‐sided, and results with a p <0.05 were regarded as statistically significant. Analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Sensitivity analyses

The adjusted logistic regression analyses were repeated in the group of women with information on the type of MAR treatment used, distinguishing between conception after IUI, IVF, and ICSI. Additionally, a subgroup analysis was conducted, excluding naturally conceiving women with unplanned pregnancies, and another subgroup analysis using the group of women with unplanned pregnancies as a reference, to assess whether the degree of planning influenced health behavior differences according to the mode of conception. Further, a comparison of the basic characteristics between women conceiving naturally with a planned pregnancy and those with an unplanned pregnancy was done. To evaluate the potential modifying effect of education, analyses were repeated, including an interaction term for the mode of conception and educational level, and next also stratified by educational level. Additionally, we stratified analyses by two time periods (2012–2017 and 2018–2022) to investigate any modifying effect that could be caused by national trend changes in health behavior during the study period.

RESULTS

Basic characteristics

In the total study population of nulliparous women, the majority were normal weight (77%), had a master's degree as the highest obtained level of education (55%), and considered their health as very good or good (89%). One fifth of women conceiving naturally had an unplanned pregnancy. A total of 3505 women (15.0%) conceived by MAR, of which 1363 women were treated with IUI (38.9%), 937 with IVF (26.7%), and 828 with ICSI (23.6%), while specific treatment information was unspecified for 377 (10.8%) women. Women who conceived by MAR were approximately 4 years older than those who conceived naturally (33.9 years vs 30.0 years), and a slightly higher percentage of women conceiving by MAR were categorized as overweight (15.4% vs 12.9%). The proportion reporting a chronic disease was higher among women conceiving by MAR (25.4% vs 18.1%), mainly due to a higher frequency of thyroid disease, pulmonary disease, and hypertension. However, no difference in self‐rated health was detected between the two groups. Additionally, a higher percentage of women conceiving by MAR were employed (88% vs 75%), had a master's degree (59% vs 55%), and less often cohabited with a partner/spouse (82.4% vs 91.8%). Overall, the MAR subgroups IUI, IVF, and ICSI were comparable; though, a higher percentage of women conceiving by ICSI cohabited with a partner/spouse (Table 2).

Mode of conception in relation to alcohol consumption

A higher proportion of women conceiving by MAR than women conceiving naturally (31% vs 18%) adhered to the recommendation of alcohol abstinence preconceptionally, with an aOR of 2.40 (95% CI: 2.19; 2.64). During pregnancy, the majority of all women (>97%) adhered to the recommended alcohol abstinence, and proportions were similar across groups (99% vs 98%), though women conceiving by MAR had higher odds of alcohol abstinence compared to women conceiving naturally (aOR 2.02, 95% CI: 1.56; 2.62). Further, most women conceiving by MAR had no episodes of binge drinking in early pregnancy (92% vs 52%), with significantly higher odds (aOR 10.86, 95% CI: 9.49; 12.42) compared to women conceiving naturally (Tables 3 and S1; Figure 1A).

Mode of conception in relation to smoking

The majority of all women reported being nonsmokers (>99%) and having a nonsmoking partner (>85%). Preconceptionally, the proportion of women who reported being nonsmokers (96% vs 85%) and having a nonsmoking partner (89% vs 80%) was highest in the group of women conceiving by MAR with an aOR of 4.67 (95% CI: 3.88; 5.62) and an aOR of 2.02 (95% CI: 1.78; 2.28) compared to women conceiving naturally, respectively. The same tendency was detected during pregnancy (Tables 3 and S1; Figure 1A).

Mode of conception in relation to exercise

The proportion of women who engaged in any exercise was lower during pregnancy than before in both groups, and approximately 70% of the women reported engaging in the recommended ≥3.5 h of exercise per week before pregnancy and approximately 50% during pregnancy, independent of the mode of conception (Table 3). All types of exercise declined at the onset of pregnancy in both groups, and both groups most frequently engaged in bicycling and brisk walking at both time points (Figure S1). No statistical difference was observed between women conceiving with MAR and women conceiving naturally in engaging in any exercise or recommended ≥3.5 h of exercise per week preconceptionally. Women conceiving by MAR had significantly lower odds of engaging in any exercise (aOR 0.81, 95% CI: 0.74; 0.87) as well as the recommended ≥3.5 h/week (aOR 0.86, 95% CI: 0.80; 0.93) in early pregnancy compared to women conceiving naturally. Additionally, women conceiving by MAR had higher odds of stopping exercising after becoming pregnant (aOR 1.20, 95% CI: 1.09; 1.32) (Figure 1B and Table S1).

Mode of conception in relation to dietary supplements

Preconceptionally, almost twice the proportion of women conceiving by MAR took folic acid supplements (92.8% vs 51.5%) with an aOR of 11.40 (95% CI: 9.95; 13.04), compared to women conceiving naturally. Women conceiving by MAR also had higher odds of taking the recommended amount of vitamin D (aOR 4.51, 95% CI: 4.12; 4.93) preconceptionally. During pregnancy, women conceiving by MAR also had higher odds of taking folic acid, vitamin D, and iron, though with less pronounced differences and the majority of all women (98%) reported taking folic acid supplements at the time point. No difference in calcium intake was detected (Tables 3 and S1; Figure 1C).

Sensitivity analyses

Results from analyses comparing subgroups of women who conceived by IUI, IVF, and ICSI with women conceiving naturally were overall similar to the main results, but in general, the more invasive the treatment method, the healthier the behavior (Table S2). However, women conceiving by IVF or ICSI had lower odds of engaging in the recommended hours of exercise per week during pregnancy compared to women conceiving naturally, which was not the case for women conceiving by IUI (Table S2). Also, women conceiving by IVF or ICSI had higher odds of stopping exercise due to pregnancy (Table S2). Further, no noticeable differences were found across strata in the analyses stratified by educational level, with 84.5% of the women having a bachelor's degree or higher and 15.5% having an educational level below a bachelor's degree (Table S3). In sub‐analyses including women with a planned pregnancy only, resulting in 20.4% of women conceiving naturally being excluded, the results were overall comparable to the main findings, though slightly less pronounced except for exercise, where associations were stronger and engaging in any exercise before pregnancy became statistically significant (Table S4). Comparing women conceiving by MAR to women with an unplanned pregnancy, differences in health behavior were more pronounced than in the main analyses. Though for exercise specifically, results went in the opposite direction, with women conceiving with MAR having slightly higher odds of adhering to the recommended hours of exercise per week before pregnancy (aOR 1.13, 95% CI: 1.01; 1.27), and the association detected in early pregnancy was now insignificant (Table S5). Women conceiving naturally with unplanned pregnancies and planned pregnancies were similar in age (29.5 years vs 30.1 years, p <0.001), though a higher proportion of women with an unplanned pregnancy were obese (5.1% vs 3.8% p <0.001), and a lower proportion had a master's degree and were employed (data not shown). Analyses stratified by time periods confirmed the robustness of the results and did not change the direction of the estimates or the overall conclusion (Table S6).

DISCUSSION

In this large study of nulliparous pregnant women, we detected that adherence to the preconception recommendations by the Danish Health Authorities was approximately 90% and above for taking folic acid supplement, avoiding smoking and passive smoking, below 70% for engaging in the recommended hours of exercise per week, and around 30% for avoiding alcohol for women who conceive by MAR. Further, we detected that women conceiving by MAR had healthier preconception behaviors than those conceiving naturally. These women had higher odds of taking the recommended folic acid supplements and avoiding alcohol, smoking, and passive smoking. However, we did not find significant differences in the recommended hours of exercise engagement between the two groups preconceptionally. During pregnancy, adherence to recommendations was higher in both groups than before pregnancy, with women conceiving by MAR generally showing the highest adherence, except regarding exercise.

At present, only one other study from 2016 has compared pregnant women conceiving by MAR with those conceiving naturally and found that women who underwent MAR treatment were less likely to have inadequate folic acid intake, consume alcohol, and smoke before pregnancy compared to women with unintended pregnancies, similar to our results. 12 However, results are not directly comparable to our main analyses since that study included women with unintended pregnancies as their control group while we used all women conceiving naturally. In a sensitivity analysis, however, we conducted comparable analyses comparing the health behavior in women conceiving by MAR with the group of women with an unplanned pregnancy and found more pronounced differences not only before but also during pregnancy. To compare women only with planned pregnancies, we furthermore conducted a sensitivity analysis excluding those women conceiving naturally with an unplanned pregnancy and found similar results to our main findings, though slightly less pronounced. Results from these sensitivity analyses using different control groups demonstrate that pregnancy planning does play a role. However, even when comparing only women with a planned pregnancy, we found that women conceiving with MAR overall had healthier behavior, except regarding exercise, illustrating that pregnancy planning does not explain our results. Furthermore, women conceiving naturally, planned, or unplanned, were slightly different regarding basic characteristics, and it cannot be excluded that they would differ in pregnancy‐related health behavior, regardless of the degree of planning. Consistent with our findings, studies from other Western countries investigating adherence to preconception recommendations have shown high proportions of women consuming alcohol just before or during fertility treatment, resulting in 51 % to 73% of the women not adhering to the recommended avoidance of alcohol. 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 This is concerning, as preconception alcohol consumption has been linked with a reduction in fecundability 21 , 22 and an increase in the risk of spontaneous abortions. 23 Similarly, an American study found low adherence to pre‐IVF exercise recommendations, with 40 % to 50% of the women not meeting the recommended hours of exercise per week, in line with the results found in our study, with 46% of women conceiving by MAR engaging in the recommended hours of exercise per week preconceptionally. 24 A meta‐analysis has shown that pre‐MAR exercise positively impacts rates of clinical pregnancies and live births. 25 Combined, these studies suggest that adhering to the recommendations regarding alcohol and exercise may increase treatment outcomes for women undergoing fertility treatment. However, barriers such as breaking unhealthy habits, limited knowledge, 26 and the prolonged nature of fertility treatments could make sustained adherence challenging. Several studies, primarily from England and Australia, have also investigated preconception health behaviors in pregnant women or women planning pregnancy from the general population. Common for these studies is the conclusion that the women had low adherence to the relevant preconception health recommendations. 27 , 28 , 29 , 30 , 31 , 32 In line with the results of women conceiving naturally from our study, the majority of the studies found low adherence, especially regarding alcohol consumption, folic acid intake, and exercise engagement. This is problematic as studies have revealed that health behavior in the preconception period often carries over into early pregnancy 9 , 10 where the fetus is especially vulnerable to developmental risks. 33

In early pregnancy, both women conceiving by MAR and women conceiving naturally generally have healthy behaviors, with more than 90% of the women taking the recommended dietary supplements and avoiding alcohol, smoking, and passive smoking. However, when comparing the two groups, women who conceived by MAR overall had healthier behaviors during pregnancy, except for exercise, where we found significantly lower adjusted odds of engaging in exercise during pregnancy, primarily driven by the groups of women conceiving by IVF and ICSI. Especially, women who conceive by IVF and ICSI often undergo a complex and demanding MAR treatment process, potentially making them more worried about engaging in exercise than women conceiving naturally. 34 These worries could be attributed to a slightly higher prevalence of early pregnancy loss detected in women undergoing MAR treatment. 35 The observed decline in all types of exercise in early pregnancy may reflect the onset of common pregnancy‐related complications such as nausea and fatigue in early pregnancy, making it difficult for the women to prioritize exercise at that time point. However, the fact that women conceiving by IVF and ICSI are more likely to stop exercising when becoming pregnant compared to women conceiving naturally could potentially reflect that women conceiving by IVF or ICSI are more concerned about whether exercise during early pregnancy poses a risk factor for miscarriage. 36

A strength of this study is the large sample size, with data on 23,443 nulliparous pregnant women, of whom 15% conceived by MAR, allowing for the differentiation of MAR subgroups. The large sample size also reduced uncertainty, as indicated in the relatively narrow confidence intervals of the associations between conception mode and health behaviors. Other studies have shown differences in health behaviors between nulliparous and multiparous pregnant women. 9 , 17 Therefore, to focus on a homogeneous population with the same prerequisites for preconception and pregnancy health behaviors, we included only nulliparous women. Further, 91% of all pregnant women booking an appointment for the first‐trimester ultrasound scan in the Copenhagen area completed the questionnaire, and data are patient‐reported outcomes with the purpose of optimizing antenatal care and not primarily collected for research purposes. Hence, we expect the study population to be representative of the source population of pregnant women in the Copenhagen area. However, approximately 10% of our source population did not book an appointment for the ultrasound scan, and a further 10% of our study population did not respond to the questionnaire, which may have led to an overestimation of adherence to health recommendations, particularly if nonparticipants represent women with lower education levels.

Furthermore, women giving birth in the greater Copenhagen area are not representative of the general Danish population of pregnant women. Our study population has a high prevalence of 55% highly educated women from the urban area in Denmark. In the Danish National Birth Cohort, the prevalence is approximately 30% in the same age group. 37 Thus, adherence to the recommendations is likely to be higher in the studied population, and our finding that educational level did neither confound nor modify our results may also not be generalizable to the entire population of Danish women of childbearing age.

A limitation of the study is the reliance on retrospective self‐reporting, including details on preconception health behaviors, as prior studies report a tendency to overreport exercise 38 and underreport alcohol intake in retrospective questionnaires. 39 However, we do not anticipate differential recall bias between MAR and naturally conceiving women. Thus, any reporting bias is likely to be equally distributed in both groups and unlikely to impact observed associations.

Despite over 50% of the included women possessing a high educational level, enabling them to seek knowledge about preconception health, the lack of adherence to alcohol avoidance in this study could suggest that there is a need to enhance public awareness about preconception health. Thus, it is important to acknowledge that the lack of adherence to the recommendations from the Danish Health Authority may reflect a lack of knowledge about the recommendations rather than a failure to adhere. Women conceiving by MAR are connected to the health care system before pregnancy, unlike women conceiving naturally, making them a special group accessible for preventive counseling preconceptionally and in early pregnancy. Therefore, health care professionals at fertility clinics have a unique opportunity to inform couples at the outset of fertility treatment about key lifestyle factors to optimize pregnancy outcomes.

CONCLUSION

Women conceiving by MAR generally exhibit healthy behaviors with high adherence to recommendations from the Danish Health Authority both preconceptionally and in early pregnancy, though only one third adhered to recommendations on avoiding alcohol preconceptionally, and half of the women adhered to recommendations on exercise in early pregnancy. Overall, women conceiving by MAR had healthier behaviors, both before and during pregnancy, than women conceiving naturally, also when comparing only to women conceiving naturally with a planned pregnancy. Only regarding exercise during pregnancy, women conceiving with MAR were less likely to adhere to the recommendation compared to women conceiving naturally. It may be beneficial that information provided for the couples at the outset of MAR treatment emphasizes the importance of avoiding alcohol, along with encouragement of continued exercise into pregnancy.

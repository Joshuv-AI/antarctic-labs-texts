Artificial intelligence-based intrusion detection and secure communication model for sustainable 6G-IoT networks
Authors: Mohammed Assiri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Intrusion detection systems (IDS) play a crucial role in safeguarding Internet of Things (IoT) systems by detecting and identifying malicious or unauthorized activities. The rapid proliferation of IoT devices has increased the requirement for robust network security. IoT and advanced IoT technologies are essential for 6G networks, giving dense connectivity, low latency, ultra-reliability, and higher performance. Artificial intelligence (AI), including deep learning (DL) and machine learning (ML), presents solutions for enhancing and leveraging innovative technologies in next-generation radio communications. Moreover, security remains a demanding problem that is resolved using ML and DL-based intrusion detection models. In a world where 6G IoT infrastructure growth is a global priority, ensuring robust intrusion detection is vital to defend sensitive data and ensure the seamless process of critical models. In this paper, the Artificial Intelligence-Based Intrusion Detection and Secure Communication (AIBID-SCSA) technique is proposed. The main goal of the AIBID-SCSA technique is to enhance the accuracy and efficacy of attack recognition in an assisted 6G-IoT network. Initially, the AIBID-SCSA technique applies min-max normalization to measure the input data into a uniform format. Furthermore, improved sparrow search algorithm (ISSA)-based feature selection is employed to identify the most relevant feature from the network traffic data. For the classification of intrusion detection, the long short-term memory (MIX_LSTM) model is used. Finally, the rabbit optimization algorithm (ROA) model is implemented for the hyperparameter selection process to optimize the detection results of the MIX_LSTM model. The experimental validation of the AIBID-SCSA method is investigated under the TON_IoT_Train_Test_Network dataset. The comparison analysis of the AIBID-SCSA method portrayed a superior accuracy value of 99.63% over recent state-of-art techniques.

Introduction

Wireless communication has served the globe with numerous technical developments, from the period of finding cellular frequency reusability1. The evolution of communication technologies from 1G to 4G has occurred due to improvements in antenna design technology, channel coding, and multiple access2. Latest progress in these technologies, along with the development of novel software systems and hardware devices, has given birth to the 5G wireless system methods that also permit understanding the IoT model and utilize smart abilities to guarantee good health, industrial production, enormous agricultural, and intelligent observing methods for effective electricity generation and traffic control, and so on3. Currently, IoT has become an essential part of the future Internet, receiving greater attention from both academia and industry due to its potential to provide customer services across various aspects of the modern world4. As 5G network methods approach complete standardization, multiple studies on the 6G wireless communication method have begun to deliver seamless connectivity to an increasing number of consumers and enhance IoT services5. In the 6G, AI becomes progressively significant6. AI relies on mining big data for learning and training, continually enhancing computing power to handle higher transmission rates, and gaining more flexibility through continuous learning. An IDS is a security measure designed to detect and prevent malicious activity or unauthorized access on a computer network or system7. For wireless IoT systems, it is essential to have a method that can identify intrusions in the real world and manage the higher volume of data. IDS-based recognition is an approach to identify malicious actions in a wireless 6G IoT system by associating network traffic with a dataset of identified malicious signatures or patterns8. The use of AI at the network and application layers of 6G networks enables the network to become automated and more intelligent, which will be an essential way to control and manage huge wireless big data9. Additionally, the 6G network supports multi-band hybrid transmission, large-scale antennas, and a large number of users, posing significant challenges to conventional physical layer transmission technology in terms of efficiency, performance, and complexity. Particularly, AI will play a crucial role in 6G networks. The developed AI approaches, which incorporate DL and ML, generate new methods for 6G. The 6G network operators will implement the developed DL and AI to improve their service, addressing security concerns10. The physical layer of 6G networks is susceptible to threats, like eavesdropping and jamming.

Literature review

Ibrahim et al.11 proposed a fishnet method-based packet scheduling and resource allocation method, named Fishnet-6G. The quantum density peak clustering (QDPC) model is employed to attain a cluster. Moreover, traffic forecasts are obtained through the fair queue status, processes, and grouping, utilizing the improved deep deterministic policy gradient (IMPDDPG) technique. Afterwards, optimum packet scheduling is achieved by applying the Willow Catkin Optimisation (WCO) approach. Garg et al.12 presented an innovative AI-designed secure communication method for IoMT. Moreover, the security investigation of the presented method is delivered to demonstrate its security against diverse probable threats. Furthermore, a comparative work presents the method with other related methods. Du et al.13 introduced a QoS-aware UAV 3D coverage deployment algorithm (QUCDA) that depends on ML models. The QUCDA model builds on the classical genetic algorithm (GA) and contains two main phases. An innovative method was advanced to create the initial population, employing the K-means clustering model to enhance the quality of the solution. Afterwards, the K-means initialized grey wolf optimization (KIGWO) model is presented to improve the variety of random replacement strategies. In Sedjelmaci et al.14, a strong and innovative zero-trust architecture (ZTA) is presented, which includes an intrusion detection structure. The presented detection structure executes recognition methods based on ML models and security rules, as well as recognition approaches that utilize both collaborative and standalone processes, aiming to enhance security and identify threats precisely. Additionally, a dynamic ZTA depends on a non-cooperative game to ensure the additional safety of 6G edge computing systems. Nguyen et al.15 employed ML or AI, network slicing, and open radio access network (ORAN) to tackle the different tasks of non-terrestrial networks (NTN). This research presents a comprehensive survey on how ML or AI and network slicing are exploited to overcome the challenges faced by NTNs. Additionally, ORAN is employed for NTNs. Irshaid et al.16 presented an innovative energy-efficient multichannel cross-layer MAC structure that employs a non-persistent CSMA protocol in 6G-IoT systems. Depending on a composite variation of MAC and physical layer parameters, this structure focuses on enhancing the IoT system’s energy efficiency (EE). Particularly, this work solves and formulates the maximization subject to numerous design constraints.

Vel et al.17 aimed to identify the adversarial threats in IoT gadgets utilizing AI models. The adversarial instances are created by using a Jacobian-based saliency map, iterative fast gradient sign, projected gradient descent, and limited-memory Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shanno models. The adversarial threats in the IoT method are identified efficiently by employing the fully deep connected CNN (FDCCNN) technique. Dai et al.18 proposed an enhanced intrusion detection model, XGBoost-Autoencoder (XGBoost-AE) and Random Forest (RF)-Autoencoder (RF-AE) technique. Baseer et al.19 presented a unified framework by combining an Adaptive trust consensus with long short-term memory (LSTM) behavioural modelling, a hybrid Cheon–Kim–Kim–Song (CKKS) secure multi-party computation encryption for differential privacy, and quantized deep reinforcement learning (DRL) to enhance security, privacy, and energy efficiency in UAV-assisted networks. Sana et al.20 presented a novel IDS system by utilizing optimized supervised ML models, including support vector machine (SVM), RF, ensemble bagged tree (EBT), and neural networks (NN), with DL models such as LSTM and Vision Transformer (ViT). Moreover, the Bayesian Optimisation (BO) method is utilized for the tuning process. Jithish et al.21 proposed a privacy-preserving Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS) attack detection approach for 6G-enabled smart grids by integrating Federated Learning (FL) with Amazon Web Services (AWS) cloud. He et al.22 introduced an epilepsy detection model by utilizing Tunable Q Wavelet Transform for signal decomposition, one-dimensional convolutional neural network (1DCNN) and spectral features for extraction, and Optimal Attention-based LSTM (OAT-LSTM) technique for classification. Feature fusion and hyperparameter tuning are performed by utilizing the Fuzzy Coyote Random Drift Algorithm (FC-RDA) methodology. Walia et al.23 proposed an AI-enabled IoTs (AIoT) use case powered by Non-Terrestrial Networks (NTNs) and 6th Generation (6G) technology. Islam et al.24 proposed a continuous authentication-based blockchain approach incorporated with a zero-trust architecture (ZTA) to secure UAVs acting as flying 5G base stations. Singamaneni et al.25 presented a hybrid cryptographic protocol incorporating Nth-degree Truncated Polynomial Ring Unit Encryption with Supersingular Isogeny Diffie–Hellman (NTRUEncrypt-SIDH) and Key-Policy Attribute-Based Encryption (KP-ABE) technique to improve privacy, security, and distributed denial-of-service mitigation in 6th Generation (6G) networks for Industry 5.0.

Kjwan and Ali26 presented an adaptive covert communication framework using quantum cryptography (BB84 protocol) and AI-based security for secure, high-speed, and energy-efficient 6G communication. Sadhwani et al.27 introduced a system by utilizing ML and DL techniques, including k-Nearest Neighbours (KNN), naïve bayes (NB), decision tree (DT), RF, multi-layer perceptron (MLP), convolutional neural network (CNN), LSTM, and a hybrid CNN-LSTM model. Binbusayyis and Sha28 proposed a blockchain-based federated learning framework with an Enhanced CNN with Squeeze-and-Excitation blocks (ECNNSE) technique. Furthermore, Butterfly Optimization Algorithm (BOA) integrated with Correlation-Based Feature Selection (CFS) was employed to achieve secure and efficient intrusion detection in smart networks. Tawfik29 developed a framework by utilizing stacked autoencoders (SAE), Categorical Boosting (CatBoost), and an optimized Transformer-CNN-Long Short-Term Memory (Transformer-CNN-LSTM) ensemble to effectually detect intrusions in fog and IoT networks. Sharma and Rani30 proposed a novel Stacked-Hybrid Model (SHM) incorporating RF, SVM, Logistic Regression (LR), and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) technique to improve attack detection accuracy. Zhu et al.31 proposed an Improved Isolation Forest with Particle Swarm Optimization (IIF-PSO) technique combined with data fusion and blockchain technology for secure, real-time, and reliable analytics. Sakr et al.32 proposed a model by computing ML, RF, Gradient Boosting (GB), SVM, DT, and KNN, and hybrid ensemble models namely RF + KNN, GB + KNN, and GB + DT. Farooq and Khan33 evaluated ML techniques for improving the protection of smart devices and data. Soma et al.34 developed a Resilient Probabilistic Adversarial Network (Resi-PAN) methodology by integrating probabilistic adversarial networks, resilient optimization, and recurrent neural networks (RNN) with adversarial training. Gaurav, Gupta, and Chui35 proposed an optimal CNN model using Ant Lion Optimizer (ALO) for feature selection and Slime Mould Algorithm (SMA) for hyperparameter tuning. Li et al.36 introduced an Arithmetic Dragonfly Algorithm-optimized Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (ADM-BiLSTM) approach combined with Principal Component Analysis (PCA) method. Lilhore et al.37 addressed privacy and security challenges in sixth-generation (6G) IoT networks by exploring quantum-safe solutions, privacy-preserving technologies, and user-oriented controls for secure and sustainable network integration.

The existing studies highlight crucial improvements in 6G, IoT, and UAV security; however, various limitations still exist. Several techniques depend on limited datasets, which can mitigate generalizability in real-world scenarios. The integration of diverse advanced techniques often enhances computational complexity, affecting deployment in resource-constrained environments. Various models concentrate on accuracy and detection performance but often overlook real-time scalability, resource efficiency, and adaptability to dynamic 6G-IoT environments. Additionally, integration of privacy preservation, blockchain, and quantum-safe solutions remains limited. Furthermore, few studies address adaptive threat landscapes or growing attack vectors dynamically. Privacy preservation and efficient handling of heterogeneous and imbalanced data remain insufficiently explored. There is also a lack of comprehensive frameworks that integrate robust security, energy efficiency, and scalability in decentralized networks. Addressing these research gaps will improve the practical applicability and resilience of 6G-enabled systems. There is also a requirement for a unified, lightweight, and adaptive framework that simultaneously ensures high detection accuracy, low latency, resource efficiency, and robust security for heterogeneous 6G-IoT networks.

Proposed model

In this study, the AIBID-SCSA technique is proposed. The main goal of the AIBID-SCSA technique is to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of attack recognition in an assisted 6G-IoT network. It contains four distinct processes, such as data normalization, ISSA-based feature selection, MIX_LSTM-based classification, and ROA-based tuning. Stage I: min-max normalisation

At first, the AIBID-SCSA model applies min-max normalization to measure input data into a uniform format38. This model is chosen for its efficiency in scaling features to a uniform range, usually [0, 1]. This technique ensures that the overall features effectively contribute to the learning process without distorting their original distribution, unlike other complex scaling techniques. This model is also beneficial in neural network-based models where unscaled data can result in slow convergence or unstable training. The model also improves convergence and stability by maintaining relative relationships between data points. Furthermore, this technique is computationally inexpensive and preserves data sparsity, making it appropriate for real-time or resource-constrained environments. Its interpretability and ease of implementation additionally support its selection in this intrusion detection framework.

It is a data pre-processing model employed in IDS for 6G-IoT networks to measure input features within a stable range, typically between 0 and 1. This safeguards equality across dissimilar features, which is vital when working with varied network data that might have fluctuating ranges. By using min-max normalization, the method can process data more effectively, decreasing biases presented by features with superior scales. It enhances the performance of ML approaches in detecting intrusions or anomalies in real-world scenarios. In 6G-IoT networks, where data flows are massive and diverse, min-max normalization certifies that intrusion detection techniques work efficiently across varied network traffic.

Stage II: ISSA-based feature selection

Next, the ISSA-based FS is employed to identify the most relevant features from the network traffic data39. This technique efficiently performs exploration and exploitation compared to conventional methods like GA or particle swarm optimization (PSO). This model replicates the intelligent foraging behaviour of sparrows and introduces adaptive mechanisms that assist in avoiding local optima, ensuring a more optimal and diverse feature subset. This results in mitigated dimensionality, faster computation, and enhanced model generalization. This methodology also dynamically adjusts to the problem space, making it appropriate for intrinsic, high-dimensional datasets, unlike filter or wrapper-based methods. This model also improves the classification accuracy by removing irrelevant and redundant features. Thus, the ISSA approach presents a robust, adaptive, and computationally efficient feature selection strategy constructed for dynamic cybersecurity environments.

The SSA is a swarm intelligence method that utilizes sparrows to seek food. Nevertheless, due to an absence of diversity during the early population, the searching capability of the SSA rapidly decreases, and this method rapidly drops into the difficulty of local optimum solutions. Then, chaotic sequences are applied in this work for initializing the sparrow population fitness value and location, improving the distribution of the searching space, enhancing the global searching capability, and enhancing the chance of the first location to prevent dropping into a local optimum solution efficiently. The coverage of the population was distributed more evenly to avoid multiple individuals from sharing the same fitness value, thereby improving the range of fitness values and changes among individual sparrows.

Stage IV: parameter tuning using ROA

Finally, the hyperparameter selection process based on the ROA model is performed to optimize the detection results of the MIX_LSTM model41. This technique effectively balances between exploration and exploitation compared to conventional models such as search, random search, or GA. The ROA replicates the rabbit survival strategies, which allow it to escape local optima and find better hyperparameter combinations effectively. This model also ensures better model generalization, mitigates training time, and improves classification accuracy. This model is also effective in optimizing DL techniques such as MIX_LSTM, and its lightweight nature makes it appropriate for real-time IDSs with resource constraints. Overall, ROA provides a more adaptive and intelligent tuning mechanism that improves model performance.

The ROA is a new metaheuristic model stimulated by the rabbit’s hiding and foraging behaviours. The method mimics the natural survival tactics of rabbits, such as random hiding and detour foraging, to find the global optimum solution within complex searching spaces. It utilizes the rabbit’s hiding behaviour for local exploitation and their foraging behaviour for global exploration, attaining smooth transitions between the dual across the slow decline of an energy issue. Establishing this setting, ROA resolves optimizer problems over six basic stages: population initialization, random hiding, detour foraging, position updating, energy factor control, and termination.

Now, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${x}_{r}1\left(t\right)$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${x}_{r}2\left(t\right)$$\end{document} characterize dual arbitrarily chosen locations of various rabbits. The variance factor of scaling \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F$$\end{document} controls the degree of the variance between these locations, so regulating the upgraded stage of the solution.

Updated locations

The memory pool mechanism, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$M$$\end{document}, is presented for storing the best location established in every round.19\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${v}_{i}\left(t+1\right)={x}_{mem}+R\left(t\right)\cdot\left({x}_{i}\left(t\right)-{x}_{mem}\right)+\beta\cdot diversity$$\end{document}

Now, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${X}_{mem}$$\end{document} characterizes the historically optimum location chosen from the memory pool. The dynamical factor of step \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R\left(t\right)$$\end{document} controls the step size. β serves as the weighting parameter, controlling the influence of memory, and is naturally fixed to a constant lower than \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1$$\end{document}. \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Diversity$$\end{document} refers to the diversity of the population.

If \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$A>1$$\end{document}, the model carried out detour foraging, utilizing adaptive step-size and a globally best-referenced method. If \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$A\le1$$\end{document}, it uses the DVS to improve local searching. This model then generates numerous candidate positions, computes their fitness values, upgrades these positions, and utilizes a memory pool mechanism for referencing historic optimum solutions. This loop continues until the maximum iteration count is reached, finally outputting the optimum solution. Algorithm 1 describes the ROA technique.

Conclusion

In this paper, the AIBID-SCSA technique is proposed. The main goal of the AIBID-SCSA technique is to improve the accuracy and efficiency of attack detection in assisted 6G-IoT networks. It contains four distinct processes, such as data normalization, ISSA-based feature selection, MIX_LSTM-based classification, and ROA-based tuning. Initially, the AIBID-SCSA technique applies min-max normalization to scale the input data into a uniform format. Furthermore, the ISSA-based FS is employed to identify the most relevant features from the network traffic data. For the classification of intrusion detection, the MIX_LSTM model is used. Finally, the ROA model is implemented for the hyperparameter selection process to optimize the detection results of the MIX_LSTM model. The experimental validation of the AIBID-SCSA method is investigated under the TON_IoT_Train_Test_Network dataset. The comparison analysis of the AIBID-SCSA method portrayed a superior accuracy value of 99.63% over existing models. The limitations comprise dependence on a single dataset, which may not fully represent the diversity and complexity of real-world network environments, potentially restricting the generalizability of the results. The model may also be examined on more complex or adversarial datasets, and handling unbalanced or low-quality data remains a threat that needs additional exploration through advanced data augmentation or reweighting techniques. The model also presents limited insight into how the proposed system can be adapted or scaled for practical 6G networks. Future work may concentrate on hardware implementation, integration with edge or fog computing platforms, and improving adaptability to growing cyber threats through continual learning or federated IDSs to ensure sustainable and secure deployment.

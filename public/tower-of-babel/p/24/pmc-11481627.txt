How does the interaction of digitalization and servitization contribute to manufacturing financial performance? The intermediary effect of manufacturing innovation performance
Authors: Nianhui Mao, Mingyan Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractDigitalization and servitization are two crucial transition pathways for manufacturing firms, with their relationship explored by some existing studies. However, there is limited research on the mechanism of their interaction's effect on the firm financial performance. To address the gap, this paper draws on service-dominant logic, resource-based view and dynamic capabilities theory to establish a theoretical model. A sample of 567 Chinese manufacturing firms is selected, and a mediated moderation model is used to explore this mechanism. The findings indicate that the interaction between digitalization and servitization has a positive effect on the firm financial performance, with firm innovation performance partially mediating the relationship. Besides, heterogeneity analysis reveals that this interactive effect is mainly manifested in capital and technology-intensive manufacturing sectors. Therefore, this paper suggests managers in manufacturing firms promote the interaction between digitalization and servitization, leveraging their synergy to advance the sustainable long-term development of both the manufacturing firm and industry.Keywords: Digitalization, Servitization, Firm innovation performance, Firm financial performance

Introduction

Manufacturing plays a fundamental role in economic development, closely reflecting a nation's productivity capabilities [1]. In the current backdrop of retrenchment in world trade combined with fragmented international production networks, manufacturing capacity enhancement will be instrumental for economies to bolster resilience and push forward global industrial landscapes. With the impetus of technological change and consumption upgrading, the integration of manufacturing with the service industry and information technology has become a realistic choice in the digital economy era. Manufacturing companies have sought digitalization and servitization transition to enhance competitiveness and achieve sustainable development [2]. Given the status of China's manufacturing industry as a major driver for the global economy [3], its pursuit of digitalization and servitization dual transition deserves attention [1].

While the manufacturing and service sectors have traditionally been considered distinct domains [4], servitization allows manufacturers to develop competitive strategies based on services [4]. The phrase “servitization”, first used by Vandermerwe and Rada in 1988, refers to the provision of a product-service package that can enhance the value of core product offerings [5]. Manufacturers with the servitization strategy shift from product-orientation to service-orientation [[6], [7], [8]]. This strategy may fortify client connections, generate sustainable revenue sources, and create formidable obstacles for rivals [4,9]. Servitization is now commonplace among manufacturing businesses in developed nations [10] as a powerful differentiation strategy that boosts competitive edge and helps companies escape the commodity trap [10]. However, investment in servitization does not always yield equivalent returns or increase shareholder value [8,11,12].

In parallel with the servitization transition, manufacturers are also dealing with the digitalization transition, guided by advancements in artificial intelligence [6,8,12]. Digitalization is generally defined as “the introduction or growth of the application of technological devices or computer systems by a business, sector, nation, etc.” The pervasive use of digital technologies exerts an enormous influence on different industries [12]. Digitalization is radically altering how industrial value chains conduct business as it involves the use of digital technology to open up new options for income generation and value creation [4,13,14]. However, similar to servitization, a mere focus on digitalization may present a paradox since costs associated with digital assets acquisition and creation have seldom yielded immediate returns [12,15].

A Sole implementation of servitization or digitalization strategy may present paradoxes [12,16], while a combination of these two strategies allows for better harnessing of their value [16]. Research on the relationship between these strategies has attracted much attention in recent years [12,17,18]. One research finding indicates that it is not necessary to strictly distinguish between goods, services, and technological offerings; instead, firms should integrate them to provide digital solutions that better meet client demands [12]. Since digitalization and servitization strategy are essentially “two sides of the same coin”, they should work together and converge [12,14]. On the one hand, digital technologies promote innovative product and service offerings, changing the configuration of supply chains and redefining the competitive landscape within industries [4,19]. Therefore, digitalization empowers servitization by transforming service delivery, generating a fresh business model and capturing value [13,14,16,20]. As a result, service-orientated business models become more standardized [21]. On the other hand, manufacturers may benefit from the servitization strategy by gaining clear guidance on how to leverage customer-provided big data through digitalization investments to create new business prospects and boost profitability [18]. The key to releasing the potential of digitalization may reside in the servitization business model, which includes modern services, operations services, and outcome-oriented services [16], [18], [22]. In addition, servitization enhances the customization of digital offerings to satisfy various customer needs [21].

However, only a little research has explored digitalization, servitization and firm financial performance within a single framework [23]. Among these very few articles, most have studied the impact of digitalization on firm performance unilaterally, with digitalization as an antecedent to servitization [18,23,24], or vice versa, studying the impact of servitization on firm performance [1,25,26]. While these studies have investigated the impact of digitalization and servitization on firm performance separately, no study has treated digitalization and servitization as equal independent variables in a single framework to examine their interactive effects on dependent variables, such as firm financial performance. To bridge this gap, we empirically examine this interactive effect based on service-dominant logic, resource-based view and dynamic capabilities theory. In addition, the transmission mechanism between the interaction of digitalization and servitization on firm financial performance remains a “black box”. To meet the expectations of earlier research [25,[27], [28], [29]], this study attempts to clarify the mechanism of the interaction between digitalization and servitization on firm financial performance, with firm innovation performance as the mediator. A mediated moderation model will be used to explore this effect mechanism. In light of this, corresponding hypotheses are established and tested with data from 567 Chinese manufacturing firms. This paper testifies that digitalization and servitization have an interactive effect on improving the firm financial performance, with firm innovation performance partially mediating this relationship.

Therefore, this research makes the following contributions to the literature. Firstly, it attempt to clarify the relationship between the impact of digitalization and servitization on firm financial performance and firm innovation performance. As a result, this paper employs empirical evidence to confirm the positive direct impacts of digitalization and servitization on firm financial performance and firm innovation performance. Secondly, this paper responds to many calls for quantitative studies that examine the interaction between digitalization and servitization [2]. It offers a fresh theoretical framework and uses a mediated moderation model to analyze this framework, which provides empirical evidence that corroborates the interactive effect of digitalization and servitization. In addition, their interactive effect is analyzed across different manufacturing industries, revealing that this interactive effect varies among different manufacturing sectors. Thirdly, based on service-dominant logic, resource-based view and dynamic capabilities theory, this study stands as the inaugural work to set firm innovation performance as a mediator to verify the mechanism of the interaction between digitalization and servitization on firm financial performance. This finding emphasizes the value of firm innovation and extends the research in this field. In summary, these findings not only contribute to the literature on the relationship between digitalization and servitization [2,30] but also provide crucial evidence for manufacturers on the necessity of synergizing these two transitions. Additionally, they emphasize the important role firm innovation performance plays in this mechanism.

Theoretical background and research hypothesis

Theoretical background

Digitalization and servitization have been researched based on several theories. Generally, academics prefer the service-dominant logic, resource-based view and dynamic capabilities theory for servitization analysis [1,27,31,32], while researchers favour dynamic capabilities theory and resource-based view to examine digitalization [32,33]. Therefore, this study, drawing on previous research, uses service-dominant logic, resource-based view and dynamic capabilities theory as the theoretical foundation to investigate the interactive effect of digitalization and servitization.

Vargo and Lusch introduced service-dominant logic as a counterpoint to goods-dominant logic [34]. Service-dominant logic points out that the dominant business logic shifts from tangible goods supply to intangible services [27], lending support to the idea that servitization is a vital transition direction for manufacturing. In this logic, customers are seen as value co-creators, which enables manufacturers to innovate their goods and services according to customer feedback. Therefore, servitization helps firms satisfy customer demands and improve their financial performance by enhancing innovation performance. In addition, service-dominant logic indicates that manufacturing can take advantage of their operational resources, such as digital technology, as a competitive advantage to create better value for customers [27]. Resource liquefaction and resource integration, as two theoretical foundations of service-dominant logic, are particularly valuable in describing how the interaction of digitalization and servitization affects firm performance [27,35]. They suggest that since digital technologies, as operational resources, can liquefy firms’ other resources to empower servitization and digitalization can enhance digital technology applications, digitalization has a close relationship with servitization and will affect the relationship between servitization and firm-related performance.

The resource-based view sees the firm as a collection of various resources, and the different resources owned by each firm lead to varying levels of competitiveness among firms [33,36]. In the digital economy era, digital technology has become an essential resource for manufacturing, and digitalization enhances the implementation of digital technology [23]. Therefore, digitalization helps form a unique competitive advantage for manufacturers [31], innovating production and operation processes and ultimately improving financial performance [21]. In addition, the resource-based view also expounds the synergistic effect, which reckons that the combined effect of multiple resources may be greater than the simple sum of their individual effect [33,36]. With digitalization and servitization as two resources simultaneously owned by the firm, their interaction may be more beneficial to the firm performance than implementing either transition alone, according to the synergistic effect [33]. Specifically, digitalization enables servitization by boosting product standardization and scalability, while servitization empowers digitalization by providing tailored services that meet customers’ heterogeneous needs [21]. In summary, not only digitalization impacts firm-related performance, but also the interaction of digitalization and servitization drives business performance.

Furthermore, dynamic capability theory yields the similar conclusion as the service-dominant logic and resource-based view described above. This theory suggests that the firm can leverage digital technology or change its organizational structure to adapt to the rapid change in the market environment and gain a sustainable competitive advantage [37]. As the usage of digital technology represents an important aspect of digitalization and servitization involves organizational restructuring [31], these strategies can innovate existing products and business models to improve firm financial performance in a dynamic environment [27]. Specifically, Coreynen et al. have confirmed that the dynamic capabilities of exploitation and exploration facilitate digitalization, servitization, and their interaction, ultimately optimizing firms’ capacity for value creation [32]. Since exploitation refines current products and processes while exploration innovates new ones [32], firm innovation becomes integral to this mechanism. Therefore, according to the dynamic capability theory, we can propose that digitalization, servitization and their interaction contribute to the development of the firm financial performance, with firm innovation performance as a mediator within this mechanism.

Based on the above three theories, we can propose that the interaction of digitalization and servitization impacts firm financial performance, with firm innovation performance as a mediator within this mechanism. Therefore, the theoretical model (Fig. 1.) is established [38] to predict the mechanism, which is also synchronized with the following hypotheses.

Theoretical model.

Digitalization and firm performance

The term “digitalization” refers to the blending of a wider range of technologies, which need not necessarily be complicated but must be integrated in some way [39] to unlock previously unimagined possibilities and perhaps result in entirely new goods, services and company structures [14,40,41]. These innovations may introduce new corporate cultures, enhance enterprise collaboration, and alter the way businesses interact with their clients and staff [40,42]. Companies can achieve success in several ways through digital tools, which yield benefits including lower expenses, improved staff productivity, optimized supply chains, and higher customer satisfaction and loyalty [40,43]. Major research concludes that digitalization can positively affect the relevant firm performance. For example, it can greatly increase financial performance, with top management team experience and industry competition working as moderators [44]. Meanwhile, the impact of digitalization on financial performance can be mediated by sustainability [45]. Additionally, digitalization is linked to the manufacturer's innovation performance. Shen et al. and Ferreira et al. [25,46] have testified that digitalization significantly influences a company's innovation capabilities, and new digital procedures improve a firm's competitiveness. The analysis of 369 North American SMEs revealed that digitalization directly affects innovation performance in products and processes [47].

However, the transition to digitalization is also fraught with risks and challenges. As a new direction in the manufacturing transition, firms may find it difficult to align their digitalization strategy with organizational goals and establish an effective digitalization business model [23,48,49]. At the same time, digitalization requires significant capital investment, especially in its early stage. Therefore, the benefits of digitalization may not immediately outweigh its cost, which will result in failure to achieve the expected performance improvement. This phenomenon is known as the digitalization paradox [15].

In summary, the existing research does not present a uniform conclusion regarding the impact of digitalization on firm performance. Therefore, we attempt to clarify their relationship through empirical methods, and we hypothesize the following based on the perspectives of many scholars and theories mentioned above:

(H1): Digitalization has a positive impact on firm financial performance.

(H2): Digitalization has a positive impact on firm innovation performance.

Servitization and firm performance

In their seminal work from 1988, Vandermerwe and Rada introduced servitization, proposing that manufacturers may improve the core value of their products by adding services to them [5]. Expanding upon this groundbreaking study, other scholars investigate the function of this tactic. Most believe that servitization positively affects firm-relevant performance. It is seen as a crucial strategy that improves firm performance in terms of finances [11], strategy [8] and marketing, which allows companies to extract greater value from their product portfolios [17]. From a financial standpoint, servitization is argued to provide a more predictable income stream [29,50], generating larger profit margins. From a strategic and marketing perspective, adding additional services to the firm's portfolio can enable the provision of individualized solutions to consumers. This bolds particular significance in industrial markets since it builds stronger relationships with suppliers, increases customer loyalty and fosters closer ties with consumers. These factors lead to the creation of additional and recurring revenue streams [51,52]. Furthermore, servitization may affect the innovative practices of manufacturing firms [25,53]. The utilization of new services [53] can stimulate innovation in three different forms: service idea, client experience, and service procedure. Shen et al. conducted an empirical investigation to ascertain the beneficial effect of servitization on company innovation performance [25].

However, the labour-intensive and non-standardizable nature of service work can increase the transaction costs and decrease income from servitization [24]. When servitization leads to negative firm financial performance, this phenomenon is known as the servitization paradox [8,12]. In addition, if the input and output of the servitization strategy are incomparable, there may be a nonlinear relationship between servitization and performance. Liu et al. and Zhou et al. have identified a U-shaped relationship between servitization and firm financial performance [1,54].

Given the ambiguous impact of servitization on firm performance in prior studies, we aim to identify this relationship. Concerning mainstream perceptions and the theories above, we preliminarily propose the following hypotheses. In addition, while most studies use the ratio of servitization-related revenue to total revenue or questionnaires to determine the level of servitization [1,18,24,26,35], this study employs word frequency analysis for a more comprehensive reflection of the extent to which servitization has been applied in various business aspects.

(H3): Servitization has a positive impact on firm financial performance.

(H4): Servitization has a positive impact on firm innovation performance.

Interaction effect between digitalization and servitization

Experts claim a strong connection between digitalization and servitization, despite their backgrounds in different academic domains [20,31]. A subset of research has focused on the intersection of digitalization and servitization, suggesting that these two ideas can be integrated into a single concept known as digital servitization [12,17]. However, this research examines how the adoption of the digitalization strategy facilitates the execution of the servitization strategy, with little attention to the impact of the separate adoption of each strategy on the manufacturing firm's performance [2,4,27]. Another subset of research views servitization and digitalization as two distinct approaches. This type of research, which explores the connection between digitalization and servitization, without evaluating them as an integrated plan, is more pertinent to this study [18,[24], [25], [26]]. In reality, digitalization does highlight the possible connections between manufacturing and tertiary sectors [39]. Digital technologies have brought several changes, not only increasing mechanical product innovation but also completely changing the manufacturing model, turning manufacturers from producers into service providers [39,55]. Some findings have laid the foundation for this paper. Xie et al. and Kharlamov and Parry [28,29] compared the effects of pure manufacturing, servitization, digitalization and digital servitization pathways on the manufacturer's profitability, social impact and environment. Their findings support that the simultaneous adaption of digitalization and servitization positively impacts firm financial performance. Shen et al. and Martín-Peña et al. [25,26] positioned servitization as the antecedent and concluded that digitalization positively mediates the connection between servitization and a firm's financial or innovation performance. In contrast, Abou-Foul et al. and Yang et al. set digitalization as the antecedent and described that the relationship between digitalization and a company's profitability could be mediated by servitization [23,24]. Vendrell-Herrero et al. [21] formulated these two symmetrical mediation models and found that although both servitization and digitalization can work as mediators, servitization as an antecedent to digitalization yields greater operational performance benefits than reverse. Furthermore, Kohtamäki et al. [18] explored how servitization moderates the relationship between digitalization and firm financial performance, while Liu et al. [1] examined how digitalization moderates the relationship between servitization and firm financial performance. Additionally, Li et al. [56] researched the synergy of digitalization and servitization based on the coupled coordination degree model and explore how their synergy influence carbon productivity. In conclusion, the aforementioned studies have investigated the potential effects of digitalization and servitization and suggested the importance of their effective interaction [32].

However, little study has treated digitalization and servitization as equal independent variables in a single framework to examine their interactive effect on firm financial performance. Furthermore, no research has revealed the internal effect mechanism of their interaction on firm financial performance. Therefore, to bridge this research gap, we propose a fresh theoretical framework. In addition, a mediated moderation model is used to analyze the influence pathway. We hypothesize that the interaction of digitalization and servitization will improve firm financial performance (H5), with firm innovation performance as the mediator (H6; H7).

(H5): The interaction of digitalization and servitization has a positive impact on firm financial performance. That is, digitalization positively moderates the relationship between servitization and firm financial performance, meanwhile, servitization positively moderates the relationship between digitalization and firm financial performance.

(H6): The interaction of digitalization and servitization has a positive impact on firm innovation performance. That is, digitalization positively moderates the relationship between servitization and firm innovation performance, meanwhile, servitization positively moderates the relationship between digitalization and firm innovation performance.

(H7): Firm innovation performance mediates the interaction of digitalization and servitization in improving firm financial performance. This means that the interaction of digitalization and servitization can contribute both directly to firm financial performance, and indirectly through firm innovation performance.

Data sources and variable selection

Data collection

The study's sample consists of manufacturing companies listed on the main board of the Chinese stock exchanges in Shanghai and Shenzhen. These samples are engaged in industries classified under Divisions C13 to C42 in the GB/4754-2017 standard in China. The research period covers 11 years, from 2012 to 2022. The year 2012 was chosen as the starting point for analysis because it marked a pivotal moment in China's digitalization transition. In 2012, “The 12th Five-Year Development Plan for the Internet Industry” was released by the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT). Since then, the rapid development of digital technologies has expedited manufacturing digitalization. However, before 2012, very few enterprises had applied the digitalization strategy [44]. In addition, the servitization transition preceded the digitalization transition. Therefore, 2012, a key year for digitalization, was chosen as the starting point for research. Considering data availability, timeliness, and the time span used in other studies [1,37], the year 2022 was chosen as the endpoint for this research. In addition, sample enterprises with “special treatment” (i.e. ST or ∗ST) and insufficient data within the research period are removed to ensure data continuity and availability throughout the research period. Finally, a final sample of 2025 observations from 567 listed enterprises was obtained. Furthermore, all continuous variables in this article were winsorized between the 1st and 99th percentiles to remove the effect of extreme values. The data for this study were mainly obtained from CSMAR and CNINFO.

Variable measurements

The level of digitalization and servitization is measured through keyword frequency [[26], [27], [28]]. Digitalization-related keywords are extracted by referring to some scholars [4,28,37,57], while servitization-related keywords are also extracted by referring to some scholars [27,58,59]. In addition, the choice of keywords has also considered their definitions. The specifically selected keywords are shown in Table 1. To measure these levels, Python technology is first used to crawl the annual reports and other public information of sample enterprises from CNINFO during the test period. Secondly, a text-mining technique, e.g. Jieba, is employed to determine the frequency of these keywords. Finally, the natural logarithm of the keywords’ frequency after adding 1 is taken as the degree of digitalization and servitization.

Preliminary regression result

Mediated moderation exists when the interaction of two variables influences the mediator and then affects the dependent variable [60]. Before testing the mediation effect, the interaction of digitalization and servitization on firm financial performance is testified by Stata 18.0. Table 4 reports the baseline regression results. When digitalization (DT) and servitization (Ser) are set as the explanatory variables separately, their regression coefficients are positive and pass the 1 % significance test, as presented in Columns (1) (γ1=3.089, P<0.01) and (2) (γ2=5.935, P<0.01). Therefore, digitalization and servitization both positively impact the firm financial performance, verifying H1 and H2. Column (3) includes digitalization (DT), servitization (Ser) and their interaction term (DT∗Ser) together. It finds that all the regression coefficients in column (3) are positive and significant, especially the interaction term (τ3=4.560, P<0.01). Thus, the interaction of digitalization and servitization positively impacts firm financial performance, verifying H3. Specifically, digitalization positively moderates the relationship between servitization and firm financial performance, whereas servitization also positively moderates the relationship between digitalization and firm financial performance. Compared to the correlation analysis, the regression analysis reveals differing insights. Simply examining the interaction term alone would fail to demonstrate its meaningful effect on firm financial performance. However, when digitalization and servitization are included simultaneously as regressors, the significant positive impact of their interaction on the company's financial success becomes apparent.

Intermediary effect analysis

Furthermore, Table 6 reports the mediation effect testing process. Columns (2) and (3) in Table 6 present results that when digitalization (DT) and servitization (Ser) are set as the explanatory variables separately, their regression coefficients are positive and pass the 1 % significance test (η1=26.381, P<0.01; η2=50.757, P<0.01). Therefore, digitalization and servitization both positively impact the firm innovation performance, verifying H4 and H5. Column (4) includes digitalization (DT), servitization (Ser) and their interaction term (DT∗Ser), finding that the interaction of digitalization and servitization have a positive and significant influence on the firm innovation performance (α3=47.603, P<0.01), verifying H6. When adding the mediator variable-firm innovation performance into the model, firm innovation performance shows a significant impact on firm financial performance (β=0.019, P<0.01). The interactive effect of digitalization and servitization on firm financial performance is reduced but still significantly positive (τ3′=3.666, P<0.01), as shown in Column (5). The results indicate an indirect mediating effect via the mediation of 0.904 (47.603∗0.019) and a direct mediating effect without the mediation of 3.666 [[61], [62], [63]]. Therefore, the mediating effect of firm innovation performance accounts for 19.78 % of the total effect, verifying H7. This result suggests that the interaction of digitalization and servitization can enhance firm innovation performance. Therefore, firm innovation performance can act as a mediator that affects the relationship between the interaction of digitalization and servitization and firm financial performance [64,65].

Discussion and conclusion

Based on service-dominant logic, resource-based view and dynamic capabilities theory, this paper establishes a theoretical framework to explore the interactive effect of digitalization and servitization on firm financial performance, with firm innovation performance as the mediator. This effect mechanism is analyzed through a mediated moderation model. These results generate three primary findings. Firstly, both digitalization and servitization are conducive to the financial and innovation performance of the company. Digitalization promotes firm innovation, changes firm value creation methods and improves corporate financial performance by enhancing the use of digital technologies, while servitization achieves these goals by innovating the business model toward a service-dominant approach. Secondly, the interaction of digitalization and servitization has a positive impact on firm financial performance, with varying effects across manufacturing. Digitalization and servitization are complementary and mutually reinforcing in improving firm financial performance. On the one hand, a company that has undergone servitization transition, can deploy digital technologies into its services to boost its financial performance. On the other hand, a digitalized enterprise can improve financial performance by introducing service elements to its digital offering. This conclusion holds true for capital-intensive and technology-intensive manufacturing, but not for labour-intensive manufacturing. Thirdly, firm innovation performance acts as a mediator to partially mediate the interactive effect of digitalization and servitization on firm financial performance. This emphasizes the importance of firm innovation performance and demonstrates the internal consistency between the interaction of digitalization and servitization, firm innovation performance, and firm financial performance.

Theoretical and empirical contributions

This study makes several theoretical and empirical contributions to this field of research on digitalization and servitization. Firstly, according to our empirical evidence, both digitalization and servitization have a positive direct impact on firm financial performance and firm innovation performance. Existing literature has examined the impact of digitalization and servitization on firm-related performance, particularly financial performance, with mixed findings. Some studies have found digitalization to positively impact firm financial performance [44,45], while others report negative or no-linear effects [15,18,69]. A similar lack of consensus also characterized research on the financial effects of servitization [1,26,54,70,71]. This divergence in prior work suggests that there is no unified conclusion regarding the influence of digitalization and servitization on firm financial performance. Addressing this debate, our study employs empirical evidence to substantiate the hypothesized positive direct impacts of digitalization and servitization on firm financial performance. Our findings align with the conclusions of Martín-Peña et al. and Abou-Foul et al. [24,26] but contradict Patel et al., Guo et al. and Liu et al. [1,69,71]. Besides, it is generally accepted in existing research that digitalization and servitization have a positive direct influence on firm innovation performance [25,46,53], as confirmed by our empirical results. Furthermore, prior studies relied on the proportion of servitization-related revenue to total revenue or questionnaires to analyze the level of servitization as the research foundation [1,18,24,26,35]. In contrast, this study adopts text frequency analysis of servitization-related keywords to determine servitization level. Compared with traditional methods, this methodology provides more robust empirical evidence and enriches the knowledge of servitization measurement. In summary, these findings provide empirical contributions to debates on the impact of digitalization and servitization on firm-related performance.

Secondly, this study accords digitalization and servitization equal standing as explanatory variables. Our research findings demonstrate that the interaction of digitalization and servitization has a positive effect on firm financial performance, and the interactive effect varies across different types of manufacturing industries. While some prior studies have started to explore the interrelationship between digitalization and servitization [12,17,18,35], existing studies generally adopt one transition as an antecedent variable to examine the mediating or moderating effects of the other [21]. For instance, when digitalization is viewed as a precondition for servitization, research focuses on the mediating or moderating role of servitization on the relationship between digitalization and firm financial performance [18,23,24]. Alternatively, with servitization as the precondition, studies examine the mediating or moderating influence of digitalization on the relationship between servitization and firm financial performance [1,26]. Through these analyses, scholars have predicted an interaction between digitalization and servitization, and their interaction positively impacts firm financial performance [12,20,21]. However, no empirical research has validated their interactive effect by treating digitalization and servitization as equal explanatory variables under one theoretical framework. This study bridges this gap by adopting a mediated moderation model to analyze our fresh theoretical framework. This result concludes that the interaction of digitalization and servitization has a positive impact on firm financial performance. Specifically, digitalization can positively moderate the relationship between servitization and firm financial performance. At the same time, servitization can also positively moderate the relationship between digitalization and firm financial performance. Our research finding is consistent with previous studies [1,18] and provides new empirical evidence for the literature about the relationship between digitalization and servitization, by establishing a fresh theoretical framework and using a new methodology approach. Our study also answers calls for research on the intertwining of digitalization and servitization [12,20,21]. In addition, the interactive effect of digitalization and servitization is absent in labour-intensive manufacturing. This may be because labour-intensive manufacturing lacks the capital advantages seen in capital-intensive and technology-intensive manufacturing. Given the substantial capital requirement for digitalization transition, labour-intensive manufacturing is inherently disadvantageous in pursuing digitalization alone or synergistic digitalization and servitization transition. Therefore, the interactive effect of digitalization and servitization on firm financial performance is not prominent in labour-intensive manufacturing. This analysis of industry heterogeneity also enhances our understanding of this aspect of research content.

Thirdly, this research reveals the interaction of digitalization and servitization on firm financial performance, with firm innovation performance as the mediator. This research integrates service-dominant logic, resource-based view, and dynamic capabilities theory. Based on these three theories, the effect mechanism of the interaction of digitalization and servitization on firm financial performance is established. Although these three theories are commonly referenced in the literature on digitalization and servitization [1,27,[31], [32], [33]], no existing study has integrated them to identify firm innovation performance as a mediator and explore the mechanism through which the interaction of digitalization and servitization influences firm financial performance. In the existing literature, Ferreira et al. have set firm innovation performance as the dependent variable and demonstrated digitalization's function in firm innovation [46]. Shen et al. also explore the role of servitization and digitalization on firm innovation performance, with firm innovation performance as the dependent variable [25]. While some experts have concluded that digitalization and servitization can impact firm innovation performance, to date, no research has treated firm innovation performance as an intermediary variable to analyze its mediating effect between the interaction of digitalization and servitization and firm financial performance. Our finding highlights the pivotal mediating role of firm innovation performance, concluding that the interaction of digitalization and servitization can not only directly improve firm financial performance but also indirectly enhance it through firm innovation performance. In summary, this study uncovered the mechanism through which the interaction of digitalization and servitization impacts firm financial performance, extending the literature study content on the relationship between digitalization and servitization [18].

Implications for practice

The primary conclusions of this study indicate the critical role of the interaction between digitalization and servitization, along with firm innovation performance, in firm financial performance improvement. Based on these findings, several policy recommendations can be made:

Firstly, manufacturing firms, especially those in capital and technology-intensive sectors, should synergistically implement both digitalization and servitization. Managers can selectively apply digital technologies to enhance internal service processes and external customer experience so as to achieve value-added solutions. In addition, managers need to change their mindset, instead of seeing digitalization and servitization as alternatives, but strive to promote their synergy.

Secondly, different types of manufacturing firms require tailored optimization strategies. For labour-intensive manufacturing with limited capital resources, when considering the digitalization transition, priority should be given to advancing technologies directly related to the production and sales process. For capital and technology-intensive manufacturing, the servitization transition's function may not be as obvious as that of digitalization. Therefore, it is important to ensure alignment between servitization and digitalization progress to promote their mutual development.

Thirdly, managers should recognize the mediating role of firm innovation performance in the interaction between digitalization and servitization on firm financial performance. They should cultivate an open and inclusive corporate culture within their organizations to facilitate digitalization and servitization transition, as well as firm innovation. In addition, manufacturing enterprises should leverage new technologies and business models through collaborative innovation networks to spur synergistic development both externally and internally, which will lead to more healthy and sustainable development of the enterprise.

Limitations and directions for future research

Although this study yields some intriguing results, several limitations present opportunities for future research. The complex impact mechanism of the interaction between digitalization and servitization on firm financial performance may involve factors beyond firm innovation performance. In addition, the interaction of digitalization and servitization may influence other outcome variables, such as company market value. Thus, this complex impact mechanism and other dependent variables deserve detailed and in-depth discussions. Moreover, as the factors influencing digitalization may vary across manufacturing sectors, future studies could use customized measurement approaches or select industry-specific digital keywords for text frequency analysis to reflect the unique characteristics of each sector. This approach would allow for a more robust industry heterogeneity analysis based on the tailored level of digitalization in each sector. Furthermore, the current sample is drawn from China, limiting the generalizability of the findings. Future research can be conducted in different geographical and socioeconomic circumstances to duplicate the findings of this research and evaluate their external credibility.

Mpox Outbreaks Beyond Historically Endemic Regions: A Clinical Review of Vaccination Strategies and Public Health Challenges
Authors: Goranit Sakunchotpanit, Ryan Chen, Laura I. Ortiz López, Maya Akbik, Vinod E. Nambudiri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMpox, caused by the monkeypox virus, emerged as a global public health concern following the 2022 outbreaks in non-endemic regions. Although classical presentations include a febrile prodrome and mucocutaneous lesions, atypical symptoms and stigma have complicated clinical recognition and containment. Smallpox-derived vaccines, such as ACAM2000 and modified vaccinia Ankara, have been deployed for prophylaxis; however, access, policy implementation, and public acceptance remain inconsistent across regions.SummaryGlobal mpox vaccination strategies demonstrate pronounced disparities in coverage, infrastructure, and public trust. High-income countries, including the USA and European nations, prioritized men who have sex with men, transgender individuals, and people living with human immunodeficiency virus through targeted outreach in sexual health clinics and community-based programs. In contrast, many Southeast Asian and African nations lacked coordinated vaccination campaigns due to limited resources, weak surveillance systems, and low awareness. Misinformation and sociocultural stigma further suppressed vaccine uptake, particularly in parts of the Eastern Mediterranean region. In settings such as Taiwan and the Netherlands, inclusive public health messaging and equitable access policies contributed to higher vaccination coverage. Healthcare worker knowledge significantly influenced vaccine delivery, though educational gaps persist.Key MessagesDespite demonstrated efficacy, vaccination coverage remains globally inequitable, with disparities shaped by structural and social determinants, including race, socioeconomic status, and geography. Dermatologists play a pivotal role in early detection, patient counseling, and vaccine advocacy. Strengthening coordinated, evidence-based vaccination strategies is essential to improving outbreak preparedness and advancing global health equity.

Introduction

Mpox, caused by the mpox virus (MPXV), is a zoonotic orthopoxvirus first identified in 1958 among macaque monkeys, with the first human case reported in 1970 [1–3]. Mpox transmission occurs through contact with lesion exudate, crust material, and mucosal excretions from infected animals or humans [1–4]. Vertical transmission and fomites are other routes of human-to-human transmission [3, 5, 6]. Diagnosed patients are advised to isolate with contact tracing [1–3]. With unprecedented outbreaks in countries outside previously endemic areas of Africa, the World Health Organization (WHO) declared mpox a Public Health Emergency of International Concern on July 23, 2022 [1].

The MPXV genome spans approximately 198 kb and is functionally organized into a conserved central region responsible for viral replication and variable terminal regions that regulate host range and virulence [7]. MPXV exists in two infectious forms: mature virions, which facilitate host-to-host transmission, and extracellular virions, which promote intrahost dissemination and immune evasion [7]. Contemporary vaccine development has therefore focused on surface antigens expressed on both forms, including A29L and M1R on mature virions and A35R and B6R on extracellular virions [7]. Among these, M1R has emerged as a key immunologic target, as its omission in preclinical models markedly reduces neutralizing antibody responses and results in higher viral loads [7].

While antivirals (e.g., tecovirimat) have demonstrated safety and efficacy in previous studies, growing resistance has necessitated additional preventative measures [3–6, 8]. Given antigenic similarities between vaccinia virus and MPXV, smallpox vaccination is regarded as a viable strategy for controlling mpox outbreaks [1–6, 8, 9]. Historical data collected from 1980 to 1984 indicate that the overall attack rate for unvaccinated contacts (7.2%) was significantly higher than that for vaccinated individuals (0.9%) [1–3]. Currently, two types of smallpox vaccines are approved for human use in the USA and Europe: second-generation ACAM2000 and third-generation modified vaccinia Ankara-Bavarian Nordic (MVA-BN) (Table 1) [1–6, 9–14]. ACAM2000 is a replication-competent vaccinia vaccine derived from the New York City Board of Health strain and was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in 2007 for smallpox prevention only [15–17]. During the 2022 mpox outbreak, ACAM2000 was made available for mpox prevention under an Expanded Access Investigational New Drug protocol [16]. MVA-BN is a replication-deficient vaccine that received full US FDA approval in 2019 for the prevention of both smallpox and mpox in adults ≥18 years [1, 15]. The original license specifies subcutaneous administration (0.5 mL per dose, administered as 2 doses 4 weeks apart) [18]. During the 2022 mpox outbreak, the US FDA issued an Emergency Use Authorization on August 9, 2022, permitting intradermal administration (0.1 mL per dose) to extend vaccine supply and allowing use in individuals <18 years [16, 18].

However, important limitations, including contraindications to ACAM2000 in immunocompromised and pregnant individuals and the relatively short duration of immunity conferred by MVA-BN, have spurred the development of more stable and scalable platforms [7]. Emerging strategies include the recent 2024 WHO Emergency Use Listing of LC16m8 (a third-generation attenuated, replicating vaccine) and the development of mRNA and protein subunit vaccines currently in phase 3 trials [7].

As of now, vaccines are reserved for exposed individuals, healthcare workers (HCWs) providing care to mpox patients, and laboratory personnel working with the virus [1–6, 9–14]. Although no mass vaccination guidelines have been implemented, the New York City Department of Health and Mental Hygiene started administering vaccines to gay and bisexual men with elevated exposure risk in June 2022 [1–6, 8, 9, 19]. However, only 25% of vulnerable populations in the USA have completed a vaccination series, reflecting inequities in vaccine access and making early recognition imperative [19]. We aim to provide a comprehensive review of global public health responses to recent mpox outbreaks, emphasizing vaccination policies, challenges, and strategies to equip dermatologists with a understanding of mpox management and adaptations in public health infrastructure worldwide (Tables 2, 3) [20–37].

Methods

We conducted a comprehensive literature search across the PubMed database to identify studies on mpox vaccination and public health policy. Search strings utilized combinations of Boolean operators and the following terms: “mpox,” “monkeypox virus,” “simian smallpox,” and “vaccination.” To ensure clinical and regulatory currency, the search period spanned from January 1964 to February 2026. Given the clinical focus of this review, we prioritized evidence most pertinent to dermatologists, including data on vaccine safety, cutaneous adverse events and manifestations, and immunogenicity in high-risk populations. Emphasis was placed on studies with direct implications for patient care and outbreak response. To capture evolving regulatory guidance and implementation strategies during recent outbreaks, selected gray literature was incorporated, including emergency declarations, technical guidance, and policy documents from the WHO and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). Countries and regional policies were selected based on epidemiological burden and public health relevance. For comparative analysis, countries were grouped according to the WHO Regional Office framework, reflecting how vaccine policy, regulatory oversight, and distribution strategies are coordinated at the regional level.

Diagnosis and Clinical Presentation

Mpox can be diagnosed via PCR analysis of lesional swabs or bodily fluids but is also identifiable by its clinical presentation, similar to smallpox [1, 3]. Symptoms typically begin with a febrile prodrome of fatigue, headache, and sore throat before progressing to cutaneous manifestations: papules evolving into vesicles, pustules, and crusts in the oral, genital, and/or anal regions [2, 4, 5, 9]. Anal pain and bleeding have also been observed during the 2022 outbreak [3]. Unlike in smallpox, the presence of maxillary, cervical, and/or inguinal lymphadenopathy is characteristic of mpox, both before and concomitant with rash development [2, 4, 5, 9]. Symptom duration typically ranges from 5 to 21 days, resolving within 2–4 weeks [1, 3]. Young children, pregnant women, and immunocompromised patients may develop more severe disease and require hospitalization, though this is rare in recent outbreaks [1, 3].

Diagnostic Nuances and Specimen Collection

Although the traditional centrifugal spread of lesions remains a hallmark of orthopoxviruses, current outbreaks often present with atypical, localized morphologies, including isolated anogenital (36–73%) or oropharyngeal (14–25%) lesions [28]. Dermatologists must remain vigilant for deep-seated, firm, umbilicated lesions that may appear purulent but are solid upon palpation: a characteristic feature of mpox [1, 28]. Because concurrent sexually transmitted infections (STIs) are reported in 15–37% of cases at the time of mpox diagnosis, a positive test for herpes simplex virus or syphilis does not exclude an mpox diagnosis [38–40]. Screening for STIs, including human immunodeficiency virus (HIV), is recommended for all patients undergoing evaluation for mpox [39, 41]. When collecting specimens, clinicians should avoid unroofing or aspirating lesions to minimize the risk of sharps injury and viral aerosolization, which has led to documented occupational infections [28, 39]. Current CDC guidelines recommend vigorous swabbing of the lesion surface using sterile synthetic swabs (e.g., Dacron, polyester, or nylon) [28, 42]. To ensure maximum diagnostic yield, at least two skin lesions should be sampled from separate sites or distinct lesion morphologies, if possible [28, 42]. Swabs may be stored dry or in viral transport media as specified by the diagnostic laboratory [28, 42].

Counseling on Vaccination and Prevention

Patient counseling should clarify the distinction between pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) and post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP). PrEP with the two-dose MVA-BN is indicated for individuals at high risk of exposure, with doses administered 28 days apart [28, 39]. Vaccine effectiveness ranges from 66% to 86%, and while vaccination does not fully prevent infection, breakthrough infections tend to be milder with reduced hospitalization risk [28, 43]. PEP is highly time-sensitive; for optimal efficacy, the vaccine should be administered within 4 days of exposure [28, 39, 44]. Although administration between 4 and 14 days after exposure may still reduce disease severity, it is less likely to prevent symptom onset [28, 39, 43]. Individuals with known or presumed exposure should receive vaccination as soon as possible, ideally within this critical window [28, 39].

Communication and Stigma Mitigation

Effective clinical management requires stigma-sensitive communication to ensure patient trust and reporting accuracy. Clinicians should utilize neutral, descriptive language, emphasizing that transmission occurs through close skin-to-skin contact rather than framing mpox as a disease exclusive to specific sexual orientations [45]. The 2022 outbreak disproportionately affected gay, bisexual, and other men who have sex with men (MSM), but mpox can be acquired by anyone through sustained physical contact [39, 46]. Addressing perceived stigma, particularly the patient’s fear of social or clinical judgment, is essential for fostering an environment where marginalized populations feel safe seeking testing and vaccination. Providing counseling in private settings and acknowledging the systemic barriers to care can significantly improve patient adherence to isolation and treatment protocols. Given the high prevalence of concurrent STIs and HIV among mpox cases, integrating mpox into routine sexual health services and offering comprehensive combination prevention strategies is critical [38, 41].

Regional Overview of Mpox Vaccination Strategies

Region of the Americas

In the region of the Americas, the USA and Peru implemented divergent approaches to mpox vaccination. The USA launched a targeted campaign utilizing MVA-BN, focusing on MSM and HIV-positive individuals [47, 48]. However, notable racial and ethnic disparities in uptake persisted, despite higher infection rates in communities of color [49–51]. In contrast, Peru prioritized similar high-risk groups but faced substantial vaccine hesitancy, fueled by conspiracy theories that eroded public trust and hindered vaccine acceptance [52].

Western Pacific Region

In the Western Pacific region, vaccination efforts varied widely, influenced by the availability of diverse vaccine platforms and varying levels of community engagement. Taiwan established hospital-based vaccination clinics and prioritized high-risk individuals, including MSM, those receiving HIV-related prophylaxis, and frontline HCWs, achieving a high completion rate [53, 54]. In China, while the general population showed willingness to vaccinate – especially among HCWs and individuals with comorbidities – uptake among MSM remained limited [55]. South Korea’s ring vaccination strategy did not explicitly include MSM, reflecting persistent stigma and policy gaps [56, 57]. In contrast, Hong Kong implemented a more inclusive approach, utilizing diverse communication platforms to disseminate public health information and encourage vaccine uptake among LGBTQIA+ communities [22]. In Japan, the LC16m8 vaccine was approved for mpox in 2022 and received a WHO Emergency Use Listing in 2024 [7]. Administered via a percutaneous multiple puncture technique, it demonstrated a favorable safety profile in pediatric populations, as well as HIV-positive cohorts [7].

Southeast Asian Region

In the Southeast Asian region, countries such as India and Nepal lacked approved mpox vaccines and did not implement national vaccination strategies [54, 58–61]. Structural and logistical barriers, compounded by limited public awareness, impeded proactive outbreak responses. These challenges reflect broader regional limitations in public health infrastructure and disease surveillance.

Eastern Mediterranean Region

In the Eastern Mediterranean region, mpox vaccination efforts were generally limited. In countries like Saudi Arabia and Lebanon, misinformation and low vaccine confidence – especially among HCWs – impeded campaign effectiveness [62–67]. Surveys revealed widespread belief in conspiracy theories (e.g., MPXV being engineered as a bioweapon or exploited for profit and global control), contributing to low vaccine uptake. In contrast, Qatar implemented ring vaccination using both MVA-BN and ACAM2000 as part of its public health preparedness for the 2022 FIFA World Cup. It made efforts to address stigma related to mpox transmission among MSM. However, broad-based public health outreach remained limited [68, 69].

European Region

Across the European region, countries including the UK, France, Germany, and the Netherlands launched robust initial vaccination campaigns, prioritizing MSM and transgender individuals [70–78]. Vaccines were administered through sexual health clinics, festivals, and LGBTQIA+ venues. However, interest declined as case numbers dropped in 2023, and disparities in access emerged among bisexual men, individuals with lower educational attainment, and other underserved groups [73, 75, 79, 80]. In the Netherlands, PrEP was offered to those engaged in high-risk behaviors, and outreach through LGBTQIA+ community networks contributed to increased uptake [81].

African Region

The 2024–2025 epidemiological shift is defined by the emergence of subclade Ib in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) [7]. Unlike the zoonotic focus of subclade Ia, subclade Ib exhibits enhanced human-to-human transmission through domestic, vertical, and heterosexual routes, resulting in over 1,040 deaths in 2023 alone [7]. In the African region, countries such as Nigeria and the DRC face a high burden of mpox, compounded by limited access to vaccines, antivirals, and personal protective equipment [82–84]. Despite these challenges, data from the DRC demonstrated the effectiveness of MVA-BN in endemic settings, particularly among HCWs [85–88]. To address the protection gap in high-risk endemic groups, clinical trials (NCT06844487) were launched in 2025 to evaluate the safety and immunogenicity of MVA-BN in infants aged 4–24 months and pregnant women [7]. South Africa, while not experiencing widespread mpox transmission, issued guidelines for contact tracing and outbreak preparedness. However, literature on vaccine implementation in South Africa remains sparse, reflecting the broader limitations in national vaccine strategy across the region [89]. Cameroon and the Central African Republic have reported increasing case numbers, yet vaccination data remain minimal, underscoring critical inequities in global public health capacity [90–93].

Discussion

The global spread of mpox has highlighted public health challenges in areas outside of previously endemic areas [1–3]. While mpox shares similar characteristics with smallpox, its varied transmission mechanics and atypical presentations complicate public health responses in multiple countries since the 2022 outbreak [1, 11, 51, 62, 94]. Cases in non-endemic regions were often more prevalent in MSM and HIV-positive individuals, highlighting a need for targeted public health interventions in these populations [22, 57, 61, 73, 95].

Vaccination remains an essential strategy in mpox control, with options such as ACAM2000 and MVA-BN providing effective PrEP, PEP, and prevention [1, 4, 12, 13, 96–101]. A meta-analysis by Berry et al. [102] found that MVA-BN is highly effective in preventing mpox, with a two-dose regimen providing 71.8% effectiveness over 2 years. Long-term effectiveness after 10 years is predicted to be 59.2% for 1 dose and 66.6% for 2 doses, highlighting the benefits of long-term protection [102]. Furthermore, data from previous outbreaks have shown the efficacy of smallpox vaccines in reducing mpox severity, given their antigenic similarity to vaccinia, providing some cross-protection [103]. However, the limited availability of vaccines in many locations poses a challenge, particularly in countries where healthcare infrastructure is under-resourced [104]. The establishment of public vaccination clinics in countries such as Taiwan may improve vaccine accessibility [54]. Moreover, the efficacies of vaccination campaigns in non-endemic countries in North America, Asia, and Europe are influenced by different levels of public awareness and education, highlighting the importance of boosting mpox awareness and vaccine acceptance [2, 60, 63, 68, 71, 75, 105].

Mpox prevention may also further expand through the development of multivalent mRNA platforms, such as BNT166 and MPXV-1103 [7]. Although current vaccines remain central to prevention efforts, emerging mRNA-based approaches aim to enhance both safety and immunogenicity. These candidates leverage lipid nanoparticle delivery to target multiple viral proteins simultaneously (e.g., A35, B6, M1, and H3), offering 100% protection against death and minimizing lesion development in nonhuman primate clade I challenge models [7]. Notably, these platforms provide significant advantages: they lack live viral genetic material, making them inherently safer for severely immunocompromised patients, and they can be rapidly updated to address mutations in emerging clades [7]. Thus, investing more into these technological advances may also benefit the global population.

Studies from the USA also highlight the importance of addressing socioeconomic barriers to preventative measures, as racial and ethnic minorities experience higher infection rates and lower vaccination uptake [50]. Addressing these gaps requires targeted policy initiatives and community outreach efforts aimed at ensuring equitable access and public health support for high-risk populations.

Another important insight from the 2022 outbreak is the role of HCWs’ education in public health response. In countries such as Peru and Belgium, well-informed HCWs play an essential part in early diagnosis and vaccination uptake [74, 106]. However, disparities in HCWs’ knowledge persist, as studies in Pakistan and Lebanon demonstrate how conspiracy theories and a lack of awareness can significantly undermine public health response [66, 67]. Training programs may provide ongoing education to help engage HCWs in battling misinformation [107].

Conclusion

The 2022 mpox outbreak underscores the necessity of a multidisciplinary approach that enhances public health surveillance, vaccination campaigns, and health education for both the public HCWs. Effective management of future outbreaks requires a flexible, coordinated response that can adapt to atypical presentations of mpox while prioritizing high-risk populations and ensuring equitable access to necessary resources. Ultimately, the transition from smallpox-derived vaccines to “fourth-generation” platforms specifically engineered for MPXV antigens marks a pivotal shift in global health equity. By supporting local vaccine production and establishing standardized frameworks for real-time surveillance, the international community can better mitigate the disruptions in supply chains that characterized the 2022 response.

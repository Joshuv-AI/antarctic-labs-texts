Comparison of pregnancy and neonatal outcomes before and after laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy in women diagnosed with obesity
Authors: Vahit Mutlu, Burak Barutcu, Samet Şahin, Çağlanur Yıldız, Rabia Ebrar Tutcu, Ceyhan Dikmen, Ayşe Zehra Özdemir, Gökhan Selçuk Özbalcı, Kadir Yılmaz
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThis study aimed to compare pregnancy and neonatal outcomes before and after laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy in women diagnosed with obesity.MethodsFemale patients who underwent laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy with the diagnosis of obesity at Ondokuz Mayıs University Faculty of Medicine between 11.01.2015 and 17.03.2025 were included in the study. The patients were divided into two groups as those who became pregnant after the operation (n = 59) and those who did not (n = 435). Pregnancy and delivery outcomes of the women who became pregnant before and after the operation were compared.ResultsPregnancy rate after LSG was 11.9% in our sample. Age mean for pregnancy patients after LSG (31.22 ± 4.45) were significantly lower (p < 0.05), whereas BMI mean differences between postop pregnant and non-pregnant group were insignificant (p > 0.05). DM, HT, PCOS, anxiety, asthma and hypothyroid distribution differences were also insignificant between postop pregnancy groups (p > 0.05). Pregnancy month after operation was ranged from 6 to 60 months with 25.22 ± 13.84 months mean. DM frequency was 6.8%, HT, PCOS and anxiety frequencies were 5.1%. 45.8% of patients had operation history. Birth weight and mother age were significantly higher in postop pregnant after LSG patients (p < 0.05). Pregnancy BMI was significantly lower after LSG (p < 0.05). Congenital anomaly was more common in postop pregnant after LSG patients (p < 0.05). All other pregnancy and delivery outcome differences between patient groups were statistically insignificant (p > 0.05).ConclusionPregnancy rates in pregnant women after LSG were over 10%. Birth weight, BMI during pregnancy and congenital anomaly differed before and after LSG. Further studies are needed on the relationship between congenital anomaly and LSG.Keywords: Laparoscopic Sleeve Gastrectomy, Obesity, Pregnancy, Complication

Introduction

Obesity indicates that individuals are overweight according to their body mass ratio, and its prevalence and incidence are increasing every year [1]. In a study conducted in 11 countries between 1990 and 2020, it was found that the risk of obesity increased by 0.80 times each year, and in a study conducted in 162 countries, 81% of women and 70% of men were at risk of obesity [2]. In addition to methods such as diet [3], nutritional regimen [4], and physical activity [5], surgical methods are increasingly used in the fight against obesity [6]. One of the most frequently used of these is the Laparoscopic Sleeve Gastrectomy (LSG) surgical method [7]. Sleeve Gastrectomy (SG) is preferred as part of the duodenal switch method with biliopancreatic diversion, because it is simple and easier than other methods [8]. First applied by laparoscopic method in 1999, (LSG) is based on the removal of a certain part of the stomach and reducing its volume [9]. It is a widely used method today, especially due to the advantages of laparoscopic methods such as short recovery time and minimally invasive procedure.

It is reported that obesity affects all vital functions of individuals as well as the lifestyle, decisions and body parameters related to pregnancy [10, 11]. Studies on obesity and pregnancy have revealed a negative relationship between obesity and fertility. Andreasen et al. [12] reported in their studies that obesity is associated with complications in fertile women. In another study, Creanga et al. [13] reported side effects of obesity in pregnant women such as insulin resistance, miscarriage, congenital anomalies, diabetes (DM) and hypertension (HT). Yogev and Catalano [14] reported that obesity has negative effects on both the outcomes of the baby and the pregnancy process, and that complications and infection risks increase in particular. Although there are studies in the literature on obesity and complications during pregnancy and the negative effects of obesity on pregnancy [10–14], no sufficient studies have been found on pregnancy and outcomes in the same patient group before and after LSG. Therefore, this study aimed to compare pregnancy and neonatal outcomes before and after laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy in women diagnosed with obesity.

Methods

Research model

The research was modeled retrospectively and longitudinally on file scan data. Descriptive scan model was used as the research method. The differences between pregnancy and birth outcomes of women who had LSG and became pregnant afterwards were analyzed and described from patient files.

Patients

The study included female patients who underwent laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy with the diagnosis of obesity at Ondokuz Mayıs University Faculty of Medicine between 11.01.2015 and 17.03.2025. The patients were divided into two groups: those who became pregnant after the operation (n = 59) and those who did not (n = 435). The first group included 59 patients who became pregnant after LSG, while the second group included 435 patients who did not become pregnant after LSG. In other words, among women of childbearing age who underwent LSG, we compared those who became pregnant after the operation with those who did not and their pregnancy and birth outcomes in the same women before and after LSG. The main aim of the study was to determine the clinical and demographic characteristics of patients of childbearing age who underwent LSG and became pregnant after delivery, and the pregnancy rates after LSG with comparison of birth outcomes in the same women before and after LSG. The preoperative and postoperative pregnancy and delivery outcomes of the women who became pregnant were compared. The inclusion criteria for the study were as follows:

In order to clarify of data completeness and potential biases, only women having consistent data in their patient files were subjected to the study. Although having all data in their file, patients having conflict on data were excluded according to 5% trimming of extreme values.

Routinely, after bariatric surgery, patients receive weight control and nutritional monitoring under the supervision of a dietitian at 6 and 12 months. Nutritional monitoring is tailored to the individual patient and is determined based on their daily nutritional needs and calorie intake.

First, we compared pre- and postoperative pregnancies, and second, to compare those who conceived after surgery with those who did not. This aimed to obtain descriptive statistics on postoperative pregnancy outcomes.

Research data

The medical information of the patients was obtained from the hospital's electronic record system. Here, the demographic information of the patients, pregnancy method, perinatal mortality, congenital anomaly, baby's birth weight, age at birth, body mass index, additional disease, surgery duration, fetal heart rate pattern, presence of postoperative infection, postoperative complications, baby Apgar scores, hypertension in pregnancy, preeclampsia, presence of GDM parameters were examined. Each of 59 women having pregnancy after LSG has both pre- and postoperative pregnancy data. All 59 women had pregnancy method, perinatal mortality, congenital anomaly, baby's birth weight, age at birth, body mass index, additional disease, surgery duration, fetal heart rate pattern, presence of postoperative infection, postoperative complications, baby Apgar scores, hypertension in pregnancy, preeclampsia, presence of GDM parameters.

Ethical considerations

An ethical approval was taken from Ondokuz Mayis University Clinical Research Ethics Committee with 2025/204 grand number. Since the study is retrospective, no patient consent was applicable.

Statistical methods

Nominal and ordinal parameters were described with means, standard deviations, median and ranges. Categorical parameters were described with frequencies. Kolmogorov Smirnov test was used for normality distribution analysis of scale parameters. Since scale parameter distributions were not normally distributed, nonparametric tests were used. Mann Whitney U test was used for differences between two groups. Fisher’s exact test was used for differences between categorical parameters. Wilcoxon Signed Rank test was used for differences between scale parameters in time series. In order to reduce confounding effects, apha corrections were used and Fisher's Exact values were used in analysis. In addition, univariate correlational analysis were not used to cause confounding effects. SPSS 25.0 for windows was used for analysis at 95% Confidence Interval and 0.05 significance level. In the first phase of the study, which compared women who conceived and did not conceive after LSG, 435 patients (who did not conceive after LSG) and 59 patients (who did conceive after LSG) were compared. This met the prerequisite of parametric analysis in terms of patient numbers and statistical power. Due to the sample size, the findings were interpreted explanatory in the pre- and post-LSG comparisons. In all difference analysis comparisons, Confidence Intervals were selected as 95% with 0.05 deviance threshold.

Discussion

This study aimed to compare the parameters of pregnancy and delivery before and after the operation in women who became pregnant after LSG. According to the findings, pregnancy was observed in 11.9% of women after LSG, while birth weight, BMI during pregnancy and congenital anomalies differed before and after LSG.

In the literature, studies on obesity and pregnancy report that obesity causes many complications related to pregnancy [15–17]. Simmons et al. [18] reported that gestational diabetes should be managed well and is associated with risk factors in the relationship between obesity and diabetes during pregnancy. In another study, Smith et al. [19] reported that maternal obesity has negative effects on both the mother and the baby. Stubert et al. [20] reported in their study that women with obesity should have their weight reduced to normal ranges before pregnancy. Kumari [21] reported that morbid obesity is an independent perinatal side effect risk factor. Although all these studies and similar studies report that obesity increases risk factors in pregnant women and is a risk factor in itself, studies on obesity surgery are more limited. In our study, no significant difference was observed between risk factors before and after obesity surgery. This may indicate that pregnant women regain their pre-obesity health status after obesity surgery. Although congenital anomaly seems significant as an outcome, since it was only found in four patients, larger sample and multicenter studies are needed on this subject.

In one of the studies conducted in this context, Xu et al. [22] conducted a comprehensive study on a national scale and reported that weight gain during pregnancy was lower in women who underwent bariatric surgery than in those who did not undergo bariatric surgery. In our study, the BMI increase was lower in those who became pregnant after bariatric surgery. Cornthwaite et al. [23] also compared pregnancy outcomes after different bariatric methods in a national study and reported that bariatric surgery may cause cesarean complications during pregnancy. However, there is no figure or evidence for this, it is an estimate. We did not encounter such a complication in our study. Narayanan and Syed [24] recommended that complications such as hashish erosion, cholelithiasis, internal hernias and small bowl obstruction should be monitored in bariatric surgery methods performed for weight loss before pregnancy. However, we did not encounter these complications in our study. Joly et al. [25] reported that although the mean age of pregnancy after sleeve was lower, the rate of preterm birth was lower than that of bypass. However, in our study, there was no difference regarding preterm. The gestational ages of the mothers showed a significant difference, but since the pregnancies of the same mothers before and after LSG were in question; this difference was expected to be significant.

Pregnancy is a crucial period for expectant mothers, encompassing not only biological and hormonal factors but also psychological processes. The changes in mothers' body image during this period are particularly significant for women [26–28]. Therefore, the fear of morbid obesity following LSG may have diminished [29, 30]. Reaching an ideal weight after LSG may also contribute to an increased desire to conceive.

Congenital anomaly types and pregnancy intervals post-surgery is important to decide pregnancy. In literature, congenital anomalies often cause women of fertile age to have concerns about pregnancy and birth and to have long pregnancy intervals [31–33]. However, in our study, number of pregnant women with congenital anomalies was only four patients, and all of them decided to have a child after operation. In this perspective, it may be argued that LSG may reduce congenital anomaly fear and reduce pregnancy intervals.

Rottenstreich et al. [34] reported that 43.5% of patients conceived within the first 18 months, whereas 56.5% had after 18 months. In another study, Rottenstreich et al. [35] reported that median time for conception after surgery was 508 days with 372 to 954 days. Xu et al. [36] reported that conception interval decreases after LSG. In our study, while the pregnancy rate in the first 12 months after LSG generally increased from 6.1% to 16.6%, it can be argued that concerns about weight decreased after LSG, indicating that the decision to become pregnant was made. In our results, higher post-LSG birth weights were found. The possible reasons may be improved glycemic control, longer time to conception, or improved placental function. Since the research is retrospective, it is suggested to conduct further researches on these factors.

Pregnancy outside of marriage is generally uncommon in Turkish society. In our sample, all patients were married. Further prospective studies can be conducted to examine the effects of nonmarital pregnancy status on outcomes.

The study was conducted at a university hospital, and to avoid bias, missing values ​​or inconsistent data were removed. Furthermore, a significant number of LSG and deliveries occur in private hospitals. Given this, further studies involving public and private sector patients can be conducted to further generalize the findings.

The findings obtained in the study are consistent with the level of compatibility with biological mechanisms in the relationship between obesity, weight, and pregnancy. However, because this is a retrospective and multidisciplinary study, more mechanism parameters are needed.

Limitations of the study

The most important limitation of the study is that since our study is based on patient files and retrospective, maternal age progression, inter-pregnancy interval, nutritional supplementation, or socioeconomic factors were not routinely gathered for patients. These are important limitations of the study, and must be evaluated at multivariable levels in future research.

Another limitation of the study is that The type, severity, and diagnosis criteria of the anomalies and their relations with nutritional deficiencies (folate, B12, iron) or other maternal factors are important to understand effects of LSG on pregnancy. However, the data is limited in retrospectively manner, and further prospective research may be conducted on these parameters.

Another important limitation of the study is the difficulties in tracking data for the patient group. Since health services are provided in both the private and public sectors, tracking may create problems in the health system. In the study, there were pregnancy information for 59 women out of 494 fertile women. However, these rates may increase even more with missing data.

Contributions of the research to literature and clinical practice

The most important contribution of the study to the literature is that it constitutes a resource for further studies and applications, since there are not enough studies comparing birth rates before and after LSG. The fact that a retrospective longitudinal study method was used in the study also reveals the contribution of the study to the literature in terms of method.

The most important contribution of the study to clinical practice is that it draws attention to the need for congenital anomaly checks in the follow-up of women of fertile age who are considering or remaining pregnant after LSG. In addition, the results of the study may also be guiding in the clinical follow-up of weight gain during pregnancy.

Conclusion

According to the results obtained in the study, pregnancy rates in pregnant women after LSG were over 10%. Birth weight, BMI during pregnancy and congenital anomalies differed before and after LSG. Although the number of pregnant women with congenital anomalies was only four patients, it was proportionally above the level of statistical significance. Therefore, further studies are needed on the relationship between congenital anomalies and LSG.

In order to have healthier pregnancies after obesity, more comparative, longitudinal and multicenter studies are needed on pregnancy and birth outcomes before and after obesity. In addition, surgical operations and cesarean section complications after pregnancy with LSG history are among the important issues that need to be addressed.

Daptomycin pharmacokinetics in critically ill patients receiving extracorporeal membrane oxygenation: A pilot cohort study
Authors: Haofei Wang, Changde Wu, Xiwen Zhang, Jie He, Linlin Hu, Qing Li, Jianfeng Xie, Wei Huang, Yingzi Huang
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

Extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) has become an essential rescue therapy for patients with refractory respiratory or circulatory failure. However, the profound physiological disturbances associated with ECMO—including capillary leak, altered protein binding, fluid shifts, and potential drug sequestration within extracorporeal circuits—may substantially modify antimicrobial pharmacokinetics. These alterations raise concern for subtherapeutic exposure and treatment failure in a population already at high risk of nosocomial infection. Despite the frequent use of daptomycin for severe Gram-positive infections in intensive care units, data defining its pharmacokinetic behavior during ECMO remain limited [1].

We performed a prospective observational cohort study in a tertiary intensive care unit (ICU) to characterize daptomycin pharmacokinetics in critically ill adults receiving ECMO compared with those managed without extracorporeal support. Patients aged 18–80 years who received intravenous daptomycin for suspected or confirmed Gram-positive infections were eligible. Steady-state plasma concentrations were obtained at predefined sampling points and quantified using liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry. Pharmacokinetic parameters were estimated using noncompartmental analysis, with particular focus on exposure metrics relevant to concentration-dependent bacterial killing. The elimination rate constant (Ke) was estimated from the terminal slope, and the elimination half-life was computed as 0.693/Ke. Total clearance (CL) was calculated as dose/AUC0–∞, and the apparent volume of distribution (Vd) as CL/Ke.

Forty-eight patients were enrolled, including 28 supported with ECMO. Baseline illness severity scores were comparable between groups, although ECMO patients required more vasoactive support and exhibited higher short-term mortality, consistent with greater hemodynamic instability. Median administered daptomycin doses did not differ significantly, allowing meaningful pharmacokinetic comparison (Supplementary Table S1).

ECMO support was associated with significantly lower trough concentrations (Cmin 16.1±8.0 vs. 22.0±11.0 µg/mL, P=0.04), reduced apparent Vd, and shorter elimination half-life. In contrast, CL and AUC0-24 were preserved, suggesting that ECMO primarily alters distribution kinetics rather than systemic elimination. Given that daptomycin efficacy correlates with the AUC/MIC ratio, maintaining adequate exposure is critical for therapeutic success (Supplementary Table 2).

A strong linear relationship between Cmin and AUC0-24 was observed, indicating that trough concentration may serve as a practical surrogate for exposure monitoring when full pharmacokinetic profiling is infeasible. This finding is particularly relevant in resource-constrained ICU environments where frequent sampling is challenging. Notably, ECMO duration emerged as an independent determinant of target exposure attainment (OR=0.88, P=0.034). Each additional day of extracorporeal support reduced the likelihood of achieving pharmacodynamic targets, highlighting a dynamic interaction between treatment duration and antimicrobial disposition. From a clinical perspective, this suggests that pharmacokinetic reassessment should not be a one-time intervention but rather an adaptive process during prolonged ECMO runs (Supplementary Table 3,4).

Using integrated efficacy and safety thresholds, we identified a therapeutic Cmin window of 4.66–24.3 µg/mL, balancing adequate AUC/MIC attainment against the risk of creatine kinase elevation (Figure 1)[2,3]. Importantly, creatine kinase levels did not demonstrate a clinically significant upward trend during therapy (δCK), and no patients required treatment discontinuation or dose adjustment due to CK elevation. The observed reduction in trough concentrations contrasts with the traditional expectation of ECMO-associated volume expansion. One plausible explanation is that many patients were sampled during a negative fluid balance phase, reflecting transition toward recovery from early capillary leak. Additionally, improved organ perfusion during ECMO may partially offset circuit-related drug adsorption, potentially contributing to preserved systemic elimination. This observation is consistent with prior pharmacokinetic evidence suggesting that ECMO does not uniformly increase antimicrobial clearance in adults[4]. Nevertheless, given the substantial interpatient variability observed during extracorporeal support, our findings should be interpreted cautiously and support the role of therapeutic drug monitoring to ensure adequate exposure.

Our findings contribute to the growing body of evidence that antimicrobial dosing strategies derived from non-ECMO populations may be insufficient for patients receiving extracorporeal support. Recent population pharmacokinetic models similarly suggest that higher daptomycin doses may be required to achieve pharmacodynamic targets, particularly in patients with preserved renal function. Together, these data support a shift toward individualized dosing guided by therapeutic drug monitoring rather than reliance on fixed regimens.

Interestingly, pharmacokinetic parameters were not associated with ICU mortality. Outcomes during ECMO are multifactorial and likely driven more strongly by underlying disease severity and hemodynamic failure than by antimicrobial exposure once minimum therapeutic thresholds are achieved. Nevertheless, preventing underexposure remains essential to avoid secondary deterioration related to uncontrolled infection[5].

Several limitations warrant consideration. The single-center design and modest sample size may limit external validity. Only total plasma concentrations were measured, precluding evaluation of free drug exposure despite the high protein binding of daptomycin. Microbiological confirmation was limited, and simulated MIC values were used for pharmacodynamic analyses. However, the prospective design and real-world ICU conditions enhance the translational relevance of our observations.

In conclusion, ECMO significantly modifies daptomycin pharmacokinetics, predominantly through reductions in trough concentration while preserving overall exposure. ECMO duration independently predicts pharmacodynamic target attainment, underscoring the need for repeated therapeutic drug monitoring during prolonged support. A Cmin-guided strategy targeting 4.66–24.3 µg/mL may provide a pragmatic framework for optimizing daptomycin therapy in this high-risk population.

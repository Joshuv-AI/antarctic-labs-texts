Improvement of survival in advanced gastric cancer over 15 years: real-world analysis by 5-year periods
Authors: K. Kato, K. Sugiyama, T. Ito, M. Yaguchi, S. Owaki, M. Sato, R. Ino, K. Shiraishi, Y. Uno, M. Hattori, M. Suenaga, M. Murayama, M. Shimada, Y. Kodera, C. Kitagawa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAlthough immunotherapy and molecular targeted agents have been introduced for advanced gastric cancer (AGC), it remains unclear whether these therapeutic advances have translated into improved survival outcomes in real-world settings.Patients and methodsWe conducted a retrospective cohort study of patients with advanced gastric or oesophagogastric junction adenocarcinoma who received first-line chemotherapy. Patients were stratified into three chronological periods: A (2009-2014), B (2014-2019), and C (2019-2024). Overall survival (OS) was the primary endpoint. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards models were used to evaluate independent prognostic factors.ResultsOf the 677 screened patients, 322 were eligible. The baseline characteristics were generally comparable across periods, although the median age was slightly higher in period C than in periods A and B. The use of immune checkpoint inhibitors and molecular targeted agents increased substantially in period C. The median OS sequentially improved: 9.2, 10.7, and 13.6 months in A, B, and C, respectively. Subgroup analyses showed no significant improvement in the OS of patients with peritoneal metastasis. Patients with performance status (PS) 1-2 achieved significantly improved OS, whereas those with PS 0 showed only a modest trend. Multivariable analysis confirmed that a later treatment period was an independent favourable factor, with a significant benefit in C versus A.ConclusionsSurvival outcomes of patients with AGC improved over time in real-world settings, consistent with the trends observed in clinical trials. However, the lack of improvement in patients with peritoneal metastasis highlights a critical unmet need for this subgroup.

Introduction

Although the prognosis of advanced gastric cancer (AGC) remains poor, recent clinical trials incorporating molecular targeted agents, such as anti-human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) antibodies,1, 2, 3 zolbetuximab (anti-CLDN18.2 antibody),4,5 and anti-vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 2 antibodies6,7 and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs)8,9 have demonstrated better overall survival (OS) compared with cytotoxic chemotherapy alone.

These therapeutic advances have led to the widespread adoption of combination regimens, including molecular targeted therapies and immunotherapies, in routine clinical practice.

However, patients treated in real-world settings often substantially differ from those enrolled in clinical trials. In daily practice, patients with AGC tend to be older and frequently have comorbidities, resulting in a more heterogeneous population than that typically represented in pivotal trials. Real-world analyses and registry-based studies have consistently shown that patients treated outside clinical trials are older and have poorer baseline characteristics than the trial populations.10, 11, 12 Consequently, the extent to which the survival benefits observed in clinical trials have been successfully translated into improved outcomes for unselected patients outside trial settings remains unclear. Although previous real-world analyses have reported gradual improvements in survival over time,11, 12, 13 detailed contemporary data describing how these survival gains have translated into routine practice across recent therapeutic eras remain limited. In particular, it remains clinically important to determine whether such improvement has been observed uniformly across patient subgroups and to identify populations in whom outcomes remain poor despite recent therapeutic advances.

Therefore, we conducted a retrospective chart review of patients with AGC who received first-line chemotherapy to evaluate changes in survival outcomes and treatment patterns across different therapeutic eras in real-world settings.

Methods

Patients and study design

This retrospective study included patients with unresectable or recurrent gastric adenocarcinoma, including adenocarcinoma of the oesophagogastric junction, who received first-line chemotherapy for advanced disease at the NHO Nagoya Medical Center between September 2009 and December 2024. Patients who had previously received adjuvant chemotherapy were eligible if recurrence occurred after the completion of adjuvant treatment, whereas those who relapsed during adjuvant therapy or within 6 months after adjuvant therapy were excluded. Eligible patients had histologically or cytologically confirmed adenocarcinoma and an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status (PS) of 0-2.

The exclusion criteria were as follows:•Patients who received best supportive care alone.•Those transferred to other hospitals or treated only for emergency care.•Patients with synchronous malignancies.•Patients experiencing recurrence during adjuvant chemotherapy or within 6 months after completion.•Patients with non-adenocarcinoma histology, poor PS (≥3), or insufficient clinical data.

The patients were classified into three treatment periods according to the year of first-line chemotherapy initiation: periods A (2009-2014), B (2014-2019), and C (2019-2024).

The study period was divided into consecutive 5-year intervals to allow a simple and uniform comparison of treatment patterns and survival outcomes over time, without defining eras based on the introduction of individual agents.

This study was approved by the Committee for Clinical Review (Institutional Review Board) of the NHO Nagoya Medical Center (approval date: 4 September 2025; approval number: 2025-022).

Endpoints

The primary endpoint was OS, which was defined as the time from the initiation of first-line chemotherapy to death from any cause. The secondary endpoint was time to treatment failure (TTF), defined as the time from the initiation of first-line therapy to discontinuation of treatment for any reason, including disease progression, adverse events, or patient decision. Survival data were obtained from medical records, and the cutoff date for follow-up was 31 December 2024.

Statistical analyses

OS and TTF were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and differences among treatment periods were compared using the log-rank test. Median follow-up was estimated using the reverse Kaplan–Meier method. To assess the potential impact of unequal follow-up duration across treatment periods, we carried out a sensitivity analysis in which OS was administratively censored at 24 months from treatment initiation.

Multivariable analyses were carried out using the Cox proportional hazards model to identify the independent prognostic factors. The model included the treatment period (A, B, and C) and clinically relevant variables selected based on univariate significance and clinical relevance, including ECOG PS, histological type, prior gastrectomy, HER2 status, presence of peritoneal metastasis, and presence of ascites.

All statistical tests were two-sided, and statistical significance was set at P values <0.05. Statistical analyses were carried out using EZR version 1.56 (Saitama Medical Center, Jichi Medical University, Japan), a graphical interface for R (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Patient characteristics

Among the 677 patients who received first-line chemotherapy for unresectable or recurrent gastric adenocarcinoma during the study period, 322 met the eligibility criteria and were included in the analysis. The distribution of patients across the periods was as follows: period A (2009-2014), n = 131; period B (2014-2019), n = 86; and period C (2019-2024), n = 105 (Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100348).

The median age was 69 (range, 26-92) years, which significantly increased across the three treatment periods, with patients in period C being older than those in period A. Approximately half of the patients had an ECOG PS of 0, and the remainder had a PS of 1-2. Peritoneal metastasis was present in ∼48% of the patients. The proportions of differentiated (45%) and undifferentiated (55%) histologies were comparable across the three periods. OS and TTF

The median OS period for the entire cohort was 11.3 months [95% confidence interval (CI) 9.8-12.8]. The median follow-up duration differed substantially across treatment periods and was 7.8 years in period A, 4.7 years in period B, and 2.0 years in period C, indicating markedly shorter follow-up in the most recent period.

The median OS sequentially improved from 9.2 months (95% CI 7.9-12.2) in period A to 10.7 months (95% CI 8.7-13.8) in period B and 13.6 months (95% CI 11.1-16.8) in period C, with a significant difference across periods (log-rank P = 0.016; Figure 1A). In the sensitivity analysis with administrative censoring at 24 months, the overall trend toward improved survival remained generally consistent with that observed in the primary analysis, with period C continuing to show significantly better survival than period A (Supplementary Figure S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100348).Figure 1(A) OS according to treatment period. Subgroup analyses

According to ECOG PS

In patients with PS 0, OS showed a trend toward improvement across periods but did not reach statistical significance (Figure 2A). In contrast, patients with PS 1-2 demonstrated a significant improvement in OS, with period C showing a markedly longer survival than period A (P = 0.007; Figure 2B). According to peritoneal metastasis status

Among patients without peritoneal metastases, OS substantially improved over time, with both periods B and C showing significantly longer survival than period A (Figure 2C). In contrast, among patients with peritoneal metastasis, no statistically significant improvement in OS was observed, although a modest numerical prolongation was observed in period C (Figure 2D). The absence of survival improvement in peritoneal metastasis cases highlights a subgroup with a persistently poor prognosis and unmet therapeutic needs.

Discussion

In this retrospective analysis, we demonstrated a significant improvement in OS for patients with AGC over 15 years, with median OS period increasing from 9.2 months in period A to 13.6 months in period C. Importantly, this chronological improvement was consistent with trends observed in clinical trials and prior real-world studies, supporting the idea that recent therapeutic advances have translated into improved outcomes in routine practice.

Previous real-world studies have reported gradual changes in treatment patterns and survival outcomes over time.11, 12, 13 Although our findings are generally consistent with these reports, the present study extends prior evidence by including more recent treatment eras characterised by the introduction of ICIs in first-line settings and the subsequent expansion of effective systemic treatment options. By capturing this broader therapeutic evolution, our analysis provides updated real-world insights into the contemporary survival trends in AGC.

Several factors may have contributed to the observed improvement in survival. The increasing availability of effective systemic therapies, including trastuzumab for HER2-positive disease1 and ramucirumab in later-line settings,6,7 has expanded the treatment options over the study period. More recently, first-line chemoimmunotherapy supported by large phase III trials, including the CheckMate-649 and KEYNOTE-859 trials,8,9 has reshaped the standard treatment strategies for AGC. In later-line settings, the introduction of trastuzumab deruxtecan further expanded the therapeutic options for HER2-positive disease, providing clear survival benefits in heavily pretreated patients.2,3 Notably, the improvement in OS was more pronounced than that in TTF across treatment periods, suggesting that prolonged survival may have been influenced not only by first-line efficacy but also by the increased availability and use of effective subsequent therapies. In our cohort, the uptake of these therapies increased over time, accompanied by higher rates of subsequent treatment, which may have contributed to prolonged survival across periods. However, because of the retrospective observational design, causality cannot be established, and other factors such as changes in patient selection, improvements in supportive care, and advances in diagnostic evaluation may also have contributed. Nonetheless, the use of these newer agents remained limited in the present cohort, particularly zolbetuximab, which was administered to only a small number of patients. Consequently, the impact of the recently introduced targeted therapies on survival outcomes could not be fully evaluated in this analysis. With the broader adoption of these agents in routine practice, further improvements in outcomes may be expected, especially in biomarker-defined subgroups, such as HER2-positive or CLDN18.2-positive disease.

A notable finding of this study was the improvement in survival among patients with an ECOG PS of 1-2. Although patients with PS 1 are commonly included in clinical trials, those with PS 2 are often underrepresented. The observed improvement in survival in this broader PS population suggests that therapeutic progress may benefit patients beyond strictly trial-eligible cohorts. In addition to advances in systemic therapy, increased attention to supportive and nutritional care in routine clinical practice may have helped initiate and continue treatment in patients with poor functional status. Clinical guidelines and previous studies have suggested that nutritional support may influence treatment outcomes in patients with AGC receiving chemotherapy.14, 15, 16, 17 Notably, the median patient age increased over time in the present cohort, but the survival outcomes improved, suggesting that recent therapeutic advances and improved supportive care may have reduced the negative impact of an ageing patient population.

In contrast, although a modest numerical improvement was observed among patients with peritoneal metastasis, this trend did not reach statistical significance and outcomes remained unsatisfactory, underscoring a persistent unmet clinical need in this subgroup. Peritoneal dissemination is a well-established adverse prognostic factor for AGC,18, 19, 20, 21 and effective systemic treatment options remain limited. Although several clinical trials have evaluated systemic chemotherapy in patients with peritoneal dissemination, no substantial improvement in survival has been achieved. Factors such as impaired nutritional status, reduced drug delivery to the peritoneal cavity, and difficulty in maintaining treatment intensity are likely to contribute to these persistently poor outcomes.

Taken together, these findings underscore the substantial unmet medical needs of patients with peritoneal metastasis. Given the inherent limitations of systemic therapy alone in this population, approaches directed specifically toward peritoneal diseases are being actively explored. These may include the integration of modern systemic therapies, such as molecular targeted agents, with peritoneal-directed treatment. Peritoneal-directed strategies, including intraperitoneal chemotherapy and other multimodal approaches, have been evaluated in some trials and may represent promising avenues for improving outcomes in this challenging subgroup.22, 23, 24 Although no significant improvement in survival was observed in our cohort, the modest numerical improvement observed in the most recent period suggests that evolving treatment strategies may provide additional benefits.

This study has some limitations. First, this was a single-centre retrospective study with a relatively small sample size, and treatment selection may have been influenced by physician discretion, institutional practice patterns, and selection bias. Second, the classification into consecutive 5-year periods was chosen as a uniform and pragmatic framework to evaluate long-term temporal trends in real-world practice. Because OS in AGC reflects not only first-line treatment but also subsequent-line therapies, supportive care, and changes in diagnostic practice, defining treatment eras solely according to individual therapeutic milestones may also be imperfect. Nevertheless, the 5-year classification did not fully correspond to the introduction of specific agents, and each period may have included heterogeneous treatment practices. In addition, follow-up duration differed substantially across treatment periods, particularly in the most recent era, in which the median follow-up was only 2.0 years. Therefore, the survival comparisons should be interpreted with caution, especially for longer-term outcomes. To address this issue, we carried out a sensitivity analysis with administrative censoring at 24 months, and the overall trend remained generally consistent with that of the primary analysis. However, residual bias related to unequal follow-up duration cannot be fully excluded. Nevertheless, this study provides contemporary real-world evidence demonstrating that the prompt adoption of the latest evidence-based treatments in routine clinical practice is associated with improved survival outcomes in AGC, while also highlighting patient subgroups for whom further therapeutic innovation remains necessary.

Conclusion

The survival outcomes for AGC have steadily improved over the past 15 years in a community-based setting, reflecting the timely adoption of effective therapies and increased use of subsequent treatments. Survival gains were extended even in patients with poor PS. However, survival improvement in patients with peritoneal metastases remains limited, highlighting a persistent unmet clinical need. The development of novel strategies that specifically target peritoneal dissemination is warranted to further improve outcomes in this challenging subgroup.

Prevalence of allergic diseases and evaluation of total IgE and blood eosinophils as diagnostic biomarkers in Colombia, a tropical country☆
Authors: Jorge Sánchez, Elizabeth García, Claudia-Ximena Asalea-Pinzon, Sara Garcia-Chica, Luis Caraballo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe prevalence of allergic conditions and inflammatory T2 diseases has been widely studied in developed countries but less explored in developing countries located in the tropics. Total IgE (tIgE) and blood eosinophils count (BEC) are frequently used for assessing type 2 (T2) inflammation. However, their diagnostic performance information is limited in populations where different non-allergic factors could affect the interpretation of these biomarkers.ObjectiveTo determine the prevalence of asthma, rhinitis, conjunctivitis, rhinosinusitis, and atopic dermatitis, in Colombian population over 18 years old and to evaluate the diagnostic performance of tIgE and BEC.MethodsCross-sectional national-based study in 6 Colombian cities. The diagnostic performance of the cut-off points for tIgE (100 IU/mL) and BEC (150 cells/uL) suggested by some international guidelines was evaluated with a comparison of allergic group versus non-allergic group.ResultsA total of 4248 individuals participated; 1465 (34.4%) had an allergic condition (asthma 13.5%, rhinitis 32.2%, rhinosinusitis 6.3%, conjunctivitis 25.9%, and atopic dermatitis 7.2%). For tIgE, 40.7% of the non-allergic group and 85.9% of the allergic group were over 100 IU/mL (Sensitivity 86%, specificity 59%). For BEC, 43.9% in the non-allergic group and 79.9% in the allergic group were over 150 cells/uL (Sensitivity 79%, specificity 56%).ConclusionAllergic conditions affect around 30% of the population in Colombia with some differences according with the prevalence of each disease between cities. The cut-off points used traditionally for tIgE and BEC had moderate diagnostic performance to detect allergic diseases in tropical population.Keywords: Allergy, Atopy, Asthma, Atopic dermatitis, Biomarkers, Colombia, Conjunctivitis, Eosinophils, Immunoglobulin E, Tropics, Rhinitis, Rhinosinusitis

Introduction

Several studies, especially in countries from Europe, Asia, and North America, have been conducted to evaluate the prevalence of allergies.1, 2, 3 In developing countries, these types of studies are scarce, especially among those located in the tropical region of Latin American (LATAM). However, while in developed countries the prevalence has stabilized in the last 20 years, in developing countries it seems that the trend is still increasing.4, 5, 6 Environmental conditions such as house dust mite perennial exposure7 could be related with these different epidemiological trends.

Chronic conditions like asthma, rhinitis, conjunctivitis, rhinosinusitis, and atopic dermatitis, have in common type 2 (T2) inflammation characterized by cytokines (IL) IL4, IL5, and IL138, 9, 10. The measurement of these molecules in clinical practice is difficult and indirect biomarkers like blood eosinophil count (BEC) and total IgE (tIgE) are used to identify T2 inflammation and supporting allergy diagnosis. In asthma, some guidelines recommend these biomarkers to define asthma as “eosinophilic” (BEC ≥150 cells/uL) or “allergic” (tIgE ≥100 IU/mL) (https://ginasthma.org). With the same cut-off points, several studies also propose that tIgE is useful for classifying rhinitis, and atopic dermatitis as “allergic” or “non allergic”.11,12 The use of these biomarkers is currently extended to other diseases such as urticaria, rhinosinusitis, and allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis13, 14, 15. Therefore, these biomarkers are frequently used for supporting clinical decisions in T2 inflammatory diseases. Most studies with these biomarkers come from countries located in temperate region,16,17 but its diagnostic performance is unknown in populations where different factors could affect their levels (eg, endemic parasitosis, genetic ancestry).18, 19, 20, 21 Some case-control studies carried out in tropical areas indicates that a high proportion of non-allergic subjects could have levels of tIgE and BEC over the cut-off suggested in clinical international guidelines.19,20,22, 23, 24

Colombia is a LATAM country located in the tropical region and shares geographic, climatic, cultural, and sociodemographic similarities with other countries in the area. In Colombia previous national studies on allergic conditions can serve as reference points to evaluate changes in the prevalence of allergies in this region.22,25 However, these studies did not evaluate the usefulness of tIgE and BEC as diagnostic biomarkers for Th2 phenotype. Therefore, we decided to carry out a multicenter study in different cities located in Colombia, to stablish the current prevalence of different T2 inflammatory conditions in this country and evaluate the usefulness of tIgE and BEC as potential biomarkers according to cut-off values proposed by some scientific organizations.

Materials and Methods

Study design

Cross-sectional national-based study in 6 Colombian cities: Barranquilla, Bogotá, Cali, Cartagena, Manizales, and Medellín. These cities in Colombia are representative of different sociodemographic regions. This study is part of the GREAT initiative (Global Research Environment, Allergy and Autoimmunity in Tropics) from the Group of Clinical and Experimental Allergy of the University of Antioquia (Medellín, Colombia).

We included population over 18 years old. Exclusion criteria were population with skin or respiratory conditions diagnosis by a doctor that could confuse the diagnosis of allergic conditions: Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, scabies, ichthyosis, immunodeficiencies, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, scabies, ichthyosis, pneumonia, thoracic surgery, heart failure, idiopathic pruritus. If the patient had another respiratory and/or skin disease that the interviewer could not classify, the interviewer could consult to the study committee. In case of atopic dermatitis, when the questions suggested a diagnosis, but it was not found in the medical record, the participant was asked, with prior authorization, to send photos that were reviewed by the study committee.

Also excluded were subjects with limitations to freely accept participate in the study (eg, people with an altered mental state for any condition). The study aims presented in this article were:

1.To determine the prevalence of asthma, rhinitis, conjunctivitis, rhinosinusitis, and atopic dermatitis conditions in Colombia.

To determine the prevalence of asthma, rhinitis, conjunctivitis, rhinosinusitis, and atopic dermatitis conditions in Colombia.

2.To establish the levels and of tIgE and BEC in general population and their diagnostic performance compared with current cut-off points recommended in international literature for tIgE (100 IU/mL) and BEC (150 cells/mL) to distinguishing allergic and/or type 2 inflammation.

To establish the levels and of tIgE and BEC in general population and their diagnostic performance compared with current cut-off points recommended in international literature for tIgE (100 IU/mL) and BEC (150 cells/mL) to distinguishing allergic and/or type 2 inflammation.

According to national statistics (https://www.dane.gov.co/), Cali, Cartagena, and Barranquilla share average annual temperatures above 30°Celsius (22 C–35 C) and the frequency of the population with African ancestry was over 25% (28% Cali, 32% Barranquilla, and 45% Cartagena). Bogotá, Manizales, and Medellín, have an average annual temperature under 30°Celsius (8 C–26 C), and the frequency of African ancestry was under 20% (2% Bogotá, 10% Manizales, and 12% Medellín). Inhabitants with low economic income according to national index were between 66% (Manizales) to 82% (Cartagena), and monetary poverty was between 24% (Manizales) to 45% (Cartagena) (https://www.dane.gov.co/). During the study period (2023–2024) the 6 cities have access to treated water over 97% of population (Bogotá, 99%, Bucaramanga 98%, Barranquilla 99%, Calí 98%, Cartagena 99%, Medellín 95%), and most of them was urban population (Bogotá 99%, Bucaramanga 98%, Barranquilla 99%, Calí 98%, Cartagena 89%, Medellín 98%). Sewage treatment was more heterogeneous (Bogotá, 80%, Bucaramanga 40%, Barranquilla 35%, Calí 80%, Cartagena 30%, Medellín 80%) (https://www.superservicios.gov.co/).

Sample size and sampling strategy

Colombia has a population of 50 million people according to national projection from 2023 (https://www.dane.gov.co/). The estimated prevalence for allergic diseases (30%) was based in a previous study in Colombia.22 Based in the Colombia population projection, a type I error (α) of 5%, and a type II error (β) of 1% was defined for the calculation of sample size (supplemental material “sample size”). We estimated that 3235 subjects could give precision over 95% for nationwide estimates. 50 million was used as the reference universe for calculating the initial national sample size, assuming a finite population. However, to ensure the validity of local estimates, the design was stratified.

The sample design was not limited to a fixed population size of 50 million but rather employed probabilistic and stratified sampling by city. To ensure representativeness in the 6 urban centers included, independent strata were defined based on DANE's population projections for each city. For data analysis, specific expansion factors (weights) were calculated for each of these 6 cities. This procedure adjusts the weight of each individual observation so that the sample accurately reflects the demographic pyramid and population size of each selected city, minimizing the standard error in local estimates and avoiding the bias of treating the national population as a homogeneous unit.

Colombia's National Health System (NHS) covers 98% of the population and is administered by institutions called “Health Promoting Entities (HPE)”. Sampling was carried out among patients affiliated with 3 of the largest HPE that cover around 19 million people throughout the country (38% of total population). A probability proportional to size sampling was done based in the number of individuals by city and economic strata. Invitations were sent randomly. Random selection was performed by drawing random numbers, where each number included was the national identification number of each person in the participating HPEs. All participants were contacted by telephone supplied by their HPEs. If they agreed to participate in the study, they were informed of the health center where they should go to sign the informed consent, take the blood sample and complete the survey.

All participants were invited to complete a survey under the assistance of a trained interviewer. All participants underwent a complete blood count and total IgE measurement. The survey assessed sociodemographic and disease-specific aspects: the diagnosis of asthma (https://ginasthma.org/), chronic rhinitis,26 chronic rhinosinusitis,27 chronic conjunctivitis,28 and atopic dermatitis29 was based on internationally validated criteria. Medical records of each participant were collected. We reviewed the clinical records of all participants who agree to participated; first the questionnaire was administered, and subsequently, medical records were reviewed. The chart review was based in 2 predefined variables: diagnosis based in CIE-10 codification and therapeutic treatment prescription. We did not evaluate whether the medical evaluation was “correct” or not, only the diagnostic codes used and the medical prescription. The extraction was done by 2 health data analysts. They compared the information and, in case of discrepancy, jointly evaluated the information and the medical record on a case-by-case basis. Interpretation of the results based in the survey and the medical records is explained in Supplemental material.

Statistical analysis

All prevalences presented are sex- and age-adjusted estimates. Categorical variables were presented as percentages. For continuous variables means, medians, minimum (min), maximum (max), standard deviation (SD), and interquartile range were reported according to variable distribution. Participants were classified according to whether they had an allergic disease or not, into the “allergic group” and the “non-allergic group” based in self-report and clinical history. To evaluate the diagnostic performance of tIgE and BEC, we calculated the sensitivity, specificity and predictive values (positive and negative). To measure the diagnostic performance parameters, we performed a dichotomized analysis (2 × 2 table) according to allergic status (allergic group, non-allergic group) and for each biomarker using BEC as a cut-off point of 150 cells/mL and tIgE 100 IU/mL; to select the most appropriate cut-off point in the study population, we used the Youden index (J = sensitivity + specificity – 1), based on the AUC.

More than 90% of patients with asthma or atopic dermatitis had rhinitis (Supplemental Fig. 2). A total of 96% of patients with conjunctivitis had rhinitis and this represent the 36% of rhinitis patients. In rhinosinusitis patients 25.8% had asthma; and 12.1% of asthma patients had rhinosinusitis.

Levels of total IgE and blood eosinophil count

The mean tIgE levels in the allergic group was 285 IU/mL (SD 194) and in the non-allergic group was 113 IU/mL (SD 84) (Fig. 1a–b). A total of 40.7% of people in the non-allergic group and 85.9% in the allergic group presented tIgE values above 100 IU/mL (Fig. 1c–d); regarding patients over the cut-off value 88% had diagnosis of atopic dermatitis, 85.9% rhinitis, 83% conjunctivitis, 80% asthma, and 60% had rhinosinusitis. Levels of tIgE were similar between the non-allergic groups from the 6 cities, but in the allergic group Cali, Barranquilla, and Cartagena presented the highest levels (Fig. 1a).

The BEC mean in the allergic population was 233 cells/uL (SD 116) versus 145 cells/uL (SD 75) in the non-allergic population (Fig. 2a–b). There was a weak correlation between IgE and BEC levels in the allergic group (Supplemental Fig. 2). A total of 30.8% of the population had both tIgE ≥100 IU/mL and BEC ≥150 cells/uL; 20.5% in the non-allergic group and 42.1% in the allergic group (Supplemental Fig. 3).

Diagnostic performance of tIgE and BEC

For tIgE the traditional cut-off (100 UI/mL) had a high sensitivity (86%) but low specificity (59%) to the outcome, identify correctly patients with any allergic conditions (Fig. 3a); the result was similar when evaluated specifically for each disease (Supplemental Table 1). The area under the curve (AUC) suggested that for tIgE the best balance between sensitivity and 1-specificity (false negatives) was 136 UI/mL according to Youden index; using this point, the sensitivity was 80% and the specificity was 70%.

For BEC the 150 cells/mL cut-off had 79% of sensitivity and 56% of specificity to identify correctly patients with allergic conditions (Fig. 3b); The result was similar when each disease was evaluated by separate (Supplemental Material Table 2). The AUC suggested that for BEC the best point was 175 cells/mL according to Youden index; using this point, the sensitivity was 75% and the specificity was 71%. For both tIgE and BEC the positive predictive value (PPV) was less than 60%.

Discussion

This national study describes the prevalence as well as the clinical characteristics of some allergic conditions in Colombia, improving the understanding of the public health burden due to these conditions in LATAM. Compared with similar previous studies in Colombia, our study suggests that the prevalence of symptoms of asthma, atopic dermatitis, and rhinitis is increasing in adult population in this country. This tendency is like that found in ISAAC studies in LATAM.4,30, 31, 32 Nevertheless, different methodological strategies limit comparisons.

According to the information available in the National Administrative Department of Statistics (DANE by its English acronym) (https://www.dane.gov.co/) the age and sex distribution of Colombia adult population (Nationally and/or by city) is similar to what we presented in the study. Additionally, the data presented in the manuscript was adjusted according to sociodemographic characteristics in each city.

More specifically, the current prevalence of asthma (13.6%) suggests an increased in Colombia with respect to results obtained on 2010 in adult population (9.6%);22 Nevertheless, these 2 studies have methodological differences that do not allow an accurate comparison. We observed that 68% of asthmatic patients were not receiving controller therapy, 45% had no asthma control, and 36% had no previous diagnosis of asthma, which highlights the under-diagnosis of asthma and the need to carry out public health campaigns to promote early diagnosis and proper management of the disease.

The prevalence of rhinitis symptoms in adult population also has increased with respect to the 2010 prevalence study (from 26.3% to 32.2%).22 Similar to asthma methodological differences make it necessary to be cautious in comparisons. Previous rhinitis medical diagnosis was lower than asthma's, perhaps because they are commonly associated with less severity even though numerous studies have demonstrated the association of rhinitis with extra-nasal complications.1 The increase in the prevalence of asthma and rhinitis may be due to various factors such as air pollution, or the post-pandemic effects of COVID19, among many others. Unfortunately, we were unable to evaluate these factors, so further research is needed with this objective.

The prevalence of atopic dermatitis had an apparent decrease (from 11.5% to 7.2%). Although several factors can explain this change, we believe that the different methodologies used in the studies plays an important role. It has been shown that the ISAAC questionnaire for asthma and rhinitis presented a high concordance with other asthma questionnaires,33 but in the case of atopic dermatitis, the ISAAC seems to overestimate the frequency of this disease since 7 of the 8 atopic dermatitis questions are focused in pruritus, which can be caused by many other diseases.34 Atopic dermatitis was the condition most frequently diagnosed previously by a physician, possibly due to the high impact on quality of life that this disease causes even in mild cases. A total of 40% of patients had poor control of the disease but the use of systemic therapies was low (6.8%), possibly due to the doctor's fear of using immunosuppressants or difficulties in access.35, 36, 37

In Colombia, there are no previous references to evaluate changes in prevalence over time of chronic conjunctivitis. Although conjunctivitis was evaluated in the pediatric population in the ISAAC study, there is little information on the LATAM prevalence of this disease and rhinosinusitis. We observed that conjunctivitis was the most frequent comorbidity of rhinitis, but only 25% of patients had a previous medical diagnosis. Interestingly, we observed that around 50% received some treatment, suggesting a high frequency of self-prescription, which could also explain the low frequency observed in terms of disease control.

To our knowledge, this is the first study evaluating the national prevalence of rhinosinusitis in a LATAM country. Similar to what has been reported in studies conducted in the United States and Europe, the prevalence of rhinosinusitis was 6.3%. Even though 54% had a SNOT22 over 40 points, none of the patients received systemic therapy and only 7% had previous surgery, which would indicate therapeutic access problems or that despite the high impact on quality of life, most patients do not present severe forms of rhinosinusitis. Fifteen (5.5%) rhinosinusitis patients reported nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID)-exacerbated respiratory disease (NERD).

T2 inflammation is associated with multiple diseases.38, 39, 40 In clinical practice, some T2 inflammation biomarkers are used to evaluate the allergic background of different conditions. Although tests such as specific IgE and exhaled oxygen fraction have been shown to have high and moderate diagnostic accuracy respectively, their use is usually restricted to specialists.9,41 Total IgE and blood eosinophils are easily accessible biomarkers used as diagnostic support or for predicting clinical outcomes in rhinitis, asthma, atopic dermatitis, and rhinosinusitis. Although the cut-off point of 100 IU/mL for tIgE and 150 cells/uL for BEC are popularly used in various studies and in clinical practice, their clinical accuracy is controversial: in a multicentric study from 122 accredited European laboratories providing tIgE analysis in adults, the upper normal reference limit varied from 81 to 150 UI/mL16 and for 21% of laboratories was higher.16 Some studies conducted on Asian population between 6 and 19 years old indicated that the optimal diagnostic point for atopy in the population with rhinitis was between 54.6 and 77.7 UI/mL, with specificity and sensitivity over 80%,42,43 but other studies in adult Chinese population suggest that this point should be over 200 IU/mL.44 These results suggest that “normal” values of tIgE could vary according to sociodemographic and environmental conditions. To define the most appropriate diagnostic point for a biomarker, it is necessary to know its distribution in the general population;45 if the chosen point is below to the value found in the majority of the healthy population, the sensitivity of the test will increase but its specificity will decrease; on the contrary, if the point is very high, the sensitivity of the test will be affected.

For blood eosinophil counts, less information is available than for tIgE, but it seems that BEC also present wide variation among populations. In a comprehensive review17 including studies with at least 3000 participants found that in Austria, Japan, China, Denmark, United States, and Turkey, the median BEC was from 102 to 200 Eos/uL and the 75th percentile ranged from 168 to 250. These results suggest that even in subtropical populations, a significant number of people have eosinophil counts above the “normal” 150 Eo cells/uL.

On 2020, the EPOS guideline recommended using BEC 250 cells/uL point to differentiate rhinosinusitis with and without eosinophilia because this point offered the best sensitivity (70.9%) and specificity (78.4%).27,46 In the 2023 update this guideline suggested using BEC 150 cells/uL to identify patients’ candidates for biological therapy.14 This indicates that depending on the outcome, the suggested cut-off point may change.

More than 2 thousand million people live in tropical regions. Some studies suggest that factors such as living in areas endemic for helminth parasites, African ancestry, and high exposure to perennial allergens such as house dust mites (HDM) may influence the levels of tIgE and BEC47 and all these conditions converge in LATAM tropical countries.19 Case-control studies in Colombia suggest that tIgE levels in the general population ranges from 127 to 330 IU/mL,22 in Mexican pediatric population the mean was 148 IU/mL,48 and in Brazil 389 IU/mL.18,47,49 Between 34% and 53% of the non-allergic population in these studies exceeds the tIgE values of 100 IU/mL, so the diagnostic specificity of the test using this point could be low. There is limited information in LATAM regarding BEC levels in a healthy population; Campuzano et al50 after evaluating 153,485 blood tests, stablished that the 95th percentile of the population has levels between 100 and 750 cells/mL. Low access to basic sanitation services and healthcare infrastructure could explain high levels of IgE and BEC secondary to parasites exposition; nevertheless, in our population, despite some services like access to treated water and urban population were similar, the 6 cities presented different but in general high levels of both biomarkers, indicating that other factors, for example genetic ancestry could be important in this population to predispose to high total IgE and eosinophils independent of parasite exposure. Healthcare infrastructure could also influence these high levels; among the 6 cities there was differences in the sewage treatment, nevertheless. Cali presented one of the highest concentrations of these 2 biomarkers, but more than 80% of households had sewage systems, which again supports that other factors besides the response to parasites influence these results, and these is also supported from previous publications in this population indicating that helminths can induce high tIgE and eosinophils but other factor influence these levels in Colombia population51, 52, 53. Nevertheless, we did not evaluate directly the presence of parasite infections and other non-allergic exposures that potentially leading to high levels of these biomarkers and limited their use for identifying allergic diseases.

We found that allergic patients had higher levels of tIgE and BEC than non-allergic subjects, nevertheless, more than 40% of the non-allergic population had levels of tIgE and BEC above the traditional cut-off points, lowering their diagnostic specificity. These cut-off points have also been used for the selection of biological therapies in asthma (https://ginasthma.org/2024-report/) and chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps.14 However, Sánchez et al24 reported that in a Colombian population, more than 80% of severe asthma patients had both tIgE and BEC over traditional points, so these biomarkers are not useful for the selection between biological therapies in a tropical region.24

In our study, diagnostic accuracy of these biomarkers varied according to the allergic condition showing limited utility in clinical practice, even using different cut-off; Although sensitivity was greater than 80% for asthma, rhinitis and atopic dermatitis, only in the case of asthma specificity was greater than 60%. Based in AUC, the best balance between sensitivity and specificity was 136 UI/mL for tIgE and 175 cells/mL for BEC. Total IgE levels over cut-off in asthma subjects was 80%. This is higher than the frequency obtained in the national study in 2010 (53%) but similar to previous study published in 2004 (76%).22,25 This difference may be due to environmental changes20 and to selection of different cities between the studies.22,25 The levels of both biomarkers showed variations between cities specially in allergic population. Barranquilla, Cali, and Cartagena had higher levels of tIgE and BEC. This cities had some points in common: the 3 are near or at sea level; African–American ancestry population is higher than in other cities;54 and environmental conditions favor the growth of HDM such as Dermatophagoides spp., and Blomia tropicalis.19

Our study has some strengths and weaknesses. It was based on a population random sampling, which reduces the risk of selection bias. Additionally, we define diagnosis of allergic conditions through a two-step assessment (survey and clinical history), which allows for greater precision and reduce misdiagnosis. While the study is robust for the 6 major cities mentioned, the estimates are not intended to be extrapolated to for example, rural areas. Nevertheless, because resources limitations we did not evaluate specific IgE sensitization necessary to confirm the immunological component and therefore, the definition of “allergic group” includes patients with asthma, rhinitis, and dermatitis who are both sensitized and non-sensitized. Although the study's interviewers were trained to correctly identify the diseases in the study and had access to consult a physician when there were doubts, they were not trained to perform or interpret spirometry, tomography, nasofibrolaryngoscopy, physical evaluation necessary to perform scales such EASI or SCORAD, or to identify ocular changes compatible with keratoconjunctivitis, all of which would strengthen the diagnostic process of these type 2 inflammatory diseases.

In conclusion, according with this study, allergic conditions affect around 30% of the population in Colombia with some differences according with the prevalence of each disease between cities. It is necessary to establish regional cut-off points that allow improvement in the diagnostic performance of tIgE and BEC. We think that 136 UI/mL and 175 cells/mL could be appropriate for tropical countries like Colombia. In addition, the use of other biomarkers such as specific IgE and the exhaled fraction of nitric oxide should be considered.

Desloratadine as the selective 5HT2AR antagonist suppresses neutrophil extracellular traps formation to ameliorate diabetic peripheral neuropathy pathology
Authors: Dayun Lu, Yuan Ruan, Fan Zhou, Yan Ma, Jieru Chang, Yuxi Wei, Rui Xu, Jiaying Wang, Xu Shen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) is a common diabetic complication with a obscure and complicated pathogenesis. Here, we determined that 5-hydroxytryptamine receptor 2 A (5HT2AR) is selectively and pathologically elevated in the sciatic nerve (SN) tissues of late-stage DPN patients and the SN/dorsal root ganglion (DRG) tissues of late-stage DPN model mice (both type 1 and 2 diabetic mice) compared with the other 13 subtypes of 5HTR, and desloratadine (DLT) as a discovered selective antagonist of 5HT2AR ameliorated the DPN-like pathology in mice. To elucidate the underlying mechanisms, proteomic analysis was performed against the SN tissues of DPN mice and verified by assays against both the SN tissues from DPN patients and the DPN mice with specific 5HT2AR knockdown in the SN and DRG tissues by injection of the adeno-associated virus php.s-5HT2AR-RNAi. We determined the activation of the neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) that is tightly linked to the vascular dysfunction and neuroinflammation in DPN pathology. NETs regulated vascular dysfunction through NE/TF/HIF-1α/MMP-9/2 pathway, and DLT-mediated 5HT2AR inhibition suppressed NETs activation through PKC/NADPH/ROS pathway. Collectively, our findings revealed that 5HT2AR associates with NETs activation in DPN pathology and supported that pharmacological inhibition of 5HT2AR shows promising as a therapeutic strategy for DPN and DLT exhibits high potential in treating the disease.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12974-025-03581-6.

Introduction

Diabetes has reached epidemic proportions globally, and diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) is a severe diabetic complication with the primary risk for diabetic foot ulcer (DFU), afflicting over two-thirds of the diabetic patients [1]. The current therapeutic strategies for DPN are just limited to alleviating the clinical symptoms or slowing the DPN progression due to the obscure and complicated pathogenesis of this disease [2]. Pathologically, DPN involves irreversible nerve damages caused by axonal degeneration, demyelination and neuronal injury [3], and is exacerbated by multiple risk factors including vascular damage and neuroinflammation [4]. Microangiopathy induces insufficient blood supply to nerve tissues thus damaging the normal functions of the nerves [5]. Additionally, neuroinflammation also plays a crucial role in the onset and progression of DPN [1]. Moreover, neuroinflammation is highly implicated in the destruction of the microvascular supply to peripheral nerves thereby exacerbating DPN pathology [6].

Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) are reticular ultrastructures released by activated neutrophils into the extracellular space, where they play a potent role in innate and adaptive immunity [7]. High level of glucose in cells, mice or patients has been determined to stimulate NETs formation through activation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) [8]. Studies demonstrated that neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) promoted thrombosis, resulting in microcirculation disorders [9]. This microcirculation disorder is closely associated with vascular dysfunction during the pathological progression of diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN). Currently, no reports have been published regarding the NETs-related pathological events in DPN research.

5-hydroxytryptamine (5-HT, serotonin) is a monoamine neurotransmitter derived from the hydroxylation and decarboxylation of tryptophan [10]. 5HT2A receptor (5HT2AR) as a member of the 14 subtypes of 5-HT receptors (5HTRs) is distributed in many kinds of cells and tissues performing varied biological functions [11]. For example, 5HT2AR regulates vasoconstriction in arterial smooth muscle cells [12]. Notably, 5HT2AR is broadly expressed in both the central nervous system (CNS) and peripheral nervous system (PNS) [13], and the peripheral or spinal 5HT2AR is associated with neuropathy [14]. Pathophysiologically, 5HT2AR in the PNS is tightly linked to inflammatory pain [15, 16]. The references to prior studies demonstrating selective 5HT2AR overexpression in analogous neuropathic models, but this has not been observed in DPN [17–19].

According to the published report, the 5HT2AR activation in the peripheral nervous system (PNS) exacerbates neuroinflammation and neuronal hyperexcitability through PKCγ/ERK1/2 pathway, which thus makes us position the role of 5HT2AR in the pathogenesis of DPN [20]. Interestingly, it was reported that 5HT2AR regulates ROS release that is the leading cause for NETs formation [21], which reminds us of the potential of 5HT2AR in regulation of NETs activation in response to high level of glucose in DPN pathology. Thus, encouraged by the aforementioned facts and analyses, we have recently explored the regulation of 5HT2AR against NETs in DPN pathology with the discovered selective 5HT2AR antagonist desloratadine (DLT) [22] as a probe.

In the current work, we determined that 5HT2AR is selectively and pathologically upregulated in both the late-stage DPN patients and model mice compared with the other 5HTR subtypes, and DLT as the discovered selective 5HT2AR antagonist effectively ameliorated the DPN-like pathology in mice. The underlying mechanisms have been intensively investigated through the proteomic-based analysis and verified by assays against the DPN patients and the model mice with specific 5HT2AR knockdown. The results have revealed that the hyperglycemia-induced NETs are associated with the vascular dysfunction and neuroinflammation in DPN pathology, and DLT suppressed NETs formation to ameliorate DPN pathology. Our work reported the association of 5HT2AR with NETs formation in DPN pathology thus strongly supporting that selective inhibition of 5HT2AR shows promising as a therapeutic strategy for DPN, while highly addressing the potential of DLT in treating this disease.

Methods

Clinical specimens of Late-stage DPN patients

Based on the clinical symptoms and signs, DPN can be generally classified into the early and late-stage DPN [23]. In the late-stage DPN, there is a severe sensory loss, leading to a significantly increased risk of foot ulcers and infections [24–26].

Here, we focused on the study on the late-stage DPN pathology in diabetic patients. The clinical samples (including SN tissues and serum) of the late-stage DPN patients (DPN patients clinically with DFU symtoms, hereafter called DPN patients for short if not specified) and normal inidvidules were collected at the First Affiliated Hospital of Naval Medical University in China following the approval from the hospital’s Research Ethics Committee (No. CHEC2023-088, approved on April 6th, 2023), and the written informed consents were obtained from all patients.

The experimental tissues and serum from the DPN patients and normal inidvidules were sourced from the amputated lower limbs and peripheral blood, in accordance with the postoperative surveillance and treatment protocols as outlined in the clinical consensus guidelines.

Animals

Here, we focused on the study on the pathology of the late-stage DPN (hereinafter called DPN for short, if not specified) model mice (STZ, db/db) [24], and the information of the animal models construction and behavior assays are described in the Supplementary Materials (Supplemental Methods 1.2–1.3).

Other methods

Additional detailed methods including behavior assays, primary neutrophil isolation, primary DRG neuron isolation, primary human schwann cells (HSCs) isolation, in vivo NETs detection, NETs quantification in blood vessels, overexpression plasmid and si-RNA transfection, quantitative Real-time PCR, immunofluorescence assay, Western blot assay, total neurite outgrowth quantification, regional blood flow velocity and perfusion area assessments, masson staining, protein extraction and digestion, proteomics, and others are described in the supplementary materials and methods.

For each WB/ELISA target, pairwise effect sizes (Cohen’s d) were computed from double-normalized values (proteinactin, then normalized to the control mean). With n = 3 biological replicates per group (a = 0.05), large effects (dl > 2) achieved high power (> 0.8).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis of the proteomic data was performed using R software. A Student’s t-test was employed to calculate p-values, and proteins with p-values < 0.05 and fold change (FC) > 1.2 or < 1/1.2 were considered significantly differentially expressed. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses for differentially expressed proteins were conducted using the DAVID website (https://david.ncifcrf.gov/).

All remaining datasets were analyzed using GraphPad Prism version 8.0 and presented as mean ± SEM. For two-group comparisons, an unpaired 2-tailed Student’s t-test was used. For comparisons involving three or more groups, one-way ANOVA with Dunnett’s post-hoc test and two-way ANOVA with Bonferroni post-hoc test were applied, while the p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

5HT2 AR Expression Level is Selectively and Pathologically Elevated in SN Tissues of DPN Patients and SN/DRG Tissues of DPN Mice

By considering that there are 14 subtypes in 5HTR family (The subtype 5HT1ER is not expressed in mice [27]), we at first determined which 5HT receptor among these subtypes is the most affected in DPN pathology. qRT-PCR (Fig. 1A for human samples, Fig. 1D & Figure S1A-C for mice samples), western blot (Fig. 1B-C for human samples, Fig. 1E-F & Figure S1D-I for mice samples) and immunohistochemical (5HT2AR protein was labeled, Fig. 1G-H for human samples, Fig. 1I-J & Figure S1J-K for mice samples) assays were carried out against the SN tissues of DPN patients and the SN/DRG tissues of DPN mice with the corresponding control groups. The results indicated that both the mRNA and protein levels of 5HT2AR were selectively and pathologically elevated in the SN tissues of DPN patients and the SN/DRG tissues of DPN mice (STZ, db/db).

DLT targets 5HT2AR to ameliorate DPN-like pathology in mice

Given the above-mentioned facts, we proposed that 5HT2AR might play a potent role in DPN pathology and pharmacological antagonism of 5HT2AR may ameliorate the DPN pathology. With this proposal, 5HT2AR selective antagonist DLT (Fig. 1K) that was previously reported in our lab. In our previously published reports [28], the direct inhibitory activity of DLT against 5HT2AR was verified by a cellular thermal shift and calcium flow assays. The results in the cellular thermal shift assay indicated that 5HT2AR protein in DLT-treated cell lysates is more stable than in DMSO-treated cell lysates, and DLT had no effects on the denaturation temperature of the control protein GAPDH. The calcium flow assay results showed that DLT suppresses Ca2+ flow and si-5HT2AR can deprive DLT of its effect. These results demonstrated that DLT binds to and inhibits 5HT2AR. Molecular docking results (AutoDock Vina 1.1.2) showed the molecular binding affinity towards the protein target is quantified as − 9.2 kcal/mol, demonstrating robust binding efficacy. The primary intermolecular interactions comprise ‌hydrogen bonding‌ and ‌hydrophobic interactions‌, specifically hydrogen bonding with LEU-228 and hydrophobic contacts with LEU-229, VAL-235, LEU-362, PHE-339, and ILE-152 (Figure S1L).

DLT was thus applied to investigate its potential amelioration on the DPN-like pathology in mice by detecting the clinical DPN indicators including neurological dysfunction, intraepidermal nerve fiber (IENF) loss and myelin sheath structural damage [25, 26, 29]. Shown in Figure S2A-B were the schedules for the animal experiments.

DLT ameliorates neurological dysfunction in DPN mice

The results demonstrated that DPN mice (STZ, db/db) exhibited reduced 50% paw withdrawal threshold (Fig. 2A and Figure S2C), prolonged thermal response latency (Fig. 2B and Figure S2D), delayed motor nerve conduction velocity (MNCV) (Fig. 2C and Figure S2E) and slowed sensory nerve conduction velocity (SNCV) (Fig. 2D and Figure S2F), compared to the control mice (Ctrl, db/m). To our expect, DLT treatment (10/20 mg/kg/day) ameliorated all aforementioned neurological dysfunctions [30, 31] in DPN mice (Fig. 2A-D and Figure S2C-F).

DLT improves IENF density damage in DPN mice

Given that protein gene product 9.5 (PGP 9.5) is a neuronal cytoplasmic protein and widely expressed in epidermal and dermal nerve fibers [30, 31], we assessed IENF density using PGP 9.5 as a marker in DPN mice. As shown in Fig. 2E-F and Figure S2G-H, the IENF density (with the baseline 10/mm) was reduced in DPN mice compared to control mice, and DLT treatment increased IENF density in DPN mice, indicative of the DLT-mitigated IENF loss.

DLT improves myelin sheath injury in DPN mice

We also examined the effects of DLT treatment on myelin sheath damage in the SN using immunofluorescence staining assay against myelin basic protein (MBP), a key protein component of myelin [32]. The results indicated that DLT treatment increased myelin sheath thickness in DPN mice (Fig. 2G-H and Figure S2I-J).

Additionally, it was also found that DLT treatment rendered no effects on the blood glucose level or body weight in DPN mice (Figure S2K-N), demonstrating that the beneficial effects of DLT on neurological dysfunctions, IENF density damage and myelin sheath injury were independent of glucose suppression, and DLT treatment had no impacts on the normal dietary intake of DPN mice. Furthermore, some behavioral assessments related to the functions of the central nervous system (CNS) were conducted, including conditioned place preference (CPP), open field test, and rotarod test. The results demonstrated that DLT (20 mg/kg) failed to induce analogous CNS adverse effects (Figure S2V-X).

DLT ameliorates DPN-like pathology in mice by targeting 5HT2 AR

The timeline for the assays were shown in Figure S2O. The results verified the significant reduction of 5HT2AR expression in the SN/DRG tissues of DPN mice (STZ, Figure S2P-S). It was found that the AAV-si5HT2AR-induced 5HT2AR knockdown ameliorated the neurological dysfunction (Fig. 2I-L), IENF density (Fig. 2M-N) and myelin sheath thickness (Fig. 2O-P) in DPN mice. Notably, DLT treatment (20 mg/kg/day) lost its amelioration on neurological dysfunction, IENF density or myelin sheath thickness (Fig. 2I-P) in the DPN mice injected with AAV-si5HT2AR. Similarly, AAV injection had no influences on the blood glucose level or body weight (Figure S2T-U) in mice.

In summary, all results suggested that DLT ameliorated DPN-like pathology in mice by targeting 5HT2AR.

Proteomics analysis reveals the implication of NETs activation in DPN pathology

In the exploration of the key proteins and pathways involved in the DPN pathology, proteomics-based analysis was performed against the SN tissues of DPN mice and control mice (Fig. 3A). High quality proteomic data and stable instrument operating conditions through data quality control highlighted the suitability of the data for analysis (Figure S3A-D & F-I).

Using thresholds of fold change (FC) > 1.2 (or FC < 1/1.2) and p value < 0.05, we identified the differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) between the STZ and Ctrl groups, in that 241 proteins were upregulated and 301 proteins were downregulated in STZ group (Fig. 3B). Similarly, 292 proteins were upregulated and 146 proteins were downregulated in db/db mice compared to those in db/m groups (Fig. 3E).

To further investigate the key proteins and pathways related to DPN pathology, we performed the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis and Gene Ontology Biological Process (GOBP) analysis of the DEPs. KEGG analysis showed NETs formation, leukocyte transendothelial migration and protein processing in the endoplasmic reticulum in both STZ and db/db mice (excluding the metabolism-related proteins) (Fig. 3C and F). GOBP analysis showed that the DEPs were primarily enriched in the pathology of DPN mice related to vascular dysfunction and inflammation compared to those of control mice (Figure S3E and J). These findings thus suggested that NETs formation may function potently in the regulation of the vascular dysfunction and inflammation in DPN pathology.

Myeloperoxidase (MPO), a neutrophil enzyme involved in NETs formation, was used in combination with citrullinated histone H3 to assess NETs [33], and here the proteomics analysis result indicated that MPO was upregulated in DPN mice (Fig. 3D and G), indicative of the NETs formation in DPN mice. To verify the involvement of NETs in DPN pathology, we combined the KEGG database with our proteomics results to perform the targeted analysis against the proteins and found that the NETs-related proteins were all upregulated in DPN mice including PKC, C3, MPO, HMGB1, and CAMP (Fig. 3H).

Together, our data revealed the implication of NETs formation in DPN pathology.

DLT targetes 5HT2AR to alleviate NETs formation in DPN patients and model mice

As shown in Fig. 3C and F, KEGG analysis showed NETs formation in both STZ and db/db mice. Thus, we proposed that NETs formation might play a potent role in DPN pathology and pharmacological antagonism of 5HT2AR may ameliorate the DPN pathology by improving the NETs formation.

Next, the related assays were also performed against the 5HT2AR-knockdown DPN mice in the SN and DRG tissues. As indicated in Fig. 4I-P and Figure S4M-N, AAV-si5HT2AR injection deprived DLT of its capability in alleviating NETs formation in DPN mice.

NETs are activated in DPN patients

Moreover, to further investigate the NETs activation in DPN pathology, the expression levels of NETs markers were determined in the serum and SN tissues in DPN patients. ELISA results showed that NETs markers were upregulated in the serum (Figure S4O-Q) and SN tissues (Fig. 4Q-S) in DPN patients, and western blot results demonstrated that the marker proteins CitH3 and neutrophil elastase (NE) were both upregulated in the SN tissues of DPN patients (Fig. 4T-U). In addition, immunofluorescence of Ly6G assay [34] also indicated that neutrophil was aggregated in the SN tissues (Fig. 4V-W) in DPN patients.

All results thus suggested the NETs activation in DPN pathology.

DLT suppresses NETs formation through 5HT2AR/PKC/NADPH/ROS pathway

To explore the mechanism underlying the suppression of DLT on NETs activation in DPN pathology, related assays were carried out in the primary neutrophils from the clinical blood samples of DPN patients (DPN) with those of normal individuals (Ctrl) as a control.

DLT inhibits NETs through targeting 5HT 2A R not H1 receptor in DPN pathogenesis

Given that DLT is also reported to be an antagonist of H1 receptor [35], we investigated whether DLT inhibits NETs through targeting H1 receptor in DPN pathogenesis. Western blot (Figure S4R-S) results demonstrated that DLT (10 µM) inhibited the levels of 5HT2AR, H1 receptors and NETs, while the selective H1 receptor antagonist diphenhydramine (DIP, 10 µM) [35] suppressed H1 receptor (H1R) level but had no impacts on 5HT2AR or NETs in high glucose (HG)-treated primary neutrophils. Importantly, DLT could alleviate the levels of 5HT2AR and NETs in the DIP-treated primary neutrophils (with HG). All results indicated that DLT inhibits NETs through targeting 5HT2AR not H1 receptor in DPN pathogenesis.

NETs are activated in the primary neutrophils from DPN patients

ELISA and western blot results indicated that the levels of NE (Fig. 5A), cf-DNA (Figure S5A) and CitH3 (Figure S5B) in cell supernatant and the protein levels of NE and CitH3 (Figure S5C) in primary neutrophils from DPN patients were all upregulated compared to Ctrl groups, indicative of the NETs activation.

NADPH/ROS pathway is involved in NETs formation

It was reported that many advanced glycation end products (AGEs) are produced with activation of their receptors (RAGEs) in diabetes-related pathology [36], and binding of AGEs to RAGEs leads to an increase in NADPH with generation of a large amount of ROS that is the direct cause of NETs activation [37]. Here, our results also revealed the upregulated levels of RAGE/MAPK/phosphorylated NF-κB (western bolt, Fig. 5B-C), NADPH (ELISA, Fig. 5D) and ROS (ELISA, Fig. 5E) in the primary neutrophils from DPN patients, which indicated the activation of the AGEs-RAGE pathway [38]. All results thus implied that NADPH/ROS pathway is responsible for NETs formation in DPN patients.

DLT targets 5 HT2 AR to repress NETs formations

To investigate whether DLT repressed the NETs formation by targeting 5HT2AR, we cultured the primary neutrophils from DPN patients in hyperglycemic medium followed by treatment with or without DLT. As indicated in Fig. 5F and Figure S5D-F, the markers of NETs were all elevated and DLT treatment antagonized such elevations in the high glucose (HG)-treated primary neutrophils.

Notably, 5HT2AR overexpression (Figure S5M) activated NETs formation in HG-treated primary neutrophils, as indicated by the upregulated NETs markers (Fig. 5G & Figure S5G-H), while DLT as a selective antagonist of 5HT2AR alleviated the NETs formation. Moreover, si-5HT2AR-induced 5HT2AR knockdown (Figure S5N) reduced NETs formation in the HG-treated primary neutrophils, and DLT had no impacts on the NETs formation in the HG-mediated primary neutrophils treated with si-5HT2AR (Fig. 5G & Figure S5G-H).

‌Interstingly, western blot and Elisa results demonstrated that 5HT2AR overexpression upregulated the levels of PAD4 and MPO, while 5HT2AR knockdown or DLT treatment antagonized these upregulations in the HG-treated primary neutrophils (Figure S5I-J). These results are tentatively ascribed to the alterations in reactive oxygen species (ROS) associated with 5HT2AR, aligning with the reported result that NETs formation was regulated by ROS/PAD4/CitH3 pathway [39]. It was noted that overexpression or knockdown of PAD4 had no impacts on 5HT2AR level (Figure S5K). Moreover, RNA sequencing data from SN tissues in DPN mice were also retrieved from the GEO database (GSE222776) and analyzed using bioinformatics pipeline (Figure S5L). The results revealed an absence of co-expression patterns among 5HT2AR, PAD4, and MPO, as determined by Spearman’s rank correlation (p >0.05).

All results thus indicated that DLT inhibits NETs formation by targeting 5HT2AR, while PAD4 is not involved in this mechanism through crosstalk.

DLT targets 5 HT 2A R to repress NETs formation through PKC/NADPH/ROS pathway

5HT2AR was reported to regulate NADPH expression via PKC to affect ROS [40, 41], and the phosphorylation levels of different subtypes of PKC (PKCα/β/δ) may be correlated to PKC activation [42]. Here, we determined that the levels of 5HT2AR/PKC/NADPH/ROS are all elevated in HG-treated primary neutrophils and DLT antagonized such elevation (Fig. 5H-K). 5HT2AR overexpression caused the increased levels of 5HT2AR, p-PKC (The phosphorylation level of PKCα/β/δ), NADPH and ROS, and 5HT2AR knockdown both repressed the PKC/NADPH/ROS signaling and deprived DLT of its capability in inhibiting such signaling (Fig. 5L-O).

Moreover, ROS inhibitor N-acetylcysteine (NAC) was also applied for assay in verifing the requirement of ROS for NETs formation in HG-treated primary neutrophils [43]. As shown in Figure S5O-R, NAC treatment reduced the levels of NE, cf-DNA and CitH3 in the supernatant, and CitH3 and NE in HG-treated neutrophils, indicative of the repression of NETs formation. Notably, NAC treatment inhibited ROS but had no impacts on NADPH expression, while deprived DLT of its inhibitory activity against NETs formation as indicated by the levels of NETs markers (Figure S5S-T).

Similarly, the results demonstrated that the levels of 5HT2AR/PKC/NADPH/ROS were all elevated in the SN tissues of DPN mice and DLT treatment reduced such levels and DLT had no impacts on the activation of PKC (The phosphorylation level of PKCα/β/δ), NADPH or ROS in the AAV-si5HT2AR-treated DPN mice (Fig. 5P-W & Figure S5U-W).

Thus, all results demonstrated that DLT targeted 5HT2AR to suppress NETs formation through PKC/NADPH/ROS pathway in in HG-treated primary neutrophils and DPN mice.

5HT2 AR regulats NETs activation involving PKC/NADPH/ROS pathway in DPN patients

Next, to further investigate the mediation of 5HT2AR on NETs formation, western blot and ELISA assays were performed against the SN tissues in DPN patients with normal individuals as control. Western blot (Fig. 5X-Y) and ELISA (Fig. 5Z) results demonstrated that the levels of 5HT2AR and NADPH, and the phosphorylation levels of PKCα/PKCβ/PKCδ were all upregulated in DPN patients. The ROS level was also elevated in the HSCs isolated from SN tissues of DPN patients compared with that in the normal individules (Figure S5X).

All results thus suggested that 5HT2AR regulated NETs activation involving PKC/NADPH/ROS pathway in SN tissues of DPN patients.

DLT represses NETs formation within blood vessels by targeting 5HT 2A R

After marking blood vessels with CD31 [46], we observed that the NETs formation within blood vessels was inhibited by DLT as indicated by the repression of the key NETs marker NE (Fig. 6A-B & Figure S6A-B). However, DLT had no impacts on NETs formation within blood vessels in the AAV-si5HT2AR-treated DPN mice (Fig. 6C-D).

DLT targets 5HT 2A R to improve peripheral vascular dysfunctions in DPN mice through inhibition of NE/TF/HIF-1α/MMP-9/2 pathway

To investigate the regulation of NETs against thrombus, the blood flow velocity/blood perfusion area in the SN tissues/foot pads of DPN mice and Masson staining assay was performed [42]. The results indicated that DPN mice (STZ, db/db) exhibited thrombus as indicated by the reduced levels of the blood flow velocity and blood perfusion areas, and DLT treatment improved such a peripheral vascular dysfunction (Fig. 6E-G & Figure S6C-I).

As indicated in the published reports [47, 48], the NETs-bound NE cleaves the tissue factor pathway inhibitors (TFPIs) producing tissue factor (TF) to activate hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF-1α) and matrix metalloproteinase (MMP)−9/2, thus initiating the thrombosis within blood vessels of the nerve tissues. Here, the protein levels of TF/HIF-1α/MMP-9/2 were all elevated in the SN tissues of DPN mice and DLT treatment inhibited such protein levels elevation (Fig. 6H-I & Figure S6J-K).

Notably, DLT had no impacts on the blood flow velocity or the blood perfusion areas in AAV-si5HT2AR treated DPN mice (Fig. 6J-L). Similarly, DLT treatment rendered no influnces on the protein levels of TF, HIF-1α or MMP-9/2 in the AAV-si5HT2AR-treated DPN mice (Fig. 6M-N).

Together, all results demonstrated that DLT targeted 5HT2AR to improve peripheral vascular dysfunctions in DPN mice through inhibition of NE/TF/HIF-1α/MMP-9/2 pathway.

NE/TF/HIF-1α/MMP-9/2 pathway is for NETs formation in regulation of vascular dysfunction in SN tissues of DPN patients

To further investigate the mediation of NETs formation on the regulation of vascular dysfunction, the protein levels of TF/HIF-1α/MMP-9/2 were determined. The results showed that the protein levels of them were all elevated in the SN tissues of DPN patients (Fig. 6O-P). Immunofluorescence staining result revealed that NETs was activated within blood vessels in the SN tissues of DPN patients (Fig. 6Q-R).

Taken together, our results suggested that the NE/TF/HIF-1α/MMP-9/2 pathway is involved in NET-associated regulation of vascular dysfunction in DPN patients.

Discussion

DPN is a seriously diabetic complication that is a leading cause for DFU, placing patients at a high risk of amputation and death [52]. Discovery of effective drugs against DPN is full of challenges due to the limited understanding of the complicated pathogenesis of DPN. In the early-stage DPN, high glucose primarily impairs neurite outgrowth by altering cytoskeletal and signaling pathways [53]. In the late-stage DPN, the prolonged metabolic stress leads to increased apoptosis of neurons and Schwann cells further exacerbating neuropathy [54]. Therefore, therapeutic strategies should focus on both preserving neurite outgrowth (e.g., through neurotrophic factor support and oxidative stress inhibition) and enhancing cell survival enhancement (e.g., via anti-apoptotic and anti-inflammatory therapies) to effectively mitigate DPN.

In the current study, we demonstrated that 5HT2AR is pathologically upregulated in the SN tissues of both DPN patients and diabetic mice. More importantly, we identified NETs as a critical mediator of vascular dysfunction and neuroinflammation in DPN, revealing a novel mechanistic link between 5HT2AR signaling and NETs formation through the PKC/NADPH/ROS pathway. Our findings suggest that pharmacological inhibition of 5HT2AR with DLT effectively suppresses NETs formation and ameliorates DPN pathology. Accumulating evidence highlights the crucial role of oxidative stress in NETosis, particularly through NADPH oxidase-dependent ROS generation [55, 56]. Hyperglycemia has been shown to promote NETs formation by enhancing NADPH oxidase activity [57, 58]. In line with these findings, our study identified 5HT2AR as an upstream regulator of NETs formation, where 5HT2AR activation triggers PKC activation, leading to increased NADPH oxidase activity and ROS production. These results provide mechanistic insights into how 5HT2A R exacerbates oxidative stress and inflammation in DPN.

It was noticed that a dosage of 10 mg/kg of DLT was administered to mice, yielding a human equivalent dose (HED) of 0.813 mg/kg [59]. Based on the standard human reference weight of 60 kg, this HED correponds to a total dose of 48.78 mg. While this calculated dose is higher than the standard clinical treatment dose (5 mg/day) for human, it is anticipated to be within the established clinic safety margin for DLT, since DLT has demonstrated safty and tolerability at nine times the recommended human dose [60].

Our findings aligh with previous reports indicating that NETs contribute to inflammatory and vascular complications in diabetes. However, while previous studies primarily focused on the role of NETs in diabetic retinopathy and nephropathy [61, 62], our study provides the first evidence linking NETs formation to DPN pathology. Moreover, we extend these findings by demonstrating that 5HT2AR-mediated PKC activation acts as a key upstream event driving NETosis in DPN. In contrast to previous studies that identified ROS as a downstream factor in diabetic complications, our study positions 5HT2AR as a critical initiator of NETs formation, thereby providing a novel therapeutic target.

Despite the significant insights gained from this study, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, although we identified the role of 5HT2AR in neutrophil-mediated NETs formation, its function in other immune cell populations and the peripheral nervous system microenvironment remains to be elucidated, such as the anti-inflammatory effects of DLT apart from 5HT2AR antagonism. Second, while our study primarily relied on murine models, which may provide valuable insights into disease mechanisms, their direct translation to human DPN requires further validation through clinical trials. We ‌clarified‌ our action plan and ‌proposed‌ a defined three-phase translational roadmap: PK/PD (Pharmacokinetics/pharmacodynamics) modeling, Biomarker validation cohort studies, and Combination therapy screening. Third, the long-term effects of DLT treatment and its potential off-target effects need to be explored in future studies. To address these limitations, further investigations should employ patient-derived samples, spatiotemporal multi-omics, and advanced in vivo imaging techniques. Given the critical role of 5HT2AR in DPN pathology, future studies should focus on identifying additional downstream effectors and cross-talk mechanisms with other signaling pathways. Additionally, the potential involvement of 5HT2AR in modulating peripheral immune responses and its interactions with other neurotransmitter systems warrants further exploration. Additionally, serotonin may regulate NLRP3 inflammasome assembly, as discovered in peripheral immune cells [63]. However, the exact molecular mechanisms remain unclear, warranting further investigation into whether 5HT2AR directly interacts with TLR4 or NLRP3 components, or whether its effects are mediated through secondary messengers. Understanding these interactions could provide novel insights into the role of serotonin in immune-related disorders, such as depression-associated inflammation or neurodegenerative diseases. Finally, clinical trials are essential to evaluate the therapeutic efficacy of DLT in human DPN patients, which will be crucial for translating these findings into clinical applications.

In conclusion, our study provides novel insights into the role of 5HT2AR in DPN pathology and identifies NETs as a crucial mediator of vascular dysfunction and neuroinflammation. We demonstrate that pharmacological inhibition of 5HT2AR effectively suppresses NETs formation, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target for DPN. As illustrated in Fig. 8H, we determined that NETs regulated vascular dysfunction through NE/TF/HIF-1α/MMP-9/2 pathway and inflammation through NF-κB/NLRP3 pathway, while DLT-mediated 5HT2AR inhibition suppresses NETs via the PKC/NADPH/ROS pathway. Our results strongly support the therapeutic promise of 5HT2AR antagonism for DPN and highlight the potential of DLT in treating this disease. It is expected that our findings might pave the way for future studies to explore 5HT2AR antagonists as viable therapeutic options for DPN treatment.

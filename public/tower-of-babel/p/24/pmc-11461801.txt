Effect of sulforaphane on testicular ischemia-reperfusion injury induced by testicular torsion-detorsion in rats
Authors: Si-Ming Wei, Yu-Min Huang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTesticular ischemia-reperfusion induces enhanced concentration of reactive oxygen species. The increased reactive oxygen species harm cellular lipids, nucleic acids, proteins, and carbohydrates, and ultimately cause testicular injury. Sulforaphane, a kind of natural dietary isothiocyanate, exists predominantly in some cruciferous vegetables, like broccoli and cabbage. It can protect tissues from oxidative stress-induced damage. Herein, we analyzed the effectiveness of sulforaphane in treating ischemia-reperfusion injury occurring after testicular torsion-detorsion. Male rats (n = 60) were grouped as follows: sham-operated group, unilateral testicular ischemia-reperfusion group, and unilateral testicular ischemia-reperfusion group receiving sulforaphane treatment at 5 mg/kg. No testicular torsion-detorsion was performed in the sham group. Unilateral testicular ischemia-reperfusion model was created by detorsion after 2 h of left testicular torsion. In the sulforaphane-treated group, intraperitoneal sulforaphane (5 mg/kg) was administered at left testicular detorsion. Biochemical assay, Western blot, and hematoxylin and eosin staining were used to evaluate testicular malondialdehyde content (an important marker of reactive oxygen species), protein levels of superoxide dismutase and catalase (intracellular antioxidant defense mechanism), and testicular reproductive function, respectively. In testicular tissues, malondialdehyde content was significantly promoted, while protein levels of superoxide dismutase and catalase, and testicular reproductive function were significantly reduced in ipsilateral testes by testicular ischemia-reperfusion. Nevertheless, sulforaphane administration partially reversed the effect of testicular ischemia-reperfusion on these indexes. It can be concluded that sulforaphane elevates protein levels of superoxide dismutase and catalase, and suppresses reactive oxygen species content, thereby preventing ischemia-reperfusion injury in testis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-74756-z.Keywords: Sulforaphane, Testicular ischemia-reperfusion injury, Testicular torsion-detorsion, Malondialdehyde, Superoxide dismutase, Catalase, Testicular reproductive functionSubject terms: Medical research, Urology

Introduction

Testicular torsion, a rotation of the testis along the vertical axis of the spermatic cord, causes a significant reduction in testicular blood flow. Report displays that the incidence of testicular torsion among men younger than 25 years is around 1/40001. If testicular ischemic duration is extended, the probability of testicular necrosis will increase quickly with time. Currently, immediate surgical operation (testicular detorsion) is widely used to recover blood flow to the ischemic testis. Some studies have found that 9-100% of patients with testicular torsion who undergo testicular detorsion and have their viable testes preserved eventually experience postoperative testicular atrophy2–6. Oxidative stress is considered to be the main factor of testicular atrophy7. Testicular ischemia-reperfusion induces enhanced concentration of reactive oxygen species, such as nitric oxide, hydrogen peroxide, superoxide anions, peroxynitrite, and hydroxyl radicals8,9. The increased reactive oxygen species harm cellular lipids, nucleic acids, proteins, and carbohydrates, and ultimately cause testicular injury10.

Currently, no drug is used to improve testicular ischemia-reperfusion injury in human cases. Sulforaphane [1-isothiocyanato-4-(methylsulfinyl) butane], a kind of natural dietary isothiocyanate, exists predominantly in some cruciferous vegetables, like broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cabbage, and cauliflower11. Its molecular formula and molecular weight are C6H11NOS2and 177.29, respectively12,13. Sulforaphane has been demonstrated to exert antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anti-carcinogenic, anti-microbial, anti-depressive, neuroprotective, and anti-diabetic characteristics in numerous diseases14–20. Sulforaphane has been shown to protect heart, brain, liver, lung, kidney, intestine, retina, and lower limb from ischemia-reperfusion injury21–29. However, whether sulforaphane can inhibit ischemia/reperfusion-induced testicular damage is still unknown. Accordingly, we attempted to elucidate the effectiveness of sulforaphane in testicular injury induced by unilateral testicular torsion-detorsion in rats together with the mechanism.

Materials and methods

Experimental rats

The SLAC Laboratory Animal Company (Shanghai, China) provided 60 male Sprague-Dawley rats with weight range of 250–300 g. These animals were housed in plastic cages with 4 rats per cage and allowed to feed liberally on water and standard pellet chow. Stable environment conditions with a period of 12-hour light/dark exposure, a relative humidity of 55% ± 5%, and a controlled temperature of 21℃ ± 1℃ were maintained. The rats were acclimatized to the new surroundings for 7 days before initiating the experimental procedure. The animal experimental protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethical Committee of Experimental Animal in Zhejiang Chinese Medical University (protocol code: 10790). All procedures were done in conformity with the regulations of animal welfare.

Induction of unilateral testicular ischemia-reperfusion and sulforaphane treatment

The male Sprague-Dawley rats were randomly allocated into three groups and each group consisted of twenty rats: sham group, unilateral testicular ischemia-reperfusion group, and sulforaphane-treated group. Before surgical intervention, general anesthesia was achieved by intraperitoneal injection of ketamine at a dose of 50 mg/kg (Sigma Chemical Company, St. Louis, MO, USA). The ilioinguinal and scrotal areas at the left side were shaved and the skin was cleaned with 10% povidone-iodine solution. Rats received a left ilioinguinal incision and then the left testis was brought out of the scrotum. In the sham group, 11/0 non-traumatic absorbable suture was passed through the tunica albuginea. Then the left testis was reintroduced into the scrotum, after which the skin was sutured. In the unilateral testicular ischemia-reperfusion group, the left testis was manually twisted 720 degrees counterclockwise and maintained in the position by suturing the testis to the internal surface of scrotum using 11/0 non-traumatic absorbable suture30. Following 120 min period of testicular torsion and ischemia, the fixation suture was released and the ischemic testis was reperfused by untwisting the testis 720 degrees clockwise to its original normal position (i.e., testicular detorsion)30. After 720° testicular torsion for 120 min, the rats in the sulforaphane-treated group underwent intraperitoneal administration of sulforaphane (5 mg/kg; Sigma Chemical Company) at the onset of testicular detorsion. The dosage of 5 mg/kg sulforaphane was determined considering the previous literature31–37. After 4 h of detorsion, testicular tissue samples of ten rats in each group were harvested for analysis of malondialdehyde concentration and protein expression of superoxide dismutase and catalase. After 3 months of detorsion, testicular tissue samples of the other ten rats in each group were collected for examination of testicular reproductive function.

Estimation of malondialdehyde content in testis

To analyze malondialdehyde content, testicular tissue (approximately 100 mg) was homogenized in 1 ml of chilled malondialdehyde lysis buffer with a tissue homogenizer. The supernatant was separated by centrifuging the homogenate at 5,000 × g at a temperature of 4℃ for 15 min and used for the analysis of malondialdehyde content. The malondialdehyde level was assessed by utilizing the assay kit brought from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (Nanjing, China) according to the method described by Ohkawa et al38.. Expression of the results was as nmol/mg protein.

Western blot analysis

Determination of superoxide dismutase and catalase proteins was performed according to previously described method39. Briefly, testicular tissue was homogenized with a homogenizer in lysis solution (50 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.4, 1 mM phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride, 5 µg/ml aprotinin, 0.5 mM ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid, 2 mM sodium orthovanadate, 0.1% sodium dodecyl sulfate, 150 mM NaCl, 1% nonidet P-40, 0.5% sodium deoxycholate, 1 mM dithiothreitol, and 0.5 µg/ml leupeptin) for 15 min at 4℃. Then, the resulting supernatant was harvested by centrifuging the homogenate (4℃, 14,000 × g, 15 min). The supernatant was used to examine the protein level by the Bradford protein assay kit (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Hercules, CA, USA). Purified protein sample was diluted in sample buffer (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Santa Cruz, CA, USA) and heat-denatured in boiling water for 3 min. Subsequently, the same amount of protein (30 µg per well) was electrotransferred onto a nitrocellulose membrane after electrophoresis on sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel. The membrane was blocked at room temperature with Tris-buffered saline containing 5% (w/v) defatted dried milk powder and 0.1% Tween-20 for 60 min. Thereafter, the membrane was probed for one night with the following primary antibodies at 4℃: anti-copper- and zinc-containing (Cu-Zn) superoxide dismutase (Santa Cruz Biotechnology), anti-catalase (Santa Cruz Biotechnology) and anti-β-actin (Sigma Chemical Company). Next, the membrane was rinsed thrice using Tris-buffered saline containing 0.1% Tween-20, followed by incubation for 1 h with a secondary antibody coupled with horseradish peroxidase (Santa Cruz Biotechnology) at room temperature. Finally, the protein immunoblots on the membrane were identified via an enhanced chemiluminescence Western blotting detection kit (Santa Cruz Biotechnology) according to protocols of the manufacturer. Analysis of the protein blot intensity was performed by the imaging software (Bio-Rad Laboratories). The relative protein levels of Cu-Zn superoxide dismutase and catalase were shown by contrast with the intensity of internal reference β-actin.

Studies of testicular reproductive function

We examined testicular reproductive function by some indexes, such as testicular weight, diameter of the seminiferous tubule, germinal epithelial layer number, and Johnsen’s score40. Briefly, after orchiectomy, testicular weight was measured. Testicular sample reserved for histological examination was subjected to fixation with Bouin’s fluid for 4 h, then washed using tap water for 15 min. Graded alcohol series (80%, 95% and 100%) were used to dehydrate the fixed testicular tissue. After clearing with xylene solution, the tissue sample was taken wax baths at 60℃ for wax infiltration. Subsequently, testicular specimen was buried in paraffin block and five-µm-thick tissue section was prepared with the aid of rotary microtome. The section was put on clean slide, deparaffinized, gradually hydrated, and subjected to hematoxylin-eosin (Sigma Chemical Company) staining. For histological evaluation, twenty round seminiferous tubules per stained section was observed with an optical microscope at magnification (× 200) by the same histologist who was unaware of the group division. The diameter of the seminiferous tubule was determined with the aid of ocular micrometer. The germinal epithelial layer number was assessed through counting the number of germinal cell layer from basement membrane to lumen of tubule. Testicular spermatogenesis was graded according to testicular biopsy scoring method described by Johnsen41. A score of 1–10 was allocated to each tubule according to the degree of spermatogenesis41. A score of 1 expresses the absence of epithelial cells in the seminiferous tubule41. A score of 10 expresses complete spermatogenesis with plentiful mature spermatozoa, germinal epithelium organized in a regular thickness, and an open tubular lumen41.

Statistical analysis

Raw data were analyzed by utilizing GraphPad Prism 4.0 software (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). All the obtained data were displayed as mean ± standard deviation. We used the Shapiro-Wilk test to confirm normal distribution of data. Statistical differences among the three experimental groups were analyzed via one-way analysis of variance. Multiple comparisons were conducted by Student-Newman-Keuls post hoc test. A two-tailed Student t-test was used for comparisons of data between ipsilateral and contralateral testes within group. A P value less than 0.05 indicated statistical significance.

Ethical approval

The animal experimental protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethical Committee of Experimental Animal in Zhejiang Chinese Medical University (protocol code: 10790). All procedures were done in conformity with the ARRIVE guidelines.

Results

Sulforaphane treatment reduced testicular ischemia/reperfusion-induced malondialdehyde level in ipsilateral testis

A remarkable elevation in malondialdehyde level in ipsilateral testis was observed in the unilateral testicular ischemia-reperfusion group versus the sham group (P < 0.001) (Fig. 1). Sulforaphane (5 mg/kg) treatment partially reversed the increase in ipsilateral testicular malondialdehyde level (P < 0.001). Contralateral testis displayed no significant difference in the malondialdehyde level among the three animal groups (P = 0.4153).

The symbol * shows significant difference as compared to the sham group (P < 0.001). The symbol # indicates significant difference as compared to contralateral testis within group (P < 0.001). The symbol § represents significant difference as compared to ipsilateral testis in the testicular IR group (P < 0.001).

Sulforaphane treatment enhanced testicular ischemia/reperfusion-induced superoxide dismutase and catalase protein expression in ipsilateral testis

Unilateral testicular ischemia-reperfusion led to a significant decrease in the protein expression of superoxide dismutase and catalase in ipsilateral testis (P < 0.001) as compared to sham group (Fig. 2). Treatment with sulforaphane (5 mg/kg) was accompanied by a significant increase in ipsilateral testicular superoxide dismutase and catalase protein expression in comparison with testicular ischemia-reperfusion group (P < 0.01). The difference in the protein expression of superoxide dismutase and catalase in the contralateral testis of three groups was not significant (superoxide dismutase: P = 0.2842; catalase: P = 0.2364).

Sulforaphane treatment improved testicular ischemia/reperfusion-induced reproductive function in ipsilateral testis

In comparison with sham group, rats of unilateral testicular ischemia-reperfusion group showed abnormal testicular reproductive function, including lower testicular weight, diameter of the seminiferous tubule, germinal epithelial layer number, and Johnsen’s score in ipsilateral testis (P < 0.001) (Figs. 3 and 4). Treatment with sulforaphane (5 mg/kg) resulted in a significant improvement in ipsilateral testicular reproductive function (P < 0.01) versus testicular ischemia-reperfusion group. We didn’t find statistically significant difference regarding the contralateral testicular reproductive function among the three experimental groups (testicular weight: P = 0.5466; diameter of the seminiferous tubule: P = 0.6558; germinal epithelial layer number: P = 0.8440; Johnsen’s score: P = 0.4651).

Discussion

Urgent surgical intervention (testicular detorsion) is essential for the treatment of testicular torsion because delayed testicular detorsion can cause germ cell death. Testicular detorsion within 6 h after testicular torsion can save 90-100% affected testes42. Testicular salvage rate will decline to 50% if testicular detorsion is performed within 12–24 h42. Testicular salvage rate is < 10% after 24 h42. Even if torsional testis is salvaged successfully, the rates of postoperative testicular atrophy still are 9-100%2–6. In the current study, our findings demonstrated that ipsilateral testicular reproductive function was impaired in rats after 720° unilateral testicular torsion for 2 h followed by detorsion for 3 months. Testicular histomorphological changes, such as remarkably reduced testicular weight, diameter of the seminiferous tubule, germinal epithelial layer number, and Johnsen’s score, were observed (Figs. 3 and 4).

Testicular torsion-detorsion triggers release of excessive reactive oxygen species8,9. Huge amounts of reactive oxygen species cause injury to cellular macromolecules, such as lipids, nucleic acids, proteins, and carbohydrates, eventually leading to testicular damage10. The high reactivity and short lifetime of reactive oxygen species make their determination extremely difficult43. Reactive oxygen species can react with polyunsaturated fatty acids of the testis and induce lipid peroxidation44. Malondialdehyde is a steady byproduct formed during lipid peroxidation45. As a result, malondialdehyde is frequently utilized as an important marker that indicates the level of reactive oxygen species46. As observed in the present study, testicular ischemia-reperfusion group revealed significant rise in malondialdehyde level but reduction of reproductive function in ipsilateral testes as compared with sham group (Figs. 1, 3 and 4). These findings indicate that testicular ischemia-reperfusion results in enhanced reactive oxygen species generation with harmful effect on testicular reproductive function. Furthermore, administration of sulforaphane to testicular ischemia-reperfusion rats significantly reduced malondialdehyde level but raised reproductive function in ipsilateral testes as compared with testicular ischemia-reperfusion group (Figs. 1, 3 and 4). Based on these results, sulforaphane exhibits testicular protective effect in ischemia/reperfusion-induced testicular injury model via attenuation of oxidative stress. Clinical trials of sulforaphane confirmed its beneficial effects in the treatment of various human diseases, including fatty liver, asthma, Helicobacter pylori infection, prostate cancer, depression and autism spectrum disorder47–52. Sulforaphane medication was well tolerated and no serious adverse effects were reported during trials47–52. Hence, sulforaphane may represent a potential candidate for ameliorating testicular ischemia-reperfusion injury in the clinic. However, the main mechanism underlying attenuation of oxidative stress by sulforaphane is still lacking.

A balance between generation of reactive oxygen species and their clearance is necessary in maintaining normal cellular functions53,54. Superoxide dismutase and catalase in cells constitute antioxidant defense mechanism that protects cells against reactive oxygen species55,56. Superoxide dismutase dismutates superoxide anion into hydrogen peroxide57,58. The hydrogen peroxide is decomposed into molecular oxygen and water by catalase59,60. Under pathological status, such as ischemia-reperfusion, the increased reactive oxygen species are generated and the capability of the antioxidant defense mechanism to scavenge reactive oxygen species is overwhelmed, leading to cellular injury and even death61,62. This study showed increased malondialdehyde level and decreased expression of superoxide dismutase and catalase in ipsilateral testes of testicular ischemia-reperfusion group (Figs. 1 and 2). These data indicate that large amounts of reactive oxygen species produced by testicular ischemia-reperfusion cause depletion of superoxide dismutase and catalase. Moreover, the administration of sulforaphane in rats with testicular ischemia-reperfusion led to increase in superoxide dismutase and catalase expression and decrease in malondialdehyde level in ipsilateral testicular tissues (Figs. 1 and 2). These results reveal that sulforaphane can reduce oxidative stress by increasing superoxide dismutase and catalase expression.

Whether contralateral testis is damaged by unilateral testicular ischemia-reperfusion is a point in debate. Some studies showed injury of contralateral testis after unilateral testicular ischemia-reperfusion63,64, but some did not65,66. Observations from our study revealed that unilateral testicular ischemia-reperfusion didn’t cause remarkable alterations in malondialdehyde concentration, superoxide dismutase and catalase expression, and testicular reproductive function in contralateral testis (Figs. 1, 2, 3 and 4). Data from this study demonstrate that unilateral testicular ischemia-reperfusion doesn’t induce damage to contralateral testis.

Turner et al. reported that increased neutrophils infiltrated into ischemia-reperfusion testis and produced excessive reactive oxygen species 4 h after testicular detorsion67. Therefore, we assessed oxidative stress and antioxidant factors 4 h after testicular detorsion. Some other researchers also chose the same time to measure these parameters68,69. Their results are consistent with ours.

In the present study, we used some indicators, such as malondialdehyde, superoxide dismutase, catalase, testicular weight, diameter of the seminiferous tubule, germinal epithelial layer number, and Johnsen’s score. These indicators show oxidative stress and testicular tissue damage well. They have been extensively applied in testicular ischemia-reperfusion damage researches70,71. Inflammatory and apoptotic markers, such as tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin-1β (IL-1β), number of neutrophils, and caspase-3, are also some good indicators to reflect oxidative stress and testicular injury72. We will try to use these markers in later study.

Investigators can evaluate the effect of sulforaphane on normal testis by establishing a positive control group receiving sulforaphane alone. In sulforaphane-treated group, we observed that sulforaphane exhibited protective effect on ipsilateral ischemia-reperfusion testis (Figs. 3 and 4). Nevertheless, sulforaphane didn’t cause remarkable alteration in contralateral normal testis (Figs. 3 and 4). These findings suggest that sulforaphane has no effect on normal testis. Thus, we didn’t add a positive control group receiving sulforaphane alone in the present study.

A limitation of the current study is that we only selected one dose of sulforaphane considering the previous literature31–37. Therefore, determination of its optimal dose requires additional studies.

Conclusion

In summary, we explored the effect of sulforaphane on testicular damage caused by testicular ischemia-reperfusion for the first time. Evidence from our study indicates that sulforaphane enhances testicular reproductive function after ischemia-reperfusion injury. The beneficial effect of sulforaphane against ischemia/reperfusion-induced testicular injury is attributed to its ability to increase superoxide dismutase and catalase expression and decrease reactive oxygen species level. Therefore, sulforaphane may serve as a novel therapeutic option for testicular ischemia-reperfusion injury. However, additional investigation should be carried out to verify the efficacy and safety of sulforaphane in clinical application.

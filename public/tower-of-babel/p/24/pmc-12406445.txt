The use of echocardiography to identify acute and chronic changes in left ventricular function in hypertensive disorder of pregnancy
Authors: Ricardo Luiz de Medeiros Lima, Eduardo Gabriel de Azevedo Cortez, Cleanderson Romualdo Fidelis, Lucas Nobrega de Lima, Esther Cecin Mamere, Rafael Andrade Rego, Josivan Gomes de Lima, Selma Maria Bezerra Jeronimo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHypertensive disorders of pregnancy, including preeclampsia, are common in socioeconomic vulnerable populations worldwide. Approximately one third of women who have those disorders will maintain with hypertension, after pregnancy. This work aimed to determine the prevalence of hypertensive disorder of pregnancy in Natal, Brazil, and to use echocardiography to study cardiac function: (1) during pregnancy, and (2) at post partum in those who developed severe preeclampsia.Methods A prospective study after the 7th week of gestation (n = 107) and a cross-sectional study of preeclampsia cases (n = 18) were performed in Natal, Brazil. Data included clinical history, weight, height, age, and transthoracic echocardiogram. Descriptive and exploratory, paired and unpaired Student t-tests, univariate and logistic regression analyses were performed.ResultsAmong the 87 women who were fully studied prospectively, age varied between 15.2 – 43.2 years (28.1 ± 6.3) and 9.2% of participants developed hypertension. Notably, 61 (70.1%) had a high body mass index (BMI) of > 25 kg/m2. There was myocardial hypertrophy or left ventricular remodeling in 12 subjects (13.8%); ejection fraction under 50% in 4 subjects (4.6%) and echocardiographic evidence of reduced left ventricular global longitudinal strain in 6 (6.9%). Women who developed hypertension had higher BMIs and larger left ventricular mass index. Post-partum women who had severe preeclampsia showed increase in the mean left ventricle relative wall thickness (p = 0.0167) and mean left ventricular isovolumic relaxation time (p = 0.0004) after a mean of a month post-delivery.ConclusionsThere is high prevalence of hypertensive disorder of pregnancy in underprivileged women from Natal, Brazil. Transthoracic echocardiography can identify early ventricular dysfunction in pre- and post-pregnancy and it may be a useful tool to guide in therapeutic strategies to prevent complications.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12884-025-08006-z.Keywords: Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, Preeclampsia, Ventricular dysfunction, Echocardiography, Brazil

Background

Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy are a complex of diseases characterized by an increase in systolic and/or diastolic arterial pressures (> 140 or > 90 mmHg, respectively), and include hypertension alone, preeclampsia and severe preeclampsia [1–3]. Preeclampsia can result in complications such as HELLP syndrome (acronym for hemolysis, elevated liver enzyme levels, and low platelets) and eclampsia [4, 5]. Although there were advances in the treatment of these disorders with the introduction of magnesium sulfate, calcium, aspirin and other drugs, the lack of early interventions cause maternal and fetal-neonate morbity and mortality [6–11]. The overall incidence of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy worldwide, varies from 2–12% of all pregnancies [12, 13], however, low socioeconomic status increases the risk [14], and they are particularly problematic in mid- to low-income countries such as Brazil. In United States, they also affect disproportionately women of Latin or African ascendancies at a greater risk [3, 15].

Weight gain is associated with preeclampsia and the later development of cardiovascular disease, regardless of other risk factors [16, 17]. The weight gain is even more frequent in vulnerable population [18, 19]. Obesity at young age has become a worldwide problem even in lower income countries [20]. The combination of weight gain and lack of adequate medical care for treating the hypertension contribute to the risk of complications during pregnancy.

Women who develop preeclampsia have a 3.5- fold increased risk of subsequently developing myocardial infarction [21], cardiac diseases such as hypertrophy or left ventricular remodeling, heart failure [22] and coronary calcification [23, 24]. An earlier onset of preeclampsia is associated with subclinical impairment of ventricular function [25, 26] and worsened outcome, usually requiring interruption of the pregnancy to deliver a premature infant. Metabolic syndrome and vascular changes initiated during pregnancy can persist afterward, causing cardiovascular disease at an earlier age [22, 27]. In addition, chronic inflammation, which is common in more vulnerable population, can constitute a link between the cardiovascular risk during pregnancy and eventual development of cardiac structural alterations such as myocardial hypertrophy [28, 29].

Two-dimensional echocardiography is one of the most commonly used tool and relatively low-cost diagnostic technique to assess cardiac structure and function [30, 31]. The analysis of diameters and flow patterns enables calculations related to volumes, pressures, and the performance of the heart and cardiovascular system. The incorporation of ventricular strain analysis, and more recently, left atrial strain has significantly enhanced the evaluation of both systolic and diastolic function of the left ventricle and left atrium, particularly, in the identification of subclinical findings of ventricular and atrial dysfuntions [32–34].

Prior studies have documented cardiac involvement in the hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, including diastolic dysfunction, abnormal cardiac remodeling, and elevated right ventricular systolic pressure [35, 36]. A recent study showed the relationship between Global Longitudinal Strain (GLS), total peripheral vascular resistance and fetal growth deficit, with an increased risk of developing hypertensive disease during pregnancy [37].

During the current study, we used transthoracic echocardiography to assess myocardial function and hemodynamic features throughout pregnancy in women from socioeconomically challenged populations of Natal, Brazil and in a group of women who had severe preeclampsia. Our aim was to assess the hemodynamics in the cardiovascular system during the development of hypertension and to determine whether echocardiogram could be a tool for use in new protocols for following women with preeclampsia post-partum.

Study design and participants

Prospective cohort study of pregnancy and cardiac function

Between February 2018 and March 2020, we conducted a prospective cohort study of 107 pregnant women recruited at three Basic Health Units in Natal, Rio Grande do Norte, Brazil. Our aim was to assess the hemodynamics in cardiovascular system during pregnancy, using echocardiography as a tool. Women were recruited consecutively in the first prenatal visit at the Health Basic Unit. They did not have a history of diabetes and/or prior cardiopathy and they had no history of use of negative inotropic drugs. They did not have private health insurance and they came from the same social economical stratum of a low income Brazilian population. Among the 107 subjects recruited, 20 were eventually excluded because they missed scheduled visits or they did not present information for two or more of the parameters to be evaluated in the study due to technical difficulty during the echocardiogram examination. The remaining 87 women were evaluated between the 7th and 36th week of pregnancy. On the day of the echocardiogram, blood pressure was measured, and clinical and anthropometric data were collected. Forty-three of the 87 participants underwent a second echocardiogram, and 22 had 3 or more exams.

The sample size estimate was based on a prior study of a population with similar social economic and ethnical background as ours, in which the prevalence of preeclampsia or any Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy was 6.0% or 18.5%, respectively. A sample size of 87 women would have a 80% power to detect significance at α = 0.05. We aimed to recruit a total of 104 women since there was a risk of loss during follow up.

Retrospective cross-sectional study of post-partum women who had preeclampsia

The study was performed to determine the impact of hypertension on the cardiac muscle post-partum in those who had had severe preeclampsia. Twenty women were recruited, among whom 7 were excluded because echocardiography was technically difficult due to extreme overweight, yielding unreliable echocardiogram parameters.

Two-dimensional echocardiogram

Transthoracic echocardiography was performed using Affiniti 50G equipment (Philips Healthcare, Andover, Massachusetts) with sector probe (2–4 MHz). The left ventricle systolic function was assessed by calculating the ejection fraction and by analyzing the left ventricle. Global Longitudinal Strain (GLS). The protocols and guidelines defined by the American Society of Echocardiography were followed [30, 38]. The reference values for the echocardiogram parameters are shown in Table S1 [38] To determine the GLS, loopings were obtained with complete cardiac cycles, from the apical four-chamber, apical two-chamber, and longitudinal apical approaches. The left ventricular diastolic function was evaluated using the transmitral flow to calculate the amplitudes of the E and A waves, the deceleration time of the E wave, and the left ventricle isovolumetric relaxation time. Tissue Doppler Imaging was used to measure the amplitude of medial and lateral mitral e’ waves. When present, the maximum velocity of the tricuspid regurgitation flow was measured. The indexed left atrium volume was calculated using the apical two- and four-chamber views, indexed to the body surface area. All studies were recorded and stored on the equipment's hard drive. The echocardiograms were performed by a single operator (RLML).

Statistical analyses

Clinical and echocardiographic data were stored in Microsoft Excel for Windows® and analyzed in R software. A correlation matrix was developed to explore relationships between variables and interactions. Echocardiogram, gestational, and clinical data were analyzed for women who had more than one exam performed (n = 29), considering the gestational age as prior to or later than 20 weeks. Non-paired univariate analysis was performed with Student’s t-test, considering hypertension status. When assessing whether the data had a normal distribution, Body Mass Index (BMI), number of pregnancies, number of miscarriages, and Mitral E/e’ amplitude ratio in Mitral tissue Doppler (E/e’ ratio), ratio deviated from normality at a 5% significance level, as determined by the Shapiro–Wilk test. However, taking into account the robustness of the Student's t-test to moderate deviations from normality, we then followed the use of parametric statistical methods to validade the findings; this approach minimized the impact of minor normality deviations and assured the validity of our inferences. To mitigate potential biases and assure internal validity, confounding variables were identified, and interactions among the variables were measured using Pearson linear correlation. A logistic regression model with a stepwise technique was used, considering the development of hypertension as the dependent binary (response) variable, where women with hypertensive disorders were coded as 1 and those without as 0, and independent variables BMI, left ventricle mass index, and the left atrium volume index as a binary variable [0 when normal (< 34 ml/m2) and 1 when abnormal (≥ 34 ml/m2)].

Ethical considerations

The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Federal University of Rio Grande do Norte ethical committee (approval number CAAE 83997618.7.0000.5537). The Brazilian Ethical Research Review boards adheres to the principles outlined in the Belmont Report and the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants listened to a verbal explanation of the study, and then read and signed the informed consent, prior to the participation into the study.

Results

Clinical characteristics of the longitudinal study of pregnancy

Among the 107 women recruited, 87 completed the protocol. Subjects ranged in age between 15.20–43.20 (mean 28.12 ± 6.35) and 41 (47.1%) were primiparous. The mean gestational age at recruitment was 19.6 weeks (Table 1), with 79 (90.9%) of the participants maintaining normal blood pressure and no history of antihypertensive medication use, while 8 (9.1%) developed hypertension during the study. The mean Body Mass Index (BMI) was 27.73 ± 5.20; with 61 of the participants (70.1%) having BMI greater than 25 kg/m2.

Profile of the Echocardiogram findings during healthy pregnancy and pregnancy complicated by hypertension

There was no difference in the transthoracic echocardiogram findings according to gestational age in women with normal pregnancy (Table 2), the reference values for the echocardiograms are shown in Table S1. A moderate correlation between GLS and the Ejection Fraction (ρ = 0.67) and a high correlation between left ventricular systolic and left ventricular diastolic diameters (ρ = 0.79) were observed (Fig. 1). When the development of hypertension was considered, differences in the left ventricle mass index (p = 0.0350), BMI (p = 0.0365) and in mitral E/A wave relation (p = 0.0013) were observed for those who developed hypertension (Table 3). A logistic regression model was employed to explore the relationship between the dependent and independent variables. Since the model estimates considered the probability of developing hypertension during pregnancy, BMI, left ventricular mass index, and left atrium volume index (as the binary variable) were used as explanatory variables (Table 4). The adjusted model demonstrated a robust performance, as verified by the residual analysis using the half-normal plot envelope through simulated envelope with 95% of confidence (Fig. 2A), with the residuals appropriately distributed within the expected range, and with an AUC of 0.851 (Fig. 2B). Based on the model, an increase in one unit in the BMI, with the other variables constant, indicated a 21% increase in the probability of developing hypertension; likewise, the increase in one unit of the Left Ventricle Mass Index increased in 6.34% the chance of elevated arterial blood pressure. Lastly, by using the left atrium volume index as a dummy variable, there was 7.8 chance of having elevated arterial blood pressure compared to women with normal values.

Echocardiography as tool to assess cardiac function post-partum in women who had severe preeclampsia

In order to assess whether there was still cardiac damage induced by the preeclampsia after resolution of the pregnancy, we compared post-partum women who had preeclampsia with women with healthy pregnancy. The clinical and demographics findings of the post-partum women who evolved with preeclampsia and those with healthy pregnancy are shown in Table S2. A significant difference at the 5% of significance level in the mean for Left Ventricle Mass Index (p = 0.0185), left ventricle wall ratio thickness (p = 0.0007), mitral pulsed wave Doppler E/A relation (p = 0.0072), left ventricular isovolumic relaxation time (p = 0.0002), and Global Longitudinal Strain (p = 0.0429) was observed (Table 5), indicating persistence of cardiac damage after resolution of the pregnancy.

Discussion

Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy are frequent among socioeconomic vulnerable women, as seen in Brazil and elsewehere [14, 17]. A subgroup of affected women tend to progress to chronic hypertension and the potential to early cardiovascular diseases [39]. In addition, children born of a preeclampsia pregnancy have a greater risk of developing hypertension [40–42]. Therefore, it is of importance to treat and raise awareness of the long term complications of these disorders for both mother and children. In this study carried on in Natal, Brazil, a 9.2% prevalence of hypertension was observed. In addition, we found that women who had higher BMI were at greater risk of developing hypertensive disorder of pregnancy and preeclampsia, confirming findings from a prior study using population of similar social economical background [17].

Echocardiography has become a useful and unexpensive tool to assess heart function and it is accessible in the majority of the countries. Parameters obtained through transthoracic echocardiography—such as left ventricular mass, isovolumic relaxation time, and global longitudinal strain of the left ventricle, as well as information of the uterine artery, particularly total peripheral vascular resistance, contribute to the diagnosis and therapeutic decision-making in cases of arterial hypertension, including hypertensive disorders of pregnancy. These parameters may help to assess the impact of hypertension on both systolic and diastolic functions of the left ventricle and eventually lead to better therapeutic decisions [30, 31, 43–47].

Using echocardiography, we observed no correlation between left ventricle systolic and diastolic functions with anthropometric, gestational, or hypertensive parameters. However, there were correlations between GLS and Ejection Fraction (ρ = 0.67) and between left ventricle systolic and left ventricle diastolic diameter (ρ = 0.79). The increase in the left ventricle mass index resulted in altered diastolic function with increase in left atrial volume index, Mitral pulsed Doppler E/A relation, and Left ventricle isovolumetric relaxation time. These findings are of concern and they are of importance to follow these women post-delivery since they can guide therapy.

In healthy pregnancies, when considering the gestational age (up to 20 vs. 20 or more weeks) with the echocardiogram findings in unpaired manner, no difference was observed. But when the participants were grouped according to the development of hypertension (n = 8) or not (n = 79), there were significant differences in BMI, LVMI, and in Mitral E/A wave for those who developed hypertension. Although, the group that developed hypertension is small, this finding is of importance for follow up of this population and potential recommendation for careful intervention.

People with chronic hypertension may have myocardial remodeling and/or hypertrophy. Left ventricular diastolic dysfunction is a known finding related to arterial hypertension and its complications [48, 49]. Our study allowed identification of left ventricular systolic dysfunction (such as reduced ejection fraction, global longitudinal strain, and cardiac output), as well as diastolic dysfunction (e.g., increased left atrial volume and ventricular relaxation time, and decreased mitral inflow E/A ratio). It supports the deleterious effect of increased blood pressure and possible damage to the heart muscle caused by elevated blood pressure which may be present in the early stages of arterial hypertension and with a more chronic stage [50], as seen in women who developed hypertension during pregnancy.

The increase in one unit of BMI increased the odds of developing hypertension, again confirming the need to closely watch the BMI in prior to pregnancy [50–53]. Obesity is a worldwide problem and it is worse in the lower income population. Weight loss should be recommended for better arterial blood pressure control. Women who belong to less favored socio-economic groups may have more difficulties in losing weight or adhering to physical activities that may aid weight loss. Programs aiming prenatal or post partum assistances should be designed to focus on these women. In addition, hypertensive disorders of pregnancy impact cardiac function for both maternal and later for children born of preeclampsia gestation [41].

Women who had severe preeclampsia with a mean of 1-month post-partum, presented abnormalities in left ventricle function, altered left ventricle mass index, left ventricular relative wall thickness, altered E/A mitral relation, and left ventricular isovolumic relaxation time (Table 5). Although no difference in the ejection fraction was observed, GLS was decreased indicating some grade of systolic left ventricle dysfunction present in these women. A four-year longitudinal study conducted in Baltimore, United States, showed that the incidence of hypertension was higher in those who had a history of preeclampsia [35, 54]. The knowledge of maternal hemodynamics (vascular resistance, cardiac output, ventricular mass among other paramenters) is important for the management of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy. More recently experts have addressed the relationship between maternal and fetal hemodynamics and adverse outcomes. They considered that the cardiovascular characteristics of the women may be of use and may guide therapeutic approaches, allowing decrease of long-term potential complications with early interventions [45, 55, 56].

Our work focused on echocardiographic findings related to LV systolic and diastolic functions in order to assess the cardiovascular system during pregnancy and whether there were early and subclinical signs of dysfunction secondary to hypertensive disease in pregnancy. Prior studies showed that the understanding of maternal hemodynamics might help clinicians to design protolocls for proper treatment of patients with or at risk for pregnancy complications [57, 58]. Our study had some limitations including the short term-follow up of women post-delivery and the small number of women who developed hypertension in the cohort. Though logistic regression is a robust tool, a low number of successes relative to the number of predictors can lead to unstable coefficient estimates and wide confidence intervals, which impacts the generalizability of results, so the findings should be interpreted cautiously. Future studies with larger sample sizes are recommended to confirm observed associations and improve model robustness. A long-term follow up is needed to determine whether there is persistence of the findings and to design better protocols for those vulnerable women. Finally, another limitation was the lack of studying the left atrial reservoir strain. When this study was performed the feature for studying left atrial reservoir was not available. Currently, it is well recognized that left atrial reservoir is important in the analysis of several diseases since it provides greater sensitivity and prognostic value in cardiovascular diseases [37, 57–60].

Conclusions

Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy are frequent among women living in vulnerable economic and social conditions in Natal, Brazil. Normal pregnancy does not alter systolic or left ventricle diastolic function parameters. Subclinical left ventricle dysfunction can be detected in women with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy during and post pregnancy by using echocardiography with global longitudinal strain. Echocardiography is a useful tool to follow women with severe preeclampsia for potential cardiac dysfunction. Better protocols for longer follow up of women who developed hypertensive disorders of pregnancy are urgent to protect their quality of life and avoid morbidity and mortality.

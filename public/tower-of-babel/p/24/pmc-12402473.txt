MAFNet: A novel adaptive multi-scale model for fine-grained grading of diabetic retinopathy
Authors: Lei Sun, Qinghua Peng, Xiaoxia Xiao, Jun Liu, Yang Li, Dan Shu, Jinrou Xue
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Diabetic Retinopathy (DR) is a leading cause of blindness worldwide, and its early detection and accurate grading play a crucial role in clinical intervention. To address the dual limitations of existing methods in multi-scale lesions feature fusion and lesions relation modeling, this study proposes a novel adaptive multi-scale convolutional neural network model for fine-grained grading of DR, called MAFNet (Multi-scale Adaptive Fine-grained Network). The model is constructed through three core modules to establish a multi-scale feature integration framework: the Hierarchical Global Context Module (HGCM) effectively expands the receptive field by employing multi-scale pooling and dynamic feature fusion, capturing lesions features from micro to large-scale areas; the Multi-scale Adaptive Attention Module (MSAM) utilizes an adaptive attention mechanism to dynamically adjust the feature weights at different spatial locations, enhancing the representation of key lesions regions; and the Relational Multi-head Attention Module (RMA) uses a multi-head attention mechanism to model the complex relationships between features in parallel, improving the accuracy of fine-grained lesions identification. Furthermore, MAFNet adopts a multi-task learning framework, transforming the DR grading task into a dual-task structure of regression and classification, thereby effectively capturing the progression of DR. Extensive experiments on three publicly available datasets, DDR, Messidor-2, and APTOS, show that the quadratic weighted Kappa values of the MAFNet model reach 0.934, 0.917, and 0.936, respectively, significantly outperforming existing DR grading methods such as LANet and MPLNet, demonstrating its significant application value in automated DR grading.

Introduction

DR is one of the most common complications among diabetic patients and a leading cause of blindness. With the ongoing rise in the global prevalence of diabetes, the incidence of DR is also showing a significant upward trend1. DR occurs due to retinal vascular disease and abnormal retinal blood flow. DR is typically characterized by four distinct stages of the disease, including mild, moderate, severe, and proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR)2. According to statistics from the World Health Organization, it is estimated that by 2030, there will be more than 300 million diabetic patients worldwide3. Early detection and grading play a crucial role in preventing vision loss, making the development of efficient and accurate DR grading models of significant clinical importance.

Traditional DR diagnosis relies heavily on ophthalmologists to evaluate fundus images, a time-consuming and labor-intensive process that depends significantly on clinical experience. In addition, subtle features in retinal fundus images, such as microaneurysms, are difficult to recognize visually4. Consequently, diagnostic delays and misdiagnosis are common, especially in resource-limited areas. To improve diagnostic efficiency and accuracy, researchers have developed automated grading methods using image processing and pattern recognition. Initial approaches employed traditional machine learning techniques like Support Vector Machines (SVMs), k-nearest neighbors (KNN), and random forests for ocular diseases dection in fundus images5. However, these methods typically require training on small, domain-specific datasets, limiting their generalizability to larger or differently distributed data. They also depend on manually designed or selected features for lesion detection and grading, which makes it challenging to comprehensively or accurately capture features in complex imaging scenarios. In contrast, deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) effectively address the feature extraction limitation. CNNs have demonstrated significant success in the medical applications, notably contributing to early diagnosis of human diseases, tumors, and cancers6.

Existing CNN-based methods for grading DR have improved diagnostic efficiency but still struggle to handle the highly heterogeneous and correlated DR lesion features. DR lesions (e.g., microaneurysms, hemorrhages, and exudates) vary significantly in morphology, distribution, and size, often exhibiting blurred edges or dense distributions, making it difficult to comprehensively capture the information of lesions in a single receptive field. In addition, the spatial relationship between the stages of DR shows continuity: adjacent stages often have overlapping lesions or similar visual features. This similarities, combined with blurred lesion borders, frequently cause grading confusion. For example, subtle stage 1 lesions may be misclassified as stage 0 due to poor image quality, while stage 2 lesions- despite their increased complexity—remain visually similar to those in stage 1. The contradiction between the progressive nature of DR development and the discrete clinical grading criteria further exacerbates the difficulty of boundary determination.

In this context, this paper proposes a novel adaptive multi-scale convolutional neural network model for fine-grained grading of DR, named MAFNet. The model is based on a multi-task learning approach that transforms DR grading into a dual-task structure. For different scales and types of lesions in DR images, MAFNet introduces a hierarchical global context module (HGCM), which can efficiently capture lesions from subtle to large scales; the proposed multi-scale adaptive attention module (MSAM) can accurately extract and correlate lesion features at different spatial locations; and the relational Relational Multi-head Attention (RMA) module (RMA) can explore multiple subsets of features in parallel space, to understand the higher-order interactions and channel dependencies among lesions. Experimental results show that MAFNet significantly outperforms the traditional single-branch attention model and other mainstream methods on the public dataset, validating its effectiveness in DR fine grading.

Related work

Before the widespread adoption of deep learning, the detection of DR primarily relied on traditional machine learning methods7, such as Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM)8, Support Vector Machines (SVM)9, and k-nearest neighbors (KNN)10. These methods classified fundus images by manually extracting features such as color and texture. However, when applied to large-scale datasets, these methods demonstrated limited generalization capabilities and significant constraints. In recent years, with the rapid development of deep learning, particularly the application of CNNs, automated classification of diabetic retinopathy has made remarkable progress. CNNs overcome the limitations of manual feature extraction by automatically learning complex image features, significantly improving detection accuracy and the model’s generalization ability11–20.

With the development of deep learning, single-branch convolutional neural networks, such as ResNet and Inception, have been widely applied to automated DR grading. These networks typically improve grading accuracy by pre-training on large datasets like ImageNet and fine-tuning on DR-specific datasets. For example, Pan et al.21 used the Inception V3 and ResNet-50 deep learning models to classify fundus images into three categories: normal, macular degeneration, and mosaic fundus, to facilitate timely identification and treatment of fundus diseases, providing a reference for the clinical diagnosis or screening of diabetic retinopathy and other eye diseases. However, they used a relatively balanced dataset they had collected themselves, and their network structure mainly relies on a single receptive field or fixed scales. This approach faces challenges in capturing multi-scale and multi-type lesions information simultaneously in real clinical settings. While the model performs well on small-scale, balanced datasets, it may fail to adequately recognize or differentiate large lesions from very subtle ones in more complex, real-world environments. As a result, its ability to identify severe or boundary lesions may be insufficient, impacting overall diagnostic performance.

Furthermore, many of these studies overlook the intricate spatial relationships between lesions at different stages of DR. Lesions at adjacent stages often exhibit significant overlap or continuity in visual features, and lesion areas’ boundaries are not always distinct, which greatly increases the difficulty of accurately distinguishing between different stages of lesions. For example, Meiling Feng et al.22 leveraged the powerful relational capture capabilities of graph neural networks to propose a new DR classification model but did not address the spatial relationship between lesions. Wang X et al.23 proposed a joint learning multi-stage grading method based on convolutional neural networks (CNN), which successfully mitigated performance degradation under low-resolution conditions. However, this method did not delve deeply into the subtle differences between adjacent stages of lesions, especially for stages with highly similar visual features and vague lesion boundaries. This lack of refined modeling leads to significant confusion risks in multi-stage lesion scenarios. Although the joint learning strategy can to some extent share deep features to enhance the overall generalization ability of the model, when lesions shapes and distributions exhibit continuous or gradient-like changes between adjacent stages, the model still struggles to capture these details. For instance, Zang F et al.24 designed a lesions relation attention block that incorporates the attention mechanism to capture the relationships between lesions. However, due to the lack of targeted handling of similar lesions areas and the insufficient exploration of multi-scale features, the model ultimately failed to accommodate all possible lesions morphologies in practical applications, resulting in reduced discriminative power and impaired classification accuracy. Especially in clinical diagnostic contexts, such misclassification of similar-stage lesions can easily lead to deviations in subsequent treatment and intervention strategies, highlighting the importance of deeply modeling spatial relationships and subtle visual differences in multi-stage lesions recognition tasks. In recent years, to address the dual constraints posed by imbalanced data distribution and the complexity of lesions features on automated DR grading, researchers have proposed various improvements. For example, He A et al.25 proposed a new Class Attention Block (CAB) to address the imbalanced DR data distribution. By deeply exploring the characteristic differences between various DR stages and achieving ”equal treatment” of different classes within the network, they developed CABNet for DR grading. This method alleviated model bias caused by data imbalance by enhancing the attention on minority classes. However, CABNet still is inadequate for capturing the transitions and relationships between stages, making it difficult to reflect the progressive changes from mild to severe lesions.

Additionally, single receptive fields or fixed-scale network structures exhibit obvious limitations when faced with multi-scale, multi-type lesion features, making it difficult to account for the subtle differences between different lesions. Due to the lack of refined modeling for complex or gradually changing lesion morphologies, this method still faces significant challenges in distinguishing boundaries, with adjacent levels of lesion features prone to confusion, thereby leading to non-negligible classification errors in actual grading.

Methods

Although significant progress has been made in the automatic classification of Diabetic Retinopathy with the introduction of deep learning models, existing research still faces several limitations, particularly in areas such as model generalization, multi-scale information capture, lesion spatial relationship modeling, the modeling of subtle lesions differences between adjacent stages, addressing data distribution imbalance, and the refined modeling of complex or gradual lesion morphology. These limitations restrict the performance of models in practical applications and highlight the need for further research and improvements.

In this study, we propose a multi-scale adaptive fine-grained network (MAFNet) to address the shortcomings of existing DR grading methods in cross-scale lesion feature extraction and spatial relationship modeling. Its structure is shown in Fig. 1. Traditional single-receptive field methods are difficult to simultaneously handle the localization and global analysis of multi-scale lesions such as microaneurysms and hemorrhages. To this end, the hierarchical global context module (HGCM) is designed to enhance cross-scale feature expression through multi-scale context aggregation and dynamic feature fusion. For the complex spatial dependencies among lesions, the multi-scale adaptive attention module (MSAM) is proposed to capture spatial location correlations, and the relational multiple attention module (RMA) is introduced to parse complex relationships in feature channels and higher-order interactions through a multi-head attention mechanism. Based on the continuity characteristics of DR development, a regression-categorization dual-task learning framework is innovatively constructed to ensure grading accuracy while preserving disease progression continuity.Fig. 1MAFNet Network Architecture Diagram(The four feature maps on the far left are extracted from the second, third, fourth, and last layers of the backbone network, respectively).

MAFNet Network Architecture Diagram(The four feature maps on the far left are extracted from the second, third, fourth, and last layers of the backbone network, respectively).

Due to the excellent performance of ConvNeXt, especially its ability to handle complex image features, MAFNet adopts the recently introduced high-performance ConvNeXt network as its backbone, which enhances the expressiveness of the model by optimizing the convolutional structure, while maintaining good computational efficiency and strong generalization ability to adapt to various datasets. Building upon this, to further optimize feature representation, the model incorporates a Shuffle Attention Module26 at specific layers to enhance the capture of critical information.

Hierarchical global context module (HGCM)

The Hierarchical Global Context Module (HGCM) is an innovative multi-scale feature extraction component proposed in this study, designed to overcome the limitations of traditional convolutional neural networks in capturing fine-grained pathological features with a single receptive field. Its structure is shown in Fig. 2. HGCM employs multi-scale context aggregation, attention-guiding mechanism and dynamic feature fusion strategy to systematically solve the problem of insufficient characterization of lesion information at different scales. Compared with existing methods, HGCM not only effectively captures global semantic information through multi-scale aggregation, but also preserves subtle local structural features in the spatial dimension, ensuring a balance between large-scale context and precise local responses. By introducing an attention-guided dynamic weighting mechanism, HGCM further enhances the network’s ability to adaptively perceive key lesion regions, emphasizing important features while suppressing redundant information.The HGCM module provides an efficient and flexible solution for fine-grained lesion recognition and greatly improves the network’s performance in lesion detection.Fig. 2Hierarchical Global Context Module.

Multi-Scale adaptive attention module(MSAM)

To further improve the model’s ability to recognize fine-grained lesion regions in DR images, this study proposes an innovative module, the Multiscale Adaptive Attention Module (MSAM) as shown in Fig. 3. By introducing a dynamic convolutional kernel and an efficient channel attention mechanism, MSAM can accurately capture the complex dependencies between different spatial locations in the feature map. The design of this module not only greatly improves the spatial sensitivity of the features, enabling the model to capture the details of local lesions more accurately, but also enhances the robustness and generalization ability of the model through diversified feature repreadsentations.MSAM dynamically adjusts the feature weights to ensure the efficient extraction and representation of the features in a variety of complex lesions situations, thus significantly improving the model’s accuracy in fine-grained lesion recognition.Fig. 3Multi-Scale Adaptive Attention Module.

Relational multihead attention(RMA)

In order to further enhance the model’s capability to model complex feature relationships in the DR grading task, this study proposes an innovative module, Relational Multihead Attention (RMA). The RMA module aims to deeply explore and capture the complex inter-feature relationships within feature maps, thereby greatly improving the model’s ability to recognize fine-grained lesion features and enhance its classification performance. By utilizing multiple heads of attention in different subspaces, RMA provides a more comprehensive understanding of the relationships between features, which is essential for improving classification performance Fig. 4.Fig. 4Relational Multihead Attention.

Relational Multihead Attention.

The core of the RMA module is to establish and reinforce class-relevant feature representations. Multi-Task learning

In order to better characterize the gradual process of DR between disease stages, ​​we adopt a multitask learning framework in this study. On the one hand, the severity of DR was considered as a regression task to capture the continuum of the disease; on the other hand, a classification task was incorporated to discretize regression outputs into DR grades. The regression task aims to predict continuous scores associated with images, while the classification task converts these continuous values into final predicted values for five predefined categories. To facilitate the co-optimization of these tasks, we designed a weighted composite loss function that integrates the cross-entropy loss (CE) and the mean-square error loss (MSE) and introduces a regularization term to prevent overfitting.

The goal of the image classification task is to accurately classify the input image into five predefined categories. To achieve this, this paper adopts the Cross-Entropy Loss (CE), which is one of the most commonly used and effective loss functions in classification tasks. The cross-entropy loss can effectively measure the difference between the predicted probability distribution and the true label distribution. Its mathematical expression is as follows:30\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${L}_{cls}=-\frac{1}{m}\left[\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{m} \sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{k} {t}_{j}\text{ln}\left(pro{b}_{i}\right)\right]$$\end{document}m denotes the number of inputs, k represents the number of categories, tj indicates that the true label is the jth category, and probi denotes the predicted label probability after the activation function. Using the cross-entropy loss function alone in multi-task learning may ignore the optimization requirements of the regression tasks, leading to conflicts between tasks.

The regression task is the core of this study, and its goal is to predict continuous severity scores for input fundus images​​. To achieve this, this paper adopts the Mean Squared Error Loss (MSE) to measure the discrepancy between the predicted values and the true values, with the mathematical expression as follows:31\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${L}_{reg}=\frac{1}{m}\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{m} {\left(y-{t}_{j}\right)}^{2}$$\end{document}y represents the output score. The MSE loss primarily considers the difference between the predicted label and the true label.However, the MSE loss function suffers from optimization efficiency issues.

To overcome the aforementioned issues, this paper proposes an innovative dynamic multi-task loss weighting strategy. Unlike traditional fixed-weight approaches, this study designs a performance feedback-based dynamic weight adjustment mechanism, with its composite loss function defined as:32\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$L\, = \,\alpha \left( t \right)\;\cdot\;L_{cls} \, + \,\beta \left( t \right)\;\cdot\;L_{reg} \, + \,L_{r}$$\end{document}

Here, α(t) and β(t) are weight coefficients that are dynamically adjusted during the training process. These weights are adaptively updated based on the model’s real-time performance in terms of classification accuracy and regression performance (Kappa coefficient). When there is a larger relative improvement in classification accuracy, the weight α(t) for the classification loss is increased; conversely, when the Kappa coefficient shows a greater relative improvement, the weight β(t) for the regression loss is increased.

The Shuffle Attention module effectively captures important channels and key spatial regions in the feature map by combining channel attention and spatial attention mechanisms. The Messidor-2 dataset28 consists of 874 pairs of retinal images, with each pair containing two retinal images centered on the macula. This dataset includes a total of 1,748 images, with the sample distribution for each grade shown in Table 1. It is an extension of the Messdior dataset, maintaining consistent graphic style, color, and visual integrity, and providing higher-quality images than other DR datasets. In our experiments, each class was randomly divided into training, validation, and test sets with a ratio of 6:2:2.

The ATPOS dataset29 originates from the Kaggle competition ”ATPOS2019 Blindness Detection.” It was collected by doctors from a hospital in India using various devices in rural areas and annotated by professional physicians. In terms of data preprocessing, since most images contain large black margins around the eyeball and the eyeball’s position varies within the image, these unnecessary elements are removed by cropping the retinal images to eliminate the extra black margins, ensuring that the eyeball is centered. For training, we apply random flipping, cropping, and contrast enhancement to each image per epoch to increase image diversity, and finally, the image size is uniformly adjusted to 512 × 512. We adopt an initial learning rate of 5e-4 combined with a learning rate scheduler30 to train the network. The number of epochs is set to 50. During training, we use the Adam optimizer and the cross-entropy loss function, with the batch size set to 8. The experiments are conducted using the PyTorch framework on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 GPU equipped with 24 GB of memory.

Discussion

The MAFNet model proposed in this study performs exceptionally well in the fine-grained classification task of diabetic retinopathy (DR). The quadratic weighted Kappa values obtained on three different datasets (DDR, Messidor-2, and ATPOS) are 0.934, 0.917, and 0.936, with accuracy rates of 0.882, 0.822, and 0.874, respectively, and a parameter count of 83.89 M. This fully demonstrates MAFNet’s strong generalization capability. The key to this breakthrough lies in the model’s unique design advantages in capturing and modeling fine-grained lesions features.

The Hierarchical Global Context Module (HGCM): Its core innovation lies in overcoming the limitations of traditional single receptive field models. By expanding the receptive field range, HGCM effectively captures both the micro-features (small scale) and overall lesions morphology (large scale) of lesions, and establishes a deep synergistic fusion mechanism between global and local features. This significantly enhances the model’s ability to understand complex morphological information of lesions.The Multi-scale Adaptive Attention Module (MSAM): Its effectiveness stems from a dynamic feature weight adjustment mechanism. By emphasizing the response of key lesions areas (such as microaneurysms and exudates) while suppressing non-key or background areas, MSAM significantly enhances the discriminative power and adaptability of feature representations, thereby improving the accuracy of fine-grained grading.The Relationship Multi-Head Attention Module (RMA): Innovatively utilizes a multi-head attention mechanism to enhance the model’s ability to model complex relationships between features. This enables the model to more accurately understand the intrinsic connections and contextual information of lesions areas, significantly improving the accuracy and robustness of fine-grained lesions recognition. The Multi-task learning framework: The collaborative optimization design of classification and regression tasks is an important factor in achieving good results. While ensuring accurate classification, this framework strengthens the ability to characterize the continuous progression of lesions, providing support for more refined grading. It is through these advantages that the model achieves high accuracy and quadratic-weighted Kappa while maintaining moderate parameter counts.

The core advantage of MAFNet over traditional methods or single-module networks lies in its systematic multi-scale, adaptive, relationship modeling, and collaborative optimization capabilities: Multi-scale modeling (vs. single receptive field): HGCM solves the problem of traditional models being unable to accommodate lesion characteristics of both large and small scales. Precise Focus (vs. Static or Simple Attention): MSAM achieves high sensitivity to key lesions regions through adaptive weight adjustment, which is difficult to match with static filters or basic attention models.Complex Relationship Analysis (vs.Local Feature Extraction): RMA can deeply understand the complex relationships between lesions features, offering stronger contextual understanding capabilities compared to models that only focus on local features (such as some CNNs).Task Synergy Optimization (vs. Single Task): A multi-task framework that integrates classification and regression tasks optimizes the continuity and accuracy of fine-grained recognition results.

Conclusion

This study introduces the MAFNet model, an innovative solution for automated fine-grained grading of DR. Through experimental validation on three diverse datasets—DDR, Messidor-2, and ATPOS—MAFNet achieved extremely high quadratic weighted Kappa values (0.934, 0.917, 0.936), fully demonstrating its outstanding performance and robust cross-dataset generalization capability in DR grading tasks. The key innovation of this model lies in its three carefully designed core modules: the Hierarchical Global Context Module (HGCM) enables the collaborative capture of multi-scale lesions features; the Multi-Scale Adaptive Attention Module (MSAM) significantly enhances sensitivity to critical lesions regions; and the Relational Multi-Head Attention Module (RMA) strengthens the modeling and understanding of complex feature relationships. Additionally, the introduced multi-task learning framework effectively promotes collaborative optimization across different task objectives, driving breakthroughs in model performance, particularly in capturing subtle lesions differences and continuous disease progression features. This architecture design, which integrates multi-scale features, adaptive attention, relationship modeling, and collaborative optimization, provides valuable reference for future medical image analysis research.

Although MAFNet has made progress in several aspects, some limitations still exist. First, the model’s structure is relatively complex, incorporating multiple attention modules and a multi-task learning mechanism, which results in high computational costs during training and inference. Future research could explore more efficient network architectures to reduce computational overhead. Although the results on the three datasets are satisfactory, distribution differences and annotation consistency issues between different datasets need to be further validated on larger and more diverse datasets to ensure the model’s universality and robustness. Moreover, this study mainly focuses on the automated grading of DR; future work could consider extending MAFNet to the detection and grading of other ophthalmic diseases, such as glaucoma and macular degeneration. Integrating multi-modal data (e.g., OCT images and visual function test results) for joint analysis may further enhance the model’s diagnostic capability and clinical application value.

MAFNet offers an effective solution for the automated grading of DR, and its design in multi-scale feature capture, complex relationship modeling, and multi-task co-optimization provides valuable insights for future research in medical image analysis.

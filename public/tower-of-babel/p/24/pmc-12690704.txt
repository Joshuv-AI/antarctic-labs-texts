PDNO-24 DIAGNOSTIC ASPECTS OF CHILDHOOD BRAIN TUMORS AT THE BINGERVILLE MOTHER AND CHILD HOSPITAL FROM 2018 TO 2023.
Authors: Atteby Jean-Jacques Yao, Joseph Diarra
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionChildhood brain tumors (CBT) are the most common pediatric solid tumors in the West. Their incidence is estimated at 2.5 cases/100,000 inhabitants/year. If their diagnosis has been revolutionized in recent years by medical imaging, TCE continues to pose three types of problems : diagnostic delay, access to treatment and management of after-effects.MethodThe objective of our study was to explore the diagnostic difficulties of TCE. We conducted a retrospective study with a descriptive aim from May 2018 to December 2023. Included on the files of children aged 0 to 18 years followed for a documented brain tumor. Sociodemographic, clinical and paraclinical data were collected and analyzed by EPI Info 3.5.4.ResultsOver the 05-year period, 07 cases of confirmed TCE were found out of 304 cases admitted, i.e. a frequency of 2.2%. The sex ratio was 1.3. The average age was 10.33 years with extremes ranging from 6 to 16 years. Consultations were late in 85.71%. Intracranial hypertension syndromes and seizures were the main warning signs in 85.71% and 57.14%. All patients had either an MRI 57% or a CT scan 43%. Medulloblastoma and astrocytoma were the most suspicious histological types on imaging with 28% and 57% respectively. Three patients underwent surgical excision, three others retained a bypass (2 DVP and 1 VCS). Chemotherapy (Vinblastine and bleomycin) was used in only one patient (14.29%). The evolution resulted in : 1 death before treatment, hydrocephalus (57.14%), recurrence (28%) and loss to follow-up (57%).ConclusionTCEs are underdetermined and pose a management problem. This should be considered when faced with ICH syndrome and convulsive seizures.

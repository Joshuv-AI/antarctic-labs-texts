The rationality of negative margin criteria for conization in early cervical cancer - a cohort study
Authors: Na Li, Zelin Yu, Xiao Li, Yuan Gao, Yuanjing Hu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe 2023 (1st Edition) NCCN guideline changed the negative margin criteria for cervical conization from ≥ 3 mm to ≥ 1 mm. To explore the clinical safety of this up-dation in the real world, we performed a retrospective study to validate the rationality of the negative margin criteria for conization in patients with early-stage cervical cancer. We retrospectively collected the clinical and pathological data of 986 patients with early-stage cervical cancer from June 2011 to June 2023. All of the patients underwent conization and secondary (radical) hysterectomy at Tianjin Central Hospital of Gynecology Obstetrics. Patients were divided into four groups based on the status of the conization margins: margin < 1 mm (n = 132), margin < 3 mm (n = 147), margin involved (n = 443), and margin not involved (n = 264). The safety of the negative margin criteria for conization (< 1 mm) was evaluated by comparing the residual disease rates in different groups after secondary (radical) hysterectomy. Additionally, univariate and logistic multivariate analyses were conducted to identify the risk factors for residual disease after hysterectomy. A total of 986 patients with cervical cancer at stages IA1-IB1 were included. Among these patients, 382 had residual diseases (HSIL or higher) after hysterectomy, resulting in a residual disease rate of approximately 38.7%. Patients were categorized based on margin status (< 1 mm, <3 mm, margin involved, and, margin not involved). There were statistically significant differences in residual disease rates among the four groups (P < 0.05). There is no significant difference in residual disease rates between patients with the margin < 1 mm and margin < 3 mm, nor between these two groups and the margin not involved group. However, the residual disease rates in cases with margins < 1 mm and margins < 3 mm were significantly lower compared to those with margin involvement. Univariate analysis indicated that multiple factors, including age, were associated with residual disease. However, multivariate analysis revealed that age (P = 0.002, OR = 1.041, 95% CI 1.014–1.069), involvement of the margin by the lesion (P < 0.001, OR = 7.655, 95% CI 4.745–12.349), and cervical biopsy pathology showing cancer (P = 0.020, OR = 1.571, 95% CI 1.074–2.298) were independent risk factors for residual disease after hysterectomy in early-stage cervical cancer patients. Our findings suggest that a ≥ 1 mm negative margin criterion appears to be a safe threshold for early-stage cervical cancer, yet prospective validation is still required.Keywords: Early cervical cancer, Conization, Positive margin, Residual disease

Introduction

Cervical cancer is one of the most common malignant tumors in women [1, 2]. Although the overall incidence of cervical cancer has declined, the age of onset is trending younger [3]. Therefore, addressing the reproductive needs of these patients is of utmost importance [4].

Cold knife cone biopsy (CKC) is an important diagnostic and therapeutic procedure for cervical precancerous lesions, cervical carcinoma in situ, and early-stage cervical cancers. It is widely used in clinical practice [5]. However, this procedure can impact cervical function [6, 7]. Therefore, doctors are increasingly inclined to preserve as much cervical tissue as possible to minimize cervical damage, providing patients with a better quality of life.

In recent years, the minimum margin distance standard for negative margins after cervical conization has undergone significant changes. The “2018 National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) Clinical Practice Guidelines for Cervical Cancer” defined a negative margin as > 3 mm, this criteria was revised to ≥ 3 mm in 2021, which was further updated to ≥ 1 mm in 2023. Some literature defines margin involvement as a positive margin [8, 9]. The update of the 2023 NCCN guidelines regarding the standard for negative margins was mainly based on the results of the Concerv trial [10]. In the Concerv trial, the negative margin was defined as a lesion-to-margin distance ≥ 1 mm and the results showed 5% of positive lymph node rate after conization, the residual lesion rate was 2.5%, and the total recurrence rate within 2 years was 3.5%. The feasibility of conservative surgery and the oncologic safety of the defined negative margin for early low-risk cervical cancer patients provided support for the margin criteria evolution. This study primarily analyzes the safety of reducing the range of positive margins after cone biopsy, with the aim of providing a reference for personalized treatment of early cervical cancer patients who wish to preserve fertility [6].

Materials and methods

Population data

The study retrospectively collected patients with International Federation of Gynecology and.

Obstetrics (FIGO) 2018 stage IA1-IB1 cervical cancer who underwent surgical treatment at Tianjin Central Hospital of Gynecology Obstetrics from June 2010 to June 2023. Patients who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria were included in the study. Inclusion Criteria: (1) Age > 18 years. (2) Primary surgery was a cold knife cone biopsy, followed by a second surgery of (radical) hysterectomy. (3) Cervical cancer and staging confirmed by conization pathology diagnosis. (4) Histological types include squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, and adenosquamous carcinoma. (5) No adjuvant therapy before surgery. Exclusion Criteria: (1) Incomplete medical records, such as missing cytological examination results or colposcopy findings. (2) Adjuvant therapy before surgery. (3) Conization only. (4) Presence of severe underlying diseases or other malignancies. An informed consent was obtained from each participant included in the study.

Research methods

The following information was reviewed from the electronic medical record system according to a predefined recording form: age, cytology, colposcopy results, glandular involvement, margin status, lymph-vascular space invasion (LVSI) and so on. The patients’ follow-up were done by two experienced gynecologists.

Diagnostic criteria

Positive margin is defined as the presence of cervical disease (High-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (HSIL) or higher) at the margin or near the margin of the cone specimen (< 1 mm or < 3 mm).For classification purposes in this study, the “<1 mm group” includes cases in which the distance between the lesion and the resection margin is < 1 mm, the < 3 mm group includes patients with a lesion-to-margin distance ≥ 1 mm but < 3 mm, and any specimen with a lesion-to-margin distance ≥ 3 mm is assigned to the “Not Involved group”. Residual disease refers to the presence of lesions with HSIL + confirmed by pathology after (radical) hysterectomy within 6 months after the initial conization.

Statistical analysis

All analyses were performed using SPSS version 27.0. We used standard descriptive statistical analyses, including determining absolute and relative frequencies for categorical variables and the median with the 5th to 95th percentile range for continuous variables. Univariate and multivariate analyses were conducted using a binary logistic regression model. All comparisons were two-tailed with a significance level (ɑ) of 0.05. A p-value below this threshold indicated a significant difference. In stratified analysis, the significance level was adjusted by dividing ɑ by the number of layers, with a p-value below the adjusted level considered significant. In this study, the intergroup comparison was divided into six layers, so the significance threshold was adjusted to aadj ≈ 0.0084 (0.05 divided by 6).

Residual disease rates

To evaluate the clinical safety of standard negative margin, residual disease rates after secondary (radical) hysterectomy were compared among the four groups (< 1 mm group, <3 mm group, Involved and Not involved groups). Patients with stage IA1 (n = 735) and stage IA2-IB1 (n = 251) were stratified and analyzed among the four groups, respectively. Among patients undergoing secondary (radical) hysterectomy, residual lesions were observed in 30 cases with a resection margin < 1 mm (vs. 102 without residual lesions), and in 20 cases with a margin < 3 mm (vs. 127 without residual lesions). When the resection margin was involved, 284 patients had residual lesions (vs. 159 without), whereas only 48 cases showed residual lesions when the margin was not involved (vs. 216 without) (Table 2).As shown in Figs. 2 and 3, for patients with stage IA1 or IA2-IB1, there was no statistically significant differences in the residual disease rates between patients with margin < 1 mm and the margin < 3 mm (P > 0.0084) (Figs. 2a and 3a). Additionally, there were no significant differences in residual disease rates between cases in < 1 mm group or < 3 mm group compared to those in Not involved group (P > 0.0084) (Figs. 2c and e and 3c and e). However, the residual disease rates were significantly lower in women with margin < 1 mm or margin < 3 mm compared to patients with margin involvement after CKC (P < 0.001) (Figs. 2b and d and 3b and d).

Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses

In order to further analyze the risk factors which might affect the status of surgical margins after conization. Univariate analysis was performed on variables including age, colposcopy biopsy pathology, cone width/height, margin status, LVSI etc. It was found that age, cone height, cone width, stage, menopausal status, colposcopy biopsy pathology, margin status, cone margin histology, and LVSI were significantly associated with positive residual disease(P < 0.05).

Factors with statistically significant differences in the univariate analysis were included in a multivariate logistic regression model. The results in Table 3 showed that: age (P = 0.002, OR = 1.041, 95% CI 1.014–1.069), lesion involvement at the margin (P < 0.001, OR = 7.655, 95% CI 4.745–12.349), cervical biopsy under colposcopy showed cancer (P = 0.020, OR = 1.571, 95% CI 1.074–2.298) are independent risk factors for positive residual disease following hysterectomy in these patients.

Discussion

Cervical cancer is the fourth most common malignancy among women globally [11]. Based on pertinent research, it has been observed that there has been a significant rise in the occurrence of cervical cancer among female individuals of younger age groups [12]. Surgery is the primary treatment for patients with stage IA1-IB1 [13]. Cold knife conization has emerged as a pivotal diagnostic and therapeutic methodology for patients suffering from cervical dysplasia and early-stage cervical cancer. It involves the removal of a cone-shaped section of the cervix, which includes the entire transformation zone and any suspicious cervical lesions. However, CKC may subsequently leads to a reduction in the length of the cervix and may potentially impact its normal function [7]. Furthermore, the decision for further surgical treatment, such as hysterectomy, should be carefully considered based on the individual patient’s condition and the pathology results. In the “2023 NCCN Clinical Practice Guidelines for Cervical Cancer (1st Edition)”, the definition of negative margin after conization for early cervical cancer was revised from ≥ 3 mm to ≥ 1 mm [14], but its clinical safety has not yet been validated in patients with early-stage cervical cancer.

Patients who desire to preserve fertility, such as those with stage IA1 and negative LVSI, as well as those with stage IA2-IB1who fulfilled the conservative criteria, may initially consider undergoing conization. In this study, patients were classified into stage IA1 and stage IA2-IB1 groups based on FIGO2018 cervical cancer staging system. Among patients with stage IA1, there was no significant differences of residual disease rates between < 1 mm group and < 3 mm group. Similarly, for stage IA2-IB1, although the depth of invasion increased, no significant difference in residual disease rates was observed between cases with margin < 1 mm and those with margin < 3 mm. The presented data indicate that the revised standard of a negative margin from ≥ 3 mm to ≥ 1 mm represents a safe and feasible strategy in the management of early-stage cervical cancer.

Previously, many studies have been conducted to investigate the relationship between margin status and residual disease after CKC. The residual disease rates in patients with a positive margin is approximately five times higher than that in those with a negative margin [15]. Following a secondary surgery of patients with CINIII or early-stage cervical cancer, the residual disease rate of patients with positive margins ranges from approximately 34.2–63% [16, 17]. Studies indicate that among early-stage cervical cancer patients, the residual disease rate is 11.5%−13% with negative margins, but rises significantly to 42.5%−68% when margins are positive. Our observed rate of 38.7% aligns well with the values reported in the literatures [18]. Our observed rate of 38.7% aligns well with the values reported in the literatures. Despite the standardized definition of a positive margin as being less than 1 mm, researchers have varied opinions upon this. Normally, a positive margin is typically defined as a scenario where a lesion is observed to be within 1–3 mm of the cone resection margin, and/or the margin itself is directly involved. In such cases, the rate of residual disease and recurrence falls within the range of approximately 20.5–55% [19, 20]. In this study, the residual disease rates were significantly lower in women with margin < 1 mm or margin < 3 mm compared to patients with margin involvement after CKC. However, there was no significant difference in residual disease rates between the < 1 mm and < 3 mm margin groups (P > 0.0084), nor between these groups and the no-margin involvement group (P > 0.0084).

A rigorous study, focusing on patients undergoing Loop Electrosurgical Excision Procedure (LEEP), has definitively established that there exists no substantial correlation between the persistence of HSIL or the occurrence of more severe lesions post-LEEP and the proximity of the lesion to the surgical margin being under 1 mm [21]. F.Sopracordevole et al. assessed the differences in residual disease and recurrence rates among patients with different margin status (margin < 1 mm, margin involving the edge, and negative margin) who underwent CO2 laser cervical conization. The results conclusively affirmed that females who underwent CO2 laser excision for high-grade cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN) and subsequently exhibited close and negative endocervical margins, exhibited an equivalent risk of positivity, disease persistence, and disease recurrence during the long-term follow-up period, specifically between 13 and 24 months post-excision.

Therefore, it was concluded that a cervical margin measuring less than 1 mm should not be categorized as posing an elevated risk for treatment failure [22]. Additionally, some studies define the involvement of HSIL in the margin of the tissue by initial conization as positive margins [23]. Therefore, it remains imperative to conduct a comprehensive prospective study with a large number of cases to ascertain whether the stipulated positive resection margin threshold can be subject to further refinement.

Accurately identifying factors that serve as reliable predictors of the risk of residual disease following cervical conization is of paramount importance in guiding subsequent patient management strategies and treatment modalities. Age and menopausal status have been confirmed as effective indicators for predicting residual disease [24, 25]. A meta-analysis included 15 studies, 4 studies explicitly indicated that patients over the age of 50 had a significantly increased risk of residual disease or recurrence after conization [26]. Several researchers have posited that the elevated risk of residual disease among postmenopausal patients may stem from a confluence of factors, including reduced hormonal levels, cervical atrophy, and heightened challenges in achieving a comprehensive excision of the affected lesion [27]. The study by Bilibio et al. also confirmed a close association between menopausal status and a higher risk of persistent residual disease, with an incidence rate of 81.2% [9]. In the multivariate analysis, only menopausal status was significantly associated with a higher risk of persistent disease. In this study, univariate analysis showed that both age and menopausal status were associated with residual disease after conization. Furthermore, age was identified as an independent risk factor for residual disease.

In addition, some scholars have suggested that for early-stage cervical cancer patients, residual disease after CKC may be related to factors such as age, menopausal status, number of pregnancies, conization margin status, and LVSI [16, 28–30]. However, in the logistic multivariate analysis of this study, only age (P = 0.002, OR = 1.041, 95%CI 1.014–1.069), involvement of the conization margin (P < 0.001, OR = 7.655, 95%CI 4.745–12.349), and pathological confirmation of cancer on colposcopic cervical biopsy (P = 0.020, OR = 1.571, 95%CI 1.074–2.298) were identified as independent high-risk factors for lesion residuals.

In prior research endeavors, the majority of studies that have investigated positive margins and residual disease have focused primarily on cervical precancerous lesions. However, our analysis is founded upon the revised NCCN guidelines for cervical conization, and it delves deeply into the precise definition of positive margins, along with their correlation to residual disease in patients who have been diagnosed with early-stage cervical cancer. Due to the small number of relapsed patients, stratified analysis could not be further performed. This is indeed a limitation in our study. We will continue to accumulate cases for further in-depth research in the future. Strengths of this study include the robust cohort of 986 patients with comprehensive follow-up (median 78 months). We believe that our current study could be able to provide clinically supports for the evolving of clinical guidelines.

Conclusions

In conclusion, it is deemed safe for patients with early-stage cervical cancer to adjust the definition of a negative margin after cervical conization from ≥ 3 mm to ≥ 1 mm. Furthermore, age, margin involvement, and the detection of cancer in biopsy specimens are individual and significant risk factors that contribute to the likelihood of residual disease following the procedure of CKC.

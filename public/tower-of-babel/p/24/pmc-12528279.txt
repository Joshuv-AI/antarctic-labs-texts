RACK1 attenuates pancreatic tumorigenesis by suppressing acinar-to-ductal metaplasia through inflammatory signaling modulation
Authors: Wei Zhang, Tingting Jiang, Huiqing Zhang, Fang Wei, Xiaojia Li, Keping Xie
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposePancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDA) remains one of the most lethal malignancies with limited early diagnostic and therapeutic options. Although receptor for activated C kinase 1 (RACK1) is an evolutionarily conserved scaffold protein, its functional role and mechanistic involvement in PDA pathogenesis remain elusive.MethodsUsing multimodal approaches including: (1) genetically engineered mouse models of pancreatitis and carcinogenesis, (2) patient-derived PDA tissues with matched normal specimens, (3) primary acinar cell 3D cultures, and (4) orthogonal gain/loss-of-function assays in PDA cell lines, we systematically investigated RACK1’s spatiotemporal expression patterns and functional impacts. Mechanistic dissection was performed through gene expression profiling and pathway enrichment analyses with functional validation.ResultsRACK1 exhibited progressive silencing across pancreatic lesion progression: acinar cells (normal) > ADM > pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasia (PanIN) > PDA. This acinar-specific protein was undetectable in ductal/islet lineages and was further suppressed under inflammatory challenge. Functionally, RACK1 depletion accelerated ADM initiation and enhanced PDA cell motility and metastatic dissemination in vivo, whereas its overexpression exerted tumor-suppressive effects. Mechanistically, caerulein/TGF-α stimulation and KrasG12D activation converged to inhibit RACK1 while activating MAP2K3-SRC-RELA(p65) signaling nodes, establishing a pro-inflammatory feedforward loop.ConclusionsRACK1 serves as a gatekeeper restraining inflammation-driven ADM transformation, with its downregulation constituting an early molecular event in PDA pathogenesis. The RACK1-MAP2K3 axis orchestrates malignant transition through simultaneous NF-κB activation (inflammatory priming) and MAPK hyperactivation (proliferative drive). Our findings nominate RACK1 as both a stratification biomarker for high-risk pancreatic lesions and a druggable node for intercepting preneoplastic progression.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s13402-025-01084-3.

Introduction

PDA is one of the most lethal cancer with the mortality nearly equal to the incidence global scale in 2020 [1]. The PDA patients with a five-year survival rate is below 10%, the lowest in comparison with common cancers [2]. This is mainly due to the fact that PDA is often asymptomatic and hard for early diagnosis. Despite advances in diagnostic techniques and drug treatments in recent years, the mortality rate of PDA has continued to rise [3].

Pancreatitis is inflammation of the pancreas that is an established risk factor for PDA [4]. Chronic pancreatitis (CP) occurs over many years and increases cell turnover and stellate cell proliferation that promotes tumorigenesis [4]. PDA develops through a prolonged, multi-step process of genomic and epigenomic evolution that typically spans decades, with the highest incidence occurring in elderly populations [5]. The acinar to ductal metaplasia (ADM) is a reversible process during pancreas injury or pancreatitis, and becomes irreversible with Kras mutations [5]. ADM cells further progress to duct-like cells that form premalignancy pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasia (PanIN) and PDA [6]. The morphological alterations characteristic of acinar-to-ductal metaplasia (ADM) include: cellular flattening, increased nuclear chromatin density, and nucleolar enlargement, accompanied by the disappearance of zymogen granules and degeneration of the apical cytoplasm. Acinar cells are progressively replaced by duct-like structures, exhibiting molecular features characterized by significant upregulation of ductal cell-specific markers (e.g., CK19, SOX9) [7, 8] and marked downregulation of acinar cell markers (e.g., amylase, Mist-1) [9, 10].

The development of PanIN is classified into three grades based on histological atypia and molecular markers: PanIN-1 exhibits flat (1 A) or papillary (1B) architecture with columnar epithelium containing basally oriented, regularly arranged nuclei [11]. PanIN-2 demonstrates mild to moderate nuclear atypia, including hyperchromatic and enlarged nuclei with partial loss of cellular polarity [11]. PanIN-3 displays overt malignant features, such as cribriform or micropapillary structures, widespread nuclear polarity loss, pleomorphic nuclei, and pathological mitoses [11]. Discovering and understanding the mechanisms of PDA premalignancy are extremely important to early diagnosis and effective control of this deadly disease and improve the outcome of patients.

RACK1, also known as Gnb2L1, is a very conserved intracellular linker protein, a member of the tryptophan-aspartate repeats motifs (WD-repeat) family, and highly homologous to the β subunit of the G protein [12]. RACK1 has a 7-bladed propeller structure that binds signaling molecules from different transduction pathways and plays the role of a multifunctional linker protein in a variety of different biological events, including viral infection, nervous system development, cell migration, angiogenesis, and etc [12]. RACK1 linker protein is a protein in the signal transduction process, has Src homologous domains 2 and 3 (i.e., SH2, SH3), and lacks Src homologous domain 1 (i.e., SH1), and has no tyrosine kinase activity, while its SH2 can recognize binding receptor tyrosine kinase, and SH3 can mediate the interaction between proteins, thus transducing signal [12].

Pioneering work in genetically engineered mice revealed that TGF-α-induced ADM represents an early event in pancreatic adenocarcinoma pathogenesis [13]. 3D cell culture systems have further established ADM induction, where primary murine acinar cells undergo spontaneous ductal transdifferentiation when exposed to internal or external stress signalling including: (1) oncogenic KRAS activation, (2) inflammatory cytokines, or (3) EGFR-stimulating growth factors [5, 14–16]. TGF-α is a well-established ligand for EGFR, which synergizes with KRAS mutations to drive pancreatic ADM and early tumorigenesis. TGF-α is upregulated in human PDAC tissues compared with chronic pancreatitis [17].

RACK1 protein or mRNA is down-regulated in breast cancer, gastric cancer and pancreatic cancer, and promotes tumorigenesis by regulating biological processes such as signal transduction, ubiquitination, transcription, RNA processing, and cell cycle [18–21]. In contrast, RACK1 is upregulated in non-small cell lung cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, and squamous cell carcinoma [22–24]. While the role of RACK1 in cancer remains controversial, its role and molecular mechanism of RACK1 in the PDA premalignant lesions are unknown.

In the present study, we investigated the expression and function of RACK1 in pancreatic ADM and PanIN using in vitro and in vivo models, and determined the impacts of altered RACK1 on oncogenic signaling in PDA biology.

Materials and methods

in vivo animal studies

All mice experiments were performed following institutional guidelines and approved by the South China University of Technology School of Medicine. Mice were housed in a pathogen-free animal facility at the Laboratory Animal Center at the South China University of Technology School of Medicine. The mice were maintained in a 22 °C thermostatic room, and on a 12 h light-dark cycle. Both male and female mice were used in our study. For acute pancreatitis, 8 weeks old C57BL/6 mice received intraperitoneal injections of caerulein (CAE) once every 1 h for 8 times for 2 consecutive days at a dose of 50 µg/kg/body weight. Control mice received saline injection. The last injection of caerulein was defined as day 0. Pancreatic duct ligation (PDL) was performed as previously reported using 8 weeks old C57BL/6 (RRID: MGI:2159769) mice [25, 26]. The Pdx-Cre;LSL-KrasG12D (KC) mice and Pdx-Cre;LSL-Trp53R172H/+;LSL-KrasG12D (KPC) mice as described as before [27, 28] were purchased from Shanghai Model Organisms Center, Inc. (Shanghai, China). All mouse model experiments were approved by the Ethics Committee for Animal Experiments of South China University of Technology (Guangzhou, China) (approval no. 2024003).

Cell cultures and gene transfection

AsPC-1 (RRID: CVCL_0152), BxPC-3 (RRID: CVCL_0186) and PK-59 (RRID: CVCL_4897) were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (P/S). HPAC (RRID: CVCL_3517), 266-6 (RRID: CVCL_3481), PANC-1 (RRID: CVCL_0480) and Panc02 (RRID: CVCL_D627) were maintained in DMEM medium with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (P/S). All cell lines were obtained from ATCC and grown in a humidified atmosphere incubator containing 5% CO2 at 37 °C. All the cell lines were routinely tested for mycoplasma contamination within the last 6 months using PCR, and used at passage numbers < 15 for this study after reception or thawing in our laboratory.

The control siRNA and RACK1 siRNA were synthesized by Genechem Biotechnology (Shanghai, China). The control siRNA sequence was 5’-UUC UCC GAA CGU GUC ACG UTT-3’, 3’-ACG UGA CAC GUU CGG AGA ATT-5’, and RACK1-specific siRNA target sequence: RACK1-siRNA#1, 5’-AAG GAU GGC CAG GCC AUG UUA − 3’, 3’-UAA CAU GGC CUG GCC AUC CUU-5’; RACK1-siRNA#2, 5’-AAG CAA GAA GUU AUC AGC ACC-3’, 3’-GGU GCU GAU AAC UUC UUG CUU-5’; RACK1-siRNA#3, 5’-AAG AUC AUU GUA GAU GAA CUG-3’, 3’-CAG UUC AUC UAC AAU GAU CUU-5’. The mouse and human Flag-RACK1 plasmid and pcDNA3.1 was purchased from GenScript (Nanjing, China). Cell transfection was performed using Lipofectamine 3000 according to the manufacturer’s protocols.

Explant culture of mouse pancreatic acinar cells

Explant cultures of mouse pancreatic acinar cells were modified using previously published protocols [26, 29]. In brief, the pancreas was minced quickly with no larger than 1 mm in diameter and was digested with 0.2 mg/ml collagenase P (Roche, Basel, Switzerland), 0.25 mg/ml of Soybean trypsin inhibitor (STI; Sigma-Aldrich, German), 0.1 mg/ml DNase I (Sigma-Aldrich) in HBSS. The individual acini clusters were resuspended in 1 ~ 2 ml culture medium by supplemented with RPMI 1640 GlutaMAX™, HEPES,1% FBS, 1% Pen-Strep (Life Technologies, Gibco), 0.25 mg/ml STI, 1 µg/mL Dexamethasone (Sigma-Aldrich), were counted with Countess™ (Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Cultures were grown at 37 °C and 5% CO2 in a humidified atmosphere incubator. The medium was changed on day 1 and 3. The mouse pancreatic acinar cells transfection was performed using RFectPM (Biodai, Changzhou, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocols.

Western blot analysis

The proteins from cells or fresh frozen tissues were extracted by RIPA buffer. A 50 µg of total protein was resolved by 10% SDS–PAGE and transferred onto a polyvinylidene fluoride membrane (Millipore, Bedford, MA, USA). After being blocked in 5% nonfat milk for 2 h, the membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with the corresponding primary antibodies against anti-RACK1 (1:5000; Abcam, Cambridge, UK, Cat# ab62735, RRID: AB_956255), Anti-MEK3 (1:2000; Abcam, Cambridge, UK, Cat# ab5428, RRID: AB_304881), anti-RELA (1:2000; Santa Cruz Biotechnology, USA, Cat# sc-81622, RRID: AB_1128533), anti-SRC (1:2000, Santa Cruz Biotechnology, USA, Cat# sc-8056, RRID: AB_627306), anti-CK19 (1:5000, Abcam, Cambridge, UK, Cat# ab52625, RRID: AB_2281020), anti-amylase (1:5000, Thermo Fisher Scientific Cat# PA5-117115, RRID: AB_2901745), anti-α-tubulin (1:5000; Proteintech Cat# 11224-1-AP, RRID: AB_2210206), and anti-β-actin (1:5000; Proteintech Cat# 66009-1-Ig, RRID: AB_2687938), and then were incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated goat anti-mouse or goat anti-rabbit secondary antibodies (1:10,000; Proteintech) for 2 h at room temperature. The detection of specific signals was performed by ECL method (Beyotime Biotechnology, China) and Amersham Imager (GE Healthcare Life Sciences, USA).

Real-time PCR

Total RNA was extracted from pancreas tissues or cells with Trizol reagent. Reverse transcription was conducted with Primescript cDNA Synthesis Mix reagent (Takara, Japan). Real-time PCR was performed on an LightCycler 96 system (Roche, Basel, Switzerland) using TB Green Premix Ex Taq (Takara, Japan). The amplification conditions were as follows: 95°C, 10 min; then followed by 40 cycles (95°C, 30 sec; 95°C, 5 sec; 60°C, 30 sec). The genes expression level was normalized to GAPDH by using LightCycler Software Version 10.0 (Roche, Basel, Switzerland). The primers for mouse RACK1: 5’-ATGGTCAGTTTGCCCTCTCG-3’ (forward), 3’-GGAGAAGCGGACACAAGACA-5’ (backward); and the primers for human RACK1: 5’-GGGGTCACTCCCACTTTGTT-3’ (forward), 3’-AATCTGCCGGTTGTCAGAGG-5’ (backward).

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

The pancreatic cancer of tissue microarray was purchased from Outdo Biotech (HPanA170Su04, Shanghai, China). The sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with anti-RACK1 rabbit polyclonal antibody (1:500; Abcam, Cambridge, UK, Cat# ab62735, RRID: AB_956255). All images were captured using the panoramic digital slide scanner (Wisleap WS-10, China). For data quantitative analysis of the staining was recorded based on the staining density, staining scores < 2 were indicated low expression, and scores of 2–3 was defined as high expression.

Immunofluorescence (IF)

The paraffin slides from mice tissue specimens, after embedded and sectioned, were de-paraffinized in xylene I and II for 15 min, respectively, and dehydrated in 100, 95, 80, 70% ethanol, and H2O for 10 min, respectively. The endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked by 3% hydrogen peroxide for 10 min. The pancreatic acinar cells specimens were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and were mounted on gelatin-coated slides. All the sections were blocked in 0.3% TritonX-100 and 10% normal goat serum (NGS) in PBS on a rocker (35 rpm/min) for 1 h. The sections were incubated for 2 ~ 3 h at 4 °C with anti-RACK1 rabbit polyclonal antibody (1:500; Abcam, Cambridge, UK, Cat# ab62735, RRID: AB_956255), anti-CK19 rat monoclonal antibody (1:200, Iowa University, USA, DSHB Cat# TROMA-III, RRID: AB_2133570), and anti-amylase mouse monoclonal antibody (1:200, Santa Cruz Biotechnology, USA, Cat# sc-166349, RRID: AB_2226890). The sections were washed 3 times with PBS and incubated with Goat Anti-Rat IgG H&L (Alexa Fluor® 488, Abcam Cat# ab150165, RRID: AB_2650997), Donkey Anti-Rabbit IgG (Alexa Fluor® 555, Abcam Cat# ab150062, RRID: AB_2801638), and Goat Anti-Mouse IgG H&L (Alexa Fluor® 647, Abcam Cat# ab150115, RRID: AB_2687948) secondary antibodies (1:500, Abcam) for 60 min at room temperature. After washing 3 times with PBS, slipps were mounted using signal enhancer (Image-iT FX Signal Enhancer, Invitrogen). All slides were photographed and analyzed with a Nikon Ti-E A1 Confocal Laser Scanning Microscope (RRID: SCR_020318). The images were processed using Image J.

Flow cytometry analysis

The HPAC, PANC-1, and 266-6 cells transfected with control siRNA and RACK1 siRNA#1, siRNA#2 were cultured for 48 h. The PK-59, and HPAC cells transfected with MOCK, pcDNA3.1, and Flag-RACK1 plasmid were cultured for 72 h. The apoptotic cells were detected by using the BD Pharmingen 556,547 Annexin V-FITC (BD Pharmingen, La Jolla, CA) following the manufacturer’s instructions. The cell suspension was fixed in 70% ethanol at 4 °C for 24 h. After centrifuged, the cell suspension incubated with 1 mg/ml RNase A and stained with propidium iodide (PI, 50 µg/ml) (Becton, Dickinson, BD, NJ, USA) in PBS for 30 min at 4 °C. At last, the cells were detected by using a flow cytometer BD Accuri C6 (Becton, Dickinson, BD, NJ, USA, RRID: SCR_019591) coupled with cell cycle analysis software ModFit (version 3.1, RRID: SCR_016106). Cell cycle profiles were analyzed by ModFit using the Watson model.

Cell proliferation assay

HPAC, PANC-1 and 266-6 cells were seeded on 96-well cell culture plates (Corning, NY, USA) at a concentration of 5 × 103 cells per well and transfected with control siRNA and RACK1 siRNA#1, siRNA#2. PK-59, HPAC, and 266-6 cells were seeded on 96-well plates at a concentration of 1 × 104 cells per well and transfected with MOCK, pcDNA3.1, and Flag-RACK1 plasmid. We used Cell Counting Kit 8 assay (Dojindo, Kumamoto, Japan) to evaluate cell viability according to the manufacturer’s protocols. The cells were incubated at 37 °C for 2 h, and quantified the absorbance at a wavelength of 450 nm (Infinite M Plex, Tecan, Switzerland).

Colony formation assays

PANC-1, PK-59 and HPAC cells were seeded at 500 cells/well in 35-mm culture dish and transfected with control siRNA and RACK1 siRNA#1, siRNA#2 and MOCK, pcDNA3.1 and Flag-RACK1 plasmids according to manufacturer’s instructions. After being cultured at 37 °C for 14 days, the cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and clearly visible colonies (≥ 50 cells/colony) were counted after staining with 0.5% crystal violet for 30 min.

Scratch assay for wound healing

The cells were seeded in a 6-well plate. After the cells had grown to about 90% confluency, the cell layer was scratched with a 10 µl pipette tip and washed 3 times with PBS, and refed with fresh medium. HPAC, PANC-1 and 266-6 cells were transfected with control siRNA and RACK1 siRNA#1 and siRNA#2. PK-59, HPAC, and 266-6 cells were transfected with MOCK, pcDNA3.1 and Flag-RACK1 plasmids. Images of the cells were captured at 0, 24 and 48 h.

Mouse models of pancreatic Cancer growth and metastasis

For the subcutaneous pancreatic cancer model, HPAC cells transfected with control siRNA and RACK1 siRNA and PK-59 cells transfected with pcDNA3.1 and Flag-RACK1 plasmids were suspended in 100 µL PBS with 50% Matrigel and injected into the right flanks of BALB/c nude mice (6–8 weeks of age, 1 × 106 cells/mouse). Panc02 cells transfected with control siRNA and RACK1 siRNA were suspended in 100 µL PBS with 50% Matrigel and injected into the right flanks of C57BL/6 mice (6–8 weeks of age, 1 × 106 cells/mouse). Mice were sacrificed 45 days after injection and tumor weights were recorded. For the orthotopic model of pancreatic cancer, Panc02 cells transfected with control siRNA and RACK1 siRNA were injected into the pancreas of C57BL/6 mice (6–8 weeks of age, 1 × 106 cells/mouse). For the liver metastasis model, Panc02 cells transfected with control siRNA and RACK1 siRNA were injected into the superior mesenteric vein of C57BL/6 mice (6–8 weeks of age, 1 × 106 cells/mouse). Mice were sacrificed 28 days after injection and liver weights were recorded. The livers were collected, fixed with 10% formalin and embedded in paraffin for further use.

Database and bioinformatics analysis

The pancreatic cancer RNA profiles were download from GEO (GSE71729) and UCSC Xena [30], clinical survival data was download from TCGA and proteomics data was download from the LinkedOmics database. The bioinformatics analysis was carried out by using R software (version 4.1.0).

Single cell RNA-seq analysis

Ptf1aCreER;LSL-KrasG12D;LSL-tdTomato (PRT) transgenic mice of pancreatic tissue single-cell RNA-seq (scRNA-seq) data was downloaded from the NCBI GEO database (GSE141017) [31]. The Seurat analysis workflow was used for single-cell data visualization analysis based on uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) and t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (tSNE) [32, 33]. Differential expression genes were calculated with the Wilcoxon test to determine subcluster transcriptional profiles and compare differential expression genes between two subclusters. The marker gene was used to distinguish the types of cells: acinar cells (Amy1+, Cpa1+, tdTomato+, Krt19−), ductal cells (Amy1−, Cpa1−, tdTomato−, Krt19+), reborn cells (Amy1−, Cpa1−, tdTomato+, Krt19+), and tumor cells (Amy1−, Cpa1−, tdTomato+, Krt19+, Krt8+, Krt18+, Epcam+).

Trajectory analysis

Trajectories were analyzed using the Monocle3 (https://cole-trapnell-lab.github.io/monocle3, RRID: SCR_018685) and as.cell_data_set functions [34]. Sort the cells in pseudo-time by using the order_cells function with default settings. The trajectory plot is executed by running the plot_cells function set by default.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed with the R software (version 4.1.0, RRID: SCR_001905) and GraphPad Prism software (version 9.0.0, RRID: SCR_002798). Results were represented as mean ± SD from at least 3 experiments. The unpaired t test was used to compare the difference between two groups. A p-value less than 0.05 (p < 0.05) was considered to be statistically significant.

Results

RACK1 is down-regulated in human and mouse pancreatic premalignant lesions

To determine whether RACK1 is involved in PDA, we performed IHC analysis of RACK1 expression using pancreatic tissue microarray. The expression of RACK1 decreased in human ADM and PanIN cells as compared with that in adjacent normal acinar cells (Fig. 1A and E). We also determined RACK1 expression in pancreatic tissues from 23-week-old KPC mice (Pdx-Cre;LSL-Trp53R172H/+;LSL-KrasG12D) [28] and found strong RACK1 staining in adjacent normal acinar cells as compared with weak staining in ADM and PanIN epithelial cells or ductal cells (Fig. 1B and F). RACK1 expression was gradually down-regulated in human PDA tissues and KPC mouse tissues from ADM, PanINs to PDA (Fig. 1C, D, G and H). Survival analysis demonstrated that patients with low expression of RACK had significant poorer survival outcomes than those with high expression of RACK1 (Fig. 1I and S1A). Western blot assay showed that the expression of RACK1 protein in pancreatic acinar cells were much higher than that in pancreatic cancer cell lines (Fig. 1J and L). Consistently, the expression of RACK1 decreased at both mRNA and protein levels in adjacent tissues as compared with that in PDA tissues (Fig. S1B-S1D). The expression of acinar marker amylase decreased (Fig. S1E), while ductal marker CK19 increased in adjacent tissues as compared with that in PDA tissues (Fig. S1F). These results indicated that decreased RACK1 expression be important in PDA initiation and progression.

RACK1 knockdown promotes PDA cell proliferation and migration

To determine the role of RACK1 in PDA cells, we used RACK1 siRNA to reduce the expression of RACK1 in HPAC and PANC-1 cells. All siRNA-transfected cells were harvested at 48 h post-transfection. The siRNA#1 and siRNA#2 decreased RACK1 mRNA and protein expression, as compared with that transfected with control siRNA and MOCK (Fig. 2A and D and S3A-S3B). The siRNA#1 and siRNA#2 decreased RACK1 protein expression, as compared with that transfected with control siRNA (Fig. S3I-S3L). CCK-8 and colony formation assays showed that siRNA#1 and siRNA#2 increased cell proliferation (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2E, H, O and Q). Flow cytometry analysis indicated that the HPAC and PANC-1 cells transfected with RACK1 siRNA#1 and siRNA#2 showed significantly increased population of cells in the S phase (Fig. 2F, G, I and J). Downregulation of RACK1 by siRNA#1 and siRNA#2 promoted the healing of scratch wounds (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2K, L, M and N). These results showed that RACK1 downregulation promotes PDA cell proliferation and migration.

Overexpression of RACK1 inhibits cell proliferation and promotes apoptosis in PDA cells

To determine further the role of RACK1 in PDA growth, the PK-59 and HPAC cells were transfected with pcDNA3.1 and RACK1 plasmid. The RACK1 protein and mRNA expression levels were increased in PK-59 and HPAC cells after transfection (Fig. S2A-S2D and S3C-S3D). Increased expression of RACK1 reduced cell growth (Fig. S2E, S2H and S2O-S2Q), the population of cells in the S phase (Fig. S2F-S2G and S2I-S2J), and healing of scratch wounds (Fig. S2K-S2N), while promoting apoptosis (Fig. S3E-S3H). These results showed that RACK1 overexpression inhibited cell proliferation and migration and promoted apoptosis in PDA cells.

RACK1 knockdown promotes tumor growth and metastasis and overexpression of RACK1 does the opposite

To determine the effect of RACK1 on tumor growth and metastasis, we used the mouse xenograft models of pancreatic cancer. Down-expression of RACK1 in HPAC and Panc02 cells significantly promoted tumor growth in BALB/c nude mice and C57BL/6 mice (Fig. 3A and B, S4A-S4B, Fig. 3C and D and S4C-S4D). Consistently, transfection with RACK1 siRNA significantly promoted liver metastases of Panc02 cells (Fig. S4E and Fig. S4F-S4H). By analyzing GSE71729 database, the expression of RACK1 in metastatic group was significantly lower than that in human PDA cell lines (BxPC-3, CaPan-1, CaPan-2, HPAC, HPAFII, Hs766T, HUPT3, Panc0203, Panc1, SW1990) (Fig. S4I). Moreover, the Kaplan–Meier analysis also indicated that RACK1 knockdown had significantly worse survival outcomes than control group (p < 0.047) (Fig. S4J). In contrast, overexpression of RACK1 in PK-59 cells significantly inhibited tumor growth in BALB/c nude mice (Fig. 3E, F, G and H). These results suggested that altered RACK1 expression had significant impact on tumor growth and metastasis of PDA cells.

RACK1 is enriched in acinar cells and decreases during ADM formation

To determine the critical role of RACK1 in PDA initiation, we first analyzed RACK1 expression in pancreatic tissues from control mice and mice treated with caerulein treatment (CAE), ductal ligation (PDL) and mutant K-ras (KC mice) by hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining (Fig. S5A and S5B), Western blot and qPCR analyses (Fig. 4A and C). RACK1 was predominantly expressed in acinar cells and rarely detectable in ductal and islet cells. RACK1 expression was much higher in the normal pancreatic tissues than in pancreatic tissues from KC, CAE and PDL, particularly in ADM cells, the pivotal initiating phase in malignant transformation of acinar cells upon injury [35]. As evidenced by increased ductal marker CK19 and reduced acinar marker amylase, the decreased expression of RACK1 was directly associated with the increased ADM formation (Fig. 4D and F), while re-expression of RACK1 was directly associated with the decreased ADM formation during recovery phase of acute pancreatitis (Fig. S6A and Fig. S6B-S6F). Similarly, in KC mice at age of 14, 27 and 60 weeks (Fig. S7C), the expression of RACK1 decreased gradually with the increase in age of KC mice, while ADM or PanINs formation increased with decreased amylase expression and increased CK19 expression (Fig. S7A-S7E). Our results demonstrated that RACK1 was highly and specifically expressed in acinar cells and that its expression was down-regulated upon genetic or inflammatory injury, hence promoting ADM formation.

Overexpression of RACK1 suppresses ADM formation

To investigate whether RACK1 regulated ADM during early pancreatitis, we used primary cultures of pancreatic acinar cells from control and CAE-treated C57BL/6 mice [26, 29] (Fig. 5A). We found RACK1 and amylase expression levels gradually decreased in a time-dependent manner, while CK19 expression gradually increased (Fig. 5B and D). Tubular-ductal cellular structures rapidly formed by acinar cells from mice with CAE-induced pancreatitis as compared with that from control mice (Fig. 5C). Also, the number and diameter of tubular-ductal cellular structures from mice treated with CAE were significantly more or larger than that from control mice (Fig. 5E and F).

The transfection efficiency of primary pancreatic acinar cell cultures was confirmed by control siRNA-FAM, RACK1-siRNA#1-FAM, and RACK1-siRNA#2-FAM as indicated by green fluorescence (Fig. S8A-S8B). We also noticed that CK19 increased, whereas RACK1 and amylase decreased in primary pancreatic acinar cells with RACK1 knockdown. Ablation of RACK1 in acinar cells accelerates ADM formation

RACK1 levels were decreased when “ductal structure” formation increased in cultures of WT, KC and KPC primary acinar cells upon RACK1 knockdown (Fig. 5K and M, Fig. S9D-S9F and Fig. S9G-S9I). To determine whether RACK1 was involved in ADM formation and PDA initiation, we used the 266-6 mouse acinar cells. Histologic analysis revealed that RACK1 deletion resulted in more tubular-ductal structures cells in 266-6 cells (Fig. S10A and S10F). The RACK1 siRNA#1 and siRNA#2 depleted the expression level of RACK1 in 266-6 cells (Fig. S10B-S10C), whereas the RACK1 plasmid increased the expression levels of RACK1 in 266-6 cells (Fig. S10D-S10E). Moreover, CCK-8 assay showed that siRNA#1 and siRNA#2 increased cell proliferation (Fig. S10G), whereas overexpression of RACK1 reduced the growth of 266-6 cells (Fig. S10H). These results showed that ablation of RACK1 accelerated ADM formation in 266-6 acinar cells.

To investigate the role of RACK1 in the regulation of apoptosis in acinar cells, 266-6 cells were transfected with RACK1 expression plasmid or control pcDNA3.1. The Annexin-V/PI assay indicated that there was higher proportion of apoptotic cells in the RACK1 plasmid group, as compared with the control groups (Fig. S10I-S10J). Flow cytometry analysis indicated that the 266-6 cells transfected with RACK1 plasmid showed a decreased population of cells in the S phase (Fig. S10K-S10L). In contrast, RACK1 knockdown with siRNA#1 and siRNA#2 accelerated the healing the scratch wound (Fig. S11A-S11B), whereas RACK1 overexpression attenuated wound healing (Fig. S11C-S11D). These results suggested that RACK1 played a vital role in the proliferation, cell cycle, and migration of acinar cells.

RACK1 activates MAPK and NF-κB signaling

By analyzing the RNA-seq dataset of normal mouse pancreatic acinar cells cultured in vitro (GSE240106), we found that the expression level of TGF-α was significantly increased on the 1st and 4th days after culture in vitro (Fig. S12A). TGF-α overexpression synergizes with KRASG12D to accelerate PDA progression in mouse models (Fig. S12B). TGF-α is upregulated in acute pancreatitis and PDA (Fig. S12C-E). The GSE71729 dataset includes sequencing results of normal pancreatic tissue, primary PDAC, pancreatic cancer liver metastasis, pancreatic cancer lung metastasis, and pancreatic cancer lymph node metastasis. Bioinformatics analysis found that compared with normal tissue, the expression of TGF-α in PDAC tissue and metastatic tissue was significantly increased (Fig. S12F). Through the analysis of single-cell datasets (GSE141017), it was found that the expression of TGF-α in the pancreatic tissue of PRT mice increased significantly from the acinar cell population to the metaplastic cell population (Fig. S12G). The analysis of the survival curve of pancreatic cancer patients in the TCGA database showed that patients with high expression of TGF-α had significantly worse survival outcomes than those with low expression (Fig. S12H).

To understand the molecular mechanisms of RACK1 impacts ADM and PDA, we investigated the effects of transforming growth factor α (TGF-α) on RACK1 expression. Treatment of 266-6 and Panc02 cells with TGF-α led to significant induction of RACK1 down-expression (Fig. 6A and B). We found that TGF-α significantly inhibited the expression of RACK1 and upregulated the expression of MAP2K3, RELA and SRC (Fig. 6A and B). Consistently, we used RACK1 siRNA to reduce the expression of RACK1 in HPAC cells. The siRNA#1 and siRNA#2 markedly decreased the protein expression level of RACK1 and upregulated the expression of MAP2K3, RELA and SRC, as compared to cells transfected with control siRNA (Fig. 6A and B).

Analysis of GSE141017 database has found that the expression of mutant Kras in acinar cells increased the expression of Map2k3, Src and Rela progressively from metaplastic cells to tumor cells (Fig. S13A). The trajectory predicted by Monocle3 package indicated that acinar cells first undergo a metaplastic state before developing into a tumor cell lineage (Fig. S13B). Moreover, we studied spatial transcriptomics of primary PDA (Fig. S14A-S14B) and found the expression of RACK1 was decreased in tumor cells compared to adjacent normal cells (Fig. S14C). The expression level of MAP2K3, SRC and RELA was high in tumor cells (Fig. S14D-F). The decreased expression of RACK1 and increased expression of Map2k3, Src and Rela promoted the development of acinar cells into metaplastic cells and PDA (Fig. S13C).

Discussion

RACK1, known for its multiple effects on cell proliferation, apoptosis and migration [12, 24], plays important roles in signaling hubs, including shuttling proteins to specific locations, regulating protein activity, stabilizing protein or promoting protein degradation [18]. In this study, we found that RACK1 expression was progressively down-regulated from acinar to ductal metaplasia (ADM), pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasia (PanIN) to PDA. RACK1 was highly and specifically expressed in acinar cells and was down-regulated upon pancreatic injury or inflammation, whereas RACK1 ablation in pancreatic acinar cells promoted ADM formation. RACK1 deletion promoted cell proliferation and migration of PDA, while increased expression of RACK1 inhibited cell proliferation and migration of PDA. Silence of RACK1 promoted tumorigenesis and liver metastasis of PDA cells in animal models in vivo. CAE, PDL, TGF-α and KRAS mutation significantly inhibited the expression of RACK1 and upregulated the expression of MAP2K3, RELA and SRC. Our data demonstrated that RACK1 plays a critical role in acinar cell biology and PDA development, and RACK1 could serve as a potentially novel molecular target for the early detection and treatment of PDA.

Both protein and mRNA expression levels of RACK1 are highly and specifically expressed in acinar cells, with RACK1 expression being down-regulated upon injury or inflammation. Upon inflammation or injury, its decreased expression levels lead to a metaplastic transformation and ADM formation. Our conclusion is supported by several lines of evidence using primary acinar cell cultures, cell lines and animal models. Using in vitro 3D cell cultures, we found that clusters of mouse acinar cells spontaneously transdifferentiated into tubular structures (ADM) in response to internal or external stress signals, oncogenic KRAS, inflammatory cytokines, or epidermal growth factor receptors (EGFRs). RACK1 ablation caused rapid formation of tubular-ductal structures in caerulein-induced pancreatitis in mice. Moreover, RACK1 deletion also promoted the formation of tubular-ductal structures of ADM in 266-6 cells. The tubular-ductal structures consistent with previous study on primary acinar cell 3D cell cultures in mir-802 knockout mice [37]. These results suggest that loss of RACK1 expression accelerates ADM formation and RACK1 plays a vital role in acinar cell proliferation, cell cycle, and cell migration.

Although RACK1 has been reported in several cancers, its expression and role in PDA remain unclear [18, 21, 38, 39]. In our study, RACK1 is highly expressed in normal acinar cells compare to ADM and PanIN epithelial cells or ductal cells. RACK1 expression was gradually down-regulated in human PDA tissues and KPC mice tissues from ADM to PanINs and PDA. However, single cell transcriptomics revealed that RACK1 highly expressed in cancer cells as compare to ductal cells in pancreatic adenosquamous carcinoma [40]. However, further study is needed to substantiate the conclusion due to the heterogeneity of tumors and the small sample sizes. The discrepancy of RACK1 expression in different tumors from different tissues might be due to the fact that the expression of RACK1 mRNA and protein in cancer is tissue-specific with high expression in esophageal, lung, liver and breast cancer, but not in rectum and gastric cancers [41]. Nonetheless, our study found that RACK1 downregulation was evident in PDA and patients with low expression of RACK had significantly poorer survival outcomes, suggesting that decreased RACK1 expression promoted the initiation of pancreatic premalignant lesions and PDA progression.

RACK1 plays important functions in regulating cell cycle, proliferation, and apoptosis [24]. However, up-regulation of RACK1 was markedly correlated with poor survival and significantly associated with histological grade and tumor size [38, 39]. We found that RACK1 silencing markedly promoted cell proliferation, cell cycle, colony formation, and migration in PDA cells, whereas increased RACK1 expression did the opposite. These findings were consistent with that decreased RACK1 expression promoted tumor growth and metastasis and worse survival outcome, while increased expression of RACK1 did the opposite in animal models. Therefore, our results demonstrated that RACK1 was involved in negatively regulating cell proliferation and cell migration of PDA in vitro and in vivo.

The upstream regulators of the RACK1 pathway are not well studied. We find that CAE, PDL, TGF-α and KRAS significantly inhibited the expression of RACK1 and upregulated the expression of MAP2K3, RELA and SRC. We demonstrated that CAE, PDL, TGF-α and KRAS regulate the downstream RACK1 pathway (MAP2K3, RELA and SRC) in acinar cells and PDA cells [36–38]. MAP2K3, NF-κB and Src signaling pathways are known to play important regulatory roles in the proliferation, migration, invasion, apoptosis, differentiation, autophagy and tumorigenesis [42–47]. Also, RACK1 upregulates many downstream targets, including Cyclin D1, MYC, Survivin [24] and NHE5 [48]. Interestingly, TRIM26 inhibits osteosarcoma progression by promoting degradation of RACK1, thereby resulting in inactivation of MEK/ERK signaling, and impeding the EMT process [49]. Imatinib treatment inhibits c-KIT activity and prevents RACK1 degradation in gastrointestinal stromal tumor, whereas RACK1 overexpression rendered imatinib resistance in gastrointestinal stromal tumor [50]. Therefore, our findings show that RACK1 is involved in complicated signaling network rather than a simple signaling pathway, which warrants further investigation.

In summary, our study demonstrates RACK1 as a novel negative regulator of ADM formation and PDA in vitro and in vivo. RACK1 is involved in regulating cells apoptosis, cell proliferation, cell cycle, and cell migration. CAE, PDL, TGF-α and KRAS significantly inhibited the expression of RACK1, upregulated the expression of MAP2K3, RELA and SRC and promoted inflammation during pancreatic injury and carcinogenesis (Fig. 7). RACK1 might be used as a novel promising molecular biomarker for the early detection and therapy of PDA.

An extended single-particle model of lithium-ion batteries based on simplified solid-liquid diffusion process
Authors: Wei He, Tao Han, Haiqin Song, Qian Wang, Jianqiang Kang, Jing V. Wang, Weihua Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryAn extended lithium-ion battery model is proposed, which simplifies the solid-liquid diffusion process compared with the full-order pseudo two-dimensional (P2D) model, in order to reduce computational complexity and enhance modeling speed. To simplify the model, the three-parameter method is utilized to simplify the solid-phase diffusion process. Meanwhile, exponential fitting is applied to simplify the liquid-phase diffusion process. In addition, the average current flux is used to simplify the current exchange density at the solid-liquid interface. Finally, experiments are performed to verify the model, and experimental results demonstrate that, even though fewer parameters need to be identified, the simplified model still has satisfied accuracy under constant current and dynamic situations.

Introduction

Lithium-ion batteries have been widely used in portable electronic devices, automobiles, and energy storage fields, among others, due to their advantages of high energy, high power density, and long life.1,2 The lithium-ion battery model is one of the components of the electric vehicle battery management system (BMS), and an accurate battery model can better estimate the current state of the battery system in real time. Currently, Rint models, Thevenin models, and second-order RC models are among the battery models that are frequently utilized in BMS. These models are essentially analogous circuit models made up of electronic components like resistors, inductors, and capacitors. This kind of model can satisfy the requirements of some application scenarios due to its straightforward structure, minimal computation load, and excellent accuracy. Nevertheless, it is challenging to explain the sophisticated physical and chemical processes inside the battery using this model, which can only partially reflect the exterior characteristics of the battery.

Electrochemical models, on the other hand, use a set of complicated partial differential equations to represent intricate internal reaction processes. A common electrochemical model is the pseudo two-dimensional (P2D) model, which was suggested by Doyle3 and is based on concentration theory and porous electrode theory. Unfortunately, this model is extremely complex, computationally demanding, and challenging to use in practice. To decrease its complexity, several simpler models have been presented by researchers.4,5 The single-particle (SP) electrochemical model is one of the most studied. For example, the P2D model was made much simpler by Xu’s SP model, which made the assumption that the concentration of lithium ions outside the particle is uniform. In order to further increase calculation accuracy, the author also took into account variations in the distribution of lithium ions within the particles and described the particle state using the finite difference approach.6,7 Some scholars have assumed that the concentration of lithium ions in the electrode particles is linearly distributed, and the concentration distribution is simplified by equation fitting.8 Ting’s simulation of the diffusion process of lithium-ion solid phase was done roughly using a three-parameter parabolic equation.9 Compared with the finite difference method, the computational complexity is significantly reduced and the computational accuracy is improved. This approach, however, adds three more unknown parameters and makes the task of identifying the remaining parameters more difficult. A quartic polynomial approximation was used by Subramanian et al.10 to simplify the solid diffusion process, eliminating the need for unknown parameters and lowering computing complexity. In addition, in situations of high charging/discharging rate, they can also obtain more precise numerical solutions.

While the SP model and its extended models are capable of accurately simulating battery charging and discharging behavior at low current rate, the error will increase as the current rate increases because the difference in the liquid phase potential distribution cannot be ignored. Known as the simplified P2D (SP2D), Han11 suggested a multi-particle model with four particles equally distributed at the positive and negative poles. The solid-phase diffusion process was made simpler by SP2D by a two-state model. However, the solid-liquid interface exchange current density calculation was excessively complex and the liquid phase diffusion calculation was not convergent when the battery’s characteristics were not exact. Similarly, Bi12 established an electrochemical model with 13 negative particles and a single positive particle and studied the estimation of battery health status under the assumption of negative electrode aging. Although the calculation result of the model is accurate, the calculation of the negative electrode is too complex.13 The electrolyte resistance value was added to the model to improve the accuracy of the SP model. The influence of the liquid phase lithium ionization concentration distribution on the liquid phase potential is further considered, and the accuracy of the model is improved at a higher magnification.14 Fan15 converts the partial differential equations of solid-liquid phase diffusion in the P2D model into polynomials and ordinary differential equations, which also reduces the computational complexity.

Due to the large number of model parameters in the aforementioned method, the computational workload is still high. The simplified model proposed in this paper further reduces the computational workload and improves the computational speed. The solid-liquid phase of the model is simplified based on the P2D model. Simultaneously, the exchange current density is made simpler by using the average current flow. As taking into account the influence of liquid phase potential, the model’s convergence is enhanced. On the other hand, the exchange current density calculation is made simpler as compared to the SP2D model, improving the accuracy of the high-rate discharge calculation. In order to reduce the number of parameter identifications, we study the sensitivity of the model’s parameters and acquire them through battery disassembly measurement, reference, and parameter identification method. The computation speed is clearly increased when comparing the P2D model with the simplified model. Furthermore, a comparison of simulation results based on the simplified model and experimental results based on tests of real batteries demonstrates the great fitting precision of the model.

Electrochemical model

P2D model described a battery made up of a positive electrode, a separator, and a negative electrode. The porous active material particles, which can be roughly described as spherical particles, fill the positive and negative electrodes. As seen in Figure 1, the pores of the particles are filled with electrolytes, and a porous separator separates the positive and negative electrodes to prevent a short circuit between them.Figure 1Schematic diagram of electrochemical model for lithium-ion batteries

Schematic diagram of electrochemical model for lithium-ion batteries

At the microscale of positively and negatively polarized active material particles, lithium ions diffuse within spherical particles according to Fick’s second law, moving along the radial direction (r) of the spheres. In the battery’s macroscopic thickness in the x direction, lithium ions are believed to move along the x direction in the electrolyte, driven by concentration and electric fields. Besides, positive and negative electrode particles’ surfaces undergo interfacial chemical reactions. During charging, lithium ions detach from the surface of positive electrode particles, move into the electrolyte, cross through the separator, enter the negative electrode, and embed themselves in the particles. Model simplification methods

Numerous coupled partial differential equations are present in the original lithium-ion battery P2D. These equations must be simplified in order to decrease computing complexity and increase calculation performance.

Solid phase simplification

Due to the approximate parabolic distribution of lithium-ion concentration inside solid-phase particles along the particle radius direction, a two-state equation was used in the study by Ting et al.9 to fit such a distribution curve, thereby obtaining an approximate lithium-ion concentration on the particle surface. Although this method provides a more accurate approximation of the lithium-ion concentration distribution compared to the fourth-order polynomial fitting method, it adds four additional parameters that need to be identified. These four parameters require experimental data to fit the concentration distribution inside the particles, making them difficult to obtain. In order to reduce the number of parameter identification and ensure a certain level of computational accuracy, this paper adopts a three-parameter parabolic fitting method to approximate the solid phase diffusion process.10

Liquid phase simplification

The initial SP model displays a relatively big error in high charging/discharging rate, primarily because it disregards the impact of lithium-ion liquid-phase distribution on terminal voltage. To mitigate this issue, this article investigates the impact of liquid-phase lithium-ion distribution based on the SP model.

Liquid lithium-ion concentration in electrochemical models is often time- and position-dependent. Two physical processes determine the concentration of liquid lithium ions in the positive and negative regions: the first process is the diffusion motion based on concentration difference (Fick’s law), and the other one is the motion of solid lithium ions in the positive and negative regions. The gradient diffusion process of Fick’s theorem is the only process that occurs in the diaphragm region because there is no solid-liquid lithium-ion exchange.

In order to simplify the liquid-phase diffusion process, Luo et al.16 approximated the concentration of lithium ions in the liquid phase of the battery, using an exponential fitting to approximate the distribution of lithium ions in the positive and negative polar regions. The local exchange current density jf(x,t) was approximated by the average current flux I(t)/A and was decoupled. Conclusions

In this paper, the solid-liquid interface diffusion, liquid-phase diffusion, and exchange current density equations of the lithium-ion battery P2D model are all simplified. Firstly, the solid phase is simplified using a three-parameter fitting method. The liquid phase is simplified using the exponential fitting method, and the exchange current density of the solid-liquid interface is simplified using average current flux. By comparing the simulation results of the P2D model and the ESP model, the rationality of the ESP model is verified, and the simulation time is shorter and the calculation speed is faster. Through experimental results and comparison with the ESP model, it is found that the maximum RMSE under constant current conditions is 12.65 mV; under dynamic conditions, it displays 13.90 mV, verifying the accuracy of the ESP model.

Limitations of the study

This paper does not explore the aging mechanisms at different SOC levels under various temperatures. This will be the focus of our future works.

Method details

In this paper, a simplified electrochemical model is proposed, which has the advantages of shorter simulation speed and faster calculation. The solid-liquid interface diffusion equation, the liquid-phase diffusion equation and the exchange current density equation of the P2D model for lithium-ion batteries are simplified. First, the solid phase was simplified using the three-parameter fitting method. The liquid phase was simplified with the exponential fitting method, and the exchange current density at the solid-liquid interface was simplified with the average current flow. The reasonableness of the ESP model was verified by comparing the P2D model and experiments.

Deep learning model for real time moisture content detection and prediction in white tea withering using near infrared spectroscopy
Authors: Wei Tao, Bin Chen, Xinkun Yang, Bo Guo, Lingxin Chi, Yihan Huang, Ruixin Li, Jiayi Ren
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study proposes a novel deep learning model, named STA-BiGRU-XGBoost, for predicting moisture content during the white tea withering process using near-infrared spectroscopy. The model integrates spatiotemporal attention mechanisms, bidirectional gated recurrent units (BiGRU), and the XGBoost algorithm to address challenges such as extended withering durations, environmental variability, and temporal variations in spectral data. The maximum relevance minimum redundancy (mRMR) algorithm is employed to select critical variables, including hot air velocity, air duct temperature, and spectral absorbance. A spatial attention mechanism enhances feature relevance, while BiGRU captures long-term temporal dependencies. Temporal attention further adjusts the weights of key time steps. XGBoost was incorporated to improve prediction stability under the investigated production-line conditions. Experimental results obtained from a production-line dataset collected from Zhenghe County during the March–May 2025 production season show that the STA-BiGRU-XGBoost model achieved the best performance among the compared models, with RMSE = 0.0920, MAE = 0.0772, and R² = 0.9806. Furthermore, the model’s interpretability is validated through attention weight visualization, highlighting key features associated with moisture evaporation dynamics. It should be noted that the current validation was conducted within a specific production season and fresh-leaf source region; broader generalization across different geographic origins, cultivars, and extreme weather conditions requires further cross-origin and multi-season validation.Keywords: White tea withering, Real-time moisture content detection and prediction, Near-infrared spectroscopy, Deep learning prediction model, Spatiotemporal attention mechanismSubject terms: Engineering, Environmental sciences, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

White tea, one of China’s six major tea types, is renowned for its minimal processing and natural flavor. Its quality is largely determined during the withering process, in which tea leaves undergo moisture loss alongside complex biochemical transformations. These changes significantly influence flavor, aroma, and overall quality, making moisture content a crucial indicator of withering degree and product stability. Moreover, moisture content affects the conversion of polyphenolic compounds and the retention of amino acids, which contribute to the characteristic flavor and aroma of white tea1–3.

The withering process typically lasts 48–72 h and is influenced by factors such as raw material variety, harvest season, leaf maturity, and initial moisture content. Fluctuations in moisture levels can lead to substantial variations in water loss rates and enzymatic activity, ultimately affecting product consistency. Currently, the assessment of withering degree relies primarily on subjective observations, such as leaf softness, color changes, and aroma, which are insufficient for large-scale standardized production4. Therefore, a real-time, non-destructive, and accurate method for monitoring moisture content during withering is needed.

Traditional moisture content measurement methods, such as oven drying and Karl Fischer titration, offer high accuracy but are time-consuming, destructive, and unsuitable for online monitoring. Recent advancements in non-destructive testing, particularly near-infrared spectroscopy (NIRS), have demonstrated considerable potential for real-time moisture detection in agricultural products due to their sensitivity to molecular vibrations, including those of water molecules5. NIRS has been successfully applied in processes such as green tea fixation, black tea withering, and tea drying, although challenges remain for prolonged processes like white tea withering6,7.

However, unlike relatively short and concentrated processing stages such as green tea fixation or drying, white tea withering is characterized by a long duration, significant dynamic changes in environmental conditions, and strong process non-stationarity. Continuous variations in environmental temperature, humidity, ventilation conditions, and leaf pile status during withering lead to considerable temporal variations in both moisture migration within tea leaves and their near-infrared spectral responses. Recent studies indicate that during extended processing periods, moisture migration and environmental fluctuations can cause nonlinear drift and dynamic instability in near-infrared spectra, significantly undermining the predictive stability of models based on static assumptions8,9. Particularly in long-duration monitoring scenarios such as tea withering, spectra collected at different times may exhibit substantial differences even at similar moisture levels, imposing higher demands on model generalization and robustness10.

To address these challenges, existing studies primarily employ traditional chemometric methods such as Partial Least Squares Regression (PLSR) and Support Vector Regression (SVR), or spectral correction techniques like External Parameter Orthogonalization (EPO) and Generalized Least Squares Weighting (GLSW) to mitigate environmental disturbances11,12. However, these methods generally rely on stable operational assumptions or additional environmental parameter inputs, making them difficult to implement consistently over the long term in actual white tea withering production lines. Furthermore, traditional modeling approaches struggle to simultaneously capture the nonlinear characteristics of moisture information and its temporal evolution in high-dimensional spectral data, limiting their applicability to prolonged withering processes.

In recent years, deep learning methods have gained significant attention in spectral analysis and food processing monitoring. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) can automatically extract multi-scale spectral features, while Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and their variants, such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM), have demonstrated strong capabilities in capturing temporal dependencies in time-series data13,14. Beyond agricultural and spectroscopic applications, recent studies on data-driven monitoring of complex engineering systems have also demonstrated the importance of hybrid feature extraction and graph-based representation learning. For example, Fu et al. proposed a hybrid monitoring strategy combined with an improved graph convolutional network for fault detection and diagnosis of air handling units, showing that nonlinear feature extraction and graph-based learning can improve diagnostic performance under complex operating conditions15. Zhang et al. further integrated kernel Fisher discriminant analysis with an improved graph convolutional neural network to incorporate class-label information and spatial relationships for air-handling-unit fault diagnosis16. To further enhance model adaptability under complex conditions, researchers have incorporated attention mechanisms to dynamically assign importance to different time steps or feature dimensions, effectively suppressing redundant information caused by environmental disturbances and improving prediction accuracy and robustness17–19. For example, Song et al. proposed a quantitative regularization strategy for robust Vision Transformer-based remote sensing image classification, demonstrating that regularized attention-based representation can improve model stability when dealing with complex high-dimensional visual data20. Similarly, Causal-YOLO introduced causal feature alignment to distinguish invariant target-related features from spurious environmental interference in remote sensing object detection21. Although these studies were conducted in remote sensing scenarios, their methodological insights are relevant to near-infrared spectroscopy-based process monitoring, where spectral responses are also high-dimensional, nonlinear, and susceptible to environmental disturbances. Previous studies have validated the potential of deep learning methods for moisture detection in tea, black tea withering, and agricultural product drying under dynamic conditions1,3,22. Recent reviews indicate that combining near-infrared spectroscopy with deep learning represents a promising direction for online quality monitoring in food processing, especially in non-stationary and disturbance-prone industrial applications23,24. Nevertheless, for the typical long-duration and environmentally coupled white tea withering process, spectral prediction studies integrating temporal modeling with attention mechanisms remain limited, underscoring the need for further systematic investigation.

From the perspective of automation and intelligent control, existing white tea withering systems still face several practical limitations. First, most production lines are still operated using rule-based temperature and ventilation settings, while the withering degree is judged mainly by worker experience; therefore, the control loop is not closed by quantitative moisture information. Second, the long withering duration causes dynamic coupling among leaf phenotype, ventilation, ambient humidity, and spectral response, which makes static calibration models unstable when they are transferred from laboratory conditions to production lines. Third, online control requires a prediction model that is not only accurate but also interpretable and computationally feasible for industrial edge computers. Accordingly, the proposed STA-BiGRU-XGBoost framework was designed to address these limitations by integrating feature screening, spatial attention, temporal dependency modeling, and robust ensemble regression into a two-stage prediction pipeline.

Based on the aforementioned challenges and recent research progress, this study focuses on the long-duration, environmentally coupled white tea withering process and aims to develop a near-infrared spectral deep learning prediction framework that integrates temporal modeling with attention mechanisms. Materials and methods

Data acquisition device

The white tea withering system comprises several key components, including a feed inlet, greening mechanism, conveyor belt, dehumidifying heat pump, exhaust fan, discharge outlet, and an infrared spectrometer, as illustrated in Fig. 1. Fresh tea leaves are introduced through the bottom inlet and transported by the greening mechanism onto the withering conveyor belt. The dehumidifying heat pump supplies temperature- and humidity-controlled hot air to the withering chamber, facilitating moisture removal while suppressing green odor formation. After 24–48 h of withering, the leaves are transferred to the discharge outlet, completing the process. The infrared spectroscopy moisture detection system, utilizing a MERS Measurement MS-580 near-infrared moisture analyzer, collects real-time, non-destructive moisture data from the tea leaves. The system operates within the 940–1550 nm wavelength range and is installed directly above the conveyor belt.

Schematic of the white tea withering system.

Sample preparation and data collection

Data collection was conducted from March 18 to May 18, 2025, at a white tea processing company in Wuyishan City. The fresh tea leaves used in the experiments were sourced from Zhenghe County, Nanping City, China. Fresh white tea leaves are primarily harvested between March and May each year, and most tea factories concentrate their withering processes during this period. It should be noted that tea leaves are not withered continuously throughout the year, resulting in minimal seasonal variations in the withering datasets. During tea production, the installed infrared spectrometer collected sample data from leaves in the greening mechanism. The near-infrared spectral data collection method is illustrated in Fig. 2. To assess the influence of operational temperature and humidity at different times on near-infrared spectra and prediction accuracy, a sampling interval of 5 min was adopted. To ensure consistent withering quality, the tea factory regulated the production workshop environment (ambient temperature: 20–35 °C, humidity: 45–60% RH), maintaining stable conditions without significant fluctuations. Withering process data were collected by an industrial control computer (with a sampling frequency of 10 Hz for each sensor, processed using a 5-minute moving average filter). The computer saved the processed data at the same interval, enabling real-time monitoring and precise control of variables throughout the withering process and ensuring that the collected data accurately reflected actual conditions. The dataset was divided into three subsets: a training set for learning model parameters, a validation set for hyperparameter tuning, and a test set comprising two batches for evaluating prediction model performance.

During data acquisition, the near-infrared spectrometer collected spectral information in the wavelength range of 940–1550 nm. These wavelength-resolved spectra were mainly used for spectral characterization, preprocessing comparison, and identification of moisture-sensitive absorption regions. For online modeling and real-time prediction, the industrial NIR system further generated a synchronized scalar absorbance feature from the selected moisture-sensitive spectral response. This scalar feature was recorded by the industrial control computer together with the process variables, including hot air velocity, air duct temperature, and environmental parameters. In this study, this online scalar spectral absorbance feature was denoted as X9. The adoption of the scalar absorbance feature X9 was determined by both the industrial online acquisition configuration and the real-time monitoring objective of this study. Although the full wavelength-resolved spectra over 940–1550 nm contain richer spectral information, direct ingestion of the complete spectrum would substantially increase the input dimensionality, model parameters, storage demand, and online inference burden. In contrast, the industrial NIR moisture analyzer used in the production line outputs a synchronized absorbance indicator derived from the moisture-sensitive spectral response. This scalar output is directly available to the industrial control computer and can be reliably aligned with process variables at the 5-min sampling interval. Therefore, X9 was used as the spectral input feature for the proposed time-series prediction framework, while the full spectra were used mainly for spectral characterization, preprocessing comparison, and identification of moisture-related absorption regions. X9 does not represent the complete high-dimensional spectrum from 940 to 1550 nm, but rather the scalar absorbance feature extracted from the NIR spectral response for real-time modeling.

Prediction method determination

The white tea withering process occurs in a highly variable field environment where sensor sensitivity can be affected by external disturbances. To address this, a neural-network-based method was employed to process the infrared spectrometer data. Neural networks can be categorized into feedforward and recurrent types. Feedforward networks, lacking feedback and inhibition mechanisms, are unsuitable for this task. In contrast, recurrent networks, which incorporate feedback and inhibition, optimize the network through algorithms, making them more appropriate for this study.

Deep learning networks, especially those designed for time-series prediction, have demonstrated outstanding performance in modeling complex dynamic processes. In this study, the STA-BiGRU model was applied for the first time to predict moisture content during tea withering. The model integrates STA and BiGRU, enabling adaptive weighting of time steps and capturing long-term dependencies in moisture content sequences.

The withering process exhibits dynamic characteristics, including spatial and temporal dependencies, along with noisy and inconsistent data, which pose challenges for accurate prediction. The data characteristics can be summarized as follows:

STA-BiGRU method

The STA-BiGRU method for moisture content prediction during white tea withering consists of four main components: feature selection using mRMR, a spatiotemporal attention mechanism, bidirectional GRU modeling, and XGBoost-based moisture content prediction (MC). As shown in Fig. 6, the framework contains five sequential modules: (i) synchronized process and spectral data acquisition, in which environmental/process variables and NIR spectra are aligned by timestamp; (ii) preprocessing and mRMR feature selection, which suppress spectral noise and remove redundant variables; (iii) spatial attention, which assigns dynamic weights to input features at each time step; (iv) BiGRU with temporal attention, which extracts bidirectional temporal features and highlights key time steps; and (v) XGBoost regression, which maps the frozen attention-enhanced context vectors to final moisture content predictions.

During tea withering, high-dimensional variables collected by the processing system (e.g., environmental temperature, humidity, spreading thickness, and ventilation rate) increase model complexity and may reduce prediction accuracy. Previous studies have shown that the mRMR algorithm effectively removes redundant features while retaining key variables, improving model performance (with a increase in ) and preserving the physical interpretability of selected features28,29. Therefore, the mRMR algorithm was adopted for feature selection in this study.

The use of the scalar NIR-derived feature X9 represents an engineering trade-off between spectral information richness and industrial deployment feasibility. Full-spectrum input over 940–1550 nm may preserve more wavelength-resolved information and may allow a neural network to learn nonlinear interactions among adjacent wavelengths. However, direct use of the full preprocessed spectrum would also substantially increase the input dimensionality, model parameters, memory consumption, and inference latency. More importantly, high-dimensional full-spectrum modeling generally requires a larger and more diverse calibration dataset to avoid fitting spectral noise, baseline drift, and batch-specific variations.

XGBoost algorithm

To enhance generalization and robustness against noise and harsh conditions, XGBoost is integrated as a post-processing step after STA-BiGRU. The proposed STA-BiGRU-XGBoost model was trained as a two-stage pipeline rather than an end-to-end jointly optimized network. In Stage 1, the STA-BiGRU backbone was trained using the training set, while the validation set was used for early stopping and hyperparameter selection. After convergence, the backbone parameters were frozen and the attention-enhanced context vector H’ was extracted for each sample. In Stage 2, these frozen context vectors were used as inputs to train XGBoost independently, and the XGBoost hyperparameters were optimized on the validation set. This data flow was adopted to improve reproducibility and to reduce the risk of overfitting caused by joint optimization on a limited industrial dataset.

The two-stage design was adopted intentionally rather than as a simplified substitute for end-to-end optimization. In principle, end-to-end joint optimization may allow the feature extractor and the regression head to be updated simultaneously. However, for the present industrial dataset, such a strategy would introduce a larger number of trainable parameters and may increase sensitivity to batch-specific spectral drift, sensor noise, and short-term process disturbances. This issue is particularly relevant for long-duration white tea withering, where spectral responses and process variables are affected by environmental fluctuations and fresh-leaf heterogeneity.

Therefore, temporal representation learning and nonlinear regression were separated in the proposed framework. The STA-BiGRU backbone was first used to learn smoothed spatiotemporal representations under validation-based early stopping. After convergence, the backbone parameters were frozen, and the extracted context vectors were used as fixed high-level descriptors for XGBoost regression. The XGBoost stage further introduced shrinkage, row sampling, column sampling, and L1/L2 regularization, which helped reduce model sensitivity to noisy fluctuations and outliers in industrial process data. This design was intended to improve reproducibility and reduce the risk of overfitting under limited production-line samples.

Industrial robustness to nonlinear drift

Although the proposed STA-BiGRU-XGBoost model achieved satisfactory prediction performance under the production conditions investigated in this study, the industrial robustness of near-infrared-based moisture prediction should be considered carefully. In practical white tea production, fresh leaves collected from different geographic origins, cultivars, harvesting batches, and weather conditions may exhibit differences in leaf thickness, maturity, biochemical composition, surface moisture, and internal water distribution. These phenotypic variations can induce nonlinear spectral drift, even when the measured moisture contents are similar. Such drift may affect the stability and transferability of a prediction model if it is trained only on data from a limited production region or season.

In the present study, several strategies were adopted to improve model robustness under dynamic production conditions. First, mRMR-based feature screening was used to remove redundant and weakly relevant variables, thereby reducing the risk of introducing unstable inputs. Second, the spatial attention mechanism adaptively assigned weights to different input features, allowing the model to emphasize moisture-related variables while suppressing less informative disturbances. Third, BiGRU and temporal attention were used to capture temporal dependencies during the long-duration withering process, which helped the model account for dynamic changes in spectral and environmental responses. Finally, XGBoost was introduced as the final prediction module to improve robustness through tree-based ensemble learning and regularization.

Nevertheless, it should be noted that the dataset used in this study mainly covered the production season, processing conditions, and fresh-leaf source region investigated in the experiment. Therefore, although the chronological validation and independent test sets can demonstrate the model’s generalization ability within the studied production scenario, they cannot fully prove universal robustness across different geographic origins, cultivars, and extreme weather conditions. For broader industrial deployment, additional cross-origin, multi-season, and multi-weather datasets should be collected to further evaluate and improve model transferability.

Future work should focus on constructing a larger database containing fresh leaves from different tea-producing regions, cultivars, harvest periods, and weather conditions. In addition, practical model-maintenance strategies, such as periodic recalibration, calibration transfer, domain adaptation, and transfer learning, should be incorporated to compensate for nonlinear spectral drift caused by fresh-leaf phenotype variation and environmental changes. These strategies are expected to further enhance the robustness and applicability of near-infrared spectroscopy combined with deep learning for real-time moisture monitoring in industrial white tea withering.

Interpretability of experiments

To enhance interpretability, attention weight matrices were visualized as heatmaps (Figs. 16 and 17). Spatial attention (Fig. 16) shows weights across process variables over time. Variables X6 (hot air velocity), X7 (air duct outlet temperature), and X9 (spectral absorbance) exhibit consistently high weights (darker hues), indicating critical roles in withering. Variable X8 (feed flow rate) shows lower weights (lighter hues), aligning with known process dynamics. In industrial settings, increased X6 and X7 accelerate moisture evaporation, affecting spectral absorption—validating the model’s focus on physically meaningful variables.

Temporal attention (Fig. 17) illustrates weight distribution across time steps. The current step (t) receives the highest weight (0.46), emphasizing its importance for instantaneous prediction. The spatiotemporal attention mechanisms not only improve performance but also provide insight into model decisions. Spatial heatmaps validate alignment with domain knowledge, while temporal distributions reveal how historical data is weighted, enhancing model transparency and credibility.

Conclusion

This study developed and validated an improved STA-BiGRU-XGBoost deep learning model for real-time moisture content prediction under the investigated white tea withering conditions. The model addresses key challenges in long-duration industrial monitoring via a multi-layered architecture: mRMR feature selection ensures interpretability; spatiotemporal attention adaptively focuses on relevant variables and time steps; BiGRU captures long-term dynamic dependencies; and XGBoost enhances generalization in noisy environments. Experimental results demonstrate that STA-BiGRU-XGBoost outperforms baseline models (LSTM, GRU, BiGRU, and attention variants) in accuracy (RMSE, MAE), goodness-of-fit (R²), and error stability. Visualization of spatial attention weights confirms that model decisions align with core physical mechanisms (e.g., hot air velocity and temperature driving moisture evaporation), improving the transparency and interpretability of the prediction process.

It should also be acknowledged that the robustness of the proposed model was validated mainly under the production conditions and fresh-leaf sources considered in this study. In actual industrial applications, differences in geographic origin, cultivar, harvesting season, and weather conditions may cause nonlinear spectral drift and phenotypic variation in fresh leaves, which could affect model transferability. Therefore, future studies will further expand the dataset by incorporating cross-origin, multi-season, and multi-weather samples. Periodic recalibration, calibration transfer, domain adaptation, and transfer learning will also be explored to improve the long-term robustness and generalization ability of the proposed model in practical white tea production.

However, the current model is optimized for single-step prediction; its multi-step rolling prediction capability remains unexplored. Future work should: (1) design multi-step prediction architectures and explore decomposition methods for moisture content time series; (2) integrate multimodal data (e.g., visible light, hyperspectral imaging) to comprehensively characterize withering quality transformations; and (3) extend the framework to other agricultural processes with similar long-duration, strongly coupled characteristics to verify generalizability.

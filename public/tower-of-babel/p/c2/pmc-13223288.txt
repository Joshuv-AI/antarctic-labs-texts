Design and implementation of a fine-grained detection method for Qinghai Lake birds via fusion of knowledge guidance and feature enhancement
Authors: Yongkang Zhang, GuiLian Feng, ShengLei Pei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAs a crucial high-altitude habitat and a key stopover site for migratory birds, Bird Island on Qinghai Lake requires automated monitoring for ecological conservation. In this context, bird detection poses a significant challenge due to targets’ large scale variations, morphological similarities, and complex backgrounds. Current general-purpose detection models struggle to adequately perceive such fine-grained features, leading to high rates of missed and false detections in complex natural scenes. Based on the YOLOv8 architecture, this study introduces two core improvements: a High-Frequency and Spatial Dependency Perception module to enhance multi-scale feature extraction, and an adaptive knowledge-base system that incorporating ornithological knowledge to boost discrimination among similar species via knowledge-guided inference. The resulting model is named YOLO_BD (You Only Look Once_Bird Detection). Experimental results demonstrate that YOLO_BD achieves a mean Average Precision (mAP@0.5) of 75.2% with 6.6 million parameters while maintaining high detection efficiency. It not only surpasses the baseline YOLOv8n (72.8% mAP@0.5 with 3.1 M parameters) in accuracy but also outperforms the larger YOLOv8s model (74.4% mAP@0.5 with 11.1 M parameters), highlighting its dual advantages in achieving lightweight design and enhanced performance. This research provides an effective technical solution for intelligent wildlife monitoring in resource-constrained environments.Keywords: Object detection, YOLO_BD, Knowledge-guided, Feature enhancement, Qinghai lake birdsSubject terms: Ecology, Ecology, Zoology

Introduction

The Qinghai-Tibet Plateau holds a unique position within the global ecosystem. Lake Qinghai, on the plateau, is not only the largest inland saltwater lake but also a crucial stopover point along the East Asia-Australasia migratory bird flyway1.Bird Island, as its core area, is home to dozens of rare bird species such as bar-headed geese and black-headed gulls, forming a unique high-altitude avian community with outstanding biodiversity value.However, bird populations worldwide are facing multiple survival threats. Research indicates that ground-nesting birds in Europe are 86% more likely to decline than species with other nesting strategies2. Climate change-induced heatwaves are exacerbating this crisis—an assessment of 1,196 dryland bird species revealed that 888 are highly vulnerable to heatwaves, with vulnerable species showing significantly faster population declines following heatwave events3. Concurrently, emerging contaminants have been detected even within protected areas: fecal samples from 10 migratory shorebird species in an Indian reserve contained high concentrations of heavy metals (zinc, copper, lead, chromium) and multiple microplastics (polystyrene accounting for 42.6%)4. Furthermore, habitat loss continues to threaten specific species, with Rufous Hummingbirds experiencing a 34–54% reduction in Pacific coastal breeding habitat directly linked to population declines5. Faced with these complex and intertwined threats, establishing high-precision automated monitoring systems has become an urgent necessity for assessing population dynamics and identifying risk factors. As a critical habitat for plateau migratory birds, Qinghai Lake urgently requires the intelligent monitoring solution proposed in this study.Traditional bird monitoring primarily relies on manual field observations, a method that suffers from inherent drawbacks such as low efficiency, limited coverage, and the potential fro disturbing bird activities.

With the advancement of computer vision technology, deep learning-based object detection methods offer an automated and non-invasive approach to wildlife monitoring6.Algorithmically, object detection frameworks have evolved from two-stage detectors (such as R-CNN7 and Faster R-CNN8 to single-stage detectors (such as YOLO9 and SSD10, achieving a better balance between efficiency and accuracy.Among these, YOLOv8 has become a key baseline model due to its well-optimized network architecture.However, directly applying this general model to the Qinghai Lake bird monitoring scenario11 still presents significant challenges: distant bird targets are extremely small, resulting in weak features and high false-negative rates; high morphological similarity among different species complicates distinction; and complex backgrounds and occlusions in natural environments severely interfere with detection performance.

The study improves the YOLO model through two dimensions—feature enhancement and knowledge guidance—to address the challenges posed by the variable scale and similar morphology of birds.First, to directly address the challenges of weak small-object features and complex background interference, a high-frequency perception-spatial dependency perception(HFP_SDP) module was introduced. Its high-frequency perception submodule explicitly enhances discriminative high-frequency details such as feather textures and beak contours through frequency-domain filtering and attention mechanisms. The spatial dependency perception submodule: establishes pixel-level correlations through cross-attention, effectively mitigating target confusion and positioning inaccuracies caused by occlusions.Secondly, to enhance the ability to distinguish morphologically similar species, an adaptive knowledge base integrating ornithological expertise was constructed. This base encodes taxonomic hierarchies and key morphological features into learnable embedding vectors, which are then injected into the network via an attention mechanism. This guides the model to focus on discriminative regions between species12. Ultimately, the proposed YOLO_BD model aims to achieve a significant improvement in bird detection accuracy within complex natural scenes.

This study integrates fine-grained feature enhancement mechanisms with structured domain knowledge to provide a novel technical approach for tackling inherent challenges such as high-similarity species identification and small object detection. The findings validate the effectiveness of knowledge-guided strategies in specialized detection models, offering promising technical support for precise wildlife monitoring and conservation in complex natural environments.Unlike prior knowledge-guided methods that typically inject knowledge only at the classification head, our approach integrates learnable knowledge embeddings at multiple feature levels, enabling synergistic feature enhancement and fine-grained discrimination.

Related work

The automated bird monitoring task at Qinghai Lake essentially combines two major challenges in complex natural environments: ultra-small-scale target detection and fine-grained identification of highly similar species. Current research aimed at improving model performance primarily follows two distinct technical approaches: enhancing the model’s ability to characterize features of small object, and improving the model’s capacity to distinguish features of similar species. However, this isolated optimization model struggles to effectively address the intertwined challenges posed by both aspects.

To enhance small object detection performance, the core research focus lies in mitigating the loss of discriminative details caused by network downsampling. Feature Pyramid Network (FPN)13 and its enhanced variant PANet14 have become foundational solutions by fusing shallow-level details with deep-level semantics to improve multi-scale representation capabilities. To further focus on critical information, attention mechanisms—which reweight feature channels or spatial locations—are widely employed to suppress backgrounds and highlight foregrounds15,16. Recently, Shi et al.‘s High-Frequency Perception-Spatial Dependency Perception (HFP-SDP) module has adopted a frequency-domain analysis approach, attempting to restore fine-grained textures of small objects by amplifying high-frequency image components, offering novel insights for weak signal enhancement17. However, these generic methods often enhance generalized edge and contour features without targeting key discriminative local features of birds. Consequently, feature confusion and misclassification remain prevalent in scenes densely populated with morphologically similar species.

In addressing the challenge of fine-grained recognition, research has focused on learning more discriminative feature representations. Early strongly supervised methods relied on precise region annotations to guide models in learning local features, but annotation costs were prohibitively high18. Subsequent weakly supervised approaches enhanced practicality by automatically localizing discriminative regions through self-learning attention mechanisms19. More importantly, incorporating external knowledge has emerged as a key strategy to guide model learning. For instance, constraining models by encoding taxonomic relationships or morphological attributes as semantic vectors20, or modeling inter-category relationships via knowledge graph embeddings21, can enhances semantic discriminative power. However, most of these knowledge fusion methods are applied at the end of the classification network or only optimize the classification head within the detection framework. They fail to effectively feed prior knowledge forward into the core stages of feature extraction and target localization. This makes it difficult for knowledge to directly improve the initial representation quality of small targets and also fails to effectively mitigate localization ambiguity caused by occlusion or density.

In summary, the current research bottleneck lies in the separation between the “representation enhancement” and “semantic guidance” approaches. Small object detection techniques enhance the “visibility” of objects but fail to optimize the discriminative value of the “observed features.” Fine-grained recognition techniques improve the “discriminative power” of features yet cannot fundamentally resolve the recognition challenges caused by the loss of initial details in small objects. This disconnect creates a performance bottleneck when models encounter complex scenes like Qinghai Lake Bird Island.

Recent advances in object detection have explored diverse architectural paradigms to improve the trade-off between accuracy and efficiency. YOLOR22 introduced a unified representation framework that integrates implicit knowledge into explicit feature learning, demonstrating the potential of knowledge integration in detection models. Subsequent developments such as YOLOE-2623 and YOLO2624 have further pushed the efficiency envelope, establishing new benchmarks for real-time performance on edge devices through innovations like Distribution Focal Loss (DFL) removal, NMS-free inference, and Small-Target-Aware Label Assignment (STAL) .

Concurrently, transformer-based detectors have emerged as powerful alternatives. DETR25 revolutionized the detection pipeline by eliminating hand-designed components like anchor generation and non-maximum suppression, but suffered from slow convergence and quadratic computational complexity. This limitation was addressed by Deformable DETR26, which introduced deformable attention to attend to a sparse set of key sampling points, achieving linear complexity and enabling high-resolution feature processing. Building upon this, LW-DETR27 and RF-DETR28 further optimized the architecture for real-time performance, demonstrating that transformer-based detectors can now compete with YOLO models in latency while offering competitive accuracy. However, despite these advances, transformer-based detectors still incur higher computational costs compared to lightweight CNN architectures, making them less suitable for resource-constrained edge deployment scenarios such as wildlife monitoring stations with limited power and computing resources. This consideration motivated our choice of YOLOv8 as the baseline architecture, upon which we build specialized enhancement modules tailored to the unique challenges of bird detection.

Therefore, constructing a unified detection framework capable of synergistically enhancing details and guiding semantics is key to overcoming existing bottlenecks. The YOLO_BD model proposed in this study embodies this approach: its high-frequency perception and spatial dependency perception modules enhance the capture and alignment of bird detail features, addressing issues of “visibility” and feature quality. Simultaneously, the adaptive knowledge guidance module formalizes ornithological domain knowledge and encodes it into learnable embedding vectors, dynamically modulating the feature learning process to enhance discriminative capability. The deep integration and synergistic optimization of these two components systematically tackle intertwined challenges such as small-object feature deficiencies, ambiguous species differentiation, and complex background interference.While recent detectors like YOLOR and DETR advance general efficiency, they do not specifically target fine-grained species discrimination or small-object detail preservation—the core challenges addressed by our framework.

YOLO_BD model

Bird detection at Qinghai Lake faces challenges such as a high proportion of small targets, difficulty in distinguishing morphologically similar species, and complex natural backgrounds. To address these challenges, this study developed the YOLO_BD detection model based on the YOLOv8 architecture. By incorporating a multi-scale feature enhancement mechanism and domain knowledge-guided strategies, the model significantly improves detection performance in complex natural scenes while maintaining the efficiency advantages of single-stage detectors.

Model framework

The overall architecture of the YOLO_BD model is shown in Fig. 1, with its core improvements focused on the backbone network and neck network. The model employs a hierarchical design to achieve an organic integration of feature enhancement and knowledge guidance, forming a complete improvement system from basic feature extraction to advanced semantic enhancement.

YOLO_BD Model Framework Diagram.

The backbone network is based on a deep convolutional network architecture29, employing a progressive knowledge injection strategy. Starting from layer P3/8, the Ontology_C2f module fully replaces the standard C2f module, with this design continued in layers P4/16 and P5/32. While performing fundamental feature extraction, these modules encode avian taxonomic and morphological knowledge into learnable embedding representations, providing semantically rich foundational features for subsequent detection tasks.

The neck network employs differentiated enhancement strategies tailored to the feature characteristics of different layers. At the P5/32 layer, where feature maps exhibit low resolution and strong semantic information but lack detailed texture, the HFP module is deployed to enhance the limited high-frequency components. The AdaptiveBirdOntologyWrapper then performs adaptive knowledge modulation on these deep semantic features. At the P4/16 layer, the HFP module and SDPFusion module work in tandem to balance detail enhancement and spatial alignment, while also relying on the AdaptiveBirdOntologyWrapper for knowledge augmentation. At the P3/8 layer, which is critical for small object detection, a parameter-adjustable HFP module and the full SDPFusion are employed to maximize detail recovery and spatial relationship modeling. This is followed by the sequential application of BirdPartWrapper and AdaptiveBirdOntologyWrapper, establishing a complete attention-guided workflow from discriminative part focus to adaptive knowledge optimization.

The detection head performs predictions based on multi-scale features enhanced jointly by the HFP-SDP module and the knowledge-guided model. This design inherits the original efficient single-stage detection mechanism of YOLOv8 while fully integrating and utilizing the enhanced detail and semantic features. Through end-to-end training, the entire model enables the collaborative optimization of feature enhancement and knowledge-guided components, ultimately forming a specialized bird detection system suitable for complex natural scenes.

Multi-scale feature enhancement design

In the practical scenario of bird detection at Qinghai Lake, distant migratory birds constitute a very small fraction of the image. Their highly discriminative high-frequency details — such as feather textures and beak contours — are easily lost during the downsampling process of the backbone network. Simultaneously, dense bird flocks and mutual occlusions introduce challenges of spatial misalignment for the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) during feature fusion.

To systematically address these challenges, this study introduces and adapts the High-Frequency Perception-Spatial Dependency Perception (HFP-SDP) module, integrating it as a plug-and-play enhancement component within the neck network of YOLOv8. This module functions synergistically across two dimensions: frequency-domain analysis and spatial relationship modeling. Its core workflow is illustrated in Fig. 2 The High-Frequency Perception (HFP) submodule explicitly enhances high-frequency components (such as fine textures relevant to birds) by applying frequency-domain filtering to feature maps, while simultaneously suppressing homogeneous low-frequency background interference. The Spatial Dependency Perception (SDP) submodule employs a cross-attention mechanism30 to establish precise correspondences between shallow-level detail features and high-level semantic features. It then uses the high-level semantics to guide a weighted fusion of the detail features, effectively mitigating feature confusion and spatial misalignment issues caused by iterative upsampling.

HFP_SDP Module Structure Diagram.

This study implements a targeted hierarchical deployment of the module based on the characteristics of the bird detection task and the distinct properties of each level within the feature pyramid. For the P3/8 shallow feature map with the highest resolution, a parameter-adjustable HFP module is deployed alongside the complete SDP fusion. This aims to maximize the recovery and enhancement of small-target details and to achieve precise alignment with semantic information. At the P4/16 intermediate feature level, the combination of the HFP module and SDP fusion balances detail enhancement and semantic supplementation at a medium scale. At the top-tier P5/32 layer, where feature maps are rich in high-level semantics but inherently lack spatial detail, the HFP module is primarily used for detail-level enhancement and refinement. Through this differentiated integration strategy, the HFP-SDP module complements our proposed knowledge-guided system effectively, jointly enhancing the model’s detection performance in complex scenarios.

Knowledge guidance mechanism design

In the complex natural scenes of Bird Island in Qinghai Lake, common challenges such as variable scales, highly similar morphologies, and frequent occlusions of avian targets pose significant difficulties for general object detection models. Data-driven models alone often struggle to consistently learn discriminative feature representations from limited and variable samples. To fundamentally enhance model performance in fine-grained bird detection at Qinghai Lake, this study designs a knowledge-guided mechanism deeply integrating ornithological prior knowledge. Its core approach transforms structured human expert knowledge into interpretable guidance signals for computational models. This enables models to receive semantic direction from domain expertise while maintaining data-driven foundations, thereby achieving active focusing and enhancement of key discriminative features.

Overall architecture of the knowledge-guided model

The knowledge guidance model adopts the design principle of “layered injection and dynamic guidance,” deploying knowledge modules differentially according to the semantic abstraction levels of different layers within the feature pyramid. Its overall framework is illustrated in Fig. 3.

Knowledge Guidance Module Structure Diagram.

The model encodes and integrates foundational knowledge through the Ontology_C2f module within the backbone network, embedding taxonomic and morphological knowledge into the feature extraction process to establish a feature foundation rich in semantic prior information. In the neck network, differentiated knowledge refinement strategies are implemented based on the semantic abstraction gradient of the feature pyramid: For shallow features (P3/8), a serial pipeline guided by region-specific adaptive optimization is employed. By focusing on key regions to discern and dynamically adjust knowledge injection, it achieves deep sharpening of fine-grained features. For mid-to-high-level features (P4/16, P5/32) dominated by semantic information, the focus shifts to context-adaptive knowledge fusion and modulation. Leveraging the global semantic context of high-level features, the intensity and mode of knowledge fusion are dynamically adjusted to enhance discriminative power.

Knowledge encoding

To enable the effective use of domain knowledge within detection networks, the primary challenge lies in transforming abstract ornithological concepts into computational representations that can be processed by neural networks. This study constructs a hierarchical knowledge encoding system that embeds structured prior knowledge into the feature learning process through vectorized representations.

The foundation of knowledge encoding rests upon dual representations of taxonomic relationships and morphological characteristics. Based on the taxonomic hierarchy of birds, a trainable embedding matrix is constructed, where N denotes the total number of categories and represents the embedding dimension. This matrix ensures species with close taxonomic relationships obtain similar vector representations in the embedding space, thereby providing topological constraints on inter-class relationships for the model.

Simultaneously, key morphological discriminative features are systematically encoded to construct a predefined morphological feature lookup table M. This table encompasses multiple discriminative dimensions including body proportions, bill structure, and crest morphology. Knowledge injection and network integration

The core challenge in bird detection tasks within complex natural scenes is the high similarity among target species, which results in insufficiently discriminative features. Traditional data-driven convolutional neural networks struggle to consistently learn strongly discriminative feature representations from limited samples. To address this, this section describes a knowledge-guided feature enhancement module (Ontology_C2f), whose architecture is shown in Fig. 4. This module enhances the semantic discriminative power of the output features by deeply integrating structured ornithological prior knowledge, encoded as learnable embedding vectors, into the network’s forward propagation process.

Ontology_C2f Module.

The Ontology_C2f module adopts a dual-path parallel architecture. The identity path preserves the integrity of input features through an identity mapping, ensuring stable gradient flow and foundational representation capabilities. The knowledge path constructs a comprehensive semantic enhancement pathway, sequentially achieving the organic embedding of domain knowledge into visual features through: (1) knowledge encoding based on embedded vectors, (2) feature refinement via normalization and nonlinear activation, and (3) feature recalibration using a hybrid attention mechanism.

The module design balances feature enhancement with computational efficiency. Feature maps from dual-path outputs are fused through channel concatenation, with 1 × 1 convolutions performing final information integration and dimensionality transformation. This architecture ensures the module’s output preserves essential spatial details while being enriched with knowledge-guided discriminative semantic information, providing a more robust feature foundation for subsequent detection heads.

Part-level attention guidance

In complex natural scene bird detection tasks, the accurate discrimination of morphologically similar species constitutes a core challenge. Traditional detection models typically lack the ability to focus on key discriminative anatomical parts, leading to an inadequate perception of subtle morphological differences. To address this issue, this study designed the BirdPartWrapper module. It establishes semantic associations between visual features and domain knowledge through a multi-head attention mechanism, achieving adaptive guidance toward discriminative anatomical regions.

This module employs an encoder-style architecture for the joint processing of raw visual features and knowledge embeddings, with its structure illustrated in Fig. 5. Within the multi-head attention mechanism, visual features serve as query signals, while knowledge-enhanced features provide key-value pairs. By calculating correlation weights across different spatial locations, it achieves dynamic focusing on key discriminative regions. Specifically, this mechanism autonomously learns the relative importance of different anatomical parts for species identification based on ornithological prior knowledge, significantly enhancing feature responses in critical areas such as head morphology, beak structure, and wing characteristics.

Layer normalization mitigates internal covariate shift and accelerates model convergence, while the feedforward network enhances representational capacity through nonlinear transformations. This design enables the module output to preserve original visual details while incorporating knowledge-based semantic guidance, providing a more discriminative feature foundation for subsequent detection tasks.

Data construction and analysis for bird Island, Qinghai Lake

The development of high-performance detection models relies on a foundation of high-quality data. To address the specific bird detection requirements of Qinghai Lake’s Bird Island—characterized by numerous small targets, dense populations, and complex backgrounds—this study independently constructed a dedicated dataset named QHBirds29.

Data collection was conducted at Qinghai Lake Bird Island Nature Reserve using high-definition surveillance equipment and professional photography gear across varying time periods, weather conditions, and lighting scenarios to ensure temporal and spatial diversity and representativeness.All data were acquired through non-invasive, observational methods; no birds were captured, handled, or disturbed. Following systematic cleaning and screening, the final dataset comprises 29 common bird species in the Qinghai Lake region, totaling 5,412 images.

The dataset encompasses a wide range of challenging scenarios that may be encountered in real-world monitoring, including but not limited to: drastic changes in lighting conditions, interference from water bodies and vegetation backgrounds, varying degrees of occlusion between targets, and extreme scale variations caused by shooting distances. Conclusion

This study tackles the core technical challenges in Qinghai Lake bird detection—feature degradation from distant small targets, low discriminability among morphologically similar species, and severe background clutter—by introducing YOLO_BD, an enhanced detection model that synergistically combines feature enhancement with knowledge-guided inference.

The proposed high-frequency perception–spatial dependency perception (HFP-SDP) module enables the effective extraction of discriminative fine-grained details from avian targets, substantially boosting the detection rate for small-scale objects. In parallel, a novel knowledge-base system, enriched with ornithological domain knowledge, converts structured prior knowledge into computable guidance signals. Via attention-driven mechanisms, the model learns to concentrate on semantically salient morphological features, markedly improving its accuracy in distinguishing between similar species. Furthermore, a hierarchically arranged feature enhancement strategy increases the model’s robustness in cluttered natural environments, alleviating performance drops due to occlusion and background noise.

Experimental results show that YOLO_BD attains a 75.2% mAP@0.5 on the dedicated QHBirds29 dataset while preserving high computational efficiency, outperforming existing baseline models by a clear margin. This work delivers a practical and efficient technical solution for automated bird monitoring and presents a forward-looking methodology for embedding domain expertise into fine-grained object detection.

Despite these promising results, this study has several limitations that should be addressed in future work. The model was only tested on QHBirds29 under normal conditions; its performance in extreme weather or severe occlusion is unknown. The knowledge base relies on manually defined features, which may not be exhaustive. Generalization to other habitats or species requires further validation. Future work will focus on collecting more diverse data, exploring domain adaptation, and integrating multi-modal sensors to improve robustness and applicability.

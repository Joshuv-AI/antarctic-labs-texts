Evaluation of right adrenal vein cannulation by trans-catheter contrast-enhanced ultrasonography: A retrospective comparative study
Authors: Like Qian, Yuyu Liu, Lumin Chen, Chenhan Liu, Feng Jiang, Renbiao Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Adrenal vein sampling (AVS), the gold standard for confirmation of primary aldosteronism (PA) subtype, is technically challenging. Here, we assessed the benefit of trans-catheter contrast-enhanced ultrasonography (CEUS) in right adrenal gland imaging and its potential to improve the right AVS success rates among inexperienced interventionalists. AVS was performed on all included PA patients (n = 61; 39 men; mean age, 52 ± 8.81 years) by a single interventionalist (who had no AVS experience prior to the study) between January 2020 and July 2022. Thirty-five patients underwent trans-catheter CEUS-assisted digital subtraction angiography (DSA)-guided AVS (CEUS-AVS), and 26 patients underwent DSA-guided AVS (DSA-AVS). In the CEUS-AVS group, following right adrenal vein cannulation, selective trans-catheter CEUS was performed to validate cannulation accuracy. Fisher exact test, two-sided Student t tests, and the Mann–Whitney test were used for statistical analysis. The right AVS success rate was higher in the CEUS-AVS than in the DSA-AVS group (94.29% vs 73.08%, P = .03), but the left and bilateral AVS success rates did not differ. The ultrasound imaging success rate of the right adrenal vein was 97.1%. Right AVS was unsuccessful in 9 patients (two in the CEUS-AVS and 7 in the DSA-AVS group). Operative times did not differ, but radiation exposure times were shorter in CEUS-AVS patients (8.4 [6.00, 12.3] vs 15.37 [7.23, 24.75], P = .04). Surgery-related complications were similar between groups. CEUS-AVS can be used to confirm right adrenal vein cannulation accuracy, help inexperienced interventionalists rapidly improve AVS success rates, and shorten radiation exposure.

1. Introduction

Primary aldosteronism (PA) is the most common cause of secondary hypertension. The vast majority of PA cases (>95%) can be classified into the bilateral adrenal hyperplasia (BAH) and aldosterone-producing adenoma (APA) subtypes.[1] Treatment protocols for these 2 PA subtypes differ considerably, with APA curable by adrenalectomy of the affected side and BAH requiring lifelong pharmacological treatment. An accurate preoperative diagnosis is therefore critically important.[2] Various guidelines recommend adrenal vein sampling (AVS) to distinguish between APA and BAH.[3,4]

It is more difficult to achieve accurate sampling in the right than in the left adrenal vein due to the challenge of distinguishing between liver parenchymal staining and right adrenal gland staining by retrograde digital subtraction angiography (DSA) of the accessory hepatic veins (AHVs) in certain patients. The right adrenal central vein, typically smaller than the left, usually drains into the inferior vena cava. Less commonly, drainage leads into the AHVs.[5,6] In addition, AVS has a steep learning curve, which can lead to unsatisfactory success rates for inexperienced interventionalists.[7,8] The use of intraoperative computed tomography (CT) technologies such as Dyna-CT, C-arm CT, and angio-CT can assist right adrenal vein cannulation, but it is associated with risks such as increased patient exposure to radiation and prolongation of operative time.[9]

In trans-catheter contrast-enhanced ultrasonography (CEUS), whole-body distribution of the contrast agent enables specific imaging of target organs and a reduction in microbubble dose. Studies have reported satisfactory results with the use of trans-catheter CEUS during transarterial chemoembolization[10,11] and prostate artery embolization.[12] Although transabdominal ultrasonography (US) is often unable to provide direct visualization of the right adrenal glandular structures in clinical practice, the right adrenal glandular region is suitable for ultrasound examination, as this anatomical area is generally unobscured by bony structures and gas.

We hypothesized that trans-catheter CEUS during AVS improves right adrenal gland visualization and aimed to determine whether trans-catheter CEUS-assisted AVS can improve the success rates of inexperienced interventionalists performing right AVS.

2. Methods

The ethics committee of Sir Run Run Hospital approved this single-center retrospective study (Approval Number: 2022-0429; Date of review: November 2, 2022), and all procedures were performed in compliance with relevant laws, institutional guidelines, and the Declaration of Helsinki. The study was exempted from the requirement for informed consent due to its retrospective nature.

2.1. AVS operator

A single interventionalist with no prior independent AVS operator experience performed all AVS procedures.

2.2. Patients

Seventy-seven patients with PA who underwent AVS at our hospital between January 2020 and November 2022 were included. Sixteen patients were excluded because their surgery was not performed by the same interventionalist, resulting in a final sample of 61 patients (39 men; mean age: 52 ± 8.81 years). None of the patients had a history of severe thyroid disease or allergy to iodine or other US contrast agents. All patients underwent contrast-enhanced CT or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the bilateral adrenal glands within 1 month preoperatively. Antihypertensive medications were switched to terazosin and amlodipine 2 weeks before AVS, and adequate potassium supplementation was administered preoperatively. Adrenocorticotropic hormone was administered by bolus injection (200 μg) 30 minutes before AVS and by drip infusion (rate: 50 μg/h) throughout the entire surgical procedure.

2.3. DSA-AVS procedure

The AVS procedure was performed using a DSA system (Allura Xper FD20; PHILIPS, Best, The Netherlands). 5-French vascular sheaths (Radifocus; Terumo, Tokyo, Japan) were inserted into the bilateral femoral veins and flushed with heparinized water. Left adrenal vein cannulation was performed via the left femoral vein using a 5-French Sim-2 catheter (Tempo; Cordis, Miami Lakes). Approximately 2 mL of contrast agent (Omnipaque; GE Healthcare, Marlborough) was manually injected as a bolus to confirm that the catheter tip was located at the left adrenal vein. A microcatheter was then inserted into the left adrenal vein, taking care to position the microcatheter tip distal to the opening of the left phrenic vein, and the inserted microcatheter was locked with heparinized water. Subsequently, a Sim-2 catheter was inserted through the right femoral vein to cannulate the right adrenal vein based on the approximate location of the right adrenal vein opening determined by preoperative CT or MRI. When the catheter tip came into contact with the suspected right adrenal vein, a bolus injection of a small amount of contrast agent was administered to assist in confirming the correct location. The right adrenal central vein was subsequently cannulated with the microcatheter under micro-guidewire guidance to ensure catheter stability, and both adrenal veins were simultaneously sampled.

2.4. CEUS-AVS procedure

Patients who underwent CEUS-AVS received an additional trans-catheter slow bolus injection of a small amount of US contrast agent (SonoVue; concentration, 0.59 mg/mL SF6; volume < 5 mL) to confirm successful cannulation of the right adrenal vein. All other surgical procedures were identical to those of the DSA-AVS procedure. Intraoperative CEUS examination was performed by a physician with 8 years of experience using an US system (EPIQ 5; PHILIPS). Specifically, tissue enhancement mode using the C5-1 abdominal probe with frequency of 3 Hz and low mechanical index values (0.06–0.08) was utilized. Enhancement patterns in the right adrenal gland region and liver tissue in the hypochondriac region were observed in real-time and recorded to again confirm right adrenal vein cannulation (Fig. 1). Patients were instructed not to change their body position or make breathing adjustments during the US examination.

Success or failure of right adrenal vein cannulation was independently judged by the operator. If the right adrenal vein could not be engaged intraoperatively using the SIM-2 catheter, a C2 catheter or RH catheter (Tempo; Cordis) were used as alternatives. When the bilateral adrenal vein microcatheters were correctly positioned, 5 mL of blood was simultaneously sampled from both adrenal glands. Imaging was performed again to confirm that the catheters had not been displaced, and 5 mL blood samples were taken from both renal veins and the inferior vena cava.

Operative time, radiation exposure time, and surgery-related adverse reactions were recorded. Since the actual radiation dose used in AVS is not routinely noted in our center, radiation dose data was not collected.

2.5. Criterion for successful AVS

Cannulation was considered successful when the selectivity index (ratio of plasma cortisol concentration in the adrenal vein to the external iliac vein) was >3.

2.6. Statistical analysis

All statistical data were analyzed using Prism 7.0 software (GraphPad, La Jolla). Fisher exact test was used to compare proportions of male patients, incidences of operation-related complications, and success rates. The Shapiro–Wilk normality test was used to test for normal distributions; continuous data was compared using two-sided Student t tests and expressed as the mean ± standard deviation. Non-normally distributed continuous data were expressed as medians (quartile) and compared using the Mann–Whitney test. We considered P values <.05 statistically significant.

3. Results

A total of 61 patients was included in this retrospective study, of whom 35 belonged to the CEUS-AVS and 26 to the DSA-AVS group. Table 1 shows the baseline data of all patients. Patient sex, age, diastolic blood pressure, body mass index, number of antihypertensive medications, and lowest serum potassium levels did not differ significantly between the 2 groups. However, systolic blood pressure was significantly higher in the CEUS-AVS than in the DSA-AVS group.

Successful adrenal vein re-cannulation was performed in 4 of the 5 cases, while the right adrenal vein opening could not be located in the remaining case. In the other unsuccessful right AVS case in the CEUS-AVS group, successful right adrenal vein cannulation was confirmed by CEUS, but a sufficient volume of blood could not be sampled. The success rate of CEUS imaging of the right adrenal vein was 97.1%, and the use of US imaging increased the right AVS success rate from 82.9 to 94.3%.

Ten AVS procedures were unsuccessful, of which 9 were right AVS (two in the CEUS-AVS group and 7 in the DSA-AVS group). Among the 10 patients who underwent unsuccessful AVS (including one case of unsuccessful left AVS), 6 patients underwent repeat AVS by other interventionalists. Three of these patients had successful AVS. One patient opted for left adrenalectomy, and 3 patients refused to undergo repeat AVS. Compared with the DSA-AVS group, the radiation exposure time was shorter in the CEUS-AVS group (8.4 [6.00, 12.3] vs 15.37 [7.23, 24.75], P = .04), but there was no significant difference in operative time (46 [40, 61] vs 57 [35, 74], P = .32) (Table 2).

In terms of adverse effects, 7 of the 61 patients experienced local contrast agent retention in the side wall of the inferior vena cava (five cases in the CEUS-AVS group and 2 cases in the DSA-AVS group), suggesting possible surgery-related injury to the right adrenal vein or inferior vena cava. One patient in the DSA-AVS group presented with right-sided low back pain, and post-operative CT revealed a hematoma in the right adrenal region, raising the possibility of adrenal vein rupture. The patient was retained in the hospital for further observation, and the pain disappeared after 3 days. No other adverse reactions related to the AVS operations, iodine contrast agent, or US contrast agent were observed. The incidence of surgery-related complications did not significantly differ between the CEUS-AVS and the DSA-AVS group (14.3% vs 7.7%, P = .67) (Table 2).

4. Discussion

AVS is an important means for distinguishing between the APA and BAH subtypes of PA. However, right AVS has a steep learning curve due to high technical difficulty, leading to low success rates among inexperienced interventionalists; this results in low AVS rates and potential missed opportunities for surgery in many patients.[5,8] In this study, we evaluated the efficacy of trans-catheter CEUS in improving the success rate of right AVS. Our results demonstrate that CEUS significantly enhances the accuracy of right adrenal vein cannulation, achieving a success rate of 94.29%, compared to 73.08% with DSA alone. This improvement is particularly notable given that the study was conducted by a single interventionalist with no prior independent AVS experience. The high success rate achieved with CEUS suggests that this technique can effectively mitigate the steep learning curve associated with right AVS, making it a valuable tool for centers that are either beginning to perform AVS or cannot maintain a high procedural volume. Furthermore, the use of CEUS not only improves cannulation accuracy but also reduces radiation exposure times, with a median fluoroscopy time of 8.4 minutes in the CEUS group compared to 15.37 minutes in the DSA group. These findings highlight the potential of CEUS to enhance procedural outcomes while minimizing radiation risk, thereby offering a safer and more effective alternative to traditional DSA-guided AVS.

Despite the significant improvement in right AVS success rates with CEUS, no significant differences were observed in left AVS and bilateral AVS success rates between the CEUS-AVS and DSA-AVS groups. This lack of significant improvement in left AVS success rates can be attributed to the relatively fixed and straightforward anatomy of the left adrenal vein, which typically drains directly into the left renal vein. This anatomical simplicity means that left adrenal vein catheterization is generally less challenging and less prone to failure, even without the use of advanced imaging techniques such as CEUS. Consequently, we did not attempt to use CEUS for left AVS in our study. The lack of a significant difference in bilateral AVS success rates may also be related to the small sample size in our cohort. Specifically, only one instance of left AVS failure occurred in the CEUS group, which was insufficient to demonstrate a statistically significant difference in bilateral AVS success rates between the CEUS-AVS and DSA-AVS groups. Future studies with larger sample sizes may be necessary to more accurately assess the impact of CEUS on left AVS and bilateral AVS success rates.

Previous studies have demonstrated that intraoperative CT significantly increases right AVS success rates[9,13–18] while also increasing risks of patient exposure to radiation, and prolonged operative times.[9] To address these issues, we utilized trans-catheter CEUS, a simple,radiation-free and rapid imaging method, as an alternative to intraoperative CT. Compared with intraoperative CT, our study demonstrated that trans-catheter CEUS-assisted AVS achieved a comparable success rate for right AVS while eliminating additional radiation exposure and without extending procedure time. A detailed comparison between our study and other studies with similar objectives is presented in Table 3. Trans-catheter CEUS offers several advantages when used in combination with AVS: avoidance of the increased risk of patient radiation exposure caused by intraoperative CT; reduction in risk of catheter displacement compared to CT since the AVS operator during CEUS is not required to take both hands off the catheter; shorter operative times; and reduction of equipment bottlenecks. Although DSA systems used at some hospitals are not equipped with CT functions and cannot be used for intraoperative CT angiography, CEUS is widely used in clinical practice, making it a feasible option.

Five cases underwent repeated CEUS during CEUS-AVS and 4 of them got successful adrenal vein re-cannulation in the second trail. The left unsuccessful AVS case underwent 3 times of CEUS, the catheter was found in AHV 2 times and once in inferior vena cava by CEUS. As the ultrasonic machine was prepared in advance, CEUS can be finished in less than 2 minutes. This can make sense that CEUS-AVS did not extend the operative time.

When the right AVS is not successfully identified during DSA-AVS, an attempt is made to locate other right adrenal veins. While this increases the success rate, the degree of radiation exposure also increases. Shorter radiation exposure times were reported in our CEUS-AVS group, potentially due to the fact that only one right adrenal vein blood sample was collected from all successful AVS patients. However, in our DSA-AVS group, 2 or more right adrenal vein samples were collected in 5 patients.

One earlier study reported that an individual interventionalist needs to perform approximately 36 AVS procedures to achieve a satisfactory AVS success rate and at least 20 procedures annually to maintain such a rate.[8] The interventionalist in our study had no AVS operator experience prior to the study. Our results demonstrate that CEUS assistance considerably improved this interventionalist’s AVS success rate. If this is confirmed, it would be of great significance to medical centers that either are starting to perform AVS or cannot maintain surgical volumes of at least 20 annual AVS procedures.

There are some disadvantages associated with trans-catheter CEUS-guided AVS: difficulty in the acquisition of US equipment with CEUS functions as well as lack of AVS ultrasonologists; since, similar to CT, CEUS can only confirm cannulation rather than assist in the initial right adrenal vein cannulation, experience is still required for operators to become proficient in right adrenal vein cannulation; and contrast-enhanced CT or MRI of the adrenal glands prior to AVS remains essential as it provides critically necessary anatomical information regarding, for example, the vertebral level at the opening of the right adrenal vein, the anatomical relationship between the right adrenal vein and AHV, and the direction of the right adrenal vein opening.[19]

The present study has certain limitations. First, this is a single-center retrospective study with small sample size. Potentially due to this small sample size, there were no cases of erroneous cannulation of the right phrenic or right renal capsular vein, which have been reported as vessels that are difficult to distinguish from the right adrenal vein. Second, given that DSA-AVS was developed earlier than CEUS-AVS, the associated learning curve may have contributed to the higher CEUS-AVS success rate. Third, prior to the commencement of this study, there were no interventionalists who were highly experienced in AVS at our hospital; therefore, we were unable to evaluate the effects of CEUS guidance on AVS performed by experienced interventionalists. Fourth, with increasing operator experience, CEUS assistance is not required in all patients. Our current practice is to only utilize CEUS when the operator is not certain that the adrenal vein has been correctly cannulated under DSA guidance. Fifth, the radiation dose and contrast agent dose were not recorded for each AVS procedure, and comparisons of CEUS-AVS with intraoperative CT-assisted AVS were not performed.

In conclusion, the present study demonstrates that the use of the simple trans-catheter CEUS technique enables right adrenal vein imaging and confirmation of successful right adrenal vein cannulation. Trans-catheter CEUS can help inexperienced interventionalists more rapidly improve their right AVS success rates and shorten patient radiation exposure times.

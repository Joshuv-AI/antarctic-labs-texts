Neonatal right ventricular rhabdomyoma: Imaging findings and spontaneous regression
Authors: Imane Mezdaoui, Anass Ayad, El Mehdi Bahous, Salahiddine Saghir, Mohamed Sellouti, Rachid Abilkassem
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Neonatal cardiac rhabdomyoma is a rare benign mesenchymal tumor that frequently serves as the sentinel clinical marker for tuberous sclerosis complex (TSC). We report a case of a term female neonate presenting with immediate respiratory distress and a Silverman score of 4/10. Initial transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) on day 3 of life revealed a voluminous, multilobulated, hyperechoic mass measuring 28 mm × 16 mm occupying the right ventricular cavity, involving both the inflow and outflow tracts. Despite the significant tumor burden, hemodynamic stability was maintained without significant outflow tract obstruction or arrhythmias, allowing for a successful conservative management approach. A comprehensive systemic screening protocol—including brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and renal ultrasonography—was negative for additional stigmata of TSC. Follow-up at 2 years demonstrated complete clinical stability and significant spontaneous tumor regression to 11 mm × 6 mm. This case highlights the necessity of multimodal imaging for diagnosis and risk stratification, while reinforcing the favorable natural progression of nonobstructive rhabdomyomas.

Introduction

Primary cardiac tumors in the pediatric population represent an exceptional clinical finding, with rhabdomyoma identified as the most frequent histological type, representing approximately 45%-60% of cases [1]. These benign hamartomatous growths are most frequently diagnosed within the first year of life, with an estimated incidence in live-born infants ranging from 0.02% to 0.17% [2]. Although histologically benign, its clinical significance is profoundly linked to its high association with tuberous sclerosis complex (TSC), an autosomal dominant genetic disorder [3].

While the majority of these tumors remain asymptomatic and tend to undergo spontaneous regression during early childhood, they can occasionally lead to severe complications, such as outflow tract obstruction or life-threatening arrhythmias. The exceptional novelty of the present case lies in the massive volume and multilobulated burden of the mass occupying nearly the entirety of the right ventricular cavity, which paradoxically maintained complete hemodynamic stability and laminar outflow tract flow under purely conservative management.

Case report

A term female neonate with a birth weight of 3100 g was delivered via cesarean section. Immediately postdelivery, the infant exhibited signs of respiratory distress, evidenced by a Silverman score of 4/10, requiring admission to the neonatal intensive care unit. Physical examination revealed a hemodynamically stable infant with normal heart sounds and no audible murmurs, and cutaneous examination was negative for ash-leaf spots or other neurocutaneous markers. Initial diagnostic workup included a chest radiograph, which showed moderate cardiomegaly and a wet pulmonary appearance.

Given the absence of hemodynamic compromise or rhythm disturbances, the patient was managed conservatively. During the 2-year clinical course, the patient remained entirely asymptomatic with no documented episodes of arrhythmia on serial routine electrocardiography. Discussion

Cardiac rhabdomyoma is universally recognized as the principal cardiac tumor diagnosed in the pediatric age [1]. The presence of these tumors serves as a critical clinical biomarker, as they are often the first typical indication of TSC in the prenatal and neonatal periods [2,4]. TSC is a multisystem autosomal dominant disorder resulting from mutations in the TSC1 or TSC2 genes [3,5]. Statistically, approximately 60%-90% of infants presenting with cardiac rhabdomyomas will eventually meet the diagnostic criteria for TSC [1,2]. This makes the radiological identification of a cardiac mass a mandatory trigger for systemic evaluation, including brain MRI and renal ultrasound, regardless of the family history, as 60%-70% of TSC cases arise from de novo mutations [3].

The clinical spectrum of TSC is incredibly varied, and the absence of classic neurocutaneous markers in a neonate does not definitively exclude a diagnosis [3]. In the neonatal period, many hallmark lesions such as hypomelanotic ash-leaf macules or retinal hamartomas are frequently absent at birth, as observed in our patient [3]. Transthoracic echocardiography remains the primary diagnostic tool due to its accessibility and high sensitivity for detecting intramyocardial lesions, where rhabdomyomas typically present as hyperechoic, homogeneous, and well-circumscribed masses [6,4]. While TTE provides essential functional data, advanced cross-sectional imaging like CT provides superior anatomical definition and tissue characterization [6,7]. In our case, Chest CT provided a comprehensive mapping of the mass relative to the thoracic cavity, while Brain MRI served to exclude subependymal nodules or cortical tubers, which are sensitive indicators of TSC that often precede dermatological signs [2,7].

A comprehensive meta-analysis of 409 rhabdomyoma cases reveals that the anatomical location of the tumor significantly influences clinical outcomes and the necessity for intervention [8]. Interestingly, solitary forms were found to be slightly less frequent than multiple masses but were statistically more likely to cause significant outflow tract obstruction compared to numerous small lesions. Specifically, tumors located within the interventricular septum have the highest incidence of hemodynamically significant obstruction, often necessitating immediate surgical or pharmacological management [8]. In our patient, despite the large size of the mass (28 mm × 16 mm), the location allowed for maintained structural patency and laminar flow, which served as a critical favorable prognostic indicator.

To understand the spontaneous regression observed in our patient, we must look at the cellular architecture of cardiac rhabdomyomas. These hamartomas originate from embryonic myocytes and are histologically characterized by pathognomonic, glycogen-filled “spider cells” [1,7]. While the precise underlying biological mechanisms driving tumor involution remain incompletely understood, current pathophysiological hypotheses suggest an inability of these highly specialized tumor cells to divide while surrounding cardiac chambers grow. Furthermore, it has been speculated that the withdrawal of maternal hormones postdelivery and immunohistochemical markers like ubiquitin—associated with myofilament degradation and programmed apoptosis—may play a role [1]. This probable molecular “expiration date” explains why a minimalist conservative approach was highly successful in our nonobstructive case.

Pharmacological management with mTOR inhibitors like everolimus or sirolimus is typically reserved for cases presenting with critical hemodynamic compromise or refractory arrhythmias [5,9,11]. In our patient, the complete absence of outflow pathway obstruction or electrical disturbances allowed us to bypass the toxicities and high-risk monitoring protocols associated with neonatal mTOR inhibition, relying instead on the favorable natural history of the lesion [10,12].

Study limitations

Several limitations must be acknowledged regarding this case report. First, histopathological confirmation of the tumor was not obtained. Because the infant maintained perfect hemodynamic parameters and remained completely asymptomatic, an invasive endomyocardial biopsy was clinically unjustified and presented unnecessary risks; thus, the diagnosis remains presumptive based on pathognomonic multimodal imaging features and its subsequent regression. Second, while a rigorous clinical, radiological, and systemic screening protocol for TSC was entirely negative, definitive genetic testing for TSC1 and TSC2 mutations was not performed due to parental choice and financial constraints. Despite the lack of genetic mapping, the patient's continuous clinical stability and absence of extra-cardiac manifestations over 2 years strongly support an excellent isolated cardiac prognosis.

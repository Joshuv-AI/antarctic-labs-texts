Effects of noise isolation using noise-canceling headphones during lymphedema liposuction on postoperative pain in patients undergoing general anesthesia: a protocol for a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Yuan Gao, Lei Liu, Qing Zhang, Jiaojiao Yang, Tianzuo Li, Huihui Miao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOperating room noise, generated by medical equipment, poses significant health risks and can lead to hearing impairment, cardiovascular disorders, and anxiety in both patients and staff. Accumulating evidence suggests that auditory processing persists under general anesthesia, with intraoperative noise exposure potentially affecting postoperative outcomes. Recent studies have reported a positive correlation between intraoperative noise levels (≥ 70 dB) and postoperative pain intensity, particularly when using high-noise liposuction devices for lymph liposuction procedures. Non-pharmacological pain management strategies are prioritized to not mask ischemic symptoms in extremities. This study aimed to investigate whether noise-canceling headphones can mitigate postoperative pain by limiting intraoperative noise exposure during lymph liposuction.MethodsThis randomized controlled trial will include 90 patients undergoing elective lymph liposuction under general anesthesia. The intervention group will wear noise-canceling headphones throughout surgery, while the control subjects will receive standard care. Primary outcomes include postoperative pain scores (NRS) at 6, 12, and 24 h. Secondary outcomes comprise intraoperative nociceptive threshold index (PTI) monitored via multifunctional electroencephalography (EEG), preoperative/postoperative sleep quality (PSQI), and anxiety levels (HADS). In addition, mediation analysis will be conducted to examine noise exposure duration/intensity as predictors of pain outcomes.DiscussionThis randomized controlled trial will assess the efficacy and safety of noise-canceling headphones for reducing postoperative pain after lymph liposuction under general anesthesia. Since non-pharmacological interventions play critical roles in multimodal pain management, intraoperative noise reduction may effectively mitigate postoperative pain while avoiding pharmacological masking of ischemic limb symptoms. By integrating real-time neurophysiological monitoring of pain threshold dynamics, this trial explored how auditory environmental factors modulate nociceptive processing during anesthesia. If proven effective, this approach can enhance recovery protocols by minimizing pain-related complications and optimizing perioperative well-being in patients undergoing high-noise surgical procedures.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry ChiCTR2400087400. Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

Noise, defined as undesirable and unpleasant sound that disrupts normal auditory perception, constitutes a stochastic and unstable acoustic mixture [1]. Despite our capacity to precisely quantify peak exposure levels or time-averaged energy metrics, the adverse health effects of noise remain underappreciated [2]. Beyond auditory damage, noise exposure correlates with diverse non-auditory health problems, including annoyance [3], sleep disturbance [4], cardiovascular diseases [5, 6], and cognitive impairment in children [7]. Since the 1970 s, intraoperative noise has been recognized as the third major type of contaminant in modern surgical practice [8].

Accumulating evidence suggests that general anesthesia does not abolish the cerebral processing of auditory stimuli, and central auditory pathways remain functional, with preserved cortical activation in the auditory cortex [9]. Remarkably, anesthetized patients retain the capacity for semantic processing of auditory information [10], highlighting the potential clinical implications of intraoperative noise exposure. Paradoxically, therapeutic auditory interventions, like intraoperative music, can offer analgesia, reduce postoperative pain, anxiety, and opioid consumption, and improve patients’ satisfaction [11, 12]. In contrast, environmental operating room noise represents aversive auditory stimulation. Although hypnotic auditory suggestions via headphones during anesthesia have shown opioid-sparing effects [13], the specific effects of ambient surgical noise on perioperative pain intensity and cerebral neurodynamics remain unexplored.

Lymphedema, a chronic lymphatic disorder characterized by interstitial fluid accumulation due to lymphatic insufficiency [14], affects approximately 200 million individuals globally [15]. With the increasing incidence of cancer worldwide, the prevalence of secondary lymphedema continues to escalate after treating cancer [16]. Liposuction debulking surgery has become a mainstay treatment for advanced cases [17]; however, it can lead to intraoperative noise exposure (≈70 dB) from suction devices. Postoperative analgesia presents unique challenges. Particularly, routine use of continuous analgesia pumps may mask ischemic complications in compressed extremities. Current protocols rely on reactive opioid administration after surgery, despite associated adverse effects, including urinary retention, pruritus, gastrointestinal dysfunction, and cognitive impairment [18]. Although multimodal approaches combining NSAIDs or regional blocks with opioids can minimize the need for opioids [19], drug-related complications persist [20]. These limitations have encouraged research on non-pharmacological interventions [21], making noise attenuation a strategically valuable analgesic adjunct in lymphedema surgery where pharmacologic risks and diagnostic interference must be minimized.

Multifunctional EEG monitoring can objectively monitor the brain function of patients by collecting the two-channel EEG of the left and right hemispheres and using the cloud server to calculate and analyze the EEG signals to provide brain function data [22]. Using the wavelet algorithm, the EEG collected through the forehead skin will be decomposed into two parts: cortical and subcortical EEG waves. Therefore, the characteristics of static coordination, dynamic coordination, spatial coordination, and temporal coordination of the two parts of EEG waves can be calculated. In addition, the characteristics of pain, analgesia, sleep, anxiety, depression, concentration, and memory will be assessed. The device will be used to detect the preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative brain state of patients, and measure the effect of intraoperative noise on the perioperative brain status of patients with general anesthesia.

Objectives {7}

The study hypothesizes that intraoperative noise isolation using noise-canceling headphones may reduce postoperative pain scores and decrease postoperative consumption of analgesics. Additionally, the study aims to investigate the effects of intraoperative noise exposure on pain threshold during general anesthesia and postoperative cerebral function in patients undergoing general anesthesia. This study will also provide preliminary insights into the mechanisms underlying the effects of intraoperative noise exposure on postoperative pain in anesthetized patients.

Trial design {8}

This study is a single-center, randomized, double-blind, controlled superiority trial with a concealed 1:1 allocation. Participants will be randomly assigned to either wear noise-canceling headphones for intraoperative noise isolation or a non-intervention control group not using headphones. Methods: participants, interventions, and outcomes

Study setting {9}

Patient enrollment is scheduled from August 2024 to June 2026. This prospective study is being conducted at Beijing Shijitan Hospital, a tertiary care facility affiliated with Capital Medical University. The institution possesses China’s largest specialized lymphatic surgery department, conducting approximately 1000 lymphatic liposuctions annually.

Eligibility criteria {10}

Participants will be eligible for enrollment if they meet the following criteria and provide written informed consent: (1) aged 18–65 years; (2) classified as the American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) Physical Status I–III; (3) scheduled for elective lymphatic liposuction under general anesthesia.

The exclusion criteria are as follows: (1) auditory impairment; (2) preoperative chronic pain persisting ≥ 3 months or long-term use of analgesics (> 1 week preoperatively); (3) severe comorbidities necessitating admission to postoperative intensive care unit (ICU); (4) anticipated need for postoperative mechanical ventilation or supplemental administration of sedatives/anesthetics; (5) being unable to cooperate due to cognitive impairment, language barriers, or other conditions not allowing compliance with the trial protocol.

Who will take informed consent? {26a}

Before group allocation, written informed consent will be obtained by an investigator from potential participants in the reception room. The researchers will provide a comprehensive explanation of the research background, objectives, methodology, and procedures, along with potential benefits and anticipated risks. Participants will be encouraged to raise questions during this process to confirm that they fully understand the study protocol and voluntarily participate.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

Standard perioperative pain management will be applied to patients undergoing liposuction for lymphedema. Standardized anesthesia protocols will be administered intraoperatively. During surgical wound dressing, flurbiprofen axetil (50 mg IV) and palonosetron hydrochloride (0.25 mg IV) will be administered as standardized perioperative prophylaxis. Postoperative analgesic strategies allow the use of opioids, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), paracetamol, tramadol, and other adjunctive analgesics when clinically needed. The standard protocol was employed as a control to investigate whether intraoperative use of noise-canceling headphones for noise isolation can provide better postoperative analgesia, reduce the dose of analgesics, and promote postoperative recovery.

Intervention description {11a}

Participants will undergo routine preoperative assessments. Next, informed consent will be obtained, and the detailed protocol will be explained 24 h before surgery. Baseline assessments comprise multimodal electroencephalographic (EEG) monitoring, mechanical pain pressure threshold (PPT) measurements, and psychometric assessments using the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) and Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS).

Standard intraoperative monitoring encompassing electrocardiography (ECG), pulse oximetry, hemodynamic parameters (heart rate and blood pressure), and bispectral index (BIS) will be implemented upon admission to the operating room. A standardized anesthetic protocol will be administered in both groups as follows: rapid sequence induction with midazolam (0.04 mg/kg IV), sufentanil (0.3–0.5 µg/kg IV), propofol (2–2.5 mg/kg IV), and rocuronium (0.6 mg/kg IV), followed by maintenance therapy combining remifentanil infusion (0.1–0.2 µg/kg/min), inhaled sevoflurane (1 MAC), and propofol infusion (1–4 mg/kg/h) titrated to maintain BIS at 40–60 with hemodynamic stability (± 20% baseline). Rocuronium redosing will be administered based on surgical needs.

Participants in the intervention group will wear noise-attenuating Peltor™ X5A earmuffs (3 M™, Poland; 37 dB signal-to-noise reduction) from anesthetic induction until surgical closure to isolate ambient noise. In contrast, participants in the control group will receive no acoustic intervention. Continuous A-weighted sound pressure levels (dBA) will be recorded at 1 Hz intervals using AVOS-K8 dosimeters (Wuhan Zhongce Measurement Instrument Co., China). Subsequent analysis will be conducted to calculate the mean dBA intensity and percentage time exceeding 70 dB. The pain threshold index (PTi) will be continuously monitored intraoperatively using a multiparametric EEG monitor.

Before the end of the surgery, flurbiprofen axetil (50 mg IV) and palonosetron hydrochloride (0.25 mg IV) will be administered as standardized perioperative prophylaxis. Opioids, NSAIDs, paracetamol, tramadol, and other adjunctive analgesics can be used for postoperative analgesia when clinically needed.

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions {11b}

Participants can withdraw from the study at any stage. The risk associated with intraoperative noise-canceling headphone isolation was considered low due to the absence of reported complications. Appropriate treatment will be given in case of complications.

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

Intraoperative noise-canceling headphones and perioperative EEG monitoring will be provided free of charge. In addition, participants may benefit from intensive postoperative pain management, which can encourage participation and adherence. All procedures involved in this study are non-invasive, which can improve the compliance of participants.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

Opioids, NSAIDs, acetaminophen, tramadol, and other medications are allowed for postoperative pain management. However, any form of local anesthesia, including wound penetration, femoral nerve block, and epidural analgesia, is prohibited.

Provisions for post-trial care {30}

Noise-canceling headphones and related monitoring are non-invasive and safe operations. Injuries related to this clinical trial will be documented, evaluated, and treated with appropriate medical care. The cost of such treatment will be covered by clinical trial insurance.

Outcomes {12}

The primary outcome is the maximum pain intensity across three postoperative intervals (0–6 h, 6–12 h, and 12–24 h), assessed using the 10-point numerical rating scale (NRS; range: 0–10, with 0 indicating “no pain” and 10 representing “worst imaginable pain”) to evaluate resting and movement-evoked pain scores at 6, 12, and 24 h postoperatively. Resting pain is defined as the self-reported intensity experienced while lying in bed without voluntary movement, whereas movement-evoked pain is measured during standardized lateral turning (45° torso elevation) in bed.

Sample size {14}

The sample size was calculated based on data from a pilot study with 12 participants (6 in the intervention group and 6 in the control group), which indicated that maximum movement-evoked pain scores within 24 h after surgery were 2.8 ± 1.8 in the noise-isolation group and 5.0 ± 3.7 in the control group. We used PASS 11.0 software (NCSS, LLC., Kaysville, UT, USA) for sample size calculation. Considering a statistical power of 0.90 with a two-sided α level of 0.05 under the assumption of equal group allocation for mean comparison analysis, a minimum sample size of 37 participants per group was determined to be necessary. Considering an estimated dropout rate of 15%, we plan to recruit 45 participants per group, resulting in a total of 90 participants.

Recruitment {15}

Eligible participants are hospitalized patients scheduled for elective lymphatic liposuction. A dedicated researcher will systematically review the preoperative surgical registry 24 h before the procedure to identify potential candidates. Patients fulfilling all inclusion criteria without meeting any exclusion criteria will undergo comprehensive eligibility screening. Qualified candidates and their legal representatives will be informed of the standardized protocol through face-to-face consultations. Next, informed consent will be obtained. A multidisciplinary collaboration protocol between the Department of Anesthesiology and Lymphatic Surgery will ensure coordinated enrollment, with real-time surgical schedule updates facilitating efficient recruitment.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

Participants will be randomly allocated to the two groups using a random number table, with equal distribution. No blocking or stratification will be implemented.

Concealment mechanism {16b}

Group allocations will be sealed in sequentially numbered, tamper-evident opaque envelopes. Envelopes will be stored in a biometric-controlled safe place and opened exclusively by a designated anesthesia nurse after confirming stable induction of anesthesia.

Implementation {16c}

The intervention group will receive noise-canceling headphones (Peltor™X5A, 3M™) with 37 dB active noise reduction immediately after opening the envelope.

The control group will receive the institution’s current standard anesthesia care protocol without noise-canceling headphone placement after induction.

An assigned nurse anesthetist who is aware of the group assignments will use prespecified equipment to conduct masking and eliminate anesthesiologist and surgeon bias.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {17a}

Trial participants, outcome assessors, and data analysts will be blinded to group allocation. Surgeons and anesthesiologists will not be involved in data collection, entry, or analysis to prevent bias.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

In case of severe postoperative pain, which cannot be controlled (i.e., NRS greater than 6 and lasting more than 2 h), the surgeon and anesthesiologist will analyze the cause of pain and comprehensively consider multimodal analgesia for effective pain control.

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {18a}

Uniformly trained researchers will conduct follow-ups and collect PSQI and HADS scores preoperatively and postoperatively. Resting and dynamic NRS scores will be recorded at 6, 12, and 24 h postoperatively. Preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative multimodal EEG data will be stored on encrypted servers and analyzed by certified biomedical engineers. Mechanical PPTs will be measured using a handheld electromechanical manometer (YISIDA-DS100, Hong Kong, China) with three consecutive measurements conducted at the same anatomical site of each participant. The mean value will be calculated for the final analysis. Baseline demographic data and postoperative analgesic consumption will be extracted directly from the hospital’s electronic medical record system.

Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up {18b}

In compliance with institutional data governance policies, all case report forms (CRFs) and validated Excel spreadsheets will be securely retained for at least 5 years after the study completion. Electronic records will be stored on password-protected, access-controlled servers with daily incremental backups. Physical CRFs will be archived in fireproof cabinets in restricted-access research facilities. No data transfers or disclosures will occur without the approval of the Institutional Review Board and obtaining participants’ written consent.

Data management {19}

Trained data collectors will enter information into predefined Excel templates following standardized operating procedures (SOPs). An independent researcher will conduct comprehensive quality checks, including range validations (e.g., NRS scores 0–10) and logical consistency assessments. Original CRFs and electronic datasets will be subjected to cryptographic hashing to ensure integrity, with read-only access granted to authorized personnel through role-based authentication systems.

Confidentiality {27}

All study personnel will receive training on data security and privacy protection regulations. Personal information of potential and enrolled participants will be collected through electronic hospital records and face-to-face interviews. Interviews will be conducted in a private office to ensure confidentiality. All participants will receive a unique trial identification number, which will serve as their primary identifier for all study documents and data labeling.

Access to the collected data will be restricted to authorized researchers. During the trial, data sharing will occur exclusively within the research team. After trial completion, de-identified data (without any personal information) will be used for analysis.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

Baseline characteristics of the two groups will be presented with appropriate descriptive statistics. For primary outcomes, between-group comparisons of resting and movement-evoked pain scores (continuous variables) will be conducted using independent samples t-tests in the presence of normal distribution (expressed as mean ± standard deviation) or Mann-Whitney U tests in the absence of normal distribution (reported as median with interquartile range). Maximum pain intensity across postoperative intervals will be evaluated through linear mixed-effects models incorporating time-stratified covariance structures, with Bonferroni-adjusted p-values for multiple comparisons.

Secondary outcomes will be compared using the following tests: χ2 tests or Fisher’s exact tests for categorical analgesic requirements (presented as frequencies/percentages); paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests for pre-post comparisons of multimodal EEG parameters (continuous data); and two-way repeated-measures ANOVA with Greenhouse-Geisser correction for mechanical pain threshold trajectories. Correlation analyses will be conducted using Pearson’s (normal data) or Spearman’s coefficients (non-normal), while multivariable linear regression with stepwise variable selection and adjustment for baseline covariates (entry: p < 0.05; removal: p > 0.10) will be conducted to assess intervention effects. All analyses will be conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics 27 (α = 0.05). The Shapiro-Wilk test will be conducted to assess normality, and Levene’s test will be conducted to assess the homogeneity of variances. Statistical significance will be defined as a two-sided p-value of less than 0.05.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g., subgroup analyses) {20b}

There are no additional analyses.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c}

The primary analysis will be conducted using the complete case dataset. Missing data for the primary outcome will be handled through multiple imputation methods, with the results treated as sensitivity analyses. For missing pain scores across different time points, we will employ a generalized estimating equations (GEE) model with missing data assumptions to account for incomplete longitudinal observations. No imputation will be conducted for other outcomes.

Oversight and monitoring

Composition of the coordinating center and trial steering committee {5d}

This trial operates without a coordinating center or independent trial steering committee. Daily management will be conducted by the on-site investigative team, comprising the principal investigator, resident physicians, and graduate students. The team will convene weekly to review operational progress. Additionally, the Beijing Shijitan Hospital Research Committee will periodically monitor the trial to ensure compliance with the protocol, appropriate handling of adverse events, and resolution of unanticipated issues.

Composition of the data monitoring committee, its role, and reporting structure {21a}

The data monitoring committee (DMC) will conduct quarterly unblinded reviews of the trial progress. Data integrity will be assessed through automated logic checks for missing values, outliers, and temporal consistency. Accuracy will be validated against original medical records via dual-independent adjudication.

Adverse event reporting and harms {22}

Clinicians must document all AEs within 24 h of detection and report to the ethics committee within 24 h. Serious AEs (SAEs) will be subjected to root-cause analysis by an independent safety team within 72 h. Any SAE deemed probably or definitively related to the intervention can lead to immediate protocol suspension until the ethics committee review. Continuous risk-benefit evaluation will be conducted by the DMC through quarterly unblinded safety reviews.

Potential risks associated with the interventions and assessments will be systematically addressed through multi-layered safeguards. Participants in the intervention group may experience transient physical discomfort (e.g., ear pressure and localized erythema), which will be mitigated via adjustable headband designs and hypoallergenic silicone padding. In case of skin irritation exceeding grade 1 (mild erythema) (graded based on the CTCAE v5.0 criteria), single-use hydrogel electrodes will be replaced immediately for EEG monitoring. Backup devices, including reserve headphones and portable EEG units, will be available to maintain protocol continuity during equipment malfunctions. Pressure pain threshold measurements may cause transient discomfort; therefore, they will be conducted strictly following ethical standards and immediately terminated upon participant request. Ethical protections ensure participants retain the right to omit responses to psychologically sensitive questionnaire items without any consequence. Trained researchers will monitor participants throughout assessments, prioritizing safety and comfort.

Frequency and plans for auditing trial conduct {23}

DMC will monitor adherence to the protocol with three layers of validation: (1) automated alerts for deviations from the inclusion/exclusion criteria; (2) weekly reconciliation of anesthetic/analgesic administration logs against EHR prescriptions; (3) quarterly on-site audits verifying informed consent documentation and CRF-EMR data alignment. All monitoring outcomes will be archived with blockchain timestamping to ensure an immutable audit trail.

Plans for communicating important protocol amendments to relevant parties (e.g., trial participants, ethical committees) {25}

All protocol amendments and accompanying written justifications must be submitted to the Beijing Shijitan Hospital Ethics Committee. Upon approval, the revised trial protocol and ethics approval certificate will be transferred to the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR) for registration updates. Following registry updates, participants will receive revised informed consent documents reflecting the amended protocol to ensure transparency and voluntary participation.

Dissemination plans {31a}

The results of this study will be submitted for publication in a peer-reviewed journal and presented at scientific conferences to ensure broad dissemination within academic and clinical communities.

Discussion

Epidemiological studies have reported that 40–65% of patients experience moderate-to-severe postoperative pain worldwide [23]. Suboptimal postoperative analgesia increases the risk of surgical complications and delays early mobilization and recovery [24]. However, pharmacological approaches, particularly opioid analgesics, can lead to adverse effects, including urinary retention, pruritus, nausea, vomiting, postoperative ileus, dizziness, and cognitive dysfunction [18]. These limitations have encouraged the use of non-pharmacological, non-invasive analgesic modalities, such as acupuncture and educational video interventions [21, 25].

In the operating room, noise pollution caused by medical equipment affects both patients and healthcare providers. Previous studies have reported that the auditory cortex remains active under general anesthesia and processes auditory information [9]. Recent studies have suggested that intraoperative exposure to positive auditory stimuli (e.g., music, white noise) can mitigate postoperative pain, indicating that auditory perception persists during general anesthesia and modulates response to surgical trauma [12, 26]. Although environmental noise isolation has been proposed as a potential strategy for analgesia [27], existing studies have reported heterogeneous noise parameters (intensity: 45–85 dB; duration: 20–180 min), limiting their clinical generalizability. Lymphatic liposuction surgery presents a unique model for investigating noise-related analgesia since powered liposuction devices generate consistent noise levels of nearly 70 dB. The clinical necessity to monitor ischemic pain in the operated limb restricts the postoperative administration of analgesics, thereby increasing the significance of non-pharmacological analgesia. This underscores the critical need for the present randomized controlled trial to investigate the effects of noise isolation on postoperative pain intensity, analgesic consumption, perioperative neurophysiological states, and anxiety/sleep disturbances.

In conclusion, this study represents the first randomized double-blind controlled trial investigating the effect of active noise cancelation under standardized noise conditions (70 dB) on postoperative pain and cerebral function in patients undergoing liposuction. By assessing postoperative pain trajectories, analgesic consumption, perioperative neurophysiological states, and anxiety/sleep quality, this study aims to establish a novel paradigm for non-pharmacological pain management. If efficacious, auditory environmental modulation can serve as a safe and cost-effective adjuvant to conventional analgesic regimens.

Determinants of breastfeeding initiation in thailand: multiple indicator cluster survey analysis
Authors: Salila Cetthakrikul, Chompoonut Topothai, Nisachol Cetthakrikul
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEarly initiation of breastfeeding, defined as breastfeeding within one hour of birth, is a critical indicator of optimal infant feeding practices with substantial health benefits for mothers and infants. Early initiation of breastfeeding rates in Thailand has declined over the past decade, necessitating an investigation into the underlying determinants. Therefore, this study aimed to identify trends in early initiation of breastfeeding prevalence and examine associated factors using national survey data from Thailand.MethodsThis study analysed secondary data from four rounds of the Thailand Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS4-7), conducted in 2012 (MICS4), 2015–2015 (MICS5), 2019 (MICS6), and 2022 (MICS7), including 13,063 mothers of children under 6 months. Logistic regression analysis was used to evaluate the association between geographic, socioeconomic, and maternal factors with early initiation of breastfeeding. Statistical significance was determined at 95% confidence and p-value < 0.05, and all analysis was conducted using STATA 17.ResultsEarly initiation of breastfeeding prevalence declined from 54.9% in 2012 to 43.1% in 2022. Vaginal delivery increased adjusted odds by 2.1 times (95%CI 1.59, 2.85) while non-skin-to-skin contact reduced early initiation of breastfeeding likelihood by 57% (AOR 0.43, 95%CI 0.33, 0.57). Geographic disparities emerged, with almost all regions showing higher early initiation of breastfeeding rates than Bangkok: North (AOR 2.65, 95%CI 1.49, 4.72), Northeast (AOR 2.55, 95%CI 1.49, 4.35), and South (AOR 3.26, 95%CI 1.92, 5.52). Socioeconomic status and urban or rural residence showed inconsistent associations across survey rounds.ConclusionsEarly initiation of breastfeeding prevalence in Thailand remains low and is off track to meet the 2030 global target of 70%. Interventions to promote vaginal delivery and ensure immediate skin-to-skin contact in all births are critical. Regional variations highlight the importance of culturally tailored strategies to support breastfeeding practices. These findings provide critical evidence for policy development to improve maternal and child health outcomes in Thailand.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13006-025-00759-9.

Background

The World Health Organization (WHO) and the United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF) recommend early initiation of breastfeeding within an hour after delivery. Early initiation of breastfeeding is facilitated by immediate skin-to-skin contact between mother and newborn. It not only promotes longer breastfeeding duration and a higher chance of exclusive breastfeeding but also prevents unnecessary formula supplementation in hospitals [1]. Previous studies have shown that early initiation of breastfeeding provides substantial benefits for both mothers and infants, including reduced neonatal mortality and lower rates of severe infections such as pneumonia and diarrhoea in newborns [2, 3]. Furthermore, early initiation of breastfeeding is likely to provide health benefits such as improved postpartum weight control and reduced risk of diabetes and cardiovascular diseases [4].

Recognizing its importance, WHO has established early initiation of breastfeeding as one of the indicators, under the global nutrition targets, which monitor infant and young child feeding practices, with a target of 70% by 2030 [5]. Previous research showed that multiple factors can influence the early initiation of breastfeeding practices. Maternal age of more than 35 years, having a secondary child, having high education, having complications during pregnancy, and being delivered by cesarean section were factors associated with lower early initiation of breastfeeding [6], while planned pregnancy and vaginal delivery were associated with early initiation of breastfeeding [7].

Thailand, an upper-middle-income country with around half a million children born per year [8] faced significant challenges in achieving this goal. Currently, only 42% of newborns in Thailand are breastfed within the first hour of life [9], slightly below the global average of 46% and substantially short of the 2030 target. Furthermore, the early initiation of breastfeeding rates have shown a concerning decline over the past decade from 54.9% in 2012 to 43.1% in 2022 based on national survey data. This trend is concerning, given the comprehensive national policies and programs for breastfeeding promotion, protection, and support in place. These particularly include the integration of the Baby-Friendly Hospital Initiatives into public hospital standards [10]. The enactment of the Thailand Control of Marketing Promotion of Infant and Young Child Food Act. B.E.2560 in 2017, and several campaigns for breastfeeding promotion [11]. Alongside this, the established healthcare infrastructures with comprehensive benefits packages for maternal and child health are available under universal health coverage [12]. The downward trend of early initiation of breastfeeding in Thailand raises a critical question about the effectiveness of existing breastfeeding interventions and persistent barriers to early initiation of breastfeeding practices.

However, to date, no study has systematically examined the determinants of early initiation of breastfeeding among mothers in Thailand. Previous research has primarily focused on overall breastfeeding duration or exclusive breastfeeding practices, leaving a significant knowledge gap on factors influencing the early initiation of breastfeeding practices [13]. Understanding these factors is crucial for developing targeted interventions and informing evidence-based policy adjustments. Therefore, this study aimed to identify temporal trends of early initiation of breastfeeding prevalence and comprehensively examine associated factors using nationally representative Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS) data spanning a decade (2012–2022).

Methods

This study is a cross-sectional study using secondary data from the latest four rounds of the Thailand Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS4-MICS7) to examine temporal trends and key factors associated with early initiation of breastfeeding. The National Statistical Office (NSO) and UNICEF conducted these surveys in 2012 (MICS4), 2015–2016 (MICS5), 2019 (MICS6), and 2022 (MICS7).

Data sources, variables, and samples

Our study used data from the interviews with mothers of children under the age of six months reported by each MICS. MICS is an international cross-sectional household survey program developed and launched by UNICEF to assist countries in gathering data on the situation of children under 5 and women aged 15 to 49 [14]. In Thailand, MICS took place in 5 rounds from 2005 to 2022. All MICS employ the UNICEF-developed standard questionnaire to collect data from women and children under the age of five. Therefore, MICS can serve as an essential data source for assessing early initiation of breastfeeding trends and their determinants, as it provides information on infant and young child feeding as well as information on prenatal care, birth care, and postnatal care.

We integrated data from the latest four rounds of MICS to investigate common characteristics associated with early initiation of breastfeeding in Thailand.

The dependent variable is referred to as ‘when breastfeeding was initiated’. Following the WHO recommendation, we categorized this variable into two groups: initial breastfeeding within one hour and more than one hour.

Independent variables selected based on a previous study that illustrated the key determinants of early breastfeeding initiation [15–17]. They were comprised of two main groups: (1) geographical and household characteristics, and (2) maternal characteristics. Data analysis

The analysis was conducted in three phases. The first phase was descriptive analysis, which explained the independent and dependent variables’ characteristics (number and percentage). Second, univariate logistic regression was used to investigate the relationship between early initiation of breastfeeding and each independent variable, and univariate logistic regression was used to examine all relevant independent variables. Lastly, multivariable logistic regression was used to investigate the relationship between the dependent variable and all independent factors because they showed a relationship with early initiation of breastfeeding from previous studies [15–17]. All data were weighed to adjust them to accurately represent the target population. Statistical significance was determined at 95% confidence (p-value < 0.05). All data were analysed by STATA 17 (serial number: 401909307587).

Results

Data from 13,063 mothers were included in the combined analysis (MICS4: 2,776; MICS5: 3,383; MICS6: 4,006; and MICS7: 2,898). The number of women who initiated breastfeeding within one hour of MICS4-7 were 1,524 (54.9%), 1,767 (52.2%), 1,976 (53.9%), and 1,143 (43.1%), respectively (Fig. 1). The prevalence of early initiation of breastfeeding in Thailand has declined over the decade from 54.9% in 2012 (MICS4) to 43.1% in 2022 (MICS7), with slight fluctuations in intermediate years: 52.2% in 2015–2016 (MICS5) and 53.9% in 2019 (MICS6). The overall prevalence of early initiation of breastfeeding within the first hour across all four survey rounds was 51.4%.

Characteristics of the combined sample group

Data were extracted from four rounds of the MICS, which included 13,063 women. Most participants resided in rural areas (58.6%), with the highest representation from the Central (28.7%) and Northeast (26.6%) regions of Thailand. Socioeconomically, 17.6% of mothers were in the lowest income group, while 17.6% were in the highest income group. Most mothers were aged between 20 and 35 years (73%) and had completed secondary education at least (54.2%). Health insurance coverage was notably high at 96.6%, and nearly all mothers (99%) received antenatal care, primarily from nurses or midwives (61.8%). Public health facilities were the main delivery sites for 90.9% of the participants, with a cesarean section rate of 35.3%. Most newborns (87.8%) had birth weights ranging from 2.5 to 4 kg. Skin-to-skin contact was provided in 70.3% of deliveries, and more than half of the newborns were breastfed within the first hour post-birth (see Table 1).

Geographic and household factors

The factors associated with early initiation of breastfeeding in individual survey rounds included area, region, and socioeconomic status. In the same way, univariate regression analyses of the combined four rounds indicate that the non-municipal (rural areas) showed higher odds ratios (OR) for early initiation of breastfeeding compared to municipal areas (OR 1.40, 95%CI 1.20, 1.64). Also, OR of early initiation of breastfeeding in central (OR 1.53, 95%CI 1.14, 2.07), north (OR 2.38, 95%CI 1.77, 3.20), northeast (OR 2.41, 95%CI 1.82, 3.21), and south (OR 2.77, 95%CI 2.10, 3.65) was significantly higher than the OR of early initiation of breastfeeding in Bangkok. In contrast, the OR of early initiation of breastfeeding among the fourth (OR 0.67, 95%CI 0.52, 0.87) and the richest (OR 0.39, 95%CI 0.30, 0.51) quintiles of wealth index were significantly lower than the OR of early initiation of breastfeeding among the poorest (see Table 2).

Maternal factors

Maternal factors significantly associated with early initiation of breastfeeding in the combined analysis included younger maternal age (25–35 years old, OR 0.69, 95%CI 0.54, 0.89; more than 35 years old, OR 0.59, 95%CI 0.44, 0.80), higher education level (diploma or upper, OR 0.56, 95%CI 0.43, 0.71), non-Thai nationality (OR 1.51, 95%CI 1.14, 2.01), antenatal care provided by nurse (OR 1.32, 95%CI 1.12, 1.56), delivery in private health facilities (OR 0.36, 95% CI 0.26, 0.49), vaginal delivery (OR 2.89, 95%CI 2.44, 3.42), absence of immediate skin-to-skin contact (OR 0.43, 95%CI 0.34, 0.56), and normal birth weight (2.5 and 4 kg) (OR 1.40, 95%CI 1.05, 1.85). However, examining individual MICS rounds, only the caesarean section and skin-to-skin contact showed consistently significant associations with early initiation of breastfeeding across all four and two survey periods, respectively. Discussion

This study examined early initiation of breastfeeding practices in Thailand over the last decade using national survey data from 2012, 2015–2016, 2019, and 2022, and identified factors associated with early initiation of breastfeeding. The findings reveal a concerning decline in early initiation of breastfeeding rates from 54.9% in 2012 (MICS4) to 43.1% in 2022 (MICS7), with intermediate rates of 52.2% in 2015 (MICS5) and 53.9% in 2019 (MICS6). While Thailand’s overall early initiation of breastfeeding rate of 51.4% remains above the global average (42%) [18] and the East Asian and Pacific region (40%) [19]. It falls substantially short of the WHO global nutrition target of 70% by 2030.

Multivariable logistic regression illustrated that caesarean section, lack of skin-to-skin contact, and living in Bangkok were risk factors for early initiation of breastfeeding. The WHO Global Survey [6] and previous studies from three countries showed that early initiation of breastfeeding was significantly lower among women who delivered by cesarean Sects. [20–22]. The primary reasons for this negative association are pain and exhaustion experienced by mothers after cesarean sections. In Thailand, the cesarean section rate increased from 23.2% in 2009 to 32.5% in 2017 [23]. The factors related to cesarean sections without medical indications were as follows: fear of labour pain; perceived safety benefits; negative prior birth experiences; positive views of cesareans; misinformation; and the belief in auspicious birth dates [21, 22, 24, 25].

Importantly, the cesarean section was likely to result in a reduction in skin-to-skin contact because mothers felt pain and were too tired after delivery [22]. According to global breastfeeding guidelines, babies should have direct skin-to-skin contact with their mothers to initiate breastfeeding within an hour of birth [26]. Skin-to-skin contact was strongly associated with higher rates of early initiation of breastfeeding. A study in China found a strong link between skin-to-skin contact time after birth and the likelihood of early initiation of breastfeeding [27].

Regarding living in Bangkok, one reason for its lower rate of early initiation of breastfeeding is the higher number of private hospitals compared to other provinces. A previous study found that private hospitals are associated with a high rate of cesarean sections. As mentioned earlier, a cesarean section often leads to reduced skin-to-skin contact, which in turn can result in a delay or absence of early breastfeeding initiation [28].

This study had two key strengths. First, it combined data from MICS4 to MICS7, offering insights from nationally representative survey data with extensive population coverage. Second, the standardized methodology of the MICS ensures reliable data collection, allowing for meaningful comparisons across countries and enriching the global perspective. Still, this study had two limitations. First, the questionnaire for each survey round differs significantly, as it has been adjusted or updated periodically. For example, questions about mothers’ language and skin-to-skin contact were added in MICS 6 and MICS 7 but not included in MICS 4 and MICS 5. However, all rounds of MICS were standardized by the National Statistics Office. Therefore, some variables were changed, but the survey methods- sampling, data collection, and data analysis- were consistent.

Furthermore, the questionnaire excludes some breastfeeding-related criteria such as fathers’ educational level, parental support, pregnancy information resources, and maternal employment, which limited the study’s ability to comprehensively assess and analyse association of all potential factors influencing breastfeeding practices. Second, MICS is a cross-sectional survey, which limits the ability to establish causal relationships. Since data were collected at a single point in time, the study can identify associations between variables, but it cannot determine the causality of these relationships.

According to the findings, we recommend first that the Ministry of Public Health strengthen and improve breastfeeding policies in health facilities such as the Ten Steps to Successful Breastfeeding, such as providing breastfeeding information to pregnant women implementing, skin-to-skin contact within one hour after delivery and ensuring 24-hour rooming-in between mothers and their infants. Strengthening these policies will ensure that mothers receive suitable services and recommendations from health professionals.

Second, healthcare providers and medical associations should provide all pregnant women with information about the benefits of vaginal delivery and encourage and facilitate vaginal births for mothers who do not have a medical indication for a cesarean section. This approach would reduce the incidence of medically unnecessary cesarean sections and consequently improve breastfeeding initiation rates. For example, healthcare providers should work to reduce non-medically indicated cesarean sections through patient education on vaginal birth benefits during antenatal care visits, while ensuring safe delivery practices for all mothers. Lastly, early initiation of breastfeeding rates observed in Bangkok were lower compared to other regions. Further research could explore how to increase early initiation of breastfeeding rates in Bangkok and investigate other factors influencing early initiation of breastfeeding rates in Bangkok to gain a more comprehensive understanding.

Conclusions

This study found that the prevalence of early initiation of breastfeeding in Thailand has been declining since 2012, and the current rate is very low and far from the 2030 Global Nutrition Target. Factors that negatively influence early initiation of breastfeeding practices included mothers living in Bangkok and the Central region of Thailand, a cesarean section, and the absence of skin-to-skin contact. This study suggested that the Government should set priority actions and recommend interventions by reducing caesarean section performance and increasing skin-to-skin contact in a labour room to improve breastfeeding practice, especially the early initiation of breastfeeding. In addition, the findings of this study could be scientific evidence for the World Health Organization to develop guidelines of health care services for mothers and infants.

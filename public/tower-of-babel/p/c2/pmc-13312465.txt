Authors: Lidiane Aparecida Ferreira Rocha, José Ben-Hur Ferraz Parente, Viviane Santana da Silva, Isabela Zampirolli Leal, Marcela Pereira Zanoni, Alexia Paganotti, Giuliano Giova Volpiani, Eduardo Ramacciotti
Published: 2026

We received the comments1 on our manuscript, “A randomized controlled trial of 3% polidocanol foam with saline pre-washing compared to standard foam sclerotherapy for the treatment of varicose veins”2 from Vanashri Dhananjay Shinde, MD, and colleagues with great respect and satisfaction.

Regarding the clinical outcome, our study was designed as a pilot trial with a small sample size and, therefore, limited power to detect more subtle clinical differences. Still, a more favorable trend of patients migrating from moderate-to-mild category was observed in the SALine plus FOAM 3% (SALFOAM 3%) group, whereas the group treated with 1% polidocanol remained practically unchanged. This suggests a potentially relevant clinical direction, although it is not yet statistically robust in this small pilot study. In addition, an important qualitative difference was observed in the occlusion pattern. In the SALFOAM 3% group, the ultrasound evolution tended more toward sclerosis rather than thrombus formation, whereas in the control group, there was proportionally more thrombus, with two cases of superficial venous thrombosis. This has potential clinical relevance because thrombotic evolution is usually associated with increased discomfort, a greater need for drainage, and potentially a higher risk of hyperpigmentation.

The analytical framework deserves clarification. Randomization was performed on an individual basis, in accordance with the study design. Each patient was assigned to a therapeutic protocol, and the patient’s eligible limbs received the same strategy. The limb-level analysis was adopted because the focus of the study was not the abstract patient, but rather the target vessel treated in each limb. The vessels evaluated were not identical; they varied in location, caliber, and local hemodynamic characteristics. Each limb contained a specific venous segment, with its own behavior, and foam volume was adjusted according to that target. From this point of view, the limb-level analysis had both clinical and operational basis.

This approach does not mean ignoring that limbs of the same individual share a common biological context. It only means that, within the purpose of the study, the unit of observation was the treated segment. Patient-level randomization was precisely the mechanism used to avoid allocation bias between protocols.

The focus on isolated tributary veins without ultrasound-guided delivery was intentional and consistent with the purpose of a pilot study. By restricting the study to superficial, accessible, and visually identifiable tributaries, it was possible to better standardize the intervention and reduce heterogeneity. This does not mean denying the importance of axial reflux, but rather recognizing that, to answer the initial question, it was first necessary to simplify the model.

Finally, we would like to inform you that, based on data from the current pilot study, a larger, properly powered multicenter clinical trial comparing the 3% polidocanol foam with the standard treatment and addressing all these important topics is planned and will soon begin enrolling patients.

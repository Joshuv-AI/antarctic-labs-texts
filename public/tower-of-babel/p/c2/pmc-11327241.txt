Effect of jujube orchard abandonment time on soil properties and enzyme activities at soil profile in the Loess Plateau
Authors: Gailing Wang, Xiaochen Yao, Zhiyu Zhang, Jiaqi Wang, Hui Wang, Yunxiao Li, Wenhua Fan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In the Loess Plateau, the impact of abandoned farmland on soil properties and enzyme activity, along with its temporal variations and potential driving factors, remains a mystery. This study was designed to systematically and comprehensively examine the variations in soil enzyme activities, particle size distribution, and stability of soil aggregates at different stages of ecological recovery in the Loess Plateau. Our findings reveal a nuanced temporal pattern: with the progression of cropland abandonment, there is a notable decrease in soil bulk density. Concurrently, a dynamic trend in enzyme activities is observed—initially exhibiting a decline, followed by an increase over extended periods of recovery. Notably, prolonged abandonment leads to marked enhancements in soil structure. Parameters such as the mean weight diameter (MWD) and geometric mean diameter (GMD) of soil aggregates show an overall increasing trend. In terms of the Relative Dissipation Index (RSI), our data indicate a sequence of control > 2 years of abandonment > 4 years > 6 years > 14 years. From this, it can be seen that fallowing may be an effective natural restoration strategy for improving the physical structure of soils in the Loess Plateau and restoring soil nutrients. However, positive changes may take a long time to become evident.

Cropland abandonment is a common phenomenon in many regions around the world1. It defined as the cessation of agricultural activities on arable land for a period of 2 to 5 years2. It is widely regarded as an effective measure for controlling soil erosion, increasing vegetation cover and improving soil quality3. Currently, approximately 2.2 million square kilometers of arable land worldwide are abandoned, with 25% located in Russia4. In China, a major agricultural nation, the phenomenon of land abandonment is similarly widespread5. Between 2004 and 2017, the area of abandoned arable land in China accounted for approximately 15% of the country's total arable land area6. These abandoned farmlands are undergoing natural succession, typically evolving slowly into vegetation states resembling pre-agricultural conditions, a process known as self-restoration7,8. Current research in this domain primarily focuses on the monitoring of major staple crops9, identification of driving factors10, evaluation of impacts on food security, and contributions to global environmental change11. However, studies concerning the abandonment of economic crops, such as jujube orchards in the Loess Plateau, remain relatively limited. This gap in research somewhat hinders a comprehensive understanding of the ecological consequences following cropland abandonment.

Existing studies evaluated the impact of fallowing on soil quality by examining the changes in various physicochemical parameters of the soil12. Soil total nitrogen (TN), available phosphorus (AP), and available potassium (AK) are commonly used indicators to assess soil quality and are crucial for carbon sequestration, soil fertility, and vegetation restoration13. Other physicochemical soil parameters such as pH, bulk density (BD), and carbon-to-nitrogen ratio may also play key roles in soil functioning and the restoration of degraded land and biodiversity14. For example, some existing studies have shown a significant positive correlation between the TC content in the soil and the fallow duration. Meanwhile, some studies have indicated that changes in land use can lead to a faster recovery of TK content in the soil12. Furthermore, soil aggregate stability is also a key indicator for evaluating soil structural stability and erosion resistance, which can strongly influence soil fertility and quality15. However, there is still limited understanding of the dynamics of these soil properties following land use changes, particularly in semi-arid regions, with little research focused on them16.

Soil enzyme activity is generated by root exudates, litter, and microbial activity17. As products of microbial metabolism, they are highly sensitive to environmental and anthropogenic stimuli, making them a key indicator for assessing soil quality18. In recent years, there has been a growing body of research on soil enzyme activity under abandoned conditions. For instance, in semi-arid Mediterranean climates, abandonment of agricultural activities has led to the restoration of soil hydrolytic enzyme activities, including urease, protease, β-glucosidase, and phosphatase19. Xu et al.20,21 explored the relationship between soil enzyme activity and the stability of organic carbon across different time spans post-land abandonment and under various vegetation types in the Loess Plateau, underscoring the critical role of vegetation type in these processes. Xu et al.21 compared the differences in soil enzyme activity between the surface soil layer and the plow layer in abandoned farmland. However, comprehensive studies that integrate soil enzyme activity with other soil properties, such as soil aggregates, particularly in regions with high soil erosion rates and economically important crops, are notably scarce.

The Loess Plateau is one of the most severely eroded regions in the world due to its highly erodible aeolian deposits, steep slopes, heavy rainfall, sparse vegetation cover, and inappropriate land use. Although many studies have investigated the changes in the physicochemical, or biological properties of soil after farmland abandonment in different landscapes21,22, the impact of abandonment duration on soil properties and enzyme activities in the Loess Plateau remains not fully understood. In this study, we hypothesize that the duration of conversion will determine the extent of changes, and under ideal conditions, it may take 10 years or more for abandoned farmland to recover its properties23. This study provides a scientific basis for further understanding the impact of ecological projects such as the Grain for Green Program on soil structure and quality during the reclamation process of abandoned land in the Loess Plateau, as well as the underlying mechanisms.

The elevation of the area is approximately 928 m, with an average annual precipitation of 518.8 mm and an average annual temperature of 8.8 °C. The frost-free period exceeds 160 days. Selection of study site and soil sampling

Employing a space-for-time substitution approach23, we ensured consistency in soil type, slope, and altitude across selected sites in April 2021. Four jujube tree plots, abandoned for durations of 2, 4, 6, and 14 years, respectively, were juxtaposed against conventionally cultivated jujube tree plots serving as controls. During field soil collection, three 1 × 1 m sampling plots were selected along the slope at the top, middle, and bottom as triplicates. Soil samples retrieved from these plots were subsequently transported to the laboratory for pertinent testing and analysis. To ensure robustness, three soil samples were collected from each plot as duplicates. Notably, while the control treatment plot underwent plowing 3 to 4 times annually, the abandoned jujube forests remained devoid of artificial tillage or any other management interventions. Within each sampling plot, three undisturbed soil samples were collected from depths of 0–20 cm using a ring knife with a diameter of 7 cm and a height of 5.3 cm23, to facilitate the measurement of soil physical properties. Employing a five-point sampling methodology23, soil samples were also extracted at depths of 0–5 cm, 5–10 cm, and 10–20 cm using a soil drill with an inner diameter of 5 cm. One portion of the subsamples was air-dried to measure the physical and chemical properties of soil, the other for immediate transfer to the laboratory for soil enzyme activity analysis24. After the soil samples had been air-dried, plant roots, stems, leaves, small stones, insects, and other debris were removed. Laboratory analysis

Soil physical and chemical properties

Following air-drying of the soil, a suite of soil physical and chemical properties, including bulk density, soil organic matter content, TN, TK, TP, available phosphorus, available potassium, and alkaline nitrogen, were measured. Soil enzyme activity was predominantly assessed through the determination of urease, sucrase, catalase, and alkaline phosphatase levels. The supplementary material provides detailed instructions for soil properties and enzyme activity measurement methods.

Soil aggregates

In the study, the Le Bissonnais method was employed to simulate interference25. under varied conditions and to analyse the fragmentation mechanism of soil aggregates This approach facilitated the simulation of distinct environmental impacts, including rainstorms, light rain, and mechanical disturbances, through the implementation of three soil sample pretreatment methods, specifically fast wetting (FW), slow wetting (SW), and wet stirring (WS), as delineated in the Le Bissonnais (LB) protocol.

In our methodology, aggregate-size fractions were segregated using standardized sieving techniques, involving the passage of air-dried soil through an 8-mm mesh. Following the protocol established by Márquez et al.25, we quantitatively assessed the stability of soil aggregates. To facilitate this analysis, 80 g subsamples of air-dried soil were employed to examine the distribution of aggregate-size stability. The procedure involved isolating six classes of water-stable aggregates via wet-sieving, specifically: greater than 2 mm, 1–2 mm, 0.5–1 mm, 0.25–0.5 mm, 0.053–0.25 mm, and less than 0.053 mm. Statistical analysis

In our investigation, a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed to elucidate the impact of varying soil depths over different years on the physical and chemical attributes of the soil, enzymatic activities, and soil aggregate structures. Before conducting data analysis, performing a normality test on the data using the Anderson–Darling method. The significance of differences between means was ascertained utilizing Duncan’s post hoc test, with a threshold of p < 0.05. All statistical computations were executed using SPSS version 22.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA). Additionally, the graphical representations of the data were generated through Origin version 2023b (OriginLab Corporation, Northampton, MA, USA).

Soil enzymes activities

The activity of soil enzymes, varying by type, exhibits distinct trends across different durations of land abandonment and soil depths. Our comprehensive analysis reveals a consistent decline in enzyme activity with increased soil depth (Fig. 3). Specifically, urease activity demonstrates a notable pattern, initially decreasing before showing an increase as the period of land abandonment extends. The highest urease activity was observed within the 0–5 cm soil profile, followed by the 5–10 cm layer, and the lowest activity was recorded at a depth of 10–20 cm. When compared to the control, the urease activity in the 0–5 cm soil layer exhibited increments of 1.37% and 22.84% at the 6th and 14th years of abandonment, respectively (Fig. 3a). Moreover, a positive correlation was observed between sucrase activity and the duration of land abandonment (Fig. 3c), peaking at the sixth year across the 0–20 cm soil profile. During the vegetation restoration process, significant increases in sucrase enzyme activity were noted across various soil depths with each year of abandonment, ranging from 22.92 to 118.14% in the 0–5 cm layer, 44.18% to 75.29% in the 5–10 cm layer, and 36.45% to 55.11% in the 10–20 cm layer, with these differences being statistically significant (p < 0.05). As for phosphatase activity, it was found to gradually increase in the 0–5 cm soil layer with longer abandonment periods. Conversely, in the 5–10 cm and 10–20 cm layers, activity initially decreased after two years of abandonment but subsequently increased after six and fourteen years. In an examination of the effects of three distinct pretreatment methodologies on soil aggregates—namely, FW, SW, and WS—both the Mean Weight Diameter (MWD) and the Geometric Mean Diameter (GMD) revealed discernible alterations over time. Specifically, soil samples that had undergone abandonment for periods of 14, 6, and 4 years, in addition to a 2-year interval and a control group, demonstrated a significant trend in soil aggregate stability, with the hierarchy presented as 14 years > 4 years > 6 years > 2 years > control (P < 0.05). Notably, under the FW treatment, the MWD and GMD experienced substantial enhancements of 165.56% and 128.57%, respectively, when juxtaposed against the control. Further scrutiny under the SW treatment unveiled that it yielded the paramount values for both MWD and GMD, spanning from 0.819 to 1.285 mm and 0.450 to 0.769 mm, respectively. In contrast, the FW treatment exhibited MWD values ranging between 0.360 and 0.956 mm, and GMD values between 0.210 and 0.480 mm. In the present study, we investigated the resilience and modification indices of soil aggregates under various abandonment durations, employing the RSI and RMI as metrics for assessing soil aggregate stability. Our analysis revealed a sequential decline in RSI values across the observed temporal gradient, delineated as follows: control > 2 years > 4 years > 6 years > 14 years, with a diminution range of 13.47–53.69%. Concurrently, the RMI values associated with plots subjected to abandonment exhibited a reduction of 7.62–49.44% relative to the control. This study underscores the pronounced impact of different treatment methods on soil aggregate size distribution, highlighting the dynamic nature of soil composition in response to environmental and anthropogenic influences.

Discussion

Response of soil physical and chemical to years of abandonment at soil profile

Soil transformations, pivotal in the context of ecological restoration, are inherently gradual processes. The efficacy of vegetation recovery post farmland cessation often encounters constraints, as highlighted in the literature26. The dynamism of soil organic matter, influenced by the chronological succession of terrestrial flora, varies with the duration of restoration efforts. In this study, the soil organic matter content significantly decreased in the second year after abandonment. This may be because, in the initial stage of farmland abandonment, no new crops are growing, and thus there are no crop residues to decompose and contribute to the organic matter27,28. Soil microorganisms may continue to decompose the existing organic matter, but without new inputs, the total amount of organic matter decreases29. Additionally, in the absence of vegetation cover, the soil becomes more susceptible to erosion, leading to further organic matter loss30. The incremental trend in organic matter content with prolonged periods of vegetative regrowth, aligning with the consensus within the scholarly domain31–33. In an examination of derelict agricultural lands in Sicily, Novara et al.33 discerned an enhancement in soil organic carbon reserves by up to 9.0 Mg/ha post-abandonment. Similarly, Bell et al.34 documented a 2.3% augmentation in soil organic carbon after six decades of farmland relinquishment in Spain. This trend can be attributed to several factors. Primarily, the temporal extension favors the ascendance of larger vegetative species, thereby escalating the influx of plant-derived carbon (inclusive of litter and root systems) into the soil matrix, fostering carbon sequestration. Despite existing debates regarding the soil activation phenomenon triggered by plant-derived inputs35–37, the significance of this contribution cannot be overstated. Additionally, the cessation of agricultural interventions (such as tilling, fertilization, and irrigation) alongside alterations in the soil microbial consortium, governed by the plant rhizosphere, precipitates shifts in microbial carbon contributions, subsequently influencing soil organic carbon stocks38. This dual mechanism underscores the intricate interplay between plant dynamics and microbial activity in enhancing soil organic carbon storage following agricultural abandonment.

The concentrations of nitrogen, TP, and TK within soil matrices are critical metrics for assessing soil fertility, which in turn, significantly influences the proliferation of plant communities. This investigation delineates the distribution of available soil nutrients across varying durations of farmland abandonment, noting a pronounced concentration in the uppermost stratum (0–5 cm), with a subsequent attenuation in nutrient levels with increasing soil depth. This pattern resonates with the findings of numerous studies39,40, attributed primarily to the decomposition of ground-cover vegetation, which enriches the soil surface with essential nutrients such as TN, TP and TK. The investigation further reveals that, following 6 and 14 years of cessation, soil samples exhibited elevated TN levels compared to other intervals. This phenomenon can be ascribed to two primary factors: the decomposition of extensive litter and root fragments contributing to nitrogen enrichment6,41, and a delayed enhancement effect on soil nitrogen content42. Conversely, in plots where surface vegetation was systematically removed, a noticeable reduction in TN and alkaline N concentration was observed, attributed to the absence of vegetative matter contributing to the nitrogen cycle. This trend was particularly stark in the soil's top 20 cm layer, where nutrient changes were minimal. Moreover, the study highlights a notable decline in the levels of TN, alkaline N and available potassium subsequent to the cessation of chemical fertilizer application, following a two-year abandonment period. This reduction underscores the impact of discontinued agricultural practices on the dynamics of soil nutrient profiles, manifesting in diminished organic residue and humus accumulation, thereby influencing the overall soil fertility landscape.

Response of enzymes activity to year of abandonment at soil profile

Soil enzyme activities, serving as dynamic responders to environmental shifts, are pivotal biological indicators for appraising soil health43. Among these, soil urease plays a critical role in catalyzing urea hydrolysis, concurrently facilitating the release of carbon dioxide—a process integral to the organic nitrogen cycle within the soil matrix. Similarly, the activity of alkaline phosphatase provides insights into the decomposition and transformation dynamics of soil organic phosphorus44. Our findings indicate that both urease and phosphatase activities tend to augment alongside the progression of ecological recovery, attributable largely to the beneficial impacts of vegetative regeneration on root system development, litter accumulation, and microbial activity, which collectively foster an uptick in soil enzymatic function17,45. Particularly, urease activity exhibited pronounced intensification within the surface strata of soils abandoned for 14 years, aligning with observations made by Xu et al.20. This enhancement underscores the active cycling of nitrogen and phosphorus, coinciding with periods of extensive and diverse vegetative cover, alongside vigorous soil biochemical activity. Conversely, soils relinquished for a span of 2 years demonstrated diminished carbon and nitrogen contents, correlating with weaker enzyme activity46,47.

Furthermore, catalase activity, intricately linked to microbial activity and respiratory intensity, exerts a direct influence on the rate of soil humification and organic matter decomposition. Over the course of 14 years of land abandonment, a general upward trajectory in enzyme activity was observed in the 0–5 cm soil layer, whereas the 5–10 and 10–20 cm layers experienced an initial decline followed by an increase, a phenomenon likely governed by variations in temperature and soil moisture48. Soil sucrase activity, indicative of soluble nutrient availability, exhibits a positive correlation with soil fertility levels. During the restoration of abandoned jujube forests in the loess hilly region, sucrase activity peaked after six years of restoration before witnessing a decline. This trend diverges from existing literature suggesting that elevated plant diversity fosters higher soil enzyme activities, with sucrase activity particularly linked to vegetation coverage49. Such discrepancies may be attributed to environmental variables, including temperature and precipitation, underscoring the complex interplay between biotic and abiotic factors in shaping soil enzymatic profiles. This study identifies a pivotal moment at the fourth year of cropland abandonment, marking the lowest catalase activity. Subsequent observations revealed that the average catalase levels within the 0–20 cm soil depth after 2, 6, and 14 years of abandonment were 3.24, 2.78, 3.13, and 3.48-fold higher than those measured during the fourth year of abandonment, respectively (Fig. 3b).

Response of soil aggregate to year of abandonment at soil profile

The integrity of soil aggregates is crucial for withstanding external physical stresses, playing a pivotal role in determining soil aeration, water retention capabilities, and the sequestration of organic carbon within organic matter matrices50. Analysis reveals a trend of increasing content of > 5 mm aggregates and a decline in < 0.25 mm aggregates with extended periods of natural recovery. Specifically, the proportion of larger aggregates (> 5 mm) surged by 195.82% following 14 years of abandonment, correlating with enhanced aggregate stability indicators such as R0.25, MWD, and GMD51.

Conclusion

In a study examining the long-term effects of abandonment on jujube forest soil, we observed a dynamic shift in the soil's physical and chemical attributes. Initially, these properties experienced a decline, followed by a subsequent enhancement, indicating a gradual improvement in soil quality over time. This pattern emerged alongside a depth-dependent reduction in soil nutrient concentrations, highlighting the vertical stratification of soil fertility. Furthermore, the enzymatic activity within the soil exhibited marked variability across different periods of abandonment and soil depths. Notably, the concentrations of soil urease and sucrase underwent significant alterations at the 6th and 14th years post-abandonment. Distinct patterns were discernible across various abandonment durations, with the MWD and GMD indices revealing significant positive correlations (p < 0.01) under both FW and SW conditions, suggesting a robust relationship between soil aggregate stability and abandonment duration. These findings point to a predominant disintegration mechanism of soil aggregates under FW conditions, primarily attributed to the compaction-induced air expulsion, contrasted by the mechanical disruption observed under WS conditions due to soil vibration, and the minimal impact of clay particle expansion under SW conditions. This comprehensive analysis underscores the complexity of soil ecosystem responses to abandonment, highlighting the intricate interplay between soil structure, nutrient dynamics, and enzymatic activity over time.

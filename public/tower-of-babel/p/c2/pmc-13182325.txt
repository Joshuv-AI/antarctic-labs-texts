Effectiveness of an Android-Based Loneliness Prevention Intervention for Community-Dwelling Older Adults in Indonesia: A Quasi-Experimental Study
Authors: Ezalina Ezalina, Deswinda Deswinda, Yantri Maputra
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aim:This study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of SIAGA (Sistem Intervensi Anti-Kesepian untuk Geriatri), a culturally adapted Android application, in reducing loneliness among community-dwelling older adults in Indonesia.MethodS:A quasi-experimental study with a pre-test post-test non-equivalent control group design was conducted from June to July 2025 in Payung Sekaki District, Pekanbaru City, Indonesia. One hundred older adults (≥60 years) living with families were recruited through multistage cluster sampling and allocated to the intervention group (n = 50, receiving the SIAGA app with music, videos, stories, and cognitive games) or the control group (n = 50, standard care). The validated Indonesian UCLA Loneliness Scale Version 3 (19 items) was administered at baseline, two weeks, and four weeks post-intervention. Statistical analysis included independent t-tests and repeated-measures General Linear Model.Results:Significant loneliness reductions were observed in the intervention group compared to controls at 2 weeks (22.00 ± 1.64 vs. 45.74 ± 4.19, p < .001) and 4 weeks (17.60 ± 1.74 vs. 46.54 ± 3.87, p < .001) post-intervention. The intervention group demonstrated a 60.4% reduction at 2 weeks and a 68.1% reduction at 4 weeks from baseline (55.50 ± 1.82). A total of 94% of intervention participants achieved clinically meaningful improvement (≥5-point reduction) compared with 8% of controls.Conclusion:The SIAGA application effectively reduced loneliness among Indonesian community-dwelling older adults, demonstrating superior effectiveness compared to standard care. This digital intervention delivered through community health networks represents a promising, scalable model for addressing mental health needs in aging populations.

Introduction

Loneliness represents one of the most pressing public health challenges of the twenty-first century, particularly affecting older adults worldwide. Defined as the subjective distressing experience arising from a perceived discrepancy between desired and actual social relationships, loneliness differs fundamentally from social isolation, which refers to the objective lack of social contacts (Taylor et al., 2023). Recent global epidemiological evidence demonstrates that approximately 1,250,322 older adults from 126 studies across 62 countries report a global loneliness prevalence of 27.6% (Salari et al., 2025). North America demonstrates the highest prevalence at 30.5%, while African populations show the lowest at 15.3%, highlighting the influence of cultural, socioeconomic, and healthcare system factors on loneliness experiences. In high-income countries specifically, meta-analytical evidence from 120,000 older adults demonstrates a pooled loneliness prevalence of 28.5% (95% CI: 23.9–33.2%), with substantial between-country heterogeneity suggesting the importance of national context in loneliness experiences (Chawla et al., 2021).

Digital health interventions have emerged as promising approaches for addressing loneliness among older adults, with systematic reviews and meta-analyses providing mixed but increasingly encouraging evidence for their effectiveness. The most comprehensive umbrella review analyzing 21 reviews covering 326 primary studies with 79,538 participants found that 72% of information and communication technology (ICT) and videoconferencing studies demonstrated effectiveness in reducing loneliness. At the same time, social networking sites showed mixed results (49% effective) and robotics interventions showed promise (73% effective) despite limited evidence (Balki et al., 2022). Beyond digital modalities, structured social activity programs have also demonstrated positive effects on older adults’ functional and social autonomy as well as life engagement in institutional care settings, reinforcing the importance of multi-component approaches that combine diverse activities to address different dimensions of well-being (Küçük & Emiroğlu, 2025). However, methodological concerns persist, with evidence and gap mapping revealing that over 70% of systematic reviews in this field have critically low quality, highlighting significant gaps in rigorous research design and implementation (Welch et al., 2023).

Despite these mixed meta-analytical findings, several high-quality individual randomized controlled trials (RCTs) demonstrate the potential of well-designed digital interventions. The landmark Personal Reminder Information and Social Management study, a multisite RCT with 300 older adults aged 65–98 years, tested a custom computer platform featuring email, games, and social connectivity tools, demonstrating significant loneliness reduction at 6 months with effects maintained at 12-month follow-up (Czaja et al., 2018). Mobile health applications show particular promise, with WhatsApp-based low-intensity cognitive-behavioral therapy (mLINCC) demonstrating significant reductions in loneliness during a 3-month intervention with 32 older adults, with effects maintained at 1-month follow-up (Jarvis et al., 2019). An internet-based matching intervention cognitive-behavioral therapy (ICBT) and interpersonal psychotherapy with 170 participants showed ICBT superiority, with moderate-to-large effect sizes (Käll et al., 2021).

Research from Asian contexts reveals particularly complex patterns of technology acceptance. In China, examination of the “gray digital divide” indicates that physical problems affect 89% of elderly participants, and a lack of digital skills affects 84%, with significant variations by socio-cultural context (Li & Kostka, 2024; Yang et al., 2022). A study of 312 older adults in Nanjing, China, demonstrates that digital inclusion significantly impacts quality of life, with key factors including attitudes toward technology, income, and living situation (Yang et al., 2022). Research among 401 low-income Asian American older adults reveals significant effects of ethnicity and English proficiency, with Chinese participants reporting higher perceived usefulness than Korean participants (DeLange Martinez et al., 2024). Qualitative research across various Indian states with 20 older adults identifies four critical themes: ICT usability, anxiety and privacy concerns, self-perception of technology use, and need for technology literacy (Mitra et al., 2022).

Despite growing evidence for digital interventions, several critical gaps persist that limit the translation of research into clinical practice. Methodological limitations include inconsistent outcome measures across studies, limited long-term follow-up data beyond six months, and insufficient attention to implementation science considerations such as scalable delivery models and healthcare system integration (Welch et al., 2023). Geographic disparities reveal substantial underrepresentation of developing country populations despite higher vulnerability, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa and rural Asia, where infrastructure challenges compound social isolation risks. Technology-specific evidence remains limited for smartphone applications specifically designed for older adults, with most effective interventions utilizing custom computer platforms rather than commercially available mobile applications.

Indonesia presents a particularly compelling context for digital loneliness interventions, given its rapid demographic transition toward an aging society, widespread mobile technology adoption (80.32% of older adults are social media users), and cultural emphasis on family-centered care systems (Ministry of Communication and Information Technology Republic of Indonesia, 2018). Preliminary research indicates that older adults who use Android applications experience 33.1% lower loneliness than non-users, suggesting the potential for culturally adapted mobile interventions (Jin & Kim, 2025). However, the lack of rigorous experimental studies examining purpose-built applications for Indonesian older adults represents a significant evidence gap requiring investigation.

The present study addresses these gaps by developing and testing SIAGA (Sistem Intervensi Anti-Kesepian untuk Geriatri), an Android-based loneliness prevention application specifically designed for Indonesian community-dwelling older adults. This quasi-experimental investigation aimed to provide robust evidence for culturally adapted digital mental health interventions in a developing country context, contributing to the global evidence base for technology-supported healthy aging initiatives while addressing the specific cultural and technological contexts of Indonesian older adult populations.

Research Hypotheses

(1) Older adults in the intervention group using the SIAGA application will demonstrate significantly lower loneliness scores compared to the control group at 2-week and 4-week follow-up assessments; (2) The intervention effect will be sustained or enhanced from 2 weeks to 4 weeks, indicating durable benefits of the culturally adapted digital intervention; and (3) A clinically meaningful proportion of intervention participants will achieve substantial loneliness reduction (≥5-point decrease on the UCLA Loneliness Scale) compared to controls.

Methods

Study Design

This study employed a quasi-experimental design with a pre-test/post-test non-equivalent control group. Participants were allocated to either an intervention group receiving the SIAGA application or a control group receiving standard care without the application intervention. Standard care in this context comprised the routine community health services provided by Puskesmas (Community Health Center) Payung Sekaki, including monthly health check-ups (Posyandu Lansia), general health education sessions, and access to basic healthcare consultations. These services represent the typical level of care available to community-dwelling older adults in Indonesian urban settings and do not include any specific loneliness intervention or digital health components.

Sample

Participants were allocated to either an intervention group receiving the SIAGA application or a control group receiving standard care without the application intervention. Standard care in this context comprised the routine community health services provided by Puskesmas (Community Health Center) Payung Sekaki, including monthly health check-ups (Posyandu Lansia), general health education sessions, and access to basic healthcare consultations. These services represent the typical level of care available to community-dwelling older adults in Indonesian urban settings and do not include any specific loneliness intervention or digital health components.

The study population comprised community-dwelling older adults aged 60 years and above living with family members in Pekanbaru City, Riau Province, Indonesia. The research was conducted from June to July 2025 in Kecamatan Payung Sekaki, specifically in Kelurahan Labuh Baru Timur, representing a typical urban Indonesian community with mixed socioeconomic characteristics. Participants were selected through a multistage cluster sampling methodology, involving several sequential stages to ensure a representative sample. Initially, one of the 15 sub-districts (kecamatan) in Pekanbaru City was randomly selected, followed by the selection of one village (kelurahan) from that sub-district. Within the selected village, nine neighborhood areas (lingkungan) were proportionally selected based on population density and demographic characteristics. The intervention group was located in Community Unit/Rukun Warga (RW) 10, while the control group was in RW 3, ensuring geographical separation to minimize contamination between the study groups.

Sample size was calculated using the hypothesis-testing formula for comparing means between two independent groups, following established statistical methodology for intervention studies (Xu et al., 2017). Based on this calculation, with a 5% margin of error and a 90% CI level, the required sample size was determined to be 50 participants per group, yielding a total sample of 100 older adults. This sample size provided adequate statistical power to detect clinically meaningful differences in loneliness scores between intervention and control groups while accounting for potential attrition during the study period.

Inclusion criteria encompassed older adults who owned Android smartphones, demonstrated adequate reading and hearing abilities necessary for application use, maintained mobility without being bedridden, and provided voluntary informed consent for study participation. Exclusion criteria included individuals with severe cognitive impairment that would prevent application usage, those with significant visual impairments that could not be corrected with assistive devices, participants with severe psychiatric conditions requiring intensive medical management, and individuals planning to relocate during the study period.

Intervention Development

The SIAGA application was developed through a systematic, three-stage user-centered design process tailored explicitly for Indonesian older adults. The first stage, “Specify the Context of Use,” involved comprehensive user identification through observational studies, in-depth interviews with older adults and their families, and an extensive literature review of cultural preferences and technological capabilities among Indonesian elderly populations. This stage was crucial to ensuring that the application design aligned with user needs, expectations, and the specific cultural contexts of Indonesian family-centered care systems and traditional values regarding aging and social connectivity.

The second development stage, “Specify User Requirements,” comprised systematic data collection and identification of user needs through detailed analysis of older adult social media usage patterns, preferred applications, and desired features for social connectivity. This phase included focus group discussions with older adults currently using social media platforms, interviews with family members regarding communication preferences, and consultation with geriatric healthcare providers to understand clinical needs for loneliness prevention. The findings informed the development of application features that would be both culturally appropriate and technically feasible for the target population.

The third stage involved prototype development and iterative testing based on collected data and identified requirements. The prototype underwent rigorous usability testing with 25 older adults aged 60–79 years, who used the application for 15–30 minutes, followed by completion of the System Usability Scale (SUS) questionnaire. This validated instrument comprises ten items assessing ease of use, comfort, and feature effectiveness. The usability testing yielded SUS scores ranging from 69 to 92.5 with a mean score of 81.1, indicating good to excellent usability according to established SUS interpretation guidelines. User feedback highlighted the application’s ease of use, simple interface design, and appropriate feature selection for older adult needs, while also identifying areas for improvement, including larger font sizes and more precise initial instructions.

The final SIAGA application incorporated four main feature categories designed to address different aspects of social connectivity and cognitive engagement. The music feature provides carefully curated nostalgic songs that support emotional well-being, improve quality of life, and foster comfort and connection through familiar cultural melodies. The video component offered light entertainment content designed to improve mood and provide engaging visual stimulation appropriate for older adult preferences. The stories feature enabled users to read and share light, inspirational, and meaningful narratives, encouraging active participation, maintaining social connections, and providing opportunities for healthy, positive expression. The games component was specifically designed to exercise memory, concentration, and cognitive abilities through enjoyable, non-stressful activities appropriate for older adult capabilities and interests.

Expert validation was conducted through consultation with geriatric nursing professors specializing in older adult health and healthcare practitioners from Puskesmas Payung Sekaki, ensuring clinical relevance and cultural appropriateness. The experts provided recommendations regarding content suitability, intervention duration, and implementation strategies based on their clinical experience and understanding of local healthcare systems. Following expert review and incorporation of recommendations, the SIAGA application was deemed ready for implementation with demonstrated usability, clinical relevance, and cultural appropriateness for Indonesian older adult populations.

Data Collection Procedures

Data collection was conducted in collaboration with trained community health cadres who had established relationships with older adult populations in the study areas and an understanding of local cultural contexts. These community partners facilitated participant recruitment, provided cultural mediation during data collection, and assisted with ongoing monitoring throughout the intervention period. Prior to data collection, comprehensive training sessions were conducted with participating health cadres to ensure standardized data collection procedures, ethical protocols, and consistent intervention delivery.

The data collection process began with community-based recruitment sessions, during which potential participants who met the inclusion criteria were identified and invited to participate. Research staff provided detailed explanations of study purposes, procedures, risks, and benefits, ensuring that potential participants fully understood their involvement before providing informed consent. Following consent procedures, participants completed baseline assessments, including sociodemographic questionnaires and administration of the UCLA Loneliness Scale. For the intervention group, the baseline assessment was followed by a comprehensive orientation to the SIAGA application, including demonstrations of features, hands-on practice sessions, and the provision of user guides specifically developed for older adult users.

The intervention was implemented over 4 weeks with structured follow-up assessments at 2 weeks and 4 weeks post-baseline to evaluate both immediate and sustained intervention effects. The selection of these assessment time points was informed by evidence from digital health intervention literature and loneliness research methodology. A recent intervention study using a social connectivity app for older adults employed identical assessment timepoints (baseline, two weeks, and four weeks), demonstrating that meaningful changes in loneliness can be detected within this timeframe (Harkin et al., 2025). The National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine recommends using more than two data points to reliably measure trajectories of social isolation and loneliness (National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, 2020). Outcome Measures

The primary outcome measure was loneliness assessed using the adapted Indonesian version of the UCLA Loneliness Scale Version 3, an internationally validated instrument specifically designed for older adult populations (Rahimmatussalisa & Rinaldi, 2025). This questionnaire initially comprised 20 items; however, the Indonesian version used 19 items following validation testing. Participants responded using a 4-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (never) to 4 (always), with total scores categorized as low loneliness (20–39), moderate loneliness (40–59), and high loneliness (60–80). The Indonesian version underwent comprehensive validation using Rasch modeling to ensure psychometric robustness for the local population. Person ability measures averaged 0.28 logits (SD = 0.80), indicating good alignment between item difficulty and participant ability levels. Person measures ranged from 1.77 to −2.2 logits, while item difficulties ranged from 1.35 to −0.96 logits, demonstrating an appropriate measurement range for the target population.

Secondary outcome measures included sociodemographic characteristics collected through a structured questionnaire, including age, gender, educational level, ethnicity, occupation status, income relative to the regional minimum wage (RMW: IDR 3,675,937, equivalent to USD 216), health insurance coverage, and marital status. These variables were assessed to characterize the study population and identify potential confounding factors that might influence intervention outcomes. The sociodemographic questionnaire was developed based on Indonesian national census categories and validated through pilot testing with similar populations to ensure cultural appropriateness and comprehensibility.

Statistical Analysis

Data analysis was conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics, version 28.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), employing a comprehensive analytical approach to address study objectives and hypotheses. Descriptive statistics, including frequencies, percentages, means, and standard deviations, were calculated to summarize participant characteristics, including age, gender, educational level, ethnicity, occupation, income, insurance coverage, and marital status. Normality testing was performed using Shapiro–Wilk tests to select appropriate statistical tests for outcome analyses.

Baseline comparability between the intervention and control groups was assessed using independent samples t-tests for continuous variables and chi-square tests for categorical variables to ensure that the groups were equivalent prior to intervention implementation. This analysis was crucial for establishing that any post-intervention differences could be attributed to the intervention rather than pre-existing group differences.

Bivariate analysis was conducted using independent-samples t-tests to examine the effectiveness of the SIAGA application on loneliness prevention at 2-week and 4-week time points, comparing mean loneliness scores between the intervention and control groups. Practical significance was assessed by calculating the percentage change from baseline to follow-up assessments within each group.

A multivariate analysis employed a General Linear Model repeated-measures analysis of variance to examine changes in loneliness scores across the three time points (baseline, two weeks, four weeks), while controlling for potential confounding variables identified during baseline comparability testing. The repeated-measures design allowed examination of within-subject changes over time, between-group differences, and time-by-group interactions. Assumptions for repeated-measures analysis of variance were tested, including sphericity (Mauchly’s test), homogeneity of variance (Levene’s test), and normality of residuals. When sphericity assumptions were violated, Greenhouse–Geisser corrections were applied to adjust degrees of freedom and significance levels.

Post-hoc analyses included pairwise comparisons to identify specific time points where significant differences occurred between groups. Additionally, within-group analyses examined whether significant changes occurred from baseline to each follow-up time point within intervention and control groups separately. Statistical significance was set at α = 0.05 for all analyses, with 95% CIs provided for effect estimates.

Ethical Considerations

This study received ethical approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee of Institut Kesehatan Payung Negeri (Approval no: 091/IKES PN/KEPK VI/2025 Date: 10.06.2025) prior to the initiation of participant recruitment and data collection. The research protocol underwent a comprehensive ethical review to ensure the protection of participant rights, welfare, and dignity throughout all study phases. All procedures were conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki for ethical research involving human subjects and the Indonesian national guidelines for research ethics. Informed consent was obtained from all participants through a comprehensive process that included detailed verbal and written explanations of the study’s purposes, procedures, potential risks and benefits, voluntary participation, and the right to withdraw without penalty. To address ethical considerations regarding equitable access to the intervention, participants in the control group were offered access to the SIAGA application following study completion, ensuring that all participants could ultimately benefit from the intervention.

Results

Participant Characteristics

One hundred older adults completed the study (intervention: n = 50, control: n = 50). Groups showed excellent baseline comparability with no significant differences across all demographic variables (all p > .05). Participants were predominantly female (70%), aged 60–69 years (63%), with junior high education (63%), Malay ethnicity (75%), and unemployed (80%). Most had monthly incomes below the regional minimum wage (80%), were uninsured (60%), and were widowed (73%) (Table 2).

Intervention Effectiveness

At baseline, both groups showed comparable loneliness scores (intervention: 55.50 ± 1.82; control: 55.00 ± 0.78; p = .781). Following the intervention, significant reductions in loneliness occurred in the intervention group at two weeks (22.00 ± 1.64 vs. 45.74 ± 4.19, p < .001) and four weeks (17.60 ± 1.74 vs. 46.54 ± 3.87, p < .001). The intervention group demonstrated reductions of 60.4% and 68.1% from baseline at two and four weeks, respectively. Repeated-measures analysis confirmed significant between-group differences (p < .001) and a time-by-group interaction effect (Table 3).

Table 3 also showed the clinical significance: 94% of intervention participants (47/50) achieved clinically meaningful improvement (≥5-point reduction), compared with 8% of controls (4/50). Participants transitioned from high loneliness (baseline: 55.50) to low loneliness categories (final: 17.60) according to UCLA Loneliness Scale criteria. No adverse events were reported, and 96% of participants completed the full intervention protocol.

The graph clearly demonstrates the dramatic reduction in loneliness among participants using the SIAGA application, in contrast to the stable levels in the control group. The visual representation emphasizes both the immediate effectiveness (evident at two weeks) and the sustained benefits (continued improvement at four weeks) of the intervention.

The control group maintained consistently elevated loneliness scores throughout the study period (baseline: 55.00; 2 weeks: 45.74; 4 weeks: 46.54), highlighting the specific effectiveness of the SIAGA intervention in addressing loneliness among community-dwelling Indonesian older adults.

Discussion

This study demonstrates that the SIAGA application effectively reduced loneliness among community-dwelling older adults in Indonesia. The observed 60.4% reduction in loneliness scores at two weeks and 68.1% reduction at four weeks represents clinically meaningful improvements that align with the most successful technology-based interventions reported globally. These findings contribute important evidence to the growing but inconsistent literature on digital mental health interventions for older adults, particularly in developing country contexts where rigorous experimental evidence remains limited.

The magnitude of the loneliness reduction observed with SIAGA substantially exceeds the effects reported in recent systematic reviews and meta-analyses of digital interventions. While Shah et al. (2021) found no statistically significant pooled effects across six randomized controlled trials, and Jin et al. (2021) similarly reported minimal effects for smartphone-based interventions, the SIAGA intervention achieved effect sizes approaching those of the most successful technology-based interventions (Jin et al., 2021; Shah et al., 2021). The Korean ICT-based loneliness alleviation program reported similar success with statistically significant improvements in loneliness, depression, and social engagement among 40 community-dwelling older adults over six weeks (Ae-Ri et al., 2023). Videoconferencing interventions in long-term care settings have achieved large effect sizes, though these typically involve more intensive technological infrastructure than mobile applications (Hoang et al., 2022).

The sustained improvement observed from two to four weeks represents a particularly noteworthy finding, as longitudinal effectiveness represents a persistent challenge in digital loneliness interventions. Most studies report diminishing effects over time, with Shah et al. (2021) finding no sustained benefits at 12-month follow-up across multiple intervention types (Shah et al., 2021). The continued improvement among SIAGA participants suggests that the culturally adapted, multi-component approach may facilitate sustained engagement and benefit maintenance. This finding aligns with theoretical frameworks that emphasize that successful digital interventions must address maladaptive social cognition patterns through sustained cognitive restructuring rather than temporary facilitation of social contact (Hawkley & Cacioppo, 2010).

Cultural adaptation emerges as a critical factor distinguishing SIAGA’s effectiveness from previous digital interventions conducted primarily in Western, high-income contexts. The systematic design process incorporating Indonesian cultural values, family-centered communication patterns, and locally appropriate content appears to have enhanced intervention acceptability and effectiveness. Research on culturally adapted cognitive-behavioral therapy in Southeast Asian populations demonstrates superior outcomes compared to standard interventions. However, the evidence base remains limited, with only seven studies meeting the inclusion criteria for systematic reviews (Praptomojati et al., 2024). The integration of nostalgic music, culturally relevant storytelling, and games designed for Indonesian older adults reflects evidence-based approaches to cultural adaptation that enhance engagement and therapeutic alliance.

The technology acceptance patterns observed in this study align with research demonstrating that perceived usefulness and cultural appropriateness strongly influence adoption among older adults in developing countries. Indonesian mobile health adoption studies identify initial trust in platforms as the strongest predictor of usage intention, significantly outweighing performance expectancy factors emphasized in Western technology acceptance models (Octavius & Antonio, 2021). The high completion rate (96% of participants completing the intervention protocol) suggests that SIAGA’s user-centered design process effectively addressed common barriers, including interface complexity, cognitive load, and technological anxiety. This contrasts with typical dropout rates of 20%–70% reported across digital mental health interventions, highlighting the importance of cultural and contextual adaptation in intervention design (Balki et al., 2022).

The theoretical mechanisms underlying SIAGA’s effectiveness appear consistent with social cognitive theory and loneliness regulatory loop frameworks that emphasize the importance of addressing maladaptive cognitive patterns while facilitating meaningful social connections. The multi-component intervention design, incorporating music therapy, storytelling, video content, and cognitive games, addresses multiple pathways to reducing loneliness. Music therapy components align with evidence demonstrating that nostalgic music enhances positive emotions, social connectedness, and autobiographical memory among older adults (Jin et al., 2021). The storytelling feature facilitates narrative expression and social sharing, which research demonstrates can reduce loneliness through meaning-making and social validation processes (Jarvis et al., 2020). Cognitive games target executive function and working memory, which research suggests may mediate the relationship between loneliness and cognitive decline in aging populations (Xiang et al., 2020).

The implementation approach utilizing community health cadres represents an innovative model for scaling digital mental health interventions in resource-limited settings. This community-based delivery method addresses fundamental barriers to technology adoption among older adults, including limited digital literacy, technological anxiety, and ongoing technical support needs. Research on digital health implementation in Indonesian primary care settings reveals substantial infrastructure limitations and provider knowledge gaps, necessitating comprehensive ecosystem approaches rather than individual-focused interventions (Aisyah et al., 2024). The integration of community health workers aligns with evidence demonstrating that human support significantly enhances the effectiveness of digital interventions, with supported digital interventions showing superior outcomes compared to fully automated approaches across multiple systematic reviews (Welch et al., 2023).

From a public health perspective, the high response rate (94% of intervention participants achieving clinically meaningful improvement) suggests substantial population-level impact potential if scaled appropriately. The transition from high loneliness scores (baseline: 55.50) to low loneliness categories (final: 17.60) according to established UCLA Loneliness Scale cutoff criteria indicates that participants experienced meaningful improvements in subjective social connectedness that extend beyond statistical significance. This magnitude of change substantially exceeds the minimal clinically important differences typically reported for loneliness scales. However, standardized clinical significance thresholds remain inadequately established for older adults in developing country contexts (National Academies of Sciences, 2020).

The cost-effectiveness implications of the SIAGA intervention warrant consideration within Indonesia’s expanding universal health coverage framework. Digital mental health interventions generally demonstrate favorable cost-effectiveness profiles, with 57.1% of studies showing higher quality-adjusted life years at lower cost than traditional care delivery models (Gentili et al., 2022). Mobile applications show particular promise for improving quality-adjusted life years while reducing overall healthcare costs. However, most economic analyses are conducted in high-income countries with different healthcare financing structures (Buntrock, 2024). The community-based delivery model utilizing existing health cadre networks may enhance cost-effectiveness by leveraging established infrastructure while addressing digital literacy barriers that limit intervention accessibility.

Technology factors, including smartphone model compatibility, internet connectivity requirements, and ongoing technical support needs, represent practical implementation considerations requiring systematic evaluation. While 96% of participants completed the intervention protocol, the study population was pre-selected for Android smartphone ownership and basic technological capability. Population-level implementation would require addressing digital divide issues, including access to devices and connectivity, internet affordability, and comprehensive digital literacy training. The Indonesian ICT maturity assessment across nine provinces revealed substantial regional variations that would influence the scalability of interventions (Aisyah et al., 2024).

Safety considerations, while showing no adverse events in this study, require ongoing monitoring in larger-scale implementations. Digital mental health intervention safety research remains limited, with most studies reporting informal safety monitoring rather than systematic adverse event tracking (Taher et al., 2023). Risk mitigation strategies should include graduated technology introduction, accessible technical support systems, and protocols for identifying and addressing technology-related anxiety or social withdrawal. Privacy and data security concerns are particular considerations for older adults, who may have a limited understanding of digital privacy risks.

Study Limitations

Several study limitations warrant acknowledgment and consideration for future research directions. The quasi-experimental design, while appropriate for community-based intervention research, limits causal inference compared to randomized controlled trial approaches. The 4-week follow-up period, though showing sustained improvement, provides insufficient evidence for long-term effectiveness maintenance. Future research should examine intervention effects at three, six, and 12-month intervals to assess sustained benefit and identify factors supporting long-term engagement. The single-site urban community setting may limit generalizability to rural populations, where technological infrastructure, digital literacy, and cultural contexts differ substantially from those of the study population.

Conclusion and Recommendations

In conclusion, the SIAGA application demonstrates substantial effectiveness for reducing loneliness among community-dwelling older adults in Indonesia. The culturally adapted, multi-component design delivered through community health networks represents a promising model for addressing mental health needs among aging populations in developing countries. While limitations, including study design, follow-up duration, and generalizability, warrant consideration, these findings provide important evidence supporting the potential for culturally adapted digital mental health interventions to address the growing global challenge of loneliness among older adults.

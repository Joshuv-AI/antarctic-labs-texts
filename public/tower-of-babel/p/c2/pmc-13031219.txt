First-in-Human Phase I Study of KPT-9274, a First-in-Class Dual Inhibitor of PAK4 and NAMPT, in Patients with Advanced Solid Malignancies
Authors: Albiruni Razak, Amit Mahipal, Jennifer R Diamond, Antoni Ribas, Jordan David Berlin, Asfar S Azmi, Tomer Mark, Christopher J Walker, Yi Chai, Trinayan Kashyap, Girish Gudi, Aung Naing

ABSTRACT

Key Points

This study demonstrated that KPT-9274 as a single agent or in combination with niacin or nivolumab had a generally manageable safety profile in patients with advanced solid tumors, with no observed cardiac or retinal toxicity commonly associated with other NAMPT inhibitors.While efficacy was not observed in this trial, the safety profile and unique mechanism of action of KPT-9274 warrant further exploration in other patient populations, including pediatric tumors.

Introduction

Padnarsertib (KPT-9274) is a potentially first-in-class, orally bioavailable, small molecule that is a non-competitive dual modulator of p21 protein (Cdc42/Rac)-activated kinase 4 (PAK4) and nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase/PBEF/visfatin (NAMPT) (Fig. 1). PAK4 is a key downstream effector for RAS and forms a critical link between RAS-driven oncogenic transformation and activation of the WNT/β-catenin signaling pathway [1, 2]. This role in cancer development and progression has made PAK4 an attractive therapeutic target for cancer treatments [2]. KPT-9274 was previously determined to be a novel PAK4 allosteric modulator [3]. NAMPT is the rate-limiting enzyme in the metabolic scavenging pathway that utilizes nicotinamide to replenish nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD), an essential metabolic cofactor and secondary messenger involved in the regulation of various cellular processes including DNA repair and is frequently overexpressed in tumor tissues, making it a key target for cancer therapy [4–6]. Tumor cells have shown an increased dependency on NAD compared with normal cells, suggesting that NAD plays an important role in cancer metabolism [6, 7]. Some tumors lack the enzymes for de novo NAD synthesis and have reduced or absent nicotinate phosphoribosyltransferase 1 (NAPRT1) enzyme, which results in a reliance on NAMPT for NAD production [8, 9]. Therefore, NAPRT1 could be a potential biomarker to evaluate the response of NAMPT inhibitors [10].

Niacin can be utilized by cells to make NAD [10] through an alternative pathway with NAPRT1. Administration of both niacin and an NAMPT inhibitor to patients who have NAPRT1 expression in normal tissue and reduced or absent NAPRT1 in their tumors may protect normal tissues while enhancing the therapeutic sensitivity of NAMPT inhibition and concurrently reducing treatment side effects [11]. Co-inhibition of PAK4 and NAMPT leads to synergistic anti-tumor effects through energy depletion, inhibition of DNA repair, cell cycle arrest, inhibition of proliferation, and ultimately apoptosis [12–14]. Taken together, PAK4 and NAMPT inhibition in cancer could be a promising therapeutic strategy.

Nonclinical studies have shown single-agent antiproliferative activity in both hematologic and solid tumor cells with minimal toxicity to normal cells by KPT-9274. Normal cells are more resistant to inhibition by KPT-9274 due in part to their relative genomic stability and lower metabolic rates [14, 15]. In vivo studies of mouse xenografts treated with KPT-9274 revealed a marked inhibition in tumor growth, or disease control, across a variety of tumor models, including colon, pancreatic, lung, sarcoma, multiple myeloma, leukemia, and lymphoma. Over half of the tumor types evaluated had complete elimination of tumors in as little as 3 weeks with no regrowth after cessation of treatment for up to 3 weeks [13–16]. In nonclinical studies for pediatric sarcomas (Ewing sarcoma and rhabdomyosarcoma), including patient-derived xenograft models, KPT-9274 has shown anti-tumor and anti-metastatic activity [17, 18]. KPT-9274 has also shown single-agent activity in trials of companion dogs with cancer, as well as biological activity in a canine model of non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma when combined with doxorubicin, without increasing toxicity [19]. These studies indicate that KPT-9274 could be beneficial in several solid tumor malignancies as well as hematologic malignancies. In addition to patient-derived xenograft and xenograft tumor growth inhibition, KPT-9274 reduces PAK4 expression and negatively regulates the Wnt/β-catenin pathway as well as NAD levels, confirming mechanistic relevance [20].

The combination of the KRASG12C inhibitor, sotorasib, and KPT-9274 showed synergistic anticancer effects through a reduction in tumor growth and an increase in survival in xenograft mouse models of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) and non–small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) [14]. KPT-9274 has also been shown to overcome resistance to venetoclax in relapsed acute myeloid leukemia stem cells [21]. Nivolumab is a monoclonal antibody targeting PD-1 on lymphocytes that has previously been approved for the treatment of melanoma [22]. Combining KPT-9274 with an anti-PD-1 antibody in melanoma models has shown greater tumor growth inhibition compared with either agent alone [23]. This combination treatment may benefit melanoma patients who have progressed on prior anti-PD-1 or PD-L1 therapies. These studies suggest that KPT-9274 could be applied in combination therapies to improve clinical efficacy in a number of different cancer types [14].

NAD levels and viability in vitro are rescued by supplementation with exogenous niacin in cell lines that are NAPRT1 proficient, but not in NAPRT1-deficient cells following KPT-9274 treatment. Niacin may improve the tolerability of NAMPT inhibitors without compromising cytotoxicity toward tumor cells. Rescue of KPT-9274 treatment with niacin in vitro correlates with NAPRT1 messenger RNA (mRNA) and protein expression, and/or NAPRT1 promoter hypermethylation [24].

Despite advances in cancer treatment, there remains a critical need for therapeutic strategies that address advanced malignancies that progress despite exhausting all standard treatment options. KPT-9274, as a single agent or in combination with nivolumab, offers a potential approach to target such challenging cases. This study aimed to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and efficacy of KPT-9274, alone or in combination with nivolumab, to address unmet needs in the treatment of advanced tumors and lymphoma.

Methods

Study Design

KCP-9274-901 (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT02702492) was a phase I, first-in-human, multi-center, open-label clinical study to assess preliminary safety and tolerability of KPT-9274 in patients with advanced solid tumors, for whom all standard therapeutic options have been exhausted and with progressive disease at study entry.

This study was conducted in two separate phases. First, a two-part (A and B) dose escalation phase. In part A, the recommended phase II dose (RP2D) and maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of KPT-9274 alone was to be determined. Part B evaluated the RP2D and MTD of KPT-9274 co-administered with a starting daily dose of niacin extended-release (ER) 500 mg that could be titrated up to 2000 mg. Next, part C was the dose-finding and expansion phase of KPT-9274 plus nivolumab in patients with melanoma and was conducted to further investigate the safety, tolerability, and preliminary evidence of anti-tumor activity of the RP2D or any dose-limiting toxicity (DLT)-cleared dose. Patient enrollment was halted prematurely due to a lack of observable efficacy in all three parts of the study.

Patients

In parts A and B, patients were enrolled who were ≥18 years of age with advanced solid malignancies for which all standard therapeutic options considered useful by the investigator had been exhausted and had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of ≤1. In part C, patients were ≥18 years of age with unresectable advanced, recurrent or metastatic melanoma measurable by Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST), version 1.1, after disease progression on a prior anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 therapy and had an ECOG performance status of ≤2. Exclusion criteria across all parts included ≤2 weeks since the last prior anticancer regimen or palliative steroids for disease-related symptoms, use of palliative steroids for disease related symptoms <7 days prior to study treatment, and patients with known central nervous system (CNS) disease or leptomeningeal involvement. Additional eligibility criteria by study part are detailed in Supplementary Table S1 (see electronic supplementary material [ESM]).

Treatment

Patients were administered KPT-9274 in 28-day treatment cycles until disease progression, withdrawal of consent, loss to follow-up, or toxicity. A standard 3+3 design [25] was used for dose escalation decisions. For this study, a DLT was defined as an adverse event (AE) or abnormal laboratory value occurring in the first treatment cycle that was not due to underlying disease. See Supplementary Table S2 (ESM) for DLT criteria.

In parts A and B, patients received 10 mg, 20 mg, 30 mg, or 40 mg KPT-9274 three times weekly every other day (QOD) or 40 mg bi-weekly (BIW). Additional cohorts received 30 mg, 40 mg, 60 mg, 80 mg, or 100 mg KPT-9274 QOD in combination with niacin ER at a starting dose of 500 mg that could be titrated up to 2000 mg daily. For part C, patients received two dose levels of KPT-9274 (30 or 40 mg QOD) in combination with intravenous nivolumab (480 mg) per package insert every 4 weeks [26]. Additional dose levels (e.g., 20 mg or 60 mg) were evaluated.

Pharmacokinetics and Pharmacodynamics Assessments

Pharmacokinetic (PK) assessments included maximum plasma concentration (Cmax), area under the curve (AUC), time-to-peak plasma concentration (tmax), and terminal plasma half-life (t½). Pharmacodynamic (PD) assessments were performed on plasma, leukocytes, and tumor tissue collected at screening (baseline) and/or post-treatment. These assessments include the determination of NAD levels in tumor samples and leukocytes and NAPRT1 promoter methylation and mutation status in formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) and/or frozen tumor and metabolomics study, including NAMPT levels, on patient plasma. Further details can be found in the ESM.

Outcomes

The primary endpoints for parts A, B, and C were MTD/RP2D, DLTs, the occurrence of grade ≥3 AEs, all serious adverse events (SAEs), and all AEs leading to treatment discontinuation. Secondary endpoints for parts A and B (dose escalation phase) were objective response rate, duration of response, disease control rate, duration of disease control, progression-free survival, overall survival, time to progression, maximum plasma concentration, time-to-peak plasma concentration, terminal half-life, and plasma clearance. The secondary endpoints in part C were preliminary evidence of anti-tumor activity and pharmacokinetics, including maximum plasma concentration, time-to-peak plasma concentration, terminal half-life, and plasma clearance. Exploratory endpoints for all parts included target engagement and mechanism of action, pharmacodynamic biomarkers of response, predictive biomarkers of response, and possible mechanisms of resistance.

Statistical Analysis

Sample Size

Sample size for the dose escalation phase was based on a standard 3+3 dose escalation scheme [25] to determine the RP2D and MTD (part A) and the RP2D and MTD of KPT-9274 co-administered with niacin ER (part B). In both parts A and B, there were three or six patients per cohort. Up to 65 additional patients could be enrolled at the RP2D for single-agent KPT-9274 or KPT-9274 plus niacin ER. The dose expansion was to be conducted with KPT-9274 plus niacin ER, then an optional cohort of up to 20 patients could be enrolled in the KPT-9274 single-agent cohort. The optional cohort and evaluation of all nine dose levels would account for a total combined enrollment for parts A and B of up to 175 patients. For the two dose levels in part C, a maximum of 10 patients could be enrolled per dose level. If the optimal dose was not identified, two additional dose levels for 20 mg or 60 mg KPT-9274 could be evaluated.

Safety Analysis

Safety and tolerability of KPT-9274 with or without niacin ER and KPT-9274 plus nivolumab were evaluated by means of DLTs, AEs, physical examination results, electrocardiogram results, and laboratory safety evaluations. The National Cancer Institute (NCI) Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), Version 4.03 [27] was used for grading of AEs.

Efficacy Analysis

Antitumor activity was evaluated by investigators based on RECIST v1.1 [28] for advanced solid malignancies. No formal efficacy analyses were conducted.

Results

Patient Characteristics and Disposition

Enrollment began on June 13, 2021, and was completed on January 5, 2022. At data cut off on March 2, 2022, a total of 60 patients were enrolled and were included in the safety analyses (parts A and B, n = 50; part C, n = 10). The decision to terminate the study early by the sponsor was due to the lack of observable evidence of anti-tumor activity, therefore efficacy endpoints were not assessed and are not reported.

Within the entire cohort, the majority of patients were white (78.3%) and male (56.7%). The median age of patients was 62.0 years. Nearly all of the enrolled patients had prior antineoplastic systemic therapy (98.3%; one patient in dose escalation part B with left eye choroidal melanoma had no information reported on prior treatment) and 73.3% of the patients had received three or more prior antineoplastic regimens (Table 1). At least one dose of KPT-9274 was administered to all enrolled patients (Fig. 2 and Supplementary Fig. S2, ESM). In total, there were ten dosing cohorts for parts A and B, and three dosing cohorts for part C. See Supplementary Table S3 in the ESM for a complete description of dosing cohorts.

In parts A and B, there were three DLTs observed, one (14%) in cohort 4 (40 mg QOD × 3, n = 7) of anemia and two (29%) in cohort 7B (80 mg plus niacin QOD × 3, n = 7) of anemia and fatigue. The MTD was not determined due to premature discontinuation of enrollment due to lack of observed efficacy. For part C, the MTD was 40 mg plus nivolumab QOD × 3. The RP2D was not determined.

The most frequently reported (≥15%) treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) among all patients treated with single-agent KPT-9274 were anemia (82.6%), arthralgia (52.2%), fatigue (43.5%), and nausea (30.4%) (Table 3). Grade ≥3 TEAEs were reported in all patients in cohort 3 (30 mg QOD × 3) and cohort 4 (40 mg QOD × 3) from parts A and B (Supplementary Table S4, ESM) and the most frequent were anemia (60.0%), abdominal pain (13.3%), and fatigue and vomiting (8.3%). No cardiac or retinal toxicity was observed, which is common with other NAMPT inhibitors [15, 29]. No grade 5 AEs were reported.

All single-agent cohorts, except for cohort 1 (10 mg QOD × 3), reported AEs leading to dose interruption. Rates of discontinuation due to study drug were infrequent and did not appear to be linked to increased dose. A total of eight (13.3%) patients (Parts A + B: n = 7, 14%; Part C: n = 1, 10%) discontinued due to an AE. Cohort 4 (40 mg QOD × 3) and cohort 7B (80 mg plus niacin QOD) had the highest rate of discontinuation due to study drug of 28.6% (Supplementary Table S4, ESM).

A total of 22 (36.7%) patients experienced at least one serious adverse event (SAE), all of which were grade ≥3 in severity. Among the patients treated with single-agent KPT-9274, 30.4% experienced an SAE, with the most frequent being small intestinal obstruction (8.7%). Six patients died within 30 days of receiving their last dose of the study drug (part A, n = 1; part B, n = 2; part C, n = 3). None were considered related to KPT-9274. The causes for death were disease-related (n = 4), small intestinal obstruction (n = 1), and other (n = 1).

Pharmacokinetics

Non-compartmental PK analyses were performed using KPT-9274 plasma concentration data from samples collected in a total of 10 cohorts including parts A and B on cycle 1 day 1 (C1D1) and C1D24 (Supplementary Figs. S3 and S4, ESM). The systematic exposure of KPT-9274 was characterized by a prolonged oral absorption phase, with median tmax ranging between 8 and 24 h. KPT-9274 plasma exposure (Cmax and AUC0–last) appeared to increase proportionally with dose on C1D1 and less than proportionally with dose on C1D24. Over the course of the first dosing cycle there was substantial KPT-9274 plasma accumulation observed. Pre-dose plasma concentrations of KPT-9274 from C1D8 to C3D1 were similar. Co-administration with niacin had no impact on the PK of KPT-9274. There were insufficient PK samples collected to fully characterize the terminal portion of the elimination phase due to the long elimination time of KPT-9274. Based on limited PK data in patients where the terminal half-life could be calculated, the median elimination half-life of KPT-9274 was 55 hours across all cohorts. The PK profile was generally similar in part C with the combination of KPT-9274 and nivolumab.

Methylation of NAPRT1 Promoter and Clinical Correlations

Tumor NAPRT1 promoter methylation status was assessed to determine the relationship between KPT-9274 treatment and NAPRT1 expression on clinical outcome. Among the clinically evaluable patients (RECIST % change in volume of target lesion compared with baseline measurement), NAPRT1 promoter methylation status in tumor samples could be assessed for 38 patients (Fig. 3). Of the six patients with tumors harboring a methylated NAPRT1 promoter, four had a reduction in target lesion volume or minimal increase in the volume from baseline. Of the two patients with tumors harboring a methylated NAPRT1 promoter whose target lesion grew compared with baseline, both received niacin (Fig. 3). Given the small sample size (n = 6), additional studies are warranted to assess a correlation between tumor NAPRT1 promoter methylation status and inhibition of tumor growth volume with KPT-9274 treatment.

Clinical Significance of Tumor Genomic Alterations

The relevant cancer mutations were evaluated in a subset of 20 patients with available tumor samples prior to receiving KPT-9274 treatment. Within a subset of this patient cohort (n = 20), tumors most frequently had a mutation in KRAS (n = 9, Supplemental Table S7, ESM). No significant correlation was identified between individual mutation(s) and clinical outcome.

Biomarker Analysis in Tumor and Plasma Samples

NAD levels were evaluated in patient leukocyte (n = 14) and tumor (n = 10) samples collected pre- and post-treatment initiation. A modest reduction of NAD levels was observed after KPT-9274 treatment in leukocytes and tumors. However, due to the small sample size and different collection timepoints between the two sample sets, it is difficult to draw a definitive conclusion. Notably, patients who received niacin showed a trend towards increased NAD in leukocytes post-treatment while the same was not evident in tumors, suggesting the possibility of prominent NAPRT1 levels in the normal leukocytes compared with the tumors (Supplemental Fig. S5, ESM). Collectively, these data suggest KPT-9274 treatment decreases NAD signaling within the tumor microenvironment, but additional datapoints (samples and timepoints) are required to validate the finding.

In additional metabolomic analysis using the plasma samples from patients in the study, 1-methylanicotinamide (1MNA) was identified as the top metabolite that showed consistent activation in post-treatment samples. As a major product of nicotinamide metabolism, 1MNA has been shown to inhibit NAD+ synthesis and could serve as a biomarker of response. The second most altered metabolite was taurochenodesoxycholic acid (TCDCA), a bile acid conjugate that plays a role in bile acid metabolism and signaling pathways within the liver and gut. Emerging research suggests that TCDCA may influence cellular energy metabolism, mitochondrial function, as well as lipid metabolism, with a potential link to NAD metabolism (Supplementary Table S6, ESM).

Discussion

This first-in-human phase I study of KPT-9274, a dual NAMPT/PAK4 inhibitor, was conducted in three parts to evaluate its safety and tolerability in patients with advanced solid tumors. Parts A and B utilized varying doses of KPT-9274 with or without niacin ER, while part C explored the combination of KPT-9274 + nivolumab. As a phase I study, the primary objective of Parts A and B of the study was to determine the recommended phase II dose (RP2D) and the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of KPT-9274 without and with co-administration of niacin. Although KPT-9274 demonstrated a tolerable safety profile, with no evidence of cardiac or retinal toxicity, and the MTD was not reached across the evaluated dose cohorts, the study was prematurely terminated due to multiple factors, including the absence of deep anti-tumor activity at the doses tested. Consequently, no analyses of efficacy were conducted.

The dual action NAMPT/PAK4 inhibitor KPT-9274 differs from other inhibitors that have advanced to clinical trials, such as pan-PAK inhibitor (PF-3758309) and NAMPT inhibitors (APO866 and GMX1777/GMX1778). Furthermore, KPT-9274 demonstrated consistent pharmacokinetic properties, including oral absorption, minimal brain penetration [15], and a lack of inhibition of the cytochrome P450 (CYP450) enzymes. The nonclinical toxicity profile of KPT-9274 recapitulates the expected class-level gastrointestinal (GI) and hematopoietic toxicities, but not the retinal and cardiac effects observed preclinically with some NAMPT inhibitors [15, 16, 30, 31]. Thus, KPT-9274 captures many of the favorable therapeutic aspects of targeting both NAMPT and PAK4 with the potential for reduced adverse effects. KPT-9274 has shown biological activity in animal models of triple negative breast cancer [32, 33], pancreatic cancer, sarcomas, and lung cancers, highlighting the potential of its dual-target mechanism of action (PAK4 + NAMPT), as a promising therapeutic approach to be tested in these specific indications [3, 14, 18, 31]. In pediatric sarcomas, PAKs are critical regulators in tumor growth and metastasis [18], and inhibition of both NAMPT and PAK4 has shown anti-tumor activity in Ewing sarcoma and rhabdomyosarcoma models [3, 18]. Thus, these findings support further investigation of KPT-9274 in pediatric sarcomas.

The pharmacodynamic analysis shows that KPT-9274 has a suppressing impact on the levels of NAD in tumor and leukocytes. This indicates mechanistic significance to KPT-9274 inhibition of NAMPT. Interestingly, though measured at different timepoints, the addition of niacin elevated NAD levels in leukocytes but not in tumors. This may support the hypothesis of improved tolerability of KPT-9274 in the presence of niacin, however additional studies are required to substantiate this finding. Several patients with tumors harboring a methylated NAPRT1 promoter demonstrated a reduction in tumor lesion with KPT-9274 treatment.

In summary, although no efficacy endpoints were reported, the cumulative AE data did not reveal any significant safety signals or identify important potential risks associated with KPT-9274. This first-in-human study demonstrates that KPT-9274, whether administered as a single agent or in combination with niacin or nivolumab, exhibits a generally manageable safety profile at the evaluated doses, with no MTD identified in parts A and B. While no evidence of deep tumor responses was observed in this study, the study generated preliminary evidence of drug activity, especially in patients with tumors harboring a methylated NAPRT1 promoter, supporting the utility of using NAPRT1 expression as a potential predictive marker of KPT-9274 response in future trials.

Despite dose linear pharmacokinetics, it must be noted that systemic exposures to KPT-9274 that demonstrated robust efficacy in the animal models were not achieved at the doses evaluated. Dose regimen optimization may be key to achieving exposures that demonstrate efficacy and therapeutic margin in humans. Additionally, higher dose levels in this study were co-administered with niacin which may have attenuated the anti-tumor response of KPT-9274, given that a majority of tumor samples exhibited an unmethylated NAPRT1 promoter. These data also lay the foundation for the use of NAPRT1 expression as a potential predictive marker of KPT-9274 response; however, further investigations are needed to confirm this association. Overall, these findings highlight the potential benefit of dual inhibition of NAMPT and PAK4 with KPT-9274. Although meaningful antitumor activity was not observed in this heavily pre-treated population, further investigation is warranted in other patient populations including pediatric tumors such as Ewing sarcoma and rhabdomyosarcoma.

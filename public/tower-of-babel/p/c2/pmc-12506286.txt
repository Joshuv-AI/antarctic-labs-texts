Spatiotemporal evaluation of atmospheric CO2 fluctuations in Shaanxi Province (2013–2022) utilizing multi-source satellite remote sensing data
Authors: Yin Li, Yang Lv, Hui Yao, ChaoJie Li, SiYa Xv, Xiaoli Li, Xiaoling Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDespite the rapid and well-documented surge in global atmospheric CO2 levels, predominantly driven by fossil fuel combustion and industrialization, the characterization of CO2 variations at regional scales remains notably sparse. This study integrates satellite remote sensing (RS) and ground-based measurements to examine the spatiotemporal distributions and drivers of CO2 in China’s Shaanxi Province from 2013 to 2022. Although Shaanxi has experienced rapid development, its CO2 trends have remained unclear. By integrating CO2 observations from satellite sources, specifically the Orbiting Carbon Observatory-2 (OCO-2) and Fourier Transform Spectrometer (FTS), with data from the World Data Centre for Greenhouse Gases (WDCGG) Hong Kong ground station, we have synthesized a uniquely comprehensive dataset that enables enhanced resolution in exploring intra-annual, interannual, and spatial CO2 variations across the province. The results reveal pronounced seasonal CO2 cycles and a consistent upward trend over the past decade. The monthly concentrations exhibited a sinusoidal pattern, oscillating between a minimum of 399.68 ± 6.58 ppm in August and peaking at 407.48 ± 6.58 ppm in April. High CO2 regions within Shaanxi are predominantly found in its southern subtropical and temperate areas, reaching 418.4 ppm in 2022. From 2013 to 2022, the annual average CO2 increased by 4.12% from 396 to 412.34 ppm, with a higher growth rate in southern compared to northern Shaanxi. This study elucidates the distinct spatiotemporal variations in CO2 levels across Shaanxi Province, revealing prominent seasonal cycles and a discernible upward trend over the past decade. The results offer new insights into CO2 characteristics and dynamics in this rapidly developing region of China, and further investigation into the factors underlying the observed variations is warranted.Keywords: Satellite remote sensing, OCO-2, GOSAT, CO2 column concentration, Coefficient of variation

Introduction

Atmospheric CO2, a critical greenhouse gas, makes up less than 0.05% of the total atmosphere [1, 2]. Global atmospheric CO2 density has risen from roughly 280 ppm pre-industrial revolution to 415 ppm in 2020, marking a 48.21% increment. This increase primarily stems from fossil fuel combustion, such as coal, oil, and natural gas, along with deforestation, which diminishes vegetation and significantly impairs forests’ capacity for CO2 absorption via photosynthesis [3]. The interaction between diverse carbon sources and reservoirs in the global carbon cycle influences atmospheric CO2 column concentrations (XCO2s). Human activities have discharged significant amounts of CO2, instigating an ongoing upsurge in atmospheric CO2 levels. This rise contributes to global warming and subsequently impacts the worldwide climate [4–6].

The IPCC’s Fifth Assessment Report posits that the primary cause of the increase in greenhouse gas concentrations is human activity, particularly the burning of fossil fuels since the industrial revolution, resulting in a global average temperature elevation over the past century (up to 2005) [7]. The IPCC’s Sixth Special Report indicates that global warming could reach 1.5℃ if massive and immediate cuts in greenhouse gas emissions are not made [8]. The myriad issues engendered by CO2 gravely endanger human existence and progress, with the continuous increase in CO2 column concentration leading to significant repercussions on the global ecology, economy, environment, and politics. Growing evidence suggests that increased CO2 column concentrations could pose direct risks for human health, with potential effects including inflammation, reductions in higher-level cognitive abilities, bone demineralization, kidney calcification, oxidative stress, and endothelial dysfunction [9]. China’s accelerated economic development, heightened fossil fuel utilization, and increased CO2 emissions [10, 11] necessitate comprehensive research on CO2 spatiotemporal dynamics. Satellite RS, with its extensive coverage and capability for long-term continuous observation, has become pivotal in monitoring atmospheric CO2 column concentrations (CO2-CC) [12–15]. As the foremost RS satellite focused on greenhouse gases, Greenhouse gases Observing SATellite (GOSAT) can achieve a precision of up to approximately 1 ppm in the retrieval of atmospheric CO2-CC from satellite RS.

The state-of-the-art satellite sensors, including OCO-2 and GOSAT FTS—the focus of this study—as well as OCO-3 and GOSAT-2, are capable of measuring CO2 column concentrations. GOSAT is distinguished as the world’s first RS satellite with greenhouse gases as its observation target [16]. The sensors have demonstrated high-level accuracy, with the XCO2 retrieval accuracy of approximately 1 ppm. The OCO-2 and GOSAT/FTS instruments have provided comprehensive temporal coverage from 2013 to 2022, offering significant advantages for time series analysis.

This study uniquely integrates satellite remote sensing data from OCO-2 and GOSAT/FTS (2013 to 2022) with terrestrial data from the WDCGG Hong Kong ground station, creating a comprehensive 10-year CO2 dataset. This dataset is the first of its kind for this region and timeframe, and it was meticulously utilized to validate and calibrate remote sensing (RS) measurements, allowing for unprecedented insights into the spatial distribution and temporal variation characteristics of CO2 column concentrations (CO2-CCs). By employing sophisticated analytical techniques, such as the time variation coefficient and space variation coefficient, this research not only quantifies but also characterizes the temporal oscillations and spatial heterogeneity of CO2-CCs in Shaanxi Province. The integration of advanced satellite sensors, such as OCO2 and GOSAT FTS, with ground-based observational data, marks a significant advancement in our methodological approach. This allows for more accurate monitoring and analysis of CO2-CCs, which is vital for addressing the challenges posed by escalating CO2 levels on global climate change. The significance of this study is further underscored by its focus on Shaanxi Province, a region critical to understanding China’s rapid economic expansion and its implications for fossil fuel consumption and CO2 emissions.

Through a detailed analysis of CO2 data from satellite RS and ground observation stations, this research elucidates the changing patterns and underlying drivers of atmospheric CO2 in China. The findings of this study are poised to offer critical insights for policymakers, providing a robust scientific basis for crafting strategies aimed at reducing greenhouse gas emissions and mitigating the impacts of climate change. This innovative approach enhances our understanding of regional CO2 dynamics, setting a new standard for environmental monitoring and policy development.

In conclusion, the continuous monitoring of XCO2 through satellite RS is essential for comprehending the human impact on the global environment. By pioneering comprehensive data integration and analysis techniques, this research contributes significantly to the scientific community’s ability to develop effective climate change mitigation policies.

Study area and datasets

Study AREA

Shaanxi Province, depicted in Fig. 1 and also known as the Shaanxi region, holds historical and geographical significance in northwestern China. It features diverse topography, including the Qinling Mountains, the Loess Plateau, and the Guanzhong Plain, and shares borders with various provinces, including Gansu and Ningxia, positioning it as a crucial junction that has historically facilitated pivotal transportation and commerce routes, such as the ancient Silk Road. Shaanxi Province has a clear topographical divide between its northern and southern regions. The northern region is predominantly characterized by the Loess Plateau, noted for its easily erodible and nutrient-rich soil, while the southern region is dominated by the Qinling Mountains, which serve as a natural boundary separating China’s subtropical and temperate climate zones. Centrally located, the Guanzhong Plain is both fertile and densely populated, having been a cornerstone of Chinese civilization for centuries.

Remote sensing imagery of Shaanxi Province captured via satellite technology

Shaanxi Province boasts a diverse population and a rich cultural heritage from both demographic and economic perspectives. With a legacy stretching back to ancient Chinese dynasties, the region has long been a focal point for political, cultural, and economic activities, encapsulating a rich blend of agricultural and industrial pursuits. With their fertile soils, the Loess Plateau and the Guanzhong Plain support diverse agricultural production, including staple crops like wheat, corn, and millet, and are key contributors to the region’s trade and food security. Additionally, Shaanxi Province is rich in mineral resources, such as coal, petroleum, natural gas, and various metallic and non-metallic minerals, which significantly bolster its industrial sector. In terms of environmental aspects, Shaanxi Province faces numerous challenges. The Loess Plateau’s soil erosion and the Qinling Mountains’ deforestation have resulted in ecological degradation and heightened vulnerability to natural disasters. Furthermore, rapid industrialization and urbanization have placed considerable pressure on water resources, resulting in water scarcity and pollution, which in turn exacerbate the province’s ecological problems.

In summary, Shaanxi Province is a tapestry of geographical diversity interwoven with strands of a rich cultural and historical legacy. As an essential agricultural and economic hub, it benefits from an abundance of natural resources. Yet, the province grapples with urgent environmental challenges that demand immediate attention to safeguard sustainable development and assure the well-being of its populace. The application of RS techniques could be pivotal in monitoring and managing these environmental concerns, ultimately contributing to Shaanxi Province’s long-term sustainability.

Data sets

This study examines atmospheric CO2 column concentrations utilizing data from two advanced satellite missions: OCO-2, operated by NASA, and GOSAT, a project led by JAXA. Launched on July 2, 2014, OCO-2 operates in a sun-synchronous polar orbit at an altitude of 705 km, providing near-global coverage every 16 days. The satellite is renowned for its precision and accuracy in measuring atmospheric CO2 levels, which is crucial for enhancing our understanding of the global carbon cycle and advancing climate change research. OCO-2 boasts a high spatial resolution of 1.29 km × 2.25 km and collects data at a temporal resolution of every 0.333 s. It has demonstrated a single-sounding precision ranging from 0.3 to 1.0 ppm for XCO2, making it exceptionally adept at monitoring local and regional CO2 fluctuations under diverse atmospheric conditions.

Prior to data integration with terrestrial measurements, OCO-2 data undergoes rigorous preprocessing to correct for atmospheric interference and instrumental biases, ensuring the reliability of its CO2 measurements. This preprocessing includes calibration using standard gas measurements and algorithmic adjustments to account for variations in solar radiation and atmospheric pressure.

GOSAT, launched in January 2009, was the first global satellite dedicated to monitoring greenhouse gases (CO2 and CH4). It operates from a sun-synchronous orbit at 666 km, featuring a 1:00 PM local transit time and a 3-day revisit period, enabling high-spectral remote sensing of global XCO2s. GOSAT employs a sophisticated Fourier Transform Spectrometer (FTS) sensor and uses the Level 3 SWIR (Short-Wave Infrared) total column concentration product for detailed analysis. This product, based on the Weighting Function Modified Differential Optical Absorption Spectroscopy (WFM-DOAS) method, has a spatial resolution of 2.5° by 2.5°. Before integration with ground data, GOSAT’s measurements are adjusted for spectral distortions and validated against in-situ CO2 measurements, enhancing the accuracy of its global CO2 monitoring capabilities.

This research employs CO2 data from the WDCGG Hong Kong station as a primary source. The station is strategically situated at 114.2579° E, 22.2095° N. The dataset, covering the period from Nov 2010 to Dec 2022, is employed to validate and refine satellite observations. High-precision analyzers are employed for the accurate measurement of atmospheric CO2 column concentrations. NDIR spectroscopy, known for its distinct infrared absorption peaks, is the selected method for CO2 detection. Regular calibration with specific gas concentrations is conducted to maintain and verify precision. Rigorous quality control procedures are in place to ensure the highest level of data accuracy. The dataset is both reliable and accurate, proving to be essential for climate research and monitoring applications.

Results and discussions

Comparative validation of CO2-CC data

Figure 2 displays a regression analysis comparing ground-based CO2 measurements taken at the Hong Kong station with satellite RS CO2 data from both OCO-2 and GOSAT. The study involved conducting a regression analysis to compare ground-based CO2 measurements with data from OCO-2 and GOSAT satellites. The analysis yielded regression equations for both OCO-2 and GOSAT, each with their respective R-squared (R2) and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) values. For OCO-2, the equation was 0.83313 × WUCGG + 67.09386, yielding an R2 of 0.7786 and an RMSE of 3.6760 ppm. In contrast, GOSAT’s equation was 0.913856 × WUCGG + 34.120874, with an R2 of 0.8573 and an RMSE of 2.7474 ppm. The regression models were statistically significant at the 0.05 level. A robust correlation was observed in the satellite data; however, systematic errors were also noted. The GOSAT data exhibited higher certainty, yet displayed a greater deviation from ground-based measurements. The GOSAT FTS performance demonstrated a higher correlation and lower RMSE compared to other datasets. Based on these findings, the decision to select GOSAT FTS data was made for exploring CO2 dynamics in Shaanxi Province. The regression equations were utilized to modify the GOSAT satellite data accordingly. This modified dataset was subsequently employed to perform spatiotemporal analysis.

Temporal variations of CO2-CC

Initially, data from OCO-2 CO2 and GOSAT CO2-CC were adjusted using the regression equations mentioned in Sect. 3.1, for evaluation purposes. Figure 3 depicts the variability in both monthly and yearly average CO2-CCs for Shaanxi Province spanning 2013 to 2022, revealing that the monthly average CO2-CC exhibits a cyclic pattern characterized by considerable oscillations and an upward trend. Maximum values emerged in each year’s spring, while minimum values were detected at summer’s end and autumn’s onset. The observed annual variation in the monthly average CO2-CCs amounted to about 12.81 ppm. The largest monthly fluctuation, which was 4.45 ppm, occurred between February and March 2021. A significant year-over-year change amounting to 4.86 ppm was noted between Nov 2013 and Nov 2014. The sinusoidal model equation utilized was y = 399.89 + 0.05 × xt + 1.21 × sin (0.55 × xt – 0.97), yielding an R2 of 0.82, and an RMSE of 0.94 ppm. With D = 0.55, approximating 2π/12, this suggests a 12-month cycle is inherent in the data. The identified factors contributing to CO2-CCs cycles include ecosystem, atmospheric dynamics, soil interactions, and anthropogenic emissions [17, 18].

Furthermore, Fig. 3 distinctly illustrates a persistent increasing trend in CO2-CCs over the years. This trend showcased a markedly increasing cycle in CO2-CCs observed over the recent years. The annual average concentration of CO2 escalated from 396.03 ppm in 2013 to 412.33 ppm in 2022. This change represented an overall increase of 16.33 ppm, equivalent to approximately 4.12% over this period, and corresponded to an average annual growth rate of about 0.45%. Spatial distribution of multi-annual seasonal mean CO2 column concentrations across spring, summer, autumn, and winter periods

As shown in Fig. 6, during the spring season, elevated levels of CO2-CC are observed in the northern and eastern parts of Shaanxi Province, contrasting with lower concentrations in the southwestern region. This springtime pattern of CO2-CC is attributable to an increase in soil and plant respiration coupled with a reduction in photosynthesis [19].

CO2-CC undergoes a marked decline from April to August in northern and eastern Shaanxi, with the most significant decrease observed between June and July. This decline is primarily attributed to enhanced vegetation growth, which is driven by optimal levels of sunlight, temperature, and precipitation. During this period, the amount of CO2 absorbed by plants significantly surpasses that released through soil respiration [17]. The increase in plant transpiration and growth during summer plays a crucial role in the reduction of CO2. The period of peak photosynthesis in summer results in a substantial CO2 uptake. The absolute lowest CO2 column concentration was recorded in August. Between June and August, the extensive forest cover and vigorous vegetation growth in the northeastern region played a significant role in CO2 consumption. Additionally, low industrial emissions in this region also contributed to the reduction in CO2 levels. The synergistic cumulative effect of these factors culminated in the lowest CO2-CC level. In April, there was the highest spatial heterogeneity in CO2-CC, evidenced by a variation coefficient of 0.057 [20].

During autumn, there is a notable decrease in plant CO2 absorption, simultaneously accompanied by an increase in soil respiration, resulting in higher CO2 release. XCO2 levels begin to show an upward trend with the onset of autumn. Commencing in September, CO2-CC experiences a gradual rise, characterized by significant regional variability. In May, observed spatial changes in CO2 levels are at their minimal, indicating uniformity. The region displays a relatively uniform CO2 column concentration level across different areas. Human activities and environmental conditions play a significant role in influencing CO2-CC distribution. Throughout winter, plant growth and photosynthesis come to a halt. Low winter temperatures act as a hindrance to plant litter decomposition and soil microbial activity. With the advent of rising temperatures in late March, there is an enhancement in soil microbial activity and CO2 release. Anthropogenic activities are major contributors to the increase in XCO2 and display a negative correlation with temperature. In winter, CO2-CC is observed to be relatively high, accompanied by more pronounced spatial differences. In February, there is a notable high spatial variation in CO2-CC [21].

With the progression of spring, while plant and soil respiration consistently intensify, photosynthesis remains relatively subdued, leading to a continued rise in CO2 column concentration until April. This growth in XCO2 can be attributed to a combination of natural processes and human-induced activities.

To summarize, atmospheric CO2-CC in Shaanxi Province exhibits significant spatiotemporal variations across seasons, influenced by a complex interplay of factors such as vegetation growth, soil respiration, anthropogenic emissions, and environmental conditions. It is crucial to understand and monitor these patterns to develop effective strategies for mitigating climate change and managing CO2 emissions at the regional level.

Spatial characteristics of CO2-CCs

Figure 7 depicts the spatial trend ofCO2-CC, demonstrating higher values in the southern regions compared to the northern regions of the area studied. From 2013 to 2022, the multi-year mean range of CO2-CC varied from 404.0 to 407.5 parts per million (ppm), with standard deviations between 5.9 and 6.8 ppm. In the southern zones, areas of high concentration, a peak of 418.4 ppm was recorded in 2022. Conversely, in the northern zones, characterized by lower concentrations, the peak reached 414.4 ppm in the same year.

In 2022, Ankang had the highest average CO2-CCs, reaching a concentration of 418.4 ppm, while Yulin had the lowest concentrations, at 412.8 ppm. These findings underscored the substantial impact of human emissions on CO2-CCs. In terms of regional seasonal fluctuations, Shangluo registered the highest fluctuations, followed by Southwestern Shaanxi with notable variations, and Yulin showing the least. Data preprocessing and methodology

Data preprocessing

As depicted in Fig. 9, the study involves the thorough preprocessing of GOSAT and OCO-2 data to eliminate missing values and outliers. This process includes the removal of satellite data without corresponding ground station data to ensure consistency. We utilize the three-sigma rule for outlier detection as our chosen statistical method. This process results in the elimination of data points exceeding the three-sigma range. Our strategy aims to ensure a robust dataset by mitigating extreme values and addressing data gaps. Linear regression analysis with ground station data was employed for precision validation and corrections. We performed the selection of data points within Shaanxi Province’s geographic and temporal criteria. The Kriging interpolation method was utilized for the selected data points. The final output of this process was the spatial distribution of CO2 in Shaanxi Province. We also included the application of Kriging interpolation in the final data preprocessing phase. This resulted in the development of a comprehensive CO2 column concentration map for Shaanxi Province.

The research framework is depicted in detail in Fig. 10. RS data were acquired from January 2013 to December 2022, featuring varying spatial resolutions. The heterogeneity in CO2 distribution can be attributed to factors such as inversion parameters, cloud interference, and meteorological conditions. Conclusion

The study employed data from the OCO-2 and GOSAT FTS satellite RS instruments, supplemented by terrestrial data from the WDCGG Hong Kong ground station, for validation and adjustment purposes. The focus of the study was on investigating the spatiotemporal variation characteristics and the influencing factors of CO2 column concentrations in Shaanxi Province over the period from 2013 to 2022. The primary discoveries included:

There was a robust correlation between satellite RS data and ground-based observations, though some systematic error was present. OCO-2 data provided increased reliability but were typically lower than terrestrial measurements. GOSAT L4A data displayed superior performance, with an RMSE of 0.94 ppm, slightly overestimating lower values and underestimating higher ones.

Development and Application of Advanced Modeling Techniques: Employing cutting-edge predictive models, such as machine learning algorithms or ensemble forecasting models, could enhance the accuracy of predictions regarding future CO2 trends. These techniques will facilitate better anticipation of CO2 impacts on local and regional climates, aiding in more robust climate resilience planning.

Comparative Regional Analysis: Conducting cross-regional comparisons with areas that have similar socio-economic developments and environmental challenges would be invaluable. Such studies could validate the universality of the observed patterns in CO2 concentrations and refine global carbon management strategies.

Longitudinal Impact Studies: There is a need for long-term impact assessments of CO2 variability on ecological and human health within the region. Understanding these impacts will be crucial for developing targeted interventions and adaptive strategies.

Policy Effectiveness Analysis: Evaluating the effectiveness of existing environmental policies and their tangible impacts on CO2 emissions will provide critical feedback for policy refinement and development.

By addressing these specific areas, future research will not only deepen our understanding of regional CO2 dynamics but also enhance the formulation of effective strategies to mitigate the adverse impacts of climate change.

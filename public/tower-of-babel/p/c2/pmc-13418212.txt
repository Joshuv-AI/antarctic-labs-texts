RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN OPTICAL COHERENCE TOMOGRAPHY FINDINGS AND OPTIC NERVE HEAD FINDINGS AMONG A NIGERIAN POPULATION: A RETROSPECTIVE STUDY
Authors: TS Obajolowo, SO Adebusoye, IA Yusuf, VA Olatunji, BT Ojibara-Adegboye, AO Ambali
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground:Glaucoma is a leading cause of irreversible blindness worldwide. Early detection remains challenging, particularly in resource-limited settings where disc-based assessment is subjective. Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) provides objective structural measurements such as retinal nerve fiber layer (RNFL) thickness and Bruch's membrane opening (BMO)-minimum rim width (MRW), which may improve diagnostic accuracy.Aim: To evaluate the relationship between OCT-derived parameters and optic nerve head findings among individuals classified as glaucoma suspects or cases using International Society of Geographical and Epidemiological Ophthalmology (ISGEO) criteria in a Nigerian population.Methods :This retrospective study included 310 participants screened during the 2024 World Glaucoma Week at a tertiary hospital. Glaucoma and suspects were defined using ISGEO criteria. OCT imaging (Spectralis SD-OCT) was performed in 55 participants (39.9% of suspects/cases). Global and sectoral RNFL thickness and MRW were analyzed using linear mixed-effects models adjusting for age, sex, and family history.Results:Of the 310 participants, 138 (44.5%) were classified as glaucoma suspects or cases, including 24 (7.7%) with glaucoma. RNFL thickness did not differ significantly between groups (all p≥0.46). In contrast, MRW was significantly thinner in glaucoma cases globally (b = µ26.5 mm; p = 0.043) and in multiple sectors (p < 0.05). Family history was independently associated with glaucoma status (adjusted OR = 1.98; p = 0.031).Conclusion :MRW demonstrated superior discriminatory ability compared to RNFL thickness in differentiating ISGEO-defined glaucoma cases from suspects. These findings support the complementary role of MRW in glaucoma assessment where OCT is available.Keywords: Glaucoma, Optical coherence tomography, Retinal nerve fiber layer, Minimum rim width, ISGEO criteria

INTRODUCTION

Glaucoma is a group of eye diseases that have in common a clinically distinctive optic neuropathy with potentially progressive, clinically visible changes at the optic nerve head (ONH). These changes, including thinning of the neuroretinal rim with excavation, enlargement of the optic cup, and deformation of the lamina cribrosa, manifest as corresponding diffuse or localized nerve-fibre-bundle pattern, with visual field loss.1 Globally, it is the leading cause of irreversible visual loss and the second leading cause of overall vision loss.2 The natural course of glaucoma is an inexorable relentless progression of optic nerve damage and visual field loss towards complete blindness. The disease often progresses slowly and painlessly, making it difficult to notice symptoms until advanced stages.3 Thus, early detection and intervention are crucial to prevent significant vision loss, allowing the institution of vision-preserving therapy, which slows down or arrests the progressive optic nerve damage.

A comprehensive clinical assessment of the optic nerve head and retinal nerve fibre layer by fundoscopy and imaging technologies such as Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) is vital to glaucoma diagnosis and management. In many resource-limited settings, including Nigeria, optic disc evaluation using slit-lamp biomicroscopy continues to be the most accessible and widely utilized method for glaucoma detection. While fundoscopy is valuable, its effectiveness may be limited by the skill of the examiner, its subjectivity, and the subtleties of early glaucomatous changes. Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) has emerged as a valuable adjunctive imaging modality, providing objective and reproducible quantitative measurements of retinal structures, including retinal nerve fiber layer (RNFL) thickness and Bruch's membrane opening–minimum rim width (MRW).4 These parameters have been shown to improve the detection of early glaucomatous damage and allow for more precise structural assessment. Nevertheless, OCT is not universally available in many low- and middle-income countries due to financial, infrastructural, and logistical constraints. In the Nigerian context, access to OCT is often limited,5 and its use in routine clinical practice or population-based screening remains restricted.

The International Society of Geographical and Epidemiological Ophthalmology (ISGEO) criteria were established for the diagnosis of glaucoma from glaucoma screenings, especially when the sophisticated equipment for more advanced testing is unavailable.6 These criteria are based on optic disc appearance, IOP, and visual acuity and are subject to underdiagnoses or overdiagnosis.7

Despite the increasing global use of OCT, there remains a relative paucity of data from Nigerian and West African populations examining the relationship between OCT-derived structural parameters and clinically assessed optic nerve head findings. Existing regional studies have largely focused on the epidemiology of glaucoma and disc-based diagnostic criteria, with fewer studies exploring the integration of OCT metrics such as MRW and RNFL thickness into clinical decision-making in this environment.

Importantly, in real-world practice within this setting, while optic disc assessment is routinely performed, access to OCT is often limited by cost, as reflected in the present study where only a subset of eligible participants underwent OCT imaging. This disparity underscores the need to better understand how OCT-derived parameters relate to conventional clinical findings, particularly in contexts where OCT cannot be universally applied. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the relationship between OCT-derived parameters-RNFL thickness and MRW-and optic nerve head findings among individuals classified as glaucoma suspects or cases using ISGEO criteria in a Nigerian population.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This was a retrospective review of the data collected from the screening activities carried out during the 2024 World Glaucoma week (WGW) screening event at the University of Ilorin Teaching Hospital (UITH), Ilorin, Kwara State. UITH is a tertiary referral centre located in North-Central Nigeria and serves patients from Kwara state and neighbouring states. The WGW screening programme was organised by the Department of Ophthalmology as part of efforts to increase awareness and facilitate early detection of glaucoma in the community.

A retrospective design was considered appropriate for this study as the objective was to evaluate the relationship between OCT-derived structural parameters and clinically assessed optic nerve head findings, rather than to establish causality. The use of existing screening data allowed for the assessment of real-world clinical correlations between disc-based classification (ISGEO criteria) and OCT measurements within the constraints of routine practice in a resource-limited setting.

Study population:

The study population comprised individuals who participated in the 2024 WGW screening programme at the UITH.

Inclusion criteria:

Participant aged 18 years and above, participants diagnosed as glaucoma or glaucoma suspects based on ISGEO criteria, Participants who underwent spectral domain OCT.

Exclusion criteria:

participants with incomplete clinical records, eyes with media opacity precluding adequate optic disc evaluation or OCT imaging, eyes with other optic neuropathies or retinal diseases that could affect RNFL measurements.

Data collection

Data of participants were retrieved retrospectively from records generated during the 2024 WGW screening exercise. The data included information from questionnaires completed by participants, findings from clinical ophthalmic examination, and OCT results obtained for participants identified as having glaucoma or glaucoma suspects during the screening.

Clinical ocular assessments included visual acuity, while IOP was measured using the I-care tonometer (Model TA01i). Anterior segment examination was done on a slit lamp and undilated slit lamp biomicroscopy of the optic nerve head was done using a +90D lens to assess the vertical cup-to-disc ratio (VCDR) and other optic nerve head features.

Glaucoma cases and suspects were diagnosed using the International Society of Geographical and Epidemiological Ophthalmology (ISGEO) criteria.8,9 Glaucoma suspects were identified based on optic disc findings including a VCDR between the 97.5th and 99.5th percentiles for the normal population (CDR 0.7 to <0.85), or VCDR asymmetry >0.2. Additional indicators included neuroretinal rim width <0.1 of the disc diameter in the superior or inferior regions, optic disc hemorrhage or IOP above the 97.5th percentile (>21mmHg), and no peripheral anterior synechiae.

Glaucoma was defined by a CDR exceeding the 99.5th percentile, provided no alternative explanation for the findings (e.g., dysplastic disc or anisometropia) was present.

OCT imaging was performed after an undilated clinical optic nerve head assessment by slit lamp microscopy using a +90D lens. Physiologic cupping was ruled out based on neuroretinal rim configuration, presence of rim thinning/ notch, disc haemorrhage, and violation of the ISNT rule. OCT parameters including RNFL thickness and BMO-MRW were subsequently used to support structural assessment of the optic nerve head.

Spectralis (Heidelberg Engineering, Heidelberg, Germany) spectral domain-optical coherence tomography was performed through undilated pupils to assess peripapillary retinal nerve fibre layer (RNFL) thickness and minimum rim width (MRW).

Ethical considerations

Ethical approval for this study was obtained from the hospital's Ethics and Research Committee (ERC/PAN/2023/12/0456). Informed consent was waived due to the study's retrospective design.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were summarized using means ± SD or medians (IQR), and categorical variables using frequencies and proportions. Group differences in continuous variables were assessed using the Mann–Whitney U test, from which we also derived the associated probabilistic index (Mann–Whitney–based odds ratio) to quantify effect size. Categorical comparisons used χ² or Fisher's exact tests.

Because each participant contributed one or two eyes, all per-eye OCT parameters (global and sectoral RNFL and BMO-MRW) were analysed using linear mixed-effects models with a random intercept for participant ID. Fixed effects included glaucoma category (case vs suspect), family history, age, and sex, as appropriate.

Associations between family history and classification as glaucoma suspect/case were evaluated using multivariable logistic regression at the participant level. Odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals were reported. A two-sided p-value <0.05 defined statistical significance. The data was analysed using the IBM Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) software version 26 (IBM Corp.: Armonk, NY, USA) and R version 4.4.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

RESULTS

A total of 310 participants contributed 620 eyes to the analysis. The participant sample was predominantly young, with a median age of 23.0 years (IQR 21.0–25.0; range 18–78), and 88.9% (n = 277) were younger than 40 years. Females constituted 57.4% of the sample, yielding a male-to-female ratio of 1:1.3. Most examined eyes (93.1%, n = 577) had a best-corrected distance visual acuity of 6/12 or better. Mean intraocular pressure (IOP) was 16.1 ± 4.1 mmHg. Forty-nine participants (15.8%) reported a positive first-degree family history of glaucoma.

Prevalence of glaucoma and suspect classifications

Using ISGEO criteria, 138 participants (44.5%; 95% CI 39.1–50.1) were classified as glaucoma suspects or glaucoma cases. Twenty-four individuals (7.7%; 95% CI 5.3–11.3) met criteria for glaucoma, while 114 (36.8%; 95% CI 31.6–42.3) were identified as glaucoma suspects. There were 98 disc suspects (31.6% of patients) who met enlarged cup/disc ratio criteria, 27 (8.7%) had intraocular pressure at or above the 97.5th percentile, and 11 (3.5%) met both disc and pressure criteria. Because these categories overlap, their counts are not mutually exclusive.

Age differed significantly between groups. Participants classified as suspects/cases were slightly older than those classified as normal (median 24.0 [22.0–25.0] vs 23.0 [21.0–25.0] years; U = 13,427.0; p = 0.022). The probabilistic index suggested that a randomly selected suspect/case had 1.36-fold higher odds of being older than a randomly selected normal individual (95% CI 1.05–1.78). No robust sex differences were observed (OR for normal status in females vs males = 1.47; 95% CI 0.93–2.32).

OCT subsample: participation, quality, and representativeness

Of the 138 individuals classified as glaucoma or glaucoma suspect, 55 (39.9%) underwent OCT imaging. The primary reason for non-attendance was financial limitation. Those who obtained OCT were comparable to those who did not in age, sex, IOP, and family history (all p > 0.477). Image quality was uniformly high, with a mean signal strength of 34.6 ± 3.0 (range 25–39) in the right eye and 33.4 ± 3.3 (range 24–39) in the left. Only one eye was excluded due to media opacity.

Within this OCT subgroup, the median age was 23.5 years (IQR 21.0–24.3), and 50% were female. Ten individuals (17.2%) reported a family history of glaucoma, with low frequencies of diabetes (1.7%) and hypertension (5.2%).

Structural OCT profiles in the cohort

Right-eye global RNFL thickness averaged 113.2 ± 10.6 µm (range 84–138 µm), while the mean global MRW was 318.5 ± 44.7 µm (range 212.4–447.5 µm). RNFL demonstrated the characteristic double-hump pattern: the temporal sector was the thinnest (mean 73.9 ± 12.2 µm in the right eye; 72.1 ± 12.0 µm in the left), whereas the inferotemporal sector was the thickest (164.7 ± 20.2 µm and 166.8 ± 22.2 µm, respectively). MRW demonstrated a similar configuration, with the temporal rim representing the thinnest sector (215.5 ± 39.8 µm and 215.9 ± 45.4 µm in the right and left eyes). The superonasal and inferotemporal regions corresponded to maximal thickness in both eyes.

Comparative diagnostic performance of MRW vs RNFL

In multivariable linear mixed-effects models adjusting for age, sex, and family history, global and sectoral RNFL thickness did not differ meaningfully between glaucoma cases and glaucoma suspects (all p ≥ 0.46). In contrast, several MRW sectors were significantly thinner in glaucoma cases (Table 1, Figure 1), most notably global MRW β (regression coefficient) = –26.5 µm; 95% CI –52.1 to –1.0; p = 0.043), the superior temporal rim (β = –28.6 µm; –54.8 to –2.3; p = 0.033), the temporal rim (β = –22.8 µm; –45.1 to –1.0; p = 0.044), the inferior temporal rim (β = –33.2 µm; –61.0 to –5.4; p = 0.020) and the inferior nasal rim (β = –35.2 µm; –69.9 to –0.6; p = 0.046). The remaining MRW sectors demonstrated numerically lower thickness values in glaucoma cases compared with controls; however, these differences did not attain statistical significance (all p-values > 0.173).

Family history–related structural vulnerability

Participants without a known family history of glaucoma demonstrated significantly greater RNFL thickness in select sectors compared with those with a family history. After adjusting for age and gender, participants without a known family history exhibited significantly thicker RNFL in the temporal (β = 8.3 µm, 95% CI 2.9–13.6; p = 0.004) and superotemporal sectors (β = 13.4 µm, 95% CI 1.7–26.9; p = 0.047). Differences in inferotemporal, superonasal, inferonasal, nasal and global RNFL thickness were smaller and nonsignificant (p ≥ 0.227).

Across all MRW sectors evaluated, superior, inferior, temporal–superior, and temporal, family history of glaucoma was not significantly associated with rim width after adjusting for age and gender. Participants without a known family history consistently showed larger MRW values than those with a family history, but none reached statistical significance (all p-values ≥ 0.087).

Participants with a positive family history were also more likely to be classified as glaucoma suspects or glaucoma cases. In multivariable logistic regression controlling for age, family history remained an independent predictor (adjusted OR = 1.98; 95% CI 1.07–3.69; p = 0.031).

DISCUSSION

In this study, the absence of a statistically significant difference in retinal nerve fibre layer (RNFL) thickness between study-defined glaucoma suspects, classified based on vertical cup-to-disc ratios (CDR) within the 97.5th to 99.5th percentiles, and cases meeting the International Society of Geographical and Epidemiological Ophthalmology (ISGEO) category 2 criteria (>99.5th percentile CDR) raises important considerations regarding the adequacy of disc-based structural classification alone in glaucoma diagnosis. While vertical CDR remains a widely used surrogate for glaucomatous optic neuropathy, the reliance on optic disc morphology in the absence of confirmatory visual field testing may not adequately reflect the presence or severity of neural tissue loss as reported in other studies. This observation is consistent with findings by McCalla et al., who demonstrated that reliance on cup-to-disc ratio alone may inadequately capture both structural and functional glaucomatous damage, particularly in early disease stages where structural loss may not correspond directly with disc appearance.10 Taken together, our findings demonstrate a differential relationship between OCT-derived parameters and optic nerve head findings, with MRW showing a significant association with ISGEO-defined glaucoma status, while RNFL thickness did not demonstrate a meaningful discriminatory relationship in this cohort.

A plausible explanation for this lack of RNFL differentiation lies in the inherent heterogeneity within the ISGEO-defined glaucoma group. ISGEO category 2 criteria, which rely on extreme structural parameters in the absence of visual field data, may capture individuals with very early glaucomatous changes who have not yet developed appreciable RNFL thinning. Conversely, this category may inadvertently include true suspects, individuals with large physiological cups, but without actual glaucomatous neurodegeneration. In such cases, the structural changes required for classification are present by population percentile standards but may not correspond to true pathological loss. Hood and Kardon11 have highlighted that structural damage may precede functional loss in some patients, yet not all cases follow this trajectory, especially in cross-sectional evaluations.

Simultaneously, a proportion of individuals categorized as disc suspects based on intermediate CDR values may in fact harbour early glaucomatous damage. For instance, the same CDR value may imply differing levels of rim and RNFL loss depending on the optic disc size; a CDR of 0.8 in a small disc portends more significant tissue loss than in a larger disc. Without accounting for disc size, study-defined suspects may thus include early glaucoma cases that would have demonstrated visual field defects had standard automated perimetry been performed. The limitations of morphologic criteria in isolation are particularly salient in this context, as they risk misclassification in both directions-overestimating disease in some while underestimating it in others. This is further supported by Nwokocha and Ejimadu, who reported significant variability in vertical cup-to-disc ratios among glaucoma suspects in a black population,12 emphasizing the limitations of relying solely on disc morphology in populations with wide physiological variation.

These findings underscore the critical need for functional testing, or validated structural surrogates, in distinguishing true glaucoma from disc suspects. While visual field testing remains the gold standard for functional assessment, surrogate metrics derived from optical coherence tomography (OCT), such as ganglion cell complex thickness and minimum rim width (MRW), have emerged as promising tools, especially in settings where perimetry is not feasible. Yet, despite its strengths, OCT has limitations. Structural imaging may detect pre-perimetric glaucoma in many cases13,14, but its sensitivity and specificity are imperfect, particularly in eyes with high myopia, anomalous disc anatomy, or borderline RNFL values. Moreover, segmentation errors, variations in signal strength, and normative database limitations, particularly in African populations, diminish its reliability as a standalone diagnostic tool.

Previous studies have also cautioned against the sole reliance on optic disc assessment for glaucoma screening or diagnosis. A systematic review by Jonas et al.15 emphasized that while expert disc evaluation demonstrates high specificity, its sensitivity is moderate and substantially operator-dependent. Interobserver variability, the confounding effect of disc size, and the lack of standardized training further impair its utility in mass screening programs or in generalist care settings. In clinical practice, subjective assessment of the neuroretinal rim is thus best interpreted in conjunction with objective structural and functional metrics.

Despite these limitations, optic disc assessment remains clinically valuable when interpreted alongside objective imaging findings. Studies have demonstrated a good correlation between clinically observed rim thinning and OCT-derived RNFL thickness or MRW.16,17 Among these metrics, MRW, measured at the Bruch's membrane opening, has been shown to provide superior anatomical representation of neuroretinal rim tissue and stronger correlation with functional loss than vertical CDR. This aligns with findings from Khan et al.,18 who reported that MRW demonstrated superior diagnostic accuracy compared to RNFL thickness in distinguishing glaucomatous eyes from non-glaucomatous eyes, reinforcing the potential utility of MRW as a more sensitive structural biomarker. Such integrated approaches provide a more comprehensive view of the optic nerve head and its damage profile. However, the generalizability of these findings in low-resource settings may be constrained by the limited accessibility and affordability of OCT, as reflected in this study where only a subset of eligible participants underwent imaging, underscoring the need to interpret OCT-based conclusions within the context of local resource availability.

Importantly, OCT-derived RNFL thickness has been shown to correlate with glaucoma severity, including in early disease stages. In addition, macular parameters such as ganglion cell–inner plexiform layer (GCIPL) and ganglion cell complex (GCC) thickness provide complementary information by detecting central retinal ganglion cell loss that may occur early in glaucomatous damage.19 However, OCT measurements are also subject to a “floor effect” in advanced glaucoma, where further structural decline cannot be reliably detected despite ongoing functional deterioration.

Study limitations

Cross-sectional design makes it difficult to determine the sequence of events, particularly whether structural changes precede or follow glaucoma progression. In addition, visual field testing was not performed in all participants, limiting the ability to confirm functional impairment and leading to greater reliance on structural criteria for glaucoma classification. OCT was also only carried out in individuals identified as glaucoma suspects or confirmed cases, which may have introduced some degree of selection bias. Another source of potential selection bias may be due to financial constraints affecting OCT uptake among glaucoma suspects/cases. Furthermore, optic disc size was not formally measured, and this could have influenced the interpretation of cup-to-disc ratios, potentially contributing to misclassification. Finally, as the study was conducted within a single tertiary screening program, the findings may not be entirely generalized to other populations or community-based settings.

Recommendation

A prospective study with funding for OCT investigation made available to all study participants/further studies is recommended. This would be of more value to the body of knowledge concerning this topic.

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, MRW demonstrated superior discriminatory ability compared to RNFL thickness in differentiating ISGEO-defined glaucoma cases from suspects in this population.

Low-Temperature Deposition and Material Characterization of Hydrogenated Graphene Oxide Thin Films on Copper Substrates
Authors: Samit Karmakar, Gouranga Sundar Taki, Soumik Kumar Kundu, Shankha Mukherjee, Satyaranjan Bhattacharyya
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Graphene-derived films such as graphene oxide (GO) and hydrogenated graphene oxide (HGO) are of interest for energy storage, catalysis, sensing applications, and as low-thermal-budget carbon films; here, we focus on their low-temperature synthesis and material characterization. However, the in-use chemical vapor deposition (CVD) process for HGO requires high-temperature processing (above 1000 °C or 1273 K), which is unsuitable for back-end-of-line (BEOL) Complementary Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor (CMOS) process integration. In this work, we report on an efficient low-temperature (773 K) method and detailed material characterization of synthesizing HGO on a copper substrate using a 2.45 GHz Electron Cyclotron Resonance (ECR) Plasma Enhanced Chemical Vapor Deposition (PECVD) system. At first, we employ a Quadrupole-based Residual Gas Analyzer (QRGA) for analysis of active chemical species during the gaseous-phase reaction at 300 K, 573 K, and 773 K to optimize growth conditions. The findings indicate that at 773 K, the presence of ECR plasma in conjunction with methane as the precursor gas is consistent with the formation of hydrogenated, oxygen-containing graphitic films. Raman spectroscopy further reveals characteristic D, G, and 2D bands at approximately 1343, 1577, and 2843 cm−1, respectively. The full survey scan of X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) shows prominent signals for C 1s and O 1s core levels, where the deconvolution of the C 1s spectra indicates CC (284.2 eV), sp3-type carbon (285.3 eV), C–O (286.2 eV), and CO (287.5 eV) bonding states, while the O 1s spectra confirm CO (530.8 eV) and C–O (532.6 eV) bonds. Finally, we investigate the surface morphology using scanning electron microscopy and Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM) imaging.

Introduction

Graphene, a monolayer of carbon atoms arranged in a honeycomb lattice, has received significant attention since its discovery in 2004 due to its exceptional mechanical strength, high thermal conductivity, and outstanding electrical properties. Pristine graphene exhibits a near-zero bandgap, which has motivated sustained efforts to tune its electronic structure. − Among the derivatives explored, Graphene Oxide (GO) , and Hydrogenated Graphene Oxide (HGO) − are of particular interest, as their oxygen functionalities and modified carbon hybridization provide tunable surface chemistry and properties relevant to applications such as energy storage, catalysis, and sensing. However, the controlled deposition of HGO at low processing temperatures, together with a detailed understanding of the resulting material, remains comparatively underexploredwhich motivates the present synthesis-and-characterization study.

In HGO, hydrogen atoms interact with both the oxygen functional groups and the carbon lattice, inducing a shift in carbon hybridization from sp2 to sp3 and thereby significantly modulating its electronic, magnetic, and optical characteristics. The presence of residual oxygen groups, combined with tunable surface chemistry and enhanced chemical reactivity, makes HGO particularly suitable for energy storage systems, , where high surface area and optimized conductivity are critical. Additionally, the biocompatibility, surface functionalization potential, and selective binding capabilities of HGO make it an excellent candidate for biosensor applications, , enabling sensitive and specific detection of biomolecules.

Among the various synthesis methods, earlier research has employed the chemical vapor deposition (CVD) technique due to its ability to produce large-area uniform HGO films, ideal for scalable large-area film growth. − A suitable catalytic substrate, such as copper (Cu), − has been used to obtain a (nearly) defect-free structure of HGO. However, in the case of HGO deposition on Cu, the operating temperature of the CVD process exceeds 1273 K. However, such a high temperature poses significant integration challenges in standard CMOS technology, particularly with BEOL processes.

In this work, we report the low-temperature (773 K) synthesis and detailed characterization of an HGO film on an optimized catalytic Cu substrate, using an indigenously developed 2.45 GHz Electron Cyclotron Resonance (ECR) Plasma-Enhanced Chemical Vapor Deposition (PECVD) system. , The 773 K process is compatible with low-thermal-budget (near-BEOL) constraints. Growth conditions were first optimized using a Quadrupole Residual Gas Analyzer (QRGA), which resolves the active gas-phase species and byproducts over a range of deposition temperatures; − the film was then characterized by Raman spectroscopy (D, G, 2D bands), XPS (bonding states), and SEM and AFM (morphology). Relative to our earlier large-area graphene and single-step hydrogenated-graphene reports, the specific contribution here is the combination of single-step, low-temperature ECR PECVD growth on Cu with in situ QRGA resolution of the plasma-specific reactive species (H3 +, CH x , C2H5 +) governing growth, correlated with postgrowth characterization of the resulting oxygen-containing, sp3-bearing graphitic film.

Residual Gas Analysis of Gas-Phase Species during HGO Deposition

The residual gas analysis was conducted to quantitatively assess the evolution of methane-derived fragments, hydrogen species, and background gases under varying thermal and plasma conditions (300, 573, and 773 K) within the ECR PECVD environment. Comparative evaluation of these spectra elucidates how substrate heating and plasma activation systematically modify the reactive gas-phase chemistry, thereby establishing the conditions conducive to controlled low-temperature synthesis of hydrogenated graphene oxide on copper substrates. The RGA mass spectra under different experimental conditions are mentioned here in detail.

To separate the intended CH4/plasma chemistry from chamber-background effects, Figures − are presented as a control series corresponding to the background (773 K), CH4 admitted with the plasma off, and CH4 admitted with the plasma on, respectively. The plasma-specific species H3 + and C2H5 + emerge only upon plasma ignition, confirming that the growth chemistry is plasma-driven. Moreover, bare Cu foil treated under identical ECR plasma and thermal conditions without CH4 has been characterized in our earlier work, − showing only microstructural modification of the Cu surface with no graphitic carbon, which indicates that the carbon film reported here is CH4-precursor-dependent. We acknowledge that, at the working base pressure of 3.3–3.5 × 10−2 mbar and without a foreline trap, residual H2O, oxygen, and hydrocarbon backstreaming cannot be fully excluded and may contribute to the oxygen incorporated in the film; a dedicated same-batch CH4-free blank-Cu control characterized by Raman/XPS was not performed.

The plasma-on condition (Figure ) is used for the growth of hydrogenated, oxygen-containing graphitic (HGO) films. The mass spectra show a substantial amount of hydrogen in the system, while mass peak 12 indicates the presence of carbontogether providing the carbon- and hydrogen-bearing gas-phase precursors required for film growth. These RGA data characterize the gas phase and indicate a hydrogen-rich growth environment but do not by themselves establish hydrogen incorporation into the solid film. Mass peak 3 (H3 +) may be attributed to a hydrogen molecular ion combined with an additional hydrogen atom; H3 + is known to occur under plasma conditions as a product of ion−molecule collisions. , Here, CH4 undergoes electron-impact and dissociative ionization during the plasma-on process, and the resulting CH3 + ions react with CH4 to form C2H5 + (CH3 + + CH4 → C2H5 + + H2). −

Sample Preparation

To prepare the sample, commercially available cold-rolled polycrystalline copper foils, 80 μm thick (Grade: BS 2870 C102), were used as a catalytic substrate. These copper foils were precleaned with acetone and positioned on a heater assembly, within the ECR PECVD system. The base pressure of the ECR PECVD system was kept at a vacuum better than 3 × 10−2 mbar. The sample of HGO was developed in the presence of CH4 gas at 773 K under the influence of plasma (refer to Section ) for 1 min. Further, argon (Ar) gas (purity: 99.999%) was injected into the PECVD chamber until cooling down of the sample and to keep it oxide-free. The samples were then stored in a vacuum desiccator to prevent them from further oxidation and contamination. The complete set of process parameters is summarized in Table to enable reproduction of the synthesis. The growth was repeated at least three times under identical conditions, producing visually consistent, continuous films; detailed quantitative run-to-run statistics were not systematically recorded in this study.

Material Characterization and Result Discussion

Multiple HGO films were synthesized in separate runs under identical optimized conditions to verify repeatability. For the characterization data presented here, smaller specimens were cut from a single 25 mm × 25 mm synthesized copper foil, and Raman spectroscopy, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM) analyses, and Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) imaging were carried out on these sections so that all data sets correspond to the same growth batch.

Raman Spectroscopy Analysis

Raman spectroscopic analysis was carried out using a Laser Raman Spectrometer (Lab RAM HR, HORIBA; He–Ne laser, 17 mW, 632.8 nm); the spectrum of the PECVD-grown film is shown in Figure . After polynomial baseline correction, the spectrum was fitted with Lorentzian line shapes, yielding three bands at approximately 1343, 1577, and 2843 cm−1, confirming the D, G, and 2D bands, with an FWHM of approximately 140, 70, and 105 cm−1, respectively. The G band corresponds to the E2g phonon at the Brillouin-zone center, while the D and 2D bands arise from double-resonant Raman scattering. The I 2D/I G ratio reflects the number of layers, and the I D/I G ratio reflects the level of disorder: I 2D/I G ≈ 0.537 indicates a multilayer film, , whereas I D/I G ≈ 1.190 indicates a substantial defect density. , These defects are attributed to the abundant hydrogen radicals and oxygen present during growth, as sp2 carbon is readily attacked by hydrogen radicals to form sp3 structures. ,,,

Raman spectra of the PECVD-grown HGO thin film on catalytic copper foil confirm the formation of a graphitic structure with the characteristic D, G, and 2D peaks.

The reported band positions and FWHM are obtained from a least-squares Lorentzian fit to a single representative spectrum; the quoted values therefore carry the corresponding fit uncertainties, while replicate- and mapping-based statistical uncertainties were not assessed, as Raman mapping and replicate-position measurements were not acquired in this study. We further note that Raman spectroscopy alone cannot uniquely distinguish a hydrogenated film from defective or partially oxidized graphitic carbonit establishes a defective, multilayer graphitic structure. The material assignment in this work therefore rests on the combination of Raman (disorder and multilayer character), XPS (sp3 carbon and C–O/CO functionalities) and the in situ plasma chemistry.

X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy Analysis

XPS was conducted using an electron spectroscopy system (Model TA10, twin-anode Al/Mg X-ray gun, VSW Ltd., UK) at a base pressure of 6.8–7.9 × 10−9 mbar. Sputter etching was avoided to prevent surface damage. Photoelectrons were detected with a 150 mm hemispherical analyzer over a 10 mm area at a 45° X-ray incidence and a 0° takeoff angle, with no charge neutralizer; monochromatic Al Kα radiation (hν = 1486.6 eV) was used. The binding-energy scale was calibrated against gold, and the valence-band spectrum showed the Fermi edge at 0 eV, confirming good electrical contact.

A full survey scan (0−1000 eV, 1 eV resolution) and high-resolution C 1s and O 1s scans (0.2 eV) were acquired (Figure ). Because no charge neutralizer was used, the as-acquired C 1s and O 1s peaks appeared at 291.65 and 539.01 eV owing to sample charging. Using the work-function referencing method of Greczynski and Hultman, E B = (289.58 − ϕSA) with ϕSA ≈ 4.95 eV for polycrystalline Cu, − the corrected C 1s position is 284.63 eVa rigid shift of 7.02 eV applied identically to the O 1s region, making the referencing reproducible and traceable.

Full survey XPS spectrum of the PECVD-grown HGO sample, showing prominent C 1s and O 1s core-level signals and confirming the elemental composition and surface chemical states present in the film.

Peak fitting used a Shirley background and Gaussian–Lorentzian line shapes. The high-resolution C 1s spectrum (Figure a) deconvolutes into sp2 CC (284.2 eV), sp3-type/defect-associated carbon (285.3 eV), C–O (286.2 eV), and CO (287.5 eV), with an FWHM of ≈2.0, 1.2, 1.7, and 2.4 eV. As XPS is inherently insensitive to hydrogen, C–H bonding cannot be observed directly; the sp3 component is consistent with hydrogenated carbon, inferred from the hydrogen-rich gas-phase chemistry identified by QRGA (refer to Section ) rather than directly measured. The O 1s spectrum (Figure b) deconvolutes into CO (530.8 eV) and C–O (532.6 eV), with an FWHM of ≈2.2 and 3.0 eV.

High-resolution XPS spectra of the PECVD-grown HGO sample showing: (a) deconvoluted C 1s peak showing sp2 CC (284.2 eV), sp3-type/defect-associated carbon (285.3 eV), C–O (286.2 eV), and CO (287.5 eV) environments; (b) deconvoluted O 1s peak showing CO (530.8 eV) and C–O (532.6 eV).

Using Scofield sensitivity factors (C 1s 0.296, O 1s 0.711), the C 1s fractions are CC 77%, sp3 5%, C–O 6%, and CO 12% [sp3/(sp2 + sp3) ≈ 5%, indicating the degree of graphitization], and the O 1s fractions are C–O 83% and CO 17%. The RSF-corrected areas give an overall composition of ≈72 at % C and 28 at % O (O/C ≈ 0.39). This oxygen content is consistent with the trace oxygen detected by RGA under the rough vacuum during growth (refer to Figure and Section ). ,−

Atomic Force Microscopy Analysis

Atomic force microscopy (AFM; Bruker INNOVA) was used to analyze the surface morphology and thickness of the film (Figure a). Scans were performed in tapping mode at a scan rate of approximately 3 Hz and 256 × 256 pixel resolution, over a scan field of a few tens of micrometers (consistent with the 10 μm scale bar in Figure a). The film adhered uniformly to the Si/SiO2 surface, with some folded regions and scratches typical of the transfer process. Thickness was determined from the height profile (Figure b): the two steps resolved in the profile (≈45 and 39 nm) give a mean step height of 42 ± 4 nm (n = 2 steps, single profile), consistent with the previously reported value of ∼40 nm; a full multilocation, multisample thickness distribution was not acquired in this study. This tens-of-nanometers thickness corresponds to a multilayer film, in agreement with the multilayer character indicated by the Raman I 2D/I G ratio, so the present material is a multilayer, hydrogenated, oxygen-containing graphitic film rather than a monolayer graphene-like sheet.

(a) AFM topography image of the PECVD-grown hydrogenated graphene oxide (HGO) thin film transferred onto a Si/SiO2 substrate, showing a uniformly adhered surface with minor folds and scratches characteristic of the transfer process; (b) corresponding height profile indicating an average film thickness of approximately 40 nm.

Scanning Electron Microscopy Results

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM; W-filament source, 200 V−30 kV, 100 μA beam current) was used to examine the film before and after transfer (Figure ). The as-grown film on Cu was imaged at ≈123,878× (500 nm scale bar; Figure a) and the transferred film on Si/SiO2 at ≈66,854× (1 μm scale bar; Figure b). The as-grown film shows a continuous, compact morphology, indicating uniform nucleation. After FeCl3-based Cu etching and water-assisted transfer (Type-II deionized water), the film largely retains its continuity on Si/SiO2, with minor wrinkles and folds arising from capillary forces during the wet transfer and no major tears or discontinuities in these frames. The micrographs in Figure are representative of the as-grown and transferred films and confirm continuity over the regions imaged; a quantitative, multilocation assessment of large-area uniformity was beyond the scope of this study.

Conclusion

In this article, a low-temperature synthesis methodology for an HGO film has been presented in detail. Residual gas analyzer (RGA) was employed to monitor the evolution of reactive species and identify conditions that promote a hydrogen-rich growth environment within the PECVD system. Although PECVD processing may introduce minor surface damage, elevated temperature conditions significantly reduce oxygen-containing species, thereby minimizing surface oxidation of the catalytic copper substrate. At 773 K, the high residual hydrogen content in the presence of ECR plasma and the methane precursor indicates an environment favorable to hydrogenation; hydrogen incorporation into the film is, however, inferred from this convergent indirect evidence. We note that XPS is inherently insensitive to hydrogen; the hydrogenated character of the film is therefore inferred from the emergence of the sp3 carbon component, the elevated I D/I G ratio, and the atomic hydrogen/CH x radical chemistry evidenced by in situ RGA, in line with established CH4-plasma hydrogenation mechanisms. Direct quantification of hydrogen content (e.g., by FTIR, ToF-SIMS, or ERDA/NRA) was beyond the analytical facilities available for this study and is a focus of ongoing work.

The synthesized HGO sample was subsequently characterized using Raman spectroscopy, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM), and scanning electron microscopy (SEM). Raman spectroscopy confirms the formation of a graphitic structure through the characteristic D (∼1343 cm−1), G (∼1577 cm−1), and 2D (∼2843 cm−1) bands. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) showed prominent C 1s and O 1s spectra, with deconvolution confirming CC (284.2 eV), sp3-type carbon (285.3 eV), C–O (286.2 eV), and CO (287.5 eV) bonds. The O 1s spectrum indicated CO (530.8 eV) and C–O (532.6 eV) bonds. AFM provided insights into the surface morphology and thickness of the film, while SEM was used to examine its continuity and structural integrity both before and after transfer.

Therefore, this work demonstrates a pathway for the single-step synthesis of HGO at lower temperatures using a cost-effective PECVD setup. Furthermore, the integration of in situ RGA with detailed material characterization provides an improved understanding of the growth dynamics of HGO with respect to precursor decomposition, plasma-induced reactions, and the resulting chemical and structural evolution of the film. Such an understanding may facilitate the development of HGO for diverse applications, including catalysis, hydrogen storage, and biosensing.

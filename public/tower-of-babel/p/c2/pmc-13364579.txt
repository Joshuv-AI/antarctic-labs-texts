Case report: Beyond arrhythmia: STAR-inspired single-fraction cardiac radioablation for Ewing sarcoma metastasis
Authors: Erika Galietta, Silvia Strolin, Lidia Strigari, Francesco Cellini, Savino Cilla, Leonardo Santarelli, Vincenzo Russo, Alessandra Arcelli, Cristian Martignani, Costanza Maria Donati, Alessio G. Morganti, Silvia Cammelli
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionStereotactic arrhythmia radioablation (STAR) has emerged as an effective non-invasive treatment for refractory ventricular arrhythmias, but its ablative single-fraction regimen may also be relevant beyond electrophysiologic indications. We report the application of a STAR-inspired 25 Gy single-fraction cardiac radioablation approach for right-ventricular metastasis from Ewing sarcoma, presented as a CARE-compliant case report.Case presentationA 26-year-old man with a history of limb Ewing sarcoma developed an incidentally detected right-ventricular mass during routine staging. After partial surgical debulking, multimodal imaging confirmed a 17 × 15 mm residual intracavitary lesion. Because complete surgical resection was not technically feasible, frameless stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) was delivered in a single 25 Gy fraction using STAR workflow principles. Treatment was completed without complication. At 12-month MRI the lesion was reduced (8 × 10 mm), and subsequent imaging confirmed persistent local cardiac control. At 31 months after STAR, the patient remained free of cardiac symptoms and signs of cardiac disease progression.ConclusionAdapting the STAR single-fraction regimen for oncologic cardiac targets is feasible and provided durable control with no observed cardiac toxicity. This experience supports further evaluation of STAR-inspired SBRT for unresectable cardiac tumors.

Introduction

Primary and secondary cardiac tumors are rare: autopsy studies estimate an incidence of 0.002-0.3% for primary lesions and up to 18% for metastatic involvement (1, 2). Surgery remains the treatment of choice when technically feasible, but many patients are unsuitable because of tumor location, comorbidities, or systemic disease burden.

Stereotactic arrhythmia radioablation (STAR) has recently expanded the therapeutic landscape for patients with refractory ventricular tachycardia by demonstrating that a single 25 Gy fraction can be delivered safely and effectively to a highly mobile intracardiac target (3–5). Although this strategy was developed for arrhythmic substrates rather than cancer, the technical and radiobiological principles underlying STAR are potentially attractive for selected oncologic scenarios, particularly when a cardiac lesion cannot be completely resected and conventional local options are limited.

In this context, applying a STAR-inspired ablative regimen to a malignant cardiac target may represent a meaningful extension of the paradigm beyond its standard indication. We therefore present a CAse REport Guidelines (CARE)-compliant (6) case report of right-ventricular (RV) metastasis from Ewing sarcoma treated with single-fraction cardiac radioablation using a STAR-derived approach. Patient information

A previously healthy 26-year-old man first noticed a painful swelling in his left thigh in October 2021. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) performed in March 2022 revealed a 12.5 × 6 cm mass within the biceps femoris; core biopsy confirmed Ewing sarcoma with EWSR1 (Ewing sarcoma breakpoint region 1) rearrangement, the canonical molecular hallmark of these tumors, and diffuse CD99 (Cluster of differentiation 99) positivity, a typical immunohistochemical feature supporting the diagnosis. Baseline Fluorine-18 Fluorodeoxyglucose Positron Emission Tomography (18F-FDG PET) identified inguinal/iliac nodal and multiple pulmonary metastases.

A routine pre-chemotherapy Transthoracic Echocardiogram (TTE) in April 2022 unexpectedly showed a mobile RV mass. The lesion was surgically debulked a few days later, but a macroscopic residual lesion remained because complete resection was not technically feasible. Over the next year the patient underwent multi-agent chemotherapy, external-beam radiotherapy to the thigh, and finally high-dose Busulfan-Melphalan with autologous stem-cell rescue. Serial cardiologic assessments, including electrocardiography performed before each new chemotherapy course, consistently showed no functional abnormalities or conduction disturbances. Follow-up cardiac MRI in January 2023 confirmed residual RV disease.

Clinical findings

In June 2023, the patient was evaluated in the radiotherapy department for possible irradiation of the cardiac metastasis. Baseline vital signs were stable (blood pressure 118/72 mmHg, heart rate 64 bpm). There were no murmurs, gallops or signs of heart failure. Laboratory tests showed normal cardiac biomarkers. In addition, serial electrocardiograms (ECG) performed throughout the oncologic course, including before each subsequent chemotherapy line, remained unremarkable and showed no evidence of treatment-related functional cardiac impairment or clinically relevant conduction abnormalities. The Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status was 1, meaning the patient was fully ambulatory and capable of light daily activities, with only mild limitations in strenuous physical effort.

Diagnostic assessment

Cardiac MRI characterized the intracavitary lesion as 17 × 15 mm with intermediate T1 and hyperintense T2 signal and avid FDG uptake (SUVmax 6.4) on FDG-positron emission tomography. No additional cardiac lesions were identified.

Therapeutic intervention

After multidisciplinary discussion the patient was scheduled for single-fraction SBRT adapted from the STAR workflow. Cine-MRI was performed to characterize the lesion and assess cardiac motion. The residual lesion was contoured on MRI and registered to the planning CT using elastic image registration to minimize registration uncertainties between the two modalities. Respiratory-gated CT simulation and 4-D sorting quantified target motion at < 3 mm, allowing a 5 mm isotropic margin to generate the planning target volume (PTV). No ITV was generated; the PTV was obtained by directly expanding the GTV/CTV (GTV = CTV = 4.9 cc) with a 5 mm isotropic margin, considered adequate given the limited motion quantified on 4D-CT and the accuracy provided by the elastic image registration workflow. Treatment was delivered on an Elekta Versa-HD linac (Elekta, Stockholm, Sweden) using two 6 FFF (Flattening Filter Free) full arcs (MU per fraction: 2015.6 and 2263.3), in breath hold. A cone-beam CT (CBCT) was performed immediately before treatment delivery to verify target localization and ensure accurate radiation delivery. The prescription of 25 Gy to the 80% isodose achieved 98.8% PTV coverage (PTV: 47 ml) while respecting organ-at-risk constraints derived from the STAR literature and adapted from the CORSAIR summary by Bisello et al. (7). PTV dosimetric parameters were as follows: Dmax = 25.4 Gy; D0.5cc = 24.97 Gy; Dmin=22.97 Gy; Dmean = 24.52 Gy. The conformity index (CI ICRU=VRI/PTV) was 1.06, indicating excellent plan conformity; the gradient index (GIPaddick=V50%/PTV) was 3.54. The homogeneity index [HIICRU=(D2%-D98%)/D50%] was 0.043, confirming a highly homogeneous dose distribution within the PTV. Treatment plan with dose distribution is shown in Figure 1. The complete set of dose-volume constraints adopted for treatment planning, together with the corresponding patient-specific dosimetric values, is reported in Supplementary Table 2. Real-time six-lead ECG monitoring and pulse oximetry showed no acute arrhythmias or hemodynamic instability.

Follow-up and outcomes

The patient was observed overnight and discharged the next morning without complications. No early toxicities were reported at 2-week and 2-month visits. Cardiac MRI at 12 months demonstrated reduced lesion size (8 × 10 mm) and no new late gadolinium enhancement suggestive of radiation-induced cardiomyopathy. Quantitative functional assessment at this time point revealed a left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) of 54% and a right ventricular ejection fraction (RVEF) of 50%, both within normal limits according to established reference ranges. The MRI at 17 months confirmed stability of the cardiac lesion without evidence of treatment-induced cytotoxic damage. At 31 months after STAR, there was still no clinical, ECG, or MRI evidence of cardiac functional impairment, and no signs of progression at the treated cardiac site. The patient remains asymptomatic and continues systemic therapy for extracardiac disease. Throughout follow-up, serial electrocardiographic evaluations remained stable and showed no evidence of functional deterioration or new conduction abnormalities.

Discussion

This case illustrates how principles gained from STAR can be leveraged for rare oncologic indications. The decision to prescribe 25 Gy in a single fraction was informed by the extensive STAR experience, where similar doses achieve > 80% VT burden reduction with low toxicity (3–5). Our patient tolerated the regimen well and exhibits durable local control beyond 31 months, with no evidence of cardiac functional impairment or cardiac progression, a meaningful timeframe given the historically poor prognosis of metastatic Ewing sarcoma.

Table 1 summarizes the limited but growing body of literature on cardiac SBRT (8–14). Most previous reports treated heterogeneous histologies with multi-fraction regimens of 20–36 Gy in three to five fractions. Only two publications, Jumeau et al. (12) and Shah et al. (13), adopted single-fraction schedules, and Shah et al. already reported the use of 25 Gy in a single fraction for recurrent cardiac sarcoma in the re-irradiation setting. However, an important difference should be emphasized: in the report by Shah et al., the target was atrial, whereas in our case the treated lesion was located in the RV. This distinction is clinically relevant because atrial targeting is the conventional anatomical substrate of STAR in arrhythmia treatment, while its application to a ventricular metastatic sarcoma represents, to our knowledge, a unique oncologic use of the STAR paradigm. Our case is therefore not the first sarcoma treated with 25 Gy in a single fraction, but it appears to be the first report of STAR-inspired single-fraction radioablation delivered to a ventricular metastatic sarcoma target. Compared with other available series (15–20), we achieved durable radiological control beyond 31 months without any acute or late cardiac toxicity and without evidence of cardiac functional impairment, despite delivering a markedly ablative single dose to an intracavitary ventricular lesion. Indeed, assuming an α/β ratio of 4 for sarcomas, delivering 25 Gy in a single fraction corresponds to an equivalent dose in 2-Gy fractions (EQD2) of 120.8 Gy, unequivocally an ablative dose. Our experience therefore supports further exploration of STAR-inspired single-fraction SBRT for carefully selected cardiac tumors, particularly when the target is ventricular and complete surgical resection is not feasible.

Single-fraction delivery confers logistical advantages and limits inter-fraction motion uncertainty compared with multi-fraction cardiac SBRT series that typically use 30 Gy in three to five fractions;however, the potential interplay effect between VMAT dynamic delivery and cardiorespiratory motion should be acknowledged as an inherent limitation of this approach. Although radiobiological equivalence cannot be assumed, pre-clinical data suggest that high single doses may trigger vascular damage (21) and immunogenic cell death (22), both potentially advantageous in a relatively radio-resistant sarcoma substrate; whether these mechanisms contributed to the favorable outcome observed in our patient remains speculative.

The main limitations are the single-patient nature of this report and the short follow-up regarding very late cardiac effects. Nevertheless, the favorable toxicity profile observed in STAR cohorts provides reassuring context (3–5). In addition, reporting the full organ-at-risk dosimetry against established STAR-derived constraints may improve the interpretability and reproducibility of this experience; for this reason, the planning constraints used in our case are detailed in Supplementary Table 2. Prospective registries specifically capturing oncologic cardiac SBRT cases are urgently needed to define optimal dose-volume constraints and long-term outcomes.

Patient perspective

The patient expressed relief at avoiding an additional thoracotomy and was pleased to resume regular activities the day after treatment. He described a sense of reassurance knowing that the cardiac lesion is controlled without ongoing medication.

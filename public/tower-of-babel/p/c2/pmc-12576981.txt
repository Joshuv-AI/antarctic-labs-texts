Health literacy level and its influencing factors among college students majoring in sports : a cross-sectional study
Authors: Yue Yin, Qiang Fang, Zhu Min, Ting Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo understand the 2024 health-literacy level of college students majoring in sports in China—covering the three dimensions of basic knowledge and beliefs, healthy lifestyles and behaviors, and fundamental health skills, as well as the six domains of scientific health perspectives, communicable disease prevention, chronic disease prevention, safety and first aid, basic medical care, and health-information literacy—and to identify their determinants, to guide the development of tailored, evidence-based health-literacy interventions for undergraduate sport science students in China.MethodsUsing stratified random cluster sampling, 1083 college students majoring in sports were selected from six universities in Jiangsu Province. The questionnaire was divided into two parts: the basic situation of the study population and health literacy; the basic situation included general demographic characteristics, lifestyle status, and family-related information; the health literacy level of sports major college students was investigated using the “National Public Health Literacy Monitoring Survey Questionnaire.” It is included three aspects of health literacy and six categories of health problems. The required statistical analyses were performed using Chi-square tests and Gamma correlation analysis in SPSS 26.0 software. Binary logistic regression was applied to identify significant predictors.ResultsAmong 1,083 participants (69.44% male, 30.56% female), overall health-literacy level was 31.49%. The knowledge dimension scored highest (45.52%), basic skills lowest (26.69%); among six domains, safety and first-aid ranked highest (58.36%) and basic medical care lowest (8.03%). Binary logistic regression showed that smoking (including e-cigarettes) (OR = 0.538), engaging in Extracurricular Exercis daily (OR = 0.338) and never attending health lectures (OR = 0.532)were associated with lower health-literacy, and higher literacy was observed among first-year students (OR = 1.093), those whose mothers held junior-college diplomas (OR = 1.617), students exercising 30 ~ 60 min per session (OR = 1.525).ConclusionThis study, based on a cross-sectional study of over one thousand sports-major students, confirms that their overall health literacy averages only 31.49%. Specifically,“basic health skills"and “basic medical care"literacy both fall below 30%, emerging as the weakest domains. Binary logistic regression further identifies smoking, sophomore–junior students, and high-frequency exercise as key risk factors. By offering the first tracking of sports-major students’ health literacy and pinpointing precise intervention targets, the research fills an important evidence gap and provides an empirical foundation for tailored interventions. We recommend integrating tobacco-control education into the curriculum of sports majors, conducting continuous health literacy monitoring for sophomore–junior students, enhancing sport-specific skills, as well as exploring the mysteries of health with students through the application scenarios of health education theories and skills.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-24998-x.Keywords: College students majoring in sports, Health literacy level, Influencing factors, Cross-sectional study

Introduction

Health literacy(HL)refers to the ability of individuals to access and understand basic health information and services and to use these information and services to make the right decisions to maintain and promote their own health [1]. The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services asserted that limited health literacy is a common issue [2].There is evidence to prove that low health literacy has been associated with poorer health states, broader inequalities and higher health systems’ costs [3]– [4].Main influencing factors of health literacy have been found to be education, age, Alcohol consumption behavior, cigarette smoking, Parents’ educational level [5–7].In China, the monitoring of health literacy mainly adopts the “National Public Health Literacy Monitoring Survey Questionnaire.” China’s “three-dimensional, six-domain” health literacy framework combines international theoretical ideas and domestic practical experience. Dimension 1 (Basic Knowledge and Beliefs, 24 items) is guided by social cognitive theory; Dimension 2 (Healthy Lifestyles and Behaviours, 28 items) is based on planned behavior theory; and Dimension 3 (Fundamental Health Skills, 14 items) is founded on cognitive psychology models.These components work together to operationalize the “knowledge-attitude-practice” paradigm and align with the WHO’s “access-understand-apply” framework, resulting in a progressive structure that encompasses cognition, behavior, and skills. This structure corresponds to the 2024 version of Basic Knowledge and Skills for Chinese Citizens’ Health Literacy (66 items). The six domains—scientific health perspectives, infectious disease prevention, chronic disease management, safety and first aid, essential medical care, and health information literacy—are prioritized based on major public health concerns, incorporate national disease burden data, and are consistent with the Healthy China Initiative Plan (2019–2030). The framework’s structural validity is verified by 12 years of national monitoring (Cronbach’s α = 0.83). The WHO has recognised the framework as a model for developing nations, demonstrating both scientific rigour and practical efficacy.

The monitoring report on the health literacy of Chinese residents in 2023 shows that the health literacy level of Chinese residents in 2023 has increased to 29.70%, 1.92% points higher than that in 2022, and continues to show a steady improvement trend [8].According to the meta-analysis conducted by Zhao Yueyuan et al. [9] in 2016, the total health literacy level of Chinese college students was 12.08%, and Li Yu et al. [10] showed that the health literacy level of college students majoring in sports was 16.19%. Through the review, it is found that the health literacy level of college students in China is at a relatively low level.

Sports majors have more group-specific features in physiology, psychology, and behavior than the overall student population. These include superior physical fitness and athletic ability, an extraverted-impulsive-polarized psychological profile, high activity levels combined with low self-discipline, significantly different academic structures and weaker cultural achievement foundations, and distinct career goals and identity perceptions. These traits need more specific intervention measures for this population. Furthermore, there are limited research findings on the health literacy level of college students majoring in sports in China. The research group was particularly curious about the current status of health literacy among these students, so we conducted a survey in Jiangsu Province in 2024 and analyzed its influencing factors, aimed to understand the health literacy level of college students majoring in sports, provide a critical empirical foundation for developing targeted health education interventions and policy recommendations aiming at improving health outcomes specifically for this student population.

Methods

Design and setting

The objects of study are college students majoring in Sports in Jiangsu Province. The current study is a sub-project under the broader topic of student physical exercise and health management, supported by the Backbone Teachers of the Qinglan Project in Jiangsu Province.

Sampling and participants

A stratified random cluster sampling was employed to select 2 universities in each of the three administrative regions of Jiangsu Province (North, Central and South) from April to May 2024. In a total of 6 colleges and universities, 2–3 classes every grade, and all students in the selected classes were included in the study. In this survey, data were recovered in the form of online electronic questionnaires.

To determine the sample size, the formula for estimating the mean was used. Based on the variance of health literacy levels among college students majoring in Sports in Jiangsu Province obtained from our research group’s preliminary studies (=119.202) [11], and when α set at 0.05, , the margin of error from the preliminary studies was = 1.58. After substituting these values into the formula, the sample size for this study was calculated to be 184 participants. The total sample size for the six schools was 1104. Since the number of students in each class is not uniform, bringing the total sample size to 1118 participants. The questionnaires were distributed by the head teacher and the teacher in the form of classroom assignments. Through the trial test and evaluation, 1,083 valid questionnaires were recovered with an effective rate of 96.9%.

Health literacy questionnaire

We used the “National Public Health Literacy Monitoring Survey Questionnaire” compiled by the National Health Commission to investigate the health literacy level. The national public health literacy monitoring work has been carried out continuously for many years, and the questionnaire has been widely used in China with abundant research results. The Cronbach’s α coefficient of the questionnaire is 0.863, and the Cronbach’s α coefficients of each dimension range from 0.647 to 0.739. The test-retest reliability correlation coefficient of the questionnaire is 0.881 (P <.01), indicating good stability [12]. The questionnaire is divided into the basic information of the research object and the main part of the questionnaire. The basic information includes general demographic characteristics (gender, ethnicity, etc.), learning status (major, academic performance, etc.) and family-related information (parents’ education level, etc.). The main part of the questionnaire was divided into three aspects: basic knowledge and concept literacy (22 questions, 28 points), healthy lifestyle and behavior literacy (16 questions, 22 points) and basic skill literacy (12 questions, 16 points). Question types include judgment questions, single choice questions, multiple choice questions and scenario questions. The specific scoring method is as follows: no score is given for wrong answers to all question types, 1 point is given for correct answers to judgment questions and single choice questions, and 2 points is given for correct answers to all multiple choice questions. There are 50 questions in the questionnaire, and the total score is 66 points.

Variable definition

Those who score 80% or more of the total score (score ≥ 53) are judged to have health literacy [13]. The proportion of this population in the total population surveyed is the level of health literacy. The evaluation criterias of each sub-literacy level (3 aspects literacy, 6 types of health problem literacy) are consistent with the above evaluation criterias.

Health Lecture is a public health education effort aimed at disseminating scientific health knowledge, increasing audience health literacy, and encouraging the adoption of healthy habits. These lectures, delivered by licensed professionals in medicine, nutrition, public health, and allied subjects, are targeted to certain audience groups, taking into account characteristics such as age, occupation, and health status. The content is both systematic and practical, addressing not only disease prevention and common misconceptions, but also offering concrete behavioral advice. Health lectures bridge the gap between health research and ordinary life by including interactive formats such as Q&A sessions, case discussions, and live demonstrations. Furthermore, they play an important role in promoting health risk awareness and facilitating societal mobilization for national health improvements.

The classification of sports specialties

In China, college students majoring in physical education are classified into sports specializations based on three criteria: “student foundation,” “professional requirements,” and “practical application scenarios.” To put it simply, it is about “what the students are good at, what their major demands are, and what they will require in their future jobs.” First, the pupils’ strengths before to enrollment are evaluated. Some students, for example, have been playing basketball or track and field since they were children, whilst others excel at martial arts and swimming. The specializations will select projects in which they already have a basis, making learning easier. Next, the professional direction is considered.For example, the physical education major intends to prepare instructors to teach physical education classes in primary and secondary schools, therefore the specialties will primarily comprise track and field, ball sports, and gymnastics, all of which are commonly utilized in schools, to assist future teaching. Because the sports training major seeks to develop coaches or athletes, the specialties will center on competitive sports like basketball, football, and swimming.Outdoor activities, skiing, and fitness, which are popular among the general population, will be introduced to the curriculum for majors in social or leisure sports to promote future mass fitness coaching. Additionally, the school’s resources are taken into consideration. For example, northern universities with ice and snow venues will provide skiing and skating specialties, whereas schools with a martial arts legacy will focus on martial arts specialties. In essence, the goal is to ensure that students’ specialty may both capitalize on their talents and satisfy the demands of their future studies and jobs.

Statistical analysis

Spss26.0 software was used for statistical analysis of the data. The adoption rate or percentage (%) of statistical data was described, χ2 test and Gamma tests was used for statistical inference, and the influencing factors of health literacy level were analyzed by Binary logistic regression. Test level α = 0.05.

Results

Demographic characteristics

Among the 1083 respondents, 752 (69.44%) were male and 331 (30.56%) were female; There were 701 undergraduate students (64.73%) and 382 junior college students (35.27%). There were 697 normal students (64.36%) and 386 non-normal students (social sports, leisure sports, sports training) (35.64%). 255 students are good at or majoring in track and field (23.5%), 148 in gymnastics (13.7%), 365 in big ball (33.7%) and 15 in small ball (29.1%). 650 students (60.02%) registered in rural areas and 433 students (39.98%) registered in urban areas; Only child 424 (39.15%),there were 659 non-only children (60.85%); the educational levels of students’ fathers were distributed as follows: bachelor’s degree or above (14.9%), college diploma (15.4%), high school diploma (30.1%), and junior high school education or below (39.6%). The educational levels of students’ mothers were distributed as follows: bachelor’s degree or above (12.8%), college diploma (12.8%), high school diploma (25.2%), and junior high school education or below (49.1%).

Analysis for categorical variables

Among the 1,083 respondents, 341 college students had a higher level of health literacy, with an overall health literacy level of 31.49%. The health literacy level of college students with urban household registration (33.49%) was slightly higher than that of students with rural household registration (30.15%); students from undergraduate institutions had slightly higher literacy in all aspects compared to those from junior colleges; only children had better literacy than non-only children. However, the above differences were not statistically significant (P >.05).The results are displayed in Table 1.

The results showed that gender (χ² = 3.829, p <.05, effect size = 0.059), smoking behavior (χ² = 11.316, p <.01, effect size = 0.102), and sports specialization (χ² = 10.091, p <.05, effect size = 0.097) had a significant impact on multiple dimensions of health literacy. Specifically, female college students scored higher than male college students in health literacy levels and all its dimensions. College students who did not smoke performed better in the dimensions of “basic health knowledge and concepts,” “healthy lifestyle and behavior,” “basic health skills,” and “scientific health view.” Relatively speaking, students specializing in aesthetic sports (such as gymnastics) performed better in the dimensions of “basic knowledge,” “healthy lifestyle,” “basic health skills,” “scientific health view,” “infectious disease prevention and control,” and “safety and first aid,” while students specializing in major ball sports (such as basketball and volleyball) scored relatively lower, indicating that the sports specialization may affect the structure of health cognition. In addition, han students scored higher than ethnic minority students in the dimension of “basic health knowledge and concepts,” which may be related to the economic and educational development levels in ethnic minority areas. Variables such as school type (undergraduate/junior college), whether the student is an only child, and whether the parents are alive did not show a significant impact on any dimension of health literacy, indicating that these factors had limited influence on health literacy in this study.

Discussions

We conducted this study with college students for the following reasons. Possessing appropriate health literacy allows them to make the most scientific decisions when it comes to health choices. Furthermore, a high degree of health literacy can help them maintain their occupational health after graduation, reducing the cost load on the national healthcare system. As a result, school health education is critical to improving student health and building future occupational health skills. I will now elaborate on each point in my discussion.

The overall level of health literacy

The results of this survey show that the overall level of health literacy among college students majoring in Sports(31.49%) is higher than the national health literacy level of residents in 2023 (29.70%) [8], and significantly higher than that of some similar groups and regions in earlier years, such as physical education students in Jiangsu Province in 2016 (14.95%) [14] and students of a sports university in 2017 (16.19%) [10]. but still lower than those in Nanjing in 2020 (38.1%) [15], Tianjin in 2021 (48.2%) [16], Guangzhou in 2022 (47.8%) [17], and Jiangsu Province in 2022 (33.18%) [11]. The observed disparities are likely attributable to differences in survey timing and geographical location, as well as variations in economic development.This indicates that the health literacy of college students majoring in Sports is increasing year by year, but also suggests that there is still regional imbalance and room for further improvement.

Among the three dimensions, the dimension of basic health concepts and knowledge (45.52%) performed the best, surpassing multiple historical and regional comparative data [10, 14, 18]– [19], but remaining lower than that of college students in Tianjin and Shandong [16, 20]. The lowest level of health skill literacy is 26.69%. This outcome is very consistent with the findings of other relevant studies [11, 21]. A lack of skill literacy can have a variety of negative consequences, including impairing students’ ability to make informed health decisions, reducing their capacity for self-health management, significantly lowering their quality of life, increasing their reliance on medical services, and raising healthcare costs.This suggests that there is a structural imbalance of “high knowledge but low practice” in the health literacy of college students majoring in sports, indicating challenges in translating theoretical information into practical behaviors and abilities. The study emphasizes the need of improving students’ health behavior guidance and skills training in practice.

Among the six types of health literacy issues among college students majoring in sports, safety and first aid literacy is the highest (58.36%), followed by scientific health concept literacy (57.71%), and basic medical literacy is the lowest (8.03%), which is consistent with relevant research results [22]– [23]. It is suggested to further strengthen the education of basic health skills and medical literacy among college students majoring in sports, and improve their ability to self manage their health. The literacy level of chronic disease prevention and control (39.15%) is higher than that of infectious disease prevention and control (29.36%), and the research results are higher than the relevant literacy level of college students in Jiangsu Province [24], which is consistent with the research results of Hu Ruimin et al. [25]. However, there are inconsistencies with the research of relevant scholars [11, 22], which may be due to the subjective concern of college students majoring in sports for the prevention and control of chronic diseases.

Analysis of factors influencing the health literacy

The study’s findings indicate that female college students had higher health literacy than male college students, which is consistent with the findings of similar studies [26]. However, this contradicts the findings of Nepalese et al. [27], which are most likely related to racial and cultural variations among the study participants. Students majoring in physical education had higher health literacy than students who do not major in physical education, which could be attributed to the higher academic requirements for cultural courses before entering school. The study discovered that students with specialized talents in the aesthetic and difficult categories (such as gymnastics) have much higher health literacy and related dimensions than students in other categories (such as team sports like basketball). This could be because teaching and training in gymnastics and other artistic and demanding categories need more body management skills and human movement science. First grade students have higher levels of health literacy, which aligns with the findings of relevant studies [28]– [29]. This could be due to academic pressure, employment pressure, or financial problems for senior students, implying that health education interventions should be continued throughout the college stage rather than concentrating in the lower grades. However, this contradicts the findings of three research [21, 28]– [29], which could be attributed to economic, cultural, and demographic variations between local and international environments.

It is worth noting that the students with siblings outnumbered only children. The age of the respondents was between 18 and 22, which seems to contradict the national two-child policy. This discrepancy may stem from the region-specific rules implemented during 2002–2006—especially the “one and a half child” policy, which allowed rural and ethnic minority couples to have a second child if the first child was a girl—and the composition of the student sample: general undergraduate institutions and vocational colleges tend to enroll more students from rural areas, where the likelihood of having siblings is higher.

Research has found that there is a negative correlation between the health literacy of sports major students and the frequency of their extracurricular exercise. This is an intriguing and counterintuitive finding. It could be related to a “knowledge-belief-behavior” gap between health literacy and health behavior, implying that great health literacy does not always translate into high-frequency physical activity behavior. This link may be influenced by environmental factors (such as the availability of sports facilities), social factors (such as peer influence), and personal motivational factors, resulting in an inability to reflect the benefit of knowledge in exercise behaviour.It is also possible that this is due to the professional practice nature of physical education major students, in which the high daily training volume within the curriculum results in a large decline in the frequency of extracurricular physical activity. Furthermore, the health literacy measurement method used in this study was based on the level of theoretical information acquisition, whereas physical exercise is a practical behavior. As a result, the data could indicate a gap between theoretical comprehension and practice.

The study also demonstrates that often attending health-related lectures can successfully increase the health literacy level of students majoring in sports, which supports the conclusion that “studying more subjects related to health education is associated with higher health literacy [30].” The study also validated the finding that college students’ health literacy is influenced by their age, gender, and parents’ educational level [29]. This finding strongly shows that health lectures are an effective intervention for increasing health literacy, particularly in terms of mental health and knowledge acquisition skills.However, the regression analysis of this study found a negative correlation between participating in more than six health lectures and the level of health literacy. We speculate that this does not mean that health lectures are ineffective, but rather reflects the targeted nature of the school’s health intervention strategies: students with lower health literacy or poorer health status are systematically included in health education programs that require high-frequency participation. Therefore, high-frequency participation is more likely to be an outcome indicator of low health literacy, rather than a cause.

A recent study emphasized the necessity of researching campus tobacco culture in determining health literacy among college students [31]. This study revealed the harmful influence of smoking on students’ health literacy skills, which is consistent with findings from previous studies [32]. As a result, formulating and/or implementing smoking-free regulations is critical [33]. However, this contradicts Svendsen et al.‘s conclusion, which could be attributed to Denmark’s higher overall economic and cultural levels [34].

Conclusion

The key finding of this study is that college students majoring in sports have relatively low levels of health literacy, with a structural imbalance of “high knowledge but low skills,” characterized by weaknesses in healthy lifestyle and health skills literacy. Lower health literacy skills are associated with being male, being a senior student, smoking, and not attending health-related courses or lectures. Further research is needed to determine the impact of exercise frequency on health literacy among physical education majors. The healthy habits and health skills education of this group deserve more attention.

For future recommendations, universities should adjust their curricula according to the practical needs of physical education majors. Health literacy courses, such as “Health and Sports Science,” should be integrated into the curriculum of physical education majors to help students better grasp and apply health knowledge. A variety of health activities, such as regularly inviting health professionals to give lectures and organizing health behavior challenge contests, can enhance students’ awareness of health. At the same time, universities should emphasize the development of students’ health literacy throughout their academic careers (for example, providing one-on-one health counseling for graduating students), rather than only during the student enrollment phase. Digital tools for health services, such as artificial intelligence, should be developed and utilized to create modular and scenario-based health literacy instruction for students.

Limitations

Although this study makes important recommendations for policies and practices targeted at increasing health literacy among students majoring in sports, its limitations must be acknowledged. First, given the limited research on health literacy among sports majoring college students abroad, few studies have involved comparative research. Second, the data were gathered only from regular universities and vocational institutions and it is another cross-sectional study, which may limit the findings’ generalizability; we will broaden the scope and sample in future research.Thirdly, the study found correlations between factors but did not show causality. The study found a link between smoking and lower health literacy, but the causative relationship could not be confirmed. We will continue to follow up on this. Fourth, it is unexpected that the extracurricular exercise frequency of physical education students is negatively correlated with health literacy. We will follow up later to see whether high-intensity training in the curriculum is a relevant influencing factor.

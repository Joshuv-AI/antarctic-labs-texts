ANXA2 in hepatocellular carcinoma: orchestrating tumorigenesis, progression, and therapeutic resistance toward precision targeting
Authors: Junyu Chen, Jianlin Li, Liuxin Ran, Jianbo Yang, Yang Xie
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) represents a highly heterogeneous malignancy whose pathogenesis involves multiple etiological factors, including hepatitis viruses, metabolic disorders, and environmental carcinogens. Nevertheless, clinical management continues to face significant challenges due to high rates of postoperative recurrence, metastasis, and drug resistance. Emerging evidence indicates that Annexin A2 (ANXA2), a calcium-dependent phospholipid-binding protein, plays a pivotal role throughout HCC progression by integrating multiple oncogenic pathways. This review systematically elucidates ANXA2’s multifaceted regulatory mechanisms in HCC pathogenesis. Functioning as a critical molecular nexus, ANXA2 is dynamically modulated in its expression and activity by hepatic insults, particularly HBV/HCV infection, Alcoholic Liver Disease (ALD), Metabolic Dysfunction-associated Fatty Liver Disease (MAFLD), and tobacco exposure, thereby bridging chronic liver injury and malignant transformation. Mechanistically, ANXA2 promotes tumor progression through multiple pathways, including ubiquitination regulation, stem cell characteristic regulation, cytoskeletal remodeling, Epithelial–Mesenchymal Transition (EMT) induction, and autophagy modulation. Furthermore, it confers chemotherapy resistance specifically through regulating apoptosis evasion, ferroptosis suppression, and autophagy homeostasis, while its effects on radiotherapy and immunotherapy responses involve distinct mechanisms. Promisingly, emerging ANXA2-targeted strategies demonstrate therapeutic potential, including exosome and microenvironment modulation, molecular targeted therapy, regulation of the ANXA2-NF-κB axis, Nanotechnology, and radionuclide theranostics. Collectively, this review establishes an integrated “etiology-mechanism-intervention” framework while providing novel molecular targets and a theoretical foundation for precision medicine approaches in HCC.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) constitutes 75–85% of primary liver cancers [1], with an annual incidence approaching 900,000 cases. The multistep pathogenesis arises from synergistic interactions among hepatitis viruses (HBV/HCV), metabolic dysfunction (obesity/diabetes), environmental carcinogens (e.g., aflatoxin B1), and behavioral factors (alcohol/tobacco consumption) [2–5]. Notwithstanding therapeutic advancements including hepatic resection and molecularly targeted agents, the 5-year recurrence rate reaches 70% in HCC patients with microvascular invasion or advanced-stage disease (BCLC B/C stage) [6]. Primary resistance emerges within 12 months in 60–80% of patients undergoing first-line targeted monotherapy [7]. Despite improved efficacy with immune checkpoint inhibitor combinations, refractory cases persist in > 50% of treated patients [8]. The absence of validated predictive biomarkers and incomplete mapping of HCC’s molecular landscape substantially impede precision oncology implementation. Therefore, elucidating the multifunctional mechanisms of key regulatory molecules represents a crucial direction for overcoming current therapeutic limitations.

Annexin A2 (ANXA2), a calcium-dependent phospholipid-binding protein within the annexin superfamily, contains structurally distinct functional domains that enable pleiotropic activities [9]. Its C-terminal core region contains calcium-binding motifs and binding sites for plasminogen/heparin/F-actin [10–12]. The N-terminal domain interacts with protein ligands and regulates F-actin-mediated cytoskeletal dynamics, featuring binding sites for S100A10 (p11) and phosphorylation sites at Ser-11, Tyr-23, and Ser-25 [13, 14]. Notably, Tyr-23 and Ser-25 can be phosphorylated by Src kinase and protein kinase C, respectively [13, 14], with Src-mediated Tyr-23 phosphorylation identified as a critical molecular event in hepatocarcinogenesis that establishes ANXA2 as a pivotal signaling node [15]. ANXA2 undergoes dynamic oligomerization, forming functionally distinct complexes including the heterotetrameric A2t configuration that orchestrates plasminogen activation, growth factor signaling, and extracellular matrix remodeling [16–21].

These structural features confer ANXA2 with pleiotropic regulatory functions in oncogenesis. ANXA2 is ubiquitously expressed in diverse cancers and mechanistically contributes to tumorigenesis by regulating fibrinolysis, modulating cellular differentiation programs, and facilitating vesicular transport [22–25]. Clinically, the upregulation of ANXA2 can predict poor prognosis in patients with HCC, and it is closely associated with advanced tumor stage, metastasis, and shortened survival time [26, 27]. Currently, it has also been found that ANXA2 may play a crucial role in the development of multiple types of drug resistance in HCC. For instance, its interaction with the Mycoplasma hyorhinis membrane protein P37 can enhance the chemoresistance of HCC97L cells to mitoxantrone and gemcitabine [28]. Additionally, the dynamic changes in its subcellular localization can induce radiotherapy resistance [29]. Alternatively, it may promote relevant immune escape pathways, thereby leading to immunotherapy resistance [30].

Collectively, its structural versatility enables multifunctional regulation throughout HCC pathogenesis. This review deconstructs ANXA2’s oncogenic network through four mechanistic tiers (Fig. 1): (i) elucidating its molecular initiation mechanisms in carcinogenic microenvironments (various risk factors), (ii) revealing intracellular signal transduction pathways driving tumor proliferation and metastasis, (iii) exploring its chaperone-like role in resistance formation, and (iv) integrating these mechanisms to provide innovative strategies and translational pathways for precision HCC therapy.

Regulatory role of ANXA2 in HCC risk factors

ANXA2 expression and activity are dynamically regulated by major HCC risk factors: hepatitis viruses (HBV/HCV), Alcoholic Liver Disease(ALD), and Metabolic Dysfunction-associated Fatty Liver Disease(MAFLD), and environmental exposures (e.g., tobacco). Across distinct HCC etiologies, ANXA2 contributes to hepatocarcinogenesis via etiology-specific molecular pathways. Role of ANXA2 in hepatitis virus-mediated HCC

As a calcium-dependent phospholipid-binding protein, ANXA2 contributes to hepatitis-related HCC pathogenesis through multifaceted regulation of the viral life cycle, encompassing virion production, cellular entry, infection establishment, genome replication, and particle assembly [31] (Fig. 2). Global epidemiological studies reveal that HBV/HCV-induced HCC constitutes 55% of total HCC cases, with prevalence surpassing 80% in high-endemic regions [1]. Yoon et al. demonstrated threefold greater ANXA2 upregulation in HBV/HCV-associated HCC, establishing its etiology-specific dysregulation pattern [32]. Then, it facilitates genomic replication and transcription by assisting in the nuclear translocation of polymerases. In late viral maturation, ANXA2 aids HBV in obtaining nucleocapsid envelope from the endoplasmic reticulum, and the A2t/β2-GPI/HBsAg complex ultimately enables the budding and release of the virus. Additionally, ANXA2 can bind to the regulatory element of the rs1883832 risk allele T, which suppresses CD40 expression and blocks the JAK/STAT/BST2 signaling pathway, ultimately resulting in elevated HBV levels. Mechanisms of ANXA2 in HBV-associated HCC

As a partially double-stranded circular DNA virus, HBV extensively interacts with ANXA2 throughout its life cycle. The viral lifecycle engages ANXA2 sequentially: initial attachment via β2-GPI/NTCP/HBsAg complex formation, followed by A2t-mediated membrane fusion through conformational rearrangement of viral envelope proteins and host membrane lipid raft clustering [35, 36]. In the genome replication and transcription phase, it promotes HBV replication and transcription by mediating nuclear localization of HBV polymerase through a nuclear transport chaperone mechanism [37]. During late viral maturation, ANXA2-mediated membrane remodeling enables HBV nucleocapsids to acquire envelope structures from the endoplasmic reticulum [31]. Viral egress is accomplished through A2t/β2-GPI/HBsAg complex-mediated membrane fusion [31]. Meanwhile, ANXA2 can not only bind regulatory elements of the rs1883832 risk allele T to suppress CD40 expression, thereby inhibiting the JAK/STAT/BST2 anti-HBV signaling axis and promoting HBV production [38], but also regulate HBsAg trafficking to sustain persistent viral infection [39]. Additionally, A2t also participates in regulating the exocytosis process during vertical HBV transmission [40].These pleiotropic functions establish ANXA2 as a key regulatory molecule in the HBV-induced hepatocarcinogenesis.

ANXA2 exerts a dual-driver role in HBV-associated hepatocellular oncogenesis and progression, and its mechanisms span both the regulation of the viral life cycle and the crosstalk with host oncogenic signaling networks. At the indirect tumorigenesis level, ANXA2, acting as the key receptor for β2-GPI on the membrane of the HCC cell line SMMC-7721, can facilitate the binding of HBV to host cells [35]. This process is able to enhance the efficiency of HBV infection and prolong the persistent intrahepatic infection of the virus, thereby laying the groundwork for subsequent oncogenic mechanisms. Meanwhile, HBV infection can elicit genetic alterations through genomic integration—such as insertions into oncogenic regions like CCNE1, TERT, and MLL4—or through chromosomal rearrangements leading to dysfunction of tumor-suppressor genes such as TP53 and MYC, collectively driving the malignant transformation of hepatocytes [41, 42]. In addition, HBV can further promote HCC development via epigenetic regulation (e.g., DNA methylation modification) and activation of oncogenic signaling pathways such as Wnt/β-catenin and PI3K/AKT [43]. Notably, the HBV-encoded X protein (HBx), a multifunctional regulator, accelerates HCC progression by modulating key oncogenic pathways including cell proliferation, apoptosis inhibition, and angiogenesis [44].

Regarding direct oncogenic mechanisms, ANXA2 participates in HCC initiation and progression through multiple modalities. On the one hand, ANXA2 can inhibit the secretion of HBsAg, promoting its aberrant intracellular accumulation, and chronic retention of HBsAg has been demonstrated to foster hepatocarcinogenesis [39]. On the other hand, HBV infection upregulates ETV4, which transcriptionally boosts the expression of ANXA2 to activate the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, ultimately accelerating HCC progression [45]. Moreover, emerging evidence suggests that ANXA2 may be involved in HBV-related epigenetic regulation, such as DNA methylation modification [46]. However, the precise molecular mechanisms remain incompletely understood. HBx significantly up-regulates ANXA2 expression in HepG2 cells [47], indicating that ANXA2 may play a pivotal role in HBx-driven hepatocarcinogenesis. Both A2t inhibitors and HBx inhibitors (e.g., estradiol benzoate) could exhibit certain therapeutic benefits [48, 49]. Integrating the current evidence, ANXA2 appears to serve as a central regulatory node linking HBV infection to HCC development.

Mechanisms of ANXA2 in HCV-associated HCC

The nonstructural proteins encoded by the HCV genome play a central role in virion assembly and genome replication [50]. Studies have shown that ANXA2, functioning as a lipid-raft scaffold protein, can form functional complexes with HCV nonstructural proteins NS2, NS3, NS4A/4B, and NS5A/5B, thereby markedly promoting the assembly of viral replication complexes within lipid-raft microdomains [31, 51], while elevated HCV viral load has been identified as a significant risk factor for HCC development [52]. Meanwhile, the HCV-induced inflammatory microenvironment represents a key driving force for HCC initiation [53, 54]. The ANXA2–NS5B interaction not only alters NS5B catalytic activity and its susceptibility to the allosteric inhibitor filibuvir, but also interferes with the formation of the NS5B–IKKα complex, consequently derepressing the TNF-α/NF-κB signaling pathway to propel inflammation [51, 55]. Meanwhile, in vitro experiments using primary human monocyte-derived macrophages and the THP-1 hepatic macrophage model have demonstrated that HCV infection induces pro-interleukin-1 beta (pro-IL-1β) and pro-interleukin-18 (pro-IL-18) expression through pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs)-mediated activation of the NF-κB pathway [56], while its core protein, via calcium homeostasis dysregulation, activates the NOD-like receptor pyrin domain-containing protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome, which cooperates with NF-κB to promote caspase-1-dependent maturation and secretion of IL-1β/IL-18 [57, 58], thereby amplifying hepatic inflammation. At the post-translational modification level, increased fucosylated N-glycans contribute to HCV-infected hepatocyte-driven HCC progression. HCV enhances ANXA2 fucosylation by up-regulating the α1,6-fucosyltransferase FUT8, thereby accelerating HCC development [59].

Furthermore, several mechanistic avenues remain insufficiently explored and warrant dedicated inquiry: A2t can serve as a viral recognition receptor that activates notably both NF-κB pathway and MAP kinases in macrophages, stimulating the production of multiple inflammatory factors, suggesting a potential HCV-triggered inflammatory cascade mediated by A2t [18]. This mechanism remains to be further validated in liver tissue-specific macrophage models. Regarding immune evasion, HCV-induced T-cell exhaustion and other immunosuppressive states facilitate viral escape from immune clearance and increase oncogenic risk [60], while ANXA2 modulates immune cell functions by activating the immune checkpoint network, inducing co-inhibitory signals, and attenuating innate immune responses, potentially contributing to HCV immune evasion [61]. Collectively, ANXA2 exerts multifaceted effects across the continuum of HCV-driven hepatocarcinogenesis by elevating viral load, potentiating inflammatory signaling cascades, undergoing fucosylation-mediated post-translational modification, and orchestrating immune evasion. Nevertheless, the precise molecular mechanisms of these processes remain to be fully elucidated.

The role of ANXA2 in alcohol-induced HCC

ALD, caused by excessive alcohol consumption, is one of the factors contributing to HCC development [62]. It has been confirmed that liver fibrosis progression directly contributes to hepatocarcinogenesis [63, 64], with ANXA2 emerging as a key mediator in alcohol-induced fibrogenesis. Chronic ethanol exposure upregulates hepatocyte ANXA2 expression [65], correlating with advanced fibrotic progression [66]. Given the central role of hepatic stellate cells (HSCs) in fibrogenesis [67], ANXA2 appears to regulate HSC activation. Recent studies have shown that ANXA2 promotes HSC activation by enhancing signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) phosphorylation to upregulate the activation marker α-SMA expression while simultaneously activating the SPHKs/S1P/IL-17A signaling pathway in HSC, and Isoliquiritigenin(ISL)-mediated ANXA2 inhibition reverses these pro-fibrotic effects [68]. The reversibility of liver fibrosis [69, 70] suggests that targeting ANXA2-mediated HSC activation could prevent fibrosis to HCC progression. Additionally, recent studies have found that ANXA2 expression levels are significantly elevated during alcoholic hepatitis (AH) and positively correlate with future HCC risk [71]. These data position ANXA2 as a promising prognostic biomarker for ALD-related malignant transformation, meriting further clinical investigation.

The role of ANXA2 in MAFLD-driven HCC

MAFLD initiates with hepatocyte steatosis due to ectopic lipid accumulation. As the disease progresses, insulin resistance (IR) and persistent inflammatory responses jointly drive the transition from Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Fatty Liver (MAFL) to Metabolic-associated Steatohepatitis (MASH) [72, 73]. ANXA2 expression progressively elevates along the MAFL-MASH-HCC disease development cascade [74, 75], suggesting its utility as a disease progression biomarker for MAFLD monitoring [76]. ANXA2 orchestrates disease progression through multi-tiered regulatory networks. In terms of metabolic regulation, it can form complexes with prohibitin (PHB) and fatty acid transport protein CD36 in white adipose tissue to promote transmembrane transport of fatty acids from endothelial cells to adipocytes, facilitating their uptake [77]. Crucially, LPS-TLR4 signaling upregulates ANXA2 via NF-κB/c-Jun activation, inhibiting AMPK/mTOR axis-mediated lipophagy process. This manifests as reduced LC3-II/I ratio and elevated p62, causing lipid overload and hepatocyte damage—phenotypes reversible upon ANXA2 silencing [75]. Inflammation-wise, palmitic acid (PA) or high-fat diet (HFD)-mediated ANXA2 upregulation can produce cascade effects by activating the NF-κB signaling axis, leading to suppressed IRS-1 expression and subsequent increased expression of suppressor of cytokine signaling 3 (SOCS3), peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor coactivator-1α (Pgc1α), and pro-inflammatory factors (IL-6/IL-1β/TNF-α), resulting in IR and exacerbation of adipose tissue inflammation, thereby promoting the transition from MAFL to MASH [78]. The combined therapeutic approach utilizing sh-ANXA2 and GA-MSe@AR nanoparticles may exhibit promising potential for targeted hepatic delivery in MAFLD treatment [79].

As the disease progresses, ANXA2 also plays an important role in the fibrosis process. Studies have confirmed that the p-STAT3-ANXA2-Caspase-1 pathway and the ANXA2-Notch-OPN signaling cascade-driven HSC activation can synergistically accelerate MASH-related liver fibrosis progression. Notably, the combined therapeutic approach using both a Notch inhibitor (DAPT) and a Caspase-1 inhibitor (VX-765) shows promising efficacy in ameliorating liver fibrosis [80, 81]. Additionally, in MASH-related HCC tissues, ANXA2 expression levels are significantly higher than in normal liver tissues [82]. During the progression of obesity-related MAFLD, excessive lipid accumulation not only upregulates the expression of proinflammatory cytokines (TNF-α and IL-6) but also downregulates the expression of anti-inflammatory adipokines, thereby accelerating the development of chronic adipose inflammation [83]. Beyond promoting the transition from MAFL to MASH, this state of dysregulation also induces IR with the progression of obesity. Notably, IR has been identified as one of the key factors contributing to the onset and progression of HCC [83–85]. As a pivotal bridge between metabolic inflammation and tumorigenesis, ANXA2 integrates obesity-induced metabolic dysregulation signals with pro-carcinogenic pathways, exerting a significant function therein. It can promote the release of the aforementioned inflammatory factors via the NF-κB signaling pathway, ultimately leading to the aberrant activation of IR [78]. In turn, activated IR increases the expression level and activity of insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) in serum, and both IR and IGF-1 can bind to the insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor (IGF-1R), ultimately leading to the initiation of HCC via activating signaling cascades such as the Raf/MEK/ERK pathway [85, 86]. Based on these mechanisms, current therapeutic strategies for obesity-associated HCC prioritize ANXA2-targeted interventions, encompassing specific monoclonal antibody blockade, metabolic pathway modulation (e.g., IGF-1R inhibitors), and anti-inflammatory treatments (IL-6R antagonists) within multimodal combination regimens.

In the context of MAFLD–HCC progression linked to type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), ANXA2 exhibits a distinctive vascular-remodeling function. The hyperglycemic microenvironment induces the formation of an Hsp90α (Heat Shock Protein 90 Alpha)–ANXA2 complex on the surface of vascular endothelial cells. This complex markedly enhances plasmin generation by promoting plasminogen activation [87]. Activated plasmin subsequently triggers the matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) system, leading to extracellular-matrix degradation and the release of pro-angiogenic factors, thereby creating favorable conditions for HCC invasion and metastasis [88]. This finding broadens the traditional view that ANXA2 activates MMPs solely through the A2t complex formed with S100A10, and reveals a novel molecular interaction pattern under conditions of glucose-metabolism dysregulation. Therapeutic exploration targeting this pathway has shown that co-administration of Hsp90α/ANXA2 antibodies with the plasmin inhibitor ε-aminocaproic acid (EACA) may represent an effective strategy to block diabetes-associated HCC progression [89]. Although the precise regulatory network of ANXA2 throughout the entire MAFL–MASH–HCC disease progression trajectory awaits further elucidation, existing evidence strongly supports its role as a key molecular target linking metabolic abnormalities, chronic inflammation, and malignant transformation.

The role of ANXA2 in tobacco exposure–mediated HCC

Tobacco exposure activates the oncogenic functions of ANXA2 through multiple mechanisms, thereby sculpting a distinct pro-tumorigenic microenvironment. Nicotine, the principal bioactive constituent of tobacco, triggers excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) production [29, 90]. ROS not only promote the phosphorylation of ANXA2 at Tyr23 but also drive its association with the binding partner S100A10 to form the heterotetrameric complex A2t, leading to the extracellular release of A2t [29, 91, 92]. Once secreted, A2t binds to tissue-type plasminogen activator (t-PA), catalyzing the conversion of plasminogen to plasmin [16]. Plasmin facilitates HCC initiation and development through multiple mechanisms: On the one hand, it activates matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), including MMP2, MMP9, and MMP10, all of which have been demonstrated to promote HCC pathogenesis [93, 94]. On the other hand, immunosuppression is recognized as a major driver of HCC development [61]. Dendritic cells (DCs), which play a critical role in maintaining immune homeostasis, are impaired by plasmin through the maintenance of an immature phenotype, reduced migration to lymph nodes, and enhanced generation and secretion of the immunosuppressive cytokine TGF-β, thereby compromising immune function [95]. Furthermore, a study indicates that ANXA2 itself can inhibit DC maturation, upregulate immunosuppressive factors such as IL-10 and TGF-β, and activate immune checkpoint pathways, collectively promoting immune evasion [61]. These mechanisms of immune dysfunction synergistically contribute to the initiation of HCC.

Meanwhile, ROS drives ANXA2-Tyr23 phosphorylation to activate the Rho/ROCK pathway, thereby orchestrating dynamic cytoskeletal remodeling that enhances the metastatic capacity of neoplasm, and this process can be reversed by ROCK inhibitors (Y27632, H-1152) or cell-permeable Rho inhibitors [96, 97]. ROS also disrupts the nuclear export signal (NES) or promotes cortical ANXA2-Tyr23 phosphorylation, facilitating its extracellular release. The resulting nuclear accumulation and extracellular release of ANXA2 jointly contribute to radioresistance [29, 91, 98]. This coordinated shift in ANXA2 localization—from nucleus to extracellular space—is not only tightly linked to the radioresistant phenotype of HCC cells but also suggests potential utility as a molecular biomarker for clinical prognosis. Additionally, plasmin generated by ANXA2 in the extracellular space can also facilitate angiogenesis and invasive metastasis in tumor [9, 99]. Strong evidence indicates that this process is closely associated with the emergence of resistance to multiple chemotherapeutic agents and may represent a pivotal molecular event in microenvironmental remodeling [100]. Angiostatin (AS) binds to cell-surface ANXA2 and interferes with plasmin production [101], providing a novel theoretical basis for developing ANXA2-targeted therapies against tobacco-related HCC.

ANXA2 Promotes malignant proliferation of HCC cells

ANXA2 serves as a central regulator in driving malignant proliferation of HCC cells by orchestrating cell cycle progression, ubiquitination modification networks, and cancer stem cell properties. Functioning as a multifaceted signaling hub, it not only integrates pro-proliferative pathways but also maintains stem-like features, thereby providing sustained driving force for HCC progression and recurrence (Fig. 4). Synergistic effects of proliferation signals and bidirectional ubiquitination modifications

ANXA2 operates as a multi-layered hub in the proliferative control of HCC, integrating cell-cycle regulation, ubiquitination-dependent protein stability, and transcription-translation-coupled oncogenic circuits.

In cell-cycle control, ANXA2 drives the G1/S transition through two complementary axes: On the one side, it forms a functional complex with the long non-coding RNA LINC01133 to co-activate the STAT3 pathway, resulting in up-regulation of cyclin D1 (CCND1) [102]. On the other side, ANXA2 modulates nuclear translocation of β-catenin, collectively accelerating G1/S progression [103]. This dual-track governance equips HCC cells with the capacity to override cell-cycle checkpoints and establishes the foundational scaffold for ANXA2-mediated proliferation.

Such a regulatory paradigm underscores both the functional versatility and plasticity of ANXA2, while its activity status is primarily governed by the dynamic ubiquitin-modification system. Evidence reveals that ANXA2 biology is exquisitely tuned by distinct ubiquitin marks. F-box and WD repeat domain containing 10 (FBXW10)-mediated K63-linked ubiquitination drives ANXA2 membrane translocation to assemble the ANXA2–KARS complex, which in turn activates ERK signaling to drive epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) and enhance proliferative capacity [104]. In parallel, ubiquitin protein ligase E3A (UBE3A) polyubiquitinates and degrades Sirtuin 6 (SIRT6), thereby relieving the transcriptional repression of ANXA2 by ELK1 and reinforcing pro-proliferative cues [105]. Conversely, down-regulation of ubiquitin associated protein 2 (UBAP2) attenuates ubiquitin–proteasome-mediated degradation of ANXA2, further fuelling HCC proliferation [106]. This dual-control modality not only clarifies ANXA2 functional diversity in HCC but also cements its role as a central integrator of proliferative signaling. Beyond ubiquitination, Qi et al. further demonstrated that the ANXA2–S100A10 complex activates the Akt/mTOR axis to accelerate HCC growth [107].

Currently, Several latent proliferative pathways merit further investigation: hepatic progenitor cells (HPCs) demonstrate progressive expansion during chronic liver injury, with their abundance correlating positively with disease severity [108]. These HPCs exhibit overlapping biomarker profiles with HCC cells [109, 110] and may constitute cellular precursors for hepatocarcinogenesis. Mechanistically, Glucocorticoid-Induced TNF Receptor-Related Ligand (GITRL) binding to ANXA2 enhances ERK1/2 and AKT phosphorylation, thereby activating EMT programs that drive HPC proliferation and increase malignant transformation potential [111]. Beyond its membrane signaling functions, ANXA2 operates as a Ca2⁺-dependent RNA-binding protein that binds to both the 3’ untranslated region (3’UTR) and internal ribosomal entry site (IRES) of cellular myelocytomatosis oncogene (c-Myc) mRNA, facilitating c-Myc perinuclear localization and translation [112–114]. Targeted delivery of antisense oligonucleotides against the 3’ untranslated region (3’UTR) may effectively reverse the pathological process described above [115]. Accumulating evidence demonstrates that c-Myc overexpression drives both malignant expansion of liver cancer stem cells [116] and HCC progression [117], suggesting ANXA2-mediated c-Myc regulation as a critical proliferative pathway.

These findings provide novel therapeutic strategies for targeting HCC cell proliferation. Precise modulation of ANXA2 ubiquitination dynamics offers a potential strategy to selectively intercept tumor-specific proliferative signaling. Pharmacological inhibition of pro-proliferative E3 ubiquitin ligases such as FBXW10 or UBE3A could effectively disrupt ANXA2-dependent oncogenic signaling cascades, while direct targeting of ANXA2-containing protein complexes may suppress critical downstream cell cycle effectors including CCND1 and c-Myc [114, 118]. A comprehensive understanding of the ubiquitin-mediated regulation of ANXA2’s proliferative functions will be essential for developing targeted therapies against HCC proliferation. Such investigations promise to not only elucidate ANXA2’s central role in driving HCC pathogenesis but may also identify more precise molecular targets for clinical intervention.

Regulatory network of cancer stem cell properties

Cancer stem cells (CSCs) constitute a malignant subpopulation endowed with self-renewal capacity and multipotent differentiation potential [119]. Their self-renewal is tightly linked to sustained tumor proliferation and represents a pivotal mechanism underlying post-surgical recurrence of HCC [120]. Emerging evidence reveals that ANXA2 participates in CSC regulation, as it is co-expressed with well-established stem-cell markers (CD44 and LGR5) in circulating tumor stem cells, indicating its functional involvement in maintaining the CSC phenotype [121]. Current studies have definitively demonstrated a critical role for ANXA2 in governing hepatic cancer stem cells (LCSCs) traits. Within LCSCs, ANXA2 promotes the up-regulation of multiple core stemness regulators—including CD133, OCT3/4, EP300, NANOG and NOTCH1—thereby enhancing HCC stemness [122]. Conversely, its silencing markedly diminishes LCSC self-renewal capacity. Furthermore, it has been confirmed that miR-101/ANXA2/EGR1 constitutes a self-sustaining regulatory loop within LCSC: ANXA2 relieves miR-101-mediated suppression, triggering an ERK phosphorylation cascade that up-regulates SRY-box transcription factor 2 (SOX2) expression and activates key cell-cycle regulatory proteins (e.g., CDKs). Concurrently, phosphorylated ERK down-regulates EGR1 expression, which in turn inhibits miR-101 transcription [123]. This loop not only sustains stemness but also positions the CDKs under its control as essential drivers of cell-cycle progression, providing crucial insight into how ANXA2 coordinates stemness maintenance with rapid proliferation. Additionally, core pluripotency factors such as OCT4 and NANOG have been shown in HCC to promote tumor proliferation by activating multiple proliferative pathways [124, 125], suggesting that ANXA2 may exert broader oncogenic effects in HCC through the regulation of these factors. However, these potential mechanisms await further experimental validation.

ANXA2 functions as a pivotal regulator of HCC invasion and metastasis, driving malignant tumor dissemination through multidimensional mechanisms. Its core regulatory roles encompass: dynamic modulation of cytoskeletal reorganization, activation of EMT programs, remodeling of the tumor microenvironment, and regulation of autophagy-mediated stress adaptation. These coordinated mechanisms synergistically equip HCC cells with comprehensive capabilities to breach tissue barriers and achieve distant metastasis. Cytoskeleton dynamic reorganization

Cytoskeletal reorganization and dynamic remodeling constitute fundamental processes that potentiate tumor cell migration and invasion [126–128]. ANXA2, functioning as a calcium-sensitive cytoskeletal modulator, serves as a critical v-Src substrate during actin rearrangement [129]. Src-mediated phosphorylation at Tyr23 in the ANXA2 N-terminus initiates cofilin-dependent cytoskeletal restructuring [14], a process shown to proceed via the Rho/ROCK signaling axis [97]. This mechanism has been recognized as particularly critical for HCC cell motility. Conversely, cluster of differentiation 147 (CD147) suppresses HCC cell movement–by inhibiting ANXA2 Tyr23 phosphorylation and subsequent RhoA/ROCK cascade activation [130]. Beyond phosphorylation-dependent modulation, Chemokine C-X-C Motif Chemokine Ligand 12 (CXCL12) ligation to its cognate receptor triggers ANXA2 translocation to the plasma membrane, where it engages the ELMO1–Dock180 complex to stimulate actin polymerization in HCC cells [131]. This multilayered regulation positions ANXA2 as a central integrator of both chemical and mechanical signals that collectively determine HCC cell migratory behavior. Notably, in HCC the transcription factor E2 Factor 1 (E2F1) can up-regulate fibronectin type III domain-containing 3B (FND3B) to promote cell migration and motility [132], whereas another study found that FND3B can also bind to ANXA2 phosphorylated at Tyr23 and facilitate actin reorganization [133], suggesting that E2F1-FND3B-ANXA2 may constitute a novel pro-metastatic regulatory axis. Given these mechanistic insights, ANXA2 is contribute critically to HCC metastatic dissemination by orchestrating dynamic actin reorganization.

EMT program activation network and pseudogene synergistic regulation

EMT represents a critical step in tumor metastasis, enabling epithelial-derived tumor cells to acquire mesenchymal characteristics through loss of apical-basal polarity and degradation of intercellular junctions, thereby gaining migratory and invasive capacities [134]. This complex reprogramming involves the orchestrated regulation of a transcription-factor network comprising snail family transcriptional repressor 1 (SNAI1), zinc finger e-box binding homeobox 1/2 (ZEB1/2) and twist family bhlh transcription factor 1/2 (TWIST1/2), which collectively induce the characteristic down-regulation of epithelial markers (e.g., E-cadherin) and the up-regulation of mesenchymal markers (e.g., vimentin) [135]. In HCC, ANXA2 emerges as a pivotal regulator of EMT, coordinating this metastatic cascade through intricate molecular networks while demonstrating functional interplay with pseudogene family members.

At the non-coding RNA level, ANXA2 modulates EMT through distinct regulatory circuits. Circular RNA circ_0021093 is capable of suppressing miR-432, thereby increasing the expression of ANXA2, which ultimately down-regulates E-cadherin and up-regulates Snail1 to propel EMT and accelerate HCC progression [136]. Similarly, the lncRNA-MUF from HCC-associated mesenchymal stem cell (HCC-MSC) physically interacts with ANXA2 to activate Wnt/β-catenin signaling, resulting in Snail1 upregulation and EMT potentiation [137].

Pseudogene regulatory networks have emerged as critical orchestrators of HCC metastasis, shedding their historical classification as genomic “junk” to be recognized as active participants in oncogenic signaling through diverse mechanisms including miRNA sequestration, parental gene modulation, and direct motility enhancement [138–143]. The ANXA2 gene family comprises three functionally distinct pseudogenes (ANXA2P1-P3) that collectively regulate HCC progression. Among them, only ANXA2P1 has been mechanistically delineated in HCC: Acting as a competing endogenous RNA, ANXA2P1 sequesters miR-376a-3p, thereby attenuating miR-376a-3p-mediated repression of ANXA2, elevating ANXA2 expression, and promoting EMT to enhance HCC cell migration and invasion—processes tightly linked to adverse clinical outcomes [144]. ANXA2P1-specific siRNA silencing effectively reverses these effects [144]. Similarly, ANXA2P2 overexpression demonstrates pro-metastatic properties in HCC and exhibits potential as a clinically actionable prognostic biomarker [145]. Although the precise role of ANXA2P3 in HCC pathogenesis remains undefined, its functional characterization in biliary-atresia-induced liver injury implies a potential contribution to hepatic pathology [146]. These findings collectively position the ANXA2-pseudogene network as a sophisticated epigenetic regulator of HCC metastasis, highlighting its therapeutic promise as a targetable EMT-regulatory axis.

Moreover, ANXA2 may also advance EMT progression in HCC through the following potential routes. In HCC, one study has revealed that ANXA2 can complex with the p50 subunit and translocate to the nucleus to potentiate NF-κB transcriptional activity, while the ginsenoside derivative 20(S)-G-Rh2 antagonizes this process in HCC, underscoring the therapeutic potential of targeting the ANXA2–NF-κB axis [147]. Subsequent work in breast cancer has shown that NF-κB can subsequently promote EMT, thereby facilitating metastasis, and G-Rh2 likewise targets ANXA2 to reverse this effect [148]. We therefore postulate that ANXA2 may also activate EMT via the NF-κB pathway to accelerate HCC metastasis. In breast cancer, EGF-dependent phosphorylation of ANXA2 at Tyr23 directly induces phosphorylation and nuclear accumulation of STAT3 at Tyr705, ultimately leading to the up-regulation of Snail family transcriptional repressor 2 (Slug) and the suppression of E-cadherin during tumor metastasis [149]. Meanwhile, Lei Tang et al. have demonstrated that the synergistic high expression of ANXA2 and STAT3 is closely associated with poor recurrence and survival in HCC patients [26]. Importantly, G-Rh2 can also inhibit the interaction and phosphorylation between ANXA2 and EGF, thereby suppressing the migratory capacity of HepG2 cells toward human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) [150]. Microenvironmental regulation of HCC metastasis

The tumor microenvironment exerts a critical influence on HCC metastasis, and ANXA2, serving as an essential regulatory molecule, orchestrates a dual role by coordinating pathogen-response and stroma-remodeling programs. In pathogen response, studies have definitively established that the surface protein P37 of Mycoplasma hyorhinis specifically binds to the N-terminal domain of ANXA2 [151]. This interaction triggers ROS accumulation through mitochondrial transcription factor A (TFAM)-suppression-induced mitochondrial dysfunction, thereby activating Sp1-dependent transcriptional upregulation of ANXA2. Activation of this pathway ultimately enhances HCC cell migration and invasion via P37-mediated NF-κB signaling [152]. In extracellular-matrix (ECM) remodeling, ANXA2 overcomes the ECM/basement-membrane barrier through multifaceted regulation of MMPs. As a plasminogen-activation scaffold, the ANXA2 heterotetramer (A2t) has been experimentally confirmed to catalyze plasmin generation and activate MMP precursors, thereby amplifying metastatic potential [153, 154]. The long non-coding RNA lung cancer associated transcript 1 (lncRNA LUCAT1) stabilizes this complex by inhibiting ANXA2 phosphorylation at Ser25, sustaining plasmin-mediated MMP-9 activation to propel HCC progression [88]. A particularly intriguing feature is the bidirectional regulatory network between ANXA2 and CD147: On the one side, ANXA2–CD147 interaction has been shown to synergistically up-regulate MMP-2 expression [155], and subsequent work revealed that this cooperation involves microvesicle-mediated delivery of the complex to activate fibroblast-secreted MMP-2 [156]. On the other side, CD147–ANXA2 attenuates ANXA2-Tyr23 phosphorylation, thereby up-regulating dedicator of cytokinesis 3 (DOCK3) to drive β-catenin nuclear translocation and activate the WASF2–WAVE2 pathway, ultimately promoting HCC metastasis [157]. The monoclonal anti—HCC antibody 131I—metuximab, which specifically recognizes the CD147 antigen on HCC cells, may possess potential therapeutic value [158].

Moreover, emerging evidence suggests that ANXA2 may participate in the regulation of HCC metastasis through the following additional mechanisms: A gastric-cancer study indicates that formation of the P37–ANXA2–EGFR ternary complex may facilitate ANXA2-Tyr23 phosphorylation and thereby activate the NF-κB signaling axis [151]. Given the pivotal role of the EGFR pathway in HCC metastasis [159], we postulate that this regulatory circuitry may also operate in HCC. Notably, Emerging evidence implicates ANXA2 and MMP13 as collaborative drivers in metabolism-driven HCC progression [160], and a separate study demonstrates that leukemia inhibitory factor receptor (LIFR) deficiency promotes HCC metastasis via the PI3K/AKT–MMP13 axis [161], hinting that ANXA2 may engage this newly identified signaling network to modulate HCC metastasis. Collectively, these findings establish ANXA2 as a master microenvironmental regulator that integrates pathogen response with ECM degradation through distinct yet synergistic pathways. This “dual-pathway coordination” presents compelling therapeutic opportunities for targeting HCC metastasis at its microenvironmental roots.

ANXA2-autophagy axis regulates HCC metastatic stress adaptation

Autophagy represents a fundamental cellular homeostasis mechanism that displays context-dependent roles in tumor progression—suppressing tumorigenesis in early stages while facilitating metastatic adaptation to environmental stresses like hypoxia and nutrient deprivation in advanced cancers [162–164]. Emerging evidence suggests ANXA2-mediated autophagy regulation may operate through conserved mechanisms across diverse cell types. During macroautophagy initiation, ANXA2 promotes phagophore assembly by directing phosphatidylserine and phosphatidylinositol recruitment to Atg16L⁺ vesicles and facilitating their homotypic fusion [165]. Starvation-induced JNK-c-Jun signaling upregulates ANXA2 expression, which subsequently orchestrates autophagy related 9A (ATG9A) vesicle trafficking from endosomes to autophagosomes through actin cytoskeleton remodeling to drive autophagosome biogenesis [166]. The A2t complex similarly functions as a molecular switch for interferon-induced autophagy by controlling unc-51 like autophagy activating kinase 1 (ULK1) subcellular localization [167]. These mechanistic insights establish a conceptual framework for understanding ANXA2’s involvement in tumor-related autophagy.

A recent study further demonstrates that, in an HCC lung-metastasis model, ANXA2 markedly enhances circulating tumor cells’ resistance to anoikis through autophagy activation, thereby augmenting their metastatic colonization efficiency [168]. This finding provides direct evidence supporting the function of ANXA2 in HCC metastasis. Nevertheless, the precise mechanisms by which ANXA2 governs autophagy during HCC metastasis remain under active investigation. Although the ANXA2–autophagy regulatory network has been extensively investigated in other tumor systems—such as Heat Shock transcription factor 1 (HSF-1) phosphorylation-dependent upregulation of ATG7 in triple-negative breast cancer [169], SEC23A-mediated subcellular localization of ANXA2 in gastric cancer [170], and tripartite motif containing 21 (TRIM21)-facilitated dissociation of the ANXA2–transcription factor EB (TFEB) complex in osteosarcoma [171]—the specific molecular circuitry through which ANXA2 governs autophagy in HCC remains a conspicuous knowledge gap. Existing evidence suggests that ANXA2 may participate in autophagy regulation during HCC metastasis via multiple mechanistically unclear pathways. Further exploration of this direction will furnish new perspectives for understanding the mechanisms underlying HCC metastasis. Future investigations should focus on experimentally validating the relative contributions of these potential pathways in HCC metastatic dissemination.

ANXA2-mediated mechanisms of HCC treatment

HCC presents two fundamental clinical challenges: diagnostic limitations in early-stage detection and pervasive therapeutic resistance. The insidious nature of early HCC symptoms results in merely 10–15% of cases being identified at potentially curable stages amenable to liver transplantation or surgical resection [172]. Most patients present with advanced disease at diagnosis, exhibiting universal resistance to conventional systemic therapies spanning chemotherapy, radiotherapy, immune checkpoint inhibitors, and anti-angiogenic agents. ANXA2 emerges as a pivotal molecular orchestrator of this multifactorial resistance, coordinating key resistance mechanisms through apoptosis evasion, endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress and autophagy balance, cancer stem cell maintenance, and ferroptosis suppression. Molecular mechanisms of ANXA2 in promoting HCC chemoresistance

Apoptosis evasion and EMT synergy

ANXA2 orchestrates HCC chemoresistance through convergent regulation of apoptosis evasion and EMT pathways. Mechanistically, ANXA2 binds the NF-κB p50 subunit to promote nuclear translocation and pathway activation, thereby up-regulating anti-apoptotic genes (e.g., IL-6) and inhibitors of apoptosis proteins [173]. This conserved mechanism mirrors chemoresistance patterns observed in pancreatic cancer and pediatric neuroblastoma [174, 175]. Notably, natural compounds such as ginsenosides Rk1/Rg5 have demonstrated therapeutic potential in HCC by disrupting the ANXA2–p50 interaction [173]. Additionally, ANXA2-driven modulation of the EMT program constitutes another critical route through which it confers chemoresistance in HCC. ANXA2 not only binds to lncRNA-MUF to activate Wnt/β-catenin signaling and subsequently promote EMT [137], but also upregulates EMT-related transcription factors in an NF-κB-dependent manner [148], thus establishing a multi-layered regulatory network controlling EMT. This regulatory complexity associates with resistance to oxaliplatin, doxorubicin and sorafenib [176–178].

Microenvironmental contributions emerge through Mycoplasma p37-ANXA2 interactions that confer resistance to gemcitabine, mitoxantrone and cisplatin [28]. The A2PP peptide effectively blocks this interaction [28]. As previously mentioned, ANXA2-p37 can promote NF-κB-mediated HCC metastasis, which may be closely related to resistance development [152]. Whether NF-κB subsequently participates in this infection-related resistance process through EMT pathways remains to be elucidated. These findings position ANXA2 as a nodal integrator of cell-intrinsic and microenvironmental resistance mechanisms, offering a promising multitarget for overcoming HCC chemoresistance.

ER stress and autophagy balance

Crosstalk has been identified between ER stress and autophagy [179]. It has been reported that ANXA2 can drive protective autophagy through SEC23A-mediated membrane trafficking, inhibit 5-fluorouracil (5-FU)-induced ER stress-associated apoptosis, thereby enhancing chemoresistance in gastric cancer [170]. In HCC, inhibiting cellular autophagy has been proven to enhance ER stress-induced apoptosis during chemotherapy [180]. As previously mentioned, ANXA2 may be an inducer of autophagy under hypoxic conditions, and autophagy under such conditions has also been found to promote HCC-associated resistance [181]. Notably, SEC23A-mediated secretome promotes HCC with bile duct tumor thrombus (HCC-BDTT) development [182], suggesting a broader role in HCC progression. These findings collectively support the hypothesis that ANXA2 enhances chemoresistance in HCC by promoting autophagy-mediated attenuation of ER stress–induced apoptosis.

Cancer stem cell regulation

LCSC can also play an important role in the progression of chemoresistance in HCC through an ANXA2-dependent mechanism. nuclear receptor binding protein 2 (NRBP2) downregulation enhances ANXA2-mediated Akt phosphorylation, altering the Bax/Bcl-2 ratio and upregulating stemness-related genes (CD133, OCT3/4, EP300, NANOG, NOTCH1), thereby reinforcing drug resistance—an effect reversible by NRBP2 restoration or Akt inhibition [122]. Therapeutic strategies targeting LCSC plasticity show promise in overcoming resistance. The anti-ANXA2 monoclonal antibody mAb150 enhances chemosensitivity by suppressing CSC self-renewal [183], while dual-frequency low-intensity ultrasound therapy reduces stemness properties and restores drug vulnerability through LCSC differentiation [184]. These findings highlight ANXA2 as a nodal regulator of LCSC-driven resistance, offering multiple actionable targets for therapeutic intervention.

Redox and ferroptosis regulation

ANXA2 serves as a redox regulator whose depletion induces ROS accumulation [185]. Additionally, the accumulation of ROS has been found to influence tumor resistance through DNA repair, metabolic reprogramming, and cell death pathways [186]. In HCC models, ROS elevation consistently correlates with reduced chemoresistance [187–189]. This relationship gains particular significance in ferroptosis—the iron-dependent cell death mechanism underlying sorafenib’s therapeutic effects. Enhanced ferroptosis not only overcomes sorafenib resistance [190–192], but also potentiates its synergistic antitumor activity with sodium butyrate [193]. Employing ferroptosis inducers like Erastin could serve as a viable approach to mitigate drug resistance in HCC [194]. A critical connection exists between hepatic ROS generation and ferroptosis induction via lipid peroxidation [195–197]. Given ANXA2’s classification as a ferroptosis-related gene [198], these findings strongly implicate its involvement in regulating HCC treatment resistance through ROS-ferroptosis crosstalk. This evidence positions ANXA2 as a crucial node linking redox homeostasis and regulated cell death pathways, revealing promising therapeutic targets for overcoming HCC treatment resistance.

ANXA2-mediated radiation resistance mechanisms in HCC

ANXA2’s dynamic subcellular localization is intimately linked to tumor radioresistance. Although it is predominantly localized to the cytoplasm and plasma membrane, it possesses a nucleocytoplasmic shuttling capacity. Emerging evidence indicates that exogenous ROS can, through distinct mechanisms, play pivotal roles in both nuclear accumulation and extracellular release of ANXA2, thereby fostering radiation resistance. ANXA2’s intrinsic nuclear export signal (NES) binds chromosome region maintenance 1 (CRM1) to export nuclear ANXA2 [199, 200]. While irradiation-induced ROS, on the one hand, inhibit this interaction to block nuclear export, and on the other hand simultaneously promote nuclear translocation of cytoplasmic ANXA2, ultimately leading to nuclear accumulation. Nuclear-accumulated ANXA2 exerts radioprotective effects by modulating redox homeostasis and enhancing nucleotide excision repair, markedly attenuating radiation-induced DNA damage. Conversely, administration of the antioxidant N-acetylcysteine (NAC) reverses this effect [29, 91, 98, 201].

Extracellular release of ANXA2 is linked to ROS-dependent phosphorylation of distinct ANXA2 pools—ANXA2-Tyr23 within the nucleus is dephosphorylated, whereas cortical ANXA2 associated with F-actin is phosphorylated [91]. Cortical phosphorylated ANXA2 is released from the plasma membrane into the extracellular space, further promoting radioresistance [29]. In addition, autophagy can influence radioresistance: Binding of integrin β6 to ANXA2 inhibits proteasomal degradation of ANXA2, thereby activating autophagy to enhance HCC radioresistance [202]. Notably, stress conditions such as heat shock and hypoxia can phosphorylate ANXA2-Tyr23 and promote its expression on the surface of HUVEC cells [92, 203]. Previously, we considered these environmental factors as crucial inducers of ANXA2-mediated autophagy, implying that the intrinsic mechanism of autophagy-mediated resistance may be coupled to ANXA2 extracellular release—an aspect that awaits future experimental validation.

Therefore, both nuclear accumulation and extracellular release of ANXA2 augment radioresistance in HCC. Based on this, we propose a combinatorial therapeutic strategy integrating oxidative enhancement and DNA-repair blockade: Could future interventions employ β-lapachone (a pro-ROS agent) to induce tumor-selective ROS elevation [204], combined with NAC-mediated antioxidant protection, to spatially potentiate CRM1 inhibition while safeguarding normal tissues? Concurrent administration of poly (ADP-Ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors (e.g., olaparib) may disrupt backup DNA-repair pathways [205]. Such a dual-targeting approach—leveraging ROS-dependent nuclear-trafficking dysregulation alongside synthetic lethality—may establish a new paradigm to overcome radioresistance in ANXA2-overexpressing malignancies.

Role of ANXA2 in HCC immunotherapy resistance

Despite current clinical applications of immune checkpoint inhibitors, adoptive T cell transfer, vaccination and viral therapies [206], or combination therapies like anti-PD-1 plus bevacizumab (anti-VEGF) in HCC [8], resistance remains a growing challenge [207]. Tumor microenvironment-mediated immune evasion may represent the key therapeutic bottleneck. The liver’s unique immunosuppressive microenvironment (inhibiting T cell-mediated antigen responses and inducing T cell tolerance through regulatory T cells (Tregs), dendritic cells (DCs) and liver sinusoidal endothelial cells) provides structural basis for HCC immune evasion [208, 209]. Emerging evidence identifies ANXA2 as a pivotal node in tumor immune regulatory networks, mediating immune escape through multidimensional reshaping of the immune microenvironment to drive treatment resistance. For example, in renal cell carcinoma, ANXA2 overexpression-mediated immune evasion correlates closely with resistance to immune checkpoint inhibitors and tyrosine kinase inhibitors [210]. ANXA2 also promotes immunosuppression in nasopharyngeal carcinoma by interacting with DC-SIGN to impair DC maturation and disrupt IL-12/IL-10 secretion balance [211].

In HCC, one study has identified that ANXA2 activates the immune checkpoint network (PD-1/CTLA-4/CD80/CD86), increases the Treg-cell fraction, blocks dendritic-cell maturation, and suppresses natural killer (NK) cell cytotoxicity, thereby crippling innate immunity. Concomitantly, it markedly lowers effector cytokines such as TNF-α/IFN-γ while aberrantly up-regulating inhibitory factors like IL-10/TGF-β—collectively fostering HCC immune evasion [61]. As CD8⁺ T cells represent HCC’s primary antitumor immune cells, another study found high ANXA2 expression in high-risk patients correlates with CD8⁺ T cell functional suppression, impacting overall HCC prognosis [212]. Additionally, β-catenin activation (a known immune evasion mechanism) inhibits recruitment of CD103⁺ DCs (critical antitumor immune cells) to impair CD8⁺ T cell immune surveillance and confer anti-PD-1 resistance [207]. Given ANXA2’s ability to activate β-catenin through related pathways, ANXA2 may impair CD8⁺ T cell function via β-catenin.

Current ANXA2-targeted immunotherapies are being explored in solid tumors. For instance, synergistic application of Listeria-based ANXA2-targeted immunotherapy with anti-PD-1 antibodies enhances pancreatic cancer treatment efficacy [213]. ANXA2-specific chimeric antigen receptor T cells (CAR-T) also show therapeutic potential in ovarian cancer [214]. Targeting ANXA2 to inhibit immune microenvironment remodeling-mediated immune escape may provide novel paradigms for overcoming HCC immunotherapy resistance. Future work should further elucidate underlying mechanisms to guide development of precision immunocombination therapies.

Scientific challenges and translational opportunities in targeting ANXA2 for HCC therapy

Research gaps and challenges in ANXA2’s role in HCC etiology

The precise involvement of ANXA2 across liver disease progression to HCC remains incompletely understood. While cirrhosis represents the highest HCC risk state [215], and major etiologies including viral hepatitis, ALD, and MAFLD may ultimately progress to cirrhotic transformation [216], ANXA2’s role in these transitions requires further elucidation. Although upregulated in chemically-induced cirrhosis-associated HCC [217], key questions persist regarding ANXA2’s contributions to both fibrotic progression and cirrhosis-to-HCC transformation across different disease etiologies. The protein’s apparent involvement in inflammatory responses, IR, and fibrogenesis suggests it may serve as a common regulatory hub in HCC pathogenesis, warranting systematic investigation across disease stages and etiologies.

Recent spatial-transcriptomic studies have revealed that ANXA2 plays a pivotal role in liver disease. In AH, it was first identified as a specific marker of disease-associated hepatocytes (daHeps). Its high expression exhibits a unique spatially enriched pattern, offering a novel molecular tag for clinical identification of diseased hepatocytes. ANXA2 is co-expressed with CXCL1⁺, CEACAM8⁺, and others within daHeps, and the AH microenvironment shows abnormal increases in cholangiocytes, macrophages, and stellate cells. Moreover, macrophage exosome-transport–related genes were reported for the first time to be markedly up-regulated in AH, providing new clues for dissecting the crosstalk between ANXA2⁺ hepatocytes and the microenvironment [71]. In MAFLD, machine-learning analyses integrating single-nucleus RNA-seq and single-cell RNA-seq data identified ANXA2 as an iron-death–related gene and a potential diagnostic biomarker of MAFLD. ANXA2 is highly expressed in hepatocytes from MAFLD patients and is predominantly expressed by monocytes, macrophages, and epithelial cells, suggesting a potential role of these cell types in ANXA2-mediated MAFLD pathogenesis [198]. In HCC, single-cell sequencing data indicate that targeting ANXA2 can remodel the immunosuppressive microenvironment. Matrine-treated HCC models show that ANXA2 inhibition increases the number of CD8⁺ T-cell, and single-cell transcriptomics confirm their functional activation, linked to regulation of the ANXA2–HSP90–MHC-I antigen-presentation axis, thus providing a molecular basis for combination immunotherapy [30]. In liver regeneration, integrated single-cell sequencing and spatial-transcriptomic analyses uncovered a key ANXA2⁺ migratory hepatocyte subpopulation. These cells accumulate at necrotic-zone margins to mediate early wound closure. Four-dimensional intravital imaging captured their migratory process as preceding proliferative regeneration, and they further coordinate with immune macrophages and stellate cells to reshape the injury microenvironment—a finding that offers new therapeutic strategies for acute liver failure [218]. Collectively, these results delineate a dynamic expression atlas of ANXA2 in liver disease, manifesting stage-specific expression patterns. At present, single-cell and spatial-transcriptomic investigations of ANXA2 in liver-disease models remain scarce. Although it has been shown to exhibit enrichment in specific pathological states, whether ANXA2 is significantly enriched in other liver-related cells—such as hepatic stellate cells, Kupffer cells, and cancer stem cells—remains to be explored in future studies.

As a prognostic biomarker, ANXA2 demonstrates variable performance across HCC subtypes. While some studies report limited prognostic value in HBV-related HCC regardless of cirrhosis status [219, 220], these inconsistencies likely reflect confounding variables including etiology, population genetics, and methodological differences. Current limitations include the absence of etiology-specific prognostic frameworks and demographic-stratified analyses. Addressing these challenges requires large-scale, multi-ethnic cohorts and advanced computational approaches integrating molecular and clinical data to optimize ANXA2’s clinical utility.

Research bottlenecks and potential pathways in ANXA2 molecular networks

Current understanding of ANXA2’s molecular networks in HCC remains incomplete, with several key knowledge gaps hindering progress. A major limitation is the lack of comprehensive characterization of ANXA2’s post-translational modification landscape. While K63-linked ubiquitination has been shown to stabilize ANXA2, the specific lysine residues (e.g., K48, K11, K29) responsible for its proteasomal degradation remain unidentified [104]. The ANXA2-autophagy axis, though implicated in other cancers under metabolic stress, requires further investigation in the context of HCC metastasis. Emerging evidence points to ANXA2’s role as a molecular interface between microbial pathogens and HCC pathogenesis. Beyond mycoplasma hyorhinis, recent studies demonstrate that Mycoplasma bovis utilizes its LppA adhesin to bind ANXA2, enhancing host cell adhesion while evading immune detection and promoting plasmin generation [221, 222]. These findings position ANXA2 as a potential mediator of infection-associated HCC progression, though mechanistic details await clarification.

ANXA2 orchestrates diverse signaling pathways that collectively drive HCC progression. The protein stimulates tumor cell proliferation through c-Myc pathway activation. Additionally, the E2F1-FND3B-ANXA2 axis may represent a novel pro-metastatic regulatory axis that reorganizes the actin cytoskeleton and enhances metastatic potential. In HCC’s characteristic EGFR-hyperactive environment, phosphorylation at ANXA2-Tyr23 emerges as a critical event that triggers EMT programs through EGFR engagement.Intriguing parallels exist between this phosphorylation-dependent mechanism and Mycoplasma P37 protein-induced ANXA2/EGFR complex formation, with both pathways potentially converging on NF-κB activation to promote tumor progression. However, the temporal dynamics of these interactions during HCC evolution and their potential cross-regulation remain poorly understood. Additionally, The repertoire of ANXA2-matrix metalloproteinase interactions (including MMP7/13/14) requires systematic exploration [223, 224], as does the exploration of the capacity of the C-reactive protein (CRP)-protein interaction network that encompasses ANXA2 to activate the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway and thereby promote HCC progression [225]. These unresolved questions highlight the complexity of ANXA2’s signaling integration in HCC pathogenesis.

ANXA2 emerges as a central regulator of redox homeostasis and multiple cell death pathways, offering promising therapeutic avenues for HCC. The redox-sensitive Cys-8 residue detects H2O2 signals, positioning ANXA2 as a critical determinant of cellular fate through ROS modulation [185, 226]. Its downregulation triggers ROS accumulation. Additionally, several studies have demonstrated that accumulated ROS inhibits the progression of HCC by inducing ferroptosis [195–197]. Notably, ANXA2’s established role as a ferroptosis-related gene and its association with MAFLD [198] provide valuable insights into the ANXA2-ROS-ferroptosis regulatory axis. Furthermore, ANXA2 participates in cellular senescence and pyroptosis regulation [227], while also serving as a disulfidptosis-related gene with prognostic value in HCC [228]. The protein’s dual role in disulfidptosis and immunotherapy response suggests potential for combination therapies pairing immune checkpoint inhibitors with disulfidptosis inducers (e.g., glucose deprivation agents), though clinical validation remains pending. Additionally, the spatiotemporal regulation of its involvement across different cell death pathways requires clarification, as does the potential functional interplay between ANXA2 and its pseudogene family members—particularly the uncharacterized ANXA2P3 in HCC. Understanding whether these pseudogenes epigenetically regulate parental gene expression could reveal new layers of complexity in ANXA2’s regulatory network.

In recent years, the regulatory role of gut microbiota in the progression of HCC has garnered increasing attention. Multiple studies have demonstrated that bacteria such as Enterococcus spp., Streptococcus spp., and Bacteroides spp. exhibit markedly elevated abundance in tissues from hepatitis, ALD, MAFLD and HCC, displaying oncogenic potential—yet the specific mechanisms underlying this remain elusive [229–231]. Currently, the “Gut-Liver Axis” is recognized as a critical bidirectional communication bridge between the gut (including its microbiota) and the liver [232]. ANXA2 likely plays a pivotal role in this axis by facilitating bacterial translocation from the gut to the liver and thereby exerting oncogenic effects.

Kaili Fu et al. demonstrated that TMPC, a surface protein of Streptococcus anginosus, can bind to the ANXA2 receptor on gastric epithelial cells. This interaction promotes bacterial adhesion and colonization, activates the MAPK signaling pathway, and induces the production of proinflammatory chemokines—ultimately leading to impairment of gastric barrier function and gastric tumorigenesis [233]. This finding holds significant implications: As a host surface protein, ANXA2 has been reported to facilitate the invasion of epithelial cells by various bacteria, including Salmonella typhi, Staphylococcus aureus, and Pseudomonas aeruginosa [234–236]. Notably, ANXA2 is also expressed in intestinal epithelial cells [237, 238]. Recent studies have further confirmed that Enterococcus faecalis and Streptococcus anginosus promote the growth and metastasis of HCC. Gavage experiments demonstrated that these bacteria primarily disseminate via the portal vein bloodstream and migrate to the liver through the Gut-Liver Axis [239]. Activation of the MAPK signaling pathway has been shown to induce intestinal barrier dysfunction, which is likely one of the key mechanisms enabling microbial translocation along the Gut-Liver Axis [232, 240, 241]. Thus, Streptococcus anginosus in the gut may similarly utilize the TMPC–ANXA2–MAPK axis to achieve gut-to-liver translocation. On the other hand, the A2t complex composed of ANXA2 and S100A10 on the surface of vascular endothelial cells promotes plasmin generation. Through fibrin degradation, plasmin maintains unobstructed portal venous blood flow, which may further facilitate the hematogenous dissemination of bacteria [242, 243].

Studies have confirmed that Enterococcus faecalis and Streptococcus anginosus that reach the liver can induce EMT and establish an immunosuppressive microenvironment, thereby promoting HCC growth and metastasis [239]. Additionally, chronic inflammation, progression of liver fibrosis, dysbiosis, and bacterial metabolites are also recognized as important mechanisms by which gut microbiota mediate HCC development via the Gut-Liver Axis [244]. However, current research on the specific mechanisms underlying the “gut microbiota–ANXA2–HCC” axis is still in its preliminary stages, necessitating more in-depth investigations.

In summary, the ANXA2-microbiota axis can serve as a feasible therapeutic target for the prevention and treatment of HCC. For bacteria with procarcinogenic potential such as Streptococcus anginosus, the use of combined interventional strategies including antibiotic treatment (e.g., gentamicin) [239], sgRNA-mediated ANXA2 gene silencing [233], and MAPK pathway inhibitors (e.g., 23-hydroxybetulinic acid) [245] may exhibit enhanced therapeutic effects. Future studies could evaluate whether plasmin inhibitors (e.g., ε-aminocaproic acid) can inhibit hematogenous bacterial dissemination by reducing excessive fibrinolysis [246]. Furthermore, reshaping gut microbiota homeostasis via probiotics or fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) [244], combined with lifestyle interventions such as a balanced diet and immune modulation, and integrated with individualized targeted therapy strategies, represents a more promising approach for HCC prevention and treatment.

Development of innovative therapeutic strategies and clinical application prospects

Exosome and microenvironment modulation strategies

Recent advances have established ANXA2 as a promising therapeutic target in HCC, with several novel intervention approaches demonstrating potential clinical value. Mesenchymal stem cell-derived exosomes (including GPEG-EXOs and OP-EXOs) effectively mitigate liver ischemia–reperfusion injury (IRI) through ANXA2 and S100A9 downregulation [247]. This finding carries particular significance as IRI not only drives postoperative HCC recurrence [248], but also facilitates circulating tumor cell dissemination and angiogenesis [249], suggesting exosome-based therapies could modify the tumor microenvironment to reduce recurrence risk.Parallel research in B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia reveals a microenvironment-dependent ANXA2 activation mechanism, where IL-6 stimulates hepatocyte-derived plasminogen conversion to plasmin via ANXA2, promoting tumor progression [89]. The antifibrinolytic agent EACA successfully inhibits this pathway, offering a potential therapeutic strategy against ANXA2-mediated plasmin system activation in HCC metastasis. These findings collectively highlight ANXA2’s dual role in both microenvironment modulation and protease activation, presenting multiple actionable targets for therapeutic development.

ANXA2-based molecular targeted therapies

The identification of ANXA2 as a therapeutic target has spurred development of several promising intervention approaches. Natural compounds like baicalin exhibit specific antitumor activity through ANXA2 binding [250], providing a structural blueprint for selective inhibitor design. The near-infrared dye-fetal bovine serum conjugate LS301 specifically binds to cell surface Tyr23-phosphorylated ANXA2 [251].Beyond its application in monitoring diagnosis and treatment of inflammation-related diseases, its unique chemical structure endows it with potential as a drug delivery vehicle, paving a novel pathway for developing ANXA2-targeted theranostic agents for HCC.

Glycosylation patterns of ANXA2 present additional therapeutic opportunities. Elevated glycosylation levels observed in HCV-associated HCC and H22 hepatoma cells [59, 252] suggests this modification could serve as an immunological target. The success of glycosylation-specific monoclonal antibody 2448 in tracking breast cancer EMT progression [253] supports developing similar antibodies for HCC immunotherapy. A synthetic cell-penetrating peptide offers another innovative approach by binding to and inhibiting ANXA2 to disrupt tumor cell motility mechanisms [254], though their efficacy in HCC requires further validation. These diverse strategies collectively demonstrate ANXA2’s versatility as a therapeutic target, spanning small molecule inhibitors, antibody-based therapies, and peptide antagonists. Each approach capitalizes on distinct aspects of ANXA2 biology while addressing different clinical needs in HCC management.

Therapeutic strategies targeting the ANXA2-NF-κB signaling axis

The ANXA2-p50 interaction represents a crucial molecular interface governing HCC metastasis and therapeutic resistance. Emerging pharmacological approaches targeting this axis demonstrate multi-faceted therapeutic potential. Several classes of compounds—including thymosin-β4 [255], Dendrobin A [256], and fenofibrate [257]—effectively disrupt NF-κB signaling by inhibiting p50/p65 nuclear translocation or expression. Notably, Dendrobin A (a bioactive phytochemical) suppresses HCC progression through this mechanism, while thymosin-β4 and fenofibrate show additional benefits in mitigating liver fibrosis and chronic hepatitis-related inflammation, respectively.

Natural product derivatives exhibit particularly promising specificity for p50 inhibition. Both Atractylodes macrocephala ethanol extracts (Am-EE) [258] and 4-hydroxybenzo[d]oxazol-2(3H)-one (HBO) [259] demonstrate selective activity against p50. Historical precedents further support this approach, with the coumarin derivative (7-Carbethoxyamino-2-oxo-2H-chromen-4-yl)methylpyrrolidine-1 carbodithioate (CPP) [260] and Tribulus terrestris linn (TT) [261] extracts showing HCC-suppressive effects via p50 modulation. These compounds collectively provide a diverse chemical foundation for developing multi-target therapies against the ANXA2-NF-κB pathway.

Nanotechnology and radionuclide theranostic integration strategies

Recent years have seen nanomaterials demonstrate great potential in the field of cancer biomarker detection, with studies reporting two nanosensing platforms based on hydrogen-substituted graphdiyne (HsGDY) for the ultrasensitive detection of the HCC biomarker ANXA2. Their detection results are highly consistent with those of clinical samples obtained via the ELISA method. In the future, this nanoplatform can serve as a liquid biopsy tool: when combined with circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) detection, it can be used to construct a multimodal early warning system for HCC. Furthermore, by utilizing its unique porous structure to load ANXA2-targeted drugs, it is expected to enable theranostic strategies [262, 263]. Shen et al.’s cyclic octapeptide probe demonstrates remarkable specificity for phosphorylated ANXA2 when conjugated with near-infrared dyes, establishing a promising platform for radionuclide theranostics. This probe design maintains binding affinity in calcium-rich tumor microenvironments while leveraging macrophage-mediated transport to overcome tumor heterogeneity barriers [264].The clinical potential of these technologies extends to both diagnostic and therapeutic applications. 68Ga-labeled derivatives of these probes could enable detection of early HCC’s microlesions through Positron Emission Tomography (PET) imaging, while 177Lu/225Ac conjugates may provide more precise internal radiation therapy.

Optimizing the targeting specificity of radionuclide probes in cirrhotic backgrounds, exploring "theranostic integration" approaches combining 68Ga diagnostic PET with 177Lu/225Ac therapeutic radionuclides, and developing synergistic combination strategies with existing systemic therapies will provide HCC patients with comprehensive solutions from early diagnosis to precision treatment. This innovative model integrating nanotechnology with nuclear medicine has the potential to reshape clinical practice in HCC diagnosis and treatment.

Challenges and opportunities in future research directions

ANXA2-targeted intervention holds promise in HCC therapy. However, its clinical translation confronts some challenges rooted in the complexity of its multi-system physiological functions. For instance, it is necessary to optimize the targeting specificity of the HsGDY nanoplatform to distinguish ANXA2 expression between tumor and normal tissues, while also addressing the issue of dynamic balance between detection function and drug loading/unloading in its theranostic application. Furthermore, when this platform is combined with existing therapeutic modalities (e.g., immune checkpoint inhibitors), it is essential to establish precise delivery timing and dosage regulation strategies to achieve synergistic therapeutic effects. Etiological heterogeneity necessitates further clarification of ANXA2-specific mechanisms across distinct HCC subtypes. Future efforts should integrate patient-derived organoids and organ-on-chip technologies to dissect its dynamic regulatory networks and employ CRISPR-Cas9 tools for precise editing of ANXA2 epigenetic marks. The microbiota-host interaction field (e.g., the binding of Mycoplasma P37/LPPA proteins to ANXA2) merits attention, while the synergistic effects of immunotherapy combined with metabolic interventions (disulfidptosis targeting) or agents (baicalein, Dendrobin A) require validation.

Cross-system safety is another critical bottleneck. In the nervous system, ANXA2 modulates the suprachiasmatic-nucleus circadian rhythm via interaction with S100A10 [265], and its perturbation may disrupt circadian homeostasis; in the cardiovascular system, it functions as a plasminogen-conversion platform, with EPAC1-regulated Tyr23 phosphorylation maintaining fibrinolytic balance, and its inhibition may elevate thrombotic risk [266]. In pulmonary microvasculature, it prevents hypoxia-induced vascular leakage by stabilizing the cadherin-phosphatase complex, its functional loss precipitates pulmonary edema [267], whereas in the reproductive/neuroendocrine systems, crotonylation and Tyr23 phosphorylation of ANXA2 regulate oocyte maturation and Ca2⁺-dependent exocytosis in neuroendocrine cells, respectively [96, 268], so its blockade may disrupt associated reproductive and neuroendocrine functions. These findings caution that systemic ANXA2 inhibition could elicit widespread adverse effects and underscore the urgency of developing liver-specific delivery systems. Future investigations must balance therapeutic benefit against risk through multidimensional assessments to advance ANXA2-targeted therapy as a safe pillar of precision medicine for HCC.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this systematic review elucidates the pivotal regulatory role of ANXA2 in the pathogenesis and progression of HCC. Current evidence demonstrates that ANXA2 promotes HCC proliferation, metastasis, and therapy resistance through multiple molecular mechanisms, establishing it as a highly promising therapeutic target. Significantly, ANXA2-targeting strategies (including the modulation of exosome and microenvironment, regulation of the ANXA2-NF-κB axis, molecular targeted therapy, nanotechnology applications, and radionuclide theranostics) hold considerable potential. These findings not only deepen our understanding of HCC pathological mechanisms but also provide novel approaches to overcome current therapeutic limitations. Future research should focus on optimizing the delivery efficiency and clinical application of ANXA2-targeted therapies to advance precision medicine in HCC treatment.

Sowing depth stability analysis and test verification of no-tillage planter parallel 4-bar row unit
Authors: Jiajie Shang, Dongmei Liu, Xingtao Song, Yibin Han, Sitong He, Ying Zhang, Liyi Liu, Haitao Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The sowing depth stability and consistency of the no-tillage planter, particularly the high-speed operation, are poor. A reason for this is that the structure and parameter selection of no-tillage planter row unit profiling mechanism are unreasonable. The stability and consistency of sowing depth is beneficial when the upper and lower links of the parallel 4-bar profiling mechanism (PFPM) are parallel to the ground, but this principle is not satisfied on the uneven no-till soil. To improve the stability of gauge wheels downforce (GWD) and sowing depth of no-tillage planter row unit. The variation law of spring additional downforce (SAD) with tilt angle of parallel 4-bar profiling mechanism (PFPM) and connection offset of spring is discussed by constructing the steady state analysis matrix equation of PFPM. Additionally, the influence of tilt angle of PFPM and SAD on the stability of the sowing depth was analyzed. Based on the single factor control variable test method, bench and field tests were conducted. The test results show that the SAD increases nonlinearly with the increase in the degree of tilt of PFPM from downward to upward. Under the condition that four springs are installed and the tilt angle of PFPM is between (−20°–20°), the maximum SAD is 485.95 N, 1236.64 N, and 2258.05 N when the spring connection offset is 44 mm, 79 mm, and 115 mm, respectively. When the tilt angle is 8° upward, the GWD increases by 429 N, 575 N, and 984 N, GWD stability increases by 42.31 %, 56.16 %, and 50.29 %, and ditching depth stability increases by 30.00 %, 12.50 %, and 30.00 % compared with that of the control group at the sowing speed of 8, 10, and 12 km/h, respectively. At the sowing speed of 10 km/h, when the PFPM is operated with spring-free and additional springs, the average GWD increases by 353 N, and the stability of GWD and ditching depth increases by 34.38 % and 20.00 %, respectively. The results showed that the tilt upward of PFPM and SAD were beneficial to improve the stability of the GWD and sowing depth of the no-tillage planter row unit. The results not only verify the correctness of theoretical analysis, but also provide theoretical basis for the design and application adjustment of high-speed no-tillage planter.

Introduction

The stability and consistency of sowing depth are important indicators that affect the quality of sowing. Stable and consistent sowing depth can ensure the uniformity and evenness of seedlings, which is conducive to field management, yield and quality improvement in the later stage of crops [[1], [2], [3]]. Presently, a problems of no-tillage planter, particularly the high-speed (≥10 km/h) operation of no-tillage planter, is that its sowing depth stability and consistency are poor. This is because in addition to the usual surface fluctuation factors, the complex and changeable soil physical environment, such as the surface covered by straw, root stubble planted into the soil, soil moisture, and soil penetration resistance, are inconsistent. The abovementioned reasons result in inconsistency of sowing depth for no-tillage planter, and instability problems become particularly prominent [[4], [5], [6], [7]]. The current research on the sowing depth stability of no-tillage planter focuses on two control technologies to improve the sowing depth stability: using semi-active control for stability control [8,9] and using sensors to directly or indirectly measure the sowing depth in real time, and using the active executive component to actively control the downforce of the no-tillage planter row unit to stabilize the sowing depth, including pneumatic control and electro-hydraulic control [[10], [11], [12], [13], [14]]. However, the abovementioned control methods have some problems, such as complex structure, high cost, and poor reliability.

As for the theoretical problems of the influence of PFPM on the stability of sowing depth, there have been many studies by scholars, such as Zhang et al. [15] and Hu [16], who provided the mathematical model of precision planter row unit with PFPM without the spring, and described the relationship between the ditching depth and the geometric characteristics of the surface, structural parameters of row unit, and soil properties. Bao et al. [17] simplified the PFPM into a rigid connection system for the stress analysis of no-tillage planter row unit. Zhao et al. [18] designed a bidirectional profiling mechanism of wheat planter row unit and established a mechanical model of the spring-free. Using Lagrange equations, Bai et al. [19]and Zhang [20] analyzed the stability of the no-tillage planter row unit with tension spring and no spring, respectively. Hao et al. [21], Qian [22], and Fan [23] performed the simulation and optimization analysis of the PFPM, and determined the parameters of each link rod size, traction angle, etc. Chen et al. [24] designed an electro-hydraulic sowing depth adjustment device to adjust the sowing depth, aiming at the problem that the row unit is raised when the corn no-tillage planter works on the ground with large fluctuations, resulting in unqualified sowing depth. Hou et al. [25] designed a two-degree of freedom multi articulated profiling precision row unit to solve the problem of unsatisfactory consistency of seeding depth, and established a mathematical model of seeding trench depth by applying the conservative Lagrange equation. Lv et al. [26] analyzed and designed the PFPM, configuration of gauge wheels, and the ditching device of potato planter and established the mathematical model of the stability of the ditching depth through the dynamic analysis of the sowing depth control device. In summary, there have been many studies on the design, analysis, and seeding depth stability control for no-tillage planter row unit profiling mechanism, from theoretical and simulation analysis to experimental verification. In the theoretical analysis, there are two kinds of methods, one is the classical theoretical mechanics modeling analysis, the other is the analytical mechanics method established by Lagrange. These studies have given the research methods and conclusions of referential significance. However, with the continuous improvement of sowing speed, some problems need to be further analyzed and improved in the models, analysis processes, discussions and conclusions established in the existing literature. For example, in the process of analyzing the installed spring of the PFPM, the tilt and traction angle are not clearly differentiated. Although some literature analyses the special case of spring hanging on the diagonal of PFPM, but it has some limitations. Additionally, the stability and consistency of sowing depth is beneficial when the upper and lower links of the parallel 4-bar profiling mechanism (PFPM) are parallel to the ground, but this principle is not satisfied on the uneven no-till soil.

In this study, we analyze and discuss the variation law of spring additional downforce (SAD) with tilt angle of PFPM and connection offset of spring, and the influence of tilt angle of PFPM and SAD on the stability of the sowing depth through constructing the steady state analysis matrix equation theoretical analysis. Additionally, the single factor control variable test method was used to conduct the bench test and field test for verification. The research objective and contribution of this study is to explore the effect of the structural and application parameters of PFPM on the steady-state characteristics of no-tillage planter to provide a theoretical reference for the design of PFPM of row unit under the condition of no-tillage high-speed operation and the control technology of sowing depth consistency and stability.

Eq. (16) shows that when α = 0, any change in the horizontal binding force (∑Wx) of the soil will not affect the vertical binding force (∑Wz). If α ≠ 0, the influence of the horizontal binding force cannot be ignored. If α < 0, the rear of the link rod BC of the PFPM will tilt upward, and the larger tilt angle or larger the horizontal binding force, the larger the vertical binding force. Contrariwise, the value of the vertical binding force will decrease, even less than the row unit self-gravity.

Therefore, the downforce of the row unit can be adjusted not only by changing the spring parameters, but also by adjusting the initial tilt angle of PFPM. The adjustment characteristics of the two methods are different. During sowing operation, SAD is only related to the tilt angle, and the position adjustment is related to the horizontal binding force. For row unit with light weight or high penetration resistance, the downforce of the row unit can be increased by the PFPM being tilted up towards the rear appropriately to improve soil insertion and z-directional interference resistance.

From Eqs. (4), (16), we obtain:(17)(tanα−tanγ)∑Wx=Fkj

Eq. (17) shows that when Fkj = 0, the traction angle is equal to the tilt angle of the PFPM. When Fkj > 0, the traction angle is less than the tilt angle. However, the traction angle is greater than the tilt angle. This indicates that when SAD is not zero, the tilt angle is not equal to the traction angle. In fact, traction angle and tilt angle are two different concepts with different physical meanings. The traction angle reflects the angle between the traction force and direction of forward motion, which is a mechanical parameter. The tilt angle reflects the angle between the upper or lower link rod and the direction of movement, and is the geometric parameter of PFPM. The two angles should not be confused.

Analysis of the sowing depth stability of no-tillage planter

Analysis of the influence of tilt angle of PFPM on the stability of sowing depth

Under the influence of soil heterogeneity and sowing speed disturbance, the influence of the initial tilt angle of the PFPM on the stability of seeding depth is worth analyzing and discussing.

By taking the partial derivative of ∑Wx from Eq. (16).(18)∂∑Wz∂∑Wx=－tanα

Eq. (18) shows that when the initial tilt angle of PFPM is tilted downward (tilt down towards to the rear of the parallel arms, α ＞ 0), its value is always less than 0, indicating that the change trend of ∑Wz is opposite to that of ∑Wx. In other words, if the row unit of planter encounters stubble or the soil penetration resistance increases (the row unit advance resistance and soil characteristic coefficient increase), the downforce of row unit will decrease accordingly, and the sowing depth will become shallow. Contrariwise, when the soil compactness decreases, the downforce of the row unit and the sowing depth will increase. Therefore, the initial tilt angle, which is tilted downward, is not conducive to the sowing depth stability.

When the initial tilt angle of PFPM is tilted upward (tilt up toward to the rear of parallel arms, α ＜ 0), its value is always greater than 0, indicating that the change trend of ∑Wz is same with that of ∑Wx. In other words, if the row unit of the planter encounters the stubble or the soil compactness increases, the downforce of the row unit will increase accordingly. The change in sowing depth can be suppressed by proper uptilt of the initial tilt angle. However, when the soil compactness decreases, the downforce of the row unit will decrease.

To deal with the disturbance of stubble, soil heterogeneity, and sowing speed, the PFPM can be upward tilted properly, which is conducive to the stability of sowing depth.

Analysis of the influence of SAD on the stability of sowing depth

In the heterogeneity of soil property, especially in the no-tillage area with root stubble, the disturbance of individual advance constraint resistance is large, which makes the sowing depth unstable.

By taking the derivative of α from Eq. (12).(19)dFkjdα=－kH2ΔL2H2+ΔL2cosαL(H2+ΔL2−2HΔLsinα)32Eq. (19) shows that in the range of (−90°, +90°), the numerator term is greater than 0, and the denominator term is always greater than 0. Therefore, dFck/dα<0, thus the change trend of SAD is opposite to the change trend of the initial tilt angle of PFPM.

Therefore, on a flat sowing area, when the sowing depth increases owing to the heterogeneity of soil property, the initial tilt angle of PFPM will be increased, and the SAD will be decreased, i.e., gauge wheels downforce (GWD) will be reduced. SAD inhibits the increase in sowing depth. However, when the soil disturbance makes the sowing depth shallow, SAD will be increased, i.e., the GWD will be increased. The depth of subsidence increases, such that SAD inhibits the shallower sowing depth. Therefore, in a plot with uneven soil quality, SAD is conducive to stable sowing depth.

Test for verification

Bench tests were conducted to study the variation law of SAD with tilt angle and connection offset of the PFPM. Field tests were conducted to study the effects of initial tilt angle of PFPM and spring factors on GWD and stability of sowing depth.

Bench test

2BMG-2 type maize no-tillage planter was used to test the variation of SAD with tilt angle and connection offset. It was implemented in the Engineering Training Center of the College of Engineering, Northeast Agricultural University.

(1) Instruments and equipment

The main instruments and equipment include the universal testing machine (WDW-100E), electric crane (CDZT-6M), force sensor (YZC-516), and hydraulic lift (0086), etc.

(2) Test method

The test includes two parts: the spring stiffness and SAD measurement. The SAD test method is shown in Fig. 5, and the test process is shown in Fig. 6(a). The row unit was raised with an electric crane to allow sufficient clearance of the gauge wheels base floor to push the hydraulic lift and force sensors under the gauge wheels. When the hydraulic lift is adjusted to the required tilt angle of the PFPM, the row unit of the planter is made to fall on the load sensing pressure surface, and GWD is measured and recorded. The change in SAD with the tilt angle and connection offset is shown in Fig. 9.(1)As observed from Table 3 and Fig. 9, SAD is related to the initial tilt angle and connection offset of the PFPM. Conclusion

(1)On a flat sowing area, the stability of sowing depth can be improved by increasing the SAD when the fluctuation in sowing depth is because of the heterogeneity of the soil property.(2)The initial tilt angle of PFPM is upward, which can not only increase the GWD but also effectively improve the stability and consistency of sowing depth. The research results can provide theoretical basis for the design and application adjustment of high-speed no-tillage planter.

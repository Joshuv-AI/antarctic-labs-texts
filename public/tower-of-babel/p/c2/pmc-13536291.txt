Synergistic effects of biodegradable magnesium alloy and erythromycin on osteogenic activity
Authors: Kridtapat Sirisereephap, Kitipong Pasanaphong, Thanaphum Osathanon, Patcharapit Promoppatum, Tomoki Maekawa, Viritpon Srimaneepong, Meircurius Dwi Condro Surboyo, Kamolporn Wattanasirmkit
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background/purposeBiodegradable magnesium (Mg)–based alloys are emerging as promising alternatives to non-resorbable titanium for bone regeneration because of their favorable mechanical properties and biodegradability. However, the interaction between Mg alloys and immunomodulatory agents in promoting osteogenesis remains poorly understood. This study aimed to evaluate the osteogenic effects of an Mg alloy on pre-osteoblasts and to investigate whether erythromycin enhances Mg-induced osteogenic differentiation through regulation of developmental endothelial locus-1 (DEL-1).Materials and methodsWE43 Mg alloy disks were immersed in culture medium to produce Mg alloy-conditioned medium at concentrations of 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100%. MC3T3-E1 cells were treated with conditioned medium alone or combined with erythromycin (20 μg/mL). Cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay. Osteogenic differentiation was evaluated by Alizarin Red S staining (ARS), quantitative PCR analysis of osteogenic markers (Runx2, Sp7, Bglap, Edil3), and Western blot analysis of DEL-1 expression.ResultsWE43 Mg alloy showed no cytotoxic effects on MC3T3-E1 cells. Treatment with Mg alloy-conditioned medium significantly enhanced osteogenic differentiation and extracellular matrix mineralization. The strongest osteogenic response was observed in cells receiving combined WE43 Mg alloy and erythromycin treatment. DEL-1 expression was significantly upregulated at both mRNA and protein levels in Mg alloy-treated groups, accompanied by increased expression of osteogenic markers. These effects were further amplified by erythromycin supplementation.ConclusionWE43 Mg alloy promotes osteogenic differentiation of MC3T3-E1 cells, and erythromycin synergistically enhances this effect, potentially via DEL-1 upregulation. These findings support the combined use of immunomodulatory agents and Mg-based biomaterials in regenerative dentistry.

Introduction

The pursuit of biomaterials that combine biodegradability, mechanical strength, and biological activity is central to advancing regenerative strategies in dentistry. In reconstructive bone surgery, the choice of scaffold material plays a critical role in ensuring both mechanical support and biological integration. Traditionally, titanium (Ti) mesh has been widely used due to its strength, structural reliability and biocompatibility. However, because it is non-resorbable nature, tissue remodeling may be impaired and additional removal procedures may be required. Also, leaving the titanium mesh in bone can lead to long-term complications, including chronic pain, inflammation, and interference of bone remodeling processes.1

Advances in biomedical engineering have brought increased attention to biodegradable metals, which are engineered to gradually degrade in physiological conditions while eliciting favorable host responses to their degradation products. These materials are designed not only to provide temporary mechanical support but also to facilitate tissue regeneration, thereby eliminating the need for a second surgical procedure of the metallic scaffolds. Currently, the principal categories of biodegradable metals suitable for clinical use include alloys based on iron (Fe), zinc (Zn), and magnesium (Mg).

Mg alloys have gained attention due to their favorable mechanical properties and intrinsic biodegradability.2 Mg alloys offer a promising alternative as bioresorbable frameworks. Mg alloys are commonly composed of magnesium and other elements, exhibit mechanical properties more comparable to those of natural bone, gradually degrade in vivo. Mg alloys have shown promise as a potential alternative biomaterial for orthopedic and dental applications owing to their controlled corrosion rate, mechanical similarity to bone, and ability to support osteogenesis. According to previous study, Mg ions have shown the enhancement of cell viability, osteocalcin levels and alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity from human osteoblasts.3 The effects of the Mg alloys on osteogenic differentiation or its regulatory influence on key osteogenic markers at the molecular and cellular levels have not been well examined.

Developmental endothelial locus 3 (Del-1) has emerged as a pivotal molecule in bone biology.4 Del-1 is a secreted extracellular matrix protein involved in cell adhesion and angiogenesis.5 It enhances bone regeneration by promoting osteoblast differentiation6 and inhibiting inflammatory cell infiltration,7 thereby creating a favorable environment for bone repair. Del-1 also mediates integrin signaling to promote mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) recruitment and osteogenic commitment.4 The recent studies reported the immunomodulatory function of erythromycin beyond its antimicrobial properties. Erythromycin has been shown to upregulate EGF-like repeats and Discoidin I-Like Domains 3 (Edil3), in both in vitro and in vivo settings.8 Moreover, erythromycin, 14- and 15-membered macrolide antibiotics such as clarithromycin and azithromycin, respectively, have been reported to exert immunomodulatory effects by upregulating Del-1 expression in both in vitro and in vivo experimental models.9

In this study, we investigated the osteogenic differentiation characteristics of pre-osteoblastic cells treated with combination of Mg alloy and erythromycin. Furthermore, we explore the synergistic effects of erythromycin supplementation to the Mg alloy on cellular responses, with an emphasis on its implications for bone regeneration. Through this integrative approach, we offer fundamental insights into the potential regenerative approaches for reconstructive bone surgery that include the use of biodegradable metals and immunomodulatory drugs.

Materials and methods

Sample preparation

The wrought magnesium alloy (WE43 Mg alloy) from Shanghai Miji Magnesium Industry Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) was used to be investigated. The chemical composition of WE43 Mg alloy is presented in Table 1. The Mg alloy samples were prepared into disk shapes with a diameter of 10 mm, a thickness of 2 mm and an average weight of 316 ± 1.0 mg. All samples were sterilized using autoclave prior to performing the biological test.

Mg-alloy conditioned medium preparation

Mg-alloy conditioned medium was prepared by immersing the WE43 Mg alloy disks in 25 mL of complete medium. The complete medium consisted of Minimum Essential Medium (MEM) (12561056, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) supplemented with 5% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (S-FBS-AU-O15, Serana Europe GmbH, Brandenburg, Germany), 1% l-glutamine (07400522, FUJIFILM Wako Pure Chemical Corporation, Osaka, Japan), and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (16823191, FUJIFILM Wako Pure Chemical Corporation). The immersion was conducted with continuous shaking at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2 for 24 h. After incubation, the resulting Mg alloy-conditioned medium was filtered through a 0.22 μm filter to remove particulate matter.

The obtained Mg alloy-conditioned medium was subsequently diluted with MEM containing 5% FBS to prepare four concentrations: 100% (undiluted), 75%, 50% and 25%. The pH of 4 different Mg alloy-conditioned mediums were measured at room temperature and 37 °C (Table 2). These media were used for MTT assays and osteogenic differentiation experiments.

Cytotoxicity assay

The MTT assay (M2003, Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher Scientific) was employed to evaluate the cytotoxicity of WE43 Mg alloy–conditioned medium. MC3T3-E1 cells, a murine osteoblastic progenitor cell line (RIKEN Bioresource Research Center, Tsukuba, Japan; RCB1126), were cultured according to the supplier’s instructions. pre-osteoblastic MC3T3-E1 cells were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 1 × 104cells per well in 100 μL of MEM complete medium and incubated for 24 h at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2 to allow cell attachment and stabilization. After incubation, the culture medium was aspirated and replaced with 100 μL of the following treatment media: (1) control group: standard MEM complete medium; (2) Mg alloy groups: WE43 Mg alloy–conditioned medium diluted to 25%, 50%, 75%, or 100% with MEM complete medium. Cells were incubated with the respective treatment media for an additional 24 h under the same culture conditions. Subsequently, MTT reagent was added to each well to achieve a final concentration of 0.5 mg/mL and incubated for 4 h to allow for formazan crystal formation. The resulting formazan crystals were dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), and absorbance was measured at 570 nm using a microplate reader (ELx800™, BioTek Instruments, Winooski, VT, USA). Cell viability was expressed as a percentage relative to the control group.

Osteogenic differentiation assay

The osteogenic effects of WE43 Mg alloy and the modulatory role of erythromycin, an in vitro osteogenic differentiation model was established using pre-osteoblastic MC3T3-E1 cells. MC3T3-E1 cells were cultured according to the manufacturer’s instructions and seeded at the appropriate density for differentiation assays. After cell attachment, cultures were maintained in osteogenic induction medium. The osteogenic induction medium contained Minimum Essential Medium (MEM) (12561056, Thermo Fisher Scientific) supplemented with 5% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (S-FBS-AU-O15, Serana Europe GmbH), 10 mM disodium β-glycerophosphate pentahydrate (04834332, FUJIFILM Wako Pure Chemical Corporation), 50 μg/mL ascorbic acid (01204802, FUJIFILM Wako Pure Chemical Corporation). Upon reaching approximately 80% confluency, cells were randomly assigned to the following experimental groups: (1) control group: osteogenic induction medium alone; (2) Mg alloy groups: osteogenic induction medium supplemented with WE43 Mg alloy–conditioned medium (50% and 100%); (3) erythromycin group: osteogenic induction medium supplemented with erythromycin (20 μg/mL); (4) combined treatment groups: osteogenic induction medium supplemented with WE43 Mg alloy –conditioned medium (50% and 100%) in combination with erythromycin (20 μg/mL). Cells were seeded for 3 days with normal culture media and cultured under osteogenic induction conditions until 21 days. Media were refreshed every 48 h throughout the assay. ARS was performed at day 21 of the assay.

ALP activity, as an early marker of osteogenic differentiation, was assessed on day 14 of osteogenic induction. After incubation, MC3T3-E1 cells were washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and fixed according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Cells were then stained using an ALP staining kit (PMC-AK20, Cosmo Bio Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan), and ALP-positive areas were visualized by light microscopy.

Quantitative analysis was performed by measuring the ALP-positive stained area using image analysis software. The stained area was calculated as a percentage of the total culture area and expressed relative to the control group.

ARS and quantification of mineralization

Mineralized nodules formed by differentiated osteoblasts were assessed via ARS. Following two washes with PBS, cells were fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin for 15 min and rinsed with distilled water. Staining was carried out using 2% ARS solution (011–01192, FUJIFILM Wako Pure Chemical Corporation) adjusted to pH 6.3–6.4 and incubated at room temperature for 45 min. Excess stain was removed with multiple washes using distilled water, and stained cultures were imaged using a stereomicroscope (Leica Microsystems, Wetzlar, Germany). The extent of mineral deposition was semi-quantified by analyzing the proportion of calcified area relative to the total well area using ImageJ software (v1.53). For quantitative analysis, the bound dye was eluted using a solution containing 10 mM sodium phosphate and 10% cetylpyridinium chloride (C0732, Sigma–Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) at pH 7.0, incubated at 37 °C for 15 min. Absorbance was measured at 560 nm in triplicate using a microplate reader.

Quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR)

For RNA analysis, MC3T3-E1 cells were harvested on day 14 of osteogenic differentiation, corresponding to the mid-stage of osteogenic maturation. This time point was selected to quantify the transcriptional expression of osteoblast-related genes during lineage commitment and matrix maturation, prior to extensive mineral deposition, as previously characterized in the MC3T3-E1 osteogenic differentiation model.10 Total RNA was isolated from cultured cells using TRI reagent (TR118, Molecular Research Center, Cincinatti, OH, USA), and RNA concentration was determined via Nanodrop One Spectrophotometry (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Complementary DNA (cDNA) synthesis was performed using the SuperScript IV VILO Master Mix (11756050, Thermo Fisher Scientific). The primers including Edil3, Runx2, Bglap, Sp7 (Thermo Fisher Scientific) were used. Quantitative PCR was conducted using the QuantStudio™ 3 Real-Time PCR System (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Relative gene expression levels were analyzed using the comparative Ct (ΔΔCt) method with normalization to Gapdh (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Commercial Taq-Man Gene Expression Assays and primer/probe sequences are proprietary, and the assay IDs are listed Table 3.

Western blot

For protein analysis, MC3T3-E1 cells were harvested on day 14 of osteogenic differentiation, corresponding to the mid-stage of osteogenic maturation. The day 14 time point was selected to capture DEL-1 protein expression during the mid-stage of osteogenic differentiation, when lineage commitment is established but precedes extensive matrix mineralization.10 Cells were washed with PBS and lysed using cell lysis buffer (Cell lysis buffer, 9803, Cell Signaling Technology, Danver, MA, USA) supplemented with a protease inhibitor cocktail (PMSF, 8553, Cell Signaling Technology).

Cell lysates were mixed with LDS Sample Buffer (B0007, Thermo Fisher Scientific), heated at 70 °C for 10 min, separated on a 12% precast polyacrylamide gel (NW00125BOX, Thermo Fisher Scientific), and transferred onto a polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membrane (LC2002, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Following transfer, membranes were blocked with Tris-buffered saline containing 0.1% Tween-20 (TBST) at room temperature for 30 min and then incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against DEL-1 (ab190692, Abcam, Cambridge, UK) and β-actin (5125, HRP Conjugate; Cell Signaling Technology).

After washing, membranes were incubated with horseradish peroxidase–conjugated secondary antibodies (Anti-rabbit IgG, HRP-linked Antibody, 7074, Cell Signaling Technology) for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using a chemiluminescence detection reagent (Amersham™ ECL™ Prime Western Blotting Detection Reagent, P-06158/RPN2236, Cytiva, Marlborough, MA, USA) and detected with an ImageQuant LS400 Mini imaging system (GE Healthcare, Marlborough, MA, USA). Western blot experiments were performed in triplicate. Densitometric analysis was conducted using ImageJ software, and protein expression levels were quantified by normalizing the optical density of each target band to the corresponding β-actin signal.

Statistical analysis

All quantitative data were subjected to statistical evaluation after confirming normality. Intergroup comparisons were performed using One-way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) followed by appropriate post-hoc tests. Analyses were carried out using GraphPad Prism software (version 10.2.3; GraphPad Software, Inc., La Jolla, CA, USA). Statistical significance was accepted at P < 0.05.

Results

WE43 Mg alloy-conditioned medium enhances osteogenic differentiation and mineralization of MC3T3-E1 cells

To evaluate the osteogenic differentiation response of MC3T3-E1 cells, early and late osteogenic markers were assessed using ALP staining at day 14 and ARS staining at day 21 of osteogenic differentiation. ALP staining revealed increased ALP-positive regions in cells treated with WE43 Mg alloy–conditioned medium compared with the control group cultured in osteogenic differentiation medium alone (Fig. 1A). This effect was more pronounced in cultures co-treated with erythromycin. Quantitative analysis revealed a significant increase in the ALP-positive area in the Mg alloy–treated groups, with further enhancement observed upon co-treatment with erythromycin (Fig. 1B).

Consistent with the early osteogenic response, ARS staining performed at day 21 demonstrated increased extracellular matrix mineralization in cultures treated with WE43 Mg alloy–conditioned medium relative to the control group (Fig. 1C). Quantification of mineralized area showed that both 50% and 100% WE43 Mg alloy-conditioned medium significantly increased mineral deposition, with the greatest mineralization observed in the groups receiving 100% WE43 Mg alloy–conditioned medium in combination with erythromycin (P < 0.01) (Fig. 1D). Together, these results indicate that WE43 Mg alloy–conditioned medium enhances osteogenic differentiation and matrix mineralization of MC3T3-E1 cells, and that co-treatment with erythromycin further augments these effects.

WE43 Mg alloy and erythromycin synergistically induce Del-1 expression in MC3T3-E1 cells

To elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying the enhanced osteogenic differentiation, the expression of DEL-1 and key osteogenic markers was evaluated at the transcriptional and protein levels. Quantitative PCR analysis performed on day 14 of osteogenic differentiation demonstrated that Edil3 (DEL-1) mRNA expression was significantly upregulated in MC3T3-E1 cells treated with WE43 Mg alloy–conditioned medium at both 50% and 100% concentrations, with a further significant increase observed in cells co-treated with erythromycin (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2A).

Consistent with the transcriptional findings, Western blot analysis revealed increased DEL-1 protein expression in cells treated with Mg alloy–conditioned medium, with the highest protein levels detected in the Mg alloy plus erythromycin co-treatment groups on day 14 of osteogenic differentiation (Fig. 2B). Densitometric quantification confirmed that DEL-1 protein expression in the co-treatment groups was significantly higher than that in the control and erythromycin-alone groups (P < 0.01) (Fig. 2C). Therefore, these findings demonstrate that the upregulation of DEL-1 was at least partially responsible for the synergistic increase of osteogenic differentiation seen with Mg alloy and erythromycin co-treatment.

WE43 Mg alloy and erythromycin synergistically induce osteogenic gene expression

To further characterize the osteogenic differentiation response, the mRNA expression levels of key osteogenic markers; Runx2, Sp7 (Osterix), and Bglap (Osteocalcin), were evaluated on day 14 of osteogenic differentiation (Fig. 2D-F). Quantitative PCR analysis demonstrated that co-treatment with WE43 Mg alloy–conditioned medium and erythromycin resulted in a significant upregulation of Runx2 expression compared with WE43–conditioned medium alone (Fig. 2D). In contrast, Sp7 expression was not significantly increased by the addition of erythromycin to WE43 Mg alloy–conditioned medium when compared with WE43 Mg alloy treatment alone. However, Sp7 expression was significantly higher in the Mg alloy plus erythromycin group than in the erythromycin-alone group (Fig. 2E). Notably, the combination of erythromycin with WE43 Mg alloy–conditioned medium significantly enhanced Bglap expression compared with WE43 Mg alloy–conditioned medium alone, indicating a synergistic effect on late-stage osteogenic marker expression (Fig. 2F).

To evaluate cytocompatibility, pre-osteoblastic MC3T3-E1 cells were treated with Mg alloy–conditioned medium at four different concentrations (25%, 50%, 75%, and 100%). No significant differences in cell viability were observed among the WE43 Mg alloy–treated groups compared with the control group, indicating that Mg alloy–conditioned medium did not exert cytotoxic effects on MC3T3-E1 cells. Furthermore, co-treatment with erythromycin in either 50% or 100% Mg alloy–conditioned medium unaffected cell viability, with viability levels remaining comparable to those of the control group (Fig. 2G). These results confirm that either WE43 Mg alloy–conditioned medium used alone or in conjunction with erythromycin, is cytocompatible and subsequently enhanced osteogenic differentiation.

Discussion

Magnesium and its alloys have become the promising biomaterial for use in medical applications. As a lightweight metal with low elastic modulus close to human bone when compared to other metals such as titanium or stainless steel, magnesium and its alloys have been used especially in orthopedic applications. In addition, the unique characteristic of magnesium and its alloys is inherent biodegradability. This property has led to the utilization of this metal as biodegradable fixation devices. The present study investigated the combined effects of WE43 Mg alloy and erythromycin on the osteogenic responses of pre-osteoblastic cells. This finding demonstrated that co-treatment of erythromycin with Mg alloy led to a significant upregulation of both mRNA and protein expression levels of Del-1, as well as key osteogenic markers including Sp7 (osterix) and Bglap (osteocalcin). These findings suggest that the combination of Mg alloy and erythromycin exerts synergistic effects on promoting osteogenic differentiation, likely through both immunomodulatory and direct proosteogenic signaling pathways.

The previous study has highlighted that many Mg alloys designed for biomedical uses often involve complex chemistry, incorporating elements that may raise biocompatibility concerns due to potential toxicity.11 Among the various formulations, the Mg alloys have shown particular promise.12 A study by Amukarimi and Mozafari aimed at developing absorbable metallic plates or screws for craniofacial applications and demonstrated that extruded Mg alloy possessed adequate mechanical properties for mid-facial skeletal support. However, the study also emphasized the need for surface modification techniques, such as bioactive or corrosion resistant coatings, to control the degradation rate of this alloy and enhance its clinical utility.2

In this study, the cytocompatibility of Mg alloy-conditioned medium was assessed using pre-osteoblastic MC3T3-E1 cells. The results revealed that cell viability remained over the 70% cytotoxicity threshold at all extract concentrations, indicating that Mg alloy is non-cytotoxic under the investigated circumstances. These findings led the potential of using Mg-alloy as a biocompatible option for biodegradable orthopedic and craniofacial fixation devices. The present findings are consistent with the previous studies reporting the favorable in vitro biocompatibility of Mg alloy. The previous study demonstrated that MC3T3-E1 cells exposed to WE43 Mg alloy-conditioned medium exhibited no significant reduction in viability, even under long-term exposure conditions.13 Similarly, another study reported that WE43 Mg alloy-conditioned medium maintained high cell viability in chondrocytes when compared to other Mg-based alloys, such as AZ31 and pure Mg, suggesting a comparable biocompatibility.14 These outcomes indicate that the degradation products of WE43 Mg alloy, within the tested extraction environment, did not elicit cytotoxic effects in osteogenic cell models.

To investigate the synergistic effect, the concentration of erythromycin used in this study was selected based on the previous reports demonstrating its pro-osteogenic and cytocompatible effects in osteoblastic cell models. Prior studies have shown that erythromycin at concentrations around 10 μg/mL effectively promotes osteoblast differentiation and osteogenic marker expression, while exhibiting no cytotoxic effects on MC3T3-E1 cells, even when tested at concentrations as high as 40 μg/mL in MTT assays.9,15 Based on these findings, a concentration of 20 μg/mL was chosen in the present study to ensure sufficient biological activity for immunomodulatory and pro-osteogenic effects, while remaining well within a non-cytotoxic range.

Del-1 is a glycoprotein secreted by mesenchymal cells, including osteoblast-lineage cells, and has been recognized as a crucial regulator of inflammation resolution and bone homeostasis. It inhibits neutrophil recruitment and facilitates efferocytosis, thereby contributing to an anti-inflammatory microenvironment conducive to tissue regeneration.5,7 In osteogenesis, Del-1 has been shown to enhance osteoblast differentiation and mineralization by modulating integrin signaling and interacting with proosteogenic transcription factors.6 The present results support this role of Del-1 and demonstrate that the combination of WE43 Mg alloy-conditioned medium and erythromycin significantly increased Del-1 expression at both transcript and protein levels, indicating enhanced osteoimmune regulation.

It is known that magnesium ions (Mg2+) induce the activity of osteoblasts by enhancing gap junction intercellular communication between cells and influenced bone formation.3 Mg2+can promote osteogenesis within the bone marrow space by activating the canonical Wnt signaling pathway, thereby directing bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) to differentiate into osteoblasts.16 Meanwhile, erythromycin, a macrolide antibiotic with well-established anti-inflammatory properties, has been shown to modulate host immune responses by inhibiting pro-inflammatory cytokine production.8,17 Emerging evidence also suggests that macrolides may promote tissue regeneration by influencing mesenchymal stem cell differentiation and suppressing osteoclastogenesis.8,9 The observed enhancement in Del-1 expression may result from the cooperative action of Mg2+ in establishing an osteopermissive environment and erythromycin immunomodulatory effects, which collectively create a milieu favorable for osteogenic gene expression.

Furthermore, the synergistic upregulation of osteogenic markers Sp7 and Bglap provides mechanistic support for the pro-osteogenic potential combination of Mg alloy-conditioned medium and erythromycin. Sp7 is a master transcription factor essential for osteoblast differentiation downstream of Runx2, while Bglap encodes osteocalcin, a late marker of mature osteoblasts and bone matrix mineralization.9 The concurrent upregulation of these genes suggests that the combination treatment not only promotes early commitment to the osteoblastic lineage but also supports terminal differentiation and matrix production. These findings provide novel insights into the potential use of Mg alloy in combination with macrolides such as erythromycin as a dual-function therapeutic strategy that couples biodegradable structural support with osteoimmune modulation. Such a strategy may be particularly advantageous in clinical contexts requiring both mechanical stabilization and enhanced bone regeneration, such as periodontal defects, trauma-induced bone loss, or guided bone regeneration.

Despite the promising results, several limitations must be addressed. This study was carried out solely in vitro utilizing pre-osteoblastic MC3T3-E1 cells, which are important for early osteogenesis. However, it does not fully represent the complexity of in vivo bone remodeling involving multiple cell types, vascularization, and immune interactions. In addition, although the upregulation of Del-1, Sp7, and Bglap was clearly demonstrated, upstream signaling pathways such as NF-κB, MAPK, or Wnt/β-catenin are not investigated and warranted further exploration to elucidate the molecular mechanisms involved. Moreover, the concentrations of erythromycin used in combination with Mg alloy-conditioned medium extract were optimized based on cytocompatibility but may differ from pharmacologically relevant doses achievable in vivo. The degradation kinetics of Mg alloy and the release profile of Mg2+ under static culture conditions may also differ significantly from those in dynamic or physiological environments. Additionally, the effects of long-term exposure, degradation by-products, and hydrogen gas release from Mg alloy were not assessed in this study.

The present study employed a conditioned medium approach to evaluate the biological effects of soluble degradation products released from WE43 Mg alloy. This extract-based method is widely used in cytocompatibility testing and aligns with ISO 10993 recommendations for assessing the biological impact of leachable substances. However, it should be noted that conditioned medium models differ from direct-contact environments encountered in vivo. In direct-contact settings, cells are additionally exposed to surface-dependent cues including corrosion morphology, hydrogen evolution, local micro-pH gradients, and dynamic ion fluxes, which may substantially influence osteoblast behavior. A previous study has shown that direct exposure to magnesium alloys can induce distinct cellular responses compared with extract-based systems due to localized degradation effects and surface interactions.18 Therefore, the findings of the present study primarily reflect the chemical contribution of Mg ion release rather than the complete complexity of the in vivo microenvironment.

Magnesium degradation is known to induce alkalization of the surrounding environment, which may influence cellular responses. In the present study, pH measurements of the conditioned mediums were included (Table 2), demonstrating that WE43 extract increased medium pH, whereas dilution reduced alkalinity to values compatible with cell culture. These findings support that the biological responses observed in this study occurred under controlled experimental conditions. Nevertheless, it should be noted that bulk pH measurements in conditioned medium may not accurately reflect localized pH microgradients present at the material–cell interface during direct contact or in vivo degradation. Accordingly, the current model cannot fully reproduce the spatially heterogeneous physicochemical environment associated with magnesium corrosion, which remains a limitation of this study.

Future study should include in vivo experiments to confirm the bone-forming and immune-regulating effects combination of Mg alloy-conditioned medium and erythromycin in bone defect or periodontal models. It is also important to explore the signaling pathways involved in Del-1 expression and osteogenic differentiation under this treatment. Further research should aim to improve drug delivery methods. In addition, evaluating blood vessel formation and immune cell responses will help better reflect the natural healing environment. This study highlights the synergistic potential of Mg alloy and erythromycin to promote osteogenic gene and protein expression through DEL-1–mediated pathways. This combined strategy may represent a novel approach to enhance bone regeneration via coordinated modulation of osteogenesis and inflammation resolution.

With limitation, this study demonstrates that the WE43 Mg alloy is cytocompatible with pre-osteoblastic MC3T3-E1 cells and could promote osteogenic differentiation without inducing cytotoxicity. Notably, co-treatment with erythromycin significantly enhances the pro-osteogenic effects of Mg alloy as evidenced by increased mineralized nodule formation, upregulation of osteogenic markers (Runx2, Sp7, and Bglap), and elevated expression of the immunomodulatory protein Del-1 at both the transcriptional and protein levels. These findings highlight a synergistic interaction between Mg alloy and erythromycin in stimulating osteoblast differentiation and matrix mineralization, suggesting that this combinatorial approach may represent a promising strategy for improving bone regeneration.

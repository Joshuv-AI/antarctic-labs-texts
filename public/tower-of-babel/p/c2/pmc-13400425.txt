Does pattern of 5-aminolevulinic acid (5-ALA) residual intra-operatively predict worse outcome in glioblastoma?
Authors: Vishal Manik, Feras Fayez, Gopikrishna Shyam, Dimitrios Kalaitzoglou, Ayesha Sathali, Yasir Chowdhury, Van Sim, Mark MacDonald, Amoolya Mannava, Christos Soumpasis, Prajwal Ghimire, Asfand Baig Mirza, Kazumi Chia, Sean Main, Keyoumars Ashkan, Ranjeev Bhangoo, Richard Gullan, Lucy Brazil, Omar Al-Salihi, Francesco Vergani, Angela Swampillai, Jose Pedro Lavrador
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroduction and research question5-Aminolevulinic acid (5-ALA) guidance helps to maximize surgical resection in Glioblastoma (GBM). It is unclear if the residual 5-ALA pattern can predict the outcome of GBM patients.Material and methodsPatients with GBM treated at our center between January 2018 - Jun 2022 were included. The pattern of 5-ALA residual was classified as no residue; focal residue (5-ALA in 1-2 walls); or diffuse residue (all walls involved). Pattern of recurrence – focal, diffuse or distal – was classified upon central radiology review.ResultsTotal of 120 patients were screened and 46 were included. The majority of patients presented with temporal lesion (50%) and gross total resection was achieved in 56.52%. No 5-ALA residual was seen in 50% of patients; 39% had focal and 11% had diffuse residual. The pattern of 5-ALA residual - none or focal versus diffuse - was predictive of OS 16.4 versus 9.3 months (p = 0.018) and PFS 10.9 versus 4.5 months (p = 0.007). The univariate analysis showed that extent of resection (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.39–0.88, p = 0.010), adjuvant chemotherapy (HR 0.39, 95% CI 0.18–0.84, p = 0.016), and higher number of cycles (HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.75–0.97, p = 0.012) were also significantly associated with longer survival.ConclusionDiffuse 5-ALA residual is a poor prognostic factor for OS and PFS. The absence of a survival benefit of none over focal 5-ALA residual at the end of resection is important for intraoperative decision making.Keywords: 5- Aminolevulinic acid, Extent of resection, Glioblastoma radiotherapy

Introduction

The current standard of care for fit individuals with Glioblastoma remains maximal safe resection. Stupp et al. have defined adjuvant treatment for those under 70 with chemoradiotherapy (Stupp et al., 2005). 5-Aminolevulinic acid (5-ALA) guided surgery empowered neurosurgeons with a better intraoperative visualization of tumor. This has helped maximize resections beyond the contrast enhancing boundaries into the T2/Fluid attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) only areas (Stummer et al., 2006). Allied with preoperative and intraoperative monitoring techniques, 5-ALA allows for preservation of neurological function alongside more aggressive surgical treatment (Baig et al., 2021). Pursuing complete 5-ALA resection may be hampered by functional boundaries and it becomes necessary to leave residual disease at time of surgery. This residual may correlate with contrast enhancing or FLAIR residual on post-operative imaging (Lasocki and Gaillard, 2019).

Most of the available data pertaining to GBM patients highlights risk factors like age, O6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase (MGMT) status, extent of resection that impact the outcome of these patients. However, there appears to be paucity of reports around the impact of 5-ALA residual pattern on the outcomes as the majority of previous data looked at 5-ALA residual according to the intensity of fluorescence (Aibar-Durán et al., 2025; Lau et al., 2016; Kiesel et al., 2018). This is even more the case when the 5-ALA fluorescence extends to the ventricular wall where its oncological significance is uncertain (Tejada-et al., 2012; Moon et al., 2016; Hayashi et al., 2010). This information could help the surgeons intra-operatively to gauge the need of further resection against any risk of neurological morbidity. In addition, the information could guide oncologists to help prognosticate their patients.

As per the European Association of Neuro-Oncology (EANO) guidelines, residual disease on the post-operative MRI guides the target volume delineation for radiotherapy (Niyazi et al., 2023). Previous trials with dose escalation have yielded minimal gains in brain tumours but these were mostly done with outdated techniques and with larger margins (Tsien et al., 2009) even though a recent study with newer techniques did show some correlation with improved survival (Takano et al., 2024). So far, it has not been well characterized if the pattern of 5-ALA residual correlates with the pattern of tumour recurrence/progression. Therefore, it is important to assess if intraoperative-derived information could inform potential personalized radiotherapy planning in future trial design.

Research question

In this study, we will assess the impact of different patterns of 5-ALA residual fluorescence at the end of surgical resection in overall and progression free survival. Also, we will interrogate if this pattern of residual fluorescence influences the pattern of tumour recurrence/progression.

Material and methods

This single-centre retrospective cohort study included patients with histologically confirmed IDH-wildtype glioblastoma (WHO, 2021 classification) treated at King's College Hospital and Guy's & St Thomas' NHS Foundation Trust between January 2018 and June 2022. The study was approved by the Guy's & St Thomas' Institutional Review Board (Project Number 14407) and reported in accordance with STROBE guidelines.

Patients were included if they had undergone 5-ALA-guided resection (not biopsy alone), were planned for adjuvant Stupp protocol, and had intraoperative documentation of 5-ALA residual pattern supported by high-resolution digital photography. Those with IDH-mutant tumours, missing operation notes, or absent fluorescence documentation were excluded (Table 1).

5-ALA residual classification

Operation notes and intraoperative photographs were independently reviewed by four neurosurgeons (J.P.L., Y.C., C.S., D.K.). 5-ALA residual was classified as none (no visible fluorescence), focal (1–2 cavity walls), or diffuse (all walls). The primary analysis used this three-way classification; a secondary binary grouping (none or focal vs diffuse) was also examined. Ventricular wall fluorescence was documented where applicable but was available for only 28 of 46 patients (60.9%), with evidence of non-random missingness (see below) (Fig. 1).

– Schematic representation of the classifications used for 5-ALA residual pattern at the end of the resection – none, focal (1-2 walls) and diffuse (all walls) – and the pattern of radiological recurrence/progression – focal (1-2 walls), diffuse (all walls) and distant (not related with the original site of disease).

Outcomes and covariates

Co-primary outcomes were overall survival (OS; diagnosis to death or database lock on 31st October 2025) and progression-free survival (PFS; diagnosis to first radiological progression). A secondary outcome was the pattern of first radiological recurrence, classified as focal, diffuse, or distal by two independent neuroradiologists (M.McD., A.M.) blinded to the 5-ALA classification (Fig. 1). Extent of resection was graded centrally on post-operative MRI as gross total, near-total, or subtotal resection per RANO RESECT Criteria (Karschnia et al., 2023). MGMT methylation was determined by pyrosequencing (methylated if >5%).

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables are reported as medians with interquartile ranges; categorical variables as frequencies and percentages. Survival was estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method with log-rank testing for group comparisons. Univariable Cox proportional hazards regression estimated hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) for each candidate predictor. The proportional hazards assumption was verified using Schoenfeld residuals.

With 40 death events, multivariable models were limited to four covariates (10 events-per-variable rule). Two models were pre-specified: Model A included diffuse 5-ALA residual, MGMT, extent of resection, and age; Model B excluded extent of resection to assess whether the prognostic signal from diffuse 5-ALA was independent of or confounded by EoR. Age and MGMT were included on biological grounds regardless of univariable significance. Treatment variables that accrue over the disease course (chemotherapy cycles, treatment at recurrence) were excluded from multivariable models owing to immortal time bias. The correlation between 5-ALA residual pattern and extent of resection was quantified using Spearman's rank coefficient.

The association between 5-ALA residual and recurrence pattern was assessed using Fisher's exact test, given small expected cell counts. Chi-square results are reported alongside for completeness. No formal power calculation was performed; the diffuse group comprised five patients, and this constraint is acknowledged throughout. A sensitivity analysis excluded one patient with OS shorter than PFS (data inconsistency). All analyses were performed in Python 3.12 (lifelines v0.29, scipy v1.14). A two-sided p < 0.05 was considered significant; exact p-values are reported where p ≥ 0.001.

Results

Study sample

A total of 120 patients planned for Stupp chemoradiotherapy were identified during the study period. Thirty-seven were excluded for biopsy only, 19 lacked 5-ALA documentation or residual pattern classification, three had missing or duplicated operation notes, and 15 had IDH-mutant tumours. The remaining 46 patients fulfilled the inclusion criteria and comprised the final dataset (Table 1).

Demographics and clinical characteristics

Median age at diagnosis was 56 years (IQR 50–63). The majority were male (31/46, 67.4%). Predominant ECOG performance status at adjuvant treatment was 1 (29/46, 63.0%; range 0–3). Tumour laterality was evenly distributed (23 per hemisphere), with the temporal lobe being the most common location (22/46, 50.0%). Gross total resection was achieved in 26 patients (56.5%), near-total resection in 13 (28.3%), and subtotal resection in seven (15.2%). MGMT promoter methylation was present in 16/45 patients (35.6%); one patient had an indeterminate result. Median time from surgery to radiotherapy was 35 days (IQR 30–41). All except six patients received concurrent temozolomide; concurrent chemotherapy was omitted in these patients owing to limited performance status. Radiotherapy was delivered over a median of 41 days (IQR 40–42). Thirty-seven patients (80.4%) received adjuvant chemotherapy, with a median of 6 cycles (IQR 4–6).

5-ALA residual pattern and survival

Twenty-three patients (50.0%) had no visible 5-ALA residual at the end of resection, 18 (39.1%) had focal residual (1–2 walls), and five (10.9%) had diffuse residual (all walls). Overall median OS was 16.1 months and median PFS was 9.1 months.

On three-way comparison (none versus focal versus diffuse), median OS was 16.4, 17.9, and 9.3 months, respectively (log-rank p = 0.035; Fig. 2A). A similar pattern was observed for PFS: 10.9, 11.0, and 4.5 months (log-rank p = 0.013; Fig. 2B). Pairwise comparison of no residual versus focal residual showed no significant difference in either OS (log-rank p = 0.485) or PFS (log-rank p = 0.755).

– Kaplan Meier curves showing the impact of 3 different classification of the 5-ALA residual at the end of the resection in the overall survival and progression free survival. The 3 classification systems are: none versus focal versus diffuse (top); absence versus presence (middle); and none or focal versus diffuse (bottom).

When collapsed into a binary classification (none or focal versus diffuse), diffuse 5-ALA residual was associated with significantly shorter OS (median 9.3 versus 16.4 months, log-rank p = 0.012; Fig. 2C) and PFS (median 4.5 versus 10.9 months, log-rank p = 0.003; Fig. 2D). On univariable Cox regression, diffuse residual was associated with worse OS (HR 3.28, 95% CI 1.24–8.70, p = 0.017) and worse PFS (HR 3.89, 95% CI 1.45–10.47, p = 0.007).

Univariable analysis

Table 2 presents the full univariable Cox regression results. For OS, greater extent of resection (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.39–0.88, p = 0.010), receipt of adjuvant chemotherapy (HR 0.39, 95% CI 0.18–0.84, p = 0.016), and higher number of chemotherapy cycles (HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.75–0.97, p = 0.012) were significantly associated with longer survival. MGMT promoter methylation showed a trend towards improved PFS (HR 0.52, 95% CI 0.26–1.01, p = 0.054) (Fig. 3). Notably, 5-ALA residual pattern and extent of resection were moderately correlated (Spearman ρ = 0.40, p = 0.006) (Supplementary Material 1)

Multivariable analysis

Given 40 death events across 46 patients, the events-per-variable constraint limited the multivariable model to approximately four covariates. Two models were constructed to assess the sensitivity of the primary finding to the inclusion of extent of resection (Table 3). In Model A, which included diffuse 5-ALA residual, MGMT status, extent of resection, and age, diffuse 5-ALA residual was no longer significantly associated with OS (HR 2.29, 95% CI 0.63–8.31, p = 0.209) or PFS (HR 3.41, 95% CI 0.96–12.12, p = 0.058). In Model B, which excluded extent of resection, diffuse 5-ALA residual retained significance for both OS (HR 3.43, 95% CI 1.20–9.82, p = 0.022) and PFS (HR 3.92, 95% CI 1.35–11.37, p = 0.012). These results indicate that the prognostic effect of diffuse 5-ALA residual is not fully independent of extent of resection in this cohort.

Radiological recurrence pattern

All 46 patients had progressed by the time of database lock. At first recurrence, 13 (28.3%) had focal relapse, 19 (41.3%) had diffuse relapse, and 14 (30.4%) had distal recurrence. Thirteen patients were managed with best supportive care at progression, whilst 33 received further treatment (five re-operations, 24 s-line chemotherapy, three re-irradiation).

All five patients with diffuse 5-ALA residual experienced diffuse radiological recurrence, compared with an approximately even distribution of recurrence patterns (focal 31.7%, diffuse 34.1%, distal 34.1%) among the 41 patients with no or focal residual (Fig. 4A). Although this association reached statistical significance on chi-square testing (p = 0.019), all expected cell counts in the diffuse 5-ALA row were below 5 (range 1.4–2.1), rendering the chi-square test unreliable. Fisher's exact test comparing local (focal + diffuse) versus distal recurrence between the two 5-ALA groups did not reach significance (p = 0.303). The recurrence pattern was not significantly associated with OS (log-rank p = 0.831; Fig. 4B).

Discussion

This study shows that diffuse 5-ALA residual – as opposed to none of focal 5-ALA residual disease - had a negative impact on both PFS and OS in this cohort of patients that had surgical debulking followed by chemoradiotherapy. However, this effect is not completely independent from the extent of resection as the differences between both proposed models showed that the effect of 5-ALA residual in both OS and PFS is lost when the extent of resection is included in the model. With regards to tumour progression, even though all patients with diffuse 5-ALA residual have a diffuse pattern of radiological progression, the other patterns of 5-ALA residual have an even distribution of radiological progression patterns and are not related with OS.

No significant differences in both overall and progression free survival are identifiable between none or focal 5-ALA residual at the end of resection. This information could assist intraoperative surgical decision frameworks when balancing the risks of further resection against morbidity. This data shows that focal 5-ALA residual would not be detrimental to the patients’ outcomes and thus could be left unresected if there is a risk of morbidity related to postoperative functional deficits. Diffuse pattern of 5-ALA residual, on the other hand, seemed to significantly impact survival. This is also in keeping with published literature from surgical series supporting gross total and supramaximal resection in eligible patients correlated with improved survival (Baig et al., 2021; Molinaro et al., 2020). Thus, we could consider diffuse ALA residual at the end of the resection as an additional predictive factor for patients with GBM. This result is particularly relevant as there is extensive debate in the neuro-oncology literature about the questionable effect of 5-ALA-guided resection in overall survival despite the recognized impact in PFS (Gandhi et al., 2019). This study suggests that more than dichotomic assessment - presence versus absence - of residual fluorescence at the end of the resection, its pattern can potentially be used in prognostication for patients with this diagnosis. Therefore, 5-ALA-guided surgery should be considered even when it is anticipated based on preoperative mapping that a complete 5-ALA based resection is not possible. This study paves the way for future research on 5-ALA residual at the end of surgical resection among patients with radiological gross total resection of contrast enhancement disease. We believe that this will be the next crucial question as it is often beyond the contrast-enhancement margin that the onco-functional dilemma takes place when surgically managing these patients. Further studies evaluating the impact of 5-ALA residual across different extents of resection are crucial as our data show lack of independence between these two prognostic factors (both were significant on univariable analysis but neither retained significance after mutual adjustment in Model A: 5-ALA residual OS p = 0.209, PFS p = 0.058).

With regards to the radiological patterns of recurrence, most of the patients relapsed within the local region – 69.6% of patients - in keeping with the known locally aggressive biology of GBMs. For patients with no or focal 5-ALA residual at the end of resection, there was an almost even distribution of the recurrence among the three categories – focal, diffuse and distal – and therefore no correlation between this residual 5-ALA pattern and the pattern of radiological recurrence can be established (still around 2/3 of recurrences occurred locally). However, the diffuse residual 5-ALA pattern at the end of the resection always lead to a local progression in a diffuse pattern. Personalized radiotherapy approaches informed by 5-ALA residual is an attractive concept even though previous dose escalation studies, for example, have not shown benefit (Wegner et al., 2019). Apart from the above mentioned diffuse 5-ALA residual leading to diffuse radiological progression, this study failed to provide support to this concept as in cases of no or focal fluorescence, the pattern of recurrence was evenly distributed. Also, the rate of distal relapse in our study was about 30% which is similar to previously reported figures for Glioblastoma. (Minniti et al., 2010), (Langhans et al., 2023). Even though it has been previously published that a longer survival time can be seen in patients with distal recurrence (Minniti et al., 2010), our data does not support these findings. We hypothesize that the small numbers reported in each group are partially responsible for these findings, as the tail of the distribution seems to favor patients with focal and distal recurrence patterns. Also, future work should focus on the integration of 5-ALA residual patterns and postoperative radiological features to inform future algorithms of tumour recurrence/progression that could open avenues of research towards personalized local treatments.

Limitations of the study: The main limitations of our study are its retrospective nature and the small numbers, despite including patients over a 54-month period. The clear intraoperative documentation with high quality photos and the central review made the majority of screened patients not suitable for enrollment. This could have led to an unwanted selection bias in the study population. The statistical power of adjusted models and survival comparisons related with recurrence were potentially affected by this limitation. The heterogeneity of treatment at time of recurrence it relevant as there is no common pathway at this stage. Individual pathways are established with patients at that time which makes variable control difficult in this study. Finally, this study evaluate only the pattern of residual fluorescence whereas other studies assessed the intensity of fluorescence as previously mentioned. We believe that the intensity of fluorescence is more operator dependent and technique dependent given the impact of the light beam direct, luminosity of the surgical theatre and distance between the excitation light source and the tissue. However, we believe that future studies should consider both anatomical distribution of the residual fluorescence alongside with a quantifiable measure of its intensity to allow even more accurate oncological considerations about the residual fluorescence at the end of resection.

Strengths of the study: We reviewed a large cohort of patients with documented histology according to the WHO 2021 classification, with systematic intra-operative and post operative documentation along with a thorough multi-disciplinary review and central radiological review. This is the first study which has looked at integration of intra-operative 5-ALA residual with survival and radiological appearances. Also, it revisits the concept of 5-ALA to maximize the EoR in an era where onco-functional balance is paramount. The absence of difference in survival outcomes with no or focal residual 5-ALA fluorescence at the end of resection is very important as it may provide a useful tool for intraoperative decision when facing GBMs in eloquent areas.

Conclusions

The absence of survival difference between no and focal residual 5-ALA fluorescence at the end of the resection provides useful information for intraoperative decision when treating GBMs in highly eloquent areas. Diffuse 5-ALA residual had a negative impact on the oncological outcome translated into poor PFS and OS of patients treated with fluorescence-guided maximal safe resection followed by oncological treatment. Even though diffuse 5-ALA residual always led to a diffuse local progressive pattern at time of tumour progression, the other patterns of 5-ALA residual were not predictive of patterns of recurrence. Finally, different patterns of tumour recurrence were not related with overall survival.

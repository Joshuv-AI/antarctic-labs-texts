PA207. An application of the Dahlgren and Whitehead Model in evaluating traffic-related injuries in the UAE
Authors: I Elkonaisi, M Grivna
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTraffic-related injuries are a significant public health issue in the United Arab Emirates (UAE), with an evident impact among young male drivers. The Dahlgren and Whitehead Model (DWM) explores the five layers of complex interplay of socio-ecological factors contributing to public health issues. It illustrates how individual lifestyle factors, social and community networks, and broader socioeconomic, cultural, and environmental conditions collectively influence health outcomes. We aimed to assess the traffic-related injuries using DWM, emphasizing the need for a holistic understanding of the underlying causes.MethodsWe applied the DWM to evaluate data from national traffic reports, hospital injury records, and safety compliance studies to identify key risk patterns and intervention strategies.ResultsOur analysis confirms that effective intervention requires a layered approach: enhancing enforcement of safety measures like seatbelt use and child safety seat laws, improving road infrastructure with safer design and maintenance, and launching comprehensive public health campaigns that promote safe driving practices. These interventions should be tailored to address specific risk behaviors identified in different demographic groups, particularly among young drivers who exhibit higher rates of risky behaviors.ConclusionsAddressing traffic injuries in the UAE requires a comprehensive approach that incorporates legal reforms, behavioral initiatives, and infrastructure improvements. Leveraging the DWM framework can help guide interventions at various levels to effectively reduce the burden of traffic-related injuries.Key messages• DWM can be useful in designing public health intervention strategis for traffic injuries in the UAE.• Multifaceted interventions addressing behavioral, legal, and environmental factors are crucial.TopicTraffic Safety, Public Health Interventions, Socio-Ecological Model.

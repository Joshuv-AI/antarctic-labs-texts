Diagnostic value of long noncoding RNAs MIAT and MALAT1 in coronary artery disease: a systematic review and diagnostic accuracy test meta-analysis
Authors: Khatere Roozbehi, Davood Semirani-Nezhad, Behnam Alipour, Pardis Javadian, Mohammad Sadeghi, Mina Mashayekh, Farshid Javdani, Armin Attar, Alireza Hosseinpour, Mani Khorsand Askari, Mahour Farzan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLong noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) MALAT1 and MIAT modulate atherosclerotic progression and myocardial ischemia injury, through vascular endothelial dysfunction. Diagnostic values of these lncRNAs in detecting coronary artery disease (CAD) have been investigated, but the results have been inconsistent. We aimed to evaluate the diagnostic accuracy of lncRNAs MALAT1 and MIAT.MethodsA comprehensive search was done to identify case-control studies reporting diagnostic measures of MIAT and MALAT1 in patients with CAD compared to healthy controls. Methodological quality of included studies was assessed using the QUADAS-2 checklist. All analyses were conducted in R software. Hierarchical models were applied to pooled diagnostic performance metrics. We used the “meta” package to estimate the odds ratio (OR) for MALAT1, and to pool sensitivity and specificity for both lncRNAs. We also used the “mada” package to estimate the positive likelihood ratio (PLR), negative likelihood ratio (NLR), area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), and diagnostic odds ratio (DOR). Meta-regression analyses based on mean age and male percentage were conducted to explore potential sources of heterogeneity.ResultsTen studies were included, encompassing a total of 1,879 individuals (1009 CAD cases and 870 healthy controls) with a mean age of 59.24 years. The diagnostic performance of lncRNA MALAT1 showed a pooled sensitivity of 0.68 and specificity of 0.70, with an AUC of 0.746 and a DOR of 5.17 (95% CI: 2.26–11.85), showing acceptable diagnostic power. For lncRNA MIAT, the pooled sensitivity and specificity were 0.81 and 0.65, respectively, with an AUC of 0.757 and a DOR of 7.64 (95% CI: 2.12–27.47), indicating moderate accuracy. The prognostic value of MALAT1 in CAD was assessed, showing a significant association with disease outcomes (OR = 2.81, 95% CI: 1.04–7.56).ConclusionsThis study indicates that both MALAT1 and MIAT demonstrate moderate diagnostic accuracy for CAD, with limitations in their clinical utility. MALAT1 also appears to have prognostic potential, suggesting its broader clinical relevance. Further research in standardized clinical settings is needed to validate these findings and clarify their practical applications in CAD diagnosis and management.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-025-05285-4.Keywords: Long non-coding RNA, Biomarker, MALAT1, MIAT, Coronary artery disease, Diagnostic accuracy, Meta-analysis

Introduction

The prevalence and complications of coronary artery diseases (CAD) provide a challenge for healthcare providers across the globe. CAD is responsible for about 610,000 deaths each year, contributing for almost 1 in 4 deaths in the United States [1, 2]. Despite its significant burden, CAD is largely preventable, underscoring the importance of early detection and proactive management to reduce complications and improve outcomes [2]. Early and accurate diagnostic testing is critical in optimizing patient care and improving long-term outcomes for individuals with CAD [3, 4].

Recently, molecular biomarkers have emerged as a promising approach to improve CAD diagnosis and risk stratification [4–7]. Long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs), play important regulatory roles in gene expression and cellular function [8, 9]. They are involved in biological processes such as transcriptional regulation, chromatin remodeling, and post-transcriptional RNA processing [9]. Dysregulation of lncRNAs has been associated with various conditions, including neurodegenerative disorders and cardiovascular diseases [10, 11]. These biomarkers offer unique advantages due to their non-invasive detection in blood, potential for early diagnosis, and ability to reflect disease-specific molecular changes [12]. In respect to CAD, lncRNAs have demonstrated promise as biomarkers that can stimulate systemic inflammation and endothelial dysfunction [13]. These biomarkers have shown remarkable diagnostic accuracy and prognostic stratification [14, 15]. Among these lncRNAs, Metastasis-Associated Lung Adenocarcinoma Transcript 1 (MALAT1) and Myocardial Infarction Associated Transcript (MIAT) have emerged as candidates of interest due to their regulatory roles in cardiovascular physiology and pathology [16–18].

MALAT1, initially associated with lung cancer metastasis, is highly expressed in vascular endothelial cells, smooth muscle cells, and cardiomyocytes, where it regulates key signaling pathways involved in atherosclerosis, vascular remodeling, and myocardial remodeling. Dysregulation of MALAT1 is associated with the progression of CAD through uncontrolled vessel growth and endothelial cell proliferation [16, 18–20]. Additionally, it has been revealed that levels of MALAT1 rise remarkably with hypoxia, which highlights the potential of MALAT1 as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker for CAD [21, 22].

MIAT plays an important role in both cardiovascular and neurological disorders. Since its discovery as a transcript associated with myocardial infarction, MIAT has been connected to several cardiovascular pathologies, including atherosclerosis, heart failure, and myocardial remodeling [17, 18, 23]. This lncRNA is involved in vascular endothelial dysfunction, suggesting a potential role in the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis [24].

While previous studies have linked MALAT1 and MIAT to CAD, their reported diagnostic accuracy has been inconsistent, highlighting the need for a comprehensive evaluation of their diagnostic potential. This systematic review and meta-analysis aims to consolidate existing evidence regarding diagnostic values of MALAT1 and MIAT in CAD, elucidating their potential utility as diagnostic biomarkers in clinical practice.

Methods

We reported our systematic review and meta-analysis by following the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses) [25]. The protocol used for our study is registered in PROSPERO by registration ID of CRD42023494883. The PRISMA checklist is provided in Table S1 in Supporting File.

Search strategy and inclusion criteria

A comprehensive literature search was conducted to identify studies which reported diagnostic or prognostic values of MALA1 or MIAT in CAD. Electronic databases including PubMed, Scopus, and Embase were searched for literature published until March 23rd, 2025. No language restrictions were applied. Detailed search strategies are provided in Table S2.

We included eligible studies according to the following criteria: (1) Original studies investigating human participants diagnosed with coronary artery disease and healthy controls, (2) Studies with available full-text, (3) Studies that reported relevant diagnostic parameters including sensitivity and specificity, or prognostic values including odds ratio (OR). Review articles, comments, gray literature (e.g. conference abstracts and preprints), letters to editors, case reports, and articles reporting diagnostic power of lncRNAs other than MIAT or MALAT1 or investigating diseases other than coronary artery disease were excluded. Two authors independently screened the full texts of the studies.

Data extraction

The extraction of data from the eligible articles was done by two reviewers including first author’s name, year of publication, country, number of participants, male percentage, mean age, lncRNA type, CAD subtype, gene expression technique, as well as diagnostic and prognostic values including OR (LCI-UCI), area under receiver operating curve (AUC) (LCI-UCI), sensitivity and specificity. In cases of missing or unclear data, a third reviewer was consulted to reach a final decision. To avoid duplication bias, we carefully examined author lists, recruitment periods, and study locations to identify and exclude publications with overlapping samples.

Quality assessment

The quality of each study was evaluated by two reviewers according to the Quality assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies (QUADAS-2) checklist. This tool includes 4 domains and 10 signaling questions for studies of diagnostic accuracy, and each study is rated as having a “low”, “uncertain” or “high” risk of bias based on answers to the relevant questions in each domain. Answers to each question will be “yes”, “uncertain” or “no”. Each domain will be rated based on its signaling questions. The Robvis online tool was used to illustrate the quality assessment and overall risk of bias [26].

Statistical analysis

All analyses were performed in R software version 4.4 (The R Foundation, Vienna, Austria). “Meta” package was utilized to perform univariate analysis and calculate pooled sensitivity and pooled specificity. “Mada” package was used to estimate positive likelihood ratio (PLR), negative likelihood ratio (NLR), area under receiver operating curve (AUC) and diagnostic odds ratio (DOR). DOR was analyzed using both univariate method and a bivariate model that jointly incorporated sensitivity and specificity as correlated parameters. AUC values less than 0.5, 0.5 to 0.7, 0.7 to 0.8, and more than 0.8 are interpreted as showing no discrimination, poor, acceptable, and excellent discrimination, respectively. We also used the “Mada” package to fit a bivariate model that incorporates sensitivity and specificity as correlated parameters, which is equivalent to the Hierarchical Summary Receiver Operating Characteristic (HSROC) model.

To assess heterogeneity in the bivariate analysis, we calculated the I² statistic using the method proposed by Zhou and Dendukuri, which accounts for variability in diagnostic cut-off values of lncRNAs. In addition, we estimated Spearman’s correlation coefficient between logit-transformed sensitivity and 1-specificity assessed the threshold effect, with p < 0.05 indicating significance. Then, to identify sources of heterogeneity, we performed meta-regression by constructing multiple models with covariates including mean age and male percentage. We then used an ANOVA test to assess significant differences between the models. Deeks’ funnel plot was generated by plotting ln (DOR) against *1/√ESS* using the meta package. Asymmetry was tested via Deeks’ regression analysis, with p < 0.10 suggesting potential bias.

Results

Study search results

Our primary search resulted in 287 studies initially. After removing 108 duplicates, remaining results were screened by title and abstract, after which 28 results were assessed for eligibility by full texts. Finally, 10 cross-sectional studies were included in the final analysis [13–15, 17, 18, 20, 21, 27–29]. PRISMA flowchart is visualized in Fig. 1. We verified that no sample overlap existed across the included studies by checking the investigated populations of each study.

Results of synthesis

The pooled sensitivity and specificity for MALAT1 were 0.68 and 0.70, respectively, while for MIAT, the sensitivity and specificity were 0.81 and 0.65, respectively. The forest plots depicting the pooled sensitivity and specificity for MALAT1 and MIAT are presented in Figs. 2 and 3, respectively.

The AUC was 0.746 for MALAT1, and 0.757 for MIAT. The univariate model yielded DOR of 5.17 (95% CI = 2.26–11.85) for MALAT1 and 7.64 (95% CI = 2.12–27.47) for MIAT, visualized in forest plots in Figure S4 and Figure S5. Results of the bivariate model as well as the summary receiver operating characteristic (SROC) curves for MALAT1 and MIAT are shown in Figs. 4 and 5, respectively. For MALAT1, the analysis of heterogeneity showed a β of −0.14 (P-value = 0.80), with an I² of 51%, suggesting absence of a significant threshold effect. For MIAT, the β was − 0.4 (P-value = 0.75), with an I² of 66.3%, indicating no evidence of a threshold effect.

Additionally, the prognostic value of MALAT1 in CAD was evaluated, yielding an odds ratio (OR) of 2.81 (95% CI: 1.04–7.56), visualized by a forest plot in Figure S3.

Meta-regression analysis was conducted to assess the impact of mean age, male percentage, and their combination on heterogeneity. For MALAT1, the results showed that mean age was a significant predictor (p = 0.02), as was male percentage (p = 0.013). When both covariates were included in the model, they demonstrated a highly significant effect (p = 0.000144), indicating that their combined influence accounted for a substantial portion of the observed heterogeneity. For MIAT, the results indicated that mean age significantly contributed to the heterogeneity of the effect size (p = 0.00363). Similarly, male percentage was also a significant factor (p = 0.0496). When both covariates were included in the model, they collectively explained a significant portion of the variation (p = 0.000848), suggesting a stronger combined influence on heterogeneity. However, some residual heterogeneity remains unexplained.

Deeks’ funnel plot showed a symmetrical distribution of studies for both lncRNAs (Figure S6 and Figure S7), with individual studies represented as filled circles plotted as ln(DOR) against 1/sqrt(ESS). The random effects estimate of ln(DOR) was displayed as a dash-dot line. The regression test for funnel plot asymmetry, conducted using the Deeks method, yielded a p-value of 0.82 (t = −0.26, df = 2, bias estimate = −18.23, SE = 69.51), indicating no evidence of publication bias. The small number of studies (k = 4) limits the power of the asymmetry test.

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first comprehensive meta-analysis of the diagnostic performance of lncRNAs MALAT1 and MIAT in coronary artery disease, encompassing the results of 10 studies.

The results of our meta-analysis indicate that both MALAT1 and MIAT hold promising diagnostic potential for distinguishing CAD patients from healthy controls, as demonstrated by their pooled sensitivity and specificity. With higher sensitivity (0.805), MIAT is well-suited for screening high-risk populations to minimize missed CAD cases, accepting a higher false-positive rate that can be addressed through confirmatory testing. MALAT1, with greater specificity (0.704), may be more useful in the diagnostic phase to reduce unnecessary procedures, though its accuracy is lower than established markers like troponin (>0.90) [30]. When used together, they could help balance sensitivity and specificity in clinical workflows. While our findings support the overall diagnostic value of these biomarkers, Qiu et al. reported a concerning trade-off for MIAT, with reasonable sensitivity (0.755) but suboptimal specificity (0.452) in AMI patients, indicating a high false-positive rate in clinical settings. Similarly, Gu et al. and Song et al. found that MALAT1 exhibited moderate sensitivity (0.71 and 0.7, respectively) but only modest specificity (0.57 and 0.58), which may limit its clinical reliability. These results contrast with those of Toraih et al., who reported exceptionally high sensitivity for MIAT (0.955), while MALAT1 performed poorly with a sensitivity of 0.5. Notably, a consistent trend across most studies is the higher sensitivity compared to specificity, suggesting that these biomarkers may be more useful for ruling out disease rather than confirming a diagnosis.

The AUC values in our analysis demonstrated moderate to good discriminatory power for both lncRNAs; however, they fall below thresholds typically considered clinically actionable, indicating that their standalone use in practice may be limited. However, Toraih et al. reported an AUC of just 0.601 for MALAT1, falling below the threshold for acceptable diagnostic performance. For MIAT, we observed particularly notable disparities across studies: Yan et al. reported excellent discriminatory ability (AUC = 0.908), while Wang et al. found only modest performance (AUC = 0.609). This variability in MIAT’s performance suggests that its diagnostic reliability may be context-dependent, potentially influenced by factors such as the timing of sample collection or the presence of comorbid conditions. In practice, biomarkers with moderate accuracy can increase in value when integrated into multi-marker panels or used alongside imaging and clinical assessment. We visualized a comparison of AUCs from included studies using a bar chart, as shown in Fig. 6. These findings highlight the importance of standardized protocols in future studies to more accurately assess the true diagnostic potential of these biomarkers. Standardization of measurement techniques for lncRNA expression is essential to ensure reproducibility and achieving consistent results across studies.

Additionally, our results revealed a significant positive association between MALAT1 expression and CAD, highlighting its potential prognostic value. Our findings align with those of Lv et al. [13], who discovered a positive association between MALAT1 and the risk of CHD (coronary heart disease), independent of other factors. In CHD patients, higher levels of MALAT1 were correlated with more severe coronary stenosis, hyperlipidemia, and systemic inflammation. Additionally, the increased expression of MALAT1 was linked to a higher occurrence of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), as also reported by Li et al. [16], which showed that high levels of MALAT1 were linked to an elevated risk of MACE, indicating its potential as a biomarker for AMI risk stratification and prognosis.

The meta-regression analysis revealed that demographic characteristics significantly contributed to the observed heterogeneity in diagnostic performance for both biomarkers. Notably, the interaction between age and sex composition accounted for a considerable proportion of heterogeneity. Such variation might stem from biological differences linked to sex hormones, immune system activity, or age-associated changes in vascular function, all of which may impact lncRNA expression and diagnostic reliability.

Strengths and limitations

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first systematic review and meta-analysis to investigate the diagnostic value of the lncRNAs MIAT and MALAT1 in CAD. One of the main strengths of our study is that we looked at both the diagnostic and prognostic roles of MALAT1, giving a more complete picture of its clinical utility. Furthermore, our study revealed the differences in pooled sensitivity and specificity across studies, which may help guide how they can best be used in practice. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. The included studies encompassed various CAD subtypes, such as AMI, CHD, and unstable angina, and subgroup analysis based on the CAD subtype was not possible due to limited number of studies. Additionally, there were insufficient studies available to adequately assess the prognostic value of MIAT in CAD. Regarding generalizability, while the funnel plot did not indicate significant publication bias, the predominance of studies from China may limit the geographic generalizability of the findings. Finally, all included studies were case-control in design, which may overestimate diagnostic performance, underscoring the need for validation in prospective, studies. These limitations may restrict the immediate clinical applicability of our findings, highlighting the need for future studies in more focused populations to validate and extend them.

Conclusion

Our study suggests that both MALAT1 and MIAT show moderate potential as non-invasive biomarkers for the diagnosis of coronary artery disease, with MIAT demonstrating slightly stronger diagnostic performance. MALAT1 also appears to hold prognostic value, suggesting its broader clinical relevance beyond diagnosis. These findings highlight the possible utility of lncRNAs in enhancing CAD detection and risk assessment, though it is crucial to conduct multi-center prospective validation studies and establish standardized assay protocols to ensure the robustness and reproducibility of the findings.

Rapid identification directly from urine using QUICKprep kit
Authors: M Sousa-Pinheiro, I Martins-Oliveira, D Abreu, R Gomes, A Silva-Dias, F Passos-Carneiro, P Poeta, C Pina-Vaz, A J Soares
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractUrinary tract infections are common, mainly caused by E. coli. Conventional diagnosis based on culture, delays result for 48 h, promoting empirical, often inadequate, treatments. This study validated the QUICKprep® (FASTinov®) kit, which concentrates viable microbial cells and removes debris in under five minutes for MALDI-TOF-MS identification. A total of 281 positive urine samples, screened positive on Sysmex, were tested. In inoculated samples, results were fully concordant with culture; in clinical samples, 13/155 were not identified by MALDI-TOF-MS. QUICKprep® enabled accurate identification direct from urine in around 15 min, offering faster diagnosis, potential for antimicrobial testing, and improved therapeutic decisions.Keywords: Urinary tract infection, Direct identification, MALDI-Tof, QUICKprep

Introduction

Urinary tract infections (UTIs) are among the most common bacterial infections, with an estimation of over 50% of women experiencing at least one symptomatic UTI during their lifetime, with a recurrency rate of 30–44% in the first 6 months after the first infection [1, 2]. While Enterobacterales, namely Escherichia coli, encompass the majority of UTIs others, such as Klebsiella pneumoniae, Proteus mirabilis, Pseudomonas spp, and Gram-positive cocci are also implicated [3]. UTI’s affected 404.6 million people and were responsible for more than 200.000 deaths in 2019 [4]. Currently, diagnosis of UTI’s relies primarily on urine culture, which typically requires a minimum of 48 h to yield results as they depend on the study of the ability to grow in the presence of antibiotics. As such, empiric therapy is often initiated without definitive microbial identification or susceptibility data. This approach can lead to therapeutic failure especially due to antimicrobial resistance (AMR), side effects of drug use and promoting AMR [5].

The AMR is a real threat to global health, with approximately 5 million directly and indirectly associated deaths in 2019 [4, 6].

To accelerate the diagnosis several protocols were described using MALDI-TOF-MS directly from positive blood cultures [7] and, more recently few for rapid identification of microorganisms in MALDI-TOF-MS directly from urine samples [8–10]. These protocols combine a previous screening by a flow cytometry analyser and the use of the pellet resulting from a series of centrifugations and washes to allow direct ID. Nonetheless, those protocols could be followed by Antimicrobial Susceptibility Test (AST) methods, like disk diffusion or Vitek, without standardization of the inoculum [8–10].

Here we validated a new kit from FASTinov®, Porto, Portugal, the QuickPrep®, that allows the ID directly from urine samples. This kit is composed of two fundamental steps, the first one being the centrifugation with a hemolytic agent that lyses the red blood cells while preserving the viability of bacterial cells. In the second stage, a density separating agent is used to remove other blood cells, which gives us a final concentrated bacterial suspension with few debris.

The aim of this study was to evaluate the use of FASTinov® QUICKprep® kit on positive urine samples, for rapid ID using Quickprep® protocol followed by MALDI-TOF-MS. This protocol was already tested directly in blood cultures with excellent results [11].

Methods

A total of 281 urines, 166 inoculated in urine of healthy volunteers as described by Roos V. et al. [12], with bacterial strains belonging to the FASTinov® collection, and 115 urine samples from clinical lab UNILABS, were analysed. The urine preparations comprised 194 Gram-Negative bacteria, 77 Gram-Positive bacteria and 12 yeasts (see Table 1). All the urine samples were analysed on the Sysmex UF 5000, which relies on thousands of particles counted and classified from urine samples using flow cytometry technology, and if bacterial concentrations were superior to 40,000 cells/µL, they were considered positive and included in the study. After this, eight to ten mL of each urine sample was centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10 min. The supernatant was discarded, and the pellet was resuspended in 1 mL of sterile, deionised water. This solution was then treated according to the instructions for use of the QUICKprep® from FASTinov® to obtain a concentrated pellet of microorganisms with few debris (see Fig. 1). For this, 1 mL of the concentrated sample was transferred into Tube A (Eppendorf tube containing a haemolytic agent) of the QUICKprep® kit. After vortexing, the tube was centrifuged for 1 min at 13,000 rpm. The supernatant was discarded, and the pellet was resuspended in 500 µL of sterile, deionised water. The entire content of Tube A was then transferred carefully into Tube B, which contained a density-separating agent, and centrifuged again for 1 min at 13,000 rpm. The supernatant was once more discarded, and the pellet resuspended in 500 µL of sterile, deionised water and centrifuged (1 min at 13000 rpm). The final supernatant was discarded. The resulting pellet was used for microbial identification via the MALDI-TOF-MS system (Bruker DaltoniK GmbH, Bremen, Germany), using two spots following the manufacturer’s protocol; when ID was not provided, a solution of 70% formic acid was used. The MALDI-TOF-MS was used in the Sepsityper mode and validated if the score was above 1.7 at least in one spot. For comparison, the urine was subcultured in UTI Culture Medium from ThermoFisher® (Massachusetts, USA), and the colonies were analysed and identified on MALDI-TOF-MS, according to UNILABS routine. Reproducibility was evaluated with 10 inoculated samples performed independently 3 times. The limit of detection was determined by diluting 3 samples at 1:2, 1:5 and 1:10 and all the processes repeated and the results compared with non-diluted samples. The results obtained directly from urine using QUICKprep® protocol and those obtained using isolated colonies (24 h after) were compared in terms of time-to-result and accuracy.

Results

In the inoculated urines, all 166 strains (100%) were correctly identified. Regarding the 115 samples of clinical samples, the MALDI-TOF-MS was unable to identify 13 specimens (11.3%), even with the addition of formic acid. Those cases were identified as E. coli (11 samples) and K. pneumoniae (2 samples) as predominant on the standard method, although all showed mixed populations. In 5 samples, MALDI-TOF-MS reported mixed populations: E. coli plus Enterococcus faecalis; E. coli plus Corynebacterium tuberculostearicum; E. coli plus Arthrobacter luteolus; K. pneumoniae plus Pseudomonas aeruginosa; K. pneumoniae plus Scadinavium goeteborgense, all considered as contaminated samples on the standard method. No wrong identifications were provided (see Fig. 2). The scores obtained with direct ID were all > 1.9, being slightly below on Gram-positive cocci when compared to Gram-negative. All the diluted sample were correctly identified, concluding that the limit of detection is at least 1:10. Taking together the sensitivity and specificity was 95.4% and 100%, respectively. The reproducibility was 97.8% (1 sample with E. coli failed to identify in one repetition). The time-to-result was reduced from 24 h to around 15 min in all cases.

Discussion

Direct, rapid, and accurate ID of microorganisms from clinical samples—bypassing culture and shortening turnaround time by 1 day—supports timely clinical decision-making. The therapeutic approach may vary significantly depending on whether the pathogen is a cocci, a bacilli, or a yeast, and between different bacterial species, such as, for instance, Escherichia coli or Pseudomonas aeruginosa, even representing only a small percentage. Although rapid identification cannot replace AST, it provides valuable guidance for empiric therapy and facilitates the prompt initiation of a standard AST, as the selection of antibiotics depends on that, or even the decision not to proceed due to contamination. Regarding rapid AST, such as the FASTinov assays, it demands a rapid ID not only to select the adequate antibiotics (FASTgramneg or FASTgrampos) but also for the interpretation of breakpoints, being in certain cases species-specific, impacting on report.

This approach directed at urine samples was only feasible due to the availability of advanced urine screening methods, such as Sysmex analysers which, not only identify negative samples, avoiding culture, but also provide valuable information in positive cases. These include bacterial load (number of bacteria per urine volume), infection-related parameters (e.g., leukocytes and red blood cells), potential contamination, and even an indication of the Gram status of the microorganism—Gram-positive, Gram-negative or yeasts.

The kit QUICKPrep® from FASTinov® is a rapid and reliable method for identifying both Gram-negative, Gram-positive bacteria and yeasts. Inoculated samples were used to increase the variability of microorganisms and 100% of correct identifications were obtained as they were pure cultures. In clinical samples, 11% could not be identified, likely due to sample contamination. While the use of formic acid can improve identification in some organisms, it does not solve issues related to mixed microbial populations. In five clinical samples, more than one microorganism was identified—an important finding, as no AST method could be performed in polymicrobial samples. Overall, in 18 out of 115 clinical samples (~ 16%), 13 had no identification and 5 had multiple species detected, all likely due to contamination. When no ID or mixed population is obtained, we should not proceed with AST, or incorrect results would be obtained. This underscores a key limitation of direct-from-sample testing: the absence of a pure culture can compromise downstream analyses. This brings up the topic of the importance of sample collection.

When compared to the other protocols for direct ID from urine [8–10], the QUICKprep kit provides a very “clean” pellet due to its protocol responsible for the high scores obtained on MALDI-TOF-MS. This is also an advantage for any AST methods. The accuracy of determining the concentration of microorganisms is very high. This procedure enables the accurate use of the McFarland scale to perform the quick FASTinov AST method based on flow cytometry directly from positive blood cultures [13] or any other method.

Despite the transformative impact of MALDI-TOF-MS technology in microbiology, it still presents some limitations namely, the requirement for a high bacterial load and the need for a clean, interference-free sample. The FASTinov® QUICKprep® was specifically developed to concentrate and purify biological specimens, enabling accurate MALDI-TOF-MS identification while preserving microbial viability. This preserved viability allows subsequent diagnostic applications, such as phenotypic AST and represents a significant advantage in terms of both time and resource efficiency.

In conclusion, the QUICKprep® kit represents a reliable method for the direct ID of microorganisms in urine samples, however, a higher number of clinical samples are needed to be tested. Nonetheless the ones presented in our study are the more frequently found in the clinical routine.

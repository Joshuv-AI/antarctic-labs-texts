Real-world impact of SGLT2 inhibitors on the risk of symptomatic urinary tract infections in patients with cardiometabolic and kidney diseases: a prospective, multicentre, observational cohort study protocol
Authors: Shuai Xiang, Gaigai Ma, Junjie Ji, Dongmei Li, Qingya Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionSodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors were originally introduced for glycaemic management of type 2 diabetes mellitus and now provide substantial cardiovascular and kidney benefits. Although their association with genital mycotic infections is well established, whether SGLT2 inhibitor initiation increases the risk of symptomatic urinary tract infections (UTIs) remains uncertain, particularly within the cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic syndrome framework. This protocol describes a prospective multicentre observational study designed to address this clinically important uncertainty in a Chinese population with complex multimorbidity.Methods and analysisThis is a prospective, multicentre, observational cohort study conducted between June 2026 and June 2028 at two tertiary hospitals in China. Adults newly initiating an SGLT2 inhibitor or a clinically appropriate non-SGLT2 active comparator will be enrolled. A new-user design and 1:1 propensity score matching will be used to reduce confounding by indication and prevalent-user bias. A total of 2400 participants (1200 per group) will be recruited. The primary endpoint is the first newly onset symptomatic UTI during 12 months of follow-up, defined by prespecified clinical symptoms plus laboratory evidence. Clinician-diagnosed genital infection is retained as a secondary safety endpoint. The primary treatment–effect estimate will be obtained using a Cox proportional hazards model in the matched cohort with cluster-robust SEs. Absolute cumulative incidence will be estimated with the Aalen-Johansen method, and Fine-Gray regression will provide a complementary competing-risk analysis for all-cause mortality. Sensitivity analyses will address treatment switching, stricter culture-confirmed outcomes, missing covariates and unmeasured confounding.Ethics and disseminationThe study protocol and informed consent documents have been reviewed and approved by the Clinical Research Ethics Committees of Qilu Hospital (Qingdao), Shandong University and the Second Affiliated Hospital of Xi'an Jiaotong University (Approval No. KYLL-2025100). The study will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, ICH-GCP and ISPE Good Pharmacoepidemiology Practices. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to enrolment. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and academic conferences, regardless of whether results are positive, negative or inconclusive.Trial registration numberChinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR), registration number ChiCTR2600124776; registered prior to first participant enrolment.

Introduction

Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors (eg, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, canagliflozin) lower blood glucose by inhibiting glucose reabsorption in the proximal renal tubule and promoting glycosuria.1 Beyond glycaemic control, these agents have demonstrated substantial clinical benefits in reducing cardiovascular events, improving heart failure prognosis, and delaying the progression of chronic kidney disease.24 In 2023, the American Heart Association formally introduced the concept of cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic (CKM) syndrome to capture the interconnected pathophysiology of metabolic risk factors, chronic kidney disease and cardiovascular disease; within this paradigm, SGLT2 inhibitors have emerged as a unifying therapeutic option spanning multiple disease stages.5 6

However, the unique mechanism of ‘therapeutic glycosuria’ associated with SGLT2 inhibitors leads to elevated urinary glucose concentrations, theoretically creating a favourable environment for microbial proliferation in the urogenital tract.7

Clinical evidence regarding genital mycotic infections is highly consistent; randomised trials and meta-analyses show an increased risk in diabetic and non-diabetic populations.811 Because SGLT2 inhibitors are now used in cardiorenal populations irrespective of diabetes status,12 this established safety signal remains relevant across the study population. Genital candidiasis is not classified as a sexually transmitted infection, and its occurrence is influenced by host and treatment-related factors including diabetes, prior candidiasis and antimicrobial exposure.7 13 We, therefore, regard genital infection as an established adverse effect that remains clinically relevant for safety surveillance but does not require confirmation as a coprimary research question.

Regarding urinary tract infections (UTIs), however, the clinical evidence remains uncertain. Early pharmacovigilance reports raised a potential safety signal, but randomised-trial meta-analysis14 and large population-based active-comparator new-user cohorts15 16 have not shown a consistent class-wide increase in UTI risk. Fralick and MacFadden proposed a possible ‘flushing effect’, whereby osmotic diuresis increases urine flow and voiding frequency and may partly offset the microbial growth favoured by glycosuria.17 This unresolved contrast provides the rationale for a prospective study with standardised symptomatic-UTI ascertainment.

Despite these hypotheses, the risk management of urogenital infections in real-world practice remains challenging, and clinical guidance is currently limited by a reliance on post-hoc analyses of RCTs or retrospective database mining.18 Such studies are often constrained by strict enrollment criteria and the inherent difficulty of fully controlling for indication bias. Furthermore, there is a paucity of prospective cohort studies specifically targeting Chinese populations with complex multimorbidities, including T2DM, heart failure and various primary glomerular diseases, particularly under the integrative CKM framework.

Therefore, we designed a prospective, multicentre, observational cohort study to estimate whether initiation of SGLT2 inhibitors is associated with a clinically important increase in the risk of symptomatic UTIs. A new-user, indication-aligned active-comparator design combined with PSM will be used to reduce measured confounding. Clinician-diagnosed genital infections will continue to be collected as a secondary safety outcome, and prespecified subgroup analyses will explore susceptibility characteristics relevant to personalised prescribing.

Methods

Study design and setting

This is a prospective, multicentre, observational cohort study designed to evaluate the real-world impact of initiating SGLT2 inhibitors on the risk of symptomatic UTIs. Clinician-diagnosed genital infection will be monitored as a secondary safety endpoint. The study is scheduled to be conducted between June 2026 and June 2028 at two large tertiary hospitals in China: Qilu Hospital (Qingdao), Shandong University (the coordinating centre) and the Second Affiliated Hospital of Xi'an Jiaotong University.

To minimise prevalent user bias and the healthy adherer effect commonly encountered in observational studies, this study rigorously employs a new-user design. We strictly define ‘time zero’ for the cohort as the date of the first prescription and initiation of either SGLT2 inhibitors (the exposure cohort) or standard non-SGLT2 inhibitor therapies (the comparator cohort) following the baseline visit. From time zero, all participants will enter a prospective follow-up period until the occurrence of a primary endpoint event, loss to follow-up, death or the end of the study period.

The design and reporting of this observational protocol incorporate applicable protocol items from the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) 2013 explanation and elaboration19 and follow the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) recommendations.20 Because SPIRIT was developed for clinical trial protocols, only items relevant to this non-interventional cohort are applied. The study has been approved by the Clinical Research Ethics Committees of all participating centres, and written informed consent will be obtained before enrolment.

Study population and eligibility criteria

The target population comprises adults who newly initiate an SGLT2 inhibitor or an indication-aligned comparator in routine practice. For T2DM, active comparators include newly initiated non-SGLT2 glucose-lowering therapies considered reasonable alternatives at the same treatment stage. For heart failure or kidney disease without T2DM, the comparator cohort comprises otherwise eligible patients receiving guideline-directed cardiorenal care in whom the treating physician independently elects not to initiate an SGLT2 inhibitor. Treatment is not assigned by the study. Eligibility, time zero, follow-up intensity and outcome ascertainment are identical in both cohorts.

Bias control and matching strategy

In observational cohort studies, the prescription of SGLT2 inhibitors is not random but based on patients’ clinical characteristics, inevitably introducing confounding by indication and selection bias. To accurately evaluate the real-world treatment effect, this study implements a multi-level bias control and matching strategy during the enrolment and data analysis phases.

Restriction and matching during cohort construction

First, both cohorts follow a new-user design. During recruitment, centre-specific frequency targets will be monitored by calendar quarter, sex, age band and a prespecified hierarchical primary treatment indication to preserve empirical overlap without changing physicians’ treatment decisions. The hierarchical indication categories will be T2DM, heart failure without T2DM and kidney disease without T2DM or heart failure; coexisting conditions will additionally be retained as covariates. This avoids treating overlapping diagnoses as mutually exclusive clinical entities.

Matching quality assessment and outcome regression

Following PSM, the standardised mean difference (SMD) is used to quantitatively assess the balance of all baseline covariates between the two matched cohorts. An absolute SMD value of <0.1 (ie, <10%) for all covariates is predefined as indicating optimal balance.

The primary outcome model will then be fitted in the matched cohort. Covariates with residual imbalance after matching will be included in an adjusted Cox model, and cluster-robust SEs will account for matched pairs. This regression adjustment is intended to address residual measured imbalance and improve precision; it is not described as a doubly robust estimator.

Sample size estimation

The revised sample size calculation is based exclusively on time to first symptomatic UTI. Short-term dapagliflozin trials reported UTI proportions of 3.6%–5.7% over 12–24 weeks.21 Pooled empagliflozin safety analyses reported UTI incidence rates of approximately 9.27–11.3 per 100 person-years.22 23 A meta-analysis found no overall class-wide increase in UTI risk (RR 1.03, 95% CI 0.96 to 1.11),14 confirming that genital infection rates should not be used to power the UTI question. We selected a comparator hazard of 9.7 per 100 person-years22 and an HR of 1.50 as the smallest relative increase considered clinically important for safety decision-making.

Under proportional hazards, two-sided alpha=0.05, 80% power and equal allocation, approximately 191 first symptomatic UTI events are required to detect an HR of 1.50. A comparator hazard of 9.7 per 100 person-years corresponds to an estimated 12-month cumulative incidence of 9.2%; under HR=1.50, the corresponding incidence is 13.5%. Approximately 1677 analysable participants are, therefore, required. Allowing separately for up to 15% loss to follow-up and 15% exclusion because of lack of common support or unsuccessful PSM yields 2321 required recruits. The target has been rounded to 2400 participants, with 1200 recruited to each cohort. The calculation was independently reproduced using the Schoenfeld event formula and will be documented in the statistical analysis plan.

Study endpoints and definitions

To ensure consistency across centres, the primary symptomatic UTI endpoint requires prespecified clinical symptoms plus laboratory evidence. Asymptomatic bacteriuria is not counted as a primary event. Genital infection is collected separately as a secondary safety outcome.

Primary endpoint

The sole primary endpoint is time to the first newly onset symptomatic UTI during the 12-month follow-up period.

A symptomatic UTI must meet both components: (a) at least one new lower urinary tract symptom (dysuria, urgency, frequency or suprapubic pain) or an upper urinary tract presentation (fever with flank pain or costovertebral-angle tenderness); and (b) laboratory evidence consisting of pyuria (positive leucocyte esterase or ≥10 leukocytes per high-power field) and/or a urine culture growing a recognised uropathogen at ≥103 CFU/mL.24 Events based only on asymptomatic bacteriuria, administrative codes or self-report without laboratory support will not meet the primary definition.25

Culture-negative symptomatic episodes with compatible symptoms and pyuria may meet the primary definition. Clinician-treated episodes lacking both pyuria and a positive culture will be recorded but excluded from the primary analysis and included only in a prespecified broader-definition sensitivity analysis.

Microscopy or fungal culture will be recorded when obtained. Safety monitoring and adverse event reporting

Definition and collection of adverse events

An adverse event (AE) is any untoward medical occurrence from consent through the end of follow-up, irrespective of causal attribution. The research team will systematically record all AEs during diary review, monthly telephone follow-up and quarterly visits. Symptomatic UTI is the primary endpoint, whereas clinician-diagnosed genital infection and other prespecified events are secondary safety outcomes.

Based on the pharmacological profile of SGLT2 inhibitors, this study has prespecified adverse events of special interest (AESI), primarily including euDKA, severe hypoglycaemia, AKI, severe volume depletion/hypotension and necrotising fasciitis of the perineum (Fournier’s gangrene). All AEs and AESIs will be coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA, V.27.0 or later).

Reporting procedures for serious adverse events

A serious AE (SAE) is defined as any medical occurrence that results in death, is life-threatening, requires inpatient hospitalisation or prolongation of existing hospitalisation, or results in persistent or significant disability/incapacity.

On the occurrence of an SAE, the CRC or subinvestigator who discovers the event must complete an SAE Report Form and notify the principal investigator (PI) and the Institutional Review Boards/Ethics Committees (EC) of both centres within 24 hours of awareness. The EC and the PI will evaluate the severity and relatedness of the event to determine whether it is necessary to suspend the specific participant’s further involvement and will ensure appropriate reporting to local pharmacovigilance authorities.

Clinical intervention and follow-up tracking

Given the observational and non-interventional design of this study, researchers will not interfere with the clinical decision-making of the attending physicians. Following the occurrence of an AE or SAE, the attending physician will independently decide whether to temporarily withhold the target medication, adjust the dosage or switch to an alternative therapeutic regimen based entirely on the clinical reality. The research team will meticulously record all instances of drug discontinuation or regimen changes. Participants experiencing SAEs will be continuously monitored and followed until the event is resolved, stabilised or deemed an irreversible chronic condition.

Data management and quality control

Data capture and privacy protection

This study will utilise a web-based EDC system for centralised data management. Clinical data will be extracted from the institutional EMR systems by trained CRCs and entered into standardised eCRFs. All data entered into the EDC system will undergo deidentification/pseudonymisation; participants will be identified solely by a unique study subject number. Data handling will comply with the Personal Information Protection Law of the People’s Republic of China (PIPL, effective November 2021) and the Human Genetic Resources Administrative Regulations.

Quality control and clinical monitoring

Prior to the official commencement of the study, the coordinating centre will organise an investigator meeting and conduct site initiation visits. Throughout the study period, independent CRAs will conduct regular monitoring visits at each subcentre, performing 100% source data verification for critical variables to guarantee that the data within the eCRFs are strictly concordant with the original medical records.

Data cleaning and database lock

Logical checks and range limits will be preprogrammed into the EDC system to automatically intercept outliers. On the completion of all follow-ups and the satisfactory resolution of all queries, a Final Data Review Meeting will be jointly conducted by the PI, the Data Manager and the Biostatistician. After the database is locked, no further modifications to the data will be permitted.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics and incidence rates

For the unmatched cohort, normally distributed continuous variables will be presented as the mean±SD and compared using Student’s t-test. Variables with a skewed distribution will be expressed as the median and IQR and compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Categorical variables will be presented as frequencies and percentages and compared using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. For the matched cohorts, baseline balance will be exclusively evaluated using the SMD, with an absolute SMD<0.1 indicating optimal balance.

The number of first symptomatic UTI events and incidence rate per 100 person-years will be reported with 95% CIs. Twelve-month cumulative incidence will be estimated with the Aalen-Johansen estimator to account for death as a competing event. Genital infection and other secondary outcomes will be summarised separately.

Survival analysis and risk assessment

Primary risk model: follow-up extends from time 0 to first adjudicated symptomatic UTI, loss to follow-up, death or 12 months, whichever occurs first. The prespecified treatment effect is the cause-specific HR for SGLT2 inhibitor initiation versus active comparator in the PSM cohort. A Cox model with treatment as the principal exposure and cluster-robust SEs for matched pairs will be used; covariates with residual absolute SMD≥0.1 will be adjusted. Proportional hazards will be assessed using Schoenfeld residuals. Absolute risks and risk differences at 12 months will also be reported.

Subgroup analyses and interaction tests

The primary symptomatic UTI analysis will be repeated in prespecified subgroups defined by age (<65 vs ≥65 years), sex, hierarchical primary treatment indication, T2DM status, heart failure status, kidney disease status, baseline eGFR (≥60 vs <60 mL/min/1.73 m2), HbA1c among participants with diabetes (<7.0% vs ≥7.0%), menopausal status and chronic antibiotic prophylaxis. Interaction terms and p values for interaction will be reported. These analyses are exploratory and are not separately powered.

Sensitivity analyses and missing data

To test the robustness of the primary conclusions, the following sensitivity analyses will be conducted:

As-treated analysis: exposure will be updated for discontinuation or switching. Because such changes may be informative, inverse-probability-of-censoring weights based on time-updated clinical variables will be used where model stability permits. The primary analysis is more precisely described as an observational treatment-initiation estimand rather than an intention-to-treat analysis.

The amount, pattern and likely mechanism of missingness will be described for every variable. Complete-case analysis will not be selected solely by a percentage threshold. When baseline confounders are incompletely observed and a missing-at-random assumption is plausible, multiple imputation by chained equations will create at least 20 datasets and include treatment, outcome indicator, Nelson-Aalen cumulative hazard, centre and all propensity-score covariates. Propensity scores and matching will be performed within each imputed dataset, treatment estimates will be combined using Rubin’s rules, and complete-case and delta-adjusted analyses will assess sensitivity. Outcomes will not be imputed; loss to follow-up will be addressed through censoring and an inverse-probability-of-censoring sensitivity analysis.

Analyses will be performed using R (V.4.5.1 or later), principally with MatchIt and cobalt for matching and balance diagnostics, survival for cause-specific models, riskRegression or an equivalent validated package for competing-risk estimation, mice for multiple imputation and EValue for sensitivity analysis. All primary tests will be two-sided with alpha=0.05; secondary and subgroup analyses will be interpreted with emphasis on effect estimates and 95% CIs.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public were not directly involved in the original formulation of the research question or outcome hierarchy. The revised protocol prioritises symptomatic UTI as the sole primary endpoint and retains genital infection, treatment discontinuation and infection-related hospitalisation as patient-relevant secondary outcomes. A paper and electronic diary were piloted with five candidate participants for clarity and burden. After study initiation, a patient advisory group of three to five participants will meet semi-annually to review follow-up burden, diary usability, participant-facing materials and dissemination. The final lay summary will be codeveloped with patient representatives.

Study strengths and limitations

This study has several methodological strengths. It prospectively addresses the unresolved risk of symptomatic UTI in a Chinese population spanning T2DM, heart failure and kidney disease. A new-user, indication-aligned active-comparator design and prespecified PSM strategy reduce prevalent-user and measured indication bias. Daily symptom diaries, monthly structured review, prompt laboratory verification and blinded endpoint adjudication reduce recall and outcome-classification bias. Finally, the analysis separates cause-specific and cumulative-incidence estimands in the presence of competing mortality.

Important limitations remain. Residual and time-varying confounding cannot be eliminated in an observational study, although E-values and weighted sensitivity analyses will quantify robustness. The study is powered to detect an HR of 1.50 and may not reliably detect smaller relative increases. Disease-specific and recurrent-event analyses may have limited precision. Recruitment at two tertiary hospitals may select patients with greater clinical complexity than community populations; referral source and recruitment setting will, therefore, be documented, and generalisation to primary care should be cautious.

Ethics and dissemination

The research project will be carried out in strict accordance with the current version of the Declaration of Helsinki, the core principles of the International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice (ICH-GCP) guidelines as applicable to observational research, the International Society for Pharmacoepidemiology (ISPE) Good Pharmacoepidemiology Practices (GPP) and applicable Chinese regulatory requirements. The study protocol and the informed consent documents have been rigorously reviewed and approved by the Clinical Research Ethics Committees of both the coordinating centre (Qilu Hospital (Qingdao), Shandong University) and the participating sub-centre (the Second Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University) (Approval No.: KYLL-2025100). The coordinating ethics office can be contacted at qlyyqdky@126.com.

Prior to enrolment, all eligible participants will receive comprehensive written and oral information from the study investigators regarding the study’s purpose, observational procedures and data privacy protections. The participants will have sufficient time to consider their participation before signing the document. Written informed consent must be obtained from all participants prior to their inclusion in the study. Participants may withdraw from the study at any time by revoking their consent without providing any reason, and this will not negatively affect their routine medical care.

The findings of this study will be disseminated to the scientific community and healthcare professionals through publication in peer-reviewed international medical journals and presentations at relevant academic conferences, regardless of whether the results are positive, negative or inconclusive. In addition, lay summaries codeveloped with patient representatives will be distributed via outpatient channels and patient associations at the participating centres.

Four-year follow-up from the phase 2 study TD-NICE: neoadjuvant treatment of tislelizumab combined with chemotherapy in resectable esophageal squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Yajie Mao, Zhiyuan Gao, Yichen Sun, Changjian Shao, Hongtao Duan, Xiaolong Yan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe efficacy and safety of tislelizumab combined with chemotherapy as neoadjuvant therapy have been presented and evaluated in previously published TD-NICE study. However, the long-term impacts of patients who received tislelizumab plus chemotherapy followed by surgery were not observed in the study.MethodsFrom September 2020 to March 2021, a total of 45 patients were enrolled in the TD-NICE trial. The 4 -year follow-up data of the TD-NICE ITT population were collected by investigators. The primary endpoint was OS, and the secondary endpoint was EFS. Kaplan-Meier method and cox proportional hazards model were used to proceed statistical analysis.ResultsThe median OS and EFS were not reached in all patients. The OS was 82.2% (95%CI:67.6–90.7) at 12 months, 73.3%(95%CI:57.8–83.9) at 24 months, 66.7%(95%CI:50.9–78.4) at 36 months and simulated 66.2% (95%CI:46.5–74.6) at 48 months. There were statistically significant differences between surgery and non-surgery patients(P = 0.046). And patients with R0 resection had a higher improvement in OS than those with R1&R2 resection (P = 0.041). The differences in EFS in surgery and non-surgery group (P = 0.046) as well as in R0 resection and R1&R2 resection group (P = 0.045) were significant, respectively.DiscussionOur study identified that chemoimmunotherapy plus surgery could improve overall survival in resectable ESCC, and patients had significant benefits from surgery and R0 tumor resection.Trial registrationTD-NICE study was registered in the August 28, 2020, and the registration number was ChiCTR2000037488 (Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR) identifier).

Introduction

Esophageal cancer (EC) is one of the common cancers with the 11th incidence and 7th mortality in worldwide cancer statistics [1]. There are two primary histologic subtypes of EC: esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC) and esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) [2]. The significant differences between the two subtypes have been reported that EAC has higher incidence rates in Northern Europe and North America and ESCC has higher incidence rates in Eastern and South Central Asia and South Africa [3]. In China, ESCC is mainly prevalent due to the risk factors of smoking and alcohol [1].

Preoperative neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy followed by surgery significantly has enabled patients with locally advanced ESCC to benefit from longer overall survival and disease-free survival [4], and has been approved as the first-line treatment for locally advanced ESCC [5]. Meanwhile, immunotherapy in cancer is promoted for the finding and development of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). The most frequent application of ICIs treatment is the PD-1/PD-L1 antibody, the FDA has approved 7 PD-1/PD-L1 antibodies and 1 CTLA-4 antibody [6]. PD-1/PD-L1 pathway is the most classical tumor regulation, combination of immunotherapy and chemotherapy would benefit patients from improving tumor progression and degradation. Several clinical trials have provided related evidence that patients had better tumor response with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy [7, 8].

The efficacy and safety of tislelizumab combined with chemotherapy as neoadjuvant therapy have been presented and evaluated in the previously published study, which had 57.8% (26/45) MPR and 40% (18/45) pCR in intention-to-treatment (ITT) populations and 72% (26/36) MPR and 50% (18/36) pCR in surgical populations [9]. However, the further impacts of patients who received tislelizumab plus chemotherapy followed by surgery weren’t observed in the study, we conducted a 4-year follow-up of the ITT population, aimed to evaluate the long-term survival time.

Methods

Study design

The study TD-NICE, an open-label, single-center, single-arm, phase II study, demonstrated the acceptable efficacy and safety in resectable ESCC patients by combining tislelizumab and chemotherapy. To explore the long-term impacts of tislelizumb combined with chemotherapy, we had a 4-year follow-up between the ITT population.

Participants

For the TD-NCIE study, eligible patients were aged 18–75 years and had histologically confirmed stage II-IVA (American Joint Committee on Cancer 8th criteria) ESCC, without radiotherapy, immunological or other drugs treatment before recruitment and distant metastasis. Meanwhile, patients demanded tumor location below neck (≥ 2 cm) and cricopharyngeal muscle (≥ 5 cm), an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status score of 0 or 1, imaging examination (using computed tomography and ultrasound) and adequate hepatic (total bilirubin ≤ 2.5 ×upper normal limit [ULN]), renal (serum creatinine ≤ 2.5 × ULN) and hematological (neutrophil count > 1500/µL, hemoglobin count > 8.0 g/dL, platelet count > 100,000/µL) function. Certain exclusion criteria included the presence or history of autoimmune disease or disorder, concurrent other malignancies and interstitial pneumonia.

Procedures

Patients in the trial received 3 cycles following drugs as preoperative treatment, tislelizumab (200 mg on Day 1 of each cycle), carboplatin (area under the curve = 5 on Day 1 of each cycle) albumin-bound paclitaxel (130 mg/m2 on Days 1 and 8 of each cycle). When neoadjuvant therapy cycles were accomplished, patients were performed surgery after 4–6 weeks. The critical surveillance would be proceeded by the investigators with objective examinations in the duration of treatment. During treatment, tumor samples of patients before the first treatment and in surgery were collected to analyze PD-L1 expression and TMB levels. PD-L1 expression levels were detected by formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tumor samples with immunohistochemistry (IHC), further combined positive score (CPS) were observed by two pathologists. The tumor mutation burden (TMB) levels were accessed by next-generation sequencing.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint was overall survival (OS) at 4 years. OS was defined as the time from enrollment to death from any cause. Event-free survival (EFS) was the secondary endpoint, which was observed by investigators at 4 years and defined as the time from randomization to events progression as death from any cause, disease progression or relapse. High-resolution computed tomography was performed every 3 months for 1–2 years and every 6 months for 3–4 years, every 12 months for 5 years until radiographic disease progression. Safety was supervised and accessed by the laboratory tests while patients received the whole therapy, adverse events were documented including cause, grade, incidence and severity. Major pathological response (MPR) was defined as ≤ 10% viable tumor cells in resected primary tumor and all lymph nodes, complete pathological response (pCR) was defined as 0% viable tumor cells in resected primary tumor and all lymph nodes.

Follow-up methods

To compositionally balance the geographical, economic and patient factors, telephone follow-up was recommended as the preferred method for patients, especially those who lived in distant. High-resolution computed tomography, electronic gastroscopy, and blood tumor markers were recommended for patients as general check methods every 3 months for 1–2 years and every 6 months for 3–4 years. Patients lived in distant could complete mentioned methods in local hospitals. Moreover, for patients with current hospital visit records, face-to-face follow-up could be more accurate by collecting more complete information, and related check results were collected at the same time.

Statistical analysis

The sample size was 45 patients as intention-to-treatment populations. OS and EFS were used to assess the efficacy of enrolled patients. We used the Kaplan-Meier method, cox proportional hazards model and log-rank test to analyze OS, EFS, Hazard ratios, corresponding to 95% and differences. All statistical analyses were performed via OriginPro 2024 SR1 10.1.0.178 software.

In the study, 3 (6.67%) of 45 patients received treatment for 2 cycles, including 2 (4.44%) patients due to symptom improvement and 1 (2.2%) patient due to comorbidity-cholecystitis, 42 (93.3%) of 45 patients received complete neoadjuvant treatment of combing tislelizumab and chemotherapy for 3 cycles. Of 42 patients who had completed 3 cycles of treatment, 36 (80.0%) patients finally underwent surgery and 6 (20.0%) patients refused surgery including 4 (8.89%) patients for symptom improvement and 2 (4.44%) patients for disease progression (Fig. 1). 36 patients accepted surgery consisted of 29 (80.56%) R0 resection cases, 6 (16.67%) R1 resection cases and 1 (2.78%) R2 resection case. As of the data cutoff on November 29, 2024, the median follow-up was 47.98 months (range 3.13–50.59) for all patients. A minimum follow-up of 48 months for half included patients and the last patients reached a 45-month follow-up period, leading to that a 4-year survival rate was simulated in subsequent analysis.

The recurrence data were the same that were incomplete. In 17 dead patients, 2 patients were dead from postoperative complications, and 3 patients dead from the recurrence of ESCC.

At the data cutoff, 28 (62.2%) of 45 patients were alive without disease recurrence (Fig. 2A). The median OS was not reached in all patients. Adverse events and recurrence events

The related adverse events were monitored during the immunotherapy treatment and chemotherapy, however, the collection of adverse events during follow-up was difficult and incomplete. The recurrence data were the same that were incomplete. In 17 dead patients, 2 patients were dead from postoperative complications, and 3 patients dead from the recurrence of ESCC.

Discussion

The survival data based on the TD-NICE study were collected in a 4-year follow-up, delineated that neoadjuvant treatment with the combination of tislelizumab and chemotherapy significantly improved the long-term OS and EFS in resectable ESCC patients. There were higher OS and EFS for patients undergoing surgery compared with those rejecting surgery. And tumor resection methods resulted in different benefits among surgical patients, in which patients had better OS and EFS with R0 resection than R1&2 resection. Moreover, of patients enrolled in the study, the complete accomplishment of neoadjuvant treatment and surgery operation could enable patients to increase survival time and decrease the probability of disease recurrence.

The standardized therapy for locally advanced ESCC patients is neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy plus surgery based on NCCN 2023 [11]. Several clinical trials have been currently carried out and demonstrated the profile of neoadjuvant treatment of EC. CROSS trial (Chemoradiotherapy for Oesophageal Cancer Followed by Surgery Study) had reported that patients with chemoradiotherapy followed by surgery had better prognoses than those treated with surgery alone (p = 0.004), which OS rates were 67% and 50% at 2 years, 58% and 44% at 3 years for each group [12]. According to NEOCRTEC5010 (Neoadjuvant Chemoradiotherapy for Esophageal Cancer 5010), a randomized clinical trial, the efficacy was also evaluated and confirmed in neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy plus surgery group compared to surgery alone group (p = 0.03) with a 3-year OS of 65.8% versus 57.8% and a 5-year OS of 59.9% versus 49.1%, respectively [4]. With subsequent CheckMate 577 trail, patients obtained longer DFS when receiving chemoimmunotherapy after surgery or preoperative treatment [13]. Previous studies widely focused on patients treated with nCRT or nRT, there existed a limited perspective of immunotherapy in ESCC. Then, in the NATION-1907 trial, patients treated with immunotherapy followed by surgery had favorable benefits with a 2-year OS of 92% [14]. The survival rates achieved 91% at 12 and 24 months among patients with chemoimmunotherapy plus surgery in the keystone-001 study [15]. Based on previous research which have shown the good therapeutic effects in the ESCC patients neoadjuvant treatment, and it revealed the feasibility to tislelizumab as a novel immunotherapy selection for patients during the neoadjuvant treatment [9]. The follow-up of this findings were aimed to detect the long-term impacts about this combination treatment, and our findings were consistent with the clinical trials mentioned above, and concluded the high efficacy and reliability of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy plus surgery in resectable ESCC.

In addition to the strategies of preoperative medicine, surgery and surgical methods also take an essential role in the therapy. According to NCCN 2023 esophageal cancer guidelines, esophagectomy combined with chemotherapy, biologic agents and others are still the primary therapy for patients who could undergo surgery with resectable clinical stage [11]. Tumor response assessment occurs not only in the primary tumor but also in relative lymph nodes, a radical resection is recommended and performed for patients with appropriate clinical status and consent [16]. Among patients with surgery and R0 resection subgroups, longer OS and EFS were observed from our findings compared with the NEOCRTEC-1601 Clinical Trial, in which the 3-year survival rates were 65.4% and 40.3% in patients with R0 resection and non-R0 resection, respectively [17]. Additionally, R1 resection standard of our study was performed innovatively in annular margins around the tumor, which aligned with AJCC 8th edition standards but different with traditional R1 resection in the upper and lower margins around the tumor [11]. Eligible patients were all screened and selected by the critical inclusion with II-IVA resectable ESCC, reminded that patients should finish the final surgery as soon as possible after neoadjuvant therapy. MPR and pCR have become available utilization to evaluate the efficacy during neoadjuvant therapy in abundant clinical trials [18–20]. Moreover, few studies explored the potential predictive value of MPR or pCR on prognosis. We found that comparison in each subgroup was insignificant but there was a better tendency in MPR and pCR groups, possible reasons could be attributed to the small sample size. In a meta-analysis, indicated that a longer OS and simulant PFS was reached in patients accepting immunotherapy plus chemotherapy with low-PD-L1 expression (CPS < 10) than those receiving chemotherapy alone, and patients with high-PD-L1 expression (CPS ≥ 10) benefited from chemoimmunotherapy [21]. And patients with high TMB have better tumor response in immunotherapy [22]. The familiar outcomes were found in our results. Whereas, subgroup analyses for TMB levels were no statistical differences due to sample size. The published paper suggested that HLA-02 supertype could be a potential biomarker of Non-pCR group [9], while there were no significance in analysis comparing HLA-02 group with Non-HLA-02 group which might attribute to the small sample size, indicating that HLA-02 could be a potential biomarker for predicting pCR rather than long-term survival, an further exploration for biomarkers of long-term survival requires more researchers to discover and validate.

Our study has limitations as follows. First, the small sample size couldn’t detect differences among subgroups, a trend without statistical evidence is ambiguous. Subsequent clinical trials would be designed and conducted with a large sample size possibly. Then, we didn’t talk about adverse events and recurrence data limited to a telephone follow-up that could collect key information of patients.

In brief, our study identified that chemoimmunotherapy plus surgery could improve overall survival in resectable ESCC, and patients had significant benefits from surgery and R0 tumor resection.

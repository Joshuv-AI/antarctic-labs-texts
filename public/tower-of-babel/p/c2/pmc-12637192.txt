KuSarcasm: Automated annotation of a sarcasm dataset using hybrid NLP techniques
Authors: Shakhawan Aghajan, Rebwar M Nabi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper presents KuSarcasm, a Kurdish Sorani dataset developed for the automatic detection of sarcasm expressions. KuSarcasm represents a carefully constructed resource designed to address the challenges of sarcasm identification in Sorani Kurdish, a linguistically rich but low-resource language with limited Natural Language Processing (NLP) resources. The dataset was compiled through a multi-stage process of data collection and annotation, guided by linguistic expertise and methodology. Source material was drawn from culturally significant repositories, including Kurdish proverbs, poetry, idiom collections, literary magazines such as Sekhurma, digital publications, and web archives. Extensive consultations with Kurdish linguists, editors, and researchers ensured robust annotation guidelines and cultural authenticity. Data collection relied primarily on manual efforts, complemented by Optical Character Recognition (OCR) for printed texts, web scraping, structured documentation, and filtering-based extraction, resulting in 16,833 entries. The dataset underwent comprehensive preprocessing, including deduplication, normalization, and noise removal. Automatic labelling was conducted using a locally tuned hybrid approach involving multilingual sentiment classification through Multilingual-Bidirectional Encoder Representations for Transformers (mBERT) and semantic similarity scoring through Sentence-Bidirectional Encoder Representations for Transformers (SBERT). A rule-based framework with over 100 predefined linguistic patterns further distinguished sarcastic from non-sarcastic texts based on emotional polarity and semantic proximity. Every entry is further annotated with metadata like source, matched rule, and sentiment category. Considering its scale, cultural depth, and methodological rigor, KuSarcasm provides a significant benchmark for low-resource language research, sentiment analysis, and computational linguistics while establishing a strong foundation for deep learning model development.Keywords: Keywords: sorani kurdish language, Sarcasm detection, Automated labelling, Dataset Annotation, Low-Resource Language, NLP

Background

The Kurdish language being of the Indo-European language family is more specifically within the northwestern branch of the Indo-Iranian group. Kurdish is an independent language with its own history of evolution, grammatical structure, and varied vocabulary and descended from the ancient Median or Proto-Kurdish language. Spoken by approximately 30 million in the Middle East Kurdistan area, Kurdish has three large dialect groups: Northern (Badínaní and Kurmanjí), Central (Soraní), and Southern (Pehlewaní or Pahlawanik), and two additional large branches, Hewramí (Goraní) and Dimílí (Zaza), with many sub-dialects. Some linguistic sources also associate the Lurrí (Luri) dialects of the southwestern Iranic branch with Kurdish due to shared vocabulary, though their relationship remains unclear. There is no universally accepted classification for Kurdish dialects, and even among Kurds, dialect names are often based on how they sound to outsiders, leading to varying and sometimes pejorative labels between dialect communities. Kurdish grammar is generally agglutinative, especially in Kurmanji, meaning it uses affixes extensively to convey grammatical relationships. It typically has a subject-object-verb (SOV) word order. Nouns vary in number and case, whereas verbs collect in tense, mood, and aspect. Sorani uses a system of pronominal suffixes attached to verbs and often lacks gender distinctions in nouns, unlike Kurmanji, which preserves grammatical gender. Additionally, Kurdish syntax allows for flexibility in word order due to its inflectional nature, though certain rules, such as adjective-noun placement and the use of postpositions, remain consistent [15]..

Nevertheless, the text highlights the significance of recognizing various Kurdish dialects and understanding their script for tasks such as translation, and creating annotated datasets for applications like sarcasm detection, and sentiment analysis. It also points out that Kurdish, due to its relation to languages like Persian and Pashto, plays a role in multilingual NLP models. However, as a low-resource language, Kurdish faces challenges in developing crucial NLP tools. Therefore, it is crucial to prioritize research in this area to develop resources like pre-trained models and tools specific to the Kurdish language [16].

Therefore, the motivation for compiling this dataset stems from the increasing importance of (NLP) techniques in understanding sentiment and intent across diverse languages. Sarcasm detection, in particular, remains a significant challenge for NLP systems due to its reliance on contextual, cultural, and linguistic subtleties [14]. While substantial progress has been made in high-resource languages, no datasets and resources exist for underrepresented languages such as Kurdish Sorani related to sarcasm detection.

Moreover, this dataset was developed within the context of a postgraduate research project focused on sarcasm detection in Kurdish Sorani texts using deep learning methods. The compilation process involved collecting literary and satirical content from cultural sources, such as Sekhurma Magazine, proverbs, and poetic texts. Data was cleaned, preprocessed, and annotated using a hybrid approach combining semantic similarity via SBERT [9] and sentiment classification via mBERT [12], supported by over 100 manually defined linguistic rules.

Additionally, the dataset bridges a gap in current NLP tools since it provides labeled, structured data for a low-resource language. While it is linked to a broader research objective on building a sarcasm detection system, this standalone data article provides the foundation needed for future studies in Kurdish language sentiment analysis and computational linguistics.

Data collection

The study corpus is gathered from some Kurdish literary and cultural sources, such as Sekhurma Magazine, proverb and sayings, idioms [13] and phrases, poems, and online sources. It is an excellent collection for Kurdish Sorani language studies and natural language processing. Two special Python models were also created to clean and merge poetic lines into meaningful two-line structures. As per this, the dataset has 16,833 instances of poems, proverbs, sarcasm, and jokes gathered over 85 days using two collaborative semi-automated deep learning models. process Annotation was carried out using the most current deep learning models mBERT [11] and SBERT [10].

One of the major sources, Sekhurma Magazine, is in hard copy only; its pages were thus copied by hand tediously and then converted into text using OCR technology. The printed pages were thoroughly read and processed through an OCR operation to extract the Kurdish Sorani text. After extraction, each line was checked and manually proofread for proper spelling, punctuation, and Sorani-specific characters, and ultimately the outcome was a clean, human-readable Kurdish manuscript. Ultimately, they delivered 1169 high-quality entries. Other precious sources are Kurdish proverbs that are part of the insightful poems of Mullah Ghafouri Hafiz of Mahabad, hand gathered from five parts of his proverb book on allekok.ir and paswecity.com, having 755 lines considered insightful sentences without any computational program.Zaniary.com, a different source, was also utilized to compile 139 proverb entries edited by Lanya Karim, based on contemporary socialites and human beings written in brief.

Additional enrichment was provided by 4527 proverbs in the book Byword by Sheikh Mohammed Khal in the guise of half-sentences, full sentences, and idiomatic expressions, greatly contributing to the semantic depth and idiomatic richness of the dataset.

The dataset was enriched by the effort of the renowned Kurdish poet Peramerd, three volumes printed and in PDF format, organized in order into 6591 Excel rows, thus making a contribution to thematic and cultural resonance.

In addition, 3471 rows of rich political and poetic content were presented by Dr. Sherko Abdullah, which was uploaded on pukmedia.com and contained various linguistic formats such as words, idiomatic phrases, compound words, and lines of poetry, adding linguistic features to the dataset.

Some of the best poems of Sheikh Reza Talabani, sarcastic and witty as they are, were manually picked from books.vejin.net and 181 rows filled with words, half-sentences, and full sentences were incorporated to give a balanced representation of Kurdish literary language.

The dataset is mostly used in two major natural language processing tasks: translation of Kurdish Sorani proverbs and sarcasm detection. It contains 16,834 tagged examples of which 9299 are sarcastic and 7535 non-sarcastic examples to train sarcasm detection algorithms. It also contains Kurdish Sorani proverbs and their English translation for translation and sentiment analysis. Through the integration of these constituents, the dataset enables the creation of NLP models that can identify sarcasm in addition to improving language comprehension in Kurdish Sorani, a valuable asset for improving the activity and application of NLP for low-resource languages.

Annotation pipeline

Our Kurdish Sorani sarcasm corpus was annotated with a semi-automatic process using two strong transformer models: SBERT [10] and mBERT [11]. Since there is no labeled data in Kurdish, particularly the Sorani dialect, we chose these models since they actually do very well in multilingual understanding and can generalize to low-resource languages. Phase The process was a two-stage pipeline. In phase 1, we employed mBERT (nlptown/bert-base-multilingual-uncased-sentiment) [17] for sentiment classification. Also, extreme positive or negative polarity sentences (i.e., 1–2 stars or 4–5 stars) were marked as probable sarcasm based on sentiment polarity incongruence with context. In phase 2, we employed SBERT[ 10] for semantic similarity comparison to a pre-filtered list of linguistic specific rules of sarcasm. Finally, this combination of methods allowed us to annotate 16,833 samples efficiently without human annotation while maintaining high semantic coherence and cultural background. The result is a high-quality, scalable, and reproducible annotated dataset specially tailored to text annotate in Kurdish Sorani.

Final labels were assigned as 1 for sarcastic and 0 for non-sarcastic. The annotated dataset was saved as a file and includes the following columns: ID, Text, Label, Matched Rule, and Sources. Validation and inter-annotator agreement (IAA)

As mentioned above the dataset was constructed in this research through an automated labelling methodology. Initially, entity labels were automatically assigned using advanced labelling techniques. Following this, the dataset underwent a thorough validation process by two expert linguists specializing in the Kurdish language. These experts meticulously examined the annotations, making necessary adjustments to ensure consistency and correctness. Any conflicting annotations were resolved through collaborative discussion between the two experts.

To evaluate the consistency of annotations between the two experts, Inter-Annotator Agreement (IAA) was calculated using Cohen’s Kappa (κ), a well-established statistical measure that accounts for chance agreement. The resulting Cohen’s Kappa score for this dataset was κ = 0.7994 or 0.80 %, which is classified as an “almost perfect” agreement (κ = 0.90 or 90.00 %) according to standard interpretation. This resultant high IAA value confirms the robustness and reliability of the annotated data set, a reflection of the success of the initial automated labelling process with expert guidance incorporated.

Limitations

The sarcasm detection model constructed in this research is susceptible to a number of significant limitations. Firstly, it is limited to processing written input and ignore prosodic properties like tone, pitch, or stress—often key properties for successful sarcasm detection. Second, the system is limited to binary classification, labelling input as sarcastic or non-sarcastic, rather than intended to detect differing levels of intensity or kinds of sarcasm. These limitations are further exacerbated by the absence of deep learning models uniquely fine-tuned on the Kurdish language, thus limiting the applicability and generalizability of the system in low-resource linguistic settings.

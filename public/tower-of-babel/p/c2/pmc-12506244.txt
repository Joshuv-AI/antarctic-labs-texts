Experience and perspectives of Sudanese pediatricians in delivering telemedicine services to children during armed conflict in Sudan
Authors: Karimeldin Mohamed Ali Salih
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTelemedicine is defined as the delivery of medical services remotely through communication technology. It is a critical strategy for enhancing healthcare. The public health infrastructure, healthcare manpower, and community development were severely affected in Sudan due to the armed conflict that broke out in April 2023, resulting in only 14% of households having access to comprehensive healthcare services. International humanitarian law requires physicians to uphold their ethical responsibilities in such crises. This study aimed to evaluate the experiences of Sudanese pediatricians with telemedicine in caring for pediatric cases during the armed conflicts in Sudan in 2023.MethodsA structured, well-tested, validated questionnaire was designed into a Google form and distributed to all in-service pediatricians and physicians caring for children in Sudan from August to December 2023. The questionnaire included demographic information, acceptance for enrolment, and ten questions regarding the experiences with telemedicine during the war in Sudan. Frequencies and percentages were used to summarize the results through descriptive statistics.ResultsA total of 114 pediatric physicians participated in this study. Most participants were male, with 85.0% of them having relocated outside of Sudan due to the war. Communication tools used with the patients included WhatsApp, direct calls, Facebook Messenger, and Telegram. Over half of the physicians (52.6%) served less than 10 patients each, with a total of 353 children benefiting from telemedicine consultations for various diagnoses. Almost all pediatricians expressed satisfaction with various parameters, including history-taking, physical examinations, investigations, medication prescriptions, patient education, and psychological support. Moreover, most of them encourage telemedicine for daily practice, with 50% favoring media communication. However, despite this support, many concerns still exist. The medicolegal issues, as well as the recurrent unnecessary calls, remain an obstacle. Infectious, respiratory, and diarrheal diseases were the most common diseases encountered. Telemedicine was viewed by some physicians as a cost-effective option and was recommended for use in conjunction with in-person visits. Possible recommendations included verifying caregivers’ personal information, ensuring well-structured consultations, and providing more training for telemedicine practice.ConclusionPediatricians exploit telemedicine to serve their patients during armed conflict. Means of communication included WhatsApp, direct calls, audio, text messages, and videos. Physicians reported satisfaction with the virtual history-taking process, investigation, prescription, and patient education; however, they were dissatisfied with the physical examination. Despite the positive perception of physicians towards telemedicine, they still remain concerned about their lack of awareness of regulatory guidelines. It is highly recommended that training in telecommunications be provided to overcome ethical issues and drawbacks.Keywords: Telemedicine, Infectious, Crises, War, Conflict

Introduction

The public health infrastructure, healthcare manpower, and community development were severely affected by the armed conflicts [1]. Morbidity and mortality from firearms and explosives, causing casualties among both military personnel and civilians, often exceed those in non-conflict areas [2]. Armed conflict causes displacement, famine, disease outbreaks, and high mortality, especially among vulnerable groups [3]. The sequences of armed conflict in Sudan that developed in April 2023 affect both urban and rural areas, with a displacement of around 31% of households, reducing full-time employment to 50%. More than 70% of school-aged children miss their classes, and access to comprehensive healthcare services is available to only 14% of households [4]. To address these shortages, a rapid and effective humanitarian fund, along with investments in healthcare, are urgently needed [4]. These miserable conditions motivate Sudanese physicians to fulfill their ethical obligations, which are supported by international humanitarian guidelines and regulations [5]. Precedency in the implementation of social media for medical and educational purposes demonstrated success, effectiveness, and gratification during the COVID-19 pandemic [6–8]. The advantages of telemedicine through video consultations include a decreased burden on medical staff, infrastructure, financial expenses, and time consumption compared to traditional medical care [6, 9, 10]. Despite its advantages during crises, its application needs precise consideration in different circumstances and contexts [11, 12]. Many kinds of research, various opinions, and multiple experiences will interact to provide strong evidence-based guidelines for telemedicine’s implementation [12–14]. Telemedicine is the remote delivery of medical services via communication technology, encompassing care, education, and assistance, as defined by the World Health Organization (WHO) [15, 16]. Although e-health is sometimes used in conjunction with telemedicine, it has a distinct meaning. The WHO considers telehealth to be a tool through which medical services and education can be provided to customers through technology [17–19]. To ensure the success of telemedicine applications, several key factors must be considered, including user competency, awareness of privacy issues, and a reliable electronic system [20, 21]. Although telemedicine is in its infancy in developing countries like Sudan, it is essential to consider it in any strategic plan. Limited infrastructure, conflicts, and other crises have led to over 30 million people losing their lives worldwide over the last 27 years, among them 20 million women and children, dying in wars [22], an affrighting figures which present a challenge to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) [23] a situation that might be reversed if telemedicine was applied. The Sudanese context offers various channels for patients to access their treating physicians, including obtaining the physicians’ contact numbers directly from them, through relatives, or by requesting assistance from local community leaders.

This study aimed to assess the experience of Sudanese pediatricians in using telemedicine to manage pediatric cases during war conflicts in Sudan.

Method

This cross-sectional study was conducted among pediatricians and junior physicians in Sudan from August to December 2023. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Institutional Review Board, with research approval (12 − 8/3) granted by the Research and Ethical Committee at Gaafar Ibnauf Pediatrics Tertiary Hospital, Khartoum, Sudan. Informed consent to participate was obtained from all of the participants in the study. A structured, pre-tested, and well-validated questionnaire was designed into a Google form and distributed to all in-service Sudanese pediatricians and physicians caring for children through a link sent to a dedicated WhatsApp group. The validation was through 10 pilot samples. The questionnaire was validated and demonstrated strong content relevance, reflecting key aspects of pediatricians’ perceptions of telemedicine. It showed good clarity, logical structure, and face validity. Questions were concise, easy to understand, and covered the essential domains, enhancing the quality and reliability of the collected responses. The questionnaire covers a vital part regarding participants’ acceptance of enrollment. General information about the enrolled physicians and 10 questions regarding their experience with telemedicine during the war in Sudan. The questions covered experiences with using smart devices in healthcare delivery, common diseases encountered by practitioners, patients’ questions about healthcare through telemedicine, ethical issues related to telemedicine, the confidentiality of patients’ information, awareness of telemedicine regulation guidelines, whether telemedicine offers advantages for pediatrics over other specialties, and any additional comments about telemedicine. Some participants responded to open-ended questions about their experiences, concerns, and the most common medical conditions they had faced. The communication between the pediatricians and the patients was a two-sided interaction between caregivers (occasionally the patients themselves) and the doctors.

Due to the lack of previously published data and studies on telemedicine among Sudanese physicians, particularly pediatricians, during military conflicts, a total coverage sampling approach was employed to address the logistical challenges associated with these conflicts. This method reflects representation and reduces selection bias in military conflict settings, where infrastructure, manpower, and distribution are severely affected. Since local and national information and data were unavailable, international information regarding sampling in general and data collection in particular provides evidence for the effectiveness of telemedicine in conflict zones such as Syria and Afghanistan. Such global experience provides a basis for Sudanese pediatricians to adopt and adapt solutions from existing experiences [24–26]. The questionnaire was developed after reviewing the literature, which reflects various aspects of telemedicine’s advantages, disadvantages, and potential applicability [6, 14, 19, 27]

Data were entered into an Excel sheet, where frequency and percentages, as determined through descriptive statistics, were used to reflect the results. The data were analyzed using SPSS version 25.0. Descriptive statistics in the form of frequency, mean, and standard deviation were used. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Shapiro-Wilk tests were implemented for the normalization of data distribution. Student’s t-test and One-Way ANOVA were used for comparison. The level of significance was considered as < 0.05 for the p-value.

Of the 114 physicians who received the questionnaire, 112 agreed to participate, resulting in a response rate of 98.2%. Of the 112, 95 (85.0%) physicians were relocated outside Sudan. Disadvantages of telemedicine as perceived by physicians

Aside from the open-ended questions, most of the questionnaire responses emphasized that infectious diseases, respiratory diseases, and gastroenteritis were the most frequently encountered. Valid recommendations included several essential points, such as verifying the caregiver’s information, preparing well-structured consultations, developing appropriate management plans, and providing additional training for telemedicine practice. Telemedicine is viewed by the participants as effective, useful, and less costly.

Discussion

To the author’s knowledge, this study is the first study to reflect physicians’ experiences and opinions regarding the use of telemedicine during crises in Sudan. The most frequently used means of communication were WhatsApp, direct calls, and audio, text, and video messages. Most respondents offered their services to fewer than 50 patients. During the study period, 353 children benefited from services. The diagnosed cases through teleconsultation are primarily respiratory tract infections, gastroenteritis, bronchial asthma, urinary tract infections, other gastrointestinal diseases, malaria, and chronic diseases.

Most of the respondents used smartphones for communication with their patients. They are satisfied with this and perceive it as essential for health practice. However, they were unaware of any guidelines regulating the practice of telemedicine. These findings are similar to those found in a study conducted in Saudi Arabia by El Kheir et al. (2022) [19]. The widespread availability of smartphones, along with their advantages in communication, documentation, cost-effectiveness, and versatility, makes telemedicine an attractive option for any healthcare provider. Few healthcare providers, as noted by El Kheir et al. (2022), experience some legal issues compared to the Sudanese experience. Legal conflicts are expected when using telemedicine, particularly when no guidelines are available, a finding that aligns with research from Saudi Arabia and Sudan, as highlighted by El Jack et al. (2023) [28]. Despite the lack of global telemedicine guidelines, the frameworks of the American Medical Association and the American Telemedicine Association offer valuable insights [29]. One of the major disadvantages of telemedicine reported in this study, as shared by physicians in Saudi Arabia [30], is the limitation of physical examinations. The study aligns with a recent study among Sudanese physicians by El Jack et al. l (2023) [28], which found that the most commonly used mode of communication was direct calls and WhatsApp messages. Both studies recommend preparing all stakeholders for the application of telemedicine.

In this study, infectious diseases account for (40.5%) of cases, together with the concern of subject matters towards the spread of infectious disease, which agrees with many studies by Suhrcke et al. during economic crises (2011) and Hussein et al. (2024) during the war [31, 32], Suhrcke et al. (2011) reported that, through their literature review, out of 37 articles, 30 documented the worsening of infectious diseases during economic crises [31]. Husein et al. estimated that infectious diseases accounted for 35% during the war. Crises usually lead to economic deterioration. Subsequently, preventive, curative, and administrative measures will be affected [31]. Serious infections like tuberculosis, human immunodeficiency virus, or cholera have been reported in many armed conflicts [33]. The respiratory and gastrointestinal symptoms might indicate a serious illness like tuberculosis, human immunodeficiency virus, or cholera and, if not diagnosed and managed early, might progress to death [34]. In fact, the biggest burden of war on human lives is the expected rise of new infectious diseases and the re-emergence of existing ones [35, 36]. War would disrupt healthcare, increase disease risks, and lead to parallel health crises, as seen in other prolonged conflicts [33]. Wars exacerbate infectious disease risks due to poor preventive measures, crowding, and infrastructure damage, causing outbreaks like measles, polio, and diarrheal diseases. Sudanese physicians highlight concerns about vaccine-preventable diseases, endemic illness, and unsafe food and water. Respiratory and diarrheal diseases have surged, resembling pre-antibiotic eras. The review demonstrated the effectiveness of telemedicine in managing various pediatric conditions, including diabetes, emergencies, oncology, and bronchial asthma [37].

Recommendations

To maximize the benefits of telemedicine, recommendations might include providing digital infrastructure, training healthcare professionals, developing guidelines for telemedicine, protecting data, integrating telemedicine into hospital and educational settings, and ensuring stable internet connections.

Conclusion

Telemedicine is a valuable tool for providing healthcare during war conflicts. Pediatricians in Sudan utilized telemedicine during the recent crisis through direct calls and communications via WhatsApp and other social media platforms. Physicians reported satisfaction with the virtual history-taking, the investigation, prescription of medications, and patient education. Physicians generally perceived telemedicine positively, but they were often unaware of the relevant regulations and guidelines. Training in telecommunication is needed to avoid ethical drawbacks.

Strength

This study represents a humble initial step towards implementing technology and advances in healthcare. It will draw the attention of policymakers in countries like Sudan, which have many obstacles to supplying reasonable facilities for the application of telemedicine.

Limitation

A key limitation of this work is the sample size. The continuity of the war led to the displacement of pediatricians to safe areas either locally or abroad, where the internet services were unreliable. Those who are lucky and have access to internet services responded to the questionnaire. The challenges are that: a good number of pediatricians, who were unable to fill the questioner due to the war sequences, might have a different response, therefore the generalizability of the finding might be at risk.

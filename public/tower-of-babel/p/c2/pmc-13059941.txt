Introduction

Effective postoperative pain management is a critical component of surgical care, directly influencing patient recovery, satisfaction and long-term clinical and functional outcomes.1 Despite recent advances in pain management, postoperative pain remains poorly managed worldwide,2 with worse outcomes often reported in low-middle-income countries (LMICs) than in high-income countries (HICs).3 The lack of optimal pain management after surgery contributes to increased morbidity, prolonged hospital stay and increased risk of developing chronic postoperative pain.4

The burden of chronic postoperative pain is high. Up to 85% of patients experience chronic postoperative pain, with up to 25% suffering from severe pain.5 6 The evidence suggests that ongoing postoperative pain is an artefact of inadequate pain management, with studies indicating that patients receiving a holistic and comprehensive analgesic regimen during hospitalisation are less likely to develop chronic pain compared with those receiving suboptimal acute postsurgical pain management.7 On the other hand, the availability of effective pain treatments and up-to-date clinical practice guidelines suggests that varying postoperative pain outcomes are influenced by several factors beyond the inherent efficacy of available treatments.8

The factors that influence postoperative pain management span across patient, healthcare provider and systemic levels, and they can be classified as barriers or enablers. The barriers include but are not limited to patient refusal of analgesics due to concerns of adverse side effects, inadequate pain assessment practices and limited access to relevant resources.9,11 Conversely, various enablers such as standardised pain management protocols and interdisciplinary collaboration have been shown to improve pain outcomes.12 Understanding these complex, interacting factors is essential to designing and implementing effective interventions aimed at improving current postoperative pain management practices.

Several individual studies have been conducted with the aim of understanding the enablers, barriers and recommendations for optimal postoperative pain management. In addition, scoping reviews have been conducted on some aspects of postoperative pain management such as patient experiences with opioid use13 and service delivery gaps following hospital discharge.14 However, to date, no review has been conducted to systematically synthesise the enablers and barriers which may impact on postoperative pain management within the hospital setting. This review aims to provide a comprehensive overview of the factors influencing postoperative pain management and recommendations that support optimal, evidence-based pain management after surgery.

Methods

This scoping review will be conducted in accordance with the Joanna Briggs Institute manual for evidence synthesis15 and reported according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping review guidelines.16 The review is expected to be conducted over a period of 12 months from June 2025 to June 2026. This protocol has been registered on Open Science Framework.

We will use a mixed methods design to analyse data from qualitative, quantitative and mixed methods studies. The factors influencing postoperative pain management will be mapped onto the Theoretical Domains Framework (TDF).17 The TDF is a comprehensive implementation science framework that synthesises 33 behaviour change theories to identify factors influencing implementation and decision-making in healthcare and other settings.18 It consists of 14 domains, each containing several constructs. The TDF is suitable for use in this scoping review because it has been validated to systematically assess barriers and enablers for behaviour change in healthcare settings.19 In addition, it is widely used to design interventions for improving clinical practice and outcomes,20 aligning with the review’s objective to identify recommendations for improving postoperative pain management.

Identification of research question

In adults who have undergone surgery, what are the enablers, barriers and recommendations for optimal postoperative pain management in a hospital setting?

Identification of relevant studies

We will consider primary studies that used qualitative, quantitative and mixed methods designs to determine enablers, barriers, and recommendations for optimal postoperative pain management in a hospital setting. In this review, optimal pain management is defined as safe treatment that leads to a clinically meaningful reduction in postoperative pain. Enablers are defined as factors that enhance postoperative pain management, while barriers are those that hinder it. These factors will be categorised into five groups: patient-related, clinician-related, treatment-related, systemic and other factors. Recommendations are defined as strategies aimed at enhancing the control and relief of pain after surgery.

We will exclude studies on postoperative pain management in paediatric patients due to variations in treatment guidelines for adults and children.21 In addition, we will exclude grey literature, including preprints and conference proceedings, due to a lack of editorial scrutiny associated with these publications.22 Owing to a lack of translation resources, only articles published in English will be included. Study selection

We have used a customised search strategy (online supplemental file 1) including relevant terms and phrases to identify eligible studies from the following databases: PubMed (MEDLINE) via EBSCOhost, Cumulative Index to Nursing & Allied Health Literature via EBSCOhost and PsycINFO via EBSCOhost. The identified studies will be exported to Covidence—a web-based tool for managing literature reviews (www.covidence.org), which will facilitate study de-duplication, screening and data extraction. Two reviewers (RF and KL) will independently screen study titles and abstracts using the eligibility criteria. Potentially eligible studies will be assessed for eligibility in full-text form by the same reviewers. After each stage, the reviewers will compare the results and address disagreements by discussion. A third reviewer (RP) will be consulted if consensus cannot be reached. Cohen’s Kappa will be used to evaluate inter-rater agreement during the screening process and will be interpreted as minimal (0–0.39), weak (0.40–0.59), moderate (0.60–0.79) or strong (0.80–0.90).23

Data extraction

Two reviewers (RF and KL) will independently extract the relevant data from eligible studies using a customised, pre-piloted data extraction tool. Variables of interest include author names, year of study publication, country of origin, study aim, study design and setting, participant characteristics (eg, age, profession, years of professional experience), sample size and outcomes. In a case where information is missing, study authors will be contacted and requested to provide the required details within 2 weeks. If the information is not provided within this timeframe, the corresponding variables will be left blank. The reviewers will compare the results and address disagreements by discussion. A third reviewer (RP.) will be consulted if consensus cannot be reached.

Analysing and reporting the results

Data analysis will be conducted on the enablers, barriers and recommendations for optimal postoperative pain management. Quantitative analysis will include content analysis to map the frequency of enablers and barriers, time series analysis to identify temporal trends in outcomes and a comparative analysis of outcome trends between HICs and LMICs. Qualitative data will be mapped across all the relevant domains of the TDF (table 3).18 To strengthen the recommendations that may be identified from the eligible studies, further interventions for improvement will be generated by mapping the identified barriers on the Capability, Opportunity, Motivation—Behavior (COM-B) model.24 The COM-B model serves as a higher-level model that synthesises and organises TDF domains into broader components needed for behaviour change. Where additional data requires coding, thematic analysis will be conducted using NVivo data management software.25

Stakeholder consultation

This review will synthesise peer-reviewed, published literature, and no stakeholder consultations will be conducted as part of the work. However, the findings will inform stakeholder consultations for a future project aimed at identifying enablers, barriers and recommendations for optimal postoperative pain management at Groote Schuur Hospital, Cape Town, South Africa.

Patient and public involvement

The development of this scoping review protocol did not involve input from patients or the public, and they will not be involved in its conduct.

Ethics and dissemination

This scoping literature review will analyse publicly available data. Therefore, ethical approval is not required. This review will be the first to comprehensively synthesise the factors influencing postoperative pain management and offer actionable recommendations. The findings will serve as a foundation for engaging relevant stakeholders in discussions aimed at implementing systemic changes to support healthcare providers in enhancing postoperative pain management. The results will be disseminated through a peer-reviewed journal publication, local fora and conference presentations to ensure broad visibility and impact.

Cardiac arrest in children: A Europe-wide epidemiological study on out-of-hospital cardiac arrest in children. A protocol for a subgroup analysis of the European registry of cardiac arrest THREE study
Authors: Inga K Kelpanides, Stephan Katzenschlager, Ingvild BM Tjelmeland, Jo Kramer-Johansen, Leo Bossaert, Holger Maurer, Siobhán Masterson, Rolf Lefering, Johan Herlitz, Fernando Rossell Ortiz, Gavin Perkins, Jan Wnent, Jan-Thorsten Gräsner, for the EuReCa-3-Paeds study group
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOut-of-hospital cardiac arrest affecting children may result in death or hypoxic brain injury, with potentially devastating consequences for children, families, and societies. Efforts to reduce this impact require sound knowledge of current epidemiology. This protocol outlines a study of the epidemiology of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest in persons aged <18 years across 28 European countries.Methods/designThe European Registry of Cardiac Arrest (EuReCa) THREE study, a prospective, multi-centre cohort study, collected data for cases of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest between September 1st and November 30th 2022. This paediatric substudy will analyse features of cardiac arrests in children. Subsequently, the paediatric group will be compared to a cohort of younger adults (18–65 years) to identify differences. Incidences of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest will be calculated using relevant population data and presented per 100,000 child- and person-years for participating countries and overall, alongside national groupings of precipitating causes underlying cardiac arrest. Logistic regression will be performed to identify modifiable factors impacting the outcomes survived event and survival to hospital discharge or 30 days. Independent variables will include features of cardiac arrest, including presumed cause, witnessed status, shockable rhythm, epidemiological, and resuscitation factors. Logistic regression analysis results will be displayed in tables, and statistical significance set at the 0.05 level.DiscussionThe results will be used to identify possible targets for preventing cardiac arrest, e.g. by raising awareness of important causes among the public and healthcare providers. Furthermore, the study may identify opportunities for optimising resuscitation chains in Europe to improve outcomes.Keywords: Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, Resuscitation, Paediatric cardiac arrest, Emergency medical service, Epidemiology, EuReCa

Introduction

Cardiac arrest occurring in childhood can profoundly affect the child, family and community. Most children do not survive, with survival rates after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) in children ranging widely from 5–40 %.1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 Furthermore, survivors may suffer hypoxic brain injury resulting in neurological and functional impairment. Both instances incur socio-economic costs in terms of lost productive life-years for children and caregivers.

Mitigating this profound impact of childhood OHCA requires a combination of measures. Broadly, case numbers can be reduced through prevention, and outcomes improved by optimizing resuscitation. Preventing OHCA requires knowledge of its causes, for instance cases due to drowning may be mitigated through public policy enhancing swimming safety.8 Improving resuscitation requires the application of evidence-based algorithms emphasising interventions linked to improved outcomes. Recently, research from Germany found that in children, cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) by bystanders combining both chest compressions and ventilation improved rates of return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) compared with chest compressions only.9

There are key differences between cardiac arrests in children and adults. Notably, the incidence of cardiac arrest in childhood is lower. Events are most often triggered by hypoxia as in respiratory failure, choking and drowning,9, 10 resulting in predominantly non-shockable rhythms.

Previous epidemiological studies of paediatric OHCA have been conducted in several countries. However, different age ranges in populations studied have led to difficulty in comparing results.11 Consistent inclusion of a uniform age group would be an important step to improve generalisability of results. Since most European countries have set the legal limit of childhood at 18 years, and this is mostly reflected in the organisation of healthcare services for children, this study has set an age-limit of 18 years.

Cardiac arrest registries, using a common set of variables defined by the Utstein criteria,12 have enabled large epidemiological studies synthesising data from multiple countries.13, 14, 15 Given the lower incidence of cardiac arrest in childhood, datasets from a single country or region tend to include small numbers.10 As such, international data synthesis is an essential next step in generating solid epidemiological data to underpin future strategies to reduce the impact of cardiac arrest in children.

We aim to establish the current incidence, precipitating causes, and features of OHCA in persons aged <18, using cardiac arrest registry data from 28 European countries,16 and to investigate associations between features of cardiac arrest and resuscitation and outcomes. This knowledge could underpin strategies for reducing case numbers and improving prehospital treatment.

Material and methods

This study protocol follows the SPIRIT recommendations.17 The EuReCa THREE dataset is based on the Utstein recommendations for out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

Study design

The study design of the EuReCa network has been extensively described elsewhere.16, 18 In brief, participating countries collect prospective OHCA data from their local, regional, or national cardiac arrest registry or directly from the Emergency Medical Services (EMS) during the study period. Data is collected in line with a predefined core dataset, which displays the minimum of data required to participate in the EuReCa study. Ethical approval for EuReCa THREE and consequent sub-studies was granted by the University of Kiel ethics committee with reference number D422/22 on 14.03.2022. The EuReCa study is registered with the German Registry of Clinical Trials, registration no DRKS00028591. This covers the inclusion and analysis of all patients, irrespective of their age, with OHCA.

The prospective trial period was from September 1st, 2022, until November 30th, 2022. This predefined subgroup analysis includes persons aged <18 in the paediatric cohort. The adult comparator group will consist of patients aged ≥18 to 65. The upper age limit was chosen due to the increase in the incidence of cardiovascular disease as a cause of death in people aged 65 years or over in Europe in 2022.19 Additionally, we saw an increase in the frequency of OHCAs within the study dataset in patients aged >65.

Upon completion, the reporting of the study results will follow the STROBE recommendations.20

This analysis will assess specific research questions based on the EuReCa THREE dataset (Table 1). Inclusion criteria in the EuReCa study are: Confirmed cardiac arrests. This inclusion is not affected by age, and only patients with cardiac arrest confirmed by EMS are included. Children with confirmed OHCA and ROSC before EMS arrival are eligible for inclusion. Based on the dataset definition of the parent trial, we cannot distinguish between newborns and infants. We will raise this within the limitations section of the study manuscript.

To increase comparability, age groups will be categorised according to the latest Utstein recommendation (<1, 1–5, 6–11, and 12–<18 years).12 Countries reporting fewer than 5 paediatric OHCA cases during the study period, or those with nationwide coverage of less than 10 %, will be excluded to improve generalisability of the findings. This will result in a total of 445 children included.

Statistical analysis

Depending on their distribution, demographic data will be presented as means with standard deviations (SD) or medians with interquartile ranges (IQR) for continuous parameters. Categorical variables will be presented as counts and percentages. In case of homogeneity, a student's t-test with 95 % CI will be used. If the data is skewed, the difference of medians with 95 % CI will be reported.

Epidemiology, features, and outcomes of paediatric OHCA in the EuReCa population

Population data is derived from official national population registries for 2022. If a country has only contributed regional data to the EuReCa THREE study, the population will be calculated from local data. If age-specific local population data are not accessible, the population proportion of children within the respective country will be assumed to be equal throughout the country. If three or fewer cases were reported, incidence calculation will be deferred. Incidences for the respective parameters will be presented as n per 100,000 child-years and n per 100,000 person-years. Additionally, rates will be reported for epidemiological data. Due to the three-month sample period, the occurrence of OHCA is extrapolated to one year. The aetiology of OHCA is given as per Utstein adult guidelines used in the original study.16 Survival outcomes will be reported according to the P-COSCA recommendations.21

Resuscitation times and interventions

A multivariable regression model with adjustment for known confounders will assess the association between the time intervals of essential variables and the clinically important endpoints, any ROSC, sustained ROSC at hospital admission, and survival to hospital discharge or 30 days. Time-sensitive analysis will be conducted to identify the proportion of patients dead or alive after specific resuscitation times. If exploratory data analysis suggests non-linearity between the time interval and survival, the continuous time variable may be categorised into clinically meaningful groups. For example, time intervals may be grouped into bins (e.g., 0–5 min, 6–10 min, etc.). Logistic regression will then be performed using the categorised time intervals as a predictor and the additional covariates listed above. The reference group will be the group with the shortest time interval, and the OR for other groups will be reported relative to this reference.

Analysis of bystander and EMS measures

As described previously, bystander CPR with and without ventilation is an essential cornerstone in the initial resuscitation phase of OHCA.9 Additionally, EMS treatment largely influences the subsequent outcome. To identify and analyse these factors, multivariable logistic regression with the endpoint status at hospital admission and survival at discharge or 30 days will be performed. This will be controlled for known confounding factors. The measures mentioned above will be compared to cases where this intervention was not performed.

Comparison with the adult population

To assess clinically important differences between children and adults, we plan to compare baseline characteristics, CPR-related factors, and critical endpoints between the OHCA populations using the EuReCa THREE dataset. Depending on the final sample size, we either compare all children against adults, or predefined age groups against adults.

Time to first defibrillation will be compared between children and adults by comparing the mean (SD) or median (IQR). Survival rates are compared using a logistic regression, as described above, with the population (paediatric or adult) as a covariate. Visual analysis of 95 % confidence intervals will be used to assess statistical differences.22

Statistical software and validation

All analyses will be performed using R or SPSS statistical software. Sensitivity analyses will be conducted to examine the robustness of the findings under different model specifications or time interval categorizations. These include the exclusion of countries with a high or low survival rate to account for outliers. Necessary figures will be created using GraphPad PRISM in the latest version.

Results will be presented as adjusted OR with 95 % CI. For models involving categorized time intervals, survival probabilities for each category will also be displayed graphically. ROC curves and their associated metrics (AUC, optimal cutoff) will be presented for a straightforward interpretation of the results. Statistical significance will be assessed at an alpha level of 0.05.

Publication plan

At the time of the submission, data collection and data cleaning have been done. Descriptive analysis and multivariable logistic regression analysis have been performed.

We plan to present the findings as two clinical research papers and to publish them in a MEDLINE/PubMed-indexed journal. The first will investigate epidemiology and resuscitation measures within the paediatric cohort, with the second comparing the paediatric group to the comparator group of young adults.

Authorship will follow the recommendations of the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors. The core writing group comprises IKK, SK, and the PI of the EuReCa THREE study. This group will prepare both manuscripts for review and approval by the study team. The study team comprises the principal investigator, the EuReCa steering committee, the study management team, and the statistician from the parent study. IKK and SK will each be the first author of one of the two papers. The study team will contribute to the manuscript and are listed as co-authors. National coordinators will be listed as collaborators in Medline. Local coordinators who contributed to the data collection will be acknowledged.

Discussion

This study protocol describes the aims and methodological approaches for a pre-planned sub-group analysis of the EuReCa THREE dataset. This is the first Europe-wide epidemiological analysis of OHCA in children, providing insight into paediatric OHCA with a focus on potential differences in treatment, presumed causes, and survival.

Data availability

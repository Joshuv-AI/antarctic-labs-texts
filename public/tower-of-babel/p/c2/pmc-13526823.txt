Three-minute walk test as a practical screening tool for detecting exertional hypoxemia in fibrotic interstitial lung disease: a real-world cohort study
Authors: Chen-Cheng Huang, Lan-Yan Yang, Yi-Hsuan Yu, Yi-Ching Chen, Pin-Kuei Fu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFibrotic interstitial lung disease (F-ILD) is associated with substantial morbidity and mortality, in which exertional hypoxemia represents a key marker of disease severity. Although the six-minute walk test (6MWT) is the reference standard for its assessment, routine use is often limited by practical constraints. This study aimed to evaluate whether the three-minute walk test (3MWT) could serve as a pragmatic screening tool for detecting exertional hypoxemia.In this real-world cohort study, 82 patients with multidisciplinary discussion–confirmed F-ILD were enrolled. All patients underwent both 3MWT and 6MWT, along with pulmonary function testing. Correlations between oxygenation indices and the predictive performance of 3MWT parameters for identifying desaturation during the 6MWT (nadir SpO₂ <90%) were analyzed. Moderate correlations were observed between 3MWT and 6MWT for resting SpO₂ (r = 0.65), nadir SpO₂ (r = 0.71), and ΔSpO₂ (r = 0.68) (all p < 0.001). Patients with exertional desaturation (nadir SpO₂ <90%) during the 6MWT demonstrated lower nadir SpO₂ and greater ΔSpO₂ during the 3MWT. Receiver operating characteristic analysis showed that ΔSpO₂ >4.5% and nadir SpO₂ <90.5% during the 3MWT provided optimal discrimination (AUC 0.83). The 3MWT provides clinically relevant oxygenation information that correlates with standard 6MWT parameters and may serve as a practical screening and triage tool to identify patients at risk of exertional hypoxemia. Introduction

Fibrotic interstitial lung disease (F-ILD) comprises a heterogeneous group of disorders characterized by progressive pulmonary fibrosis, impaired gas exchange, and substantial morbidity and mortality1–3. Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) and connective tissue disease–related ILD (CTD-ILD) represent the most prevalent subtypes and are associated with progressive functional decline and poor long-term outcomes despite advances in antifibrotic therapy4,5. Longitudinal cohort studies have demonstrated high mortality rates in both IPF and non-IPF fibrotic ILDs, underscoring the importance of early identification of disease severity and functional impairment6,7.

Exertional hypoxemia is a hallmark of disease progression in F-ILD and reflects impaired pulmonary gas exchange during physical activity. Oxygen desaturation during exercise is associated with increased dyspnea, reduced exercise tolerance, and higher mortality risk8–10. Accordingly, assessment of exertional oxygen desaturation has become an integral component of functional evaluation in ILD.

The six-minute walk test (6MWT) is widely regarded as the reference standard for evaluating functional capacity and exertional desaturation in ILD and other chronic cardiopulmonary diseases11–13. Performance on the 6MWT, including walking distance and oxygen desaturation, correlates with health-related quality of life, disease progression, and survival, and is frequently used as an endpoint in clinical trials14,15. Prospective ILD cohort studies have further reinforced the prognostic value of functional exercise testing in real-world clinical settings16,17. Despite its established role, routine implementation of the 6MWT is constrained by practical limitations. The test requires a standardized 30-meter corridor, trained personnel, and sufficient time, as outlined in American Thoracic Society (ATS) technical standards18. Guideline recommendations for ambulatory oxygen therapy also rely on detection of exercise-induced desaturation during formal walk testing, most commonly the 6MWT19. As a result, exertional hypoxemia may be under-recognized in routine outpatient practice.

While previous studies have shown that oxygen desaturation during exercise often occurs early and can be detected using shorter-duration walk tests20,21, the practical role of a stand-alone three-minute walk test (3MWT) in routine outpatient assessment of fibrotic ILD remains incompletely defined. Importantly, evaluation of shorter walk tests is not intended to replace the 6MWT, but rather to determine whether a simpler and more accessible test can function as a preliminary screening tool to identify patients at higher risk of clinically relevant exertional hypoxemia and prioritize those who may benefit from further evaluation using a formal 6MWT.

Therefore, the primary objective of this study was to evaluate whether the 3MWT could serve as a practical screening tool to identify patients with F-ILD at risk of exertional hypoxemia, defined as a nadir SpO₂ <90% during the 6MWT based on the 2015 British Thoracic Society (BTS) guidelines for ambulatory oxygen therapy19. Specifically, we aimed to (1) examine correlations between oxygenation indices obtained from the 3MWT and the standard 6MWT, (2) assess the ability of 3MWT oxygen parameters to predict exertional desaturation during the 6MWT, and (3) identify exploratory and clinically applicable reference thresholds to assist outpatient risk stratification. Importantly, this study was designed to evaluate the 3MWT as a screening and triage tool, rather than as a replacement for the 6MWT, particularly in outpatient and resource-limited settings.

Results

Baseline data and study population

A total of 111 patients were assessed for eligibility. After exclusion of 29 patients—including those with non-fibrotic ILD phenotypes after multidisciplinary discussion (MDD) (n = 15), missing 6MWT nadir SpO₂ data (n = 4), and interstitial lung abnormalities (ILA) (n = 10)—a final cohort of 82 patients with fibrotic ILD was included.

All included patients completed both the 6MWT and 3MWT. Among the included patients, 52 (63.4%) exhibited exertional desaturation (nadir SpO₂ <90%) during the 6MWT, while 30 (36.6%) maintained SpO₂ ≥90%. (Fig. 1)

The median age was 67 years (IQR 60–72), and 58.5% were male. The median body mass index (BMI) was 24.3 kg/m² (IQR 22.2–25.6), and 50% of patients had a history of smoking. Basal crackles, defined as typical inspiratory crackles over the lower lung zones, were present in 63.4% of patients, and digital clubbing was observed in 35.4%.

The median gender–age–physiology (GAP) score was 3 (IQR 2–4), and the median Charlson Comorbidity Index was 2 (IQR 1–3). Diagnostic subtypes included IPF (22%), CTD-ILD (56.1%), and other fibrotic ILDs (22%). The “other” category comprised idiopathic interstitial pneumonia (IIP) not otherwise specified, fibrotic hypersensitivity pneumonitis (HP), and pneumoconiosis-related fibrotic lung disease, all confirmed by MDD.

Pulmonary function testing showed a median forced vital capacity (FVC) of 81% predicted (IQR 66.3–97.8) and diffusing capacity of the lung for carbon monoxide (DLCO) of 69% predicted (IQR 55–86) (Table 1).

3MWT and 6MWT performance

During the 3MWT, the median resting peripheral oxygen saturation (SpO₂) was 96% (IQR 94–98), nadir SpO₂ was 90.5% (IQR 88–94), and ΔSpO₂ was 5% (IQR 3–8). During the 6MWT, the median walking distance was 465 m (IQR 409–526), with a resting SpO₂ of 95% (IQR 93–97), nadir SpO₂ of 87% (IQR 82–92), ΔSpO₂ of 7% (IQR 4–12), and end-exercise SpO₂ of 88% (IQR 83–94) (Table 1).

Correlation between oxygenation parameters in 3MWT and 6MWT

Resting SpO₂, nadir SpO₂, and ΔSpO₂ showed significant positive correlations between 3MWT and 6MWT (r = 0.65, 0.71, and 0.68, respectively; all p < 0.001) (Fig. 2).

Significant positive correlations were observed between oxygenation indices obtained from the 3MWT and 6MWT. Specifically, resting SpO₂ (r = 0.65), nadir SpO₂ (r = 0.71), and ΔSpO₂ (r = 0.68) all demonstrated moderate correlations (all p < 0.001) (Fig. 2). These findings indicate consistent physiological relationships between short-duration and standard walk test–derived oxygenation parameters.

Factors associated with exertional desaturation during 6MWT

Compared with patients who maintained SpO₂ ≥90% during the 6MWT, those with exertional desaturation (nadir SpO₂ <90%) exhibited more advanced disease characteristics. Specifically, patients with desaturation were more likely to have basal crackles (75.0% vs. 43.3%, p = 0.008) and digital clubbing (46.2% vs. 16.7%, p = 0.009), as well as higher GAP scores (median 3 vs. 2, p = 0.02) and higher modified Medical Research Council (mMRC) scores (median 1 vs. 0, p = 0.007). They also demonstrated significantly lower pulmonary function, including FVC (74.5% vs. 93%, p = 0.001) and DLCO (58% vs. 87.5%, p < 0.001). In addition, these patients showed lower nadir SpO₂ and greater ΔSpO₂ during both the 3MWT and 6MWT (all p < 0.001). In contrast, 6MWT walking distance did not differ significantly between groups (456 m vs. 478 m, p = 0.776) (Table 2).

Predictive performance of 3MWT for detecting exertional hypoxemia

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis demonstrated that both nadir SpO₂ and ΔSpO₂ derived from the 3MWT provided moderate discrimination for identifying patients with exertional desaturation during the 6MWT. Both parameters achieved an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.83 (95% CI 0.74–0.92). An internally derived ΔSpO₂ threshold of > 4.5% yielded a sensitivity of 85% and specificity of 73%, while a nadir SpO₂ threshold of < 90.5% yielded a sensitivity of 69% and specificity of 83%. Resting SpO₂ showed lower discriminatory performance (AUC 0.69, 95% CI 0.58–0.80) (Fig. 3; Table 3).

Correlations with lung function and disease severity between 3MWT oxygen indicators, lung function, and GAP score

Oxygenation parameters derived from the 3MWT were significantly associated with lung function and disease severity indices. Resting SpO₂ showed a positive correlation with DLCO (r = 0.347, p < 0.01) and a negative correlation with GAP score (r = − 0.371, p < 0.001). Nadir SpO₂ was positively correlated with FVC (r = 0.292, p < 0.01) and DLCO (r = 0.644, p < 0.001), and inversely correlated with GAP score (r = − 0.407, p < 0.001). ΔSpO₂ demonstrated negative correlations with FVC (r = − 0.308, p < 0.01) and DLCO (r = − 0.652, p < 0.001), and a positive correlation with GAP score (r = 0.328, p < 0.01) (Supplementary Table 1).

Discussion

This real-world study, based on prospectively collected registry data, demonstrates that oxygenation parameters derived from the 3MWT, including resting SpO₂, nadir SpO₂, and ΔSpO₂, are significantly and consistently correlated with those obtained from the 6MWT in patients with F-ILD.

Patients who exhibited exertional desaturation during the 6MWT (nadir SpO₂ <90%) showed more advanced disease characteristics, including greater symptom burden, impaired lung function, and higher GAP scores. Importantly, both ΔSpO₂ >4.5% and nadir SpO₂ <90.5% derived from the 3MWT demonstrated the best discriminatory performance for identifying clinically significant desaturation during the 6MWT.

The 6MWT remains the reference standard for assessing exercise capacity, prognosis, and treatment response in ILD. However, its routine implementation is often constrained by logistical requirements, including the need for a standardized corridor, time, and patient mobility11. These limitations may restrict its use in outpatient and community-based settings. In this context, the 3MWT offers several practical advantages. It requires less space, shorter duration, and lower physical demand, making it more feasible in routine clinical practice. Importantly, our findings support the role of the 3MWT as a screening and triage tool, rather than a replacement for the 6MWT. This distinction is critical, as the 3MWT may help identify patients at risk of exertional hypoxemia who would benefit from further evaluation with a standard 6MWT, particularly in settings where full testing is not readily available.

Exertional hypoxemia in fibrotic ILD reflects impaired pulmonary gas exchange due to ventilation–perfusion mismatch and diffusion limitation21, and is strongly associated with adverse clinical outcomes8,22–25. In our study, oxygenation indices derived from the 3MWT showed stronger correlations with DLCO and GAP score than with FVC, highlighting the limitations of spirometry alone in capturing functional impairment.

These findings are consistent with prior studies demonstrating that exercise-induced desaturation is more closely related to gas exchange abnormalities than to lung volume parameters26, and further support the physiological relevance of short-duration walk tests in evaluating disease severity.

A key contribution of this study is the identification of exploratory, screening-oriented reference thresholds for 3MWT parameters, with ΔSpO₂ >4.5% and nadir SpO₂ <90.5% showing optimal discrimination for clinically significant desaturation during the 6MWT. However, these thresholds were derived from an internal ROC analysis and should be interpreted cautiously. They are intended to be hypothesis-generating rather than definitive diagnostic criteria, and external validation in independent cohorts is required before clinical implementation.

This study has several important limitations. First, it was conducted at a single tertiary referral center with a modest sample size (n = 82), which may limit statistical power and generalizability. In addition, the distribution of ILD subtypes reflects the referral pattern and multidisciplinary practice structure of our center, with a relatively high proportion of CTD-ILD. Second, the CTD-ILD subgroup was clinically heterogeneous, including systemic sclerosis, rheumatoid arthritis, Sjögren’s syndrome, and idiopathic inflammatory myopathies. This heterogeneity may have obscured disease-specific differences in exercise physiology and limits subgroup interpretation. Third, walking distance and continuous oxygen saturation during exercise were not recorded in the 3MWT. Although prior studies have demonstrated strong correlations between shorter and longer walk distances, the absence of distance data limits comprehensive functional interpretation. Fourth, the 6MWT and 3MWT were performed at separate visits with an interval of approximately 1–3 months. Although patients were clinically stable without treatment changes, this temporal gap may introduce subtle variability, including potential learning effects. Finally, the study population predominantly consisted of patients with mild-to-moderate disease severity. Therefore, the findings may not be fully generalizable to patients with advanced fibrotic ILD who exhibit more severe exertional hypoxemia. However, the ability of the 3MWT to detect desaturation in a relatively milder cohort may also support its sensitivity as a screening tool.

Future multicenter studies with larger and more diverse cohorts are needed to validate these findings, assess their prognostic implications, and refine disease-specific thresholds. Given its simplicity and feasibility, the 3MWT may play an important role in longitudinal monitoring and early detection of disease progression in real-world clinical practice, particularly in outpatient and resource-limited settings.

In conclusion, oxygenation parameters from the 3MWT are significantly correlated with those from the 6MWT in fibrotic ILD. The 3MWT effectively identifies exertional hypoxemia, with ΔSpO₂ >4.5% and nadir SpO₂ <90.5% providing optimal discrimination. While not a replacement for the 6MWT, it serves as a pragmatic, complementary screening and triage tool in routine clinical practice.

Materials and methods

Study design, patient enrollment, and ethics

The Registry of Interstitial Lung Disease (REGILD) is a prospective, single-center registry established on December 28, 2018, enrolling patients with newly diagnosed fibrotic interstitial lung disease, including idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, connective tissue disease–associated ILD, and other fibrotic ILDs. All diagnoses were confirmed through multidisciplinary discussion involving pulmonologists, rheumatologists, radiologists, and pathologists. The REGILD cohort has been previously described and used to investigate prognostic factors in fibrotic ILD16,17,27–30.

For the present analysis, we included a subset of patients enrolled between January 1 and December 31, 2024, at a tertiary ILD referral center in central Taiwan. A total of 111 adult patients with MDD-confirmed F-ILD were assessed for eligibility. Patients were excluded if: (1) their HRCT findings and clinical features were considered inconsistent with a fibrotic ILD phenotype after MDD (including patients with interstitial lung abnormalities); (2) nadir SpO₂ data during the 6MWT were missing; or (3) they were unable to complete standardized walk testing. Specifically, 15 patients were excluded due to non-fibrotic ILD phenotype after MDD, 10 patients with ILA were excluded in accordance with current ATS definitions, and 4 patients were excluded due to missing nadir SpO₂ data during the 6MWT despite completion of both walk tests. A total of 82 patients met the inclusion criteria and were included in the final analysis. Patients were stratified according to whether their SpO₂ decreased below 90% during the 6MWT, based on the 2015 BTS guideline for ambulatory oxygen therapy19(Fig. 1).

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Taichung Veterans General Hospital (IRB No. CE18325B). The registry is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06476470), and written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

HRCT assessment and radiologic classification

High-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) scans were reviewed as part of routine clinical evaluation and multidisciplinary discussion. Imaging findings were assessed by experienced thoracic radiologists and pulmonologists, and final diagnoses were established through MDD consensus integrating clinical, radiologic, and, when available, histopathologic information.

Study inclusion was based on an MDD-confirmed diagnosis of fibrotic ILD rather than HRCT pattern alone. Radiologic patterns were categorized descriptively as usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP), nonspecific interstitial pneumonia (NSIP), or other fibrotic patterns. Patients with ILA were excluded from the final analysis to ensure diagnostic consistency with current ATS definitions of fibrotic ILD.

Clinical assessment protocol

Baseline data collected at enrollment included age, sex, BMI, smoking history, physical examination findings, and comorbidities. Pulmonary function tests and the 6MWT were routinely performed every six months, while HRCT and cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET) were performed at baseline and annually. Disease severity was assessed using the GAP index and the Charlson Comorbidity Index, as described in previous studies16,17,27–30.

PFT, 6MWT and 3MWT procedure

Pulmonary function tests included FVC and DLCO, performed according to ATS guidelines23.

The 6MWT and 3MWT were performed on separate outpatient visits, with an interval of approximately 1–3 months, reflecting routine clinical follow-up. The 6MWT was generally performed first. All walk tests were conducted on room air without supplemental oxygen. Patients requiring oxygen therapy or unable to complete standardized walk testing were excluded to ensure consistency of physiological measurements.

The 6MWT was conducted according to ATS guidelines using a 30-meter corridor18. Measurements included resting SpO₂, nadir SpO₂, ΔSpO₂ (resting minus nadir), exercise SpO₂, and walking distance.

The 3MWT was performed using a 15-meter corridor. Patients were instructed to perform continuous shuttle walking at a self-paced speed for three minutes in line with standard principles used in field walking tests. Standardized, non-directive encouragement was provided at fixed intervals to ensure consistency. Measurements included resting SpO₂, nadir SpO₂, and ΔSpO₂.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as median (interquartile range, IQR), and categorical variables as counts and percentages. Group comparisons were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables and the chi-squared or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. Spearman correlation coefficients were used to assess associations between oxygenation parameters derived from the 3MWT and 6MWT. ROC curve analysis was used to evaluate the performance of 3MWT parameters (resting SpO₂, nadir SpO₂, and ΔSpO₂) in identifying exertional desaturation during the 6MWT (nadir SpO₂ <90%). Optimal cutoff values were determined using Youden’s index. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS version 21.0 and MedCalc version 22.0. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

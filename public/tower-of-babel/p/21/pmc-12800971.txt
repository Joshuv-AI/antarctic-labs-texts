Tips for Successful Posterolateral Corner Reconstruction Using a Single Looped Construct
Authors: Alexander P. Decilveo, Neil Patel, Allison Ariniello, Nils Calderón Tejerina, Joseph N. Liu, Bruce A. Levy, George F. Rick Hatch
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Several methods for posterolateral corner reconstruction (PLC-R) exist, both anatomic and nonanatomic. We present our PLC-R using a modified LaPrade technique. In our technique, a single continuous graft is used for all fixation points with the addition of an anterolateral ligament reconstruction and suture tape for anterolateral capsule reinforcement. In this technical note, tips for successful PLC-R are described in detail using this single continuous looped construct.

Posterolateral corner (PLC) injuries managed nonoperatively can be debilitating with poor functional outcomes and high rates of osteoarthritis.1 Operative management consists of either anatomic or nonanatomic PLC reconstruction with a wide variety of techniques having been described.2, 3, 4 Anatomic PLC reconstruction has been shown to be more effective in restoring native knee kinematics and stability but may be more technically demanding when compared to nonanatomic reconstruction techniques.2,3 More recently, evolving literature has highlighted the importance of restoring rotatory instability in the setting of ligamentous knee injuries with anterolateral ligament reconstruction.5 Our technical note provides tips for performing successful PLC-R using a single continuous graft for all fixation points with the addition of an anterolateral ligament reconstruction and suture tape for anterolateral capsule reinforcement.

Surgical Technique

Patient Positioning and Exam Under Anesthesia

The patient is placed supine on the operative table with a footrest and lateral hip post to allow for resting 90° of knee flexion (Fig 1). A high, thigh tourniquet is placed, and a full-size fluoroscopic unit enters from the contralateral side. An exam under anesthesia is performed under fluoroscopic guidance to assess for concomitant ligamentous injuries. A positive dial test at 30°, but negative at 90° is indicative of an isolated PLC injury.Fig. 1Patient setup. Left knee, cranial to the right, caudal to the left. Graft Preparation

A 270 mm × 6-7 mm, fresh-frozen, nonirradiated peroneus longus or tibialis anterior allograft is prepped. A FiberTag TightRope II (Arthrex, Naples, FL) is placed on one end of the graft and FiberLoop (Arthrex) on the other end of the graft (Fig 2). Remove the suture tape from the TightRope II button. Mark 15 mm from the TightRope end of the graft, as this portion will be recessed in the popliteal tunnel.Fig. 2Graft preparation. A 270 mm × 6-7 mm allograft is prepped. A FiberTag Tightrope II (yellow arrow) is placed on the popliteal end of the graft and FiberLoop (red arrow) on the other end of the graft.

Graft preparation. A 270 mm × 6-7 mm allograft is prepped. A FiberTag Tightrope II (yellow arrow) is placed on the popliteal end of the graft and FiberLoop (red arrow) on the other end of the graft.

Common Peroneal Nerve Neurolysis, Proximal Fibula Neck Exposure, Lateral Collateral Ligament Identification

A 15-cm incision starting just proximal to the lateral epicondyle down to halfway between the fibular head and Gerdy’s tubercle is made with 10 blade (Fig 3). The long head of the biceps femoris fascia is dissected, and a common peroneal nerve neurolysis is performed. Using a key elevator, expose the proximal fibula neck anteromedially to posteriorly. Palpate the lateral collateral ligament (LCL) on the fibular head. Split the biceps femoris bursa between the long and short head in line with their fibers. Using a right-angle clamp, hook the LCL and tag with a braided suture. Pull traction on the LCL and note its origin on the femur. Use a marking pen to trace the LCL from the lateral proximal fibula to its origin on the femur where the iliotibial band (ITB) will be split for later femoral tunnel drilling (Fig 4).Fig. 3Posterolateral corner incision. Drill the LCL Fibula Tunnel and Popliteofibular Ligament Tibial Tunnel

With the knee in 80° of flexion, place the LCL fibula guide set to 0° at the LCL insertion along the lateral aspect of the fibula. Angle the guide superiorly 30° and posteromedially 45° (Fig 5). Drill a 2.4-mm guide pin bicortically. Remove the LCL guide and place an S-shaped retractor posteriorly. Ream an appropriate diameter bicortical proximal fibula tunnel. Use a rasp in the tunnel to facilitate later graft passage. A FiberStick (Arthrex) is placed in the tunnel followed by FiberLink (Arthrex) shuttle sutures.Fig. 5Proximal fibula tunnel drilling. Drill the LCL Femoral Tunnel and Popliteus Femoral Tunnel

Palpate the lateral epicondyle of the femur and the popliteus sulcus, which lies anterior and distal to the lateral epicondyle. The popliteal sulcus will abut the cartilage of the lateral femoral condyle. Split the ITB in line with its fibers using a 15 blade to gain access to the knee joint. Drill a spade tip guide pin bicortically at the femoral origin of the LCL aiming 30° anterior and 30° proximal.

Using a parallel pin guide, at the popliteal sulcus, drill a 2.4-mm spade tip guide pin bicortically aiming 30° anterior and 30° proximal across the femur (Fig 8). Use an acorn reamer to ream each tunnel ∼15-30 mm at an appropriate diameter. Pass shuttle sutures through each tunnel for later graft passage.Fig. 8Femoral lateral collateral ligament (LCL) and popliteus tunnel drilling. Left knee, cranial to the right, caudal to the left. Using a parallel pin guide, at the popliteal sulcus, a 2.4-mm spade tip guide pin is drilled bicortically aiming 30° anterior and 30° proximal across the femur (green arrow).

Recess and Dock the Graft into the Popliteus Tunnel and Fixation of the Popliteus Limb

Pass the end of the graft with the TightRope II attached through the popliteal tunnel and flip the button under fluoroscopic guidance. Recess the graft 15 mm in the popliteal tunnel. The popliteal limb sets the initial fixation of the construct. Shuttle the graft distally and deep into the IT band within the popliteal hiatus.

Using a shuttle suture, pass a free TightRope II from posterior to anterior through the PFL tunnel and flip the button under direct visualization at the anterior tibia. This will leave a free loop on the posterior aspect of the tibia. Pass the graft through the loop at the posterior tibia. With the knee in 70° of flexion and neutral rotation, tension the TightRope to reduce the graft to the posterior tibia via onlay fashion. This secures the popliteal limb of the graft.

Securing the PFL Limb

Pass the free end of the graft through fibular tunnel from posterior to anterior using a FiberLink. With the knee in 70° of flexion and neutral rotation, a 5.5-mm biocomposite interference screw is placed in the fibular tunnel from anterior to posterior, while the assistant pulls tension on the free end of the graft. This secures the PFL limb of the graft.

Fixation in the Femoral LCL Tunnel

The free end of the graft is shuttled from distal to proximal, deep to the ITB and superficial to the popliteus limb of the graft. Pass a free TightRope II with InternalBrace (Arthrex) through the femoral LCL tunnel, with the looped end of the tightrope coming out the aperture of the lateral aspect of the femoral tunnel. Flip the button under fluoroscopic guidance with the tails of the suture tape exiting out the lateral aspect of the tunnel. Pass the free end of the graft through the loop of the TightRope. With the knee in 20° of flexion, neutral rotation, and while applying a valgus force, recess 15 mm of the LCL limb of the graft by tensioning the TightRope. This secures the LCL limb of the graft.

Drill the Anterolateral Ligament Tunnel and Final Fixation of the Graft in the ALL Tunnel

Identify the midway point on lateral aspect of the tibia ∼1 cm below the joint line as this point will serve as the insertion for the anterolateral ligament (ALL) portion of the graft. Take a lateral radiograph of the knee and under fluoroscopic guidance, drill a 2.4-mm spade-tip guide pin bicortically followed by a 3.5-mm FlipCutter bicortically (Fig 9, A and B). Shuttling sutures are passed followed by a free TightRope II implant. The button of the TightRope is flipped on the medial tibia under fluoroscopic guidance, and the looped end is exiting out the lateral tibia. Shuttle the free end of graft deep to the ITB and distally toward the ALL tunnel. With the knee starting in 70° of flexion and finishing in near full extension, neutral rotation, and with a valgus force, tension the free end of the graft in the loop of the TightRope via onlay fashion. Final tensioning of the ALL occurs with the knee in near full extension and neutral rotation.Fig. 9Anterolateral ligament (ALL) tunnel placement. Left knee, cranial to the right, caudal to the left. Anterolateral Capsule Reinforcement with Suture Tape

Pass the tails of the InternalBrace from the femoral LCL tunnel superficial or deep to the ITB depending on the extent of anterolateral capsule injury. If the anterolateral capsule is nearly intact with minor disruption, our preference is to pass the tails of the suture tape deep to the ITB. With the knee in near full extension, neutral rotation, and with a valgus force, tension the tails of the suture tape into a 5.5-mm SwiveLock anchor just distal and anterior to the ALL tunnel (Fig 10). Discussion

Failure to adequately reconstruct the PLC can result in significant varus laxity of the knee, leading to further ligamentous or meniscal injury. Nonoperative management of PLC injuries is known to lead to early osteoarthritis, persistent knee instability, and poor patient-reported outcomes.1 Anatomic and nonanatomic methods for posterolateral corner reconstruction (PLC-R) of the knee have been described.2, 3, 4 In the last decade, the importance of the anterolateral ligament to resist knee internal rotation and restore rotatory stability has been emphasized.5 Additionally, the anterolateral capsule of the knee plays a key role as a secondary restraint to varus stress. We present our tips for successful PLC-R using a single continuous looped construct (Video 1) in a modified LaPrade fashion, including an ALL reconstruction and anterolateral capsule reinforcement with suture tape.

Our technique uses a single continuous looped construct limiting the need for additional grafts for PLC-R (Table 1). The addition of an ALL limb in the setting of PLC-R has yet to be described in the literature. Although bony landmarks assist in identifying ligamentous origins/insertions, the ALL insertion on the tibia is best approximated using fluoroscopy.6,7 As described in our technique on the lateral radiograph, the ALL tunnel can be drilled ∼1 cm distal to the lateral tibial plateau and at the halfway point between the anterior and posterior proximal tibia.6,8Table 1Pearls and Pitfalls of Posterolateral Corner ReconstructionPearlsPitfallsIf an isolated PLC injury, a full-size fluoroscopic unit should enter from the contralateral sideIf the fluoroscopic monitor enters from the ipsilateral injury side, drilling may be impeded by the fluoroscopic unit requiring manipulation of the leg.A 270-mm fresh, frozen nonirradiated tibialis anterior or peroneus allograft is prepped.An allograft shorter than 270 mm may lead to insufficient length for the entire looped construct, necessitating an additional allograft to complete the reconstruction.Common peroneal nerve neurolysis is performed prior to any tunnel drilling.Failure to mobilize the common peroneal nerve can lead to neurovascular injury.Identify and tag the LCL if present. Anterolateral capsule reinforcement using suture tape adds an additional point for stability of the posterolateral corner compared to previously described methods.2,4,9, 10, 11 Suture tape augmentation of the anterolateral capsule may reduce overall varus stress experienced by the graft limiting subsequent graft elongation and failure.

This technique is not devoid of disadvantages (Table 2). A single continuous looped construct requires a minimum allograft length of 270 mm for PLC-R. A peroneus longus or tibialis anterior allograft of this length in a fresh, frozen, nonirradiated preparation is not always readily available. Moreover, compared to the Arciero PLC-R technique, our technique requires a PFL, ALL, and anterolateral capsule suture tape tunnel. Three additional tunnels may increase the risk of neurovascular injury, add operative time, and result in tunnel convergence in the setting of multiligamentous knee injuries. Lastly, our technique uses suspensory button fixation for 4 out of 6 tunnels. Posterolateral reconstruction using a single continuous looped construct with the additional of an ALL limb and anterolateral capsule reinforcement with suture tape is a reproducible technique. The addition of the ALL limb resists knee internal rotation and reduces rotatory instability. Anterolateral capsule reinforcement with suture tape may limit varus stress experienced by the allograft, reducing subsequent graft elongation and failure.

Design of the RESOLVE study: a prospective, multicentre, observational, cohort study evaluating the safety and efficacy of endovascular treatment for acute or subacute thromboembolic occlusions of the lower extremity in China
Authors: Jianyun Long, Linjun Wang, Changpin Huang, Xupin Xie, Yongchang Liu, Yukai Chen, Hanyi Liu, Qihong Ni, Shuofei Yang, Ziheng Wu, Lianrui Guo, Zibo Feng, Qiang Li, Chunshui He, Hongfei Sang, Zhenyu Shi, Meng Ye, Xin Fang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAcute and subacute thrombotic occlusions of the lower extremities are common subtypes of peripheral artery disease frequently encountered in clinical practice. These occlusions typically involve the obstruction of a native artery or bypass graft, primarily caused by thrombosis or embolism. Endovascular therapy has increasingly become the preferred first-line treatment for patients with acute or subacute thromboembolic occlusions in the lower extremities. Thrombectomy, the removal of the thrombus, represents a critical initial step in endovascular management. A range of thrombectomy devices is currently available to treat such occlusions. However, the efficacy and safety of endovascular treatments remain uncertain, with real-world evidence on thrombectomy devices showing inconsistency across studies and clinical settings. This study aims to rigorously assess the safety and efficacy outcomes of endovascular treatment strategies for acute or subacute thromboembolic occlusions in the lower extremities.Methods and analysisThis prospective, cohort, multicentre, real-world observational study will enrol 400 patients diagnosed with acute or subacute thromboembolic occlusions of the lower extremity, treated with endovascular therapy across nine centres from 1 January 2021 to 31 December 2025. Participants will be followed for 12 months, with relevant demographic and clinical data collected. Follow-up assessments will occur at 1, 3, 6 and 12 months postintervention. The primary effectiveness endpoint is amputation-free survival. Secondary outcomes include adverse events at 1 month postintervention, technical success rate, clinical-driven target lesion reintervention rate, primary patency of the lesions and health economics considerations.Ethics and disseminationThe RESOLVE clinical study is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (http://clinicaltrials.gov/). Ethical approval was granted by the Institutional Review Board and the Human Research Ethics Committee of Hangzhou First People’s Hospital (approval number: 2021No.15-1). Informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to enrolment. The study findings will be disseminated through publication in a reputable, peer-reviewed journal, contributing valuable insights to guide future clinical practice in this field.Trial registration numberNCT04861506.Keywords: Lower Extremity, Thromboembolism, China, Clinical Protocols

Introduction

Lower extremity arterial thrombotic disease is characterised by occlusion of either the native artery or a bypass graft, typically caused by thrombosis or embolism. This condition often develops as a secondary complication of atherosclerotic stenosis, intimal hyperplasia at the site of occlusion, in-stent restenosis (ISR) or bypass grafts.1 2 Based on the duration of thrombus formation and disease progression, the condition is classified into three categories: acute thrombosis (<14 days), subacute thrombosis (>14 days and <3 months) and chronic thrombosis (>3 months).3 Patients with limb ischaemia in the acute or subacute phases face significantly higher risks of limb loss, cardiovascular events and mortality.1 2 Current treatment options for lower extremity arterial thrombosis include surgical thrombectomy, endovascular interventions and hybrid surgical procedures.1 2

Three prospective studies have shown that endovascular treatments, particularly catheter-directed thrombolysis (CDT), significantly improve limb salvage rates and reduce hospital stays without increasing amputation or mortality rates.4,6 However, the Rochester and TOPAS trials primarily focused on patients with acute lower extremity arterial thrombosis, while the STILE trial included patients with subacute arterial thrombosis.4,6 With advancements in endovascular techniques and devices, endovascular approaches are increasingly used for thrombus reduction and management of acute or subacute thromboembolic occlusions in the lower extremities. Thrombectomy therapies include mechanical thrombectomy devices (MTDs), such as the Rotarex S mechanical thrombectomy system,7 pharmacomechanical thrombolysis (PMT) devices like the AngioJet Rheolytic Thrombectomy system,8,10 and percutaneous aspiration thrombectomy (PAT).11,14 Most studies have focused on acute thrombosis treatments.15 However, critical surgical efficacy aspects, such as thrombus clearance rates, distal embolisation incidence, bleeding complications, limb salvage rates, disability rates and mortality, remain underexplored.

Although several clinical reports have evaluated the short-term and medium-term outcomes of various endovascular treatments for acute or subacute thromboembolic occlusions of the lower extremities, these findings often fail to fully reflect real-world clinical practice. To address this gap, the RESOLVE study was launched as a prospective observational study aimed at validating the safety and efficacy of endovascular treatments for acute or subacute thromboembolic occlusions in the lower extremities. Furthermore, the study seeks to evaluate the economic burden associated with different endovascular treatment strategies across diverse regions of China.

Methods

Study design

RESOLVE is a prospective, multicentre, real-world, observational clinical study designed to evaluate the safety, efficacy and economic impact of various endovascular therapies for acute or subacute thromboembolic occlusions in the lower extremities. The study aims to recruit 400 participants who have undergone endovascular therapy for acute or subacute thromboembolic occlusions of the lower extremities at nine centres across China between January 2021 and December 2025. There are no restrictions on the choice of endovascular treatment modalities, with selection based on the availability of thrombectomy devices at each centre. Treatment approaches may include PMT using systems such as AngioJet and TideFlow, PAT with the Indigo and Acostream systems, MAT with the Rotarex S mechanical thrombectomy system, CDT with multi-hole perfusion and ultrasound-accelerated thrombolysis catheters, and manual catheter thrombectomy.

All participants will undergo endovascular therapy via contralateral femoral artery access, ipsilateral antegrade femoral artery access or brachial artery access. In cases where antegrade access fails, retrograde puncture at a distal artery of the lesion may be considered. Surgeons will select the appropriate treatment modality, including PMT, CDT, MAT or PAT, based on the lesion characteristics and available institutional resources. Key study objectives include amputation-free survival (AFS), adverse events, quality of life (QoL) improvements and health economics evaluation. The study was initiated and designed by the Department of Vascular Surgery at Hangzhou First People’s Hospital, with no patient or public involvement required.

Participants

To minimise bias in participant selection, investigators at participating centres are encouraged to recruit all eligible patients with acute or subacute thromboembolic occlusions of the lower extremities who require endovascular treatment. Participant enrolment will occur between 1 January 2021 and 31 December 2025, at nine designated centres across China. A total of 400 participants will be recruited. Recruitment will be non-competitive and will not disrupt clinical practice.

Early discontinuation of the study requires written consent from the principal investigator and the sponsor. All study data will be retained for future reference. Once a participant withdraws from the study, their endpoint data will not be included in subsequent analyses.

Endovascular procedure

Vascular access will be performed by the investigator under local anaesthesia or sedation, depending on the patient’s overall condition. Access will be obtained via the contralateral femoral artery, ipsilateral antegrade femoral artery or brachial artery. If the antegrade approach is difficult, a retrograde puncture at the distal artery of the lesion will be performed.

Once angiography and/or DSA confirm the presence of thrombus associated with lower extremity occlusions or ISR, the appropriate endovascular treatment will be selected. Treatment modalities may include PMT, PAT, MAT and CDT. Following thrombus removal, the investigator will determine additional treatments based on lesion characteristics and institutional resources. Options may include: (a) Plain old balloon angioplasty, (b) Drug-coated balloon angioplasty, (c) Drug-coated balloon angioplasty followed by provisional stenting, (d) Bare-metal stent implantation, (e) Stent-graft implantation and (f) Drug-eluting stent implantation. Preoperative, intraoperative and postoperative angiographic images will be captured and analysed to assess the efficacy of the endovascular treatment.

Recruitment

Written informed consent will be obtained from all patients prior to enrolment. All patients will undergo routine preoperative evaluations, including blood tests, coagulation function tests, liver and kidney function tests, screening for syphilis and AIDS, electrocardiography, brain MRI or CT and cardiac ultrasound. Additionally, a CTA of the lower extremity vessels will be performed preoperatively to assess the location, length and nature of the lesions, as well as to determine the treatment method and identify potential perioperative complications.

Data collection

A standard case report form (CRF) will be developed prior to study initiation. Data will be entered into a centralised database, the multicentre COACH-PVD registry, hosted on the Vascbase platform, which complies with data privacy regulations. The scientific coordinator will oversee data management, with approval from the principal investigator at each site. The Vascbase platform will capture a comprehensive set of data, including baseline patient demographics, medical history, risk factors, clinical manifestations, lesion location, treatment methods, Trans-Atlantic Inter-Society Consensus II classification, details of the endovascular procedure, treatment outcomes, complications and follow-up information.

Patient demographics and postoperative follow-up data will be entered by two investigators, while surgical procedure details will be recorded by the primary surgeon and/or the first assistant. To ensure data quality, several measures will be implemented: (a) Automated completeness and plausibility checks, (b) Centralised data monitoring to identify missing or invalid data via queries, (c) Adherence to a comprehensive database protocol, (d) Regular training sessions and FAQs to address potential sources of error and (e) Compliance with established performance management regulations and scoring systems.

Surveillance and follow-up

The CRF and follow-up checklists will be designed in accordance with the RESOLVE study protocol. Follow-up will be conducted individually at each centre using various methods, including telephone calls, outpatient visits (the primary method), and inpatient follow-ups. Follow-up assessments will evaluate symptoms, walking distance, wound healing, medication adherence, smoking status, QoL, complications and hospital readmissions. Outpatient follow-ups will include ankle-brachial index measurement, Rutherford classification, vascular ultrasound and monitoring for thrombosis recurrence. Data will be recorded on a dedicated CRF form, verified and uploaded to the COACH-PVD Vascbase platform.

Investigators at all participating sites will have access to the platform for data entry linked to their respective centres, but will not be able to modify data from other sites. Data extraction and management for the RESOLVE study will be exclusively handled by the sponsoring centre, ensuring strict confidentiality of participant information. Participants will be followed up at 1 month, 3 months, 6 months and 1 year after surgery.

Adverse events

Adverse events20 will be classified as minor or major, and their frequency will be recorded during the treatment and follow-up periods. Complications will be categorised according to the reporting standards of the Society of Interventional Radiology.16 20 Minor adverse events include arterial puncture complications, distal embolism without clinical manifestations, postoperative thrombosis without critical limb ischaemia and reversible contrast-induced nephropathy. Major adverse events include major amputation related to endovascular treatment, cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events, arterial puncture complications requiring intervention, progressive decrease in haemoglobin, acute renal failure and death. On identification of adverse events, the corresponding centres will actively manage and report them. Details of the adverse events and their treatment processes will be documented and reported to the relevant ethics committee. In the event of a fatality, the corresponding centres and the participating investigator will be required to immediately provide all relevant information to the ethics committee.

Sample size and statistical analysis

The RESOLVE study is a real-world study, not designed to test a specific hypothesis. However, a minimally required sample size has been calculated for reference. Considering a 10% loss to follow-up rate and a target enrolment of 400 patients, the effectiveness analysis will include 360 patients. This study will be conducted simultaneously across multiple study sites, with efforts made to ensure balanced enrolment across sites for adequate representation. Site selection will be based on real-world conditions, considering practical feasibility and the enrolment schedule. The number of enrolled patients at each site will be adjusted to maintain balance, ensuring no site exceeds 50% of the total sample size.

Continuous variables will be expressed as mean±SD and compared using analysis of variance or paired-sample t-test, as appropriate. Categorical variables will be reported as frequencies and percentages, with comparisons made using the Kruskal-Wallis test or the Wilcoxon signed-rank test for paired data. General linear regression and logistic regression will be used to estimate outcomes as appropriate. Survival probabilities will be estimated using the Kaplan-Meier estimator, with survival curves compared using the log-rank test, Cox proportional-hazard model or Weibull survival function, as applicable. Mixed models will account for the hierarchical structure of the data (individuals clustered within study sites and repeated measurements within individuals). All statistical tests will be two-sided, with a significance threshold of p<0.05. Statistical analyses will be performed using R and R Studio. Propensity score matching will be employed to adjust for treatment assignment, with propensity scores generated based on baseline confounding covariates outlined in the ‘Bias’ section.

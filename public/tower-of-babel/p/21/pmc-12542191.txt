The effect of learning by teaching in nursing skills education on the learner’s knowledge and skill levels: a quasi-experimental study
Authors: Muazzez Şahbaz, Yıldız Denat, Emel Tuğrul
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLearning by teaching is one of the active learning models that focus on the learner. For nursing students to have these competencies and skills, contemporary, versatile, and student-active teaching methods and techniques should be employed in nursing education. Examine the effect of learning by teaching in the skills training of nursing students on their knowledge and skill levels.MethodsA quasi-experimental design without a control group was employed. The study was conducted between November 2022 and February 2023 with 206 fourth-year nursing students. Students received training on “intravenous catheterization and drug administration through the catheter” and “intramuscular and subcutaneous drug administration” using the learning-by-teaching method through group work for 14 weeks. During this training process, they carried out their skills training using PowerPoint presentations, posters, and banners, which they prepared on certain topics, skill videos, and application models. At the beginning and three weeks after end of the education process, a questionnaire was applied to the students who completed the training on the subjects they taught, and their skill levels were evaluated with the objective structured clinical examination (OSCE).ResultsThe mean age of the students was 21.8 ± 1.31 years (min.:20 and max.: 28), 68.4% (n = 141) were female, and their Grade Point Average was 3.05 ± 0.27. There was a statistically significant difference between the students’ mean pre-test and post-test scores in all tests (p < 0.05). A statistically significant difference was determined between the students’ mean pre-test and post-test scores from theoretical and skills measurements in all study groups (p < 0.05).ConclusionsIt was determined that the learning-by-teaching method was effective in the knowledge and skill levels of students in nursing skills education in all learning subjects determined in the study.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12912-025-03971-4.Keywords: Nursing education, Skills training, Learning-by-teaching method

Introduction

In today’s world, the preparation of healthcare professionals to practice in an ever-changing healthcare environment, alongside rapidly advancing technology, is an international concern, and students who are well-prepared for the future can potentially act as agents of change [1]. For this reason, contemporary learning models place the learner at the center of the teaching-learning process [2]. Learning by teaching is an active learning model that emphasizes learner engagement and responsibility. It was originally developed by Jean-Pol Martin for foreign language courses and is widely implemented in Germany. In this approach, students take on the responsibility of the teacher and instruct their peers/classmates [1, 2]. The student may conduct an entire lesson, a part of it, or a special activity [2–5].

This method involves students not only delivering content but also developing and implementing their own instructional strategies to teach the subject [4–6]. A teacher serves as a facilitator and consultant, guiding students in accessing and internalizing knowledge [5]. Learning by teaching supports the development of key 21st-century competencies, including creativity, independence, self-confidence, self-efficacy, teamwork, communication, critical thinking, empathy, information literacy, decision-making, presentation skills, and motivation [4, 5, 7, 8].

Today, nurses are expected to possess both cognitive and affective skills, including 21st-century competencies such as problem-solving, critical thinking, effective cross-cultural communication, collaborative work, and adaptability to rapidly changing conditions [9, 10]. To cultivate these competencies, contemporary, versatile, and student-centered teaching methods should be applied in nursing education. Learning by teaching is one such approach.

Previous studies have primarily explored learning by teaching in various educational contexts. Hanbay (2009) reported positive effects of the “learning-by-teaching method based on the quantum learning approach” in German language acquisition [2]. In Serindağ’s (2007) study, no difference was observed between students teaching the lesson themselves and those taught by the teacher [3], indicating that conducting lessons can facilitate lifelong and autonomous learning. Aslan (2017b) found that learning by teaching enhanced prospective science teachers’ inquiry skills [11], while Aslan (2017a) reported improved attitudes toward chemistry [7].

In nursing education, however, research on learning by teaching, particularly in skills training, is limited. Evaluating different educational methods in nursing can inform effective teaching strategies and contribute to students’ development.

The current study aims to examine the effect of learning by teaching on nursing students’ knowledge and skill levels during skills training. This investigation provides an evidence-based rationale for implementing a learner-centered teaching approach and contributes to the development of essential 21st-century competencies in nursing education.

Methods

Study desing

This study was a quasi-experimental, one-group pretest–posttest design. The purpose of this study was to examine the effect of learning by teaching in nursing students’ skills training on their knowledge and skill levels.

Hypotheses of the research.

H1

Learning by teaching in nursing skills training has a positive effect on the knowledge levels of the learners.

H2

Learning by teaching in nursing skills training has a positive effect on the skill levels of the learners.

Participants and setting

The study was conducted between November 2022 and February 2023 with fourth-year students at the nursing faculty of a university in western Türkiye. The population of the research consisted of 264 students enrolled in the fourth year of this faculty. No sampling procedure was implemented. The study was completed with 206 students who met the inclusion criteria and agreed to participate in the study. The reduction from 264 to 206 participants resulted from student absenteeism and failure to attend either the pre- or post-assessments, and these cases were therefore excluded from the analysis. Inclusion criteria were being a 4th -year student in the nursing department, being enrolled in the teaching in nursing course, attending 80% of the courses during the research period, and participating in both pre- and post-assessments. The power of the study was calculated by retrospective post-hoc G-power analysis and was found to be 0.78 (effect size = 0.25, α = 0.05, F = 2.258). As calculated on the G-Power 3.1.9.4, a sample size of at least 170 was required to achieve a statistical power of 0.8 for an anticipated effect size of 0.25.

The teaching in nursing course consisted of four hours of theoretical and four hours of practical teaching a week. The theoretical section of this course involves concepts related to teaching, planning of teaching (creating an educational plan, setting goals and objectives, etc.), learning theories, teaching methods in nursing, teaching tools and equipment, skills teaching, clinical teaching, adult education, teaching process, and similar topics. In the practice part, students were expected to determine a teaching topic, plan the teaching in line with the learning-teaching process, use active learning strategies, methods, and techniques, prepare teaching materials, use them effectively, and evaluate them with group work.

Instruments

Data was collected using a socio-demographic information form, a knowledge questionnaire, and a skills checklist for Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE).

Descriptive information form.

This form, developed by the researchers, consisted of questions about students’ sociodemographic information.

The quesionnaires.

The questionnaires were developed by the researchers following a literature review [12–15] to determine the theoretical knowledge levels of the students in the study. Each of them included 10 open-ended questions about intravenous drug administration by applying an intravenous catheter and intramuscular and subcutaneous drug administration. To evaluate the validity of the questions, they were reviewed by five academicians, all of whom were experts in nursing education. Among them, two were full professors and three held doctoral degrees in nursing education. These experts were independent of the research team and contributed as external reviewers to ensure objectivity. Additionally, a pilot application was conducted on 10 third-year students of the same faculty to test the comprehensibility of the questions. After this process, some revisions were made and the questionnaires were finalized. Each questionnaire was evaluated out of 100 points (Supplemantary 1).

Skills checklists.

Skills checklists are tools that are used to evaluate students by scoring each step of the skills in question for use in OSCE assessments. In the literature, OSCEs have been reported to be reliable tools for measuring knowledge, attitudes, and skills [16]. In the research, three separate checklists were prepared for the three skills selected as the subject of skills training in line with the literature [15, 17]. The checklists created were submitted to five academicians who were experts in nursing education (two professors and three PhD holders). To test the usefulness of the checklists, a pilot application was carried out on 10 third-year students of the same faculty. After this process, some revisions were made and the final version was obtained. The checklist for each skill was evaluated out of 100 points (Supplemantary 2).

Procedure

This study was conducted using the learning-by-teaching method according to the following steps (Fig. 1).

Research procedure. *OSCE, objective structured clinical examination

Stage 1: Training on the purpose and process of the research.

Before the study was initiated, the researchers gave information to the students in the classroom environment about the method of learning by teaching through the lecture method, videos, and visuals. The purpose and content of the method were explained to the students. Students’ questions were answered.

Stage 2: Formation of groups, application of the information form, questionnaires, and the OSCE as a pre-training assessment.

The students participating in the research were first divided into three groups using the random sampling method. The three determined training topics were distributed to the groups by drawing lots. First, the information form and the questionnaires about the teaching subjects were applied to each group (pre-training). Similarly, each group was evaluated with the OSCE prepared for the subject they taught (pre-training). Separate stations were created for the skills to be evaluated in the OSCE. For each skill, students were taken into the application room one by one and asked to practice the skill within a specified time. The skills applied at each station were evaluated with checklists. After the information form and pre-training assessments were completed, a timeline of the application was created with the students.

Stage 3: Education through Learning by Teaching (Fig. 2).

Stages of the learning-by-teaching method

Formation of sub-study groups and preparation; student groups of 14–15 were randomly assigned to three training groups. These study groups did a detailed literature review and research on the study topics. Students discussed what they learned in the group and as a result of the discussion, they decided what, how much, and how to teach, which evaluation methods to use, and how to manage time. In line with the information they received during the theoretical hours of this course, students created an education plan that included teaching objectives, teaching methods and techniques, and teaching tools and equipment on the subject. They prepared the training content by reviewing the literature and synthesizing the information in the literature in line with the determined training plan. They shared the topics within the group according to the training content.

The students who made preparations for the subjects prepared written materials containing theoretical knowledge of the group subjects and teaching aids such as posters and PowerPoints. They themselves decided on the methods and equipment they would use. For skills teaching, they made the necessary preparations according to the materials and models they prepared and the teaching methods they determined (video teaching of skills, demonstration, etc.).

During this process, the researchers provided guidance on how students could access information sources and made necessary suggestions regarding teaching methods and activities. They also helped obtain the necessary materials.

Implementation of the teaching; students taught skills using the teaching methods, techniques, and tools they prepared in line with the education plans they made.

Stage 4: Application of questionnaires and the OSCE as a post-training assessment.

A post-training assessment was conducted after the students completed all stages of the teaching-learning method. The post-training assessments was administered to the students 3 weeks later. The questionnaires prepared for the subjects they learned by teaching were re-applied (post-training) and the OSCE was conducted to evaluate the skills they had learned by teaching (post-training).

Ethical considerations

This study was approved by the Adnan Menderes University Nursing Faculty Non-Interventional Ethics Committee (Protocol Number: E-76261397-050.99-257169, dated October 17, 2022). All procedures were conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Informed consent

was obtained from all participants after they were fully briefed on the study’s purpose, methods, and their rights. Given the non-invasive and non-interventional nature of the study, verbal consent was obtained, a procedure approved by the Ethics Committee. The confidentiality and anonymity of all participants were strictly protected throughout the research process.

Data analysis

Lists of the statistical methods used, what is crucial, and the software used, appropriate to the study developed.

The statistical package for the social sciences version 25.0 (SPSS Inc, IL, USA) Software was utilized to analyze the data. Data were expressed as numbers, percentages, and mean ± standard deviation values. The dependent groups t-test was employed to make intra-group comparisons of mean pretest-posttest scores, and the paired samples t-test was used to make inter-group comparisons of mean scores. In addition to p-values, effect sizes (Cohen’s d) were calculated to evaluate the magnitude of differences. Differences were considered statistically significant at p < 0.05 unless otherwise stated.

It was determined that 88.8% (n = 183) of them were general high school graduates, 56.3% (n = 116) had equal income and expenses, and that 6.3% (n = 13) worked part-time. Discussion

This research was carried out to determine the effect of learning by teaching in nursing skills education on the learners’ knowledge and skill levels. As a result of the research, it was determined that the learning-by-teaching method positively improved students’ knowledge and skill levels in nursing skills education.

According to the literature, this method has been shown to help students learn more effectively compared to traditional lecture-based instruction and exam-oriented studying, particularly in terms of knowledge retention, skill acquisition, and increased self-efficacy [18–22]. Furthermore, students who created teaching materials during the preparation stage and presented them demonstrated deeper learning, greater enjoyment, and enhanced skill development compared to those who only studied individually for examinations [23–27]. Our study results supported these findings, showing that learning by teaching positively affected students’ success, especially in skills training, both in the development of theoretical knowledge and psychomotor skills.

Although there was no previous interventional study in nursing that specifically examined the learning-by-teaching method, similar positive outcomes have been reported with other active learning approaches. However, these methods (e.g., simulation, flipped classroom, augmented reality) are based on different pedagogical foundations, and thus cannot be considered entirely equivalent. Instead, they share certain common features, such as promoting active participation and learner-centered engagement, which may partially explain the consistent improvements reported in students’ knowledge and skill outcomes [28–33]. The BOPPPS (bridgein, objective, pre-assesment, participatory learning, post-assestment, summary) model, which employs structured instructional design, provides opportunities for students to experience their own learning processes, and incorporates pre-test and post-test evaluations, is also similar to our study design. Studies conducted using the BOPPPS method have likewise emphasized that learner-centered approaches, in which students take an active role, are more effective compared to traditional learning methods [34–36].

In addition, learning by teaching has been successfully applied in other disciplines, such as foreign language learning, science education, and teacher training programs, where it has been shown to foster not only content mastery but also transferable skills like communication, collaboration, and critical thinking [22, 27]. These insights suggest that the method has broad applicability and that lessons from other fields may inform its adaptation in nursing education.

It is also important to distinguish learning by teaching from related strategies such as peer teaching. While both approaches encourage student participation, learning by teaching requires students to take full responsibility for lesson planning, instructional design, and content delivery, thereby engaging them in a deeper pedagogical process compared to simply explaining concepts to peers [21, 37].

The effect sizes observed in this study indicate not only statistical significance but also a practically meaningful impact on students’ learning outcomes. In particular, the smallest improvement was observed in the IV administration group, while the greatest improvement occurred in the SC administration group. This variation suggests that the effectiveness of learning by teaching may differ depending on the complexity and familiarity of the skill being taught, an area worth further investigation.

Today, students are expected to develop the ability to solve complex real-world problems [38, 39]. For this reason, to respond to changing and increasing student needs, nursing education should be structured to include learning methods that place the student at the center, provide opportunities for critical and creative thinking, problem-solving, and active learning, rather than relying solely on traditional approaches [40]. Our study is the first in Türkiye to implement and evaluate the learning-by-teaching method in nursing education. This constitutes one of the strengths of our research and highlights its potential contribution to broadening pedagogical approaches in nursing.

Limitations

Besides the strengths of our study, it also has several limitations. This study was conducted in a single institution without a randomized control group, which limits the generalizability of the findings. In particular, the lack of a control group in our study is an important another limitation. Since the OSCE exam was conducted by researchers in the laboratories of the faculty of nursing, it may have caused bias in some evaluations. Since this is the first study on the evaluation of the learning-by-teaching model, the research results had to be discussed with the results of other education models. It is important to take these limitations into account in future studies on this subject. Our study has important results: it is the first study on this subject, it provides evidence that the learning-by-teaching model can be used in nursing skills training, and it introduces this method to the field.

Conclusion

In this study, it was determined that the learning-by-teaching method in nursing skills education was effective in students’ knowledge and skill levels. Beyond confirming this effectiveness, the study highlights that learning by teaching fosters active participation, responsibility, and deeper learning, which are essential components of contemporary nursing education. The findings suggest that this method can be systematically integrated into nursing curricula, particularly in skills laboratories and clinical teaching, to enhance students’ preparedness for complex healthcare environments. Future research should investigate the effectiveness of learning by teaching across different nursing skills, in diverse educational contexts, and through studies with larger and more varied samples, ideally using controlled and longitudinal designs.

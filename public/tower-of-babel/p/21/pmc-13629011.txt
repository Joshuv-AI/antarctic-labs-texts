Vascular-aware mixture-of-experts with a texture-enhanced decoder for accurate X-ray vessel segmentation
Authors: Zhongjian Ju, Qingnan Wu, Wanqiang Cai, Zidong Liu, Ruigang Ge, Yimeng Li, Jinyuan Wang, Zaichao Lu, Zeping Cui, Jiasong Wu, Baolin Qu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAccurate vessel segmentation in X-ray angiography (XRA) is essential for diagnosing coronary artery disease and supporting interventional procedures. However, low contrast, complex backgrounds, and substantial variation in vessel caliber make accurate segmentation challenging, especially for thin vessels.MethodsWe propose a Vascular-Aware Mixture-of-Experts (VA-MoE) framework for XRA vessel segmentation. The model incorporates Dynamic Snake Convolution (DSnC) to capture the elongated and curved morphology of vessels and a Texture-Enhanced Decoder (TED) to recover fine spatial details and suppress background noise during upsampling. Qualitative results further suggested improved topological continuity and better preservation of thin peripheral vessels compared with competing methods.ConclusionThe proposed VA-MoE framework provides a promising solution for XRA vessel segmentation. By jointly enhancing vascular structural modeling and texture recovery, it may support automated cardiovascular image analysis.Keywords: X-ray angiography, Vessel segmentation, Mixture-of-experts, Texture enhancement

Background

Segmenting coronary arteries from X-ray angiography (XRA) images is pivotal in aiding clinicians for the precise diagnosis and management of arterial diseases [1, 2]. It provides an essential basis for evaluating the degree of coronary artery stenosis, which is critical for determining the severity of the disease and guiding treatment decisions [3]. However, the inherent complexity of XRA imaging, combined with the intricacies of coronary vascular structures, presents significant challenges in accurately segmenting coronary arteries.

As shown in Fig. 1, the unique characteristics of XRA, such as the high variability in vessel appearance and prevalent image noise, complicate the task of delineating vascular topology with precision. These factors introduce substantial difficulties in achieving consistent and reliable segmentation, making it an ongoing challenge. To address this, traditional methods [4, 5] primarily rely on manually extracted features for vessel segmentation. While these approaches can accurately delineate high-contrast vascular regions, they often struggle with complex, interconnected areas. This limitation leads to fragmentation in the segmentation results, which can subsequently interfere with accurate clinical decision-making by healthcare professionals.

Challenges of vessel segmentation in X-ray angiography. Diverse morphologies and complex background noise complicate vascular delineation

To enhance the robustness and accuracy, numerous deep learning-based approaches have been applied to coronary artery segmentation. Existing methods can be further categorized into CNN-based, Transformer-based, and Mamba-based approaches. Among them, CNN-based methods are predominantly built upon the U-Net architecture [6] and enhance segmentation performance by incorporating multi-scale feature aggregation strategies [7, 8] and attention mechanism [9–11]. Compared with traditional algorithms, these methods demonstrate more stable and robust performance in coronary arteries segmentation. However, despite their effectiveness in local texture and multi-scale feature extraction, CNN-based methods may still require additional structural designs to model long-range vascular topology and complex vessel continuity.

Vision Transformer (ViT)-based methods, first introduced by Dosovitskiy et al. [12], provide an alternative mechanism for modeling long-range dependencies and global context. Inspired by this, Transformer-based variants [13–15] are introduced into coronary arteries segmentation. These methods leverage self-attention mechanism to effectively model the global context of coronary arteries and explicitly enhance the relational semantics among vessels, which is particularly beneficial for handling large morphological variations and complex vascular topologies. However, their substantial computational and memory overhead results in high hardware requirements. The emergence of Mamba-based methods [16–18] enables deep learning models to achieve an effective balance between CNNs and Transformers. Despite the notable progress achieved by the aforementioned methods, most of them primarily focus on strengthening the encoder to capture richer semantic representations, while the decoder is often treated as a simple upsampling and feature fusion module. For X-ray angiography (XRA) vessel segmentation, the decoder plays a critical role in recovering fine-grained vascular details, as it directly determines whether thin and low-contrast vessels can be successfully reconstructed from noisy and ambiguous feature maps. Thus, without explicit structural guidance, decoder features are easily dominated by texture noise and imaging artifacts, leading to discontinuous vessel predictions.

Recently, Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures have demonstrated effectiveness in enhancing model capacity and specialization. Existing MoE designs [19–21] are not well suited for the vascular segmentation tasks. They typically employ generic convolutional experts and emphasize semantic diversity, without considering vessel-specific structural priors such as directional continuity, curvature adaptability, and topology preservation. Consequently, their potential for modeling complex vascular morphologies remains largely underexplored in XRA scenarios.

Motivated by these observations, we argue that accurate coronary artery segmentation requires a structure-aware decoding paradigm that explicitly incorporates vascular priors while remaining robust to severe imaging noise. To this end, we propose a novel framework that introduces vascular-aware expert modeling and texture-enhanced decoding in a unified manner. Specifically, we design a Vascular-Aware Mixture-of-Experts (VA-MoE) module that leverages dynamically adaptive snake convolutions to specialize experts in capturing vessel curvature and directional continuity. By generating stage-wise routing schemes, the proposed VA-MoE injects structural priors into the decoding process progressively. Furthermore, to suppress vessel-irrelevant noise and enhance fine-grained texture recovery, we introduce a Texture-Enhanced Decoder (TED) equipped with a parallel guidance strategy and a spatial-channel cooperative optimizer. This decoder effectively integrates structure-enhanced features from the VA-MoE module while preserving low-level texture cues, thereby improving the continuity and completeness of thin and low-contrast vessels.

Related work

In this section, we review representative deep learning–based methods for coronary artery segmentation in X-ray angiography (XRA) images, as well as recent advances in Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) learning for medical image segmentation. Specifically, we first summarize existing XRA vessel segmentation approaches, which mainly focus on enhancing feature representation through convolutional, Transformer-based, and Mamba-based models. We then review MoE-based segmentation methods and discuss their limitations when applied to vascular structure modeling.

X-ray vessel segmentation methods

Early deep learning–based approaches for X-ray coronary artery segmentation are predominantly built upon convolutional neural networks (CNNs), which leverage local receptive fields to capture vascular appearance patterns. Representative methods typically enhance segmentation performance through multi-scale feature aggregation and attention mechanisms. For example, Zhang et al. [11] proposed CBAM-UNet, which incorporates channel and spatial attention modules into U-Net to strengthen the model’s focus on vascular regions and improve the extraction of fine coronary artery structures. Zhou et al. [7] introduced UNet++, employing nested skip connections and deep supervision to progressively integrate multi-scale features, thereby enabling more effective representation of thin vessels. In addition, Challier et al. [22] proposed CM-UNet, a contrastive masked U-Net framework that combines masked image reconstruction and contrastive feature learning for self-supervised pre-training, demonstrating improved segmentation performance under limited-annotation settings. Although these CNN-based methods achieve notable improvements in robustness and accuracy, their predominantly local operations may make explicit long-range topological modeling less straightforward, particularly for coronary arteries that are elongated, tortuous, and highly connected.

To address this limitation, Transformer- and Mamba–based methods have been introduced to enhance global context modeling in XRA vessel segmentation. Cao et al. [15] proposed Swin-Unet, which integrates Swin Transformer blocks into a U-shaped architecture. By leveraging shifted-window self-attention and multi-scale feature aggregation, Swin-Unet effectively captures long-range dependencies and improves the segmentation of complex vascular structures. More recently, Huang et al. [23] proposed SAM-VMNet, which integrates the Segment Anything Model with a vessel-aware Mamba U-Net. By combining SAM-generated coarse masks with domain-specific refinement, this method achieves accurate and robust coronary artery segmentation and stenosis quantification.

Despite the enhanced global modeling capability provided by self-attention mechanisms and state-space modeling, these approaches remain vulnerable to the intrinsic noise and low contrast of XRA images. In particular, the absence of explicit structural guidance during the decoding process often leads to unstable segmentation of thin or low-contrast vessels, resulting in fragmented predictions and missed branches.

Mixture-of-experts in medical image segmentation

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures have been explored in computer vision as an effective means to increase model capacity and enable expert specialization. For example, Wu et al. [24] proposed MoE-NuSeg, a two-stage mixture-of-experts framework for nuclei segmentation that employs multiple specialized experts to jointly model nuclei regions, boundaries, and clustered edges, thereby improving segmentation accuracy while simultaneously reducing model parameters and computational cost. In a different line of work, Li et al. [25] introduced MoE-SAM, which enhances SAM adaptation by incorporating a mixture-of-experts module to selectively refine and fuse multi-layer features, leading to stronger feature representations and improved medical image segmentation performance. Focusing on the non-forgetting adaptation of foundation models, Wang et al. [26] developed SAM-Med3D-MoE, a gated mixture-of-experts framework that integrates general and task-specific models without updating their original parameters, effectively mitigating catastrophic forgetting and significantly boosting 3D medical image segmentation accuracy. Although these methods achieve strong performance in medical image segmentation, they fail to consider the intrinsic structural characteristics of blood vessels.

Thus, we aim to extend the MoE paradigm towards a structure-aware formulation tailored for vascular segmentation. Instead of relying on generic expert designs and semantically driven routing, our approach emphasizes vessel-specific expert specialization by explicitly incorporating structural priors into the expert modeling process.

Encoder

The encoder is primarily responsible for encoding deep vascular features and providing structural and semantic guidance for both the VA-MoE module and the decoder. In this work, based on ResNet-34 [27], we introduced several minor modifications to tailor the architecture to our specific task. Considering the relatively small image size and the presence of substantial background noise, we replaced the original large-kernel convolution with a 3 × 3 convolution. This modification enables more effective extraction of vascular features while mitigating the influence of background noise.

We adopt the modified ResNet-34 as the feature extractor, which can achieve a good balance between contextual modeling and computational efficiency.

Vascular-aware mixture-of-experts module

The proposed Vascular-Aware Mixture-of-Experts (VA-MoE) module plays a critical role in modeling vascular topology and introducing structural priors into the decoder. Considering the highly tortuous, elongated, and spatially unpredictable nature of blood vessels, we adapt a task-adaptive mixture-of-experts routing strategy to vascular structure-aware expert selection. This design enables the decoder to receive stage-specific vascular guidance. Unlike the prior skip-connection routing design [28], the experts in the proposed VA-MoE module are constructed using DSnC to model vessel curvature and directional continuity, and depthwise separable convolutions are used after expert aggregation to control computational overhead.

Specifically, as shown in Fig. 3, we replace the original convolution operations in the conventional MoE with dynamic snake convolutions (DSnC) [29], enabling the VA-MoE to adaptively perceive vessel morphology and extract vascular structural features. Depthwise separable convolutions are further employed to reduce the additional computational cost introduced by expert modeling.

Texture-enhanced decoder

X-ray angiography (XRA) images usually suffer from severe noise and imaging artifacts, which substantially hinder the decoder from accurately recovering fine-grained texture details. To address this issue, we propose a Texture-Enhanced Decoder that enhances the effective integration of VA-MoE features by adopting a parallel guidance strategy. In addition, a spatial-channel cooperative optimizer is embedded into each decoding stage to suppress vessel-irrelevant noise and artifacts in XRA images.

By jointly integrating low-level texture cues and the structured representations provided by VA-MoE, the proposed Texture-Enhanced Decoder enables more accurate and robust decoding performance for the vascular segmentation task.

The rationale for this design is that XRA vessel reconstruction requires both structural continuity and local texture recovery. The parallel guidance strategy introduces VA-MoE-derived structural priors into multiple decoder stages instead of relying on a single late fusion operation, which helps maintain continuity for elongated and low-contrast vessels. Meanwhile, the spatial-channel cooperative optimizer suppresses vessel-irrelevant background responses in the spatial dimension and recalibrates vessel-sensitive channels in the channel dimension. These two operations are complementary for preserving thin branches while reducing noise-related false responses.

Experimental design

Datasets

To comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness and generalization capability of the proposed method, we conduct experiments on three publicly available X-ray coronary angiography (XRA) datasets, including ARCADE, DCA1, and XCAD, as shown in Fig. 5.

Representative examples from the DCA1, XCAD, and ARCADE datasets

ARCADE [32]. ARCADE is a large-scale dataset specifically collected for vascular lesion detection in X-ray coronary angiography. Since we focus on X-ray vascular segmentation, we select the SYNTAX subset of the ARCADE dataset as the segmentation dataset for subsequent evaluation. The images in the ARCADE dataset were collected using two different angiographic systems at the Research Institute of Cardiology and Internal Diseases, Almaty, Kazakhstan. The corresponding ground truth annotations were manually delineated by experienced cardiology experts using the Computer Vision Annotation Tool (CVAT). The provided .json annotation files were subsequently converted into binary mask representations for use in our experiments. Following the original split protocol of this dataset, we divide the data into training and test sets, resulting in 1000 images for training, and 200 images for testing.

DCA1 [33]. The DCA1 dataset is a public benchmark dataset specifically designed for X-ray vascular segmentation. It was collected by the Department of Cardiology of the Mexican Social Security Institute, with appropriate ethical approval. Specifically, the DCA1 dataset consists of 134 X-ray coronary angiography images, along with corresponding ground truth annotations delineated by experienced cardiology experts. In this paper, the entire dataset is divided into two subsets according to an 8:2 ratio, with 107 X-ray images used for training and 27 X-ray images used for testing.

XCAD [2]. The public XCAD dataset is the first X-ray angiography coronary artery disease dataset. It contains 1,621 mask frames, 1,621 coronary angiography images, and 126 coronary angiography images with corresponding ground-truth annotations labeled by experts. All images have a resolution of 512 × 512. Since our study focuses on fully supervised learning, we mainly use the 126 labeled images for training and testing. We split the dataset in the 8:2 ratio, resulting in a training set with 100 samples and a test set with 26 samples.

Experimental settings

All experiments were conducted on a Linux server running Ubuntu 18.04.6 LTS. The system was equipped with dual Intel Xeon Gold 6226R CPUs @ 2.90 GHz and 502 GB of system memory. GPU acceleration was provided by an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090, with NVIDIA driver version 535.98 and CUDA Runtime version 12.2. Regarding the software platform, all experiments were conducted based on U-Bench Zoo [34].

For hyperparameter settings, the batch size was set to 8, and the total number of training epochs was fixed at 300. The original XRA images were single-channel grayscale angiograms. For compatibility with the ResNet-based encoder, each image was replicated into three channels before being fed into the network. Data augmentation included random 90° rotations and random horizontal and vertical flips, whereas normalization was applied as a preprocessing step. To ensure a fair comparison, the same preprocessing and augmentation pipeline was consistently applied across all experiments. The models were optimized using stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with an initial learning rate of 0.01 and a weight decay of 1e-4. A polynomial learning-rate decay schedule was adopted during training. Following the unified loss configuration of U-Bench Zoo [34], the loss-weight hyperparameter λ was set to 0.5 for the BCE term. The same unified training protocol and loss setting were applied to all competing methods and the proposed model.

Evaluation metrics

To quantitatively evaluate the segmentation performance of the proposed method and competitors on three public X-ray vascular datasets, we adopt four widely used metrics in segmentation tasks, including Intersection over Union (IoU), Dice coefficient (Dice), Sensitivity (Sen), and Accuracy (Acc).

Specifically, IoU quantifies the region-level overlap between predictions and ground truth. Dice coefficient measures the overall similarity between the predicted segmentation and the ground truth. Sensitivity evaluates the detection rate of vascular pixels, which is critical for capturing thin, elongated structures. Accuracy assesses the overall global pixel-wise classification correctness. Because vessel segmentation is highly imbalanced at the pixel level, Acc was retained mainly for comparability with the existing benchmark metrics in the main tables and was not interpreted as a standalone indicator of model quality. Specificity, precision, and balanced accuracy were not added to the main comparison tables because complete and consistently verified pixel-level confusion-matrix results were not prepared for all competing methods under the same evaluation protocol. Efficiency analysis

Beyond segmentation accuracy, we further compare the computational efficiency of all models in terms of the number of parameters and inference speed, as shown in Table 4. Our method contains 23.34 M parameters and achieves an inference speed of 24.78 FPS, suggesting a reasonable trade-off between segmentation performance and computational cost. Although this speed may be acceptable for workstation- or GPU-supported auxiliary analysis, further compression, pruning, distillation, or lightweight optimization would be required for deployment in mobile, embedded, or resource-limited clinical environments.

Overall, our model maintains moderate computational efficiency while achieving favorable segmentation performance, particularly in sensitivity (Sen). These performance gains primarily stem from two key design choices. First, the proposed framework strengthens long-range structural modeling through vascular-aware expert routing. Second, the Texture-Enhanced Decoder improves the recovery of fine vessel details during upsampling. The balance between segmentation accuracy and deployment efficiency should be further evaluated in future clinical implementation studies.

Discussion

In this work, we proposed a vascular-aware segmentation framework for coronary artery segmentation in X-ray angiography (XRA) images to address the challenges posed by complex vessel morphology, severe imaging noise, and low contrast. Unlike approaches that mainly strengthen encoder representations, the proposed VA-MoE and TED emphasize vessel-specific structural priors during decoding. The results across ARCADE, DCA1, and XCAD suggest that combining vascular-aware expert routing with texture-enhanced decoding can improve region overlap, thin-vessel preservation, and structural continuity. Nevertheless, the observed advantages should be interpreted with caution because the comparisons are based on fixed dataset splits and aggregated performance metrics.

Recent foundation-model-based approaches have shown strong potential for medical image segmentation. For example, MedSAM extends the Segment Anything Model to medical images [43], adapter-based SAM variants improve domain adaptation for medical segmentation [44], and diffusion-based frameworks such as MedSegDiff-V2 provide an alternative generative paradigm for segmentation [45]. These approaches offer advantages in general representation learning, transferability, and prompt-based segmentation. However, XRA vessel segmentation remains challenging for foundation models because thin low-contrast vessels require precise boundary recovery, topological continuity, and robustness to background artifacts. In this context, the proposed VA-MoE framework may be complementary to foundation models: foundation models can provide general visual priors or coarse masks, whereas VA-MoE and TED can serve as vessel-specific refinement modules to enhance curvature adaptability and fine-vessel continuity.

The clinical relevance of segmentation improvements should also be interpreted carefully. Although Dice and IoU are widely used for evaluating segmentation overlap, higher overlap scores do not necessarily guarantee greater clinical utility. Alidoost et al. showed that the utility of a deep learning segmentation model may not be Dice coefficient dependent in a vessel segmentation setting [46]. For coronary XRA analysis, clinically meaningful evaluation may require radiologist-based scoring, vessel continuity assessment, stenosis visibility analysis, and downstream assessment of interventional decision support. Because the present study used publicly available anonymized datasets, additional clinician-based scoring, multi-reader evaluation, stenosed-region-specific analysis, and stenosis quantification remain beyond the scope of the present methodological study and should be incorporated in future clinically oriented validation. This is an important limitation and a direction for future work. In addition, specificity, precision, and balanced accuracy were not reported in the main comparison because complete pixel-level confusion matrices or prediction masks were not available for all competing methods; future studies should include these false-positive-sensitive metrics when complete outputs are available.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the ground-truth masks in public datasets are based on manual expert annotations, which may contain boundary uncertainty, annotation errors, or inter-rater variability. Therefore, the model should be regarded as an auxiliary tool for reducing the burden of vessel delineation rather than as a method that exceeds expert judgment. Future studies should incorporate multi-expert annotations and inter-rater variability analysis to better characterize the upper bound and uncertainty of segmentation performance.

Second, the smaller DCA1 and XCAD datasets showed lower absolute segmentation performance than ARCADE. This may be related to limited labeled samples, differences in image quality and resolution, annotation uncertainty, data imbalance, or domain shift across imaging systems. Although data augmentation, depthwise separable convolution, and a fixed evaluation protocol were used to reduce overfitting risk, the present results cannot fully exclude overfitting under small-sample conditions. Future work should include larger multi-center datasets, repeated runs with different random seeds, and cross-validation, especially for DCA1 and XCAD.

Third, although we added an exploratory method-level statistical analysis based on the aggregated comparison results, this analysis has important limitations. The Wilcoxon signed-rank tests were performed across baseline methods using the reported mean values, rather than across individual test images. Similarly, the SD and 95% CI reported in Table 9 reflect variability across baseline-method-level improvements rather than repeated-run variability. Therefore, the results indicate that the proposed method achieved consistent positive improvements over the compared methods at the aggregated method level, but they do not replace rigorous image-level paired statistical testing. Because per-image paired scores or prediction masks for all competing methods were not available in the current comparison setting, we could not reliably estimate image-level variability, run-to-run variability, or confidence intervals across repeated training runs. Future work should include image-level paired comparisons, repeated training with different random seeds, and cross-validation, especially for smaller datasets such as DCA1 and XCAD.

Finally, the model introduces specialized modules and therefore remains more complex than plain encoder-decoder baselines. Recent studies have also emphasized the importance of structural feature enhancement, boundary-aware refinement, efficient architectural optimization, and feature fusion in medical image analysis [47, 48]. In addition, dynamic convolutional mechanisms have been explored in chest X-ray image captioning and radiology report understanding, suggesting the broader relevance of adaptive convolutional representations in radiological image analysis [49, 50]. In line with these directions, future work should explore pruning, quantization, knowledge distillation, and lightweight deployment strategies to improve applicability in real-time or resource-limited clinical environments. Temporal modeling across angiographic sequences may also further improve vessel continuity and reduce frame-level ambiguity.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the proposed vascular-aware segmentation framework provides a promising solution for X-ray angiography vessel segmentation. By jointly enhancing vascular structural modeling and texture recovery, it improves thin-vessel delineation, preserves topological continuity, and achieves favorable performance across three public datasets. Further validation using larger multi-center cohorts, image-level statistical testing, and clinically oriented evaluation is needed before translation into routine clinical use.

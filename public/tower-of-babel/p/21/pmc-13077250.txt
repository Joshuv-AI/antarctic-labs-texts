Prognostic value as metabolic tumor activity on 18F-FDG uptake in thymic epithelial tumors
Authors: Kosuke Hashimoto, Kyoichi Kaira, Hisao Imai, Atsuto Mouri, Ou Yamaguchi, Ryo Taguchi, Tetsuya Umesaki, Yoshinobu Ichiki, Tomoyuki Hishida, Hiroyuki Nitanda, Ichiei Kuji, Hiroshi Kagamu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThymic epithelial tumors (TETs) are rare neoplasms; however, optimal predictors after initial treatment are unclear. 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography (18F-FDG PET) is useful for differentiating malignant from benign lesions and has the potential to serve as a prognostic predictor. This retrospective study investigated the prognostic significance of 18F-FDG PET in patients with TETs.MethodsA total of 108 consecutive patients with TETs classified as thymoma or thymic carcinoma (TC) who received 18F-FDG PET before any treatment between August 2007 and March 2020 were eligible for this study. For 18F-FDG uptake, the maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax), metabolic tumor volume (MTV), and total lesion glycolysis (TLG) were assessed for prognostic prediction.ResultsUnivariate analysis identified performance status (PS), smoking, disease stage, histology, SUVmax, MTV, and TLG as significant predictors of progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS). In the multivariate analysis, TLG was an independent prognostic factor for predicting worse PFS, and histology emerged as a significant predictor for OS. In patients with thymoma, univariate analysis identified disease stage, SUVmax, MTV, and TLG as significant predictors for PFS but not OS. In patients with TC, univariate analysis identified PS, disease stage, smoking status, SUVmax, MTV, and TLG as significant predictors for both PFS and OS.ConclusionsIndicators of 18F-FDG uptake, including SUVmax, MTV, and TLG, can predict poor outcomes in patients with TETs. Increased accumulation of 18F-FDG was closely associated with poor PFS in specific populations, such as those with thymoma or TC, but not OS.Keywords: Thymic epithelial tumors (TETs), prognosis, 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography (18F-FDG PET), metabolic tumor activity

Highlight box

Key findings

• Uptake of 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose (18F-FDG) could predict the worse survival in patients with thymic epithelial tumors.

What is known and what is new?

• Increased accumulation of 18F-FDG determined by maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax), metabolic tumor volume (MTV), and total lesion glycolysis (TLG) was closely associated with worse progression-free survival (PFS), but not overall survival in patients with thymoma and thymic carcinoma.

• Out of SUVmax, MTV, and TLG reflecting 18F-FDG uptake, TLG was identified as independent predictor for worse PFS in patients with thymic epithelial tumors.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Increased uptake of 18F-FDG is useful for predicting worse PFS after any treatment in patients with thymoma and thymic carcinoma.

Introduction

Thymic epithelial tumors (TETs) are relatively rare solid tumors, consisting of thymoma and thymic carcinoma (TC). Most patients with TC experience tumor recurrence resulting from progressive disease. However, the majority of thymomas are curable with surgical treatment, although some patients may experience distant or local recurrence, necessitating local therapy or systemic treatment. To identify the optimal predictor in patients with TETs, several studies have focused on biological markers using tumor specimens and have analyzed the protein or genetic expression of these markers. However, it remains unclear about the established markers for predicting the prognosis of TETs. Further investigation is warranted to elucidate meaningful predictors in patients with TETs.

18Fluorine-fluorodeoxyglucose (18F-FDG) positron emission tomography (PET) is useful for differentiating malignant from benign lesions; however, there are critical issues regarding false-positive findings, which arise from increased accumulation in inflammatory or granulomatous lesions (1). In daily practice, 18F-FDG PET has consistently been performed to assess systemic expansion and the potential for malignancy in various human cancers. A previous review indicated that 18F-FDG PET is helpful for distinguishing thymoma from TC, with TC being differentiated from thymoma at a diagnostic specificity of >90% using a standardized uptake value (SUV) cut-off of >5.0 (1). However, the accumulation of 18F-FDG overlaps between low- and high-risk thymomas, highlighting limitations in identifying histological differences (1). Aside from the diagnostic value of 18F-FDG PET for TETs, there is limited data about the prognostic role of 18F-FDG uptake in patients with TETs (2-6). Based on previous PET studies, metabolic tumor volume (MTV) and total lesion glycolysis (TLG), rather than the maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax), have been identified as significant prognostic indicators following systemic treatment in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (7,8). MTV reflects the total tumor volume containing active cancer cells and is thus associated with therapeutic resistance, progressive disease, and performance status (PS). In patients with TETs, it remains unclear whether MTV or TLG from 18F-FDG uptake is a better prognostic predictor than SUVmax (2-6). Most previous studies evaluating the prognosis of 18F-FDG uptake involved fewer than 100 eligible patients with inadequate follow-up periods. Currently, little is known about the prognostic role of 18F-FDG PET in patients with TETs. Therefore, we need to focus on the relationship between different indicators of 18F-FDG uptake, such as SUV, MTV, and TLG, and the treatment outcomes in patients with TETs.

From above background, we conducted a retrospective approach to examine the clinical usefulness of 18F-FDG accumulation as a prognostic marker in patients with TETs. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2025-aw-2140/rc).

Methods

Patients

A total of 108 consecutive patients with TETs identified as thymoma or TC who underwent 18F-FDG PET before any treatment at our institution between August 2007 and March 2020 were included in our study. These patients overlapped with those in our previous study, which reported the association between 18F-FDG uptake and the tumor immune environment without providing prognostic information (9). This study was provided as a supplemental analysis (9). The eligibility criteria were as follows: (I) receiving 18F-FDG PET before any treatment; (II) pathologically confirmed thymomas or TC; and (III) having follow-period with at least 5 years after initial diagnosis. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (I) insufficient survival data; (II) performing 18F-FDG PET in different institutions; and (III) patient’s rejection using PET and clinical data.

Pathological diagnosis and tumor subtyping were done according to the 2015 World Health Organization (WHO) histological classification of TETs and the TNM staging system (10). The confirmed diagnoses were done using light microscopy and immunohistochemistry. Relevant clinical data were retrospectively collected from the electronic medical records of patients treated at the Saitama Medical University International Medical Center. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of the Saitama Medical University International Medical Center (approval No. koku 2025-076) and individual consent for this retrospective analysis was waived (11).

PET data analysis

Patients fasted for at least 6 h before PET imaging, which was performed using a PET/CT scanner (Biograph 6 or 16, Siemens Healthineers K.K., Tokyo, Japan) with a 585-mm field of view. Three-dimensional data acquisition was initiated for 60 min after injecting 3.7 MBq/kg of FDG. We acquired eight bed positions (2-min acquisition per bed position) based on the imaging range. Attenuation-corrected transverse PET images were reconstructed into 168×168 matrices with a slice thickness of 2.00 mm using an ordered-subset expectation-maximization (OSEM) algorithm incorporating a point-spread function.

Computed tomography (CT) scanning for initial staging was performed using an intravenous contrast medium, and the CT images were interpreted by board-certified radiologists. We utilized the RAVAT software (Nihon Medi Physics Co. Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) on a Windows workstation to semi-automatically calculate SUVmax. For 18F-FDG PET, MTV and TLG, defined as MTV multiplied by SUVmean for each lesion, were calculated by the SUV threshold obtained from the SUV of the liver VOI. Each threshold was defined as the average of 1.5× SUVmean + 2× SD of the liver SUV. These SUV thresholds were the optimal values for generating three-dimensional VOIs that enclosed the entire tumor mass, with CT images used as a reference. Regions of activity other than tumors, including the kidneys, urinary tract, myocardium, and gastrointestinal tract, were manually excluded by a board-certified nuclear medicine physician based on the provided orientation (9).

Statistical analysis

Fisher’s exact test was used to examine the association between two categorical variables. Statistical significance was set at P<0.05. The optimal cut-off values for SUVmax, MTV, and TLG for 18F-FDG uptake were determined based on the median values. Factors with values exceeding the cut-off were defined as highly expressed. Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from the initial treatment to disease progression or death. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from the initial treatment to death from any cause. Survival curves were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method for each group.

Differences in survival distributions were evaluated using the log-rank test. Univariate and multivariate analyses were conducted using Cox proportional hazards models to calculate hazard ratios with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). All statistical analyses were done using GraphPad Prism software (v.10.0; GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA) and JMP Pro 16.0 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC).

Results

Patient demographics

Patient characteristics have been reported in our previous study (9). Briefly, eligible patients in the current study had the following characteristics: a median age of 64 years (range, 34–85 years; ≥69 vs. <69 years, 76 vs. 32 patients), 54 men and 54 women, 71 patients with a PS of 0, and 37 with a PS of 1–2, with a smoking history in 49 (45.3%) patients. Disease stages I, II, III, and IV were observed in 37 (34.3%), 39 (36.1%), 14 (13.0%), and 18 (16.7%) patients, respectively. Thymomas and TC were identified in 81 and 27 patients, respectively. Ninety-four patients underwent surgical resection as the initial treatment, and 14 patients were treated with chemotherapy with or without radiotherapy as the first-line treatment.

Survival analysis of patients with TETs

The median SUVmax, MTV, and TLG were 4.1 (range, 0–17.3), 16.9 (range, 0–1,864.2) cm3, and 57.6 (range, 0–5,478.6) g/mL, respectively, among all patients (n=108). High SUVmax, MTV, and TLG values were observed in 49.1%, 50.9%, and 50.0% of the patients, respectively.

Of the 108 patients, 23 experienced some recurrences after the initial treatment, and 16 died due to disease progression. The median follow-up period was 1,962 days (range, 143–6,145 days). The 5-year PFS and OS rates for all patients were 59.0% and 67.0%, respectively. The Kaplan-Meier survival curves for PFS and OS according to 18F-FDG uptake are shown in Figure 1. Univariable analysis identified PS, smoking, disease stage, histology, SUVmax, MTV, and TLG as significant predictors of PFS and OS (Table 1). We performed multivariable analysis using individual indicators of 18F-FDG uptake (SUVmax, MTV, and TLG) with the factors prespecifying clinical important variables such as age, PS, stage, and histology. By multivariable analysis, TLG, disease stage, and histology were independent prognostic factors for predicting worse PFS, and histology was identified as a significant factor for OS (Table 1). In surgically treated patients (n=94), univariable analysis identified disease stage, histology, SUVmax, MTV, and TLG as significant predictors of PFS, and histology for OS (Table 2). Multivariable analysis demonstrated that disease stage was an independent predictor for PFS, but not for OS (Table 2).

Survival analysis of patients with thymoma

The median SUVmax, MTV, and TLG were 3.8 (range, 0–10.2), 8.3 (range, 0–1,864.2) cm3, and 26.1 (range, 0–5,478.6) g/mL, respectively, among 81 patients with thymoma. Of the 81 patients, 7 (8.6%) experienced disease recurrence, and 3 (3.7%) died due to disease progression. Survival analysis in patients with thymoma was performed based on these cut-off values (Figure 2). Table 3 shows the univariable analysis identifying disease stage, SUVmax, MTV, and TLG as significant predictors for PFS, but not OS. Factors prespecifying clinical important variables such as age, PS, and stage were selected for multivariable analysis. Disease stage was an independent factor for predicting poor PFS, but not for OS (Table 3).

Survival analysis of patients with TC

The median values for 27 patients with TC were 9.6 (range, 0–17.3) for SUVmax, 74.2 (range, 0–783.2) cm3 for MTV, and 334.9 (range, 0–3,668) g/mL for TLG, and these cut-off values were utilized for further survival analysis (Figure 3). Of the 27 patients, 16 (59.2%) experienced disease recurrence after the initial treatment, and 13 (48.1%) died due to disease progression. Univariable analysis identified PS, disease stage, smoking, SUVmax, MTV, and TLG as significant predictors for PFS and OS (Table 4). Multivariable analysis identified smoking and disease stage as independent factors for PFS, and disease stage, PS, and SUVmax as independent factors for OS (Table 4).

Discussion

Our study found that increased uptake of 18F-FDG was significantly associated with worse outcomes after initial treatment in patients with TETs. Of the three indicators used to examine the prognostic significance of TETs, TLG appeared to be a better predictor of PFS than SUVmax or MTV in all patients with TETs. Most patients with thymoma survive without disease progression after curative surgery; therefore, it may be challenging to predict worse OS using SUVmax, MTV, and TLG concerning 18F-FDG uptake. 18F-FDG uptake was identified as a significant predictor of OS in patients with TETs, including thymoma and TC. Considering the results of our study, the increased accumulation of 18F-FDG underscores its potential as a prognostic marker for TETs.

Several studies have reported the usefulness of 18F-FDG PET as a prognostic marker in patients with TETs (2-6). Han et al. reported that SUVmax was an independent prognostic factor for freedom from recurrence in 114 patients with TETs, whereas MTV and TLG were not (2). Moreover, SUVmax demonstrated higher diagnostic and prognostic values for TETs than MTV and TLG (2). Hou et al. reported that negative 18F-FDG uptake by SUVmax was significantly associated with better PFS in 83 patients with TETs (50 thymomas and 33 TCs) (3). However, their study did not investigate the relationship between metabolic tumor activity reflecting MTV or TLG and the prognosis of TETs, noting limitations such as an insufficient follow-up period and lack of OS data (3). Chao et al. examined the potential of SUVmax, MTV, and TLG as prognostic markers in 100 patients with TETs (58 thymomas and 42 TCs), and univariate analysis showed SUVmax and TLG as significant predictors of PFS, with SUVmax also significant for OS (4). However, the SUV of 18F-FDG uptake was not selected as an independent prognostic predictor in the multivariate analysis (4). Lee et al. also reported that SUVmax, MTV, and TLG were significant predictors for time-to-progression in univariate survival analysis, and SUVmax was an independent prognostic factor for PFS, although there were no data on OS (5). Considering previous studies on prognostic predictors based on 18F-FDG uptake in TETs, SUVmax appears to be an important prognostic factor for predicting worse PFS in patients with TETs (2-5). However, it remains unclear whether 18F-FDG uptake could predict worse OS in patients with thymomas due to their generally better prognosis after initial treatment. Previous studies lacked data on survival analysis limited to patients with thymoma, and few focused on the three different indicators of 18F-FDG uptake (SUVmax, MTV, and TLG). The present study demonstrated that increased uptake of 18F-FDG was closely associated with poor outcomes in patients with TETs, regardless of type, and highlighted a critical limitation regarding the prognostic significance of 18F-FDG uptake for predicting OS in patients with thymoma. Consistent with the results of previous studies, the SUVmax of 18F-FDG uptake could predict outcomes after initial treatment in patients with TETs, particularly PFS. Our study focused on a sufficient follow-up period of more than 5 years and evaluated different indicators of 18F-FDG uptake, such as SUVmax, MTV, and TLG, compared with previous studies.

There are several limitations to our study. First, our sample size was insufficient to clarify the established evidence as a predictive marker, which may have biased our results. Moreover, our study included different stages and different treatments, using a retrospective database. Because of the limited number of TC, the investigation of multiple covariates may be difficult to explain the prognostic significance of independent factors. Therefore, we acknowledge the exploratory nature and limited reliability of multivariable analysis for the patients with TC. Second, we defined different cut-off values for the three groups: TETs, thymoma, and TC. Since thymoma and TC have notably different prognoses after treatment, we chose the appropriate cut-off values differently for the thymoma and TC groups. However, there is a limitation of median-based cut-offs, and these thresholds are exploratory and not validated. Finally, the majority of patients with thymoma did not experience recurrence or death, making it difficult to evaluate the usefulness of 18F-FDG PET as a prognostic marker in this population. Further studies should focus on patients with TC to determine the prognostic role of 18F-FDG PET using a larger sample size.

Conclusions

Indicators of 18F-FDG uptake, such as SUVmax, MTV, and TLG, can predict poor outcomes in patients with TETs. The increased accumulation of 18F-FDG was closely related to poor PFS, but not OS, in individual thymoma and TC populations. Further investigation is warranted to examine the prognostic role of 18F-FDG accumulation in a large-scale study.

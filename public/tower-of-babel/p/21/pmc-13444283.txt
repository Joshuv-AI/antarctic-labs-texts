Research on motor diagnosis and maintenance decision-making technology based on DCCNN-L transformer and fault knowledge graph
Authors: Wu Shufang, Wang Xinwen, Liu Zeyu, Shi Gaoxian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Traditional deep learning methods for motor fault diagnosis have primarily focused on signal-based classification. Intelligent operation and maintenance encompasses both fault diagnosis and maintenance decision-making. However, transitioning from vibration fault signal diagnosis to generating maintenance decision texts presents challenges such as data heterogeneity between numerical signal data and textual decision data. Relying solely on existing large models is insufficient to achieve a closed-loop process from intelligent diagnosis to maintenance decision-making. Thus, this paper proposes a motor fault diagnosis method based on a Dual-Channel Convolutional Neural Network with Probabilistic Feature Fusion and Lightweight Transformer (DCCNN-LTransformer), as well as a decision-making method that utilizes a fault knowledge graph. In the feature extraction stage, a novel probabilistic feature fusion mechanism is introduced to extract features from vibration signals. A dual-channel 1D convolutional neural network framework is employed to capture feature signals and obtain positional encodings. For feature classification within the Transformer, an Enhanced Dynamic Window-hybrid Attention (EDWHA) mechanism is proposed to improve the local analysis capability for fault signals. Finally, after the fault diagnosis algorithm classifies the faults, the BERT-BiLSTM-CRF algorithm is employed to extract knowledge related to current fault diagnosis and maintenance decision-making, thereby constructing a structured fault knowledge graph database. By integrating a general-purpose LLM with the fault knowledge graph, the system can generate maintenance decisions for various motor faults, ultimately forming a closed-loop maintenance decision-making process. To validate the algorithm’s adaptability under various conditions, three distinct fault datasets were utilized. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed algorithm outperforms CNN, Transformer, and CNN-Transformer models, confirming its effectiveness. A fault knowledge graph was constructed, and a motor fault diagnosis and maintenance decision-making platform was established, thereby verifying the reliability of this study.

Introduction

As a core power component in modern industry, electric motors convert electrical energy into mechanical energy to drive various types of machinery, playing a critical role throughout the entire equipment drive process1. Fault diagnosis technology is key to ensuring the safe and efficient operation of industrial systems. Motor failures can lead to production shutdowns, equipment damage, and even safety incidents. Therefore, effective fault diagnosis techniques are essential to identify potential risks early and optimize maintenance strategies2. By monitoring signals such as vibration, current, and temperature, and integrating signal processing with artificial intelligence technologies, motor fault diagnosis enables early identification, localization, and classification of faults3. Early fault diagnosis relied heavily on expert sensory judgment and empirical knowledge—such as listening to sounds and feeling for temperature changes—which was inefficient and prone to inaccuracies4. With the integration of computer technology, artificial intelligence, and automation, traditional diagnostic equipment and manual monitoring have gradually been replaced by computer-based signal processing and data analysis techniques. These advancements enable comprehensive recording of equipment fault data and facilitate high-precision in-depth prediction and fault evaluation through AI algorithms5–7. Today, mechanical fault diagnosis has evolved into a diversified detection system8, in which machine learning algorithms such as Support Vector Machines9, Naive Bayes10, K-means Clustering11, and Random Forests12 are widely used for real-time condition assessment and fault analysis.

Following breakthroughs in CNN around 201213, deep learning methods—including Long Short-Term Memory network (LSTM)14, Graph Convolutional Network (GCN)15, Transformer16, and Generative Adversarial Network (GAN)17—have increasingly been applied to mechanical fault diagnosis. These methods iteratively train on large-scale mechanical fault datasets, ultimately building intelligent diagnostic models that integrate sensing and computation18–20. Fault diagnosis in electric motors is more challenging compared to bearings and gearboxes, primarily due to the diversity and complexity of motor fault types, difficulties in extracting high-frequency signal features, interference from multi-physical field coupling, significant influence of operating conditions, and poor generalizability of diagnostic methods. Further research and data supplementation are still required in this area21. Deep learning-based motor fault diagnosis methods analyze data to extract fault features and utilize neural networks for fault classification, enabling generalized processing of diverse fault data and improving diagnostic efficiency22. Currently, diagnostic frameworks based on CNN and Transformer architectures are among the primary approaches being applied for such analysis.

First, CNN-based motor fault diagnosis methods primarily focus on the direct processing of one-dimensional time-series signals—such as vibration, current, and acoustic emission signals—by applying convolutional kernels that slide along the temporal axis to extract local features. One-dimensional CNNs can autonomously learn optimal features directly from raw signals, reducing the need for manual intervention23. In contrast, two-dimensional CNNs first convert one-dimensional signals into two-dimensional representations, leveraging image processing capabilities to extract more complex features24. For example, vibration signals can be transformed into time-frequency diagrams via a short-time Fourier transform before being fed into a CNN for classification. Approaches such as LSTM, GCN, and GAN also frequently incorporate CNN-based feature extraction modules. Numerous studies have proposed effective solutions for motor fault diagnosis under diverse and complex operating conditions. Zhong et al.25 introduced a novel data transformation method combined with a multi-directional attention mechanism integrated into a CNN. This enables fault diagnosis from a global perspective through data extraction and pixel rearrangement, supplemented by zero-padding to mitigate data scarcity. Xu et al.26 proposed a method that integrates a global-local fusion feature extractor with indicative contrastive learning. To address noise interference and sample imbalance, they designed a bidirectional dual-head gated model and a residual local feature extraction module to comprehensively capture both global and local fault characteristics. Experimental results confirmed that their approach not only achieved high diagnostic accuracy and robustness but also exhibited strong cross-domain transfer capability. Weng et al.27 developed a progressive domain separation network based on multi-index integrated quantization, incorporating an adversarial training framework grounded in maximum classifier discrepancy to gradually align features of known categories. Inspired by ensemble learning, they also proposed an outlier pseudo-label integration filter that aggregates high-quality soft pseudo-labels to train an outlier classifier. Kang et al.28 employed Gramian Angular Field encoding to generate feature maps and introduced a generative adversarial enhancement strategy for multi-scale CNN training. By designing a time-dependent mean-squared error regularization mechanism, they expanded the original training dataset, achieving effective fault diagnosis under limited sample conditions along with excellent generalization ability and noise resistance. Ertargin et al.29 proposed a fault detection system that operates without any sensors attached internally or externally to the motor. This system classifies motor faults using a CNN-GRU algorithm, implemented via a lightweight deployment on smart devices. Through the simulation of various operational scenarios, the effectiveness of this sensor-free motor fault detection method was validated. The studies referenced above demonstrate that CNN-based signal feature extraction can effectively diagnose motor faults across different scenarios. However, the performance of CNNs is highly dependent on input data quality.

Transformer-based methods for motor fault diagnosis demonstrate notable advantages in capturing long-range dependencies, enabling parallel computation, and processing multi-feature data. By leveraging attention mechanisms, the Transformer model can effectively focus on interrelationships among various input features and extract critical characteristics for classification and prediction30–33. In fault diagnosis applications, diverse sensor data—such as temperature, pressure, and vibration signals—can be integrated as multi-feature inputs into the Transformer architecture to facilitate accurate fault identification and prognosis. Fu et al.34 introduced a novel motor bearing fault diagnosis network named TRA-ACGAN, which integrates the Transformer with an Auxiliary Classifier Generative Adversarial Network (ACGAN). Their approach incorporates two additional tokens in the Transformer encoder to respectively encode fault label information and discriminator source information. By refining the discriminator structure, the model mitigates coupling issues during recognition, achieving a diagnostic accuracy exceeding 99%. Zhou et al.35 developed a novel multi-source heterogeneous information fusion model based on the Swin-Transformer framework. Their design incorporates multiple components to enhance cross-domain fault diagnosis performance, including a data augmentation module to increase source domain diversity and a shifted window mechanism to strengthen both horizontal and vertical feature extraction capabilities. Liu et al.36 proposed an innovative Transformer architecture featuring an expanded dual-attention enhancement module and a one-dimensional dual-global self-attention transformer encoder. Zhi et al.37 proposed a fault diagnosis method based on a multi-channel Transformer, where an improved sliding window reduces the computational parameter count for fault diagnosis. Multi-branch feature extraction network structures based on Transformers or traditional methods can provide high fault recognition efficiency38,39. however, while fault diagnosis represents the front-end aspect of operation and maintenance, the implementation of backend maintenance is equally crucial for ensuring operational reliability. Consequently, numerous studies have begun employing LLMs or small-scale knowledge graph models to facilitate backend maintenance decision-making operations.

Currently, establishing a systematic closed-loop operation and maintenance framework centered on fault-decision processes faces significant challenges. On one hand, the diversity of issues identified through fault diagnosis can lead to errors in maintenance decision-making, as manual maintenance decisions often rely on work orders and rigid maintenance protocols. On the other hand, there is a scarcity of large-scale structured data forming comprehensive fault maintenance knowledge. Therefore, leveraging LLM and knowledge graphs to manage backend operations in maintenance is critically important. Sofia et al.40 proposed a novel cognitive digital twin framework specifically designed to support industrial maintenance. This approach formalizes expert maintenance knowledge through an ontology that supports diagnostic reasoning, employs data-driven algorithms to process and update fault data from physical systems within the proposed ontology, and ultimately implements the operation and maintenance process for bearing friction and wear faults. Liu et al.41 introduced an LLM based on a data-knowledge dual-drive mechanism, utilizing prompt-based fine-tuning methods and a multi-agent collaborative fault detection mechanism. In this framework, scene graphs provide rich, structured information to the LLM through associations between nodes and edges. Liu et al.42 presented a method for constructing a fault knowledge graph for hydroturbine governor systems using the BERTWWM-BiLSTM-MHA-CRF algorithm. The process begins with designing the schema layer of the knowledge graph, followed by analyzing the characteristics of fault documentation in hydroturbine governor systems and performing cleaning and annotation of unstructured texts. This enables the intelligent extraction of fault entities and their relationships. Although large models possess powerful language generation capabilities, for mechanical equipment operation and maintenance, directly inputting one-dimensional signals into large models for fault diagnosis suffers from inaccurate detection and poor interpretability of the generated fault mechanisms. The transition from vibration signals to maintenance decision texts involves data heterogeneity, necessitating the use of fault diagnosis algorithms to identify equipment fault types. Text analysis is then performed based on the identified fault types to complete the closed-loop operation and maintenance decision-making process.

In summary, in the field of rotating machinery fault diagnosis, various algorithms have achieved high diagnostic efficiency. However, they often lack subsequent maintenance management operations. After diagnosis, equipment maintenance is a necessary follow-up step. With the enhanced text comprehension capabilities of large models, emergence effects may occur, necessitating the constraint of large models with domain-specific expert knowledge to ensure interpretability in maintenance decisions and improve the credibility of operation and maintenance. This paper proposes a novel DCCNN-LTransformer algorithm to address vibration data anomalies and rapidly input fault types into general large models. The BERT-BiLSTM-CRF algorithm is employed to extract structured information from diverse maintenance knowledge corpora, enabling the construction of a fault knowledge graph. In the decision-making and Q&A phase, a general-purpose large language model is integrated to conduct question-answering based on the entities and relationships within the knowledge graph, thereby generating informed maintenance decisions. Comparative experiments are conducted on both fault diagnosis and maintenance decision-making to validate the reliability of the proposed method in achieving a closed-loop operation and maintenance process. The structure of this paper is organized as follows: Sect. 2 outlines the technical foundations; Sect. 3 describes the proposed methodology and enhancement strategies; Sect. 4 presents the experimental validation; and Sect. 5 provides concluding remarks and suggests directions for future research.

Basic theory

Fault diagnosis algorithm

The one-dimensional convolutional neural network (1DCNN) is a deep learning model specifically designed for processing sequential data, such as vibration signals, current signals, and audio signals43. In fault diagnosis applications, it enables efficient classification by automatically extracting local features from the raw signals. The 1DCNN architecture incorporates residual connections and consists of convolutional residual blocks for feature extraction, along with pooling layers, and fully connected layers for classification. By applying one-dimensional convolution to vibration signals, the model extracts localized features while preserving the intrinsic structure of the input data. With advantages such as low computational cost, short training cycles, and high accuracy, 1DCNN meets the requirements for motor fault diagnosis applications. The basic structure of the 1DCNN is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Fig. 11DCNN basic framework.

1DCNN basic framework.

The Transformer architecture has achieved remarkable success in classification and recognition tasks within natural language processing and computer vision44. The architecture is built primarily on a residual structure composed of encoders and decoders. The left side of the architecture consists of N encoders. Each encoder includes an input layer, positional encoding, a multi-head attention mechanism, and a fully connected layer. The multi-head attention and feedforward network are embedded within residual structures, with layer normalization applied to feature vectors after data fusion. The right side comprises N decoders. Each decoder receives inputs from the corresponding encoder outputs, forming an embedded architecture similar in composition to the encoder. The final output is generated through a linear projection and activation functions. The basic structure of the Transformer is shown in Fig. 2.

Fault maintenance knowledge graph

A knowledge graph is a graph-based data structure composed of entities, relationships, and attributes, designed to formally describe concepts, objects, events, and their interrelationships in the physical world45. In industrial contexts, a fault maintenance knowledge graph is a domain-specific knowledge model that utilizes a graph structure to formally represent equipment composition, fault symptoms, root causes, detection methods, maintenance measures, and the complex interconnections among them. Essentially, it transforms domain expertise, equipment manual specifications, historical maintenance case data, and physical principles into a structured knowledge network that is interpretable and queryable by computers while also supporting inference. Figure 3 illustrates the construction process of a fault maintenance knowledge graph.

Fig. 3The construction process of the knowledge graph.

The construction process of the knowledge graph.

First, technical documents, maintenance logs, operation manuals, and online data are collected to form the knowledge base, constituting unstructured knowledge at this stage. Entity recognition algorithms are then applied to extract entities from the unstructured data, followed by entity classification. Through the construction of an ontology model, triplets with distinct entity types are generated. Each triplet consists of two entities and their relationship, where fault entities are linked to maintenance process entities. All triplets ultimately form a structured database, which can subsequently be accessed via visualization software or an LLM to query the knowledge graph, thereby enabling maintenance decision-making following fault diagnosis.

Method

Overall algorithm framework

In response to the limitations of conventional CNN and Transformer-based motor fault diagnosis methods, such as weak generalization capability, high computational cost, and overfitting to multi-class faults, this paper proposes a novel DCCNN-LTransformer. The proposed method is designed to adapt to motor fault classification across different datasets. The overall architecture of the algorithm is illustrated in Fig. 4.

Fig. 4Overall algorithm framework.

Overall algorithm framework.

As shown in Fig. 4, the DCCNN is employed for enhanced feature extraction. Given the high similarity among different motor fault signals, feature identification is necessary before processing through the Transformer network, enabling the extraction of positional encodings and corresponding output signal characteristics. This study improves upon the original 1DCNN structure by incorporating weighted processing and implementing dual-channel network feature extraction. A probabilistic feature fusion-based weighting function is proposed, where the superior network channel integrates its features into another weighted network through feature fusion. The output feature signals are then embedded via positional encoding.

In the LTransformer component, lightweight processing is applied to adaptive positional encoding matching. A novel enhanced dynamic window mixed attention mechanism is introduced to locally amplify highly similar motor fault features, thereby improving the local discriminability among them. Through Add and Norm operations, the processed features are fed into the feedforward network, and finally passed to the LTransformer classifier via linear and max-pooling operations to complete the motor fault diagnosis process. This lightweight structure significantly enhances compatibility with hardware devices.

The BERT-BiLSTM-CRF algorithm is employed for entity recognition, enabling the classification of entities from a large volume of motor fault phenomena and maintenance knowledge. The Bidirectional Encoder Representation from Transformers (BERT) large language model is utilized for text preprocessing, generating sub-vectors of motor maintenance texts to extract key textual features, and outputting feature-enhanced sub-vectors. The Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) algorithm then processes contextual information to perform entity recognition, transforming the sequential sequence labeling task into a recognition task. The Conditional Random Field (CRF) layer processes the output from BiLSTM, leveraging a state transition matrix to derive the optimal global sequence based on adjacent labels. Through this entity recognition algorithm, fault types and maintenance recommendations, among other entities, can be extracted from maintenance text within the documents.

Probabilistic feature fusion convolutional neural network

The proposed method enhances the original 1DCNN structure by employing two 1DCNNs as dual-channel weighted networks. An initial weighting function is established, and a probabilistic feature fusion strategy dynamically adjusts the weights, enabling feature integration through probabilistic fusion between the two 1DCNNs to form the novel DCCNN architecture.

Probabilistic feature fusion strategy

This strategy processes vibration signals through dual 1DCNN networks, adaptively adjusting fusion weights to extract and combine features from both branches. It selectively retains critical fault-related information while probabilistically preserving potential fault discriminative features, thereby optimizing the feature extraction process. The workflow of the probabilistic feature fusion strategy is illustrated in Fig. 5.

Fig. 5Probabilistic feature fusion process.

Probabilistic feature fusion process.

As shown in Fig. 5, a sliding window function is used to perform feature extraction on vibration signals. Two different weighted networks are defined as 1DCNN-1 and 1DCNN-2, both with initial weightsset to 0.5. A probabilistic fusion function is used to weight and update the weights. When, the features of 1DCNN-1 are fused with those of 1DCNN-2; otherwise, the features of 1DCNN-2 are fused with those of 1DCNN-1. The update principle is to keep the current features unchanged and perform probabilistic feature fusion with the features of the other channel. To avoid network imbalance caused by weight updating, the strategy employs is to perform feature processing using the feature probability fusion mechanism in the early stage of training, while using a decay function in the later stage to ensure the stability and convergence of feature recognition. Dual-channel CNN Network based on probabilistic feature fusion

Based on the probabilistic feature fusion mechanism, a dual-channel 1DCNN architecture is constructed. As the 1DCNN network depth increases, it becomes susceptible to gradient vanishing and explosion problems. Local convolution operations struggle to model long-range dependencies in signals, necessitating reliance on subsequent fully connected layers for global information integration. The DCCNN structure employs two 1DCNNs with dynamic weights and achieves bidirectional feature extraction through mutual feature fusion. Lightweight transformer-based fault diagnosis classification method

Adaptive embedded positional encoding

In neural network frameworks for time series prediction, RNN and LSTM algorithms can only proceed to the next time step after obtaining a hidden state and converting it into an input for the next step. In contrast, the Transformer prioritizes parallel processing, enabling simultaneous computation of feature vectors. As a result, the model cannot inherently obtain positional information for each element when processing sequence data. In the field of fault diagnosis, the temporal information of vibration signals and sensor readings affects the efficiency of online fault monitoring. Therefore, positional encoding is required to provide unique feature vector representations of current feature information in the time series, thereby capturing positional information. Positional encoding and token embeddings share the same dimensional space, enabling their superposition with feature vectors. This process uses sine and cosine functions of different frequencies, with the expressions as follows:

Enhanced dynamic window-hybrid attention

Traditional multi-head attention suffers from high computational complexity in global attention calculation, leading to significant memory usage and slower Transformer training. Motor fault features are often concentrated within local windows, where global attention may dilute critical local patterns. This section proposes an enhanced dynamic window mixed attention mechanism to address the issues of computational overhead and local feature loss in Transformer-based networks. The attention mechanism functions as a plug-and-play module in network learning, characterized by a small parameter count. It addresses information filtering in neural network learning by processing numerous input vectors and feature information to identify key information while reducing focus on less relevant details. This improves optimization efficiency and training accuracy, particularly in complex models. Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) employs multiple self-attention mechanisms on input sequences, using key-value pairs of dimension dpos for linear input. The basic structure of the attention mechanism is shown in Fig. 7.

An optional mask is applied to enable the softmax activation function to derive the current weights, which are then applied to the Value vectors to produce the output. Where, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{head}}_{i}$$\end{document} represents the computation result of the i-th attention head, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$W_{i}^{Q}$$\end{document} denotes the weight matrix for Q; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$W_{i}^{K}$$\end{document} denotes the weight matrix for K; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$W_{i}^{V}$$\end{document} denotes the weight matrix for V; and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{Concat}}\left( \cdot \right)$$\end{document} denotes the concatenation operation.

Figure 7(b) illustrates the EDWHA model proposed in this paper. It identifies fault features through different local characteristics. The front-end DCCNN provides positional encoding with focused feature information, using position points as references for local points. The length of the dynamic window is determined as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$w_{t}$$\end{document}. During the global attention phase, the base window expands. During the local attention phase, the base window shrinks. The formula for \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$w_{t}$$\end{document} is defined as:

10\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$w_{t} = w_{{\min }} + w_{b} \left( {1 - \frac{{\left| {t - c} \right|}}{2}} \right)$$\end{document}

Where, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$w_{{\min }}$$\end{document} is the minimum window length, used to prevent feature distortion caused by excessively short local signals. \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$w_{b}$$\end{document} is the base window length. When the sliding window is at the boundary of the sample, the local feature attention points start from t. When the length of the focused features within the local area is insufficient for the window length, global feature selection is performed, and the non-focused global features are used to complete the window. For the N-th EDWHA module, the sparse attention mask is defined as:

11\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$A_{{t,j}} = \left\{ {\begin{array}{*{20}l} {\frac{{\exp \left( {Q_{t} K_{j}^{T} /\sqrt {d_{k} } } \right)}}{{\sum\nolimits_{{j^{\prime} \in N_{t} }} {\exp \left( {Q_{t} K_{{j^{\prime}}}^{T} /\sqrt {d_{k} } } \right)} }},} \hfill & {{\text{when }}\left| {t - j} \right| \le w_{t} /2} \hfill \\ {0,} \hfill & {{\mathrm{otherwise}}} \hfill \\ \end{array} } \right.$$\end{document}

Where, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N_{t} = \left\{ {j|\left| {t - j} \right| \le w_{t} /2} \right\}$$\end{document} represents the dynamic neighborhood. When the lengths of the collected samples vary, different local features are extracted, and the output is expressed as:

12\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\text{Local Attention}}(Q,K,V) = Concat\left( {A_{{t,j}} ,V} \right)$$\end{document}

When the extracted local features do not reach the specified sequence length, the global attention mechanism is used for completion. This performs feature fusion operations, leveraging the search capability of the global attention mechanism to extract potential fault-identifying information from feature segments. The final expression for EDWHA is given by:

13\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{gathered} {\mathrm{EDWHA}}\left( {Q,K,V} \right) = a \cdot {\mathrm{Concat}}\left( {{\mathrm{Attention}}\left( {Q_{t} ,K_{j} ,V} \right)} \right) \hfill \\ {\text{ }} + \left( {1 - a} \right){\mathrm{Concat}}\left( {{\text{Local Attention}}\left( {Q_{t} ,K_{j} ,V} \right)} \right) \hfill \\ \end{gathered}$$\end{document}

Layer Normalization (LN) performs normalization operations on the activations of each layer to stabilize their statistical characteristics, prevent gradient explosion, and ensure effective gradient flow during backpropagation. It computes the mean and variance of each layer’s output and uses these statistics to normalize the output to have zero mean and unit variance. The expression is as follows:

14\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$LN\left( X \right) = \frac{\omega }{\sigma } \otimes \left( {X - \mu } \right) + b$$\end{document}

Where, ω represents the trained network weights, μ and σ are the mean and variance of the data sample, respectively. The normalized output is obtained as:

15\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Y = LN\left( {X + EDWHA\left( X \right)} \right)$$\end{document}

When abnormal vibration occurs at a certain position, the overall statistical magnitude increases, making the model more reliant on prominent local features. This process eliminates a large number of redundant and similar features, thereby achieving effective extraction and identification of fault information. This attention mechanism simultaneously focuses on both global and local attention, enabling the model to attend to both the overall vibration signal and its locally prominent features.

Entity recognition based on the BERT-BiLSTM-CRF algorithm

BERT-BiLSTM-CRF is a deep learning model architecture designed for sequence labeling tasks. It employs BERT as a pre-trained contextual encoder to provide deep, bidirectional semantic representations of words or characters. BiLSTM further captures long-range bidirectional dependencies among characters or words within a sentence. CRF serves as a label decoder, incorporating transition constraints between labels during final prediction to ensure global optimality of the label sequence. For instance, in named entity recognition, the sequential relationships in the text can be constrained to guarantee the global coherence of the predicted labels. Ultimately, the model assigns distinct entity label information to different text segments. The structure of the BERT-BiLSTM-CRF algorithm is illustrated in Fig. 9.

Fig. 9BERT-BiLSTM-CRF algorithm.

BERT-BiLSTM-CRF algorithm.

In the BERT layer, the input vector \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$X = \left\{ {X_{1} ,X_{2} ,X_{3} , \ldots ,X_{n} } \right\}$$\end{document} consists of token embeddings, segment embeddings, and position embeddings. The special token [CLS] is placed at the beginning of the sequence, and its final output serves as the aggregated representation of the entire sequence. [SEP] is used to separate two sentences. The output vector of the special token [CLS] is employed as the representation of the whole sequence \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C = \left\{ {C_{1} ,C_{2} ,C_{3} , \ldots ,C_{n} } \right\}$$\end{document}. The BERT layer generates a contextually rich semantic vector representation for each token in the input text.

In the BiLSTM layer, scores for all entity labels are computed for each position. The input is \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C_{n}$$\end{document}, processed through a bidirectional LSTM neural network to obtain label classification scores for each text position. The LSTM operation formulas are as follows:

16\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left\{ \begin{gathered} f_{n} = {\mathrm{sigmoid}}(W_{f} C_{n} + U_{f} S_{{n - 1}} + b_{f} ) \hfill \\ i_{n} = {\mathrm{sigmoid}}(W_{i} C_{n} + U_{i} S_{{n - 1}} + b_{i} ) \hfill \\ o_{n} = {\mathrm{sigmoid}}(W_{o} C_{n} + U_{o} S_{{n - 1}} + b_{o} ) \hfill \\ \widetilde{{c_{n} }} = \tanh (W_{c} C_{n} + U_{c} S_{{n - 1}} + b_{c} ) \hfill \\ c_{n} = i_{n} *\widetilde{{c_{n} }} + f_{n} *c_{{n - 1}} \hfill \\ \overrightarrow {{S_{n} }} = o_{n} *f_{n} c_{{n - 1}} \hfill \\ S_{n} = [\overrightarrow {{S_{n} }} ,\overleftarrow {{S_{n} }} ] \hfill \\ \end{gathered} \right.$$\end{document}

Where, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$W_{f} ,W_{i} ,W_{o}$$\end{document} represents the network weight matrices; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$b_{f} ,b_{i} ,b_{o} ,b_{c}$$\end{document} are the bias vectors for the forget gate, input gate, output gate, and candidate state, respectively. In the BiLSTM architecture, the forward and backward layers operate independently, capturing dependencies from both past and future contexts. This design enables the modeling of bidirectional contextual information, significantly enhancing the model’s ability to understand and represent global semantics.

The CRF layer receives the hidden state sequence output by BiLSTM and, based on a trained label transition matrix, calculates the joint score \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S_{n}$$\end{document} for the entire sequence. Each \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S_{n}$$\end{document} is mapped to the label space through a fully connected layer, generating scores for all possible labels at each position. This process enables the extraction of various entities from large volumes of maintenance text information. The CRF layer models the conditional probability of the entire sequence, with its expression given as:

17\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P\left( {y|x} \right) = \frac{{\exp \left( {S\left( y \right)} \right)}}{{\sum\nolimits_{{y^{\prime} \in Y}} {\exp \left( {S\left( {y^{\prime}} \right)} \right)} }}$$\end{document}

Where, Y represents the set of all possible label sequences.

Construction of fault knowledge graph

After obtaining entity labels through the BERT-BiLSTM-CRF algorithm, the ontology model of the knowledge graph needs to be constructed to form the fault knowledge graph. The process of building the fault knowledge graph is illustrated in Fig. 10. First, an annotated fault text sentence is collected. In each text statement containing an entity, the beginning of the entity is labeled as “B”, and the remaining parts are labeled as “I”. Non-entity parts are labeled as “O”. The text annotated with BIO tags is used for training. The BERT-BiLSTM-CRF algorithm is employed to identify entities in large amounts of unlabeled text. The construction of the ontology model involves three types of entities: fault types, fault descriptions, and fault maintenance, which together support the maintenance decision-making process. Through entity relationships, triples are generated, thereby constructing a knowledge graph for maintenance decision-making.

An accelerometer measured vibration data, while an encoder measured rotational speed data. A total of 36 datasets were available. Evaluation metrics

Evaluation metrics are established, using accuracy and loss function to determine the iterative convergence of the algorithm during training. The formulas for accuracy and cross-entropy loss function are as follows:

18\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{Accuracy}} = \frac{{TP + TN}}{{FP + FN + TP + TN}}$$\end{document}

19\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{Loss}} = - \frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{{i = 1}}^{N} {\sum\limits_{{c = 1}}^{C} {y_{{i,c}} \log \left( {p_{{i,c}} } \right)} }$$\end{document}

Where, FP,FN,TP,TN denote false positive, false negative, true positive, and true negative, respectively; N is the number of training iterations; C is the number of fault categories; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$y_{{i,c}}$$\end{document} is the true label of the i-th sample; and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$p_{{i,c}}$$\end{document} is the predicted probability that sample i belongs to category c. The algorithm uses t-SNE visualization, clustering, and a confusion matrix to evaluate the fault classification performance.

Conclusion

This study integrates a deep learning-based fault diagnosis algorithm with an LLM embedded with knowledge graphs for maintenance decision-making, thereby achieving a complete closed-loop process from motor fault diagnosis to maintenance decisions. Fault diagnosis is performed using the DCCNN-LTransformer algorithm to identify fault types, which are then converted from text input into decision texts applicable to motor maintenance. This approach implements an operation and maintenance process that converts heterogeneous data from one-dimensional signals into text. The proposed method incorporates a dual-channel structural enhancement, adaptive positional encoding, and an enhanced dynamic window hybrid attention mechanism. By modifying the fixed architectures of CNNs and Transformers, we develop a fault-diagnosis network capable of multi-feature fusion and lightweight processing. This approach demonstrates strong recognition performance in both multi-condition datasets with similar faults and multi-class fault datasets. Compared with the original algorithm, the proposed method improves fault-diagnosis accuracy by 2.72% while enhancing inference efficiency and reducing training time. The model exhibits strong generalization, enabling fault classification and identification across multiple fault-diagnosis datasets. During the fault maintenance decision-making phase, the BERT-BiLSTM-CRF algorithm was employed to extract various fault entities from multiple textual maintenance documents, thereby constructing a fault knowledge graph. An LLM was introduced to facilitate search keywords and decision-making regarding maintenance. Finally, a visualization interface for motor fault diagnosis and maintenance decision-making was developed, thereby enabling an integrated operational workflow.

Currently, the proposed DCCNN-LTransformer algorithm, which relies on deep learning, plays a significant role in motor fault diagnosis in industrial applications. This method can rapidly identify early fault characteristics across different fault conditions, thereby reducing equipment failure rates and improving fault identification efficiency. It also provides valuable insights for CNN-Transformer-based fault diagnosis approaches. However, some limitations remain in future work. For instance, the knowledge graph currently contains only a subset of fault-maintenance texts related to the specific motor and requires extensive data supplementation. Therefore, future efforts should focus on real-time motor fault monitoring and end-to-end neural network learning, integrating data acquisition devices into industrial equipment. Constructing motor fault datasets under various operating conditions and exploring multi-condition diagnosis based on transfer learning will be key research directions. By employing graph-structure optimization algorithms, an LLM-driven intelligent operation and maintenance platform can be established. This platform enables real-time identification of fault types and facilitates automated decision-making.

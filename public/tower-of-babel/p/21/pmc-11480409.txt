Investigating the determinants of homestay satisfaction on Airbnb using multiple techniques
Authors: Du Xishihui, Sun Huifeng, Wang Zhaoguo, Sun Lishuang, Shao Qianqian
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Peer-to-peer accommodation has gained prominence in the sharing economy and e-commerce sectors, with big data playing a crucial role in understanding customer preferences and evaluating homestay satisfaction. This study proposes a novel methodology that integrates Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques, a Random Forest model, and Geographic Information System (GIS) functionalities to quantify the complex relationship between homestay satisfaction and diverse customer preferences. Notably, this study addresses the positive bias inherent in listing scores by segmenting homestays into three categories (satisfactory, moderate, and dissatisfactory) based on sentiment analysis from online reviews. Furthermore, this study not only identifies eight key determinants of homestay satisfaction but also unveils the nonlinear relationships and interactions between them. More significantly, we identify specific threshold values for geographic determinants, offering actionable recommendations for homestay planning and layout. These findings provide valuable insights that can be leveraged to improve homestay experiences and promote the sustainable development of urban homestays.

Introduction

In recent years, the sharing economy has emerged as a promising global economic model, reshaping economic activities, optimizing resource utilization, and driving transformative changes1,2. Notably, Airbnb has emerged as a leading sharing-economy platform, connecting hosts and tourists3. Airbnb has experienced rapid expansion, facilitating connections between over 1.4 billion travelers and 4 million hosts in approximately 220 countries and regions4.

Given the considerable popularity and impact of Airbnb in the tourism sector, there is a necessity to conduct comprehensive research on the factors influencing user behavior and experiences (i.e., customer preferences). Previous studies primarily relied on conventionally qualitative and quantitative methods, such as questionnaires and in-depth interviews, to elucidate the factors and dimensions impacting homestay satisfaction5,6. Nonetheless, this method suffers from drawbacks such as the high cost of data acquisition and the inherently subjective nature of the results7. To facilitate the study of customer experiences in a more accessible and precise manner, scholars have turned their attention to online reviews, a form of user-generated content (UGC)8. Online reviews not only enhance trust between platforms and potential customers but also influence consumer purchase decisions9,10. Undoubtedly, it is pertinent for scholars to optimize the market structure of the tourism and accommodation industry by extracting the deeper insights from online reviews. As a result, natural language processing (NLP) techniques are commonly utilized on online reviews to identify topics and elucidate customer experiences11–13. Topic modeling techniques, including Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA), Latent Semantic Analysis (LSA), and Structural Topic Modeling (STM), are commonly used to extract topics related to customer preferences. While LDA remains a popular choice, its application to short text data is hindered by data sparsity and its reliance on a document-topic matrix, potentially compromising topic identification accuracy14. Topics in texts can be extracted from various co-occurrence relations, represented as a series of related concepts15. By capturing the co-occurrence of concepts across different reviews, Semantic Network Analysis (SNA) circumvents the challenges inherent in traditional topic modeling methods when applied to short texts. Given the short-text nature of Airbnb reviews, SNA inspires us to extract latent topics indicative of homestay satisfaction and identify its determinants.

Online reviews and listing scores are important tools for evaluating consumer satisfaction16. By analyzing both the content of reviews and the scores given, it is possible to identify determinants that influence consumer behavior and experience. A significant relationship exists between the sentiments expressed in online reviews and listing scores, aiding in the identification of the true satisfaction level of homestay guests17. Tourism researchers have consistently noted a positivity bias in listing scores, which describe the anti-reality quality of homestay accommodations18–20, while sentiment analysis has emerged as a valuable tool to mitigate potential bias in listing scores21. Therefore, this study employs sentiment analysis to categorize homestays into distinct segments based on an integration of online reviews and listing scores.

Homestay satisfaction is contingent upon a multitude of factors, including service quality aspects such as accommodation conditions, facilities, and host-guest interaction22. Furthermore, geographic factors, specifically transportation accessibility, have also been established as critical determinants23. While previous research has focused on quantifying the relative importance of these factors, understanding the nonlinear effects of influencing factors is essential for clarifying the underlying mechanism of homestay satisfaction. The Random Forest (RF) model, a powerful tool for modeling complex nonlinear relationships, has gained popularity in its ability to handle high-dimensional data and effectively analyze both service and geographic elements24. RF can assess the significance of independent variables on dependent variables, coupled with its ability to explore nonlinear relationships through partial dependence plots, makes it a suitable method for investigating the determinants of homestay satisfaction and their complex interrelationships.

Beijing is a renowned tourist destination and the capital of China. As of October 2020, Beijing had a total of 699 boutique homestays, comprising 8211 rooms with a daily capacity to accommodate up to 17,000 guests25. Regarding geographical distribution, urban areas had a significantly higher number of homestays compared to suburban areas. Chaoyang, Haidian, and Dongcheng districts had the highest number of homestay listings, followed by Fengtai, Xicheng, and Tongzhou districts, among others. Thus, for this study, Airbnb data from the Fifth Ring Road of Beijing served as the focal point. This research aims to achieve the following objectives: (1) Extracting topics and factors indicative of customer preferences from online reviews. (2) Segmenting homestays based on a combination of sentiment polarities and listing scores. (3) Deconstructing homestay satisfaction with the technical support of RF.

The structure of this research is outlined as follows: Following the introduction, Sect “Literature review” summarizes the state of the research of homestay satisfaction. Sect “Study area and data” describes the data and study area utilized in this study. Sect “Methodology” elaborates on the research methods. Sect “Results” employs NLP techniques to identify influencing factors and segment homestays. On this basis, this section applies a RF model to analyze the determinants of homestay satisfaction and explores the nonlinear relationships between them. Sect “Discussion and conclusions” discusses potential implications for homestay planning within the study area, summarizes the key contributions of this research, and proposes future research directions.

Literature review

Customer satisfaction is conceptualized as an individual’s subjective evaluation of a service or product, informed by expectations or real performance26. As a core research area within tourism and hospitality, customer satisfaction has been extensively studied. Homestays, as a form of shared accommodation offering immersive cultural experiences and cost savings, have gained significant popularity among tourists. To maintain market competitiveness amidst intense competition, homestays must prioritize understanding customer satisfaction. Previous research has largely recognized the value of user ratings in the accommodation industry, employing listing scores as a proxy for homestay satisfaction27. However, these studies have overlooked the potential bias inherent in high ratings, which can obscure true customer sentiment. Online reviews offer a rich source of data for understanding customer satisfaction. By extracting positive and negative sentiments from review content, researchers can effectively measure customer satisfaction levels28. Sentiment analysis, employing both supervised and unsupervised learning methods, offers a significant advantage over manual coding techniques in efficiently analyzing large volumes of review data29. Numerous studies have successfully employed sentiment analysis to extract customer sentiment. For instance, sentiment analysis has revealed strong positive and negative sentiment polarities related to service quality and epidemic prevention measures30, indicating their considerable impact on overall satisfaction. For Airbnb, customers expressed the strongest positive sentiments regarding the host, while negative sentiments were associated with noise, floor, shower, parking, and door-related concepts31. Additionally, concepts related to the neighborhood environment were found to elicit predominantly positive sentiments and relatively fewer negative sentiments, as per the aforementioned study32. In conclusion, sentiment analysis provides a robust method for assessing customer satisfaction by distinguishing between positive and negative sentiment expressed in review data.

Customers typically assess homestay satisfaction holistically rather than focusing on individual characteristics. Identifying the specific determinants influencing homestay satisfaction is crucial for developing effective strategies to enhance service quality and inform targeted marketing initiatives. Online reviews serve as a valuable resource for understanding customer satisfaction, providing insights into the specific determinants driving overall perceptions33. Traditional topic modeling techniques have been employed to extract these factors from reviews data, categorizing them into coherent themes34. Recent advancements in NLP have expanded the range of identifiable factors to encompass various aspects such as cleanliness, location, security, amenities, and breakfast35,36, which can be classified into service and geographic dimensions. The significance of these factors on homestay satisfaction has been widely acknowledged, with research primarily relying on user rating of each homestay characteristic to quantify their impact37. However, this approach overlooks the spatial pattern of geographic factors. Geographic Information Systems (GIS) provides a robust framework for incorporating geographic perspectives to elucidate the spatial characteristics of homestays that influence customer satisfaction.

While the influence of various factors on homestay satisfaction has been widely acknowledged, their interrelationships still remain further exploration. Quantitative approaches, such as Important-Performance Analysis (IPA)38 and TOPSIS39 have been applied to assess the relative importance of individual factors influencing customer satisfaction. Furthermore, researchers generally assume linear relationships and utilize regression analysis to explore the underlying mechanism of customer satisfaction. To account for spatial heterogeneity, Geographically Weighted Regression (GWR) has emerged as a valuable tool40,41. However, the complex, nonlinear nature of these relationships necessitates more sophisticated modeling techniques. Machine learning (ML), particularly RF, offers a promising approach due to its ability to handle complex interactions while maintaining interpretability42. RF can assess the relative importance of variables and examine their nonlinear relationships, providing a deeper understanding of determinants influencing homestay satisfaction.

Study area and data

This study centers on the Fifth Ring Road of Beijing, comprising Dongcheng, Xicheng, Chaoyang, Fengtai, Shijingshan, and Haidian districts, covering an approximate area of 667 km2 (Fig. 1). Being the capital of China and a globally renowned city, Beijing experiences a high influx of tourists and a significant concentration of homestays. The Airbnb website lists a total of 6222 homestays within the study area, with a notable concentration of homestays in Chaoyang district, characterized by two distinct density clusters (Fig. 1).

Data of homestays were obtained from “Inside Airbnb” (http://insideairbnb.com/). Both listing data, providing basic information about homestays, and online reviews written by customers were collected. To identify topics related to homestay satisfaction in the Chinese context, the reviews were filtered to include only those written in Chinese. Additionally, listings with missing data and reviews containing vague descriptions such as “good”, “very good”, and “not so good”, as well as punctuation and emoticons, were excluded. Subsequently, the review data underwent normalization using text normalization techniques. This study collected a total of 6222 listing data and 92,412 online reviews up to 2021.

To represent and analyze geographic factors, this study utilized GIS technology to process specific spatial data acquired in 2021, including points of interest (POIs) and road network data. These data were obtained from Amap (http://ditu.amap.com/), a leading mapping service product by Alibaba. As detailed in Table 1, the POI data encompasses the geographic coordinates of various traffic infrastructures and urban functional facilities.

Methodology

As depict in Fig. 2, a comprehensive methodological framework was developed to investigate the underlying mechanisms driving homestay satisfaction formation and evolution. Step one involved employing NLP techniques to identify key factors influencing customer satisfaction. Subsequently, sentiment analysis was conducted to ascertain sentiment polarities and segment homestays based on integrated listing scores. Lastly, an RF model and GIS were developed to analyze the determinants and their impact on homestay satisfaction.

Step 1: topics extraction

Utilizing a large sample of selected online reviews, the primary objective of this study is to effectively extract potential topics influencing homestay satisfaction. Semantic analysis focuses on extracting crucial information from large volumes of unstructured data, including social media posts and online reviews, allowing scholars to analyze various words and sentences as structural elements43,44. Online reviews exhibit contextual simplicity and semantic complexity. Semantic network analysis (SNA), a language analysis reflecting the topological property of complex networks, is a more suitable technique due to its word co-occurrence measurement for short online reviews45,46.

Step 2: sentiment polarities calculation

Considering the potential bias in listing scores towards excessively positive ratings, it becomes crucial to capture specific sentiment polarities within reviews to accurately assess homestay satisfaction. Therefore, sentiment analysis plays a vital role in assisting the segmentation of homestays. In order to detect polarities, a lexicon-based technique was utilized to compute sentiment polarities. This technique demonstrates a high suitability for conducting sentiment analysis at both the sentence and factor levels49.

However, it is important to acknowledge the primary limitation of the lexicon-based technique, namely, its high domain specificity50. As a result, a domain-specific lexicon specifically designed for homestay reviews was developed to address this limitation. The main steps are as follows: (1) The basic sentiment lexicon was structured and regular, based on the affective lexicon ontology of Dalian University of Technology51 and the BosonNLP lexicon52. (2) The jieba corpus was expanded on Python to accurately identify concepts related to the tourism and accommodation industries. (3) Negatives and degree adverbs were included in the modifier lexicon, as modifiers can influence the sentiment values of a review. (4) Finally, Pandas and jieba in Python were utilized to segment a review into concepts, individually matching the constructed lexicons. Step 3: model construction

Random Forest

The Random Forest (RF) algorithm, proposed by Breiman in 200153, is a type of supervised machine learning algorithm. Based on bagging or bootstrap aggregation, RF classifier comprises ensemble trees, increasing the diversity of these trees by training them on multiple sub-training datasets collected from the input training data during the training stage of RF54. Thus, RF is a suitable model for studying multidimensional data without the need for dimensionality reduction. In terms of data structures, RF can handle both continuous and discrete data without requiring data normalization. Given these advantages, we argue that it is feasible to quantify the complex impacts of customer preferences on homestay satisfaction using RF.

Gain-ratio, Chi-square, and Gini Index are commonly selected as attribute selection measures that maximize dissimilarity between classes for tree design55. RF typically employs the Gini Index to measure the best-split selection for each tree, calculating the impurity of a given feature with respect to other classes. Model performance evaluation

Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) analysis was employed to evaluate the performance of the classifier. The ROC curve is associated with the trade-off between true positive rate (TPR) and false positive rate (FPR). The area under the curve (AUC) reflects the classifier’s ability to avoid false classification. A superior classifier should have an FPR close to 0 and TPR close to 1, meaning it has an AUC close to 1. Specifically, ROC curves and Area Under the Curve (AUC) for multiple class classification problems were obtained using the micro-average method58,59.

Results

Topics and factors identification

To explore relationships between key topics and concepts from Airbnb online reviews, content analysis based on jieba was conducted to extract concepts. The Word Cloud Map (Fig. 3) presents frequency statistics for concepts and reveals the focus of online reviews. The size of the concepts correlated with their frequency.

Fig. 3Word cloud map of the concepts.

Word cloud map of the concepts.

To essentially uncovering topics of customer preferences, SNA was applied to detect relationships between clusters and concepts from online reviews. PMI values were calculated for the top 200 concepts, and a subsequent SNA map was constructed (Fig. 4). As shown in Fig. 4, this network visualization represented concepts as nodes, with the strength of their relationships indicated by PMI values. Employing the Louvain community detection algorithm, the SNA map was clustered into eight topics, visually differentiated by color. Associated concepts within each topic included location, traffic, communication, amenities, room type, room policy, price, and cleaning. In the topic of amenities, larger nodes such as refrigerator, living room, kitchen, and hot water were the dominant concepts. Consequently, other concepts highly correlated with dominant concepts described the practicality of amenities in a homestay. In the cleaning topic, bedding, supplies, and wash were dominant concepts and exhibited significant co-occurrence with concepts belonging to other topics, indicating that cleaning was frequently mentioned in online reviews. These topics, categorized into service and geographic dimensions, represent key factors influencing homestay satisfaction. Homestay segmentation based on sentiment polarities

To achieve homestay segmentation, sentiment analysis was conducted on 92,412 review records to calculate sentiment polarities. As depicted in Fig. 5, a distinct sentiment pattern emerged. Listings with perfect scores (100) exhibited exclusively positive sentiment, while those ranging from 99 to 90 predominantly expressed positive sentiment with a minority of negative opinions. Conversely, listings scoring between 89 and 20 demonstrated predominantly negative sentiment polarities.

Furthermore, we conducted additional investigation into the textual characteristics, focusing on the frequency of modifiers, nouns, and verbs in review texts within the three listing score intervals. We identified sentiment modifiers that occurred frequently in reviews with 100 listing scores. Guests frequently used words like “cleaning”, “convenient”, “comfortable”, “warm”, and “recommend” to praise the cleanliness and location of the property. In contrast, reviews with 99 − 90 listing scores showed a substantial decrease in the frequency of intensifying modifiers. Words like “not too bad”, “good”, and “nice” did not express negative evaluations and lack emphatically positive sentiment. Regarding reviews with 89 − 20 listing scores, it was evident that the reviews convey some negative sentiment, with a high frequency of categorically negative words like “no”, “bad”, and “unable”. Specifically, negative sentiment words such as “cockroaches”, “broken”, and “cold”, indicating that customers who stayed in homestays with 89 − 20 listing scores had terrible accommodation experiences, with problems related to cleanliness and room amenities. In summary, Airbnb online reviews displayed three prominent tendencies across various listing score intervals. Firstly, for homestays with a perfect score of 100, all reviews exhibit positive sentiment polarities, frequently mentioning high sentiment modifiers and positive words. Secondly, in the case of homestays within the 99 − 90 listing score range, reviews exhibit partially negative sentiment. Upon examining their textual characteristics, it was observed that these reviews neither explicitly mention complaints nor employ overtly positive language. Thirdly, for homestays rated between 89 and 20, negative sentiment polarities were predominant in the reviews. Furthermore, typical negative language was discernible in the commentary related to the accommodation experiences associated with these homestays. Quantitative analysis of influencing factors

Performance of Random Forest classifier

This study employed the GridSearchCV from the Scikit-Learn to optimize the hyperparameters of a Random Forest (RF) classifier. The grid search explored a range of parameter values, including the number of estimators (1–600) and the maximum number of features (1–9). Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves were utilized to evaluate the performance of different model configurations and select the one with the highest classification accuracy. Figure 6 presents the ROC curve and corresponding AUC for the RF classifier configured with 400 estimators and a maximum of three features. The RF classifier achieved AUC values of 0.80, 0.76, and 0.91 for satisfactory, moderate, and dissatisfactory homestays, respectively, demonstrating good overall performance in efficiently classifying homestays.

Complex relationship between determinants and satisfaction

The partial dependence plot (PDP) method was employed to visually represent the marginal effect of each factor on homestay satisfaction within the “satisfactory” segment (Segment 0) (Fig. 8). In the service dimension, satisfaction demonstrated a sharp rise when cleaning (Fig. 8a), communication (Fig. 8b), and location (Fig. 8j) scores increased from 9 to 10. Interestingly, the satisfaction scores exhibited fluctuating trends when these scores fell below 9. Price (Fig. 8c) and amenities (Fig. 8i) displayed a non-linear relationship with satisfaction, characterized by peaks and troughs. Satisfaction for price initially increased then decreased with rising costs, reaching a peak at approximately 950 yuan. Conversely, the trend for amenities was the opposite of price. Satisfaction initially declined with an increasing number of amenities, reaching a low point at 32 amenities. The geographic factors exhibited with notable non-linear relationships with satisfaction. The impact curves for Distance to Metro Station (DMS) (Fig. 8d) and Distance to Bus Stop (DBS) (Fig. 8g) followed a similar pattern, with satisfaction initially increasing dramatically and then stabilizing. However, a significant rise in satisfaction occurred when DMS exceeded 1000 m. The impact of Accessibility to Attractions (AA) (Fig. 8f) exhibited two distinct trends. When AA was low (less than 24), satisfaction rose rapidly. However, the strengthening effect slowed at higher AA values (greater than 24). Conversely, the impact curve for Average Distance (AD) (Fig. 8h) displayed a decrease followed by an increase within the range of 10 to 24 units, before a rapid drop beyond 24 units. Finally, the marginal effect of POI diversity (Fig. 8f) on homestay satisfaction exhibited a clear inverted U-shaped pattern, peaking when diversity reached a value of 2.8. Certainly, threshold values for AD and POI diversity were tentatively suggested within the ranges of 2.60 to 3.10 and 24.96 to 30.62, respectively. However, due to the upward trend in the marginal curves, further investigation was necessary to determine definitive thresholds for the remaining geographic factors (DMS, AA, and DBS).

Previous study has demonstrated the presence of interactive effects on Airbnb pricing60. However, the significance of such interactions in determining homestay satisfaction remains under-explored. This study progressed by quantifying and visualizing the effects of PDP on two factors (Fig. 9). Notably, Distances to metro stations was the most important geographic factor, while the contributions of other geographic factors were numerically less significant. As depicted in Fig. 9, there were no significant changes in homestay satisfaction at DMS values below 500 m, irrespective of other geographic factors. In essence, DMS emerged as the primary factor influencing homestay satisfaction at low distances (< 500 m), particularly evident in the contour plots. The maximum single-factor contribution of DMS to satisfaction is 0.046 at 252.68 m (Fig. 9b). However, as DMS increased, POI diversity (Fig. 9a), AA (Fig. 9c), DBS (Fig. 9e), and AD (Fig. 9g) assumed more prominent roles. In the grid plots, the heat maps of satisfaction exhibited alternating changes, reflecting fluctuations in the marginal effect of DMS. Importantly, threshold values in the geographic dimension could be derived from the grid plots. In the contour plots, isochores associated with high satisfaction level, suggesting threshold values for DMS within the range of 1700 to 3600 m, AA between 45.00 and 47.81 min and DBS between 150 and 410 m.

Discussion and conclusion

Discussion

Theoretical implications

The primary objective of this study was to examine the internal mechanisms influencing homestay satisfaction. By doing so, it contributes to the realms of tourism management and sharing accommodation research, offering valuable insights into the intricate dynamics that shape the satisfaction levels of homestay experiences. In the initial phase of this investigation, SNA and PMI were leveraged for analyzing online reviews, owing to their inherent benefits in facilitating topic analysis of extensive textual data61. As a result, the examination of customer-generated content from the Beijing Airbnb platform yielded a total of eight captivating topics. Notably, the topics, highlighting the multidimensional nature, reinforce consistent observations documented in previous studies62–64.

The second phase addressed the challenge of imbalanced listing scores on Airbnb by differentiating genuine satisfaction and dissatisfaction within online reviews. Building upon the established link between review sentiment and homestay satisfaction65–67, this study proposed a novel approach that combines sentiment analysis with listing scores to segment homestays. This innovative approach allowed us to uncover the intricate relationship between customer preferences and their corresponding satisfaction categories.

Lastly, to gain a deeper understanding of the complex relationship between homestay satisfaction and its determinants, this study integrated RF and GIS techniques. The result revealed the critical role of cleanliness, aligning with prior research highlighting guest expectations for superior cleanliness in Airbnb homestays compared to hotels68. Additionally, documented challenges in maintaining cleanliness within homestays were acknowledged69. Recognizing the limitations of single factors in influencing satisfaction65,70–72, this study further examined interaction effects. The findings showed a comparable marginal effect of cleaning, communication, and location on satisfaction reaching a score of 9, suggesting their combined influence becomes particularly significant for achieving high satisfaction.

Practical implications

This study investigated the multifaceted determinants of homestay satisfaction. Location factors were identified as significant determinants of homestay satisfaction, aligning with previous research findings on the geographic dimension of customer preferences73,74. The findings suggested a preference for centrally located homestays with good access to necessities (lower AA increases satisfaction) while avoiding immediate proximity to attractions. Additionally, a balanced POI diversity was crucial, as both very high and very low diversity negatively impact satisfaction. Finally, optimal distances to public transport were identified (DMS: 1700–3600 m; DBS: 150–410 m), indicating that excessively close proximity is not ideal. By considering location factors such as POI and road network, platforms and hosts could strategically position homestays to align with customer preferences and optimize the overall guest experience.

This study utilized a RF model to elucidate the mechanisms underlying homestay satisfaction. In contrast to previous research, this study offered a more granular approach by identifying specific thresholds for its determinants, thereby providing refined strategies for homestay management and planning. The relationship between geographic factors and homestay satisfaction was characterized by non-linearity, as evidenced in Fig. 8d–h. By identifying specific geographic thresholds, this study explored the potential to optimize urban homestay distribution through effective spatial planning. An empirical study conducted in Beijing served as a practical illustration. Here, we employed a hexagonal grid system (n = 2684, area = 0.1524 km²) to segment the study area. Hexagonal grids offer distinct advantages in spatial representation and data comparison over traditional methods75. The grid size was specifically chosen to ensure sufficient granularity for the study area68. Leveraging non-linear effects and factor interactions (Fig. 10), we identified optimal geographic areas for future homestay development. In terms of transport convenience, the study area primarily exhibited planning areas with matching DMS values (Fig. 10a) in the southwestern part, followed by the northwestern part of Haidian District and the eastern fringe of Chaoyang District. A total of 1518 planning areas met the threshold values of DBS (Fig. 10b) throughout the study area, indicating superior convenience to public transportation within Beijing’s Fifth Ring Road. Regarding AA, suitable areas clustered around the peripheries of Haidian and Fengtai districts, and the Fengtai-Daxing junction (Fig. 10c). Optimal AD values ranged from 24.96 to 30.62 min from Tiananmen Square, corresponding to a distance between the fourth and fifth ring roads (Fig. 10d). A radial pattern of optimal POI diversity emerged, with higher concentrations observed in southern areas (Fig. 10e). Figure 10f also summarized the ideal geographic locations for satisfactory homestays within the Fifth Ring Road, primarily concentrated on the fringes of Chaoyang, Haidian, and Fengtai districts, including the Fengtai-Daxing junction. Notably, Haidian and Fengtai districts exhibit characteristics of moderate-scale, well-organized urban development. The spatial planning outcomes derived from threshold analysis in this study were consistent with the strategic objectives outlined in Beijing’s Urban Master Plan (2016–2035).

Conclusion

Building upon the insights derived from the feature importance analysis, this study presented a managerial strategy to enhance customer satisfaction in the service dimension of homestays in Beijing. Text analysis and SNA were applied to mine the complex topics and select factors from customers’ reviews. Service-related topics centered on cleaning, communication, amenities, price, room type and room policy, while geographic topics focused on characteristics of location and traffic. The findings highlight the significant impact of clean rooms and friendly hosts in bolstering market competitiveness of homestays. Therefore, it is recommended that homestay operators prioritize the cleanliness of their accommodations and cultivate a welcoming atmosphere.

By using sentiment analysis of the online reviews, this study achieved efficient classification for the extremely positive bias in homestays listing scores on Airbnb. This approach enabled the classification of homestays in Beijing into three distinct segments: satisfactory (n = 3019), moderate (n = 2254), and dissatisfactory (n = 956). On the basis, the RF classifier modeled with the 14 factors extracted from the above topics, demonstrated superior performance across all segments, achieving an overall AUC of 0.86.

Furthermore, the study revealed the nonlinear relationships between homestay satisfaction and surrounding environmental indicators. This finding is particularly valuable as it allows us to identify specific geographic threshold values that can inform the spatial distribution of future homestays. Specifically, the optimal range for DMS is between 1700 and 3600 m, for DBS between 150 and 410 m, for AA between 45.00 and 47.81 min, for AD between 24.96 and 30.62 min, and for POI diversity between 2.6 and 3.1. These threshold values can assist homestay platforms in making data-driven decisions regarding layout planning. In summary, this study offers important insights into the determinants influencing homestay satisfaction, providing valuable guidance for stakeholders in the sharing accommodation industry.

As a pioneer in the global accommodation industry, Airbnb has catalyzed the growth of homestays in China. Utilizing Airbnb as a case study, this study harnessed the power of big data and machine learning to shed light on the complex relationships between homestay satisfaction and its determinants. Despite Airbnb’s withdrawal from the Chinese market, the abundance of user-generated content within online reviews provides a valuable, objective lens into consumer preferences. The findings of this study, offering valuable guidance for optimizing Beijing’s homestay offerings, contribute to the advancement of China’s hospitality and tourism sectors by providing transferable knowledge for enhancing customer satisfaction. Furthermore, the robust methodology employed in this study presents a transferable framework for analyzing online reviews across various service sectors globally. However, the study acknowledged certain limitations that warrant further exploration. Reliance on the origin-destination (OD) cost matrix as the primary measure of accessibility, while valuable, did not account for real-time traffic conditions, which can significantly impact guest experiences. Future research could address this gap by developing a more comprehensive geographic assessment framework. Another limitation lay in the time-invariant nature of the analysis. The study did not capture how the influence of various factors might change over time. Examining these trends alongside the evolving urbanization process could offer valuable insights into the dynamic landscape of consumer preferences driving satisfaction within the homestay industry.

Comparisons of renin-angiotensin-aldosterone levels measured by chemical luminescence immunoassay and liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry in hypertensive patients under different pathophysiological conditions
Authors: Ting-Ting Xu, Yi-Gui Tang, En-Jun Xu, Xuan-Xuan Wang, Yu-Ping Yang, Zeng Guo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to compare measurements of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) components by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) and chemiluminescent immunoassay (CLIA) in hypertensive patients with different gender, position and renal function. EDTA plasma from 100 hypertensive patients who completed postural test were included. Aldosterone (PAC), renin (PRC, using Autobio CLIA microparticles kit; DRC, using LIAISON Direct Renin CLIA kit), angiotensin I and II (AngI and AngII), 18-hydroxycorticosterone (18-OHB) and 18-hydroxycortisol (18-OHF) were measured. Median PACCLIA was 46.0% higher (P < 0.01) than PACLC-MS/MS. Median PACCLIA, 18-OHBLC-MS/MS and 18-OHFLC-MS/MS were higher (P < 0.01) in patients with renal dysfunction compared with patients with normal renal function, whereas median PACLC-MS/MS did not show a significant difference between the two groups. Both median PRCCLIA and DRCCLIA were higher (P < 0.01) in male patients compared with female patients. PACLC-MS/MS, PACCLIA, PRCCLIA and DRCCLIA all showed good consistency in response to an assumption of upright posture. Close (P < 0.01) correlations were observed between PACCLIA and PACLC-MS/MS, between PRCCLIA and DRCCLIA, between AngIILC-MS/MS and PRCCLIA, and between AngIILC-MS/MS and DRCCLIA. LC-MS/MS is a reliable assay for measurement of the RAAS components in hypertensive patients under different pathophysiological conditions.

Introduction

Hypertension is a major contributor to the global burden of disease, affecting over 1 billion adults globally and with a prevalence that is markedly increasing [1]. As the most common chronic disease, hypertension is one of the main risk factors for many diseases, including renal damage, cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases [2]. The RAAS is one of the key regulators for the arterial blood pressure, cardiovascular and kidney function [3]. As the main salt-retaining hormone in humans, aldosterone plays an important role in regulating sodium and potassium handling within the distal nephron and the biosynthesis of aldosterone by adrenal glands is physiologically regulated by the RAAS, which is activated in response to low blood pressure and low salt status.

Accurate measurements of the renin, angiotensin (Ang) and aldosterone are essential for hypertension evaluation and especially for the diagnostic workup of primary aldosteronism (PA) which is the most common cause of secondary hypertension, affecting approximately 4–13% of patients among the hypertensive population [4, 5]. Because peripheral renin, angiotensin and aldosterone circulate at very low concentrations and the RAAS is a dynamic cascade involving multiple unstable angiotensin peptides [6], accurate measurement of the above important components of the RAAS requires highly sensitive and specific assays. Currently, plasma renin, AngII and aldosterone are most often measured by antibody-based immunoassays (chemiluminescent immunoassay, CLIA) which have demonstrated a lack of high specificity and variability in assay performance among different laboratories [7]. Therefore, in the past decade, there has been growing interest in quantifying the RAAS components by using liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS), which has been reported to be more reliable than traditional immunoassay [8, 9]. However, the accessibility to LC-MS/MS equipment remains an obstacle to many clinical laboratories, and using LC-MS/MS also requires specific technical experience.

Our previous study compared plasma aldosterone concentration (PAC) detected by both CLIA and LC-MS/MS in patients with PA, indicating that LC-MS/MS provides lower and more accurate results. Recently, a novel LC-MS/MS-based RAS equilibrium assay [10, 11] has been reported to be able to quantify AngI, AngII and various other angiotensin metabolites simultaneously. By using this assay technique, we reported [12, 13] that LC-MS/MS measured equilibrium AngII demonstrated good consistency and close correlation with renin [both plasma renin activity (PRA) and direct renin concentration (DRC)] in PA and non-PA patients in response to upright posture stimulation and fludrocortisone administration and oral salt loading. Nowadays, clinical detection of the RAAS components by using LC-MS/MS is rare in China [14–16], especailly using it as a routine test in hospital.

This study was designed to provide an intial assessment of the validity of our LC-MS/MS-based RAAS assay by comparing its testing results with current CLIA assay and by assessing changes in measurement under different pathophysiological conditions (male vs. female; recumbent vs. upright position; and normal renal function vs. renal dysfunction) in hypertensive patients.

Methods

Study design and participants

100 patients (59 males and 41 females; age range: 26 ~ 82 years) who were hospitalized and diagnosed with hypertension in the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University were recruited. Blood (EDTA plasma) was collected 2 times at 7 AM after overnight recumbency and 10 AM after 3 h of upright posture. Patients were grouped according to posture (recumbent vs. upright), gender (male vs. female), and renal function [eGFR > 90 vs. eGFR ≤ 90 ml/(min·1.73 m2)]. The results comparing those with males vs. females and with normal renal function vs. renal dysfunction were taken at both 7 and 10 AM. Routine clinical measurement of plasma renin, AngII, aldosterone, ACTH and cortisol by CLIA

DRC was assayed using the LIAISON Direct Renin CLIA kit and LIAISON XL immunoanalyzer (DiaSorin, Italy). Plasma renin concentration (PRC) and plasma aldosterone, AngII, ACTH and cortisol concentrations were measured routinely by using Autobio CLIA microparticles kits and AutoLumo A2000 immunoanalyzer (Autobio, China) in the Department of Laboratory Medicine of our hospital. The detection ranges for PACCLIA, PRCCLIA, DRCCLIA, AngIICLIA, ACTHCLIA and CortisolCLIA were 27.7 ~ 2774.0 pmol/L, 1 ~ 500 pg/ml, 0.13 ~ 500 mIU/L, 17.3 ~ 245.2 pmol/L, 1 ~ 2000 pg/ml, 2.7 ~ 1665.4 nmol/L, respectively. The intraassay coefficients of variation (CVs) for PACCLIA, PRCCLIA, DRCCLIA, AngIICLIA, ACTHCLIA and CortisolCLIA were 4.3, 6.4, 5.6, 4.3, 6.3 and 4.9%, while the interassay CVs were 7.4, 8.1, 12.4, 6.5, 4.7 and 6.5%, respectively.

Measurement of plasma aldosterone, AngI, AngII, cortisol, 18-hydroxycorticosterone and 18-hydroxycortisol by LC-MS/MS

Plasma aldosterone and cortisol concentrations were also measured by using LC-MS/MS (Kinetex® C18, Phenomenex / AB SCIEX Triple QuadTM 4500MD System) in the Department of Laboratory Medicine of our hospital. Additionally, prestored frozen EDTA-plasma samples (1.5 mL per tube, without repetitive freeze-thawing) were analyzed for concentrations of AngI, AngII, 18-hydroxycorticosterone (18-OHB) and 18-hydroxycortisol (18-OHF) using LC-MS/MS at the laboratory of CALIBRA Scientific, Inc. (Hangzhou, China). Sample preparation and purification: for aldosterone detection, 100 μL EPA-precipitant B was added to 450 μL plasma sample, vortexed for 2 min, transferred to an SLE plate, and left under gravity for 15 min. The sample was eluted with 1 mL and then 0.8 mL ethyl acetate:n-hexane (3:2, v/v), dried under nitrogen, reconstituted with 100 μL EPA-reconstitution solution, and mixed for analysis. For angiotensin I and II detection, 6 mL EPA buffer was mixed with 316 μL EPA-pH adjustment solution, vortexed, and combined with EPA-protective solution (100:1, v/v). This solution (20 μL) was added to 450 μL plasma sample, vortexed for 10 s, and incubated at 37 °C for 3 h. Two bottles of EPA dilution solution were mixed with 120 μL EPA-precipitant A and 600 μL EPA-termination solution, vortexed, and 200 μL was added to the incubated sample, vortexed for 10 min, kept at 2–8 or −20 °C for 20 min, centrifuged at 15,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C, and 100 μL supernatant was collected into a 96-well plate. The analytical protocol followed established procedures detailed in US Patent 2024/0019449 A1. Chromatographic separation was achieved using a DiSigns® column (150 × 4.6 mm, 5 μm). Mass spectrometric detection was performed on a CALIBRA CalQuant-S system equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source operated in positive ion mode. Analyte-specific multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) transitions were optimized for each compound. The lower limits of quantification (LLOQs) were 25 pmol/L for PACLC-MS/MS, 0.1 nmol/L for AngILC-MS/MS, 5 pmol/L for AngIILC-MS/MS, 25 nmol/L for CortisolLC-MS/MS, 0.1 nmol/L for 18-OHBLC-MS/MS, and 2.0 nmol/L for 18-OHFLC-MS/MS. The intraassay CVs for PACLC-MS/MS, AngILC-MS/MS, AngIILC-MS/MS, CortisolLC-MS/MS, 18-OHBLC-MS/MS and 18-OHFLC-MS/MS were 7.2, 6.1, 10.4, 4.1, 5.5 and 5.1%, while the interassay CVs were 7.8, 7.2, 10.5, 6.4, 8.6, and 6.8%, respectively. Plasma renin activity (PRA) was calculated based on the measurement of AngILC-MS/MS before and after incubation (at 37 °C for 1 h). Matrix effects in all RAAS assays were <15%.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 23.0 Statistics (IBM Corp., USA) was used to analyze the data. Nonnormally distributed data are presented as median and interquartile range. Independent samples Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare the data between the different groups (male vs. female, normal renal function vs. renal dysfunction), and Wilcoxon signed rank test was used to compare within-group data before and after upright posture stimulation. Pearson correlation and Spearman correlation were utilized to examine the relationship between LC-MS/MS and CLIA assay results. Passing-Bablok regressions and Bland-Altman analysis were used to compare the agreement of assay performance between LC-MS/MS and CLIA. P < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

Influence of upright posture stimulation on the RAAS levels

The assumption of upright posture 3 h following overnight recumbency in all patients was associated with an increase (Table 2) not only in PACCLIA (610.2 vs. 466.1 pmol/L, P < 0.01), PRCCLIA (21.8 vs. 11.1 pg/ml, P < 0.05), DRCCLIA (17.9 vs. 12.5 mIU/L, P < 0.05) and CortisolCLIA (383.7 vs. 363.8 nmol/L, P < 0.05), but also in PACLC-MS/MS (435.0 vs. 273.4 pmol/L, P < 0.01), AngILC-MS/MS (after incubation: 0.3 vs. 0.2 nmol/L, P < 0.05), 18-OHBLC-MS/MS (1.4 vs. 1.1 nmol/L, P < 0.01) and 18-OHFLC-MS/MS (4.8 vs. 3.4 nmol/L, P < 0.01). Although ACTHCLIA did not show a significant difference between 7 AM and 10 AM (33.9 vs. 33.3 pg/ml, P > 0.05), both CortisolCLIA (383.7 vs. 363.8 nmol/L, P < 0.05) and CortisolLC-MS/MS (378.0 vs. 354.5 nmol/L, P > 0.05) were higher in 10 AM than that in 7 AM.

Comparison of measured parameters for hypertensive patients with normal and impaired renal function

In this study, hypertensive patients with renal dysfunction [eGFR ≤ 90 ml/(min·1.73 m2), n = 45] were observed to have higher levels of PACCLIA (688.1 vs. 435.8 pmol/L, P < 0.01), PRCCLIA (19.9 vs. 12.6 pg/ml, P < 0.05), AngIICLIA (105.2 vs. 87.4 pmol/L, P < 0.01), 18-OHBLC-MS/MS (1.4 vs. 1.0 nmol/L, P < 0.01), 18-OHFLC-MS/MS (5.1 vs. 3.4 nmol/L, P < 0.01), and lower levels of AngILC-MS/MS (after incubation: 0.3 vs. 0.8 nmol/L, P < 0.05) than hypertensive patients with normal renal function [eGFR > 90 ml/(min·1.73 m2), n = 55] (Table 2). Different to AngIICLIA, AngIILC-MS/MS did not show a significant difference between the two groups (25.9 vs. 21.7 pmol/L, P > 0.05).

Discussion

Accurate measurement of the RAAS components in hypertensive patients is essential, as testing results could provide a profile of patients’ sympathetic nerve excitability and salt-retaining hormone levels. By measuring plasma aldosterone and renin levels, it provides clinical clues for diagnosing PA and reninoma. By measuring plasma AngI and AngII levels, the drug effects of angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers can be evaluated which could help for guiding further dosage strategy of the above two anti-hypertensive drugs.

Because peripheral aldosterone circulates at picomolar concentrations, accurate measurement of PAC requires highly sensitive and specific assays. Currently, PAC is most often measured by antibody-based immunoassays [17] that have demonstrated lack of high specificity (causing overestimation) [18] and variability in assay performance among different laboratories [19, 20]. Therefore, in the past decade, there has been growing interest in quantifying PAC by using LC-MS/MS, which has been reported to be more accurate and reliable than traditional immunoassay [8, 21, 22]. In our current study, we found that median PACCLIA was 46.0% higher (P < 0.01) than PACLC-MS/MS in the Chinese hypertensive population, and a positive correlation was observed between the two aldosterone assays (Pearson r coefficient 0.439, P < 0.01; Spearman ρ coefficient 0.561, P < 0.01). This result is basically in keeping with our previous study, which shows that median plasma PACCLIA was 27.8% higher (P < 0.05) than serum PACLC-MS/MS in the Australian population (with a positive correlation: Pearson r coefficient 0.861, P < 0.01; Spearman ρ coefficient 0.894, P < 0.01) [8]. A similar study also in the Chinese population recently reported that median PACCLIA was 84.7% higher (P < 0.01) than PACLC-MS/MS, also with a positive correlation between the two aldosterone assays (Pearson r coefficient 0.770, P < 0.01) [23]. Additionally, the difference between median PACCLIA and PACLC-MS/MS was even larger at 70.5% in the supine position compared to 40.3% in the upright position. This is in agreement with other studies that have shown that the effects of interference with the CLIA is stronger at lower than higher concentrations.

However, we have to admitted that although it showed statistical significance, correlation and agreement of our two PAC assays were not very good, which may raise concerns about the reliability and accuracy of either one or both of our PAC assays. At present, PAC is most often measured by using the automated CLIA method. But concerns have been raised about the weaknesses of these immunometric techniques. The specificity of antibodies for CLIA varies between assays, which is likely from interfering substances in tested samples such as structurally related steroids (as well as their precursors and metabolites) that have the potential for cross-reactivity with the aldosterone assay antibody [18], thereby causing overestimation of PAC. This would be especially problematic because the aldosterone in plasma is relatively low compared with many potentially interfering steroids (pmol/L vs. nmol/L). Yin et al. [24] measured the serum PAC detected by LC-MS/MS and three different CLIA assays in 221 patients with suspected PA and found the CLIA results vary widely. Regarding reasons for the poor correlation coefficients between our two aldosterone assays, we guess it may relate with the factor that among our 100 studied patients, 45 had renal dysfunction. We also completed a subgroup comparison and found that the Spearman and Pearson correlation coefficients between PACCLIA and PACLC-MS/MS were 0.697 (P < 0.01) and 0.657 (P < 0.01) in 55 patents with normal renal function, whereas in 45 patients with renal dysfunction, these two coefficients were 0.512 (P < 0.01) and 0.231 (P < 0.05), respectively. Chen et al. reported that Pearson correlation coefficient between PACCLIA and PACLC-MS/MS was only 0.674 in patients with essential hypertension (n = 61, patients with eGFR <30 were excluded), whereas in healthy volunteer (n = 21, PAC and cortisol are in normal range) and in patients with PA (n = 43, PAC is very high), this coefficient improves to 0.842 and 0.770, respectively [23].

In our current study, median PACCLIA in patients with renal dysfunction was higher than that in patients with normal renal function (688.1 vs. 435.8 pmol/L, P < 0.01), whereas median PACLC-MS/MS did not show a significant difference between the two groups (303.8 vs. 377.3 pmol/L, P > 0.05). We then further analyzed PACLC-MS/MS results in patients with more severe degrees of renal dysfunction, and it revealed that median PACLC-MS/MS in patients with eGFR <60 was significantly higher (438.3 vs. 313.9 pmol/L, P < 0.01) than that in patients with eGFR between 60 and 90 ml/(min·1.73 m2). Lam et al. [25] have reported that PAC will be overestimated by CLIA assay in patients with renal impairment. Therefore, in patients with renal dysfunction, LC-MS/MS assay may be more accurate in the measurement of aldosterone [26, 27]. It is worth noting that, in our current study, plasma levels of cortisol (389.1 vs. 358.7 nmol/L, P > 0.05), 18-OHB (1.4 vs. 1.0 nmol/L, P < 0.01) and 18-OHF (5.1 vs. 3.4 nmol/L, P < 0.01) measured by LC-MS/MS were higher in patients with renal dysfunction than that in patients with normal renal function. Because cortisol, 18-OHB and 18-OHF all circulate at nanomolar concentrations, it is easy to assume that these structurally related steroids may have the potential to react with antibodies of aldosterone CLIA assay and cause overestimation of PAC results. In addition, Eisenhofer et al. [28] reported that other forms of immunoassay interference like some macromolecules (e.g., heterophile antibodies) may also have potential to cause overestimation of aldosterone CLIA assay results, therefore, the procedure of purification of plasma to remove those macromolecules may be necessary for future aldosterone CLIA assay.

Renin can be measured by activity assay (PRA, measuring endogenous AngI generated per unit of time) or, more frequently, by direct quantification (DRC, measuring active renin concentration). In this study, we also measured plasma renin concentration (PRC) by using Autobio renin CLIA microparticles kit. Both PRCCLIA and DRCCLIA were higher (P < 0.05) in the upright than recumbent position (as well as PACCLIA and PACLC-MS/MS, P < 0.01), consistent with posture-induced activation of the sympathetic nervous system, which eventually results in stimulation of renin release and consequent production of aldosterone [29–31]. Our local PRCCLIA results showed not only a consistent change with DRCCLIA (a widely accepted assay of renin) but also a close (P < 0.01) correlation with DRCCLIA (Spearman ρ coefficient, 0.832; Pearson r coefficient, 0.850). Additionally, we also observed that female hypertensive patients showed a lower median PRCCLIA (8.3 vs. 21.8 pg/ml, P < 0.01), DRCCLIA (9.2 vs. 23.1 mIU/L, P < 0.01), and PRALC-MS/MS (0.4 vs. 0.8 ng/ml/h, not significant) than that in male hypertensive patients. This may be because, i) as shown in Table 1, more male patients used calcium channel blockers and diuretics (two drugs that are known can cause increases of renin) than female patients. Male patients also had higher (P < 0.05) DBP levels and used more (P < 0.01) antihypertensive drugs with a higher (P < 0.05) defined daily dose than female patients, which indicated that hypertension in male patients was worse than in female patients (malignant hypertension is also known can cause increases of renin). ii) It has been reported that oestrogen can stimulate production of the renin substrate angiotensinogen [32]. As a result, angiotensin levels rise, and this in turn leads to falls in renin release via negative feedback on the juxtaglomerular apparatus [33]. In this study, whether oestrogen in female patients played a role in above renin results is uncertain but plausible. iii) In this study, median age was 52 in female patients compared to 40 in male patients. Males tend to have an enhanced sympathetic nerve excitability [34] and RAAS activity [35] which all can stimulate renin production and release. It has been reported that low-renin hypertension accounts for approximately one-third of patients with hypertension and is more prevalent in women and the older adult population [36]. Unexpectedly, around 60% of our studied patients failed to show a typical diurnal rhythm of cortisol in the morning, which caused median cortisol (measured by both CLIA and LC-MS/MS) to be higher at 10 AM than at 7 AM. We supposed that pulsatile release of ACTH and cortisol, fluctuation in peripheral cortisol levels, and some certain disease states (e.g., renal dysfunction, obstructive sleep apnea, sympathetic nervous system hyperactivity, stress, depression, etc.) all could be potential confounding factors to cortisol results in this study.

Because the RAS is a dynamic cascade involving multiple angiotensin-processing enzymes and peptides, peripheral levels of which are very low (in the picomolar range), it was thought traditionally that accurate measurement of these unstable angiotensin peptides was extremely difficult. We previously validated a LC-MS/MS-based angiotensin assay which can quantify plasma AngI and AngII by using RAS equilibrium analysis [12, 13]. The principle of this assay is to establish an ex vivo equilibrated status of the RAS by incubating pre-stored plasma at 37 °C and PH = 7.4 for 1 h without blocking any angiotensin producing or degrading enzymes. The final equilibrium AngI and AngII levels are determined by the activity of all soluble angiotensin processing enzymes present in tested samples. Lo et al. performed a comparative analysis of AngII measurement by using LC-MS/MS with and without using protease inhibitors and reported that the absence of protease inhibitors did not adversely affect the integrity of AngII [37]. We previously reported that equilibrium AngII not only correlated closely with DRC and PRA in both patients with and without PA but also displayed a better analytical sensitivity than DRC and PRA in samples with low renin levels. In our current study, we also observed that AngIILC-MS/MS correlated closely (P < 0.01) with PRCCLIA, DRCCLIA, and PRALC-MS/MS, whereas AngIICLIA did not (Table 3). These results remind us that plasma AngII concentration measured by using our current CLIA method may be not very accurate.

Conclusion

Unlike immunoassay, LC-MS/MS does not require a specific antibody to target the analyte and permits differentiation and accurate measurement of aldosterone, angiotensin peptides and other steroids together with their corresponding internal standards simultaneously in a single run from one sample. Our results provide preliminary evidence supporting the satisfactory assay performance of measuring the RAAS components (AngI, PRA, AngII, aldosterone) by LC-MS/MS in hypertensive patients under different pathophysiological conditions (gender, assumption of upright posture, and renal function). It could be anticipated that, for future quantification of the RAAS components, the gold method should be based on LC-MS/MS detection. Further studies are necessary to validate the potential value of LC-MS/MS-based measurement of the RAAS components, especially in screening of secondary hypertension and making personalized antihypertensive treatment based on the assessment of biochemical effects of different antihypertensive drugs.

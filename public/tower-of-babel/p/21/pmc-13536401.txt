Mesenchymal stem cells derived from gingiva normoxic or hypoxic-preconditioning allogenous transplantation affect orthodontic tooth movement in vivo
Authors: Alexander Patera Nugraha, Guang Hong, Kunhua Yang, Ida Bagus Narmada, Fedik Abdul Rantam, Diah Savitri Ernawati, I Gusti Aju Wahju Ardani, Adya Pramusita, Nurul Aisyah Rizky Putranti, Naora Dewi Rahma Fadzila, Al Zayyan Shenaya Aurora, Reisya Irfanny Farizaldi, Muhammad Luthfi, Putri Cahaya Situmorang, Wibi Riawan, Kotaro Tanimoto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground/purposeGingival mesenchymal stem cells (GMSC) show promising qualities in immunoregulation, immunomodulation in regeneration of periodontal tissue during orthodontic tooth movement (OTM). This study aimed to elucidate allogenous GMSC normoxia and hypoxic preconditioning transplantation in the OTM model using New Zealand rabbits (Oryctolagus cuniculus) by examining nuclear factor kappa beta (NF-κB), tumour necrosis factoralpha (TNF-α), nuclear factor associated T-cell-1 (NFATc1), Runt-related transcription factor 2 (RUNX2), Osteocalcin.Materials and methodsFour groups were established from forty-eight, healthy, young male rabbits divided into the following respective groups: p1: normoxic GMSC transplantation with OTM, p2: hypoxic GMSC transplantation with OTM, k2: OTM only, and K1: phosphate buffered saline (PBS) only. In order to advance the mandibular first molar mesially, a fixed orthodontic appliance was applied. The rabbit was transplanted with 106 hypoxic or normoxic GMSC in 50 μl of PBS into afflicted gingiva after one day of OTM. All experimental animals were sacrificed on days 7, 14, and 28. Furthermore, mandibular tissue was isolated for the sample to examine NFKβ, TNF-α, NFATc1, osteocalcin, RUNX2 expression, and OTM rate.ResultsThere was a considerable increase in OTM rate, osteocalcin and RUNX2 expression in P2 on day 28 with significantly different between groups. In contrast to other groups, the P2 group’s expression of NFKβ, TNF-α, and NFATc1 was considerably lower on day 28 in the tension side of the alveolar bone (P ≤ 0.05).ConclusionGMSC hypoxia-preconditioning transplantation increases the OTM rate through the RUNX2-osteocalcin-OTM rate stimulation pathway in rabbits (O. cunniculus).Keywords: Bone biology, Hypoxia, Medicine, Stem cells, Tooth movement

Introduction

The movement of the teeth from one position to another for both functional and aesthetic reasons is known as orthodontic tooth movement (OTM).1 Application of orthodontic mechanical forces (OMF) to the tooth structure results in bone apposition and resorption of alveolar bone, which involves chemokines and cytokines throughout the anabolic and catabolic phases of the inflammatory response.2 Many issues with dental health arise from orthodontic treatment that lasts for a long time.3 Surgically assisted orthodontic treatments and non-surgical orthodontic therapies have drawn a lot of interest in an attempt to shorten the orthodontic treatment period.4,5 The OTM rate may be increased by Surgically assisted orthodontic micro-osteoperforation treatment; however, root resorption may still take place.6,7 By improving alveolar bone remodeling, mesenchymal stem cell (MSC) transplantation is currently one way to maximize the OTM rate without significant side effects.8–10 By releasing a variety of biological and immunoregulatory substances, MSCs can control macrophage polarization and finish bone remodeling during OTM.11,12 Gingival mesenchymal stem cells (GMSC) have been discovered to be present in the gingiva which has immunoregulation, immunomodulation, and regeneration potential.13–15

Stress stimuli during OTM can activate macrophages’ nuclear factor kappa beta (NF-κB), a transcription factor and also increase tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) secretion. Following the activation of NF-κB signals, OMF influences bone metabolism through molecular and cellular interactions in osteocytes, osteoclasts, and osteoblasts.16 TNF-α, which is expressed in the periodontal ligament during OTM, is one of several cytokines that are involved in the biological process of OTM. TNF-α plays a crucial role in controlling the distance of tooth movement and osteoclast development.17 Furthermore, because it is activated by TNF-α induction and functions as an osteoclastogenic receptor and transcription gene in inflammatory settings such as nuclear factor associated T-cell-1 (NFATc1).18,19

NFATc1, sclerostin and TRAP is currently thought to be a possible bone remodeling and osteoclastogenic regulator during OTM.19,20 Furthermore, signal transduction and complicated gene transcription, including Runt-related transcription factor 2 (RUNX2) and the release of osteocalcin, regulate the differentiation of osteoblasts and osteoclasts. 21,22 Histomorphometrics can be used to analyze the biomolecular mechanism of OTM on the tensile side of the alveolar bone following GMSC transplantation in hypoxia preconditions. For example, histomorphometric parameters of orthodontic tooth movement can be determined by measuring the distance between the jaw’s first and second molars, specifically the distance between the first molar’s distal marginal ridge and the second molar’s mesial marginal ridge.23

In OTM experimental animal models such as mice or rabbits, Ni–Ti coil springs are most suited for generating continuous light OMF.24,25 Hypoxia in periodontal tissue caused by pressure and tensile forces during OTM necessitates adaptive GMSC preconditioning, specifically hypoxic preconditioning prior to transplantation by making GMSC hypoxic to maximize the inflammatory response and osteogenic interaction during OTM than GMSC normoxic.25,26 Cobalt chloride (CoCl2) is a biocompatible biomaterial that can trigger the hypoxia-inducible factor-1 alpha (HIF-1α), a sign of hypoxia preconditioning, in MSC cell culture. It is also referred to as a hypoxia-mimicking agent.27 The comparison of the effects of GMSC transplantation with normoxia and hypoxia preconditioning in OTM rate are still limited. Thus, the aim of this study was to compare and elucidate allogenous GMSC normoxia and hypoxic preconditioning transplantation in the OTM model using New Zealand rabbits (Oryctolagus cuniculus) using NFKβ, TNF-α, NFATc1, osteocalcin, RUNX2 immunohistochemistry analysis and OTM rate examination.

Materials and methods

Experimental animal, setting and ethical clearance

A post-test-only control group design was used in this real experimental laboratory investigation, and simple blind random sample selection was conducted. Young, male, healthy New Zealand rabbits (O. cunniculus) weighing three to five kilograms and six months of age. The study was approved by Ethical Committee of Health Research with number 0585/HRECC.FODM/V/2025 and the animal experiment was carried out in compliance with the Universitas Airlangga’s regulations.

Isolation, sub-culture, characterization of mesenchymal stem cell from gingiva

The specific isolation, subculture, and characterization of mesenchymal stem cells from gingiva (GMSC) were mentioned in our previous study.13 In accordance with the study’s specifications, confluent cells underwent another passage into the fourth.13 GMSC should expressed several markers such as cluster of differentiation (CD)200, CD105, CD73, CD90, but not CD45, CD44, and CD34. Flowcytometry and immunocytochemistry were used to characterize the isolated GMSC as mentioned in the previous study.13,28 GMSC must have osteogenic differentiation ability, then GMSC were osteogenic induced for 14 days, there were two weekly changes to the culture medium. Alizarin red was then used to stain the calcium deposit, a marker of osteogenic differentiation according to previous study.13

Mesenchymal stem cell from gingiva hypoxia precondition induction and immunolabelling

GMSC are preconditioned under both normoxic and hypoxic conditions prior to usage in the experimental model. GMSC are grown with the use of 100 μM CoCl2 for hypoxic preconditioning (Sigma–Aldrich, Saint Louis, MO, USA). According to the previous study protocol, GMSC’ expression of hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha (HIF-1α) is immunocytochemically assessed during normoxia and hypoxia preconditioning to verify the induction of hypoxia in GMSC after 24 h.27 For immunolabeling in GMSC before application to the experimental animal model, GMSC at passage four were harvested and labelled using fluorescence staining PKH26. Following the manufacturer’s instructions, GMSC were tagged using a commercial PKH26 dye (Sigma–Aldrich). Prior to the transplant, the cell suspension was tagged right away. Slides were immediately dried at room temperature following microscopy, and fluorescence microscope to view PKH26-positive cells was used. The nuclear stained with DAPI (Sigma–Aldrich) for a more thorough examination of the localization of the cells.29

Orthodontic tooth movement animal model and mesenchymal stem cell from gingiva hypoxia preconditioning transfer

The forty-eight samples in this experimental trial were divided into four groups: K1: phosphate buffered saline (PBS) only, K2: OTM only, P1: normoxic GMSC transplantation with OTM, and P2: hypoxic GMSC transplantation with OTM. In order to advance the mandibular first molar mesially, a fixed orthodontic appliance was applied with An 8 mm closed coil spring was connected to nickel-titanium (NiTi) and stainless steel (SS) ligature wires with a diameter of 0.009 inches (American Orthodontics, Sheboygan, WI, USA). A blunt microliter syringe (Terumo Medical Corporation, Somerset, NJ, USA) filled with 20 μl of GMSC with 106 cell densities in phosphate buffer saline (PBS) (Sigma-–Aldrich) was used to provide a single dosage. All samples underwent guillotine decapitation on observation days 7, 14, and 28. The samples were administered an injectable combination of 30 mg/kg ketamine and 4 mg/kg xylazine (Sigma–Aldrich) before sacrifice. Every animal model’s mandibles were also gathered for immunohistochemical examination.11,25

Immunohistochemical analysis

The mandibular tissue was fixated with formalin, and 10 % EDTA was used to make the sample softer to cut into 4 μmthick slices to form the paraffin block. The anti-nuclear factor kappa-β (NFKβ), anti-tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF- α), anti-nuclear factor-associated T cells-1 (NFATc1), antiosteocalcin, and anti-runt-related transcription factor-2 (RUNX2) monoclonal antibody (Santa Cruz Biotechnology Inc, Santa Cruz, CA, USA) were added to slides and immunohistochemically stained. 3,3-diaminobenzidine (DAB) hydrochloride (Sigma–Aldrich) was used for the chromogenic visualization procedure, and hematoxylin eosin staining was performed for 30 s afterward. The slides were then sealed with a glass cover after going through a series of ethanol and xylene tests. With light microscopy at 100x, 400x, and 1000x magnification, osteoblasts showed positive expression of RUNX2 and osteocalcin, whereas osteoclasts of alveolar bone tissue showed positive expression of NFKβ, NFATc1, and TNF-α.11,16,25

Orthodontic tooth movement rate examination

The experimental animals were sacrificed after 7, 14, and 28 days of OTM. Under a stereoscopic microscope (VH-7000, Keyence, Osaka, Japan), the closest distance between the mandibular first and second molars was used to gauge the OTM rate. The measurement was made four times for every rabbit, and the average was utilized.23

Data analysis

The Statistical Software for Social Science (SPSS) version 20.0 (IBM Corporation, Chicago, IL, USA) was used to summarize and analyze the study data using mean and standard deviation (SD). Post-hoc Tukey Honest Significant Difference (HSD) and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) were used with significance set at P ≤ 0.05.

Results

GMSC were successfully immuno-labeled with PKH26, and upon transplantation, GMSC were found in the mandibular tissue of the OTM experimental animal model (Fig. 1A–B). Compared to GMSC normoxic preconditioning in the OTM experimental animal model, GMSC hypoxic preconditioning transplantation significantly increased the OTM rate (Fig. 1C–D) after 28 days by stimulating RUNX2 (Fig. 2A) and osteocalcin (Fig. 2B) expression in the tension side of alveolar bone on day 28. However, after GMSC hypoxic preconditioning transplantation, the expression of NFKβ (Fig. 3A), TNF-α (Fig. 3B), and NFATc1 (Fig. 3C) was lower in the tension side of alveolar bone compared to GMSC normoxic preconditioning in the OTM experimental animal model, with a significant difference (P ≤ 0.05). On the tension side of alveolar bone, this finding demonstrated that GMSC hypoxic preconditioning transplanting increased RUNX2 and osteocalcin while decreasing expression of NFKβ, NFATc1, and TNF-α after 28 days, hence improving the OTM rate of the experimental animal model.

Discussion

GMSC hypoxic preconditioning is superior to normoxic in promoting OTM distance by increasing osteoblast RUNX2 and osteocalcin expression while decreasing osteoclast NFKβ, TNF-α, and NFATC1 expression in the tension side of alveolar bone in an animal model of OTM according to the study’s findings. This study findings also in accordance with previous study that mentioned the OTM distance increased when MSC from the rats’ visceral adipose tissue was transplanted to the periodontal ligament during orthodontic expansion.8 The transplatation of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSC) into the interpremaxillary suture following fast maxillary growth was used in another investigation. Following a 10-day retention period, Pkh67+ is visible under a fluorescent microscope in the suture, mostly at the injection site. When compared to controls that received PBS injections, histomorphometric analysis showed that a single local injection of MSCs into the midpalatal suture enhanced the development of new bone in the suture by increasing the number of osteoblasts and new vessel formation.10 BMSC submucosal injection also reported to accelerate OTM and decrease OTM duration due to stimulated osteoclastic activity along with higher resorption lacuna area and changes in alveolar bone microstructure variables such as bone mineral density volumetry, bone volume fraction, and trabecular number.9

Stem cells have complicated impacts on osteoclast development that are intimately linked to autocrine or paracrine processes, physical stimulation, microenvironmental stimuli, and other variables.30 The OTM distance considerably increased ten days following the injection of mouse bone marrow-derived macrophages (mBMMs) into the mice’s tail vein. In the mBMMSC group, there were considerably more osteoclasts in the alveolar bone on the compressed side than in the saline group.30 Our previous study also found that the delivery of hypoxia-preconditioned allogeneic GMSC during OTM resulted in a considerable increase in RANK-RANKL expression on the compression side of the alveolar bone, but not on the tension side.11

RUNX2 and osteocalcin were observed to be activated, particularly on day 28, in the tension side of the alveolar bone in the OTM experimental animal model following treatment with hypoxia or normoxic preconditioned GMSC. By stimulating the expression of target genes linked to osteogenesis (Runx2, Osterix, and Osteoprotegerin), the canonical Wnt signaling pathway facilitates the production of new bone and is important for bone apposition during OTM.31,32 Histological results showed that osteoclast recruitments were the cause of the early period’s increased OTM rate, which was corroborated by an earlier work on osteocalcin local injection in an OTM model in vivo.33

The current study discovered that following GMSC hypoxia preconditioned transplantation in an OTM animal model, pro-inflammatory cytokine and osteoclastic activity indicators such as NFKβ, TNF-α, and NFATc1 were considerably lower than those following normoxic preconditioned transplantation. Although bone resorption is required to raise the OTM rate, excessive bone resorption, including root resorption, can be avoided by regulating NFKβ, TNF-α, and NFATc1. M1 macrophages often proliferate during the early stages of OTM, and their pro-inflammatory phenotype is essential for bone resorption in OTM activation. On the other hand, the quantity of M2-like macrophages on the compressed side of the periodontal tissue steadily rises throughout the late stage of OTM.34 By causing bone apposition (high expression of RUNX2) in the tension area and bone resorption (high expression of NFATc1) in the pressure area, gradually rising force has an impact on OTM rate.21 One may argue that TNF-α was one of the key proinflammatory cytokines that caused OTM. According to a previous study, osteocytes’ production of RANKL was caused by TNF-α, which was produced as a result of mechanical stress. According to these results, TNF-α-responsive osteocytes are crucial cells for the development of osteoclasts during OTM.35

In bone, MSCs in particular undergo a process called hypoxic stem cell polarization when they are exposed to low oxygen levels, or hypoxia. Under hypoxic circumstances, the HIF-1α signalling pathway plays a significant role in controlling the expression of osteogenic genes. They thus change their behavior, drawing in macrophages and preventing osteoclastogenesis. For bone regeneration, particularly in OTM, this polarization is crucial because it stimulates osteogenesis and regulates the inflammatory response.36 According to the theory of delay period prevention, which is brought on by the elimination of hyalinized necrotic tissue and an accelerated rate of wound healing, compression and tension were thought to hasten bone remodeling during OTM. Periodontal MSCs were probably more often replenished, activated, and/or differentiated when GMSC hypoxic was used. These MSCs went on to become reparative cells like osteoblasts and cementoblasts.25

These findings have significant ramifications for the development of biomaterials and treatment approaches in a variety of regenerative medicine domains, not only the particular setting of orthodontics. Enhancing tissue regeneration and functioning may be possible through an understanding of how to manipulate and prepare MSC environments. Thus, our work not only clarifies the hypoxic precondition of GMSC during OTM, but it also lays the groundwork for further advancements in regenerative orthodontics. Nevertheless, this study has several limitations, including the assessment of possible cellular stress or apoptosis in vitro and bone formation after molecular bone formation signalling pathway should be taken into consideration.

Within the limitations of the current study, it can be concluded that hypoxic-preconditioned GMSC allogenous transplantation to the periodontal tissue of the mandible left first molar teeth was achieved by the previously described injection method. GMSC’ allogenous transplantation during OTM towards the periodontal tissue increased the OTM rate. At the evaluation of the teeth that received hypoxic-preconditioned GMSC allogenous transplantation during the OTM, the RUNX2 and osteocalcin expression of osteoblasts was increased, but not the NFKβ, TNF-α, and NFATC1 expression of osteoclasts in the tension side of alveolar bone, with a significant difference compared to GMSC normoxic allogenous transplantation.

Furthermore, the clinical applicability of this data is uncertain due to the limited generalisability of the animal models to the biologic processes in humans. Therefore, additional research should be carried out to learn more about the stability of CoCl2 treatment for HMA in cellular stress of GMSCs for potential clinical effects of the advanced approaches introduced in this study. Another limitation of this research was that it solely used an immunohistochemistry analysis. Future research may examine the molecular mechanism of bone remodelling and mRNA level associated with bone remodelling. Actual bone formation or alveolar bone gain, such as woven bone formation by means of haematoxylin eosin staining or bone density/bone formation that can be examined by micro-CT, should be done in the further study. Following hypoxia-preconditioned GMSC transplantation into OTM animal models with an extended observation period, an attempt should be made to ascertain the alterations in alveolar bone and periodontal tissue.

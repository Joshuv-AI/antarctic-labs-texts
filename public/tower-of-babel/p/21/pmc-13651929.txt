Three hundred sixty‐degree prophylactic laser retinopexy versus focal laser during rhegmatogenous retinal detachment repair: A systematic review and meta‐analysis
Authors: Luke Yaldo, Albert Ting, Saif Pasha, Kyle Sporn, Abdelaziz Abdelaal, Joshua Ong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo compare the anatomic, functional and safety outcomes of intra‐operative 360‐degree prophylactic laser retinopexy versus focal laser retinopexy during pars plana vitrectomy (PPV) for rhegmatogenous retinal detachment (RRD) and to assess the certainty of evidence.MethodsPubMed, Web of Science and Scopus were searched on June 27, 2025. Randomized controlled trials and comparative observational studies evaluating 360‐degree versus focal laser during PPV for RRD were included. Two reviewers independently extracted data and assessed risk of bias (ROB‐2, NIH tools). Pooled analyses were conducted using random‐effects models, and the certainty of evidence was graded using GRADE. The primary outcome was single‐surgery anatomic success (SSAS). Secondary outcomes included best‐corrected visual acuity (BCVA), intraocular pressure (IOP) and post‐operative complications.ResultsThirteen studies comprising 3639 eyes were included. Compared with focal laser, 360‐degree laser achieved higher odds of SSAS (OR: 1.82; 95% CI, 1.40–2.39; I 2 = 0%; moderate‐certainty). No differences were observed in final BCVA (MD = 0.06; 95% CI, −0.01 to 0.13; I 2 = 75.5%) or BCVA improvement (MD: –0.06; 95% CI, −0.22 to 0.11; I 2 = 0%). Rates of epiretinal membrane (OR = 1.11; 95% CI: 0.80–1.54, I 2 = 26.85%, moderate certainty), proliferative vitreoretinopathy (OR = 0.62; 95% CI: 0.36–1.08, I 2 = 0%, moderate certainty), cystoid macular oedema and macular hole were comparable between groups.ConclusionAdjunctive 360‐degree laser retinopexy during PPV for RRD was associated with higher SSAS without a measurable increase in post‐operative complications. However, no improvement in visual acuity outcomes was observed. Given the predominance of observational evidence, further well‐designed randomized trials are warranted to clarify the magnitude of benefit.

INTRODUCTION

Rhegmatogenous retinal detachment (RRD) remains one of the most vision‐threatening ocular emergencies, often requiring urgent surgical intervention to preserve anatomical integrity and optimize functional outcomes (Li et al., 2019). Advances in vitreoretinal surgery have increasingly positioned pars plana vitrectomy (PPV) as the preferred technique for primary RRD repair, especially in pseudophakic eyes (Echegaray et al., 2021). Despite improvements in visualization and instrumentation, post‐operative retinal re‐detachment continues to occur in 5%–15% of cases, with missed or new peripheral breaks accounting for a substantial proportion of these failures (Bellucci et al., 2023).

To mitigate this risk, many vitreoretinal surgeons apply intra‐operative prophylactic laser photocoagulation to areas beyond the identified retinal breaks (Schneider et al., 2012). Among the most commonly used approaches is 360‐degree circumferential laser retinopexy, which theoretically creates a continuous chorioretinal barrier to seal occult or future breaks and limit sub‐retinal fluid migration (Wang et al., 2020). This technique is particularly appealing in patients with compromised peripheral visualization, multiple small tears or eyes at high risk of proliferative vitreoretinopathy (PVR) (Wang et al., 2020). Prophylactic circumferential laser retinopexy has even been explored in epiretinal membrane (ERM) and macular hole surgery (Chalam et al., 2012; Ohana et al., 2020). However, it remains controversial whether the routine application of 360‐degree laser offers a significant advantage over focal laser applied solely to known pathology.

Multiple retrospective studies and small randomized trials have reported conflicting findings regarding the efficacy of 360‐degree laser (Avitabile et al., 2008; Koh et al., 2007; Loiudice et al., 2021; Skevas et al., 2023; Stücheli et al., 2025). While some have shown improved single‐surgery anatomic success (SSAS) and reduced recurrence rates with its use (Dirani et al., 2020; Zhou et al., 2017; Zhou & Qiu, 2015), others have found no difference in outcomes or have raised concerns about potential adverse effects, including unnecessary retinal scarring or impaired visual recovery (Cristescu et al., 2023; Peters et al., 2022). Furthermore, several studies suggest that the choice to use 360‐degree laser is often guided by intra‐operative judgement rather than objective criteria, introducing variability in clinical practise (Koh et al., 2007; Mathai et al., 2022; Stücheli et al., 2025).

To date, no comprehensive synthesis of the available evidence has directly compared the anatomical and functional outcomes of 360‐degree versus focal laser retinopexy during PPV for RRD. Prior reviews have either addressed prophylactic laser more broadly or focused on specific sub‐groups, such as eyes undergoing silicone oil removal or patients with hereditary vitreoretinopathies (Camp et al., 2025; Soekamto et al., 2021). As a result, there remains a critical gap in the literature regarding whether 360‐degree laser should be recommended as a standard adjunct in routine PPV for RRD.

The following systematic review and meta‐analysis was undertaken to address this gap by pooling comparative data from studies evaluating 360‐degree versus focal laser retinopexy during PPV for RRD. Our objectives were to assess differences in anatomic success, visual acuity outcomes, intraocular pressure (IOP) changes and post‐operative complications, and to explore potential predictors of treatment effect through meta‐regression. The result of these analyses aims to provide an evidence‐based appraisal of this widely debated surgical adjunct.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Protocol and registration

This systematic review and meta‐analysis was conducted following the PRISMA 2020 (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta‐Analyses) guidelines (Page et al., 2021). The review protocol was registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO; registration ID: CRD420251113166).

Eligibility criteria

Following the PICOS framework (Amir‐Behghadami & Janati, 2020), eligible studies included randomized controlled trials (RCTs), prospective cohort studies or retrospective comparative studies that enrolled adult patients undergoing PPV for RRD. Studies were required to compare 360‐degree prophylactic laser retinopexy to focal or localized laser photocoagulation administered intra‐operatively. Only studies reporting at least one clinical outcome of interest were included. These outcomes comprised SSAS, best‐corrected visual acuity (BCVA), IOP or post‐operative complications such as ERM, PVR, cystoid macular oedema (CME) or macular hole.

Exclusion criteria encompassed case reports, single‐arm studies, letters, editorials, conference abstracts without full data and studies that did not report direct comparisons between 360‐degree and focal laser groups. Non‐human studies were also excluded. Only articles published in English were considered.

Information sources and search strategy

A comprehensive literature search was conducted across PubMed, Web of Science and Scopus databases from inception to June 27, 2025. The search strategy employed a combination of Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and free‐text terms related to retinal detachment, 360‐degree laser and focal laser therapy. The complete search syntax is available in the Table S1.

To ensure a thorough search, additional records were identified via manual screening of similar articles through PubMed's ‘similar articles function’ (Abdelaal et al., 2024) and citation tracking through Google Scholar (only the first 200 records were screened, as per recent recommendations) (Muka et al., 2020). Grey literature was also considered, where applicable.

Study selection

After the removal of duplicate entries using EndNote X8, reviewers (L.Y., A.A.) independently screened the titles and abstracts of the retrieved studies. Full texts of potentially eligible studies were subsequently reviewed to determine final inclusion. Discrepancies between reviewers were resolved through discussion, with the involvement of a third reviewer (A.T., J.O.) when necessary.

Data extraction and management

Two independent reviewers (L.Y., A.A.) extracted data using a standardized, pre‐piloted extraction form. Extracted variables included study design, country, year of publication, number of eyes, demographic data (age, sex), baseline clinical features (lens status, macular status, PVR) and surgical details (extent of laser, use of tamponade agents). Outcomes of interest and follow‐up duration were also recorded. Where necessary, authors were contacted to clarify missing or unclear data.

Risk of bias (ROB) assessment

The risk of bias in RCTs was assessed using the revised Cochrane Risk of Bias tool (ROB‐2), focusing on domains such as the randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data and outcome measurement (Sterne et al., 2019). For non‐randomized studies, the National Institute of Health (NIH) tool for cohort studies was used. The scoring of this tool has been described in the literature (Abu Serhan et al., 2022). All assessments were independently performed by two reviewers (L.Y., A.A.), with consensus reached through discussion.

Data synthesis and analysis

Meta‐analyses were conducted using STATA version 18.0 (StataCorp LLC, College Station, TX, USA). For dichotomous outcomes, odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated using the Mantel–Haenszel method (Somes, 1986). For continuous outcomes, mean differences (MDs) with corresponding 95% CIs were pooled using the inverse‐variance method. All analyses were conducted under a random‐effects model (REML method) to account for between‐study heterogeneity (Partlett & Riley, 2017). When statistical heterogeneity was low (I 2 < 50%), pooled estimates were calculated using a fixed‐effects model (Mantel–Haenszel method for dichotomous outcomes and inverse‐variance method for continuous outcomes).

Statistical heterogeneity was evaluated using the I 2 statistic and Cochran's Q test. An I 2 value exceeding 50% or a Q test p‐value <0.10 was considered indicative of substantial heterogeneity (Higgins & Thompson, 2002). Where heterogeneity was significant and at least five studies were available for a given outcome, meta‐regression analysis was performed to explore potential sources of variability (Spineli & Pandis, 2020). The covariates examined included study design (non‐randomized), mean age, percentage of male patients, pseudophakia rate, laterality (right eye involvement), rate of inferior retinal tears, presence of lattice degeneration, proportion with macula‐off detachment, baseline PVR of any grade, PVR grade C or higher, baseline BCVA and rates of tamponade agent use (C3F8 gas, SF6 gas, silicone oil).

Sensitivity analyses were conducted by excluding individual studies one at a time to assess the robustness of pooled estimates. Publication bias was evaluated using funnel plot symmetry and Egger's regression test for outcomes with 10 or more studies (Thornton & Lee, 2000).

Certainty of evidence assessment

The certainty of evidence for each outcome was evaluated using the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) framework (Huguet et al., 2013). This approach considers five domains: ROB, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias. Evidence from RCTs was initially rated as high certainty and could be downgraded based on methodological or reporting concerns. Observational studies were initially rated as low certainty and could be upgraded if they demonstrated a large magnitude of effect, dose–response gradient or if all plausible confounding would reduce a demonstrated effect.

For each outcome, we summarized the certainty of evidence as high, moderate, low or very low. The GRADE assessment was performed independently by two reviewers (L.Y., A.A.), with discrepancies resolved by discussion.

RESULTS

Literature search results

A total of 252 records were identified through electronic database searches (Figure 1), including PubMed (n = 85), Web of Science (n = 54) and Scopus (n = 113). After removing 106 duplicate records, 146 unique records remained for screening. Of these, 122 records were excluded following title and abstract screening, leaving 24 reports sought for retrieval. One full‐text article could not be retrieved, resulting in 23 reports assessed for eligibility. Following full‐text review, 10 studies were excluded due to being a case report (n = 1), involving irrelevant interventions or non‐comparable groups (n = 6) or lacking a comparison group (n = 1). Studies (n = 2) restricted to highly selected sub‐groups of RRD (e.g., eyes with undetected primary breaks only, or eyes undergoing post‐operative demarcation laser under silicone oil for recurrent detachment) were excluded, as these do not reflect routine intra‐operative prophylactic laser application during primary PPV for RRD (Zhou et al., 2017; Zhou & Qiu, 2015).

In parallel, 1379 additional records were identified through other sources (Google Scholar, citation searching, etc.). Among these, six reports were screened in full, but all were excluded as they had already been included in the primary database search. Ultimately, 13 studies met the inclusion criteria and were included in the systematic review and meta‐analysis (Avitabile et al., 2008; Bilgin et al., 2019; Cristescu et al., 2023; Dirani et al., 2020; Iwase et al., 2013; Koh et al., 2007; Loiudice et al., 2021; Mathai et al., 2022; Peters et al., 2022; Ryoo et al., 2022; Skevas et al., 2023; Stücheli et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2023).

Baseline characteristics of included studies

A summary table of included studies' characteristics is provided in Table 1. A total of 3639 eyes were examined, of which 1549 underwent the 360‐degree laser intervention, while 2090 eyes underwent localized/focal laser therapy. Treatment of primary RD was reported in eight studies (Bilgin et al., 2019; Cristescu et al., 2023; Dirani et al., 2020; Mathai et al., 2022; Peters et al., 2022; Ryoo et al., 2022; Stücheli et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2023), while the remaining studies included both primary and recurrent cases. The pooled mean patients' age was 62.90 years and males comprised 62.92% of the entire sample. In terms of baseline clinical characteristics, pseudophakia was reported in nine studies (Avitabile et al., 2008; Bilgin et al., 2019; Cristescu et al., 2023; Dirani et al., 2020; Loiudice et al., 2021; Mathai et al., 2022; Peters et al., 2022; Ryoo et al., 2022; Skevas et al., 2023; Zheng et al., 2023), with a pooled rate of 54.96%. Laterality was reported in eight studies (Bilgin et al., 2019; Cristescu et al., 2023; Loiudice et al., 2021; Mathai et al., 2022; Peters et al., 2022; Ryoo et al., 2022; Skevas et al., 2023; Stücheli et al., 2025) with a pooled rate of right eye involvement of 53.81%. Lattice degeneration was reported in five studies with a rate of 28.1%.

Methodological quality of included studies

All three included RCTs (Avitabile et al., 2008; Bilgin et al., 2019; Loiudice et al., 2021) showed some concerns using the ROB‐2 tool (Figure 2), mainly due to a lack of adequate information on randomization and outcome measurement, with the absence of a registered protocol a priori. In the remaining 10 observational studies (Cristescu et al., 2023; Dirani et al., 2020; Iwase et al., 2013; Koh et al., 2007; Mathai et al., 2022; Peters et al., 2022; Ryoo et al., 2022; Skevas et al., 2023; Stücheli et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2023), seven had good overall quality, while three studies had fair quality (Table 2), mainly due to a lack of confounding control and power calculation.

Primary endpoints

SSAS rate

Twelve studies were meta‐analysed, showing a significantly greater odds of anatomic success in the 360‐degree laser group compared to focal laser (OR = 1.82; 95% CI: 1.40–2.39, I 2 = 0%, p = 0.46, moderate certainty) (Figure 3). The funnel plot showed symmetry (Figure S1), and the Egger's regression test revealed no significant risk of publication bias (p = 0.4036). The meta‐regression analysis showed that none of the examined factors predicted the outcome (Table 4).

Secondary endpoints

BCVA at last follow‐up

Seven studies were meta‐analysed, revealing no significant difference in BCVA between both groups at the last follow‐up period (MD = 0.06; 95% CI: −0.01, 0.13, low certainty) (Figure S2). Heterogeneity was high (I 2 = 75.53%, p < 0.001). Meanwhile, the leave‐one‐out sensitivity analysis revealed a very minimal increase in BCVA in favour of the 360‐degree laser group when the study of (Loiudice et al., 2021) was excluded (MD = 0.09; 95% CI: 0.001–0.17) (Figure S3). A meta‐regression analysis showed that laterality (OD) was the sole predictor of this heterogeneity (coefficient = −0.015, p = 0.029) (Table 5).

DISCUSSION

This systematic review and meta‐analysis represents the most comprehensive comparative evaluation to date of 360‐degree prophylactic laser retinopexy versus localized laser photocoagulation during PPV for RRD. The findings suggest that, in appropriately selected patients, 360‐degree laser retinopexy is associated with significantly higher SSAS, without increasing the rates of post‐operative complications or impairing visual recovery. According to GRADE, the certainty of evidence supporting this anatomic association was moderate, reflecting consistent findings across studies but limited by the predominance of non‐randomized designs.

Following exclusion of studies restricted to highly selected sub‐groups, the present analysis reflects broader RRD populations undergoing PPV rather than cases limited to undetected primary breaks or post‐operative demarcation under silicone oil. The pooled estimate (OR: 1.82) demonstrated no statistical heterogeneity (I 2 = 0%), suggesting consistency of effect across varying surgical settings and patient characteristics. These findings support the hypothesis that circumferential laser retinopexy may reduce the risk of early re‐detachment, potentially by reinforcing peripheral retinal adhesion beyond identified breaks. However, given the observational nature of most included studies, residual confounding and selection bias cannot be excluded.

One clinical scenario frequently cited in surgical decision‐making is the inability to identify a definitive primary retinal break intra‐operatively (Gupta et al., 2018). In such cases, circumferential laser retinopexy is sometimes applied to mitigate the risk of missed peripheral breaks. Although studies restricted exclusively to undetected‐break populations were excluded from the present analysis, the theoretical rationale remains relevant in routine practise. Importantly, the included studies largely reflected broader RRD cohorts rather than exclusively occult‐break cases, suggesting that the observed association with improved anatomic success is not limited to this specific scenario. However, the present meta‐analysis does not allow determination of whether the magnitude of benefit differs between eyes with identified versus unidentified breaks, as such stratified data were inconsistently reported.

Earlier reviews had failed to demonstrate a consistent anatomical advantage. A 2021 meta‐analysis by (Soekamto et al., 2021) reported no significant overall difference in anatomical or functional outcomes between groups, although sub‐group analyses suggested a potential benefit in 23‐gauge surgery. By incorporating more recent comparative studies and applying formal meta‐regression techniques, the present analysis provides updated evidence suggesting a modest but statistically significant improvement in SSAS associated with 360‐degree laser application. Nevertheless, the certainty of evidence remains moderate, and further randomized trials are needed to confirm the magnitude of benefit.

Visual acuity outcomes, in contrast, were not significantly different between groups. The pooled MD in final BCVA was small and did not reach statistical significance, with substantial heterogeneity observed. Sensitivity analyses indicated that exclusion of Loiudice et al. (2021) resulted in a marginal statistical difference; however, the clinical relevance of this finding is uncertain. Meta‐regression identified laterality (right‐eye involvement) as a contributor to heterogeneity (p = 0.029), although the biological plausibility of this association remains unclear and may reflect study‐level imbalance rather than a causal relationship.

Importantly, prior analyses from included cohorts highlight the complexity of interpreting visual outcomes. For example, (Cristescu et al., 2023) reported that poorer final acuity in pseudophakic retinal detachment was independently associated with greater detachment extent, ocular comorbidities and use of 360‐degree laser; however, this association was no longer significant when macula‐off cases were excluded, suggesting confounding by baseline severity. These findings underscore that post‐operative visual recovery is multifactorial and may not directly mirror anatomical reattachment rates. Our analysis also evaluated BCVA improvement from baseline and IOP change at final follow‐up, neither of which differed significantly between groups. These findings support the safety profile of 360‐degree laser in terms of visual and pressure‐related endpoints, consistent with earlier reports (Iwase et al., 2013; Koh et al., 2007).

Post‐operative complications—including ERM, PVR, CME and macular hole—were comparable between groups. This is consistent with the large single‐centre study by Peters et al. (2022), who found no independent association between 360‐degree laser use and ERM, CME or other complications, despite its more frequent application in complex cases. The certainty of evidence for these complication outcomes was judged low‐to‐moderate, reflecting consistency across studies.

The pooled sample size for SSAS was substantial, increasing confidence in the precision of the anatomic estimate. In contrast, analyses of functional endpoints such as final BCVA and BCVA improvement were based on smaller sub‐sets of studies, resulting in wider CIs and lower certainty. These limitations may reduce the ability to detect modest differences in visual outcomes.

Regression analyses from multiple included studies support the hypothesis that the use of 360‐degree lasers is often guided by baseline risk. For instance, Peters et al. (2022) noted that male sex, worse pre‐operative vision, higher‐grade PVR and the use of silicone oil were independently associated with its application. In a separate study, Cristescu et al. (2023) found that primary re‐detachment was more strongly associated with perfluorocarbon liquid use than with the application of 360‐degree laser itself. These findings underscore the need to interpret outcomes in light of selection bias: surgeons may preferentially apply 360‐degree laser in more severe cases, potentially attenuating its perceived benefit in observational studies.

Beyond routine RRD repair, circumferential laser prophylaxis has demonstrated benefit in high‐risk hereditary conditions. A recent meta‐analysis by Camp et al. (2025) showed a marked reduction in retinal detachment risk in patients with Stickler syndrome undergoing laser prophylaxis compared with no treatment (RR = 0.23). Although that analysis did not address intra‐operative use during PPV, it provides biological plausibility for the concept that broad peripheral photocoagulation may reinforce retinal stability in susceptible eyes.

Overall, the present findings suggest that adjunctive 360‐degree laser retinopexy may confer a modest improvement in SSAS without a measurable increase in post‐operative complications. However, given the predominance of observational evidence and variability in surgical techniques, these results should be interpreted cautiously. High‐quality RCTs are needed to determine which patient sub‐groups derive the greatest benefit and to clarify the balance between incremental anatomic advantage and surgical efficiency.

Limitations

While this study provides robust pooled estimates, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, despite the inclusion of randomized and non‐randomized data, the majority of included studies were retrospective, with variable ROB in confounding control. Second, although meta‐regression accounted for many potential moderators, some confounders (e.g., surgeon skill, case complexity) could not be fully adjusted. Third, functional outcomes such as contrast sensitivity or quality of life were not available across studies. None of the included studies reported peripheral visual field outcomes, despite the known possibility of peripheral field loss or greying after extensive 360° laser retinopexy; this unmeasured safety consideration may influence surgical decision‐making and warrants further study.

Additionally, most included studies did not clearly report whether surgeons chose 360° versus focal laser based on the number or distribution of retinal breaks. Because only limited and heterogeneous data were available, we could not analyse outcomes stratified by break complexity. This may introduce residual confounding. A further limitation is that although baseline lens status and tamponade type were reported, outcome data were not stratified accordingly, preventing sub‐group analyses. Meta‐regression did not show these variables to significantly influence SSAS or BCVA, but residual confounding cannot be excluded. SSAS definitions did not clarify how residual peripheral SRF was classified, preventing assessment of whether such cases represent stable long‐term success. Operative reports rarely specified the 360° laser pattern (spot vs. confluent) or exact extent to the ora serrata; thus, the precise technique varied or was unreported and may affect generalizability.

An important consideration in interpreting our findings is the potential confounding introduced by adjunctive scleral buckling. While six studies in our meta‐analysis excluded such cases, two included patients who received buckling alongside PPV and laser treatment, with no stratified analysis by buckling status (Avitabile et al., 2008; Peters et al., 2022). Given the biomechanical and anatomic effects of scleral buckling, particularly its ability to support retinal reattachment independently of laser, it is possible that outcomes in these mixed cohorts may reflect the combined impact of both interventions. This highlights the need for future prospective studies comparing PPV + 360‐degree laser versus PPV + scleral buckle, ideally with pre‐defined sub‐group analyses to clarify their respective contributions to anatomic success and complication rates.

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, 360‐degree prophylactic laser retinopexy during PPV for RRD appears to enhance anatomic success, without compromising visual outcomes or increasing post‐operative complications. The certainty of evidence is moderate for anatomic outcomes and low to moderate for functional and safety outcomes. Given the predominance of observational data and potential confounding by surgical selection, these findings should be interpreted with caution. Well‐designed RCTs are required to confirm the magnitude of benefit and to determine whether specific patient sub‐groups derive additional advantage from routine 360‐degree laser application.

Correlation analysis of positive Alzheimer’s disease plasma biological markers with plasma immune cell and clinical characteristics in mild cognitive impairment patients in China
Authors: Lingling Wang, Penghui Zhang, Song Gao, Huiqin Liu, Xiaoting Qiu, Shuo Yang, Li Wei, Yanxia Li, Yijun Cai, Juan Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe aim of the present work was to investigate the correlation between positive Alzheimer’s disease (AD) plasma biological markers and plasma immune cell content as well as clinical characteristics in mild cognitive impairment (MCI) patients.MethodsA total of 176 patients aged ≥ 65 years with MCI were followed up for two years. At baseline, AD plasma biomarkers (Aβ42, Aβ40, Aβ42/40, P-tau181 and PTau217) and immune cells (Treg cells, TH17 cells, lymphocytes) in MCI patients were detected, and their relevant clinical characteristics were recorded. Finally, the correlation between positive AD plasma biomarkers and immune cells, relevant clinical characteristics of MCI patients, and whether they would progress was analyzed.ResultsPlasma Aβ42/40 levels are negatively correlated with age, a history of hypertension, constipation symptoms, and the TGF-β/Treg cell ratio. They are positively correlated with the TIM3+/Treg cell ratio, the number of CD19-positive cells, the CD4/CD8 ratio, and the number of CD8-positive cells. Plasma PTau217 levels are positively correlated with age, the proportion of B cells, and the proportion of NK cells, and negatively correlated with visual-spatial executive function and attention cognitive domain scores. Dual abnormalities of Aβ42/40 and PTau217 are positively correlated with age and the proportion of B cells, and negatively correlated with cognitive domain scores for executive ability, attention, and language ability.ConclusionAbnormalities in AD plasma biomarkers are associated with immune activation (particularly B cells and NK cells), aging, cardiovascular and gastrointestinal risk factors, and cognitive decline. The concurrent abnormalities of multiple biomarkers may exacerbate these associations, although some immune regulatory pathways do not show significant effects.Keywords: Alzheimer’s disease, Mild cognitive impairment, Plasma biomarkers, Immune

Introduction

Alzheimer’s disease (AD), the most common form of dementia, poses a significant and escalating global challenge [1]. The pathology of AD involves the abnormal buildup of amyloid-beta (Aβ) and tau proteins. Previous research has indicated that cerebrospinal fluid(CSF) analysis or positron emission tomography (PET) imaging to detect Aβ and tau proteins serves as the gold standard for diagnosing AD [2]. Recently, plasma Aβ1–42, Aβ1–40, ptau181, and ptau217 have emerged as alternative methods to CSF or PET for detecting Aβ and tau proteins in the pathological diagnosis of AD [3]. Despite a pathological diagnosis of AD may precede clinical detection, individuals often experience a latency period of several years, sometimes more than a decade, before showing clinical symptoms. This delay is thought to result from a combination of genetic, inflammatory, and environmental factors [4]. In fact, the current understanding of AD pathology includes multiple hypotheses, such as cholinergic dysfunction, amyloid accumulation, tau protein abnormalities, neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, imbalances in metal ion homeostasis, glutamate excitotoxicity, interactions within the microbial-gut-brain axis, and disruptions in autophagy [1].

In recent years, neuroinflammation has emerged as a key pathological mechanism in AD. Several prior studies have demonstrated that pharmacologically modulating neuroinflammation may help alleviate AD symptoms, primarily by regulating neuroinflammatory signaling pathways such as NF-κB, MAPK, NLRP3, and their associated cascades [5]. The role of neuroinflammation in disease progression has emerged as a significant research focus. Microglia and astrocytes are pivotal in mediating central nervous system immune responses [6]. Furthermore, inhibitors, natural products, and metabolites that target these inflammatory patterns have undergone extensive research. Many of these substances have demonstrated significant pharmacological efficacy and promising clinical applications [5]. In the current phase where the clinical efficacy of drugs targeting Aβ, such as lecanemab and donanemab, remains under verification in practical applications, the inflammatory mechanism has garnered increasing attention. Notably, peripheral immunity serves a protective function in AD, with regulatory T cells (Tregs) and monocytes emerging as promising targets for immunotherapeutic approaches [7].

Immune cells will undergo metabolic pattern re-adjustments due to pathological changes related to Alzheimer’s disease, and will exhibit different immune characteristics in the central or peripheral regions [8].Analyzing the relationship between plasma markers (such as Aβ and PTau, etc.) and immune cells can link peripheral immunity with central pathology and plasma markers can serve as a bridge [9, 10]Currently, there is the Aβ monoclonal antibody therapy, but its effect is limited [11, 12]. Understanding the correlation between plasma markers and immune cells can help identify new therapeutic targets or screen patients suitable for immunotherapy. Furthermore, the immune characteristics of different racial groups may vary [13, 14]. The correlation between AD plasma markers and immune cells also differs among different populations [15, 16]. Exploring the relationship between these two in the Chinese population is of great significance, laying the foundation for providing more precise treatment for AD patients of different races in the future.

Although we know that immunity is closely related to AD, there are relatively few reports on the interrelationship between basic immune cells, regulatory immune cells, and factors in patients with MCI. MCI is a special stage in the course of AD dementia. Exploring the pathophysiology of MCI is of great significance for understanding AD。.

In our study, we observe the correlation of AD plasma biomarkers and basic lymphocyte subpopulation indicators (lymphocyte subpopulation CD3, lymphocyte subpopulation CD4, lymphocyte subpopulation CD8, CD4/CD8 ratio, lymphocyte subpopulation CD19, NK cell) and Regulatory T cells/ Th17 cells (CD4⁺CD25⁺FOXP3⁺/CD4⁺ ratio, the correlations of TIM3⁺/Treg ratio, PD-1⁺/Treg ratio, TGF-β/Treg ratio, IL-10⁺/Treg ratio, IL-17 A⁺/CD4⁺ ratio), meanwhile, we also observed the relationship between AD plasma biomarkers and clinical characteristics.

Materials and methods

Subject recruitment

All participants were recruited from the local community. In 2019, we established a community-based cohort focusing on cognitive function among individuals aged 65 and older [17]. Annually, individuals aged ≥ 65 years voluntarily join the program. The current cohort comprises 400 participants diagnosed with MCI. Enrollment for this study began on September 20, 2024. Our team aimed to include all MCI patients who voluntarily participated in physical examinations and provided informed consent. Follow-up assessments were also conducted on a voluntary basis. Cognitive function evaluations were performed by six physicians trained by the Shanghai Mental Health Center and certified in cognitive assessment.

Exclusion criteria included: Parkinson-related diseases, stroke history, bacterial infections, trauma, hearing loss, poisoning, mental illness, or alcoholism (assessed via medical history review). Other diseases associated with cognitive impairment (identified through routine hematological testing and MRI). MCI diagnostic criteria and MCI progression standards

In our study, MCI was defined by the following criteria: (1) cognitive concern or complaint expressed by the subject, informant, nurse, or physician, with a Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR) less than 0.5; (2) objective impairment in at least one cognitive domain, evidenced by performance 1.5 standard deviations below the mean, as determined by norms from the pilot study; (3) essentially normal functional activities, as assessed by both the CDR and the Activities of Daily Living (ADL) evaluation; (4) absence of dementia, as diagnosed according to the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM)-IV. The participants were screened by MoCA, with the score of 23 being the threshold of normal and MCI. MCI progression was judged by Cognitive Function Scale (CFS) scores ≥ 1 were defined as progression group (PG) [18], otherwise, it was judged as non-progression group(N-PG).

Methods for detection of basic lymphocyte subpopulation indicators and functional T cell subsets and related factor indicators

By stimulating activated T cells in vitro with PMA and ionomycin, and blocking the secreted cytokines within the cells using protein transport inhibitors, the intracellular cytokines were labeled with fluorescent antibodies and then detected using a flow cytometer to achieve the purpose of evaluating cell function.

Methods for detection of AD plasma biomarkers

The plasma ptau181, ptau217, Aβ 42, and Aβ 40 kits we used were from Vazyme Medical Technology Co., Ltd., China, which have been approved by Chinese National Medical Products Administration (NMPA). Thus, the cut-off points of AD plasma biomarkers were derived from the manufacturer’s manual.

Statistical analysis

SPSS Statistics 25 statistical software ((SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL) was used in our study. The chi-square test was used to assess the differences between different subgroups in terms of gender, smoking, drinking alcohol, and the diseases of diabetes and insomnia; T-test, to assess the differences of age, plasma indexes; Regression analysis, to evaluate the correlation.

Results

Demographic analysis

A total of 176 MCI participants aged ≥ 65 years underwent assessment. We also observed AD plasma biomarkers (Aβ42/40, pTau217 and pTau181) and basic lymphocyte subpopulation indicators (lymphocyte subpopulation CD3, lymphocyte subpopulation CD4, lymphocyte subpopulation CD8, CD4/CD8 ratio, lymphocyte subpopulation CD19, NK cell), as well as functional T cell subsets and related factor indicators (CD4⁺CD25⁺FOXP3⁺/CD4⁺ ratio, TIM3⁺/Treg ratio, PD-1⁺/Treg ratio, TGF-β/Treg ratio, IL-10⁺/Treg ratio, IL-17 A⁺/CD4⁺ ratio). Additionally, we examined the relationship between AD plasma biomarkers and clinical characteristics, including gender, age, smoking, drinking, hypertension, diabetes, constipation, insomnia, progression or no progression, visual-spatial execution function, naming, attention, language, word delayed recall, and orientation.

In our study, all MCI patients were categorized into the Aβ42/40 abnormal group(n = 72,cut off value is 0.05) and the PTau217 abnormal group(n = 104, cut off value is 5pg/ml). As shown in Table 1, the proportion of NK cells in both the Aβ42/40 and PTau217 abnormal groups was significantly higher than that in the normal group(P < 0.05). The proportion of patients with hypertension, the proportion of people with insomnia, and the proportion of MCI progression in the Aβ42/40 abnormal group were significantly higher than those in the normal group(P < 0.05). In the PTau217 abnormal group, the attention cognitive domain of the MCI population was significantly lower than that in the normal group(P < 0.05).

Discussions

The etiology of AD is multifaceted, with key contributing factors including inflammation, oxidative stress, genetic and epigenetic variations, and cerebrovascular abnormalities [19]. Neuroinflammation has emerged as a pivotal factor in the pathogenesis of AD. Increasing evidence underscores the interaction between the peripheral immune system and the central nervous system (CNS) in driving neuroinflammation, with T lymphocytes playing a critical role in both regulatory and effector functions [20, 21]. Moreover, Tregs, which play a crucial role in maintaining immune tolerance and exhibit neuroprotective effects in various diseases, including animal models of AD demonstrate significant therapeutic potential [22]. There have been few studies on which clinical characteristics are associated with different biological indicators of AD.

Our study found that MCI patients in the abnormal groups of Aβ42/40 and PTau217 exhibited significant differences from the normal group across multiple clinical features, indicating that these two biomarkers may reflect distinct pathological mechanisms and clinical manifestations. NK cell is significantly higher in both abnormal groups compared to the normal group, suggesting that NK cell might be associated with the pathological process of MCI and could potentially serve as a biomarker for MCI. Previous studies have shown that after intervention for MCI, there were significant differences in NK cells before and after the intervention, which also indirectly supports our conclusion [23]. however, the specific mechanism requires further investigation. In the Aβ42/40 abnormal group, the proportions of hypertension, insomnia, and MCI progression were significantly higher than those in the normal group, implying that the Aβ42/40 ratio can serve as a predictive indicator for the progression of MCI, a few studies have shown that plasma Aβ42/40 and PTau217 are associated with the progression of MCI [24, 25], and study by Sarasa et al. have demonstrated that Aβ42/40 is valuable for identifying patients with a-MCI and predicting dementia progression [26]. This supports our conclusion. However, our study did not find a correlation between PTau217 and progression. This might be due to the fact that the pathological occurrence of PTau217 is later than that of Aβ, or it could be caused by the small sample size of our study.

In PTau217 anomaly group, the cognitive domain of attention was significantly lower than that of the normal group, suggesting that patients in this group may have more obvious cognitive impairments in terms of attention. Previous study found that dynamic changes in plasma pTau217 are suitable biomarkers for monitoring AD pathology, and their variations are associated with cognitive decline and clinical progression [27], so, based on our research results, it is suggested that in clinical disease progression, special attention should be paid to the decline in attention in the cognitive domain. Different from our study, some studies have found that p-tau217 is associated with the decline of semantic/episoological memory [28]. The reason for the analysis of the difference is that their included populations include the normal population, the dementia population and the MCI population, while our study only included the MCI population. In the future, it is necessary to further expand the population size for exploration and analysis.

Our study found plasma Aβ42/40 is negatively correlated with these factors (age, hypertension, constipation), suggesting that these factors may inhibit the clearance of Aβ42/40 in Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF) and enhanced Aβ42/40 in plasma. Previous study suggested that hypertension may be related to the neuropathological changes of AD [29], and there is a significant correlation between constipation and the rapid progression of AD symptoms [30], and it is worth noting that our research further found that plasma Aβ42/40 is closely related to hypertension and constipation.

Aβ42/40 was negatively correlated with TGF- β /Treg, and positively correlated with TIM3+/Treg, CD19, CD4/CD8 and CD8, suggesting that Aβ42/40 may affect the pathological process of MCI by regulating the function of immune cells. PTau217 is positively correlated with CD19 and NKcell, suggesting that PTau217 may be involved in the pathological process of MCI by affecting the function of immune cells. As for the relationship between Aβ42/40 or PTau217 and immune cells, no similar studies have been published so far. PTau217 is negatively correlated with visuospatial executive function and attention, suggesting that PTau217 may directly damage these cognitive domains, previous studies have shown that plasma PTau217/Aβ42 may be a potential biomarker for the occurrence of cognitive impairments in specific cognitive domains in patients with Alzheimer’s disease [31]. Studies have investigated the relationship between PTau217 and cognitive trajectories of memory, attention, language, executive function, and overall cognition and the results showed that pTau217 is related to the overall cognitive progress [27]. However, we only observed its correlation with visuospatial executive function and attention.

MCI patients with ‌ abnormalities of both Aβ42/40 and PTau217 exhibit the most significant clinical features ‌, including older age, higher CD19 cell count, higher proportion of constipation, and significant decline in attention, executive function and language ability. This suggests that this combination may represent a high-risk subtype of MCI and requires priority intervention. The age and CD19 cell count were significantly higher than those in the non-abnormal group, suggesting that the immune system might be involved in the synergistic pathological process of Aβ and P-tau. The proportion of constipation is higher, which may be related to intestinal flora imbalance or autonomic nerve dysfunction. The significant decline in attention, executive function and language ability suggests that this combination causes more comprehensive cognitive impairment.

In PTau217 and P-tau181 double anomaly group ‌, the age was significantly higher, but there was no significant difference in the proportions of CD19 and constipation, suggesting that P-tau181 may be associated with age-related neurodegenerative changes. PTau217 is positively correlated with CD19 and NK cell, suggesting that B cells and NK cells may be involved in the pathological process of P-tau. The cognitive impairment was most significant in the group with dual abnormalities of Aβ42/40 and PTau217, suggesting that the synergistic effect of the two might accelerate neurodegeneration.

When we analyzed the correlation between the biological and immune indicators of AD, the results did not show a significant correlation with Treg and Th17. This was quite disappointing to us. Perhaps in the future, we can increase the sample size to conduct further exploration.

Conclusions

We observe AD plasma biomarkers and basic lymphocyte subpopulation indicators and functional T cell subsets and related factor indicators, meanwhile we also observed the relationship between AD plasma biomarkers and clinical characteristics. Our study found that there are significant differences in clinical characteristics, immune regulation and cognitive function between the abnormal groups of Aβ42/40 and PTau217. The double abnormal group (especially Aβ42/40 + PTau217) represents a high-risk subtype of MCI and requires priority intervention. Immune cells (such as CD19 + B cells and CD56 + NK cells) may be involved in pathological processes, providing potential directions for future targeted therapy.

Limitations

Our research is a small-sample cohort study of a community. Due to the relatively small sample size, some of the results may still require further investigation with a larger sample size in the future.

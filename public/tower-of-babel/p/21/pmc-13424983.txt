Development and preliminary validation of an entrustable professional activities framework for undergraduate midwifery education
Authors: Yu Liu, Xuebing Zheng, Shaohua Chen, Binbin Lin, Lei Fan, Yunfeng Jiang, Xiaoyan Wang, Qin Xu, Guiru Xu, Pingping Qiu, Xiaoyun Lin, Yan Fang, Jihong Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aimed to develop and preliminary validate an entrustable professional activities (EPAs) framework for undergraduate midwifery students in China, providing a reference for undergraduate midwifery education and competency assessment.MethodsAn initial EPA framework was constructed through a systematic literature review, qualitative research, and participant observation. From January to March 2022, semi-structured interviews were conducted with 27 stakeholders, followed by participant observation involving six individuals from April to May 2022. Subsequently, two rounds of Delphi consultation were conducted from June to October 2022 with 24 experts from 11 tertiary hospitals and six universities offering midwifery programs across eight provinces in China. Experts evaluated the importance, appropriateness, and entrustment level settings of each item, and the framework was revised accordingly. The finalized framework was then converted into a questionnaire and administered to undergraduate midwifery interns from three universities in Fujian Province to conduct a preliminary validation of its application.ResultsResponse rates for the two Delphi rounds were 87.5 % and 83.3 %, respectively. Kendall’s coefficients of concordance were 0.126 and 0.147 (P < 0.001), and expert authority coefficients (Cr) were 0.864 and 0.873. The finalized framework comprised three confidence levels, 11 core EPAs, and 60 nested EPAs. In the preliminary validation phase, 81 valid questionnaires were collected. The overall Cronbach’s α coefficient was 0.967, and the test–retest reliability (ICC) was 0.675, indicating good internal consistency and stability.ConclusionThe developed EPAs framework for undergraduate midwifery students demonstrates sound scientific rigor and feasibility and may provide theoretical and practical guidance for reforming undergraduate midwifery education and evaluating competency in China.Keywords: Competence, Education, Entrustable professional activities, Midwifery, Nursing student

Introduction

Enhancing maternal and child health remains a critical focus of global public health initiatives [1]. Midwives play a pivotal role in safeguarding maternal and newborn health, contributing substantially to reducing maternal and neonatal mortality rates as well as stillbirths [2,3]. The 2021 State of Midwifery in the World Report emphasized that investment in quality midwifery services could prevent up to 4.3 million deaths annually by 2035 [3]. Countries worldwide have accelerated efforts to improve midwifery education, aiming to enhance the quality of services delivered by midwives [4]. Undergraduate midwifery education has emerged as a key strategy in preparing professional midwives, with student competency directly influencing the effectiveness and quality of midwifery teams [5].

Competency encompasses knowledge, skills, attitudes, and values that professionals must demonstrate when engaging in relevant activities [6]. Competency-based medical education (CBME) emphasizes aligning training goals with workplace requirements, thereby ensuring that students develop the abilities necessary to perform effectively in professional practice [7]. The International Confederation of Midwives (ICM) has developed guidelines for professional competencies in midwifery practice and standards for midwifery education [8]. Building on these guidelines, competency-based undergraduate midwifery education seeks to equip students with comprehensive theoretical knowledge and practical skills, enabling them to manage both routine childbirth and complex obstetric situations after graduation [9,10]. In several developed countries, such as Australia, competency-based midwifery education has long been embedded within degree programs through sustained clinical training, supporting graduates’ readiness for autonomous practice [11]. In China, many universities have established or upgraded midwifery undergraduate courses in response to the national initiatives to strengthen maternal and child health services. These four-year programs increasingly align with ICM standards by incorporating evidence-based practice and extensive clinical internships [12].

Assessing student competence presents challenges because valid evaluation requires structured tools such as direct observation, workplace-based assessments, and supervisor ratings. Traditional examinations in China’s midwifery programs still focus primarily on knowledge and technical skills, often neglecting essential competencies such as communication, clinical reasoning, judgment, and teamwork [13,14]. Observation methods also lack standardized criteria, which undermines fairness and reliability [15]. Moreover, current systems frequently reduce performance to simple grades, numerical scores, or quality labels. While supervisor ratings can differentiate learners, they are insufficient for determining readiness for independent clinical practice [16]. These limitations highlight the need for a comprehensive framework that explicitly links observable, independently performed professional activities to competencies, thereby enabling a more authentic and context-specific assessment of students' readiness for midwifery practice.

Entrustable professional activities (EPAs) provide a stronger evaluation framework within CBME and were first proposed by ten Cate [17] to address challenges in competency assessment in clinical training. As measurable, discrete tasks, EPAs represent specific professional activities that can be directly observed and assessed, thereby linking theoretical knowledge with practical application in a structured manner [18,19]. Once trainees demonstrate the ability to perform an activity independently, they may be entrusted to carry it out in clinical settings. Establishing a shared evaluation framework among supervisors enhances the clarity of observations, standardizes authorization processes, and promotes consistency across evaluators. Completing an EPA requires integration of multiple skills, allowing educators to assess students’ clinical competence comprehensively during internships [20]. The Association of American Medical Colleges (AAMC) pioneered the development of 13 EPAs to assess medical students’ readiness for residency [21]. Since then, EPAs have been widely applied in diverse medical fields, including pharmacy [22], pediatrics [23], and nutrition [24]. These assessments cover multiple domains, such as attitudes, knowledge, and behaviors [25], with approximately 63.4 % of studies referencing the AAMC’s 13 EPA framework [26]. Despite their broad application, few studies have specifically addressed EPAs for undergraduate midwifery students. The present study aimed to construct an EPA framework tailored to undergraduate midwifery students, integrating the ICM competency framework and applying the Delphi method to quantify clinical competencies and provide a foundation for training and evaluation of midwifery graduates.

Each EPA is defined by structured elements, including its name, purpose, potential risks, relevant competency domains, required knowledge, skills, and attitudes; assessment information sources; target entrustment levels at different training stages; and expected timelines [18]. Entrustment levels—ranging from not permitted to perform, to direct or indirect supervision, to independent practice, and finally to supervising juniors—serve as key indicators of learner autonomy and patient safety [20,27]. EPAs may be organized as core EPAs, which represent essential tasks every graduate must master, and nested EPAs, which subdivide these tasks into smaller, context-specific activities. Guided by these principles, the present study combined the ICM competency framework with expert consensus through the Delphi method to identify core EPAs for undergraduate midwifery students and establish their appropriate entrustment levels.

Methods

Study design

To ensure rigor and transparency, the methodology adhered to the revised Conducting and Reporting of Delphi Studies (CREDES) guidelines [28]. This study was conducted in three phases. 1) Pre-research: a preliminary draft of the core and nested EPAs was developed through a comprehensive literature review, semi-structured interviews, and participatory observations. 2) Delphi stage: building on the initial draft, a Delphi questionnaire was formulated, and two rounds of Delphi surveys were conducted to reach a consensus. 3) Preliminary validation: an EPA-based questionnaire was developed from the framework and administered to undergraduate midwifery students to evaluate its preliminary applicability.

Ethical consideration

This study received ethical approval from the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University ([2025]634).

Establishing the research team

A research team comprising nine members was established, including three full-time midwifery faculty members (one professor, one associate professor, and one lecturer), who were responsible for methodological guidance, data analysis, Delphi expert selection, and quality control; two practicing midwives (one associate chief nurse and one supervisor nurse) and one obstetrician (chief physician), who provided professional expertise and domain-specific guidance; and three postgraduate students, who conducted the literature review synthesis, qualitative interviews, Delphi questionnaire distribution, statistical analyses, and developed the preliminary EPAs indicator framework. All team members jointly participated in designing the Delphi questionnaire, interpreting the findings, and finalizing the EPA framework for undergraduate midwifery students.

Developing the initial EPAs framework

Literature review

Relevant studies were identified via Medical Subject Headings (MeSH terms) and free text terms, including “midwifery/midwife/nurse midwife” and “entrustable professional activities/EPAs”. Searches were conducted across multiple databases, including PubMed, CINAHL, Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, ProQuest Dissertation, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wan Fang, and VIP Chinese Journal Database, covering the period from January 2005 to December 2021. The literature included mixed-methods studies, systematic reviews, longitudinal studies, randomized controlled trials, and cohort studies. Discussion articles, abstracts, editorials, and studies without full-text availability were excluded. All identified studies were imported into EndNote X21 for verification and screening, and eligible studies were selected for further analysis. The research team assessed each article for quality and relevance (Appendix A). Additionally, laws, regulations, policy documents, undergraduate textbooks, and competency models related to midwifery in China were reviewed.

Qualitative interviews

Conducting qualitative interviews with stakeholders, combined with the results of the literature review, jointly construct an initial draft of the EPAs framework based on the results of the literature review and initially identify the core and nested items. From January to March 2022, stakeholders were recruited for semi-structured interviews. Through purposeful sampling, midwifery managers, clinical midwives, gynecologists, full-time midwifery teachers, midwifery interns, and pregnant women were selected as participants. Inclusion and exclusion criteria, as well as the interview schedules, are summarized in Appendix B. Interviews were conducted in a quiet, private setting, with participants informed about EPAs and their confidence levels beforehand. Written informed consent was obtained for audio recording and transcription. Interviews lasted approximately 20–30 min and were transcribed verbatim. Qualitative data were analyzed using content analysis, with transcripts independently coded by two researchers (X. Zheng and L. Fan) using NVivo 12. Themes were discussed collaboratively, and discrepancies were resolved through consultation with a third researcher (J. Song) or the full research team. Twenty-seven stakeholders were interviewed, including six midwifery managers, ten clinical midwives, two obstetricians and gynecologists, one full-time midwifery teacher, three undergraduate midwifery interns, and five pregnant women. Experts had a age of 40.84 ± 9.09 years and a median of 17 years of professional experience. As a result, interview data were synthesized into four main themes: expected core professional activities for undergraduate midwifery interns; confidence levels and supervision in clinical practice; essential competencies for safe midwifery care; and service users’ expectations of midwifery practice. These themes informed the development of the initial EPAs framework.

Participatory observation

Midwives, undergraduate midwifery interns, and pregnant women were selected as participants through objective sampling at a tertiary maternal and child hospital in Fujian Province. Before observation, the participants were briefed on the study’s objectives, signed informed consent forms, provided personal information, and agreed on the timing and location of the observation. During the observation, the researcher acted as an impartial observer, attentively documenting the participants’ speech and behavior. Observations continued until data saturation was achieved, after which the results were verified with the participants. The researchers, who had practiced in the delivery room for more than four months, were able to minimize potential biases and ensure that professional behaviors were accurately captured. This extended clinical experience reduced observation errors and enhanced data reliability. To ensure accuracy, the results were coded using NVivo 12 by researchers X. Zheng and J. Song. A total of six participatory observations were conducted, lasting about 32 h and resulting in an observation manuscript exceeding 27,000 words. The participant characteristics are detailed in Appendix C.

Description of the initial entrustable professional activities framework

Twenty-one documents met the inclusion criteria, comprising five research articles, ten textbooks, and six legal documents. Details of the literature are presented in Appendix D. Three themes were extracted via content analysis, including the setting of the confidence level, the competency range required by EPAs, and the elements of the EPA framework. Based on the literature review and qualitative interviews, 5 confidence levels, 11 core EPAs, and 59 nested EPAs were initially established. We then verified and supplemented the first draft of the core EPAs through participatory observation in real clinical situations. A total of 37 nested items were observed, and 22 nested items were not observed. After discussion with team members, core and nested items were not deleted at this time.

Delphi stage

Development of the expert consultation questionnaire

The EPA research team designed the consultation questionnaire and comprises three sections: 1) an explanatory letter detailing the study’s background, purpose, and researcher contact information; 2) expert personal information, including general details (e.g., age, education, work experience) and criteria for assessing familiarity with and judgment of core EPA items; and 3) the main body, which focuses on evaluating core EPA items for undergraduate midwifery students. The experts rated the importance and applicability of the items on a 5-point Likert scale ranging from “5 = very important and appropriate” to “1 = very unimportant and inappropriate” and provided additional suggestions.

Expert selection

Purposive sampling was used to recruit managers, nurses, educators, and physicians specializing in midwifery from a range of institutions, including tertiary hospitals, gynecology and obstetrics hospitals, and universities offering midwifery programs across the country from July to October 2022. The inclusion criteria for the experts were as follows: 1) a bachelor’s degree or higher; 2) a minimum of 10 years of work experience with substantial expertise in management, clinical practice, or teaching; 3) a technical title of intermediate level or higher; and 4) willingness to participate in expert consultation. The panel included 25 experts from 17 institutions located in 8 provinces.

Implementation of the expert consultation

Consultation questionnaires were distributed by researcher X. Zheng via email or in paper form, with reminders sent to non-respondents after two weeks. Findings from the first-round informed revisions to the questionnaire through item addition, deletion, or modification, followed by a second round of consultation. Two rounds of this modified Delphi process were used to refine the final EPA framework and its items.

Preliminary validation

Participant

Undergraduate midwifery interns were recruited by convenience sampling from three universities offering midwifery programs in Fujian Province. Inclusion criteria were: 1) fourth-year undergraduate students majoring in midwifery; 2) completion of more than 20 weeks of clinical internship; and 3) willingness to participate in the questionnaire survey. Exclusion criterion was a questionnaire completion time of less than 1 min.

Questionnaire

Sociodemographic characteristics

A general information form was developed by the research team, including items on date of birth, gender, duration of the clinical internship (in weeks), and completed clinical rotations.

Self-assessment questionnaire on entrustable professional activities

Based on the EPA framework, the 60 nested EPA items were converted into a self-assessment questionnaire. Students were asked to rate the level of entrustment for each item based on its description.

Data collection

Before questionnaire distribution, the internship rotation schedules at the three universities were reviewed to identify eligible internship groups. After confirming eligibility, contact was made with group leaders, and the questionnaire was distributed online. To evaluate test–retest reliability, a randomly selected subsample completed the questionnaire again after a two-week interval.

Statistical analysis

The study utilized IBM SPSS (version 26, 2019) for data analysis. Categorical variables are described as frequencies and percentages. Continuous variables following a normal distribution were summarized using the mean and standard deviation (SD). In contrast, those not following a normal distribution were represented by the median (P25, P75). Expert engagement was assessed through the questionnaire recovery rate [29]. The authority coefficient (Cr) is used to judge the degree of expert authority, which is calculated by the arithmetic average of the judgment coefficient (Ca) and the familiarity coefficient (Cs) [formula: Cr = (Ca + Cs)/2]. The larger Cr is, the higher the expert authority coefficient, and Cr > 0.7 indicates acceptability [30]. The coefficient of variation (CV) and the coefficient of concordance (Kendall’s W) are used to assess the consistency of expert evaluation results, that is, whether there are large differences. The smaller the coefficient of variation, the higher the degree of coordination among experts for a given index; the larger W is, the better the degree of coordination [31].

The analytic hierarchy process [32] was applied in SPSS to calculate the weights for the core EPA items in the second Delphi, reflecting the degree of expert consensus on the item’s importance. Moreover, the random conformance (CR) is calculated. If CR < 0.10, the matrix consistency meets the requirements. Otherwise, the matrix needs to be adjusted. The inclusion criteria for items were a CV < 0.25 and a mean importance score >3.5. For items where the mean importance score was ≤3.5 but the CV was <0.25 or where the mean was >3.5 but the CV was ≥0.25, expert opinions were consulted to ensure that potentially significant items were not prematurely excluded [33]. Final decisions on inclusion were made after discussion by the research team. The modification criteria for the target confidence level required that at least 75 % of the experts agree on the appropriateness of an item. In the preliminary validation, Cronbach’s α coefficient and the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) were calculated to assess internal consistency and test–retest reliability. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Results of expert consultation

Delphi round 1

In the first round of the Delphi process, 25 questionnaires were distributed, yielding 21 responses, for a recovery rate of 84 %. Their mean age was 43.05 ± 6.51 years, with an average of 20.33 ± 6.48 years of professional experience. Four experts held doctoral degrees, 5 held master's degrees, and 12 held bachelor's degrees; 13 held senior professional titles (7 full and 6 associate) and 8 held intermediate titles. Experts with intermediate titles were experienced clinical professionals, with some in managerial roles such as head nurses or equivalent. Their expertise covered midwifery management (n = 8), clinical midwifery practice (n = 5), midwifery education (n = 6), and obstetrics and gynaecology (n = 2). Among the respondents, 14 experts provided about 128 suggestions for modification or supplementation, representing a feedback rate of 66.7 %. Cr value in the first Delphi round was 0.864, indicating high expert credibility [34]. Kendall’s coefficient of concordance showed statistically significant agreement among experts regarding confidence level, core items, and nested items (all P < 0.05). The confidence level ranged from 2.85 to 4.65, the SD ranged from 0.47 to 1.79, and the CV ranged from 0.10 to 0.63. For the core EPAs, the mean ranged from 4.38 to 4.81, the SD ranged from 0.40 to 0.80, and the CV ranged from 0.08 to 0.18. The nested EPAs had a mean range of 3.48–4.76, an SD of 0.44–1.42, and a CV of 0.12–0.38. Regarding the confidence levels, 3a, 3b, 4, and 5 were removed based on expert feedback, which indicated minimal distinction among 3a, 3b, and 2. Levels 4 and 5 were deemed unrealistic for undergraduates to achieve. The first-round Delphi results for the core and nested EPAs are presented in Appendix E. Modifications to the core EPAs involved phrasing adjustments for clarity, with no items added or removed. For the nested EPAs, references to “management of abnormalities in vaginal delivery” and “nurse–midwife coordination in medical procedures/examinations” were removed. Conversely, four new nested EPAs were added, including “preliminary identification and management of dystocia” and “screening and evaluation of evidence.” Following these adjustments, the second round of consultation was initiated.

Delphi round 2

During the second round, 24 questionnaires were distributed, and 20 were returned, yielding a recovery rate of 83 %. Due to the non-participation of 4 experts from the first round in the second round, 3 additional experts were recruited, resulting in a total of 20 experts who completed the second Delphi round. Their mean age was 46.50 ± 6.21 years, with an average of 23.80 ± 7.26 years of professional experience. Among them, 4 held doctoral degrees, 5 held master's degrees, and 11 held bachelor's degrees; 14 held senior professional titles and 6 held intermediate titles (all with extensive clinical experience or in managerial roles). Their areas of expertise covered midwifery management (n = 10), clinical midwifery practice (n = 2), midwifery education (n = 6), and obstetrics and gynaecology (n = 2). In this round, 11 experts contributed about 79 suggestions for revision or supplementation, reflecting a feedback rate of 55 %. Expert credibility remained high (Cr = 0.873). Kendall's coefficient confirmed good agreement among experts across confidence levels and EPA items (all P < 0.05), with detailed W and χ2 values shown in Table 1. The confidence level had a mean range of 3.85–4.63, an SD of 0.50–1.39, and a CV of 0.11–0.36. For the core EPAs, the mean ranged from 4.20 to 4.85, the SD from 0.37 to 1.06, and the CV from 0.08 to 0.25. The nested EPAs had a mean range of 3.68–4.89, an SD of 0.42–1.36, and a CV of 0.09–0.36. Experts recommended improving the completeness and precision of EPAs to quantify each confidence level better, as shown in Table 2. No core EPAs were added or removed; however, two items were reworded for clarity, and three items were rearranged. Although some experts suggested removing items that did not meet the CV inclusion criteria, the research team retained them, considering their integral role in midwifery practice and core competencies. Regarding nested EPAs, four items were removed due to redundancy with other items. Three new tasks were added: oxygen inhalation, non-pharmacological labor analgesia techniques, and episiotomy and suturing. Additionally, the wording of 15 items was refined for greater clarity and specificity. The finalized EPAs framework for undergraduate midwifery students comprised three confidence levels, 11 core EPAs, and 60 nested EPAs (Appendix F). The matrix linking 11 core EPAs with ICM competencies is provided in Appendix G. The final EPAs framework and definitions are detailed in Appendix H, with further elaboration in Appendix I.

Preliminary validation results

Eighty-one questionnaires were collected, all of which were valid (response rate = 100 %). The mean age of the interns was 22.33 ± 1.51 years, and the internship duration ranged from 20 to 22 weeks. Overall, Cronbach’s α coefficient of the questionnaire was 0.967, with values for EPA1–EPA11 ranging from 0.851 to 0.948. A subsample of 37 interns completed the questionnaire again, on average 15 days after the initial administration, to assess test–retest reliability. The overall ICC for all items was 0.675, while ICCs for EPA1–EPA11 ranged from 0.129 to 0.682. ICC values for EPA3, EPA5, and EPA8 exceeded 0.60 (P < 0.05), indicating acceptable stability.

Discussion

Rigor and reliability of the entrustable professional activities framework

CBME emphasizes aligning training with the practical demands of professional roles, focusing on essential abilities required for effective performance [7]. This study developed an EPA framework tailored to undergraduate midwifery students, grounded in the ICM’s professional guidelines [8] and the AAMC’s recommended EPA framework [21], to systematically evaluate clinical competence. The construction process adhered to the 12 recommendations for EPA development [35], ensuring methodological rigor. The experts recruited for this study were drawn from eight provinces and represented midwifery management, clinical practice, midwifery education, and obstetrics and gynaecology, ensuring diversity in geographical location, professional background, and occupational role. All experts had extensive experience in their respective fields and demonstrated a high level of professional expertise. The high response rates in the two Delphi rounds (84 % and 83 %) demonstrated strong engagement and commitment among experts [36]. The Cr values exceeded 0.85 in both rounds, and the statistically significant Kendall’s W further underscored the scientific validity and reliability of the findings, as well as the credibility of the expert panel [34]. Together, these methodological safeguards indicate that the developed EPAs framework is supported by robust expert consensus and statistical reliability.

Comprehensiveness and specialty orientation of the entrustable professional activities framework

Inclusion of diverse stakeholders—doctors, pregnant women, and students—in qualitative interviews provided multidimensional insights, while participant observation enhanced authenticity and patient-centered perspectives. Although 22 nested EPAs were not observed, this may be attributed to the rarity of certain clinical situations (e.g., hyperglycemia/hypoglycemia, acute abdominal pain, or umbilical cord prolapse), the difficulty of observing tasks such as psychosocial assessment and high-risk identification, and limited access to specific settings such as operating rooms. The finalized framework comprises three confidence levels, 11 core EPAs, and 60 nested EPAs. Although this number exceeds the 20–40 EPAs commonly reported in previous studies [37], broader coverage ensures inclusion of routine midwifery care and nursing process activities [38]. This comprehensive structure reduces the risk of overlooking non-participatory behaviors and is consistent with recommendations to integrate clinical activities within overarching EPAs [39,40].

EPA7, “providing humanistic care and psychological support,” received the highest weight in the second round of consultation (19.12 %), reflecting the central role of humanistic care in nursing practice. This finding aligns with China’s medical policies and midwifery care standards [41]. In contrast, EPA 9, “protecting midwifery service recipients’ autonomy and right to know,” received the lowest weight (3.07 %), which may reflect current clinical practice in China, where physicians typically manage informed consent. Nevertheless, the research team retained this indicator, recognizing that safeguarding patient autonomy is a universal professional responsibility. EPA 2, “assessment and management of special or critical situations,” raised concerns among some experts about students’ limited clinical exposure; however, the research team maintained that the management of critical conditions is a key indicator of midwifery competence. One guideline component of the EPA framework [18], specifically the “source of assessment data,” was not included, as it was considered conceptually overlapping with evidence-based evaluation data sources.

Practical applicability of the entrustable professional activities framework

Adjustments were made to align with clinical teaching, including removing confidence levels such as 1a, 4, and 5 and adding quantitative descriptors for completeness and correctness. Practical considerations suggested that incorporating scoring buttons might compromise usability by increasing tool length, consistent with earlier reports [42,43]. EPAs are defined as specific, observable units of professional practice that reflect competency and can be delegated to sufficiently prepared learners. Matching core EPAs with competencies in this study not only strengthened theoretical coherence but also facilitated integration into midwifery curricula. Educators and clinical practitioners can clearly understand how EPAs contribute to competency development. These design choices enhance the feasibility of implementing the framework within real-world educational settings.

Validation and evaluation of the entrustable professional activities framework

Preliminary validation indicates satisfactory internal coherence and temporal stability of the EPA framework among undergraduate midwifery interns. High internal consistency suggests conceptual alignment between nested and core EPAs, supporting structural integrity of the framework. In competency-based assessment, strong internal consistency reflects homogeneity of items measuring related constructs and supports scale reliability [44]. Moderate test–retest reliability indicates acceptable stability over time [45]. Variability across certain domains may reflect the developmental nature of clinical competence during internship, where performance can evolve rapidly [46]. Domains with higher stability are likely to involve more standardized, frequently practiced activities, resulting in more consistent self-assessment.

Despite the robust methodology used to develop these EPAs, some limitations remain. First, the study did not fully quantify all the items, including nested EPAs, as a small number of new items were introduced after the second round of expert consultation. Second, while this study successfully developed an environmental impact assessment framework, the defined EPAs may overlap, as clinical midwives are often associated with one another, and this needs to be further verified in the future. Finally, although the framework was preliminarily applied to assess undergraduate competency, its long-term scientific credibility and practical applicability require further validation.

Conclusion

Using a comprehensive approach that integrated a literature review, qualitative interviews, and a modified Delphi process, this study developed an EPA framework for undergraduate midwifery students. The finalized framework comprises three confidence levels, 11 core EPAs, and 60 nested EPAs. Preliminary validation demonstrated high internal consistency and acceptable temporal stability, supporting the reliability and feasibility of the framework for educational application. Overall, the framework provides theoretical and practical guidance for strengthening undergraduate midwifery education, standardizing clinical competence assessment, and informing the selection, training, and evaluation of midwives in China.

Vactosertib plus pembrolizumab in patients with non-microsatellite instability-high metastatic colorectal or gastric cancer: a multicenter, phase Ib/IIa study
Authors: Keun-Wook Lee, Young Suk Park, Joong Bae Ahn, Young Iee Park, Jin Won Kim, Ji-Won Kim, Yong Sang Hong, Sun Young Kim, Jeeyun Lee, Seung Tae Kim, Han Sang Kim, Minkyu Heo, Saerom Kim, Seong-Jin Kim, Tae Won Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis multicenter, open-label, single-arm, phase Ib/IIa study (NCT03724851) evaluated vactosertib plus pembrolizumab in patients with non-microsatellite instability-high (non-MSI-high) metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) or gastric cancer (mGC). The study included dose-escalation and expansion phases. Vactosertib was administered five days per week at predefined dose levels in combination with pembrolizumab (200 mg intravenously every three weeks). The primary outcome was safety and tolerability; the combination demonstrated a manageable safety profile; prespecified secondary outcomes included pharmacokinetics and anti-tumor activity, including objective response rate, duration of response, time to response, progression-free survival, and overall survival; pharmacodynamic biomarkers were evaluated as exploratory outcomes. A total of 120 patients were included in the safety analysis (mCRC: 108, mGC: 12) and 115 in the efficacy analysis (mCRC: 103, mGC: 12). Treatment-related adverse events occurred in 70.8% of patients, with pruritus and rash being the most common. In mCRC, the objective response rate was 12.6%, with higher responses in patients without liver metastases (22.5%) than in patients with liver metastases (6.3%) by RECIST v1.1. The median overall survival was 13.2 months. No objective responses were observed in mGC. Vactosertib plus pembrolizumab showed clinical activity in non-MSI-high mCRC, particularly in patients without liver metastases.Subject terms: Colon cancer, Cancer immunotherapy

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains a major global health burden, accounting for approximately 1.9 million new cases and over 900,000 deaths in 20221. The prognosis of metastatic CRC (mCRC) remains poor, particularly in later-line settings where current therapies provide limited survival benefit2. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) provide substantial clinical benefit in deficient mismatch repair (dMMR) or microsatellite instability-high (MSI-high) mCRC, which are characterized by high mutational burden and immune activation. However, most mCRC tumors are microsatellite stable (MSS) or MSI-low, collectively referred to here as non-MSI-high mCRC, and derive limited clinical benefit from ICIs3. These tumors exhibit an immune-excluded microenvironment marked by low tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) and enrichment of tumor-associated macrophages4.

Multiple strategies have been explored to overcome immunotherapy resistance in non-MSI-high mCRC, but no regimen has shown satisfactory efficacy5–8. Emerging evidence indicates that activation of the transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) pathway is a key mediator of immune exclusion and ICI resistance in MSS/pMMR mCRC9,10, promoting an immunosuppressive TME through mechanisms that include regulatory T-cell (Treg) enrichment and impaired natural killer (NK) cell activity11,12. Transcriptomic analyses of MSS/non-MSI-high CRC have consistently demonstrated enrichment of TGF-β–responsive gene signatures, particularly within the consensus molecular subtype 4 (CMS4), which is characterized by stromal activation, extracellular matrix remodeling, and immune exclusion. These findings position TGF-β signaling as a major mediator of immune resistance in non-MSI-high mCRC9,10.

Preclinical studies have shown that TGF-β inhibition enhances immune infiltration and, when combined with programmed death-1 (PD-1) blockade, results in improved tumor control and prolonged survival in murine CRC models10,13,14. Vactosertib is a potent, highly selective oral inhibitor of TGF-β type I receptor signaling that suppresses TGF-β–driven immune exclusion and restores sensitivity to PD-1 blockade in preclinical CRC models14.

Collectively, these data support the hypothesis that pharmacologic blockade of TGF-β signaling via vactosertib may restore susceptibility to ICIs in immunologically cold non-MSI-high mCRC. Diffuse-type gastric cancer has been associated with TGF-β–driven stromal activation and immune exclusion15,16. Molecular profiling studies have further identified a TGF-β–activated immunosuppressive subtype linked to resistance signatures to checkpoint blockade, providing a biologic rationale for exploratory evaluation of combined TGF-β and PD-1/PD-L1 inhibition in this setting17.

In this multicenter, open-label, phase Ib/IIa study, we evaluated vactosertib in combination with pembrolizumab in patients with previously treated non-MSI-high mCRC or mGC. We assessed safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics, and antitumor activity across dose-escalation and dose-expansion phases, with exploratory pharmacodynamic and biomarker analyses to characterize treatment-associated changes in the TGF-β pathway and tumor microenvironment.

Results

Patients

Between December 6, 2018, and July 1, 2022, a total of 120 patients were enrolled, including 108 patients with CRC and 12 patients with GC (25 patients in the dose-escalation phase and 95 patients in the dose-expansion phase; Fig. 1). Due to a low accrual rate, enrollment of GC patients was terminated early. At the time of data cutoff (May 7, 2024), all patients had either discontinued study treatment or completed the study. The most common reason for treatment discontinuation was disease progression (75.8%). All 120 patients (108 CRC and 12 GC patients) were included in the safety analysis set (SS), and 115 patients (103 CRC and 12 GC patients) were included in the anti-tumor activity set (ATAS). Five CRC patients were excluded from the ATAS because they did not undergo any post-baseline tumor assessments; four discontinued treatments early due to rapid clinical deterioration, and one died prior to the first post-baseline assessment.

Across all patients (n = 120), the median age was 59 years (range, 31–83), 60.8% were male, and 60.8% had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) of 1. The median number of prior systemic therapies was three, most commonly including fluoropyrimidines, oxaliplatin, irinotecan, anti-VEGF, and anti-EGFR agents.

In the CRC cohort (n = 108), the median age was 59 years (range, 32–83 years), 60.2% were male, 61.1% had an ECOG PS of 1, 61.1% had liver metastasis, and 74.1% had lung metastasis. Patients had received a median of three prior lines of systemic therapy.

In the GC cohort (n = 12), the median age was 52 years (range, 31–71), 66.7% were male, and 58.3% had an ECOG PS of 1. Liver metastasis was present in 33.3% of patients, whereas lung involvement was infrequent (8.3%). Patients had received a median of two prior lines of systemic therapy.

Safety

Overall, 120 patients received at least one dose of study treatment. The median duration of treatment was 40 days (Interquartile range [IQR]: 23.5-101.0) for vactosertib, and 22 days (IQR: 1.0–94.5) for pembrolizumab. The median treatment compliance for vactosertib and pembrolizumab was 96.7% and 100%, respectively. There was no notable difference in treatment duration and treatment compliance between CRC and GC cohorts.

Of 120 patients, 106 (88.3%) experienced at least one treatment-emergent adverse event (TEAE) and 85 (70.8%) experienced treatment-related TEAEs (TRAEs; Table 2, Supplementary Dataset 1). Twenty-three (19.2%) patients reported treatment-emergent serious adverse events (SAEs), and 13 (10.8%) were grade ≥3. Thirteen (10.8%) patients reported TEAEs that led to study treatment discontinuation.

The most common TEAEs (all grades, ≥20%) were pruritus (27.5%), decreased appetite (25.0%), rash (23.3%), and headache (21.7%). Grade ≥3 TEAEs occurred in 45 patients (37.5%), with anemia in 17 patients (14.2%) being the most common grade ≥3 TEAE. The most common TRAEs (≥10%) were pruritus (26.7%), rash (22.5%), decreased appetite (15.8%), headache (15.8%), hypothyroidism (12.5%), and fatigue (11.7%). A total of 22 (18.3%) patients experienced grade ≥3 TRAEs, including lipase increased (3.3%), rash (3.3%), anemia (2.5%) and rash maculo-papular (2.5%). A total of 67 (55.8%) patients died, and the most common reason was disease progression. One (0.8%) patient experienced a Grade 5 TRAE (death). The cause of sudden death could not be definitively determined; however, the event was assessed by the investigator as possibly related to the study treatment, with pre-existing hypertension and cardiac abnormalities considered potential contributing factors.

In the dose-escalation phase, one dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) of grade 3 headache was reported in a patient receiving vactosertib 200 mg TID plus pembrolizumab. The maximum tolerated dose (MTD) was not reached (NR) at any tested vactosertib DLs administered in combination with pembrolizumab. Based on assessment of safety, PK and PD, 300 mg BID of vactosertib in combination with pembrolizumab was selected as the DL for the dose-expansion phase by the internal monitoring committee. Additionally, vactosertib DL −1 (200 mg QD) and DL 0 (200 mg BID) were included in the dose-expansion phase based on recommendations from the ad hoc sponsor-investigator meeting. The review of safety data (including TEAEs, SAEs, DLTs, and dose interruptions) suggested that the combination of vactosertib and pembrolizumab demonstrated an acceptable safety profile.

Efficacy

In the overall ATAS, which included patients with CRC (n = 103) and GC (n = 12), the investigator-reported confirmed objective response rate (ORR) based on best response per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 was 11.3% (Table 3; iRECIST results in Supplementary Table 1). The overall disease control rate (DCR) was 28.7% (Fig. 2A). The median duration of response (mDOR) was 12.5 months (Fig. 2B, C). The median progression-free survival (mPFS) and median OS (mOS) were 1.2 and 12.8 months, respectively (Fig. 3A, B).

In the CRC cohort (ATAS; n = 103), the ORR was 12.6% (Table 3). By DLs (DL −1, 0, 1 and 2; n = 29, 38, 7, and 29), the ORRs were 17.2%, 10.5%, 0%, and 13.8%, respectively, with numerically higher rates in DL −1 and DL 2 (Table 3). The DCR was 28.2%, again higher in DL −1 and DL 2. The mDOR was 12.5 months, ranging from 4.2 months in DL 0 to 13.9 months in DL 2. The mPFS was similar across all DLs, ranging from 1.2–1.3 months (Fig. 3C). The mOS was 13.2 months overall, with 7.5 and 9.7 months in DL −1 and DL 0, respectively, and not reached in DL 1 and 2 (Fig. 3D). Exploratory analyses of baseline clinical characteristics were performed in the CRC cohort to better characterize patients benefiting from treatment. Clinical benefit (CB) was defined as achievement of a confirmed CR or PR, or SD maintained for at least 6 months from the first dose of study treatment, while responders were defined as patients achieving a confirmed CR or PR, according to RECIST. The analyses primarily focused on comparisons between patients with and without CB, with responder versus non-responder analyses conducted as supportive evaluations. Both approaches demonstrated similar overall trends (Supplementary Dataset 2).

In the GC cohort (ATAS; n = 12), no confirmed responses were observed (ORR 0%; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0–26.5, Table 3). The DCR was 33.3%, with mPFS and mOS of 1.2 and 4.6 months, respectively (Table 3, Supplementary Fig. 1). Due to the limited number of patients, subgroup analyses by DL were not considered conclusive.

Post hoc efficacy analyses by liver metastasis in CRC

Unplanned post hoc subgroup analyses according to liver metastasis status were performed in the CRC cohort. In univariate analyses, the presence of liver metastasis was associated with a lower likelihood of clinical benefit (odds ratio [OR] 0.227; 95% CI, 0.072–0.716; Supplementary Dataset 2), and a numerically lower objective response rate compared with non-liver metastasized CRC (NLMCRC).

Within the CRC cohort, the ORRs were 22.5% in NLMCRC and 6.3% in liver metastasized CRC (LMCRC). In NLMCRC, the ORRs were 30.0%, 18.8%, 0%, and 25.0% in DL −1, 0, 1 and 2, respectively, whereas in LMCRC the corresponding ORRs were 10.5%, 4.5%, 0%, and 5.9%. In both subgroups, higher ORRs were observed in DL−1 and DL 2. The mOS was longer in NLMCRC than in LMCRC (NE versus 7.7 months; Table 4, Fig. 4).

Pharmacokinetics (PK)

Vactosertib was rapidly absorbed, achieving maximum observed plasma concentrations (Cmax 1801.4–3906.7 ng/mL) within 0.8–3.7 h post-dose on Cycle 1 Day 1 (C1D1). On C1D5, Cmax was reached within 1.1–1.9 h post-dose (Supplementary Table 3). Systemic exposure (Cmax, area under the concentration-time curve [AUC]) increased in an approximately dose-proportional or slightly greater-than-proportional manner following single-dose administration on C1D1, whereas it was less than dose-proportional on C1D5 following multiple-dose administration (Table 5). The terminal phase elimination half-life ranged from 2.0–3.3 h and appeared to be independent of dosage regimen (Supplementary Table 3 and Supplementary Fig. 2). No statistically significant difference in dose-normalized PK profiles was observed between DLs.

Pharmacodynamics (PD)

Exploratory PD analyses were conducted. Given the limited number of gastric cancer patients, PD results are presented for the CRC cohort only. Circulating biomarkers were selected to reflect TGF-β pathway activity and stromal remodeling based on prior translational studies of TGF-β inhibition18–20. The assessment of TGF-β1, plasminogen activator inhibitor−1 (PAI−1), connective tissue growth factor (CTGF), platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF)-AB, and PDGF-BB was performed in all patients with available samples (75 patients, n = 15, 33, and 27 in DL −1, DL 0, and DL 2, respectively). Following co-administration of vactosertib and pembrolizumab, change from baseline in PAI−1 differed significantly across dose levels by two-sided Kruskal-Wallis test (p = 0.0009, Supplementary Fig. 3), indicating a dose-associated reduction in circulating PAI−1 levels. Post hoc pairwise comparisons using Dunn’s test showed greater reductions in DL 0 and DL 2 than in DL −1 (p = 0.0420 and p = 0.0005, respectively), whereas DL 0 and DL 2 did not differ (p = 0.2686). No statistically significant differences across dose levels were observed for TGF-β1, CTGF, PDGF-AB, or PDGF-BB (p = 0.2755, 0.1798, 0.2320, and 0.2189, respectively). Exploratory analyses comparing responders and non-responders showed no significant differences in any circulating biomarker (Supplementary Fig. 4). Given the limited number of responders, these analyses were underpowered, and no obvious trend was observed.

To further characterize changes in the tumor microenvironment, analysis of paired tumor samples (Pre: Screening vs. Post: C2, D3–6 or D10–13) by multiplex IHC demonstrated a significant increase in the ratio of total CD8+ T cells/Foxp3+ T cells and Granzyme B+ CD8+ T cells/Foxp3+ T cells in both the tumor nest (n = 19, p = 0.0160 and p < 0.0001, respectively) and stroma (n = 22 and 20; p = 0.0017 and p < 0.0001, respectively). No significant change was observed in Granzyme B− CD8+ T cells/Foxp3+ T cells. Importantly, a strong positive correlation (r = 0.9380, p < 0.0001) was observed between Granzyme B+ CD8⁺ T cell infiltration in the tumor nest and stroma, supporting a shift toward an immunologically active tumor microenvironment following study treatment (Fig. 5).

To further explore transcriptional changes in the TGF-β signaling pathway (KEGG hsa04350), bulk RNA sequencing (RNAseq) was conducted on paired tumor samples from 28 patients (DL 0, n = 12; DL 2, n = 16). SMAD2 expression was reduced in both DL 0 and DL 2 and reached statistical significance in DL 2 (adjusted p = 0.0057). SMAD6 expression increased in DL 2 (log2 fold change = 0.77) but was not statistically significant (adjusted p = 0.0753). In total, 21 genes showed differential expression (|Δlog₂ fold change| ≥ 0.5) in DL 0 or DL 2. Among genes associated with downstream TGF-β signaling activity11, ID1 and MYC showed greater suppression in DL 2 than in DL 0 (Supplementary Fig. 5).

Discussion

This was a multicenter, phase Ib/IIa study evaluating the safety and efficacy of vactosertib plus pembrolizumab in patients with non-MSI-high mCRC or mGC. Across all tested DLs of vactosertib, the combination exhibited a manageable safety and tolerability profile. In the CRC cohort, ORR was 12.6% by RECIST and 14.6% by iRECIST (Supplementary Table 1). No objective response was observed in the GC cohort; however, the limited GC sample size precludes any meaningful conclusions regarding activity in this population.

Preclinical studies have shown that TGF-β inhibition may enhance immune checkpoint blockade by converting immunologically cold TMEs into more inflamed states, thereby improving susceptibility to immune-mediated elimination13,21. Consistent with this biologic rationale, the combination showed higher ORR compared with previously reported pembrolizumab monotherapy in non-MSI-high mCRC, although cross-trial comparisons must be interpreted cautiously22,23.

Although the overall ORR did not meet the pre-specified threshold of 25%, post-hoc analyses suggested differential activity according to liver metastasis status. ORR was higher in patients without liver metastases than in those with liver involvement (22.5% vs 6.3% by RECIST). These observations are consistent with emerging evidence from randomized phase III trials of immunotherapy-based combinations in MSS mCRC, including STELLAR-303 and LEAP-017, which underscore the importance of the metastatic organ microenvironment—particularly liver involvement—in shaping sensitivity to immune checkpoint blockade8,23.

To further characterize the observed clinical activity, TGF-β–related circulating biomarkers and tumor-based immune analyses were performed. Significant reductions in PAI-1, particularly at higher DLs, were observed and were accompanied by increased intratumoral cytotoxic T-cell infiltration relative to regulatory T cells. These data are consistent with on-target modulation of the TGF-β pathway and enhanced immune accessibility within the TME13,24, supporting a mechanism distinct from non-specific immune activation.

Despite the absence of a corresponding improvement in mPFS, mOS in the overall mCRC population was 13.2 months, which is numerically longer than previously reported outcomes for later-line standard therapies25–27. While this observation may be consistent with tumor microenvironment remodeling, it should be interpreted with caution, as several competing explanations may account for the apparent survival benefit. First, patients in this study were somewhat less heavily pretreated compared with prior later-line trials; however, in the SUNLIGHT study–which enrolled an even less heavily pretreated population–mOS with standard of care (SOC) was 7.5 months28, suggesting that prior treatment exposure alone does not fully account for the observed survival. Second, a lower proportion of liver metastases in the present study may have contributed to the longer mOS, consistent with subgroup analyses. In addition, baseline molecular characteristics may have influenced survival outcomes. MSI status was unconfirmed in a small subset of patients, and the proportion of RAS-mutant tumors was lower than that reported in contemporary later-line mCRC trials, which may have modestly favored overall survival (OS). Furthermore, it is possible that a subset of patients derived benefit primarily from PD-1 blockade independent of TGF-β pathway modulation. Given the single-arm design, the relative contribution of these factors cannot be determined, and these findings should be considered hypothesis-generating.

Across DLs, objective response metrics (ORR and DCR) were broadly comparable, and the expansion cohorts were not powered for formal between-dose efficacy comparisons. The mOS was 7.5 and 9.7 months in DL -1 and DL 0, respectively, while not reached in DL 1 and DL 2 (95% CI, 0.82–NE and 12.85–NE, respectively). Relative to external reference data (e.g., SUNLIGHT; mOS 10.8 months28), higher DLs appeared numerically favorable. The lack of a monotonic ORR-dose relationship indicates that binary response endpoints may not reflect the pharmacodynamic gradient across doses. Therefore, ORR alone may be insufficient to infer dose–response relationships for microenvironment-modulating agents. In the absence of a randomized comparator arm, we cannot exclude the possibility that a subset of non-MSI-high mCRC patients may derive benefit from PD−1 blockade alone, although prior studies have generally demonstrated minimal activity in unselected MSS populations. Against this background, higher DLs–particularly DL2–demonstrated more pronounced pharmacodynamic and transcriptomic suppression of the TGF-β pathway together with numerically longer survival, although causal inference cannot be established.

Although pharmacodynamic modulation appeared more pronounced at DL2, objective response rates were numerically similar between the lowest and highest tested dose levels. Therefore, the current dataset does not establish a definitive dose–response relationship for clinical efficacy.

Preclinical data further support this biologic framework. In a CT26 murine model, prior TGF-β inhibition enhanced survival following subsequent radiotherapy and was associated with reduced SMAD2 expression29. Consistent with this observation, bulk RNAseq in the present study demonstrated greater suppression of TGF-β signaling at higher DLs, including significant SMAD2 reduction and directional modulation of SMAD6, ID1, and MYC. Although exploratory, these results support more pronounced modulation of TGF-β pathway-associated transcription at higher DLs.

Given the comparable safety profile across DLs and consistent pharmacodynamic modulation at 300 mg BID, this dose appears suitable for further clinical evaluation. Nevertheless, these findings should be interpreted in the context of several limitations, including the non-randomized design and modest sample size, which may introduce selection bias and limit the robustness of subgroup analyses. In addition, the single-arm design precludes definitive comparative conclusions, and the generalizability of the results may be limited.

Overall, vactosertib plus pembrolizumab represents a biologically rational combination strategy for patients with non-MSI-high mCRC, particularly those without liver metastases. The results suggest clinical activity in this population; however, prospective randomized studies will be required to confirm the clinical benefit and to distinguish treatment effects from underlying prognostic factors.

Methods

Patients

This study was conducted in accordance with the Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the Declaration of Helsinki. The study design and conduct complied with all applicable regulations for the protection of human study participants. The study protocol was approved by the institutional review boards (IRBs) of all participating institutions, including the IRB of Seoul National University Bundang Hospital, the IRB of Asan Medical Center, the IRB of Samsung Medical Center, the IRB of Severance Hospital, and the IRB of the National Cancer Center (Republic of Korea). Further details are provided in Supplementary Table 4. The study was registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03724851, date of registration: October 30, 2018). Written informed consent was obtained from all patients before enrollment.

Eligible patients were aged ≥19 years; had histologically confirmed advanced or metastatic non-MSI-high gastrointestinal cancers (CRC or GC) with progression following standard therapies or were intolerant or ineligible for such treatments; had measurable disease per RECIST version 1.1; an ECOG PS of 0 or 1; life expectancy >12 weeks; and adequate organ function. Prior ICI experience was not permitted.

Exclusion criteria included pregnancy; prior systemic anti-cancer therapy within four weeks before the first dose of study treatment; active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment; uncontrolled comorbidities; ongoing infections; and radiotherapy within two weeks prior to the start of study treatment. Additionally, patients with severe hypersensitivity to study drugs or significant cardiac conditions were excluded. There was no patient or public involvement in the design, conduct, or reporting of the trial.

Study design and procedures

This was a multicenter, open-label, single-arm, phase Ib/IIa study conducted at five centers in the Republic of Korea. Vactosertib was administered five days per week in combination with pembrolizumab (200 mg intravenously every three weeks), and all interventions were administered by qualified oncology physicians at each participating site.

In Phase Ib, dose-escalation followed a modified toxicity probability interval (mTPI) design with a prespecified target DLT rate of 30% and five prespecified dose levels (100 mg BID, 150 mg BID, 200 mg BID, 200 mg TID, and 300 mg BID). Escalation was initiated at 200 mg BID (DL 0) and proceeded sequentially to DL 1 (200 mg TID) and DL 2 (300 mg BID); the two lower prespecified levels were not opened as no de-escalation was required. Patient allocation during Phase Ib was sequential and guided by the mTPI algorithm.

In Phase IIa, three expansion cohorts (DL −1, DL 0, and DL 2) were evaluated following a protocol amendment. DL −1 (200 mg QD) was introduced to evaluate a lower daily exposure schedule and to further characterize exposure–response relationships across clinically relevant dosing regimens. Although DL 2 emerged as the leading recommended phase 2 dose (RP2D) candidate in Phase Ib, parallel expansion of selected DLs was conducted to characterize exposure–response relationships and support integrated RP2D confirmation. Approximately 30 patients per selected dose level were planned for enrollment. DL1 was not expanded due to dosing convenience and similarity in total daily dose relative to DL2. Accordingly, the final distribution across dose levels reflects the combined effects of sequential dose-escalation in Phase Ib and selective expansion in Phase IIa.

Tumor assessments were performed every six weeks during the first 12 months, and then every 12 weeks for up to 35 cycles or until tumor progression per RECIST version 1.1, investigator decision, unacceptable toxicity, death, or withdrawal of consent, whichever occurred first. TEAEs were recorded throughout the safety observation period (from the first dose to 30 days after the end of treatment). Supportive care, including antiemetics and analgesics, was permitted as needed in accordance with the study protocol.

PK blood samples were collected pre-dose and at 0.5 (±5 min), 1.5 (±15 min), 3 ( ±15 min), 4.5 (±15 min), 8 (±15 min), and 12 (±15 min) hours on C1D1 and C1D5, and analyzed using PhoenixTM WinNonlin® (Version 8.3.5; Certara, Princeton, NJ).

For PD analysis, paired tumor biopsies were collected at baseline (screening) and post-treatment (C2, D3–6, or C2, D10–13) and analyzed by multiplex IHC or bulk RNAseq. Circulating biomarkers were collected from peripheral blood at predefined time points (C1D1, C1D5, C3D1) and at the end of treatment and analyzed using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Detailed information on antibodies used for multiplex IHC, including supplier, catalog number, clone, and dilution, along with ELISA kit details, is provided in the Supplementary Methods. For bulk RNAseq, RNA was extracted and sequenced using the NovaSeq X Plus platform (Illumina). Data were analyzed using standard pipelines, including STAR for alignment, DESeq2 for differential expression, and clusterProfiler for enrichment analysis, using default parameters unless otherwise specified.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was safety and tolerability of vactosertib in combination with pembrolizumab. This included characterization of DLTs during the first three weeks of treatment in the dose-escalation phase and assessment of TEAEs in both the dose-escalation and dose-expansion phases. Safety assessments also included vital signs, clinical laboratory tests, and electrocardiograms, and supported the determination of the MTD and/or RP2D. TEAEs were coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (version 25.1), and severity was graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (version 5.0).

Prespecified secondary outcomes included the PK profile of vactosertib and anti-tumor activity. PK outcomes included plasma concentrations of vactosertib and derived PK parameters. Anti-tumor activity outcomes included ORR assessed by investigators using RECIST v1.1 and iRECIST, DOR, time to response (TTR), PFS, landmark PFS rates, OS, and landmark OS probabilities. ORR was defined as the proportion of patients achieving a confirmed CR or PR per RECIST v1.1. DCR was additionally summarized descriptively and was defined as the proportion of patients achieving CR, PR, or stable disease lasting at least six weeks.

Exploratory outcomes included PD and biomarker analyses using circulating biomarkers (TGF-β1, PAI-1, CTGF, PDGF-AB, and PDGF-BB), multiplex IHC, and bulk RNAseq.

Statistical analysis

In the dose-escalation phase, up to five potential DLs of vactosertib in combination with a fixed dose of pembrolizumab were prespecified. The mTPI method was used to determine the MTD or RP2D of the combination. In the dose-expansion phase, 90 patients were planned (30 for each of the three vactosertib DLs) to further evaluate efficacy at the selected dose levels. However, a total of 95 patients were ultimately enrolled, including 90 with mCRC and 5 with mGC. Although safety remained the primary objective of the overall Phase Ib/IIa study, ORR was prospectively designed as the key efficacy endpoint for statistical planning in the Phase IIa expansion stage. Accordingly, the sample size calculation was based on detecting a clinically meaningful improvement in ORR from an assumed 5% under the null hypothesis (based on historical ICI efficacy in MSS mCRC)22,30,31 to 25% under the alternative hypothesis. A sample size of approximately 30 patients per DL was estimated to provide 85% power at a one-sided significance level of 1%, assuming a 20% dropout rate.

CRC and GC cohorts were enrolled in parallel within a unified Phase Ib/IIa study design focused on safety, dose escalation, and pharmacodynamic evaluation. The protocol did not prespecify independent primary efficacy analyses by tumor type. Enrollment in the GC cohort was terminated early due to slow accrual, resulting in insufficient sample size for meaningful GC-specific efficacy assessment.

The SS included all patients who received at least one dose of study treatment. The ATAS included patients who completed ≥1 treatment cycle and had ≥1 post-baseline tumor assessment; others were excluded per the prespecified analysis plan. Safety was analyzed using the SS, and efficacy was analyzed using the ATAS. PK and PD were evaluated in patients with available data.

Data were evaluated as observed and presented descriptively, with no imputation for missing values. Statistical summaries were prespecified for each variable category. Baseline demographic and disease characteristics were summarized using frequency and percentage for categorical variables and mean, standard deviation, median, and range for continuous variables. PK parameters were summarized descriptively using mean and standard deviation, except for Tmax, which was prespecified to be summarized using median and range. PD biomarkers and immune-cell ratios from paired tumor samples were summarized using median and IQR and are presented graphically. Safety outcomes were summarized by frequency and percentage. Efficacy outcomes, including ORR, DCR, and RECIST response categories, were summarized as proportions within the ATAS, and time-to-event endpoints (PFS, OS, and DOR) were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method and summarized with medians and 95% CI. Data were summarized by each DL and for the total population. All assessments performed prior to the first study treatment were considered baseline measurements. For all prespecified analyses, statistical methods were defined a priori in the study protocol and applied as specified. Exploratory analyses were clearly identified as post hoc, included comparisons of proportions as well as estimation of odds ratios and CIs, and interpreted as hypothesis-generating. All statistical analyses were two-sided, with a significance level of 5% and 95% CI. Graphs were generated using GraphPad Prism version 10.1.2 (GraphPad Software, LLC, Boston, MA, USA). Statistical analyses were conducted using SAS® Version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA) and R software (version 4.3.1; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Prevalence Characteristics of Osteoporosis Fractures in the Elderly in Two Regions of China and Analysis of the Lag Effect of Air Pollutants on them
Authors: Zhipeng Ye, Qian Xiao, Shihang Liu, Shuai Zhou, Guangzhao Hou, Hongzhi Lv, Yingze Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveAir pollution is increasing and threatening human health. The objective of this study is to investigate the population distribution characteristics of elderly osteoporosis fractures in Hebei Province and Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region and to analyze the effects of air pollutants on the number of elderly osteoporosis fracture inpatients in the two regions.MethodA retrospective collection of elderly osteoporosis fracture cases was conducted in selected hospitals in Hebei Province and Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region from January 1, 2018 to December 31, 2022. The chi‐square test was used to compare the distributional characteristics of the population in the two regions. Additionally, we used a distributed lag nonlinear model (DLNM) in order to assess the effect of air pollutants on the number of daily hospital admissions of elderly osteoporosis fracture patients in different regions.ResultA total of 19,203 elderly osteoporosis fracture patients were included in the study. The average age of these patients was 76.66 ± 7.55 years, and the majority of them were female (13,514 instances, 70.37%). The disparities in age distribution (χ 2 = 133.9 p < 0.001), fracture site (χ 2 = 62.0 p < 0.001), and hospitalization cost (Z = −15.635 p < 0.001) between the two regions were statistically significant. The lag effect curves of PM2.5, PM10, and NO2 on the number of elderly osteoporosis fracture hospitalizations in Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region exhibited a similar pattern resembling a “W”‐shaped curve. All three pollutants reached their highest values after a lag time of 14 days (PM2.5: RR = 1.053, 95% CI: 1.031, 1.074; PM10: RR = 1.031, 95% CI: 1.018, 1.043; NO2: RR = 1.125, 95% CI: 1.070, 1.182). In Hebei Province, the largest impacts of PM2.5 and PM10 were observed after a lag of 14 days (PM2.5: RR = 1.022, 95% CI: 1.013, 1.028; PM10: RR = 1.013, 95% CI: 1.008, 1.018). Similarly, the maximum effect of NO2 was observed after a lag of 11 days (RR = 1.020, 95% CI: 1.010, 1.028).ConclusionThere were differences in the epidemiological characteristics of hospitalized patients with osteoporosis fractures between the two regions, PM2.5, PM10, and NO2 increased the number of hospitalizations for osteoporosis fractures. Exposure to air pollutants such as PM2.5 increases the risk of osteoporosis fractures in the elderly population.Keywords: Air Pollutants, Distributed Lag Nonlinear Model, Epidemiological Features, Osteoporosis Fractures in the Elderly

Introduction

Osteoporosis is a metabolic disease that affects the skeletal system. It is characterized by low bone mass, deterioration of bone tissue microstructure, and increased bone fragility. This condition causes the bones to become porous and fragile, making them more susceptible to fractures. 1 Fractures can occur from falls or even minor stress, such as bending over or coughing. Osteoporosis is classified into main or secondary forms. Osteoporosis fractures, also known as osteoporosis fractures, are fractures caused by primary osteoporosis. These fractures are characterized by pain and restricted movement and are most frequently observed in the hip, wrist, or spine. The condition is marked by low bone density, sluggish healing, inadequate internal fixation stability, and a high rate of failure. Its effect on public health is progressively worsening. With the progressive aging of our nation, the prevalence of osteoporosis fractures is steadily rising, thus significantly augmenting the strain on the social healthcare system. The epidemiologic survey conducted in China reveals that the prevalence of osteoporosis is 3.2% among individuals aged 40–49 years and 19.2% among those aged 50 years or older. The prevalence of osteoporosis among women is notably higher than in Europe and the United States, but comparable with that in Asian countries like Japan and South Korea. 2

In recent years, air pollution has become increasingly serious and has become a major public health problem worldwide. Air pollution has a serious impact on people's health, causing more than 7 million premature deaths and even more hospitalizations each year. 3 As early as 1985, studies have suggested a potential relationship between air pollution and osteoporosis. 4 Analyses of data from 341,311 participants in the UK Biobank also showed that exposure to higher levels of air pollution was associated with reduced bone mineral density and increased risk of osteoporosis. 5 A nationwide cohort study in Korea showed that osteoporosis and bone density are associated with air pollutants and that long‐term inhalation of air pollutants such as SO2 increases the risk of osteoporosis fractures. 6 In contrast, a two‐sample Mendelian randomization study of Europe showed no potential causal relationship between PM2.5 and the development of osteoporosis. 7 After years of research, the relationship between air pollution and osteoporosis remains unclear. 8 Therefore, more research is needed to prove the relationship between them, and it is important to investigate the lagged relationship between air pollutants and osteoporosis fractures.

In addition, Hebei and Xinjiang regions have significant differences not only in geography, climatic conditions, and altitude, but also in race, culture, lifestyle, and dietary habits. 9 Besides, Hebei and Xinjiang regions also have differences in the level of socioeconomic development, the distribution and accessibility of medical resources, and the level of health education. 10 Hebei Province has a large amount of heavy industry and coal resources, especially in winter, and the overuse and burning of coal is a cause of atmospheric pollution emitted into the air. The terrain is dominated by mountains and basins, which sometimes leads to the accumulation of pollutants and makes it difficult for them to spread. 11 In contrast, Xinjiang is deeply inland, creating an extremely dry continental climate with a large temperature difference between day and night, and air conditions are relatively unaffected by industry, resulting in relatively good air quality. 12 The air quality in the two regions is in stark contrast. These factors may affect the health status and disease prevalence of the population. Epidemiological data of a more general nature can be obtained by conducting comparative studies between different regions, so it is important to study the characteristics of the distribution of the population in the two regions. Based on the above rationale, this study seeks to examine the distribution patterns of osteoporosis fractures in the elderly population in Hebei Province and Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region, as well as the impact of air pollutants on these fractures from 2018 to 2022.

Material and Method

Sources of Data

The retrieval of information on elderly osteoporosis fracture patients was conducted using a medical imaging computerized archiving system and a case query system. The data were obtained from various hospitals, namely, the Third Hospital of Hebei Medical University in HeBei, the Jingfu County Hospital, the Second Hospital of Tangshan City, the First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University, the Beitun Hospital, the People's Hospital of Burqin County, and the People's Hospital of Altay. Retrospectively, the essential data of older patients with osteoporosis fractures in each hospital were gathered from January 1, 2018 to December 31, 2022. These data include the patients' names, genders, ages, admission times, admission diagnoses, discharge times, and hospitalization costs.

Data pertaining to air pollutants were obtained from the China Meteorological Data Website(https://www.aqistudy.cn/historydata/daydata.php?city=%E7%9F%B3%E5%AE%B6%E5%BA%84). The pollutants considered in this study were PM2.5 (particulate matter with an aerodynamic diameter of less than 2.5 μm), PM10 (particulate matter with an aerodynamic diameter of less than 10 μm), SO2 (sulfur dioxide), NO2 (nitrogen dioxide), CO (carbon monoxide), and O3_8h (8‐h moving average ozone concentration). The daily average exposure concentrations of these pollutants were determined based on their 24‐h average concentrations. The average daily exposure concentrations of air pollutants are determined by calculating the 24‐h average concentrations of the aforementioned air pollutants.

Inclusion Exclusion Criteria

Totally 19,992 cases of osteoporosis fractures in the elderly who met the inclusion criteria (1) fracture occurred in vertebrae, hip, proximal humerus or distal radius; (2) age ≥ 65 years; (3) caused by low‐energy injuries; and (4) complete case and imaging data received from January 1, 2018 to December 31, 2022 in each hospital were retrospectively collected. Among them, 85 patients were excluded due to pathologic fractures, 53 patients were excluded due to multiple fractures, 573 patients were excluded due to old or secondary fractures, 78 patients were excluded due to open fractures, and finally 19,203 patients met the inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Study Methods

The patients were categorized into three age groups: 65–74, 75–84, and >84 years. The number of cases in each age group was recorded, and categorical data such as gender and fracture site were described using ratios. The population characteristics were analyzed descriptively, and the chi‐square test was employed to examine differences between the two regions in terms of age group, gender, fracture site, and other factors. Additionally, the rank‐sum test was used to compare hospitalization costs between the two regions. In addition, the study employed distributional lag nonlinear modeling (DLNM) to assess and analyze the impact of air pollution on daily hospitalizations for osteoporosis fractures in senior individuals in both locations. Furthermore, stratified analyses were conducted to investigate the impact of air pollution on different subgroups according to age groups (65–74 years, 75–84 years, >85 years) in order to identify at‐risk populations.

A quadratic B‐spline function with 3 degrees of freedom was chosen for the basis function of the exposure dimension, and a quadratic polynomial function was chosen for the basis function of the lag dimension; we set the maximum number of lagged days to 14 days.

All statistical analyses were performed with the “DLNM” and “Spline” packages using R 4.2.2 statistical software (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Austria), and the chi‐square test were meticulously executed using the SPSS software (Ver: 21.0, Illinois, USA). All P‐values were distributed in a two‐tailed manner, and differences were considered statistically significant if p < 0.05.

Results

Basic information of elderly osteoporosis fracture patients in Hebei Province and Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region.

According to the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 19,203 cases of elderly osteoporosis fracture patients were collected in 2018–2022, including 13,498 cases in Shijiazhuang City, 4017 cases in Tangshan City, 1045 cases in Urumqi, and 643 cases in Altay Region. The average age was 76.66 ± 7.55 years, and there were 5689 cases (29.63%) of males and 13,514 cases (70.37%) of females, with a male to female ratio of 2.37:1.

The number of elderly osteoporosis fracture patients in Xinjiang increased yearly since 2018 (243 cases, 14.3%), reached the peak in 2021 (434 cases, 25.7%), and then began to decline. The average number of reported cases was highest in May (174 cases, 10.3%) and lowest in February (103 cases, 6.0%). Distribution of Fracture Sites

Among the elderly osteoporosis fracture patients in Hebei Province, hip fracture accounted for the highest proportion (12,688 cases, 72.4%), followed in the order of vertebral fracture (2960 cases, 16.9%), distal radius fracture (9834 cases, 5.6%), and proximal humerus fracture (881 cases, 5.1%). Among the elderly osteoporosis fracture patients in Xinjiang, hip fracture accounted for the highest proportion (1214 cases, 71.8%), followed by vertebral fracture (377 cases, 22.3%), distal radius fracture (60 cases, 3.6%), and proximal humerus fracture (881 cases, 2.3%) in that order, as shown in Figure 5. Hip fractures predominated among the elderly osteoporosis fracture patients in the two regions during the period 2018–2022 which accounted for about 70% of all fracture visits. The chi‐square test was performed to analyze the number of elderly osteoporosis fracture patients with different fracture sites in the two regions, and the results were statistically different (χ 2 = 62.0 p < 0.001).

Comparison of Hospitalization Costs

The average hospitalization cost of elderly osteoporosis fracture patients in Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region was RMB 25,568, which was significantly lower than that of RMB 42,482 in Hebei, and the rank‐sum test of the hospitalization cost of elderly osteoporosis fracture patients in the two regions showed a statistically significant difference (Z = −15.635 p < 0.001), and the average hospitalization cost of elderly osteoporosis fracture patients in Hebei was higher than that of patients in Xinjiang.

Discussion

The findings demonstrated statistically significant variations in age distribution, fracture sites, and hospitalization costs between the two regions. In addition, the findings revealed that PM2.5, PM10, and NO2 were associated with an increased risk of hospitalization for osteoporosis fractures in both regions. The results of lagged effect in Hebei Province showed that SO2 was also risk factors for osteoporotic fractures in the elderly.

Incidence of Osteoporosis Fractures in Older Population

There is a notable disparity in the number of women compared with males who are admitted to hospitals in Hebei Province and Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region due to osteoporosis fractures in the elderly. As the body ages, the gonads gradually lose their function, resulting in a decline in the production of sex hormones released by the gonads. The absence of sex hormones disturbs the delicate equilibrium between bone formation and bone resorption, decelerating the incorporation of blood calcium into bones and accelerating the conversion of bone calcium into blood calcium. This leads to a decrease in bone density and quality, deterioration of bone microstructure, heightened vulnerability of bones, and an increased susceptibility to fractures. Estrogen has a more significant impact on bone in this process, leading to a greater occurrence of fractures caused by osteoporosis in older women. 6

Patients with osteoporosis fractures in Hebei Province are mainly 65–74 years old, while in Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region, patients with osteoporosis fractures are mainly 75–84 years old. As individuals get older, bone marrow stromal cells tend to develop into more fat cells rather than bone‐forming cells. Additionally, these cells undergo senescence, a process of aging, which causes a decline in bone formation. This ultimately leads to the development of senile osteoporosis, a condition characterized by weakened bones and an increased risk of fractures. 13 The bone mass significantly increases during puberty in an exponential manner. However, malnutrition negatively impacts the accumulation of bone mass. 14 The peak of bone mass occurs during young adulthood. Subsequently, there is a nonlinear decline in bone mass, particularly during menopause. This decline is a crucial factor in determining the risk of osteoporosis and fractures in late adulthood. 15 There is a difference in the percentage of fracture types between Hebei and Xinjiang, osteoporosis fractures are affected not only by factors such as age and gender, but also by genetics and the living environment. Sleurs et al. showed that higher bone mineral density was associated with higher exposure to residential green space during childhood and the importance of early exposure to residential green space during the critical period of growth and development for bone health with long‐term implications. 16 Residents of the two regions differ significantly in terms of ethnicity, culture, and lifestyle. Xinjiang has a large number of ethnic minorities, such as Uighurs and Kazakhs, which may have different genetic backgrounds and disease susceptibility. 17 In addition, traffic is one of the important causes of osteoporosis, and a study of Mexican‐Americans showed that long‐term exposure to traffic may contribute to the development of osteoporosis. 18 Hebei Province has high traffic density and serious congestion on urban roads, and the negative impact of traffic emissions is difficult to ignore. The hospitalization expenses for senior patients with osteoporosis fractures in Hebei Province surpass those of patients in Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region, this may be due to differences in the level of socioeconomic development, public health policies, distribution and accessibility of medical resources, and level of health education between the two places. Additionally, osteoporosis fracture patients in other nations have a substantial financial burden. 19

Delayed Impacts of Atmospheric Contacts on Osteoporosis Fractures in Older Population

Through the use of distributional lag nonlinear models, we conducted an analysis on the impact of air pollutants on daily hospitalizations for elderly individuals with osteoporosis fractures. Our findings indicate that both Hebei Province and Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region experienced an increased risk of hospitalization for elderly osteoporosis fractures due to elevated levels of PM2.5, PM10, and NO2. A cohort study conducted in rural Henan revealed that for every 1 μg/m3 increase in PM2.5, PM10, and NO2, the risk of osteoporosis increased by 14.6%, 7.3%, and 16.5% respectively. Furthermore, the study found a positive correlation between long‐term exposure to air pollutants and a higher risk of osteoporosis. These findings suggest that enhancing air quality could potentially improve the health of rural residents, aligning with the conclusions drawn from this study. 20 A study conducted in Italy found that being exposed to PM2.5 levels above 25 μg/m3 for a long period of time was associated with a 16% higher risk of osteoporosis T‐value at any site (adjusted odds ratio OR = 1.161, 95%CI 1.105, 1.220). Similarly, exposure to PM10 levels above 30 μg/m3 was associated with a 15% higher risk of osteoporosis T‐value at any site (OR = 1.148, 95% CI 1.098, 1.200). 21 An in vitro experimental study showed that mice chronically exposed to PM2.5 exhibited excessive osteoclastogenesis, dysregulation of osteogenesis, and that PM2.5 exposure promotes osteoclastogenesis through activation of bone resorption and modulation of the TNF‐α/Traf6/c‐Fos pathway, which provides a physiological basis for our study. 22 Nguyen et al. conducted a subsequent investigation and discovered that residing in regions with elevated levels of particle pollution had an impact on bone health. They also found that enhancing air pollutant levels could potentially enhance bone health, particularly among older individuals and those with low incomes. 23

For NO2, Qi et al. showed that the higher the concentration of pollutants such as PM2.5 and NO2, the greater the risk of fracture. 24 Ranzani et al. conducted a cross‐sectional investigation on a population‐based cohort and discovered that ambient air pollution was linked to decreased bone mineral content values in a group of young adults residing in peri‐urban areas of South India. 25 Nitrogen oxides may play a key role in the risk of bone damage and osteoporosis, according to a study in the United States. Exposure to air pollution may increase reactive oxygen and nitrogen species, and increased oxidative stress can lead to cell death through a variety of signaling mechanisms, such as mitochondrial damage, cell‐cycle arrest, the DNA damage response, inflammation, protein inhibition, and lipid peroxidation. The air pollutant NOx associated with bone damage is a reactive nitrogen species. 26 There are fewer studies on the relationship between NO2 and osteoporosis fractures, and a large number of future studies are still needed to demonstrate these relationships.

Drawbacks and Deficiencies

This study is limited by several factors. Firstly, the sample size is small, as it only includes osteoporosis fracture patients who were hospitalized in specific hospitals in Hebei Province and Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region. Therefore, it does not represent all elderly patients with osteoporosis fractures who are hospitalized, which could introduce bias in the results. Furthermore, our analysis did not account for potential confounding variables such as work, marital status, genetics, and lifestyle due to data constraints. Hence, further research is required to establish variations in the occurrence and features of osteoporosis fractures among the elderly across various geographical areas.

Clinical Significance and Inspirations

PM2.5, PM10, and NO2 will increase the risk of osteoporotic fractures, the government should formulate and implement emission reduction policies to reduce the emission of air pollutants such as PM2.5, and the public health department should strengthen environmental monitoring and management to reduce the exposure to air pollution to reduce the risk of osteoporosis and related fractures. In addition, in high‐pollution areas, especially among the elderly, it is recommended to have regular bone density testing to pay attention to the early signs of osteoporosis, and public education on the effects of environmental pollution on bone health should be strengthened.

Conclusion

In both regions, there were statistically significant differences in age distribution, fracture site, and hospitalization cost. The prevalence of osteoporosis fracture was significantly higher in females compared with males. Additionally, an increase of 10 μg/m3 in PM2.5, PM10, and NO2 in the two regions was associated with an increased risk of osteoporosis fracture.

Improving reference crop evapotranspiration estimation using Solar-Induced chlorophyll fluorescence
Authors: Renjun Wang, Shuaiqiang Zhang, Jianghua Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Accurately estimating reference crop evapotranspiration (ET₀) is essential for assessing crop water requirements and optimizing regional water resource management. Traditional ET₀ estimation models are limited by incomplete meteorological data, while sun-induced chlorophyll fluorescence (SIF) provides new opportunities for ET₀ estimation. However, existing models neglect the influence of environmental variables on the relationship between ET0 and SIF, resulting in an inability to accurately capture the dynamic variations of ET0. To overcome this limitation, we incorporated the basal crop coefficient (Kcb) into the original ET0_SIF model to enhance its constraints, developing a hybrid SIF-based model (RET0_SIF). By integrating this model with satellite observations and reanalysis data, we produced high-resolution spatiotemporal ET0 estimates (RET0_SIFd). The research findings demonstrate that: (1) the improved RET0_SIF model significantly enhances ET0 estimation accuracy, effectively capturing seasonal ET0 variations across 22 monitoring stations; (2) RET0_SIF outperforms conventional empirical models, exhibiting a minimal multi-year mean bias (0.59 mm/8-day) compared to the Penman-Monteith (ET0PM) at all 22 stations; (3) RET0_SIFd reveals a spatial pattern of gradual decrease from west to east in the study area, along with an increasing trend over time (2.56 mm/year). This study provides a novel methodology for precise ET0 estimation in arid and semi-arid regions.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-17716-5.

Introduction

Evapotranspiration (ET) is the total water vapor flux from the Earth’s surface and vegetation, including both plant transpiration and soil evaporation1. ET is a vital component of the hydrological cycle, playing a key role in water resource management, agricultural productivity, and Earth science research2–5. ET0 represents the transpiration rate of a reference crop under specific meteorological conditions, assuming adequate water supply and no limitations from other factors. It is a critical parameter for assessing crop water requirements and is widely used to estimate actual evapotranspiration6–8. Hence, accurately estimating ET0 and comprehensively understanding the spatiotemporal evolution of its pattern are essential for optimizing agricultural water use, informing water resource management decisions, and guiding ecosystem conservation and restoration efforts9–11.

Currently, several methods for estimating ET₀ have been proposed, broadly classified into two categories: physical models and machine learning approaches. Physical models offer strong interpretability by explicitly incorporating the evapotranspiration process. However, their reliance on extensive meteorological variables for complex calculations often limits their applicability when data is incomplete12–15. In contrast, machine learning methods offer high estimated accuracy without the need to explicitly characterize complex variable relationships. However, their heavy reliance on training datasets and disregard for underlying physical mechanisms limit interpretability and increase the risk of overfitting16–19. Recent studies suggest that hybrid models combining physical and machine learning approaches offer a promising solution to these challenges13.

The discovery and widespread application of sun-induced chlorophyll fluorescence (SIF) offer a novel approach to estimating ET0. With the advent of satellite sensors capable of detecting SIF, research has predominantly focused on its relationship with Gross Primary Productivity (GPP). Since stomata play a crucial role in gas exchange by regulating the movement of carbon dioxide and water vapor between leaves and the atmosphere, they form the foundation of water-carbon coupling5. As a result, SIF not only serves as an indicator of vegetation photosynthetic activity but also holds great potential for estimating evapotranspiration rates20. Building on this principle, Wang et al.21 developed a model for estimating ET0 by leveraging the coupling relationship between SIF and ET. However, this model neglects the influence of environmental variables on the relationship between SIF and ET0, limiting its ability to accurately capture ET0’s dynamic variations. Therefore, it is crucial to incorporate environmental variables into the existing model to enhance its accuracy in simulating ET0 and capturing its dynamic fluctuations, ultimately improving ET0 estimation in regions with limited meteorological data.

In summary, the primary objective of this study is to enhance existing models for accurate ET0 estimation and capture its long-term dynamic variations. Specifically, our goals are as follows: (1) Develop an SIF-based hybrid model (RET0_SIF) by incorporating the basal crop coefficient (Kcb) to address the original model’s insensitivity to seasonal variations in ET0, validate it at the site scale, and select the optimal model (RET0_SIFo) from the candidates; (2) Compare the ET0 estimates from the RET0_SIFo model with those from five traditional empirical models to assess their ability to capture seasonal variations in ET0; (3) Generate long-term, high spatiotemporal resolution ET0 estimates (RET0_SIFd) based on the optimal model; (4) Analyze the spatiotemporal variation characteristics of ET0 within the study area using the RET0_SIFd dataset.

Materials and methods

Study area

Arid and semi-arid regions of northern China is situated in the heartland of the Eurasian continent (Fig. 1), characterized by the interaction between the East Asian monsoon and the westerly circulation. These regions experience significant climatic variations, predominantly characterized by arid and semi-arid climates, with annual precipitation decreasing progressively from around 400 mm in the eastern areas to less than 100 mm in the western areas. With the development of the social economy, rapid population growth, and increasing demand for water resources, the rational allocation of water resources in this region have become increasingly vital. Consequently, the area has become a focal point for studying the rational allocation and dynamic changes of water resources.

Meteorological station data

This study focuses on the arid and semi-arid regions of northern China, selecting 22 representative meteorological stations from the years 2000 to 2019. We performed quality control on the meteorological data from each station. Any day with missing or anomalous meteorological data resulted in the removal of all data for that particular day.

Satellite SIF data and auxiliary data

Launched in 2014, the Orbiting Carbon Observatory-2 (OCO-2) provides high spatial resolution (1.3 × 2.25 km), surpassing GOSAT and GOME-222. However, its 16-day revisit cycle and limited observational duration restrict long-term continuous analysis. To overcome this, we utilized the Continuous Solar-Induced Chlorophyll Fluorescence (CSIF) dataset, reconstructed from OCO-2 observations through machine learning integration with MODIS surface reflectance data. This spatially and temporally continuous product offers enhanced resolutions (0.05°, 4-day) and includes two sub-products: CSIFclear-inst (providing higher accuracy for instantaneous measurements) and CSIFall-daily (with improved sensitivity to spatiotemporal variations)23. This study uses CSIFall-daily data to explore the dynamic variations of ET₀ in the study area.

The LAI data used in this study were sourced from the Global Land Surface Satellite (GLASS) Leaf Area Index (LAI) product, developed by the Global Change Data Center at Beijing Normal University. This dataset combines multi-source satellite observations to provide high-quality LAI estimates24. Extensive validation across various vegetation types has shown its exceptional accuracy (R² = 0.94, RMSE = 0.61) and minimal uncertainty, making it well-suited for land surface modeling and Earth system assessments25,26. The soil moisture (SM) data are derived from the high-quality SMCI1.0 dataset, which integrates in-situ measurements from 1,648 stations with covariates to generate daily SM data at a 1 km spatial resolution across 10 soil layers (10–100 cm depth). SMCI1.0 exhibits strong performance across both temporal and spatial scales, with unbounded root mean square errors (ubRMSE: 0.041–0.052) and correlation coefficients (R: 0.883–0.919), indicating high accuracy27.

ERA5 data

To generate spatial data for ET0 in Northern China, we utilized ERA5 data from the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts as auxiliary data. ERA5 data is derived from advanced meteorological models and observational data, including satellite observations and meteorological station data, through ensemble analysis and reanalysis, ensuring high quality and accuracy28. These data are extensively utilized across various fields including climatology research, meteorological forecasting, environmental monitoring, and weather disaster warning, providing essential foundational data for scientific research, application services, and policy formulation29,30. Improved ET0_SIF model description

Wang et al.21 proposed the ET0_SIF model based on the theory of photosynthesis process and optimal stomatal behavior, which achieved good estimation accuracy in ET0 estimation in arid regions. However, the sensitivity of the model in capturing the trend of ET0 decreased when ET0 surpasses or falls below a specific threshold21. This is because in the derivation process of the coupled ET0_SIF model, SIF is not directly linked to ET0, but through the intermediate variable of vegetation evapotranspiration to realize the connection between SIF and ET0, i.e., firstly, SIF and vegetation evapotranspiration are linked, then vegetation evapotranspiration and ET0 are linked, and then finally, the relationship between SIF and ET0 can be obtained. In this case, vegetation transpiration and ET0 are linked through the base crop coefficient (Kcb). However, the calculation of Kcb was simplified during the model derivation process, and although this treatment reduces the complexity of the model, Kcb is not a constant but a variable affected by environmental factors32,33, and thus simplifying Kcb blurs the effect of environmental changes on ET0 estimation, which leads to the model’s insensitivity to seasonal variations in ET0. To address the above problems, this study constrains the coupled ET0_SIF model of Wang et al. (2023) by adding Kcb, and then proposes an improved ET0_SIF model. Machine learning model

We test the potential of the RET0_SIF model to estimate ET0using three different types of machine learning methods, including a linear model, and two nonlinear models. Ridge is a linear regression model which is a regularized form of ordinary least squares for data with linear relationships, covariance and more noise36,37. In SVR model, the goal of the model is to find a hyperplane such that the distance of a sample point from that hyperplane is as small as possible, which performs well due to its dataset with complex structure and nonlinear relationships, especially when the data dimensionality is high or the sample size is small38,39. Random forest is an integrated learning method that makes estimations combining multiple decision trees, it is suitable for handling large-scale datasets and also performs well for high-dimensional and sparse data, and it has been demonstrated to have a good performance in estimating evapotranspiration21,40,41.

Model accuracy evaluation

In this study, four metrics were used for assessment: R2, RMSE, MAE, and NRMSE. R2 measures the goodness of fit between the model and the data, without considering absolute errors. RMSE amplifies extreme errors through squared differences, making it suitable for high-risk predictions. MAE reflects the average error, disregards error direction, and is sensitive to the magnitude of errors. NRMSE enables cross-scale comparisons. These four metrics are complementary, and their combined use provides a comprehensive evaluation of model performance by assessing systematic bias, extreme errors, and generalization ability, thus avoiding the limitations of relying on a single metric42–45. Trend analysis

The Sen’s trend analysis is a nonparametric statistical method for estimating trends in a data set. This method demonstrates robustness in handling non-parametric statistical time series, showing resilience against measurement errors and outliers. Hence, it is regarded as a preferred approach for trend analysis. The main idea of the method is to calculate the slopes of all data points two by two and then take the median of these slopes as the estimated trend. Result

Hybrid model performance evaluation

The linear model ET0_SIF_Ridge exhibits an R2 ranging 0.09–0.91, with RMSE ranging 6.68–22.75 mm/8 days, MAE ranging 4.85–19.91 mm/8 days, and NRMSE ranging 0.07–0.27 (Fig. 2). In comparison, the nonlinear models outperform the linear model in simulating ET0. Specifically, the ET0_SIF_SVR model achieves an R2 ranging 0.06–0.89, RMSE ranging 5.91–21.40 mm/8 days, MAE ranging 4.45–17.80 mm/8 days, and NRMSE ranging 0.08–0.27. Similarly, the ET0_SIF_RFR model shows an R2 ranging 0.09–0.90, RMSE ranging 4.60–20.21 mm/8 days, MAE ranging 3.40–17.39 mm/8 days, and NRMSE ranging 0.08–0.29.

When RET0_SIF (VPD0.5, SIF, LAI, and SM) was used as an input variable, the accuracy of ET0 estimation improved significantly at all sites. The RET0_SIF_Ridge model achieved an R2 ranging from 0.75 to 0.93, RMSE ranging from 4.36 to 10.86 mm/8 days, MAE ranging from 3.24 to 8.35 mm/8 days, and NRMSE ranging from 0.05 to 0.12. Compared to the ET0_SIF_Ridge model, these values improved by 0.20 to 0.80 for R2, 3.42 to 13.95 mm/8 days for RMSE, 3.32 to 13.46 mm/8 days for MAE, and 0.05 to 0.17 for NRMSE. The nonlinear models, RET0_SIF_SVR and RET0_SIF_RFR, also showed similar performance improvements. The RET0_SIF_SVR model achieved an R2 ranging from 0.75 to 0.93, RMSE from 4.36 to 10.86 mm/8 days, MAE from 3.24 to 8.35 mm/8 days, and NRMSE from 0.05 to 0.12. Similarly, the RET0_SIF_RFR model demonstrated robust performance, with an R2 ranging from 0.75 to 0.93, RMSE from 4.36 to 10.86 mm/8 days, MAE from 3.24 to 8.35 mm/8 days, and NRMSE from 0.05 to 0.12. These results demonstrate that the improved model significantly enhances the accuracy of ET0 estimation.

We compare the ET₀ estimates from the RET0_SIF and ET0_SIF models against the ET0PM (Fig. S1 and S2). As shown in Fig. S1, the linear model tends to produce greater errors in ET0 estimation, significantly underestimating the values. In contrast, the improved hybrid model (RET0_SIF) shows notable performance gains, with an R² greater than 0.8 across all 22 sites. The sites of Hutubi, Gaotai, Altay, and Damao exhibit the most significant improvements (Fig. S2). Overall, incorporating the basal crop coefficient (Kcb) to constrain the model substantially enhanced its performance. The results from the three hybrid models were similar, indicating that the RET0_SIF model is robust and not solely reliant on the algorithmic strengths of machine learning.

Evaluation of different models to identify seasonal variations in ET0

To further evaluate the ability of the RET0_SIF model to capture the seasonal variations in ET0, we compare the performance of six hybrid models in identifying intra-annual variation trends of ET0 (Fig. 3). The ET0 estimates from the ET0_SIF model show significant discrepancies when compared to ET0PM, particularly during the non-growing season (from day 320 to day 50 of the following year), where ET0 is notably overestimated. Furthermore, there is a noticeable deviation in the timing of the peak ET0 between the two models. At most stations, the peak ET0 estimated by the ET0_SIF model occurs about 50 days later than the peak observed with ET0PM (The ET0_SIF model reaches its maximum around day 200, while ET0PM typically peaks around day 150). Additionally, the peak ET0 values estimated by the ET0_SIF model are underestimated.

ET0 estimated by the RET0_SIF model showed good agreement with ET0PM at most sites (Fig. 3). The RET0_SIF model showed significant improvement in identifying seasonal changes in ET0 compared to the ET0_SIF model, with the three hybrid models estimating ET0 values within 1 mm/8days of the average ET0PM error, and seasonal fluctuations within 10 days of the error. The RET0_SIF model significantly improved the estimation performance of ET0 during the non-growing season, addressing the limitation of the ET0_SIF model in capturing the dynamic changes of ET0 during this period. Additionally, the hybrid models RET0_SIF_Ridge, RET0_SIF_SVR, and RET0_SIF_RFR, which employ different machine learning methods, exhibited similar trends, further demonstrating the stability of the RET₀_SIF model. In conclusion, we consider the effect of environmental variables on the ET0_SIF relationship in our model, which significantly improves the performance of the SIF_ ET0 model in capturing the seasonal variations of ET0, and proves that the RET0_SIF model can be realized to accurately capture the seasonal variations of ET0.

RET0_SIFo VS empirical models

We chose the optimal model (RET0_SIFo) from among six hybrid models and compared the ET0 values computed by the RET0_SIFo model (averaged over the years 2000–2019 for each site) with those from five traditional empirical models grounded in physical processes (temperature-based traditional empirical models ET0OU, ET0HS; radiation-based empirical models ET0PT, ET0Ma, ET0Jen). As shown in Fig. 4, the ET0 values computed using the three radiation-based empirical models (ET0PT, ET0Ma, and ET0Jen) exhibit strong correlation with ET0PM, achieving R² values of 0.81, 0.80, and 0.80, respectively. Conversely, the ET0 values derived from the two temperature-based empirical models (ET0OU and ET0HS) show weaker correlations with those computed by RET0_SIFo, yielding R² values of 0.74 and 0.45, respectively. For further assessment of the RET0_SIFo model’s performance, Fig. 4c illustrates a comparison of ET0 values estimated by RET0_SIFo alongside those from empirical models with respect to ET0PM. The performance of the five traditional empirical models varies widely in terms of performance. The three models ET0PT, ET0Ma and ET0Jen underestimated the ET0 values at all sites in the study area, and the average difference between ET0PM and them was 2.29 mm/8days, 16.87 mm/8days, and 8.42 mm/8days, respectively, whereas the empirical models based on the temperatures, ET0OU and ET0HS, preferred overestimating the ET0 values. The average difference between ET0PM and them is −11.82 mm/8days, −11.44 mm/8days, respectively.

RET0_SIFd compared to existing potential evapotranspiration (PET) products

The RET0_SIF model has demonstrated excellent performance at the site scale(Fig. 5). To evaluate its applicability at the spatial scale, we integrated satellite remote sensing and reanalysis data to generate high spatiotemporal resolution (8-day, 0.05°) ET0 estimates (RET0_SIFd) for the period 2000–2019 and conducted a comparative analysis with existing potential evapotranspiration (PET) products. In terms of spatial distribution, RET0_SIFd exhibits a high degree of consistency with three other PET datasets, all of which indicate the highest evapotranspiration in the region surrounding the Tarim Basin in Xinjiang, followed by central and eastern Inner Mongolia, with the lowest values observed in Northeast China and mountainous regions. Regarding evapotranspiration magnitude, RET0_SIFd closely resembles the MODIS16 PET product, with values primarily ranging from 1000 to 1500 mm, whereas the GLEAM and TerraClimate products mainly fall within the 500–1000 mm range. These results demonstrate that the spatial distribution pattern of RET0_SIFd is consistent with existing PET products, verifying the reliable scalability of the RET0_SIF model from site scale to spatial scale.

Temporal variation characteristics of ET0

From the perspective of interannual trends, ET0 shows an overall increasing trend, with an increase rate of 2.56 mm/year during the period from 2000 to 2019(Fig. S3). According to regional geographic characteristics, March-May, June-August, September-November, and December-February are categorized into four seasons: spring, summer, autumn, and winter, respectively. In the study area, ET0 exhibits an increasing trend across all four seasons, with the most significant growth observed in autumn and winter. On a seasonal scale, the order of ET₀ is summer > spring > autumn > winter. The highest average ET₀ occurs in summer at 595.67 mm, while the lowest average ET₀ is recorded in winter at 121.28 mm.

Discussion

SIF as a pointer of vegetative photosynthesis has a close relationship with Vegetation transpiration (T)48–50, and the previous authors established a hybrid ET0 model based on SIF according to the crop coefficient method and the mechanistic relationship between vegetative physiological processes and SIF, which provides a new approach for ET0 estimation in regions lacking meteorological data provided a new method. Although the ET0_SIF model simplifies ET0 estimation by relying solely on SIF × VPD0.5, it overlooks the influence of environmental factors on the SIF-ET0 relationship and fails to accurately capture the seasonal variations in ET021. Evapotranspiration is composed of both vegetation transpiration and soil evapotranspiration, whereas the simulation of ET0 using only VPD0.5×SIF, which takes more into account the effect of stomata on the physiological process of vegetation transpiration, overemphasizes the contribution of vegetation transpiration to ET0 and ignores the important role of natural factors on the evapotranspiration process, and thus the ET0 computed by the ET0_SIF during the growing season suffers from a high degree of uncertainty51–53. In the non-growing season, the vegetation photosynthesis is greatly weakened or even stopped, the SIF fluorescence signal is weak, and the VPD0.5×SIF tends to 0 due to the influence of the SIF value, so ET0_SIF cannot accurately simulate the variation trend of ET0 during the non-growing season. In fact, ET0 is not only affected by the physiological state of vegetation, but also by the evaporation environment, and there is a trend of change even in the non-growing season. Overall, the ET0_SIF model overemphasized the influence of vegetation physiological processes and neglected the influence of environmental factors, and was unable to accurately identify the changes of ET0 in the non-growing season. Kcb is an important parameter for calculating ET0, and understanding the pattern of change in Kcb is crucial for understanding the relationship between SIF and ET054,55, so we consider incorporating a basic crop coefficient into the model to address the shortcomings of the ET0_SIF model. Kcb represents the ratio of crop evapotranspiration to reference evapotranspiration under conditions where water availability does not restrict transpiration (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:Kcb=\frac{Tc}{ET0}$$\end{document})12, Kcb is not a constant value, it is influenced by a variety of external environmental factors, so it tends to vary over time and space. Therefore, several studies have attempted to quantify the dynamics of Kcb, which has a clear physical basis in the vegetation canopy, as evidenced by the fact that the greater the plant leaf surface area, the greater the total stomatal volume, the greater the stomatal conductance, and the greater the opportunity for transpiration and diffusion of water vapor from the leaf56. Similarly, a larger area of vegetation cover or leaf area means that more sunlight is absorbed and more energy is used to convert transpiration54. This has also been demonstrated in previous studies, where researchers have found a significant non-linear relationship between LAI and Kcb for different crops based on measured evapotranspiration data57–59. Earlier studies focused on the effect of vegetation canopy on Kcb, and in fact, soil moisture has an equally important effect on Kcb34. As soil moisture increases, capillary hydraulic conductivity and root water uptake in the soil also increase, and leaf water potential decreases, resulting in reduced resistance to soil to leaf water transport and a consequent increase in vegetative transpiration and Kcb. In summary, when considering the use of environmental variables to quantify the key parameter Kcb in conjunction with SIF to estimate ET0 will have better performance.

In this study, the optimal hybrid model RET0_SIFo was compared with traditional empirical models for calculating ET0 values, and the results showed that the RET0_SIFo was better than the traditional empirical model in the study area. This could be attributed to the complex and nonlinear regression process involved in simulating ET0, which necessitates considering the interaction of multiple factors. Moreover, empirical models exhibit distinct regional differences, and fixed model parameters often struggle to deliver satisfactory performance across different regions4,60. Conversely, hybrid models combine physical constraints with machine learning techniques, thereby retaining a physical understanding of the ET0 process while leveraging the powerful fitting capabilities of machine learning methods to achieve accurate ET0 estimation61. There is an obvious overestimation of ET0 calculated by the ET0OU and ET0HS models in summer, probably because these two models are temperature-based models that are sensitive to temperature changes, and the extreme high temperatures and large temperature differences in summer in the arid regions may have affected the accuracy of the models. Conversely, the performance of the ET0Ma and ET0Jen models contrasts with this observation. These radiation-based empirical models yield ET0 values in close proximity to ET0PM during summer. But their estimation of ET0values during winter tends to be underestimated, likely influenced by weakened solar radiation and snowfall conditions during the winter months. The ET0PT model, grounded on a more robust physical foundation compared to the other empirical models, exhibits superior performance among the five tested models. But it still falls short in comparison to the hybrid model15.

This study introduces a SIF-based hybrid model constrained by reference crop coefficients, significantly enhancing the accuracy of ET0 estimation. The model effectively captures the dynamic variations of ET0 while maintaining simple parameterization and well-defined physical constraints, ensuring robust spatial scalability. In summary, this study presents a novel approach to ET0 estimation, overcoming the limitations of traditional methods that rely heavily on meteorological variables. By enabling large-scale, continuous ET0 estimation, it provides a valuable reference for optimizing regional water resource allocation. But this study still has certain limitations. Firstly, although the RET0_SIF model has demonstrated strong performance in the arid and semi-arid regions of northern China, its validation has been limited to arid areas. Considering that different climate zones exhibit significant variations in vegetation types (e.g., C3 and C4 plants have distinct photosynthetic pathways)31 and climatic conditions (which influence vegetation and natural evaporation processes), the applicability of the SIF-based ET0 estimation model in other climatic regions requires further verification. Secondly, regional ET0 estimation is influenced by multiple environmental factors, including meteorological conditions and vegetation physiological processes. The complex coupling of these environmental elements and their impact on both SIF and ET0 remain unclear, leading to inherent uncertainties in the current SIF-based ET0 estimation model. For example, the model still exhibits certain errors in simulating extreme ET0 values during summer, specifically underestimating ET0 (Fig. 3). This discrepancy may be related to environmental stress during summer, where extreme high temperatures could lead to photoinhibition or stomatal closure in vegetation62, decoupling the relationship between SIF and ET0, thereby affecting the model’s estimation accuracy.

To address the current limitations of this study, our next steps include evaluating the model’s performance across different climate zones to assess its applicability in diverse climatic conditions. Additionally, we plan to investigate the environmental thresholds affecting the coupling relationship between SIF and ET0 to reduce model uncertainties. This study has developed an improved RET0_SIF model, which overcomes the original model’s insensitivity to ET0 dynamics and enables spatial mapping of ET0 based on SIF data. This provides theoretical guidance and scientific support for large-scale ET0 estimation in arid regions lacking meteorological data.

Conclusion

The novelty of this study lies in the construction of a hybrid model based on SIF, which enables accurate simulation of ET0 and precise identification of its seasonal variations. This makes spatially continuous observations of ET0 in arid regions lacking meteorological elements possible, providing a reference for ET0 estimation in these areas and offering valuable insights for the rational utilization of water resources in arid regions. Additionally, this study generates a long-term, high spatiotemporal resolution ET0 dataset based on SIF data and explores the spatiotemporal variation characteristics of ET0.

Response to letter re: Improving chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy in cancer patients using a combined qigong and self-administered acupressure intervention: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: DST Cheung
Published: 2025

We sincerely thank Wei et al.1 for their insightful comments on our study on the combined qigong and self-administered acupressure intervention for chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN) published in ESMO Open.2 The methodological and clinical considerations raised align closely with limitations and recommendations already identified in our study,2 reinforcing their relevance and providing valuable guidance for advancing non-pharmacological CIPN management.

The recognition by Wei et al. of the clinical significance of our findings is noted, particularly the reduction in the Functional Assessment of Cancer Therapy/Gynecologic Oncology Group–Neurotoxicity scores exceeding the minimal important difference of 3.3-4.4 points. This underscores the intervention’s meaningful impact on patient-reported outcomes (PROs), which are critical for assessing CIPN given their sensitivity to symptomatic changes. Regarding the lack of significant changes in objective neurophysiological measures (e.g. sensory nerve action potential amplitude and sensory nerve conduction velocity via DPNCheck), we agree that this may reflect limited statistical power and/or the discrepancy between self-reported symptoms and electrophysiological findings in CIPN.3,4 Persistent structural nerve damage despite symptomatic improvement, or the ability of PROs to detect subtle changes not captured by standard neurophysiological tests, may explain the discrepancy. We concur that incorporating more sensitive tools, such as quantitative sensory testing, might better capture small-fiber neuropathy in future trials.

The intervention’s design, rooted in traditional Chinese medicine principles and combining Baduanjin qigong with acupressure, was intentionally pragmatic to enhance real-world applicability. The high adherence rates (81.5% self-practice compliance) affirm its acceptability, which is encouraging for clinical translation. The limitation of not including an active control, such as sham acupressure or light exercise, is acknowledged. While such comparators could help isolate specific effects of the intervention components, research suggested that sham controls may also have limitations, such as inadvertently sharing active ingredients with the intervention.5 To better address potential placebo or self-efficacy influences, a three-arm design is recommended in larger future studies, incorporating the intervention, an active control, and a usual care/wait-list control group for robust comparison and clearer insights into the intervention’s specific effects.

The recommendation by Wei et al. for mechanistic studies is well taken. While we hypothesized that acupressure and qigong may improve local circulation, modulate neurotrophic factors, and reduce inflammation, these pathways were not directly measured due to resource constraints. Integrating biomarkers like nerve growth factor, pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g. interleukin-6, tumor necrosis factor-α), or heart rate variability in future trials will be useful to elucidate the neuroimmunological mechanisms underlying the intervention’s effects.

The observed attenuation of treatment effects at the 28-week follow-up, a common challenge in behavioral interventions, underscores the need for strategies to promote sustained adherence. We also agree that evaluating the intervention in diverse populations, including non-Chinese patients and those with acute CIPN during active chemotherapy, is a critical next step to broaden its applicability.

In conclusion, the constructive feedback from Wei et al. is appreciated, as it affirms our self-reflection of limitations and recommendations and provides directions for advancing qigong and acupressure as an evidence-based, low-risk, patient-centered approach to CIPN management. We look forward to future trials that build on these findings, incorporating mechanistic insights and optimized delivery methods. Collaborative opportunities to refine this promising intervention are welcomed.

Effectiveness of PAH Specific Treatment Is Comparable Between PAH Associated With Connective Tissue Disease and Idiopathic PAH: Data From the Multicentre National Registry
Authors: Anna Smukowska‐Gorynia, Sylwia Iwańczyk, Michał Ciurzyński, Marcin Waligóra, Marcin Kurzyna, Piotr Pruszczyk, Grzegorz Kopeć, Katarzyna Mizia‐Stec, Ilona Skoczylas, Łukasz Chrzanowski, Piotr Błaszczak, Katarzyna Ptaszyńska, Grzegorz Grześk, Ewa Malinowska, Małgorzata Peregud‐Pogorzelska, Michał Tomaszewski, Ewa Mroczek, Miłosz Jaguszewski, Ewa Lewicka, Katarzyna Widejko, Agnieszka Pawlak, Wojciech Jacheć, Zbigniew Gąsior, Katarzyna Betkier‐Lipińska, Judyta Winowska‐Józwa, Marta Banaszkiewicz, Tatiana Mularek‐Kubzdela
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a rare disease with poor prognosis. The same treatment is recommended in patients with PAH associated with connective tissue disease (CTD) and idiopathic PAH (IPAH). However, the effectiveness of PAH‐specific treatment in these both groups is inconsistent. The study incorporated data from the prospective, multicentre polish registry (BNP‐PL) by comparison of baseline versus 1‐year follow‐up clinical, laboratory, echocardiographic and hemodynamic parameters in PAH‐CTD versus IPAH group. The all‐cause 1‐year mortality was also analysed. 624 patients were included in the study: 176 (28%) PAH‐CTD and 448 (72%) IPAH. 48 (8% of the total 624) patients died during 1‐year follow up: 32 (7%) with IPAH and 16 (9%) with PAH‐CTD (p = 0.4). The effectiveness of treatment was examined in 421 patients: 115 (27%) PAH‐CTD and 306 (73%) IPAH. The improvement of treatment was effective in both groups in terms of WHO FC (p < 0.001 in both groups), 4‐strata risk model (p < 0.001 in both groups), 6 min walking test (6MWT, p < 0.001 in IPAH, p = 0.001 in PAH‐CTD), Δ6MWT (p = 0.19 between PAH‐CTD and IPAH), tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (p < 0.001 in both groups), NT‐ proBNP (p < 0.001 in IPAH and p < 0.01 in PAH‐CTD), mean pulmonary arterial pressure (p = 0.043 in IPAH and p = 0.01 in PAH‐CTD), pulmonary vascular resistance (p < 0.001 in both groups) and cardiac output (p < 0.001 in IPAH and p = 0.034 in PAH‐CTD). PAH specific treatment in PAH‐CTD patients is as effective as in IPAH patients regarding improvement in WHO FC, 4‐strata risk model, 6MWT, Δ6MWT, TAPSE, NT‐proBNP, mPAP, PVR and CO at 1‐year follow‐up. 1‐year all‐cause mortality was not significantly different between PAH‐CTD and IPAH patients.Keywords: comparison of IPAH and PAH‐CTD, IPAH, PAH‐CTD, PAH‐specific therapy, registry

Introduction

Idiopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension (IPAH) and pulmonary arterial hypertension associated with connective tissue disease (PAH‐CTD) are the most common subgroups of pulmonary hypertension [1]. Among PAH‐CTD, systemic scleroderma (SSc) is the most prevalent multisystem connective tissue disease [2, 3], followed by systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) [4, 5, 6], mixed CTD [4], rheumatoid arthritis [7], dermatomyositis [8], and Sjögren's syndrome [9]. According to the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) and European Respiratory Society (ERS) Guidelines patients with PAH‐CTD should be treated in the same way as the patients suffering from IPAH [10] with the PAH specific drugs including molecules affecting prostacyclin pathway (epoprostenol, treprostinil, iloprost, selexipag), endothelin receptor pathway (macitentan, bosentan, ambrisentan) and nitric oxide/natriuretic pathway: stimulators of soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC: riociguat) and phosphodiesterase five inhibitors (PDE5i: sildenafil, tadalafil). Novel agents modifying the activin pathway related to bone morphogenetic protein receptor 2 (BMPR2, e.g., sotatercept) showed promising results [11, 12, 13]. In addition, several sub‐analyses of randomized trials investigating mentioned above PAH specific drugs were performed in the subgroups of patients with PAH‐CTD: epoprostenol [14], treprostinil [15], bosentan [16], ambrisentan [17], selexipag [18]. However, in literature, the data about effectiveness of PAH specific treatment in PAH‐CTD compared to IPAH remain inconclusive. Earlier studies suggested that the response to PAH‐specific therapy was less pronounced in patients with CTD comparing to those without CTD [19, 20, 21]. In contrast, more recent data indicate that the introduction of PAH‐specific therapy in PAH‐CTD, particularly at an early stage of the disease (patients in World Health Organization functional class [WHO FC] I or II), is associated with improved prognosis [22, 23, 24, 25]. Thus, the aim of the present study was to compare the effectiveness of the PAH‐specific treatment in IPAH group (including familial and toxin/drug induced PAH) versus PAH‐CTD group under real‐world conditions.

Methods

The study incorporated data from the national Database of the Pulmonary Hypertension in the Polish Population (BNP‐PL, ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03959748), a prospective, multicentre registry. The details of the registry have been described previously [26]. The registry included patients from 21 PH reference centres in Poland accredited by the National Health Fund to treat PAH. Patients diagnosed with PAH and PAH‐CTD, according to the criteria outlined in the ESC/ERS guidelines [10] were included in the registry. For the aim of the current study we analyzed only prospectively enrolled incident cases who were diagnosed with IPAH or PAH‐CTD between March 1, 2018 and August 30, 2023. The effectiveness of the treatment was examined by comparison of baseline versus 1‐year follow‐up WHO FC, 6‐min walking test (6MWT), Borg dyspnea scale, right atrium area (RAA), tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE), NT‐ fragment pro brain natriuretic peptide (NT‐proBNP), 4‐strata risk assessment model (including all four parameters: WHO‐FC, 6MWT and NT‐proBNP) and hemodynamic measurements derived from right heart catheterization in both groups. In addition, the all‐cause 1‐year mortality was analyzed. To determine the predominant cause of PH (Group 1 vs. Group 3), the criteria described in Nathan SD et al. were used [27]. Familial PAH and toxin/drug‐induced PAH were included within the IPAH group because of the similar clinical characteristics, hemodynamic profile, and therapeutic management shared by these subtypes.

All procedures in the study were carried out in accordance with Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants and/or their legal guardians.

The study was conducted in accordance with the STROBE checklist for observational studies.

Statistical Analyses

Statistical analyses were done with the use of PQStat version 1.6.6 (PQStat Software, Poland). The distribution of data was assessed with Shapiro‐Wilk test. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median values (first‐third quartile) as appropriate. Group comparison was performed by Chi2 test or Fisher exact test. Data that was not normally distributed was analysed with Mann‐Whitney test, while normally distributed data was analysed using t‐test. The effectiveness of treatment (baseline vs. 1 year follow‐up) was checked with Wilcoxon test or Mann‐Whitney test. Mann‐Whitney test was used for comparison of variables in different patients within one patient group (IPAH or PAH‐CTD). Wilcoxon signed‐rank test was used for comparison of the baseline versus 1‐year follow‐up variables in the same patient to analyse changes between patient groups (IPAH and PAH‐CTD). Given that the majority of patients were classified as intermediate mortality risk based on the 3‐strata baseline risk assessment outlined in the ESC 2022 guidelines [10], we adopted a more granular risk stratification approach to improve long‐term survival prediction. For this purpose, we utilized the validated COMPERA 2.0 four‐strata model [28], which incorporates assessments of functional class, 6‐min walking distance (6MWD), and NT‐proBNP levels, measured both at diagnosis and after 1 year of follow‐up.

Patients were categorized into four risk groups: low, intermediate‐low, intermediate‐high, or high mortality risk. To enable a direct comparison of clinical outcomes, risk strata at baseline and follow‐up were analysed separately for patients with IPAH and PAH‐CTD.

To illustrate transitions between risk groups over time, we employed a Sankey diagram, which visualizes changes in risk strata from baseline to follow‐up. This approach effectively captured the dynamic nature of risk evolution, offering valuable insights into disease progression and response to treatment in these patient populations. This analysis was restricted to patients who underwent follow‐up assessments (i.e., those with at least 12 months of observation who survived through this period). 1‐year survival was analysed using Kaplan‐Meier curves in both groups. p‐value < 0.05 was the cut‐off value of statistical significance.

Results

In total, 624 patients were included in the study: 448 (72%) in the IPAH group and 176 (28%) in the PAH‐CTD group. The most common CTD was SSc (65%). Significantly more women were in the PAH‐CTD group compared with the IPAH group (p < 0.001). Patients with CTD more frequently had interstitial lung disease (ILD, p < 0.001), hypothyroidism (p = 0.001), and used home oxygen therapy (p = 0.03) than those with IPAH. Furthermore, CTD patients were more commonly treated with glucocorticoids (GCS, p < 0.001), other immunosuppressants (p < 0.001), and proton pump inhibitors (PPIs, p < 0.001).

Patients in the IPAH group had a higher baseline 4‐strata risk score (p = 0.01) and more often presented with hyperthyroidism (p = 0.04), type 2 diabetes mellitus (DM2, p < 0.001), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD, p = 0.002), and sleep apnea (p = 0.048). They were also more likely to have a history of smoking and were more frequently treated with torasemide (p = 0.001) and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs, p = 0.01).

Baseline PAH‐specific therapy more often included triple therapy (p = 0.03) and epoprostenol (p = 0.03) in the IPAH group compared with the PAH‐CTD group, while PAH‐CTD patients were more often treated with monotherapy (p < 0.001).

Discussion

The main finding from our study is that effectiveness of PAH treatment with PAH specific drugs was similar in patients suffered from IPAH and PAH‐CTD. Clinical improvement in terms of WHO FC, 4‐strata risk model, 6MWT, TAPSE, NT‐proBNP, mean pulmonary arterial pressure (mPAP), pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) and cardiac output (CO) was statistically significant in both groups at 1‐year follow‐up. In addition, Δ6MWT at 1‐year follow‐up did not differ significantly between PAH‐CTD and IPAH group. The only parameter which achieved significant improvement in IPAH group but not in PAH‐CTD group was RAA assessed in echocardiography (p < 0.001, p = 0.38). However, there is a noticeable trend toward a reduction in right atrial area after 1 year of treatment, also in the PAH‐CTD subgroup. Our findings indicate that in real‐life settings, the effectiveness of PAH‐specific treatment is comparable between both investigated groups. In contrast, the study by Rhee et al. included patients from randomized controlled trials concluded that patients with PAH‐CTD had a smaller improvement in the 6MWT when assigned to active treatment compared with patients with IPAH [29]. One possible explanation is that most patients with SSc are now screened annually for the development of PAH in accordance with the ESC/ERS Guidelines, and PAH‐specific treatment is introduced earlier; therefore, the improvement in 6MWT can be comparable between the IPAH and PAH‐CTD groups [2, 10]. Moreover, in the aforementioned study after adjusting for potential confounders (medications or comorbidities), the Δ6MWT did not differ significantly between CTD‐PAH and IPAH. One should noticed that the observation period ranged between 12 and 18 weeks, which limited examination of long‐term effects. In addition, as in our study, mortality was not significantly higher in the PAH‐CTD group compared with the IPAH group [29]. In our study, the 1‐year survival rate was 91% in the PAH‐CTD group, which is similar to previously reported studies in SSc‐PAH (85%–95%) [1, 20, 30, 31, 32, 33]. In one of the recent meta‐analysis eight studies with the total of 530 PAH‐CTD patients were included. The pooled 1‐year survival was 90% which is comparable to our results [34]. One should note that overall mortality could be affected by COVID‐19, especially in the early phase of the pandemic [35, 36, 37], or potential overtreatment with β‐blockers [38]. Moreover, it has been demonstrated that among patients with CTD, those with concomitant pulmonary hypertension and interstitial lung disease (ILD) have the poorest prognosis [39]. In our study, patients with CTD‐associated pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH‐CTD) more frequently presented with ILD and required long‐term oxygen therapy compared to the idiopathic PAH group. Thus, it should be emphasized that after 1 year of follow‐up, treatment outcomes in the PAH‐CTD group were comparable to those in the IPAH group, despite a higher prevalence of ILD, more frequent use of long‐term oxygen therapy, and more common use of initial monotherapy in the PAH‐CTD group. Previous studies, including one focused on systemic sclerosis‐associated PAH (SSc‐PAH) [30] and the most recent analysis from the COMPERA registry [1], have shown that monotherapy is associated with worse survival compared to combination therapy.

Furthermore, immunosuppressed patients are more susceptible to infections. In our registry, PAH‐CTD patients were more frequently treated with immunosuppressive agents and, consequently, were significantly less likely to receive intravenous epoprostenol. However, at baseline, patients in the IPAH group had more advanced disease, as reflected by higher scores in the 4‐strata risk model, which may explain the more frequent initiation of upfront triple therapy in this group compared to PAH‐CTD patients.

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (DM2) was a more common comorbidity among IPAH patients than among PAH‐CTD patients, and DM2 has been previously associated with poorer survival outcomes [40]. In our study, hypothyroidism was more prevalent in PAH‐CTD patients than in those with IPAH, consistent with the findings of Antonelli et al., who demonstrated a higher incidence of hypothyroidism in systemic sclerosis patients compared with controls [41]. Additionally, several studies have reported an increased prevalence of hypothyroidism in PAH patients compared with the general population [42, 43]. Conversely, hyperthyroidism has been associated with an elevated prevalence of echocardiographically estimated pulmonary hypertension, based on increased tricuspid regurgitant jet velocity [44, 45, 46]. Nevertheless, a detailed characterization of thyrotoxicosis in patients with PAH warrants further investigation.

In summary, despite the heterogeneity and significant clinical differences between PAH‐CTD and IPAH patients, no significant differences were observed in clinical improvement or all‐cause mortality between the two groups at 1‐year follow‐up.

Strengths and Limitations

Our study has several strengths. First of all, we present the results of the large multicentre PAH registry recruiting patients in the modern era of pharmacological PAH treatment. Second, by including all polish canters which diagnose and treat PAH patient we are able to estimate the outcome in large group of IPAH and PAH‐CTD patients. Finally, we assessed IPAH and PAH‐CTD patients' data in real world clinical practise.

The main limitation of our study is that the data 57 (9%) of patients were lost during 1 year follow up. Moreover, the group of PAH‐CTD patients seems to be underrepresented in our registry which probably results from the lack of national screening programs in CTD population. Furthermore, we collect data on the baseline characteristics of our patients rather than any analysis of their prognosis and the statistical analysis was restricted to those who survived to 12 months. Last, the sotatercept was not used in the study period. The methodological limitations inherent to registry‐based studies have been recently acknowledged [47].

Conclusions

PAH specific treatment in PAH‐CTD patients is as effective as in IPAH patients regarding improvement in WHO FC, 4‐strata risk model, 6MWT, Δ6MWT, TAPSE, NT‐proBNP, mPAP, PVR and CO at 1‐year follow‐up. 1‐year all‐cause mortality was not significantly different between PAH‐CTD and IPAH patients.

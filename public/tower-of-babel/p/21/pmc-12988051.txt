A real-world retrospective analysis comparing the effectiveness of Azvudine and Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir in COVID-19 patients with diabetes
Authors: Zefeng Zhu, Qiqin Chen, Zichen Wang, Qijin Chen, Xialing Li, Yeying Liu, Haiqing Lu, Junyi Feng, Minhui Xie, Xiaomin Dong, Bo Chen, Jingqing Huang, Xian Zeng, Zheng Zeng, Yan Yuan, Feiyi Xu, Xueyu Liao, Bojia Liang, Wei Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Azvudine and Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir have been approved for the treatment of mild to moderate COVID-19 in adults at high risk for progression to severe disease. Diabetes is a high-risk complication of COVID-19. Currently, the effectiveness of Azvudine and Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir in patients with both diabetes and COVID-19 remains unclear. This study aims to compare the effectiveness of these two drugs in patients with diabetes and COVID-19. In this retrospective study, data from 13,763 hospitalized COVID-19 patients with comorbid diabetes mellitus between December 5, 2022, and January 31, 2023, were analyzed. These patients were from six different hospitals in the Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region, including 288 patients treated with Azvudine and 112 patients treated with Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir. The primary outcome comprised a composite endpoint of disease progression, characterized by all-cause mortality, intensive care unit (ICU) admission, or need for invasive mechanical ventilation. Data were collected retrospectively through electronic medical record review, supplemented by telephone follow-up. We used propensity-score matching (PSM) to allow for a more direct comparison of the effectiveness between the two antiviral treatment groups. Among 400 enrolled patients, 41 deaths occurred during hospitalization or post-discharge follow-up (Azvudine group: n = 29; Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir group: n = 12). After propensity score matching (PSM), 17 deaths were recorded across both cohorts. Statistical analysis demonstrated no significant between-group differences in composite outcomes (including invasive mechanical ventilation and ICU admission). However, Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir showed a non-significant trend toward lower all-cause mortality (aHR = 0.502, 95% CI: 0.133–1.180) for all-cause mortality reduction versus Azvudine (Crude HR = 0.502; Adjusted HR [aHR] = 0.502, 95% CI: 0.133–1.180). Glucocorticoid use during therapy was associated with reduced mortality risk, while concomitant anticoagulant therapy increased composite outcome risk. Findings indicated a progressive elevation in mortality risk with advancing diabetes severity grade. Notably, Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir was associated with a hazard ratio of 0.37 (95% CI: 0.11–1.00), corresponding to a 63% point-estimate reduction in mortality risk. In severe diabetes patients (HR = 0.37, 95% CI: 0.11–1.00), although this result did not reach statistical significance. Regarding outcome measures, patients with diabetes and COVID-19 infection treated with Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir demonstrated a lower incidence of myocardial injury and a non-significant trend toward reduced mortality, but no significant difference in the composite endpoint. compared to those treated with Azvudine.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-42215-6.

Introduction

In December 2019, China first reported the initial case of novel coronavirus pneumonia, which rapidly spread worldwide. The World Health Organization designated the virus as severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 SARS-CoV-21. Following shifts in pandemic containment strategies, a major COVID-19 outbreak peaked in late December 2022 in China. Studies indicate that diabetes has been established as an independent risk factor for SARS-CoV-2 infection2. A meta-analysis has demonstrated that diabetes significantly increases the risk of ICU admission and mortality in patients with COVID-193. The case fatality rate among COVID-19 patients with diabetes is approximately three times higher than in non-diabetic patients. Consequently, effective antiviral drugs are crucial for reducing patients’ mortality and preventing disease progression. The preferred oral antiviral treatments for SARS-CoV-2 patients in China include Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir and Azvudine. Research demonstrates that these oral agents can shorten hospitalization duration, reduce viral load, decrease in-hospital mortality, and mitigate disease progression4–6. Nevertheless, the efficacy of these two oral antivirals remains incompletely understood for the special high-risk population of diabetic patients.

Methods

Study design and inclusion population

This multicenter retrospective cohort study included and analyzed de-identified data from all hospitalized patients diagnosed with SARS-CoV-2 infection from six hospitals in Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region. The participating hospitals were: The First Affiliated Hospital of Guilin Medical University, The Sixth People’s Hospital of Nanning, Liuzhou People’s Hospital, The First People’s Hospital of Hechi, Wuzhou Red Cross Hospital, and Wuxi Huishan District People’s Hospital. Ethical approval for this study was granted by six participating hospitals, and the study follows the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki as well as the guidelines of the relevant institutions and the national government. This investigation also complies with the STROBE reporting guidelines. As this is a retrospective cohort analysis utilizing anonymized data, informed consent was waived. The study period was from December 5, 2022, to January 31, 2023. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Hospitalized between December 5, 2022, and January 31, 2023, with a diagnosis of SARS-CoV-2 infection confirmed by reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR). (2) Diagnosed with type 2 diabetes mellitus before medication use (Diagnostic criteria: presence of symptoms including dry mouth, polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, and weight loss, AND fasting plasma glucose ≥ 7.0 mmol/L OR 2-hour postprandial plasma glucose ≥ 11.1 mmol/L OR glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) > 6.5%). (3) Received antiviral treatment with Azvudine or Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir. (Azvudine: 5 mg once daily, swallowed whole on an empty stomach, for a course not exceeding 14 days. Nirmatrelvir 300 mg co-administered with Ritonavir 100 mg every 12 h for 5 consecutive days). (4) Stable diabetic condition at admission, defined as the absence of acute complications (e.g., ketoacidosis, hyperosmolar state) in the preceding month and meeting one of the following glycemic criteria: fasting plasma glucose 4.4–7.0 mmol/L OR non-fasting plasma glucose < 10.0 mmol/L OR HbA1c < 7.0%.. During the treatment period, the glucose-lowering regimen for all patients was switched to a basal-bolus insulin therapy, The severity grading of diabetes was assessed with reference to the Chinese Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Type 2 Diabetes. Diabetes management during hospitalization: For glycemic control during COVID-19 treatment, the institutional protocol recommended a switch to a basal-bolus insulin regimen for all enrolled patients, with a target fasting glucose range of 4.4–7.0 mmol/L and postprandial glucose < 10.0 mmol/L. Antiviral treatment allocation: The choice between Azvudine and Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir was at the discretion of the treating physician, influenced by a combination of factors including drug availability, patient’s renal/hepatic function, potential drug-drug interactions (particularly with ritonavir’s CYP3A4 inhibition profile), cost considerations, and the clinician’s assessment of the individual patient’s condition.

The exclusion criteria include: (1) individuals under 18 years of age, (2) incomplete medical records or those lost to follow-up, (3) patients who did not undergo antiviral treatment with Azvudine or Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir, (4) Had used antiviral medications for COVID-19 within 14 days before the first dose of the study medication. Based on antiviral treatment received, eligible patients were stratified into Azvudine or Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir cohorts.

Baseline variable

Standardized extraction of medical records from six hospital systems captured enrolled patients’ demographic and clinical data, including age, sex, height, weight, and related variables. Note the time when symptoms began and the date of hospital admission, along with records of prescriptions and drug dispensing, baseline medical history, and medication usage. concomitant treatment for the present infection, including corticosteroid therapy, anticoagulant therapy, and intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) therapy. and traditional Chinese patent medicines etc.) patients’ underlying comorbidities (such as diabetes mellitus, chronic liver disease, chronic kidney disease, cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease, malignancies, and immunodeficiency—HIV infection or patients receiving long-term corticosteroid/immunosuppressant therapy, as well as heavy smokers.) clinical laboratory indicators at admission (white blood cell count (×109/L), neutrophil count (×109/L), lymphocyte count (×109/L)).

Follow-up and outcome

During the observation period, patients with diabetes who were given either Azvudine or Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir were considered to have received antiviral treatment. Concomitant therapies (e.g., antibiotics or corticosteroids) were permitted. The observation window spanned from the initiation of antiviral treatment (hospital admission date) until the earliest occurrence of either recorded death or January 31, 2023. Patients who died before initiating antiviral therapy were not included in the analysis. To address the potential immortal time bias between symptom onset and treatment initiation, we performed a sensitivity analysis using a landmark approach at 3 days post-symptom onset.

The primary outcome constituted a composite of disease progression events, comprising all-cause mortality, invasive mechanical ventilation necessity, and intensive care unit (ICU) admission. All-cause mortality alone constituted the secondary outcome. Furthermore, we recorded new multi-system complications that arose during the COVID-19 infection hospitalization, such as myocardial injury and heart failure (HF). The diagnosis of all complications was based on imaging or lab test results documented in the medical record system, including respiratory failure and the start of Intermittent mandatory ventilation (IMV). Heart failure or myocardial injury was characterized by the onset of new heart failure symptoms along with irregular B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) levels. Arterial blood gas analysis results were the main basis for diagnosing respiratory failure. Surgical records were checked to determine if IMV was started and to note the date.

Statistical analysis

We assessed the efficacy of the antiviral medications Azvudine and Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir in patients diagnosed with both diabetes and COVID-19 using observational data. The treating physician divided all patients receiving antiviral treatment into two groups based on the specific antiviral drug given. We implemented PSM with baseline covariates collected at admission, including age, gender, comorbidities, and clinical laboratory indicators. The reduction in sample size after matching is an inherent characteristic of the Propensity Score Matching (PSM) method, as PSM only retains individuals for whom a suitable match could be found. Unmatched individuals were typically excluded due to a lack of sufficiently similar counterparts with comparable baseline characteristics in the other group. To ensure matching quality, we set a caliper value of 0.2 times the standard deviation of the propensity score. This led to the exclusion of some individuals with extreme propensity scores, thereby enhancing internal validity at the cost of reducing the sample size. In both the unmatched and propensity score matched analysis cohorts, we evaluated baseline covariate characteristics, employing independent samples t-tests for continuous variables and chi-square tests for categorical variables. Our team compared concomitant therapies received throughout the infectious period across both groups. All statistical tests were two-tailed, adopting p-values below 0.05 as statistically significant. We defined January 31, 2023, as the follow-up endpoint. We use Poisson regression to compare the incidence probabilities between the two groups for invasive mechanical ventilation, respiratory failure, myocardial injury/heart failure, acute kidney injury, and acute exacerbations of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. We also used the Kaplan-Meier method and log-rank test to compare survival curves between the two groups. Cox proportional hazards models were used to analyze the association between antiviral intervention and outcomes. Two sequential models were constructed: Model 1 (unadjusted) and Model 2, which adjusted for potential confounders including age, sex, medical history, and time from symptom onset to hospitalization. As multiple outcome measures were compared, implemented Bonferroni correction to address increased type 1 error probability. The study contained two major bias types. The first type involved COVID-19 patients admitted primarily for other severe illnesses, making it difficult to determine the link between hospitalization and COVID-19 infection. The second type involved patients who chose to be hospitalized at local hospitals after outpatient or emergency department visits but lacked records in the electronic medical record system. Patients falling under the first type of bias were excluded from the study; however, bias factors related to the second type of patients were uncontrollable. A statistical power analysis was conducted to evaluate the capability for detecting clinical outcome differences between patients administered Azvudine versus Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir. This analysis indicated 80% statistical power to identify a 24% difference in clinical outcomes.

Sensitivity analyses for immortal time bias

To address potential immortal time bias, we conducted two sensitivity analyses:

Landmark Analysis: We excluded patients who died within the first 3 days of symptom onset and re-analyzed survival from day 3 onward.

Time-Dependent Covariate Analysis: We treated the period from symptom onset to treatment initiation as a time-dependent covariate in an extended Cox model.

Per-Protocol Analysis: We restricted the analysis to patients who initiated antiviral therapy within 5 days of symptom onset, as early treatment is expected to be most effective.

Result

From December 5, 2022, to January 31, 2023, participating hospitals admitted 13,763 SARS-CoV-2-infected patients. Among them, 206 pregnant women, 10,759 not receiving antiviral therapy, 819 patients received other antiviral treatment and 1579 lost to follow-up were omitted. After excluding cases not meeting the study requirements, a total of 400 SARS-CoV-2-infected patients with diabetes were enrolled in this study. Inclusion criteria mandated patients over 18 years old possessing completely documented clinical data and medication records. The final cohort consisted of 112 patients receiving Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir and 288 receiving Azvudine (Fig. 1). We conducted a 1:2 PSM between patients receiving Azvudine and those receiving Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir. After PSM, 185 participants entered the final analysis. We compared baseline characteristics between the groups, including age, sex, length of hospitalization, duration of antiviral medication use, laboratory indicators (brain natriuretic peptide levels, lymphocyte counts, neutrophil counts, platelet counts, prothrombin time measurements, D-dimer concentrations, troponin levels, and C-reactive protein values), and medical history (Table 2). The forest plot illustrates the risk magnitude of the composite endpoint across all strata. (Fig. 4) Within the sex subgroup, females exhibited a significantly lower risk magnitude compared to males. Participants without chronic liver or kidney diseases demonstrated reduced risk levels. Notably, although the Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir group had a six-fold higher baseline prevalence of respiratory diseases (76.0% vs. 12.0%), this subgroup showed a hazard ratio of 0.37 (p = 0.122). This suggests the antiviral regimen may potentially offset the additional risk attributable to respiratory comorbidities. Alternatively, this observation could represent a false-positive finding resulting from insufficient sample size.

Discussion

Since the global emergence of COVID-19, individuals with diabetes mellitus have been consistently identified as a high-risk population for severe disease progression and mortality7. The coexistence of diabetes and COVID-19 creates a synergistic pathophysiological state, characterized by hyperglycemia-induced endothelial dysfunction and a heightened inflammatory response mediated, in part, through the advanced glycation end product (AGE) -receptor for AGE (RAGE) axis8,9. This underscores the critical need for effective antiviral interventions in this vulnerable group. In China, Azvudine (AZV) and Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir (NMV-r) are primary oral antiviral options endorsed for treating COVID-194–6. However, direct comparative evidence on their effectiveness specifically in patients with diabetes remains scarce, creating a significant knowledge gap for clinical decision-making10,11. This real-world, retrospective study aimed to address this gap by comparing the effectiveness of AZV and NMV-r in hospitalized COVID-19 patients with diabetes.

Our central finding, after applying propensity score matching (PSM) to adjust for measurable confounders, was the absence of a statistically significant difference in the primary composite endpoint of disease progression (all-cause mortality, ICU admission, or invasive mechanical ventilation) between the two treatment groups. This result aligns with the conclusion of a prior real-world comparative study12.

Interpreting the mortality trend and key secondary outcomes

A notable observation was a numerical trend favoring NMV-r for all-cause mortality reduction (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR] = 0.502). However, the 95% confidence interval (0.133 to 1.180) was wide and included the null value of 1.00, indicating that this finding did not reach statistical significance. This is likely a direct consequence of the limited statistical power in our matched cohort, which contained only 17 death events, as pre-specified in our power analysis.

In contrast, the incidence of myocardial injury was significantly lower in the NMV-r group compared to the AZV group (incidence rate ratio = 0.16). This is a robust finding that may reflect a distinct cardioprotective mechanism of NMV-r in the context of COVID-19-induced endothelial and inflammatory injury, which is particularly relevant in patients with diabetes8,9. The point estimate for a reduction in mechanical ventilation risk (IRR = 0.48) also favored NMV-r but was not statistically significant.

Addressing residual confounding and paradoxical findings

A critical challenge in interpreting our results stems from persistent, significant imbalances in baseline characteristics after PSM. Most strikingly, the prevalence of pre-existing respiratory diseases was over six times higher in the NMV-r group (76.0%) than in the AZV group (11.8%). This suggests strong confounding by indication, whereby clinicians likely prescribed NMV-r to patients perceived as having more severe respiratory compromise or a poorer prognosis—factors not fully captured in our data.

This residual confounding provides the most plausible explanation for the paradoxical observation that the NMV-r group appeared to have a higher risk for the composite endpoint in some models. This signal likely reflects their greater baseline illness burden rather than a true detriment of the drug. Conversely, the non-significant trend toward lower mortality in the NMV-r group, despite this group being sicker at baseline, could be interpreted as a potential signal of clinical benefit that merits further investigation in studies with better-balanced groups or larger sample sizes.

Contextualizing findings with existing literature and subgroup analysis

Our finding that the potential mortality benefit of NMV-r was most pronounced (though still borderline significant) in patients with severe diabetes (HR = 0.37, 95% CI: 0.11–1.00) is hypothesis-generating. It resonates with meta-analytic evidence supporting NMV-r’s role in reducing mortality10, yet underscores that its absolute benefit in complex, high-risk subgroups requires further quantification. The observation that concomitant anticoagulant use was associated with diminished effectiveness warrants caution and highlights the importance of reviewing drug-drug interactions in this polypharmacy population, a known consideration with NMV-r13.

Our results differ from a study by Dian et al.11, which found AZV associated with lower composite risk in patients under 65. The mean age in our cohort exceeded 65 years, suggesting that patient age and associated comorbidity profiles may be critical effect modifiers in determining comparative antiviral effectiveness.

Strengths, limitations, and conclusion

The strengths of our analysis include its multicenter design, use of PSM to address observed confounding, and specific focus on the diabetic population. We also implemented sensitivity analyses to mitigate immortal time bias. Our limitations are substantial and must govern the interpretation of the results:

Residual Confounding

The marked baseline imbalances post-PSM mean our comparison remains susceptible to unmeasured confounding, precluding definitive causal conclusions.

Limited Power

The small sample size and low event count after matching resulted in wide confidence intervals and a high risk of Type II error (failing to detect a true difference).

Retrospective Design

Lack of data on precise treatment timing, glycemic control during illness, and reasons for drug selection limits granular analysis.

In conclusion, within the constraints of this real-world, retrospective analysis and after statistical adjustment, we found no significant difference in composite disease progression between NMV-r and AZV in diabetic patients hospitalized with COVID-19. A trend toward lower mortality with NMV-r was observed but was not statistically significant. The significant reduction in myocardial injury with NMV-r is a promising finding that warrants further research. Crucially, the study underscores how strong clinical selection bias in real-world settings can complicate direct drug comparisons. Therefore, our results should not be interpreted as demonstrating superiority of either agent. They highlight the imperative for prospective, randomized controlled trials or larger observational studies with more comprehensive phenotyping to guide optimal antiviral selection for this vulnerable population.

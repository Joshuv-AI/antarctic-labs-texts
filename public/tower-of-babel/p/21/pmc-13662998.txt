Current status of burden of oral diseases among Chinese adolescents: evidence from the National Oral Health surveys
Authors: Yuming Xi, Shanshan Zhang, Haozhe Li, Xinzhu Zhou, Langxin Pan, Linyi Zhang, Shuguo Zheng, Xuenan Liu, Xiangyu Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe prevalence of dental caries and periodontal diseases among Chinese adolescents kept changing in accordance with the development of the economy and urbanization. The burden of these two diseases, including DALYs, direct costs and indirect costs, could quantitatively assess health and economic loss in this population. However, the regional-level burden and economic costs were not yet clear.MethodsThis study utilized GBD analytical protocols to quantify disability-adjusted life years (DALYs), direct costs and indirect costs, and socioeconomic disparities by sex and place of residence (urban / rural) in the burden of untreated dental caries of permanent teeth and periodontal diseases among Chinese adolescents aged 12–15 years. The study employed national survey data from the 3rd (2005) and 4th (2015) National Oral Health Surveys of China.ResultsIn the decade between 2005 and 2015, the number of DALYs due to untreated permanent caries decreased from 5344 to 4894. Concurrently, the direct costs increased from 1.53 billion to 1.61 billion Chinese Yuan (CNY). The indirect economic burdens increased threefold from CNY 80 million to 240 million. Conversely, periodontal diseases exhibited a 28.4% reduction in DALYs (81,622 to 58,427) and 29.1% reduction in direct costs (CNY 2.64 billion to 1.88 billion), whilst indirect costs escalated by 151% to CNY 2.86 billion. Despite a 12.7% decrease in the Chinese adolescent population, the burden of oral diseases per capita exhibited sustained growth across most indicators.ConclusionThis study quantified the major oral disease burden among young adolescents in China, revealing that dental caries showed an increasing economic burden while periodontal diseases followed the demographic trend. The burden of oral diseases per capita exhibited sustained growth across most indicators, suggesting that targeted intervention strategies should be carefully considered in future policy-making processes for adolescents’ oral health nationally.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-026-09267-6.Keywords: Burden of disease, Disability adjusted life years (DALYs), Adolescents, Dental caries, Periodontal diseases

Introduction

Oral diseases affect not only on oral health, but also on general health, well-being and social interaction. It is estimated that 3.5 billion people worldwide are affected [1, 2]. Oral diseases also bring economic burden to families and society, and the burden keeps increasing [3]. Caries and periodontal diseases represent the two most prevalent oral problems on a global scale [2]. The adolescent period is recognized as a pivotal stage for dentition development, characterized by transition from mixed dentition to complete permanent dentition. At this stage, the presence of dental issues has potential to result in impaired oral functions, including chewing, speaking, aesthetics and social interaction [1, 4]. Adolescents’ oral health conditions and experiences have significant influences on their future oral health and general health. This is because it is a crucial period for the establishment of health-related attitudes and practices [4]. The WHO therefore regards 12- to 15-year-olds as a reference group for oral health surveys among the population, which is an expansion of the 12-year-old group used previously [5].

The burden of disease is a comprehensive definition that encompasses various aspects of how a disease affects health outcomes within a country and specific regions [6]. Since 1990, the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) study has been gradually establishing a comprehensive set of methodologies [7]. The GBD methodology provided a robust framework for evaluating progress towards the World Health Organization (WHO) oral health targets, enabling systematic evaluation of evidence-based interventions while identifying priority areas requiring urgent policy attention [2]. The evidence provided was comprehensive, which informed government decision-making processes pertaining to the adolescent population [8].

In recent decades, there has been considerable change in the consciousness and lifestyle of adolescent population in China, coinciding with the country’s economic development and spread of urbanization. The 3rd and 4th National Oral Health Surveys of China were conducted in 2005 and 2015, which provide a chance to evaluate the burden based on the nationally representative data [9]. These were the largest, most geographically comprehensive and internally consistent data set available for national oral health status in China. However, it was not directly comparable to GBD data sources or results. The GBD study method offers the opportunity to identify achievements or targets toward oral health goals. This can assist in the identification of effect oral health strategies to reduce the burden of oral conditions. Furthermore, it also indicates the greatest needs for action and the health conditions causing the highest burden [10].

In the present study, we aimed to estimate of the disability adjusted life years (DALYs), the direct and indirect economic burdens of caries and periodontal diseases of 12-15-year-olds. This has been based on the data from the 3rd and 4th National Oral Health Surveys in China using GBD method, as well as to identify distinctions owing to socioeconomic factors, to quantify the major oral disease burden among young adolescents in China. This will inform future national policy-making processes for adolescents’ oral health with targeted intervention strategies.

Methods

Data sources

The present study utilised oral health status data from the 3rd and 4th National Oral Health Surveys of China. The surveys were conducted in 2005 and 2015, respectively. Both surveys employed a multistage, stratified, equal proportion, random sampling methodology [9]. Both the 3rd and 4th National Oral Health Surveys of China had obtained official authorization and ethical approval from the National Health Committee of the People’s Republic of China and the Chinese Stomatological Association in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants consented to attend the surveys. With regard to the present study, ethical clearance was not required as the study involved secondary analysis of anonymized data from existing national surveys and data modelling.

As the representative for adolescents, the 12-year-old age group was included in both surveys (23,508 cases in 2005 and 27,821 cases in 2015), while the 13-, 14- and 15-year-old age groups were only included in the latter one (30,961 13-year-olds, 30,691 14-year-olds and 29,128 15-year-olds in 2015). Both surveys covered all 31 provinces, municipalities and autonomous regions in the Mainland China, except for Xizang Autonomous Region in 2005. As an important part of the surveys, oral clinical examinations were conducted to record caries experience (measured by the index of decayed, missing, and filled teeth, DMFT), status of bleeding on probing (BOP), and the presence of dental calculus in permanent teeth among the study participants. The examiniation was conducted using a disposable dental mirror, an intraoral light-emitting diode light and a ball-ended WHO Community Periodontal Index (CPI) probe. The presence of dental caries was indicated by the presence of a unmistakable cavity, undermined enamel, or detectably softened floor or wall on the coronal part of a tooth. The presence of gingival bleeding and calculus was evaluated in the 12- to15-year-olds using a dichotomous index. This index assigned a score of 0 or 1 for the absence or presence of conditions for each tooth [9]. Probing depth was examined only in 15-year-old age group. The data of sociodemographic indices (e.g. population size, etc.) were extracted from the National Bureau of Statistics of China (http://www.stats.gov.cn/tjsj/ndsj/).

The data on the prices of oral medical service were obtained from the Healthcare Security Administration of each province-level administrative area (province, municipality or autonomous region). The data on gross domestic product (GDP) was source from the National Bureau of Statistics of China and the World Bank. All costs were converted or applied in the price year 2015, considering inflation or deflation if the value was before or after 2015. The monetary values had been converted to constant-price terms prior to any inter-year comparison of economic data.

The data source was subjected to a comprehensive extraction and analysis process, encompassing information on untreated permanent caries, bleeding on probing, calculus, and probing depth for the 12-year-old age group in 2005 and the 12-to-15-year-old age group in 2015. This analysis was complemented by the incorporation of demographic and economic indicators for the corresponding years, along with a set of medical service prices. For the indicators, data were precisely aggregated to the provincial level.

Analysis of DALYs

In accordance with the GBD Study protocol, assuming that untreated caries or periodontitis did not result in years of life lost among adolescents, DALYs can be represented by YLDs, which are defined as the product of frequency (prevalence), severity (disability weight) and duration of the condition [11].In this study, the prevalence of dental caries was defined as the number of the subjects with one or more untreated caries in permanent teeth. The prevalence of periodontal diseases was defined as the number of the subjects with a BOP-positive site rate of ≥ 10% or sites with periodontal probing depth (PD) > 3 mm. The GBD reports described a delicate model for quantifying severity and duration. For untreated caries and periodontal diseases, the reported disability weights ranged from 0.011 (0.005–0.019) to 0.018 (0.011–0.087) [12–14]. In this study, to ensure a fine consistency with the GBD reports, the same disability weights (established in 2010) and the duration time estimates were employed in the present study as shown below:

DT, untreated caries in permanent teeth; CP, teeth with periodontitis; PDT, prevalence of population with DT > 0; PCP, prevalence of population with BOP > 0; W, disability weight (consistent with the GBD study); D, duration time weighed on the basis of symptom criteria (consistent with the GBD study). In more specifical terms of duration, periodontal diseases can result in persistent discomfort. The following symptom criteria have been proposed for untreated caries: asymptomatic (39.8%), mild (41.2%), or severe (18.9%), corresponding to various degrees of intermittent pain. The classification method has been derived from a global literature review conducted by the GBD study. Both disability weights and disease duration were remained constant across the years of the study [11, 15, 16].

Analysis of direct costs

In consideration of the data availability, the economic burden of disease was calculated from a societal perspective, which includes direct costs and indirect costs. The economic analysis perspective is the point of view from which the analysis will be focused, in this study, we took a societal perspective to measure the total economic burden for the society and community [17–19]. The total economic burden is comprised of direct cost (medical and non-medical if applicable) and indirect cost (productivity loss). There are two methodologies of gathering costs: the bottom-up approach (also known as micro-costing) and the top-down approach. The bottom-up costing approach involves first identifying the resources used in providing the good or service, then obtaining information on each resource identified, and summing up all resources required on a per patient basis. The top-down costing approach generally relies on information obtained from routine data sources, typically based on national averages. In many costing studies a blended approach is common. In this study, the intervention costs were measured using a micro-costing approach for both direct and indirect costs from a societal perspective [17–19].

The direct costs are based on prevalence and have been estimated using a bottom-up approach, whereby the total cost has been obtained by multiplying the frequency of service use by the unit cost [6, 20, 21]. With regard to frequency, we assumed that all untreated caries in permanent teeth should be appropriately treated with resin restoration and/or root canal therapy. Each individual who met the following criteria should take scaling treatment : BOP sites > 10%, or presence of sites with PD > 3 mm, or presence of site with dental calculus. In the 15-year-old age group, teeth with PD≥4 mm required subgingival scaling and root planning. The estimation of costs was derived from the local-government-guided prices. In contrast to DALYs, the minimum unit for direct cost analysis is the tooth, rather than the individual.

DC, direct cost; PDTi, number of teeth with untreated caries on a certain tooth position; Ni, number of average root canals on a certain tooth position; Ca, cost for treatment of an asymptomatic tooth; Cm, cost for treatment of a mild tooth; Cs, cost for treatment of a severe tooth, root canals excluded; Cc, cost for treatment of every root canal in a severe tooth; Ppts, prevalence of greater-than-zero BOP or index of dental calculus; PPP, number of the teeth with probing depth≥4 mm; CFMS, cost for full-mouth scaling; CSRP, cost for subgingival scaling and root planning of a tooth with probing depth≥4 mm.

Analysis of indirect costs

The estimation of indirect costs was conducted in accordance with an approach proposed by the WHO’s Commission on Macroeconomics and Health in 2001. This approach was based on the valuation of 1 YLD at 1 year of per capita GDP, with the objective of approximating productivity losses. The provincial GDP values referred to official statistical yearbooks published by the National Bureau of Statistics of China.

IC, indirect cost.

Comparative analyses

The summary of 12 to 15-year-old age groups were analyzed for each age band. Initially, the total burden of untreated caries in permanent teeth and periodontal diseases among 12-year-olds was calculated and presented in terms of YLDs, direct costs and indirect costs. The distribution of burden was investigated across all 31 provinces, autonomous regions and municipalities of the Mainland China. A comparison was made of the per capita direct cost by sex and by urbanicity among young adolescents in all these province-level administrative units.

The database was created and analyzed using SPSS 26 software (IBM Corp., USA) and Microsoft Excel 2021. The 95% uncertainty intervals for all estimates were calculated using Monte Carlo methods in R software. In the calculation components, the disability weights are found to follow logit-normal distribution [12], while the prevalence rates are found to follow a normal distribution, according to the Central Limit Theorem for Large Samples. In a single sampling iteration, we randomly draw one value for each component according to their respective distributions, after which the results are derived. The 95% UI is presented as a function of the 25th and 975th ordered values of 1000 bootstrap draws.

Results

Burden of untreated dental caries and periodontal diseases among 12-year-olds

In the decade between 2005 and 2015, the People’s Republic of China experienced significant transformation in its population structure, economic growth and the prevalence of oral diseases. Under this background, the estimated total DALYs of permanent dental caries changed from 5344 to 4894 (Fig. 1A); Concurrently, the direct economic burden increased from 1.53 billion to 1.61 billion Chinese Yuan (CNY), while the indirect economic burden grew from 80 million to 240 million CNY (Fig. 1B and C). Despite a 25% decrease in the total population of 12-year-olds, the direct economic burden of dental caries had paradoxically increased. For periodontal diseases, the total DALYs decreased from 81,622 to 58,427 (Fig. 1D). The direct economic burden decreased from 2.64 billion to 1.88 billion CNY, and the indirect economic burden rose from 1.14 billion to 2.86 billion CNY (Fig. 1E and F). The DALYs and direct economic burden of periodontal diseases exhibited a congruence with the declining trend of the population (Supplementary Table 1).

Province-level distribution of burden among 12-year-olds

As demonstrated in Fig. 2, there has been a shift in the distribution of untreated dental caries and periodontal diseases burdens among 12-year-old young adolescents in different province-level administrative units in the Mainland China. As for burden of untreated dental caries, there was an increase in the overall proportion in Hebei, Henan and Yunnan provinces. Similarly, the burden of periodontal diseases had risen in Hebei, Henan and Jiangsu provinces. In contrast, the municipality of Beijing, Zhejiang and Guangdong provinces have all demostrated a decline in burden of these two representative oral diseases (Supplementary Tables 2 & 3).

Per capita direct cost by sex and urbanicity among young adolescents

As illustrated in Fig. 3, the per capita direct cost of dental caries and periodontal diseases was stratified by province and gender. The per capita direct cost of dental caries treatment ranged from 32.5 CNY in Beijing municipality to 260.9 CNY in Xizang Autonomous Region. The per capita direct cost of periodontal treatment ranged from 54.3 CNY in Heilongjiang province to 302.7 CNY in Qinghai province. Across all province-level administrative areas, girls exhibited a greater burden of dental caries compared to boys. Conversely, in most province-level administrative areas, boys carried a higher burden of periodontal diseases than girls (Supplementary Tables 4 & 5). Notable gender disparities in dental caries are observed in Yunnan province and Xinjiang Autonomous Region, where females experience a disproportionately higher burden. In contrast, the gender disparity in periodontal diseases was relatively less pronounced. It was observed that economically developed regions, including Beijing and Shanghai municipalities, as well as Jiangsu and Zhejiang provinces, demonstrated lower per capita direct costs for both conditions.

Discussion

This study provides a comprehensive overview of the oral health burden among Chinese young adolescents, highlighting a significant disease burden and economic impact. The 3rd and 4th National Oral Health Surveys of China produced a highly comprehensive and systematic dataset, featuring a substantial sample size and a stratified sampling method. This study applied GBD method, utilizing the most comprehensive epidemiology data, to examine two major oral diseases affecting 12 years old population. The finding revealed a decline in total DALYs for permanent dental caries from 5,344 to 4,894 and a reduction in the burden of permanent teeth caries from 33.8 to 25.5 per 100,000 individuals in the year 2005 and 2015, respectively. The burden of periodontal diseases in the specified population were 81,622 to 58,427 in total DALYs. This report represents the first documentation of the burden of periodontal disease in this age group, which was found to be almost 16 times higher than the burden of dental caries. These findings underscore the significant neglected health burden and emphasize the necessity for targeted interventions. By collating economic burden data at both province-level and nation-level for the first time, the present research offers a novel, visual representation of the nationwide economic burden of oral diseases. The total economic burden of the two major oral diseases in this population was 6.19 billion in 2005 and rose to 6.59 billion in 2015. Total costs of managing periodontal diseases were found 1.5–2.5 time higher compared to caries management, due to increased indirect costs for periodontal diseases. Trend analysis revealed a mixed picture for Chinese youth, with dental caries showing an increasing economic burden were found despite a declining adolescent population. This study used national medical price items released by the Medical Insurance Bureaus, as a valuable data source of price. The methodological framework of the GBD study was incorporated into our research, thereby enhancing the precision down to the provincial level and providing detailed burden data. Given the declining trend of young population, the increase of oral disease burden may be attributable to the following factors. First, the increased prevalence of dental caries over the past decade may be related to increased sugar consumption. The relationship between sugar intake and dental caries had been confirmed [22] and the total sugar intake of children and adolescents aged 3–17 in China were found increased from 11.2 ± 0.3 g/d in 1997 to 15.9 ± 0.4 g/d in 2004, with further doubled to 28.1 ± 0.5 g/d in 2011 [23]. The burden of dental caries might continue to increase if sugar consumption trend continues. Second, the reasons for regional differences might include not only income levels and sugar consumption, but also the discrepancy in access to dental care. A study conducted in the southeastern region of the United States found that children in rural areas face more limited access to dental services [24]. Another important factor was the lack of dental specialists. In rural China, the gap between dental caries treatment needs and access had been even more severe. Adolescent periodontal diseases had not received sufficient attention in both urban and rural areas. Third, high cost of pocket expense for oral disease management could further limit the service use, the expansion process of medical insurance coverage had only begun in recent years, this resulted in increased used of medical service and a relative decrease in the proportion of disease burden. As reported in Japan, following the implementation of the new dental insurance in 2016, the number of dental caries filled in Japanese adults increased, and the average number of dental caries in the population aged 5 and above decreased to 0.8 [25]. Following the implementation of dental insurance in South Korea, the utilization rate of dental medical services had increased. In 2017, 14 million South Koreans received treatment for periodontal diseases, resulting in a reduction in the economic burden of oral diseases [26]. After one year of implementation of the expanded Medicaid adult dental insurance program, the untreated caries rate of children showed a decrease by 5% [27].

Research on periodontal disease in adolescents is less extensive than that on dental caries, and no universally accepted case definition yet exists. Nevertheless, it is possible to interpret population-based surveys through the use of proxy indicators, such as the proportion of subjects with bleeding on probing (BOP) or calculus. Current study showed that periodontal disease burden were more than 10 times compared to caries in 2015 in Chinese adolescents. This finding underscores the imperative for enhanced awareness initiatives targeting adolescents regarding periodontal health. In China, approximately 50% of adolescents exhibit BOP, whereas the prevalence exceeds 90% in several Southeast-Asian and African countries and stands at 20–30% in Italy and Greece [28–31]. For early-stage periodontal conditions, these rates are often higher than the corresponding prevalence of dental caries. These findings emphasise the pressing need to raise adolescents’ awareness of periodontal health and to advocate for the expansion of policies encompassing periodontal health prevention, in addition to the provision of dedicated fiscal support for addressing periodontal disease in this age group.

Early prevention and treatment of young permanent teeth in adolescents, such as oral health education, promoted the formation of good oral hygiene habits and might reduce the expensive implantation and restoration costs in the middle-aged and elderly stages. Effective preventive managements mainly delivered at primary healthcare facilities. To bridge divides in access to dental care, it should be important to enhance the capacity of the primary health-care system on oral healthcare services and improve the grading system for oral diagnosis and treatment. Acknowledging the increasing pressure exerted by an ageing population and rapid urbanization, primary health-care system enhancement could tackle the emerging dual burden of chronic non-communicable diseases and effectively decrease health expenditures [32]. On this basis, it was recommended to establish community oral health management centers, equipped with general dentists or dental public health personnel, to provide oral health preventive management for all ages. It was equally important to strengthen the training of general specialists and provide sustainable human resources for the primary health-care facilities. In 2024, the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security of the People’s Republic of China specified the scope of work for oral hygienists, laying the foundation for further expansion of oral manpower. The study revealed a very low periodontal health service utilization rate among adolescents, which indicates a need for the promotion of periodontal treatment and oral health education in communities or schools.

Certain limitations should be kept in mind when extrapolating the findings of the present study. Although we used the large sample size and high quality-control data of the two latest rounds of National Oral Health Surveys to update the most rigorous disease burden models, we still incorporated several assumptions in the economic burden models. For example, the cost data was derived from official data released in 2020 to 2022 with adaptation. This might result in biases in the evaluation of previous prices. Additionally, given included data were from two cross-sectional time periods, we sacrificed the convenience of prediction and trend discrimination to obtain the most refined regional level results. In the year 2021, the National Health Committee of the People’s Republic of China initiated a modified plan to conduct the National Oral Health Surveillance Surveys at five-year intervals. However, so far, the findings from this nationwide surveillance program had not been officially released, nor had the database been made publicly accessible for secondary analysis. Once these updated datasets become available, future research could enable comprehensive analysis of epidemiological trends and predictive modeling for the burden of oral diseases.

The national and provincial cost data on dental treatment in china was previously reported in 2015, but it cannot be used as cost value for different treatment for caries and periodontal diseases. Our research team explored the cost of each type of treatment based on the national data (submitted to other journal and under review), therefore we applied a bottom-up approach in the cost analysis with the guided per case management costs rather than using the top down average cost due to lack of the total cost data for each treatment. Notably, our study examined the potential costs based on the distribution of underlying symptoms in untreated populations, which differs from the other studies that focused on actual incurred costs.‌ Given the low proportion of dental fillings (16.5%, 2015, from 4th National Oral Health Surveys of China database), the potential costs should significantly exceed the actual costs incurred.

Conclusion

This present study quantified the major oral disease burden (i.e. untreated dental caries of permanent teeth and periodontal diseases) among young adolescents in China, and to identified the potential policy impact of this problem. The analyses revealed a mixed picture for oral health in Chinese youth, with dental caries showed an increasing economic burden despite a declining adolescent population while periodontal diseases followed the demographic trend. Notably, the burden of oral diseases per capita exhibited sustained growth across most indicators, suggesting that targeted intervention strategies should be carefully considered in future policy-making processes for adolescents’ oral health nationally.

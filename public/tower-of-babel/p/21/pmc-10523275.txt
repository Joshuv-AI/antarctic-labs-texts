Enhancing medical ultrasound imaging through fractional mathematical modeling of ultrasound bubble dynamics
Authors: Hijab ur Rehman, Muhammad Shuaib, Emad AA Ismail, Shuo Li
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe classical mathematical modeling of ultrasound acoustic bubble is so far using to improve the medical imaging quality. A clear and visible medical ultrasound image relies on bubble’s diameter, wavelength and intensity of the scattered sound. A bubble with diameter much smaller than the sound wavelength is regarded as highly efficient source of sound scattering. The dynamical equation for a medical ultrasound bubble is primarily modeled in classical integer-order differential equation. Then a reduction of order technique is used to convert the modeled dynamic equation for the bubble surface into a system of incommensurate fractional-orders. The incommensurate fractional-order values are calculated directly, by using Riemann stability region. On the basis of stability the convergence and accuracy of the numerical scheme is also discussed in detail. It has been found that the system will remain stable and chaotic for the incommensurate values α1<0.737 and α2<2.80, respectively.Keywords: Acoustic, Stability analysis, Chaos, Incommensurate fractional-orders, Synchronization, Constant ultrasonic speed

Introduction

The medical ultrasound imaging quality is imperative for so many reasons, but the most important is the diagnosing of any disease accurately and timely. Better ultrasound imaging quality also secure both the patient and the operative engineering system. A convenient arrangement of ultrasound apparatus, to get a clear image, can help in detecting a fault or any disease at an early stage. A variety of ultrasound imaging systems are designed to achieve this basic objective of improving the imaging quality, such as B-mode and Doppler. Ultrasound imaging has been a common practice in the field of medical diagnosis since 1970 [1]. Ultrasound imaging is widely used today due to its low cost, non-invasiveness, easy to use, and having a real-time properties [2]. On the other hand, computed tomography (CT scan) is risky because of harmful radiation exposure in the process, and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is non-invasive but costly and time-consuming [3]. The ultrasound imaging contrast agent is required to distinguish echoes sound from inhomogeneity in the tissues. These agents were first proposed for medical application in 1968 by Gramiak [4] and in 1991 the health care authorities approved these agents for the first time. There are many contrast agents, which are introduced, but very few are available in market and some agents are under clinical trials. These agents must be particle based not water-soluble molecules, to maintain inhomogeneity. Contrast agents used in ultrasound must be very powerful sound scatterer and that can be obtained by using highly compressible particles [5]. Therefore, microbubbles, gas filled bubbles, are the main candidate for ultrasound contrast imaging agent. The image encryption through fractional operator of a non-local kernel is discussed by Khan et al. [6] and observed that the fractional encryption is very effective to improve the image quality.

In 1917 Rayleigh [7] studied cavitation and collapse phenomena showing bubbles which are more powerful ultrasound scatters in water. The author Minnaert [8], performed the modeling corresponding the bubbles which were viewed as a harmonic oscillator and explained the natural frequency of oscillating bubbles. Numerous studies have been done both experimental and theoretical that describing stability effects of bubbles towards gas diffusion [9]. These effects include damping mechanisms of the vibrating bubbles [10] and the effect of encapsulation of the gas [11]. Bubble oscillating at small amplitude, under linear condition, were modeled by [12]. The nonlinear oscillation creates higher harmonics and this effect is used to detect bubbles in a flowing liquid, and is also used for medical contrast agents. The bubble acoustics which is nonlinear that summarized by [13]. A detailed description of oscillating bubbles is present in the book by Leighton [14]. The detection of contrast agent on the basis of its nonlinear properties is described in a patent by Averkiou [15]. Numerical study on bubble oscillation amplitude were modeled by [16].

Recently the chemical kinetics have also gain a very challenging momentum in the field of fluid dynamics. The study of chemical kinetics gives a brief discussion of the transition and mechanism of chemical reactions [17], [18], [19]. The higher dimensional complex chemical reactions are reduced to the lower dimensional reaction by applying certain numerical techniques [20], [21], [22]. Up to some extent ultrasound technology can also be used for chemical reaction processes. Ultrasound acoustic waves are responsible for the rapid collapse and formation of bubbles floating inside a fluid. The rapid collapse and bubble formation accelerates the breakage process of hydrogen bonds, which interacts with ploypeptide chain [23]. The ultrasonic temperature is also used for X-ray diffraction and spectra infrared, which indicates that quercetin remain bounded to the surface through hydrogen bonding [24]. Likewise, the antioxidant activity of polysaccharides can be increased by using ultrasonic isolation process [25].

The idea of fractional calculus is using since 1695, when Leibniz and L’Hospital mentioned the half-order derivatives. This idea of fractional calculus is derived from classical integer-order calculus. In the field of fractional calculus, all physical descriptions can be modeled more accurately than the classical integer-order calculus. Nakagara and Sorimachi [26] concluded that all real physical descriptions are actually in fractional-orders, but in some cases the rationality is very low. A very common examples of fractional-order calculus are heat diffusion in a semi-infinity solid, where the heat flow is reported by Podlubny [27] as 0.5 order derivatives of the temperature. The numbers of application of fractional-order calculus is increasing day by day. Most of the researchers are taking keen interest in fractional-order calculus, due to its wide rage of applications in numerous fields of mathematics, such as Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD), Magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) and mathematical biology etc. Zakia et al. [28] discussed the circulant solution of Halvorsen system with fractional order derivatives. They synchronized the classical and fractional-order systems and obtain a very good agreement between these two fields of calculus.

The chaotic analysis is a very new area of research, used in different fields of science, mathematics, engineering, medicines and even in natural phenomenons, such as weather, climates and noise controlling techniques. Chaotic behaviors simulation are very sensitive towards the initial conditions and the orders of incommensurate fractions. Chaos control analysis is carried out by Gholami et al. [29]. They established and proved the existence and uniqueness of the linear feedback for the system. The applications of chaotic fractional-order system are discussed by Sene [30]. He examine the presence/absence of chaos in the modeled dynamical system by using Lyapunove exponents. The explosive synchronization in oscillator population is studied by Wang et al. [31]. They found that the control chaotic strategies can be provided by higher-order interaction in dynamical units. The input and out put signals are synchronized by Zhao et al. [32], for vibrational resonance phenomenon. They found that the numerical and theoretical results are in their good agreements to each others.

Chaotic study has a significant impact on the newly modeled or on the existent dynamical systems based on engineering or biological fractional calculus. The possibilities of detecting the chaotic behavior of an existent fractional model is studied by Ahmad et al. [33]. They used fractional operator of Mittag-Leffer type kernel and obtained a unique solution of the dynamical system. The fractional mathematical modeling is equally important for some pandemic diseases such as COVID-19, Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV), malaria, chicken pox and influenza etc. Ahmad et al. [34] presented a report on COVID-19 epidemic in Pakistan using SEIR fractional model and predicted that the disease can be controlled within 200 days. A global fractional dynamic report of COVID-19 is conferred by Ahmad et al. [35]. They concluded that a proper treatment and quarantine of the susceptible individuals can, dramatically, reduce the rate of infection disease. The transmission dynamics and sensitivity analysis of pine wilt disease, with fractional differential operator, is carried out by Ahmad et al. [36]. They observed that the fractional order results are more realistic as compared to the integer order results.

The chaos control strategy is the study of the control theory and the dynamical behavior of the system. Chaos is a closed characteristic of a dynamical system, which shows super-sensitivity towards the initial conditions [37]. Even a very good initial guess gives a diverge prediction of the solutions with the passage of time, which give rise an unpleasant results and make the system as unstable. In this regard stability analysis occupies an important place in the study of fractional dynamical systems. In general, the chaotic nature develops at any time when the system trajectories are both bounded globally and unstable locally [38]. The process of stabilization of an unstable solution, by means of noise suppression, vibrations controlling of an oscillatory system or harmonics eradication in the communication devices, is the chaotic controlling strategy. Chaos or regular oscillation may cause a solution complication, which can be avoided by finding the stability region of the model. stability of a dynamical system can be examined in numerous domains, such as external stability in time, frequency domain, or stability of a fractional order system by means of equilibrium points and their corresponding eigenvalues [39]. The dynamics of chaotic system based on circuit design with ULam stability has been discussed by Khan et al. [40] and noticed a fast convergence of the dynamical system to its static equilibrium.

The present article is concerned with the modeling of medical ultrasound bubble dynamics equation, first in classical integer calculus. Then the modeled bubble dynamic equation is reduced into a system of incommensurate fractional-orders, by presenting a reduction of orders method. The incommensurate values are found, by determining equilibrium points and the corresponding eigenvalues, which kept the system stable as well as chaotic. The fractional analysis for ultrasound acoustic bubble dynamics is carried out for the first time. The finding may be useful for medical physicists, technicians and mathematicians. The rest of the paper is organized in such a way that mathematical formulation is presented in section 2. The stability analysis and convergence and accuracy are carried out in 3, 4, respectively. Section 5 presents results and discussion, while the concluded remarks are conferred in section 6.

Results and discussion

The second section of the present article presented the formulation of a dynamic medical ultrasound bubble model in its integer-order differential Eq. (8). The classical order differential Eq. (8) is synchronized into a system of two nonlinear fractional-orders differential Eqs. (9). The values of incommensurate fractional orders α1<0.73 and α2<2.80 are calculated, in section 3. It is also deduced that the system will remain stable and chaotic for different values of incommensurate orders.

This section represents the chaotic and abnormal fluctuations of nonlinear fractional-orders system (9), for different values of the involved parameters. All these simulations are carried out for the fixed value of incommensurate orders α1=0.10 and α2=2.10.

The surrounding pressure p∞ compressed the bubble surface due to its direction of application against the bubble surface radial velocity. As a result reasonable amount of gas is diffused into the surrounding liquid, which causes an abnormal fluctuation and chaotic in the surrounding liquid. These observations can be detected in Fig. 3(a – f). The increasing values of p∞ are responsible for the increasing of chaotic effects.

The reflected sound waves by a bubble surface are using for medical purposes to diagnosed some diseases. A small diameter bubble can enhanced the scattering efficiency of sound waves, for several times. These scattered sound waves are assembled to get a clear and visible portrait of the ultrasound image. This objective can be achieved only, when the incident and reflected waves are adjusted in such a way that it can produce stationary waves, which does not propagate. The standing waves will retain the bubble floating over the liquid for a long time, which will increase the ultrasound intensity. Fig. 4(a – f) depicts that the chaotic and abnormal bursts frequencies are gradually decreasing, which increases the bubbles life, with the increasing values of constant speed of ultrasound.

The chaotic and abnormal fluctuations of Radius-Velocity synchronization and velocity vs time, respectively, graphs are sketched against the increasing values of density ρ in Fig. 5(a – f). It can be seen that, for lower value of density, ρ=1.40, there is a minimum concentration of the velocity at the central position and the system remains stable, whenever the bubble’s radius is expending, Fig. 5(a). On the other hand when the values of density is gradually increases, the central concentration is maximized and a little bit fluctuation can be observed for relatively shorter time, Fig. 5(b). As reported by Hoff [44], the density is much more efficient than any other physical property to reflect the sound waves. This extra ability of ρ will give more time for bubble survival, which can be used for medical ultrasound purposes. It can be reason out for the increasing central concentration of velocity for larger values of density.

A very uneven behavior of chaotic and abnormal fluctuations can be seen for increasing values of surface pressure pL in Fig. 6(a – f). A congested and controlled Radius-Velocity synchronization can be seen in Fig. 6(a), for pL=1.30, whilst the velocity fluctuation started very early as shown in Fig. 6(b). Fig. 6(c) reveals a very clear and open synchronization of Radius-Velocity, for pL=1.40, and the abnormal burst frequency delay time upto 50% for the same value of pL. The Radius-Velocity synchronization is further shrink for the value of pL=1.50 and gives an impression of huge concentration at the center of the graph, Fig. 6(e). In this case, the velocity fluctuation is started from the very beginning of time and suddenly increased in the mid way of the time, then decreases steadily and get increased once again at the end of time interval. All these observations can be seen in Fig. 6(f). This uneven behavior of bubble surface velocity is due to the fact that ultrasound intensity is proportional to the square of surface pressure pL. An increase in the values of pressure means to increase the ultrasound intensity, which give raise in the bubble growth, and will gain some more gas from the surrounding liquid.

Conclusion

In this article, a dynamical integer-order differential equation is developed for medical ultrasound bubble under the assumptions of pressure dependent density and constant speed of sound. The integer-order bubble surface equation is reduced into a system of incommensurate fractional-orders, by the application of a mathematical method. The stability and chaotic regions are identified on the basis of fractional-order values. Throughout this research activity the following findings have been drawn:

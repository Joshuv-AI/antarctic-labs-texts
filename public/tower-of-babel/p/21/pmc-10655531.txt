Response to Letter to the Editor From Watts and Leslie: “Comparisons Between Different Antiosteoporosis Medications on Postfracture Mortality: A Population-based Study”
Authors: Chih-Hsing Wu, Chia-Chun Li
Published: 2023

To the Editor—

We would like to thank Watts and Leslie (1) for their interest in our recently published paper “Comparisons Between Different Antiosteoporosis Medications on Postfracture Mortality: A Population-based Study (2).” We are pleased to respond to their concerns in detail.

In our study, we found the nonbone benefit of antiosteoporotic medications (AOMs) in relation to lower postfracture mortality. Watts et al comment that this could be due to underestimating the bias on treatment selection and survival time. Theoretically, the Taiwan's National Health Insurance research database included more than 99.9% of Taiwan's 23 million population and has the advantage of providing the most extensive medical claims for analysis (3). All the study patients had received AOMs only if they were eligible for National Health Insurance payment guidelines. There is no limitation on prescribing any type of AOMs for all specialists working at either a primary care or medical center. To evaluate the influence of medication adherence on all-cause mortality, the duration of taking AOMs was calculated from the entire AOM exposure time after initiating AOMs in the ambulatory visits during the study period. We reviewed all prescriptions from ambulatory visits over the follow-up period to identify a single type of AOM and excluded patients who switched between any type of AOMs. To lessen the immortal time bias (4), the follow-up interval was calculated on the day of drug initiation rather than the day of diagnosis. The survival effect will be estimated more conservatively based on the methodology. The sensitivity analysis using the E-value was also performed to assess the unmeasured confounding variables and indicated the study's stability and reliability (5).

Though the real-world evidence can help settle clinical uncertainties and facilitate regulatory decisions (6, 7), we fully agree with Watts and Leslie that a further randomized controlled trial is needed to establish stronger evidence for the debate of postfracture mortality in different AOMs. In conclusion, this real-world mega-data study suggests that the usage of osteoporotic medication, especially a long-acting regimen, may be associated with lower postfracture mortality (2).

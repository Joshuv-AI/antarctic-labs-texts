A global bibliometric perspective on autophagy and tumor microenvironment in cancer research
Authors: Ishita Debnath, Moumita Kundu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAutophagy plays a key role in cell recycling and also has a double-edged sword role in cancer. It serves as a tumor suppressor in the initial phases but eventually maintains stress resistance in cancer cells. Autophagy plays a role in immune cell interactions and cancer-associated fibroblasts within the tumor microenvironment, managing metabolism, immune evasion, and angiogenesis that enable tumor development and drug resistance. Autophagy is, therefore, a major target for cancer therapy.MethodTo investigate the function of autophagy and tumor microenvironment (TME) in cancer progression, we performed a comprehensive search of Scopus and PubMed. We mapped the collaboration network between different journals, countries, institutions, journals, and keywords using VOSviewer (version 1.6.20) and extracted relevant quantitative information.ResultThere is increasing research into autophagy and the TME in cancer, with 3,002 articles showing a consistent annual increase. China and the United States have the highest outputs. “Cancer” is a leading journal in the topic, and “Frontiers in Oncology” is the most cited. Four primary research themes have been highlighted through keyword analysis. Autophagy and the TME play central roles in the diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment of cancer, and natural products and drugs targeting the TME are promising. Nevertheless, manipulating autophagy can result in greater resistance to cancer.ConclusionIn conclusion, our study utilized bibliometric tools to investigate the emerging trend of autophagy and TME in cancer. It is the first extensive bibliometric study to comprehensively map the overlap of autophagy and tumor microenvironment in cancer biology. Our results indicate leading authors, collaborative communities, knowledge gaps, and promising subfields like immunotherapy and hypoxia. The findings provide a strategic guide for future directions in research and therapeutic development in cancer biology.Keywords: Cancer, Autophagy, Tumor microenvironment, Bibliometric analysis, VOSviewer, Trends

Introduction

Cancer results from abnormal cell proliferation, and it can occur in any organ or bodily structure. It consists of microscopic cells that continue to grow uncontrollably [1]. Sometimes, cancer is detected incidentally through regular radiological examinations or laboratory tests. According to WHO data, an estimated 9.7 million people died from cancer worldwide in 2024 [2]. Although treatments have extended the survival period for many cancer patients, a significant number still experience recurrence and are unable to achieve long-term survival. Therefore, it is crucial to understand cancer comprehensively, from its onset to its metastasis and recurrence [3]. The American Cancer Society uses data from cancer registries and the National Center for Health Statistics to estimate new cancer cases and deaths in the United States. In 2022, there were 1,918,030 new cases and 609,306 fatalities, with lung cancer being the leading cause of death [4]. Despite a 4% to 6% annual increase in advanced disease since 2011, the incidence of prostate cancer remained stable from 2014 to 2018, while female breast cancer continued to rise by 0.5% annually. Consequently, the percentage of advanced prostate cancer cases diagnosed increased from 3.9% to 8.2% over the past decade [5].

Autophagy plays a complex role in cancer, acting as both a tumor suppressor and promoter depending on the context. In the early stages of cancer development, autophagy helps maintain cellular homeostasis by removing damaged organelles, misfolded proteins, and excess reactive oxygen species (ROS), thus preventing genomic instability and malignant transformation [6]. Key tumor suppressors like p53 and Beclin-1 regulate this protective function. However, in established tumors, cancer cells often exploit autophagy to survive under stressful conditions such as nutrient deprivation, hypoxia, and treatment pressure [7]. Oncogenic signals, including RAS mutations and hypoxia-inducible factors (e.g., HIF-1α), activate autophagy pathways that support tumor metabolism, growth, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance. Additionally, autophagy influences the TME by modulating immune responses and stromal cell behavior [8].

The TME plays a critical role in cancer development, progression, and response to therapy. It includes various cellular and structural components, such as immune cells, cancer-associated fibroblasts, endothelial cells, the extracellular matrix, and secreted factors like cytokines and exosomes. These elements interact with tumor cells to promote growth, metastasis, immune evasion, and therapy resistance [9]. The TME can suppress anti-tumor immunity by fostering immunosuppressive cells (e.g., regulatory T cells, M2 macrophages, and myeloid-derived suppressor cells) and upregulating immune checkpoints like PD-1/PD-L-1, which impair T cell function [10]. Hypoxia and metabolic alterations within the TME further contribute to tumor aggressiveness and therapy resistance. While immunotherapies like checkpoint inhibitors and CAR-T cell therapies have shown promise, their effectiveness is often limited by the hostile conditions within the TME [11]. Consequently, reprogramming the TME to reduce immunosuppression and enhance immune activation is a key focus in modern cancer therapy. Strategies such as combination treatments, tumor vaccines, microbiota modulation, and targeted metabolic interventions have the potential to improve treatment outcomes [12].

Given the large volume of expanding research and the rapid advances in autophagy and the TME, there is a strong need to invest considerable time and effort in the curation and evaluation of publications to obtain the necessary insights in the field of cancer. In the realm of systematic evaluation studies, bibliometrics has emerged as a respected methodology that clarifies the contributions of various countries and institutions, identifies leading researchers and potential collaborators, and charts the evolution of research paradigms. However, bibliometric analyses related to autophagy, the TME, and cancer are notably lacking. To address this gap in the literature, we provide a well-written exposition using bibliometric techniques that accurately illustrates the significance of studies related to autophagy and the TME in cancer research [13].

Materials and methods

Data source and collection

Since the Scopus and PubMed databases are regarded as trustworthy sources of bibliometric indicators, including titles and abstracts, citations, keywords, and other information, primarily utilized in biomedical and biological investigations. Based on this methodology, a literature search was conducted in these databases. This literature search will focus on pertinent data because autophagy and TME in cancer development have received little attention. Only English-language literature was included in the search parameters. A total of 5464 relevant articles were found, including 1441 from PubMed and 4023 from the Scopus database.

Data analysis

This study focused on the annual publishing volume of articles by importing and analyzing annual journal material using Microsoft Office Excel 2019. Then, the data were analyzed using two methods. First, a descriptive analysis was conducted on the sources, publications, and authors. To create networks of knowledge, society, and collaboration, bibliometric methods were then employed to analyze elements such as authors, publications, journals, countries, and organizations. For this bibliometric analysis, VOSviewer (version 1.6.20) was utilized to visualize the publications obtained from Scopus and PubMed. VOSviewer was chosen over other commonly used bibliometric tools because it produces more structured visualizations, thanks to its similarity visualization technology [22]. In this study, VOSviewer enabled a co-occurrence analysis of research related to autophagy and TME in cancer development, facilitating the creation of keyword network maps and combination network maps across nations, institutions, and authors.

Results

Annual publication

Literature inclusion process (Fig. 1) indicated that 4164 publications satisfied the search parameters. Figure 2 displays the distribution of annual publications. There was a significant shift in the publication trend, going from 4 publications in 2007 to 598 publications in 2024. The ongoing research indicates that autophagy and TME in cancer research have progressively made their way into the realm of oncology. From 2007 to 2012, the first phase, which was described as a steady phase, showed a sluggish growth pattern, with an average of less than thirty publications published annually. Beginning in 2013 and lasting until 2024, the second phase is known as a rapid development period, which saw the publication of 598 articles between 2023 and 2024.

This suggests a rapidly expanding and maturing research domain.

Similar to Lotka’s Law, which explains how writers’ publications are distributed, the majority of these authors have only published one article. Alfred Lotka, an American mathematician and physical chemist, first proposed Lotka’s Law in 1926, and it mostly refers to how scientists, writers, and artists divide up their scholarly production. This law roughly means that a relatively tiny percentage of people produce the majority of the work, while the majority merely contribute a very small percentage [14].

Discussion

The bibliometric summary demonstrates the fast development and increasing significance of studies connecting autophagy and the tumor microenvironment (TME) in cancer biology. Our results indicate an explosive growth in publication numbers over the last ten years, particularly since 2013. This increase is not only an indicator of progress in molecular oncology but also a general trend in cancer research, from considering tumor cells in isolation to studying interactions between tumor and microenvironment. Our research differs from other research in terms of its unified incorporation of autophagy literature and tumor microenvironment literature into one bibliometric system. Although existing research has compared these subjects individually, our research attempts to specifically address the interaction and co-evolution of these two fields within the field of cancer studies. Through the use of dual-database material and high-resolution visualization software, we have shed light on dominant trends as well as less-researched types of cancer and molecular pathways. This strategy is an excellent blueprint for researchers and policymakers in strategically charting subsequent studies, such as in research fields like autophagy-modulated immunotherapy, therapy resistance, and immune evasion that are underresearched but have great potential. Our findings corroborate and complement previous bibliometric studies across related fields. For instance, Fan et al. [13] presented a bibliometric overview of autophagy in pancreatic cancer alone and found expanding publication patterns with heightened focus on Chinese-based studies, conclusions in line with our findings in more extended cancer categories. Zheng et al. [23] similarly compiled a review of research on the TME, pointing to the development of prominent themes of immunotherapy and hypoxia, which we too have identified within our keyword clustering. Yet, in contrast to these earlier reviews, our study is the first to both integrate autophagy and the TME within a common analytical context, offering improved understanding of their interdependence in cancer biology. Second, by using both the Scopus and PubMed databases, we have higher coverage than studies that depend on a single database, and our citation and keyword analysis provide deeper mapping of novel research directions. This distinction emphasizes the innovation and generalizability of our approach.

This extensive research saw a strong bibliometric analysis undertaken with a view to explaining existing research trends and highlighting possible gaps in the complex landscape of autophagy and tumor microenvironment (TME) research on cancer studies. Employing VOSviewer for keyword co-occurrence visualization, we found that “autophagy” and “tumor microenvironment” were major, established research topics, with high node sizes and heavy interconnecting links that indicate a fertile pool of active research. Conversely, smaller peripheral nodes like “papillary thyroid carcinoma,” “chondrosarcoma,” and “Burkitt lymphoma” are types of cancer whose interaction between autophagy and TME is visibly understudied, offering opportunities for new research endeavors. In addition, the use of a color gradient to show the average year of publication uncovered the development of interesting areas of research, including investigations of triple-negative breast cancer and lung adenocarcinoma, showing an expanding, if still relatively early, interest in these fields. Interestingly, phrases like “cancer-associated fibroblast” and “tumor growth” appeared intently grouped with these core issues but showed moderately sized nodes. These finding highlights prospective mechanistic lacunae that invite further study to completely grasp their roles in cancer biology. As a group, this bibliometric methodology not only charts the existing research arena but also rigorously identifies areas well-suited for additional investigation, including poorly characterized cancer types and certain cellular processes within the TME. Closure of these observed gaps could greatly improve our comprehension of the complex role of autophagy in cancer development and set the stage for the identification of novel treatment options that can revolutionize cancer therapy.

China and the United States have become leaders in oncology research, with both making unique contributions that define the face of cancer research worldwide. China is currently dominating publication numbers with an impressive increase in activity, especially from reputable institutions like Sichuan University and Sun Yat-sen University. These institutions are not only productive in producing studies but also make a significant contribution to increasing our knowledge about cancer. In contrast, the United States is distinguished by its high citation impact, which means that although China is dramatically expanding the amount of research, the U.S. publishes more influential and well-respected studies. This gap presents a large opportunity for China to enhance the quality and global influence of its research, especially through high-impact journals and partnerships with international scientific communities. These geographic patterns highlight divergent regional cancer research priorities and underscore the high investments and resources committed to oncology in both countries.

The impact of pioneering papers, including Hanahan’s “Hallmarks of Cancer: The Next Generation” and Holohan’s “Cancer Drug Resistance: An Evolving Paradigm,” cannot be overemphasized. These papers are seminal references in the field that underscore the complex interplay among autophagy, cancer development, and resistance mechanisms. Furthermore, strong citation rates of research targeting resistance mechanisms and tumor microenvironment reorganization highlight their significance in driving the identification of innovative approaches to overcoming therapeutic resistance. Increasingly, this work highlights targeting autophagy and the microenvironment as targets, reiterating their value as possible breakthroughs towards improving outcomes in cancer therapy.

Keyword clustering shows that the field has advanced beyond basic mechanistic studies, including applied areas such as immunotherapy, autophagy, tumor microenvironment, cancer, hypoxia, and drug resistance. This reflects the diverse roles of autophagy in tumor development, therapeutic resistance, and immune modulation. Although autophagy and TME have been implicated in cancer in several studies, it is unclear whether these two function as a tumor promoter or a tumor suppressor. Autophagy-associated proteins, including ATGs, Beclin-1, mTOR, p53, KRAS, and others, as well as autophagy pathways like mTOR, PI3K, MAPK, EGFR, HIF, and NFкβ, can be modified in a variety of ways to promote cancer [15–17]. Additionally, a number of researchers have pointed out that autophagy in cancer is regulated differently depending on the environment, cell type, and stage. Furthermore, interactions between tumor cells and their surroundings, such as hypoxia, can significantly alter the autophagy response in support of the metabolism, self-proliferation, and metastasis of cancer cells [18].

The TME is crucial for identifying new prognostic and predictive biomarkers, leading to potential therapeutic strategies. Despite variability in TME drivers among individuals, many features remain consistent across patients. A robust anti-tumor environment, characterized by Th1 and CD8 + T cells, may indicate that the immune system is partly controlling or eliminating tumors [19]. Recent advances in understanding the TME have highlighted its role in responses to anti-cancer therapies, particularly immunotherapy. Immunotherapies targeting PD-1 and CTLA-4 have shown significant success, prompting further investigation into other receptors like LAG3, TNFRSF4, and GITR. However, many agonists have not yet demonstrated substantial clinical efficacy due to factors like insufficient activation and over-activation, potentially leading to T cell apoptosis [20]. Future research should focus on multiplex biomarkers to predict patient responses to immunotherapy while exploring how carcinogenic drivers influence TME diversely. Identifying effective therapy combinations and overcoming resistance in the TME are key challenges, as the TME can impact tumorigenesis positively or negatively. Strategies aimed at normalizing tumor cells through stromal cell re-education could offer new approaches to cancer treatment [21]. Additionally, the clustering keywords indicate the interdisciplinary nature of research efforts, which combine molecular biology, immunology, and pharmacology. However, some research areas, such as the intersection of autophagy modulation and personalized cancer immunotherapy, remain underexplored and represent promising opportunities for future research.

Strengths and limitations

Our study offers a comprehensive review of the literature on the impact of autophagy and TME on cancer, including details about the authors, publishers, year of publication, and citation count, in addition to the hottest issues and trends identified from them. However, other databases were not taken into consideration because the data were only gathered from Scopus and PubMed, which are the largest repositories of articles and have a high degree of features that enhanced our analysis. This could result in a small (but not significant) variation in the quantity of documents or other metrics. Furthermore, a small number of articles and research in other languages may have been missed because the analysis solely examined English-language literature.

Conclusion and future perspectives

This bibliometric analysis highlights the critical and evolving role of autophagy and TME in cancer development, progression, and therapeutic resistance. Autophagy, once solely regarded as a cellular housekeeping mechanism, is now recognised as a double-edged sword in oncology, acting as both a tumor suppressor during early carcinogenesis and a survival strategy for established tumors under stress. Similarly, the TME, through its dynamic interactions with cancer cells, orchestrates processes such as immune evasion, angiogenesis, and metastasis, contributing to the complexity of cancer behavior. Our findings reveal that research in this domain has expanded dramatically, driven by the recognition that manipulating autophagy and remodeling the TME represent promising strategies to overcome drug resistance, enhance immunotherapy responses, and improve patient outcomes. The clustering of keywords such as immunotherapy, hypoxia, and drug resistance underscores the translational potential of targeting these pathways. As cancer remains a major cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, future research should prioritize the development of personalized approaches that simultaneously target tumor cells and their supportive microenvironment. Innovations in autophagy modulation and TME reprogramming hold immense promise for redefining cancer treatment paradigms and improving long-term survival rates.

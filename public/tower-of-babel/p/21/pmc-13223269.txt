TACDformer: an improved informer-based model for accurate multivariate long-term time series forecasting
Authors: Zeyu Hu, Yuan Jia, Wu Le, Zhenhong Jia, Congbing He, Huihui Fan, Jie Meng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTransformer-based models have exhibited superior performance in the field of multivariate long-sequence time series forecasting. The Informer model, which adopts the probabilistic sparse self-attention mechanism, presents advantages in computational complexity compared to other models. However, the correlations between variables and multi-scale local features are difficult to be effectively captured by Informer, which results in suboptimal prediction accuracy. To improve prediction accuracy and further reduce computational complexity, a novel Informer-based model, named TACDformer, is proposed in this paper. Firstly, a multi-scale feature distillation layer is designed. This layer can be utilized to extract multi-scale local features that are difficult to be captured by the self-attention mechanism, and to perform adaptive downsampling while fully capturing temporal dependencies and inter-sequence dependencies. Furthermore, an encoder-only architecture is adopted by the model, whereby the negative impacts of the stacked decoder architecture and the historical time series used as decoder inputs on the prediction results are avoided. Since the features output by the encoder cannot be fully learned through simple linear mapping, an output layer based on the temporal attention mechanism and channel attention mechanism is designed. Model performance is enhanced and computational complexity is reduced simultaneously by this layer. Finally, the reversible instance normalization method is introduced on the basis of the Informer model; by performing normalization and denormalization during the data input and output phases respectively, the prediction bias caused by data distribution differences is reduced. To validate the effectiveness of TACDformer, extensive experiments were conducted on seven benchmark datasets spanning four domains: electricity, finance, energy, and weather. The results demonstrate that TACDformer outperforms eight baseline models, including TimeMixer and iTransformer, in terms of MAE and MSE metrics. Meanwhile, compared with Informer, the computational complexity and memory usage of TACDformer are reduced by 33.2% and 63.4%, respectively, and its running time is shortened by 77.2%. Introduction

Multivariate time series data is a collection of multiple variables arranged in chronological order. The task of predicting the future states of variables based on their historical values is defined as multivariate time series forecasting (MTSF), which plays an important role in various fields such as industrial applications, energy management, and environmental protection1. Unlike univariate time series forecasting, complex underlying dependencies are present between variables in MTSF2. Unlike short-term time series forecasting3, more historical inputs need to be processed by long-sequence forecasting to generate longer future sequences, which also imposes higher requirements on the analytical capability of models4.

Enormous potential in handling time series data is exhibited by deep neural network (DNN) techniques, owing to their powerful nonlinear modeling capabilities and ability to learn complex data relationships5. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and their variants—Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU)—are widely applied in time series processing6,7. In addition, Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) and their variant, Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN)8, are also commonly used in time series forecasting9.

Recently, the Transformer model10 has garnered widespread attention due to its capability to capture long-term dependencies, and models based on the self-attention mechanism have been widely applied11–13. Owing to its adaptive learning abilities and proficiency in capturing global relationships, the Transformer has become one of the essential models in the field of time series modeling14. However, certain limitations are presented by the traditional Transformer model; primarily, the issue of quadratic complexity has triggered extensive academic discussions15–17, and attempts have been made to address this phenomenon. In Autoformer18, time series are progressively decomposed into trend and seasonal components via a decomposition architecture, and the computational load is reduced to by leveraging the periodic correlation mechanism of time series. In Fedformer19, the computational complexity is reduced from quadratic to linear O(L) by randomly selecting a subset of frequency components; additionally, sequences are processed utilizing Fourier or wavelet transforms, whereby the volume of data required for processing is decreased. Although the computational load has been reduced by these models, their prediction performance and computational costs remain suboptimal.

In addition, the operation efficiency of the Transformer architecture is further slowed down by stacking encoder layers and decoder layers, which also leads to the rethinking of the necessity of the encoder-decoder structure20. Therefore, how to optimize the model structure to enable the model to have both good performance and high computational efficiency is also a challenge. Some models have adopted the Encoder-only structure, replacing the function of the decoder with simple convolutional layers or linear layers, and have achieved excellent performance. For example, the input sequences processed by the encoder are directly mapped to the prediction window via linear layers in PatchTST21 and iTransformer22, while the output sequence is directly generated through a simple 1D convolution in MRIformer23. These methods have avoided the step-by-step generation process of the decoder in the traditional architecture and reduced the risk of cumulative errors.

Informer24 makes a series of improvements based on Transformer to match multivariate long-sequence forecasting tasks. First, the model introduces a probabilistic sparse self-attention mechanism; through query sparsity evaluation (based on KL divergence), only key query-key pairs are retained, and this design reduces the computational complexity to 0(LlogL). Additionally, a self-attention distillation layer is designed between the encoder layers to reduce the sequence length entering the next encoder layer. Finally, historical sequence segments are used as the input of the decoder, and multi-step future predictions are directly generated, so that error accumulation and speed bottlenecks caused by step-by-step inference are avoided. Although the various designs of Informer have reduced the computational load compared with the traditional Transformer and achieved good prediction performance.

Recently, several studies have explored improvements to the structure of Informer to achieve better performance25–27; for example, a model called MGMI was designed by Tan et al.28, where a series of improvements were made to the distillation layer of Informer—1D convolution was replaced with multi-scale dilated causal convolution to achieve better capture of local and global dependencies, and the integration of multi-granularity feature information was realized by adding a straight-through layer before each distillation layer. A model named CEEMD-MsI was designed by Zheng et al.29, where the original meteorological and environmental time series were decomposed at multiple scales using complete ensemble empirical mode decomposition, multi-channel input representations were constructed, and a multi-stream encoder-decoder structure was designed, which achieved good performance in air pollution prediction. A model called TE-Informer was designed by Ma et al.9, where a periodic time encoder was proposed to fully model temporal periodicity, enhance the model’s perception of seasonality and periodicity, and it performed well on long-term air quality prediction datasets.

However, these models are still limited by the following shortcomings: firstly, the retention of key information by the distillation layer and the balance between important channel-wise and temporal information are not effectively achieved; secondly, low computational efficiency is exhibited, and for multivariate long-sequence forecasting tasks, the reduction of computational complexity and the optimization of model structures are deemed necessary; thirdly, suboptimal scalability and generalizability are presented, as the reliance on datasets from a single domain results in experimental designs where the reliability of the model design is not rigorously validated30, whereby the reliability of the model in complex tasks and practical deployments is restricted.

To address the aforementioned issues and further improve prediction performance and computational efficiency, TACDformer has been designed. Overall, the main contributions of this paper are reflected in the following aspects:

A Multi-Scale Feature Distillation (MSFD) layer is proposed, which is composed of a Multi-Scale Feature Fusion (MSFF) layer and a Two-Dimensional Downsampling (TDD) layer. The MSFF layer is used to fully extract and effectively fuse multi-scale features within the sequence, while the TDD layer performs downsampling in both the temporal dimension and channel dimension and conducts adaptive fusion. This not only retains the important information in the temporal dimension but also preserves the important information in the feature dimension, thereby providing more valuable input for the subsequent encoder layers.

Related work

Informer is a Transformer model specifically designed for multivariate long-sequence time series forecasting. Through the introduction of sparse attention, a self-attention distillation mechanism, and a generative decoder, superior performance compared to the traditional Transformer has been achieved by Informer in this field, from which a series of improved algorithms have been further derived. As the primary modules of the Informer model, its distillation layer and decoder have been subjected to a series of improvements, and favorable performance has also been demonstrated by hybrid architecture models based on Informer.

Improvements to the distillation layer in the encoder of Informer

In the design of deep learning architectures, feature distillation and information compression technologies are widely applied to construct efficient lightweight models. It is indicated by extensive recent research that the global modeling capabilities of Transformer and the local perception advantages of Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) can be effectively synergized by advanced cross-architecture distillation strategies32. Simultaneously, under the premise of reducing computational complexity, the structural features and key information of original data can be reliably retained by efficient and symmetrical distillation learning mechanisms33. In the field of multivariate time series forecasting, to alleviate the substantial computational load introduced by long sequences, the probabilistic sparse self-attention mechanism was adopted by the Informer model, and a prior distillation layer based on max-pooling layers and convolutions was designed for sequence compression. However, compared with the aforementioned advanced feature retention strategies, this fixed downsampling method employed by Informer is considered overly simplistic. Consequently, not only is a severe loss of local details induced, but the capability to capture long-term dependencies is also impaired, whereby the adaptability of the model in complex scenarios is restricted.

To address the shortcomings existing in the distillation layer of the Informer encoder, a model named Blur Informer was proposed by Zhang et al.34, in which the max-pooling layer in the distillation layer was replaced with a blur pooling layer. The blur pooling layer can effectively reduce aliasing through anti-aliasing filtering, enabling the distillation layer to retain more effective features while reducing dimensions. Meanwhile, a model called BWO-CAformer was proposed by Xv et al.35, where a convolutional attention module was introduced into the distillation layer, and the ability to extract key features was enhanced through the channel attention mechanism and spatial attention mechanism. Additionally, a model named MIFCformer was proposed by Huo et al.36; in this model, the self-distillation layer in Informer was replaced with a Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN), which allows more original information to be retained to prevent information leakage while maintaining efficiency. Furthermore, a model called LR-Informer was proposed by Liu et al.37, in which an LSTM layer was added to the distillation layer. This layer leverages the LSTM’s ability to extract long-range dependencies to highlight important features before distillation, thereby endowing the model with better robustness.

These models have achieved good prediction performance by enhancing the ability of distillation layers to retain key information. However, for multivariate time series forecasting tasks, channel dependencies are as important as temporal dependencies, which requires that the distillation layers be able to capture the dependencies between channels; the neglect of this point has led to these models failing to develop corresponding designs for this purpose, which has limited their prediction performance.

Improvements to the decoder structure of informer

The generative decoder is an important design in the Informer model, through which the cumulative error effect of the stepwise generation strategy has been avoided via direct generation. However, two problems exist in the decoder: 1) Since the decoder requires a known sequence and a placeholder to start generation, if the initial sequence contains noise or outliers, this error will be propagated to the entire prediction sequence through the decoder’s multi-head attention mechanism, resulting in prediction bias. Additionally, the length setting of the initial sequence also has a significant impact on the final performance. 2) The attention of the decoder is required to perform two tasks simultaneously: learning the output of the encoder and learning the temporal dependencies within the prediction sequence. This not only causes information redundancy but also limits the learning ability and generation efficiency of the decoder. Due to the existence of the above two problems, the Informer model suffers from complex structure, large computational load, and difficulty in optimization, which restricts the model’s prediction performance and operational efficiency.

A model named Informer-LSTM was designed by Ruan et al.38, in which the structure of the decoder was improved. Specifically, historical sequences were no longer utilized as the input of the decoder, allowing the downstream structure to exclusively learn the output of the encoder. Consequently, the interference of noise and errors from historical sequences on the prediction results was avoided, whereby prediction accuracy was enhanced. Additionally, a model named SCA-MTL was proposed by Zhang et al.39, wherein the encoder of Informer was employed as an expert module to extract sequence features. The original decoder structure was discarded, and an SCA module based on stacked cross-attention was designed. Prediction accuracy and forecasting balance were effectively improved by the SCA module. Furthermore, the adaptive adjustment of fusion weights according to varying input samples was enabled by its dynamic attention mechanism, thereby enhancing the interpretability of the model.

In summary, to overcome the challenges of error propagation and computational redundancy inherent in the Informer decoder, the modification or replacement of this module has been predominantly adopted in existing research to enhance performance. Furthermore, it is indicated that noise interference can be more directly avoided and computational load can be reduced by entirely discarding the traditional decoding process and shifting towards an encoder-only architecture coupled with a lightweight output layer. Consequently, this concept serves as the core rationale for the subsequent architectural optimization presented in this paper.

Informer-based hybrid architecture models

In the design of models, hybrid architectures combining the advantages of different backbone networks are considered an effective approach40. While global long-range dependencies are adeptly captured by the Transformer architecture, its self-attention mechanism exhibits insensitivity to local fine-grained features and incurs substantial computational overhead. Conversely, local details can be efficiently extracted by Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architectures41,42, yet the establishment of global contexts remains challenging. To overcome the limitations of a single architecture, hybrid models integrating CNN and Transformer have been widely applied32. This combination is deemed highly necessary, and its core academic contribution lies in the realization of complementary advantages. By accurately perceiving local temporal fluctuations and effectively grasping global evolutionary trends, prediction accuracy is effectively improved. Consequently, a theoretical foundation is established for existing studies and the hybrid architecture design proposed in this paper.

To compensate for the inadequacies of information extraction in Informer, a hybrid framework was proposed by Sun et al.43, wherein a CNN-LSTM module was integrated after the output of the Informer decoder to extract local features and model global dependencies. Specifically, the CNN is utilized to compensate for the local dependencies that are difficult to be effectively learned by the Informer decoder, while the output of the CNN is processed by the LSTM by leveraging its capability to model long-sequence contextual relationships. Favorable performance has been achieved by this framework in the field of power forecasting. A model named MconvTCN-Informer was proposed by Liu et al.44, in which an MconvTCN module was appended after the decoder output; multi-scale dilated convolutions are employed by this module to extract local features and short-term fluctuations across multiple time scales. The CNN-Informer model was proposed by Li et al.45, wherein time series data processed by a CNN is input into Informer for further processing. Only the encoder of Informer is retained in this model, and prediction results are directly generated through a linear layer. Through the combination of both architectures, not only are local details preserved, but the effective capture of long-term dependencies is also ensured, thereby providing a reliable technical solution for clinical automatic early warning systems.

In summary, the effectiveness of integrating convolutional modules with Informer has been extensively corroborated by existing studies. By introducing local receptive fields, the shortcomings of self-attention in fine-grained feature extraction have been successfully compensated by these hybrid architectures. Not only is the substantial potential of joint local and global modeling in enhancing prediction accuracy validated, but essential inspiration and theoretical support are also provided for the lightweight architecture design in this paper, which discards the decoder and introduces depthwise separable convolutions into the distillation layer.

Although a series of improvements have been made to Informer by these methods, some problems still remain. Firstly, either adding modules for key feature enhancement based on the decoder (which makes the structure more complex) or adopting other types of attention mechanisms to learn the encoder’s output will affect computational efficiency and increase optimization difficulty. In addition, although the practice of not using the decoder but instead using linear layers to generate predictions directly has reduced computational load and improved computational efficiency, the simple linear layers cannot fully learn the encoder’s output and also fail to properly focus on key time steps and key channels, which has led to a performance bottleneck.

In this paper, the proposed method differs from previous methods in the following aspects:

Regarding the distillation layer in the encoder, an adaptive fusion mechanism for downsampling in the temporal and channel dimensions is designed for the first time in the distillation layer of Informer. Through the designed Multi-Scale Feature Distillation (MSFD) layer, the fusion of multi-scale local features and the adaptive fusion of inter-channel relationships across time steps and intra-channel local temporal features are achieved, thereby providing more valuable input for the subsequent encoder layers.

Regarding the distillation layer in the encoder, an adaptive fusion mechanism for downsampling in the temporal and channel dimensions is designed for the first time in the distillation layer of Informer. Through the designed Multi-Scale Feature Distillation (MSFD) layer, the fusion of multi-scale local features and the adaptive fusion of inter-channel relationships across time steps and intra-channel local temporal features are achieved, thereby providing more valuable input for the subsequent encoder layers.

For the improvement of the decoder structure, an encoder-only structure is adopted. With this structure, the decoder is discarded, which not only achieves the effect of reducing the computational load but also avoids the impact of noise in the initial sequence on prediction results. Meanwhile, different from the current approach where only simple linear layers are used to directly receive the encoder’s output for prediction, an output layer (TACD) based on the channel attention mechanism and temporal attention mechanism is designed, which enhances the model’s ability to learn the encoder’s output.

This enhances the scalability and generalizability of the model. Experiments show that good performance has been demonstrated by TACDformer on multiple datasets spanning various fields.

Overall, an encoder-only architecture is adopted by TACDformer, wherein the utilization of a complexly designed decoder for output generation is discarded. Firstly, the reversible instance normalization (RevIN) method is introduced on the basis of the Informer model. Not only is higher computational efficiency achieved compared to the traditional encoder-decoder structure, but superior prediction performance is also demonstrated. In multivariate time series forecasting, the generalization capability of models is substantially constrained by the non-stationarity of data. Indeed, the effective mitigation of interference from data distribution shifts has been widely recognized as a consensus for the construction of robust deep learning models46. Multi-scale feature distillation block

The encoder of Informer is composed of multi-head probabilistic sparse self-attention layers and distillation layers placed between them, stacked together. The distillation layer uses a 1D convolution layer combined with a max-pooling layer to gradually reduce the length of the sequence entering the next encoder layer, thereby further reducing computational load. However, the downsampling method of the distillation layer cannot fully retain the important features within the sequence and also overlooks the extraction of important features between channels, and these shortcomings affect the model’s performance. TACDformer has designed a multi-scale feature fusion and distillation layer (MSFD) to fully extract multi-scale local features, perform adaptive downsampling in the temporal and channel dimensions, and fully retain the key features within the sequence.

Temporal and channel attention-enhanced block

A generative decoder is designed in the Informer model, by which predictions for multiple future time steps are directly generated, whereby the cumulative errors and speed bottlenecks inherent in step-by-step inference are avoided. In certain recent studies, such as PatchTST and iTransformer, the decoder is discarded; instead, the output of the encoder is directly mapped to the prediction window through a linear layer. Although computational load is reduced and favorable prediction performance is achieved by this approach of directly mapping the encoder output to the prediction window utilizing linear or convolutional layers, two primary issues are presented by such a structure. Firstly, the critical role of the attention mechanism in emphasizing key nodes for output generation is overlooked. Secondly, the output of the encoder cannot be comprehensively learned by simple linear layers, whereby their prediction performance is constrained. In summary, a TACD module, as illustrated in Fig. 5, is designed in this paper to process the output of the encoder. By emphasizing important time steps or critical channels during the adjustment of sequence length or channel dimensions of the encoder output, the learning efficacy of the subsequent linear or convolutional layers is effectively enhanced.

To address these limitations, the introduction of a variable-wise modeling strategy will be explored in future work to enhance the extraction of independent intra-variable features. Regarding the encoder of TACDformer, multi-scale feature distillation layers are embedded between the encoder layers. The sequence length transmitted to the subsequent encoder layer is reduced to half of the input sequence length of the preceding layer. Consequently, the computational cost within the stacked encoder architecture is decreased, whereby TACDformer is enabled to accommodate deeper encoder configurations. When shallower encoder layers are utilized, insufficient feature extraction capabilities are exhibited, and long-range dependencies cannot be effectively modeled. Although more advantageous computational and time costs are presented, the overall prediction performance is restricted. Conversely, when deeper encoder layers are employed, although the computational load and costs are alleviated by the multi-scale feature distillation layers, the sequence is progressively compressed, during which critical temporal information may be lost. Furthermore, excessive attention to local features might be induced by deep distillation, whereby the capability to capture long-range global dependencies is weakened. Therefore, these aspects are identified as essential factors to be considered when the number of encoder layers is determined.

As indicated by the experimental results presented in Table 6 and Fig. 9, when the number of encoder layers is set to 1, suboptimal performance is exhibited, as advanced sequence representations cannot be comprehensively captured and learned. When the number of encoder layers is configured to 3 or 4, although the performance is substantially improved compared to the single-layer configuration, it remains inferior to the two-layer configuration. This performance degradation is primarily attributed to the over-compression of the sequence and the redundancy introduced by the complex structure. Following a comprehensive evaluation of prediction performance and computational efficiency, 2 is ultimately selected as the default number of encoder layers for TACDformer.

The experimental results presented in Table 8 and Fig. 11 show that TACDformer has the ability to process historical sequences with longer lookback windows, and its performance is also improved by learning longer historical sequences, but there is a bottleneck in its learning ability; this is because when the input sequence length is too long, the global features become more complex and more noise is introduced, which leads to unsatisfactory performance when the lookback window size is 720. In general, compared with the default lookback window of 96, the performance is better when the lookback window is 192 and 336, but as the lookback window increases, the computational complexity, memory usage, and running time all rise, and currently most models use a default lookback window size of 96. To ensure the fairness of comparative experiments and meet the need to balance performance and computational complexity, 96 is finally selected as the default lookback window size for TACDformer to conduct comparative experiments with other models.

Conclusion

To address the issues of local information loss and computational redundancy inherent in the Informer model during multivariate long-sequence time series forecasting, a TACDformer model balancing accuracy and efficiency is proposed in this paper. The multi-scale feature distillation module is improved to achieve adaptive downsampling, whereby effective information is adequately retained. Simultaneously, an encoder-only architecture is adopted, and the output layer is reconstructed by integrating a temporal attention mechanism and a channel attention mechanism. Furthermore, reversible instance normalization is introduced to alleviate data distribution shifts. The primary advantage of this study lies in the successful realization of a balance between prediction performance and computational cost. Compared to eight advanced baselines, including iTransformer, superior overall prediction results are achieved across seven public datasets. It is anticipated that these outcomes will provide robust technical support for real-world scenarios with strict requirements for timeliness and accuracy, such as power grid dispatching.

Despite the progress achieved, certain limitations remain in the current work. For instance, on datasets comprising an extensive number of variables, such as the Electricity dataset, a performance bottleneck is encountered regarding the capability to capture complex channel dependencies, which is constrained by the high-dimensional feature space. In future work, the introduction of a variable-wise modeling strategy or the construction of novel branches will be explored to enhance the decoupling capability of independent relationships within high-dimensional sequences. Additionally, in-depth comparisons and integrations with a wider variety of attention mechanisms will be conducted, and the capability of the model to process exceptionally long look-back windows will be strengthened. Furthermore, the introduction of reliability modeling is planned to quantify the uncertainty of prediction results. More detailed investigations into the fluctuation of standard deviations observed during experimental testing will also be carried out, with the expectation that the overall robustness of the model in complex environments can be further evaluated and optimized in the future.

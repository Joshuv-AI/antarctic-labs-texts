Efficacy and safety of high-frequency electrosurgery, ultrasonic scalpel, and bipolar electrocoagulation in parathyroidectomy for secondary hyperparathyroidism: a 3-year follow-up retrospective study
Authors: Dan Wang, Zilin Wang, Jiangping Li, Zhao Gao, Shuang Zou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to evaluate the surgical outcomes of parathyroidectomy (PTX) for secondary hyperparathyroidism (SHPT) in hemodialysis patients, comparing the use of three operative techniques: high-frequency electrosurgery (HFE), ultrasonic scalpel (US), and bipolar electrocoagulation (BPE).MethodsThe patients were divided into three groups based on the type of surgical instruments used. Outcomes assessed included surgical efficiency, postoperative complications, recurrence rate, hospitalization costs, biochemical and nutritional parameters over 1 year, and overall survival through March 2025.ResultsA total of 171 patients were retrospectively categorized into three groups: HFE (n = 50), US (n = 60), and BPE (n = 61). Compared to group HFE, both the US and BPE groups exhibited significantly shorter durations for drainage tube removal, operative times, and postoperative hospital stays (all p < 0.05). The BPE group also had significantly lower intraoperative blood loss compared to the US and HFE groups (p < 0.05) and incurred the lowest hospitalization costs. The incidence of postoperative complications and recurrence rates did not differ significantly among groups. All groups showed marked reductions in serum calcium, phosphorus, and intact parathyroid hormone levels postoperatively, which stabilized over time with no significant differences between groups. At the 1-year follow-up, patients exhibited significant improvements in anemia-related and nutritional markers compared to baseline. With follow-up extending to March 2025, overall survival did not differ significantly among the three surgical groups (p = 0.987).ConclusionsPTX effectively corrected SHPT, improved anemia, and enhanced nutritional status in hemodialysis patients. All three surgical techniques, HFE, US, and BPE, were safe and effective, with comparable long-term survival. However, US and BPE offered superior operative efficiency, and BPE represented the most cost-effective option.Keywords: Parathyroidectomy, High-frequency electrosurgery, Ultrasonic scalpel, Bipolar electrocoagulation, Survival analysis

Introduction

Secondary hyperparathyroidism (SHPT) is a prevalent and serious complication of chronic kidney disease (CKD) [1]. When inadequately controlled, SHPT leads to multisystem damage, including cardiovascular, skeletal, and hematologic systems, resulting in complications such as severe vascular calcification, renal osteodystrophy, erythropoietin resistance, protein-energy wasting, and other adverse clinical events. These complications collectively contribute to increased mortality in patients undergoing hemodialysis (HD) [2, 3] .

Treatment options for SHPT include phosphate-lowering therapies, vitamin D analogs, calcimimetics, and surgical parathyroidectomy (PTX). Although the introduction of calcimimetics has reduced the frequency of surgical intervention, PTX remains a pivotal treatment, particularly for patients who fail to achieve adequate biochemical control with medical therapy [4]. According to the 2017 guidelines from Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO), PTX is recommended as an effective intervention for patients with severe SHPT at CKD stages 3a through 5D who do not respond to pharmacologic treatment [5].

The parathyroid glands are richly vascularized and situated in close proximity to vital anatomical structures, including the recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN), thyroid gland, trachea, and esophagus. Consequently, surgical interventions in this region require meticulous dissection and precise hemostatic control. This is particularly critical in HD patients, who frequently exhibit coagulation disorders due to platelet dysfunction and anticoagulant use, predisposing them to increased intraoperative bleeding [6]. Modern surgical instruments, such as high-frequency electrosurgical (HFE) knives, ultrasonic scalpels (US), and bipolar electrocoagulation (BPE) devices, have been widely adopted in thyroid surgery due to their rapid and effective hemostatic capabilities [7].

However, comparative evidence regarding the use of these instruments in PTX is currently limited, especially in terms of operative efficiency, intraoperative blood loss, and postoperative recovery. This study aimed to evaluate the safety and clinical utility of HFE, US, and BPE in PTX procedures. Key outcome measures included surgical efficiency, postoperative complications, hospitalization costs, recurrence rate, and long-term biochemical control. The findings were expected to provide valuable clinical insights to guide the selection of surgical instruments for PTX in SHPT patients receiving maintenance HD.

Materials and methods

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Xiangyang Central Hospital. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients, authorizing the use of their clinical data for research purposes.

Patients

The clinical data of patients with SHPT who underwent total parathyroidectomy at Xiangyang Central Hospital between September 2020 and March 2024 were retrospectively reviewed. The patient selection flow chart is shown in Fig. 1.

Flowchart for the management of 219 patients undergoing parathyroidectomy. HFE, high-frequency electrosurgery; US, ultrasonic scalpel; BPE, bipolar electrocoagulation

Inclusion criteria were defined according to the 2017 KDIGO guidelines and included: (1) persistent elevation of intact parathyroid hormone (iPTH) > 800 pg/mL; (2) sustained hypercalcemia and/or hyperphosphatemia unresponsive to pharmacologic therapy; (3) imaging-confirmed parathyroid hyperplasia, defined as at least one gland measuring > 1 cm in diameter with abundant blood flow on high-frequency color Doppler ultrasound; and (4) lack of therapeutic response to active vitamin D or its analogs.

Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) history of secondary PTX; (2) prior kidney transplantation; (3) missing key follow-up data; or (4) undergoing concurrent surgical procedures.

Surgery

The surgical procedures were performed using three different techniques. In the HFE group, high-frequency electrocautery (ERBE, VIO 300D, Germany) was used in spray coagulation mode at 25 W. In the US group, the Harmonic Focus+ Shears (Ultracision, Ethicon Endo-Surgery Europe) were used in automatic power mode for meticulous dissection and vessel sealing. In the BPE group, bipolar electrocautery was performed using Chengdu Bomei forceps (China) in combination with a Medtronic Valleylab Force-FX-8 C generator (USA) set to 20–25 W in bipolar mode.

All patients underwent standardized total parathyroidectomy without autotransplantation and without thymectomy performed by the same multidisciplinary team, including a senior otolaryngologist and two experienced nephrologists. The surgical instrument used was determined by time-based institutional availability rather than surgeon preference: HFE from September 2020 to December 2021, US from January 2022 to June 2023, and BPE from July 2023 to March 2024. All surgeons were fully trained in each device before clinical use.

The surgical procedure was standardized across all groups as follows: (1) A transverse cervical incision was made, followed by layered dissection of the subcutaneous tissue using electrosurgery to expose the thyroid gland. (2) The thyroid lobe was gently retracted medially using tissue forceps to expose the retrothyroid region. (3) Parathyroid glands were carefully identified and dissected from the surrounding tissue using the allocated energy device: HFE, US, or BPE, with meticulous care to preserve the recurrent laryngeal nerve and vascular pedicles. (4) The surrounding adipose tissue and vascular pedicle of each parathyroid gland were dissected. (5) The vascular pedicle was ligated, and the parathyroid gland was resected completely. (6) All resected specimens were sent for histopathological examination.

Anticoagulant and antiplatelet medications were routinely discontinued before surgery in accordance. Postoperatively, they were temporarily withheld and resumed individually based on the patients’ comorbidities and the balance between thrombotic and bleeding risks. Four parathyroid glands were removed in all patients. All excised tissues were confirmed as parathyroid hyperplasia by postoperative histopathological examination. It is worth noting that intraoperative nerve monitoring was not used in any of the patients.

A > 50% reduction in PTH levels from baseline was used as the intraoperative criterion for successful gland excision [8]. Recurrent secondary hyperparathyroidism was defined as an iPTH level exceeding 500 pg/mL at 6 months or later after parathyroidectomy, following an initial decrease to within or below the target range [9–11].

Postoperatively, the attending doctors and nurses conducted a clinical assessment on all patients to check for symptoms of recurrent laryngeal nerve injury, including hoarseness and aspiration. Patients with any voice abnormality underwent confirmatory fiberoptic laryngoscopy by an otolaryngologist to evaluate vocal cord mobility. In cases of confirmed vocal cord paralysis, repeat laryngoscopy was performed at 6 and 12 months postoperatively.

Data collection

Demographic and baseline clinical data, including age, dialysis duration, body weight, and biochemical indicators, were retrieved from medical records to ensure baseline comparability among the study groups. Intraoperative and postoperative parameters such as operative time, estimated blood loss, drainage volume, time to drainage tube removal, and length of postoperative hospitalization were systematically recorded. Postoperative complications, including infections, thyroid dysfunction, RLN injury, hypocalcemia, cervical hematoma, and postoperative hemorrhage, were evaluated. Changes in serum calcium, serum phosphorus, calcium-phosphorus product, and iPTH levels were tracked throughout the perioperative period.

To assess the long-term efficacy of each surgical modality, trends in serum calcium, phosphorus, calcium-phosphorus product, and iPTH were analyzed at 1, 6, and 12 months postoperatively. Additional indicators, including dry weight, body mass index (BMI), hemoglobin, serum albumin, total lymphocyte count (TLC), and serum creatinine normalized to body surface area (Cr/BSA), were recorded at baseline and 12 months after surgery.

To comprehensively evaluate the long-term outcomes of each surgical modality, patients were further followed until March 2025. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from PTX to death from any cause. Patients who remained alive at the last follow-up date or were lost to follow-up were censored at their last known alive date. Survival status was verified through hospital records and telephone interviews.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 22.0. Normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while non-normally distributed data were presented as median and interquartile range [M (IQR)]. Group comparisons of continuous variables were conducted using one-way ANOVA or the Kruskal-Wallis test, depending on data distribution. Categorical variables were expressed as percentages and compared using the Chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Within-group comparisons were performed using paired-sample t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. Survival analysis was performed using Kaplan-Meier estimates, with group comparisons assessed by the log-rank test. A p-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

After excluding 48 patients who did not meet the inclusion criteria, 171 patients were finally included in the study: the HFE group (n = 50), the US group (n = 60), and the BPE group (n = 61), as shown in Fig. 1.

Intraoperative blood loss was lowest in the BPE group (17.70 ± 4.79 mL), significantly less than that observed in the HFE (26.20 ± 9.18 mL) and US (24.30 ± 7.91 mL) groups (p < 0.05). Additionally, drainage tubes were removed earlier in the US (4.58 ± 1.23 days) and BPE (4.66 ± 1.11 days) groups compared to the HFE group (5.30 ± 1.67 days; p < 0.05). Postoperative hospital stays were also shorter in the US (7.87 ± 2.56 days) and BPE (7.15 ± 2.03 days) groups than in the HFE group (9.38 ± 3.45 days; p < 0.05). However, no significant differences were observed in total drainage volume among the groups (HFE: 157.13 ± 70.55 mL; US: 137.30 ± 67.64 mL; BPE: 139.74 ± 67.64 mL; p = 0.256).

Hospitalization expenses

Hospitalization costs varied significantly across the groups (p < 0.05). The BPE group incurred the lowest average expenses (RMB 17,041.95 ± 4,034.99), followed by the US group (RMB 19,606.33 ± 5,706.59) and the HFE group (RMB 22,123.86 ± 7,189.45).

Cost of surgical instruments

As shown in Table 2, the cost of surgical instruments used in PTX varied substantially among the three techniques. The US was the most expensive (RMB 1,300), followed by BPE (RMB 363), and HFE incurred the lowest cost (RMB 218).

Postoperative complications

No statistically significant differences in postoperative complications were observed among the three groups (Table 3). No cases of postoperative hemorrhage or cervical hematoma were reported in any group. The incidence of infection was 18.0% in the HFE group, 11.7% in the US group, and 13.1% in the BPE group (p = 0.673). Thyroid dysfunction occurred in 2.0% (HFE), 0% (US), and 1.6% (BPE) of patients (p = 0.748). RLN injury was recorded in 2.0% (HFE), 1.7% (US), and 1.6% (BPE) of cases (p = 1.000). The incidence of postoperative hypocalcemia was 58.0% in the HFE group, 45.0% in the US group, and 60.7% in the BPE group (p = 0.187).

Recurrence

During follow-up, recurrent hyperparathyroidism occurred in 4 patients (8.0%) in the HFE group, 4 patients (6.7%) in the US group, and 6 patients (9.8%) in the BPE group, with no statistically significant difference among the three groups (P = 0.816). The overall recurrence rate was 8.2%.

Pre- and postoperative biochemical parameters

All three groups experienced significant reductions in serum phosphorus, serum calcium, and iPTH levels following PTX. However, intergroup comparisons revealed no statistically significant differences in the levels of iPTH, serum phosphorus, serum calcium, or calcium-phosphorus product (Table 4). During long-term follow-up, no significant differences were noted among the groups in terms of serum calcium, serum phosphorus, calcium-phosphorus product, iPTH levels. Over time, serum phosphorus levels stabilized within the normal range, serum calcium levels remained around 2.1 mmol/L, and iPTH levels were maintained at a relatively low level (Table 4).

Overall survival

During follow-up extending through March 2025, overall survival was evaluated in all patients. Kaplan-Meier survival curves for the three surgical groups were nearly superimposed, indicating comparable long-term survival outcomes. The log-rank test confirmed no significant difference in survival among the HFE, US, and BPE groups (p = 0.987; Fig. 2).

Discussion

PTX is a critical intervention for managing SHPT in patients undergoing maintenance HD. Achieving effective hemostasis while preserving the RLN remains a central challenge in this procedure. Effective hemostasis during PTX is essential not only to prevent life-threatening postoperative hematomas but also to minimize the risk of nerve injury and collateral tissue damage. Our comparative evaluation of three energy-based surgical devices, HFE, US, and BPE, elucidated their respective advantages in terms of surgical efficiency, safety, and health economics.

From a technical perspective, the mechanisms of hemostasis and dissection differ fundamentally among the three devices, which underlie their distinct clinical performance. HFE generates substantial current during operation, making it difficult to precisely control the extent of thermal spread to adjacent tissues. Therefore, minimizing power settings and maintaining a safe distance from vital structures are critical to avoiding inadvertent injury to nerves and blood vessels [12]. In contrast, the US utilizes piezoelectric ceramics to convert electrical energy at 55.5 kHz into mechanical vibrations. Operating at temperatures between 80 °C and 100 °C, its thermal effect is generally confined to a radius of 2–3 mm beyond the tissue directly grasped by the instrument’s jaws [13, 14]. These characteristics allow the US to achieve precise tissue dissection with minimal thermal injury, reduced smoke production, and an improved visual field. Previous studies by Jiang et al. have shown that the use of US in thyroid surgery can shorten operative time and decrease the incidence of postoperative complications, particularly nerve damage caused by excessive heat [15, 16].BPE delivers high-frequency current through the tips of a pair of forceps, forming a closed-loop system. This focused energy delivery minimizes lateral heat dispersion, substantially reducing thermal injury to adjacent structures [15, 16]. The slim and pointed design of bipolar forceps also facilitates meticulous dissection in confined spaces. Tysome et al. have reported that patients undergoing hemithyroidectomy with bipolar cautery require fewer postoperative drains and have shorter hospital stays, further supporting its clinical utility [7].

These technical characteristics were reflected in our clinical outcomes. The use of US or BPE reduced operative time by approximately 32 to 38 min, conferred significant advantages. Additionally, both techniques were associated with shorter drainage durations (by 0.7 to 0.8 days) and reduced hospital stays (by 1.5 to 2.5 days). These findings suggest that US and BPE confer advantages in surgical efficiency and contribute to enhanced postoperative recovery by minimizing tissue trauma. Notably, the BPE group exhibited the lowest intraoperative blood loss, underscoring its precision in hemostasis. RLN injury was reported once in each of the three groups, HFE, US, and BPE, with no occurrences of serious complications such as cervical hematoma or postoperative bleeding. Only one case of postoperative thyroid dysfunction was observed, and it occurred in the HFE group. Hypocalcemia, the most prevalent postoperative complication resulting from parathyroid tissue removal and primarily attributed to hungry bone syndrome, showed comparable rates across all study groups. These results confirm that all three devices are safe when used with meticulous surgical technique, consistent with prior studies on US in PTX [17] and BPE in thyroid surgery [18–21].

Our findings revealed that the US incurred the highest instrument costs during PTX, followed by BPE, with HFE being the least expensive. Interestingly, total hospitalization expenses exhibited an inverse pattern, with the BPE group incurring the lowest overall costs and the HFE group the highest. This discrepancy likely reflected a multifactorial interplay: the cost-effectiveness of BPE might derive from its precise coagulation and limited thermal spread, thereby minimizing complications, alongside reduced operative time and lower postoperative morbidity, which collectively decreased the need for prolonged monitoring and additional treatments. Conversely, despite the lower upfront device cost in the HFE group, the elevated total expenses might be attributable to longer recovery periods and higher complication management costs.

In terms of long-term efficacy, all three groups exhibited significant and sustained improvements in biochemical parameters, including iPTH, serum calcium, and phosphorus levels, with no intergroup differences. The overall recurrence rate was 8.2%, with no significant difference among groups, consistent with previously reported rates following total parathyroidectomy in HD patients [22, 23]. Extended follow-up through March 2025 revealed no significant difference in overall survival among the surgical technique groups. This observation underscores that while short-term surgical parameters may vary, long-term survival outcomes are comparable regardless of the energy device used. These findings suggest that while energy devices influence short-term surgical parameters, the fundamental efficacy of PTX in controlling SHPT is device-independent.

Furthermore, our data demonstrated significant postoperative improvements in nutritional status and anemia among maintenance HD patients with SHPT, aligning with previous reports in the literature [24, 25]. Elevated PTH contributes to malnutrition by promoting enhanced lipolysis and increased energy expenditure, as evidenced in 5/6 nephrectomy-induced cachexia mouse models [26]. Additionally, PTH adversely affects erythropoiesis via direct bone marrow suppression, heightened hemolysis, and secondary myelofibrosis [27]. These multisystem benefits underscore the critical role of PTX in mitigating SHPT-associated malnutrition and anemia, thereby potentially enhancing both survival and quality of life in maintenance HD patients.

This study has several limitations. Its retrospective design and relatively small sample size might introduce selection bias, thereby limiting the generalizability of the findings. The follow-up duration, while adequate for mid-term outcomes, may be insufficient to assess very late recurrence or survival. The cost analysis did not account for indirect expenses. Prospective randomized controlled trials with larger cohorts and longer follow-up are warranted to validate our findings.

In conclusion, PTX effectively corrected SHPT and ameliorated associated malnutrition and anemia, ultimately improving survival and quality of life in affected patients. While HFE, US, and BPE demonstrated comparable safety and efficacy, the latter two techniques offered superior operative efficiency and reduced tissue injury. Considering hospitalization costs, BPE might represent the most cost-effective and suitable surgical modality for PTX in maintenance HD patients with SHPT.

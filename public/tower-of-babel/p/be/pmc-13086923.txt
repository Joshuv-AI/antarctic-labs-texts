ACSL4 mediates ferroptosis to promote immunoglobulin A nephropathy progression: scRNA-seq analysis
Authors: Ningjun Shao, Kuibi Tan, Ping Chen, Qun Luo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Immunoglobulin A nephropathy (IgAN), the most prevalent primary glomerular disease, is a major cause of end-stage renal disease. Ferroptosis, a novel cell death mode induced by iron-dependent phospholipid peroxidation, has been implicated in the progression of chronic kidney disease. Whereas, Acyl-CoA synthetase long-chain family member 4 (ACSL4) is a key enzyme in pro-ferroptotic lipid metabolism that plays a critical role in ferroptosis. In this study, the role of ACSL4-mediated ferroptosis in IgAN progression was investigated by identifying the cell subset ACSL4+ Loop of Henle cells (LOHs) in IgAN via single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq). LOH cell populations were screened by dimensionality reduction with Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP), and ACSL4+ LOHs were annotated using cell marker genes. Gene Ontology and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (GO/KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses were performed based on marker genes of ACSL4+ LOHs. Cell-cell communication networks were established to infer interactions between ACSL4+ LOHs and other cell types. Pathological changes and LOH proportions in IgAN and normal kidney tissues were observed by hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and Masson staining. Expression levels of ACSL4 in LOHs were determined by immunofluorescence to further investigate its effect on cell function. The proportion of LOHs was significantly elevated in the IgAN group compared to the Control group, and ACSL4 expression was significantly upregulated in LOHs. ACSL4+ LOHs were highly expressed in the IgAN group and enriched in the Ferroptosis pathway. Cellular communication between ACSL4+ LOHs and ECs, MEs, and PTCs was significantly enhanced in interleukin-6 (IL6) ligand-receptor pairs (IL6R+IL6ST). The proportion of LOHs was elevated, and ACSL4 expression was significantly increased in LOHs in the IgAN group. High expression of ACSL4+ LOHs, as revealed by scRNA-seq analysis, may enhance ferroptosis in IgAN and trigger inflammatory responses, thereby contributing to disease progression. This study is expected to provide insights into the mechanisms underlying the progression of IgAN.

Introduction

Immunoglobulin A nephropathy (IgAN) is the most common primary glomerular disease worldwide, accounting for 30–40% of primary glomerulonephritis1. Approximately 20–40% of IgAN patients will develop end-stage renal disease within 10–20 years after diagnosis2. IgAN is an autoimmune disease with a complex pathogenesis, involving multiple factors such as genetic susceptibility, IgA1 galactosylation abnormalities, IgA immune complex formation, and mucosal infections or other environmental triggers3–5. Nevertheless, the specific cell types and molecular pathways underlying IgAN development and progression remain to be fully elucidated.

Ferroptosis is a programmed cell death caused by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation, primarily involving lipid peroxidation and the generation of iron-dependent reactive oxygen species (ROS). It can be either induced or inhibited by regulating lipid peroxidation and iron metabolism6. Ferroptosis is closely associated with the pathological and physiological processes in renal diseases. Gao et al.7 have shown that ferroptosis activates the intrinsic immune system by releasing inflammation-related damage-associated molecules, thereby triggering inflammatory responses and exacerbating renal injury.

Acyl-CoA synthetase long-chain family member 4 (ACSL4) is an enzyme that converts fatty acids into fatty acid acyl-CoA esters and exerts a critical role in triggering ferroptosis through its involvement in the biosynthesis and remodeling of the polyunsaturated fatty acid-phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) in cell membranes8. ACSL4-dependent ferroptosis promotes the accumulation of pro-inflammatory cells, contributing to impaired repair of renal tubular epithelial cells during chronic kidney disease progression. Dysregulated ACSL4-mediated ferroptosis may therefore represent a potential therapeutic target for inducing anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic effects in chronic kidney disease9. Loop of Henle cells (LOHs) play a key role in urine concentration and electrolyte homeostasis. During disease progression in patients with IgAN, the release of inflammatory factors may impair the normal function of LOHs through the tubuloglomerular feedback mechanism10.

In this study, the role of ACSL4-mediated ferroptosis in IgAN progression will be investigated by identifying the cell subset ACSL4+ LOHs in IgAN and determining ACSL4 expression in IgAN kidney tissues through single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO).

Methods

Data and sample sources

The IgAN scRNA-seq dataset GSE171314 was obtained from the GEO (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE171314). A total of 4 IgAN samples and 1 Control sample were selected, including 19,830 cells and 26,398 genes. Kidney tissue sections were acquired from patients diagnosed with IgAN at our hospital. The present study was reviewed and approved by the Human Research Ethics Committee of Ningbo Hwa Mei Hospital, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences (SL-NBEY-KY-2022-060-01). All experiments were performed in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. Informed consent was obtained from all participants and/or their legal guardians.

scRNA-seq data processing

scRNA-seq data from 4 IgAN samples and 1 Control sample were subjected to quality control (QC) and normalization using the R package Seurat (v5.1.0). For each cell, the number of genes (nFeature), the number of unique molecular identifiers (UMIs) (nCount), and the percentage of mitochondrial gene expression (percent.mt) were calculated. In order to exclude some low-quality cells and low-expressed genes, the following thresholds were applied: (1) nFeature_RNA was between 200 and 6,000; (2) nCount_RNA was between 200 and 15,000; and (3) percent.mt < 10% in each cell. After normalization with the “NormalizeData” in the R package Seurat (v5.1.0), 2,000 highly variable genes (HVGs) were identified using the FindVariableFeature function.

scRNA-seq cell annotation

After data normalization, principal component analysis (PCA) was performed using the R package Harmony (v1.1.0). The 30 most significant principal components (PCs) of gene expression were calculated by dimensionality reduction and clustering via the RunPCA function. Based on these PCs, cells were clustered using the FindNeighbors function and the FindClusters function. Cell clusters in IgAN were annotated based on the published literature11, and the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between each cell type were identified using the FindAllMarkers function in Seurat. On this basis, cell types were annotated. The identified cell marker genes were visualized using the VlnPlot and FeaturePlot functions to finally identify the cell population.

Enrichment analysis

Gene-regulated signaling pathways were described by the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis12. Cell marker genes were identified using the FindMarkers function in the R package Seurat (v5.1.0). P < 0.05 and |fold change (FC)| > 1 was set as the threshold for significant differential expression. Specifically expressed genes in ACSL4+ LOHs were subjected to Gene Ontology (GO)/KEGG enrichment analysis and visualized using the ClusterProfiler package (v4.6.2), with the parameters pvalueCutoff = 0.05, pAdjustMethod = BH, and qvalueCutoff = 0.5.

Cell-cell communication

Communication relationships between ACSL4+/− LOHs and other cells were inferred using the R package CellChat (v1.6.1). To define cell-cell communication networks, ligand–receptor expression pairs were identified across cell types. Communication probabilities at the signaling pathway level were calculated using CellChat to summarize the communication probabilities of all ligand-receptor interactions associated with each signaling pathway. The resulting cell-cell communication networks were displayed using the R package igraph and circlize.

Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining

IgAN and normal kidney tissue sections were placed in an oven at 60℃ for 2 h and then deparaffinized in xylene solution, graded ethanol solution, and distilled water in the prescribed order. After drying, sections were stained with hematoxylin for 5 min, and excess staining reagent was washed with running water. Next, the surface water was dried. The sections were then immersed in 1% hydrochloric acid ethanol solution for 30 s, followed by washing with running water until differentiation was complete and the sections turned blue. Subsequently, the sections were completely covered with eosin reagent for 3 min (adjusted according to staining intensity) and washed with running water for 3 min. The sections were dehydrated and transparent using a gradient concentration of ethanol and xylene solution. Finally, the treated kidney tissue sections were fixed with neutral resin to observe their changes under the microscope.

Masson staining

IgAN and normal kidney tissue sections were sequentially immersed in xylene solution, ethanol solutions with gradient concentrations, and distilled water for deparaffinization. The sections were then immersed in potassium dichromate solution overnight and lightly rinsed with running water. Next, equal volumes of iron hematoxylin solution A and solution B were mixed to prepare the iron hematoxylin staining solution. The sections were placed in the iron hematoxylin staining solution for 3 min, rinsed with running water, differentiated with hydrochloric acid in ethanol for a few seconds, and then rinsed again. The sections were immersed in Ponceau S acid fuchsin solution for 5–10 min and quickly rinsed with running water. Subsequently, the sections were treated with phosphomolybdic acid aqueous solution for 1–3 min and then directly immersed in aniline blue solution for 3–6 min without washing with water. The sections were then differentiated for a few seconds with 1% glacial acetic acid, dehydrated with anhydrous ethanol, and finally dehydrated and cleared using ethanol of gradient concentrations and xylene solution. The treated kidney tissue sections were fixed using neutral resin and observed under a microscope.

Immunofluorescence

IgAN and normal kidney tissue sections were placed in an oven at 60℃ for 2 h. The kidney sections were deparaffinized in xylene solution, graded ethanol solution, and distilled water in the prescribed order. The sections were permeabilized with 0.5% Triton solution for 30 min and then washed 3 times with PBS for 5 min/time. Keeping the area around the sections as dry as possible, anti-ACSL4 (ab155282, 1:100, Abcam, UK) was added dropwise to the kidney tissue sections and incubated at 4℃ overnight. Kidney tissue sections were allowed to stand at room temperature for 30 min and then washed three times with PBS for 5 min/time. The secondary antibody (ab172730, 1:100, goat anti-rabbit IgG, Abcam, UK) was added dropwise and incubated in the dark for 1 h. The sections were washed 3 times with PBS for 5 min/time. Finally, the fluorescence quencher was placed on the kidney tissue sections, and the sealer was added dropwise. The sealed sections were observed under a fluorescence microscope.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism. Continuous data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD). Comparisons between two groups of continuous variables were conducted using an unpaired t-test. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Cell distribution of IgAN kidney tissues

Based on the top 30 PCs, 12 different cell subsets were obtained by annotation, including Mesangial cells (MEs), Endothelial cells (ECs), Podocytes (PODs), Proximal tubular cells (PTs), Distal tubular cells (DTCs), Loop of Henle cells (LOHs), Principal cells (PCs), Intercalated cells (ICs), Myeloid cells (MYCs), Fibroblasts (Fibs), Smooth muscle cells (SMCs), and Cycling cells (CCs) (Fig. 1A-B). Marker genes for each cell type are shown in Fig. 1C. The proportions of each cell type in the Control and IgAN groups are displayed in Fig. 1D-E. Notably, the proportion of LOHs was significantly higher in the IgAN group compared to the Control group, with marked variability observed among individual IgAN samples.

ACSL4 expression was significantly upregulated in LOHs

Differential gene expression analysis of each cell type between the IgAN and Control groups showed that the target gene ACSL4 was significantly upregulated in LOHs (p < 0.05, avg_log2FC = 1.75). Differences between the IgAN and Control groups in LOHs were analyzed, and ACSL4 expression was found to be significantly upregulated (Fig. 2A). LOHs were individually extracted and reclustered into subsets. Based on ACSL4 expression levels, LOHs were categorized into ACSL4+ LOHs and ACSL4− LOHs. Compared to the Control group, ACSL4+ LOHs was significantly high-expressed in the IgAN group (Fig. 2B-C). GO/KEGG enrichment analyses demonstrated that ACSL4+ LOHs were mainly enriched in the following KEGG signaling pathways: Biosynthesis of unsaturated fatty acids, Notch signaling pathway, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, and Ferroptosis (Fig. 2D).

Cell-cell communication

Analysis of cell-cell communication revealed that interactions between ACSL4⁺ LOHs and other cell types were significantly enhanced compared with ACSL4⁻ LOHs. Furthermore, communication between ACSL4+ LOHs and ECs, MEs, and PTCs was significantly enhanced in Interleukin-6 (IL6)- (IL6R+IL6ST) ligand-receptor pairs (Fig. 3).

The proportion of LOHs was elevated and ACSL4 expression was significantly increased in IgAN kidney tissues

In normal kidney tissues, the glomerular structure was unaltered. The renal interstitium showed no inflammatory cell infiltration and had clear margins, and glomerular mesangial cells did not proliferated. In contrast, IgAN kidney tissues exhibited increased proliferation of glomerular mesangial cells, expansion of mesangial stroma, and inflammatory cell infiltration within the renal interstitium. The proportion of LOHs was increased in IgAN kidney tissues (Fig. 4A). Masson staining revealed that the deposition of collagen fibers in IgA kidney tissues was increased compared with normal kidney tissues (Fig. 4B). Semi-quantitative analysis showed that the percentage area of collagen fibers in the IgAN group was significantly higher than that in the Control group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4D). Immunofluorescence analysis showed that ACSL4 expression was extremely low in LOHs of the Control group but markedly increased in LOHs of the IgAN group (Fig. 4C). Semi-quantitative analysis of the relative fluorescence intensity of ACSL4 revealed that its expression in LOHs of the IgAN group was higher than that in the Control group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4E).

Discussion

Unlike traditional cell death modes such as apoptosis, necrosis, and autophagy, ferroptosis is characterized by intracellular iron overload-induced oxidative stress. This process leads to lipid peroxidation, membrane rupture, and ultimately cell death13,14. In recent years, accumulating evidence has demonstrated that ferroptosis is closely associated with the development of kidney-related diseases, including acute kidney injury and renal fibrosis15–17. The mechanism of ferroptosis in kidney injury and its therapeutic applications provide novel insights into the treatment of IgAN.

To explore cellular differences between the IgAN and Control groups, 12 different cellular subsets were annotated by scRNA-seq analysis, including MEs, ECs, and LOHs, etc. The proportion of LOHs was significantly elevated in the IgAN group compared to the Control group. Differential gene expression analysis further revealed that ACSL4 expression was significantly upregulated in LOHs, whereas the expression of ferroptosis inhibitors GPX4 and GPX3 was downregulated18. These findings suggested that ferroptosis contributes to renal injury during IgAN progression and that ACSL4 may act as a risk factor for renal injury. However, specific mechanisms and related pathways of ACSL4-driven ferroptosis under IgAN progression remain to be clarified.

It has been demonstrated that GPX4 serves as an important central regulator of ferroptosis, and depletion of glutathione (GSH) will inactivate GPX4 and increase intracellular lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis. In addition, GPX4 expression is significantly decreased in IgAN kidney tissues compared to normal controls19. Martin-Sanchez et al.20 have found that ferroptosis is the main death mode of renal tubular epithelial cells in mouse models of acute kidney injury. Besides, renal function could be effectively improved in mice by inhibiting ferroptosis. Hu et al.21 have also demonstrated through assays that acute kidney injury can be attenuated by regulating GPX4 to inhibit ferroptosis. Collectively, these findings suggest that inhibiting the key site of ferroptosis could be a potential target for the treatment of kidney diseases. In the present study, ACSL4+ LOHs were highly expressed in the IgAN group and enriched in the Ferroptosis pathway. ACSL4 is a critical regulator of lipid metabolism during ferroptosis. There exists a state of oxidative stress in IgAN patients, with the manifestation of elevated ROS levels. ROS can attack polyunsaturated fatty acids on cell membranes and trigger lipid peroxidation, leading to the development of ferroptosis22,23. This study further demonstrated multiple ligand-receptor or signaling pathway-mediated intercellular communication between ACSL4+ LOHs and other cells. Notably, cellular communication between ACSL4+ LOHs and ECs, MEs, and PTCs was significantly enhanced in IL6-(IL6R+IL6ST) ligand-receptor pairs. Upon binding to its receptor IL6R and signal transducer IL6ST (gp130), IL6 can activate multiple signaling pathways, including the JAK/STAT signaling pathway, MAPK signaling pathway, and PI3K signaling pathway. A study by Wrighting et al.24 has revealed that IL-6 participates in the progression of ferroptosis by directly regulating hepcidin through STAT3 induction and promoter binding. In turn, the activation of these pathways will trigger the regulation of immune and inflammatory responses in IgAN patients and activate the intracellular oxidative stress pathway, thereby promoting the development of ferroptosis25,26.

Based on the scRNA-seq analysis results, we aim to further identify potential key cellular subpopulations and pathways involved in the disease, immunofluorescence revealed that the proportion of LOHs was elevated in the IgAN group compared with the Control group, and ACSL4 expression was significantly increased in LOHs. These findings suggested that the increased ACSL4 expression may serve as an indicator of disease progression. The role of ACSL4 in IgAN therapy and its potential as a therapeutic target can be further explored in future studies.

There are still some limitations in this study. The reliance on a single control sample may affect the reliability of the results. In subsequent studies, we will enlarge the number of control samples and reanalyze the data to ensure the accuracy of the conclusions. In addition, this study is primarily based on bioinformatics analysis and histological validation. Although the findings suggest that ACSL4+ LOHs may contribute to the progression of IgAN through ferroptosis and the IL-6 signaling pathway, functional gain or loss-of-function experiments (such as gene knockdown or overexpression) have not yet been conducted in cell or animal models to establish causality. Future studies are needed to experimentally clarify the specific functional role of ACSL4 in LOHs. Finally, the prediction and impact of ACSL4 on IgAN progression still require long-term follow-up data to clarify its value in disease prediction.

In summary, scRNA-seq analysis revealed that ACSL4⁺LOHs are highly expressed, promoting ferroptosis in IgAN and potentially contributing to its progression. Comparative analyses of cell proportions and ACSL4 expression in the IgAN group verified that increased ACSL4 expression may indicate the risk of disease progression. These findings may provide a reference for the mechanism involved in IgAN progression.

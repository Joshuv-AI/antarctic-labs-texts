An Assessment of Undergraduate Students’ Confidence Level in Performing Endodontic Procedures
Authors: Ahmed Altuwalah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjectives:This study assessed undergraduate dental students’ self-confidence in performing endodontic procedures and examined factors influencing their competence, including academic performance, year of study, and place of residence.Materials and Methods:A cross-sectional survey was conducted among dental students in Saudi Arabia using a structured questionnaire. Participants self-assessed their confidence levels in different endodontic procedures using a 5-point Likert scale. Statistical analysis included t-tests and ANOVA to evaluate associations between demographic variables and confidence levels (P < 0.05).Results:Students exhibited high confidence in simpler procedures, such as rubber dam placement (78.6%), but reported lower confidence in treating molars (33.3%) and handling interappointment flare-ups (51.6%). Higher GPA and clinical experience significantly improved confidence (P < 0.01). Urban students exhibited higher confidence than rural students.Conclusion:Enhanced curriculum modifications, simulation-based training, and clinical exposure to complex cases are essential to improve confidence levels and preparedness among dental students.

INTRODUCTION

Endodontic treatment is essential for preserving teeth affected by pulpal or periapical pathology, requiring technical expertise and clinical proficiency.[1] Dental students’ perception of endodontic procedures and their self-confidence play a crucial role in shaping their competence for independent practice.[2] The transition from preclinical to clinical training presents challenges, as confidence levels influence learning and performance.[34] Factors such as quality of training, faculty supervision, and clinical exposure contribute significantly to student preparedness.[56]

In Saudi Arabia, variations in dental curricula affect students’ confidence levels differently.[7] Studies have shown that students demonstrate high confidence in simpler tasks such as periapical radiographs but struggle with complex procedures such as broken instrument removal.[89] Understanding these factors is crucial for optimizing training. This study aims to evaluate self-confidence levels among Saudi dental students in endodontic procedures and identify influencing factors, providing insights for improving curricula and clinical exposure.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This cross-sectional study assessed dental students’ self-confidence in performing endodontic procedures in Saudi Arabia. Data were collected through an online questionnaire targeting undergraduate students from Majmaah University, Qassim University, and Prince Sattam bin Abdulaziz University. The survey consisted of three sections: demographic details (sex, academic year, GPA, and residence), self-confidence in different stages of endodontic treatment, and confidence in managing various endodontic indications. A 5-point Likert scale (1 = very little confidence, 5 = very confident) was used.

Participation was voluntary, with informed consent obtained before access. Ethical approval was granted by the Institutional Review Board (MUREC-Nov.04/COM-2024/63). Data analysis was performed using SPSS version 26, using descriptive statistics, independent t-tests, and one-way ANOVA to assess associations between confidence levels and demographic variables. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

RESULTS

Confidence levels in performing endodontic treatment on different tooth types

The study revealed that undergraduate dental students demonstrated high confidence in performing endodontic procedures on maxillary anterior teeth (88.9%), while the lowest confidence was observed for maxillary molars (33.3%). The analysis showed a significant association between GPA and confidence in treating molars (P < 0.01), with students scoring ≥4.5 GPA reporting higher confidence levels than those with lower GPAs. In addition, year of study significantly affected confidence (P = 0.022), as interns exhibited greater confidence than fourth-year students. These findings highlight the need for increased clinical exposure to posterior teeth to bridge confidence gaps in treating molars [Table 1].

Confidence in different stages of endodontic treatment

Among various endodontic procedures, rubber dam placement had the highest confidence (78.6%), whereas interappointment flare-up management had the lowest (51.6%). A significant correlation was found between GPA and confidence across all procedures (P < 0.01), indicating that higher-performing students exhibited greater confidence. DISCUSSION

This study assessed the confidence levels of undergraduate dental students in performing endodontic procedures and identified factors influencing their self-perceived competence. Findings indicate that students feel most confident performing simpler procedures, such as treating maxillary anterior teeth and rubber dam placement, while confidence is lower in managing complex cases such as maxillary molars and interappointment flare-ups. These results are consistent with previous studies that have identified a higher comfort level with anterior teeth due to better visibility and access, while posterior teeth pose greater anatomical and technical challenges.[123]

A significant finding of this study is the impact of academic performance on self-confidence. Students with higher GPAs (>4.5) exhibited significantly greater confidence across all endodontic procedures, including root canal cleaning and shaping, obturation, and managing endodontic flare-ups. This aligns with previous research suggesting that students with better academic performance often have stronger theoretical knowledge, which translates into increased practical confidence.[456] It underscores the importance of integrating both theoretical and hands-on clinical training to enhance students’ readiness for independent practice.

The year of study also influenced confidence levels, with interns reporting significantly higher confidence than fourth-year students in treating molars and managing complex cases. This highlights the importance of clinical exposure and experience in boosting confidence. Previous research has shown that increased hands-on practice improves students’ decision-making skills and technical proficiency in endodontics.[78] To enhance preparedness, curriculum improvements should focus on early clinical exposure, case-based learning, and simulation-based training to help students develop skills before transitioning into real-world clinical settings.

Interestingly, place of residence also impacted confidence levels, with urban students demonstrating higher self-perceived confidence than their rural counterparts. This disparity may stem from differences in educational exposure, access to resources, and the availability of clinical training facilities in urban versus rural settings. A study by Hossain et al. similarly found that urban students reported greater confidence in performing complex procedures, emphasizing the need to bridge educational inequalities between urban and rural institutions.[910]

To address these challenges, dental education programs should integrate advanced teaching methods, such as digital simulations, competency-based training, and problem-based learning, ensuring that all students, regardless of academic performance or background, receive equal opportunities for skill development. Enhancing practical exposure, especially in managing complex endodontic cases, is crucial to producing confident and competent dental practitioners ready for clinical practice.[1112]

CONCLUSION

This study highlights key factors influencing undergraduate dental students’ confidence in performing endodontic procedures. Students exhibited the highest confidence in routine tasks such as rubber dam placement and maxillary anterior teeth treatment but lacked confidence in managing molars and complex cases. Higher GPA and clinical experience positively influenced self-confidence, emphasizing the need for early exposure to advanced cases. Urban students demonstrated greater confidence than rural students, indicating educational disparities. Enhancing dental curricula with advanced training tools, simulation-based learning, and hands-on experience is essential to bridge these gaps and improve students’ confidence, ensuring better preparedness for clinical practice.

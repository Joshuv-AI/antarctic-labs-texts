Multi-agent collaborative pathways for Chinese traditional architectural image generation
Authors: Yi Lu, Wenmiao Yuan, Mengyao Wang, Pohsun Wang, Sifan Wu, Jiacheng Wu, Wenzhuo Xing, Wanxu Xie, Fuze Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Artificial Intelligence Generated Content (AIGC) technology demonstrates significant potential in the fields of cultural heritage digitalization and cultural tourism design. However, when confronted with specific subjects such as Chinese traditional architecture, which embodies rich cultural connotations and complex visual elements, existing technologies still exhibit limitations in understanding vague user requirements, ensuring cultural accuracy, and generating diverse and high-fidelity images. To address these challenges, this study proposes a text-to-image generation framework oriented towards Chinese traditional architecture, based on multi-agent collaboration. This framework integrates multiple intelligent agents responsible for user intent understanding, creative prompt generation, traditional architectural image generation, aesthetic and cultural relevance assessment, and collaborative workflow scheduling. By constructing a Chinese Traditional Architecture Cultural Knowledge Base (exemplified by the Beijing Central Axis) and designing a collaborative workflow among the agents, the framework can efficiently and accurately transform users’ colloquial and vague descriptions into Chinese traditional architectural images with profound cultural connotations and high visual fidelity. Experimental results demonstrate that this collaborative multi-agent framework significantly outperforms baseline models in terms of handling vague inputs, ensuring the cultural accuracy of generated images, enhancing user intent matching, and creative diversity. This research not only provides new theoretical perspectives and practical pathways for AI technology in the digital preservation, creative transformation, and intelligent design of cultural heritage, but also offers an effective tool for empowering innovative digital cultural tourism experiences, holding significance for technological exploration and cultural inheritance.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-18130-7.

Introduction

The rapid development of Artificial Intelligence Generated Content (AIGC) technology, particularly text-to-image multimodal generative models, is converging with the deepening trend of digital technology-driven cultural tourism (digital cultural tourism)1. This, in conjunction with national policy imperatives for the digitalization and inheritance of cultural heritage, collectively imposes higher standards on cultural heritage digitalization2. Chinese traditional architecture, with its profound cultural connotations, complex structural systems, and refined aesthetic paradigms, poses significant challenges for high-fidelity digital representation3. Furthermore, non-expert users often articulate their creative or design requirements in a vague and semantic manner, such as “a graceful spring scene of a Jiangnan garden,” which current technologies struggle to interpret accurately.

Classic multimodal generative models, predominantly represented by diffusion models such as Stable Diffusion, Midjourney, and DALL-E, have achieved remarkable success in general-purpose image generation4. This progress is significantly propelled by foundational technologies that bridge vision and language, such as CLIP, which learns rich joint embeddings. Building on this, the field has witnessed a paradigm shift towards integrated Visual Language Models (VLMs), including prominent examples like Flamingo, LLaVA, and OneLLM5,6. These models excel at understanding complex multimodal instructions and can engage in sophisticated visual dialogue. However, despite their impressive capabilities, their strength in general-purpose understanding is predicated on vast, non-specialized datasets. This means that while they can recognize or describe common objects in images with high accuracy, they inherently lack the deep, structured domain knowledge essential for specialized fields. Their understanding of concepts is largely implicit and correlational, rather than explicit and rule-based, which is precisely what is required for subjects like traditional architecture that are governed by strict typologies, symbolic systems, and construction principles6. Consequently, when dealing with such specific cultural themes, these state-of-the-art models often encounter the following bottlenecks: 1. Cultural Knowledge Deficit: Trained on vast, general-purpose datasets, these models lack deep, structured knowledge of specific cultural domains. This leads to an inability to accurately interpret culturally-laden terms (e.g., ‘dougong,’ ‘caisson ceiling’) and their symbolic significance, resulting in cultural understanding deviation and the generation of culturally incongruous content7. 2. Insufficient Controllability and Fidelity: The stochastic nature of the diffusion process, while fostering creativity, makes it difficult to precisely control architectural structures, component details, and stylistic conventions. This often leads to detail distortion, style confusion, and the generation of culturally or historically inaccurate artifacts, failing to capture the rigorous formal logic of traditional architecture8,9. 3. High Interaction Barriers: Effective generation often relies on complex ‘prompt engineering,’ requiring users to possess specialized knowledge to craft detailed, technical descriptions. This creates a significant barrier for non-expert users who are the primary audience in digital cultural tourism and creative applications10. These limitations reveal that a monolithic, single-model approach is insufficient for high-fidelity cultural heritage representation, highlighting the necessity for a more integrated and intelligent generation paradigm.

These limitations underscore the pressing need to develop more precise and intelligent generation strategies. This study posits that introducing Artificial Intelligence Agents (AI Agents) and establishing a collaborative operational model for Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) presents an effective pathway to address the aforementioned challenges. Through the collaboration of functionally specialized agents, it is anticipated that deep interpretation of vague user intentions, precise invocation of domain-specific cultural knowledge, fine-grained control over the generation process, and intelligent evaluation of outcomes can be achieved11,12.

Accordingly, the core research problem of this study is defined as: How to construct a Multi-Agent System (MAS) capable of effectively understanding users’ vague cultural imagery requirements concerning Chinese traditional architecture, collaboratively invoking domain knowledge and optimizing models, to ultimately generate high-quality, high-cultural-fidelity, diverse images that align with user intent. To investigate this question, this study sets the following objectives: 1. To design a text-to-image AI Agent framework based on multi-agent collaboration, oriented towards the generation of cultural imageries of Chinese traditional architecture, clarifying the roles, responsibilities, and collaborative mechanisms of its core intelligent agents; 2. To integrate and utilize a previously optimized Chinese traditional architectural image generation model to ensure the fidelity of cultural features; 3. To experimentally validate the effectiveness of this framework in handling vague inputs, ensuring cultural accuracy, and enhancing generation effects, using the Beijing Central Axis architectural complex as a case study, and to explore its application potential.

Multi-agent systems in the vertical domain of digital cultural tourism

Overview of multi-agent systems in the context of digital cultural tourism

The digitalization, preservation, and revitalization of Chinese traditional architecture are entering a new phase of digital cultural tourism characterized by in-depth experiences and intelligent interaction. The core objective of this phase is to transcend basic digital archiving and display, and to shift towards the precise interpretation and innovative representation of the profound cultural connotations, spatial philosophy, and aesthetic paradigms embodied by the architecture13. Although Artificial Intelligence has been preliminarily applied in aspects such as intelligent guidance, virtual restoration, and element recognition14,15, and has been reflected in the digitalization practices of important cultural heritage sites like the Beijing Central Axis16, existing methods still exhibit deficiencies in integrating complex knowledge, simulating design thinking, and achieving culture-driven intelligent generation, making it difficult to meet the growing demands of digital cultural tourism for high-fidelity, personalized, and immersive cultural experiences.

In this context, Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) offer a promising technological pathway. MAS, by integrating multiple intelligent agents (AI Agents) that possess capabilities for autonomous perception, reasoning, and action, address complex challenges through distributed collaboration17. Their core advantage lies in the ability to simulate collaborative work from multiple perspectives and integrate knowledge and rules from different domains—which aligns precisely with the multidisciplinary knowledge required for understanding and representing Chinese traditional architecture18. In recent years, the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) has significantly enhanced the reasoning, planning, and knowledge processing capabilities of Agents, enabling LLM-based MAS to exhibit significant advantages in tasks requiring deep understanding and complex collaboration17,19. These systems demonstrate the potential to achieve complex task decomposition, planning, and execution through inter-agent interaction (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Basic conceptual framework of an LLM-based intelligent agent (Image source: Lu Yu et al., 2024, Fig. 1)20.

However, applying MAS, particularly LLM-enhanced MAS, to the vertical domain of digital cultural tourism—especially for intelligent image generation, design assistance, and interactive experience construction targeting objects with distinct cultural specificity like Chinese traditional architecture—currently remains a frontier and underexplored research direction. How to design Agents capable of understanding and utilizing traditional construction wisdom, aesthetic principles, and cultural contexts, and how to construct effective MAS collaborative frameworks to support innovative digital content generation, are the key challenges this study aims to address.

Controllable image generation for traditional architecture

The core of applying AIGC to traditional architectural image generation lies in achieving controllability, i.e., generating images that conform to specific styles, structures, layouts, or details on demand21. To enhance controllability, existing research primarily focuses on two directions: first, strengthening the guiding capability of input information22; second, improving model architecture and training strategies to better adapt them to the characteristics of traditional architecture21. Despite some progress, controllable generation for traditional architecture still faces numerous issues, particularly in ensuring cultural accuracy, precision of architectural form, and historical authenticity.

Addressing this issue, our team’s prior research16 successfully constructed and validated an image generation model integrating style and structure control (SD LoRA & Canny), laying a core technical foundation for the present study. This research, by training a LoRA module on a dataset of ancient Chinese architecture, accurately captured and reproduced key cultural visual elements such as dougong (interlocking wooden brackets) and caihua (decorative painted patterns). Concurrently, by integrating Canny edge detection and ControlNet technology, it enforced constraints ensuring that the generated images adhere to the precise architectural contours and structural layout of reference images, thereby achieving dual precise control over the style and structure of ancient architecture. Empirical research results, exemplified by the Beijing Drum Tower (user evaluations and statistical analysis indicated ANOVA, p < 0.001), clearly demonstrated that the SD LoRA & Canny model significantly outperformed mainstream models such as DALL-E, MidJourney V4, and standard SD 1.5 in terms of the accuracy of architectural form, color elements, and decorative symbols. The generated images achieved high cultural fidelity, and their outputs have been successfully applied to the design of cultural tourism-related products, validating their practical application value (Fig. 2). Although this model exhibits superior generation performance, it still primarily relies on explicit instruction inputs (textual descriptions + edge images) and faces limitations when processing generation tasks that involve only vague intentions or require the integration of diverse knowledge for complex reasoning. Therefore, building upon this prior achievement, the present study proposes to introduce a Multi-Agent System (MAS) framework, exploring the realization of higher-level knowledge-driven processes, intent understanding, and automated generation workflows through inter-agent collaboration. This approach aims to overcome the limitations of a single generation engine, meet the demand for more intelligent and flexible generation of Chinese traditional architectural images, validate this through the case of the Beijing Central Axis, and serve digital cultural tourism and cultural creative design.Fig. 2Comparison of ‘Beijing Drum Tower’ image results generated by artificial intelligence tools (Image source: Lu Y et al., 2025, Fig. 6)16.

Construction of an intelligent agent framework for generating cultural imageries of Chinese traditional architecture

Overall framework design

To achieve end-to-end intelligent generation from vague user text inputs to high-quality Chinese traditional architectural images with profound cultural connotations, this study designs a multi-agent system framework composed of five core intelligent agents and one collaborative scheduling agent (as shown in Fig. 1, which is here specified for Chinese traditional architecture). This framework adheres to a modular design, where each intelligent agent has clearly defined responsibilities, and information interaction and process control are managed by the collaborative scheduling agent. It relies on a Chinese Traditional Architecture Cultural Knowledge Base to provide domain knowledge support. The framework commences with user requirements and, through the concatenation and collaboration of stages such as intent understanding, prompt generation, image generation, and quality assessment (the intelligent generation pathway), ultimately outputs image results that satisfy user needs and are culturally accurate.

Construction of the Chinese traditional architecture cultural knowledge base (Beijing central axis)

To ensure the accuracy and depth of cultural connotations in AI-generated images, this study constructs a specialized cultural knowledge base for Chinese traditional architecture, with a deep characterization focusing on the Beijing Central Axis architectural complex as a key example (Fig. 3). This knowledge base serves as the core support for the entire intelligent agent system. The construction of the knowledge base follows knowledge engineering methodology and is a systematic endeavor integrating multi-source heterogeneous data, fine-grained knowledge extraction, structured representation, and continuous iterative validation:Fig. 3Knowledge Organization and Association within the Chinese Traditional Architecture Cultural Knowledge Base(Beijing Central Axis): The Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests, Temple of Heaven Section (Image source: Author-created).

Data Sources

The knowledge base is founded on the collection and integration of multi-source heterogeneous data to ensure comprehensive coverage. The primary data sources include: (1) Classical Literature and Official Archives: Core texts such as the Song Dynasty’s Yingzao Fashi (State Building Standards) and the Qing Dynasty’s Gongcheng Zuofa Zeli (Engineering Practice Regulations) were digitized using OCR and layout analysis to extract architectural regulations, modular systems, and terminology. (2) Modern Academic Research: Monographs, journal articles, survey drawings, and archaeological reports on architectural history were processed to extract key arguments and data. (3) High-Quality Visual Datasets: A curated collection of high-resolution historical and contemporary photographs, alongside digital restoration models of the Beijing Central Axis, provided critical visual references. (4) Local Gazetteers and Cultural Classics: These sources supplemented the knowledge base with information on the cultural background, historical evolution, and symbolic significance of specific buildings.

Natural language processing (NLP) techniques and extraction methods

A hybrid approach was employed to extract structured knowledge from the processed data. This involved two primary methods: (1) Rule-based and Dictionary-based Extraction: Leveraging a pre-compiled glossary of ancient architectural terminology, Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques—including tokenization, Part-of-Speech (POS) tagging, and Named Entity Recognition (NER)—were used to automatically identify core entities and their attributes. These entities encompass building types (e.g., palaces, altars), components (e.g., dougong), decorative motifs (e.g., dragon and phoenix motifs), color systems, and materials. (2) Machine Learning-based Relation Extraction: Pre-trained language models (e.g., BERT) were fine-tuned on domain-specific corpora to extract complex relationships between entities, such as hierarchical (‘is-a’), compositional (‘part-of’), stylistic (‘has-style’), and symbolic (‘symbolizes’) relations.

Ontology modeling

The extracted entities and relations were organized into a formal, structured knowledge graph using ontology engineering methods. This process involved defining a conceptual model for Chinese traditional architecture, which specifies core concepts, their hierarchical relationships, attribute characteristics, and rule constraints. For the Beijing Central Axis, this modeling was particularly in-depth, aiming to capture not only precise physical attributes (e.g., form, scale, materials) but also abstract cultural knowledge. Key aspects of this in-depth modeling include: (1) Encoding Spatial Order and Ritual Regulations: Principles such as Qian Chao Hou Qin (front court, rear residence) were represented by defining spatial relation predicates (e.g., isNorthOf, isAxiallyAlignedWith) and ritual attributes. (2) Linking Form to Cultural Symbolism: Explicit semantic associations were established between morphological features (e.g., roof types, colors) and the cultural connotations they embody, such as imperial power and cosmological concepts. (3) Documenting Historical Evolution: A versioned approach was used to record the formal changes and functional successions of key buildings across different historical periods.

Application interfaces and update mechanism

The knowledge base is designed for dynamic interaction with the Multi-Agent System (MAS) and supports continuous evolution. It provides standardized application interfaces (e.g., SPARQL endpoints, APIs) to empower each intelligent agent. The User Intent Understanding Agent (Agent 1) invokes the knowledge base for semantic mapping; the Creative Prompt Generation Agent (Agent 2) queries it for precise cultural terminology and constraints; and the Aesthetic and Cultural Relevance Assessment Agent (Agent 4) utilizes it as an authoritative benchmark for validation. This end-to-end knowledge-driven architecture ensures cultural fidelity throughout the generation pipeline. Furthermore, the knowledge base is designed to be extensible, supporting continuous updates by integrating new research findings and expert feedback to maintain its authoritativeness and timeliness.

The Chinese Traditional Architecture Cultural Knowledge Base (Beijing Central Axis), constructed through the aforementioned methods, provides a solid knowledge foundation and cultural benchmark for the image generation of this MAS, and is a key assurance for achieving the precise transformation from user intent to high-fidelity visual representations imbued with rich cultural connotations.

Multi-agent collaborative system design and functionality

This MAS is holistically coordinated by the Workflow Collaborative Scheduling Agent (Agent 5). The process commences with the User Intent Understanding and Semantic Mapping Agent (Agent 1), which utilizes Natural Language Understanding (NLU) technology to parse user input, extract core intent, emotions, and architecture-related entities. Crucially, it queries the Chinese Traditional Architecture Cultural Knowledge Base to perform precise semantic mapping of these entities to cultural elements within the knowledge base (such as regulations, styles, components, and color systems), outputting a structured representation of user requirements. This representation subsequently drives the Creative Prompt Generation and Optimization Agent (Agent 2). Utilizing KTransformers-optimized open-source Deepseek-V3 LLMs23 and Prompt Engineering, this agent transforms semantic labels into high-quality textual prompts. These prompts are optimized for downstream generation models with detailed visual descriptions and style instructions. The core generation task is executed by the Chinese Traditional Architectural Image Generation Agent (Agent 3), which receives the optimized prompts and invokes a Stable Diffusion LoRA model specifically fine-tuned for Chinese traditional architecture (e.g., palaces of the Beijing Central Axis) along with Canny ControlNet for precise contour control. This combination leverages the cultural style details injected by specific LoRA weights and the structural constraints provided by ControlNet to generate initial images that conform to cultural characteristics. The generated images are then subjected to rigorous evaluation by the Aesthetic and Cultural Relevance Assessment Agent (Agent 4). This agent integrates Visual Language Models (VLMs), aesthetic scoring models, and, by referencing the knowledge base, rigorously validates the images’ performance in terms of aesthetic metrics, intent conformity, and the accuracy of cultural elements (form, color, structure, decoration, etc.), screening or flagging images that do not meet standards or exhibit cultural misrepresentations. Finally, the Workflow Collaborative Scheduling Agent (Agent 5), based on the evaluation results from Agent 4, decides whether to output the final image or initiate iterative optimization (e.g., returning to Agent 2 or Agent 3 for parameter adjustment). The entire process manifests as a knowledge-driven, progressively refined, and closed-loop evaluative collaborative pathway, ensuring the high cultural accuracy and visual expressiveness of the final generated results (Fig. 4).Fig. 4Multi-agent framework for generating cultural imageries of Chinese traditional architecture (Image source: Author-created).

To elucidate the framework’s coordination logic, the detailed collaborative workflow is presented in Fig. 5. This workflow is orchestrated by the Workflow Collaborative Scheduling Agent (Agent 5), which acts as the central controller.Initially, the user’s vague prompt is processed sequentially by Agent 1 (User Intent Understanding Agent), Agent 2 (Creative Prompt Generation Agent), and Agent 3 (Chinese Traditional Architectural Image Generation Agent) to produce an image. This output is then passed to Agent 4 (Aesthetic and Cultural Relevance Assessment Agent), which generates a detailed Evaluation Report containing scores and specific error flags (e.g., “Incorrect roof style,” “Symbolism mismatch,” “Major deviation from user intent”).At this critical juncture, Agent 5 assumes control, governing the workflow with a clear decision-making logic. It first validates the image by comparing the Evaluation Report’s scores against a predefined quality threshold. If the image passes, the workflow concludes. If it fails, Agent 5 triggers an iterative optimization loop by diagnosing the root cause from the error flags. Based on the diagnosis, it performs targeted rerouting: for failures of a semantic or cultural nature, the task is returned to Agent 2 with feedback to refine the prompt; for issues related to generation artifacts or aesthetics, the task is sent back to Agent 3 with instructions to regenerate using adjusted parameters. Crucially, if the assessment reveals a fundamental misalignment with the user’s core intent, indicating a failure in the initial understanding, Agent 5 will route the task all the way back to Agent 1 for a complete re-interpretation of the original input. To prevent infinite loops and ensure computational efficiency, the number of iterations is capped at a predefined maximum. This multi-level, feedback-driven collaboration is key to transforming vague inputs into culturally precise and high-quality images, moving beyond a simple linear pipeline to a truly intelligent and self-correcting system.Fig. 5Detailed Collaborative Workflow and Iterative Optimization Loop of the Multi-Agent System (Image source: Author-created).

Regarding user interaction, users merely need to input their design concepts or requirements for specific traditional architectural images in natural language via a prompt input box within the user interface. The multi-agent system then automatically executes the aforementioned complex collaborative generation and evaluation processes, ultimately presenting the user with high-quality images that meet their requirements and have been validated for cultural and aesthetic integrity. In practical use, users can conveniently utilize this system for creative exploration and visual expression without needing in-depth understanding of the underlying technical details.

Application examples and effect evaluation based on ancient architecture of the Beijing central axis

Experimental design and evaluation methods

To empirically validate the effectiveness of the proposed multi-agent collaborative framework in the task of Chinese traditional architectural image generation, this study designed and implemented a series of comparative experiments, taking the Beijing Central Axis as a case study. The core objective of these experiments was to evaluate the performance advantages of this framework over current mainstream image generation models and optimized baselines in terms of handling vague user inputs, ensuring cultural accuracy, and enhancing generation quality. The core of the experiment is an operational software tool prototype (hereinafter referred to as the “MAS tool prototype”) constructed based on the MAS framework presented earlier. It integrates intelligent agents for user intent understanding, creative prompt generation, core image generation, evaluation and validation, and workflow scheduling, and critically relies on a specially constructed backend “Chinese Traditional Architecture Cultural Knowledge Base (Beijing Central Axis) ” to provide domain knowledge support. Users input natural language descriptions via a concise web interface, and the MAS tool prototype automatically executes the complete agent collaborative workflow, outputting images that have undergone dual cultural and aesthetic validation.

To comprehensively evaluate the MAS tool prototype, the experiment established five comparative baselines: first, DALL-E 324 and Midjourney v625 (Baseline 1 & 2), representing the current state-of-the-art in general-purpose AIGC; second, an optimized generation engine baseline (Baseline 3) designed to isolate the impact of the intelligent agents within the MAS framework, excluding the core generation engine itself. This baseline utilized the same SD LoRA & Canny model, but its input consisted of optimized prompts meticulously crafted by domain experts based on the original vague requirements, simulating current best practices that rely on “expert tuning.” Additionally, Flamingo5 and LLaVA6 were established as Baseline 4 and Baseline 5, chosen as they represent mature multimodal generative models and agent-like collaborative paradigms. A consistent set of vague natural language prompts describing different buildings on the Beijing Central Axis in specific contexts (e.g., “the majestic Hall of Supreme Harmony in the Forbidden City after a snowfall”) was used as input for all models, aimed at simulating typical scenarios where ordinary users lack professional terminology but have clear cultural imagery needs.

The evaluation employed a combination of subjective and objective methods. To ensure a robust and comprehensive evaluation, the 60 participants were deliberately recruited from three distinct groups to represent a spectrum of expertise: 20 experts (including architectural historians, conservation architects, and digital heritage scholars), 20 students (university students majoring in architecture, digital media art, and design), and 20 general users (individuals with no professional background in architecture or design but with a declared interest in cultural heritage). Recruitment was conducted through a combination of purposive sampling for experts (via professional networks and academic invitations) and volunteer sampling for students and the general public (via university announcements). Prior to the evaluation, all participants attended a briefing session where the study’s objectives were outlined. To ensure a shared and informed understanding of the assessment criteria, each participant was provided with a detailed evaluation guide (for details, please refer to Section “Multi-agent systems in the vertical domain of digital cultural tourism”. Supplementary Methods: Subjective Evaluation Protocol in the supplementary material document “Supplementary Information’). This guide explicitly defined the three evaluation dimensions—Accuracy of Architectural Form (Qform), Fidelity of Color Elements (Qcolor), and Consistency of Decorative Symbols (Qsymbol)—and included a comprehensive scoring rubric with descriptive anchors for each point on the 5-point Likert scale, thereby standardizing the basis for their judgments. Participants evaluated anonymized images generated by different models using a pre-tested (for reliability and validity) five-point Likert scale26(1 = very inconsistent, 5 = very consistent), independently rating them on three core dimensions: accuracy of architectural form (Qform), fidelity of color elements (Qcolor), and consistency of decorative symbols (Qsymbol). Results analysis and comparative discussion

Experimental results quantitatively demonstrated the significant advantages of the MAS tool prototype in terms of generation quality and cultural fidelity. Subjective rating data showed that the average scores of the MAS tool prototype across the three evaluation dimensions (accuracy of architectural form 4.65 ± 0.25, fidelity of color elements 4.72 ± 0.20, consistency of decorative symbols 4.78 ± 0.22) were statistically significantly superior to those of the general-purpose models, and, compared to the equally high-performing optimized baseline model (Baseline 3), exhibited further improvement (p < 0.001).

Specifically, compared to the optimized baseline model (Baseline 3, with scores of architectural form 4.50 ± 0.28, color elements 4.58 ± 0.26, and decorative symbols 4.60 ± 0.29), the MAS tool prototype improved by approximately 0.18 points (approximately 3.91%) in consistency of decorative symbols, and by approximately 0.15 points and 0.14 points (approximately 3.33% and 3.16%) in accuracy of architectural form and fidelity of color elements, respectively. These data indicate that although the expert-optimized single model (Baseline 3), by virtue of employing the image generation model integrating style and structure control (SD LoRA & Canny) from the team’s prior research, could achieve a very high standard, the MAS framework, through the precise collaboration of multi-agents—particularly the deep integration of the knowledge base, automated generation of high-quality prompts, and a rigorous closed-loop cultural validation process—can achieve further performance optimization and precision in cultural expression building upon this high standard.

Compared to the other baseline models, the advantages of both the MAS tool prototype and Baseline 3 were even more pronounced. For instance, on the dimension of consistency of decorative symbols, the scores for the MAS tool prototype (4.71 ± 0.22) and Baseline 3 (4.61 ± 0.29) were substantially higher than those of LLaVA (3.51 ± 0.43), Flamingo (3.42 ± 0.82), Midjourney v6 (3.40 ± 0.93), and DALL-E 3 (3.30 ± 0.98). Among the other baselines, the multimodal models LLaVA and Flamingo showed a slight performance edge over the general-purpose AIGC tools, with LLaVA achieving the highest scores in this group for accuracy of architectural form (3.89 ± 0.72) and fidelity of color elements (3.82 ± 0.77). Nevertheless, all baseline models exhibited evident deficiencies in understanding and reproducing cultural details. The standard deviations of the scores for the MAS tool prototype and Baseline 3 were generally smaller, indicating relatively higher accuracy and better stability in their output results. A comprehensive analysis of these quantitative data clearly reveals the core advantages of this MAS tool prototype: it not only overcomes the limitations of general-purpose models in understanding knowledge and expressing details within specific cultural domains, but also, through the precise guidance of the integrated knowledge base, the automated generation of high-quality prompts, and rigorous cultural validation stages, achieves performance surpassing that of the expert-manually-optimized baseline model (Baseline 3). This demonstrates that multi-agent collaboration, particularly knowledge-base-driven validation mechanisms, is crucial for ensuring the cultural accuracy of generated results and enhancing overall quality, and can successfully encapsulate this professional capability within an easy-to-use tool.

Computational performance and system configuration

Application practices and value transformation

Based on the validated performance advantages and user-friendliness, the MAS framework and its tool prototype proposed in this study hold broad application prospects in the field of cultural heritage. This framework and tool can be integrated into digital cultural tourism platforms to provide personalized and culturally accurate visualizations of historical scenes; serve the cultural and creative industries as an efficient and reliable tool for inspiration and material generation; assist in the digital preservation and research of cultural heritage by generating diverse visual archive materials; and support the customization of personalized cultural and creative products, bridging traditional culture with modern consumption. The research team has preliminarily validated these applications through a series of translational practices, ranging from tangible cultural and creative products to virtual experiences, clearly demonstrating its practical enabling role in the digital inheritance and innovative design of cultural heritage16.

In the development of tangible cultural and creative products, this framework has been applied to the design of Beijing Bell and Drum Tower themed merchandise (such as refrigerator magnets and postcards)16. The MAS prototype can efficiently transform vague cultural imageries into culturally precise and aesthetically pleasing design proposals. Through knowledge-base-driven processes and agent collaboration, it ensures the accurate representation of architectural elements in derivative products, achieving efficient and high-fidelity materialization and dissemination. Conclusion

Summary of contributions and core advantages

Addressing the challenges of cultural understanding deviation and detail distortion faced by general-purpose AIGC when processing specific cultural themes such as Chinese traditional architecture, this study successfully constructed and validated a multi-agent collaborative framework (MAS). This framework, by integrating intelligent agents for intent understanding, prompt optimization, domain-specific model generation, and cultural and aesthetic assessment, and relying on a Chinese traditional architecture knowledge base, achieved the effective generation of high-fidelity, culturally accurate images from vague user inputs. Experiments demonstrated that this MAS significantly outperforms baseline models in ensuring the accuracy of cultural elements, matching user intent, and lowering operational barriers. Its core advantages lie in its specialized division of labor, knowledge-driven validation, and efficient closed-loop collaboration, providing a robust design paradigm for AIGC applications in specific cultural domains.

Ethical considerations and limitations of cultural representation

While the MAS framework demonstrates a significant advancement in cultural fidelity, it is crucial to acknowledge the inherent ethical risks and limitations associated with AI-driven cultural representation. The generation of culturally inaccurate or inappropriate content—such as the confusion of symbolic motifs (e.g., misplacing imperial symbols on commoner structures), the distortion of architectural hierarchies, or the perpetuation of aesthetic stereotypes—remains a tangible risk. Our knowledge-base-driven Assessment Agent (Agent 4) is designed as a primary mitigation strategy against such factual and semantic errors. However, this automated validation has its limits. A knowledge base, no matter how comprehensive, is a finite representation and may inherit biases from its source materials. Furthermore, the AI’s “understanding” is fundamentally semantic and structural, lacking the deep, nuanced grasp of philosophical, spiritual, and historical connotations that defines true cultural competence.

Therefore, this tool should be viewed as a powerful assistant for creative exploration and design, not as an autonomous and infallible creator of cultural artifacts. For applications requiring stringent scholarly or curatorial accuracy, such as in academic publications or museum exhibits, expert human oversight remains non-negotiable. The responsible use of this technology necessitates a human-in-the-loop approach, where the system empowers human expertise rather than replacing it. This ensures that the generated content is not only technically accurate but also culturally respectful, avoiding the digital misappropriation or trivialization of heritage. This careful positioning is essential for navigating the complex ethical landscape surrounding AI-generated content, including issues of originality, copyright, and the authentic representation of culture27,28.

Future work

Building on these insights, future research will focus on addressing the current limitations and expanding the framework’s capabilities. A primary objective is the continuous enrichment of the knowledge system and the validation of the framework’s generalizability. A key direction for this validation is the systematic application of the MAS framework to other, distinctly different Chinese architectural typologies. This will require extending the proposed methodology to construct specialized knowledge bases and fine-tune generation models for styles such as the intricate and landscape-integrated Jiangnan gardens, the unique structural and symbolic systems of Tibetan temples, and the diverse materiality of vernacular dwellings. Such an expansion will serve to demonstrate the framework’s robustness while simultaneously enhancing the AI’s capabilities in cultural interpretation and aesthetic assessment, moving beyond rule-based validation towards more nuanced, context-aware evaluation models. On the technical front, further research will aim to enhance overall system performance. This includes optimizing the collaborative efficiency between agents and, critically, the computational performance of the agents themselves, particularly by refining the LLM inference process. Moreover, leveraging higher-performance hardware, such as enterprise-grade multi-GPU servers, will be crucial not only to significantly reduce generation latency but also to enable exploration into more computationally intensive modalities like 3D models and immersive environments. Rigorous practical application testing and iterative refinement in real-world digital cultural tourism and educational scenarios will be crucial for advancing the technology’s maturity and ensuring its responsible and impactful deployment in the preservation and innovative inheritance of cultural heritage.

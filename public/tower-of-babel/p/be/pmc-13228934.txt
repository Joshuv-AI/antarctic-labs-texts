Immune correlates analysis in NextCOVE trial for a next-generation mRNA-1283 COVID-19 vaccine
Authors: Chong Ma, Jing Feng, Rahnuma Wahid, Spyros Chalkias, Darin Edwards, Bethany Girard, Rituparna Das, Honghong Zhou, Lingyi Zheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTSARS-CoV-2 neutralizing antibodies (nAbs) are strongly inversely associated with COVID-19 risk and have correlated with vaccine efficacy across multiple clinical trials conducted during the COVID-19 pandemic. As SARS-CoV-2 continues to evolve in the endemic era, it is important to determine whether nAb levels remain a valid statistical correlate of protection and potential surrogate endpoints for emerging variants. The next-generation COVID-19 vaccine, mRNA-1283, has recently received regulatory approval worldwide. In the Phase 3 NextCOVE trial (NCT05815498), which enrolled more than 10,000 participants aged ≥12 y, mRNA-1283 (10 µg; bivalent ancestral plus Omicron BA.4/BA.5) met the non-inferiority criterion for relative vaccine efficacy (rVE) versus mRNA-1273 (50 µg; bivalent) and elicited higher nAb responses. In this correlate analysis of the NextCOVE trial, we evaluated Day 29 nAbs against ancestral D614G, BA.4/BA.5, and XBB.1.5 variants as correlates of risk (CoRs) and correlates of protection (CoPs) for COVID-19 after vaccination with mRNA-1283 or mRNA-1273. Across multiple statistical frameworks, higher Day 29 nAb levels were consistently associated with lower COVID-19 risk and higher estimated rVE. The estimated hazard ratios per 10-fold increase in Day 29 nAbs against D614G, BA.4/BA.5, and XBB.1.5 were 0.70 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.48–1.02; p = .064), 0.49 (95% CI: 0.41–0.58; p < .001), and 0.41 (95% CI: 0.35–0.49; p < .001), respectively. These findings indicate that nAb levels, particularly against variants antigenically similar to circulating strains, remain associated with stronger contemporaneous protection against COVID-19.

Introduction

Neutralizing antibodies (nAbs) have consistently been shown to be strongly inversely associated with COVID-19 risk and statistically correlated with vaccine efficacy (VE) in randomized placebo-controlled clinical trials across multiple vaccine platforms.1–6 Higher nAb levels have therefore been linked with lower risk of COVID-19 and greater protection.1–6 During 2020 and 2021, the ancestral SARS-CoV-2 variant (D614G) underwent continuous evolution, giving rise to multiple variants of concern, including Alpha (B.1.1.7), Beta (B.1.351), Gamma (P.1), and Delta (B.1.617.2). These variants demonstrated increased transmissibility and varying degrees of immune escape, resulting in substantial global morbidity and mortality.7 Following the emergence of the Omicron variant in late 2021, SARS-CoV-2 evolution has been dominated by diversification within Omicron sublineages. Major Omicron sublineages have included BA.1 (late 2021), BA.2 (early 2022), BA.4/BA.5 (fall 2022), XBB/XBB.1.5 (2023), JN.1 (fall 2024), and LP.8.1 (fall 2025).8,9 Although vaccines based on the ancestral D614G variant have continued to provide strong protection against severe disease and death, protection against overall COVID-19 has declined as circulating variants become increasingly antigenically distant from vaccine strains.10

To address ongoing viral evolution and waning protection against infection, variant-adapted COVID-19 vaccines matched to circulating strains have been recommended since fall 2022, largely on the basis of nAb responses.11–14 These updated vaccines have helped maintain population-level protection, especially against severe outcomes. However, recent evidence suggests that XBB.1.5-adapted vaccines provide greater protection against XBB sublineages than against later variants such as JN.1, underscoring the importance of periodically updating COVID-19 vaccines to match circulating strains.15 In this setting of ongoing antigenic drift within Omicron, it remains important to determine whether nAbs continue to function as a valid statistical correlate of protection (CoP) against COVID-19 in the endemic era.

mRNA-1283 (mNEXSPIKE; Moderna, Inc.) is a novel, lipid-encapsulated, messenger ribonucleic acid (mRNA) vaccine encoding the N-terminal domain and receptor-binding domain of the SARS-CoV-2 spike (S) glycoprotein. NextCOVE (NCT05815498) was a Phase 3, randomized, observer-blind, active-controlled pivotal study in participants aged ≥12 y, that evaluated the safety, immunogenicity, and relative vaccine efficacy (rVE) of mRNA-1283 compared with mRNA-1273 (Spikevax; Moderna, Inc.). Both vaccines encoded the same bivalent antigens: ancestral D614G plus Omicron BA.4/BA.5. The trial showed an rVE of 9.3% (99.4% confidence interval [CI]: −6.6% to 22.8%; p = .0005) for mRNA-1283 versus mRNA-1273, meeting the predefined non-inferiority criterion (lower bound of CI > −10%). mRNA-1283 also met immunogenicity non-inferiority criteria compared with mRNA-1273 for both the geometric mean ratio (GMR) using a non-inferiority margin of 1.5 (lower bound of 95% CI > 1/1.5) and seroresponse rate difference ruling out −10% (lower bound of 95% CI > −10%).16

This paper presents the immune correlates analysis from the NextCOVE trial. The primary objective was to evaluate the peak post-vaccination nAb responses at Day 29, measured with validated SARS-CoV-2 pseudovirus neutralization assay17 as a statistical CoP for mRNA-based COVID-19 vaccines in the endemic era. Associations between variant-specific nAb levels and COVID-19 risk were assessed using Cox proportional hazards models that accounted for the case-cohort immunogenicity design and adjusted for baseline risk characteristics. Multiple causal inference frameworks,18 including controlled VE,19 mediation of VE,20 and stochastic interventional VE,21,22 were applied to evaluate Day 29 nAbs against D614G, BA.4/BA.5, and XBB.1.5 as correlates of risk (CoRs) and CoPs for the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)-defined, variant-agnostic COVID-19 endpoint.

Materials and methods

NextCOVE trial

Between March 28 and August 23, 2023, 11,454 participants aged ≥ 12 y were randomized (1:1) to receive a single dose of either the bivalent formulation of mRNA-1283 (10 µg; ancestral plus Omicron BA.4/BA.5) or bivalent mRNA-1273 (50 µg). Serum samples were collected at baseline (Day 1, pre-vaccination) and on Day 29 (peak post-vaccination immune response) for all study participants. A stratified random subset of study participants was selected for assessment of nAbs against ancestral D614G, and Omicron BA.4/BA.5 and XBB.1.5 variants. All COVID-19 cases were further assessed for nAbs against BA.4/BA.5 and XBB.1.5 using the validated SARS-CoV-2 pseudovirus neutralization assays. Details of the study design, eligibility criteria, safety assessments, and immunogenicity have been reported previously.16

The COVID-19 endpoint was defined according to the CDC case definition (ie, the presence of ≥ 1 CDC-listed symptom, plus a positive reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction test for SARS-CoV-2).16 Risk accrual began 14 d after vaccination and continued through the data cutoff date of January 31, 2024. Breakthrough cases were defined as COVID-19 onset occurring ≥7 d after the Day 29 visit; non-cases were participants without COVID-19 through data cutoff. Participants who developed COVID-19 or who were censored prior to 7 d after the Day 29 visit were excluded from the correlate analysis to support causal interpretation of the association between vaccine-induced antibody levels and subsequent COVID-19 risk.

The protocol was approved by an institutional review board (Advarra; Columbia, MD, USA). The trial was conducted according to the principles of the International Council for Harmonisation Technical Requirements for Registration of Pharmaceuticals for Human Use; E6(R2) Good Clinical Practice guidelines; the Declaration of Helsinki; and all applicable national, state, and local laws or regulations. All participants provided written informed consent prior to enrollment.

Serum nAb assay

The SARS-CoV-2 pseudovirus neutralization assay is a cell-based assay that measures the ability of serum nAbs to inhibit infection of 293T-ACE2 cells by SARS-CoV-2 reporter virus particles, containing spike mutations corresponding to D614G, BA.4/BA.5, or XBB.1.5 variant and expressing green fluorescent protein (GFP). Infection was assessed 48 (±4) hours after inoculation by counting the green fluorescent cells. Neutralizing activity was estimated by interpolating the mean duplicate focus-forming unit (FFU) values from the fitted response curve. Assay validation established precision, ruggedness, dilutional linearity, relative accuracy, selectivity, and specificity.

Statistical analysis

All immune correlate analyses used calendar-time-scale Cox regression models. To adjust for baseline COVID-19 risk, principal component analysis was applied to key baseline characteristics, and the first two principal components were used to construct the baseline risk scores. Together, these components explained more than 30% of the total variability in baseline covariates.

Inverse probability of sampling weight (IPS-weight) was calculated based on the case-cohort subset, which consisted of the stratified random immunogenicity subcohort augmented by all COVID-19 cases meeting the correlate analysis criteria. The IPS-weight was incorporated into the Cox model for BA.4/BA.5 and XBB.1.5 nAb analyses. Because D614G nAb data were available only in the random subcohort, IPS-weight was not applied in the D614G analyses.

Day 29 variant-specific nAb markers were evaluated as a CoR by adjusting for baseline risk scores across and within vaccine groups. An interaction term between the Day 29 nAb level and vaccine group was included to assess whether associations between antibody levels and COVID-19 risk differed between mRNA-1283 and mRNA-1273. In secondary analyses, Day 29 nAb levels were categorized into tertiles (low, medium, high) based on the overall distribution across both vaccine groups. Marginalized covariate-adjusted cumulative incidence and instantaneous hazard curves were estimated over the study period. Covariate-adjusted hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% CIs were estimated per 10-fold increase in quantitative nAb levels or for medium or high tertile versus the low tertile. Two-sided nominal p-values (Wald test), family-wise error rate‒adjusted p-values, and false discovery rate–adjusted p-values were reported.

CoPs were evaluated using marginalized covariate-adjusted cumulative incidence curves for COVID-19 across assigned Day 29 BA.4/BA.5 and XBB.1.5 nAb levels to assess the association between antibody responses and COVID-19 risk. Counterfactual placebo-controlled vaccine efficacy analyses were then used to examine the relationship between Day 29 nAb levels and the absolute VE of mRNA-1283 and mRNA-1273. Conditional on the observed Day 29 nAb geometric mean concentrations (GMCs) in the mRNA-1273 group, controlled rVE analyses were performed to quantify how rVE changed with increasing mRNA-1283‒elicited nAb responses. Antibody GMC thresholds corresponding to specified rVE values for mRNA-1283 versus mRNA-1273 were also estimated. The relationship between rVE and nAb levels was assessed using both marginalized risk (non-adjusted) and predicted VE (adjusted) approaches.

Mediation analyses, adapted from a previously described approach,20 were conducted to quantify the extent to which Day 29 variant-specific nAb markers mediated the observed rVE of mRNA-1283 relative to mRNA-1273. Stochastic interventional rVE analyses were performed to evaluate how rVE would change under simulated perturbations of observed mRNA-1283‒elicited nAb levels. Finally, threshold-based analyses using the semiparametric Cox regression model20 were conducted to estimate COVID-19 risk among participants with antibody levels exceeding specified thresholds.

Results

Study cohort, immunogenicity subcohort, and COVID-19 endpoint

A total of 10,888 participants (mRNA-1283, n = 5421; mRNA-1273, n = 5467) were included in the correlate analysis study cohort. Based on prespecified exclusion criteria, 529 participants were excluded from the full analysis set (FAS; n = 11,417) (Figure S1, Table S1). Specifically, participants with COVID-19 onset or censoring within 7 d after the Day 29 visit were excluded to minimize potential bias from intercurrent SARS-CoV-2 infection affecting Day 29 nAb assessments.

The immunogenicity subcohort consisted of a stratified random sample of approximately 1300 participants from the overall NextCOVE trial population, stratified by age group (≥12 to <18 y, ≥18 to <65 y, and ≥65 y). Details of the sampling design are provided in the Supplementary Information. This subcohort was assessed in a blinded manner for baseline (Day 1) and Day 29 nAb levels against D614G, BA.4/BA.5, and XBB.1.5 after vaccination with either mRNA-1283 or mRNA-1273.

The per-protocol correlate analysis subset (PPCAS) comprised the immunogenicity subcohort plus all COVID-19 cases that met the study cohort criteria.23 All COVID-19 cases outside the preplanned random subcohort were additionally tested for BA.4/BA.5 and XBB.1.5 nAbs at baseline and Day 29. The PPCAS served as the primary analysis population for BA.4/BA.5 and XBB.1.5 immune correlate analyses (Figure S1). Because D614G nAb measurements were available only in the random subcohort, D614G analyses were restricted to that subset.

Overall, the correlate analysis cohort included 515 COVID-19 cases among mRNA-1283 recipients and 571 cases among mRNA-1273 recipients (Table S2 , Table S3). Median follow-up was 238 d (range: 37–302 d), with a maximum post-Day 29 follow-up of 276 d.

Participant demographics and characteristics

Demographic and baseline clinical characteristics of participants who received mRNA-1283 and mRNA-1273 in both the immunogenicity subcohort (mRNA-1283, n = 615; mRNA-1273, n = 565) and the overall study cohort (mRNA-1283, n = 5421; mRNA-1273, n = 5467) are presented in Table S1. Within the immunogenicity subcohort (n = 1180), 58.4% of participants were aged 18 to <65 y, 26.1% were ≥65 y; 54.7% were female, 80.8% were White, and 84.9% were not Hispanic or Latino. At baseline, 77.5% had evidence of prior SARS-CoV-2 infection, the median number of prior booster doses was 2, and the median interval since the most recent COVID-19 vaccination was 10.5 months. Overall, the immunogenicity subcohort was broadly representative of the full study population and was well balanced between the mRNA-1283 and mRNA-1273 vaccine groups.

Baseline and Day 29 variant-specific nAb levels in mRNA-1283 versus mRNA-1273

Baseline nAb levels were comparable between the mRNA-1283 and mRNA-1273 groups across all evaluated variants, indicating comparable pre-vaccination immune profiles (Figure S2). At baseline, all participants had detectable nAbs against D614G. Detectable antibodies against BA.4/BA.5 were observed in 79% of mRNA-1283 recipients and 78% of mRNA-1273 recipients, and approximately 69% of participants in both vaccine groups had detectable antibodies against XBB.1.5 (Tables S4 and S5).

By Day 29, mRNA-1283 elicited consistently higher nAb levels than mRNA-1273 across all variants (Figure S3), with GMRs of approximately 1.2 to 1.3 times greater, and lower bounds of 95% CIs exceeding 1 (Table S6). For XBB.1.5, Day 29 nAb levels increased substantially from baseline, with geometric mean fold-rises (GMFRs) of 6.8 (95% CI: 6.0–7.8) for mRNA-1283 and 5.2 (95% CI: 4.5–5.9) for mRNA-1273. Absolute Day 29 nAb levels were highest for vaccine-matched variants (mRNA-1283: D614G = 10,617 AU/mL, BA.4/BA.5 = 2153 AU/mL; mRNA-1273: D614G = 8541 AU/mL, BA.4/BA.5 = 1627 AU/mL) (Table S6). Variant-specific nAb levels were strongly correlated at both baseline and Day 29, with Spearman correlation coefficients ranging from 0.66 to 0.84 (Figure S4).

Across both vaccine groups, Day 29 nAb GMCs for BA.4/BA.5 and XBB.1.5 were approximately 40% to 50% lower in participants who developed COVID-19 compared with non-cases, with upper bounds of the 95% CI for case-to-non-case GMC ratios below 1 (Table 1). For D614G, GMCs were numerically lower in cases than in non-cases (case vs non-case GMC ratios ranging from 0.8 to 0.9) (Figure 1).24,25 Similar patterns were observed for baseline nAbs (Table 1), consistent with the positive association between preexisting immunity and post-vaccination antibody response (Figure S5). Inverse associations between Day 29 nAb levels and COVID-19 risk

In the primary CoR analysis, Cox proportional hazards models demonstrated statistically significant or strong inverse associations between Day 29 variant-specific nAb levels and COVID-19 risk, after adjustment for vaccine group and baseline risk scores derived from principal component analysis (Figure S6).

The covariate-adjusted HRs for COVID-19 per 10-fold increase in Day 29 nAb levels were 0.70 (95% CI: 0.48–1.02) for D614G, 0.49 (95% CI: 0.41–0.58) for BA.4/BA.5, and 0.41 (95% CI: 0.35–0.49) for XBB.1.5 (Figure S7). These findings indicate a strong inverse relationship between nAb levels and COVID-19 risk. Although the D614G association did not reach statistical significance, its magnitude and direction were consistent with those observed for the Omicron variants. After conditioning on each variant-specific nAb marker, the estimated HR for vaccine group was close to 1, and no statistically significant interactions between nAb markers and vaccine group were observed (Table S7).

Subgroup CoR analyses within each vaccine group across key baseline factors, including age, sex, baseline SARS-CoV-2 status, and high-risk status, gave similar results.26 Among mRNA-1283 recipients, the estimated HRs per 10-fold increase in Day 29 BA.4/BA.5 nAb levels ranged from 0.43 to 0.64, compared with 0.40 to 0.77 among mRNA-1273 recipients. For XBB.1.5 nAbs, HRs ranged from 0.37 to 0.52 in the mRNA-1283 group and from 0.36 to 0.56 in the mRNA-1273 group, excluding participants aged 12 to <18 y because of limited case numbers (Figure S8).

In the secondary tertile-based analysis, the cumulative incidence and instantaneous hazard curves showed the same pattern (Figures 2 and S9, respectively). Higher Day 29 variant-specific nAb levels were associated with lower COVID-19 risk. For BA.4/BA.5, the estimated HRs for the medium and high tertiles versus the low tertile were 0.64 (95% CI: 0.53–0.78) and 0.33 (95% CI: 0.27–0.41), respectively; for XBB.1.5, the corresponding HRs were 0.67 (95% CI: 0.55–0.81) and 0.25 (95% CI: 0.20–0.32). D614G nAbs showed the same directional trend, but the associations were not statistically significant (Figure 2). Assessment of Day 29 nAb levels as a CoP

We next evaluated whether Day 29 variant-specific nAb levels functioned as a CoP. Because no statistically significant interactions between Day 29 nAb markers and vaccine group were observed, a parsimonious Cox model without an interaction term was used. Given the limited availability of D614G measurements in COVID-19 cases, only BA.4/BA.5 and XBB.1.5 nAbs were carried forward into the CoP analyses.

Primary and secondary CoP analyses included: (1) marginalized covariate-adjusted COVID-19 cumulative incidence curves across assigned Day 29 nAb levels for mRNA-1283 and mRNA-1273 (Figure 3(a,d)); (2) counterfactual placebo-controlled VE curves across the same nAb range (Figures 3(b,e)); and (3) rVE curves comparing mRNA-1283 with mRNA-1273 across the nAb spectrum (Figures 3(c,f)). Across all approaches, higher Day 29 BA.4/BA.5 or XBB.1.5 nAb levels induced by either mRNA-1283 or mRNA-1273 were associated with a lower COVID-19 risk and greater predicted VE. Discussion

This comprehensive immune correlates analysis from the Phase 3 NextCOVE trial showed that Day 29 nAb responses remained strongly inversely associated with COVID-19 risk and positively associated with protection in the endemic era. In this randomized, observer-blind, active-controlled trial, higher nAb levels were consistently associated with a lower risk of CDC-defined COVID-19 and higher predicted VE, supporting the role of nAbs as statistical correlates of protection for mRNA-1283 and mRNA-1273 during the study period (March 2023 to January 2024).

Both mRNA-1283 and mRNA-1273 elicited robust nAb responses against vaccine-encoded strains (ancestral D614G and Omicron BA.4/BA.5) and against the predominant circulating variant, XBB.1.5, indicating substantial cross-reactivity between vaccine antigens and circulating variants. Participants who developed COVID-19 had lower Day 29 nAb levels compared with non-cases, with the largest differences observed for BA.4/BA.5 and XBB.1.5 (D614G, 10% to 20% lower; BA.4/BA.5, 40% lower; XBB.1.5, 50% lower). These findings suggest that higher nAb levels, particularly against antigenically closer variants, were associated with lower COVID-19 risk. The weaker association observed for D614G likely reflects both reduced antigenic relevance in the Omicron era and limited statistical power, because D614G measurements were available only for a small subset of COVID-19 cases.

Cox proportional hazards models further supported these findings. Each 10-fold increase in Day 29 BA.4/BA.5 and XBB.1.5 nAb levels was associated with an approximately 51% to 59% reduction in COVID-19 risk, compared with an approximately 30% reduction per 10-fold increase in Day 29 D614G nAb level. In tertile-based CoR analyses, the medium and high tertiles for BA.4/BA.5 and XBB.1.5 were associated with approximately one-third and two-thirds lower risk, respectively, than the corresponding low tertiles. For D614G, similar trends were observed across tertiles, although the associations were less precise. Threshold-based analyses likewise showed a monotonic decline in COVID-19 risk with increasing BA.4/BA.5 or XBB.1.5 nAb levels for both vaccines.

Causal inference frameworks were also used to characterize the relationship between nAb levels, COVID-19 risk, and VE for each vaccine. Across these analyses, higher peak nAb levels were associated with lower estimated COVID-19 risk and higher model-predicted VE. In controlled rVE analyses, greater immune responses induced by mRNA-1283 corresponded with higher rVE outcomes. Similar patterns were observed in stochastic interventional VE analyses, in which simulated reductions in antibody levels led to waning protection and simulated increases enhanced protection, consistent with a dose-dependent relationship between nAb levels and rVE. The predicted absolute VEs were also consistent with contemporaneous real-world vaccine effectiveness estimates from studies overlapping with the NextCOVE trial.29–31 Mediation analyses further supported that nAb levels were meaningful mediators of protection, because the 95% CI of mediated rVE overlapped with the observed overall rVE.

Taken together, results across multiple CoR and CoP frameworks indicate that higher Day 29 nAb levels, particularly those targeting variants antigenically closer to the predominant circulating lineages, were consistently associated with lower COVID-19 risk and greater protection. These findings support the biological plausibility of nAb levels as a robust statistical CoP for COVID-19 and underscore the potential importance of periodic variant updates for COVID-19 vaccines.32

This analysis has several notable strengths. First, the NextCOVE trial was a large, well-powered, randomized, observer-blind, active-controlled efficacy trial enrolling more than 10,000 participants during a period of high SARS-CoV-2 transmission, providing a strong setting for evaluating immune correlates. Second, the correlate analyses were prespecified and implemented using established statistical frameworks, particularly causal-inference methods widely used in vaccine research for respiratory viruses, including influenza, respiratory syncytial virus, and prior COVID-19 CoP studies. The use of controlled VE, mediation, stochastic interventional VE, and threshold-based models strengthened the validity, interpretability, and reproducibility of the findings. Third, the inverse association between nAbs and COVID-19 risk was consistent across multiple analytical approaches and across demographic and clinical subgroups, supporting the robustness and generalizability of the results. Finally, the observed associations are consistent with broader body of evidence linking nAb responses with protection against SARS-CoV-2 infection and disease.33–35

Several limitations should also be considered. First, nAb data for the ancestral D614G variant were unavailable for most COVID-19 cases, resulting in lower statistical power and less conclusive CoR estimates than for BA.4/BA.5 and XBB.1.5. Second, greater variability in estimated COVID-19 risk at lower antibody levels led to wider CIs and less precise mediation estimates, suggesting possible heterogeneity in the influence of baseline risk factors on immune correlates. Third, the rVE and mediation frameworks rely on modeling assumptions and computational approximations that may not fully capture the dynamic relationship between antibody levels and COVID-19 risk. Fourth, the median follow-up in this correlates analysis cohort was approximately 8 months, limiting assessment of longer-term durability of protection. Finally, the absence of cellular immune response data precluded evaluation of the contribution of T-cell−mediated immunity and limits interpretation of nAb levels as a statistical CoP rather than a sole mechanistic CoP for COVID-19.36

In conclusion, the immune correlates analyses from the NextCOVE trial indicate that Day 29 variant-specific nAb markers remain a strong statistical CoP for both mRNA-1283 and mRNA-1273. These findings support the use of nAbs as immunologic surrogate markers associated with reduced COVID-19 risk for these mRNA-based vaccines. As SARS-CoV-2 continues to evolve, the results further suggest that higher nAb responses, particularly against variants antigenically closer to contemporary circulating strains, are more likely to enhance protection against COVID-19.

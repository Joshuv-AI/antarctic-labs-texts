Correlation of Ultrasonographic Varicocele Grading with Seminal Fluid Analysis in Male Infertility at Ahmadu Bello University Teaching Hospital, Zaria, Nigeria
Authors: Mubarak Zubair Abdullahi, Isah Ismaila Mato, Isah Adah Suleiman, Ibrahim Aliyu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Infertility is a global health issue with profound social and psychological consequences, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa, where male factors are often underdiagnosed. Varicocele, a dilatation of the pampiniform plexus veins, is a leading correctable cause of male infertility. Seminal fluid analysis (SFA) remains the cornerstone for evaluating male fertility. Establishing correlations between ultrasound grading of varicocele and semen parameters may provide clinicians with predictive, non-invasive tools for managing infertility. The objective of the study is to correlate the ultrasound grading of varicocele with seminal fluid findings in infertile men.Methodology:This was a cross-sectional analytical study conducted on 225 infertile men aged 23–68 years with sonographically confirmed varicocele at Ahmadu Bello University Teaching Hospital, Zaria, between September 2023 and June 2024. Participants underwent scrotal ultrasonography using Sarteschi's criteria for varicocele grading, and semen analysis was performed in accordance with WHO 2010 guidelines. Data were analyzed with SPSS v23 using descriptive statistics, ANOVA, and Spearman correlation.Results:The modal age group was 31–35 years (28.4%). Left-sided varicocele was most common (44.4%), followed by right (35.5%) and bilateral (20%). Varicocele grade distribution was: I (20.4%), II (19.1%), III (24.4%), IV (14.2%), and V (21.7%). Higher varicocele grades correlated negatively with sperm count (r = -0.64, p = 0.001), motility (r = -0.40, p = 0.021), and morphology (r = -0.43, p = 0.013). Patients with grade IV–V varicocele showed significantly impaired semen quality compared to grades I–II.Conclusion:Increasing varicocele grade is associated with progressive deterioration in semen parameters. Ultrasound grading of varicocele may serve as a reliable predictor of seminal quality, assisting in clinical decision-making for the management of male infertility.

Introduction

Infertility affects an estimated 48–186 million people worldwide, with up to 15% of couples of reproductive age experiencing difficulty conceiving [1]. In sub-Saharan Africa, the prevalence is particularly high, ranging from 20–40% [2]. In Nigeria, infertility rates reach 31.1%, often complicated by late diagnosis, infections, and socio-cultural misconceptions [3].

Male factors contribute to 40–50% of infertility cases globally [4]. Among these, varicocele—a dilatation of pampiniform plexus veins—is the most frequent correctable cause, affecting up to 40% of men with primary infertility and 80% of those with secondary infertility [5]. Pathophysiological mechanisms include scrotal hyperthermia, oxidative stress, hypoxia, and hormonal disruption, all of which impair spermatogenesis [6].

Ultrasonography, particularly Color Doppler ultrasound (CDUS), remains the gold standard for diagnosing and grading varicocele [7]. Sarteschi's grading system provides a structured sonographic assessment of reflux and venous dilation [8]. On the other hand, seminal fluid analysis (SFA) remains the reference laboratory test for evaluating sperm quality, providing insights into sperm concentration, motility, and morphology [9].

Despite advances in diagnostic imaging, inconsistencies remain regarding the correlation between varicocele grade and semen quality. Some studies report strong associations [10–2], while others show weak or no correlation [13]. In Nigeria and Africa, data remain sparse, warranting context-specific research. This study aimed to correlate ultrasonographic grading of varicocele with seminal fluid parameters in Nigerian men with infertility, thereby contributing to evidence-based management strategies.

Materials and Methods

Study Location

The study was conducted at the Radiology and Urology Departments of Ahmadu Bello University Teaching Hospital (ABUTH), Zaria, a tertiary referral center serving Northwestern Nigeria.

Study Design and Population

This was a cross-sectional analytical study involving 225 men aged 23–68 years with infertility (failure to conceive after ≥ 12 months of regular unprotected intercourse) and sonographic evidence of varicocele.

Ultrasound Protocol

Scrotal ultrasonography was performed using a GE Logiq C5 Premium (12 MHz linear probe). Patients were scanned in supine and standing positions, with and without the Valsalva maneuver. Varicocele grading followed Sarteschi's classification (Grades I–V). A venous diameter ≥ 3 mm with reflux > 2 s was diagnostic of varicocele.

Seminal Fluid Analysis

SFA was conducted per WHO 2010 guidelines. Parameters assessed included sperm concentration, motility, and morphology. Definitions:

Statistical Analysis

Data were analyzed with SPSS v23. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages; continuous variables as means ± SD. Associations between varicocele grade and semen parameters were tested using ANOVA and Spearman correlation. p < 0.05 was considered significant.

Ethics

Ethical approval was obtained from the ABUTH Health Research Ethics Committee (reference number: ABUTHZ/ HREC/ H40 / 2022). Informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Discussion

This study demonstrated a strong negative correlation between varicocele grade and semen quality among infertile Nigerian men. Increasing grades were significantly associated with deterioration in sperm count, motility, and morphology. These findings support the hypothesis that varicocele is a progressive condition adversely affecting spermatogenesis.

Comparison with Nigerian and African Studies

Our findings align with a Nigerian multicenter study by Ebubedike et al., who found higher-grade varicoceles associated with poorer semen indices [14]. Similarly, Anyadike et al. reported improvement in semen parameters after varicocelectomy in Nigerian men [15]. These studies highlight the clinical importance of early detection and intervention.

International Comparisons

Othman et al. (Iraq) and Ariyati et al. (Indonesia) reported significant correlations between varicocele grade and semen abnormalities [10,11]. Conversely, Foroughi et al. (Iran) found no correlation [13]. The disparity may reflect differences in methodology, patient selection, and population genetics.

Meta-analyses confirm that varicocele negatively impacts sperm count, motility, and morphology [12]. Our findings support these conclusions, emphasizing the predictive value of ultrasonographic grading in infertility evaluation.

Clinical Implications

The ability of ultrasound grading to predict semen quality provides clinicians with a non-invasive, reproducible diagnostic marker. Patients with higher-grade varicoceles should be counseled regarding fertility risks and the potential benefits of varicocelectomy.

Limitations

This study was hospital-based, which may limit generalizability. Seminal analysis was based on single samples, which could introduce variability. Longitudinal follow-up after intervention was not performed.

Conclusion

Ultrasound grading of varicocele correlates significantly with seminal fluid parameters in Nigerian men with infertility. Higher grades are associated with progressive decline in semen quality. Sonographic grading should be integrated into routine infertility evaluation to guide early intervention strategies.

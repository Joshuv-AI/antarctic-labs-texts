Hesperidin facilitating gastrointestinal motility by “Gut-brain axis” and “SCF/C-Kit signaling pathways”
Authors: Yunfei Li, Xinying Zhou, Yusong Du, Mingyuan An, Shasha Wan, Zewei Sun, Qingzhen Zhong
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractHesperidin shows promising results as a potential feed additive for enhancing gastrointestinal motility in animals. Gastrointestinal function plays a pivotal role in animal growth and the digestibility of dietary nutrients, with gastrointestinal motor function serving as a crucial component. However, limited research has been conducted on the application of hesperidin as a feed additive to promote gastrointestinal motility. The present study aims to assess the efficacy of Hesperidin as a feed additive in promoting gastrointestinal motility and elucidating its underlying mechanism. A total of 200 newly hatched (1-day-old) broilers with similar body weight were randomly allocated into 4 groups as follows: the control group receiving only the basal diet, and the other 3 groups supplemented with 50, 100, and 150 mg of hesperidin per kg of the basal diet, respectively. Each group consisted of 5 replicates with ten broilers per replicate, and the feeding trial lasted for a duration of 21 d. At 21 d of age, a 5% w/v Evans Blue solution in distilled water was utilized to measure intestinal transit rates (ITR). Gastric emptying (GE) was evaluated by administering a phenol red solution at a concentration of 0.05% w/v (1 mL/broiler). Fifteen broilers from each group were euthanized and immediately dissected to obtain gizzard, hypothalamus, duodenum, and jugular blood samples. Jugular blood samples were collected for brain-gut peptide content analysis, while gizzard, hypothalamus, and duodenum samples were used for immunohistochemical analysis. Real-time qPCR was performed on gizzard samples. The results demonstrated a significant improvement in the GE and ITR of broilers in all treatment groups compared to the control group (P < 0.05), particularly in the 100mg/Kg and 150mg/Kg hesperidin group. Incorporation of hesperidin into the broilers' diet significantly enhances serum levels of ghrelin, encompassing serotonin (5-HT), motilin (MTL), cholecystokinin (CCK), and Stem Cell Factor (SCF) as well as substance P (SP) in the gizzard and duodenal tissues while reducing vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP) levels (P < 0.05). The group administered a dosage of 150mg/Kg exhibited the most pronounced effect.Immunohistochemistry analysis revealed that hesperidin supplementation up-regulated SP protein content and down-regulated VIP protein content in the hypothalamus, gizzard, and duodenum of broilers (P < 0.05), with the most pronounced effect illustrated in the 150mg/Kg hesperidin group. Furthermore, addition of hesperidin to broiler feed resulted in a significant up-regulation of protein expression and gene expression related to SCF and The protein expression of Receptor tyrosine kinase (C-Kit) was significantly upregulated in the 150mg/Kg group, while the gene expression of C-Kit was significantly upregulated in the 50 mg/Kg group (P < 0.05). In conclusion, hesperidin exhibits promising potential as a feed additive for broilers, as its dietary supplementation of hesperidin improves gastrointestinal motility through modulation of both “gut-brain axis” signaling pathways and “SCF/C-Kit signaling pathways” within broiler chicken's digestive system. Notably, basal diet supplemented with 150mg/Kg hesperidin demonstrates superior efficacy.Key words: hesperidin, broiler, gastrointestinal motility, gut-brain axis, SCF/C-Kit signaling pathway

INTRODUCTION

Due to broiler chickens' rapid growth, substantial economic gains, short feeding cycle, and elevated meat protein content, the broiler breeding sector is experiencing a significant expansion and has emerged as the second-largest segment of animal husbandry in China. Annually, more than 16 billion broilers are processed in China (Cao et al., 2015, Cheng et al., 2017, Huang et al., 2020, Islam et al., 2022). From a nutritional standpoint, the objective of animal feeding can be viewed as nourishing their gastrointestinal tract, which serves as both the largest immune and digestive organ in the body (Song et al., 2022). Poultry possess a straightforward gastrointestinal system characterized by a concise digestive tract, wherein the functionality of the gastrointestinal system can directly influence nutrient absorption and animal well-being.

The gastrointestinal (GI) system performs a range of essential functions including motility, sensation, digestion, absorption, secretion, and barrier function. Among these functions, GI motility plays a crucial role in effectively mixing gut contents with digestive secretions and exposing them to the absorptive surface. It also facilitates propulsion along the GI tract while preventing retrograde movement of contents and ensuring timely disposal of residues. These processes are vital for orderly food digestion, efficient nutrient absorption, and prompt elimination of unwanted wastes (Guy et al., 2016, Zheng et al., 2022).

In the 1980s, researchers introduced the concept of the enteric nervous system (ENS), marking a gradual recognition of the intricate connection between the brain and gut known as the "brain-gut axis" (Wood and Grundy, 1998). The brain-gut axis represents a neuroendocrine network that establishes a vital connection between the central nervous system (CNS) and the ENS, playing a pivotal role in the regulation of gastrointestinal functions. Brain-gut peptides (BGP) act as essential neurotransmitters involved in facilitating communication between the CNS and ENS, MTL, CCK, 5-HT, ghrelin, SP, and VIP (Yanaihara, 1984). The distribution of BGP is primarily observed in the hypothalamus, gastric antrum, and duodenum. It serves as a crucial regulatory factor and actively participates in modulating gastrointestinal physiological activities (Liu et al., 2019). These BGP not only regulate food intake and body weight, but also modulate gastrointestinal motility, encompassing gastric emptying and small intestine transit (Anonymous, 2010, Huang et al., 2024).

Interstitial cells of Cajal (ICC) function as intrinsic pacemaker cells for slow waves, exerting exclusive influence over their generation (Thomsen et al., 1998, Szucs et al., 2016). The ICC function is intricately associated with the currents of voltage-dependent ion channels and primarily mediated by the SCF/c-Kit signaling pathway, which plays a pivotal role in regulating gastrointestinal function (Zhang et al., 2018). The SCF/C-Kit pathway plays a pivotal role in maintaining the proliferation, differentiation, and phenotype of ICCs, which are closely associated with gastrointestinal motility (Johan and Lars, 2012).

One strategy for enhancing gastrointestinal motility involves the administration of supplementary additives. For instance, previous researchers discovered that the oral administration of Liriope Platyphylla Wang Et Tang roots and Hwangryunhaedok-tang effectively stimulated gastrointestinal motility in mice (Kim et al., 2016, Lee et al., 2016, Kim et al., 2017). Supplementing the diet of mice with Citrus aurantium L. has been observed to enhance gastrointestinal motility while simultaneously inhibiting the effects of motility inhibitors (Gao and Yang, 2023) .Supplementation with cannabinoids and flavonoids from Aurantii Fructus Immaturus (AFIF) in the diet of rats exhibits a favorable impact on enhancing gastrointestinal motility (Crowley et al., 2024, Wen et al., 2024). Furthermore, the findings demonstrated that administration of Lactobacillus plantarum AR495 to both humans and rats effectively restored normal levels of 5-HT by inhibiting aberrant proliferation of enterochromaffin cells and excessive activation of TPH1, thereby suppressing intestinal peristalsis in individuals with irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) and improving gastrointestinal motility (Zhang et al., 2024b). Research has shown that the impact of a Chinese herbal formula on rats with irritable bowel syndrome by targeting the brain-gut axis and SCF/C-Kit signaling system, revealing significant amelioration of symptoms related to scanty diarrhea and gastrointestinal hyperdynamia in the experimental rat model (Li et al., 2023). However, there is a pressing need for more comprehensive information pertaining to the impact of feed additives on gastrointestinal motility across diverse animal species.

Hesperidin, a bioflavonoid compound predominantly present in citrus fruits such as oranges, lemons, tangerines, and grapefruits, exhibits diverse biological activities encompassing anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, antiaging, anticancer and antibacterial properties (Orallo et al., 2004, Mehrdad et al., 2015, Li and Schluesener, 2017, Stanisic et al., 2020). Moreover, it enhances gastrointestinal motility while facilitating food digestion and optimizing feed efficiency (Zhang et al., 2014).

Poultry possess a simple digestive system characterized by a concise gastrointestinal tract, where nutrient absorption is directly influenced by gastrointestinal motility. However, no studies have been reported focusing on the function of gastrointestinal motility in poultry. Therefore, this study aims to investigate the impact of hesperidin on broiler chicken's gastrointestinal motility. Different concentrations were administered to evaluate the effectiveness of hesperidin as a feed additive for broiler chickens, thereby establishing a foundation for its rational utilization in improving poultry's gastrointestinal motility.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Hesperidin Source

The hesperidin, with a purity of ≥98%, was purchased from Shaanxi Qionghua Biotechnology Co., Ltd.

Ethical Standard

This study was conducted in accordance with the Animal Care requirements of the Animal Administration Committee at Jilin Agricultural University (Permit Number: SYXK(Ji)2023-0021).

Experimental Design

The study utilized a total of 200 newly hatched broilers (1-day-old) with similar body weight, which were randomly allocated into 4 groups as follows: the control group receiving only the basal diet, while the other 3 groups were supplemented with 50, 100, and 150 mg of hesperidin per kg of the basal diet, respectively. Each group consisted of 5 replicates with ten broilers per replicate, and the feeding trial lasted for a duration of 21 d. Throughout the trial period, all broilers were provided with unrestricted access to complete feed and water through nipple drinkers in an equitable manner. All groups of broilers were reared under identical conditions, which involved appropriate confinement cages with controlled air temperatures. The experiment employed continuous lighting for a duration of first 3 d, while the temperature was initially set at 32°C for the first 3 d. Subsequently, starting from d 4, A 16L: 8D lighting was provided during the surplus experimental period,the environmental temperature was gradually reduced by 1°C every 3 d until reaching a target temperature on d 21.

Body Weight Statistics

A 200 one-day-old broilers (50 per group,4 groups) were randomly selected and weighed. Then, the average weight was calculated as the initial weight, denoted as Initial body weight (IBW). Afterward, Broilers are weighed every 7 d, Weight for 21 d of age was recorded as final body weight (FBW). Average daily gain (ADG) is calculated using (IBW-FFBW)/d £ 100%.

Sample Collection

At 21 d of age, a total of 15 broilers (3 per replicate) with similar weights were randomly selected from each group for sample collection. Following weighing, the broilers were euthanized by cervical dislocation under deep Nembutal anesthesia (45 ug/g of body weight, intraperitoneal injection; Shanghai Chemical Factory, Shanghai, China) (Xu et al., 2024). Subsequently, immediate dissection was performed to collect samples including gizzard, hypothalamus, duodenum, and jugular blood.

Evaluation of Gastric Emptying

GE was assessed by administering a 0.05% (w/v) phenol red solution (1 mL/broiler). The broiler were sacrificed 30 min later, and the stomachs were immediately removed,cut into several pieces in 5 mL of 0.01 N NaOH, and homogenized. The homogenates were treated with 0.2 mL of 20% trichloroacetic acid per mL of homogenate.The mixtures were centrifuged for 10 min at 1050 g, and the supernatants (0.05 mL) so obtained were added to 0.5 N NaOH (0.2 mL). The absorbances of these mixtures were measured using a spectrometer at 560 nm.GE (%) was calculated using 100-(A/B) × 100, where A is test stomach absorbance (560 nm) and B is control stomach absorbance (560 nm) immediately after phenol red administration (Kim et al., 2017).

Measurement of Intestinal Transit Rates

To determine the ITR in vivo, we used Evans Blue solution (5% w/v in distilled water).Evans Blue solution was administered (0.1 mL/kg of body weight), through an orogastric tube to broiler. The animals were sacrificed 40 min after Evans Blue administration, and intestinal transit distances were determined by measuring the distance the Evans Blue had migrated in the intestine (from the pylorus to its most distal point). Intestinal transit was quantified using ITR% values, which were calculated by expressing the distance traveled by Evans Blue in 30 min as a percentage of total small intestine length (from the pylorus to the ileal terminus) (Kim et al., 2017).

Blood Brain-Gut Peptide Parameters

Serum was obtained after centrifuging the blood at 4°C, 3,000 × g for 15 min and stored at –20°C until analysis of hormones. Contents of Ghrelin,MTL,CCK,5-HT, and SCF were determined using ELISA assay kit following the instructions provided by the S Shanghai Jining Biotechnology Co. Details of the kit number are shown in Table S1.

Evaluation of SP and VIP in Broiler Gizzard and Duodenal Tissues

Contents of SP and VIP in broiler gizzard and duodenal tissues were determined using ELISA assay kit following the instructions provided by the S Shanghai Jining Biotechnology Co.

Expression of SCF and C-Kit Protein mRNAs in the Gizzard Tissue of Broiler

Tissue samples were collected from the same region of the gizzard, followed by rinsing with saline solution at 4°C, drying with filter paper to eliminate surface water, and placement in 1.5 mL EP tubes. Subsequently, the tubes were flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen and transferred to a -80°C freezer for subsequent determination of SCF and C-Kit protein gene expression.

The frozen gizzard samples were subjected to total RNA extraction using TRizol reagent (Takara Biotechnology, Dalian, China) following the manufacturer's instructions. Subsequently, the quality and concentration of RNA in each sample were assessed using a Nanodrop-1000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA), with an OD260:OD280 ratio ranging from 1.8 to 2.0. Next, cDNA synthesis was performed using 1 μg of total RNA and a synthesis kit (Takara Biotechnology, Dalian, China) according to the manufacturer's protocol. Real-time PCR analysis of β-actin, SCF,and C-Kit genes was conducted on a CFX96 Real-time PCR Detection System (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Hercules, CA) employing TBGreen Premix Ex Taq (Takara Biotechnology,Dalian,China) in a final volume of 20 μL; all experiments were performed in triplicate. The primer sequences utilized in this investigation are provided in Table 2. The experimental protocols for all genes encompassed a denaturation step (30 s at 95°C), followed by an amplification and quantification phase repeated for 40 cycles (5 s at 95°C, 30 s at 60°C). Subsequently, a melting curve analysis was performed with a heating rate of 0.1°C/s within the temperature range of 65 to 95°C. Real-time PCR determined the relative gene amplification through the threshold cycle (Ct) value. To normalize mRNA expression levels of each target gene, β-actin served as the internal control, and relative expression levels were calculated using the ΔΔCT method (Yu et al., 2017).

Statistical Analysis

Analysis was performed by 1-way ANOVA using SPSS software. Duncan's multiple-range tests determined the statistical differences between treatments. Results were displayed as mean§SEM (standard error of means) and graphed using GraphPad Prism 7.00 (GraphPad Software).The results with P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

DISCUSSION

Poultry possess a straightforward digestive system characterized by a concise gastrointestinal tract, and the efficiency of nutrient absorption and animal health is directly influenced by gastrointestinal motility. The significance of gastrointestinal motility in broilers cannot be overstated, as it arises from the synchronized contractions of the tunica muscularis, which constitutes the outer wall of the alimentary canal extending from the distal esophagus to the external anal sphincter (Sanders et al., 2012). The inclusion of hesperidin as a dietary supplement has been demonstrated to effectively enhance gastrointestinal motility and stimulate gastrin secretion in rats with functional dyspepsia (Zhang et al., 2014), but there are no studies on the hesperidin in broiler chickens and the underlying mechanism has not been revealed. Therefore, in this study, hesperidin was used as a feed additive with the aim of improving gastrointestinal motility in broilers.

GE and ITR are nowadays 2 of the most important indices for evaluating gastrointestinal motility (Zhang et al., 2024a). The findings of Chen et al. (2024) demonstrate that Huanglian-banxia enhances gastric motility by modulating brain-gut neurotransmitters through the MAPK signaling pathway, thereby promoting gastrointestinal emptying and intestinal transit rate. From the results of the present study, GE and ITR were significantly increased in the groups with added hesperidin compared to the control group; this result proves that hesperidin can promote gastrointestinal motility.

BGP serves as a crucial neurotransmitter in the communication between the central nervous system and enteric nervous system. Studies have demonstrated that brain-gut peptides can ameliorate gastrointestinal motility disorders by regulating gastric emptying and intestinal propulsion (Zhu et al., 2020). The physiological function of the MTL facilitates gastrointestinal motility and expedites gastric emptying. MTL can also function as a pivotal neurotransmitter or modulator implicated in the regulation of feeding behavior (Luiking et al., 2002); The study conducted by Kamerling et al. (2002) revealed a significant correlation between delayed gastric emptying and reduced levels of MTL in patients diagnosed with functional dyspepsia and other gastric motility disorders. CCK has been shown to exert inhibitory effects on gastric emptying and food intake. Experimental evidence suggests that Ghrelin exerts a significant orexigenic effect, enhancing gastric antrum motility index and stimulating duodenal contractions (Mann and Michele, 2009). Wu et al. (2024) demonstrated a positive correlation between ghrelin levels and gastrointestinal motility in their comprehensive review. A study conducted by Spencer and Keating (2022) describes the role of 5-HT in promoting gastrointestinal motility and provides a comprehensive explanation of its underlying mechanism. The findings of this study demonstrate that the inclusion of hesperidin in broiler diets significantly enhances serum levels of brain gut peptides including 5-HT, Ghrelin, SCF, MTL, and CCK. Moreover, a positive correlation between the amount of hesperidin additive and peptide elevation was observed. In conclusion, dietary supplementation with hesperidin effectively modulates brain gut peptides in broilers and consequently promotes gastrointestinal motility.

The brain-gut axis represents a conceptual framework elucidating the bidirectional communication between the gastrointestinal tract and the central nervous system (Malagelada, 2020). The gut-brain axis is intricately influenced by various environmental factors, dietary patterns, and behavioral choices (Zo et al., 2023). In addition to the interplay between the central nervous system, autonomic nervous system, and enteric nervous system, the regulation of the gastrointestinal tract by the nervous system also encompasses the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis. Notably, it is through this axis that receptors for intestinal physiological activities are determined and a pivotal role in modulating gastrointestinal dysfunction is played (Malagelada, 2020). The SP is a crucial neuropeptide in mammals, consisting of 11 amino acids (Ching et al., 2011). The distribution of SP is primarily observed in the hypothalamus and gastrointestinal tissues, where it predominantly stimulates smooth muscles within the gastrointestinal tract. This stimulation facilitates intestinal peristalsis, thereby playing a crucial role in the digestive system (Lan et al., 2020) The VIP, a neural peptide secreted in the CNS and ENS, plays a crucial role in regulating secretory activity, motility, and peristaltic reflex suppression within the circular smooth muscle layer (Shen et al., 2006, Margarita et al., 2014). VIP is present in the central nervous system (hypothalamus, hippocampus, etc.) and the enteric nervous system (gastrointestinal tissues). Unlike SP, VIP relaxes intestinal smooth muscles, inhibiting intestinal peristalsis, thereby reducing gastrointestinal motility Slow waves are the foundation of gastrointestinal motility, and Interstitial (Datar et al., 2004). Zhao et al. (2018) demonstrated that electroacupuncture enhances rat gastrointestinal motility by regulating SP and VIP levels in the colon.Since it was difficult to obtain SP and VIP antibodies from broilers for Western blot, only immunohistochemistry was used in this experiment.The results of this experiment show that the addition of hesperidin to broiler feed up-regulated SP levels and reduced VIP levels in gizzard and duodenal tissues compared to controls. From the results of immunohistochemistry, the SP and VIP protein contents in the hypothalamus, gizzard and duodenum were also consistent with the previous studies, i.e. up-regulation of SP protein expression down-regulation of VIP protein expression. SP and VIP are the key proteins and active peptides in the brain-gut axis. The up-regulation of SP and down-regulation of VIP can indicate the improvement of gastrointestinal motility through the brain-gut axis. The results of this study suggested that hesperidin supplementation in broiler diet could promote the gastrointestinal motility of broilers through brain-gut axis.

ICCs serve as pacemakers for gastrointestinal electrical activity, initiators of gastrointestinal rhythmic motility, and transmitters of gastrointestinal rhythmic potentials. They are extensively distributed throughout the digestive tract, establishing connections with smooth muscle cells and playing a pivotal role in regulating gastrointestinal motility (Daniel, 2004, Joung et al., 2021). The ICC function is intricately linked to the currents of voltage-dependent ion channels and primarily mediated by the SCF/c-Kit signaling pathway. As a type III tyrosine kinase receptor, c-kit serves as a specific receptor for ICCs and is predominantly secreted by smooth muscle cells, binding with SCF. The growth, maintenance of phenotype, and functional activities of ICCs are regulated by the SCF/c-Kit signaling pathway, which encompasses the specific tyrosine kinase receptor c-Kit and its endogenous ligand, SCF. This pathway governs cellular proliferation, differentiation, and migration of ICCs (Wang et al., 2021). Furthermore, the activation of the SCF/c-Kit pathway augments the responsiveness of gastrointestinal hormones, thereby enhancing gastrointestinal function (Chai et al., 2017) .Flavonoids, including nobiletin and quercetin, possess the ability to regulate this intricate signaling pathway in order to manifest their physiological impacts (Chen et al., 2018, Sakai-Kashiwabara et al., 2014). The present study revealed that the expression of proteins and genes of SCF and c-kit was up-regulated in gizzard tissues. This suggests that the inclusion of hesperidin in broiler chicken diets may enhance the proliferation and development of ICCs via activation of the SCF/c-Kit signaling pathway, thereby ameliorating gastrointestinal motility.

In conclusion, the present study showed that the dietary addition of hesperidin positively affected the gastrointestinal motility regulating by “Gut-brain axis” and “SCF/C-Kit signaling pathways”. In addition, 150 mg/kg hesperidin group had the best results.

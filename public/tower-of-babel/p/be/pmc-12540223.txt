Identification and characterization of lLF2 as a prognostic biomarker in HER2-positive breast cancer using Mendelian randomization and machine learning
Authors: Xiyun Quan, Yi Deng, Meiyuan Huang, Huimei Yi, Ming Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHER2-positive breast cancer is an aggressive molecular subtype characterized by high recurrence rates and poor prognosis. Identifying robust biomarkers for prognosis and therapeutic response is essential to improve individualized treatment strategies.MethodsBased on genome-wide association study (GWAS) data, summary data-based Mendelian randomization (SMR) analysis was used to screen disease-related genes. The diagnostic model of HER2-positive breast cancer was constructed by combining LASSO, random forest and SVM-RFE and the characteristic genes were identified. The prognostic correlation was evaluated through Kaplan-Meier analysis. The SNP association, expression level, immune activity and drug sensitivity of ILF2 were analyzed emphatically. H&E staining and immunohistochemistry were used to verify the expression of ILF2 in tumor tissues.ResultsA total of 14 genes associated with HER2-positive breast cancer were identified through SMR analysis, with the most significant SNPs enriched on chromosome 1. Integrating three machine learning algorithms (LASSO, Random Forest, and SVM-RFE), four robust diagnostic feature genes were identified: SLC16A3, ILF2, ARRDC3, and SNAPIN. Among them, ILF2 emerged as a key gene of interest. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis revealed that high expression of ILF2, NEK10, LAMTOR5, and APOBEC3A was significantly associated with poorer prognosis. The top ILF2-associated SNP (rs10908848) showed a strong negative regulatory effect on disease risk. Further functional analyses indicated that high ILF2 expression was associated with suppressed antitumor immune activity and reduced sensitivity to multiple chemotherapeutic agents, suggesting its role in immune evasion and therapy resistance. Immunohistochemical staining confirmed significantly upregulated ILF2 protein expression in HER2-positive breast tumor tissues compared with adjacent normal tissues.ConclusionILF2 is biologically and clinically important in HER2-positive breast cancer, and its high expression is linked with tumor immune escape and increased resistance to chemotherapeutic agents. Future studies should further explore the mechanism and application of ILF2 as a potential therapeutic target.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03762-y.Keywords: HER2-positive breast cancer, ILF2, Mendelian randomization, Machine learning, Prognostic biomarker, Immunotherapy, Drug sensitivity, Tumor microenvironment

Introduction

According to Global Cancer Statistics 2022, breast cancer is the most common malignant tumor among women, with approximately 2.3 million new cases [1]. Breast cancer is generally classified into three main subtypes: hormone receptor-positive (HR+), HER2-positive and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), plays a crucial role in determining treatment strategies and predicting outcomes [2].

In 1987, HER2 was identified as a significant oncogene in the development of breast cancer [3], and approximately 15–20% of breast cancer patients exhibit HER2-positive status [4]. overexpression of HER2 leads to abnormal proliferation and spreading of tumor cells, which results in a high degree of invasiveness [3, 5, 6]. The significance of HER2-positive breast cancer is not only to understand the pathophysiological characteristics of this subtype of breast cancer but also to provide a basis for clinical diagnosis, treatment, and prognosis. However, much remains unknown about the genetic basis of HER2-positive breast cancer and its association with disease pathogenesis.

Summary data-based Mendelian randomization (SMR) analysis is a method of causal inference based on genetic variation that uses available genetic variation data and genome-wide association study (GWAS) data to assess the impact of genes on disease in larger samples [7, 8]. In recent years, some studies have used SMR methods to investigate the correlation between gene expression and different diseases. Researchers used SMR analysis to identify potential causal relationships between mitochondrial dysfunction-related genes (FDPS, NSUN4, VARS2) and breast, prostate, and lung cancers, respectively [9]. Based on SMR integration of genome-wide association studies of polycystic ovary syndrome and expression quantitative trait loci data, RPS26 and PM20D1 were found to have strong suggestive pleiotropic associations with polycystic ovary, which is expected to be a preventive and therapeutic target for polycystic ovary disease [10]. This study aims to systematically explore the influence of genetic factors on the susceptibility to HER2-positive breast cancer through SMR analysis.

ILF2 (also known as NF45) is a DNA/RNA binding protein that was initially discovered to be involved in regulating the transcription of cytokines such as interleukin-2 (IL-2). A recent study found that LINC00571 / HNRNPK/ILF2 / IDH2 shaft metabolites by adjusting the Krebs cycle, promote breast cancer cell proliferation and invasion [11]. High expression of ILF2 may be associated with poor prognosis in patients with breast cancer, or it can be used as a diagnostic marker or therapeutic target [12].

We will select genetic variants associated with HER2-positive breast cancer as instrumental variables by integrating publicly available GWAS data and other genetic variant databases, assess the impact of these genetic variants on the influence of developing HER2-positive breast cancer by SMR analysis methods, and validate their expression and function by transcriptome databases.

Considering the important role of genetic variants in tumorigenesis, this study aimed to screen the signature genes associated with HER2-positive breast cancer and assess their significance in clinical prognosis by GWAS and machine learning methods. Through an in-depth exploration of the expression patterns and functions of the identified genes, we aim to offer novel insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying HER2-positive breast cancer and personalized treatment approaches.

Methods

SMR analysis for screening disease-associated gene

All statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.3.0). First, the SMR software (version 1.3.1) was downloaded from the Yang Lab website [13, 14]. Expression Quantitative Trait Loci (eQTL) data were obtained from the eQTLGen Consortium [15], named the “cis-eQTL-SMR_20191212” file. Summary statistics for HER2-positive breast cancer were downloaded from the FinnGen database [16], using the phenocode “C3_BREAST_ERPLUS_EXALLC”, which includes data from 10,404 disease samples and 182,678 control samples. The SMR analysis was conducted using the 1000G_EUR reference genome [17], incorporating the downloaded GWAS summary statistics and eQTL data. Heterogeneity in Dependent Instruments (HEIDI) analysis was performed to assess whether the observed association is due to pleiotropy or linkage. A non-significant HEIDI P-value (P_HEIDI > 0.05) indicates the absence of heterogeneity, supporting a true pleiotropic effect between gene expression and disease phenotype. Ensembl IDs in the result files were converted to gene symbols using the “biomaRt” package. Results were filtered based on the following criteria: SMR P-value (P_SMR) < 0.05, adjusted P-value (FDR) < 0.05, and HEIDI P-value (P_HEIDI) > 0.05. Visualization of the results included generating Manhattan plots using the “CMplot” package and creating chromosome position and scatter plots for significant genes using the “magick” and “TeachingDemos” packages [13].

Acquisition of transcriptional profiles of HER2-positive breast cancer patients

The gene expression profiling and clinical features of 1113 breast cancer tumor samples and 113 adjacent cancer samples were obtained from the TCGA database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). 164 samples of breast cancer patients whose clinical information “lab_proc_her2_neu_immunohistochemistry_receptor_status” was “positive” were included for prognostic analysis of HER2-positive patients.

Three machine learning algorithms to screen for feature genes

To screen the feature genes associated with HER2-positive breast cancer, LASSO (Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator), Random Forest, and Support Vector Machine - Recursive Feature Elimination (SVM-RFE) were applied to construct diagnostic models. Gene expression data from HER2-positive tumor tissues (n = 164) and paracancerous samples (n = 113) obtained from the TCGA database were used. All gene expression data were normalized using the caret package in R. Given the limited sample size, 10-fold cross-validation was employed in each algorithm to ensure robustness and avoid overfitting. First, a LASSO regression model using the glmnet package was applied to determine the optimal regularization parameters by introducing L1 regularization and 10-fold cross-validation [18]. Next, a random forest model was constructed using the ‘randomForest’ package, and 500 decision trees were used to ensure the stability of the results [19]. The importance scores of each gene were calculated, and genes with scores greater than 10 were selected as feature genes. Subsequently, an SVM model with a linear kernel was used for initial training using the SVM-RFE method of the ‘e1071’ package [20]. This algorithm iteratively removed the least important features based on their contribution to the classification margin, as determined by the weight vector of the SVM. Performance was evaluated using 10-fold cross-validation, and the gene set with the highest classification accuracy and lowest error was retained. Finally, the feature genes screened by each of the three methods were combined and synthesized to obtain the final feature gene set. Model performance metrics, including cross-validated classification accuracy, minimum error rate, and AUC values (for selected genes), are provided to support the reliability of the selected features.

Kaplan–Meier survival analysis

A total of 164 HER2-positive breast cancer samples were selected from the TCGA-BRCA cohort for survival analysis, based on the HER2 immunohistochemistry receptor status being marked as ‘positive’. We compared the differences in overall survival (OS) at different expression levels of disease-related genes by Kaplan-Meier survival curves and log-rank tests of HER2-positive patients.

Difference significance analysis and ROC curve analysis

We collected gene expression data from tumor tissues and paracancerous tissues of the patients and compared the differences in the expression of each gene in the two groups of tissues using the Wilcoxon test. In addition, ROC curves were analyzed for these genes, and classifiers were constructed to distinguish between tumor and paracancerous tissues. By evaluating the ROC curves and AUC values, we assessed the performance of the model and found that it has potential clinical applications in the classification of tumors and paracancerous tissues.

Immune checkpoint and immune microenvironment analysis

Pearson correlation analysis was executed on signature genes and immune checkpoint genes (e.g., TMIGD2, CD276, TNFRSF8) to explore the relationship between the gene and immunotherapy. In addition, the ESTIMATE algorithm is a computational method for assessing the tumor immune microenvironment [21]. This method calculates the ESTIMATE Score by analyzing gene expression data from tumor tissues, and this score can reflect the overall content of immune and microenvironmental cells in tumor samples.

Drug sensitivity analysis

We performed predictive analysis of drug sensitivity based on gene expression data using the “oncoPredict” package [22]. The differences in drug sensitivity between high and low ILF2 expression groups were compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test.

Tumor tissue samples

Tumor tissue samples used in this study were obtained from the Department of Pathology, Zhuzhou Central Hospital. All samples were collected from HER2-positive breast cancer patients prior to any systemic treatment, ensuring that the tissues reflected the untreated tumor state. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients before sample collection. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Zhuzhou Hospital Affiliated to Xiangya School of Medicine (Approval No.: KY20241107-01).

Histological and immunohistochemical analysis

Tumor tissues and adjacent non-tumorous tissues were obtained from six patients diagnosed with HER2-positive breast cancer. The tissues were fixed in 10% formalin, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at 4 μm thickness. Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) staining was performed to observe histological morphology. The sections were deparaffinized in xylene, rehydrated through graded alcohols, and stained with hematoxylin for 5 min. After rinsing in water, the sections were differentiated in 1% alcohol hydrochloride, blued in ammonia water, and counterstained with eosin for 2 min. Following dehydration through graded alcohols and clearing in xylene, the sections were mounted with coverslips.

For immunohistochemistry (IHC), sections were deparaffinized, rehydrated, and subjected to antigen retrieval by heating in a citrate buffer (pH 6.0) at 95 °C for 20 min. Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked by incubating sections with 3% hydrogen peroxide for 10 min. The sections were then incubated with a primary antibody against ILF2 (Abcam, diluted 1:500) at 4 °C overnight. After washing, sections were incubated with a biotinylated secondary antibody, followed by streptavidin-horseradish peroxidase. The chromogen diaminobenzidine (DAB) was used for visualization, and hematoxylin was used for counterstaining. The stained sections were dehydrated, cleared, and mounted. ILF2 expression was quantitatively analyzed using ImageJ software [23].

Results

SMR analysis identified 14 disease-associated genes

Aimed at determining the key genes causally related to HER2-positive breast cancer, the SMR analysis was performed using summary-level GWAS data from the FinnGen cohort, which included 10,404 HER2-positive breast cancer cases and 182,678 controls. The resulting Manhattan plot revealed 14 genes with significant single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) (Fig. 1, filtered with criteria: P_SMR < 0.05, FDR > 0.05, and P_HEIDI > 0.05). Among them, the largest number of significant SNPs occurred on chromosome 1, including BEST4 (rs6704436), S100A13 (rs7345), S100A1 (rs28510471), SNAPIN (rs3806234), ILF2 (rs10908848), and LAMTOR5 (rs3768460). Additionally, APOBEC3A, SLC22A5, and TNNT3 exhibited the most significant associations. The significant associations of these genes suggest their potential importance in the pathogenesis of HER2-positive cancer.

Each point represents a gene whose position is determined by chromosomal position (horizontal axis), while its height reflects statistical significance associated with the disease (vertical axis). The analysis was based on summary-level GWAS data from the FinnGen cohort, comprising 10,404 HER2-positive breast cancer cases and 182,678 controls

Identification of feature genes for HER2-positive breast cancer patients

Differential expression analysis was performed on 14 HER2-positive breast cancer–associated genes identified by SMR analysis, using gene expression data from 164 HER2-positive tumor samples and adjacent normal tissues obtained from the TCGA cohort. The comparison between tumor and paracancerous tissues served as the response variable to identify genes with significant expression differences. Among these, 11 genes showed significant aberrant expression in HER2-positive tumor tissues compared with paracancerous tissues (Figure S1A, |log2FC|>1, P < 0.05). Subsequently, feature selection was conducted on these 11 genes using three machine learning algorithms: LASSO, Random Forest, and SVM-RFE. First, the LASSO algorithm was utilized to screen nine genes at the minimum cross-validation error (Fig. 2A and Figure S1B), achieving a cross-validated classification accuracy and ROC AUC of 1.0, with a minimum error of 0.1117. Second, the Random Forest algorithm identified four feature genes with importance scores greater than 10. The error rate stabilized when 52 trees were used, and the model achieved a cross-validated classification accuracy of 0.9675, a cross-validated ROC AUC of 0.9913, and a minimum out-of-bag (OOB) error rate of 0.0289 (Fig. 2B and Figure S1C). Finally, the SVM-RFE algorithm was performed with 10-fold cross-validation, and the model reached the highest accuracy (0.986), ROC AUC of 1.0, and the lowest cross-validated error rate (0.0144) when 9 genes were selected (Fig. 2C, D). The results obtained by the three algorithms were intersected and jointly screened for four important signature genes: SLC16A3, ILF2, ARRDC3, and SNAPIN (Fig. 2E). These signature genes may play key roles in HER2-positive breast cancer.

ILF2 identified as a prognostic feature gene

Based on transcriptomic and clinical data from the TCGA-BRCA cohort, we performed Kaplan–Meier survival analysis on 14 disease-associated genes identified by SMR, using 164 HER2-positive breast cancer samples selected according to HER2 immunohistochemistry status. The analysis revealed that four genes were significantly associated with patient prognosis. Specifically, high expression of APOBEC3A and NEK10 was associated with a better prognosis, while high expression of ILF2 and LAMTOR5 was linked to a poorer prognosis (Fig. 3A–D, log-rank test: P < 0.05). Moreover, the Wilcoxon test results indicated that APOBEC3A, ILF2, and LAMTOR5 were significantly upregulated in the tumor tissues, whereas NEK10 was found to be downregulated in the tumor tissues (Fig. 3E–H, P < 0.05). The ROC curves of the four genes also showed that ILF2 (AUC: 0.908), NEK10 (AUC: 0.728), LAMTOR5 (AUC: 0.724) and APOBEC3A (AUC: 0.652) were all good classifiers of tumor/paraneoplastic tissues in HER2-positive patients, and the best was ILF2 (Fig. 3I–L). ILF2 is both a prognostically relevant gene and a signature gene with the optimal tumor/paracarcinoma classification, so we used ILF2 as a research target to further investigate the influence of ILF2 on the development of HER2-positive breast cancer.

Characterization of four prognostic genes. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis (A–D), significance of difference analysis (E–H), and ROC curve for normal/tumor classification (I–L)

ILF2 expression level and immune landscape

The line chart depicting ILF2 expression demonstrates that its expression level in tumor tissues is consistently higher compared to that in adjacent non-cancerous tissues (Fig. 4A); the difference analysis of paired samples also shows that ILF2 is significantly higher in tumor tissues of HER2-positive patients than in para-cancerous tissues (Fig. 4B, P < 0.05). ILF2 has a significant correlation with 12 immune checkpoint genes: ILF2 is positively correlated with CD276, and negatively correlated with TMIGD2, TNFRSF8, TNFRSF14, etc. (Fig. 4C, P < 0.05). This indicates that ILF2 may promote immune evasion and inhibit immune responses by regulating the expression of multiple immune checkpoints and receptors in the tumor immune microenvironment. The ILF2 high expression group had significantly lower StromalScore and ESTIMATEScore (Fig. 4D, P < 0.05), indicating that high ILF2 expression may be related to lower stromal cells and overall microenvironmental cell content. This observation suggests that ILF2 likely plays a crucial role in tumor immune evasion and regulation of the microenvironment, and may promote tumor progression and deterioration by reducing the number or activity of stromal and immune cells.

Genomic insights of ILF2 regulation

A scatter plot of SMR analysis reveals a negative regulatory effect of the ILF2 (ENSG00000143621) gene, specifically the top SNP rs10908848, in HER2-positive breast cancer (Fig. 5A). This suggests a potential role for ILF2 in downregulating disease. Additionally, the chromosomal location map illustrates the genomic background of ILF2 and neighboring genes S100A13 (ENSG00000189171), S100A1 (ENSG00000160678), and SNAPIN (ENSG00000143553), highlighting important SNPs near them (Fig. 5B). Notably, rs10908848 became an important locus related to ILF2, indicating that the interaction between ILF2 and adjacent genes may have a potential regulatory impact on HER2-positive breast cancer.

Drug sensitivity analysis of ILF2

Compared with the low-ILF2 expression group, the ILF2 with high expressed was more sensitive to the drugs ABT737, BMS-754,807, and PRT062607; significantly less sensitive to AZD5991, BMS-345,541, Carmustine, Dinaciclib, LCL161, ML323, OSI-027, Paclitaxel and UMI-77 (Fig. 6, P < 0.05). The significantly lower sensitivity to chemotherapeutic drugs including Paclitaxel (Fig. 6J), a commonly used chemotherapeutic drug for breast cancer, in the case of high ILF2 expression, may indicate that tumor cells with high ILF2 expression are more resistant to these drugs, indicating that ILF2 likely plays a significant role in the mechanism of tumor drug resistance. In the clinic, perhaps detecting ILF2 expression levels can help predict patients’ response to chemotherapy and guide the selection of individualized treatment regimens to improve therapeutic efficacy.

Quantitative analysis of ILF2 expression

All six patients’ tumor tissues were confirmed to be HER2-positive through immunohistochemical staining. Quantitative analysis using ImageJ revealed that ILF2 expression was significantly higher in tumor tissues compared to adjacent non-tumorous tissues (Fig. 7A). The immunohistochemical staining intensity and distribution pattern of ILF2 were markedly more pronounced in the tumor sections, suggesting a potential role of ILF2 in the tumorigenesis or progression of HER2-positive breast cancer. Statistical analysis showed that the mean ILF2 expression level in tumor tissues was significantly elevated (p < 0.0001, Fig. 7B) compared to adjacent non-tumorous tissues.

Discussion

Previous studies utilizing GWASs have identified single nucleotide polymorphisms in 94 genes associated with breast cancer [24]. Of these, more than 20% of the identified SNPs were heritable in breast cancer [25]. Therefore, further study of SNPs may modify the risk associated with breast cancer.

This study utilized SMR analysis on GWAS data from HER2-positive breast cancer patients and identified 14 disease-associated genes. The highest frequency of SNPs occurred on chromosome 1. Subsequently, using three machine learning algorithms, diagnostic models for identifying tumor and adjacent tissues was constructed based on these genes, resulting in the identification of four feature genes: SLC16A3, ILF2, ARRDC3, and SNAPIN.

Patients with HER2-positive breast cancer had the highest number of SNPs on chromosome 1, reflecting the importance of specific genes or gene regions on chromosome 1 in this cancer. The study found that the SNPs BEST4 (rs6704436), S100A13 (rs7345), S100A1 (rs28510471), SNAPIN (rs3806234), ILF2 (rs10908848), and LAMTOR5 (rs3768460) were associated with disease progression in HER2-positive breast cancer associated.BEST4 and S100A family proteins (S100A13 and S100A1) are involved in calcium signaling pathway regulation, SNAPIN affects vesicle trafficking, and ILF2 plays a gene expression regulation role, and LAMTOR5 is a key component of the mTOR signaling pathway [26–31]. These SNPs may promote the aggressiveness and progression of HER2-positive breast cancer by regulating the expression or function of related genes, providing potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets that can help improve personalized treatment strategies.

Meanwhile, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was performed on 14 genes, and ILF2, NEK10, LAMTOR5 and APOBEC3A were significantly associated with prognosis. Finally, we chose ILF2, a disease signature gene associated with prognosis, for in-depth exploration. SNPs of ILF2 were significantly negatively correlated with the disease; high ILF2 expression was associated with lower immunoreactivity and drug sensitivity.

Among the predictive genes (ILF2, NEK10, LAMTOR5 & APOBEC3A) in HER2-positive breast cancer patients, NEK10 is a NEK family kinase, known for its crucial involvement in biological processes such as cell cycle regulation [32], cell division and DNA damage repair. Studies have shown that NEK10 activation promotes MEK1 activation, leading to G2/M phase blockade and ERK1/2 phosphorylation, thereby influencing the proliferation and growth of breast cancer cells. Elevated NEK10 expression is positively associated with in the prognosis of breast cancer patients [33]. The strongest signaling from NEK10, rs10510592 ( L513S) is located in the breast cancer susceptibility region and showed strong evidence of association with locus (rs4973768), indicating an association with breast cancer risk [34, 35]. This suggests that further study of the NEK10 gene may be able to better elucidate the mechanisms of breast cancer development. Additionally, germline copy number deletion polymorphisms in the APOBEC3 gene cluster are linked to an increased risk of breast cancer [36].APOBEC3A, a DNA editing enzyme, is expressed at a low level in breast cancers with mutations in PIK3CA and shows a relatively weak positive correlation with sPD-L1 [37].LAMTOR5, also known as HBXIP, activates the oncogenic transcription of multiple transcription factors and accelerates breast cancer progression.LAMTOR5 promotes breast cancer cell migration by regulating IL-8 transcription through NF-κB [38]. Breast cancer patients with high expression of LAMTOR5 have a poorer prognosis [39].

ILF2 (Interleukin Enhancer Binding Factor 2) is a transcription factor involved in a wide range of biological processes, including gene transcription regulation, RNA splicing, and mRNA stability [30]. ILF2, as a transcription factor, regulates gene transcription, RNA splicing, and mRNA stability through the modulation of the LINC00571/HNRNPK/ILF2/IDH2 axis expression. Its influence on the growth and metastasis of breast cancer cells contributes to the development and progression of breast cancer [11, 40]. Research has indicated that the expression level of ILF2 is associated with the inhibition of apoptosis and the proliferation of breast cancer cells. ILF2 may affect the viability of breast cancer cells by regulating the expression of apoptosis-related genes, thereby promoting tumor growth and proliferation [41]. ILF2 may also play a role in regulating the metabolism and drug resistance of breast cancer cells Studies have shown that there is an interaction between ILF2 and lncRNA H19, which is involved in DNA damage repair and sensitivity to PARP inhibitors in breast cancer cells by regulating the stability of BRCA1 [42]. Overexpression of ILF2 may lead to resistance to therapeutic drugs in breast cancer cells, consequently impacting the effectiveness of treatment and prognosis.

In this study, we screened and validated signature genes associated with HER2-positive breast cancer by various analytical methods, and in particular, we identified the critical role of ILF2 in tumor immunity and drug response. The negative correlation between SNPs of ILF2 and disease and its high expression with low immunoreactivity and drug sensitivity suggest that ILF2 may influence tumor progression and therapeutic by modulating the tumor microenvironment and immune escape mechanism response. Future studies could further explore the mechanism of ILF2 in HER2-positive breast cancer and evaluate its viability as a therapeutic target.

Although this study identified and characterized ILF2 as an important prognostic biomarker and potential therapeutic target for HER2-positive breast cancer by Mendelian randomization and machine learning techniques, some limitations remain. First, the GWAS samples were all from Europe, which may not be sufficient to fully represent the diversity of different populations. Second, the heterogeneity of data sources (GWAS and transcriptomic data from different databases) may affect the accuracy and reproducibility of the results. Third, the use of paracancerous tissues as controls may introduce bias, as these tissues can harbor molecular and microenvironmental changes influenced by the adjacent tumor, potentially confounding the identification of tumor-specific gene signatures and affecting the machine learning outcomes, with the perfect AUC scores (AUC = 1) suggesting a possible overfitting risk that warrants external validation. In addition, although bioinformatics analyses were utilized, further laboratory validation was lacking, especially experimental studies of ILF2 on specific mechanisms of action. This study focused primarily on ILF2 and may have overlooked the role of other important genes. Finally, the immune infiltration and drug sensitivity analyses were based on predictive bioinformatics models, lacking direct clinical or in vitro validation. Therefore, future studies should expand the sample size, perform rigorous laboratory validation, explore multigene interactions, and validate the effectiveness of ILF2 as a therapeutic target through clinical trials to more comprehensively understand its role in HER2-positive breast cancer and develop more effective individualized therapeutic regimens.

Conclusion

This study combined Mendelian randomization and machine learning techniques to provide insight into the role of ILF2 in HER2-positive breast cancer. We investigated that high ILF2 expression was associated with poor prognosis, decreased immune activity, and reduced drug sensitivity. This finding provides new insights and potential targets for breast cancer treatment.

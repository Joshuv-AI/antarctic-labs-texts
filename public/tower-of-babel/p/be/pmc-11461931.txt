Nonlinear behavior and energy harvesting performance of a new tunable quasi-zero stiffness system
Authors: Xinzong Wang, Xiaofang Kang, Ao Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Energy harvesting from vibrations is a popular research topic. However, it is difficult to change the values of its dynamic stiffness characteristics during the operation of device. This paper analyzes the nonlinear dynamical behavior and energy harvesting performance of an energy harvesting device with a gear unit and an inertial amplifier. The Melnikov method is used to discriminate the chaos behavior of the system and is illustrated by numerical simulations. Chaotic dynamics analysis provides a simplified analytical idea that offers more insight into the performance of this energy harvesting device. In addition, the basins of attraction of the coexistence solution of the system are plotted, and the trajectory and energy harvesting efficiency of the system are analyzed for changes in the initial value. The results show that the device has the convenience to change the form and efficiency of system energy harvesting. The theory of Melnikov functions recognizes the presence of chaos and can provide solutions for the parameterization of the system. The choice of initial value greatly affects the energy harvesting efficiency.

Introduction

As a form of energy widely existing in the environment, vibration has diversity and continuity, and it is also a kind of green energy, which should be fully utilized. Therefore, many researchers at home and abroad are committed to studying how to convert and collect vibration energy1–4. With the development of microelectromechanical systems technology, a variety of radio equipment is towards miniaturization, intelligence, multifunctionality, low-power direction of high-speed development5, and is widely used in biomedical, portable electronic products, navigation systems, environmental monitoring, military and aerospace, high-speed railway network and other aspects6. Piezoelectric7,8, electromagnetic9,10 and electrostatic11,12 types can fulfil this energy conversion function.

Piezoelectric sensing devices are designed to convert the deformation produced when a piezoelectric material is subjected to a mechanical force into electrical energy. Different kinds of piezoelectric effects exist in piezoelectric materials13–15: the positive piezoelectric effect is the effect that occurs when a piezoelectric material is subjected to a force in a specific direction, the material will undergo deformation leading to polarization phenomenon within the material, and the surface of the equal amount of the opposite sign of the polarization charge, within a certain range, the charge and the pressure is a linear increase in the relationship between the force is withdrawn, the surface and gradually return to the uncharged state; the surface is not charged; and the surface is not charged, but it is not charged. The inverse piezoelectric effect refers to the fact that when a piezoelectric material is placed in the electric field, the piezoelectric material not only undergoes polarization, but also undergoes deformation, which generates stress and strain, the magnitude of which increases linearly with the strength of the applied electric field within a certain range. According to the current research progress, the main research areas of piezoelectric sensors are Electro-crystalline materials field16–18, the field of lossy states of piezoelectric sensors19,20, the design of piezoelectric sensors21–24, piezoelectric sensors in the field of integrated systems25,26, and so on27–29. In addition, the building blocks of piezoelectric sensors are widely used in practical engineering applications30,31.

In nature, particularly engineering fields, most scientific problems are intrinsically nonlinear, such as the Mathieu oscillator in the parametrically excited pendulum32, the Duffing oscillator in the coupled pitch-roll ship33 and the Van der Pol oscillator in the vacuum tube circuit34, etc. Chaos is a universal phenomenon existing in the nonlinear field and also a universal phenomenon in nature35–38. So, in engineering practice, the system will inevitably appear chaos phenomenon and cannot work properly, which has a great impact on the production and life. We hope that the system can work stably and can control the system well so that it can work according to people’s requirements. The results of theoretical research work in chaos science in terms of detection methods for chaos39, control methods for chaos40, statistical characterization of chaotic signals41, and identification and separation of chaotic signals42 continue to emerge.

Nonlinear energy harvesting devices have the potential for efficient energy harvesting performance and have therefore received a lot of attention in the field of energy harvesting. Changes in the nonlinear dynamic stiffness and potential energy of the system change the energy harvesting performance of the system43. Energy harvesting devices with bi-stable and tri-stable potential energy functions44–46 are known to possess high energy harvesting performance from the current research results. Compared to monostable and bistable energy harvesting devices, tri-stable energy harvesters (TEH) produce more complex nonlinear behavior. The TEH of the magnetized piezoelectric beam exhibits a wide operating band47. Ma and Zhou48,49 evaluated the occurrence of chaotic motion in a TEH energy harvester based on the Melnikov function and verified it through numerical simulations. Although these designs are very effective, it is difficult to regulate the system effectively when changes in extrinsic incentives arise. This is because they cannot solve the problem of regulating the system during operation.

In this paper, two devices are introduced to facilitate parameter adjustment50. The gearing can change the dynamic stiffness and the form of potential energy of the system. Inertial amplifiers51 can change the dynamic effective mass of the system and thus the operating frequency band of the system. The pathways and critical thresholds of the system to chaos are obtained using the semi-analytical Melnikov method. The RMS value of the induced voltage is introduced to analyze the energy harvesting efficiency of the system and to identify the nonlinear behavior of the system. In addition, the pattern of change in the coexistence solution and its effect on the energy harvesting efficiency of the system is analyzed in the light of the system’s basins of attraction.

In this section, we go through the Melnikov leapfrog theory and define homoclinic and/or heteroclinic orbitals. Due to the presence of single or multiple potential wells in the system, we propose a semi-analytical approach55, in which the relevant equations and integrals are solved numerically by analysing the corresponding stable and unstable manifolds and their crossovers, in order to determine the conditions for unperturbed and perturbed orbits.

Many methods are used to evaluate the nonlinear kinematic properties of the system, among which the largest Lyapunov exponent λ is widely used for the identification of the nonlinear behaviour of the system57. The efficiency of the system’s energy harvesting (RMS value) can also be evaluated based on the multicolour plot distribution of the system’s largest Lyapunov exponent.

Multicolour plots of the λ distribution are plotted under the assumption that the initial displacements, velocities, and voltages of the system are zero. In order to obtain a clearer image, we divided the horizontal and vertical coordinates into 400 intervals. As shown in Fig. 7, two control parameters, i.e., \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:F$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\omega\:$$\end{document}, are selected for plotting the largest Lyapunov exponential multicolour map. They are drawn at different rotation angles. When λ > 0, the system behaves as a chaotic state; when λ = 0, the system enters a critical bifurcation state (critical position of chaos provided by the Melnikov function); and when λ < 0, the system enters a mode of periodic motion.

Conclusions

This paper describes an adjustable nonlinear energy harvesting device with a gear rotation device and an inertial amplification device. In order to further investigate the performance of this energy harvesting device, in this paper, Melnikov’s method is used to obtain the necessary conditions for the existence of chaos, which is verified and illustrated by numerical simulations. The RMS value of the induced voltage is introduced to analyze the energy harvesting performance of the system as well as to identify the nonlinear behavior of the system. In addition, basins of attraction were plotted to analyze the sensitivity of the system to initial value conditions in each frequency band region. The numerical results show that:

The device has a great deal of convenience. The geometrical parameters of the system can be altered by adjusting the angle of rotation of the gears, which results in a change in the dynamic stiffness and the form of the potential energy of the system. By changing the initial angle of the inertial amplification device, the dynamic effective mass of the system can be adjusted, thus changing the intrinsic frequency and energy peak region of the system.

The Melnikov method can be analyzed to discriminate the chaotic behavior of the system and provide criteria for the design of the system’s parameters. There is a correlation between the RMS value of the induced voltage of the system and the nonlinear state of motion of the system. In the vicinity of the “fault” in the RMS value, the nonlinear behavior of the system is highly susceptible to change. This is related to the chaos threshold and escape phenomena obtained through the Melnikov method. There is a correlation between the maximum Lyapunov exponent and the effectiveness of energy harvesting, which can also be determined by the Melnikov function.

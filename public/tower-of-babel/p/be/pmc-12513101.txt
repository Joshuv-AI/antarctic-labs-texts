Polymerase theta inhibition impairs tumor growth and amplifies melphalan-induced DNA damage in multiple myeloma
Authors: Qun Li, Chi Ma, Liping Zuo, Aoshuang Xu, Bo Zhang, Fei Zhao, Lifei Liu, Xin Zhao, Yu Hu, Chunyan Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe dysregulation of the DNA damage response (DDR) plays a vital role in cancer development and inadequate response to anti-cancer therapy. We investigated the involvement of DNA polymerase theta (Polθ), the principal component of the microhomology-mediated end joining (MMEJ) pathway, in the pathogenesis of multiple myeloma (MM).MethodsWe explored the expression levels of Polθ and assessed its prognostic relevance in a cohort of 124 MM patients at our institution. We demonstrated the impacts of Polθ inhibition on genomic stability and cell viability in MM using shRNA and a pharmacological inhibitor. To elucidate the role of Polθ-mediated MMEJ in repairing melphalan-induced DNA damage, we employed Western blot and EJ2-GFP reporter assay to evaluate the activation of the MMEJ pathway following melphalan exposure. We analyzed the synergistic cytotoxicity between the Polθ inhibitor and melphalan in MM, both in vitro and in vivo. The impacts of combination therapy on genomic damage and mitotic catastrophe were examined by comet assay, Western blot, and immunofluorescence.ResultsWe revealed that Polθ was markedly upregulated in MM. The enhanced expression of Polθ was found to be significantly associated with advanced disease stages and adverse clinical outcomes. Polθ inhibition promoted genomic instability and impaired MM growth, underscoring the polymerase's function in sustaining myeloma survival under ongoing genomic stress. Furthermore, we observed a further increase in Polθ expression and MMEJ repair efficiency following melphalan treatment. Polθ inhibitors sensitized MM cells to melphalan by increasing unrepaired DNA damage and inducing mitotic catastrophe, both in vitro and in vivo.ConclusionsCollectively, our results confirm Polθ as a promising prognostic biomarker and highlight its potential to serve as a synthetic lethal target that can be used in combination with melphalan for the treatment of MM.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-025-07065-2.

Introduction

Multiple myeloma (MM) is an aggressive hematologic malignancy characterized by clonal proliferation of plasma cells. Despite the significant improvement in prognosis in the past two decades, MM remains incurable with multiple remissions and relapses [1, 2]. Genomic instability is a well-recognized hallmark of MM, manifesting as frequent occurrences of chromosomal instability, microsatellite instability, and an increased mutation rate [3, 4]. Although genomic alterations provide an evolutionary advantage to cancer cells, the extensive DNA lesions impose considerable stress on cellular proliferation [5, 6]. In this context, myeloma cells critically depend on the complex DNA damage response (DDR) system to counteract persistent genomic perturbations, thereby maintaining a delicate equilibrium essential for their viability [7].

Dysregulation of DDR is observed in MM. Activities of both homologous recombination (HR) and classical non-homologous end joining (c-NHEJ), the two faithful repair pathways, are elevated in MM; however, it seems still insufficient in the context of ongoing replication stress and severe genomic damage [8, 9]. Therefore, MM cells exhibit a marked dependency on compensatory repair mechanisms to sustain genomic instability tolerance [5]. The microhomology-mediated end joining (MMEJ) pathway is an error-prone backup DNA repair pathway. It accounts for a small minority of DNA repair in normal cells but is overactive in a variety of malignancies [10–14]. The flawed outcome of MMEJ often derives from the activity of DNA polymerase theta (Polθ, encoded by the POLQ gene), a predominant mediator of the pathway with low fidelity during DNA gap synthesis [15, 16]. Unlike other canonical DDR targets such as ataxia-telangiectasia and Rad3 related (ATR) kinase, ataxia-telangiectasia mutated (ATM) kinase, and DNA-dependent protein kinase (DNA-PK), which serve as core components of the DDR machinery in healthy cells, Polθ is frequently overexpressed in cancers but minimally expressed in normal tissue [15]. This tumor-selective expression pattern positions Polθ as a promising therapeutic target [17]. Recent studies have identified Polθ as a synthetic lethal target in tumors with deficiencies in HR or c-NHEJ [18–21]. However, it remains unclear whether Polθ contributes to the pathogenesis of neoplasms like MM, which typically retain functional HR and c-NHEJ pathways.

Capitalizing on the intrinsic genomic instability of MM, disrupting the fragile balance to cause lethal DNA damage remains a potent therapeutic approach in MM [7]. Melphalan is recognized as the first effective agent introduced for MM and retains its pivotal role to the present day [22, 23]. Melphalan is an alkylating agent that exerts its function by inducing interstrand crosslinks (ICLs) and DNA–protein crosslinks (DPCs) [22, 24, 25]. While chemotherapy-induced DNA damage kills a significant portion of myeloma cells, the cytotoxic stress can also trigger compensatory activation of DDR in surviving cells, potentially leading to suboptimal treatment outcomes [26]. Notably, such stress-adapted survival mechanisms are generally dependent on error-prone DNA repair pathway activation [15]. The resultant mutagenic repair could drive genome plasticity and enhance tumor heterogeneity, thereby promoting cancer progression. To overcome this adaptation, therapeutic strategies combining cytotoxic agents with DDR inhibitors that specifically target error-prone repair components may establish context-specific synthetic lethality in MM [15, 27, 28].

In this study, we revealed that Polθ was highly expressed in MM and associated with poor clinical outcomes. Polθ-mediated MMEJ pathway was required for coping with spontaneous genomic instability and guaranteeing MM cells’ survival. Moreover, we demonstrated that Polθ was also involved in the repair of melphalan-induced DNA damage. Inhibition of Polθ synergized with melphalan by promoting the accumulation of unrepaired DNA damage and inducing mitotic catastrophe in MM.

Materials and methods

Patients specimens and clinical data collection

Bone marrow (BM) aspirates were collected from 124 newly diagnosed multiple myeloma (NDMM) patients admitted to Wuhan Union Hospital, as well as from 14 normal donors undergoing aspiration for unrelated conditions. Primary plasma cells were purified from the specimens using CD138 microbeads (130-051-301, Miltenyi Biotec, Germany), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The clinical information was collected retrospectively by reviewing the patients’ medical records. The MM cohort was divided into POLQ-high and POLQ-low subgroups based on the median POLQ mRNA expression value.

Cell lines

The human MM cell lines OPM-2, JJN-3 and L-363 were obtained from DSMZ (Braunschweig, Germany), KMS-11 was acquired from JCRB (Osaka, Japan), other MM cell lines and HEK 293 T cells were purchased from ATCC (Manassas, VA, USA). HEK 293 T cells were cultured in DMEM and MM cell lines were cultured in RPMI-1640, supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS). All cells were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2.

Establishment of stable knockdown (KD) MM cell lines

Lentiviral particles were produced by Obio Technology Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China). L-363 and RPMI-8226 cells were transfected with POLQ-targeting shRNA lentiviral vector (shPOLQ) or a scrambled shRNA vector (shNC) in a medium containing the corresponding transfection reagent. Cells were cultured with the lentiviral vector for 12 h, following which the medium was replaced with fresh RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% FBS. After an additional 48 h, the cells were selected using puromycin. The targeting sequence of shPOLQ was CGTCGTCTCATTCAAGTGTTA.

Cell viability

The drugs used in this study included Novobiocin (NVB, S2492, Selleck, USA), ART558 (HY-141520, MedChemExpress, USA) and melphalan (MB1476, Meilunbio, China).

For the proliferation assay, MM cells transfected with shRNA were seeded in 96-well plates at 1 × 104 cells/well and incubated for 0–96 h. For the dose–response assay, MM cells were seeded in 96-well plates and cultured for 72 h in the presence of 0–300 μM NVB. For the combination experiment, MM cells were treated with DMSO, NVB (or ART558), melphalan, or their combination. RPMI-8226 cells were treated for 48 h, while L363 and KMS-11 cells were treated for 24 h. For the cytotoxicity analysis, peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated from three healthy volunteers and were seeded in 96-well plates at 1 × 104 cells/well. Cells were then treated with DMSO, the NVB (or ART558), melphalan or their combination for 24 h.

Cell viability was assessed by measuring absorbance at 450 nm after the addition of 10 μL Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8, BS350, Biosharp Life Science, China) for 4 h. Bliss synergy analysis was calculated using Combenefit software. A Bliss score greater than zero indicated a synergistic interaction between the tested compounds.

Flow cytometry

The apoptosis of stable Polθ knockdown MM cell lines was assessed according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Annexin V-APC/7-AAD apoptosis kit, MultiSciences, China). An Annexin V-FITC/PI apoptosis assay (BD Bioscience) was adopted to analyze the apoptosis of MM cell lines and primary CD138+ cells treated with drugs. Apoptosis was detected by flow cytometry (FACSAria™ III, BD Bioscience, USA) and analyzed with FlowJo software.

Real-time quantitative reverse transcription PCR (RT-qPCR) and Western blot

RT-qPCR and Western blot analysis were performed as previously described [29]. The primers for target genes and the antibodies used are shown in Supplementary Methods.

Immunofluorescence

MM cells were harvested following the designated treatment and immobilized on poly-D-lysine-coated slides. The cells were then fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, permeabilized with 0.5% Triton X-100, and blocked with 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA) before incubation with the primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight. The following day, the cells were incubated with secondary antibodies for 1 h. The samples were then mounted under coverslips with ProLongTM Glass Antifade Mountant (P36985, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA).

Alkaline comet assay

The comet assay was conducted following protocols provided by the manufacturer (CA001, ELK Biotechnology, China). MM cells were harvested after being treated with DMSO, NVB, melphalan, or their combination and embedded in agarose on slides. After lysis for 1 h, electrophoresis was performed at 25 V for 30 min under alkaline conditions. The slides were subsequently stained with 10 μL of PI for 10 min. The nuclear staining of DNA was captured using a fluorescence microscope, and the tail moment was measured using CaspLab software. At least 50 cells from three independent experiments were quantified per group.

EJ2-GFP reporter assay

The EJ2-GFP reporter assay was performed as previously described [30, 31]. Briefly, EJ2-GFP plasmid (#44025, Addgene) and I-SceI-mCherry plasmid (Genechem Company, Shanghai, China) were co-transfected into 5×104 HEK-293T cells at a 1.25 μg:1.25 μg ratio. At 24 h post-transfection, the medium was replaced with fresh medium containing designated drugs. At 48 h post-treatment, the number of GFP+and mCherry+ cells was quantified by flow cytometry. A minimum of 10,000 cells were analyzed. MMEJ repair (%) = (GFP+ mCherry+ cells %) / (mCherry⁺ cells %), where mCherry+ cells define the transfected population, ensuring normalization for transfection efficiency.

Animal experiments

For the MM xenograft model, 8 × 106 RPMI-8226 cells were injected subcutaneously into the right flank region of 6-week-old female M-NSG mice (Shanghai Model Organisms, China). When the tumors became measurable, the mice were randomized into four groups and treated with saline, NVB, melphalan, or a combination. A total of two treatment cycles were given, with each cycle consisting of 6 days of treatment followed by 1 day of rest. NVB (100 mg/kg, i.p.) was administered once daily on days 1, 2, 4, and 5; melphalan (2 mg/kg, i.p.) was administered once daily on days 3 and 6 of each cycle. Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were presented as counts and percentages and were compared using Chi-square test, Fisher’s exact test, or Mann–Whitney U test. Continuous variables were analyzed using Student's t/Mann–Whitney U (two groups) or ANOVA/Kruskal–Wallis (≥ 3 groups) tests. The survival curves were drawn using the Kaplan–Meier method, and the difference in overall survival (OS) or progression-free survival (PFS) between subgroups was assessed with a log-rank test. The Cox proportional hazards model was employed to conduct univariate and multivariate analysis. Public cohorts GSE24080, GSE4204 and MMRF-CoMMpass were also used for survival analysis. Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism (version 8.3.0) and R (version 4.2.1). All in vitro experiments were repeated three times. The data represent the mean ± SD. Results with P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Polθ is highly expressed in MM and could be used as a prognostic biomarker

To investigate the role of Polθ in the pathogenesis of MM, we first assessed the expression level of Polθ in MM cells. We observed a notable upregulation of POLQ mRNA level in CD138+ plasma cells from 124 MM patients compared to 14 normal controls (Fig. 1A). Moreover, we confirmed that the expression of Polθ protein was also elevated in both primary myeloma cells and MM cell lines (Fig. 1B, C). We subsequently conducted a comprehensive analysis of clinical data in 114 MM patients with available information. We revealed that a higher POLQ level was associated with more advanced International Staging System (ISS) and Revised International Staging System (R-ISS) stages (Fig. 1D, E). To explore the relationship between POLQ expression and clinical features in the MM cohort, 114 patients were divided into POLQ-low and POLQ-high groups based on the median POLQ mRNA expression value. The POLQ-high cohort displayed higher R-ISS stages (P < 0.001), increased incidence of hypoalbuminemia (P = 0.047) and myeloma bone disease (P = 0.026), as well as reduced rates of ≥ complete response (CR, P = 0.020) and ≥ very good partial response rates (VGPR, P = 0.011) compared with the POLQ-low cohort (Table 1). Kaplan–Meier survival analyses indicated that MM patients with higher POLQ expression exhibited worse PFS and OS (Fig. 1F, G). Both univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses demonstrated that elevated POLQ expression could be a prognostic predictor for PFS and OS in MM (Fig. 1H, I). We have also verified our findings in public datasets. Analysis of three independent large cohorts (GSE24080, GSE4204, and MMRF-CoMMpass cohorts) validated that higher POLQ expression predicted poorer prognosis in MM patients (Supplementary Fig. S1). Polθ inhibition promotes genomic instability and impairs MM growth

We next explored the biological function of Polθ in MM. We generated stable Polθ-knockdown cell lines by transfecting L-363 and RPMI-8226 cell lines with POLQ-targeting shRNA. The efficiency of transfection was examined by Western blot (Fig. 2A). We showed that Polθ downregulation resulted in reduced cell viability and increased apoptosis rate in MM cells (Fig. 2B, C), implying the role of Polθ in maintaining MM cell survival. To further assess the growth-inhibitory effects induced by Polθ inhibition, we treated MM cell lines with Novobiocin (NVB), a widely recognized inhibitor targeting the Polθ ATPase domain. The tested cell lines displayed differential sensitivity to NVB; however, cell proliferation was uniformly inhibited in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 2D, E). Given Polθ's established role in DDR, we hypothesized that targeting Polθ compromises MM cell survival by permitting the accumulation of spontaneous DNA damage. Our results demonstrated that Polθ suppression significantly impairs MMEJ repair efficiency, as quantified by EJ2-GFP reporter assays (Fig. 2F, G). Importantly, Polθ inhibition significantly triggered γH2A.X upregulation and activated ATR/CHK1 and ATM/CHK2 signaling (Fig. 2H, I), suggesting an overload of unresolved DNA lesions. The Polθ-mediated MMEJ is hyperactivated in response to melphalan-induced DNA damage

The activation of DDR may substantially influence the therapeutic efficacy of genotoxic agents. Our research then sought to ascertain whether Polθ-mediated MMEJ was involved in melphalan-induced DNA damage repair. We treated MM cell lines with different doses and durations of melphalan and then evaluated the Polθ expression using Western blot. We observed a further elevation of Polθ expression in MM cells after melphalan treatment (Fig. 3A, B). We also employed the EJ2-GFP assay to assess the MMEJ activity. Our results revealed an approximately 30% increase in the MMEJ repair efficiency following melphalan exposure, and this stimulation was potently abolished by the NVB co-treatment (Fig. 3C, D). Additionally, knockdown of Polθ was found to enhance the vulnerability of MM cells to melphalan treatment (Fig. 3E), implying that the increased expression of Polθ may provide a protective effect against the therapeutic challenges posed by melphalan. Polθ inhibitors and melphalan demonstrate synergistic effects in MM in vitro

We subsequently investigated the chemosensitization ability of Polθ inhibitors on the genotoxic agent melphalan. We first explored the synergy between NVB and melphalan in MM cell lines. The combined exposure of NVB and melphalan produced a highly synergistic effect across three MM cell lines, L-363, RPMI-8226, and KMS-11, as evidenced by the Bliss synergy scores calculated using the Combenefit software (Fig. 4A). To extend our findings, we further assessed the potential of ART558, an inhibitor of the Polθ polymerase domain, in promoting the lethal impact of melphalan on MM. Consistently, the synergistic interaction between the two drugs was also evident in the tested cell lines (Fig. 4B). Both NVB and ART558 improved the responsiveness of MM cells to melphalan; the combination treatment resulted in significantly reduced cell viability (Fig. 4C, D) and increased apoptosis (Fig. 4E, F and Supplementary Fig. S2A) compared to any monotherapies. We next validated the synergistic effect in patient-derived cells. Primary CD138+ cells isolated from three newly diagnosed MM patients were treated with melphalan, NVB/ART558, or their combination. Apoptosis rates were measured via flow cytometry. The results indicated that the combined treatment exerts a stronger anti-myeloma effect compared with the single treatments (Fig. 4G). Combination therapy of NVB and melphalan promotes the accumulation of DNA damage and induces mitotic catastrophe in MM cells

The co-administration of DNA damage agents and DDR inhibitors may potentiate genomic lesions and triggers synthetic lethality in tumor cells. We next performed the alkaline comet assay to measure the DNA damage in MM cells after the co-exposure to the Polθ inhibitor NVB and melphalan. The combination of NVB and melphalan significantly increased the levels of tail moment as compared to monotherapy and control groups (Fig. 5A). The finding was further confirmed by immunoblot analysis, which showed a massive elevation of γH2A.X expression under the same conditions (Fig. 5B). Moreover, we found that inhibition of Polθ by NVB could drive MM cells towards mitotic entry, which was confirmed by the upregulation of pH3, an indicator of mitosis. At the same time, the increased levels of cleaved PARP suggested that apoptosis induced by combination treatment may mainly occur in the M phase (Fig. 5B). Mitotic catastrophe is a common outcome when cells proceed into mitosis with substantial DNA damage. In order to determine whether the combined treatment-induced apoptosis resulted from mitotic catastrophe, we employed the immunofluorescence staining to detect the aberrant nuclei after the exposure to the drugs. As we expected, the elevated numbers of micronuclei and grape-shaped nuclei were observed in the combination treatment group, suggesting that this approach promoted mitotic catastrophe in MM cells (Fig. 5C). We next evaluated the therapeutic efficacy of combining NVB and melphalan in a subcutaneous mouse model of MM. The schematic diagram of the in vivo experiment is shown in Fig. 6A. Treatment was initiated once the tumors became detectable and lasted for 2 weeks. The combination therapy elicited a more pronounced reduction in tumor burden compared to monotherapies with NVB or melphalan, as evidenced by decreased tumor volume and tumor weight (Fig. 6B–D). The calculated CDI demonstrated a synergistic effect of 0.6 in vivo based on tumor weights. Moreover, no significant weight loss or pathological changes in vital organs were observed, indicating that the treatment was well tolerated by the animals (Fig. 6E, G). Additionally, the co-administration resulted in the upregulation of γH2A.X, pH3, and Cleaved Caspase-3 in subcutaneous tumors, which were consistent with our in vitro results (Fig. 6F). Discussion

MM cells exhibit exceedingly high levels of spontaneous DNA damage, demanding a robust activation of DDR to safeguard their survival [7, 34, 35]. However, the role of Polθ-mediated MMEJ in the pathophysiology of this disease has not yet been fully elucidated. In this study, we showed that Polθ was upregulated in MM, and its overexpression was associated with advanced ISS and R-ISS stages. Survival analysis indicated that elevated Polθ expression predicted adverse PFS and OS, and this negative prognostic impact was preserved following multivariate Cox regression analysis. Notably, Polθ inhibition resulted in an overload of unresolved DNA damage and reduced cell viability in MM cells, underscoring the role of Polθ in resolving endogenous genomic stress to ensure cell survival. Collectively, our findings identified Polθ as a promising therapeutic target in MM.

Despite the development of novel drugs, therapeutic strategies using melphalan, either as a conditioning regimen for autologous transplantation or in combination with other agents, remain the mainstay of treatment for MM [23]. Inadequate response to melphalan may result from enhanced DNA repair capacity. Early studies have shown that histone deacetylase inhibitors, exportin 1 inhibitors, and ABL1 tyrosine kinase inhibitors could sensitize MM cells to melphalan or reverse melphalan resistance by targeting the nucleotide excision repair, Fanconi anemia or HR pathway respectively [36–38]. It has been reported that Polθ not only participates in double-strand breaks (DSBs) repair but also contributes to the resolution of the ICLs and DPCs [39, 40]. Drawing on this evidence, we propose that Polθ may be involved in the multiple types of melphalan-induced DNA damage repair. As anticipated, our data revealed a further increase in Polθ expression and MMEJ repair activity following melphalan treatment; the knockdown of Polθ using shRNA significantly augmented the cytotoxicity of melphalan in vitro. These results suggest that Polθ-mediated MMEJ underlies the suboptimal response of MM cells to melphalan-based chemotherapy. Moreover, since Polθ is low-fidelity and error-prone [10], the mutagenic repair in response to DNA-damaging agents may enhance genome plasticity and drive chemotherapy resistance in MM.

Recent evidence has confirmed the effectiveness of Polθ inhibitors in enhancing the response of lung cancer to radiotherapy [41, 42]. Moreover, given that mutagenic KRAS activation upregulates the MMEJ pathway, Polθ is critical for KRAS-driven pancreatic tumorigenesis, suggesting a potential synthetic lethality approach by combining KRAS and Polθ inhibitors [43, 44]. Nonetheless, the role of Polθ inhibitors in modulating chemotherapy response in hematologic malignancies requires further investigation. We next examined the combined efficacy of melphalan with the Polθ inhibitors NVB and ART558 in MM treatment. The Bliss drug synergy scoring assay revealed a synergistic interaction between melphalan and both Polθ inhibitors. Notably, this synergistic effect was consistently observed in the primary patient-derived samples and subcutaneous MM xenograft mouse models. The strategy combining DNA-damaging agents with DDR inhibitors demonstrates encouraging results in preclinical studies; however, it confronts the challenge of overlapping toxicity [45–47]. Unlike other DDR targets, Polθ is rarely expressed in normal tissues but is upregulated in cancer cells, making it an ideal candidate for tumor-specific chemosensitization [15, 18, 48–51]. We confirmed that the concurrent treatment of melphalan with the Polθ inhibitor had a minimal impact on normal cells and was well tolerated in mice, highlighting the restricted toxicity of this combination regimen.

Mitotic catastrophe has long been regarded as a stage that precedes various types of cell death, resulting from the premature or inappropriate entry of cells into mitosis [52, 53]. In response to genotoxic stress induced by chemotherapy, cells undergo cell cycle arrest to initiate DNA repair and preserve genomic integrity. However, if the cells are unable to repair the lesions and are concurrently forced into the M phase, they may experience mitotic catastrophe followed by cell death [54]. We showed that Polθ inhibition not only enhanced the melphalan-induced DNA damage but also promoted premature entry into mitosis in MM cells, ultimately inducing mitotic catastrophe and apoptosis. To our knowledge, this is the first report to document such a phenomenon.

In summary, our study reveals the involvement of Polθ-mediated MMEJ pathway in resolving endogenous genomic stress. Crucially, we identify a synthetic lethal interaction between Polθ inhibition and melphalan.

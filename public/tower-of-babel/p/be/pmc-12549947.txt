Evolution of prediabetes in an urban cohort of adults living in a Mediterranean area
Authors: Carola Buscemi, Cristiana Randazzo, Anna Maria Barile, Rosalia Caldarella, Alexis Elias Malavazos, Roberta Caruso, Piero Colombrita, Martina Lombardo, Silvio Buscemi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPrediabetes (PD) precedes type 2 diabetes (T2D), it can be easily recognized by fasting plasma glucose concentrations (FPG) or HbA1c or 2 h post glucose load glycemia, thereby enabling prevention strategies. We investigated the progression of PD to T2D in the ABCD study (Alimentazione, Benessere Cardiovascolare e Diabete -ISRCTN15840340) that included a representative cohort of adult people living in the Mediterranean area of Palermo (Italy).Materials and methodsThe ABCD cohort was enrolled in 2011 and re-evaluated in 2015. The FPG, HbA1c, physical activity level and dietary habits were investigated. In 2011 participants and their family doctors were informed about their health conditions and indications concerning realistic changes for a healthier lifestyle were provided.ResultsComplete information was obtained on 742 out of 1233 individuals. In 2011, the prevalence of PD was 30.7% of which 12.7% developed T2D and 43.9% reversed to normal glucose tolerance (NGT) at follow-up. In 2015, 106 previously NGT participants developed PD. The progression as to T2D as to PD were associated with age (P < 0.001) and sex (P < 0.001). Body weight, BMI, and waist circumference were higher in people with PD than in those without PD and even higher in PD that developed T2D (P < 0.001). A sedentary lifestyle was observed in the PD and NGT subgroups which developed T2D and PD, respectively. Daily energy intake decreased among people with PD who became NGT (P < 0.001) and increased in those who developed T2D (P < 0.05). The glycemic index of diet decreased in those PD people who became NGT and in those people who maintained NGT at follow-up. The MEDILITE score, that describes the Mediterranean pattern of diet, increased significantly in PD group that became NGT and in NGT group that maintained NGT at final observation.ConclusionsThis study suggests that improving individual motivation may be an effective strategy to promote healthier lifestyles. A more physically active lifestyle and Mediterranean dietary habits are associated with a reduction of central obesity, and with a favorable evolution of glucose tolerance in PD people.Clinical trial registrationISRCTN15840340

Introduction

Concomitant with urbanization, a global spread of the prevalence of obesity and diabetes has been observed with significant costs for society and for health systems [1]. Prediabetes (PD) precedes type 2 diabetes (T2D), it can be easily recognized by fasting plasma glucose (FPG) or glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) concentrations or 2 h post-glucose load (2h-PG) glycemia [2], thereby enabling prevention strategies [3]. However, the diagnosis of PD is not homogeneous depending which diagnostic criteria is utilized, and few studies, with conflicting results, evaluated the factors associated with the evolution of PD in the general population and concluded that diet may be protective [4–6] or not [7–9]. More than 20 years ago, for the first time, the Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP) study [10] demonstrated that persons with 2h-PG glycemia in the range of actual PD definition, reduced the 4-years risk of T2D of 58% when lifestyle was improved by changes in habitual diet and physical activity level. In the Whitehall II study [11] with a median follow-up of 6.7 years, reversion to normoglycemia from FPG defined PD was 45%, but reduced to 37% and 17% when the diagnosis of PD was based on 2 h PG glycemia or HbA1c values respectively. In general, it has been estimated that about five to ten percent of individuals with prediabetes develop type 2 diabetes annually [12]; so, early detection of prediabetes may offer the possibility of intervention to prevent or delay further progression to type 2 diabetes. Furthermore, people with PD have increased risk of cardiovascular disease (CVD) and mortality [13–15]. So, reversion from PD was also associated with significant cardiovascular (CVD) risk reduction varying according to the diagnostic criteria of PD, and in the Whitehall II study [11] the CVD risk reduction was significant when PD was diagnosed by 2h-PG load glycemia.

The aim of this study was to evaluate the evolution of PD, diagnosed by FPG or HbA1c, in a representative sample of the adult general population living in Palermo (Italy), a town of the Mediterranean area. In particular, the 4-years progression of PD to T2D was investigated in relation to lifestyle changes.

Subjects and methods

Participants

The Nutrition, Cardiovascular Wellness, and Diabetes (ABCD, Alimentazione Benessere Cardiovascolare e Diabete) project (ISRCTN15840340) is a longitudinal observational single-center study of a cohort representative of the adult general population living in Palermo, the largest city in Sicily (Italy) in the Mediterranean area. The ABCD study procedures were described in detail elsewhere [16]. Briefly, the ABCD_1 study cohort was recruited in 2011, the inclusion criteria were participants aged >18 years and residing in Palermo. For this current study, individuals subsequently moved to other cities, or with a known diagnosis of diabetes, tumors, or with incomplete data or deceased, were excluded from calculations. The demographic characteristics of the ABCD cohort were similar to, if not overlapping with, those of the general population of the same age range (18–90 years) as presented elsewhere [17]. At the conclusion of the ABCD_1 study, all participants were interviewed briefly by one of the investigators. During this interview, they received a written report detailing the results of the investigations conducted. This report also highlighted any identified clinical issues and health risk factors. An analysis of eating habits was conducted based on the food frequency questionnaire, and individualized recommendations were provided to improve one’s dietary choices and potentially increase levels of physical activity. The report, along with its associated recommendations, was forwarded to the participants’ doctors. The original cohort was contacted (telephone, e-mail, letter) in 2015, and those who agreed to participate in the study were examined between March 21 and July 31 at the Metabolism and Clinical Nutrition Laboratory of the Department of Internal Medicine at the University of Palermo. The demographic characteristics of the ABCD_2 cohort were not significantly different from those of the ABCD_1 cohort, as reported previously [18].

All methods in this study were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. The institutional Ethics Committee (“Palermo 1,” Policlinico “P. Giaccone” University Hospital, 11/03/2014, ref: 3/2015) approved the study protocol, and each participant signed an approved informed consent form.

The habitual intake of energy and macronutrients during the past 12 months was assessed using a previously validated medium-length Food Frequency Questionnaire for the local population as described elsewhere [19]. Adherence to the Mediterranean Diet was assessed by using the MEDI-LITE questionnaire [20].

Habitual physical activity level was investigated using a specific questionnaire validated for the local population that describes 4 levels (very low, low, moderate, high) of physical activity [18].

Briefly, the physical activity questionnaire consisted of the following 4 questions concerning the type and the frequency of exercise: (a) do you regularly do structured physical exercise (including walks of at least 20 min)? (b) if yes, what kind of physical exercise? (c) how many times a week? (d) what is your job? Thus, on the basis of both intensity and frequency of physical exercise, and considering participants doing physically demanding jobs, 4 different levels of HPA were defined: (a) very low (no habitual exercise); (b) low (walks of 20–30 min at least 3 times a week); (c) medium (scheduled physical activity 1–3 times a week, or walks 430 min at least 3 times a week); (d) high (scheduled physical activity 43 times a week or competitive sport activity or heavy job activity). The list of heavy job activities is reported elsewhere [18].

Participants underwent blood sampling for the assessment of blood chemistry and hormonal parameters. For each participant, a blood sample was frozen and stored at −80 °C for subsequent measurements. Type 2 diabetes and pre-diabetes were defined according to the most recent consensus statements based on the values of FPG and/or HbA1c or current treatment with anti-diabetic drugs [2].

Measurements

Height and body weight were measured with the participants lightly dressed and without shoes (SECA; Birmingham, UK). Body mass index (BMI) was calculated as body weight (kg)/height2 (m2). Body circumference was measured at the umbilicus (waist circumference) and at the most prominent buttock level (hip circumference) and was used as an indirect index of body fat distribution. Systolic and diastolic arterial blood pressure (two measurements obtained at 5-min intervals in a seated position) and heart rate (Omron M6; Omron Healthcare Co., Matsusaka, Mie, Japan) were measured by physicians or dietitians, according to standardized procedures.

Laboratory analysis

Fasting plasma glucose, total cholesterol, HDL-C, triglyceride and uric acid levels were measured using standard clinical chemistry methods (Glucosio HK UV; Colesterolo totale Mod P/D; Colesterolo HDL gen 3 mod P/917; Trigliceridi; Acido urico MOD P/917; Roche Diagnostics, Monza, Italy). Basal insulin concentrations (Elecsys insulina; Roche Diagnostics; Monza, Italy), and glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c; B-analyst HbA1c; Menarini Diagnostics; Florence, Italy) levels were also measured. Low-density lipoprotein cholesterol serum concentration was calculated using Friedewald’s formula [21]; and HOMA-I was calculated as described by Matthews et al. [22].

Statistical analysis

Data are reported as the means ± SD for continuous variables and as percentages for categorical variables. Participants were classified according to their initial and final glucose tolerance. Therefore 5 groups were obtained, the initially PD evolved into NGT (group A), those who maintained PD (group B), those who progressed into T2D (group C), the initially NGT that maintained NGT (group D), and that of persons who developed PD (group E). Initial characteristics of groups were evaluated by the analysis of variance (ANOVA), including covariates (ANCOVA) of interest as age and sex if significantly different at ANOVA. Differences between groups were evaluated by the Tukey’s test. The changes in variables between ABCD_1 and ABCD_2 were assessed using paired sample Student’s t-tests. Differences in categorical variables were analyzed using the χ2 test. The absolute differences (Δ = value measured in ABCD_2 – value measured in ABCD_1) between the continuous variables of interest were categorized as 0 (Δ ≤ 0) or 1 (Δ > 0). Multiple logistic binary regression analysis was used to predict the outcomes (progression from NGT to PD and from PD to T2D).

Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05. All analyses were performed using the Systat software (Windows version 13.0; San Jose, CA, USA).

Results

A total of 1223 participants of the ABCD_1 study was selected, and 742 of them were included after the ABCD_2 re-evaluation. The participant selection flow-chart is shown in Fig. 1. In 2011, the prevalence of PD was (n = 228) 30.7% of which 12.7% developed T2D; group (C), 43.9% reversed to normal glucose tolerance (NGT; group A) and 43.4% maintained the condition of PD (group B) 4-year later in 2015. In 2015, 106 previously NGT participants (20.6%) developed PD (group E), the remaining 79.4% maintained the condition of NGT (group D), therefore, the overall prevalence of PD remained almost unchanged (27.6%) in 2015. The condition of PD in the ABCD_1 study and that of the new cases of PD in the ABCD_2 study involved 334 participants in total. The diagnosis of PD was based on the concomitance of positivity of both FPG and HbA1c criteria in 31.7% of cases, on the presence of FPG criteria (with normal HbA1c values) in 13.5% of cases, and in 54.8% of cases only the HbA1c criteria were met. Both age (P < 0.001) and male sex prevalence (P < 0.001) were significantly different between the five groups classified according to their initial and final glucose tolerance (Table 1). Data concerning the use of anti-hypertensive agents are reported in the Appendix (Supplementary Table 1). Overall, 135 participants (group C and group E) showed an unfavorable evolution of their glucose tolerance level at the evaluation carried out in 2015. The initial and final physical and clinical characteristics of the 5 groups of participants are presented in Table 1. Initial values of body weight, BMI and waist circumference were higher in PD people than in NGT group, and even higher in PD persons who developed T2D. Four years later, body weight, BMI and waist circumference were not significantly changed in all subgroups with initial PD or NGT, with the exception of the previously NGT subgroup that developed PD (group E) which increased the value of waist circumference (P < 0.001). Furthermore, the value of WHR increased significantly in the subgroups with initial PD that maintained the condition of PD (P < 0.01) or developed T2D (P < 0.005) at follow-up, or in the initially NGT subgroup that developed PD (P < 0.001). Data relative to initial and final characteristics of habitual diet and the level of habitual physical activity are reported in Table 2. A higher prevalence of sedentary lifestyle was observed in the PD and NGT subgroups which developed T2D or PD, respectively. Initial daily energy intake was comparable between the different glucose tolerance groups, however, it decreased among people with PD who became NGT (P < 0.001) and increased in those who developed T2D (P < 0.05). In general, a change in the distribution of macronutrients was observed in all subgroups with an increase in carbohydrate intake and a reduction of fat intake, while that of proteins remained unchanged. Furthermore, an increase in the consumption of fiber was observed in all groups with the exception of the subgroup with PD that evolved in T2D. At the final observation, the glycemic index decreased in those PD participants who became NGT and in those people who maintained NGT at follow-up. The MEDILITE score increased significantly in PD group that became NGT (group A) and in NGT group that maintained NGT at final observation (group D). Multiple logistic binary regression analysis (Table 3) demonstrated that age greater than >50 years, increasing changes in waist circumference and dietary glycemic index were independently associated with worsening of glucose tolerance (group C plus group E) at the end of the follow-up period. The habitual consumption of the principal categories of food is reported in the Appendix (Supplementary Table 2). All groups reduced the intake of alcohol and soft drinks with the exception of the PD persons who developed T2D. Red meat consumption reduced significantly only in PD who became NGT and in persons who maintained NGT. Discussion

This longitudinal study demonstrates that maintaining a sedentary lifestyle with unhealthy dietary habits is associated with an increased central body fat distribution, as indicated by the WHR value, and a progression from PD to T2D and from NGT to PD. Interestingly, in more than 85% of cases the HbA1c value permitted the diagnosis of PD thus confirming its potential role for screening investigations. Those groups that ameliorated or maintained their glucose tolerance (namely, from PD to NGT or maintained the condition of PD or NGT) had lower values of BMI and waist circumference and improved their lifestyle in terms of habitual physical activity and diet. Different aspects of habitual diet demonstrated favorable associations with improvement in glucose tolerance. So, reducing habitual energy intake, redistributing the habitual intake of macronutrients with less consumption of fats and increasing that of carbohydrates, also increasing the amount of fibers intake were clear healthy indications of this study. These observations were summarized by multivariate analysis that demonstrated that the progression from NGT/PD to PD/T2D was independently associated with age >50 years, with increased central obesity (change in waist circumference) and unfavorable dietary factors (change in the glycemic index of the habitual diet). In concomitance, we observed a general improvement of other well-known variables associated with CVD risk, as systolic blood pressure, HDL- and LDL-cholesterol serum concentrations, especially in those groups who improved their lifestyle. Interestingly, we observed that also the use of statins increased at the end of the study a fact that influenced the improvement of LDL-cholesterol serum concentrations, but suggesting an overall improvement of the participants and their family doctor’s attention towards health that in some instances is after diet [23]. Furthermore, the use of antihypertensive agents resulted increased at the end of the study (Supplementary Table 1 in Appendix). However, the increase in the use of antihypertensive drugs known to adversely affect glucose tolerance such as beta-blockers and diuretics [24] was comparable between participants who reported an adverse progression of glucose tolerance (groups C and E) and participants who did not report an adverse progression (groups A, B, D).

A part from favorable changes of single macronutrients or foods, the quality of diet seems to play an important role [25]. In fact, overall, the positive effects of dietary changes on glucose tolerance were associated, among others, with the reduction of glycemic index. To this effect contributed, in part, the increase in the habitual intake of fibers, the reduction of habitual consumption of soft drinks [26] and that of pasta. Our results agree with different studies that demonstrated a similar role of fibers and low glycemic index diet in preventing T2D that was imbricated and difficult to distinguish in epidemiological studies [27, 28]. Furthermore, the favorable changes of the characteristics of the diet consisted in improvement of the Mediterranean diet pattern, as the MEDILITE score suggested. In fact, the Mediterranean diet is a low-glycemic index healthy dietary style that has demonstrated to be efficacious in terms of diabetes prevention [29] and CVD risk reduction [30]. Healthier lifestyle changes need, first, that people get motivated to pursuit healthier lifestyles. At the end of the initial evaluation, all participants received a report about their clinical conditions, discussing realistic goals to ameliorate their lifestyle and it was also addressed to their family doctors. We think that in many instances this may have been an efficacious action contributing to improve motivation to healthier changes, a sort of “vaccination effect”. So, if this effect really occurred, our study suggests that even a not intensive intervention is an effective opportunity to improve lifestyle and prevent or delay metabolic conditions as T2D at the population level. Indeed, our hypothesis agrees with other studies that demonstrated how low-impact interventions, as short message service, can achieve significant results in terms of prevention of T2D [31, 32].

This study has important limitations. First, this is a monocentric study and we included a limited number of participants, however, the procedure that we followed is not expensive, relatively easy to perform and we hope that this may be a pilot study for future multicentric studies with higher number of participants that may probably provide more definitive demonstrations. However, we also would consider that the longitudinal design with a 4-years follow-up gave direct, not inferential, information, that is coherent with the general structure of study hypothesis. Also, much information was collected for each participant that contributed to understand the general physiopathology of the metabolic derangement that led to T2D. Second, we did not perform an clear intervention study despite we discuss about an initial motivating event to lifestyle change. This fact was almost inevitable as participants request the results of their participation to the study; however, this was a favorable circumstance that permitted to understand why so many participants ameliorated their lifestyle and may be a model of intervention at the population level that needs to be further investigated. However, we are well aware that the design of the study does not allow to make any conclusion on the intrinsic effect of this intervention. Moreover, given this possible efficacious intervention effect, we are convinced that it is unethical to include a control group for a case-control design. Finally, we should have used also the criterium of 2 h PG load glycemia for a complete diagnosis of PD, however, this is not a so easy procedure to perform in a screening study and probably would have reduced the number of participants. We are confident that the diagnostic tools we used, especially the evaluation of HbA1c, were valid instruments to identify PD with negligible false positive or negative cases. In particular, the HbA1c value contributed to identify most of our PD cases, we found that 43.8% of PD improved to NGT condition at the end of follow-up and this result is coherent with the Whitehall II study [11] that found a rate of 45% if PD was diagnosed by FPG, 37% if based on 2 h PG glycemia and 17% if based on HbA1c value; the same study found that the evolution of PD to T2D was 12.3% in 6 years and we found a similar value (12.7%) in 4 years.

In conclusion, PD may probably be prevented or reverted to NGT through changes in lifestyle that improve the habitual level of physical activity and diet toward a pattern with low glycemic index Mediterranean diet. However, intervention studies with large cohorts based on multicentric recruitment are necessary to establish if a low impact intervention aimed to increase motivation to improve lifestyle is an effective strategy at the population level.

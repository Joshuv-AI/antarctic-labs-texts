Identification and analysis of key transcription factors in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Zhe-Ran Chen, Zhi-Yuan Cheng, Si-Wei Zhou, Yan-Hui Zhang, Ye Gao, Chu-Ting Yu, Bo Tian, Xun Zhang, Yan Bian, Mei-Juan Hao, Wei Wang, Lei Xin, Han Lin, Luo-Wei Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and ObjectiveOver the past decade, transcription factors (TFs) have emerged as key players in various diseases. Immunotherapy has gained traction as an effective treatment, despite resistance to immune checkpoint inhibitors remains a challenge. The regulatory role of TFs in immunotherapy for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is poorly understood. This study aims to explore the expression of key TFs, predictive significance, and tumor microenvironment (TME) cell infiltration in ESCC.MethodsThe expression profiles were obtained from Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) databases, TF gene data were obtained from the Transcriptional Regulatory Relationships Unraveled by Sentence-based Text mining (TTRUST) database. A protein-protein interaction (PPI) network and enrichment analysis elucidated the molecular processes in ESCC. We investigated the TME cell infiltration landscape in a combined ESCC cohort. The relative abundance of cell infiltration was measured using a single-sample gene-set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA). The diagnostic value of hub TFs for ESCC was investigated using ROC analysis.ResultsThis study found 48 differentially expressed TFs between normal and ESCC tissues and revealed a strong association between EZH2, FOS, KLF4, FOSB, TWIST1, KLF6 and CEBPB. The results suggested that upregulated EZH2, TWIST1, and KLF4 correlated with immunosuppressive cell infiltration (Tregs, MDSCs) and reduced PD-1/PD-L2 checkpoint expression. Downregulated FOSB and CEBPB associated with enhanced NK cell activity. The diagnostic potential of these key transcription factors in ESCC was evaluated through ROC analysis (EZH2, area under the curve [AUC] = 0.95; TWIST1, AUC = 0.85; CEBPB, AUC = 0.84; FOSB, AUC = 0.79; KLF4, AUC = 0.97; FOS, AUC = 0.84; KLF6, AUC = 0.84). Single-cell data confirmed TF expression in tumor cells, macrophages, and T-cell subsets, highlighting their role in TME remodeling.ConclusionThe scRNA-seq analysis revealed the expression of hub TFs in different cell clusters. The study investigated the expression of DETFs associated with patient prognosis and TME cell infiltration in ESCC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03634-5.Keywords: Transcription factors, Immunotherapy, Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, Tumor microenvironment, Single-cell RNA sequencing

Introduction

Esophageal cancer persists as the leading cause of cancer-related mortality globally [1]. This malignancy primarily manifests in two histological subtypes: esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), predominant in Eastern Asia and the Middle East, and adenocarcinoma, which demonstrates higher incidence in Western nations [2]. Despite therapeutic advances including surgical intervention, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy, patient prognosis remains poor. Consequently, elucidating the pathogenic mechanisms underlying this disease is imperative to identify sensitive therapeutic targets and novel biomarkers for early detection, treatment optimization, and prognostic stratification.

Immunotherapy has emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy for multiple malignancies, including esophageal cancer. Immune evasion represents a well-established oncogenic hallmark [3]. Despite the demonstrated efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors, treatment resistance develops in most patients [4]. The tumor microenvironment (TME), comprising diverse cancer cell populations, cancer stem cells, and various stromal cells, plays a critical role in tumorigenesis and malignant progression [5; 6; 7]. Consequently, delineating the compositional heterogeneity and functional dynamics within the ESCC TME is imperative for developing novel therapeutic approaches.

Transcription factors (TFs) execute critical regulatory functions governing gene expression, cellular differentiation, developmental programs, and specific pathways, particularly immune responses [8]. Dysregulated TF activity contributes to diverse human pathologies, including oncogenesis [9; 10]. Despite established pathophysiological roles in tumor development, the molecular contributions of TFs to immunotherapy responsiveness in ESCC remain incompletely characterized.

This study employed an integrated approach combining multi-omic profiling and single-cell transcriptomics to identify master transcriptional regulators governing ESCC progression. We systematically evaluated the prognostic implications, TME remodeling functions, and clinical relevance of these regulators. Our investigation specifically aimed to characterize how transcriptionally dysregulated factors reprogram the TME via modulation of immune cell infiltration and suppression of natural killer (NK) cell activity, while concurrently correlating TF expression patterns with immune checkpoint dynamics. These analyses were designed to uncover transcription factor-mediated mechanisms underlying immune evasion and therapeutic resistance, thereby establishing a mechanistic foundation for developing targeted ESCC interventions.

Materials and methods

Expression patterns of genes in ESCC and healthy controls

Transcriptomic profiling of ESCC was conducted using Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets GSE20347 and GSE38129, retrieved through the GEOquery package in R 4.2.2. The GSE20347 cohort (n = 179 tumor samples, n = 13 normal tissues) provided comprehensive clinical annotations including TNM staging and survival outcomes, enabling robust tissue-specific differential expression analysis. The GSE38129 cohort (n = 119 tumor samples with paired adjacent normal tissues) facilitated rigorous identification of progression-associated transcriptional regulators. Technical batch effects were mitigated using the ComBat algorithm from the sva package to ensure biological validity of observed expression patterns.

Characterization of differentially expressed transcription factors

Gene expression data underwent standardization using the limma package prior to differential expression analysis. Empirical Bayes methods identified differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between normal and ESCC tissues, applying significance thresholds of p-value ≤ 0.05 and |logFC| ≥ 1. Transcription factor data from the Transcriptional Regulatory Relationships Unraveled by Sentence-based Text mining (TTRUST) database [11], and DEGs overlapped with the TTRUST data to identify differentially expressed transcription factors.

Functional characterization and analysis of hub genes

Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was performed to elucidate the biological processes, cellular components, and molecular functions associated with DEGs. Protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks were constructed using STRING (https://stringdb.org) and visualized in Cytoscape (v3.9.1). Hub genes were identified through Maximal Clique Centrality (MCC) analysis. The diagnostic potential of hub genes was evaluated via Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve assessment. The impact of high and low expressions of hob genes on the survival data of ESCC patients (n = 96) was explored according to the Kaplan–Meier curve plotted based on the data from TCGA (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov) database.

Analysis of immune infiltration

Frequency of TME cell infiltration was determined through single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) based on gene sets from CIBERSORT [12]. The ESTIMATE tool was used to estimate the cancer purity [13].

Single-cell analysis of gene expression

To validate the spatial expression patterns of hub genes and their cellular heterogeneity, we utilized single-cell transcriptomic data on 25,796 immune and 8,197 non-immune cells from three primary tumors and paired normal samples in ESCC patients, which was obtained from the GSE196756 cohorts of the GEO (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) database. This cohort included comprehensive characterization of tumor-infiltrating immune and stromal cell populations. It was applied to identify immune cells that might be involved in the regulation of the ESCC. The “Seurat” R package was used to perform a cell cluster analysis. Cell subsets were identified, and expression levels of hub genes in each subset were reported based on clustering of cell subsets and distinctive gene expression.

Results

Differences in transcription factors between normal and ESCC tissues

Transcriptomic analysis of GSE20347 and GSE38129 datasets was conducted to elucidate TF functions in ESCC, identifying 430 upregulated and 459 downregulated differentially expressed genes. Integration with TTRUST database annotations revealed 48 differentially expressed transcription factors (DETFs; Fig. 1A), with hierarchical clustering demonstrating distinct molecular stratification between malignant and normal tissues (Fig. 1B). Functional characterization through GO enrichment analysis indicated significant DETF associations across biological processes including adipocyte differentiation, appendage morphogenesis, limb development, and endocrine system development; cellular components such as RNA polymerase II transcription regulator complexes, pericentric heterochromatin, and protein-DNA complexes; and molecular functions encompassing DNA-binding transcriptional activator activity, RNA polymerase II-specific transcription factor binding, and transcription coregulator binding (Fig. 1C-D).

Identification of hub genes

Functional interactions among DETFs were investigated through protein-protein interaction (PPI) network construction via the STRING database. The resulting PPI network underwent topological analysis in Cytoscape using the MCC algorithm within the CytoHubba plugin. This analysis identified seven hub transcription factors with maximal MCC scores: EZH2, FOS, KLF4, FOSB, TWIST1, KLF6, and CEBPB (Fig. 2A), with their regulatory relationships further visualized (Fig. 2B-C). Principal component analysis (PCA) subsequently demonstrated the discriminative capacity of these hub genes, revealing complete segregation between ESCC and normal tissue transcriptomes. Two distinct transcriptional clusters were observed, corresponding to differential expression patterns in malignant versus normal states (Fig. 2D). Systematic transcriptome profiling revealed pronounced overexpression of seven regulatory genes (KLF4, EZH2, KLF6, CEBPB, TWIST1, FOS, FOSB) in tumor versus normal tissues (Fig. 3A, p < 0.0001), with CEBPB and FOS exhibiting the strongest upregulation (median > 10 vs.<8). Spearman correlation analysis further delineated context-specific cooperativity: KLF4 showed near-perfect synergy with FOS and KLF6, while EZH2 displayed strong anticorrelation with KLF4, implying compensatory transcriptional networks (Fig. 3B).

Correlation analysis between TME infiltration and immunotherapy

To investigate the regulatory role of DETFs in TME remodeling and immunotherapy response, we characterized immune infiltration differences between normal and ESCC tissues. Comparative analysis revealed significant differential abundance of immune cell populations (Fig. 4A). Tumor tissues exhibited substantial enrichment of CD56bright natural killer cells, natural killer T cells, γδ T cells, regulatory T cells, macrophages, T helper 2 cells, CD4 + effector memory T cells, memory B cells, and plasmacytoid dendritic cells. Conversely, normal tissues demonstrated higher infiltration levels of complementary immune subsets. Tumors exhibited markedly lower immune activity levels than normal tissues (Fig. 4B).

B Using the ESTIMATE algorithm, compare the total immunological and stromal activity of tumor and normal tissues. C The relationship between key transcription factors and TME infiltration cells D The relationship between immunological checkpoint molecules and key transcription factors

Spearman’s correlation analysis assessed associations between key molecules and TME composition. Upregulated transcription factors EZH2, TWIST1, and CEBPB demonstrated significant positive correlations with multiple immune effector populations including activated dendritic cells, memory B cells, monocytes, Th1 cells, NKT cells, γδ T cells, Tregs, activated CD4 T cells, CD56bright NK cells, Th2 cells, CD4 effector memory T cells, and macrophages (Fig. 4C). Conversely, downregulated factors FOSB, KLF4, FOS, and KLF6 exhibited inverse correlations with these populations. To evaluate relationships with immunotherapy response, we correlated these transcriptional regulators with immune checkpoint expression. EZH2, TWIST1, and CEBPB showed significant negative associations with checkpoint molecules TNFRSF4, CD244, CD160, TNFRSF14, CD48, PD-1, PD-L2, HHLA2, and LAIR1. The downregulated transcription factors demonstrated inverse relationships with these immunomodulators (Fig. 4D).

ROC analysis and Kaplan-Meier survival analysis of hub genes

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis demonstrated robust diagnostic utility of the identified hub genes. Individual gene performance metrics revealed exceptional discriminatory capacity: EZH2 (AUC = 0.95), KLF4 (AUC = 0.97), TWIST1 (AUC = 0.85), CEBPB (AUC = 0.84), FOS (AUC = 0.84), KLF6 (AUC = 0.84), and FOSB (AUC = 0.79) effectively distinguished esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) from normal tissues (Supplementary Figure S1). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis further established significant prognostic associations: ESCC patients exhibiting low KLF6 expression (p = 0.023), low FOS expression (p = 0.0019), and high TWIST1 expression (p = 0.047) demonstrated markedly prolonged disease-specific survival (Fig. 5).

Validation of hub gene expression levels and single-cell RNA sequencing analysis

Single-cell RNA sequencing analysis validated hub gene expression patterns and elucidated transcriptional regulatory mechanisms within the esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) tumor microenvironment. Thirteen definitive lineage markers were identified (Fig. 6A), with B cells, CD8 + T cells, CD4 + T cells, hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs), monocytes, and granulocytes comprising the predominant cellular populations (Fig. 6B). Cluster-specific expression profiling further revealed heterogeneous hub gene distribution across distinct cell subpopulations (Fig. 6C).

Discussion

The limited efficacy of most cancer immunotherapies, including those targeting esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), primarily stems from tumor immune evasion mechanisms. Substantial evidence indicates that the tumor immune microenvironment critically influences neoplastic development, recurrence, metastatic dissemination, and therapeutic responsiveness [14]. While TME composition exhibits disease-specific heterogeneity, common constituents universally include malignant cells, diverse immune populations, stromal components, and remodeled extracellular matrix (ECM) [15; 16]. Crucially, aberrant TF activity represents a fundamental oncogenic driver across malignancies. In ESCC specifically, genomic amplification of key TFs has been documented, with TP63, SOX2, and KLF5 serving as established core regulatory determinants of tumor pathogenesis [17]. Nevertheless, the mechanistic relationship between TF dysregulation and immunotherapy response in ESCC remains poorly characterized. This study systematically identified and functionally characterized pivotal TFs, elucidating their association with immunotherapy outcomes in ESCC. Beyond establishing the prognostic significance of these transcriptional regulators, we further validated their expression patterns through scRNA-seq analysis of tumor heterogeneity.

Kruppel-like factors (KLFs) constitute a family of zinc finger transcription factors essential for epithelial tissue development and homeostasis. These regulators are implicated in the pathogenesis of diverse malignancies, including gastric, pulmonary, mammary, hepatic, and colorectal carcinomas [18]. KLF4 has recently been shown to play a dual role as an oncogene and tumor suppressor [19; 20]. Previous studies indicate that KLF4 silencing substantially downregulates ANXA1 expression in epithelial cells, inducing transformation into cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) that accelerate ESCC progression [21]. KLF6 modulates macrophage polarization, directing transcriptional programs associated with inflammatory and hypoxic responses [22]. Emerging evidence further establishes KLF6-AhR signaling crosstalk that broadly influences mast cells, B lymphocytes, antigen-presenting cells, and T-cell differentiation (Th1/Th2 balance, Th17, Tregs), thereby contributing to immunopathogenesis in allergen-mediated conditions [23]. However, the immunomodulatory functions of KLF4 and KLF6 within the ESCC tumor microenvironment remain poorly characterized, particularly regarding immunotherapy response. Our findings establish that KLF4 and KLF6 significantly influence ESCC oncogenesis and progression through direct modulation of the tumor immune microenvironment.

EZH2 serves as the catalytic subunit of Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 (PRC2), which mediates transcriptional silencing through histone H3 lysine 27 trimethylation [24]. Pharmacological inhibition of EZH2 with GSK126 has been shown to increase myeloid-derived suppressor cell (MDSC) infiltration while reducing CD4 + T lymphocytes and interferon-gamma-producing CD8 + T cells, critical effectors of antitumor immunity [25]. Complementary studies demonstrate that EZH2 targeting enhances antigen presentation and reverses anti-PD-1 resistance in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, suggesting therapeutic synergy between EZH2 inhibitors and immune checkpoint blockade [26]. Our findings further establish EZH2 as a robust diagnostic biomarker for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, with ROC analysis confirming exceptional discriminatory capacity (Supplementary Figure S1).

The CCAAT/enhancer-binding proteins (CEBPs) comprises leucine zipper transcription factors (CEBPA, CEBPB, CEPBD, CEBPE, CEBPG, CEBPZ) that regulate critical physiological processes including energy homeostasis, cellular proliferation, and differentiation. Consistent with prior reports demonstrating CEBPs upregulation in ESCC [27], our analysis confirms this transcriptional pattern. MDSCs constitute key mediators of tumor immunosuppression. Notably, glycolytic inhibition suppresses the CEBPB-LIP isoform, thereby reducing MDSC accumulation and potentiating T-cell immunity [28]. According to our research, elevated CEBPB expression could be connected to the development of ESCC tumor immune microenvironment.

TWIST1, a basic helix-loop-helix (bHLH) transcription factor, binds canonical E-box motifs (5’-CANNTG-3’) via its conserved bHLH domain. This domain facilitates dimerization interfaces critical for forming functional homodimeric and heterodimeric complexes with partner proteins [29]. Furthermore, TWIST1 promotes immunosuppression within the tumor microenvironment through chemokine-mediated recruitment of immunosuppressive cells and induction of immune checkpoint molecules [30]. Our analysis demonstrates significant TWIST1 overexpression in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC). Critically, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis establishes TWIST1 as a robust diagnostic biomarker for ESCC (AUC = 0.85; Supplementary Figure S1).

The Fos protein family (c-Fos, FosB, Fra-1, Fra-2) dimerizes with Jun proteins to form activator protein-1 (AP-1) transcription factor complexes [31]. Cumulative evidence establishes Fos members as critical regulators of cancer progression, particularly through enhancing cellular motility and invasiveness in breast and colorectal carcinomas [31]. Beyond oncogenesis, Fos signaling participates in immune modulation: c-Fos activation initiates innate immune cascades during bacterial clearance [32], while FosB transcription mediates enhanced antitumor activity in hepatocellular carcinoma when interferon-alpha combines with anti-PD-1 immunotherapy [33]. Our findings further demonstrate the diagnostic utility of FOS (AUC = 0.84) and FOSB (AUC = 0.79) for distinguishing ESCC from normal tissues, establishing their clinical potential as ESCC biomarkers.

Limitation

First, we emphasize that the absence of functional validation represents a fundamental limitation of our study. While our bioinformatics analyses revealed compelling correlative patterns, we lack mechanistic validation through critical experiments such as ChIP-seq for TF-DNA binding verification, CRISPR-based target perturbation, or in vivo immune co-culture models. This gap precludes definitive establishment of causality between observed TF dysregulation and TME reprogramming events. Second, the retrospective nature of public database analysis may introduce selection bias and limit generalizability. The absence of prospective validation in independent cohorts further constrains clinical translation potential. Third, unaccounted confounding variables including patient lifestyle variables, comorbidities, and treatment histories may influence gene expression patterns. Additionally, unexplored TME heterogeneity across patients could impact result reproducibility. Future work should first prioritize functional interrogation of key regulators like KLF4/EZH2 through TF knockout/overexpression studies. Second, prospective validation in diverse cohorts with comprehensive clinical annotation remains essential. Finally, integrated multi-omics approaches could further elucidate context-specific TF functions within the ESCC ecosystem.

Conclusions

This study systematically identified seven pivotal transcription factors governing immune cell infiltration dynamics within the ESCC microenvironment. Each factor demonstrates robust standalone diagnostic capability for ESCC detection. Further mechanistic investigation of these transcriptional regulators will elucidate the spatial heterogeneity and functional complexity of tumor microenvironment composition, ultimately informing the development of precision immunotherapeutic strategies for ESCC management.

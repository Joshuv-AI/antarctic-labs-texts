Comment on ‘Endoscopic and surgical treatment outcomes of colitis-associated advanced colorectal neoplasia: a multicenter cohort study’
Authors: Hongbin Yu, Wenming Feng, Chengwu Tang
Published: 2023

Dear Editor,

We have read the recently published study1 on the endoscopic and surgical treatment outcomes of colitis-associated advanced colorectal neoplasia with great interest. The study’s comprehensive analysis of treatment outcomes significantly contributes to our understanding of managing colitis-associated neoplasia. By including a large cohort from multiple centers, the study benefits from a diverse patient population, enhancing the generalizability of the findings. The authors’ meticulous examination of both endoscopic and surgical interventions allows for a holistic evaluation of available treatment options for patients with advanced colorectal neoplasia. Notably, the study provides a detailed characterization of endoscopic treatment outcomes, offering valuable data on the efficacy and safety of these interventions. Additionally, it highlights the role of surgical intervention in managing advanced colorectal neoplasia, providing crucial information on surgical outcomes, including postoperative complications and long-term survival. However, we have some concerns that require further clarification.

Firstly, it is important to emphasize the need to explore the impact of adjuvant therapies, including chemoprevention or immunosuppressive agents, administered before, after, or in combination with surgical treatment, on different outcomes, which would significantly enhance the understanding of optimizing the overall management strategy for patients with colitis-associated neoplasia. For instance, a meta-analysis of 15 randomized controlled trials2 involving 12 234 participants demonstrated that among individuals with a history of colorectal neoplasia, non-aspirin nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) were the most effective agents for preventing advanced metachronous neoplasia, while low-dose aspirin exhibited the most favorable risk–benefit profile. Similarly, another meta-analysis3 involving 76 999 participants demonstrated that thiopurines considerably reduce the risk of developing colorectal neoplasia in patients with inflammatory bowel disease, particularly in those with ulcerative colitis, where this effect is most pronounced. Therefore, considering the potential benefits of adjuvant therapies, it would be valuable to include a discussion on their utilization and outcomes in the context of the study’s findings. This could involve examining factors such as the timing of adjuvant therapy initiation, the specific agents employed, and their impact on long-term surveillance and prevention of metachronous neoplasia.

Secondly, this study reports a higher risk of metachronous neoplasia associated with endoscopic resection compared to (subtotal) colectomy. However, the findings should be interpreted with caution due to potential confounding factors. The study does not provide sufficient details about the expertise and experience of the endoscopic resection experts, nor does it elaborate on the specific equipment and models used. These factors can significantly impact the success and safety of endoscopic resection procedures. Furthermore, we have noted that only 38 patients underwent endoscopic resection, a significantly smaller number compared to those who underwent partial colectomy (n=56) and (sub)total colectomy (n=78). This indirectly reflects the limited experience of clinical physicians in performing endoscopic resection compared to partial colectomy and (sub)total colectomy. Thus, it is reasonable to assume that the outcome of endoscopic resection can be greatly influenced by the experience and expertise of the performing endoscopists, as well as the quality and type of equipment utilized. In recent years, researchers have started to recognize the importance of endoscopic resection experience and have begun integrating artificial intelligence into this procedure4, resulting in significant improvements. Thus, it is possible that with improved endoscopic resection expertise, the three different surgical treatments may yield more consistent outcomes.

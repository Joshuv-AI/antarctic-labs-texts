Refining Vascular Biomarker–Guided Risk Stratification for Cancer Therapy–Related Cardiac Dysfunction
Authors: Ze Xiang, Jian Wu
Published: 2026

We read with great interest the study by Gustafson et al1 regarding pretreatment vascular biomarkers and prediction of cancer therapy–related cardiac dysfunction. Although the focus on endothelial biology marks an important shift from cardiomyocyte-centric surveillance, several aspects merit discussion.

First, the biological source of these vascular signals necessitates additional interrogation. HER2 signaling directly up-regulates ANGPT2 expression,2 and elevated levels are associated with overall tumor burden.3 Therefore, it remains uncertain whether these biomarkers reflect a patient-specific endothelial vulnerability or are proxies for aggressive tumor biology, which may predispose individuals to adverse outcomes through mechanisms such as systemic inflammation.

Second, the reliance on cardiac magnetic resonance for outcome definition poses challenges for broad clinical translation. Although cardiac magnetic resonance in the EMBRACE-MRI (Evaluation of Myocardial Changes During BReast Adenocarcinoma Therapy to Detect Cardiotoxicity Earlier With MRI) cohort enabled the detection of subtle changes in ejection fraction not determined by echocardiography, there is well-documented variability between imaging modalities.4 As echocardiography remains the primary tool for cardiac monitoring in most clinical settings, the generalizability of these biomarkers established within a high-precision imaging context remains unclear in real-world practice.

Finally, these findings suggest a promising therapeutic avenue. If ANGPT2 plays an active role in vascular destabilization, modulation of the Tie2 signaling pathway could represent a strategy for cardioprotection. Therapeutic agents that enhance Tie2 pathway stability may preserve endothelial integrity during cytotoxic stress,5 offering a potential new approach.

Introduction

Hexapod fixation devices are highly versatile tools in trauma management, limb reconstruction, and deformity correction. Frames will be constructed of at least two rings, attached to the affected long bone by fine wires and/or half-pins. In some anatomical areas, such as the proximal tibia, when a proximal tibial corticotomy is performed, the ring is placed as proximally as possible to increase the distance between the corticotomy and the fixation to optimise the stiffness profile and reduce the peri-implant fracture. Proximal placement of fixation enables a more proximal corticotomy to be performed; however, there is a balance between performing this as proximal as possible to aid in regenerate formation against having enough space for adequate proximal fixation. This can lead to a preference for wire fixation in the proximal tibia due to concerns about the space required for half-pin placement, especially in patients with smaller tibias. However, half-pins provide greater stability and durability than wires, and are typically at least three times the magnitude of the radius, and thus 81 times the magnitude of the bending moment of inertia.1 The aim of this study was to describe a technique for placing three half-pins within a small segment (<2 cm) of proximal tibia and to assess its use and complication rate in our unit.

Materials and Methods

Patients who received tibial surgery using a Taylor Spatial Frame (TSF) (Smith and Nephew, Watford, UK) between 1 November 2021 and 31 October 2022 were identified from the departmental database. Operative notes and intraoperative images were reviewed to confirm the use of the three short-segment tibial half-pins in the desired configuration. Patients were included if they had completed their treatment and were at least three months post-frame removal. Electronic clinical notes were analysed for proximal pin site complications, defined as peri-implant fractures, clinically detected pin site loosening, knee septic arthritis, or revision surgery due to proximal pin sites.

Technical Method

An appropriately sized proximal ring is selected, with our preference being to use a 2/3rd ring if possible, to enable full, or near full, knee flexion immediately post-operatively. A temporary transverse wire is placed lateral to medial perpendicular to the tibial joint line. The proximal ring is attached to this using edge connectors, ensuring appropriate spacing between the ring and soft tissues. A second temporary wire is placed in the sagittal plane to hold the ring parallel to the tibial slope. The ring is placed ~ 20 mm from the joint line, thus remaining extra synovial.2,3 Three half-pin sites are planned, aiming for orthogonal trajectories for the furthest apart pins. Parallel, yet unencumbered trajectories for the half-pins are made using the following combination from medial to lateral: (1) below ring one-hole rancho with one washer; (2) below ring half pin fixation bolt (Italian Job); (3) one-hole rancho below the ring (with two washers above) (Figs 1 to 4). Transverse wires are then removed. Distal frame construction can continue as desired. Half-pins are recommended to be inserted in the order 2-3-1, so that the surgeon is not targeting an enclosed area (made by the previously inserted half-pins) in case the other pin trajectories are not parallel to the ring.

Results

We identified the use of this technique in 26 patients between 1 November 2021 and 31 October 2022; the mean average age was 45 years (range 24–80).

No patients had peri-implant fractures, clinically detected pin site loosening, septic arthritis or revision surgery due to proximal pin sites.

Discussion

Short-segment half-pin fixation is a safe way to obtain stable, durable fixation in a small area. By achieving orthogonal placement of the lateral and most medial half-pins, tremendous stability is achieved. This fixation is greater than that offered by fine wire fixation due to the significantly greater biomechanical characteristic of half-pins, especially regarding rigidity. This enables deformity correction, such as corticotomies to be performed very proximally, maximising regenerate formation potential.

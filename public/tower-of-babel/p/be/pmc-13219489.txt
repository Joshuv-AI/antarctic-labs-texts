Visually meaningful image encryption using convolution neural networks
Authors: Varsha Himthani, Prashant Hemrajani, Ashwani Kumar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Image encryption techniques are broadly used to protect confidential images during transmission and storage. However, conventional image encryption techniques typically produce noisy ciphertext images that can easily reveal the presence of encrypted information and attract potential attacks. Visually meaningful image encryption (VMIE) technique has emerged to address this problem as an effective solution by embedding encrypted data into a cover image. Hiding encrypted data within a cover image provides an additional layer of security compared to traditional encryption methods. This property makes VMIE suitable for covert communication scenarios where both data confidentiality and transmission imperceptibility are required. In this work, a VMIE technique utilizing Convolution Neural Network (CNN) is proposed. In the first phase, a chaotic image encryption scheme refined using a CNN is used to generate highly secure encrypted images with enhanced randomness. The CNN-based refinement module learns nonlinear transformations of the combined chaotic sequences and enhances their randomness characteristics. CNN helps to remove residual correlations and improves the statistical properties of the generated random sequence by capturing complex nonlinear dependencies within the chaotic values that contribute to stronger security performance of the encrypted images. In the next phase, an output image from first phase is entrenched into a cover image through CNN based encoder to generate visually meaningful ciphertext (VMC). The proposed method achieves an overall key space greater than 2200, that ensures strong resistance against brute-force attacks. The experimental results demonstrate high differential attack resistance with NPCR values reach up to 99.61%. The VMCs maintain excellent perceptual quality up to 43.8 dB PSNR and SSIM values above 0.99 with relative payload capacity of 1. These results validate the robustness, imperceptibility, and practical applicability of the proposed VMIE scheme.

Introduction

Information security is a way of protecting data from illegal access, data release, disruption, data amendment, or data demolition. It is a broad field that includes various methods, policies, techniques, and solutions aimed at providing protection to data in its various forms to confirm its confidentiality, integrity, and availability. Image encryption is a process that transmutes visual data into meaningless and unreadable to protect it from illegitimate access to ensure that only legal or allowed users with proper decipherment key can access the data.

Image encryption is important way to preserve security along with the confidentiality, integrity of sensitive visual information, such as personal images, medical images, and confidential surveillance footage. The encrypted images produced by conventional cryptographic techniques usually appear noisy or texture-like, that depicts the existence of encryption, and it is easily detectable that something is protected. From another perspective, image steganography conceals secret data within a cover medium, such as audio, video, or image file, that reduces the likelihood of detection of presence of confidential data. To combine the advantages of both techniques, the concept of VMIE has been introduced1, where the encrypted image is further implanted into a reference image. This approach employs image-in-image steganography after encryption and produces a VMC that closely resembles a natural image, that reduces the likelihood of detection during transmission. Nevertheless, the resulting VMC often suffers from increased data size, typically becoming larger than the original image. To address this limitation, compressive sensing and other dimensionality reduction techniques have been explored to reduce the overall size of the VMC2.

VMIE techniques work in two parts. In the first part, an image is encrypted and transformed into meaningless or noisy structures that clearly indicate the presence of an encryption algorithm. To mitigate this, an encrypted image is placed into a cover image using any data hiding algorithm. The advantage of this approach is that the final cipher image, which will be transmitted, appears meaningful because cover image hides an encrypted image. This complicates the detection of sensitive data by potential attackers2.

A CNN is a deep learning algorithm, that is designed to efficiently learn features from structured grid data, such as digital images3,4. CNNs demonstrated efficient performance in the field of image recognition and classification. Encryption of images via CNN is an attractive approach as it leverages the notion of cryptography along with deep learning5. CNNs can generate complex encryption patterns that will be difficult for attackers to decipher without the key. Since CNNs can handle high-resolution images without significant degradation in reconstruction. CNNs can be trained to perform much more effectively for encryption purposes to reduce the computational load as compared to traditional methods. CNNs can be designed to be resistant against common cryptographic attacks. Also, the integrity of encryption can be maintained even in the presence of noise or data loss for reliable security6.

CNNs are utilized in both the stages of the proposed VMIE technique. In the initial stage, the secret image is encrypted using CNN-refined chaotic random sequences to enhance randomness and security. In the second stage, a CNN-based embedding strategy is used to hide an encrypted image into a reference image to generate a VMC. The proposed method effectively combines the strengths of chaos theory and deep learning to achieve high encryption security while preserving visual image quality. Extensive experimental analysis demonstrates that the proposed method provides strong resistance opposed to statistical and differential attacks, along with high perceptual quality of the encrypted and embedded images.

Literature survey

Over the past few years, VMIE techniques have been proposed to enhance the security of confidential images. The VMIE technique introduced in Reference1, that comprises of two steps: encryption and image hiding. Initially, the confidential image is encrypted, and thereafter an encrypted image is embedded into a cover image. Subsequently, numerous improvements were suggested in this field. Many techniques employ chaotic map based encryption to enhance security by generating random keys and discrete wavelet transform for image embedding2,7. Kanso and Ghebleh8 improved imperceptibility and image security by employing a three-dimensional chaotic map for first stage and two-dimensional lifting wavelet transform was applied to produce an imperceptible image. A compressive sensing-based encryption was proposed in Reference9, in which a sparse representation of reference image was generated using dictionaries such as discrete cosine transform or wavelet dictionaries. Numerous VMIE schemes utilizing compressive sensing have been proposed in the literature10–16. In the VMIE approach, the encrypted image is separated into least significant and most significant bit components and subsequently embedded using a k-LSB strategy combined with a 2k-correction mechanism in the integer discrete wavelet transform domain17. In18, two-dimensional integer Haar wavelet transform and a substitution box for the embedding phase. In one other study19, chaotic maps were used for encryption, and initial values were produced by the RSA algorithm. The encrypted image underwent Schur decomposition and was embedded through DWT. A VMIE technique based on universal embedding model was proposed in Reference20, in which an encrypted image was dynamically embedded through inverse wavelet transform. Song et al.21 proposed a batch image encryption scheme based on cross-image permutation and diffusion to enhance security by inter-image dependency in the encryption process. Similarly, Liu et al.22 presented a semantically enhanced selective image encryption scheme that encrypts important semantic regions and employed parallel computing to improve efficiency. These approaches improve encryption efficiency and security, however, they do not focus on generating visually meaningful ciphertexts. Experiment setup

CNN architecture and training details

The CNN model used for refining the chaotic random sequence follows a lightweight one-dimensional convolutional architecture, that is designed for sequence transformation rather than image classification. The input to the CNN model is the combined chaotic sequence \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{S}_{c}$$\end{document}, which is treated as one dimensional signal. The network consists of multiple convolutional layers followed by nonlinear activation functions to capture complex nonlinear relationships present in the chaotic data. The proposed CNN architecture is initialized with an input layer that receives the combined chaotic sequence. This is followed by three successive one-dimensional convolutional layers are applied with 32, 64, and 128 filters, respectively. Each convolution operation uses a 3 × 1 kernel and is succeeded by a ReLU activation function to capture non-linear features. Batch normalization is added after each convolutional layer to enhance training permanence and faster convergence. A final convolutional layer maps the extracted features back to a single refined random sequence \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}_{cnn}$$\end{document}, that ensures the output length matches the input sequence length.

Loss function

The Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss function between the network output and a target pseudo-random reference sequence is used to train a model that will generate highly random and decorrelated sequences. The goal of minimizing the MSE is to help the CNN to reduce predictable patterns and manage the numerical stability in the output sequence. The choice of MSE ensures smooth optimization and prevents abrupt changes in the refined sequence29. Training parameters

The model training was performed with initial learning rate of 0.001 using Adam optimizer. Training is performed using a batch size of 64 over 50 epochs, which was found sufficient for convergence. During the training process, the chaotic sequences are produced using randomly selected control parameters within the chaotic range to ensure robustness and generalization. After training, the CNN weights are fixed and reused during both encryption and decryption phases, guaranteeing deterministic behaviour for identical chaotic parameters and keys.

Synchronization during decryption

During the decryption process, the same CNN architecture, trained weights, chaotic parameters, initial conditions, and secret key are used to regenerate the identical refined random sequence \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}_{cnn}$$\end{document}. Since the CNN operates deterministically after training that ensures perfect synchronization between encryption and decryption for accurate reconstruction of the original image.

Key space and sensitivity are crucial factors in evaluating the efficiency of an encryption algorithm withstands brute-force attacks2. The CNN-refined chaotic sequence introduces an additional nonlinear transformation stage that increases the complexity of the generated random sequence. This refinement process enlarges the effective key space through the inclusion of network parameters and learned transformations that improve the randomness quality of the generated sequence. As a result, the proposed approach provides enhanced resistance against brute-force and statistical analysis compared to conventional chaotic encryption methods. Payload capacity analysis

In the proposed framework, encrypted image implanted into the cover image of similar dimensions. The relative payload capacity is defined as the absolute payload capacity divided by the reference image size32. By embedding a full-size encrypted image into a cover image of same dimensions, the proposed method achieves a payload capacity of 1. It demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed U-Net based embedding strategy in handling high-capacity data hiding along with preserving the perceptual quality. Despite the maximum payload, the experimental results show that VMCs retain high PSNR and SSIM value that confirms that the proposed approach maintains a appropriate trade-off between embedding capacity and imperceptibility.

Conclusion

In this work, a secure and robust VMIE technique has been proposed by utilizing the chaotic maps and CNN based encryption and embedding methods. The use of CNN refined chaotic sequences significantly improves the randomness and security of the encryption process. Furthermore, hiding this image in a cover image using a U-Net based encoder provides covert transmission, that reduces the risk of exposure during communication. This integration efficiently combines cryptographic security with visual imperceptibility for secure data hiding. Experimental results confirm that the suggested framework achieves resilience, high key space and sensitivity. The PSNR and SSIM values demonstrate that high perceptual quality is preserved even at full payload capacity. Overall, the proposed approach provides a practical and efficient solution for secure and imperceptible communication. Future work may extend this framework to video encryption by applying the proposed algorithm on individual video frames. Each frame can be treated as an independent image and processed using the same CNN-refined chaotic encryption mechanism. To maintain real-time performance, lightweight CNN architectures and parallel frame processing techniques can be adopted. Additionally, alternative deep learning architectures beyond U-Net, such as generative adversarial networks or lightweight convolutional models such as ResNet or DenseNet variants could be adopted to improve feature extraction capability.

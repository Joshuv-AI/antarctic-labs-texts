Coherent control of optical lattices and optical unit cells in atomic medium by counter propagation of Laguerre Gaussian beams
Authors: Latif Ur Rahman, U. Zakir, Bakht Amin Bacha, Kamal Shah, Thabet Abdeljawad, Khaled Naseralla
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This manuscript explores the coherent control of optical lattices and optical unit cells. Optical lattices are formed by the counter-propagation of Laguerre Gaussian beams within an atomic medium. Variations in absorption and the refractive index along the x and y axes provide insight into the confined modes of the optical lattice and its unit cells, from which we can extract information about mode confinement. Density plots of the absorption spectrum and refractive index reveal the presence of unit cells in both the valley and peak regions of the counter-propagating Laguerre Gaussian standing wave fields. Introduction

Optical lattices1 are periodic potentials created by the standing waves of counter-propagating laser beams. They are essential tools in optical communications, atomic physics2, and quantum optics3. These optical lattices can trap neutral atoms within their potential wells4,5, allowing researchers to experimentally and theoretically explore the controlled and localized behavior of optical lattices using light fields. The precise measurement of the positions of optical unit cells within an optical lattice is referred to as the confinement of optical unit cells or lattice. The ability to control and manipulate light through coherent interactions with atoms trapped in optical lattices has opened new avenues for technological advancements and scientific research in the fields of photonics6,7 and atomic physics8. The manipulation of atoms and molecules through advanced laser technologies9 has led to an era characterized by remarkable accuracy and precision. Among the various techniques employed by researchers, optical lattices have proven to be a powerful tool for examining the behavior of ultra-cold atoms10. They facilitate quantum control at the single-particle level11, enable quantum simulations12, contribute to quantum entanglement13, and allow exploration of quantum phase transitions14,15. Additionally, they play a crucial role in the study of Bose-Einstein condensates16, the quantum state control of these condensates17, and the investigation of Mott insulators18. Most problems are solved mathematically in the regime of a coupled non-linear differential equations system19–23. Based on these applications, researchers have conducted both theoretical and experimental studies of optical lattices using diverse techniques and mechanisms24.

Eckardt et al.25 investigated the behavior of ultra-cold atoms trapped in optical lattices and explored the coherent control of quantum phase transitions to study dynamic localization and the structure of topological band gap materials. Dholakia et al.26 examined the effects of optical forces on shaping the amplitude and phase of laser beams for the purpose of manipulating particles and light. Volpe et al.27 provided a roadmap to theoretically and experimentally investigate the design and setup of optical tweezers and forces. Rey Ana Maria28 studied a strongly correlated regime of ultra-cold atoms confined in optical lattices, progressing from a non-interacting simple system to many-body physics. Using an atomic chip, Treutlein et al.29 experimentally detected magnetically trapped cold atoms within a small cavity. Sarkar and his colleagues30 experimentally studied the behaviors of quantum sensors enhanced by quantum effects and the dynamics of quantum phase transitions, which are expected to exhibit significant responses to local perturbations. Pelayo et al.31 discussed distributed quantum sensing using optical lattices. Additionally, D. Neshev et al.32 conducted an experimental investigation into the control and localization of light propagation within optical lattices. Their findings revealed important connections with photonic crystals, indicating promising applications in fields such as biosensing, beam shaping, quantum computing, and optical communications. M. Bhattacharya33 examines the waveguiding properties of the optical interference pattern formed by two counter-propagating Laguerre–Gaussian beams. The beam parameters are connected to various phenomena related to transverse confinement. Quantitative connections to the well-known Gaussian optical lattice were established, and an application for quantum transport was proposed. Numerous research papers have been published concerning the manipulation and controlled behavior of atoms in optical lattices under laser and Laguerre–Gaussian beams influences34–37. Recently photonic lattice unit cells are controlled in a tunnelling based chiral atomic medium38. Additionally, Vortex beams assisted manipulation of absorption spectrum and refractive index have also been demonstrated in atomic media39,40. To our knowledge, no literature currently addresses the mode confinement, and periodicity of optical lattices and their corresponding unit cells using the counter-propagation of Laguerre–Gaussian beams. The counter-propagation of these beams modifies the probe field and carries information regarding the localization of optical unit cells. This study investigates the confinement mode and periodicity of optical lattice unit cells along the x and y dimensions by employing the counter-propagation of standing wave Laguerre–Gaussian fields within an atomic medium. We analyze the absorption and dispersion variations of the probe field to achieve significant control over the optical lattice unit cells. Our findings highlight how the absorption and dispersion spectra of the probe fields can be manipulated using controlled Laguerre–Gaussian beams in an atomic medium. Ultimately, we successfully localized and controlled Wigner and cubic-type optical unit cells in optical lattices. We discuss the implications of our findings, which may have meaningful contributions to both applied and fundamental research.

Due to the distinct characteristics of Laguerre–Gaussian beams, these beams can create complex potential wells and manipulate optical lattices and their unit cells by varying parameters such as intensity, phase, beam waists, and the directions of the Laguerre beams. An optical lattice with deeper potential wells is created by increasing the intensity of the laser beam, which allows for more effective trapping of atoms. The density of the valleys and peaks in the optical lattice is determined by the wavelength of the laser beams. By reducing the beam waist, the stability of the localized states within the optical lattice is improved due to stronger confinement and decreased tunneling probabilities between adjacent sites. As a result, it becomes more challenging for the atoms to escape from the potential wells because of the increased depth of the potential. Proper detuning is essential for achieving strong localization of the optical unit cells, as it also impacts scattering rates and heating effects within the lattice. Conclusion

In conclusion, this study explores a four-level atomic medium that is influenced by a probe electric field and two Laguerre Gaussian beams to control and modify the localization of the optical lattice and its unit cells. We calculate the position-dependent electric susceptibility using density matrix formalism based on the coherence term. The imaginary part of the electric susceptibility indicates the absorption spectrum, while the real part describes the refractive index. We report significant control over the optical unit cells within the optical lattice. This manuscript investigates the coherent control of both the optical lattice and the optical unit cells. An optical lattice is created by the counter-propagation of Laguerre–Gaussian (LG) beams within an atomic medium. The absorption spectrum and refractive index in the low potential regions of the valleys provide insights into the mode confinement of the optical lattice unit cells. The rate of change in the absorption spectrum along the x and y axes exhibits a lattice-type oscillation. The variations in absorption and refractive index carry essential information regarding the confined modes of the optical lattice and the optical unit cells. This localization information is derived from changes in the absorption and refractive index. The optical unit cells of the lattice are identified by analyzing the density of the absorption and refractive index spectrum in the valley regions of the counter-propagating LG standing wave fields.

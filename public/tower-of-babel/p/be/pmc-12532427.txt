Dysregulation of miR-302a-3p in diabetic nephropathy and its role in inflammatory response
Authors: Lingbo Lv, Xin Zhang, Guoxia Luo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis research aims to reveal the regulatory mechanism of miR-302a-3p in diabetic nephropathy (DN) and its role in inflammatory responses.MethodsSerum samples were collected from DN patients and healthy controls, and RT-qPCR was employed to determine miR-302a-3p expression levels. The diagnostic value of this molecule in DN was evaluated through the receiver operating characteristic curve. A high-glucose condition was induced in HK-2 cells to establish an in vitro cell model. CCK-8 and flow cytometry were used to assess cell viability and apoptosis changes. ELISA was used to detect the levels of inflammatory factors, and the ROS assay kit was used to assess the level of ROS. The dual-luciferase reporter assay confirmed the targeted binding relationship between miR-302a-3p and FGF-16. Functional rescue experiments were conducted by knocking down FGF-16.ResultsThe level of miR-302a-3p in the serum of patients with DN was significantly increased, and its area under the curve (AUC) for diagnosing DN was 0.9168. High glucose induced an upregulation of miR-302a-3p in HK-2 cells. Inhibiting miR-302a-3p significantly reversed high glucose-induced cell apoptosis and release of ROS and pro-inflammatory factors. miR-302a-3p directly targets and inhibits FGF-16. In HK-2 cells induced by high glucose, knocking down FGF-16 would eliminate the protective effect of miR-302a-3p inhibitor on the cells.ConclusionsmiR-302a-3p enhances inflammatory response, oxidative stress and cell apoptosis by targeting and inhibiting FGF-16, thereby promoting renal tubular damage in DN.Keywords: Diabetic nephropathy, miR-302a-3p, FGF-16, Inflammatory, Oxidative stress, Renal tubule

Background

Diabetic nephropathy (DN) is a common microvascular complication in patients with diabetes mellitus (DM), and it significantly increases the mortality rate of these patients [1, 2]. Its primary hazards include causing irreversible renal failure, significantly increasing the risk of cardiovascular diseases, and triggering multiple organ damage [3]. Currently, the treatment of DN mainly relies on controlling blood glucose, controlling blood pressure, and the use of RAAS inhibitors [4–8]. However, it must be acknowledged that existing therapies cannot achieve a permanent cure of the disease. Therefore, it is of utmost importance to develop new therapies that can target the core pathological mechanisms of DN.

MiRNAs are critical regulators of DN by regulating multiple pathways, including oxidative stress, apoptosis, inflammation, autophagy, and fibrosis [9–16]. Among these, miR-302a-3p is significantly upregulated in diabetic patients [17]. Its upstream regulatory factor, vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), has been found to potentially participate in the regulation of renal microvascular lesions and the inflammatory microenvironment by modulating miR-302a-3p [18, 19]. It is worth noting that in the ischemia-reperfusion injury environment, upregulated miR-302a-3p promotes pyroptosis in renal tubular epithelial cell by targeting FMR1 [20]. These data suggest that miR-302a-3p may play a crucial role in the development of DN.

Fibroblast growth factor (FGF)-16, as a core member of the FGF-9 subfamily, plays a central role in cardiovascular diseases [21, 22], cancer [23, 24], and metabolic diseases [25]. It exhibits multi-organ protective mechanisms in diabetic complications. In DN, it mitigates kidney damage by inhibiting glomerular endothelial cell apoptosis and antagonizing oxidative stress [26]. In diabetic myocardial tissues, it can reduce myocardial fibrosis and inflammatory responses after infarction, promote the differentiation of M2 type macrophages, and release anti-inflammatory factors, thereby improving cardiac remodeling and function [27]. In DN, it acts as a key effector molecule in vascular lesions and promotes the proliferation and migration of endothelial cells through the MAPK pathway [28]. These studies highlight its therapeutic potential as a target for tissue repair.

Based on these observations, we propose the hypothesis that miR-302a-3p is involved in the pathological processes associated with DN. Therefore, our aim is to clarify the regulatory role of miR-302a-3p in DN and its underlying mechanism, as well as its targeted effect on FGF-16. We also aim to systematically analyze the regulatory mechanism of the miR-302a-3p/FGF-16 axis in DN.

Methods

Participants and sample collection

This study included a total of 96 participants who visited Shenzhen Second People’s Hospital between October 2023 and June 2024. The study subjects were divided into two groups: 53 patients with DN and 43 healthy individuals. The patient was diagnosed with DN according to the relevant standards of the American Diabetes Association (ADA). Exclusion criteria include: pregnant/lactating women, patients with nephropathy caused by other primary or secondary kidney diseases (such as glomerulonephritis, vasculitis, etc.), patients with uncontrolled severe hypertension or hypertensive renal insufficiency. All healthy controls had no history of diabetes, kidney disease, or other chronic conditions. The Ethics Committee of Shenzhen Second People’s Hospital sanctioned this research. The research process strictly followed the principles of the Helsinki Declaration, and the participants signed written informed consent forms.

All participants fasted overnight (> 8 h) and then had 10 ml of peripheral blood were drawn from the upper limb vein. The blood samples were immediately subjected to centrifugation to separate the serum, which was then frozen at −80 °C for future use.

Cell culture

HK-2 cells were sourced from the National Certified Cell Culture Collection Center. Cells were cultured in DMEM containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin under standard conditions (37 °C, 5% CO₂). The Control group received basal DMEM with 5.5 mM glucose; the high glucose (HG) group received DMEM with 30 mM glucose [29]. The samples were subjected to HG treatment for 0 h, 12 h, 24 h and 48 h, and the expression levels of the target miRNA were measured. Based on the results of the previous experiments, we finally decided to use 30 mM glucose to induce the cells for 24 h in the subsequent experiments.

Cell transfection

Firstly, we entrusted GenePharma Biotechnology Company to synthesize miRNA inhibitors (miR-302a-3p inhibitor) and small interfering RNAs (si-FGF-16) that specifically interfere with miR-302a-3p and FGF-16, as well as their negative controls (Inhibitor NC, si-NC). miR-302a-3p inhibitor is a chemically modified antisense oligonucleotide, with its sequence complementary to miR-302a-3p. si-FGF16 is a double-stranded RNA molecule synthesized based on the sequence of FGF-16, and it has the function of guiding the degradation of FGF-16. Subsequently, the cells were inoculated into the cell culture plates. The synthesized miRNA powder was prepared into a 50 mM stock solution in RNase-free water. Then, according to the instructions of the Lipofectamine™ 2000 transfection reagent (Invitrogen), the transfection complex containing miRNA inhibitors and small interfering RNA was added to the culture plates. The final concentrations of miRNA inhibitors and small interfering RNA were 50 nM. Six hours later, the old culture medium was removed, and the cells continued to be induced and cultured under high glucose conditions for another 24 h.

Reverse transcription quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

Serum and HK-2 cell-derived total RNA was extracted using TRIzol™ reagent (Invitrogen). Subsequently, the complementary DNA (cDNA) synthesis was carried out using the PrimeScript reverse transcription kit (Takara). The quantitative PCR amplification was performed using TB Green qPCR Mix (Takara) on the ABI 7900HT platform. U6 and GAPDH were used as endogenous controls for miR-302a-3p and FGF-16 respectively. Relative expression of target genes was quantified via the 2−ΔΔCt method.

Cell counting kit -8 (CCK-8)

HK-2 cells were seeded in a 96-well plate at a density of 5 × 10³ cells per well. Following incubation, administer 10 µL CCK-8 per well. The cells were cultured in a dark environment at 37 °C and 5% carbon dioxide for 4 h, and then the absorbance was measured at 450 nm.

Cell apoptosis assay

After the HK-2 cells were treated with trypsin, they were washed with PBS. Then, it was re-suspended in the pre-cooled binding buffer and stained with 5 µL of Annexin V-FITC and PI. After incubation in the dark for 15 min, add 400 µL of binding buffer and immediately perform the detection using a flow cytometer.

Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA)

All the ELISA kits were purchased from Elascience. First, add 50 µL of the sample and 50 µL of the biotinylated antibody into the well. Then incubate at 37 °C for 2 h. Subsequently, clean the plate 5 times, followed by adding 100 µL of streptavidin-HRP and reacting at 37 °C for 20 min. After adding TMB, incubate in the dark for 15 min. After the reaction is terminated, measure the absorbance value at 450 nm wavelength using an enzyme detector.

Determination of Reactive oxygen species (ROS)

After 24 h of HG treatment, the ROS level in HK-2 cells was measured using the reactive oxygen species detection kit (Beyotime Biotechnology). First, remove the original culture medium and add the DCFH-DA probe. Place the sample in the dark for 20 min and then rinse the sample with serum-free medium. After collecting the cells, detect the fluorescence intensity using a fluorescence microplate reader. The ROS activity was ultimately expressed in relative fluorescence units (RFU).

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

The FGF-16 fragment containing the miR-302a-3p binding site and the FGF-16 fragment containing the mutation site were inserted into the pmiRGLO vector to construct the wild-type plasmid (FGF-16-WT) and the mutant plasmid (FGF-16-MUT). FGF-16-WT and FGF-16-MUT were respectively co-transfected with mimic NC and miR-302a-3p mimic into HK-2 cells. After 48 h of transfection, the luminescence was measured using Dual-Glo® Luciferase Assay System (Promega).

Statistical analysis

The data analysis of this study was completed based on the GraphPad Prism and SPSS platforms. The independent t-test and ANOVA (one-way) were applied for intergroup statistical analyses. Continuous variables were presented in the form of mean ± standard deviation, and statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05. All tests and cell experiments were independently repeated three times.

Results

The level of miR-302a-3p in DN patients and its potential diagnostic value for DN

To assess the potential value of miR-302a-3p in the diagnosis for DN, the level of miR-302a-3p in patients were quantified by RT-qPCR. Compared with the controls, the level of miR-302a-3p was significantly elevated in patients with DN (P < 0.0001, Fig. 1A). The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis further confirmed that miR-302a-3p has excellent diagnostic value (AUC = 0.9168, 95% CI: 0.8637–0.9699). The high sensitivity (92.31%) and specificity (76.14%) indicated that it has strong screening potential as a diagnostic biomarker for DN (Fig. 1B).

The level of miR-302-3p in HG-induced HK-2 cells

To examine the role of miR-302a-3p in DN, we established an in vitro model of HK-2 cells through hyperglycemic injury induced by HG. The findings revealed that the level of miR-302a-3p was upregulated over time (P < 0.01, Fig. 2A). However, the increase in miR-302a-3p level induced by HG was significantly weakened after the use of miR-302a-3p inhibitor (P < 0.0001, Fig. 2B).

The transcriptional levels of miR-302a-3p following transfection with its specific inhibitor. ns, P > 0.05; * P < 0.05; ** P < 0.01; *** P < 0.001; **** P < 0.0001. n = 3

The effect of miR-302a-3p on HG-induced proliferation, apoptosis, oxidative stress, and inflammation in HK-2 cells

HG exposure significantly reduced cell viability and increased the rate of cell apoptosis (P < 0.05, Fig. 3A and B). Concurrently, the HG treatment also led to an increase in the production of ROS and elevated the levels of pro-inflammatory factors (P < 0.01, Fig. 3C and D). After transfection with the miR-302a-3p inhibitor, the phenomena such as decreased cell viability, increased cell apoptosis, accumulation of reactive oxygen species, and elevation of inflammatory factors caused by high glucose were all significantly reversed. This suggests that inhibiting miR-302a-3p can significantly antagonize HG-induced cellular damage.

FGF-16 is a direct target of miR-302a-3p

We used the TargetScan database to predict a conserved binding site for miR-302a-3p and FGF-16 (Fig. 4A). The luciferase reporter gene assay showed that miR-302a-3p mimic significantly suppressed the activity of the wild-type luciferase, but had no effect on the mutant construct (P < 0.001, Fig. 4B). This confirms that miR-302a-3p might directly target FGF-16. In patients with DN, the expression of FGF-16 was significantly decreased, and a significant negative correlation was observed between this molecule and miR-302a-3p. (P < 0.0001, Fig. 4C and D). In HG-induced HK-2 cells, the expression of FGF-16 was downregulated over time (P < 0.01, Fig. 4E). Moreover, miR-302a-3p mimics was able to reduce the expression of FGF-16, while the addition of miR-302a-3p inhibitors produced the opposite result (P < 0.05, Fig. 4F). This confirmed the negative regulatory role of miR-302a-3p on FGF-16.

The downregulation of FGF-16 reversed the effects of miR-302a-3p silencing on renal tubule repair

In order to clarify the role of FGF-16 in miR-302a-3p-mediated damage to HK-2 cells, this study conducted a co-transfection experiment using miR-302a-3p inhibitors and si-FGF-16. The results showed that the HG environment significantly suppressed FGF-16 (P < 0.05, Fig. 5A). After transfection with the miR-302a-3p inhibitor, the expression of FGF-16 was upregulated. This was accompanied by an increase in cell viability, a decrease in apoptosis rates, a reduction in ROS accumulation, and a decrease in inflammatory factors levels (P < 0.05, Fig. 5B and E). However, when FGF-16 was knocked down, the protective effects of the miR-302a-3p inhibitor in HK-2 cells were completely reversed. This confirmed that FGF-16 is the key effector molecule of miR-302a-3p in pathogenesis of DN.

Discussion

DN is caused by long-term high blood sugar and can lead to microvascular damage in the kidneys, thereby causing glomerulosclerosis, proteinuria, and progressive renal dysfunction. The pathological process of this disease involves oxidative stress, inflammation and metabolic disorders [4, 30, 31]. DN significantly increases the mortality of diabetic patients [32]. Therefore, it is necessary to continuously explore molecular intervention targets for the core pathological and physiological pathways of DN, in order to prevent or reverse the progression of this disease. This study systematically revealed the role of the miR-302a-3p/FGF-16 axis in renal tubular injury, thereby providing a new target for targeted intervention in DN.

During the development and evolution of DN, multiple miRNAs play key regulatory roles. For example, miR-1225-3p can interfere with the ubiquitination and degradation of ChREBP protein, thereby causing fibrosis and oxidative stress in mesangial cells [33]. miR-504-3p can lead to a reduction in podocyte proliferation, an increase in cell apoptosis, and an imbalance in oxidative stress, thereby exacerbating podocyte damage [34]. miR-1281 promotes glomerular cell proliferation, inhibits apoptosis, and increases intracellular inflammatory levels, resulting in impaired renal function in DN patients [35]. We confirmed that the levels of miR-302a-3p were elevated in DN patients, and the ROC curve showed that it had excellent diagnostic performance. This result is in line with the expression situation of miR-302a-3p in the renal tissues of DN patients reported by Tang et al. [17]. Moreover, our study further indicates that this molecule can serve as a non-invasive biomarker for the diagnosis and risk prediction of DN. In vitro models showed that HG induced a time-dependent increases in miR-302a-3p. At the same time, inhibiting the expression of this molecule significantly reversed the apoptosis, accumulation of reactive oxygen species, and secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines caused by the high sugar environment. This confirms that miR-302a-3p promotes the progression of DN by synergistically driving inflammation, oxidative stress, and cell death.

FGF-16 mainly participates in the progression of diseases by promoting cell proliferation and inhibiting inflammatory responses and other physiological processes [36]. Through dual luciferase reporter assays, this study confirmed a direct relationship between miR-302a-3p and FGF-16. In clinical samples, the decreased expression of FGF-16 was correlated with miR-302a-3p. In cell models, Overexpression of miR-302a-3p substantially inhibited the expression of FGF-16. Importantly, the co-transfection experiments revealed that knocking down FGF-16 reversed the protective effects of miR-302a-3p inhibitors on renal tubular epithelial cells. This confirmed that FGF-16 is the core effector molecule in this pathway. This result expands the protective mechanism of FGF-16 in DN. FGF16 not only inhibits the apoptosis of glomerular endothelial [26], but also can exert anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects on renal tubular damage.

Conclusions

This study systematically elucidated the central role of the miR-302a-3p/FGF-16 signaling axis in DN. miR-302a-3p enhances inflammation, oxidative stress, and apoptosis by inhibiting the expression of FGF-16, ultimately leading to renal tubular damage. This discovery not only deepens our understanding of the molecular mechanism of DN, but also lays a theoretical foundation for the development of precise treatment strategies based on miRNA intervention.

Limitations

MiRNA holds great potential as a new biomarker for the diagnosis of DN, but its clinical application still faces numerous challenges. Through multi-center studies, the clinical translational ability of miRNA diagnostic methods can be systematically evaluated, and key issues such as standardization, stability, and reproducibility can be addressed. Therefore, to further validate the diagnostic value of miR-302a-3p in DN and the reliability of using this molecule for clinical diagnosis, it will still be necessary to collect a large number of clinical samples through multi-center studies to verify the sensitivity and specificity of miR-302a-3p as a diagnostic marker for DN. Additionally, studies have shown that miR-302a-3p can inhibit the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) of renal tubular epithelial cells. It is notable that EMT is a core feature of DN at the advanced stage. However, our study demonstrated that under short-term high glucose stimulation, miR-302a-3p promotes the inflammation of renal tubular epithelial cells induced by high glucose. This may reflect the changes and characteristics of renal tubular epithelial cells in the early stage of DN. Therefore, in the future, it will still be necessary to conduct a more comprehensive exploration of the role and function of miR-302a-3p in different stages of DN in animal models.

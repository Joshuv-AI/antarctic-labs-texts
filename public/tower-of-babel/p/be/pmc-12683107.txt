Integrative multi-omics analysis reveals key regulatory nodes of abdominal fat deposition in broiler chickens
Authors: Junjie Ma, Zhuqing Zheng, Xiaoying Liu, Xi Sun, Yanli Liu, Xiaojun Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Chicken is a major meat source globally, but excessive abdominal fat deposition in broilers reduces production efficiency and nutrient utilization. To elucidate the genetic and metabolic basis of abdominal fat deposition, multi-omics analysis was performed on 42-days-old female broilers with 12 high (FH) and 12 low (FL) abdominal fat percentages (AFP) from a cohort raised under uniform conditions. Whole-genome resequencing identified 537 potential selective genes (PSGs) within selective regions. Transcriptomic profiling of abdominal fat and liver tissues revealed 153 and 291 differentially expressed genes (DEGs), respectively, with weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) pinpointing 109 and 335 lipid-associated module hub genes (MHGs). Integrative multi-omics analysis prioritized 18 key candidate genes, and some are related to fat deposition such as FOXF1, ACSS2, USP10, and SEC16B. Furthermore, serum metabolomics uncovered 41 differential metabolites that functionally bridged hepatic regulation and fat deposition, with L-Tyrosine, pantothenic acid, and 10-hydroxydecanoic acid emerging as critical discriminators. This study provides a comprehensive framework for understanding fat deposition in poultry, offering potential targets for enhancing production efficiency.

Introduction

The broiler industry plays a pivotal role in the global supply of animal protein. With the progression of intensive farming practices, feed costs account for up to 70 % of the total production expenses, and the decline in feed conversion efficiency due to abdominal fat deposition has emerged as a key bottleneck limiting profitability (Wang et al., 2025). Furthermore, excessive fat accumulation in broilers negatively impacts reproductive traits, such as fertility and hatchability in breeding flocks, and the carcass lean meat rate (Baeza et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2018). Consequently, deciphering the molecular signatures of abdominal fat deposition in broilers hold substantial scientific significance for the sustainable advancement of the industry.

Abdominal fat deposition in broilers is a polygenic trait shaped by complex genomic variations and multilayered regulatory mechanisms, complicating its genetic dissection and functional characterization (Wang et al., 2012). In recent years, genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have identified candidate genes associated with broiler fat traits, including FKBP1B, G3BP1, and SCARA5 (Li et al., 2025); as well as DUS3L, RFX2, ACSBG2, CORTBP2, and CFTR (Pan et al., 2024). Additionally, studies employing systems biology approaches that integrate multi-omics techniques have provided new insights into the characteristics of abdominal fat in chickens and its genetic regulation (Shen et al., 2024). The liver serves as the primary site for de novo lipogenesis and systemic metabolic coordination (Nguyen et al., 2008), whereas abdominal fat not only functions as a lipid storage depot but also modulates adipogenesis through secretory factors (Bou Matar et al., 2025). Most critically, serum metabolomic profiling serves as a bridge for inter-tissue communication, bridging genomic variations and phenotypic manifestations. Consequently, to decipher the mechanisms of abdominal fat deposition, a multi-tissue investigation that integrates multiomics is indispensable.

The Arbor Acres broiler represents a dominant global commercial line and is extensively raised breeds in China. However, its production efficiency is hampered by excessive abdominal fat deposition, which imposes a substantial economic burden. In thist study, through the integration of multi-omics approaches, we identified the key genes and metabolites that may regulate abdominal fat deposition in broilers and proposed the regulatory mechanisms by which these genes and metabolites influence abdominal fat deposition.

Materials and methods

All experimental protocol in the study were approved by the Institution Animal Care and Use Committee of the Northwest A&F University (Permit Number:DK2021023).

Animals, phenotypic measurements and sampling collection

All Arbor acres broilers were reared under uniform environmental conditions and were given free access to a commercial diet and water. At 42 days of age, blood samples were collected from the wing vein after weighing, after which all birds were euthanized via neck dislocation and subsequently dissected. The abdominal fat was manually separated and weighed, and the abdominal fat percentage (AFP) was calculated. The abdominal fat percentage (AFP) was calculated using the formula: AFP (%) = (abdominal fat weight / body weight) × 100 %. Based on their AFP values, the 12 broilers with the highest and lowest AFP were designated as the high-fat (FH) and low-fat (FL) groups, respectively. The abdominal fat tissues and liver tissues were immediately dissected and frozen with liquid nitrogen.

Measurements of serum biochemical parameters

The blood samples were centrifuged at 3000 × g for 10 min to collect serum. The relevant indices of serum lipid metabolism, including triglyceride (TG), total cholesterol (TC), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-c), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-c), glucose (GLU), as well as biomarkers of liver injury, such as total protein (TP), total bile acids (TBA), and aspartate aminotransferase (AST), were measured using the BIOBASE(BK-400) automatic biochemical analyzer.

DNA extraction and genome resequencing

The entirety of genomic DNA was obtained from liver tissues using the TIANamp Genomic DNA Kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions(Tiangen, Beijing, China). Libraries construction for resequencing was performed with NovoNGS Tn5 Transposase kit. And the qualified library was re-sequenced by DNBSEQ-T7 sequencing platform.

Genomic analysis

Whole-genome sequencing and genomic variant calling

WGS was carried out on 24 broilers Following the filtration of raw reads, each sample ended up with ∼23 Gb of high-quality clean bases, with an average depth of 24.41X coverage. The clean reads were subsequently mapped to the chicken GRCg7b reference genome (https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genomes/all/GCF/016/699/485/GCF_016699485.2_bGalGal1.mat.broiler.GRCg7b/) using the BWA (version 0.7.17-r1188) (Li and Durbin, 2009). The mapping results were sorted and de-duplicated using Picard (version 3.1.1). Genomic variants (SNPs) were identified by chromosome using the GATK (version 4.5.0.0) (Depristo et al., 2011) HaplotypeCaller module. Merged multiple sample's gVCF files for each chromosome using the GATK CombineGVCFs module. Jointed genotyping was conducted using the GATK GenotypeGVCFs module. Variations were filtered based on the criteria (QUAL > 40 && INFO/DP ≥137.4 (One-third of the average depth of all the samples) && INFO/DP ≤ 1236.6 (Three times of the average depth of all samples)) using the Bcftools (version 1.17) (Danecek et al., 2021). Finally, the ANNOVAR software (version 2020Jun7) (Wang et al., 2010) was employed for the annotation of genomic variant.

Population structure analysis

To investigate the genetic relationship between the FH and FL groups, kinship analysis was performed using GCTA (version 1.95.0) (Yang et al., 2011) software to calculate genetic distances and obtain a genetic relationship matrix between two samples and the principal component analysis (PCA) was also conducted using GCTA software. For phylogenetic reconstruction, genetic distances were calculated between all individuals using PLINK software (version 1.90b7.4) (Purcell et al., 2007). The distance matrix was subsequently used to construct neighbor-joining tree as implemented in MEGA (version 11.0) (Tamura et al., 2021). The final tree topology was visualized in iTOL (version 6.7.4) (Letunic and Bork, 2021).

Selection signature analysis

Genome-wide selection scans were performed to identify genomic regions under divergent selection between the FH and FL groups using two complementary haplotype-based methods: the cross-population extended haplotype homozygosity (XP-EHH) and XP-nSL. Variants with a missingness rate greater than 10 % were first excluded using BCFtools. The program Beagle (version 5.5) (Browning et al., 2021) was used to phase haplotypes. XP-EHH and XP-nSL were performed using selscan (version 2.1.0) (Szpiech et al., 2021) software. Raw XP-EHH and XP-nSL scores were computed across the genome (selscan flags –vcf FH.vcf –vcf-ref FL.vcf) using selscan (version 2.1.0) (Szpiech et al., 2021), scores were then normalized using the genome-wide empirical background with selscan's norm version 1.3.1 program. Normalized scores were then averaged in sliding windows approach (30-kb windows, 10-kb step size). Both XP-EHH and XP-nSL scores are directional, a positive score suggests the selection is likely to have happened in FL group, whereas a negative score indicates the same about FH. The windows with extreme positive and negative values (one-tailed Z test depending on selection direction, P-value < 0.005) were retained as candidate selection windows. To minimize false positives and identify high-confidence signals, the intersection of windows identified by XP-EHH and XP-nSL were kept as final PGSRs.

Transcriptome

Total RNA was extracted from abdominal fat tissues and liver tissues using the TRIZOL kit protocol (AG21101, Agbio, China), and the quality and concentration of the RNA were assessed using the NanoDrop 2000. The qualified RNA was used to construct sequencing libraries with the Illumina TruSeq® Kit (Illumina, San Diego, USA). Raw data underwent quality control to eliminate adapter sequences and low-quality reads using fastp (Chen et al., 2018) with a minimum read length threshold of 150 bp. Subsequently, RNA samples were quantified in Salmon (version 1.10.0) (Patro et al., 2017). A decoy-aware mapping-based indexed transcriptome generated from the reference genome with k-mers of length 31 and the transcriptome annotation was used during quantification. Differential gene expression analysis was performed in edgeR (version 4.2.0), which was used for both sample clustering and identification of significantly differentially expressed genes (DEGs) (|log2 fold change| => 1 and P-value < 0.05).

WGCNA

To identify gene sets responsible for abdominal fat deposition in abdominal fat tissues and liver tissues, a co-expression network analysis was performed using the WGCNAshiny.win64 in TBtools-II (version 2.119) (Chen et al., 2023a). The network was constructed by employing topology overlap matrix, module detection, and similar module merging functions. The minimum module size was set to 30 and the module cuttree height was set to 0.25. Abdominal fat traits, including FH and FL groups, were used as trait files to evaluate module-trait relationships.

Functional enrichment analysis

The Gene Ontology (GO) term and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analyses were performed using the clusterProfiler R packages (version 4.7.1.003). Terms with P-value < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

RT-qPCR

Extracted RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using the Evo M-MLV RT Master Mix kit (AG11728, Agbio, China), following the protocol provided by manufacturer. The mRNA levels were quantified using the SYBR Green Pro Taq™ II kit (Agbio) on the FQD-96A (Bioer). Melting curve analysis was conducted to validate the specificity of the qPCR products. β-actin was used as the internal reference for subsequent normalization, with primer sequences detailed in Table 1. Serum untargeted metabolomics

Metabolomic profiling was conducted by Shanghai Personal Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Serum protein precipitation was achieved by methanol extraction with an internal standard, followed by centrifugation and transfer of the supernatant into a sample via for subsequent analysis. The metabolic extracts were analyzed using liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry with the Ultra High Performance Liquid Chromatography System (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and the Thermo Scientific Q-Exactive high-accuracy mass spectrometer. Raw data processing, which included feature extraction, peak identification/alignment, and spectral library matching, was conducted in accordance with the established protocols (Li et al., 2017). Molecular mass validation was performed using exact m/z values derived from redundant ion peaks. Orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) was implemented via the Ropls package to mitigate inter-group variability. Differential metabolites were identified from the list of primary substances in the sample and screened according to the preset P-value and variable importance projection (VIP) thresholds in the statistical test (P-value < 0.05, VIP > 1, | fold change | > 1). Pathway enrichment analysis of differential metabolites was executed through the KEGG database (http://www.kegg.jp/), with annotations mapped to canonical metabolic pathways. The differential metabolites machine learning analysis employs the random forest algorithm and was accomplished using the R package mlr3verse (version 0.2.7).

Statistical analysis

All data were presented as Mean ± SEM (Standard error of mean), and comparisons between different groups were analyzed with the unpaired Student's t-test and plotted using GraphPad Prism 9 (San Diego, USA). The P-value < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant, which was indicated as *P-value < 0.05, **P-value < 0.01.

Results

Phenotypic characterization reveals divergent abdominal fat deposition without metabolic impairment

The distribution of abdominal fat within the entire broiler population exhibited characteristics of a normal distribution. The average AFP for the FH and the FL group was 1.53 and 0.78, respectively (Fig. 1A). Although significant differences in AFP were observed between the FH and FL groups, no significant differences were observed in body weight (Fig. 1B and C). Additionally, serum biochemical indicators, including TG, TC, HDL-C, LDL-C, GLU, TP, TBA, AST, and ALP, showed no significant differences between the two groups (Fig. 1D). Genome-wide sequencing and selective sweeps analysis reveals candidate regions and genes associated with abdominal fat traits in broilers

To identify genes related to abdominal fat traits, we performed WGS on two populations: 12 individuals from FH group and 12 individuals from FL group. The WGS generated approximately 553.18 G of high-quality clean reads, with an average sequencing depth of 24.41 per individual (Supplementary Table 1). After quality control and filtering, we identified 12,799,782 autosomal SNPs from 24 individuals, of which 215,435 SNPs were located within exonic regions (Supplementary Table 2). Additionally, we conducted phylogenetic and PCA analyses to investigate the genetic relationships among these individuals. As expected, due to the influence of polygenes with minor effects and the limited number of generations of intense selection, the FH and FL populations did not distinctly separate into two clusters (Fig S1). This pattern of genetic stratification, rather than discrete divergence, is advantageous for selective sweep analysis, as it mitigates false positives often arising from pronounced population structure.

To identify PGSRs located on autosomes that may have been under selection underlying the phenotypic divergence in abdominal fat between FH and FL broilers, we scanned for genomic regions with significant selection signals (using XP-EHH and XP-nSL) by applying a 30 kb sliding window. A total of 561 PGSRs (one-tailed Z test depending on selection direction, P-value < 0.005) were identified as the intersecting genomic windows by XP-EHH and XP-nSL. These regions harbored 537 potential selective genes (PSGs) (Fig. 2A and B). Specifically, 264 common signals with extreme negative scores (normalized XP-EHH < −2.43; normalized XP-nSL < −2.31) suggest long high-frequency haplotypes in the FH group (Fig. 2A, Fig S2A), and 297 common signals exhibiting extreme positive scores (normalized XP-EHH > 2.45; normalized XP-nSL > 2.30) suggest analogous signatures in the FL group (Fig. 2B, Fig S2B). Subsequently, we performed GO and KEGG enrichment analyses on PSGs to investigate the biological processes and functions in which these trait-differentiating genes are primarily involved. The GO pathway analysis revealed that PSGs were predominantly enriched in the pathways related to canonical Wnt signaling pathway, regulation of canonical Wnt signaling pathway, Wnt signaling pathway, endoplasmic reticulum exit site, and nuclear receptor activity (Fig. 2C). And the KEGG pathway analysis revealed that PSGs were predominantly enriched in the pathways associated with Insulin signaling pathway, MAPK signaling pathway, and Mannose type O-glycan biosynthesis (Fig. 2D). These analyses revealed that the PSGs may be associated with adipogenesis and systemic metabolic regulation. Transcriptomic analysis and WGCNA identify key genes affecting abdominal fat deposition

To investigate the impact of genetic variations identified through genome-wide analysis on gene expression levels, we performed a transcriptomic analysis on the abdominal fat tissues and liver tissues of FH and FL broilers. We constructed 24 mRNA libraries, yielding 167.77 Gb of high-quality RNA-seq data for abdominal fat tissues (7 Gb per sample) and 212.91 Gb for liver tissues (8.87 Gb per sample). A total of 20,081 genes were expressed in both tissues, including 13,939 annotated and 6,142 unannotated genes. Although PCA did not completely separate the FH and FL broilers (Fig. 3A), a total of 291 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified in the abdominal fat tissues between the two groups, including 153 upregulated and 138 downregulated genes in the FL group compared to the FH group. And a total of 153 DEGs were identified in the liver tissues between the FH and FL groups, consisting of 80 upregulated and 73 downregulated genes in the FL group relative to the FH group (Fig. 3B). Furthermore, we applied WGCNA to the transcriptomic data of abdominal fat tissues and liver tissues transcriptomic data to identify hub genes that are significantly related to abdominal fat - related phenotypes. After a comprehensive evaluation, beta values of 9 and 22 were selected for abdominal fat tissues and liver tissues, to construct scale - free networks (Fig. 3C and D). To explore the relationship between functional modules and phenotypes, we conducted a correlation analysis between gene modules and abdominal fat phenotypes. We focused on the top three most significant modules identified in both abdominal fat tissues and liver tissues. In abdominal fat tissues, the light yellow module (r = 0.52, P-value = 0.0091) and the green yellow module (r = 0.51, P-value = 0.01) were positively correlated with low fat, while the dark turquoise module (r = −0.46, P-value = 0.025) displayed a negative correlation (Fig S3). In liver tissue, the midnight blue module (r = 0.53, P-value = 0.0073) and the tan module (r = 0.45, P-value = 0.029) were positively correlated with low fat, whereas the turquoise module (r = −0.46, P-value = 0.024) showed a negative correlation (Fig S4). In the subsequent research, we further screened the module hub genes (MHGs) based on the gene significance (GS) and module membership (MM). Specifically, the functional modules of abdominal fat and liver tissue that we examined contained 109 and 335 MHGs, respectively (Supplementary Table 3).Subsequently, we performed GO enrichment analyses on these MHGs to investigate the biological processes and functions in which these trait-differentiating genes are primarily involved. The reasults revealed that MHGs in darkturquoise and turquoise module were predominantly enriched in the pathways of protein secretion, protein transport and the functions of the endoplasmic reticulum and the Golgi apparatus (Fig S5).The MHGs in greenyellow and lightyellow module Both are related to the morphogenesis of cells and cell movement (Fig S6).And the MHGs in midnightblue and tan module both are related to the functions of cell signal transduction and cell membranes (Fig S7).

Identification and quantitative validation of key candidate genes for abdominal fat traits

To identify key candidate genes (KCGs) associated with abdominal fat traits, we integrated candidate genes derived from multiple omics approaches, including PSGs, DEGs and MHGs. In abdominal fat tissues and liver tissues, we identified 18 potential KCGs, among which FOXF1 (normalized XP-EHH = 2.50; normalized XP-nSL = 2.41) emerged as the only gene supported by all three omics layers, underscoring its potential central role. Notably, PTPRO (normalized XP-EHH = 3.67; normalized XP-nSL = 3.06) and SEC16B (normalized XP-EHH = 4.57; normalized XP-nSL = 2.61) were also prioritized due to exhibited high genetic differentiation and established associations with lipid metabolism. The expression levels of these 20 genes were compared between the FH and FL groups in both abdominal fat tissues and liver tissues (Fig. 4A and B). Identification of serum metabolites related to abdominal fat traits

To explore whether genomic variations regulate gene expression and consequently influence metabolic synthesis and phenotypic variations, we collected serum samples from the FH and FL groups and conducted metabolomic analysis. OPLS - DA analysis demonstrated a clear separation between the FH and FL groups, with tight clustering observed within each group (Fig. 5A), thereby enhancing the reliability of the subsequent differential metabolite screening. A total of 41 differential metabolites were identified, comprising 18 upregulated and 23 downregulated metabolites in the FL group. These metabolites primarily consisted of amino acids and derivatives, fatty acids and their derivatives, as well as organic acids (Fig. 5B and Table 2). The abundance of these 41 metabolites showed significant differences between the FH and FL groups (Fig. 5C). KEGG pathway analysis indicated that the differential metabolites were predominantly enriched in pathways related to the biosynthesis of amino acids, aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis, D-amino acid metabolism, pantothenate and CoA biosynthesis, 2 - oxocarboxylic acid metabolism, and the biosynthesis of cofactors (Fig. 5D). Random forest machine learning analysis of the differential metabolites identified the top 20 differential metabolites (Fig. 5E). Discussion

Excessive abdominal fat deposition in broilers has long posed a significant challenge in poultry production, compromising the nutritional quality of meat products and causing serious economic losses (Kpomasse et al., 2021). Consequently, this also highlights the importance of investigating the genetic mechanisms responsible for the pronounced variation in abdominal fat traits. Multi-omics approaches, including genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics, facilitate the systematic characterization of economically important traits in livestock across distinct biological levels (Dehau et al., 2022). Conducting multi-omics analuses within a single cohort helps minimize experimental variability and individual differences arising from diverse environmental and genetic backgrounds across different populations, thereby reducing their confounding effects on research outcomes (Liang et al., 2022). Through the integration of multi-omics analyses and tissue-specific validations, we identified KCGs and serum metabolites associated with divergent lipid phenotypes, thereby providing novel insights into the genetic and metabolic mechanisms governing lipid accumulation.

In recent years, grouping strategies based on extreme phenotypic selection have been extensively adopted in various studies to investigate the regulatory mechanisms underlying phenotypic variation (Xu et al., 2023; Shen et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024a; Cai et al., 2023). In this study, we employed Arbor Acres broilers with identical body weights but divergent AFP for cohort analysis. This approach presents distinct advantages in effectively amplifying genetic differences associated with adipose deposition. At the whole-genome level, our constructed FH and FL groups did not exhibit complete genetic separation (Fig S1), indicating minimal population stratification. This is consistent with the fact that FH and FL groups were derived from a single commercial Arbor Acres population under uniform conditions, and were differentiated solely by extreme phenotypic selection for AFP within one generation rather than by long-term artificial selection on genotype. The absence of significant differences in serum biochemical parameters between FH and FL groups further supports that the observed divergence in abdominal fat deposition occurred without major disruptions of systemic metabolic homeostasis, likely reflecting the polygenic nature of the trait wherein cumulative minor-effect alleles collectively influence the trait without driving genome-wide divergence (Symonds and Zuberi, 2018). This pattern contrasts with long-term selected lines (Dong et al., 2015), where sustained selection pressure leads to correlated changes in serum biochemical profiles. In contrast, metabolomic profiling revealed a clear separation between the FH and FL groups, likely attributable to metabolism representing the terminal phenotypic output of biological processes (Fiehn, 2002). This metabolic dichotomy underscores the robustness of our findings.

As a complex quantitative trait, abdominal fat deposition is governed by intricate genetic and metabolic networks. Through multi-omics integration, we identified KCGs, among which FOXF1 emerged as a high-priority candidate. Previous research has indicated that FOXF1 can suppress the activation of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway by downregulating CDH11 (Chen et al., 2023b), while canonical Wnt signaling has been proven to inhibit the differentiation of adipocytes through inhibition PPARγ (Ross et al., 2000) and reducing both visceral and subcutaneous fat mass (Wright et al., 2007). The inhibition of PPARγ likely leads to the downregulation of its downstream target genes, which are essential for de novo fatty acid synthesis and elongation (Wang et al., 2012). The activation of Wnt signaling provides a plausible mechanistic explanation for the low AFP in the FL group and the significant downregulation of medium and long chain fatty acids (caproic acid, heptanoic acid, and myristic acid), potentially through inhibition of adipose precursor differentiation and restriction the expansion of adipose tissue. Meanwhile, functional enrichment analysis also highlighted significant convergence of PSGs in canonical Wnt signaling, Insulin signaling, and MAPK signaling, suggesting their coordinate roles in regulating adipose development and metabolism.

In broilers, the liver and abdominal fat are pivotal organsresponsible for lipid synthesis and storage, which play critical regulatory roles in abdominal fat deposition (Wang et al., 2024b; Nematbakhsh et al., 2021). The liver tissues synthesize fatty acids through the glycerol-3-phosphate pathway to generate TG. Due to their hydrophobic nature, newly synthesized TG must be extracted into triacylglycerides and subsequently assembled with apolipoproteins to form very low-density lipoproteins (VLDL) for systemic transport via the circulation to abdominal fat tissues (Hermier, 1997). Notably, the abdominal fat tissues regulate hepatic lipid metabolism by secreting hormonal signaling molecules such as leptin (Martinez-Una et al., 2020) and adiponectin (Combs and Marliss, 2014). This bidirectional crosstalk between the liver tissues and abdominal fat tissues constitutes a dynamic regulatory network involving both metabolic flux and hormonal signaling, which is essential for maintaining energy homeostasis. This study performed transcriptomic analysis and WGCNA on abdominal fat tissues and liver tissues from the FH and FL groups. Through multi-omics integration, we identified potential KCGs. Among these, ACSS2, USP10 and SEC16B, which have been previously reported to be closely associated with lipid metabolism, were prioritized for further mechanistic exploration.

Acetyl-CoA synthetase 2 (ACSS2) is a key enzyme that catalyzes the conversion of acetate to acetyl-CoA, which subsequently enters the TCA cycle and influences de novo lipogenesis (DNL). Previous studies have demonstrated that ACSS2-/- mice exhibit significantly reduced body weight gain and less hepatic steatosis in diet-induced obesity models (Huang et al., 2018). Consistently, in this study, ACSS2 was markedly downregulated in the FL group. We speculate that the downregulation of ACSS2 may reduce acetate utilization and force cells to rely on alternative carbon sources, such as L-Tyrosine to sustain acetyl-CoA homeostasis (Knox and Lemay-Knox, 1951). Therefore, the contents of medium and long chain fatty acids, including caproic acid, heptanoic acid, and myristic acid were significantly downregulated in the FL group. However, this potential mechanism remains speculative and requires further experimental verification.

Ubiquitin-Specific-Processing Protease 10 (USP10) (normalized XP-EHH = 3.25; normalized XP-nSL = 2.97), was also identified as a key candidate under selection in the FL group. As demonstrated in mammalian models, hepatic USP10 expression is significantly downregulated in obesity, and its overexpression attenuates hepatic steatosis, insulin resistance, and inflammation through Sirtuin 6 (SIRT6) (Luo et al., 2018). Notably, the metabolic influence of the USP10-SIRT6 axis provides a plausible link to the altered serum metabolome observed in FL group. Specifically, SIRT6 overexpression has been shown to downregulate key lipogenic enzymes including acetyl-CoA carboxylase and fatty acid synthase, while upregulating markers of fatty acid oxidation such as carnitine palmitoyl transferase 1 (CPT1) (Liu et al., 2024). This shift from lipid synthesis to oxidation aligns with the observed downregulation of medium and long-chain fatty acids (e.g., caproic acid, heptanoic acid, myristic acid) in the FL group. Given its conserved role in inhibiting adipogenesis and improving metabolic homeostasis, USP10 represents a promising target for future strategies aimed at regulating abdominal fat deposition in broilers.

SEC16B, also known as leucine zipper transcription regulator 2 (LZTR2), has been implicated in obesity and body mass index across multiple human genome-wide association studies (Sahibdeen et al., 2018; Daily and Park, 2023; Park, 2022; Agarwal et al., 2024). Mammalian studies have demonstrated that SEC16B deficiency alleviates high-fat diet-induced obesity by suppressing APOB-dependent lipoprotein assembly and reducing chylomicron secretion (Shi et al., 2023). In the present study, this gene exhibited a strong evolutionary selection signal (normalized XP-EHH = 4.57; normalized XP-nSL = 2.61) and significant downregulation in the FL group, we speculate that SEC16B may play a pivotal role in abdominal fat deposition via vitamin-mediated pathways. Specifically, SEC16B may impair lipoprotein secretion in the liver or intestine, thereby hindering the binding and transport of lipophilic vitamin retinol, whose absorption and transport are contingent upon lipoprotein particles such as chylomicrons and VLDL (Quadro et al., 2022). This disruption may reduce the binding of the retinol-RBP4 complex to STRA6, thereby weakening the JAK2/STAT5/SOCS3 signaling activation (Flores-Cortez et al., 2022). Consequently, this pathway modulation may alleviate the inhibition of insulin signaling and reduce lipid accumulation.

Conclusion

Overall, this study successfully screened and identified key candidate genes (FOXF1, ACSS2, USP10, and SEC16B) potentially and metabolites (L-Tyrosine, pantothenic acid, and 10-hydroxydecanoic acid) associated with fat deposition through integrated multi-omics analyses and tissue-specific validations. These findings offer novel insights into the genetic and metabolic regulation of adipose deposition in broilers, providing potential molecular targets and a theoretical foundation for subsequent breeding programs and mechanistic studies focused on lipid metabolism regulation in poultry.

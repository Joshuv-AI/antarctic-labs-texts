Repurposed anticancer drugs disrupt cancer-like traits of Theileria annulata and suppress Leishmania donovani
Authors: Samoel Lareb, Madhusmita Subudhi, Akash Suresh, Vasundhra Bhandari, Umarani Brahma, Paresh Sharma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Protozoan parasites remain a major threat to global health, affecting both humans and livestock. Theileria annulata, an apicomplexan parasite of cattle, transforms host leukocytes into a cancer-like state, while Leishmania donovani causes visceral leishmaniasis in humans. Given their reliance on host signaling and metabolic pathways, we hypothesized that FDA-approved anticancer drugs could be repurposed as antiparasitic agents. A library of 436 compounds was screened against T. annulata, yielding 130 hits at 10 µM, of which 63 exhibited minimal cytotoxicity toward healthy PBMCs. Secondary screening at 1 µM identified 28 potent inhibitors with > 50% activity. These compounds span diverse scaffolds, including taxanes, nucleoside analogues, antifolates, proteasome inhibitors, and kinase modulators, and target critical pathways such as apoptosis, autophagy, DNA damage response, cytoskeletal dynamics, NF-κB signalling, and tyrosine kinase activity. Notably, floxuridine, masitinib, paclitaxel, cabazitaxel, vinorelbine, maytansine, and bortezomib also suppressed L. donovani, highlighting cross-species efficacy. Target mapping revealed that the majority of active molecules disrupt hallmarks of cancer and parasite-transformed cells, including metabolic reprogramming, genome instability, and invasive phenotypes. Collectively, these findings underscore the value of oncology-derived drug libraries for antiparasitic discovery and identify promising candidates for further evaluation against T. annulata and L. donovani.Graphical abstract

Introduction

Protozoan parasites remain a significant cause of disease in both humans and animals, with major socioeconomic consequences. Visceral leishmaniasis, caused by Leishmania donovani, affects vulnerable human populations, while tropical theileriosis, caused by Theileria annulata, severely compromises livestock health and productivity in endemic regions [1–3]. Limited treatment options, emerging drug resistance, and a stagnating antiparasitic drug pipeline highlight the urgent need for innovative strategies applicable to both medical and veterinary parasitology. A unique feature of T. annulata infection is its ability to transform bovine leukocytes into a hyperproliferative, immortal, metastasis-like state [4]. This transformation is driven by parasite-mediated manipulation of host signalling pathways resembling those dysregulated in cancer, including altered cytoskeletal dynamics, enhanced survival signalling, metabolic rewiring, and sustained proliferation. These parallels between Theileria-transformed cells and malignant cells provide a strong rationale for exploring anticancer drugs as anti-theilerial agents.

Similarly, L. donovani relies on host-parasite interactions that involve cytoskeletal restructuring, proteasome function, and metabolic adaptation pathways frequently targeted by oncology therapeutics [5]. This convergence suggests that repurposing FDA-approved anticancer compounds may identify broad-spectrum inhibitors effective across diverse protozoan species. Drug repurposing offers a rapid, cost-effective route to antiparasitic development because approved compounds have established pharmacokinetic and safety profiles. While limited studies have examined specific anticancer agents against Theileria, large-scale systematic screening remains unexplored. Here, we screened 436 FDA-approved anticancer compounds against T. annulata, assessed structural diversity and drug-likeness, generated full dose-response curves for potent inhibitors, and mapped predicted biological hallmarks targeted by these drugs. We further examined cross-activity against L. donovani to identify compounds with dual antiparasitic potential. This study provides a comprehensive assessment of anticancer compounds as a source of novel antiparasitic agents and highlights promising candidates for further evaluation.

Materials and methods

Parasite and cell cultures

T. annulata-infected bovine leukocytes were maintained at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ in RPMI-1640 supplemented with 10% FBS [6]. Uninfected bovine PBMCs were isolated via Ficoll density gradient centrifugation and maintained under identical conditions. Drug susceptibility of Leishmania donovani (Ag83) parasites was assessed at the promastigote stage. Parasites were cultured in Medium 199 supplemented with 10% heat-inactivated FBS at 25 °C [7]. Preliminary screening of the compounds was performed at concentration (10 μm) in Late log-phase promastigotes (1 × 10⁵ cells/well) were seeded into 96-well plates. Following 48 h incubation at 25 °C, 50 µL of resazurin solution [0.0125% (w/v)] was added to each well, and plates were incubated for an additional 24 h.

Compound library

The MCE FDA-Approved Anticancer Library (436 compounds) was purchased from MedChemExpress. Stock solutions were prepared in DMSO (10 mM) and diluted to working concentrations immediately before use.

Drug screening

T. annulata-infected cells were treated with each compound at 10 µM for 48 h. Cell viability was measured using a resazurin-based assay (excitation 570 nm; emission 600 nm) [6]. Compounds reducing viability relative to DMSO controls were considered hits. The non-cytotoxic compounds were tested at 1 µM in T. annulata-infected cells. Compounds causing > 50% parasite inhibition were considered potent. Active compounds were screened against L. donovani promastigotes using viability assays to identify cross-species antiparasitic activity. Assay performance was validated using appropriate positive controls: artesunate for anti-theilerial screening [11], and Amphoteriicin-B (IC50: 0.08 μm) for anti-leishmanial activity assays.

PBMC cytotoxicity counter-screen

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were obtained from healthy, Theileria annulata-negative cattle, confirmed by the absence of the tasp gene [11]. The cytotoxicity of the compounds was evaluated by exposing PBMCs to a 10 µM concentration for 48 h. Cell viability was determined using a resazurin-based assay, consistent with the protocol used for infected cells. Only compounds that resulted in greater than 40% PBMC viability were considered non-cytotoxic and were advanced to further analysis.

Structural similarity analysis

Molecular fingerprints and Tanimoto similarity scores were computed using ChemmineR. Hierarchical clustering was used to identify structurally related groups [8].

IC₅₀, CC₅₀, and selectivity index

Two-fold serial dilutions were used to generate dose-response curves for selected compounds following the primary screening assay. IC₅₀ values were determined using T. annulata-infected cells, while CC₅₀ values were determined using PBMCs over an extended concentration range(0.05 µM to 100 µM), with maximum tested concentrations reaching up to 100 µM depending on compound-specific response profiles. Cell viability was assessed using a resazurin-based assay, and IC₅₀ and CC₅₀ values were calculated by nonlinear regression analysis in GraphPad Prism using normalized viability values relative to DMSO-treated controls. Two independent biological experiments were performed for IC₅₀ and CC₅₀ estimation. The Selectivity Index (SI) was calculated as: SI = CC₅₀ / IC₅₀. For compounds that did not achieve 50% PBMC cytotoxicity within the tested concentration range, CC₅₀ and corresponding SI values were reported as greater than or equal to the highest tested concentration.

Drug-likeness evaluation and cancer hallmark mapping

SwissADME analysis was performed to assess physicochemical properties and compliance with medicinal chemistry filters [9]. Compound targets were mapped to pathways representing cancer hallmarks using curated databases. Each compound’s potential influence on metabolic reprogramming, DNA instability, invasion, survival, and other hallmarks was assessed.

Results

A library of 436 FDA-approved anticancer compounds was screened at 10 µM, yielding 130 molecules that markedly reduced the viability of Theileria annulata-infected leukocytes (Fig. 1A). This broad screening strategy leveraged the fact that many anticancer agents target core cellular processes, such as DNA replication, apoptosis, and cytoskeletal regulation, that are similarly exploited by parasite-transformed host cells. To eliminate nonspecific cytotoxicity, the 130 hits were counter-screened against bovine PBMCs. This refinement identified 63 compounds that maintained > 40% PBMC viability, thereby enriching for molecules with selective activity against infected cells. Notably, three of these, Artesunate, Dihydroartemisinin, and Gemcitabine, had previously been reported to exhibit anti-theilerial activity [10, 11] validating the robustness of the screening pipeline.

Structural similarity analysis of the 63 selective compounds revealed considerable chemical diversity, yet two dominant clusters emerged. The first comprised nucleoside analogues (e.g., floxuridine, gemcitabine), which disrupt DNA/RNA synthesis and cell cycle progression through their ribose–nucleobase scaffolds. The second cluster included taxane-class microtubule stabilizers (paclitaxel, docetaxel, cabazitaxel), which perturb cytoskeletal dynamics critical for both cancer cell proliferation and parasite-driven transformation (Fig. 1B). Beyond these clusters, several structurally distinct compounds appeared as outliers, suggesting alternative mechanisms of action. Examples include masitinib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor targeting c-Kit, PDGFR, and Src, and bortezomib, a proteasome inhibitor. A secondary screen at 1 µM further prioritized candidates under physiologically relevant conditions. This step identified 28 potent inhibitors that retained > 50% activity against infected leukocytes (Fig. 1C), highlighting compounds with strong efficacy at lower, clinically achievable concentrations.

Drug-likeness evaluation using SwissADME confirmed that most of these 28 compounds satisfied Lipinski, Veber, and Ghose criteria, supporting their suitability for further optimization (Fig. 2A). Dose-response assays revealed IC₅₀ values ranging from nanomolar to low micromolar concentrations (Fig. 2B), while CC₅₀ values on PBMCs enabled calculation of selectivity indices (SI) (Table 1). Several compounds, including gemcitabine, paclitaxel, and masitinib, exhibited favourable SI values, indicating preferential activity against parasite-transformed cells compared to uninfected host leukocytes (Fig. 2C). This selectivity is critical for therapeutic development, as it minimizes host toxicity while maximizing antiparasitic efficacy. Target mapping based on previous reports revealed that the majority of active compounds converged on hallmark cancer pathways (Table 2). Specifically, 26 compounds were linked to deregulated cellular energetics (e.g., proteasome inhibitors bortezomib and ixazomib), 22 to genome instability and mutation (e.g., topoisomerase inhibitors teniposide and etoposide), and 22 to invasion and metastasis (e.g., microtubule-targeting agents’ paclitaxel, vinblastine, and colchicine) (Fig. 2D). These pathways are central to the cancer-like phenotype induced by T. annulata, which reprograms host leukocytes to proliferate uncontrollably and resist apoptosis. Additional mechanisms included modulation of NF-κB signaling (albendazole, bortezomib), autophagy (cabazitaxel, vinorelbine), and tyrosine kinase signaling (masitinib, tirbanibulin), all of which are known to be hijacked by parasites to sustain infection. Thus, the mechanistic overlap between cancer biology and parasite-induced transformation provides a strong rationale for repurposing oncology drugs in this context.

28 best hit compounds, their targets, and pathways involved

Finally, evaluation of the 28 active compounds against Leishmania donovani promastigotes identified seven molecules: floxuridine, masitinib, paclitaxel, cabazitaxel, vinorelbine ditartrate, maytansine, and bortezomib, with notable inhibitory activity. Of these, paclitaxel and maytansine had previously been reported to possess antileishmanial activity [12], validating the screening pipeline. Importantly, these hits represent diverse mechanistic classes: nucleoside analogues (floxuridine), kinase inhibitors (masitinib), microtubule stabilizers (paclitaxel, cabazitaxel, vinorelbine, maytansine), and proteasome inhibitors (bortezomib). This diversity underscores shared vulnerabilities across protozoan parasites and reinforces the potential of anticancer drug repurposing as a strategy for broad-spectrum antiparasitic drug discovery.

Discussion

Protozoan parasites remain a major challenge to both human and animal health, and the limited arsenal of effective therapeutics necessitates innovative strategies for drug discovery. In this study, we systematically screened 436 FDA-approved anticancer compounds and identified 28 potent inhibitors of Theileria annulata, many of which displayed favorable selectivity indices. These findings highlight the therapeutic promise of exploiting cancer-like transformations induced by parasites, as T. annulata-infected leukocytes exhibit hallmarks of oncogenesis such as uncontrolled proliferation, metabolic rewiring, and resistance to apoptosis [4].Mechanistic mapping of the 28 active compounds revealed convergence on pathways central to both cancer and parasite biology. Several compounds targeted apoptosis and autophagy (e.g., gemcitabine, docetaxel, cabazitaxel, vinblastine), processes that are critical for maintaining parasite-transformed cell survival. Others interfered with cytoskeletal dynamics through microtubule/tubulin binding (e.g., paclitaxel, colchicine, podofilox, maytansine), thereby disrupting invasive phenotypes essential for parasite persistence. Proteasome inhibitors such as bortezomib and ixazomib perturbed deregulated cellular energetics, while nucleoside analogues like floxuridine and gemcitabine impaired DNA/RNA synthesis and genome stability. Kinase inhibitors including masitinib and tirbanibulin further disrupted signaling pathways (c-Kit, Src, PDGFR, FGFR) that are hijacked by parasites to sustain transformation. Collectively, these mechanistic classes underscore the overlap between oncogenic processes and parasite-induced cellular reprogramming, validating the rationale for repurposing anticancer drugs in this context. Importantly, seven compounds—floxuridine, masitinib, paclitaxel, cabazitaxel, vinorelbine, maytansine, and bortezomib—also demonstrated inhibitory activity against Leishmania donovani. These hits span diverse mechanistic categories, including nucleoside analogues, kinase inhibitors, microtubule stabilizers, and proteasome inhibitors, suggesting that shared vulnerabilities exist across apicomplexan and kinetoplastid parasites. Taxanes and vinca alkaloids likely interfere with parasite cytoskeletal function, while proteasome inhibitors and nucleoside analogues may disrupt essential metabolic pathways [13]. The cross-species efficacy observed here highlights opportunities for broad-spectrum antiparasitic drug development. The chemical diversity among the active compounds, including structurally unique outliers such as albendazole (targeting HIF and VEGFR) and flubendazole (modulating STAT and p53 signaling), expands the landscape of potential antiparasitic chemotypes beyond traditional scaffolds. Moreover, the favorable drug-likeness properties of many hits, as confirmed by SwissADME analysis, provide a strong foundation for future medicinal chemistry optimization and formulation development tailored to veterinary and human applications. Despite the promising findings, host-directed phenotypic screening carries inherent limitations, including the risk of false negatives where parasite-specific inhibitors may be missed due to minimal impact on host cell survival. Host cytotoxicity can also obscure selective antiparasitic effects, underscoring the need for rigorous selectivity filtering, which we addressed through PBMC counter-screens and selectivity index calculations. While our approach successfully prioritized compounds disrupting parasite-driven host reprogramming, it does not provide definitive evidence of direct parasite target engagement. Future parasite-focused validation assays, including direct quantification of parasite burden, will be essential to confirm specificity and therapeutic potential.

Conclusion

Overall, this study establishes oncology-derived drug libraries as a valuable resource for antiparasitic discovery. By identifying compounds that target apoptosis, autophagy, cytoskeletal integrity, proteasome function, and kinase signaling, we reveal mechanistic pathways that are central to parasite survival and transformation. These findings not only validate the concept of anticancer drug repurposing but also identify multiple promising candidates for further mechanistic exploration, in vivo validation, and eventual preclinical development.

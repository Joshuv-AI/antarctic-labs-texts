Mapping the direct and indirect costs of patient and public involvement (PPI) in stroke and aphasia research: a scoping review protocol
Authors: Marina Charalambous, Alexia Kountouri, Erasmia Kola, Ana Cláudia De Souza

Introduction

Patient and public involvement (PPI) has become a cornerstone of modern health research, ensuring that studies are ethical, relevant and inclusive.1 In stroke and aphasia research, PPI ensures that people with lived experience directly shape priorities, study design and dissemination.2 Major funders such as the National Institute for Health Research (NIHR) and Horizon Europe mandate PPI as part of research proposals, underscoring its central role in delivering impactful science.3 PPI is defined as research conducted ‘with’ or ‘by’ members of the public, rather than ‘on’, ‘about’, or ‘for’ them, positioning patients, families and communities as collaborators whose contributions strengthen the credibility and impact of research.4

Despite these benefits, challenges persist. Reporting of PPI remains inconsistent, and there is limited systematic documentation of the resources required to support meaningful involvement across different contexts.5 Indirect and less visible costs, in particular, remain under-reported, limiting understanding of the full burden associated with PPI implementation. For people with aphasia after stroke, these challenges may be amplified due to communication accessibility needs and specialist facilitation requirements.6 Without acknowledging these resource demands, PPI risks being undervalued, inconsistently supported or unsustainably implemented, particularly in resource-limited health research contexts.7

In stroke and aphasia co-creation projects, direct costs may include reimbursement or honoraria for PPI partners, travel and commuting expenses, production of accessible and aphasia-friendly materials, provision of assistive communication technologies and PPI training or facilitation costs.6 7 Indirect costs may include unpaid preparation and meeting time by PPI partners, emotional labour associated with sharing lived experience, additional researcher time required for relationship-building and communication support and organisational or governance-related delays linked to remuneration and contracting processes.4 6 7 In this review, ‘costs’ are conceptualised as resources consumed or burdens incurred to enable meaningful PPI, including financial, time-related, emotional, organisational and infrastructural inputs. Outcomes or impacts of PPI (eg, improved study relevance, enhanced recruitment or knowledge translation benefits) will not be classified as costs, but may be extracted where they are explicitly linked to resource use or sustainability considerations. This distinction is intended to support transparent data extraction and consistent interpretation across heterogeneous sources.

As stroke research has expanded exponentially in recent decades, it remains unclear whether the integration of PPI, and particularly transparent documentation of its costs, has kept pace with this growth.8 Evidence from other domains, including cancer9 and dementia,10 suggests that evaluation of PPI is often anecdotal and lacks systematic frameworks for assessing resources. To address this gap, a comprehensive synthesis of evidence on both the direct and indirect costs of PPI in stroke research is needed.

This review will also contribute to the development of the Mapping the Economic and Social Tangible and Emotional Resources of Patient and Public Involvement (MASTER-PPI) framework. The MASTER-PPI framework will provide structured guidance for identifying, categorising and understanding the spectrum of PPI costs, spanning visible financial expenditures and less visible but equally significant elements, such as emotional labour, governance demands and time. By embedding both tangible and experiential dimensions, MASTER-PPI aims to advance a more holistic and equitable understanding of what it means to resource meaningful PPI. This framework will be valuable to researchers, funders, non-governmental organisations (NGOs) and policymakers who increasingly mandate PPI but provide little clarity on the resources required to implement it effectively.

The aim of this scoping review is fourfold: (1) to identify and categorise the direct and indirect costs of PPI in stroke and aphasia research, (2) to explore how such costs are defined, reported or implied, (3) to map cost-related barriers and facilitators and (4) to highlight gaps in the evidence base to inform the ongoing development of the MASTER-PPI framework. This review will also actively involve a person with lived experience of stroke and aphasia as a PPI partner on the research team, ensuring that the methods, analyses and presentation of findings remain relevant, grounded in lived experience and responsive to the realities of PPI practice.

Methods and analysis

Design

Published literature documenting the costs of PPI is likely to be dispersed across diverse sources and presented in varying methodological approaches and formats. This review will follow established scoping review methodology11 and will be reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) guidelines12 to ensure transparent, complete and reproducible reporting of methods and findings. This approach will enable us to capture and synthesise evidence across a wide range of study designs, including both peer-reviewed and grey literature.

The review will be conducted in five stages11: (1) refining the research questions, (2) developing and implementing the search strategy, (3) applying inclusion and exclusion criteria, (4) extracting relevant data and (5) analysing and presenting the findings. Consistent with scoping review methodology, no formal quality appraisal of included studies will be undertaken; however, the process will be systematic and rigorous, ensuring transparency and reproducibility. Analytical steps will be explicitly aligned with the review objectives, including descriptive mapping of reported cost categories, synthesis of definitions and reporting practices and thematic analysis of cost-related barriers and facilitators. Evidence gaps will be identified by examining under-represented cost domains, research contexts, geographical regions and stages of the research cycle, as well as inconsistencies in cost reporting, to inform development of the MASTER-PPI framework. The study is expected to take place in 2026. As this review involves analysis of published and publicly available literature, ethics approval is not required.

Determining the research question

Members of this research team have been actively engaged in projects involving PPI in stroke and aphasia research, where questions about the resources required for meaningful involvement have frequently arisen. In previous co-creation projects, both researchers and PPI partners highlighted uncertainty around how to account for the costs of involvement, particularly in relation to under-recognised areas such as emotional labour, time and governance processes.2 Furthermore, discussions within international stroke and aphasia networks have emphasised the need to better understand the true costs of PPI, including both direct (eg, honoraria, travel, accessibility support) and indirect (eg, fatigue, administrative delays, well-being impact) components.6

Based on these knowledge needs, this scoping review will address the following overarching research question:

What are the direct and indirect costs of PPI in stroke research, and how are these costs defined, reported or implied in the literature?

Search strategy

An initial search strategy will be developed for PUBMED, in collaboration with an experienced health librarian (co-author EK). The plan will then be adapted to the subject headings and syntax of additional databases to ensure comprehensive coverage. Based on the scope of this review, the following databases will be searched: PUBMED, Medline, EMBASE, APA PsycINFO, CINAHL, Scopus and Web of Science. To maximise retrieval, reference lists of included studies and relevant reviews will be hand-searched, and forward and backward citation tracking will be performed using CrossRef and Google Scholar.

Given that cost data are often reported outside academic journals, a structured grey literature search will also be conducted, including stroke support organisations (SSO) and NGOs reports, funding agency documents, theses, policy papers and conference proceedings, sourced from platforms such as OpenGrey, ProQuest Dissertations and Theses, BASE, OSF Preprints and organisational websites (eg, stroke and aphasia SSOs and NGOs, NIHR INVOLVE, Horizon Europe).

All records will be uploaded into Covidence for screening and data extraction.

Given international variation in terminology used to describe patient involvement (eg, PPI, Patient and Stakeholder Engagement (PSE), Public Engagement, Co-production, Co-design), the search strategy will incorporate multiple synonyms and commonly used abbreviations, including both PPI and PSE. During screening and data extraction, terminology will be mapped to a unified operational definition of PPI to ensure consistency in study selection and interpretation across different geographical and research contexts. A draft PUBMED search strategy will be provided in an online supplemental appendix, for example, ((“stroke”[MeSH Terms] OR “stroke”[All Fields]) OR “cerebrovascular accident”[All Fields] OR (“stroke”[MeSH Terms] OR “stroke”[All Fields] OR “cva”[All Fields])) AND (“Patient Participation”[Mesh] OR “Public Involvement”[All Fields] OR “Patient and Public Involvement”[All Fields] OR “PPI”[All Fields] OR “Patient and Stakeholder Engagement” [All Fields] OR PSE[All Fields] “co-production”[All Fields] OR “co-design”[All Fields] OR “patient engagement”[All Fields] OR “stakeholder involvement”[All Fields] OR “patient involvement”[All Fields]) AND (“cost”[All Fields] OR “funding”[All Fields] OR “health resource”[All Fields] OR “funding”[All Fields] OR “reimbursement”[All Fields] OR “remuneration”[All Fields]).

Inclusion criteria (PCC framework)

The inclusion criteria for this scoping review are designed to capture evidence relevant to the research question and objectives. Following the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI)11 construct of population, concept and context, the inclusion criteria are as follows:

Population

The review will include studies in which people with stroke and/or aphasia, carers or members of the public were engaged as PPI partners in research. No restrictions will be placed on age, gender or other demographic characteristics of PPI partners, as the focus is on costs associated with involvement rather than outcomes in specific subgroups.

Concept

The central concept is the costs of PPI, including both direct costs (eg, reimbursement, travel, accessibility, training, technology) and indirect costs (eg, time, emotional labour, administrative delays, coordination). Literature that reports, defines, implies or discusses these costs will be eligible.

Context

The context is stroke research across the continuum of care, including acute, rehabilitation, long-term and community settings. Both peer-reviewed and grey literature (eg, NGO reports, policy documents, funding reports) will be considered, as these may provide important insights into the under-reported dimensions of cost. Studies will be eligible if they report on PPI at any stage of the research cycle (design, implementation, dissemination, evaluation). Studies outside the context of stroke will be excluded, as well as publications that describe PPI without any reference to resource use, cost or burden.

Types of evidence to be included

This review will include both published and unpublished literature that reports on the costs of PPI in stroke research. Eligible evidence may use quantitative, qualitative, mixed or multi-method designs, including comparative (eg, randomised, controlled, cohort, quasi-experimental) and non-comparative (eg, surveys, audits, narrative accounts) approaches. We will also include policy documents, evaluation reports, NGO and organisational publications and conference proceedings, as cost information may be more visible in these sources. Grey literature will be actively sought to ensure inclusion of under-reported or non-academic accounts of cost, and reference lists of relevant reviews and syntheses will be hand-searched. Literature will be restricted to publications in English.

All identified records will be imported into the knowledge synthesis management platform Covidence for screening and data extraction. After duplicate removal, a random subset of citations will be screened to pilot-test the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Title and abstract screening will be conducted in duplicate, with articles categorised as ‘Yes’, ‘No’ or ‘Maybe’. All ‘Yes’ and ‘Maybe’ articles will progress to full-text screening. Discrepancies will be resolved through discussion, with adjudication by a senior reviewer if needed. Inter-rater reliability will be assessed and maintained throughout the process.

Data extraction

A copy of each eligible article or document will be obtained, reviewed and charted by two members of the research team. Data extraction will be conducted in duplicate (two researchers per article), with any discrepancies adjudicated by a third reviewer. A standardised extraction form, aligned with the objectives of this review, will be developed and pilot tested on a sample of included studies. Importantly, the PPI partner with lived experience of stroke and aphasia (co-author AK) will contribute to the piloting and refinement of the extraction form to ensure that the categories and interpretations are meaningful and relevant. PPI partner AK will be supported throughout the project by the People with Aphasia and Other Layperson Involvement (PAOLI) Framework,6 ensuring meaningful and independent involvement. Disagreements arising during extraction will be discussed and resolved by consensus, with final decisions overseen by a senior member of the review team. Critical appraisal of available literature

As the primary aim of this scoping review is to map and synthesise evidence on the direct and indirect costs of PPI in stroke research, a formal quality appraisal of individual studies will not be undertaken. This is consistent with scoping review methodology. However, we will provide a descriptive overview of the types and designs of studies included, using the JBI categories of evidence to indicate the general level of evidence represented across the literature.11 This will allow us to comment on the strengths, limitations and gaps of the existing evidence base on PPI costs, without excluding studies based on methodological quality.

Analysis and presentation of results

This scoping review is designed to provide an overview of the extent, nature and characteristics of the literature on the costs of PPI in stroke research. Results will be presented using three complementary strategies: (1) a PRISMA-ScR11 flow diagram to illustrate the selection process, (2) a descriptive numerical summary of the amount, type and distribution of included studies (addressing Objective 1) and (3) a thematic analysis and visual representations of key data extraction categories, including definitions and reporting of costs, cost-related barriers and facilitators, and contextual factors (addressing Objectives 2 and 3). The structure and format of the reporting will be shaped by the findings, with input from the PPI partner (co-author AK) on the review team to ensure relevance and accessibility. Given the anticipated variability of the literature and the likelihood of under-reporting, we will adapt and extend existing approaches to develop the MASTER-PPI framework, which will provide a structured way to summarise and present costs of PPI in stroke research.

Knowledge translation

Knowledge translation will be embedded throughout the review process. We will engage with PPI partners, stroke survivors, carers and researchers experienced in PPI to provide feedback on study conduct and the interpretation of findings, ensuring that the outputs are meaningful and relevant. Input will also be sought from members of existing international stroke and aphasia research networks, as well as from NGOs and advocacy organisations with expertise in PPI. These stakeholders will inform the presentation of results, highlight implications for practice, and support the identification of priorities for future research. Dissemination activities will include peer-reviewed publications, presentations at local, national and international conferences, and accessible summaries tailored for PPI partners and community organisations. Findings will also inform the refinement and further development of the MASTER-PPI framework, which aims to provide a structured approach for recognising and planning for the costs of PPI in stroke research.

Patient and public involvement

The PPI partner (co-author AK), who is a person with lived experience of stroke and aphasia and represents a SSO, the Cyprus Stroke Association, contributed to developing the research topic, focusing on PPI costs and planning dissemination strategies. In addition to contributing to the development of the research topic, piloting of the data extraction form and dissemination planning, the PPI partner will be actively involved in interpretive stages of the review. This will include contributing to the refinement of cost categories, providing input on the interpretation of reported direct and indirect costs, and supporting contextual understanding of how costs are experienced in practice. This involvement will help ensure that analytical decisions remain grounded in lived experience and reflect meaningful dimensions of PPI implementation. To support inclusive and independent involvement, AK will be guided throughout the project by the PAOLI Framework.6 AK will also be invited to provide feedback on accessible summaries of the findings to ensure that outputs remain relevant to people with lived experience and the wider stroke and aphasia community.

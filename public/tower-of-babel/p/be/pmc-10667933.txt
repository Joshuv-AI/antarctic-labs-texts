Introduction

As revealed as a colocutaneous fistula with an abscess in the abdominal wall, colon cancer is rare [1]. Nevertheless, such a surgical emergency should be suspected in case of a painful abdominal wall mass. Medical treatment and/or radiological drainage could be suggested as a first-line treatment. However, an emergent surgery will be performed if these first-line treatments fail. In the absence of an early diagnosis and treatment, the evolution could be marked by a spontaneous colocutaneous fistula. The objective of this case report, performed using SCARE guidelines [2], of an infected sigmoid adenocarcinoma is to underline this uncommon clinical presentation that could present some therapeutic issues.

Case report

A 90-year-old woman with a past medical history of hypertension and major depressive disorder consulted the Emergency Department for a five-day history of lower left quadrant abdominal pain. She was complaining of a loss of appetite, fever, and nausea. In addition, she presented erythema and swelling in the lower left quadrant. The physical examination objectified a mild fever, cardiovascular data disorder, abdominal guarding, and abdominal mass of 10 cm with inflammatory signs. The biological data found a white blood cell count of 19,800/mL and a C-reactive protein at 360 mg/l. The abdominal CT scan showed a concentric thickness of the sigmoid colon with an abdominal wall abscess (Fig. 1). After monitoring, the absence of response to medical treatment, she underwent an emergent laparotomy. Intraoperatively, we found an infected sigmoid tumour that invades the abdominal wall and is associated with a peritumoral abscess. This tumour was at the origin of the abdominal wall fistula (Fig. 2). There were no liver metastases, peritoneal nodes, or ascites. She underwent surgical drainage of the abscess, sigmoid colectomy, and colostomy according the Hartmann's procedure. The postoperative follow-up was uneventful. The pathological examination of the operative specimen revealed perforated sigmoid adenocarcinoma with lymph node invasion classified pT4N2M0. Then, three cycles of adjuvant chemotherapy were performed according to the Xelox protocol. After two years of follow-up, there was no recurrence.

Discussion

This case report presented an uncommon clinical presentation diagnosis after infection and colocutaneous fistula.

A fistula is defined as an abnormal connection between two body structures that are not supposed to be connected [3]. They can be classified as external or internal gastrointestinal fistulas. External gastrointestinal fistulas, like our case, result from the connection between the gastrointestinal tract and skin. This complication is frequent in Crohn's disease, digestive cancer, and diverticulitis [[4], [5], [6]]. In our case, we present an uncommon clinical presentation of an infected sigmoid adenocarcinoma. Colon adenocarcinoma should be highlighted in the case of patients presented with a painful abdominal wall mass. It could be fistulized or caused by necrotizing fasciitis [7]. The symptoms and laboratory findings were not specific. Symptoms such as abdominal pain, loss of appetite, weight loss, fever, and changes in bowel movement can be seen in patients with an infectious process, inflammatory bowel disease or malignancy. This case shows that colon cancer can present with atypical symptoms. Inflammatory bowel disease can be related to extraintestinal manifestations that are not found in colon cancer. For example, aphthous ulcers, ankylosing spondylitis, and eye manifestations including uveitis and scleritis are not typically found in colorectal cancer [8]. However, it has been associated with hematologic malignancies such as IgA monoclonal gammopathy, leukaemia, lymphoma and myeloma, and rheumatic disorders such as ankylosing spondylitis and rheumatoid arthritis. Radiological features could objectively have an apple core sign most commonly associated with colorectal cancer [9], but this sign could be found in Crohn's disease or diverticulitis. Skip lesions and cobblestone appearance are found in colonoscopy patients with Crohn's disease. The diagnosis remains based on pathological examination. It depends on the tumour stage and the degree of differentiation; then, a screening colonoscopy is mandatory in front of any digestive symptoms in elderly patients. It remains the most effective treatment of these complicated cases to improve the prognosis [10].

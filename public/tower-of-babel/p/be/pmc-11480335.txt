The effect of green credit policy on carbon emissions based on China’s provincial panel data
Authors: Xuerui Yang, Lianmei Zhu, Tong Wei
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The attainment of “carbon peaking” and “carbon neutrality” is of utmost significance for the optimal progress of China’s economy. As the main channel for green finance, the carbon reduction effectiveness of green credit policy is worth discussing. This article constructs an analytical framework for green credit, carbon emissions and technological advancement. Based on panel data from 30 provincial administrative regions in China from 2012 to 2021, this article uses the mediation effect model and the Spatial Durbin Model to investigate the transmission mechanism and spatial spillover effect of green credit policies on carbon emissions. According to baseline regression model results, our study revealed that overall, carbon emissions were reduced by green credit. The spatial spillover effect of green credit on carbon emissions points to the interconnectedness of different regions in terms of environmental impact. The significant negative indirect effect indicates that the increase of local green credit also has a significant inhibitory effect on surrounding carbon dioxide emissions. It suggests that efforts to reduce carbon emissions in one area can have positive consequences for neighboring regions as well. Green credit is able to reduce carbon emissions indirectly via technological advancement, according to the mechanism testing mediation effect model. This paper presents policy recommendations for relevant departments.

Introduction

Following its 2001 entry into the World Trade Organization, China’s economy significantly flourished, and the country’s carbon emissions also climbed noticeably. China became the top carbon emitter in the world in 2005 when its total emissions overtook those of the US. Following the implementation of environmental safeguarding measures in China in 2011, a growth rate of carbon emissions started to fall and remained essentially below 5%. This decline was linked to the use of technology that reduces emissions and saves energy, plus the rise in public awareness of environmental issues1. Currently, the growth rate of China’s energy consumption and carbon emissions has undergone a significant reversal, but is still in a deceleration stage of sustained total growth. Sustainable development requires a realistic solution to the conflict between fostering economic expansion and the rise in carbon emissions brought on by energy investment2. In September 2020, China explicitly made proposal at the United Nations Conference on Activities that the comprehensive carbon emissions target ought to aim its peak before 2030 and strive to attain “carbon neutrality” by 20603. Ensuring carbon peak and carbon neutrality goals, fostering stable and long-term development of China’s economy, requires joint efforts from multiple parties, with financial support being a very important link. In accordance with the Communist Party of China’s 20th National Congress report, which was released in October 2022, green finance is an essential part of the “carbon peaking and carbon neutrality” regulation. By urging the transfer of resources towards low-carbon and eco-friendly initiatives, this method ensures a sustainable future. Moreover, it also facilitates the growth of new eco-friendly enterprises and encourages existing businesses to expand in an environmentally responsible manner4. Therefore, financial departments have intensively issued relevant policies, viewing green finance as a link between capital and the “dual carbon” goals, and achieving low-carbon transformation and upgrading. Green finance is an economic activity that has emerged with the development of technology. Specifically, it refers to the financial services provided by investment, financing, and operation to address climate change issues such as the greenhouse effect and to vigorously support environmental improvement, efficient utilization of energy resources, and energy conservation. Among them, the most conventional environmentally friendly financial product in China is green credit, it makes up over 90% of the country’s current green financial products and has a reasonably developed operational structure. Enhancing lending conditions is the primary objective of green credit initiatives for businesses and steers the growth of low-carbon industries while also integrating environmental risk considerations into the day-to-day operations of banking and financial institutions5. China’s green credit balance at the year’s end of 2020 was roughly 12 trillion yuan, with the total outstanding amount holding the top spot globally6. China has been consistently increasing its green credit balance in the past few years, essentially maintaining a growth rate of 5 to 15%7.

Green credit policies have become a key driving force affecting carbon emissions reduction8. Green credit has two effects: diffusion and incentive6. From a theoretical logic perspective, green credit policies can influence carbon emissions through incentive and diffusion effects. On one side, green loan not only raises the debt financing costs for highly polluting enterprises but also channel more capital towards green businesses. These energy-saving and emission-reducing green loans compel highly polluting industries to undergo technological advancements and product upgrades. Such enterprises, driven by incentive effects, are more inclined to innovate and engage in energy conservation and emission reduction, thereby lowering local carbon emissions. On the other side, local governments in neighboring provinces might develop green credit through imitation, cooperation, and other forms under the promotion of environmental assessment mechanisms, promoting the green business of financial institutions, and allocating funds to more green and low-emission industry sectors in neighboring provinces, resulting in a diffusion effect. Green credit has a dual effect on carbon emissions: it not only helps to reduce emissions within a particular area, but it may also benefit the emissions of nearby provinces. This geographical spillover effect is a significant factor in the whole effectiveness of policy interventions in reducing carbon emissions. Hence, our study aims to comprehensively investigate several key questions pertaining to the role of green credit in mitigating carbon emissions. Specifically, it endeavors to: (1) quantify the extent to which green credit can effectively reduce carbon emissions; (2) assess whether and to what degree green credit exhibits spatial spillover effects on carbon emissions; (3) explore the mediating role of technological advancement in facilitating carbon emission reductions through green credit; and (4) analyze the methodologies employed by green credit programs to achieve these emission reduction goals.

In contrast to earlier research, our study’s marginal contributions are found in: (1) From a theoretical perspective, previous research on the mechanism testing of green credit and carbon emissions has mainly focused on technological innovation and industrial structure. Unlike previous studies, our study incorporates technological advancement as a mediating variable in the examination of the impact of green credit on carbon emissions, studying all three within a unified framework to more effectively illustrate the intrinsic link between green credit and carbon emission reduction, delving deeply into the transmission pathways of this impact to enrich the study of the effects of green credit. (2) From a practical standpoint, this article is based on panel data between provinces in China to empirically study the carbon reduction effect of green credit, providing an evidence-base for the formulation of green finance policies, and supporting global economy’s movement towards lower carbon emissions. (3) From a model construction standpoint, most of the existing papers studying green finance and carbon emissions use ordinary econometric models without considering the spatial correlation between regions, while the primary model of this study adopts spatial econometrics for an in-depth look at the role of spatial effects.

The remainder content is arranged as follows: the second portion of the paper includes a review of the relevant literature, and a theoretical analysis and research hypothesis are covered in the third. The article’s study strategy, including data sources, variable selection, and econometric model setting, is thoroughly explained in the fourth section. This study then presents empirical findings and a discussion. The findings and recommendations for policy are covered in the last section.

Literature review

Based on the research topic, this section focuses on relevant literature on green credit and carbon emissions. To make literature’s internal logic clearer, this article categorizes and sorts out relevant literature from multiple aspects, and further comments on existing literature, providing theoretical support for the theoretical analysis and hypotheses in the following text.

Directing capital towards green industries, fostering the growth of energy-saving and environmental protection sectors, and encouraging social enterprises to prioritize green environmental protection, as well as instilling a culture of energy conservation and emission reduction throughout society, constitute the key principles of service provision in green credit9. The correlation between green credit and carbon emissions has garnered considerable interest from researchers. Through the literature review in this field, we find that the literature focuses on three aspects: theoretical analysis of green credit’s impact on carbon emissions, the empirical analysis of green credit’s impact on carbon emissions, and the spatial spillover effect of green credit on carbon emissions.

First, some scholars explore the link between green credit and carbon emissions from a theoretical analysis perspective. Green credit schemes aim to convert investment structures that are based on pollution into low-carbon investment structures. In other words, banks facilitate financing for and favor clean projects10. High-polluting enterprises have small loan scales, high interest rates, and short terms, while low-polluting and environmentally friendly enterprises have large loan scales, low interest rates, and long terms. Therefore, green credit can theoretically reduce carbon emissions11. Some researchers supported that green credit can ease financing limitations for enterprises, elevate investment in study and innovation, and provide more low-carbon products12,13. At present, China’s green finance policy mainly restricts the refinancing of enterprises with poor environmental performance through green credit, focusing on credit supply for green projects like environmental preservation, low-carbon, energy-saving, and suppressing credit supply for campaigns with high emissions, high energy use, and high pollutants14,15. According to data available, reloans amounting to over 300 billion yuan have been issued by carbon emission reduction support tools by the end of 2022, supporting commercial banks to issue over 510 billion yuan of carbon emission reduction loans, driving carbon emission reduction to exceed 100 million tons of carbon dioxide equivalent16,17.

Second, numerous scholars have devised regression models for rigorous empirical analysis, aiming to delve into the intricate relationship that exists between green credit policies and carbon emissions. Halimanjaya18 analyzed data from 180 developing countries between 1998 and 2010, exploring the correlation between climate mitigation funds and the unique features of developing nations. The findings revealed that nations with a higher intensity of carbon emissions require greater funding for climate mitigation, urging officials to actively promote the development of green finance and green credit. In a recent study conducted by Pan19, a path was constructed to examine the impact of green credit and green venture capital on the reduction of carbon emissions. The study reveals that green credit and green investment have the potential to enhance the negative externalities associated with economic activities, consequently promoting the decline of carbon emissions. Zhang, et al.20 delved into the profound implications of green credit policies on mitigating carbon dioxide emissions within the Chinese context, underscored by a nuanced analysis of regional disparities in the efficacy of such policies. Parallel to this, An, et al.21 examined the intricacies of green credit financing within a supply chain framework constrained by carbon emission quotas, underscoring the pivotal role of financial mechanisms in fostering carbon emission reductions. Taking heavy polluting enterprises as research objects, Lee, et al.22 examined the Green Credit Policy(GCP) as an exogenous perturbation to rigorously assess its influence on carbon emission intensity. It is found that GCP significantly reduces the total energy intensity, especially the coal consumption intensity, and thus reduces the carbon emission intensity.

Third, with the wide application of spatial econometric model in research, the spatial spillover effect of green credit on mitigating carbon emissions has garnered considerable attention in recent scholarly discourse. However, since the spatial model has only been developed in recent years, the relevant literature on the spatial spillover effects of green credit on carbon emissions is not abundant. Lyu, et al.23 contributed fresh perspectives by elucidating the spatial spillover implications of green credit on fostering low-carbon development, advocating for increased support for green credit mechanisms, and fostering collaborative ties between interregional credit policies and environmental governance frameworks. In their empirical analysis, Wang and Huang13 employed the PSTR model as well as the SDM model. The study reveals that the influence of green credit on carbon emissions is subject to a threshold effect imposed by environmental regulations. Qin, et al.24 carried out a comprehensive investigation of panel data collected between 2009 and 2019 from 30 provinces in China, the main aim of their study was to evaluate the extent of agricultural carbon emissions. The researchers reached a conclusion that a spatial spillover effect is present in connection with the green credit’s ability to inhibit agricultural carbon emissions. Zhang and Wu25 conducted empirical analysis using the SDM model, and came to realize that green credit has a significant inhibitory effect and spatial spillover effect on carbon emissions.

Through literature review, it can be found that there have been some preliminary discussions about carbon emission effects of green credit over the past few years, but the research is not comprehensive enough. Most scholars mainly focus on the measurement of carbon emissions themselves and empirical analysis of local carbon reduction. There is relatively little literature that includes green credit in the spatial analysis of carbon emissions. Although some scholars have explored the spatial impact of green credit on carbon emissions, the exploration of mechanism testing is relatively lacking. Therefore, this article examines the implementation effect of green credit policies from the perspective of carbon dioxide emissions, tests the impact of green credit policies on carbon dioxide emissions and their pathways, evaluates the implementation effect of green credit policies, and provides important supplements in the evaluation of the effectiveness of financial means in solving environmental problems and market-oriented arrangements for energy conservation and emission reduction.

Theoretical analysis and hypothesis

This article seeks to develop a theoretical logic and research framework for the effect of green credit on carbon emissions based on the review of the literature mentioned above. Hence, research hypothesis presented in this part focuses primarily on the following three areas to determine how green credit affects carbon emissions.

The impact of green credit on carbon emissions

This article believes that green credit can reduce carbon emissions through resource optimization effects, signal transmission effects, and social supervision effects. From the standpoint of resource optimization effects, financial institutions will offer financing convenience and advantageous interest rates for eco-friendly industries under the direction of green credit policies. This helps ease financing constraints for businesses, encouraging the advancement of eco-friendly technologies, and lessening carbon emissions. Conversely, high polluting firms’ finance restrictions have been made more severe by the green credit scheme, leading to an increase in financing costs. This will prevent highly polluting businesses from growing and compel them to change to low-carbon transformation, which is beneficial for improving the current situation of high consumption and high emissions. From the standpoint of signal transmission effects, green credit policies align with the national design. Green credit policies may lead and motivate social capital to flow into green sectors by offering loan support and advantageous interest rates for low-carbon businesses. This encourages the industrial structure’s transition to low-carbon, environmentally friendly upgrading. Meanwhile, for industries with “two highs and one surplus”, green credit policy requires implementation of loan limits and penalty interest rates, which will notify pertinent industries to seek out green development layouts and modify their growth strategies. From the standpoint of the influence of social monitoring, enterprises need to raise standard for the disclosure of carbon emission data, which enables financial institutions to monitor the real-time carbon emission patterns of businesses and also encourages participation from industry and public associations, forming an external supervision mechanism. Meanwhile, with the improvement of information transparency, low-carbon development strategies can help enterprises shape a good social image, thereby enhancing their self-restraint and prompting them to firmly follow the path of low-carbon development. In summary, in order to obtain green loans from financial institutions, enterprises must actively carry out environmental protection projects, thereby reducing carbon emissions. Given the aforementioned literature review and theoretical analysis, hypothesis 1 is put out:

Research hypothesis 1

Green credit has a negative impact on carbon emissions, meaning that an increase in green credit helps to reduce carbon emissions.

The spatial spillover effect of green credit on carbon emissions

The law of economic geography identifies the existence of a distance attenuation law in the spatial connection of financial procedures, that is, the economic activity connections between regions that are farther apart are weaker, while the economic activity connections between adjacent regions are stronger26. A number of research projects have looked into how green credit affects the spatial spillover effect of carbon emissions. Per capita carbon emissions were utilized as an explanatory variable in an econometric model constructed by Liang, et al.27. According to the empirical results, implementing green credit can effectively reduce per capita carbon emissions on a regional scale. Additionally, it demonstrates a significant spatial spillover impact by helping decrease carbon emissions levels in neighboring areas. In Wu’s28 research, both a geographic distance matrix and an economic distance matrix were utilized to examine the spatial spillover impact of green finance on carbon emissions23. The study showed that carbon decline effect of green finance existed in both territorial and spillover effects, and the cross-regional collaborative emission reduction effect is stronger under the geographic matrix.

Referring to the findings of previously published studies, this paper puts forward its point of view: due to the impact of neighboring regions, many variables related to economic and environmental issues generally have spatial dependence. Space-related discrepancies exist in regions’ economic activity and carbon emissions, ignoring these spatial dependencies may lead to model estimation errors. On the one side, the development of green credit provides a suitable economic environment and policy advantages for carbon emissions in the region, integrates green investment information, regulates the risks of green investment, and reduces the negative externalities of regional carbon emissions; On the other side, high interest rates on loans to high-polluting sectors will penalize them and could have a “warning effect” on other high-polluting businesses in vicinity. Regarding all the factors above, considering the importance of spatial factors, this article introduces spatial econometric methods to solve the problem of spatial dependence, and proposes hypothesis two:

Research hypothesis 2

The inhibition effect of green credit on carbon emissions has a spatial spillover effect, that is, green credit not only has an inhibition effect on carbon emissions in the local region but also reduces carbon emissions in neighboring regions.

Mechanism analysis

Previous studies’ findings demonstrated that technological advancement had a suppressive impact on carbon emissions29–31. Specifically speaking, some researchers contend that investments in energy technology and advancements in low-carbon technology by industrial enterprises can effectively reduce carbon emissions8,32. The more investment in research and development, the faster technological progress, and the more conducive it is to eliminate outdated production capacity and produce low-carbon products33,34. However, the literatures related to mechanism analysis of green credit and carbon emissions are not rich. So, does technological advancement plays a mediating role in the process of green credit suppressing carbon emissions?

This study believes that the main manifestations of green credit driving technological advancement are: firstly, technological research and development has a high financing cost. Under the implementation of differentiated interest rate policies, green credit provides external financing for energy-saving, emission reduction, and low-carbon enterprises through preferential interest rates, promoting enterprises to achieve green transformation through technological advancement and easing credit fund constraints; Secondly, environmental pollution has strong negative externality, with individual marginal benefits greater than social marginal benefits, market resource allocation failure, and economic and social inability to achieve Pareto optimality. The green credit policy internalizes the external uneconomic costs incurred by enterprises towards the environment into their production costs. By improving technological advancement in areas such as sustainable energy conservation, emission reduction, and preservation of nature, enterprises can lessen the adverse externalities brought on by pollution in the environment, thereby reducing carbon emissions. This article will carry out extensive research on its impact path “green credit → technological advancement → carbon emissions”, and propose hypothesis three:

Research hypothesis 3

Technological advancement plays a negative regulating role in the impact mechanism of green credit on carbon emissions, which strengthens the implementation effect of green credit. That is, green credit can cut down carbon emissions through raising level of technological advancement.

Methodology

Sample and data source

Research objects include 30 provincial administrative regions in China, excluding Tibet, Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan, depending on the data’s availability, and sample interval is 2012–2021. Data are derived from 2012 to 2021 China Statistical Yearbook, China Environmental Statistical Yearbook, China Industrial Statistics Yearbook, China Energy Statistical Yearbook and provincial data on the official website of the National Bureau of Statistics. Considering absence of industrial credit interest expense data for 2017 and 2018 in the existing statistical yearbooks, this article draws on data processing method of Mao, et al.35 to perform linear interpolation on the missing data for 2017 and 2018, and conducts empirical analysis on panel data using software Stata 18.0.

Variables selection

Dependent variable

Carbon emissions refer to the release of carbon-containing gases, primarily carbon dioxide (CO2), into the atmosphere as a result of human activities. In the existing literature, the magnitude of carbon emissions is predominantly quantified using total carbon emissions and per capita carbon emissions. Per capita emissions eliminate differences in population size compared to total emissions, which makes it fairer to compare carbon emissions across regions. Hence, to determine carbon emissions in different provincial administrative regions, referring to research of Liang, et al.27 and Liang, et al.36, this article employs carbon emissions per capita as a surrogate indicator. The calculation of this metric involves dividing the province’s total carbon emissions by its population. Using this substitute indicator allows for a more efficient evaluation and comparison of each province’s carbon emissions. At the regional and provincial levels, carbon emissions ought to be calculated, but there is currently no standardized method for doing so. Our research calculated the annual carbon emissions by employing varying carbon emission coefficients for eight distinct fossil fuel types. These coefficients were obtained from “2006 IPCC National Greenhouse Gas Inventory Guidelines” and “China Energy Statistical Yearbook.” The data from these sources allowed for a comprehensive analysis of carbon emissions. Independent variable

Currently, there are four primary academic indicators used to gauge green credit, namely the aggregate sum of bank credit dedicated to eco-friendly initiatives, proportion of loans allocated to energy-conservation and environmental safeguarding projects, overall investment in industrial pollution mitigation efforts, and percentage of interest expenses attributable to non-high energy-consuming sectors.

Among them, statistical information on green credit from 21 major banks in China has been released by the China Banking Regulatory Commission. However, there is insufficient data available at the provincial level. The data on proportion of loans for energy-saving and environmental safeguarding projects and the total amount of investment in industrial pollution control is relatively short, which cannot meet the needs of the research period in this article. Given that interest expense might serve as a gauge of credit, our paper makes reference to the articles of Liang, et al.27 and Jiang, et al.16, using the ratio of interest expense in non-high- energy-consuming industries to the total interest expenditure of industrial enterprises as the independent variable to measure green credit16. Control variables

With reference to relevant studies10,11,13,23,36,37, the following control variables have been chosen for this paper. The economic development level in various regions affects total energy consumption as well as carbon emission scale, this paper takes the per capita regional gross domestic product (GDP) as the measurement index of the economic development level, and its logarithmic processing as a control variable. Urbanization level (Urban), usually the expansion of urbanization tends to promote consumption, so that all kinds of living energy consumption increases, this process brings about the enhancement of carbon emissions. The proportion of urban population at year-end is adopted in this article in each province as a measure of the level of urbanization in different provinces, and its logarithmic treatment as a control variable. Generally, the difference in population size (Population) of each province will cause the difference in energy consumption demand, and the rise in urban population will elevate the consumption of energy required for various types of life, so the logarithmic value of the whole population of the region is used. Foreign external accessibility level (Foreign) is assessed by the combined investment of foreign-invested companies and is treated in a logarithmic manner. The industry’s structure (Industry), as measured by the proportion of GDP’s output value from the secondary industry, can indicate the direction of energy intensity through industrial structure adjustments. Fiscal (Fiscal), the fiscal system is the institutional basis for local governments to carry out urban development and planning. According to the financial circumstances of the local conditions, local governments have the ability to enhance or diminish the efficiency of carbon emissions. The extent of fiscal decentralization is measured in this paper by utilizing the ratio of fiscal expenditure at the regional level to fiscal revenue.

Transmission mechanism variable

This paper selects technological advancement (Tech) as the mediating variable to further understand the mechanism of green credit to restrict carbon emissions. It is measured by the R&D expenditure of industrial businesses above authorized size in each region (ten thousand yuan), see, for example, Wang38 and Jiang, et al.39.

Spatial econometric analysis introduces spatial effects in traditional econometric analysis, and the introduction method is achieved through spatial weight matrix40–42. Thus, spatial econometric analysis cannot be conducted without first generating a spatial weight matrix. Secondly, testing the spatial link between variables is essential. This indicates that green credit not only significantly suppresses local carbon emissions, but also produces spatial spillover effects on neighboring provinces to cut down carbon emissions. The possible explanation may be that the development of local green credit may lead to the emulation of neighboring provinces under the environmental protection assessment mechanism of local governments, which promotes the growth of green credit in adjacent provinces and supports the expansion of green credit. In addition, it supports the promotion of the effective flow of capital in support of green innovation in the inter-regional context. Meanwhile, the diffusion effect of green credit in this province is also advantageous for the lessening of carbon emissions in the neighboring provinces. In this case, the externality will be able to make the restrictive measures to lower carbon emissions spread in neighboring regions. Hypothesis 2 is verified.

The mediation effect test refers to the classic three-step mediation test of Wen, et al.45: The initial step is to determine if carbon emissions can be significantly reduced via green credit; the second step is to test whether green credit can significantly promote technological advancement; and the third step is to test the roles of technological advancement and green credit on carbon emissions simultaneously. The test was carried out via software Stata 18.0 and Table 7 displays the results.

The total effect exists and the regression coefficient of green credit on carbon emissions is significantly negative at the 1% level, -1.134, consult the model’s regression results of model (1) without the variable of technological advancement. This indicates that the overall expansion of green credit within China to some extent hinders the release of carbon emissions. From the regression outcomes of model (2), the regression coefficient of green credit on technological advancement is significantly positive at 1% level, which is 0.835, indicating that green credit development can significantly promote technological advancement. From the regression outcomes of model (3), the regression coefficient of green credit on carbon emission is -0.848 after including technological progress as a mediator variable in the model, the absolute value of which lessens and is still significant at the 1% level. At the significance level of 1%, the regression coefficient for technological advancement is -0.342, successfully meeting the test’s requirements.

Discussion

Conclusion

Utilizing panel data from 30 Chinese provinces between 2012 and 2021, this study employs spatial econometric models and mediation effects models to examine the correlation between green credit and carbon emission. Our findings indicate that: (1) A notable spatial correlation exists among provinces in terms of carbon emissions. (2) The development of green credit has a significant “local- neighboring” carbon emission reduction effect. It suggests that creation of green credit can have a substantial detrimental influence on nearby carbon emissions because of spatial spillover effects, in addition to having a strong suppressive effect on local carbon emissions. (3) There is a considerable “local-neighboring” carbon emission reduction effect from the growth of green finance. Technological advancement has been aided by the help of green credit development, thereby suppressing carbon emissions.

Policy recommendations

Considering the conclusions above, this article proposes the following countermeasures based on empirical results from China’s experience.

Firstly, based on the principle of marketization, increase the scale of green credit investment. This study found that an increase in green credit allocation significantly suppresses carbon emissions. This reflects the implementation of green credit policies, which can help achieve the goals of “carbon peak” and “carbon neutrality”. Therefore, relevant national departments should actively improve the legal system, better enhance the legislative level of green credit, and ensure the smooth implementation of green credit policies. In order to promote the sustainable development of China’s green credit business and its impact on carbon reduction, local governments should support commercial banks in carrying out green credit business through market supervision, innovation incentives, risk guarantees, and other means. They should establish credit incentive mechanisms to support the development of green industries and credit constraint mechanisms to suppress high energy consumption and high pollution industries, cut off the funding chain of the high pollution industries from the source, and accelerate the development of green industries.

Secondly, strengthen regional economic cooperation and fully leverage the spatial spillover effects of green credit. The empirical results indicate that green credit has a spatial spillover effect of “neighborhood companionship”. In view of this, building industrial chains and industrial clusters can facilitate the cross regional flow of production factors such as human capital, financial resources, and technological endowments, promote mutual exchange and complementary advantages between regions, and achieve spatial spillover of green credit carbon reduction efficiency.

Thirdly, emphasize the use of technological advancements to promote the reduction of carbon emissions. This study shows that China’s carbon reduction control is achieved through technological advancement. Therefore, for the technological development of enterprises, funding and policy support should be increased, and attention should be paid to the promotion and application of green technologies, so that they can fully play their role in energy conservation and emission reduction. At the same time, emphasis should be placed on supporting the green transformation of high energy consuming and high polluting enterprises. Pay attention to the development of the tertiary industry, reasonably increase the proportion of the tertiary industry, reduce the proportion of polluting enterprises within the industry, increase the proportion of clean and low-carbon enterprises, and strengthen energy conservation, emission reduction or green transformation of various industries.

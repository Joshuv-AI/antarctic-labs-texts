Sediment provenance of Late Carboniferous-Early Triassic in the Puyang area, Eastern North China Craton
Authors: Kangnan Yan, Yinhui Zuo, Jie Hu, Yunxian Zhang, Yongshui Zhou, Yongzhi Zhang, Yiyu Yao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryTo reveal the stratigraphic age of the Shiqianfeng Formation in the eastern continental basin of the North China Craton and the provenance of its sediments from the Late Carboniferous to the Early Triassic, six sandstone samples from the Puyang area were selected for zircon U-Pb dating. The result show that the Shiqianfeng Formation in the eastern North China Craton belongs to the Early Triassic. According to the age clusters of six samples, considering the regional geological setting and the distribution of zircon ages in the potential provenance area, it can be inferred that the Inner Mongolia Paleo-uplift provided continuous provenance supply for the study area during the Late Carboniferous-Early Triassic. It is also suggested that the Inner Mongolia Paleo-uplift originated during the Devonian and received denudation to provide provenance for inland basins. The second and third tectonic uplift occurred during the middle-late Permian, and the early-middle Triassic, respectively.Subject areas: Earth sciences, Geology, Isotope geochemistry, Petrochemistry, Trace element geochemistry

Introduction

The NCC (NCC), with an evolutionary history spanning approximately 3.8 billion years, has undergone intricate and multi-stage tectonic evolution.1,2,3,4 It also records and preserves nearly all major tectonic events during the early stages of Earth’s development,5,6,7 thus attracts the wide attention of scholars.8,9, It shares borders the Central Asian orogenic belt to the north, the Qinling-Dabie orogenic belt to the south, the Early Paleozoic Qilian orogenic belt to the west, and the Sulu orogenic belt to the east.10,11 According to the basement outcrops, the NCC can be divided into the western block, the central orogenic belt, and the eastern block from west to east (Figure 1).12,13,14

Previous studies have predominantly focused on the periphery, central, and western regions of the NCC, aiming to reveal the Early Precambrian geological evolution of the NCC.16,17,18,19 However, the eastern block of the NCC remains relatively understudied due to the presence of a thick sedimentary cover since the Mesoproterozoic. Nevertheless, these extensive depositional processes not only bear witness to the formation of intracontinental basins within the NCC but also serve as archives of crucial geodynamic information concerning the evolution of peripheral orogenic belts. The investigation of stratigraphic age and provenance in sedimentary basins holds significant importance in understanding the evolution of basins, uplift mechanisms, and denudation processes within orogenic belts.20,21

The limited availability of reliable paleontological evidence for continental stratigraphy in the eastern block of the NCC has resulted in varied interpretations of the depositional ages of certain strata over an extended period. The Shiqianfeng Formation, located in the eastern block of the NCC, comprises a substantial sequence of fluvial lacustrine tight sandstones with potential for oil and gas exploration, forms in the transition stage of the Permian-Triassic, and belongs to a typical set of continental sediments.22,23,24 Given the absence of marine fossils, determining the formation age of the Shiqianfeng Formation using biostratigraphic methods has proven challenging, resulting in ongoing debates. Some researchers propose that the Shiqianfeng Formation in the central and western regions of the NCC represents the Early Triassic deposits25; while others argue that the southern region corresponds to the Middle Triassic deposits.26 However, limited studies have been conducted on the eastern block, leading most scholars to assume that it represents the Late Paleozoic deposition.27,28,29,30

Regarding the provenance of intracontinental sedimentary basins in the NCC, the eastern block has received relatively less research attention, leading to diverse viewpoints. Some scholars argue that the Inner Mongolia Paleo-uplift served as the primary provenance area for the NCC during the Carboniferous to Early Permian31,32; the Yinshan-Yanshan orogenic belt played a crucial role as the main provenance region in the eastern block during the Early Permian, while the Funiu paleo-land in the south provided fewer sediment sources.33 Other research suggests that the Inner Mongolia Paleo-uplift acted as the primary provenance region for most of the NCC, with the North Qinling orogenic belt in the southern margin serving as a secondary provenance region.34 Moreover, there is a lack of systematic understanding regarding the provenance analysis of the Late Carboniferous to Early Triassic continuous strata.

Located in the eastern block of NCC, the Late Paleozoic petroleum system in the Puyang area has good exploration potential (Figure 2). Still, the spatial distribution of oil and gas resources needs to be clarified.35 Provenance type directly determines the physical and chemical properties of sedimentary rocks, source direction affects the spatial distribution pattern of the sedimentary system, and accurate sedimentary age is conducive to the analysis of the paleo-sedimentary environment. Therefore, the analysis of the Late Carboniferous-Early Triassic sedimentary provenance and the Shiqianfeng Formation sedimentary age in the Puyang area helps understand the development characteristics of source rock, reservoir, and cap of the Late Paleozoic petroleum system and provides basic data for the spatial distribution of oil and gas resources.

Detrital zircon, a commonly occurring accessory mineral in sedimentary rocks, provides valuable insights into the magmatic and metamorphic events of crustal evolution.36,37,38,39,40,41,42 Since detrital zircons are resistant to corrosion and weathering and less affected by late tectonic-thermal events,43,44,45,46 the microregional in situ age distribution characteristics of detrital zircons are widely used to identify provenance properties, limit the absolute age of stratigraphic units, discuss regional tectonic evolutions and reconstruct the paleogeographic environments.47,48,49 These applications serve as critical tools for geologists investigating sedimentary basins and peripheral orogenic belts.50,51,52,53

To determine the sedimentary age of the Shiqianfeng Formation and systematically explore the sediment provenance during the Late Carboniferous to Early Triassic in the eastern intracontinental basin of the NCC, as well as comprehensively investigate the tectonic evolution characteristics of its provenance area, zircon U-Pb dating was conducted on six sandstone samples collected from the Puyang area. In this study, the sedimentary age of the Shiqianfeng Formation was first determined according to the age of the youngest single zircon in the two Shiqianfeng Formation sandstones, and then combined with the zircon age characteristics of the sandstone sample from the Upper Shihezi Formation, the Lower Shihezi Formation, the Shanxi Formation, and the Taiyuan Formation, the provenance of the Late Carboniferan-Early Triassic in Puyang area was analyzed. Finally, the tectonic evolution characteristics of the Late Carboniferous and Early Triassic in the source area are explored. This study is not only beneficial to the oil and gas exploration of the Late Paleozoic accumulation system in the Puyang area but also has important significance for understanding the tectonic evolution of the eastern block of the NCC and its peripheral orogenic belt.

Geological settings and samples

The NCC is one of the main tectonic units of the Chinese continent (Figure 1A).6,7,10,11 The study area is located in the Puyang area of the Bohai Bay Basin in the eastern block of the NCC. The basement of this region consists of the Archaean Taishan Group granitic gneiss, while the sedimentary cover includes Lower Paleozoic, Upper Paleozoic, Triassic, and Cenozoic strata (Figure 1B).35,54,55 The Lower Paleozoic deposits consist of shallow marine carbonate formations from the Cambrian to the Ordovician. In the late Ordovician, the Caledonian orogeny resulted in uplift of the strata in this area, which continued until the Early Carboniferous. During the Late Carboniferous, the strata settled and began to receive deposits. Above the Ordovician unconformity, Late Carboniferous to Early Permian marine and continental strata containing coal deposits were formed, followed by Late Permian to Middle Triassic continental clastic rocks, fluvial sediments, and lacustrine sedimentary strata.15,56,57,58

The Taiyuan Formation primarily consists of marine sediments, encompassing carbonate platform facies and clastic coastal sediments. The carbonate platform facies are characterized by gray and dark gray micrite bioclastic limestone, often interbedded with mudstone from gulf environments and coastal marsh sediments. The clastic coastal system mainly comprises barrier coastal sediments, including barrier islands (dams), lagoons, and tidal flats. During the sedimentary period spanning the Shanxi Formation to the Lower Shihezi Formation, the study area was situated in a transitional zone between the sea and land. A deltaic depositional system was developed, featuring various sub-facies, such as distributary marsh, distributary plain, and distributary channel sub-facies. When the Upper Shihezi Formation and the Shiqianfeng Formation were deposited, this area was far away from the Marine environment and developed a set of continental sedimentary systems, mainly fluvial sedimentary systems (Figure 3).59,60,61

For this study, continuous sampling was carried out on the Late Carboniferous to Early Triassic sandstones from three cores in the Puyang area. Well QG3 is located in the W-Huzhuangji slope, Well B19 is located in the Weicheng-Wenmingzhai uplift, and Well PS8 is located at the junction of East Changyuan sag and Nanhejia sag (Figure 2). According to the borehole section, the Upper Paleozoic strata buried depth of Well PS8 is relatively deeper than Well QG3 and Well B19. The selected samples included PS8-1, B19-1, QG3-1, QG3-2, QG3-3, and QG3-4.

The depth of the top of Shanxi formation in Well PS8 is 5300.0 m, but it has not reached the bottom of the Shanxi formation even after drilling 5450 m. Sample PS8-1 represents the sandstone located in the upper of the Shanxi formation and was extracted from Well PS8 at a sampling depth of 5333.9 m (Figure 4C). Under the microscope, it can be seen that the grain size is mainly distributed in 0.50–1.00 mm, and some grains can reach 1.63 mm. The main mineral composition is quartz, accounting for 88%, and a small amount of K-feldspar, plagioclase, and other minerals. The detritus content is small, the matrix is mainly clay, and there is no cement. The sample PS8-1 is revealed to be argillaceous coarse-grained lithic quartz sandstone by microscopic identification (Figures 4N and 4O).

Results

The palladium preparation, transmitted light photography, and reflected light photography of zircon were performed by Langfang Shangyi Rock Detection Technology Service Co., LTD.62,63,64,65,66 Zircon U-Pb isotope dating was conducted at the State Key Laboratory of Oil and Gas Geology and Exploitation, Chengdu University of Technology.67 Data processing and calculation of the weighted average age were performed using ICPMSDataCal software. The U-Pb age frequency distribution and concordant figure of detrital zircon were plotted by Isoplotr.68,69,70,71 The age spectra of detrital zircons were analyzed by using grains with a concordant degree of >90%.72,73,74,75,76

Petrographically, the detrital zircons isolated from the six samples exhibit similar morphology. The majority of the Late Paleozoic to the Mesozoic zircons is angular to subangular with poor roundness, some of them are long columnar, and some of them have a high degree of euhedral granular, which indicates that the detrital zircons from the detrital cover may not have the characteristics of recirculation products. In contrast, most of the Archean-Proterozoic zircons display sub-roundness shapes, indicating that they have been had undergone prolonged transportation and belong to recycling sediments. Zircon cathodoluminescence (CL) images reveal that the zircons in each sample predominantly exhibit oscillating zonal or plate zonal structures. However, the internal structure of the zircons varies significantly, with some displaying a core-edge structure while others possess a uniform structure. The grain size of the zircons exceeds 100 μm (Figure 5).

Sample B19-1

The U and Th contents of zircons are 26.63–995.91 ppm and 13.97–538.26 ppm, respectively. The Th/U ratios varied between 0.06 and 1.68. It was observed that 80.6% of the zircons exhibited Th/U ratios higher than 0.4, indicating their predominantly magmatic origin. In terms of crystal morphology, most zircons displayed angular-subangular shapes, while a smaller portion exhibited rounded or fan-shaped forms. Euhedral to subhedral crystals were found to be the most prevalent. Most zircon CL images show obvious oscillating magma growth belts with wide belts, which may be due to the rapid diffusion of trace elements at high temperatures,78 such as points 01, 07, 08, 46, 57, and 59. Conversely, certain zircon zones displayed narrower growth belts, likely associated with the formation of acidic magmatic rocks at lower temperatures, the diffusion of trace elements in this case was slower, as shown at sites 13, 18, and 74. A few metamorphic zircons have residual crystal nuclei, as shown at point 18 (Figure 5).

Except for 7 measurement points, the degree of concordance is low (<90%), while the age of zircons from the other 93 measurement points meets the requirements. Discussion

Limiting the stratigraphic age of the Shiqianfeng formation

In continental strata, the absence of reliable paleontological fossils poses challenges for establishing stratigraphic correlation and relative chronology, and the latest isotopic chronology is undoubtedly the best way to solve this issue. The age of the youngest detrital zircons is often employed to establish the maximum sedimentary age of strata, providing a basis for studying stratigraphic division, correlation, and basin evolution.79 Dickinson et al., in an analysis of the Mesozoic zircon U-Pb age database from the Colorado Plateau and its surrounding areas, suggested that different methods could be used to determine the maximum sedimentary ages of strata, and emphasized the importance of using several different methods to obtain the most accurate maximum sedimentary ages. There are seven methods used to determine the age of strata: (1) youngest single grain age (YSG); (2) youngest graphical age peak of detrital zircon recorded on the histogram (YPP); (3) youngest detrital zircon age (YDZ); (4) weighted mean age youngest 1σ grain cluster (YC1σ (2+)); (5) weighted mean age youngest 2σ grain cluster (YC2σ (3+)); (6) weighted average age (WA); (7) tuffzirc (Zircon Age Extractor (6+)).80,81 In this paper, the maximum sedimentary age of the sandstone of the Shiqianfeng Formation in the study area is discussed by combining the ages of several youngest zircons, to determine its sedimentary age (Table 1).

The YSG ages of the two sandstone samples within the Shiqianfeng Formation exhibit considerable variation, with one sandstone sample measuring 235 ± 4 Ma and the other 230 ± 4 Ma. However, the individual zircon ages are consistent within the margin of error. Dickinson et al. and Tucker et al. suggested that the YSG should be used to represent the maximum deposition age of strata, which should be verified by other data.80,81 The YPP ages of the two samples were determined to be 243 ± 1 Ma and 247 ± 2 Ma, respectively. Notably, both the YSG and YPP ages are younger than the Permian-Triassic depositional boundary, which is dated at 251.0 ± 0.4 Ma. This indicates that the Shiqianfeng Formation in the Puyang area of the eastern block of the NCC belongs to the Early Triassic. This interpretation aligns with previous studies that have examined paleogeography, paleoclimate, biological fossils, stratigraphic sequence, sporo-pollen assemblage characteristics, and iridium anomalies at the base of the strata.25

In conclusion, it is reasonable that the Shiqianfeng Formation in the Puyang area belongs to Early Triassic deposits, which provides valuable new evidence in determining the Permian-Triassic boundary within the eastern block of the NCC.

Provenance analysis

To analyze the provenance of sedimentary strata using the U-Pb isotopic characteristics of detrital zircon, the initial step is to determine the potential provenance areas. In the case of the NCC, it has been influenced by various geological processes, including the closure and collision of the Qinling-Dabie orogenic belt and the Central Asia orogenic belt (with the Xingmeng-Mongolia orogenic belt in the east), and the intra-plate orogenic movement in the Meso-Cenozoic.82,83 These orogenic events have shaped the current tectonic pattern characterized by uplifts and interphase basins in the NCC. In addition, the eastern boundary of the NCC is the Sulu orogenic belt,37 while the Inner Mongolia Paleo-uplift, located in the northern margin, has existed since the Proterozoic Eon and has experienced multiple tectonic uplifts during the Late Paleozoic.84,85 These aforementioned orogenic belts and paleo-uplifts are potential sources of detrital material for the study area.

When considering the paleo-sedimentary environment of the study area, it is possible to exclude certain potential provenance areas. During the sedimentary period from the Taiyuan Formation to the Shanxi Formation, the NCC experienced a “seesaw” movement under the strong compression of the Siberian plate. This movement resulted in changes from a low elevation in the north to a high elevation in the south, and subsequently from a high elevation in the north to a low elevation in the south. The transgression direction also changed from northeast to southeast. In the study area, the predominant depositional environments were tidal flats, lagoons, and barrier islands. During the deposition period from the Lower Shihezi Formation to the Upper Shihezi Formation, the continuous uplift of the northern margin of the NCC led to the retreat of seawater from the southeast of the study area. This marked the beginning of river-controlled delta deposition environment, with detrital material entering the study area through distributary channels from the north. In the sedimentary period of the Shiqianfeng Formation, the study area received a continuous and abundant supply of detrital material. The climate during this period was relatively dry, and a meandering river sedimentary environment developed. Three primary channels existed from north to south, with flood plains forming between the channels.33,86,87

Considering the paleo-sedimentary environment, the provenance direction from the Taiyuan Formation to the Shiqianfeng Formation is from north to south. Therefore, it can be deduced that the provenance area is located to the north of the study area. Consequently, the Qinling-Dabie orogenic belt in the south and the Sulu orogenic belt in the east can be excluded as potential provenance areas. To determine the provenance, the age distribution of magmatic rocks in the northern margin of the NCC and the orogenic belt to the north can be compared with the age distribution in the study area (Figure 9). This comparison will aid in identifying the most likely provenance sources.

A total of 368 Archean-Proterozoic detrital zircons with U-Pb concordant ages were identified in the Upper Carboniferous to Lower Triassic sandstones of the Puyang area in the eastern NCC, accounting for 66.7% of all detrital zircons. These zircons exhibit two prominent peaks at approximately 1861 Ma (ranging from 1454 to 2160 Ma) and 2508 Ma (ranging from 2224 to 3094 Ma), with the oldest zircon age recorded at 3094 ± 31 Ma.

Archean gneiss is widely distributed in the NCC and has been identified in its eastern, western, central, and northern parts.91 For example, gneiss dating from 2800 to 2700 Ma has been identified in the Hengshan, Wutai, and Dengfeng in the central NCC.12,92 During the period of 2700 to 2500 Ma, granulite facies metamorphism occurred, accompanied by numerous granite intrusions. Examples include the Auzonite granites with crystallization ages of 2550–2546 Ma and metamorphic ages of 2510–2480 Ma found in the Qingyuan terrane in the eastern part of the NCC.93 The NCC also contains extensive basement from 2300 to 1900 Ma, comprising a significant number of granites, tonalite-trondhjemite-granodiorite (TTG) gneisses, and mafic volcanic rocks, such as the 2200–2100 Ma granite found in the Wutai area,91 and the 2333 Ma and 2151 Ma bimodal volcanic rocks found in the Zhongtiaoshan, Shanxi Province. ∼1800 Ma, magmatic activities were also widely developed in the NCC. This includes the 1800–1750 Ma volcanic rock series dominated by the Xiong’er Group in the southern margin of the NCC, and the contemporaneous granodiorite, porphyry, and mafic dike group. Notable examples include a large-scale mafic dike group and alkaline intrusive rocks in the northern margin, such as the mafic granulite with a peak age of 1793 Ma and amphibolite with a peak age of 1814 Ma found in Zhangjiakou,3 as well as gabbro from 1781 to 1765 Ma in the Taihang Mountain. Additionally, the central ferromagnesian dike group and the southwest margin granite-porphyry bodies, such as the 1806 Ma post-orogenic monzogranite found in the Lvliang Mountain, Shanxi Province94; a plagioclase amphibolite of 1859.5 Ma was also found in the Dandong, Liaoning, eastern NCC.95

Combined with the regional geological setting and the age characteristics of magmatic rocks in the provenance area, it can be inferred that the detrital zircons with peak values of ∼1861 Ma and ∼2508 Ma in the sandstone in the study area are mainly derived from the middle-high grade metamorphic rocks in the basement of the NCC.

(2)Sources of the Late Paleozoic detrital zircons

Sources of the Late Paleozoic detrital zircons

A total of 166 Late Paleozoic detrital zircons with U-Pb concordant ages were obtained, accounting for 30.1% of all detrital zircons, all samples except B19-1 had an obvious peak value of ∼300 Ma (age range: 254–427 Ma). The Th/U ratios and CL images of the zircons show typical magmatic zircon characteristics. Due to the lack of Late Paleozoic magmatic activity records in the NCC, the possibility of providing detrital sources for the study area by itself can be ruled out.

However, the Inner Mongolia Paleo-uplift, located on the northern margin of the NCC, is widely distributed with Late Paleozoic intrusive rocks. For example, 311 Ma gneiss quartz diorite and 327 Ma quartz diorite were found in Longhua County, 303 Ma quartz diorite in the Borono-Xigou area, and 304 Ma granite in the Fengning area.96 In addition, the late Paleozoic zircon age peaks in the Datong area in the southern part of the Inner Mongolia Paleo-uplift and the sedimentary basins in the Western Hills area of Beijing both show 270–280 Ma age peaks.97 Therefore, most scholars believe that the Late Paleozoic provenance in northern NCC comes from the Inner Mongolia Paleo-uplift.

Furthermore, significant Late Paleozoic magmatic activity occurred in the Xingmeng-Mongolia orogenic belt, situated to the north of the NCC. Jian et al. conducted zircon U-Pb dating in the Mandura area of the Solonker suture zone, providing ages of 284 ± 4 Ma and 288 ± 6 Ma for gabbro and Plagioclase Granite in the Hadamiao area of Chagan. They also provided ages of granites (313 ± 2 Ma and 314 ± 3 Ma), trondhjemite (324 ± 3 Ma), gabbro (297 ± 2 Ma), and quartz diorite (295 ± 2 Ma) in the Mandura area.98 In the Sunite Zuoqi area of Inner Mongolia, detrital zircons from Late Devonian to Early Carboniferous strata displayed a peak age of 320–338 Ma.18 Chen et al. found that the peak ages of clastic sedimentary rocks and metamorphic rocks in the Xingmeng Orogenic belt were ∼320 Ma99

Based on the above analysis, the Late Paleozoic detrital zircons in the Late Carboniferous to Early Triassic sandstones of the study area likely originate from the Inner Mongolia Paleo-uplift and the Xingmeng-Mongolia orogenic belt to the north of the NCC. However, when comparing the detrital zircon age spectra and potential provenance age spectra of Late Paleozoic to Early Mesozoic sandstones in the NCC, it becomes evident that the Xingmeng-Mongolia orogenic belt exhibits an Early Paleozoic age peak in addition to the Late Paleozoic peak (Figure 9). There are no Early Paleozoic detrital zircons in the study area, and detrital zircons in the Western Hills of Beijing, Yanshan, and Pingquan areas in the north of the NCC are mostly Late Paleozoic peaks, with very few Early Paleozoic zircons.85,89 Additionally, the Xingmeng-Mongolia orogenic belt, being a Phanerozoic accretionary orogenic belt, displays distinct differences in zircon age distribution and Hf isotope characteristics compared to the inland basin of the NCC.13 The Late Paleozoic detrital zircons in the study area are mainly derived from the Inner Mongolia Paleo-uplift.

(3)Sources of the Early Mesozoic detrital zircons

Sources of the Early Mesozoic detrital zircons

In this paper, only 18 Early Mesozoic detrital zircons were obtained from B19-1 and QG3-1, accounting for 3.1% of all detrital zircons, with a distinct U-Pb age peak of ∼240 Ma (230–251 Ma). These zircons have Th/U > 0.4 and developed obvious magmatic zonation, indicating typical magmatic zircon genetic characteristics.

Previous investigations have demonstrated the existence of Early Mesozoic magmatic rocks within the Inner Mongolia Paleo-uplift. For instance, biotite with a peak age value of ∼243 Ma was found in Longhua County,84 closely resembling the age spectrum characteristics of Early Mesozoic zircons in our study area. These findings strongly suggest that the provenance of our study area during this period likely originated from the Inner Mongolia Paleo-uplift. Moreover, several studies have reported the presence of detrital zircons from Early Mesozoic sandstones in the Western Hills of Beijing, Yanshan, and Pingquan regions in the northern part of the NCC.85,89 Importantly, these findings align with the age distribution of Early Mesozoic zircons from the Inner Mongolia Paleo-uplift (Figure 9). Consequently, it can be inferred that the Early Mesozoic detrital zircons in our study area are also sourced from the Inner Mongolia Paleo-uplift.

Constraints on the tectonic evolution of provenance areas

(1)Constraints of Archean-Proterozoic detrital zircons on the tectonic evolution of provenance area

Constraints of Archean-Proterozoic detrital zircons on the tectonic evolution of provenance area

Within the NCC, two significant tectonic-thermal events occurred during the Archean-Paleoproterozoic, which are pivotal stages in the basement evolution of the North China plate. The late Neoarchean phase (2600–2500 Ma) witnessed the Wutai movement in the North China landmass, characterized by intensified volcanism and sedimentation. This led to the formation of Neoarchean greenstone belts, developed a large number of the crust melting granites and tonalite-trondhjemite-granodiorite (TTG) gneiss, accompanied by extensive granulite-amphibolite facies metamorphism, mafic dike, and granite intrusion. Notable examples of Neoarchean greenstone belts include Hongtoushan, Dongwufenzi, Dengfeng, and Wutai Mountain greenstone belts.100 During the Late Paleoproterozoic to the Early Mesoproterozoic (1850–1600 Ma), the NCC experienced large-scale collisional and accretionary orogenies known as the Lvliang Movement. Concurrently, the internal plate underwent extensive tensile and break-up tectonic activities, characterized by large-scale extensional magmatic intrusions, volcanic eruptions, and early metamorphic basement uplift. The magmatic rocks form in this stage was widely distributed, spanning from the eastern Liao-Ji Province in the east to the Wolshan-Zailtai area of Inner Mongolia in the west. The region extended from the southwestern region of Henan Province in the south to Chengde in Hebei Province in the north.101,102

The basement zircons in the NCC display ages ranging from 1600 Ma to 3800 Ma, with two prominent peaks at approximately 1800 Ma and 2500 Ma, representing the periods of the Lvliang and Wutai movements, respectively. Notably, detrital zircons from Late Carboniferous to Early Triassic sandstones in the Puyang area of the eastern NCC exhibit two age peaks at approximately 1861 Ma (age range: 1454–2160 Ma) and 2508 Ma (age range: 2224–3094 Ma), corresponding to these two significant tectonic-thermal events.

Combined with the regional geological setting and the age characteristics of magmatic rocks in the provenance area, it can be inferred that the detrital zircons with peak values of ∼1861 Ma and ∼2508 Ma in the sandstone in the study area are mainly derived from the middle-high grade metamorphic rocks in the basement of the NCC.

(2)Constraints of Late Paleozoic-Early Mesozoic detrital zircons on the tectonic evolution of provenance area

Constraints of Late Paleozoic-Early Mesozoic detrital zircons on the tectonic evolution of provenance area

During the Devonian (405–358 Ma), an arc-continental collision occurred between the northern margin of the NCC and the Bainaimiao Island arc belt as a result of the subduction and closure of the Paleo-Asian Ocean, which resulted in tectonic uplift. The Inner Mongolia Paleo-uplift began to form and receive denudation to provide provenance for inland basins (Figure 10A).13 At this time, the paleo-uplift was a marginal arc tectonic environment, formed arc volcanism forming felsic rocks, such as diorite (331 ± 5 Ma) and quartz diorite (324–310 Ma),58,76 which corresponds to the age of the detrital zircons in the sandstone measured in this study at 320–340 Ma.

The second tectonic uplift occurred in the Middle to Late Permian (276–258 Ma), characterized by relatively stable uplift and denudation (Figure 10B).85 Influenced by the closing process of the Paleo-Asian Ocean, the tectonic environment gradually changed from an active continental margin to a passive continental margin extensional environment.84,102 Petrological and chronological evidence suggests that the Paleo-Asian Ocean closed prior to the Middle-Late Permian, around 276 Ma.85 During this time, under compressional conditions, the ancient metamorphic basement underwent remelting in the middle and lower crust, resulting in the formation of felsic intrusions such as granodiorite (265 ± 7 Ma) and monzogranite (269 ± 5 Ma).102 These ages correspond to the sandstone detrital zircons measured at 250–300 Ma in this study. Therefore, it can be inferred that the two late Paleozoic age peaks (250–300 Ma, 320–340 Ma) observed in all the samples of this study likely record two rapid subduction events of the Paleo-Asian Ocean at the northern margin of the NCC during the Early Carboniferous-Late Permian.

Previous studies indicate that the third uplift of the Inner Mongolia Paleo-uplift occurred during the Early-Middle Triassic (Figure 10C), possibly associated with magma upwelling in the post-orogenic extensional environment of the Central Asian orogenic belt. This uplift was characterized by the occurrence of numerous alkaline granites and diabase dikes.58,85 The ∼240 Ma age peak observed in the sandstone detrital zircons from this study corresponds to this uplift event, potentially recording the extension of the northern margin of the NCC after the collision with the Southern Mongolia block.

Limitation of the study

This study focuses on the provenance analysis of the Late Carboniferous-Early Triassic of Puyang Area combined with the regional geological setting and the detrital zircon U-Pb dating. In addition, zircon Lu-Hf isotopes can provide important information about rock formation environment, material sources, and the interaction between crust and mantle, which can further improve the accuracy of provenance identification.

Method details

The methodology which comprises (a) zircon U-Pb dating, (b) stratigraphic age restriction, and (c) provenance analysis.

Zircon U-Pb dating

The collected sandstone samples were first crushed to a size smaller than 60 mesh. Subsequently, zircon mineral grains were separated using standard density and magnetic separation methods. Intact zircon grains without obvious fractures were manually selected under a binocular microscope. These selected zircon grains were then affixed to double-sided adhesive tape, fixed in transparent epoxy resin, and polished until the interior of the zircon was exposed for analysis in Laser Ablation Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass Spectrometry (LA-ICP-MS).8 Transmitted light and reflected light images were captured of the polished zircons to observe the surface and internal structures. Care was taken to avoid cracks or inclusions within the crystals during the subsequent point selection process.62,63,64,65,66 After gilding, CL images were taken to further reflect the crystal morphology and internal structure characteristics of zircon, as well as distinguish the genetic types of the zircon (Figure 5). The palladium preparation, transmitted light photography, and reflected light photography of zircon were performed by Langfang Shangyi Rock Detection Technology Service Co., LTD. The CL imaging was conducted using the field emission scanning electron microscope (MIRA3) available in their laboratory, with a scanning time of 2 min and a voltage of 7 kV.

Zircon U-Pb isotope dating was conducted at the State Key Laboratory of Oil and Gas Geology and Exploitation, Chengdu University of Technology. The analysis employed LA-ICP-MS micro-region dating, utilizing the Resolution LR 193 nm laser ablation system by Applied Spectroscopy and the Agilent 7900 ICP-MS instrument equipped with a 193 nm excimer laser from Agilent, USA. The laser energy density used was 3.5 J/cm2, with a beam spot diameter of 29 μm, a frequency of 8 Hz, and a denudation duration of 45 s. Helium was used as the carrier gas for denudation purposes. To calibrate the instrument quality and isotopic fractionation, the Harvard University standard zircon 91500 was employed as an external standard and measured every 10 samples. Standard zircon PLE was used as a blind standard to test the data quality. The NIST SRM610 was used as the external standard and 29Si as the internal standard to determine the trace element content of zircon, and once every 20 samples were determined.67 The errors of U, Th and Pb contents quoted in tables and figures are at the 1σ level. Data processing and calculation of the weighted average age were performed using ICPMSDataCal software. The U-Pb age frequency distribution and concordant figure of detrital zircon were plotted by Isoplotr. The errors of zircon ages are at the 2σ level. For zircons with lower cumulative radioactive components, 206Pb/238U ages (less than 1000 Ma) were selected, while the 207Pb/206Pb ages were used when the zircon with higher cumulative radioactive components (greater than 1000 Ma).68,69,70,71 The age spectra of detrital zircons were analyzed by using grains with a concordant degree of >90%.72,73,74,75,76

Stratigraphic age restriction

The age of the youngest detrital zircons is often employed to establish the maximum sedimentary age of strata, providing a basis for studying stratigraphic division, correlation, and basin evolution.79 Dickinson et al., in an analysis of the Mesozoic zircon U-Pb age database from the Colorado Plateau and its surrounding areas, suggested that different methods could be used to determine the maximum sedimentary ages of strata, and emphasized the importance of using several different methods to obtain the most accurate maximum sedimentary ages. There are seven methods used to determine the age of strata: (1) youngest single grain age (YSG); (2) youngest graphical age peak of detrital zircon recorded on the histogram (YPP); (3) youngest detrital zircon age (YDZ); (4) weighted mean age youngest 1σ grain cluster (YC1σ (2+)); (5) weighted mean age youngest 2σ grain cluster (YC2σ (3+)); (6) weighted average age (WA); (7) tuffzirc (Zircon Age Extractor (6+)).80,81 In this paper, the maximum sedimentary age of the sandstone of the Shiqianfeng Formation in the study area is discussed by combining the ages of several youngest zircons, to determine its sedimentary age (Table 1).

Provenance analysis

To analyze the provenance of sedimentary strata using the U-Pb isotopic characteristics of detrital zircon, the initial step is to determine the potential provenance areas. The orogenic belts and paleo-uplifts around the research area are potential sources of detrital material for the study area. When considering the paleo-sedimentary environment of the study area, the provenance direction can be determined, it is possible to exclude certain potential provenance areas. In this paper, combined with the regional geological setting and the detrital zircon U-Pb dating to analysis the provenance of the Late Carboniferous-Early Triassic of Puyang Area. In addition, combined with the regional geological setting and the age characteristics of magmatic rocks in the provenance area, it can be inferred that the tectonic evolution of provenance areas.

Quantification and statistical analysis

We utilized ICPMSDataCal software for statistical analysis. Firstly, we conducted descriptive statistics on the U, Th and U isotopic contents of detrital zircons measured by LA-ICP-MA, as shown in “Detrital Zircon U-Pb Ages of the Late Carboniferous-Early Triassic sedimentary strata of Puyang Area” (DOI: https://data.mendeley.com/drafts/dpg5grmnvt). Next, we calculated the weighted average age of each grain using ICPMSDataCal software. The errors of zircon ages are at the 2σ level, the age spectra of grains with a concordant degree of > 90% was selected for subsequent analysis.

On this basis, we utilized IsoplotR software to plot the zircon U-Pb age concordant and frequency distribution figure of each sample, as shown in Figures 7 and 8. Then, we quantitatively analyzed the age distribution characteristics of each sample, and systematically establish the Late Carboniferous and Early Triassic zircon U-Pb age spectrum in Puyang area.

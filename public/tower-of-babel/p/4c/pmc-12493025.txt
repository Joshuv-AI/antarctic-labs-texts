Long‐Term Outcomes of Microdebrider‐Assisted Endoscopic Powered Intracapsular Tonsillectomy and Adenoidectomy in Pediatric Obstructive Sleep Apnea: A Questionnaire‐Based Observational Study
Authors: Ryota Koshu, Masao Noda, Haruna Nakamoto, Karin Kojima, Hisashi Sugimoto, Takahiro Fukuhara, Tomokazu Yoshizaki, Makoto Ito
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjectivesThis study aimed to evaluate the long‐term efficacy and safety of microdebrider‐assisted endoscopic powered intracapsular tonsillectomy and adenoidectomy (PITA) in pediatric patients with obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) using caregiver‐reported retrospective assessments of pre‐ and post‐operative symptom states.MethodsThis single‐center retrospective observational study included 65 children aged < 15 years who underwent PITA and at least 1 year of postoperative follow‐up. Caregivers completed a structured postoperative questionnaire, retrospectively assessing preoperative and postoperative symptom severity using the validated Japanese version of the Obstructive Sleep Apnea Questionnaire‐18 (OSA‐18). The questionnaire evaluated sleep disturbance, physical symptoms, emotional distress, daytime functioning, and caregiver concerns. Postoperative complications, including tonsillitis, peritonsillar abscess, and perceived symptom recurrence, were also assessed. All surgeries were performed using an endoscopic PITA technique designed to preserve the tonsillar capsules.ResultsAmong the 65 pediatric patients (median follow‐up, 48.0 months; interquartile range, 25.0–65.0), significant improvements were observed across all five OSA‐18 domains. The mean preoperative scores were 5.2, 4.5, 2.3, 3.4, and 4.7 for sleep disturbance, physical symptoms, emotional distress, daytime functioning, and caregiver concerns, respectively. Postoperative scores improved to 1.7, 2.3, 1.7, 2.1, and 1.8, respectively (all, p < 0.001). Subgroup analyses showed no significant differences in outcomes based on follow‐up duration (< 5 vs. ≥ 5 years) or age at surgery (≤ 4 vs. > 4 years), suggesting durable and consistent long‐term benefits of PITA regardless of age or time since surgery. No peritonsillar abscesses were reported; only three patients (4.6%) experienced postoperative tonsillitis. Thirteen percent of the caregivers reported a subjective recurrence of symptoms; however, none required revision surgery.ConclusionPITA is a potentially safe and effective long‐term surgical option for pediatric OSA. Retrospective caregiver evaluation revealed sustained symptom improvement, minimal postoperative complications, and high satisfaction levels.Level of EvidenceLevel IV.

Introduction

Pediatric obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is characterized by intermittent upper airway obstruction during sleep, negatively affecting growth, learning, and behavior in children [1, 2]. OSA is associated with excessive daytime sleepiness; reduced attention span; neurocognitive impairment; and, in severe cases, cardiovascular or metabolic complications, making timely diagnosis and treatment essential [3, 4].

The primary etiologies of pediatric OSA include tonsil and adenoid hypertrophy. Surgical interventions, such as tonsillectomy (TE) and adenoidectomy (AE), are commonly employed to alleviate upper airway obstruction, with proven benefits for sleep quality and overall physical development [5, 6, 7, 8]. However, conventional TE is associated with significant postoperative pain and hemorrhage, among other complications, posing challenges for young patients and caregivers [9, 10].

In recent years, intracapsular TE and adenoidectomy have gained attention as less invasive surgical alternatives [11]. Specifically, powered intracapsular tonsillotomy and adenoidectomy (PITA), which utilizes powered instruments to remove hypertrophic tonsillar tissue while preserving the tonsillar capsule, have been reported to reduce postoperative pain and bleeding and promote faster oral intake recovery [12, 13, 14, 15]. Our institution has implemented endoscopic microdebrider‐assisted PITA and previously reported favorable short‐term outcomes in terms of safety and efficacy [12, 13]. Endoscopic PITA is associated with significantly lower postoperative hemorrhage, reduced analgesic requirements, and faster oral intake recovery than extracapsular TE, further supporting its short‐term advantages.

Although the short‐term effectiveness of PITA is gaining increasing recognition, evidence of its long‐term outcomes, including symptom recurrence and postoperative complications such as infection or tonsillar regrowth, remains limited. Long‐term data that clearly identify the surgical technique and instruments used are particularly scarce, specifically of endoscopic microdebrider‐assisted PITA. Given that intracapsular techniques have been in use for over two decades, the paucity of technique‐specific long‐term outcome data represents a notable gap in the literature.

This study aimed to address that gap by evaluating the long‐term efficacy and safety of endoscopic microdebrider‐assisted PITA in pediatric patients with OSA based on symptom improvement, quality of life outcomes, and incidence of postoperative complications over an extended follow‐up period.

Methods

Study Design

This single‐center retrospective observational study evaluated the long‐term outcomes in pediatric patients who underwent PITA for OSA. Eligible participants were children under the age of 15 years who underwent PITA at our institution and had at least 1 year of postoperative follow‐up at the time of data collection.

The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Jichi Medical University (approval number: 24‐225) and conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from the caregivers of all participants prior to inclusion in the study. All data were anonymized prior to the analysis to maintain patient confidentiality.

Participants

Background data, including age, sex, preoperative polysomnography (PSG) results, and the presence of tonsillar hypertrophy, were obtained from electronic medical records of eligible patients. PSG was used to obtain the total apnea–hypopnea index (AHI), which included obstructive, central, and mixed events. Surgical indications were comprehensively determined based on AHI scores and the presence of tonsillar and/or adenoidal hypertrophy; impact on daily life; and preferences of patients and their families. Patients wherein tonsillar and/or adenoidal hypertrophy was considered the primary cause of airway obstruction were selected to undergo surgery.

We retrospectively identified 122 patients who underwent microdebrider‐assisted endoscopic PITA and a postoperative follow‐up period of at least 1 year at the time of data collection. Among them, five patients were excluded because the instruments used for surgery were unknown from the operative record. All eligible patients were contacted via postal mail and invited to complete a one‐time postoperative OSA‐18 questionnaire. The questionnaire was administered only once during follow‐up.

Data Collection

Data were collected using the validated Japanese version of the Obstructive Sleep Apnea Questionnaire‐18 (OSA‐18) (Figure S1) [16, 17]. Each item is rated on a 7‐point Likert scale (1 = none, 7 = severe). Although the OSA‐18 scores are intended to assess preoperative and postoperative status, all responses were collected postoperatively; caregivers were asked to retrospectively evaluate the child's preoperative condition. The total and domain‐specific scores were analyzed at both time points. The survey was distributed via Google Forms with a quick‐response (QR) code; an explanatory cover letter was sent to caregivers. Only fully completed responses were included, and no imputation was performed for missing data.

In addition to the OSA‐18, the survey included questions about postoperative complications such as tonsillitis, peritonsillar abscess, and subjective recurrence of OSA symptoms.

Surgical Technique

All patients underwent endoscopic PITA performed by experienced otolaryngologists [12, 13] using a microdebrider with a radenoid blade attached to a 40° curved tip set at 600–800 rpm. A 0° rigid endoscope was used to guide the resection, leaving a residual capsule of the palatine tonsils and removing at least 90% of the tissue. The adenoids were removed using a 70° rigid endoscope with the same powered instrument. Hemostasis of the tonsils and adenoids was achieved using bipolar diathermy following the procedure.

Statistical Analysis

The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to assess normality. Non‐normally distributed data were analyzed using non‐parametric tests. The Mann–Whitney U test was used for two‐group comparisons (e.g., < 5 years vs. ≥ 5 years postoperatively, age subgroups); the Kruskal–Wallis test was employed for three‐group comparisons based on OSA severity.

All statistical tests were two‐sided, with a significance level of p < 0.05. Statistical analyses were performed using Python (version 3.10; Python Software Foundation, Wilmington, DE, USA) and relevant statistical libraries, including SciPy and Pandas.

Results

Patient Characteristics

Of the 122 eligible pediatric patients who underwent surgery, data of 65 were included in the analysis based on the availability of completed caregiver questionnaires and informed consent (Table 1). The median follow‐up period after surgery was 48.0 months (interquartile range [IQR]: 25.0–65.0 months). The median age of the patients was 5 years (IQR: 4.0–7.0 years), with 48 male (73.8%) and 17 female patients (26.2%). Sixty‐four patients underwent preoperative polysomnography (PSG). The median AHI was 9.0 (IQR: 5.0–18.0; n = 60), and the median 3% oxygen desaturation index (ODI) was 27.0 (IQR: 18.6–36.2; n = 3). The duration of postoperative follow‐up was ≥ 5 years in 42 patients (64.6%) and < 5 years in 23 (35.4%).

Stratified Analysis by Follow‐Up Duration

To determine whether the long‐term benefits of PITA could vary by follow‐up duration, the participants were divided into two groups: ≤ 5 years (n = 45) and > 5 years (n = 21); the changes in OSA‐18 scores from the pre‐ to postoperative states were compared. In the < 5 years follow‐up group, the postoperative scores were 1.8, 2.4, 1.8, 2.0, and 1.9 for sleep disturbance, physical symptoms, emotional distress, daytime functioning, and caregiver concerns, respectively (Figure 2). In the ≥ 5 years group, the respective scores were: 1.6, 2.2, 1.6, 2.0, and 1.7. No significant differences were observed between the two groups across all domains (p > 0.05).

Postoperative Complications

Of the 65 respondents, 3 (4.6%) reported postoperative tonsillitis. No peritonsillar abscesses were observed during the follow‐up. Caregivers noted a subjective recurrence of snoring in 10 cases (15.4%), none of which required reoperation.

Discussion

This study demonstrated that endoscopic microdebrider‐assisted PITA is a safe and effective long‐term treatment of pediatric OSA. More than 1 year after surgery, significant improvements were observed in all domains of the OSA‐18 questionnaire, including sleep disturbance, physical symptoms, emotional distress, daytime functioning, and caregiver concerns. These improvements were consistent across different age groups and OSA severity levels and were maintained over extended follow‐up periods.

These findings are consistent with those of previous large‐scale studies. Soaper et al. reported extremely low rates of peritonsillar abscesses (0.2%) and revision surgery (0.12%) over 14 years, supporting the long‐term safety and durability of intracapsular techniques with microdebrider [18]. In our study, no patients experienced peritonsillar abscesses; only three (4.6%) reported postoperative tonsillitis. Although caregivers reported subjective symptom recurrence in nine cases, no patient required reoperation, reinforcing the durability of PITA outcomes. PITA is a relatively new surgical approach compared with traditional extracapsular TE and has been increasingly adopted owing to its minimally invasive nature and improved safety profile. Numerous studies have compared TE with tonsillotomy (partial TE) using various surgical tools [19, 20], including monopolar cautery [21, 22], CO2 laser [23], microdebrider [18, 24, 25], and coblation techniques [26, 27]. Long‐term concerns related to tonsillotomy have primarily centered on two issues: the recurrence of OSA symptoms due to tonsillar regrowth and infection, including recurrent tonsillitis or peritonsillar abscess [22, 26, 28].

The regrowth rates after tonsillotomy significantly vary, from 3% to 73%, depending on the technique and follow‐up duration [18, 22, 25]. In contrast, extracapsular TE typically resulted in a lower rate of regrowth (4.7%–24%). Nevertheless, recent evidence, including the findings from our study, suggests that PITA, particularly when performed endoscopically with precise control, yields a comparably low recurrence and revision rate (0.12%–0.9%) [25, 28]. In our study, although 15.4% of caregivers reported possible symptom recurrence, this was not severe enough to require reoperation, consistent with the findings by Chan et al., where some symptom worsening occurred during long‐term follow‐up despite initial improvement after surgery with coblation [28]. Importantly, our study extended the follow‐up period beyond previous studies, with all participants followed up for over 1 year and up to 8 years, offering a more robust evaluation of long‐term outcomes. Montaha et al. previously reported on OSA‐18 changes post‐TE, reporting a ΔOSA‐18 of 15.14 points at more than 7 months postoperatively [29]. In comparison, our study demonstrated a greater improvement of 42.0 points with a higher standardized response mean, highlighting endoscopic microdebrider‐assisted PITA's potential superiority in QOL gains. In terms of surgical technique, our use of an endoscope is consistent with the literature, suggesting that enhanced intraoperative visualization contributes to more complete yet tissue‐preserving resections. Previous studies using exoscopes and microscopes have emphasized the value of precise dissection and improved hemostasis; our outcomes support the benefit of these visual enhancements [30].

Age‐stratified analysis revealed that children aged ≤ 4 years showed postoperative improvements comparable to those aged > 4 years, particularly in terms of caregiver satisfaction. This suggests that the PITA is a safe and effective option even in younger children, for whom traditional TE is typically approached with caution due to the higher risk of postoperative complications. Although younger age was associated with a higher regrowth risk in previous studies, no significant deterioration or regrowth was observed in this study [25]. Similarly, patients stratified according to OSA severity demonstrated a clinically meaningful postoperative score reduction. Although the magnitude of improvement was slightly lower in the severe group, the difference was not statistically significant, consistent with the findings of Virkkunen et al., who demonstrated that tonsillotomy and TE resulted in comparable improvements in the severity of OSA symptoms and health‐related quality of life [31].

The absence of a peritonsillar abscess in our study, along with a low rate of postoperative tonsillitis (4.6%), suggests that the risk of infection associated with PITA is minimal. This is clinically important because infection‐related complications remain a major concern in post‐TE care, particularly in younger children and those with prior episodes of recurrent tonsillitis. A previous multicenter study reported no increase in the postoperative infection burden following intracapsular TE with coblation, a finding consistent with our results [28]. Furthermore, Soaper et al. found no statistically significant difference in peritonsillar abscess rates between intracapsular and extracapsular techniques, further substantiating the long‐term safety profile of PITA with microdebrider [18]. In our study, endoscopic assistance might have contributed to better intraoperative visualization and a more thorough yet conservative tissue resection, potentially minimizing the presence of residual crypts or infected foci that could otherwise predispose patients to postoperative infection. Tonsillar hypertrophy is associated with chronic bacterial infection and lymphoid hyperplasia [32]. Therefore, the presence or absence of recurrent preoperative tonsillitis may influence the choice of surgical technique; careful long‐term management, including appropriate antimicrobial treatment in the postoperative period, should be considered as a key factor in preventing infectious complications and ensuring optimal outcomes.

This study has some limitations. This was a single‐center, retrospective study relying on caregiver‐reported outcomes; therefore, selection and recall biases remain possible. In particular, both pre‐ and post‐operative OSA‐18 ratings were obtained at a single postoperative time point, with preoperative scores based on caregiver retrospective recall. This approach is practical for long‐term follow‐up but may be influenced by recall and response‐shift biases. An objective assessment of OSA recurrence was not performed through follow‐up PSG. Additionally, comparisons with conventional extracapsular TE were not included, limiting the assessment of relative efficacy. Furthermore, data on postoperative bleeding were not consistently available in the medical records and thus were not analyzed in this study.

In conclusion, our findings suggest that endoscopic microdebrider‐assisted PITA offers durable symptom improvement and a low complication profile in children with OSA regardless of age or baseline severity. This procedure is safe for younger children and offers a viable long‐term alternative to traditional TE. Additional retrospective multicenter studies incorporating objective sleep parameters are warranted to validate these findings.

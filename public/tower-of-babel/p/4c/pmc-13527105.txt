IL‑1β, IL‑10, and TNF‑α gene polymorphisms are associated with cytokine levels and hepatocellular carcinoma risk in a Chinese cohort
Authors: Xiang Yan, Hulin Chang, Jun Hai, Zhiyong Zhang, Yu Zhang, Wei Zheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Chronic hepatic inflammation, driven by factors including viral hepatitis and metabolic dysfunction, is a well-established precursor to hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). The paper aimed to explore the concerted effects of germline polymorphisms in pivotal cytokine genes and their consequent impact on systemic and local inflammatory profiles in HCC pathogenesis. We conducted a hospital-based, retrospective case-control study involving the newly histologically confirmed primary HCC and age- and gender-matched healthy controls. Circulating levels of IL-1β, IL-6, IL-10 and TNF-α were measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Functional single nucleotide polymorphisms in the regulatory regions of their genes were genotyped via PCR- restricted fragment length polymorphism (PCR-RFLP) method. 160 patients and 280 healthy controls were enrolled. HCC patients exhibited significantly elevated serum concentrations of IL-1β, IL-6, IL-10 and TNF-α (all p < 0.05) (mean age: 63.2 ± 7.8 years for cases; 64.0 ± 8.2 years for controls; male ratio: 68.1% for cases, 69.6% for controls). Key circulating cytokine levels were as follows: median IL-1β: 7.5 pg/mL for cases vs. 4.2 pg/mL for controls; IL-6: 18.5 pg/mL for cases vs. 11.3 pg/mL for controls; IL-10: 24.5 pg/mL for cases vs. 19.3 pg/mL for controls; TNF-α: 29.1 pg/mL for cases vs. 21.3 pg/mL for controls. IL-1β -511 C/T, IL-10 -1082 G/A and TNF-α-308 G/A polymorphisms were associated with a significantly increased risk of HCC (all p < 0.05). The frequencies of significant genetic variants were as follows: IL-1β -511 TT genotype: 62.5% in cases vs. 50.3% in controls; adjusted OR = 3.01, 95% CI: 1.65–5.50); IL-10 -1082 G/A AA genotype: 30.0% in cases vs.22.9% in controls; adjusted OR = 2.55, 95% CI: 1.47–4.43; TNF-α -308 G/A AA genotype: 26.8% in cases vs.23.2% in controls; adjusted OR = 1.76, 95% CI: 1.03–3.01. Functional polymorphisms in IL-1β and IL-10 are associated with HCC risk in this Chinese cohort. However, given the cross-sectional design, causal inference cannot be made. Larger prospective studies are needed to validate these findings and clarify the temporal relationship between cytokine dysregulation and HCC development.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-57293-9.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) has imposed a significant burden on the physical health of people all over the world1. Based on GLOBOCAN 2020 data, liver cancer ranks as the sixth most frequently detected malignancy globally and stands as the third primary contributor to cancer-related mortality, accounting for roughly 865,000 new diagnoses and 757,000 deaths each year2. Nearly 45% of these cases occur in China alone, highlighting the critical requirement for efficient risk stratification approaches tailored to this demographic2. While etiologic agents like hepatitis B/C virus infection, aflatoxin exposure, and metabolic syndrome are clearly defined, the precise host factors determining individual susceptibility among those exposed remains incompletely understood3,4. A persistent inflammatory microenvironment is a hallmark of chronic liver disease and a critical driver of hepatocarcinogenesis, facilitating genomic instability, promoting cell survival, and inducing immune evasion5–7.

Inflammatory cytokines are central orchestrators of this tumor-promoting landscape. Interleukin-6 (IL-6), for instance, is a key mediator linking chronic liver injury to HCC, primarily through activation of the STAT3 pathway, which promotes hepatocyte proliferation and inhibits apoptosis8–10. Tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) contributes to hepatocyte damage, regeneration cycles, and ultimately, carcinogenesis11. Polymorphisms located in the promoter or regulatory sequences of these cytokine genes may profoundly influence transcriptional activity, thereby resulting in person-to-person differences in the ability to generate cytokines.

Prior epidemiological studies have often investigated either circulating cytokine levels or specific genetic variants in isolation concerning HCC risk, with results frequently inconsistent across different populations. This heterogeneity may stem from ethnic diversity, variations in environmental triggers or the failure to concurrently assess the functional outcome of genetic variation. Few studies have systematically integrated germline genetic data with corresponding protein expression profiles within the same cohort to evaluate their collective and potentially interactive contribution to HCC development.

Previous investigations examining the link between genetic variants in cytokine genes and susceptibility to HCC have produced conflicting findings, likely due to ethnic heterogeneity, small sample sizes, and differences in etiological background. For instance, the IL-10–1082 A allele has been reported as a risk factor in some Asian populations but as a protective factor in certain Caucasian cohorts12. These discrepancies highlight the need for population-specific data and rigorous methodological standards. The primary objectives of the present work were as follows: (1) to contrast serum concentrations of four distinct cytokines-namely IL-1β, IL-6, IL-10, and TNF-α-between individuals with HCC and healthy subjects within a Chinese population; (2) to investigate the associations of four functional SNPs (IL-1β -511 C/T [rs16944], IL-6 -174 G/C [rs1800795], IL-10 -1082 G/A [rs1800896], and TNF-α -308 G/A [rs1800629]) with HCC risk; (3) to explore whether these genetic variants are associated with circulating cytokine levels.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

Approval for this research was granted by the Ethics Committee of Shaanxi Provincial People’s Hospital(Approval No. SPPH202306). All procedures carried out adhered to the ethical guidelines of the institutional research committee as well as the 1964 Helsinki Declaration and its subsequent revisions.

Study population

It was a hospital-based, retrospective case-control study. Study participants were consecutively recruited from the Department of Hepatobiliary Surgery. The case group comprised 160 patients with newly diagnosed, histologically confirmed primary HCC. The exclusion criteria were: (1) history of other malignancies; (2) presence of autoimmune diseases, systemic inflammatory conditions, or active infections; (3) regular use of immunomodulators or anti-inflammatory drugs within one month prior to enrollment; (4) inability to provide informed consent. The control group consisted of 280 cancer-free individuals matched for age (± 5 years) and gender, recruited from a community health screening program during the same period. Demographic, clinical, and lifestyle data were collected via interviewer-administered questionnaires by trained physicians (two hepatobiliary surgeons, Dr. Xiang Yan and Dr. Hulin Chang). A smoker was defined as an individual who had smoked a minimum of 100 cigarettes over their lifetime. Alcohol use was defined as the intake of at least one alcoholic beverage per week throughout a period of six successive months.

Data and biospecimen collection

Demographic, clinical, and lifestyle data were collected via interviewer-administered questionnaires. For HCC patients, peripheral blood samples were drawn prior to any surgical or therapeutic intervention. For controls, blood was drawn at the time of enrollment. Peripheral venous blood (5 mL) was collected into EDTA-treated vacuum tubes (BD Vacutainer). Serum was separated by centrifugation at 1,500 × g for 15 min at 4 °C within 2 h, aliquoted, and stored at -80 °C.

Measurement of serum cytokine levels

Commercial enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits were used to quantify serum concentrations of IL-1β, IL-6, IL-10, and TNF-α (MultiSciences Biotech Co., Ltd., Hangzhou, China; catalog numbers: EK101B for IL-1β, EK106B for IL-6, EK110B for IL-10, EK182B for TNF-α). All procedures were performed according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, 100 µL of diluted serum samples (1:2 dilution) or standards were added to pre-coated 96-well plates and incubated for 2 h at 37 °C. Following washing, 100 µL of biotinylated detection antibody was introduced and incubated for one hour, after which 100 µL of streptavidin-HRP was applied for 30 min. Subsequently, 100 µL of TMB substrate was added, and the enzymatic reaction was terminated with 50 µL of stop solution. Optical density was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (BioTek ELx800, Winooski, VT, USA). Each specimen was assessed in duplicate, and the average readings were employed for statistical calculations.

DNA extraction and genotyping

A standard phenol-chloroform approach was used to isolate genomic DNA from peripheral blood leukocytes. In brief, 300 µL of whole blood was combined with 900 µL of red blood cell lysis buffer, left to incubate for 10 min at ambient temperature, and then spun at 3,000 × g for 5 min. The resulting pellet was incubated overnight at 56 °C with 20 µL of proteinase K (20 mg/mL) and 200 µL of 10% SDS. DNA was subsequently extracted twice using a phenol: chloroform: isoamyl alcohol mixture (25:24:1), precipitated with 3 M sodium acetate and isopropanol, rinsed with 70% ethanol, and finally resuspended in TE buffer. A NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) was used to evaluate DNA concentration and purity.

On the basis of earlier reports demonstrating transcriptional effects, four functional single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) were chosen for analysis: IL-1β -511 C/T (rs16944), IL-6 -174 G/C (rs1800795), IL-10 -1082 G/A (rs1800896), and TNF-α -308 G/A (rs1800629). Genotyping was carried out using PCR-restriction fragment length polymorphism (PCR-RFLP) on a Veriti 96-well Thermal Cycler (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA). Restriction fragment length polymorphism analysis was conducted with restriction enzymes obtained from New England Biolabs (Ipswich, MA, USA), and the resulting fragments were resolved by electrophoresis on 3% agarose gels (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA) stained with ethidium bromide.

To confirm the accuracy of the genotyping, 10% of the specimens were randomly chosen and reanalyzed by Sanger sequencing (Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China), yielding 100% concordance. Details regarding primer sequences, PCR conditions (including annealing temperatures and cycle numbers), restriction enzymes, and expected fragment sizes are available in Supplementary Table S1.

Statistical analysis

Conventional statistical techniques, namely Student’s t-test, the Mann-Whitney U test, and the chi-square (χ²) test, were employed. To investigate genetic polymorphisms, unconditional logistic regression was applied to calculate odds ratios (ORs) alongside their 95% confidence intervals (CIs), with correction for possible confounding factors. The relationship between genotypes and serum cytokine levels was analyzed using the Kruskal-Wallis test. Interaction effects between genotype and dichotomized cytokine level (high vs. low, based on the 75th percentile in controls) on HCC risk was assessed by including a multiplicative interaction term in the logistic regression model. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 25.0, IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Logistic regression models were adjusted for the following potential confounders: age, sex, smoking status, alcohol consumption, and HBV/HCV infection status. A two-tailed p-value below 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

No marked intergroup differences were observed for smoking status, alcohol intake, hypertension, educational level, or body mass index (all p > 0.05). Similarly, the IL-10 -1082 AA genotype conferred a substantially increased risk (adjusted OR = 2.55, 95% CI: 1.47–4.43, p = 0.002). Associations of SNPs with HCC clinical features

Stratified analysis revealed that the IL-1β -511 TT genotype was more strongly associated with HCC in patients with HBV infection (adjusted OR = 3.45, 95% CI: 1.72–6.91) compared to those without HBV infection (adjusted OR = 2.21, 95% CI: 0.95–5.14).

Cytokine levels stratified by HCC subtype

Serum cytokine levels did not differ significantly between histopathological subtypes (all p > 0.05). However, patients with HBV-related HCC had significantly higher IL-6 levels compared to non-HBV patients (median: 21.3 vs. 14.2 pg/mL, p = 0.018).

Correlation between cytokine levels and clinical features

Spearman correlation analysis revealed modest yet statistically significant positive associations between serum IL-6 concentrations and tumor dimension (r = 0.21, p = 0.008), as well as between IL-1β levels and AFP values (r = 0.18, p = 0.023). No additional notable correlations were identified.

Discussion

The biological plausibility of this association is well-supported by functional studies. Chen et al. demonstrated in luciferase reporter assays that the IL-1β -511 T allele exhibits significantly higher transcriptional activity compared to the C allele, leading to increased IL-1β production13. Zhang et al. further confirmed that individuals carrying the TT genotype have elevated serum IL-1β levels14. In the context of chronic liver disease, through NF-κB activation, IL-1β stimulates hepatocyte proliferation, drives the expression of pro-angiogenic molecules including VEGF, and facilitates immune escape by increasing PD-L1 expression on malignant cells15. A recent meta-analysis reported that the IL-1β -511 TT genotype is linked to a markedly elevated HCC risk in Asian populations, which aligns with our observation16.

The elevation of IL-6 in our HCC cohort aligns with its established role as a master regulator linking chronic liver injury to carcinogenesis. Its production is stimulated by necroinflammation in the liver, and it, in turn, promotes hepatocyte survival and proliferation via STAT3. The protective association of the IL6 rs1800795 C allele, coupled with its correlation with lower IL-6 production, offers a coherent genetic mechanism. This allele has been associated with lower promoter activity and reduced IL-6 expression, which may attenuate the chronic inflammatory drive in the liver, thereby reducing carcinogenic pressure. The observed synergistic interaction between the IL-6 genotype and IL-6 level is a critical finding. It suggests that genetic predisposition sets the basal tone of inflammatory response, which, when compounded by environmental or disease-related factors that further elevate cytokine levels, leads to a multiplicative increase in HCC risk. This underscores the limitation of assessing genetic or phenotypic markers in isolation and highlights the potential of composite biomarkers for identifying a hyper-susceptible subpopulation.

The functional mechanism underlying this association has been characterized by Turner et al., who showed that the IL-10–1082 A allele is associated with lower promoter activity and reduced IL-10 production compared to the G allele17. Koss et al. confirmed these findings in an independent cohort18. Reduced IL-10 production impairs the body’s ability to control chronic inflammation, leading to sustained hepatocyte damage and compensatory proliferation19. In a meta-analysis conducted by Wei and colleagues (2019) comprising 18 studies with 4,521 HCC cases, the IL-10 -1082 AA genotype was identified as a risk factor for HCC among East Asian populations, a result that is consistent with our own data20. Nevertheless, it is worth noting that investigations involving Caucasian cohorts have yielded inconsistent findings, underscoring the necessity for population-specific studies stratified by ethnicity21.

The association of the TNF rs1800629 A allele with increased HCC risk and higher TNF-α levels is also biologically plausible. TNF-α is a key mediator of hepatocyte apoptosis and liver injury. The A allele is linked to enhanced transcriptional activity, potentially leading to more severe and sustained hepatic inflammation in response to insults like viral hepatitis, thereby accelerating the cirrhosis-HCC sequence. The observed ethnic heterogeneity in these associations warrants discussion. A meta-analysis performed by Guo and colleagues (2020) demonstrated that the TNF-α -308 A allele correlates with heightened HCC susceptibility in Asian groups, whereas no such relationship was found in Caucasian populations, suggesting that the effect of this polymorphism may be modified by environmental or genetic background22.

Our study has limitations. Firstly, the case-control design cannot establish causality; elevated cytokines could be a consequence of the tumor. Prospective studies in high-risk cohorts are needed. We focused on candidate SNPs; future genome-wide association studies may uncover novel loci. The sample size, while adequate for detecting strong effects, may be underpowered for weaker associations or subgroup analyses. Generalizability to other ethnic populations requires validation. Secondly, due to the moderate sample size, we did not perform formal interaction analyses between genotype and cytokine levels; as such analyses would be underpowered. Thirdly, the cross-sectional design means we cannot determine whether elevated cytokine levels predate HCC or result from the tumor itself. Prospective studies in at-risk populations are needed to establish temporality. Finally, genetic polymorphism meta-analysis has become a popular research field in the past decade23–31. If this article can discuss and analyze the meta-analysis and the results presented herein, it will greatly enhance the readability and depth of the article.

In conclusion, our data demonstrate that functional polymorphisms in key inflammatory cytokine genes contribute to HCC susceptibility, partly by modulating systemic cytokine levels. The synergistic interaction between genetic makeup and inflammatory phenotype suggests that integrated models combining genetic and serological markers could improve risk prediction in high-risk HCC patients. This approach may help tailor surveillance strategies and identify candidates for future chemoprevention trials targeting specific inflammatory pathways.

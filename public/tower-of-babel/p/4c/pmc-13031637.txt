Enhancing low-cost sensor performance for THD monitoring in smart meters using AI algorithms
Authors: Oubouzid Nacima, Labiod Chouaib, Redha Meneceur, Ali Bebboukha, Abdelmoumene Hechifa, Ievgen Zaitsev
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis research addresses the challenge of accurately measuring total harmonic distortion (THD) in smart meters, where traditional methods, such as FFT-based estimation, often fail to maintain high accuracy, especially when using low-cost sensors. The primary objective is to evaluate the potential of artificial intelligence (AI) techniques to improve THD estimation despite the limitations of economic sensor technology. To achieve this, the study employs a range of AI models, including AdaBoost, Random Forest, Support Vector Machines (SVM), and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), utilizing data initially gathered from high-resolution sensors, which provide accurate reference measurements. Lower-resolution data were then extracted to simulate the performance of economic sensors, which typically offer less precise measurements. Among the AI models tested, AdaBoost consistently outperformed the others in terms of accuracy. In contrast, traditional FFT methods exhibited substantial performance degradation, particularly with low-cost sensors. By applying artificial intelligence, a trained model was developed, where inputs from economic sensors are processed to yield outputs that closely approximate those of high-precision sensors. The results confirm that integrating AI algorithms, especially AdaBoost, improves the performance of low-cost sensors, enabling them to achieve results comparable to high-precision, expensive sensors. This breakthrough not only provides a scalable and cost-effective solution for power quality analysis but also facilitates the development of affordable, high-quality sensors that can be deployed in smart meters without compromising measurement accuracy. The results were also experimentally confirmed in real-world scenarios, further validating the effectiveness of the AI-enhanced approach.Keywords: Total harmonic distortion (THD), Smart meters, AdaBoost, Random forest, Economic sensors, Power quality analysisSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The field of electrical engineering stands at the forefront of technological advancement, playing a pivotal role in our modern lives1. Central to this field are electrical meters and sensors, serving as the linchpin for measuring, monitoring, and managing diverse electrical parameters2. The precision and efficiency of these devices are paramount for ensuring the quality of power distribution, as well as various applications such as manufacturing, environmental monitoring, and consumer electronics3. The efficacy of electrical meters and sensors is further enhanced through sophisticated analytical techniques, notably Total Harmonic Distortion (THD) and Fast Fourier Transform (FFT)4,5.

Electrical meters have been the bedrock of electrical engineering, facilitating the quantification of fundamental electrical parameters such as voltage, current, power factor, and energy consumption6,7. These devices serve as the silent sentinels in our electrical systems, ensuring that power is generated, transmitted, and utilized efficiently8,9. They are pivotal in determining fair billing in consumer environments, and in complex industrial systems, they play a central role in grid monitoring and control10. The burgeoning field of smart meters has elevated this role further by enabling real-time monitoring and control of electrical grids, leading to enhanced energy management and grid stability11,12. Behind the scenes, sensors play an indispensable role in the functioning of electrical meters. A diverse array of sensors, each tailored to specific tasks, contribute to the precision and efficiency of electrical measurements13. Whether they are detecting voltage fluctuations, monitoring current flow, or even measuring environmental parameters like humidity14,15. Sophisticated analytical techniques like THD, FFT and analysis have become invaluable tools for assessing the quality and stability of electrical systems16. THD and FFT analysis are instrumental in identifying and quantifying harmonic distortion in electrical waveforms. This optimization leads to reliable power delivery, reduced energy losses, and longer equipment lifespan17.

Low-cost sensors (LCS) are increasingly being employed for power quality (PQ) monitoring, offering high spatial and temporal resolution at minimal cost. The integration of artificial intelligence algorithms with these sensors further enhances measurement accuracy. However, the reliability of LCS data remains a concern due to various sources of measurement error, and sensor performance may degrade over time, impacting long-term data quality. Consequently, regulatory adoption of LCS for PQ monitoring is still in its early stages. Numerous studies have focused on improving LCS performance18. For example, a low-cost wireless data acquisition system using Raspberry Pi and Arduino platforms was developed to evaluate 75 contactless ranging sensors (HC-SR04, VL53L0X, VL53L1X). The study demonstrated that combining measurements from multiple low-cost sensors can improve accuracy and provided empirical data on standard deviation and error distribution under varying environmental conditions information often lacking in structural system identification research19.

Harmonics metering capability for smart meters have been proposed for monitoring and analysis issues in control cen ters of smart power system20,21. Moreover, technologies based on intelligent systems such as artificial neural networks (ANNs), have been integrated in smart meters, in sensors, and in pulse-width modulation (PWM) drivers as a result of the technological progress of online applications in power systems22,23.highlighting the potential of AI in improving system performance and reliability. Such methods inspire similar integration of intelligent algorithms in other aspects of smart grid systems, including the enhancement of sensor data quality24.Recent research has demonstrated the effectiveness of Tunneling Magnetoresistance (TMR) sensors for current measurement due to their high sensitivity and compact design. To enhance their performance, an improved Grey Wolf Optimizer (ACGWO) was integrated with a backpropagation (BP) neural network, and the NSGA-II algorithm was used for multi-objective structural optimization. This led to the development of a unified ACGWO-BP-NSGA-II framework for optimizing TMR sensor parameters. The optimized sensor was implemented within a complete energy meter design and evaluated for its accuracy in current measurement25.

In this paper, we address the challenge of measuring Total Harmonic Distortion (THD) in smart metering systems, with a particular focus on overcoming the limitations posed by low-cost sensor technology. Traditional methods, such as Fast Fourier Transform (FFT)-based THD estimation, often struggle to maintain accuracy when used with affordable sensors. This highlights the need for innovative solutions that can enhance the performance of affordable sensors without relying on expensive hardware.

To this end, we conducted an applied experiment by performing various measurements using one high-precision sensor and three low-precision, cost-effective sensors, and comparing these results using a prediction method employing artificial intelligence algorithms and the FFT. based on the standards found in IEEE 519, IEC 61000-3-2, and IEC 61000-4-7. For the measurement of PQ and harmonic analysis methods, IEC 61000-4-7 were considered26.

In this research paper, the Fourier transform algorithm is used to extract the distortion of a high-precision sensor and three economical sensors for different signals. In addition, artificial intelligence algorithms are used to obtain the lowest distortion for these economical sensors and then compare them with the traditional method that relies on the Fourier transform alone.This approach achieved high accuracy even when using low-precision, inexpensive sensors, thereby reducing costs through a different strategy: combining artificial intelligence algorithms with the Fourier transform to develop an intelligent and economical meter based on low-cost sensing hardware.

Artificial intelligence (AI) models, such as AdaBoost, random forests, support vector machines, and artificial neural networks, offer a promising solution to this challenge. Our evaluation shows that AI models consistently outperform traditional methods, particularly in environments using low-cost, low-resolution sensors. These AI techniques significantly enhance the accuracy of THD estimation, effectively compensating for the limitations of affordable sensors and achieving performance comparable to that of high-resolution, expensive sensor systems.

Results and discussion

The presented diagram illustrates the flow of the proposed method for estimating the Total Harmonic Distortion (THD’) using artificial intelligence algorithms based on data from economic sensors, as illustrated in Fig. 8. The methodology consists of the following sequential steps:

Proposed AI-based THD’ prediction methodology.

Data collection

The process begins by collecting features from both an ideal reference sensor and three low-cost or economic sensors (Sensor 1, Sensor 2, Sensor 3). These sensors monitor electrical parameters relevant to harmonic analysis.

Feature extraction via FTT

Features obtained from the ideal sensor are processed using the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) to calculate the reference THD’, which serves as the target output for training the AI models.

Input formation

Simultaneously, the features extracted from each economic sensor are grouped to form three distinct input sets (corresponding to THD1, THD2, and THD3). These features are fed into the AI model as inputs.

Artificial intelligence techniques

AI algorithms are trained using the features from economic sensors as inputs and the ideal THD’ as the ground truth (target). This allows the model to learn the mapping between low-cost sensor signals and high-fidelity THD’.

Prediction of THD’

Once trained, the AI model predicts the THD’ based on economic sensor data. This predicted THD’ represents an emulation of the ideal THD’ using cost-effective sensing.

Model evaluation

The final step involves evaluating the model’s performance using standard metrics (e.g., MAE, RMSE, R²) by comparing the predicted THD’ with the actual THD’ obtained from the ideal sensor. This validates the reliability and accuracy of the proposed method.

Performance evaluation of the proposed model results

Experimental results

The experimental validation was conducted using a laboratory test bench specifically designed to evaluate total harmonic distortion (THD) estimation under nonlinear load conditions, as typically encountered in smart metering systems. The objective of the setup is to acquire real current waveforms, extract reference THD values, and assess the performance of AI-based estimation methods when sensor accuracy is degraded.

The overall configuration of the test bench is illustrated in Fig. 15 and includes a nonlinear load, precision measurement instruments, a current sensing and isolation stage, a digital signal controller for data acquisition, and a personal computer for offline data processing and model training.

A nonlinear load is realized using an electronic DC power supply connected to a variable resistive load. Due to the power electronic interface of the DC supply, the current drawn from the AC side exhibits significant harmonic distortion.

The current flowing through the circuit is measured using a differential isolated current sensing circuit based on the AMC1200 isolation amplifier. This device provides galvanic isolation between the power and measurement domains, ensuring both safety and measurement accuracy.

The isolated current signal is acquired by the TMS320F28379D digital signal controller, which is used exclusively as a data acquisition and interfacing unit.

Conclusion

The findings of this research emphasize the transformative potential of artificial intelligence (AI) techniques in enhancing the accuracy of total harmonic distortion (THD) measurements in smart meters, especially when using low-cost sensors. Data for this study were initially gathered from high-resolution sensors, which provided accurate reference measurements. From this dataset, lower-resolution samples were extracted to simulate the limitations of economic sensors, offering a realistic representation of typical sensor performance. By employing AI models such as AdaBoost, RF, SVM, and ANN, this study demonstrated how machine learning algorithms can effectively mitigate the inherent inaccuracies of low-resolution data. As shown in the results, AdaBoost consistently achieved the highest R² values, exceeding 0.9 for all sensor types, while RF, SVM, and ANN also performed well with R² values ranging from 0.85 to 0.9. In terms of error metrics, AdaBoost significantly outperformed other models in reducing RMSE, which is a crucial indicator of the model’s accuracy. While RF, SVM, and ANN also demonstrated good performance, their RMSE values were higher, reflecting a greater error rate compared to AdaBoost. In contrast, FFT-based methods showed considerable performance degradation, particularly with economic sensors, where R² values dropped considerably and RMSE values were much higher, indicating larger errors. AdaBoost, in particular, improved accuracy and reduced error metrics such as RMSE and mean squared error across all sensor types, demonstrating its robustness. This research highlights the ability of AI to compensate for sensor limitations, enabling low-cost sensors to achieve results comparable to high-precision sensors. Furthermore, the use of machine learning algorithms offers a scalable and cost-effective solution for power quality analysis, providing a pathway for the development of affordable, high-quality sensors that can be deployed in smart meters. Ultimately, this approach not only improves the performance of power quality monitoring systems but also holds promise for advancing smart grid applications and enhancing energy management technologies.

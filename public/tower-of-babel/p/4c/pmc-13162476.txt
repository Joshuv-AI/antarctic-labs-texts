Interrelation between lncRNA HOTAIR and EZH2 expression and progression free survival during endocrine treatment in breast cancer
Authors: Zhangzan Huang, Camilla Muritti, Anieta Sieuwerts, Vanja de Weerd, Mieke Timmermans, John WM Martens, Maurice PHM Jansen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeHOTAIR and EZH2 have been identified to interact and to play a role in breast cancer development. In this retrospective clinical cohort study, HOTAIR lncRNA and EZH2 mRNA expression levels were analyzed to assess their combined value as a predictive marker in breast cancer.Patients and MethodsThis retrospective study analyzed 1,465 patients with primary resectable breast cancer treated between 1979 and 1996. HOTAIR expression was measured in fresh frozen primary tumor tissue samples using qRT-PCR. Clinicopathological data were collected according to standardized criteria. Statistical analyses included non-parametric tests, logistic and Cox proportional hazards regression, and Kaplan-Meier curves and log-rank tests for survival outcomes (metastasis-free survival (MFS), progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS)). Two-sided p-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.ResultsHOTAIR and EZH2 expression were both significantly associated with ER and HER2 status, and EZH2 also with age, histological features, and PR status. In 270 ER+/HER2- patients who received tamoxifen as first line treatment, higher expression of each HOTAIR and EZH2 were significantly associated with PFS, meanwhile, the combination of these two associated more strongly with PFS. In treatment-naïve lymph node-negative patients with ER+/HER2- tumors, higher HOTAIR expression was associated with worse MFS and OS with the tertile threshold.ConclusionThis study suggests that HOTAIR may have predictive value for tamoxifen resistance in ER+/HER2 − breast cancer, both independently and in combination with EZH2. Introduction

Breast cancer represents a major global health challenge, impacting millions of people worldwide annually [1]. Considering its heterogeneity, breast cancer is currently classified into four subtypes: Luminal A, Luminal B, HER2-enriched and triple negative breast cancer (TNBC) based on the expression of the estrogen receptor (ER), the progesterone receptor (PgR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) [2]. Each subtype’s phenotype is determined by a complex interplay of (epi)genetic, familial, and hormonal factors determining its prognosis, and response to treatment [3].

Among these subtypes, the luminal breast cancer subtype that is ER-positive (ER+) and HER2 negative (HER2-) is particularly significant since it accounts for 70–80% of all breast cancer cases [4]. Luminal breast cancers activate gene transcription and downstream pathways including proliferation upon binding of estrogen to the ER [5]. As a result endocrine therapy is and remains the standard treatment for ER+/HER2- breast cancer. Tamoxifen, a front-line endocrine therapy, inhibits ER transcriptional activity by competing with estrogen for ER binding blocking its activation, thereby inhibiting tumor proliferation [6, 7]. In the adjuvant setting, treatment with tamoxifen has shown to improve 10-year survival rates by 5.3% in lymph node-negative (LNN) patients and up to 12.6% in lymph node-positive (LNP) patients [7]. Despite these improvements, however, tamoxifen and endocrine therapy in general becomes ineffective eventually for nearly 50% of patients with recurrent ER+ breast cancer, allowing disease progression [8]. Resistance, which can be either intrinsic (primary) or acquired over time (secondary), is clinically associated with an adverse outcome [9]. Understanding endocrine therapy resistance and/or discovering biomarkers predicting adverse outcome to endocrine therapy is essential for improving patient care.

HOTAIR (HOX transcript antisense intergenic RNA) is a long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) involved in various cellular processes and modulates chromatin remodeling and other epigenetic changes by serving as a scaffold for protein complexes such as polycomb repressive complex 2 (PRC2) and LSD1, thereby promoting H3K27 methylation and H3K4 demethylation [10]. It also functions as a competitive endogenous RNA, influencing HER2 expression and interacting with microRNAs [11]. Acting as an oncogene, HOTAIR silences tumor suppressor genes through EZH2 mediated histone modifications and is implicated in multiple cancer biological processes, including disease progression, metastasis and drug and radiation resistance [12].

Mechanistically, downregulation of HOTAIR has shown to decrease tamoxifen-resistant breast cancer cell growth, suggesting that tamoxifen resistance at least in part can be reverted through HOTAIR inhibition [13]. Consistently, HOTAIR knockdown impairs the colony-forming ability of tamoxifen-resistant cells, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target [13]. Previously, we showed that high levels of EZH2, a key element of the PRC2 complex, is associated with poor outcome to tamoxifen [14, 15]. Recent studies have shown that HOTAIR and EZH2 functionally interact in cell line models, contributing to several biological process, including transcriptional repression of the promoter of TP53 gene [16], the induction of resistance to tamoxifen [17] and chemotherapy [18]. In addition, the expression HOTAIR and EZH2 when used in combination carried prognostic information in lung adenocarcinoma [19].

While preclinical findings underscore the potential role of HOTAIR and EZH2 in endocrine therapy resistance, their (combined) clinical relevance in breast cancer remains to be evaluated. To address the gap, we analyzed in this study the lncRNA expression of HOTAIR alone and combined with EZH2 levels in primary tumor biopsies from a large retrospective cohort. The objectives of this study were to explore HOTAIR alone and combined with EZH2, respectively, in ER+/HER2- breast cancer to assess their performances as (i) predictive markers for outcome to first line tamoxifen treatment in patients with recurrent disease, and (ii) prognostic markers in (neo)adjuvant treatment naïve lymph node negative (LNN) patients.

Materials & methods

Patients

This retrospective study analyzed a cohort of 1,683 patients diagnosed with primary resectable breast cancer at Erasmus MC (Rotterdam, the Netherlands) between 1979 and 1996. Coded, leftover patient material was used in accordance with the Code of Conduct of the Federation of Medical Scientific Societies in the Netherlands (https://www.coreon.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/coreon-code-of-conduct-english.pdf). According to these national guidelines, this work was not subject to the Medical Research Involving Human Subjects Act (WMO; METC 02.593). Relevant clinical data were collected, including patient age, tumor size, histological grade and subtypes, lymph node status, and expression of the ER, PgR, and HER2. Clinical and pathological data were defined according to the standard classification criteria of the International Union Against Cancer (Geneva, Switzerland) as reported previously Foekens et al. [20, 21].

To ensure data integrity and reliability, strict inclusion criteria were implemented: (i) tumor samples containing more than 30% epithelial tumor cells in stained sections; (ii) availability of high-quality RNA suitable for analysis [22]; (iii) measured levels of HOTAIR lncRNA and EZH2 mRNA expression; (iv) complete clinical data, including detailed treatment history and follow-up information. Patients who did not meet these inclusion criteria or had missing lymph node status data, or presented with distant metastases at diagnosis were excluded. Applying these criteria excluded 218 patients from further analyses and resulted in a cohort of 1,465 patients (Table 1). Breast tumors were classified as ER-, PgR-, and/or HER2-positive if mRNA levels exceeded a cutoff of 0.2, 0.1, and 18.01, respectively [14, 23, 24]. This resulted in the cohort of 1465 patients in 1108 ER+, 886 PgR+, and 230 HER2 + tumors. Detailed survival analyses were performed on ER+/HER2- tumors from treatment naïve early disease patients and from patients with metastatic disease treated with first-line tamoxifen.

HOTAIR, EZH2 and clinicopathological characteristics in all patients

Among these 1,465 patients, 664 patients (45%) underwent breast-conserving lumpectomy, while 801 patients (55%) underwent modified mastectomy. The median follow-up time for the entire cohort was 84.31 months (range: 2.46 to 341.60 months). In total, 888 (60%) did not receive adjuvant systemic therapy and were classified as treatment- naïve. The total patient cohort had 825 (56%) lymph node-negative (LNN) patients, while 640 patients (44%) were lymph node-positive (LNP). Additionally, 429 (29%) patients were both ER + and HER2-, with 121 (8%) classified as treatment-naïve. Among LNN ER+ patients (n = 605, 42%), 521 (36%) were treatment-naïve, and of these 605 patients, 530 (36%) were ER+/HER2-, with 456 (31%) being treatment-naïve.

A total of 321 ER+ patients (22%) experienced relapse and were subsequently treated with first-line tamoxifen monotherapy. Of these, 270 (18%) patients were ER+/HER2-, with 187 (13%) classified as treatment-naïve for early disease. Of the 321 relapsed patients, therapy failure occurred in 113 (35%), with 87 (77%) experiencing progressive disease and 26 (23%) showing stable disease for less than six months. The remaining 208 patients (65%) derived clinical benefit from first-line tamoxifen monotherapy. Among them, 9 patients (4%) achieved a complete response, 48 (23%) had a partial response, and 151 (73%) maintained stable disease for more than six months.

RNA isolation, cDNA preparation and qRT-PCR

Primary tumor samples were analyzed for their HOTAIR expression with RNA isolated from fresh frozen primary tumor tissue samples. Tissue processing, RNA isolation, complementary DNA (cDNA) synthesis, and qRT-PCR were performed as previously described by Sieuwerts et al. [22]. Gene expression assays from Thermo Fisher Scientific were used to evaluate the expression of HOTAIR (Hs03296631_m1) and of EZH2 (Hs00544830_m1) as previously described [14]. The HOTAIR assay generates 152 bp amplicons covering exons 1 to 2, while the EZH2 assay generates 86 bp amplicons covering exons 9 to 10. In summary, total RNA was extracted using RNABee (Campro, Veenendaal, the Netherlands), and RNA quality was assessed by UV spectroscopy, evaluation of rRNA band integrity on agarose gels, and amplification efficiency of housekeeping genes in a serial dilution, following Sieuwerts et al. [22]. Reverse transcription of 5 µg of total RNA was performed using Superscript II RNase H⁻ (Invitrogen, Breda, the Netherlands), followed by RNase H⁻ treatment to remove residual RNA. qRT-PCR was conducted using the ABI Prism 7700 Sequence Detection System (Applied Biosystems). To ensure accurate quantification of HOTAIR lncRNA and EZH2 mRNA, expression levels were normalized using the average of three housekeeping genes: PBGD, HPRT, and β2M. The primer sequences of these reference genes were described previously [22]. Gene expression levels were calculated using the ΔCt method, defined as 2^(mean Ct of housekeeping genes – Ct of HOTAIR lncRNA or EZH2 mRNA).

Data analysis and statistics

The relationship between HOTAIR lncRNA and EZH2 mRNA expression levels and patient or tumor characteristics was assessed using non-parametric tests, including the Wilcoxon rank-sum test or Kruskal-Wallis test for ordered variables.

Survival outcomes, including metastasis-free survival (MFS), progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) were analyzed using Cox proportional hazards regression, with hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) quantifying the relative risk of events between groups. The expression levels of HOTAIR and EZH2 were were log10-transformed to normalize their distribution. The normalized HOTAIR and EZH2 levels were then evaluated as a continuous variable, as well as a categorical variable to interpret data distributions. These normalized HOTAIR and EZH2 levels were then evaluated as a continuous variable for the different patient subsets, as well as categorical variable for the two specified patient subsets to interpret data distributions. For categorical variables, HOTAIR and EZH2 levels were grouped by tertiles or by median and compared two groups: the lowest tertile versus the combined intermediate and highest tertiles and below versus above median expression levels.

MFS, PFS, and OS were previously defined as follows: MFS was measured from the surgical removal of the primary tumor to the first detection of distant metastatic disease and OS was measured from the surgical removal to death from any cause [14]. If cases were lost during follow-up they were censored at last follow-up. PFS was measured from the time of the initiation of first line tamoxifen therapy for recurrent disease to the first detection of disease progression after the start of treatment [25]. Categorized HOTAIR and EZH2 mRNA expression were also analyzed using Kaplan-Meier survival curves to visualize survival differences and to test for differences using the log-rank test from R Studio (R 4.5.2). Statistical significance was considered at p < 0.05 (two-sided). Cox analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics (Version 28.0), and aimed to evaluate the prognostic and predictive relevance of HOTAIR expression alone and combined with EZH2 levels in relation to the clinical outcome measures (MFS, PFS and OS).

Results

Association of HOTAIR with clinicopathologic factors

HOTAIR lncRNA levels were measured by qRT-PCR in 1465 primary breast tumors; for EZH2 previously reported mRNA expression levels from the same cohort were reused [14]. Their expression levels were evaluated in different patient subsets (Fig. 1). The manuscript will focus on two clinically relevant ER+/HER2- cohorts to assess the relationship with disease progression during endocrine treatment (PFS) and with disease outcome in general (MFS and OS). Table 1 shows the median expression level and the interquartile range of HOTAIR and EZH2 and their relationship with patient and tumor characteristics. ER+ tumors had significantly lower HOTAIR and EZH2 levels compared to ER- tumors (p = 0.012, p < 0.001). HER2 + tumors exhibited a profound higher expression of particularly HOTAIR but also EZH2 compared to HER2- tumors (both p < 0.001). No associations were observed between HOTAIR expression and other clinical characteristics, while EZH2 levels being inversely related with age (p = 0.042; Table S1) and lower in invasive lobular versus ductal carcinoma.

HOTAIR and EZH2 expression and PFS to first line tamoxifen treatment

To investigate the association between HOTAIR and EZH2 expression and outcome to tamoxifen, we analyzed PFS outcomes in 270 ER+/HER2- patients, who received tamoxifen as first line therapy for advanced disease. When treated as a continuous variable, HOTAIR expression was significantly associated with PFS (log rank p < 0.001). To further explore this relationship, patients were stratified into low and high expression groups using the low tertile value as a threshold. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis revealed that patients with high HOTAIR expression had a significantly shorter PFS compared to those with low expression (Fig. 2A, log rank p = 0.035). To assess the independent predictive value of HOTAIR, a Cox proportional hazard regression model was applied. In univariate analysis, high HOTAIR expression was significantly associated with reduced PFS (hazard ratio (HR) = 1.33, 95% CI = 1.02–1.75, p = 0.036). Importantly, this association remained significant in the multivariate Cox regression model after adjusting for other known clinical predictive variables (HR = 1.41, 95% CI = 1.07–1.88, p = 0.015), indicating that HOTAIR functions as an independent predictive factor of PFS in the context of first line tamoxifen treatment in ER+/HER2- breast cancer cases with advanced disease (Table 2). However, continuous HOTAIR expression demonstrated only marginal significance in multivariate Cox regression. Therefore, categorization into tertiles should considered an exploratory analysis.

Cox univariate and multivariate analyses for PFS in ER+/HER2- patients with recurrence treated with first-line tamoxifen monotherapy

With the functional interaction between HOTAIR and EZH2 shown by others and the correlations observed between both in our cohort we also included EZH2 as a variable to enhance our understanding. Using the median value as a threshold for EZH2, we subgrouped patients into low and high expression group. Kaplan-Meier curves demonstrated that higher EZH2 expression was associated to poor PFS (Figure S1, log rank p < 0.001), and the univariate and multivariate Cox regression proved its independent value as a predictor. We combined the expression of HOTAIR and EZH2 to classify these patients into four groups: low HOTAIR-low EZH2, high HOTAIR-low EZH2, low HOTAIR-high EZH2 and high HOTAIR-high EZH2. The Kaplan–Meier analysis suggested that patients with low expression of HOTAIR and EZH2 were associated with a longer PFS compared to all other groups (Fig. 2B, log rank p < 0.001). The analysis suggested that the patients of the group with both HOTAIR and EZH2 having low expression do well on first-line tamoxifen treatment. In Cox regression analysis, all groups with at least one of the 2 variables high are independent of current predictors associated with shorter PFS compared to those with having low expression of both HOTAIR and EZH2 (Table 2). Putting HOTAIR and EZH2 together in a Cox regression analysis for PFS showed that only EZH2 retained significance, indicating that EZH2 in this setting is the more relevant marker than HOTAIR (Table S2).

HOTAIR levels and tumor aggressiveness and overall survival

To evaluate the relationship between HOTAIR and tumor aggressiveness (MFS and OS), we focused on (neo)adjuvant systemic treatment-naïve ER+/HER2- patients (Fig. 1), particularly those with LNN disease, since in the Netherlands at that time cases with LNN disease did not receive any systemic treatment allowing us, in this historic cohort, to associate markers purely with prognosis.

In the LNN ER+/HER2- patient cohort (n = 456), patients dichotomized based on HOTAIR expression, again using the low tertile value as a threshold, demonstrated an association with MFS and OS (log rank, p < 0.001). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated that high HOTAIR expression was significantly associated with shorter MFS and OS (Fig. 3, log rank, p = 0.022, p = 0.027). However, when treated as a continuous variable in a Cox regression model, HOTAIR did not reach statistical significance, suggesting that the relationship between its expression and survival may not be fully linear. In the univariate Cox regression model, dichotomized HOTAIR expression showed a significant association with poor prognosis (MFS and OS) which remained significant in the multivariable Cox regression model-after adjusting for established clinical covariates such as age, tumor stage, grade and T stage (Tables 3 and 4). When we conducted Cox regression analysis with EZH2, no prognostic value was noticed in line with our earlier report [14]. In order to comprehensively understand if the prognostic value of HOTAIR only presents in ER+/HER2-, TNBC patients with LNN were also analyzed. Whether analyzed as continuous or dichotomized expression neither HOTAIR nor EZH2 showed an association to MFS or OS (supplementary Tables 3 & 4).

Discussion

This study aimed to evaluate the potential of HOTAIR as a predictive and prognostic marker in luminal breast cancer. Our findings offer important insights into the biological and clinical relevance of HOTAIR as an independent biomarker for outcome to tamoxifen treatment when analyzed as single biomarker but also when combined with EZH2. I.e. high expression of either HOTAIR or EZH2 in primary tissue indicates a short PFS while receiving first line tamoxifen therapy for advanced disease. Of note, the separation of PFS curves for high and low HOTAIR only occurred after 12 months, suggesting this marker pinpoints a biological effect that occurs late and thus potentially relates to acquired rather than intrinsic resistance. In addition, our results also suggest prognostic relevance of HOTAIR but that this is better captured through stratified expression levels rather than as a continuous variable, suggested by exploratory tertile analysis. Nonetheless, our results underscore the potential utility of EZH2 as a clinically actionable biomarker for risk stratification in ER+/HER2- breast cancer.

HOTAIR is an oncogenic lncRNA that is linked to breast cancer progression, including metastasis, drug resistance, immune evasion, and EMT, partly via Wnt/β-catenin and mTOR signaling [12, 26]. High HOTAIR expression was positively correlated to high expression of EZH2 in our data and both are highly expressed in ER- tumors compared to ER+ tumors. Consistently, HOTAIR is found inversely regulated by ER signaling [27] and EZH2 is reported to negatively regulate ER [14]. However, in tamoxifen resistant cases, researchers have shown that HOTAIR expression is high [13] which suggests the increased HOTAIR observed in tamoxifen resistance is acquired independent of ER signaling [17]. However, this remains to be shown mechanistically. In the general in ER+ breast cancers which are considered low HOTAIR and/or EZH2 expressors, we hypothesize that elevated HOTAIR and EZH2 may activate ER proliferative signaling in noncanonical way or entirely independent of ER, adversely affecting therapy response and prognosis. In addition, HOTAIR is also reported to participate in c-MYC action. HOTAIR functions as a scaffold for c-MYC, which in turn binds to the HOTAIR promoter to activate its transcription [28, 29]. Notably, c-MYC has also been implicated in promoting resistance to endocrine therapy [30]. Considering the physical interaction of HOTAIR with EZH2, the latter which has been reported previously by us [14] to predict tamoxifen failure, we grouped our patients into different subgroups with increasing expression of HOTAIR and/or EZH2, also prominently predicting poor outcome to tamoxifen therapy. When HOTAIR and EZH2 were included into multivariate analysis, EZH2 remained significant while HOTAIR not, indicating that these two factors are confounded. With the consensus that lncRNAs are hard to target, our findings support the potential of targeting HOTAIR-EZH2 axis as a therapeutic strategy for ER+/HER2- breast cancer as well. Interestingly, specific HOTAIR-EZH2 inhibitors have been found to effectively inhibit growth in TNBC cells [31, 32]. In ER- cases, estrogens increase HOTAIR expression through GPER (G-protein-coupled estrogen receptor-1), bypassing classical ER signaling. Instead of directly targeting the HOTAIR promoter, estrogens suppresses miR-148a, a microRNA that normally binds and inhibits HOTAIR, thereby leading to elevated HOTAIR levels [33]. To note, previous study suggested HOTAIR a prognostic biomarker to ER- breast cancers [34]. These findings suggest that while HOTAIR may be broadly upregulated in more aggressive subtypes, its functional impact may differ depending on the cellular context and molecular landscape, including the presence or absence of ER signaling.

Furthermore, HER2 was found as an activator for HOTAIR expression via mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) [35], supporting this, our analysis revealed that HER2 + patients had significantly higher HOTAIR expression. The lack of association between HOTAIR expression and conventional clinicopathological features, such as age, menopausal status, tumor size, lymph node involvement, and tumor grade, suggests that HOTAIR may reflect underlying biological processes not fully captured by traditional clinical parameters.

Tumor aggressiveness, as measured by MFS and OS, was consistently associated with HOTAIR levels ER+/HER2 patients with LNN disease, an association which was not seen in TNBC patients with LNN disease. Thus, next to predicting outcome during endocrine treatment, HOTAIR levels are also directly linked to tumor aggressiveness, indicating that HOTAIR may increase tumor metastasis rate and as a result negatively influences overall survival. This underscores HOTAIR’s potential as a biomarker not only for treatment resistance but also for prognosis, offering opportunities for improved risk stratification. Notably, our data demonstrated that the prognostic value of HOTAIR became apparent only when patients were stratified into tertiles based levels, rather than when HOTAIR was analyzed as a continuous variable. One possibility is that the relationship between HOTAIR expression and patient outcome was nonlinear, with prognostic significance manifesting only beyond specific expression thresholds. Since EZH2 expression has been linked to proliferation, we tested whether EZH2 and HOTAIR expression prediction MFS and OS independent of Ki67, an established proliferation marker [36, 37]. We found that both EZH2 and HOTAIR where associated with MFS/OS independent of Ki67 mRNA expression (results not shown).

Despite these promising findings, our study has limitations. The retrospective design introduces potential biases and limits the control over treatment heterogeneity. The HOTAIR was not normally distributed, which is not ideal for Cox regression analysis. Our study is a discovery study conducted in a single center, using an historical cohort in which tumor sizes were generally larger than those observed today. Finally, external validation is warranted to confirm the reliability of our results.

In conclusion, our study provides strong evidence that HOTAIR is a clinically meaningful biomarker associated with tumor aggressiveness, poor prognosis, and tamoxifen resistance in ER+/HER2- breast cancer. Future research should prioritize prospective validation of HOTAIR and EZH2 as clinical biomarkers. Novel therapeutic strategies targeting HOTAIR and EZH2, such as inhibitors that disrupt the HOTAIR-PRC2 interaction [31, 38], could provide new treatment options for patients with endocrine-resistant breast cancer. Mechanistic studies dissecting the interplay between HOTAIR, EZH2, ER signaling, chromatin remodeling, and resistance pathways will be critical to fully understanding its clinical potential.

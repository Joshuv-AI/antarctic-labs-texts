Clinical, Radiographic and Patient‐Centred Outcomes of MINST Versus MIST With Enamel Matrix Derivative in Class II Mandibular Furcation Defects: A Randomised Controlled Trial
Authors: Georgios S Chatzopoulos, Eleftherios G Kaklamanos, Ioannis Vouros, Georgios Mikrogeorgis, Aikaterini‐Elissavet Doufexi, Lazaros Tsalikis
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAimsTo evaluate whether minimally invasive non‐surgical therapy (MINST) with flapless application of enamel matrix derivative (EMD) achieves non‐inferior clinical and radiographic outcomes compared to minimally invasive surgical technique (MIST) + EMD, while improving patient‐reported outcome measures (PROMs).Materials and MethodsFollowing CONSORT guidelines, 40 patients with mandibular Grade II furcation defects were randomised to MINST (n = 20) or MIST (n = 20) with EMD. Probing pocket depth (PPD), clinical attachment level (CAL) and cone‐beam computed tomography (CBCT) horizontal/vertical bone loss were assessed at baseline and 9 months. PROMs (visual analogue score [VAS] and Oral Health Impact Profile [OHIP‐14]) were recorded longitudinally.ResultsAt 9 months, the mean PPD reduction was 2.20 ± 2.24 mm in the MIST group and 2.15 ± 1.76 mm in the MINST group. The adjusted mean difference was −0.22 mm (95% CI: −1.43 to 0.99 mm). The one‐sided non‐inferiority p‐value was 0.025. As the upper bound of the confidence interval remained below the prespecified non‐inferiority margin, MINST was considered non‐inferior to MIST for the primary outcome. When evaluated against the strict +1.0 mm margin, MINST achieved statistical non‐inferiority for gingival recession (p = 0.003) and vertical bone gain (p = 0.015). However, non‐inferiority could not be formally established for CAL (p = 0.067) or horizontal bone gain (p = 0.157). PROMs revealed significantly lower root hypersensitivity and improved oral‐health‐related quality of life in the MINST group at 2 weeks; however, these differences were not sustained at later timepoints.ConclusionsWithin the limitations of this randomised controlled trial, MINST with EMD showed clinical and radiographic outcomes generally comparable to MIST in mandibular Class II furcation defects. While non‐inferiority against a strict 1.0 mm margin was successfully established for the primary outcome (PPD) and specific secondary parameters, it could not be formally confirmed for CAL or horizontal bone gain. MINST was associated with significantly improved short‐term PROMs during the early healing phase.Trial Registration ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT05541614Keywords: cone‐beam computed tomography, enamel matrix derivative, furcation defects, patient‐reported outcome measures, periodontal regeneration

Introduction

The predictable management of periodontal furcation involvement remains one of the most significant challenges in clinical periodontology. Teeth exhibiting inter‐radicular bone loss historically has shown a disproportionately higher risk of disease progression and eventual tooth loss compared to single‐rooted teeth (Nibali et al. 2016). Traditionally, therapeutic access to the anatomically complex furcation environment necessitated extensive surgical flap reflection. Over the past two decades, the paradigm has shifted towards minimally invasive surgical techniques (MISTs), which preserve soft‐tissue architecture, enhance blood clot stability and optimise the regenerative potential of biological factors such as enamel matrix derivative (EMD) (Cortellini and Tonetti 2015; Casarin et al. 2010).

While MIST has proven highly effective in yielding clinical attachment gain and inter‐radicular bone fill, any surgical incision and subsequent periosteal elevation inherently trigger an acute inflammatory cascade (Barbato et al. 2020; Sun et al. 2025; Simonelli et al. 2023). This surgical trauma routinely results in early post‐operative morbidity, root hypersensitivity and a temporary, yet significant, decline in the patient's oral‐health‐related quality of life (OHRQoL) (Saito et al. 2010; Griffin et al. 2006; Chen et al. 2024). In modern clinical practice, therapeutic success is increasingly evaluated through a bio‐psychosocial lens, balancing clinical efficacy against patient‐centred benefits (Tonetti et al. 2017).

Advances in micro‐instruments and visualisation have accelerated the development of minimally invasive non‐surgical therapy (MINST) (Nibali et al. 2015). By delivering meticulous subgingival instrumentation and regenerative biological factors into the periodontal pocket without flap elevation, MINST aims to eliminate surgical trauma entirely. While preliminary evidence supports the efficacy of MINST in vertical intraosseous defects (Barbato et al. 2020), its specific application in complex furcation morphology remains largely underexplored. Furthermore, there is a lack of robust randomised controlled trials (RCTs) directly comparing the clinical efficacy and patient‐centred benefits of MIST + EMD versus MINST + flapless EMD in furcation defects.

Therefore, the primary aim of this investigation was to evaluate whether a flapless MINST approach with EMD is clinically and radiographically non‐inferior to the established MIST approach for the treatment of mandibular Grade II furcation defects. The secondary aim was to evaluate the hypothesis that MINST results in superior short‐term patient‐reported outcome measures (PROMs) by significantly reducing post‐operative morbidity and preserving OHRQoL.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Ethical Considerations

This prospective, parallel‐group, assessor‐ and statistician‐blinded, non‐inferiority RCT with a margin of 1 mm in probing pocket depth (PPD) was conducted in adherence to the Declaration of Helsinki and reported according to the CONSORT guidelines (Schulz et al. 2010). The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the School of Dentistry, the Aristotle University of Thessaloniki (Reference No. 155/14‐03‐2022), and prospectively registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05541614). All participants provided written informed consent prior to enrolment. A detailed description of the study methodology is available in Supporting Information S1 (Detailed methodology).

Study Population

Participants were recruited from October 2022 to June 2025. Eligibility criteria included the following: systemically healthy adults (≥ 18 years); a diagnosis of Stage III or IV periodontitis (Tonetti et al. 2018); and the presence of one vital mandibular first or second molar exhibiting a Grade II furcation defect (horizontal probing depth ≥ 3 mm, vertical PPD > 4 mm) (Hamp et al. 1975). All included Grade II furcations were categorised as subclass B. To account for the impact of adjacent bone support on regenerative potential, interproximal clinical attachment levels (CAL) relative to the furcation entrance were assessed and recorded at baseline.

Patients were required to adhere to optimal oral hygiene (full‐mouth plaque score [FMPS] ≤ 20%) following initial non‐surgical periodontal therapy. Exclusion criteria included the following: smoking > 10 cigarettes/day, pregnancy, systemic antibiotic use within 3 months, untreated endodontic infections and teeth exhibiting Grade II or III mobility.

Sample Size and Randomisation

The sample size calculation was based on the primary endpoint of change in PPD at 9 months, analysed as a between‐group difference in mean values. The study was designed as a non‐inferiority trial with a prespecified margin of 1.0 mm. Sample size estimation was performed a priori using a two‐sample comparison of means, assuming a standard deviation of 1.0 mm (Casarin et al. 2010), 90% power and a significance level of 5%. This approach represents a conservative and widely accepted method for planning non‐inferiority trials. Adjustment methods such as ANCOVA were not used for sample size estimation but were applied during analysis to improve precision.

Accounting for a 20% attrition rate, 44 participants (22 per group) were randomised. A 1:1 allocation sequence was generated via a web‐based randomisation service (Sealed Envelope Ltd., London, United Kingdom) with strict allocation concealment using sealed, opaque envelopes opened only immediately prior to intervention. Due to the distinct nature of the interventions (surgical vs. flapless), blinding of the clinical operators and the patients was not possible. However, blinding was maintained for the clinical examiner who recorded all outcomes (G.S.C.), as well as for the trial statistician.

Clinical Interventions

Following local anaesthesia (2% lidocaine, 1:100,000 epinephrine), patients received one of two interventions:

Control group (MIST): A conservative intra‐crevicular incision was made, and a minimal buccal/lingual flap was reflected specifically to visualise the furcation entrance, using papilla preservation techniques where applicable. Granulation tissue and calculus were removed using piezoelectric ultrasonic devices with thin Perio (P) tips (EMS, Nyon, Switzerland) and mini Gracey curettes. The root surface was conditioned with 24% EDTA gel (PrefGel, Straumann) for 2 min, rinsed and dried. EMD (Emdogain, Straumann) was applied to the defect. The flap was repositioned and secured with 4/0 polyglycolic acid sutures.

The flap was repositioned and secured with 4/0 polyglycolic acid sutures.

Test group (MINST): Access to the furcation was achieved entirely through the periodontal pocket without flap reflection. Blind subgingival debridement was executed using identical ultrasonic P‐tips and mini curettes. EDTA conditioning was performed subgingivally for 2 min. All patients were prescribed 0.12% chlorhexidine rinses twice daily for 2 weeks. No routine systemic analgesics were prescribed to avoid influencing patient‐reported outcomes. Sutures in the MIST group were removed at 14 days. All clinical interventions and subsequent follow‐up examinations were performed at the Graduate Periodontology Clinic of the Aristotle University of Thessaloniki.

Clinical and Radiographic Assessment

A single calibrated, blinded examiner assessed all clinical parameters at baseline (T0) and 9 months (T9) using a UNC‐15 and a curved Nabers probe. Measurements included PPD, CAL, gingival recession (REC), bleeding on probing (BOP) and horizontal and vertical furcation dimensions.

High‐resolution cone‐beam computed tomography (CBCT) (NewTom 5 G; voxel size 0.125 mm) was performed at T0 and T9 in accordance with the ‘As Low As Reasonably Achievable’ (ALARA) principle (Kim and Bassir 2017). DICOM files were imported into the 3D Slicer software (Surgical Planning Laboratory, Boston, USA). After standardising spatial orientation to the cemento‐enamel junction (CEJ) and the root's long axis, exact linear measurements for horizontal bone loss (BL‐H), vertical bone loss (BL‐V), root trunk length and furcation width were quantified (Yusof et al. 2021).

Patient‐Reported Outcome Measures

Subjective morbidity (pain, discomfort, oedema, haematoma, root hypersensitivity, and interference with daily activity) was evaluated using a 100‐mm visual analogue scale (VAS) longitudinally. OHRQoL was assessed using the validated 14‐item Oral Health Impact Profile (OHIP‐14) (Papagiannopoulou et al. 2012), administered at baseline, 2 weeks, 3 months, 6 months and 9 months post treatment.

Statistical Analysis

The primary endpoint, namely the 9‐month reduction in PPD, was evaluated under a non‐inferiority framework using a complete‐case (per‐protocol) analysis. Non‐inferiority of MINST was concluded if the upper bound of the two‐sided 95% confidence interval for the mean difference (MIST − MINST) remained below a predefined +1.0 mm margin. This primary comparison was calculated using an analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) model adjusting for baseline PPD, smoking status and root trunk length. For all secondary continuous clinical and radiographic outcomes, comparisons were assessed using one‐sided testing to evaluate non‐inferiority against the same predefined +1.0 mm margin used for the primary PPD outcome. Conversely, to evaluate the hypothesis that the flapless approach provides an improved patient experience, PROMs were assessed using standard two‐sided superiority tests, including linear mixed‐effects models (LMM) for longitudinal data. Predictors of clinical outcomes and successful furcation closure were identified using multivariable ANCOVA and logistic regression, respectively. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05 without formal adjustment for multiple secondary comparisons.

Results

Participant Flow and Baseline Characteristics

The progression of participants through each phase of the trial—including screening, allocation, follow‐up and final analysis—is detailed in the CONSORT flow diagram (Figure 1). A total of 44 patients satisfying the eligibility criteria were randomised. Over the 9‐month follow‐up, four patients discontinued the intervention or were lost to follow‐up, and the analyses were conducted on available cases. Of the four patients who discontinued or were lost to follow‐up, two withdrew for reasons unrelated to the intervention and two were lost after the 3‐month visit; their baseline characteristics were comparable to those who completed the study, and no intervention‐related adverse events were reported. Consequently, 40 participants (20 MIST + EMD, 20 MINST + flapless EMD) were included in the final analysis. The study population comprised 24 females (60%) and 16 males (40%), and 17 patients (42.5%) identified as smokers (< 10 cigarettes/day). Baseline characteristics were broadly comparable between groups, with no clinically meaningful imbalances observed (Table 1).

Clinical Periodontal Outcomes

At 9 months, the mean PPD reduction at the treated furcation site was 2.20 ± 2.24 mm in the MIST group and 2.15 ± 1.76 mm in the MINST group (Table 2). The unadjusted mean difference (MIST − MINST) was 0.05 mm. In the primary ANCOVA model adjusted for baseline PPD, smoking status and root trunk length, the adjusted mean difference was −0.22 mm (95% CI: −1.43 to 0.99 mm) (Table S1). As the upper bound of the confidence interval remained below the prespecified non‐inferiority margin of +1.0 mm, the null hypothesis of inferiority was rejected, and MINST was considered non‐inferior to MIST for the primary endpoint. The one‐sided non‐inferiority p‐value was 0.025. However, the upper confidence limit approached the margin closely, indicating limited precision and suggesting that the conclusion should be interpreted with caution. When applying the +1.0 mm non‐inferiority margin to the secondary clinical outcomes, MINST successfully achieved non‐inferiority for REC (p = 0.003). However, because the upper bounds of the confidence intervals for CAL (1.31 mm) and furcation depths exceeded the margin, non‐inferiority could not be formally confirmed for these parameters (Table 2). Patient‐Reported Outcome Measures

During the critical 2‐week early healing phase, the flapless approach showed significant short‐term improvement. Root hypersensitivity was significantly exacerbated in the surgical MIST group (45.9 ± 22.9) compared to the flapless MINST group (24.8 ± 28.2) (p = 0.025) (Table 4). Concurrently, total OHIP‐14 scores increased significantly in the MIST group at 2 weeks (23.3 ± 9.4) while the MINST group experienced significantly less disruption (15.3 ± 10.0, p = 0.008) (Tables 5 and S3). By 9 months, both groups showed significant equivalent improvements in OHRQoL compared to baseline.

Multivariable Analysis and Predictors of Healing

Analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) showed that the treatment modality (MIST vs. MINST) was not a significant predictor of either PPD reduction or CAL gain when controlling for baseline severity, smoking and root trunk length. No statistically significant association was observed, although estimates were imprecise and a clinically important effect cannot be definitively ruled out. Baseline defect depth was the only statistically significant predictor within the model for clinical healing (p < 0.01) (Table S1). Furthermore, multivariable logistic regression indicated that BL‐H was the only significant predictor of achieving partial furcation closure (OR = 0.49, p = 0.030), regardless of the treatment group (Table 6).

LMMs assessing longitudinal PROMs confirmed that allocation to the MIST group was a significant independent predictor of a worse overall quality of life trajectory across the 9‐month follow‐up (β = 6.55, p = 0.009) and identified a near‐significant interaction effect representing an acute spike in hypersensitivity at 2 weeks exclusive to the surgical group (Table S4).

Spearman correlation analyses revealed no statistically significant relationships between the absolute magnitude of clinical or radiographic tissue healing (ΔPPD, ΔCAL, ΔBL‐H, ΔBL‐V) and early VAS symptom burden or final OHIP‐14 changes (p > 0.05 for all comparisons) (Table S5).

Discussion

Summary of Key Findings

The present RCT challenges the traditional necessity of surgical flap reflection in the regenerative treatment of Class II furcation defects. By comprehensively evaluating clinical variables, 3D CBCT changes and longitudinal PROMs, this study demonstrates that a flapless, minimally invasive approach (MINST) combined with EMD yields clinical and radiographic outcomes generally comparable to the surgical standard (MIST), despite not meeting the strict non‐inferiority margin for all secondary parameters. Critically, MINST offers an improved patient experience during the early wound‐healing phase by averting the ‘surgical spike’ in morbidity and preserving OHRQoL.

Interpretation of Primary and Secondary Outcomes

The present study evaluated the non‐inferiority of a flapless minimally invasive approach compared to a minimally invasive surgical technique for the treatment of mandibular Class II furcation defects. Although the observed mean PPD reductions were highly comparable between groups, formal interpretation of non‐inferiority requires careful consideration of the confidence interval. The adjusted between‐group difference was −0.22 mm (95% CI: −1.43 to 0.99 mm). Because the upper bound remained below the prespecified margin of 1.0 mm, the criterion for non‐inferiority was met. However, the proximity of this bound to the margin indicates limited precision. This limited precision is likely related to the higher than anticipated variability observed in the study (SD 1.7–2.2 mm), compared to the assumptions used in sample size calculation. While MINST formally failed to meet the strict +1.0 mm non‐inferiority threshold for CAL and horizontal bone gain, it is critical to note that the study was powered specifically for the primary endpoint (PPD). The higher natural variance observed in these secondary parameters inherently limits the precision required to successfully pass strict non‐inferiority testing.

However, the most distinguishing findings emerged from the secondary, patient‐centred endpoints. In modern clinical practice, therapeutic success encompasses not only anatomical regeneration but also the minimisation of physical and psychological costs to the patient. Differences in patient‐reported outcomes were primarily observed during the early healing phase. At 2 weeks, the MINST group demonstrated significantly lower root hypersensitivity and improved OHRQoL. By avoiding periosteal elevation, bone denudation and the subsequent apical shift of the gingival margin, the MINST approach preserved the soft‐tissue seal, prevented dentinal tubule exposure and drastically reduced hypersensitivity. However, these differences were not sustained at later timepoints, suggesting that the benefit of the flapless approach is limited to the early postoperative period.

Crucially, the 4.4‐point deterioration in OHIP‐14 scores observed in the surgical group at 2 weeks exceeds the established minimally clinically important difference (MCID) threshold of 3 to 5 points for this instrument (Locker et al. 2004; Myint Oo et al. 2020). This confirms that surgical flap reflection induces an acute decline in quality of life that is not merely statistically significant but also highly perceptible and disruptive to the patient. Conversely, the 1.6‐point shift in the MINST group remained well below the MCID, demonstrating that the flapless approach successfully shields patients from clinically meaningful early morbidity.

Baseline root trunk length may significantly influence postoperative hypersensitivity. Following surgical flap reflection (MIST), the resulting apical shift of the gingival margin over a long root trunk exposes a large area of dentinal tubules, increasing thermal sensitivity. In contrast, the flapless MINST approach inherently preserves the supracrestal soft‐tissue architecture. By minimising postoperative recession, this preserved tissue acts as a biological barrier over the root trunk, highlighting a distinct, patient‐centred advantage of MINST in mitigating hypersensitivity.

Results in the Context of Prior Evidence and Hypotheses

These findings validate our a priori hypothesis and contribute significantly to a shifting paradigm in periodontology. While previous systematic reviews have established the superiority of MIST over open‐flap debridement alone (Graziani et al. 2015), the biological requirement for any flap has recently been questioned. Our multivariable ANCOVA and logistic regression models demonstrated that the choice of instrument access (surgical vs. flapless) was not a significant predictor of healing. Instead, the baseline defect anatomy—specifically the initial horizontal depth (BL‐H)—was the sole significant determinant of achieving a successful furcation grade closure. This biologically supports the central thesis of MINST: provided the clinician can adequately debride the root surface and apply the biological agent, the innate regenerative capacity is dictated by the spatial geometry of the defect, rendering surgical reflection largely redundant (Nibali et al. 2015). However, the multivariable models should be interpreted cautiously, given the sample size.

The application of EMD ideally requires a blood‐free environment, as its biological activity can be significantly diminished in the presence of blood (Miron et al. 2012). While surgical flap reflection in MIST provides direct visual control to secure haemostasis prior to root conditioning, achieving complete bleeding control within a blind, non‐surgical pocket during MINST is inherently challenging. This potential for EMD dilution or partial deactivation by continuous bleeding represents a primary technical limitation of the flapless approach, which could theoretically compromise its regenerative predictability compared to surgical access. Soft‐tissue characteristics, including gingival phenotype and tissue thickness, play an important role in healing dynamics and long‐term stability, with thicker phenotypes showing more favourable responses to surgical and regenerative interventions due to their enhanced resistance to mechanical and inflammatory challenges. The presence of cervical enamel projections (CEPs) may further compromise treatment outcomes by promoting plaque accumulation and interfering with connective tissue attachment, thereby negatively affecting periodontal stability.

Strengths and Limitations

The primary strengths of this study include its rigorous RCT design, the integration of 3D CBCT radiographic validation over traditional 2D limitations and the exhaustive longitudinal assessment of PROMs. The application of advanced multivariable modelling ensured that anatomical and behavioural confounders did not skew the non‐inferiority results.

Limitations must be acknowledged. The variability in the primary outcome exceeded initial assumptions (approximately 1.7–2.2 mm), potentially reducing statistical power. In addition, the analyses were conducted on a complete‐case basis due to loss to follow‐up. Also, multiple secondary outcomes were evaluated without correction for multiplicity, increasing the risk of type I error. Moreover, the number of predictors in multivariable models may be high relative to sample size, affecting model stability. The 9‐month follow‐up period is sufficient to evaluate early hard‐ and soft‐tissue remodelling and the trajectory of immediate post‐operative morbidity; however, long‐term maintenance of the stabilised furcations must be verified. Furthermore, the high variability inherent in subjective VAS scoring suggests that larger cohort studies may be beneficial for detecting subtler differences in rare adverse events.

Beyond the standard vertical and horizontal defect dimensions, the predictability of furcation regeneration is heavily influenced by local micro‐anatomy. For instance, the morphological shape of the furcation vault—whether a narrow ‘V’ shape or a wider ‘U’ shape—can significantly impact both the efficacy of initial subgingival instrumentation and the subsequent containment of the biological agent. Likewise, the baseline width of keratinised tissue (KT) is a critical determinant of soft‐tissue stability and overall regenerative potential. In the present trial, while adequate soft tissue was confirmed, the exact width of KT was not systematically quantified as a primary variable; nor was the morphological shape of the furcation vault (U vs. V shape) recorded. Consequently, the absence of these specific baseline stratifications represents a limitation of this study and underscores the necessity of comprehensive site evaluation when comparing surgical and non‐surgical regenerative therapies.

Future Research Directions and Clinical Relevance

Future research should focus on long‐term volumetric CBCT tracking of MINST‐treated furcations beyond the 5‐year mark to determine whether non‐surgical stabilisation equates to long‐term tooth retention. The clinical implications of this study are immediate and impactful. Given its equivalent regenerative potential, combined with significantly reduced postoperative hypersensitivity and the circumvention of early QoL disruptions, MINST should be strongly considered as a primary patient‐centred alternative for the management of moderately involved mandibular furcation defects, particularly as the flapless approach may mitigate pre‐existing tooth mobility by minimising structural trauma.

Conclusion

Within the limitations of this RCT, MINST demonstrated clinical and radiographic outcomes generally comparable to MIST. While non‐inferiority against a strict 1.0‐mm margin was successfully established for the primary outcome and specific secondary parameters, it could not be formally confirmed for CAL or horizontal bone gain. MINST was associated with improved short‐term, patient‐reported outcomes during early healing, although these differences were not sustained.

Longitudinal study of central obesity and glucose–lipid metabolism in predicting tumor remission among fertility-sparing endometrial cancer patients
Authors: XiaoDan Li, YuXuan Wei, XiaoWei Li, Jingjing Gong, Dandan Yang, Yuanyuan Gong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study aimed to predict tumor remission by monitoring obesity-parameter changes during weight loss in EC patients and to identify the most reliable indicators for forecasting tumor complete remission (CR).MethodsA prospective longitudinal study was conducted to track changes in obesity parameters during a 6-month weight-loss period and to predict 9-month CR. Body morphology indicators, including weight, body mass index (BMI), waist circumference (WC), hip circumference (HC), waist-to-height ratio (WHtR), waist-to-hip ratio (WHR), lipid accumulation product (LAP), and body roundness index (BRI), and metabolic measures such as triglycerides (TG), hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c), fasting insulin (FINS), and insulin resistance (IR) were assessed. Multivariable logistic regression was used to evaluate associations between these parameters and CR, and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analyses were performed to determine optimal cut-off values for predicting remission.ResultsA total of 84 patients were included. Multivariable logistic regression identified WHR as a significant independent predictor for CR (P = 0.015, OR=1.111 95% CI: 1.021–1.209). ROC analysis revealed optimal cut-off values for WHR (0.90), TG (1.38), and FINS (18.93). The AUC values were 0.71, 0.63, and 0.67 respectively, indicating moderate predictive value. Calibration and decision curve analyses confirmed the models' stability and clinical benefit.ConclusionsWHR may serve as an additional predictor of obesity in CR. of obesity for CR. It is a convenient and quick method for monitoring and developing individualized treatments for fertility preservation and weight reduction strategies.

Introduction

Endometrial cancer (EC) is the sixth most common malignancy among women, with more than 427,000 new cases reported worldwide in 2022 [1]. An increasing proportion of these cases occurs in younger patients, many of whom opt for fertility-preserving therapy [2]. Current guidelines recommend high-dose progestational hormone therapy, typically 400–600 mg/day of medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA) or 160–320 mg/day of megestrol acetate (MA) for approximately six months, as the standard fertility-sparing approach. Achieving complete remission (CR) is the primary therapeutic objective [3], [4]. A substantial body of evidence has demonstrated a strong association between obesity and EC; specifically, each 5-unit increase in body mass index (BMI) is linked to an approximate 50% elevation in EC risk [5], [6]. Obesity is also a major predictor of suboptimal tumor remission during fertility-preserving treatment. Furthermore, progestin therapy itself may exacerbate weight gain by increasing appetite, thereby contributing to metabolic dysregulation and potentially promoting progesterone resistance [7]. Consequently, monitoring and mitigating obesity are critical for improving treatment efficacy [8]. Although emerging studies suggest that weight management may enhance CR rates, the specific patterns and trajectories of weight-related parameters during such interventions remain insufficiently explored [9]. Further research is needed to clarify how targeted weight management can optimize remission and support successful fertility preservation among patients with EC.

Obesity-related parameters play a pivotal role in characterizing and regulating obesity. Numerous studies have demonstrated that early trends in these parameters can facilitate timely disease prediction and provide effective early-warning signals [10]. Among these, BMI is the most widely adopted metric; although it offers a general assessment of adiposity, its utility is limited. Because BMI incorporates only height and weight, it may overestimate obesity in individuals with high muscle mass or elevated body density, thereby failing to accurately reflect true adipose burden.

Extensive prior research has established that WHR outperforms BMI in predicting cardiovascular disease risk [11]. Similarly, indices such as the body roundness index (BRI) and WHtR have demonstrated strong predictive value for hypertension and metabolic syndrome [12]. Beyond these traditional measures, a variety of advanced anthropometric and metabolic indicators, such as the visceral adiposity index (VAI), lipid accumulation product (LAP), body fat mass(BFM), basal metabolic rate (BMR), A Body Shape Index (ABSI), visceral fat area (VFA), and triglycerides(TG) are increasingly employed to assess cardiovascular and metabolic risk [13]. In the context of EC, emerging evidence suggests that combining BRI and WHR with pathological characteristics may enhance the prediction of treatment response, providing a multifactorial framework for assessing therapeutic outcomes.

Current research on EC increasingly emphasizes the importance of weight management [14]. However, there remains a paucity of evidence regarding how specific obesity indicators influence CR during weight reduction. The present study aims to evaluate the predictive value of various anthropometric and metabolic obesity parameters for tumor remission among EC patients undergoing structured weight-loss interventions.

This study adopts a prospective, observational, longitudinal design, focusing on individuals receiving both weight-management therapy and fertility-preserving treatment. We anticipate that our findings will provide a robust theoretical foundation for optimizing weight-management strategies and enhancing therapeutic efficacy. Moreover, the results of this study have the potential to inform clinical decision-making and improve treatment guidance for fertility-preserving management in patients with EC.

Methods

Study design

This was a prospective, single-arm interventional study conducted at Peking University People’s Hospital from January 2023 to December 2024. The study aimed to observe the longitudinal changes in central obesity and metabolic parameters during a standardized weight-management program. All participants signed informed consent.

Participants

Eligible patients had FIGO 2009 stage IA disease, with no evidence of metastatic spread or myometrial invasion on imaging. Additional inclusion criteria included a BMI ≥ 25 kg/m², suitability for fertility-preserving treatment, and participation in a structured weight management program. Exclusion criteria included inability to complete follow-up assessments, the presence of severe cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease, and pregnancy during the study period.

Initially, 100 eligible patients were screened. Of these, 16 patients were excluded based on our pre-specified exclusion criteria due to their inability to complete the scheduled follow-up assessments. Consequently, 84 participants who completed the full program were included in the final analysis.

Weight-management intervention

All participants underwent a standardized, multidisciplinary weight-management program based on the Medical Research Council framework [15]. The intervention included a structured nutritional plan emphasizing high protein and low-GI foods, combined with 150 min of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise per week. The program was divided into an intensive weight-loss phase, a transition phase, and a maintenance phase.

Adherence was monitored via a digital health platform. Fertility-sparing regimens consisted of oral medroxyprogesterone acetate or megestrol acetate. Dosages were adjusted based on histological response assessed every 3 months.

Fertility-sparing treatment

Initial regimens consisted of oral medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA, 250–500 mg/day) or megestrol acetate (MA, 160 mg/day) based on the Chinese expert consensus. Treatment efficacy was assessed every 3 months via endometrial biopsy. Dosages were adjusted or combined with a levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine system based on the histological response: patients with persistent lesions underwent dose escalation or regimen switching, while those achieving complete remission continued maintenance therapy.

Data collection and outcomes

Obesity-related parameters were assessed at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months, with tumor remission evaluated at the 9-month follow-up.

Body morphology parameters

WC, HC, and BMI were measured using standard protocols [11]. Additional indices included: ABSI: WC/(BMI^(2/3) × height^(1/2)), BRI: 364.2 − 365.5 × [1 − (WC(m)/2π)²/(0.5 × height(m))²] [21], VAI: [WC/(36.58 + 1.89 × BMI)] × (TG/0.81) × (1.52/HDL-C) [23], LAP: (WC (cm) − 58) × TG [12], [13], [16.], [17].

Body composition index

Body composition parameters were assessed using a Body Composition Analyzer following the manufacturer’s instructions. Primary measures included BFM, percent body fat (PBF), BRI, and VFA [18].

Biochemical parameters

Laboratory measurements included TG, total cholesterol (TC), fasting blood glucose (FBG), hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c), and fasting insulin (FINS). Insulin resistance was evaluated using the homeostasis model assessment (HOMA-IR), calculated as: HOMA-IR = FBG (mmol/L) × FINS (mU/mL) / 22.5.

Tumor outcome

CR at 9 months served as the primary tumor outcome indicator. CR was defined as the absence of endometrioid adenocarcinoma on post-treatment histopathology, accompanied by complete atrophic degeneration of glands and interstitial metaplasia. During the 6-month treatment period, hysteroscopic evaluation was performed at follow-up visits as clinically indicated, generally at intervals of about 3 months. Slides were reviewed by two blinded pathologists.

Statistical analyses

Data were analyzed using SPSS 26.0 and R 4.5.1. Continuous variables were compared using t-tests (Mean ± SD) or Mann–Whitney U tests (Median [IQR]), while categorical variables were summarized as frequencies (%). Longitudinal changes were assessed via Linear Mixed-effects Models with Group, Time, and their interaction as fixed effects. Independent predictors of remission were identified using multivariable logistic regression, ensuring no multicollinearity (VIF < 5). ROC analysis determined optimal cutoffs and predictive accuracy. Model robustness and clinical utility were further validated using calibration plots, Decision Curve Analysis (DCA), and 1000 bootstrap resamples. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Based on clinical significance and the need to minimize redundancy, we constructed a multivariable model focusing on three distinct metabolic domains: WHR, TG, and FINS. As shown in Table 3, WHR was identified as a significant independent predictor of complete tumor remission (P = 0.015; OR = 1.111; 95% CI: 1.021–1.209 per 0.01-unit increase). Additionally, TG (P = 0.161; OR = 1.874; 95% CI: 0.778–4.514) and FINS (P = 0.407; OR = 1.019; 95% CI: 0.974–1.066) also remained independent predictors in the final model. All predictors exhibited VIF values below 1.5, confirming the stability of the regression estimates. (Table 3).Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis was employed to evaluate the predictive performance of metabolic markers for tumor remission. WHR demonstrated the highest predictive accuracy with an AUC of 0.71 (95% CI: 0.53–0.83), yielding a sensitivity of 0.75 and a specificity of 0.78 at a cut-off of 0.9 (P < 0.001). TG and FINS also showed significant predictive value, with AUCs of 0.63 and 0.67, respectively. (Table 4). Calibration curves confirmed that the predicted probabilities were highly consistent with actual observations, supported by low Mean Absolute Error values for WHR (0.09), TG (0.066), and FINS (0.062). Discussion

This is the first prospective longitudinal study on the predictive effect of central obesity and glucose-lipid metabolism on tumor remission in fertility-sparing EC patients. The study analyzed the impact of changes in obesity indicators on tumor remission through baseline, 3-month, and 6-month repeated observations. Comparing the complete remission and incomplete remission groups of EC patients, We identified an association between pathophysiological characteristics and biochemical manifestations of glucose-lipid metabolism [19]. Abnormal fat distribution is the pathological basis and the root cause, while abnormal glucose-lipid metabolism indicators are the direct biochemical consequence [20]. We found that central obesity indicators, especially WHR and metabolic indicators TG, and FINS, can serve as potential assessment values for tumor remission status in fertility-sparing EC patients [21].

We found that at each time point, central obesity indicators, including WC, WHtR, and WHR, were significantly higher in the incomplete remission group than in the complete remission group, with the most significant difference in WHR. Dynamic analysis showed that WHR in the complete remission group continuously improved over time, while the change in WHR in the incomplete remission group was smaller, suggesting the presence of uncorrectable visceral fat accumulation [22]. Notably, there was no significant difference in directly measured VFA between the two groups, indicating the effectiveness of WHR as a simple clinical indicator and its more accurate reflection of differences in fat distribution function related to metabolic remission [23]. An elevated WHR indicates a large accumulation of visceral fat in the abdomen [24]. This type of visceral fat has active metabolism, easily producing free fatty acids and inflammatory factors, which directly enter the liver and drive insulin resistance and lipid metabolism disorders [25]. Moreover, this fat distribution pattern is more strongly regulated by genetics and hormones and has a poor response to conventional lifestyle interventions, resulting in the refractory abnormal fat distribution in the incomplete remission group [26].

We observed widening divergences in VAI and LAP between groups over time. By 6 months, LAP and TG were significantly higher in the incomplete remission group. A clinically relevant interaction for TG (P = 0.065) highlighted distinct trajectories: while the complete remission group showed continuous improvement, the incomplete group exhibited worsening TG and stagnant LAP, indicative of persistent lipid metabolism disorder. The main reason for this phenomenon is that visceral fat is in a state of chronic inflammation, hypoxia, and fibrosis, leading to adipocyte dysfunction, resulting in hyperlipolysis, reduced adiponectin secretion, and continuous release of free fatty acids and pro-inflammatory signals into the blood [27], [28]. Excessive free fatty acids flow into the liver, stimulating the liver to synthesize TG, forming a vicious cycle of visceral fat dysfunction - hypertriglyceridemia, a process that cannot be reversed even with weight loss [29].

The difference between the two groups may be due to abnormal fat distribution leading to visceral fat dysfunction, which further results in abnormal glucose and lipid metabolism indicators. The incomplete remission may be caused by persistent fat dysfunction resulting from refractory visceral fat accumulation; even with a decrease BMI, the pathophysiological problem cannot be corrected, ultimately manifesting as abnormal biochemical indicators [30].

Therefore, in clinical practice, a dual assessment framework of morphology and function needs to be established. Among the morphologic indicators, WHR demonstrated the strongest independent predictive value for 9-month CR. Although LAP reflects the degree of lipid accumulation, it was not included in the final multivariable regression model primarily due to significant multicollinearity with other core metrics like WHR and TG. Instead, FINS was incorporated as a key metabolic predictor, highlighting the critical role of the insulin-glucose axis in tumor response. Our results suggest that a WHR cutoff of 0.90, a TG cutoff of 1.38 mmol/L, and a FINS cutoff of 18.93 μU/mL provide the most effective clinical net benefit. While LAP can still serve as a supplementary reference for observing overall metabolic trends, the primary focus for predicting tumor remission should be on these refined independent markers. Furthermore, treatment needs to shift from simple progestin therapy to precise interventions that target and correct fat distribution and function, further assisting in complete tumor remission [31].

Strengths and limitations

Strengths of this study include its prospective longitudinal design, which establishes a temporal link between dynamic changes in central obesity and tumor remission. By integrating morphological and metabolic assessments, we confirmed that WHR reflects fat function better than VFA and elucidated the "visceral fat–metabolic disorder–remission" axis.

However, limitations include the single-center, single-arm nature and short 9-month follow-up, which restrict generalizability and long-term outcome assessment. Additionally, while anthropometric and metabolic markers serve as valuable proxies for the metabolic environment, they lack the sensitivity of direct tumor biomarkers and should supplement, not replace, pathological diagnosis. Large-scale multi-center trials are needed to validate the proposed cut-offs.

Conclusion

This prospective longitudinal study of fertility-preserving patients with EC confirms the predictive value of central obesity and glucose–lipid metabolism indicators for tumor remission, elucidating their underlying pathophysiological relationships. WHR more accurately captured functional differences in adipose distribution. Metabolic indices demonstrated a progressively widening divergence in LAP and TG levels between the incomplete and CR groups. Our findings also highlight the need to establish an integrated “morphology–function” assessment framework, employing WHR ≥ 0.90 and TG ≥ 1.38 mmol/L as key cut-off points and utilizing LAP as an efficient screening indicator. Moreover, treatment strategies should extend beyond progestin monotherapy to include targeted interventions aimed at correcting both adipose distribution and metabolic dysfunction.

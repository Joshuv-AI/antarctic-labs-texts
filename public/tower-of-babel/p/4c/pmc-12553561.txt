Thulium laser versus coblation tonsillectomy: an experimental comparison of efficacy
Authors: Kun Du, Liyun Yang, Huang Shuixian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
To compare the effects of thulium laser (TmLR) versus low-temperature plasma radiofrequency ablation (COBLATION) on perioperative parameters, serum inflammatory factors, and postoperative healing following tonsillectomy in New Zealand rabbits. Twenty-four rabbits were randomly selected. The left tonsil underwent TmLR, while the right underwent COBLATION. Postoperative wound healing on both sides was compared. Another twenty-four rabbits were randomly divided into two groups, undergoing bilateral tonsillectomy using either TmLR or COBLATION. Surgical duration, intraoperative blood loss, postoperative water intake, weight changes, postoperative inflammatory factor levels, and pathological analysis of the fibrin membrane covering the wound were compared between the two groups. The TmLR group had significantly shorter surgical duration and less intraoperative blood loss than the COBLATION group (P < 0.05). Postoperative water intake was significantly higher in the TmLR group (P < 0.05). Weight loss was significantly less in the TmLR group (P < 0.05). Endoscopic examination at 4 h post-surgery revealed better wound conditions in the TmLR group. However, at postoperative days 1, 3, and 5, the COBLATION side showed better recovery, with statistically significant differences in wound scores (P < 0.05). By postoperative day 7, both sides recovered well with no significant histological differences between groups (P > 0.05). Hematological analysis revealed no statistically significant differences in inflammatory factor levels between the two groups. Pathological analysis indicated a more pronounced inflammatory reaction in the TmLR group at day 3 compared to COBLATION. By day 7, the pathological manifestations were similar in both groups. Thulium laser tonsillectomy offers advantages in terms of shorter surgical time, reduced intraoperative bleeding, and potentially lower postoperative pain (as inferred by higher water intake and less weight loss). There was no statistically significant difference in overall efficacy between the thulium fiber laser system and the low-temperature plasma system. The thulium laser can be considered a viable alternative instrument for tonsillectomy.

Introduction

The optimal technique for tonsillectomy remains a subject of ongoing debate [1]. In cases where conventional surgical methods yield suboptimal outcomes or pose elevated risks, various novel energy-based devices [2]—such as lasers, ultrasonic scalpels, cryogenic plasma knives [3], and electrosurgical instruments —have been progressively introduced into clinical practice to enhance surgical safety and efficacy [4]. Among these, low-temperature plasma ablation has gained widespread clinical adoption owing to its advantages of minimal intraoperative bleeding [5], clear surgical field, and reduced postoperative pain [6]. This technique operates by generating a plasma field around the electrode via an electrically conductive medium. The energized particles within this region possess sufficient energy to disrupt molecular bonds in biological tissues, resulting in molecular dissociation at relatively low temperatures (40–70 °C). This process leads to water evaporation, protein denaturation, tissue necrosis, and ultimately ablation or cutting [7]. However, the single-use nature of cryogenic plasma devices entails considerable operational costs [8], which has limited their widespread use in primary healthcare settings.

Laser surgical systems, as an emerging energy platform, enable precise tissue incision and coagulation through thermal effects generated by focused laser beams. These systems offer notable benefits such as non-contact operation, high precision, minimal trauma, and operational flexibility [9]. The thulium laser, primarily generated from thulium-doped fiber or thulium-doped yttrium aluminum garnet (Tm: YAG) crystals, operates at a wavelength of approximately 1.9–2.1 μm [10], which is close to the absorption peak of water. This characteristic endows it with strong water absorption properties. When thulium laser radiation is applied to biological tissues with high water content, light energy is absorbed by water molecules and rapidly converted into thermal energy. According to Niemz et al. [11], a temperature increases to 45–50 °C from the normal human body temperature (37 °C) induces tissue necrosis and forms zones of thermal damage; when the temperature exceeds 60 °C, thermal denaturation of proteins occurs, leading to tissue coagulation; further heating beyond 100 °C causes vaporization of tissue water, resulting in carbonization and ablation. With continuous advancements in thulium laser technology and the growing emphasis on minimally invasive surgery, this laser system has demonstrated broad application prospects across various surgical disciplines due to its shallow tissue penetration, limited thermal damage, and remarkable hemostatic efficacy [12].

Recent years have witnessed a growing body of comparative studies on tonsillectomy techniques, particularly comparing conventional tonsillectomy with various advanced modalities such as monopolar electrocautery, low-temperature plasma, carbon dioxide laser, and ultrasonic scalpels. These thermal-based methods, which ablate tonsillar tissue through heat energy, are reported to reduce operative time and minimize perioperative bleeding [13]. In a meta-analysis by Albazee et al. [14], which analyzed postoperative pain duration and bleeding patterns, outcomes between carbon dioxide laser and low-temperature plasma techniques were found to be comparable without significant differences. Compared with laser technology, ablation tonsillectomy was associated with a significant reduction in intraoperative blood loss and operative time; however, these differences appear to lack clinical significance in surgical practice. Moreover, that study focused specifically on carbon dioxide laser and did not address thulium laser. Although thulium laser has demonstrated promising applications in other surgical fields [15], evidence regarding its use in tonsillectomy remains scarce. To date, no direct comparative studies have been published evaluating thulium laser versus low-temperature plasma—two advanced technologies in this field.Therefore, this study aims to explore the potential application of thulium laser in minimally invasive otorhinolaryngologic surgery, leveraging its excellent tissue-cutting and hemostatic capabilities. Through animal experiments, we will evaluate its performance in tonsil tissue ablation, hemostatic efficiency, and postoperative healing processes, thereby assessing its clinical translational value in the treatment of otorhinolaryngological diseases.

Materials and methods

Ethical considerations

Animal experiments were conducted using New Zealand White rabbits. Forty-eight male rabbits, weighing approximately 3.0–3.5 kg and age-matched to their body weight, were included. All animals were confirmed healthy upon receipt from a commercial supplier. Rabbits were individually housed under standard laboratory conditions in the institutional animal facility. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Pudong New Area Gongli Hospital (Approval No. GLYYls2025-005).

All animals underwent a comprehensive physical examination within two days prior to surgery to confirm they were in good health. Each experimental rabbit was weighed once upon arrival at the facility and again immediately before the surgical procedure. Preoperative blood samples were collected. Animals were fasted (food withheld) for 12 h prior to the scheduled anesthesia induction; the fasting period did not exceed 48 h. Feeding was resumed more than 4 h after full anesthetic recovery.

Experimental Groups and Surgical Procedures

The Coblation Surgical System PLA-600 (Chengdu MeCan Medical Science & Technology Co., Ltd.) and the Thulium-Doped Fiber Laser Therapeutic Apparatus SRM-T1MSA (Shanghai RECON Laser Technology Co., Ltd.) were used in this study.

Unilateral Comparison Group (Healing Assessment): Twenty-four rabbits were randomly assigned to this group. The left tonsil in each rabbit underwent resection using a thulium laser (TmLR) system (SRM-T1MSA) with the laser power set at 6 W. The right tonsil underwent resection using a low-temperature plasma radiofrequency ablation (COBLATION) system (PLA-600) with the ablation mode set to “Cut” level 05 and the coagulation mode set to level 02.

Bilateral Comparison Groups (Perioperative Parameters): An additional twenty-four rabbits were randomly assigned and further randomly divided into two groups (n = 12 per group). Thulium Laser Surgery Group (TmLR Group): Both tonsils were resected using the thulium laser, with the power set at 6 W. COBLATION Surgery Group: Both tonsils were resected using the COBLATION system, with the ablation mode set to “Cut” level 05 and the coagulation mode set to level 02. Representative intraoperative views of the surgical fields during TmLR and COBLATION procedures are presented in Fig. 1.

To ensure technical consistency and minimize inter-operator bias, all tonsillectomy procedures were performed by the same experienced otolaryngologist. Surgical duration (Measured in minutes (min)) and intraoperative blood loss (Measured in milliliters (mL)) were recorded for each procedure. Upon completion of all surgeries, animals were monitored until fully recovered from anesthesia and subsequently transferred back to the animal housing facility for standard postoperative care. Postoperative water intake (Measured in milliliters (mL)) and body weight changes (Measured in Kilogram (kg)) were monitored and recorded.

Postoperative wound healing was assessed using digital endoscopy at 4 h, 1 day, 3 days, 5 days, and 7 days following surgery. The morphology of the fibrin membrane and the healing status of the bilateral tonsillar fossae were evaluated and scored according to the following scale:

Blood samples were collected via venipuncture of the marginal ear vein preoperatively and on postoperative day 5 (POD 5). These samples were analyzed for white blood cell (WBC) count, platelet (PLT) count, high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) level, procalcitonin (PCT) level, and albumin (ALB) level.

The fibrin membrane tissue from the surgical wounds of both the TmLR group and the COBLATION group was collected. Specimens were immediately fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin for 48 h. Following fixation, tissues were dehydrated through a graded ethanol series, cleared in xylene, embedded in paraffin wax, and sectioned at 3–5 μm thickness. Sections were then stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for histopathological analysis.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS software (version 27.0). Continuous data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD). Based on the results of normality tests, intergroup comparisons were made using the independent samples t-test for normally distributed data or the Mann-Whitney U test for non-normally distributed data. Statistical significance was defined as a two-tailed P-value < 0.05.

Results

The mean operative time for TmLR group was significantly shorter than that for COBLATION group (8.94 ± 0.43 min vs. 11.61 ± 2.28 min, p < 0.05). While intraoperative blood loss was minimal in both groups, the TmLR group demonstrated significantly less bleeding compared to the COBLATION group (p < 0.05). At postoperative day 3, water intake was significantly greater in the TmLR group than in the COBLATION group (p < 0.05). Furthermore, the COBLATION group exhibited significantly greater weight loss compared to the TmLR group (p < 0.05). Discussion

The results of this study demonstrated that the TmLR group exhibited a significantly shorter operative time and reduced postoperative hemorrhage compared to the COBLATION group, with statistically significant differences (p < 0.05). Furthermore, as indicated by postoperative water intake and changes in body weight before and after surgery, the TmLR group experienced a lower degree of pain than the COBLATION group, and this difference was also statistically significant (p < 0.05). These findings suggest that thulium laser can shorten operative time, decrease intraoperative bleeding, and alleviate postoperative pain. The core advantage of thulium laser lies in its wavelength characteristics, which enable high precision, minimal thermal damage, and rapid recovery, making it particularly suitable for minimally invasive procedures and surgeries in functionally sensitive regions.

Postoperative endoscopic follow-up at 4 h revealed superior wound conditions in the TmLR group compared to the COBLATION group. This difference may be attributed to the relatively larger probe size and broader active area of the coblation device, which could potentially cause more extensive collateral tissue damage. However, on postoperative days 3 and 5, the COBLATION group demonstrated better wound healing, with statistically significant differences in recovery scores (p < 0.05). The mechanism underlying this improved mid-term recovery may involve the plasma-induced protein denaturation characteristic of coblation technology, whereby the resulting layer of denatured collagen forms a protective barrier during the healing process [16].

Histopathological analysis on postoperative day 3 revealed a more pronounced infiltration of neutrophils and inflammatory cells in the TmLR group compared to the COBLATION group. This heightened early inflammatory response following thulium laser surgery can be attributed to its mechanism of action, which relies on localized instantaneous high-intensity thermal energy to ablate tissue, thereby inducing more acute tissue reaction initially [17].

Hematological tests performed on postoperative day 5 indicated that serum levels of the inflammatory marker C-reactive protein (CRP) were lower in the TmLR group than in the COBLATION group, although this difference was not statistically significant (p > 0.05). In contrast, procalcitonin (PCT) levels were significantly lower in the TmLR group (p < 0.05).

By postoperative day 7, electronic endoscopic evaluation showed satisfactory and comparable wound healing in both groups. Histopathological findings were also similar between the two groups, with no statistically significant differences (p > 0.05), indicating that both techniques eventually achieve comparable and satisfactory long-term therapeutic outcomes.

Postoperative pain represents a primary concern for patients during recovery following tonsillectomy. It is important to note that this study employed a New Zealand rabbit model, in which postoperative pain was assessed indirectly through changes in body weight and water consumption. These metrics may not fully correlate with subjective pain experiences in human patients.

In a clinical study by Singh et al. [18] involving 60 pediatric patients who underwent either laser or coblation tonsillectomy, no statistically significant difference in postoperative pain was observed between the two groups as measured by a subjective visual analog scale (VAS). In contrast, Magdy et al. [19] reported that coblation ablation was associated with significantly reduced duration of pain compared to laser techniques. Furthermore, Baghdasaryan et al. [20] compared thulium laser tonsillectomy with conventional cold dissection in adults and demonstrated that the thulium laser technique was superior in terms of both intraoperative bleeding control and postoperative pain reduction, suggesting a potential advantage of thulium laser in tonsillectomy procedures.

It is important to acknowledge certain limitations inherent in the present study. First, animal models cannot fully recapitulate the clinical scenario of human tonsillectomy. The anatomical structure, tissue biomechanical properties, and physiological responses of animal models differ inherently from those of humans, which may introduce discrepancies when extrapolating our findings to clinical practice.

Second, several outcomes of this study are susceptible to subjective bias. Despite the implementation of standardized assessment protocols, the evaluation of certain parameters (e.g., postoperative wound healing) may still be influenced by the observer’s subjective judgment, potentially affecting the objectivity of the results. Additionally, the operator’s proficiency level represents another confounding factor; variations in surgical technique and experience can directly impact operative efficacy. All surgical procedures in this study were performed by a single surgeon. While this standardized approach enhances the internal validity of the comparative analysis between the two techniques (by minimizing inter-operator variability), it may compromise the generalizability of the results to a broader cohort of surgeons with varying levels of expertise. This limitation should be considered when interpreting the applicability of our findings in real-world clinical settings.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study demonstrated that thulium laser tonsillectomy in a New Zealand rabbit model significantly reduced operative time, intraoperative blood loss, and postoperative pain compared to coblation surgery. No statistically significant differences were observed in wound healing outcomes between the two groups. These findings suggest that the thulium-doped fiber laser system may serve as a viable technical option for tonsillectomy in clinical practice.

Donor-targeted anti–HLA-A2 antibody shows nanogram-level efficacy in a novel GVHD mouse model
Authors: Kouta Niizuma, Alyssa H. Chang, Yoshia Okamoto, Jeewon S. Kim, Kazunari Sasaki, Stephan Busque, Ravindra Majeti, Hiromitsu Nakauchi, Yusuke Nakauchi
Published: 2026

TO THE EDITOR:

Acute graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) remains a major barrier to allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell (HSC) transplantation, and severe cases of GVHD are often fatal despite intensive immunosuppression. Corticosteroids, JAK inhibitors, and antibody-based therapies constitute the current standard treatment, yet outcomes for steroid-refractory GVHD remain poor.1, 2, 3, 4 GVHD after solid organ transplantation is less common but is similarly difficult to treat and still lacks effective targeted therapies.5,6

Several approaches aim to mitigate GVHD by depleting alloreactive T cells, including CD45RA+ T-cell depletion,7 posttransplant cyclophosphamide,8 and CD137-directed strategies.9 We reasoned that HLA allele–specific targeting of donor alloreactive cells could provide a more precise modality, particularly in haploidentical HSC transplantation, in which a defined HLA mismatch can be exploited for selective donor immune targeting. Therefore, we evaluated AN7, a donor HLA-A2–directed antibody designed to eliminate pathogenic donor immune cells while preserving donor hematopoiesis and multilineage reconstitution.

In our previous work, we showed that a murine anti–HLA-A2 antibody attenuated GVHD in a xenogeneic peripheral blood mononuclear cell (PBMC) transfer model,10 but that system lacked HSC transplantation and did not permit an assessment of dose sensitivity or therapeutic selectivity. The clinical potential of donor-directed serotherapy was highlighted by a case in which plasma containing high-titer donor allele–specific anti–HLA-A2 antibodies led to complete resolution of steroid-refractory GVHD after HLA-mismatched kidney transplantation,11 thereby supporting the therapeutic concept and motivating the development of allele-specific monoclonal antibodies with defined pharmacologic properties.

Here, we describe AN7, a new anti–HLA-A2 monoclonal antibody with enhanced translational potential. AN7 was generated by immunizing HLA-A24–transgenic mice with a recombinant HLA-A2 tetramer to enrich for allele-specific responses while limiting cross-reactivity to conserved HLA epitopes.12 Hybridomas were screened using HLA-coated beads and HLA-A2+ PBMCs, and clone AN7 was selected for potent, selective binding to HLA-A2 and the closely related allele HLA-A68 (Figure 1A-B).13 AN7 is a mouse IgG2aκ antibody (mAN7; Figure 1C). Its variable regions were sequenced to generate a recombinant murine IgG2aκ (recAN7) and a human chimeric IgG1κ (cAN7), and both retained HLA-A2/A68 specificity by flow cytometry (Figure 1D-E). Because HLA class I molecules engage the inhibitory receptor LILRB1 on myeloid cells and natural killer cells,14 we asked whether AN7 could disrupt this checkpoint. In competitive binding assays, preincubation of HLA-A2 beads with mAN7 markedly reduced subsequent LILRB1-Fc binding, and mAN7 partially displaced prebound LILRB1-Fc (Figure 1I), indicating that mAN7 can interfere with HLA-A2–LILRB1 interactions in vitro. Given that mice lack LILRB1 (and instead express the functional ortholog PIR-B), this checkpoint axis could not be evaluated in our murine model. However, relief of LILRB1-mediated inhibition may represent an additional mechanistic advantage for translating AN7 to human disease.

To model clinical transplantation and define a therapeutic window, we established an immunocompetent allogeneic-GVHD (allo-GVHD) model using lethally irradiated BALB/c mice that underwent transplantation with 5 × 106 T-cell–replete bone marrow cells from HLA-A2–transgenic C57BL/6 donors.16 This fully major histocompatibility complex–mismatched combination induces rapidly fatal GVHD, whereas T-cell–depleted bone marrow grafts do not cause GVHD in this setting (supplemental Figure 2A-B). On day 7, when mice already exhibited overt GVHD, we administered a single dose of mAN7 at 0.1, 1, 10, or 100 ng, along with a 10-ng isotype control. Guided by clinical estimates that donor-specific antibodies can act at doses ≤6 μg/kg,11 we explored these ultralow doses. A single 1- to 100-ng dose of mAN7 conferred long-term survival in roughly half of the recipients, whereas all isotype- and 0.1-ng–treated animals died within 2 weeks (Figure 2C-D), defining an effective, well-tolerated dose window of 1 to 100 ng.

To probe early effects, we analyzed leukocyte subsets 1 day after mAN7 administration. Overall, donor-versus-recipient chimerism did not change significantly, and no statistically significant differences in donor CD4+ or CD8+ T-cell frequencies were observed in the spleen between mAN7-treated and control mice at this time point (supplemental Figure 2C-E). Importantly, all mAN7-treated survivors ultimately exhibited robust donor-derived hematopoietic reconstitution in the bone marrow and spleen, with HLA-A2+ donor T cells, B cells, and myeloid cells being readily detectable (Figure 2E-F). Phenotypic long-term (LT)-HSCs (CD45+ H-2Kb+ Lin− Sca-1+ c-Kit+ CD150+) stained positive for HLA-A2 and remained detectable 1 day after mAN7 exposure, with donor-versus-recipient chimerism shown in Figure 2G (left). The proportion of LT-HSCs among donor-derived cells did not differ significantly between mAN7-treated and control mice (Figure 2G, middle-left) nor did the absolute numbers of LT-HSCs and donor T cells (CD45+ H-2Kb+ CD3+; Figure 2G, middle-right). Notably, HLA-A2 surface expression density (median fluorescence intensity) was significantly lower on LT-HSCs than on donor T cells in both groups (P < .01; Figure 2G, right), consistent with published transcriptomic data showing substantially lower HLA class I expression in HSCs than in mature immune cells.17 These findings indicate that HLA-A2+ LT-HSCs are not acutely depleted at therapeutic doses and that the lower antigen density on HSCs may contribute to their relative sparing by AN7.

These data, along with previous work showing that hematopoietic stem/progenitor cells (HSPCs) express class I molecules but differ from mature T cells and B cells in antigen density, activation state, and cycling,18,19 suggest that HSPC preservation reflects multiple protective features rather than the absence of antigen. HSPCs occupy bone marrow niches, cycle slowly, and likely have lower effective exposure to circulating antibody- and Fc receptor-bearing effector cells. This contrasts with activated donor T cells. Additionally, mAN7 is administered at nanogram doses. A large fraction of the antibody is probably used by highly activated, HLA-A2–high effector cells in blood and target tissues, leaving little free antibody to reach quiescent HSCs. Thus, niche localization and low proliferation may reduce effective AN7 exposure to HSCs. However, this mechanism has not been demonstrated directly, and there may also be additional contributing factors.

To further assess donor-specific immunosuppression, we performed a mixed lymphocyte reaction using mitomycin C–treated BALB/c splenocytes as stimulators and splenocytes from HLA-A2–transgenic C57BL/6 mice as responders. AN7 at picogram to low-nanogram concentrations (1 pg to 10 ng) significantly suppressed T-cell expansion (Figure 2H), consistent with potent, allele-restricted inhibition of donor responses.

Collectively, these findings support a model in which AN7 broadly depletes activated donor immune cells in severe allo-GVHD while permitting subsequent donor-derived multilineage reconstitution, a transient donor-targeted reset analogous in concept to the broad T-cell depletion achieved with antithymocyte globulin but focused on a defined HLA allele. Our data suggest that such allele-focused reset strategies warrant revisiting, particularly in the setting of severe GVHD, where current therapies remain inadequate. Whether AN7 discriminates between alloreactive and nonalloreactive T cells, and whether T-cell receptor repertoire diversity, pathogen-specific immunity, and graft-versus-leukemia effects are preserved, remain important questions for future studies.

Our group previously established the principle that anti-HLA antibodies can target pathogenic donor-derived cells in a murine GVHD model,10 a concept subsequently translated to the clinic by others using polyclonal donor-specific antibodies in steroid-refractory GVHD.11 This study advances this framework by demonstrating that AN7, an allele-specific monoclonal antibody, achieves therapeutic efficacy in a fully major histocompatibility complex–mismatched allo-GVHD model at nanogram-level doses (∼0.05-5 μg/kg), representing a 1000- to 100 000-fold reduction compared with typical therapeutic antibodies. This combination of allelic precision and ultralow effective dosing, along with preservation of donor hematopoiesis, represents a substantive advance over prior polyclonal approaches and suggests a wide safety margin with practical advantages for manufacturing and cost. HLA-A2 is the most common HLA-A allele in the United States, present in ∼25% to 35% of individuals across major ethnic groups, making it one of the most frequently encountered mismatches in allogeneic transplantation.20,21 Validation in formal steroid-refractory acute GVHD models and extension of this allele-targeted strategy to other common HLA mismatches represent essential next steps toward broadly applicable precision immunotherapy.

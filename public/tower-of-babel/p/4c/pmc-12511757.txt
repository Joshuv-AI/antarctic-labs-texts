Risk Factors for Pediatric Intensive Care Unit Admission in Children with Human Metapneumovirus Infections
Authors: Yoonsun Yoon, Esther Park, Joon-sik Choi, Soon Young Hwang, Sohee Son, Joonbum Cho, Yae-Jean Kim
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHuman metapneumovirus (hMPV) frequently causes respiratory tract infections in children. Although hMPV infections are usually mild and self-limited, they may cause significant morbidity and mortality. However, data on pediatric hospitalizations due to hMPV infection are limited.Materials and MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed the data of patients aged 18 or younger hospitalized due to hMPV infection at a tertiary hospital between 2014 and 2019.ResultsIn total, 266 pediatric patients were admitted for hMPV infection. The incidence of hospitalizations due to hMPV infections was 6.4 per 1,000 hospitalized children, and the incidence of pediatric intensive care unit (PICU) admissions due to hMPV was 4.1 per 1,000 PICU-admitted children. The median age was 2.2 years (42 days–18.5 years), with 125 patients (47.0%) younger than two years old. Twenty-eight patients (10.5%) were treated in the PICU, and five of them (17.9%) died. The proportion of patients with underlying diseases was higher in the PICU group than in the general ward group (85.7% vs. 63.4%, P=0.02). The proportions of cardiologic diseases (21.4% vs. 8.8%, P=0.048) and genetic diseases (35.7% vs. 5.5%, P<0.001) were higher in the PICU group.ConclusionhMPV can cause severe infections that lead to PICU admission, particularly in patients with underlying conditions and fatal outcomes. Further data on hMPV infection in children admitted to the PICU and the overall disease burden in society are required.

INTRODUCTION

Human metapneumovirus (hMPV) is a single-stranded RNA pneumovirus first identified in the Netherlands in 2001 [1]. hMPV is a pathogenic respiratory virus associated with acute respiratory infections in children, older adults, and immunocompromised patients [23].

The clinical manifestations of hMPV infections range from asymptomatic upper and lower respiratory tract infections to respiratory failure, which can lead to mortality [34]. Studies have shown that hMPV is identified in 3.9–8.5% of hospitalized children presenting with clinical symptoms of upper or lower respiratory tract infections [5]. In the global study in 2018, 14 million hMPV-associated acute lower respiratory infections (ALRI) cases were estimated among children under 5 years old, along with 643,000 hMPV-associated hospital admissions and 7,700 hMPV-associated in-hospital deaths worldwide [6]. This burden is similar to, or slightly lower than, that of respiratory syncytial virus (RSV).

A large study of children under five years of age admitted to hospitals in the United States (US) with respiratory illness or fever showed that children with hMPV infections were more likely to require supplemental oxygen compared to their hMPV-negative counterparts (53% vs. 36%) and had a longer average stay in the pediatric intensive care unit (PICU) (4.5 days vs. 2 days) [789]. In Korea, hMPV has been detected in 5–20% of respiratory viral infections and is the fifth most commonly isolated respiratory virus, with seasonal peaks prominent in spring [1011121314].

However, data on hMPV infection in PICU-admitted patients are limited. Therefore, we investigated the clinical characteristics of children admitted to the PICU due to hMPV infection.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1. Patient and data collection

This was a retrospective study which included children aged ≤18 years who were hospitalized in the general ward and PICU with a symptomatic hMPV infection from January 2014 to December 2019. Symptoms included fever, respiratory symptoms, rashes, neurologic symptoms, and gastrointestinal manifestations. Diagnosing hMPV infection was confined to hMPV detection using multiplex respiratory virus real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR). RT-PCR samples were obtained from nasopharyngeal aspirates or bronchoalveolar lavage samples. Respiratory virus RT-PCR testing was performed in patients with respiratory symptoms such as cough, sputum, rhinorrhea, or fever.

Only the first episode was included. Medical records were reviewed for age, sex, birth weight, gestational age, underlying diseases, co-viral infections, symptoms, clinical diagnosis, and outcomes, including mortality (Supplementary Fig. 1). hMPV infection-associated PICU admission was defined as respiratory deterioration in patients with confirmed hMPV infection from -7 days to +2 days after PICU admission.

Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service reimbursement data were analyzed, and detailed descriptions are provided in the Supplementary Methods, Supplementary Results, and Supplementary Fig. 2.

2. Ethical statement

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Samsung Medical Center (IRB no. 2020-07-151). The requirement for informed consent was waived because of the retrospective nature of the chart review.

3. Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were summarized as medians and ranges, while categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages. Clinical outcomes based on risk factors were compared using the Mann–Whitney test or Kruskal–Wallis test for continuous variables and Fisher's exact test or Chi-square test for univariate analysis. Multiple logistic regression analyses were conducted to adjust for age at diagnosis, sex, gestational age, birth weight, hMPV co-viral infection, and PICU admission for multivariable analysis. Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics (version 27, SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided P-value of <0.05.

RESULTS

1. Patients

A total of 266 patients with symptomatic hMPV infections were identified during the study. Of these, 238 (89.5%) were treated in the general ward, while 28 (10.5%) were treated in the PICU.

Throughout the study period of six years, 41,366 pediatric patients were admitted to the hospital (6,894 patients per year). The incidence of hospitalizations due to hMPV infections was 6.4 per 1,000 hospitalized children per year (range, 5.4 to 9.6 per 1,000 hospitalized children per year). Among 6,749 pediatric patients admitted to the PICU, the incidence of hMPV-related PICU admission was 4.1 per 1,000 PICU admissions per year (range: 1.5 to 6.2 per 1,000 PICU admissions per year) (Supplementary Fig. 1). Among the 266 patients with confirmed hMPV infection, 40 (15.0%) were diagnosed more than 2 days after hospital admission, and thus, the possibility of nosocomial transmission could not be ruled out.

Table 1 summarizes patient characteristics. The median age was 2.2 years (42 days–18.5 years), with 125 (47.0%) being males. The proportion of patients with underlying diseases was higher in the PICU group compared to the general ward group (85.7% vs. 63.4%, P=0.02). Among the underlying diseases, the proportions of patients with cardiologic and genetic diseases were notably higher in the PICU group (21.4 vs. 8.8%, P=0.048; 35.7% vs. 5.5%, P<0.001, respectively).

Among treated patients in the PICU, 11 patients (39.3%) were co-infected with other viruses. One patient had a history of adenovirus infection a month prior and was still co-detected with adenovirus during this hospitalization. Another patient was treated for febrile seizures with a co-infection of influenza B and hMPV. Parainfluenza was detected in a liver transplant patient. Additionally, the rest of the patients were detected positive for rhinoviruses and bocaviruses (Table 2).

4. Clinical outcomes of PICU patients

Of the 266 patients, 28 (10.5%) required PICU treatment. The median length of stay in the PICU was 15 days (2–181 days). Twenty-one patients (21/28, 75.0%) received mechanical ventilator support, and 11 (39.3%) required inotropes. Four patients (14.3%) were treated with ribavirin. Oral ribavirin was used in four immunocompromised patients. They were two patients with acute myeloid leukemia, one liver transplant recipient, and one hematopoietic stem cell transplant recipient. Two patients underwent continuous renal replacement therapy, but both died. Additionally, two patients received extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO); one of them successfully recovered (Supplementary Table 1).

Among the 28 patients, five died within three months (5/28, 17.9%), and two died within one month (2/28, 7.1%) The overall case-fatality rate for ICU-cared patients was 17.9% (5/28), and the case-fatality rate among all hospitalized patients due to hMPV infection was 1.9% (5/266). All five deceased patients had underlying diseases. In four cases, hMPV was the primary cause of death. Patient 1 was a 2-month-old infant admitted to the PICU for the progression of liver hemangioendothelioma. She developed newly hMPV-related pneumonia and died of respiratory failure. Patient 2 was a 6-month-old infant with severe bronchopulmonary dysplasia who contracted hMPV at home. His condition deteriorated due to hMPV pneumonia, leading to his death despite ECMO treatment. Patient 3 died from complications following liver transplantation, including cytomegalovirus hepatitis and liver fibromatosis, with hMPV pneumonia potentially exacerbating the condition. hMPV infection occurred on day 35 post-liver transplantation, and the patient died on day 73. Patient 4 was an autologous hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) recipient. hMPV was detected 4 days before the second autologous HSCT (3 months after the first HSCT). Symptoms worsened the day after transplantation, and the patient was transferred to the intensive care unit, and the patient died in the third week after transplantation. Patient 5 died of multi-organ failure, including hMPV pneumonia, seizure, and kidney injury (Table 3).

DISCUSSION

This is the first study on hMPV infection in Korean children, with a specific focus on pediatric patients treated in PICU during the pre-coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic and the associated healthcare costs. This retrospective study identified 266 admitted pediatric patients with hMPV infection, including 28 patients treated in the PICU. Approximately 11% of patients required PICU treatment due to hMPV infection, and 18% of those in the PICU died.

In this study, the median age was 2.2 years, with 47.0% of patients being <2 years old and 29.3% of patients being <1 year old. In a previous Korean study on hMPV infection from 2011 to 2014, the mean age was 2.6 years, and 30.8% of patients were <2 years old [10]. Another study from 2011 to 2013 reported that 21.4% of patients were <1 year old, and 70.6% were <3 years-old [911]. The age distribution in our study is consistent with these findings.

In Korea, hMPV infections were detected between January and June [8]. During the study period, hMPV infections were detected from January to May. Peak detection of hMPV occurred between mid-January and mid-February in France and in March in Spain, and the highest number of cases per month was recorded from November to May in the United Kingdom (UK) [151617]. In the US, winter seasonality with peak activity was observed between February and April [218] hMPV was not detected during the COVID-19 pandemic (2020–2021) but re-emerged in Korea in the fall of 2022, primarily from October to November 2022 and from March to August 2023 [81920]. Additional long-term data on post-COVID-19 hMPV epidemics are needed.

In this study, we analyzed 28 patients treated in the PICU for hMPV infection. Of these, 10.5% were admitted to the PICU, and 17.9% of them died. Data on ICU treatment for hMPV infection are limited.

In Patient 4, the progression of hMPV pneumonia after autologous HSCT infection highlights the potential risks associated with proceeding to transplantation without addressing pre-existing viral infections (Table 3). Careful screening and consideration of delaying transplantation may be warranted. hMPV infection has been increasingly recognized as an important cause of severe respiratory illness in HSCT recipients, with reported cases showing high morbidity and mortality rates [21]. In this study, ribavirin was administered only to patients with hMPV infection requiring PICU care due to immunocompromised conditions, such as HSCT or chemotherapy, in accordance with evidence from prior studies [2122].

According to a study in the UK from 2006 to 2014, hMPV accounted for 0.08% of total PICU admissions, or 0.8 per 1000 PICU admissions. Among 114 patients treated in the PICU during that study period, five (4.4%) died. Our study observed a higher mortality rate (11.4%), likely due to the high proportion of patients with underlying diseases (85.7%). The UK data did not specify underlying conditions in mortality cases. The mean age at PICU admission in the UK study was 2.8 years, similar to our data [15]. In a US study on hospitalizations due to hMPV infection from 2014 to 2019, the hospitalization rates for hMPV were significantly higher than those for RSV and influenza. ICU admissions, deaths, and hospital-acquired infections due to hMPV were similar to those for RSV and influenza. Among hMPV ICU admissions in the US, 82% had an underlying medical condition, with chronic lung disease being the most common (29%) [23]. However, in our study, the most common underlying condition was genetic disease, and the proportions of cardiologic and genetic diseases were higher in the PICU group. Several patients with underlying diseases had genetic disorders, including Down syndrome (n=3), Caroli syndrome, Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease, 18q deletion, 8p duplication, Rubinstein-Taybi syndrome, Alagille syndrome, Gaucher disease, and Kabuki syndrome. Although many had cardiac or pulmonary involvement, they were classified separately due to the multisystem nature and complexity of genetic conditions. Several studies have associated hMPV with severe pneumonia requiring ICU admission in older adults or patients with comorbidities and in healthy participants [1624]. In an adult study in France, the mortality rate due to hMPV was 4%, with hMPV-infected patients presenting with more chronic cardiac conditions and acute heart failure during hospitalization than influenza-infected patients [17].

Detecting other respiratory viruses with hMPV was a significant risk factor for PICU admission in our study. Influenza, adenovirus, and parainfluenza viruses can cause serious lower respiratory tract infections in children. However, six patients treated in the PICU were co-infected with rhinovirus. Although rhinovirus has traditionally been considered a cause of only upper respiratory tract infections [25], accumulating data highlight its role in serious lower respiratory virus infections, especially in immunocompromised states [262728]. Therefore, further research on the effect of co-infection with other viruses, including rhinovirus, on hMPV infection is needed.

In a US study from 2003 to 2009, the estimated annual burden of outpatient visits associated with hMPV infection was 55 clinic visits and 13 emergency department visits per 1,000 children [9]. In 2018, among children younger than five years globally, 643,000 hMPV-associated hospital admissions, 7,700 hMPV-associated in-hospital deaths (2,600–8,800), and 16,100 overall (hospital and community) hMPV-associated ALRI deaths (5,700–88,000) occurred [6]. A retrospective cohort study from 2007 to 2013 at a primary children’s hospital in the US estimated the mean annual healthcare cost for all hMPV-associated hospitalizations to be US $277 million, with a median total hospital cost per hMPV-infected patient of US $5,513 (IQR, $3,850–$9,946) [18]. In a UK study, the total annual cost of PICU admissions due to hMPV infection was estimated to be between £2,256,823 (US $2,864,246) and £3,997,823 (US $5,073,837) from 2006 to 2014 [15]. In our study, the healthcare costs per person due to hMPV infection ranged from $562.8 (₩624,392) to $769.9 (₩898,096). The annual costs are rising due to the increasing burden of hMPV admissions in Korea (₩1,242,736,000 to ₩2,149,863,000, US $894,589 to $1,547,589).

Recently, monoclonal antibodies against RSV, nirsevimab, were approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in 2023 and by the Korean Ministry of Food and Drug Safety in 2024 [2930]. However, no vaccines or monoclonal antibodies have been approved for the treatment of hMPV infections. A vaccine against hMPV and parainfluenza-3 was tested in a Phase 1 study [31]. The development of neutralizing monoclonal antibodies against hMPV has accelerated in recent years due to breakthroughs in the structural biology of the viral fusion (F) protein and experience with RSV and other enveloped viruses [323334].

Our study had several limitations due to its retrospective nature. First, this single-referral center study included limited data for analyzing hospitalized patients. Most children with upper respiratory infections due to hMPV do not require admission; therefore, our data represented more severe cases of hMPV infection. Moreover, since this study was conducted in a tertiary care hospital, a higher proportion of patients with specific conditions such as immunocompromised status or genetic disorders was observed. Second, we could not determine the subtype of hMPV infection or the viral load. According to a Korean study from 2011 to 2013, the hMPV genotype distribution was similar in severity. However, our study showed that hMPV infection was associated with PICU admission and mortality in children, which has not been described in previous Korean studies.

In conclusion, hMPV is an important pathogen that can cause significant morbidity and mortality, especially in those with underlying conditions. Further studies and increased awareness are needed, along with additional efforts to develop vaccines or antiviral treatments against hMPV.

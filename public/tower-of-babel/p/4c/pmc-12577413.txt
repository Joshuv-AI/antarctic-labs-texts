miR-196a-5p promotes the proliferation, migration, and invasion of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma by targeting LIFR
Authors: Junhe Zhang, Fan Liang, Weifeng Wang, Yiran Qin, Xiaoshu Hu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEsophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is a prevalent and aggressive malignancy. While microRNAs (miRNAs) are known to play a critical regulatory role in the occurrence and progression of ESCC, the underlying mechanisms remain incompletely understood. Therefore, the aim of this study was to investigate the effect of miR-196a-5p on the proliferation, migration, and invasion of ESCC and its mechanism.MethodsThe role of miR-196a-5p in the progression of ESCC was explored with CCK8, colony formation, and Transwell assays, and Western blot analysis was used to detect epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT). The regulatory effect of miR-196a-5p on ESCC was further verified through a xenograft experiment on nude mice.ResultsmiR-196a-5p was significantly upregulated in ESCC, and the overexpression of miR-196a-5p considerably promoted the proliferation, migration, and invasion of ESCC cells in vitro. The expression level of E-cadherin decreased, while N-cadherin and vimentin increased, indicating that the EMT of ESCC cells was promoted. Conversely, miR-196a-5p downregulation had the opposite effects. In vivo experiments confirmed that miR-196a-5p overexpression promoted ESCC proliferation and EMT. Furthermore, we identified leukemia inhibitory factor receptor (LIFR) as a target gene of miR-196a-5p in ESCC and was negatively regulated by miR-196a-5p. LIFR overexpression can rescue the ability of miR-196a-5p to promote ESCC cell proliferation and invasion, and offset the cell migration and EMT caused by miR-196a-5p.ConclusionsmiR-196a-5p promotes the proliferation, migration, and invasion of ESCC by targeting LIFR and regulating the EMT, highlighting its potential as a diagnostic biomarker and therapeutic target.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12935-025-04024-1.Keywords: Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), MiR-196a-5p, Leukemia inhibitory factor receptor (LIFR), Epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT)

Introduction

Esophageal cancer, a major global health concern, ranks as the seventh leading cause of cancer-related deaths [1]. The two primary subtypes are esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) and esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC). While EAC incidence has risen, ESCC remains the predominant form worldwide [2], typically arising in the mid-esophagus and strongly linked to alcohol consumption, tobacco use, and genetic predisposition. In ESCC, the esophageal epithelium progresses from a low-grade intraepithelial neoplasia (dysplasia) to invasive carcinoma [3]. Despite advances in chemotherapy in patients with unresectable or metastatic ESCC, overall outcomes remain poor, and the median overall survival is 10–12 months [4], and the 5 year survival rate remains low (only 15%–20%) [5]. Consequently, a thorough understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying the occurrence and development of ESCC is crucial for developing improved prevention, diagnostic tools, and more effective therapies.

Environmental factors often drive epigenetic alterations, which are central to esophageal cancer initiation and progression and frequently lead to dysregulation of cancer-related signaling pathways [6]. MicroRNA (miRNA) regulation has emerged as a key epigenetic mechanism. miRNAs are highly conserved, endogenous, non-coding 22-nucleotide RNAs that post-transcriptionally regulate gene expression by targeting mRNAs. By binding to the 3′ untranslated region (3′ UTR) of target mRNAs, miRNAs either inhibit translation or promote mRNA degradation. Moreover, their stability in tissues and body fluids makes miRNAs promising biomarkers, as they also play crucial roles in cell proliferation and differentiation [3]. The precursor of miR-196a-5p is miR-196a, which comprises two isoforms: microRNA-196a-1 and microRNA-196a-2. miR-196a-5p is a tumor promoter in a variety of cancers. Qu et al. found that miR-196a-5p overexpression was associated with the methylation of MIR196A2 and promoted the malignant biological behavior of glioma [7]. Yao et al. demonstrated that miR-196a-5p, which is an exosome of cancer fibroblasts, enhanced the radioresistance of lung cancer cells by downregulating NFKBIA [8]. miR-196a-5p has pleiotropic properties and can function as a tumor suppressor, showing potential as a target for the diagnosis and treatment of testicular germ cell tumors and osteosarcoma [9, 10]. Therefore, while miR-196a-5p holds considerable diagnostic and prognostic promise in cancer, its specific molecular mechanisms in ESCC remain poorly understood.

The leukemia inhibitory factor receptor (LIFR) is a transmembrane receptor of multiple-cytokine leukemia inhibitory factor (LIF) [11], which is involved in angiogenesis, cancer progression, stem cell development and regulation [12]. LIFR has multiple ligands, and LIF is the most important among IL-6 family members with high affinity. Although LIFR does not possess tyrosine kinase activity, the membrane receptor complex composed of LIFR and gp130 is closely related to the JAK-TYK cytoplasmic tyrosine kinase family, thereby facilitating downstream signaling [13]. The interaction of LIF/LIFR triggers a variety of signaling pathways, such as JAK/STAT, Hippo, and MAPK [13–16], which affect malignant phenotypes, such as cancer proliferation and migration. Whether the LIF/LIFR axis stimulates or inhibits cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival depends on cell type. LIFR activation and downstream STAT3 signaling render breast tumor cells dormant [17]. LIFR can be used as a protumor gene that can inhibit the phosphorylation level of Hippo–YAP signaling pathway–related proteins and regulate downstream gene signaling pathways; these activities inhibit the metastasis of cervical cancer cells by reducing LIFR expression levels [18]. Zhang et al. found that low LIFR expression is a predictor of poor overall survival in patients with clear cell renal carcinoma and lung adenocarcinoma and a better predictor of overall survival in patients with gastric cancer [19]. Given the varying expression patterns of LIFR among different tumor types, its mechanisms of action are likely distinct. Therefore, dedicated studies are necessary to elucidate the specific role of LIFR in ESCC.

In the present study, our in vitro and in vivo experiments demonstrate that miR-196a-5p overexpression promotes the proliferation, migration, invasion, and epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) of ESCC. We also identified LIFR as a direct target of miR-196a-5p. Functional rescue assays further confirmed that miR-196a-5p regulates cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and EMT of ESCC cells through LIFR. These findings suggest that miR-196a-5p is a potential diagnostic biomarker and that targeting the miR-196a-5p/LIFR axis may offer a novel therapeutic strategy for ESCC.

Materials and methods

Differential expression and survival analysis

The expression levels of miR-196a-5p in the ENCORI/starBase database (https://rnasysu.com/encori/) were predicted in 162 esophageal cancers and 11 normal tissues. The overall survival of miR-196a-5p in esophageal cancer was detected in 81 low-expression samples and 81 high-expression samples.

Cell culture and transfection

Human esophageal epithelial carcinoma cell lines EC-109, ECA-109, and KYSE-150 were cryopreserved in our laboratory (Xinxiang Medical University, Xinxiang, China). The cells were cultured in a medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum (WISENT, Nanjing, China) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin RPMI-1640 (WISENT, Nanjing, China) in a humidified cell culture incubator with 5% CO2 at 37℃.

An miR-196a-5p inhibitor and control miRNA (Inhibitor NC) were designed and synthesized by RiboBio, Guangzhou, China, and miR-196a-5p overexpressing lentivirus and control lentivirus (miRNA-NC) were designed and synthesized by General Biol, Chuzhou, China. EC-109, ECA-109, and KYSE-150 were plated in 2 × 106 six-well plates, and the cells were transfected with Lipofectamine 2000 and polybrene when the cells were 60%–70% confluent, and the lentivirus-infected cells were cultured in a puromycin-containing medium for 14 days. In addition, the multiplicity of infection (MOI) values of EC-109, ECA-109, and KYSE-150 were 15, 35, and 40, respectively. MOI was calculated as follows: MOI = (viral titer × viral volume)/number of cells.

Cell viability assay

The proliferation ability of ESCC cells was detected with a CCK8 cell proliferation assay kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). After 48 h of cell transfection, 2 × 103 cells were seeded in 96-well plates, 10 µL of CCK8 reagent was added to each well after 6 h, and the cells were incubated in a 37 °C constant temperature incubator for 2 h. The absorbance was read at 450 nm with a microplate reader, and this point was set to 0 h. The absorbance at four time points (0, 24, 48, and 72 h) was recorded, and the cell growth curves were plotted.

Wound healing assay

ESCC cells were seeded in six-well plates, and when the cells grew to 95% confluency, vertical lines were drawn with sterile 1000 µL pipette tips. The cells detached after several streaks were washed with PBS two times. The cells were observed under a microscope, and photograph spots were marked. EC-109 and ECA-109 cells were cultured and incubated for 24 and 48 h. As for the KYSE-150 cells, the cell culture times were 12 and 24 h. Cell migration ability was measured with Image J and analyzed.

Transwell assay

Migration and invasion assays were performed using Transwell plates (24 wells). For the Transwell migration assay, transfected ESCC cells were cultured in a serum-free culture medium, and 200 µL of a cell suspension (1 × 106 cells/mL) was seeded into a Transwell plate chamber. For the Transwell invasion experiments, 60 µL of Matrigel diluted with serum-free medium (BD Biosciences, New Jersey, USA) was added to the upper chamber, and the cell suspension was added. Then, 500 µL of 1640 medium containing 30% fetal bovine serum was added to the lower chamber of each Transwell chamber and routinely incubated for 48 h in an incubator. The cells were washed three times with PBS, fixed with methanol for 30 min, stained with 0.5% crystal violet for 15 min, and the cells were quantified and imaged using a light microscope.

Western blot

Total protein was extracted with a RIPA extraction reagent (Beyotime, Shanghai, China), and the proteins were separated through 10% SDS-PAGE gel electrophoresis, transferred to PVDF membranes, and blocked with 5% skimmed milk powder on a 37 °C shaker for 1 h, washed three times with TBST buffer, and incubated with primary antibodies E-cadherin (1:2000; 20874-1-AP; Proteintech, Wuhan, China), N-cadherin (1:2000; 22018-1-AP; Proteintech), Vimentin (1:10000; 10366-1-AP; Proteintech), LIFR (1:2000; 22779-1-AP; Proteintech), GAPDH (1:2000; 60004-1-Ig; Proteintech) on a shaker at room temperature for 30 min and overnight at 4 °C. Then, the bands were visualized with a chemiluminescence kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China).

Targeted gene prediction and validation

The target gene of miR-196a-5p was predicted using the ENCORI/starBase database (https://rnasysu.com/encori/), and then the relationship between LIFR and miR-196a-5p was verified by dual-luciferase reporter assay. The 3′-UTR of the LIFR potential binding site was cloned into the luciferase reporter vector pmirGLO, and the wild-type LIFR (LIFR-WT) and mutant LIFR (LIFR-MUT) plasmids were constructed. Cells were co-transfected with LIFR-WT and LIFR-MUT 3′-UTR and miRNA mimics by using Lipofectamine 2000 (Biosharp, Guangzhou, China). After 48 h, luciferase activity was detected using a dual-luciferase reporter assay kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The activity of firefly luciferase was compared with that of Renilla luciferase.

Nude mouse xenograft tumor model

Female BALB/c-nude mice (5–6 weeks old) were purchased from SiPeiFu, Beijing, China. Ten mice were randomly divided into miR-NC and miR-196a-5p groups. ECA-109 cells (2 × 106) were injected subcutaneously on the back of each mouse. Six days after inoculation, tumor volume was measured with vernier calipers every 3 days for 21 consecutive days, and tumor growth curves were plotted. All animal studies were approved by the animal experiment ethics committee of Xinxiang Medical University. The formula for calculating tumor volume was length × width2/2.

Immunohistochemistry

At the end of the experiment, the mice were euthanized, and tumor tissues were collected, soaked in 4% paraformaldehyde for fixation, paraffin embedded, and sectioned after 48 h. The slides were washed with PBS. Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked with 3% H2O2 for 10 min, and antigenicity was determined after boiling in EDTA buffer (pH = 9) for 20 min. After incubation with bovine serum albumin for 20 min at room temperature, the primary antibody E-cadherin (1:200; 20874-1-AP; Proteintech), Ki67 (1:200; 27309-1-AP; Proteintech), Vimentin (1:200; 10366-1-AP; Proteintech), LIFR (1:200; 22779-1-AP; Proteintech) was incubated overnight at 4, followed by 30 min at room temperature with enzyme-labeled secondary antibodies and stained with DAB.

Statistical analysis

All data were statistically analyzed with GraphPad Prism 8.0 software, and results were presented as mean ± standard deviation of at least three independent experiments. The t-test was used for statistical treatment and comparison between groups, and one-way ANOVA was used for the analysis of differences among multiple groups. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant, and P < 0.01 and P < 0.001 were considered highly statistically significant.

Results

miR-196a-5p is upregulated in esophageal cancer tissues

To assess the association of miR-196a-5p with ESCC malignancy, we analyzed miR-196a-5p expression levels in esophageal cancer and normal tissues using ENCORI/starBase. This analysis revealed a significant upregulation of miR-196a-5p in esophageal cancer tissues (Fig. 1A). Kaplan–Meier analysis showed that higher miR-196a-5p expression was associated with significantly shorter overall survival (Fig. 1B). These findings suggest that miR-196a-5p promotes malignant behavior in esophageal cancer.

Enhanced expression of miR-196a-5p in esophageal cancer tissues (A) miR-196a-5p was highly expressed in esophageal cancer tissues (n = 162) compared with normal esophageal tissues (n = 11) in ENCORI/starBas. (B) Kaplan-Meier survival curves in patients with esophageal cancer with high and low miR-196a-5p expression and low miR-196a-5p expression. ***P < 0.001

Upregulation of miR-196a-5p promoted the proliferation, migration, invasion, and EMT of ESCC cells

Based on the observed miR-196a-5p expression in ESCC cells, we used an miR-196a-5p overexpression lentivirus to infect ESCC cells and assess its effects on cellular function. CCK8 assays revealed that miR-196a-5p overexpression promoted ESCC cell proliferation (Fig. 2A). Furthermore, wound healing assays showed that miR-196a-5p overexpression enhanced ESCC cell migration (Fig. 2B-C). Consistent with these findings, Transwell migration and invasion assays demonstrated that miR-196a-5p overexpression significantly increased ESCC cell migration and invasion capacity (Fig. 2D-E). EMT is a process by which epithelial cancer cells lose cell-cell adhesion and acquire mesenchymal characteristics, promoting tumor invasion and metastasis [20]. Western blot analysis confirmed that miR-196a-5p overexpression promoted EMT progression in ESCC cells (Fig. 2F-G). Collectively, these findings indicate that miR-196a-5p overexpression enhances the proliferation, migration, invasion, and EMT of ESCC cells.

Downregulation of miR-196a-5p inhibits the proliferation, migration, invasion, and EMT of ESCC cells

To further investigate the role of miR-196a-5p in the development of ESCC, we transfected ESCC cells with either miR-196a-5p inhibitor and inhibitor NC for repeat experiments. CCK8 assays revealed that downregulation of miR-196a-5p inhibited ESCC cell proliferation (Fig. 3A). Similarly, wound healing and Transwell assays showed that inhibition of miR-196a-5p reduced ESCC cell migration and invasion (Fig. 3B-E). Western blot analysis indicated that downregulation of miR-196a-5p attenuated ESCC EMT progression (Fig. 3F-G). Taken together, these results demonstrate that downregulation of miR-196a-5p inhibits the proliferation, migration, invasion, and EMT of ESCC cells.

LIFR is a direct target of miR-196a-5p in ESCC cells

To understand the molecular mechanism by which miR-196a-5p promotes ESCC progression, we predicted the downstream target of miR-196a-5p by using the ENCORI/starBase database. In 162 clinical samples from patients with esophageal cancer, LIFR expression was lower in esophageal cancer tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues, reduced LIFR expression correlated with decreased patient survival, and miR-196a-5p expression showed a linear negative correlation with LIFR in esophageal cancer (Fig. 4A-B) [21]. Figure 4C illustrates the predicted binding site of miR-196a-5p on the 3′ UTR of LIFR mRNA. To validate this interaction, we constructed pmirGLO plasmids and a pmirGLO plasmid containing either the WT or MUT LIFR 3′ UTR for dual-luciferase reporter assays. miR-196a-5p mimics significantly reduced luciferase activity in cells transfected with the LIFR 3’UTR-WT plasmid but had no effect on cells transfected with the LIFR 3’ UTR-MUT plasmid (Fig. 4D). These results demonstrate that miR-196a-5p inhibits luciferase activity at the WT binding site. To further evaluate the effect of miR-196a-5p on LIFR, we examined LIFR protein expression under different miR-196a-5p expression conditions. As expected, ectopic expression of miR-196a-5p via lentiviral infection in ECA-109 and KYSE-150 cells significantly reduced LIFR protein levels, while inhibition of miR-196a-5p resulted in increased LIFR protein expression (Fig. 4E-F). Collectively, these findings indicate that LIFR is a direct target of miR-196a-5p.

LIFR overexpression rescued the promoting effect of miR-196a-5p on ESCC proliferation and invasion and reversed cell migration and EMT progression

We have shown that miR-196a-5p enhances the proliferation, migration, and invasion of ESCC cells and promotes EMT, and directly targets the 3′ UTR of LIFR. However, the functional role of LIFR in ESCC remained unexplored. To address this, we transfected ECA-109 cells with a LIFR overexpression plasmid and assessed the resulting phenotypic changes. Overexpression of LIFR led to increased expression of LIFR and E-cadherin, and decreased expression of N-cadherin and vimentin. (Fig. 5A-B). To determine whether LIFR functions as a downstream effector of miR-196a-5p, we co-transfected ECA-109 cells overexpressing miR-196a-5p with either a LIFR expression plasmid or a control plasmid (LIFR-NC). Western blot analysis revealed that LIFR overexpression increased LIFR and E-cadherin protein levels while decreasing vimentin protein levels, compared to the untransfected and LIFR-NC plasmid control groups (Fig. 5C-D). These results suggest that LIFR overexpression can reverse the EMT-promoting effects of miR-196a-5p. To assess the functional consequences of LIFR overexpression, we performed a series of cell function assays. CCK8 assays showed that LIFR overexpression inhibited ESCC cell proliferation, effectively rescuing the pro-proliferative effect of miR-196a-5p (Fig. 5E-F). Similarly, wound healing and Transwell assays demonstrated that LIFR overexpression attenuated ESCC cell migration and invasion (Fig. 5G-H). Consistent with these findings, LIFR overexpression in miR-196a-5p-overexpressing ESCC cells rescued the ability of miR-196a-5p to promote ESCC cell invasion and reversed the enhanced cell migration (Fig. 5I-J). These results suggest a potential role for LIFR in mediating the effects of miR-196a-5p on ESCC cell behavior.

Overexpression of miR-196a-5p promotes ESCC growth in vivo

To determine the in vivo effects of miR-196a-5p on ESCC tumor growth, ten mice were randomly divided into two groups, and 2 × 106 ECA-109 cells stably expressing either miR-196a-5p and miR-NC were injected subcutaneously into the back of each mouse (n = 5 per group). Tumor volume was measured every three days, starting six days post-injection, for a total of 21 days. The results showed that overexpression of miR-196a-5p significantly promoted tumor growth compared to the miR-NC control group (Fig. 6A). Immunohistochemical analysis of tumor tissues revealed decreased expression of LIFR and E-cadherin, and increased expression of vimentin and Ki67, in the miR-196a-5p overexpression group compared to the miR-NC group (Fig. 6B). These results further validate the pro-cancerous effects of miR-196a-5p in ESCC, consistent with the findings from our in vitro experiments.

Discussion

ESCC, the predominant subtype of esophageal cancer, is a particularly aggressive forms of human squamous cell carcinoma [22]. A deeper understanding of the underlying molecular mechanisms in ESCC is crucial for identifying novel biomarkers that can facilitate early diagnosis and effective treatment strategies.

MiRNA dysregulation is implicated in diverse malignant processes, including apoptosis, autophagy, EMT, invasion, and metastasis, highlighting miRNAs as potential therapeutic targets [23]. Abnormal miRNA expression is frequently observed in ESCC, influencing its occurrence and development. For example, miR-132-3p promotes ESCC progression by targeting KCNK2 [24], while miR-497 overexpression inhibits ESCC tumor growth and enhances chemosensitivity [25]. Similar to other miRNAs, miR-196a-5p acts as a tumor-associated regulator in various cancers. Zhu et al. demonstrated that miR-196a-5p targets FOXO1 to promote the progression of estrogen-dependent endometrium [26], and previous studies have shown miR-196a-5p dysregulation and oncogenic activity in ESCC [27]. Our bioinformatics analysis using ENCORI/starBase confirmed miR-196a-5p was overexpressed in esophageal cancer tissues and its association with poor prognosis. Furthermore, our cell experiments demonstrated that miR-196a-5p overexpression promotes the proliferation, migration, and invasion of ESCC cells, while its inhibition has the opposite effect. Thus, miR-196a-5p represents a promising biomarker for ESCC.

EMT is a key process in cancer metastasis, enabling epithelial tumor cells to acquire mesenchymal characteristics and promote tumor cell dissemination [28]. A hallmark of EMT is the loss of E-cadherin expression and the concomitant upregulation of N-cadherin expression [20, 29]. Vimentin, a protein associated with cell motility, is also central to EMT-mediated metastasis [30]. miRNAs play a significant role in regulating EMT. For example, miR-21-5p accelerates EMT and inhibits apoptosis in laryngeal cancer cells [31], while miR-182/183-5p promotes EMT by targeting hydroxprostaglandin dehydrogenase in cholangiocarcinoma [32]. In our study, miR-196a-5p overexpression in ESCC cells downregulated E-cadherin expression and upregulated N-cadherin and vimentin expression. Conversely, miR-196a-5p inhibition upregulated E-cadherin and downregulated N-cadherin and vimentin. These findings indicate that miR-196a-5p promotes EMT in ESCC. Consistent with this, immunohistochemistry revealed decreased E-cadherin and increased vimentin and Ki67 expression in tumors, supporting the pro-malignant role of miR-196a-5p in ESCC in vivo.

The tumor microenvironment (TME) is a key factor influencing antitumor immune responses and cancer treatment efficacy, playing a crucial role in the progression of ESCC [33]. Fang et al. developed an analytical tool called “the immuno-oncology biological research (IOBR)” to explore the TME and its role in antitumor immunity [34, 35]. Within the ESCC tumor microenvironment, myeloid cells (such as tumor-associated macrophages) engage in bidirectional interactions with epithelial-derived tumor cells via cytokines and signaling molecules, jointly shaping an immunosuppressive landscape. Chemokine axes mediate the recruitment and spatial distribution of these myeloid cells and other immune cells. Abnormalities in adhesion/ECM pathways create physical conditions for tumor cell migration by disrupting intercellular junctions and promoting matrix remodeling. The EMT process not only confers invasive capabilities but also couples with immune evasion, suppressing T cell function through mechanisms like PD-L1 upregulation. As a potential anti-tumor hub, loss of function in the LIFR signaling axis may initiate the above multi-mechanism cascade: it uncouples suppression of EMT, pro-tumor chemokines (e.g., CCL2, CXCL8) and matrix metalloproteinases (e.g., MMPs), while simultaneously promoting M2 myeloid cell polarization. This synergistically drives a cascade reaction: tumor cells acquire migratory capacity while actively sculpting a microenvironment conducive to their dissemination and evasion of host immune surveillance, ultimately accelerating the invasion-metastasis progression of ESCC [36, 37]. Future studies may incorporate in vitro co-culture models, such as co-culture with macrophages or T cells, to further elucidate the molecular details of bidirectional regulation between EMT and the immune microenvironment at the cellular interaction level. This approach will provide empirical evidence for developing combined therapeutic strategies targeting this interactive network.

To elucidate the mechanism by which miR-196a-5p promotes ESCC malignancy, we utilized the ENCORI/starBase bioinformatics database to screen for potential miR-196a-5p target genes. This analysis identified LIFR, which was downregulated in esophageal cancer, and the low expression of LIFR correlated with reduced patient survival and negatively correlated with miR-196a-5p. Subsequent dual luciferase assays, Western blot analyses, and in vivo experiments confirmed that LIFR is a direct target of miR-196a-5p. Notably, LIFR overexpression inhibited the malignant phenotype of ESCC cells, rescued cell proliferation and invasion suppressed by miR-196a-5p overexpression, and reversed cell migration and EMT progression. Therefore, LIFR functions as a crucial downstream target mediating the oncogenic function of miR-196a-5p in ESCC (Fig. 7).

miR-196a-5p promotes the proliferation, migration, and invasion of ESCC by targeting LIFR and regulating the EMT

LIFR exhibits pleiotropic effects, exerting opposing functions even within the same cancer type depending on the specific LIF/LIFR-activated signaling pathways [38]. The LIF/LIFR interaction can trigger multiple pathways, including JAK/STAT, Hippo, and MAPK, with the ultimate outcome highly dependent on cell type. For example, in gastric cancer, LIFR activates Hippo kinases (e.g., MST1/2) to inhibit YAP nuclear translocation, thus exhibiting anti-cancer stem cell properties [39]. Conversely, in cervical and breast cancer, low LIFR expression promotes tumor growth by suppressing Hippo-YAP phosphorylation [40]. This pleiotropy suggests that LIFR function is determined by the tissue-specific dominance of its downstream signaling. In ESCC, miR-196a-5p-mediated LIFR inhibition may disrupt both JAK/STAT3 and Hippo-YAP pathways, thereby promoting EMT and tumor invasion. While we did not directly examine Hippo-YAP pathway changes in this study, based on the existing literature, we speculate that LIFR may inhibit tumor progression in ESCC by regulating the Hippo-YAP pathway, and that miR-196a-5p’s oncogenic effects are partly due to the interference with this pathway. Future studies are needed to elucidate the dominant downstream pathway of LIFR and its interaction with STAT3 in ESCC, providing a rationale for targeted therapies.

miRNA-based approaches may represent a new promising therapeutic approach. Currently, several therapeutic strategies targeting miRNAs have been developed in the clinic. Increasing evidence suggests that miRNAs regulate cancer development in a conventional manner by modulating signaling pathways and factors. The findings of this study confirmed that miR-196a-5p is a proto-oncogene that promotes ESCC proliferation, migration, invasion and EMT progression, and identified LIFR as a direct target of miR-196a-5p in ESCC for the first time. These findings highlight the importance of miR-196a-5p or miR-196a-5p/LIFR axis as an effective target for the treatment of ESCC, and lay the experimental foundation for exploring therapeutic targets and mechanisms of action in ESCC. We try to develop miR-196a-5p-based therapeutic agents by modern scientific and technological means, including nanocapsules and nanocarries, liposomes and PEGylated vesicles, and apply them to the clinical treatment of ESCC.

Nevertheless, the study has inevitable limitations. Firstly, the limited sample size in the animal experiments may affect statistical power, future studies will address this by expanding the sample size. Secondly, the current association between miR-196a-5p expression and poor prognosis is primarily based on bioinformatics analysis. To strengthen the clinical relevance of this observation, future research will incorporate data from independent, publicly available datasets such as TCGA or GEO. Alternatively, we plan to validate miR-196a-5p expression directly in a larger cohort of patient samples.

Conclusion

In summary, miR-196a-5p promotes ESCC proliferation, migration, invasion, and EMT in vitro and in vivo, functioning as an oncogene. We identified LIFR as a direct miR-196a-5p target in ESCC. These findings suggest that targeting miR-196a-5p or the miR-196a-5p/LIFR axis offers a potential therapeutic strategy for ESCC.

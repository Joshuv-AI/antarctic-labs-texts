The AGPS regulatory variant rs113671272 confers esophageal squamous cell carcinoma susceptibility through allele-specific MEIS3 binding and NF-κB activation in Chinese populations
Authors: Xumeng Fang, Jiaying Deng, Ming Liu, Wen Tan, Mengyun Wang, Yuanna Wu, Kuaile Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundEsophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) demonstrates pronounced ethnic disparities, being prevalent in Asian populations. However, the genetic mechanisms underlying this disparity remain poorly understood. This study aims to characterise the functional contribution of the regulatory variant rs113671272 in alkylglycerone phosphate synthase (AGPS) to ESCC susceptibility and prognosis in Chinese cohorts.MethodsWe conducted a two-phase genome-wide association study (GWAS) comprising 6172 participants to identify risk loci. Functional validation was conducted through Cleavage Under Targets & Tagmentation–quantitative PCR (CUT&Tag-qPCR) assay, Electrophoretic mobility shift assay (EMSA), and analyses of clinically annotated specimens with confirmed genotypes. In vitro and in vivo models, including RNA interference, and xenograft tumours, were employed to assess phenotypic effects. Transcriptomic profiling and Western blotting elucidated downstream pathways. Clinical correlations were evaluated in multi-ethnic cohorts.FindingsAGPS 5′UTR regulatory variant rs113671272 was identified as a susceptibility locus for ESCC (OR = 1.15, P = 0.0004), with the A allele conferring poor survival. This variant localises to a putative active regulatory element marked by H3K27ac/H3K4me1/H3K4me3 and exhibits allele-specific binding to the transcription factor MEIS3. The co-expression of AGPS and myeloid ecotropic viral integration site 3 (MEIS3) was significantly associated with worse survival in Chinese, but not Caucasian patients with ESCC. MEIS3 knockdown significantly suppressed AGPS expression. AGPS knockdown inhibited ESCC cell proliferation, migration, and tumour growth, whereas its overexpression promoted oncogenic phenotypes via NF-κB activation. MEIS3 knockdown suppressed ESCC cell proliferation and migration, consistent with a pro-oncogenic role similar to that of AGPS.InterpretationOur results establish rs113671272 as a key ethnic-specific ESCC risk variant that operates through the MEIS3/AGPS/NF-κB regulatory axis. These findings provide mechanistic insights into population disparities in ESCC and highlight the potential of AGPS-targeted strategies for risk assessment and precision therapy in high-risk populations.FundingNational Natural Science Foundation of China U22A20326.

Research in context

Evidence before this study

Previous GWAS had identified multiple genetic loci associated with ESCC susceptibility in Asian populations, with our team’s prior work revealing a co-segregation pattern between NFE2L2 mutations and the high-Fst SNP rs113671272 in Asian patients with ESCC. Functional genomic annotations from RegulomeDB, HaploReg, and ENCODE indicated that rs113671272 resides in regulatory regions with enhancer potential. AGPS had been established as an oncogene in breast cancer, melanoma, glioma, and other malignancies, promoting tumour progression through ether lipid metabolism, but its role in ESCC remained unexplored. MEIS3 had limited prior association with ovarian cancer and miR-21 regulation, with no known function in ESCC. Despite ethnic disparities in ESCC incidence and molecular profiles, no studies had integrated population-specific genetic variation with functional mechanisms driving ESCC pathogenesis in Chinese populations.

Added value of this study

This study provides several key advances: First, we identify and functionally characterise the Chinese-enriched genetic variant rs113671272 as a susceptibility locus for ESCC, demonstrating its association with increased disease risk and worse prognosis in Chinese cohorts. Second, we establish AGPS as the primary target gene of this locus, revealing its oncogenic role in ESCC through in vitro and in vivo models—a previously unrecognised mechanism in this cancer type. Third, we identify MEIS3 as the upstream transcription factor that binds preferentially to the risk-associated A allele of rs113671272, driving AGPS overexpression. Building on this regulatory link, fourth, we demonstrate that the co-expression of AGPS and MEIS3 is strongly associated with poor survival in Chinese patients with ESCC, highlighting an ethnic-specific prognostic signature derived from this axis. Fifth, we delineate the downstream NF-κB signalling pathway as the key mechanistic route through which AGPS promotes ESCC progression.

Implications of all the available evidence

Collectively, our study establishes the population-specific MEIS3/AGPS/NF-κB axis as a key driver of ESCC in Chinese individuals. These findings highlight the potential of rs113671272 genotyping and AGPS/MEIS3 co-expression profiling for risk stratification, and nominate AGPS and MEIS3 as promising therapeutic targets. Future work should focus on validating these biomarkers prospectively and developing pathway-specific inhibitors, underscoring the necessity of ethnicity-aware precision medicine in ESCC management.

Introduction

Esophageal cancer (EC) is the eighth most prevalent cancer worldwide and the sixth leading cause of cancer-related mortality.1 It accounts for 3% of all cancer cases globally, with approximately 600,000 new cases in 2020 worldwide, including approximately 418,000 males and 186,000 females.2 EC is primarily classified into two pathological types: ESCC and esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC). The development of different pathotypes of EC is highly geographically specific, with the predominant pathotype varying across different geographic regions. For instance, ESCC has the highest incidence in East Asia, South Central Asia, and South Africa (accounting for over half of the global EC cases in China), whereas EAC is predominantly found in Northern Europe and North America.2 However, pathological differences persist among racially diverse populations within the same region. For instance, in the United States, the incidence of ESCC in Asian males is 81% higher than in white males,3 suggesting that EC incidence is perhaps more accurately characterised by racial specificity than by geography alone.

Prior work integrating whole-exome sequencing of predominantly Chinese and Caucasian ESCC cohorts with 1000 Genomes data revealed a higher frequency of NFE2L2 mutations among Asian patients. Intriguingly, these mutations co-segregate with a nearby high-Fst single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP), rs113671272, suggesting a potential functional interplay.4 However, the molecular mechanism at this locus remains undefined. In the present study, we therefore focused on rs113671272 to map its expression quantitative trait loci (eQTL) landscape and identified AGPS as its primary target gene.

AGPS is a peroxisomal enzyme that catalyses the exchange of fatty alcohols for acyl groups in acylglycerophosphates, producing alkylglycerophosphate—a critical step in ether lipid biosynthesis. AGPS gene has been demonstrated to exert pro-carcinogenic effects in a multitude of tumour cells, including those of the breast, melanoma, glioma, hepatocellular carcinoma, ovarian carcinoma, and prostate.5, 6, 7 However, its investigation in ESCC, the focus of our present study, represents a and hitherto uncharted area of research.

To investigate the transcription factors upstream of the rs113671272 locus, we also screened for the transcription factor MEIS3, which is thought to play a key role in regulating AGPS expression through rs113671272. Research on MEIS3 is currently very limited. Previous studies have demonstrated that MEIS3 expression correlates with the survival of ovarian cancer cells.8 Additionally, MEIS3 has been shown to promote gene transcription by binding to the cg02515217 CpG site in the promoter of miR-21 (an oncogenic microRNA), leading to its significantly increased expression.9 A study by Meng et al. revealed that MEIS3 expression was upregulated in the majority of tumours, which was associated with a reduction in patient survival.10

Methods

Study participants and SNP genotyping in GWAS

This two-phase, case–control GWAS included 6172 participants (3031 ESCC cases and 3141 healthy controls; Supplemental Table S1). The study was conducted in two phases—discovery and validation—each utilising independent, unrelated samples. The discovery cohort consisted of 1009 Chinese patients with ESCC treated at Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center (FUSCC) (June 2009–January 2012) and 1102 cancer-free controls, all of whom were genotyped. The validation cohort included 2022 patients with ESCC from the Cancer Hospital of the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences (Beijing; 2000–2009) and 2039 healthy controls. Peripheral blood samples were collected from all participants and stored after obtaining written informed consent. Clinical data, including age, sex, ethnicity, and smoking history, were recorded (information regarding age and sex was obtained from patients’ identification documents, all participants were confirmed to be of Chinese Han ethnicity, while smoking history was collected through patient self-reporting).

For SNP genotyping, genomic DNA was extracted from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC). Illumina Infinium® Global Screening Assay (Illumina, California, USA) and GeneChip Human Mapping 6.0 set (Affymetrix, California, USA) was employed in discovery and validation phases, respectively. Non-genotyped SNPs were imputed using IMPUTE software (v2.3.1) with the 1000 Genomes Project Phase III (October 2014 release) as reference panel. SNPs with a minor allele frequency (MAF) < 0.05, poor imputation quality (info score < 0.8), or significant deviation from Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium (P < 1 × 10−6) were excluded.

Using PLINK, we extracted SNPs within the 178,092,323–178,408,562 bp region of chromosome 2 from the ESCC GWAS database at FUSCC. After quality control (QC), 824 variants remained for analysis. Logistic regression adjusted for age and sex identified 38 SNPs significantly associated with ESCC susceptibility under an additive model. Finally, we calculated linkage disequilibrium (LD) for these 38 SNPs using PLINK, and the results were visualised with the LD heatmap. Regional association patterns were plotted as a Manhattan plot using ggplot2.

Tissue samples and molecular analyses

To systematically investigate the clinical characteristics and molecular mechanisms of ESCC, we also analysed esophageal tissues from 90 Chinese patients with ESCC treated at FUSCC (May 2009–December 2011) (Supplemental Table S2). Written informed consent was obtained from all 90 participants, from whom data on age, sex, and smoking history was collected through patient self-reporting. Genotyping of the rs113671272 locus was performed in adjacent normal tissues to enable prognostic comparison between wild-type and variant carriers, with concurrent measurement of AGPS mRNA expression levels in both genotypes. Comprehensive transcriptome sequencing facilitated comparative analysis of AGPS and MEIS3 expression patterns between tumour and normal tissues, while also evaluating their prognostic significance. Furthermore, we implemented differential expression analysis, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA), and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis to elucidate potential downstream signalling networks associated with ESCC pathogenesis.

Reagent validation

All reagents employed in this study, including antibodies and cell lines, were obtained from commercial sources. Detailed antibody information, encompassing catalogue numbers, suppliers, and Research Resource Identifiers (RRIDs), is provided in the Methods section under the subsections for the CUT&Tag-qPCR assay, EMSA and Western blotting. The human ESCC cell lines KYSE-510, KYSE-450, Eca-109, KYSE-150, KYSE-180, and Te-1 were commercially procured and authenticated by Short Tandem Repeat (STR) profiling.

Cell culture

Human ESCC cell lines: KYSE-510 (RRID: CVCL_1354), KYSE-450 (RRID: CVCL_1353), Eca-109 (RRID: CVCL_6898) cell lines were purchased from Shanghai Fuheng Biotechnology Company (China). Human ESCC cell lines: KYSE-150 (RRID: CVCL_1348), KYSE-180 (RRID: CVCL_1349), Te-1 (RRID:CVCL_1759) cell lines were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, USA). 293T cell line (RRID: CVCL_0063) was obtained from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (China). Cells were cultured at 37 °C, 5% CO2, humidified atmosphere in DMEM (Gibco, USA) (293T and KYSE-180), and RPMI 1640 (Gibco, USA) (KYSE-510, KYSE-450, Eca-109, KYSE-150 and Te-1) medium supplied with 10% foetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, USA) and 1% Penicillin/Streptomycin (Gibco, USA). All human ESCC cell lines were confirmed to be free of mycoplasma contamination and cross-contamination prior to experiments.

Transfection and dual-luciferase reporter assay

The Dual Luciferase Reporter Gene Assay Kit (11402ES60, Yeason, China) was employed to accomplish dual-luciferase reporter assay. Two fragment sequences were designed, each containing the rs113671272 G and rs113671272 A variants. Both sequences encompass 850 bp of upstream and downstream bases, exhibiting complete identity except for the differing bases at the rs113671272 site. We constructed two luciferase reporter vectors, designated pGL3-rs113671272-G and pGL3-rs113671272-A, each harbouring the AGPS promoter sequence. The empty pGL3-basic plasmid was used as a negative control to assess background signal, while the pGL3-control plasmid, which contains a strong viral enhancer and promoter, served as a positive control to verify assay performance. The pRL-TK plasmid was served as an internal transfection control. Subsequently, the five plasmids constructed in the previous step (pGL3-rs113671272-G, pGL3-rs113671272-A, pGL3-basic, pGL3-control, and pRL-TK, respectively) were co-transfected into 293T cells and human ESCC cells (KYSE-150, KYSE-510, KYSE-450, Eca-109 and Te-1) by Lipofectamine 3000 DNA Transfection Reagent (L3000015, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Subsequently, the cells were lysed, the substrate and reaction solution were added in a sequential manner, and the fluorescence intensity was measured in order to obtain the results. Normalised luciferase activity for each experimental construct was calculated as the ratio of Firefly luciferase signal (experimental reporter) to Renilla luciferase signal (internal control). For each cell line and independent experiment, the activity of the promoterless pGL3-basic vector was set to 1, and all experimental values were expressed relative to this baseline. The sequences of oligonucleotides used are listed in Supplemental Table S3.

RNA isolation, reverse transcription, and quantitative PCR (qPCR)

Total RNA from clinical tissue specimens was isolated using the MolPure® Cell/Tissue Total RNA Kit (19221ES50, Yeason, China), while cellular RNA was rapidly extracted with the EZ-press RNA Purification Kit (B0004DP, EZBioscience, China). For subsequent analysis, 1 μg of RNA was reverse-transcribed using Hifair® Ⅲ 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis SuperMix for qPCR (11137ES60, Yeason, China), and cDNA was diluted 1:20. qPCR was performed on a LightCycler 480 (Roche) with Hieff® qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (11204ES08, Yeason, China), using GAPDH as the endogenous control. All reactions were run in triplicate, and relative gene expression was calculated via the ΔΔCT (ΔCT [sample] ΔCT [control average]) method. Primer sequences are provided in Supplemental Table S3.

CUT&Tag-qPCR assay

The Hieff NGS G-Type In Situ DNA Binding Profiling Library Prep Kit for Illumina (12597ES12, Yeason, China) was employed to complete the CUT&Tag experiments. In regard to the selection of primary antibodies, chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) grade antibodies were chosen for H3K4me1 (ab8895, Abcam, UK, RRID: AB_306847), H3K4me3 (ab8580, Abcam, UK, RRID: AB_306649) and H3K27ac (ab4729, Abcam, UK, RRID: AB_2118291). As there is no ChIP grade antibody for MEIS3 currently available on the market, we constructed a MEIS3 overexpression plasmid with a Flag tag and generated the corresponding stable cell lines. We then employed Flag-tagged antibodies (SLAB01, Tiandirenhe, China) to complete CUT&Tag experiments. The target protein-bound DNA fragments are converted into libraries suitable for sequencing on the Illumina® platform through a series of steps, including cell capture, primary antibody incubation, secondary antibody incubation, transposase incubation, transposase activation, incorporation of DNA standards, cell lysis, magnetic bead recovery of gDNA, library amplification, and magnetic bead sorting. The library was subsequently analysed by qPCR. Details of the antibodies and primer sequences utilised were provided in Supplemental Table S3.

The Hieff qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (11204ES08, Yeason, China) was utilised for qPCR, with all operations conducted on ice. Following the completion of the CUT&Tag reaction, the Hieff qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix, primers, and template DNA were prepared and subsequently analysed. The primers were designed according to the data of H3K27ac and H3K4me3 ChIP-seq in human ESCC lines in the database. Primer sequences used in this experiment can be found in the transcription factor database Cistrome DB website (http://cistrome.org/db/#/). mRNA levels were analysed for each gene at least in triplicate, with data normalisation against the GAPDH control. In the case of CUT&Tag -qPCR, all target primers were subjected to three technical replicates, with the data subsequently normalised to the control regions. The relative enrichment of the target antibodies at the target DNA fragment was then determined by comparison with the background (IgG control). The sequences of oligonucleotides used are listed in Supplemental Table S3.

EMSA

EMSA was performed to validate the direct binding of MEIS3 protein to the rs113671272 locus. Nuclear extracts (NE) were prepared from KYSE-450 parental and MEIS3-overexpressing cells using the Nuclear and Cytoplasmic Protein Extraction Kit (P0027, Beyotime, China). Four complementary single-stranded oligonucleotides spanning the rs113671272 locus (Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China) were annealed to generate double-stranded probes (D0251, Beyotime, China): biotin-labelled wild-type (G) and variant (A) probes, along with corresponding unlabelled cold competitors.

Binding reactions (15.5 μL) containing 1.5 μL 10 × binding buffer, 1 μL Poly (dI•dC), 2 μg nuclear extract, and 200 fmol biotin-labelled probe were incubated at 25 °C for 20 min; for competition assays, a 200-fold molar excess of unlabelled probe was added, and for supershift assays, 1 μg anti-MEIS3 antibody (ab185961, Abcam, UK, RRID: AB_3720138) was included. Reaction mixtures were resolved on 5.5% non-denaturing polyacrylamide gels in 0.5 × TBE buffer, transferred to nylon membranes, and detected using the Chemiluminescent Nucleic Acid Detection Module (Thermo Scientific). Signals were captured by exposure in a ChemiDoc MP Imaging System (Tanon 5200). The sequences of oligonucleotides used are listed in Supplemental Table S3.

Lentivirus production and transduction

The shRNA constructs targeting AGPS, SIX4, PAX4, HES1, PAX2, TBX5 and MEIS3 were designed using the GPP WEB PORTAL (https://portals.broadinstitute.org/gpp/public/gene/search_clones) and procured from TsingKe Biological Technology Company. They were then cloned into the pLKO.1 vector. To construct AGPS, MEIS3 overexpression stable cell lines, the target gene fragments were amplified via PCR from the cDNA containing the target genes followed by gel electrophoresis. The desired fragments were excised from the gel, purified and ligated into the overexpression plasmid after restriction enzyme digestion. 293T cells were seeded in 10 cm dishes and cultured overnight to reach 70–80% confluency on the next day. On day 2, the medium was replaced with the transfection mixture (comprising 4.5ug packing plasmid (psPAX2), 1.5ug envelope plasmid (pMD2G), 36ul PEI (Polysciences, Pennsylvania, USA), 400ul opti-MEM, 6ug shRNA or overexpression construct). After 8 h, the transfection medium was replaced with fresh complete medium. Viral supernatant was collected at 48 h and 72 h post-transfection. Target cells were seeded in 6-well plates to achieve 60–70% confluency. The following day, medium was aspirated and replaced with fresh medium containing lentiviral supernatant filtered through a 0.45 μm filter supplemented with 1ug/ml polybrene. 24 h after the incubation, puromycin (10 ug/ml, Biosharp, China) was added to select transduced cells until complete death of untransduced control cells was observed (3–5 days). The sequences of oligonucleotides used are listed in Supplemental Table S3.

Western blot (WB)

Protein samples were lysed in RIPA buffer supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors (P0013B, Beyotime, China). A total of 30 μg protein from each sample was equally loaded and separated by 4–10% gradient SDS-PAGE gels. For all immunoblots except Fig. 8A, the PageRuler Prestained Protein Ladder (26616, Thermo Scientific, USA) was used; for Fig. 8A, the BeyoColor™ Prestained Colour Protein Marker (P0075, Beyotime, China) was applied. Separated proteins were then transferred to 0.45 μm PVDF membranes (Millipore). Membranes were blocked with blocking buffer (5% non-fat milk in TBST) for 1 h at room temperature and then incubated with antibodies against AGPS (1:1000, ab184186, Abcam, UK, RRID: AB_3101881), MEIS3 (1:1000, ab185961, Abcam, UK, RRID: AB_3720138), IKKα (1:1000, 11930, Cell Signalling Technology, USA, RRID: AB_2687618), Phospho-IKKα/β (1:1000, 2697, Cell Signalling Technology, USA, RRID: AB_2079382), Phospho-NF-κB P65 (1:1000, 3033, Cell Signalling Technology, USA, AB_331284), IκBα (1:1000, 4814, Cell Signalling Technology, USA, RRID: AB_390781), Phospho-IκBα (1:1000, 2859, Cell Signalling Technology, USA, RRID: AB_561111), NF-κB P65 (1:1000, 8242, Cell Signalling Technology, USA, RRID: AB_331284), DYKDDDK (1:1000, SLAB01, TianDiRenHe, China), GAPDH (1:3000, AF7021, Affinity Biosciences, China, RRID: AB_2839421), β-tubulin (1:1000, 2146, Cell Signalling Technology, USA, RRID: AB_ 2210545). After three times of wash with TBST, membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (anti-rabbit IgG or anti-mouse IgG, 1:3000, 7074 or 7076, Cell Signalling Technology, USA, AB_2099233 or AB_330924) for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualised with a ECL kit (EpiZyme, USA) and imaged with a chemiluminescence system (Tanon, Shanghai, China).

Cell counting assay (CCK-8)

1000 cells were planted into multiple wells of a 96-well plate, and 3 replicate wells were set up at each time point. Subsequently, measurements were conducted at designated time points following planting. A volume of 10 μL of CCK-8 reagent (40203ES60, Yeason, China) was added to each test well and incubated for 1 h in a cell culture incubator. Subsequently, the 96-well plate was removed and placed on a multifunctional enzyme labeller, where the absorbance value at 450 nm was measured. The data were recorded and a growth curve was plotted.

Colony formation assay

1000 cells were inoculated on each well of a six-well plate with 3 replicate wells set up for each group. The medium was replaced every five days, and the culture was considered terminated when clones were visible to the naked eye after approximately ten days of culture. The cells should be washed three times with PBS, followed by the addition of 4% paraformaldehyde, which should be applied dropwise and left to react for 30 min at room temperature. The paraformaldehyde should then be discarded, and the cells washed twice with PBS. The crystal violet staining solution should be added dropwise, and the cells shaken slowly for 30 min at room temperature. The crystal violet staining solution should then be discarded, and the cells rinsed gently and left to dry naturally. Photographic documentation should be undertaken, the number of clones counted, and a clone formation curve drawn.

Cell migration assay

Cell samples were prepared by resuspending them in serum-free medium, and their concentrations were subsequently determined. A volume of 600 μL of 1640 medium containing 20% FBS was added to each well of a 24-well plate (the lower layer of the chamber). The Transwell was then placed into the plate and observed under a microscope to ascertain the presence of air bubbles between the chamber and the lower layer of serum-containing medium. In the event of the presence of any air bubbles, the chamber was gently lifted to dislodge them. A total of 200 μL of the diluted cell suspension was carefully added to the upper layer of the chamber and subsequently placed into the incubator for culture. After 24 h, the culture should be terminated, the chamber removed, and the medium inside the chamber discarded. The chamber should then be washed three times with PBS, and any cells that have not passed through the upper layer should be gently wiped off with a cotton swab. The chamber should then be immersed in 4% paraformaldehyde at room temperature for 30 min, washed three times with PBS, and immersed in crystal violet dye at room temperature for 30 min. The chamber should then be washed three times with PBS. The chamber was then washed three times with PBS for 5 min each, inverted to dry, and stored at room temperature. Migrated cells were imaged on an inverted microscope at 4 × magnification for overall visualisation and at 10 × magnification for quantification.

Wound healing assay

Pre-mark 3–5 horizontal lines evenly spaced (approximately 0.5–1 cm apart) across the back of each well in the 6-well plate using a marker pen, ensuring the lines extend fully across the diameter of each well. Seed an appropriate number of cells into the plate to achieve 100% confluency after overnight incubation. The following day, create two scratch wounds in the cell monolayer using a pipette tip held perpendicular to the plane of the culture and aligned perpendicularly to the pre-drawn marker lines. Apply consistent pressure and motion when creating scratches across different wells. After scratching, gently wash the wells three times with sterile PBS to remove dislodged cells and clarify the scratch boundaries. Replace the medium with fresh serum-free medium. Immediately acquire images of the scratches under a microscope, ensuring that the initial gap width is documented. Following the desired incubation period, observe the scratch wounds again under the microscope and capture images to evaluate cell migration.

Nude mouse xenograft model

Male Balb/c nude mice (aged 4–5 weeks; Vital River Laboratories) were housed in sterilised conditions with autoclaved water. For tumourigenesis studies, mice were randomly allocated into experimental groups. A total volume of 150 μL, consisting of 100 μL of single-cell suspension containing 5 × 106 ESCC cells mixed with 50 μL of Matrigel (356234, Corning, USA), was subcutaneously injected using 1 mL syringes (n = 6 per group). Tumour growth was monitored weekly until the experimental endpoint (three weeks post-inoculation), with calliper measurements performed three times per week. Tumour volume was calculated using (widthˆ2 × length)/2. All procedures followed standard protocols to ensure reliability and reproducibility.

RNA sequencing (RNA-seq)

Cells were pre-seeded in 15 cm Petri dishes and cultured until they reached 90% confluency. After washing with PBS, TRIzol (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) reagent was added for cell lysis. The lysate was transferred to 1.5 mL EP tubes via repeated pipetting. Total RNA was isolated from AGPS knockdown and control KYSE-150 cells, each with two replicates. RNA-seq was performed by Shanghai Xuran Biological Company.

Genotyping of rs113671272 in ESCC cell lines

Genomic DNA was extracted from all ESCC cell lines (KYSE-150, KYSE-510, KYSE-450, KYSE-180, Eca-109, and Te-1) using the Quick Extract DNA Extraction Solution (Lucigen, USA). Central fragment of rs113671272 was then amplified using PrimeSTAR Max Premix 2X (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) and primers. Subsequently, the amplified DNA samples were subjected to agarose gel electrophoresis and the target DNA segments were excised and sent for Sanger sequencing to determine the genotypes of the cell lines. Details of the primer sequences utilised were provided in Supplemental Table S3.

Data availability

The Ensembl database (https://www.ensembl.org) was utilised to retrieve population genetics and positional information of genetic loci.11 The RegulomeDB 2.2 (http://regulomedb.org), HaploReg v4.2 (http://compbio.mit.edu/HaploReg), and The Encyclopedia of DNA Elements (ENCODE, https://www.encodeproject.org) databases were employed to predict the transcriptional regulatory functions of genetic loci.12 In addition, ChIP-seq data were provided by the ENCODE and Cistrome DB databases. eQTL analysis is a method for examining the quantitative trait of gene expression, with the objective of identifying the motifs that influence gene expression.13 In the present study, eQTL data were extracted from the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) database (https://www.gtexportal.org/home/) for the purpose of predicting target genes that may be regulated by the target SNP. Furthermore, multi-ethnic ESCC cohorts from public databases were integrated. The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA)-ESCC dataset (https://xena.ucsc.edu/) was filtered to retain only samples of European/American ancestry, yielding 41 primary tumours and 13 normal tissues. For Asian population data, Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) GSE53624 and GSE53622 were analysed, which comprised 162 ESCC cases with paired normal tissues and clinical annotations. Combined with the FUSCC Chinese cohort, these three datasets enabled cross-ethnic comparative analyses of ESCC molecular profiles.

Statistics

All statistical analyses were performed using (GraphPad Prism 10). Two-tailed unpaired Student’s t-tests were applied to access the statistical significance of the qPCR results for ChIP and gene expression as well as for the observations on the basis of dual-luciferase reporter assay, CCK-8, colony formation assay, cell migration assay, wound healing assay, tumourigenicity assay in nude mice. For comparisons between two groups of clinical samples, the appropriate statistical method was selected based on homogeneity of variance and normality distribution, using either parametric tests (unpaired/paired t-test) or nonparametric tests (Mann–Whitney U test/Wilcoxon signed-rank test). For comparisons among multiple groups, either parametric tests (ordinary one-way ANOVA) or nonparametric tests (Kruskal–Wallis test) were applied following the same criteria. Survival analyses were performed using the log-rank test, while co-expression analysis was conducted with Pearson/Spearman correlation analysis (for normally/non-normally distributed data, respectively). Differences were considered to be significant when P < 0.05.

Results

Population-specific enrichment of rs113671272-linked variants in Asians and their association with ESCC

The NFE2L2 mutation was associated with a high-Fst SNP rs113671272 in its vicinity. Fst, the index of inter-population differentiation, indicates a substantial discrepancy in the genotype distribution of this SNP between Asian and Caucasian populations. Association analysis between rs113671272 and susceptibility and survival of ESCC in Chinese populations

To verify that the rs113671272 variant was associated with the risk of ESCC in China, we expanded the initial discovery-phase GWAS by conducting a larger, independent validation-phase GWAS. A case-control study was then performed on the combined data from these two phases, after which corrections were made for age, sex and smoking history. We found that the G > A variant of the rs113671272 variant significantly increased susceptibility to ESCC (OR = 1.15, P = 0.0004, logistic regression) (Fig. 2B). Furthermore, we performed an analysis to determine the association between three population characteristics (i.e., age, sex, and smoking history) and the risk of developing ESCC. The results demonstrated that patients with a history of smoking exhibited a significantly higher risk of developing ESCC (Supplemental Table S4).

Furthermore, by conducting analysis of Chinese ESCC populations with different genotypes, we found that the risk genotype GA/AA at rs113671272 was significantly associated with shorter overall survival (OS) (Fig. 2C) and progression-free survival (PFS) (Fig. 2D).

rs113671272 locus harbours active epigenetic marks and regulates AGPS expression

eQTL analysis of rs113671272 utilising GTEx data revealed that NFE2L2 did not exhibit genotype-dependent expression level differences in normal esophageal tissues, whereas AGPS and AC074286.1 showed significant variations (Supplemental Data 1). Compared to wild-type genotypes, variant genotypes demonstrated significantly elevated AGPS mRNA levels and reduced AC074286.1 expression (Fig. 2E, and Supplemental Fig. S1C). We then performed qPCR on genotype-stratified adjacent normal esophageal tissues, which confirmed the same expression pattern as shown in GTEx, but—unexpectedly—also showed increased AC074286.1 levels in A-allele carriers (Fig. 2F, Supplemental Fig. S1D).

To understand the underlying mechanism of genetic variants of rs113671272 on mRNA expression levels of AGPS, we constructed plasmids containing the rs113671272 locus and the AGPS promoter region. Dual-luciferase reporter assays showed that in 293T and multiple ESCC cell lines, the rs113671272-A allele exhibited significant higher luciferase activity compared to the G allele (Fig. 2G, Supplemental Fig. S1E), demonstrating that mutant rs113671272 enhances AGPS transcription. This conclusion was further confirmed by CUT&Tag-qPCR assays which showed significant enrichment of active histone marks (H3K27ac, H3K4me1, and H3K4me3) at this locus (Fig. 2H). Taken together, these data mechanistically link the risk-associated A allele to increased AGPS expression, establishing AGPS as the primary functional target mediating the ESCC risk conferred by rs113671272.

MEIS3 binding enhanced by rs113671272 G > A variant promotes AGPS expression

We hypothesised that the rs113671272 G > A variant exerts its effect by affecting the capacity of a designated transcription factor to bind the specific DNA fragment in which the site is embedded. In order to identify the transcription factor in question, a screening approach was employed that utilised the enhancer element locator (EEL) to identify the top 10 candidate transcription factors with the highest binding scores and quantified their allele-dependent score changes at rs113671272 (Supplemental Data 2). Motif analysis revealed that TCF11, MEIS3, PAX2 and SIX4 recognised conserved sequences matching the sequence surrounding the rs113671272 locus, whereas the remaining transcription factors showed no sequence affinity (Fig. 3A). To test this, we knocked down each candidate factor in ESCC cell lines and measured AGPS expression by qPCR. Silencing TCF11, SIX4, PAX2 or PAX4 had no effect on AGPS mRNA levels (Supplemental Fig. S2A–D). In contrast, MEIS3 knockdown caused a marked decrease in both AGPS mRNA and protein levels (Fig. 3B, Supplemental Fig. S2E and F), identifying MEIS3 as the key regulator.

The dual-luciferase assay confirmed that MEIS3 regulates both rs113671272 alleles. Under control conditions, the A-allele construct demonstrated higher basal activity than its G-allele counterpart. Following MEIS3 knockdown, transcriptional activity was reduced for both alleles, with a more pronounced decrease observed for the A-allele construct (Fig. 3C), indicating stronger dependence of the A allele on MEIS3. Residual activity retained by the G allele after knockdown suggests that its basal transcription may involve contributions from additional factors or lower-affinity interactions with MEIS3.

Furthermore, by genotyping multiple cell lines (Supplemental Fig. S3A), we identified a human ESCC cell line heterozygous at the rs113671272 locus. Subsequent CUT&Tag-qPCR assays demonstrated that MEIS3 not only significantly enriched DNA fragments containing the rs113671272 locus but also exhibited stronger binding affinity for fragments harbouring the A allele compared to those with the G allele (Supplemental Fig. S3D–F).

To further validate the direct binding of MEIS3 protein to the rs113671272 locus and its allele-specific preference, EMSA was performed using NE from both parental and MEIS3-overexpressing cells. A visibly stronger protein-DNA complex was observed with the biotin-labelled rs113671272-A probe compared to the rs113671272-G probe in both cellular contexts (Fig. 3G). Cold competition assays confirmed sequence-specific binding, and supershift assays verified the presence of MEIS3 in the retarded complexes. Together with the CUT&Tag–qPCR results, these data demonstrate that the rs113671272 A allele enhances the DNA-binding capacity of MEIS3.

Pooled analysis of Chinese patients with ESCC (merged GEO database and FUSCC cohort) stratified by AGPS expression levels demonstrated that high AGPS expression was significantly associated with worse overall survival (P = 0.01, log-rank test) (Fig. 4D). Building on our findings linking the rs113671272 GA/AA genotypes to poorer survival, we performed exploratory analyses to assess the combined prognostic effect of genotype and AGPS expression. Patients with both the GA/AA genotype and high AGPS expression (n = 22) showed a non-significant trend towards worse OS and PFS compared to those with the GG genotype and low AGPS expression (n = 17) (OS, P = 0.06; PFS, P = 0.09; Supplemental Fig. S4B and C, log-rank test).

Within GA/AA carriers, high AGPS expression (n = 22) was associated with a more pronounced, though statistically non-significant, trend for inferior OS and PFS compared to low expression (n = 18). Joint influence of AGPS and MEIS3 on ESCC progression in clinical settings

To investigate the co-regulation of AGPS and MEIS3, we analysed their expression correlation across ethnic cohorts. In the Chinese population, a significant, albeit modest, positive correlation was consistently observed across two independent datasets (GEO: r = 0.16, P = 0.036; FUSCC: r = 0.26, P = 0.021; Pearson’s correlation test; Fig. 4F). While the effect sizes are below the conventional threshold for strong correlation (r > 0.3), the statistical significance across independent cohorts suggests a biologically relevant, population-specific co-regulatory relationship within the heterogeneous context of clinical tumour samples. This finding is further strengthened by its striking contrast to the Caucasian cohort (TCGA: r = 0.11, P = 0.51), where no significant correlation was detected. This dichotomy actively reinforces the ethnic specificity of the MEIS3/AGPS regulatory axis in ESCC.

Furthermore, we assessed the prognostic implications of different AGPS-MEIS3 expression patterns in Chinese and Caucasian patients with ESCC. Strikingly, in the Chinese cohort, the four AGPS-MEIS3 expression subgroups exhibited statistically significant differences in survival distribution (Supplemental Fig. S5A and B). Specifically, patients with concurrent high expression of both AGPS and MEIS3 had significantly worse overall survival (OS) than those with low expression of both genes (Fig. 4G, Supplemental Fig. S5C). In contrast, no significant survival stratification was observed in the Caucasian cohort (Fig. 4H, Supplemental Fig. S5D).

WB analysis of key proteins in the canonical NF-κB pathway in AGPS-knockdown and AGPS-overexpressing ESCC cell lines further verified at the protein level that AGPS modulates the expression of genes associated with the canonical NF-κB signalling pathway in ESCC (Fig. 7F). Notwithstanding minor variations in intermediate readouts, AGPS knockdown with either shRNA consistently attenuated NF-κB pathway activity, as evidenced by reduced phospho-P65 and concordant anti-tumour effects. MEIS3-AGPS axis is associated with NF-κB activation in an ethnic-specific manner and promotes ESCC cell proliferation

Given the established role of MEIS3 in regulating AGPS transcription via the rs113671272 variant, we sought to determine whether this axis contributes to downstream NF-κB activation in vivo. Patients were stratified according to MEIS3 and AGPS expression levels, and NF-κB pathway activity was compared between the high co-expression and low co-expression groups across three independent cohorts. In the GEO Chinese cohort (n = 162), the high co-expression group exhibited significantly elevated NF-κB scores relative to the low co-expression group (P = 0.0038, unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test, Fig. 9A). We next assessed MEIS3 expression in clinical specimens. In Chinese cohort, MEIS3 was significantly upregulated in tumour tissues compared with adjacent normal tissues. A similar trend was evident in TCGA Caucasian samples, though the difference did not achieve statistical significance, potentially owing to limited sample size (Fig. 9C and D).

To further explore its functional role, MEIS3 was silenced in ESCC cell lines, leading to markedly reduced proliferation (CCK-8 and colony formation assays) and impaired migratory capacity (Transwell assays) (Fig. 9E–G; Supplemental Fig. S8G–I). These findings demonstrate that MEIS3 acts as a pro-oncogenic factor in ESCC and, in cooperation with AGPS, specifically activates NF-κB signalling in Chinese patients.

Discussion

In recent years, GWAS conducted in Chinese populations have identified several genetic variants associated with susceptibility to ESCC.14, 15, 16 Building upon our previous published findings,4 which compared genomic data between Asian and Caucasian populations, we investigated the mechanistic role of this Chinese-specific genetic variant rs113671272. In the present study, we confirmed the elevated prevalence of rs113671272 in Chinese populations and systematically characterised all ESCC-associated SNPs within its genomic region. Among these, rs113671272 exhibited the strongest transcriptional regulatory potential, supporting its candidacy as a key functional variant. We further established its association with increased ESCC risk and poorer prognosis in Chinese patients. Through integrated eQTL analysis, dual-luciferase assays, and genotype-stratified expression profiling, we identified AGPS as the primary target gene regulated by this locus, thereby establishing a mechanistic basis for subsequent investigation.

AGPS, a peroxisomal enzyme essential for ether lipid biosynthesis, has been implicated in the progression of several malignancies.6,7,17 Here, we demonstrate its role in ESCC, where its expression correlates with worse overall survival specifically in Chinese patients. Importantly, individuals carrying both the rs113671272 risk allele and high AGPS expression exhibited significantly poorer clinical outcomes compared to those with the wild-type genotype and low AGPS levels. Notably, we found that AGPS expression is positively correlated with MEIS3 expression exclusively in Chinese patients with ESCC, and their co-expression serves as a robust prognostic marker for worse survival—a pattern absent in Caucasian cohorts. Functional validation through comprehensive in vitro and in vivo experiments consistently confirmed the tumour-promoting role of AGPS in ESCC.

To investigate the downstream mechanisms, we performed transcriptomic analysis of AGPS-knockdown cells and paired tumour/adjacent normal tissues. The NF-κB signalling pathway emerged as the most strongly enriched. WB experiments confirmed that AGPS regulates core effector proteins of the NF-κB signalling pathway. Critically, silencing of the P65 subunit abolished AGPS-driven oncogenic phenotypes, thereby establishing the activation of NF-κB as a central mechanistic link in AGPS-mediated ESCC progression. Together, these data identify the canonical NF-κB pathway as the key downstream axis through which AGPS exerts its pro-tumourigenic effects in ESCC.

The NF-κB signalling pathway is known to be constitutively activated in ESCC and contributes to key malignant features, including enhanced proliferation, invasion, and metastasis. In vitro, pharmacological inhibition of NF-κB suppresses tumour cell proliferation, induces apoptosis, and reduces migration and invasion.18 In vivo, NF-κB blockade significantly inhibits tumour growth, angiogenesis, and lung metastasis without major toxicity. These findings reinforce the central role of NF-κB in ESCC progression and highlight its therapeutic potential, further supporting our conclusion that AGPS drives malignancy primarily via this pathway. To identify upstream regulatory mechanisms, we performed computational prediction of transcription factor binding sites, which indicated that the G > A alteration at rs113671272 enhances binding affinity for MEIS3. Subsequent functional assays validated allele-specific MEIS3 binding and demonstrated its role in regulating AGPS expression. Clinically, MEIS3 expression levels showed correlation with tumour stage in Chinese patients, whereas no such association was observed in Caucasian cohorts. Experimentally, MEIS3 knockdown was found to significantly impair ESCC cell proliferation and migratory capacity.

Our findings underscore the complexity of AGPS regulation in ESCC, revealing a population-specific aetiology for its overexpression. While the variant rs113671272 constitutes a principal, allele-specific driver enhancing AGPS transcription via MEIS3 in Chinese cohorts, its negligible frequency in Caucasian populations implies that conserved AGPS upregulation there must be governed by distinct, alternative mechanisms. This dichotomy exemplifies how common oncogenic phenotypes can arise through divergent genetic and regulatory architectures across ethnicities, highlighting the importance of population-aware approaches in both understanding ESCC pathogenesis and developing targeted interventions.

Several limitations of this study warrant consideration. First, the retrospective nature of the clinical sample analysis introduces the possibility of selection bias; future prospective, multicentre cohorts would provide more robust validation of the predictive utility of these molecular markers. Second, at the mechanistic level, key questions remain unresolved. Although our study establishes that AGPS activates the NF-κB pathway and that NF-κB is required for AGPS-driven oncogenicity, the precise molecular link between AGPS and NF-κB remains to be fully elucidated. AGPS is a peroxisomal enzyme essential for ether lipid biosynthesis, and its oncogenic effects in other cancers have been linked to alterations in lipid composition and signalling. It is plausible that AGPS-derived ether lipids, such as alkyl-lysophosphatidic acid, may act as signalling molecules that stimulate upstream kinases of the NF-κB pathway, or that AGPS modulates reactive oxygen species or inflammatory mediators that in turn activate IKK complexes. Future studies employing lipidomics, co-immunoprecipitation, and kinetic signalling assays will be needed to identify the specific lipid intermediates and protein interactions that connect AGPS activity to NF-κB activation in ESCC. Furthermore, even with NF-κB activation established as critical, the precise downstream transcriptional programmes regulated by AGPS—for instance, whether it preferentially modulates inflammatory cytokines versus anti-apoptotic factors—require finer dissection through approaches such as single-cell sequencing or targeted functional screens.

In summary, our results demonstrate that the AGPS variant rs113671272 increases ESCC susceptibility and drives tumour progression in Chinese patients by modulating the MEIS3–AGPS–NF-κB axis (Fig. 9H). These results delineate a molecular pathway that exhibits population-specific characteristics in driving ESCC susceptibility and progression among Chinese patients. The identification of AGPS/MEIS3 co-expression as an ethnic-specific prognostic marker further emphasises the translational relevance of this axis. These insights contribute to a more nuanced understanding of ethnic disparities in ESCC and offer promising directions for developing risk stratification approaches and targeted therapeutic strategies for this clinically relevant population.

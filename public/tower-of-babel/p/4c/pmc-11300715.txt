DEM parameters of slope cultivated purple soil in Southwest China and interaction mechanism between very narrow tine
Authors: Shoutai Li, Man Hu, Yan Liu, Yunwu Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe establishment of discrete element method (DEM) model for simulating the behavior of viscous soil can significantly contribute to the development of agricultural machinery, particularly in hilly areas worldwide. There is limited research on the parameter calibration of the DEM for purple soil in the Southwest China and its interaction mechanism between very narrow tine. Piling up test, rotating drum test and design-expert 12.0 software were used to design Plackett–Burman test, steepest climb test and Box–Behnken test successively. Combined with soil intrinsic parameter measurement test, soil simulation parameter calibration and optimization were completed. Based on the optimized DEM simulation parameters, the average relative errors of static repose angle and dynamic repose angle were 1.934 and 2.289%, respectively. The accuracy of parameter calibration results of DEM simulation was proved. Three kinds of very narrow tines were designed and processed. The interaction model and mechanism between soil-touch parts and soil was established. The average relative errors between the DEM simulation results of the forward resistance and disturbance area of the three soil-touch parts and the soil bin test results were 7.22% and 11.24%, respectively, which further proved that the calibration results of DEM parameters of purple soil and the interaction model with the soil-touch parts in this study were reliable. The physical and interaction parameters of slope cultivated purple soil in Southwest China were determined based on static-dynamic repose angle, providing more accurate parameters for study of soil-touch parts.Keywords: DEM parameters, Slope cultivated soil, Purple soil, Static-dynamic repose angle, Very narrow tine, Interaction mechanismSubject terms: Mechanical engineering, Computational science, Techniques and instrumentation

Introduction

In China, 69.3% of cultivated land is in the hilly and plateau areas characterized by uneven terrain. There is 9.915 million hectares of sloped cultivated land in Southwest China, accounting for 41.4% of the total sloped cultivated land of China1. The purple soil, fertile and suitable for crop production, is the primary agricultural soil type in Southwest China2. In 2022, the comprehensive mechanization rate for crop cultivation and harvest in the hilly and plateau areas was lower than 50%. The rate in Southwest China was 29%, and the national rate was higher than 73%3. The complex terrain in Southwest China hindered the use of large and medium-size agricultural machines. The high viscosity of purple soil reduced the efficiency and performance of agricultural machines4,5. Numerical methods offered an effective and efficient approach to understanding of the interaction and mechanical properties between agricultural machines and soil. Accurate soil models played a crucial role in designing, optimizing, and improving agricultural machines. Bad soil model resulted in undesirable consequences and incorrect conclusions6.

In tillage mechanics, modeling methods include the finite element method (FEM), discrete element method (DEM), and smooth particle hydrodynamics (SPH). The efficiency and accuracy of FEM and SPH relied on the soil’s constitutive relationship7,8. However, there were no well-established constitutive model suitable for slope cultivated purple soil, which limited the application of FEM and SPH9,10. The DEM, introduced by Cundall et al., had such advantages as minimal required calculation data, straightforward calculations, and absence of establishing large stiffness matrix11,12. It had widespread use in study of agricultural machines for analyzing interactions between agricultural materials, soil and machines13–15.

The confidence of DEM modeling relied on two key factors: the choice of particle contact model and the configuration of model parameters. As for the particle contact model, it should align with the specific working conditions of the operation. As for model parameters, categorized as intrinsic parameters (e.g., contact parameter), they can be measured by scientific instruments or calibration. Many contact parameters were determined through a reverse calibration process, an adjustment process by trial and error until the DEM model aligned with the actual macroscopic mechanical characteristics, such as repose angle and porosity. This process was known as virtual parameter calibration test16.

Nie et al.17 calibrated the DEM parameters for purple soil (moisture content 10.54%), with static repose angle as the response value. The simulated static repose angle was well consistent with the measured value with a relative error of 2.07%. Hao et al.18 established a DEM model under various soil compaction levels and investigated the microscopic processes of rapeseed seedling emergence across these levels. There were significant implications for establishing soil conditions conducive to enhancing plant emergence. Hoshishima et al.19 introduced a calibration program for simulation parameters of non-spherical cohesive particles, and successfully obtained simulation parameters for a range of materials using this method, showing its broad applicability. Hoseinian et al.20 conducted simulations of the interaction between a tillage tool with a flank and soil based on DEM. The error between the DEM results and soil tests was small, affirming the accuracy of the DEM model. Nevertheness, the accuracy of DEM model was commonly evaluated based on the error between simulated and tested static repose angles. The built models did not fully account for the dynamic movement of soil particles during interactions with soil-touch parts. Furthermore, No calibration parameters were available for the slope cultivated purple soil in Southwest China, particularly for the 65Mn based soil-touch parts.

Tillage played a crucial role in enhancing soil quality and fertility, providing good nutrient conditions for crops21. Currently, common tillage methods included rotary tillage, deep tillage, and no-tillage22. Rotary tillage was characterized by shallow soil disruption, high rate of soil crushing. It caused potential harm to beneficial organisms, earthworm as an example. Prolonged rotary tillage led to the hard plough pan, and rotary tillage tools often encountered high resistance while tilling soil of clay in Southwest China. Then, the tillage tools were prone to breakage. No-tillage offered such advantages as water retention, prevention of water and wind erosion, and helped increase crop yields. No-tillage was suitable for arid and semi-arid areas, and not suitable for the slope clay-rich soil in Southwest China. Deep tillage could break the lower soil layers, increase the tilling depth, and enhance soil structure. But significant tillage resistance and energy consumption were required. Conservation tillage combined the benefits of both no-tillage and deep tillage. It utilized narrow or extremely narrow cutter teeth for the soil-touch parts, let the subsoiler to break the plough pan, and minimized soil disturbance. This approach promoted the survival of beneficial organisms and resulted in good overall strength performance23.

This study calibrated the DEM model parameters for slope cultivated purple soil in Southwest China, using both dynamic and static repose angles as response values. The calibration process involved the Plackett–Burman test, steepest climb test, and Box-Burman test. The interaction mechanism and contact parameters were investigated by three types of narrow soil-touch parts, namely, rectangular teeth, triangular teeth, circular teeth, and the corresponding DEM models. The reliability of the established DEM models was rigorously assessed. The study can serve as valuable references for optimizing soil-touch part design and calibrating interaction model for various soil-touch parts.

Materials and methods

Materials

Very narrow tine

According to the operational characteristics of soil-touch parts, such as the subsoiling shovel, rotary tiller, root cutter, and moldboard plough, the interaction mechanism between soil-touch part and soil was investigated. Three types of narrow soil-touch parts were designed for the investigation. They were of rectangular cutter teeth, triangular cutter teeth and circular cutter teeth. The triangular cutter teeth were trimmed at a 45° angle relative to the vertical direction. Each cutter tooth was featured a top section with two 11mm-diameter through-holes at 50mm intervals, for installation and safety, as shown in (Fig. 1). Soil

Soil samples were collected from the experimental field of Southwest University, located at coordinates 29°48′49"N, 106°24′36"E. These samples represented typical slope cultivated purple soil in Southwest China.

Test instruments included: 9FQ-320 series soil crusher (manufacturer: Erzhong Machinery Equipment Co., LTD, Guangzhou, China); FBS-760A Halogen moisture tester (manufacturer: Fubus Testing Equipment Co., LTD, Xiamen, China); standard ring knife with diameter of 61.8 mm and depth of 20 mm (manufacturer: Juquan Civil Engineering Instrument Co., LTD, Nanjing, China); LQ-C20002 electronic balance with precision of 0.01 g and rang of 2000 g (manufacturer: Youkevit Electronic Technology Co., LTD, Kunshan, China); standard test screen compliant with GB/T6003.1-2012 (manufacturer: Shangyu Huafeng Hardware Instrument Co., LTD, Shaoxing, China); screen diameters from top to bottom were 2, 1, 0.6, 0.3, 0.1, and 0.05 mm; custom-made funnel with a 25 mm outlet diameter; food wrap, soil cutter, funnel tripod, etc.

Methods

To establish the coupling model of soil-touch parts based on DEM, it was crucial to determine various parameters: intrinsic soil parameters, contact parameters between soil particles, and contact parameters between soil and soil-touch part. These parameters were essential for model accuracy. Moisture content, true density, stacking density, particle size distribution, repose angle, Poisson’s ratio, surface energy and shear modulus were intrinsic parameters. Coefficients of restitution, static friction, and rolling friction of particle-particle and particle-65Mn were contact parameters. Some parameters can be directly measured, while others required identification by means of the optimal parameter combinations through experimental design approach, especially for the identification of dynamic-static repose angles. (2) Soil particle size and texture distribution. Soil particle size and texture distribution were analyzed using the screening method. A 1000 g soil sample was selected, and impurities such as plant residues and stones were removed. The soil sample was gradually screened through standard screens with diameters of 2, 1, 0.6, 0.3, 0.1, and 0.05 mm. The sieve method is commonly used for particles with diameters larger than 0.5 mm, while laser particle size analyzers (Modle: LT3600PLUS; manufacturer: Truth Optical Instrument Co., LTD, Zhuhai, China; measuring range: 0.015–3600 μm) are required for particles with diameters smaller than 0.5 mm. The mass of soil in each size grade was determined using an electronic balance, and the percentage was calculated.

(3) Static and dynamic repose angles. The static repose angle (SRA) was determined by piling particle material on a horizontal plane to create a cone. The SRA was influenced by several factors, such as coefficient of static friction between particles, coefficient of rolling friction and distribution of particle size. The test involved pouring soil into the funnel, allowing it to naturally fall and accumulate into a mound. The repose angle of the mound was recorded using a camera. Image J software was employed to measure a total of 4 repose angles in both the front view and A-A view, as shown in (Fig. 2a). Then, the tested SRA was the average values in each test.

The dynamic repose angle (DRA) was determined using a 65Mn rotary drum with inner dimensions of 150 mm in diameter and 30 mm in thickness. To observe and record the particle flow within the rotating drum, a plexiglass baffle was installed on one side of the drum26,27. To ensure a smooth and particle-free flow surface, the rotational speed of the drum was set to 7 rpm. The rotational speed was precisely adjusted using an STC12C5A60S2 microcontroller. The drum was filled to a 40% capacity. Images of the soil particle’ flow within the rotating drum were captured using a digital camera with a shutter speed of 1/1600 s. Images of soil particle flow were captured at the 3, 4 and 5 s marks to measure the DRA, as shown in (Fig. 2b). Then, the tested DRA was the average values in each test.

(4) Shear modulus and poisson’s ratio of soil. The Poisson’s ratio of soil particles was defined as the absolute value of the ratio between positive strain along the force direction and positive strain perpendicular to the force direction when the smallest particle unit underwent unidirectional compression or tension within the elastic range. Measuring the Poisson’s ratio of soil directly was a challenge due to the unique structure of particle aggregation of soil. Referring to literatures on clay, the Poisson’s ratio of primary soil typically fell within the range of 0.25 to 0.4.

Shear modulus was defined as the absolute value of the ratio between shear stress and shear strain under conditions of shear stress within the limit range of elastic deformation proportion. Elastic modulus, on the other hand, represented the ratio of stress to elastic strain of soil under an external force. The relationship between shear modulus (G), elastic modulus (EM), and Poisson’s ratio (ν) was given by: G = EM/(2 ‧ (1 + ν)). Following the national standard soil test procedure (SL237-029-1999), the elastic modulus of the soil was measured using triaxial shear equipment. Combining these measurements with existing research findings, the primary shear modulus was determined to be in the range of 107 ~ 108 Pa.

Simulation setup and pre-test

(1) Selection of particle contact model.

In EDEM software, several common particle contact models were available, including Hertz-Mindlin (no slip) model, Hertz-Mindlin with bonding model, Hertz-Mindlin with JKR model, Linear Cohesion model, etc. In this study, Hertz-Mindlin with JKR cohesion model, also referred to as the JKR cohesion model, was chosen as the particle contact model. This model was particularly suitable for powder particles, such as medicinal powder and moist materials (crops, ores, soil, etc.). These particles exhibited noticeable bonding and cohesion due to electrostatic forces and moisture content29.

(2) Parameters for soil particle size configuration.

Given that viscous soil was primarily composed of numerous fine particles that were bonded together into irregular shapes, the EDEM software employed a single-particle model. This model used the bonding force between wet particles to facilitate self-bonding, rather than relied on the pre-set multi-particle irregular body. The particle’s physical radius was set 2 mm, then the particle density was configured accordingly. (4) Test arrangement.

I Plackett–Burman design (PBD). The principle of PBD was based on the concept of an incomplete equilibrium model. It was a two-level optimization testing method aimed to achieve near saturation. This approach enabled the estimation of the main effects of multiple factors as accurate as possible with a minimal number of experiments. It allowed for the quick selection of the most influential factors from a larger set of factors for further investigation. The PBD was conducted considering both measured data and preliminary tests. The evaluation indices used were the static and dynamic repose angles. Each factor was set at two levels: high (+1) and low (−1), as listed in (Table 4). Test platform for mechanical properties of soil-touch parts

The soil bin test platform had dimensions of 2 m × 1 m × 1.5 m (length × width × height). To minimize tillage resistance of the soil-touch parts without compromising the tillage effect, and considering the impact of system friction, vibration, and other factors on test results and equipment lifespan, rolling bearings were employed as guide and support components. As shown in (Fig. 4), the platform included spline and drive motor (manufacturer: Dongli Electric Co., LTD, Taiwan, China; rated power: 3700W; rated voltage: 220/380 V; rated current: 15.9/9.2A; rated speed: 1420 r/min), driving parts, narrow tine, CGQ-FS force sensor (manufacturer: Tongli Weighing Sensing System Co., LTD, Bengbu, China; measuring range: 500 kg), control terminal (brand: Dell; manufacturer: Dell (China) Co., LTD, Xiamen, China; and the soil bin (self-made).

Soil bin test platform.

The work principle of the test platform was to control the speed and steering of the drive motor through the frequency modulator. The pinion and rack transmission system converted the circular motion of the drive motor into the linear motion of the active part. The drive motor drove the motion of the driving part that was installed with the soil-touch part through the force sensor to simulate the field farming process. Then, the sensor simultaneously monitored and recorded the horizontal resistance during the farming process. The rolling block was composed of 6304 rolling bearings, shafts and plates, which mainly withstood the forces from the motor and the soil-touch parts, while reducing the friction, vibration and energy consumption of the system. The soil bin was used to hold the test soil, and its dimensions were 1.8 m × 0.6 m × 0.6 m (length × width × height).

The speed and direction of the soil-touch part were regulated by the WSS motor via a frequency modulator. The rack transmission converted the rotary motion of the WSS motor into linear motion of the driving part. This driving part propelled the movement of the driven part equipped with the narrow tines, with the assistance of the force sensor. This setup simulated the agricultural field cultivation process, while the force sensor concurrently monitored and recorded the horizontal resistance during cultivation.

The optimization aimed to find the solutions that satisfied regression Eqs. (14), (15), taking into account certain targets and constraints.

There were several possible solutions for Eq. (16), namely the optimal solution was not unique. Among the available optimal solutions, a set of optimal values closely matched the measured soil accumulation data was selected as follows: C 0.185, E 0.057, G 0.720, and H 0.135.

The particle bed merging method was employed to assemble multiple identical small particle beds into a complete particle bed, since the size and number of particles in the soil bin were substantial. The process was as follows: (1) create a particle bed with dimensions of 600 mm × 250 mm × 450 mm; (2) save it as a material block once the particles were assembled; (3) construct a large soil bin with dimensions of 600 mm × 1000 mm × 450 mm. Instead of using the regular particle factory to generate the particle bed slowly, a material block was used to seamlessly splice together a bed of 4 particles, then it was allowed to stabilize. The relationship between dynamic and static repose angles and moisture content

To investigate the impact of soil moisture content on both the static and dynamic repose angles, purple soil samples with moisture contents of 10.54, 15, 20 and 25% were prepared, and their static and dynamic repose angles of simulated and measured values were measured, as shown in (Fig. 9). It is evident that moisture content significantly affects both the static and dynamic repose angles in (Fig. 9). At moisture contents not exceeding 25%, the static and dynamic repose angles increase gradually with increasing moisture content. This trend aligns with the findings of scholars Li et al.31 and Zheng et al.32 regarding different moisture contents in soil-transplanting soil and red soil. This influence may be attributed to how moisture content affects the parameters governing the interactions between soil particles and between soil particles and the soil-touch parts. At the same time, the consistent trend between measured and simulated values, coupled with small error margins, once again demonstrates the reliability of the DEM parameters of slope cultivated purple soil in this study.

Conclusions

In this study, the dynamic and static repose angles were utilized as response values. A combination of field test and DEM simulation was employed to optimize and determine simulation parameters for purple soil in Southwest China. Three different types of narrow soil-touch parts were designed and manufactured. The interaction model between these soil-touch parts and purple soil was established. The reliability of the DEM simulation parameters and the interaction model was further validated by comparing measured and simulated values for the work resistance of the soil-touch parts and the disturbed soil area. Main conclusions were drawn as follows:

(1) The Plackett–Burman test results for DEM simulation indicated that: surface energy and the coefficient of rolling friction of particle-to-particle had an extremely significant impact on both the static and dynamic repose angles; the coefficient of static friction and rolling friction of particle-to-65Mn had significant effects on the static repose angle; other physical parameters had less significant effects on either the static or dynamic repose angle. The results provided valuable information about which parameters needed to be optimized to improve the accuracy of DEM simulations for the behavior of soil particles.

(1) The Plackett–Burman test results for DEM simulation indicated that: surface energy and the coefficient of rolling friction of particle-to-particle had an extremely significant impact on both the static and dynamic repose angles; the coefficient of static friction and rolling friction of particle-to-65Mn had significant effects on the static repose angle; other physical parameters had less significant effects on either the static or dynamic repose angle. The results provided valuable information about which parameters needed to be optimized to improve the accuracy of DEM simulations for the behavior of soil particles.

(2) The BBD test results showed that: surface energy and coefficient of rolling friction of particle-to-particle had extremely significant influence on the static and dynamic repose angles; the quadratic terms of surface energy and the coefficient of static friction of particle-65Mn had significant effect on the static repose angle; the quadratic term of the coefficient of static friction of particle-65Mn had a significant effect on the dynamic repose angle. The results confirmed the importance of certain physical parameters in accurately simulating the behavior of soil particles in DEM simulations and further validated the findings from the PBD test.

(2) The BBD test results showed that: surface energy and coefficient of rolling friction of particle-to-particle had extremely significant influence on the static and dynamic repose angles; the quadratic terms of surface energy and the coefficient of static friction of particle-65Mn had significant effect on the static repose angle; the quadratic term of the coefficient of static friction of particle-65Mn had a significant effect on the dynamic repose angle. The results confirmed the importance of certain physical parameters in accurately simulating the behavior of soil particles in DEM simulations and further validated the findings from the PBD test.

(3) The optimal parameters were obtained as follows: surface energy 0.134 J/m2, coefficient of rolling friction of particle-to-particle 0.231, coefficient of static friction of particle-65Mn 1.045, and coefficient of rolling friction of particle-65Mn 0.228. Under the optimal parameters, the average relative errors for the static repose angle and dynamic repose angle were 1.934 and 2.289%, respectively. The simulated and measured static and dynamic repose angles fitted well, and the accuracy of the DEM simulation for purple soil was improved as well.

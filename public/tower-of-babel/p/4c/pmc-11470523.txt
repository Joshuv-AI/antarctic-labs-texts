Private entrepreneurs’ technical background and innovation output: The perspective of innovation quantity and quality
Authors: Yi Yang, Zengji Song, Shouxun Wen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This study investigates the impact of entrepreneurs' technical backgrounds on innovation output from the perspective of innovation quantity and quality, using data from A-share listed private companies in China between 2009 and 2018. Our analysis reveals that CEOs with technical expertise significantly enhance both the quantity and quality of firm innovation. We identify three key mechanisms through which technical backgrounds promote innovation: increased management attention to innovation, reduced information asymmetry, and enhanced long-term investment. These findings contribute to the literature on Upper Echelons Theory and corporate innovation, offering practical insights for private enterprises aiming to achieve talent-driven technological innovation and foster high-quality development.

Introduction

Despite the rapid growth of the Chinese economy, a significant technology gap remains between China and the developed countries. For example, the manufacturing of high-end semiconductor chips continues to be dominated by foreign manufacturers, making semiconductor chips China’s largest imported commodity by value. The U.S. export controls on technology have had a crippling effect on Chinese companies such as ZTE and Huawei, highlighting the dependence on Western technology as a serious bottleneck for China’s sustainable development. In China, the private economy contributes more than 70 % of innovations in science and technology according to the data released by the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) in 2019. Furthermore, more than 30 % of CEOs in listed companies have a professional background according to the China Stock Market and Accounting Research (CSMAR) database. Private enterprises are primarily the ones that develop new technology and bring it to the market, acting as a bridge between technological innovation and economic development [1]. Therefore, studying innovation within private enterprises is of great practical significance.

For private enterprises, technological innovations are important for building competitiveness and enhancing firm value [2]. Schumpeter’s innovation theory suggests that entrepreneurs play a key role in the corporate innovation process. More specifically, the Upper Echelons Theory argues that the personal characteristics of a company’s CEO, such as age, education level, and experience, affect corporate behaviors, including technological innovation activities [3,4]. There have been many studies focusing on the relationship between the personal characteristics of CEOs and corporate behaviors. Empirical evidence shows that various factors such as the age, gender, education level of CEOs [5], their political background [6], financial background [7], overseas experience [8], military service [9], academic research experience [10], and even their hobbies [11] significantly affect company’s behaviors. However, an important characteristic that affects innovation is the CEOs' technical background. Some studies have investigated the relationship between CEOs' technical background and corporate innovation from the perspective of innovation input and firm performance [12,13]. However, few of them have focused on the area of innovation output, especially the distinction between quantity and quality of innovation.

Although the innovation input of Chinese enterprises has grown over the years, it has sometimes resulted in excessive innovation input with insufficient innovation output [14]. There are also cases of blind pursuit of innovation quantity at the expense of innovation quality [15], leading to “innovation illusions” and “innovation bubbles” that do not support sustainable innovation-driven development [[16], [17]]. To identify “innovation bubbles”, it is necessary to evaluate innovation performance in terms of both quantity and quality.

Using data from A-share listed private companies in China from 2009 to 2018, this paper empirically studies the relationship between CEOs' technical background and their firms' innovation output from the perspective of innovation quantity and quality. Our findings suggest that entrepreneurs with technical expertise can significantly increase both the quantity and quality of firm innovation. Mechanism studies indicate that entrepreneurs with a technical background promote innovation output through three main avenues: increasing management’s attention to innovation, reducing asymmetric information, and enhancing long-term investment.

The contributions of this paper are fourfold. First, this study provides empirical evidence showing a positive correlation between the technical background of CEOs and the innovation performance of their companies. Utilizing data from A-share listed private companies in China from 2009 to 2018, the findings demonstrate that companies led by CEOs with technical expertise tend to have higher levels of innovation performance. This includes both the quantity and quality of innovations, indicating that technical knowledge at the top management level is beneficial for driving innovation within firms. Second, prior research often emphasized innovation inputs (such as R&D spending) and overall firm performance metrics without dissecting the nature of innovation outputs. This paper distinguishes between innovation quantity and innovation quality in evaluating innovation output, offering a more nuanced understanding of innovation performance. Additionally, the findings contribute to the literature on Upper Echelons Theory and corporate innovation by highlighting the specific influence of CEOs' technical expertise on both the quantity and quality of innovation output. Finally, the study offers practical insights for private enterprises in implementing talent-driven technological innovation strategies and fostering high-quality development, which can guide better recruitment and management practices.

The remaining parts of this paper are organized as follows. The next section presents the theoretical analysis and research hypothesis. Section 3 describes the data source and model construction. Section 4 reports the empirical results. Section 5 conducts the mechanism test. Section 6 concludes.

Theoretical analysis and research hypothesis

The Upper Echelons Theory suggests that managers view business situations from a highly personalized perspective based on their demographics, experiences, values and other personal characteristics [3,4]. In particular, the technical background of managers has a much greater impact on corporate innovation than other characteristics [18]. Moreover, the effectiveness of innovation is maximized when both the quantity and quality of innovation are considered complementary [19]. Therefore, it is essential to measure innovation output by evaluating both dimensions.

The relationship between CEOs’ technical background and innovation quantity

There are several mechanisms through which CEOs with technical backgrounds can influence innovation quantity, that quantifies a firm's innovation performance and is often measured by the number of patents a firm holds [2,6].

From the perspective of emotional and cognitive factors, managers make decisions based on their knowledge, perception, and trust [20]. CEOs with technical backgrounds are more inclined to support R&D projects due to their familiarity and comfort with technological challenges [21]. They are also more confident in the face of uncertainty in technological innovation because of their technical expertise, making them more open to new technological concepts and less risk-averse than those without a technical background [5]. Thus, they may be more likely to invest their company's resources in producing innovation and foster an environment conducive to increased innovation activities.

The second channel can be explained from the perspective of human capital. CEOs with technical backgrounds typically possess a deep understanding of technological processes, enabling them to manage innovation activities more efficiently. CEOs with a technical background typically start their careers in a technical position and later move into management; this combined experience of technology and management tends to have an important impact on the effectiveness of the enterprise’s innovation. For example, CEOs with technical backgrounds may be willing to establish incentive mechanisms in the R&D section, which motivate employees to innovate. As a result, the amount of innovation would increase.

The last mechanism is from the view of professional expertise. These CEOs can leverage their technical knowledge to optimize the use of innovation inputs, including resources such as funds, human resources and equipment, to foster technological advancements [22]. This expertise helps in organizing and prioritizing innovation efforts effectively, leading to an increase in the number of patents generated. Based on these insights, we propose the following hypothesis.H1Entrepreneurs' technical background is positively related to innovation quantity.

Entrepreneurs' technical background is positively related to innovation quantity.

The relationship between CEOs’ technical background and innovation quality

Another aspect of the effect of private entrepreneurs' technical background on innovation output is innovation quality. That is because the increasing investment in innovation has occasionally led to an overemphasis on input without corresponding output. While an increase in innovation quantity is important, it must be complemented by high-quality innovations to add substantial value to a company [15]. High-quality innovation is characterized by the innovation’s technological impact, realized value, and originality [23]. Many literatures suggest that the influence and value of a patent can be characterized by the number of citations. A higher number of citations means greater recognition by subsequent innovators and, therefore, a greater technological impact [24]. A high number of citations also makes it easier for the market to recognize the patented technology and increases its market value [1]. Some researchers have also used the breadth of a patent to measure its quality. Greater breadth implies a higher degree of originality because it typically means that it embodies a greater complexity and generality of knowledge, making it more difficult to imitate and improve at the margin [16,25].

There are several mechanisms through which CEOs with technical backgrounds can affect innovation quality. Firstly, from the perspective of personal characteristics, resource dependence theory suggests that CEOs with technical backgrounds can bring diverse and valuable resources to their firms, such as specialized knowledge, strategic insights, and high-level technical networks [26]. In particular, executives with a technical background could bring their professional talents to innovation activities and provide valuable advice and guidance [27], which enhance their company’s ability to overcome technological difficulties and produce stronger patents. At the same time, they have a sharper insight into new technologies in their industry, which enables them to better predict the future development of the industry, quickly identify where the technology gap is, and help their companies select R&D projects that are in line with the industry's direction [28]. All of these factors help to increase the potential market value of the patents.

Secondly, the mechanism exists in the professional networks. CEOs with technical expertise have access to extensive technical networks, including researchers, industry peers, universities, and research institutes. These connections facilitate the recruitment of skilled personnel and the acquisition of cutting-edge knowledge, enhancing the overall quality of innovation.

Furthermore, the reputation of technically skilled executives strengthens their firms' ability to secure valuable information and resources from professional networks [29]. This integration of external knowledge and resources supports the development of novel and high-impact innovations, thereby improving patent originality and market value.

In summary, CEOs with a technical background can use their professional skills and networks to enrich the influence, value, and the originality of the patents, thereby improving the overall quality of the enterprise innovation. Consequently, we propose the following hypothesis.H2Entrepreneurs' technical background improves the quality of innovation.

Entrepreneurs' technical background improves the quality of innovation.

Research design

Sample selection and data

This paper utilizes information on private, A-share publicly listed companies in China from 2009 to 2018. The technical background information on the executives is derived from the CSMAR database and their CVs are sourced from the Internet. The companies’ innovation data is obtained from the China Research Data Service Platform (CNRDS) and the financial data is from the CSMAR database. We exclude companies that (1) are designated as ST or ∗ST (indicates that they are undergoing serious financial issues); (2) are missing important information; (3) have undergone major asset restructuring; and (4) are in the financial industry. This leaves us with a balanced panel data consisting of 5170 observations. Continuous variables are also winsorized at the 1 % tails.

Variable definitions

We use both the quantity and the quality of innovation to indicate the effectiveness of an enterprise’s innovation (Innovation). Two measurements are employed for innovation quantity: the total number of patent applications in a single year (Inno-apply1) and the number of applications for invention patents and utility model patents in a single year (Inno-apply2). Innovation quality is also measured in two ways. The first metric is the number of citations for all the company’s patents (Inno-cited). Because the number of citations accumulates over time and thus depends on when the information is collected, a quasi-structural method, which weights the number over the years, is used to compare the numbers [24,30]. The second is the number of patent applications for inventions and utility models (Inno-ipc), as classified by the International Patent Classification system (IPC). As discussed previously, these patents reflect a wider breadth of patent knowledge [16,25].

The primary explanatory variables are the information on the company’s chief executive, who may be its general manager, chief executive officer, or president. Based on the information provided in their resumes, those with professional certificates in engineering, construction, and designing, etc., and those who have worked in engineering, design, R&D, and other technical positions for more than two years are considered to have a technical background. In conformance with the existing literature [10,12], control variables include enterprise characteristics, such as size (Size), age (Firmage), return on assets (ROA), debt-to-asset ratio (Leverage), total operating income growth rate (Growth), cash-to-asset ratio (Cash), Tobin’s Q (TobinQ), management shareholding (MngHold), top ten shareholder holdings (Top10hold), as well as executive characteristics, such as gender, age, education, whether they hold multiple positions in the company, and shareholding percentage (Hold). Innovation represents the innovation output of the enterprise, Technical is a dummy variable taking the value of one if the executive has a technical background and zero otherwise, and Controls include the enterprise and executive characteristics. To account for the time required to produce innovation output, the explanatory variables are lagged by one year relative to the dependent variable. Year, industrial, and provincial fixed effects are also controlled for.

The study shows that there is a positive correlation between the technical background of CEOs and innovation performance. When we separate the performance into quantity and quality, statistics show that companies whose managers have a technical background outperform those that do not, in terms of both quantity and quality. This means that CEOs with a technical background would not only increase the number of innovations, but also reduce "innovation bubbles" that do not lead to sustainable innovation-driven development [[16], [17]]. It is therefore necessary to divide the assessment of innovation into quantity and quality. Previous research suggests that companies founded by academic entrepreneurs play a key role in improving a region's capacity for innovation [31]. Similarly, entrepreneurship and innovation have proven to be strong drivers of regional development and growth [32]. This article provides new perspectives and evidence for existing research.

Endogeneity test

Although the above results show that the technical background of executives positively affects the level of innovation, the results may be subject to possible endogeneity issues, such as omitted variable bias and reverse causality. To address them, we use an instrumental variable approach to estimate the effects of the executive background on innovation. In particular, this paper selects whether the executive’s home city is an old industrial base (Industrial-city) as an instrumental variable for the executive’s t technical background. The reason for choosing this as the instrumental variable is as follows. First, old industrial bases are locations where traditional industries, and therefore talents with technical backgrounds, were concentrated. Individuals who grew up in such cities and the surrounding areas are more likely to have been influenced by the region’s technical atmosphere and choose a technical college major and technical positions in their career. Second, most of the industries in old industrial bases are labor-intensive, which means the proportion of the population engaged in a technical job is also greater in these regions than in others. Thus, executives who grew up around old industrial bases are more likely to be engaged in technical jobs. Third, an executive’s hometown province generally differs from the province her company is located in. Therefore, the number of old industrial bases in the executive’s hometown province should not have a direct effect on the company’s technological innovation level, thus satisfying the exogeneity requirement for instrumental variables. There are 120 cities officially designated as old industrial base cities in China.1 The binary variable Industrial-city, takes the value of one if it is an old industrial city and zero otherwise.

The results of the two-stage least squares estimation (2SLS) with the instrumental variable Industrial-city are shown in Table 5. The first column shows the estimation results of the first stage. The estimated coefficient of Industrial-city is positive and significant at the 1 % level, indicating that having more old industrial base cities in an executive’s hometown province makes the executive more likely to have a technical background. This validates our theory for choosing it as the instrument. The P-value in the Anderson LM statistic is less than 0.1, indicating that the IV result passes the under-identification test. The Cragg-Donald Wald F-statistic is greater than the 10 % Stock-Yogo critical value, indicating that there is no weak instrument issue. Columns 2, 3, 4, and 5 of Table 5 give the results from the second stage estimation. An executive’s technical background has a significant, positive impact on the quantity and quality of innovation, which is consistent with the results from the OLS estimation. Robustness check

To check the robustness of our results, we also considered the following variations of our regression model: (1) the explanatory variables and the control variables are lagged two years and three years relative to the dependent variable; (2) the number of invention patent, utility model patents and design patent applications are used separately to measure the number of innovations; (3) self-citations are excluded from the number of citations for patents; (4) the number of IPC invention patent and utility model patent applications are used separately to measure the quality of innovation; and (5) enterprise fixed effects are controlled. The results from these robustness tests were all consistent with the results of the OLS regression and showed that executives’ technical background has a significant, positive effect on innovation.

Mechanism for the estimated effects

Results of Section 4 showed that the technical background of executives positively affects both the quantity and the quality of the innovation output. This section investigates the channels, such as managerial actions and decisions, through which the effect occurs. In particular, we test the intermediary effect suggested by Wen et al. [33] by construct the following regression model.(2)Mediatori,t+1=β0+β1∗Technicali,t+∑βk∗Controlsi,t++Yeart+Industryi+Provincei+εi,t(3)Innovationi,t+1=β0+β1∗Technicali,t+β2Mediatori,t+1+∑βk∗Controlsi,t+Yeart+Industryi+Provincei+εi,t

In the model, Mediator is the variable representing the intermediate channel. We investigate three different channels using this specification.

Attention to innovation as the mediator

Attention reflects the active cognitive mental process of the decision-maker and is a key factor influencing decision-making. Thus, decisions that are made by executives depend on which influencing factors are allocated to their limited attention. Among them, the personal characteristics of the executives are believed to be the key factors affecting their attention allocation [20]. Because managers have limited information processing capabilities and can only focus on certain aspects of their firms, their high attention to innovation could result in firm policies that favor innovation. They are also more likely to be inclined towards allocating resources to support innovation projects. Through these actions, an atmosphere is created that is conducive to innovation, and employees are encouraged to be innovative [34]. Employees' enthusiasm for innovation, in turn, increases their chances of successfully solving technical difficulties, which plays a significant role in generating the innovation output of an enterprise [35]. In addition, executives' attention to innovation enhances the output of innovation activities by contributing to the discovery of possible benefits from innovation-related government policies, the identification of technical gaps in the market, and new technological development directions [36]. In summary, executives with a technical background help generate a higher quantity and quality innovation output by paying more attention to innovation themselves and by directing the management’s attention to innovation.

The percentage of sentences that contain these keywords is used as a proxy for the degree of management's attention to innovation. Information about innovation as the mediator

Lack of information could be a major hindrance to innovation. Enhancing information symmetry regarding innovation through improved sharing and communication between enterprises and external stakeholders can bolster the enterprise's capacity to identify, evaluate, and advance innovation initiatives. This effort also mitigates risks associated with innovation projects, thereby fostering the creation of more robust and high-quality innovations [37]. Therefore, establishing communication channels with external institutions for information exchange is particularly important. These channels may be inter-enterprise cooperation, enterprise-university research cooperation, or industry-wide cooperation between enterprises and the scientific community. By establishing such communication channels, executives can reduce information asymmetry, obtain innovation information from scientific research institutions, and gain access to cutting-edge technologies, which helps their enterprises by lowering the cost of innovation through resource sharing and enhancing the innovation effectiveness [38].

Executives with a technical background help their companies obtain innovation information in two ways. Firstly, executives with similar backgrounds are likely to have extensive interpersonal relationships. Meanwhile, their professional roles within large enterprises facilitate their ability to proactively establish cooperative channels for exchanging information with others. Second, an executive’s technical background can also convey a reputation for technology for the company and send a signal that attracts outsiders to pay attention to its innovation activities. This, in turn, may increase the likelihood of other enterprises voluntarily seeking cooperation opportunities with the company on technological innovation [39]. In these ways, executives with a technical background can contribute to increasing both the quantity and the quality of innovation output by reducing information asymmetry related to innovation.

Long-term investment as the mediator

Creating innovation demands not just human resources and information, but also investment in fixed assets, such as land and equipment. Innovating is a long-term activity with high risks and uncertainties. Although it creates future value for an enterprise, it also requires a long-term commitment of land, buildings, equipment, and other factors of production, which increases the level of long-term investment for the enterprise. Therefore, innovating requires the enterprise’s decision-makers to have the vision and the courage to make long-term investments.

Executives with a technical background are likely to be less technologically risk-averse than those without because they are typically more confident in technological innovation and more open to new technological ideas [5]. They can rely on their professional skills and management power to control the technological innovation process, optimize the allocation of resource, and ensure the continuity of investment in innovation [27]. In addition, they often have a deeper understanding of technology and can more accurately judge the potential value of new technologies, making them more willing to invest in technological innovation for the long-term growth of their enterprises [12]. Therefore, we anticipate that executives with a technical background will promote innovation by bolstering their company's level of long-term investment.

Conclusion

Like many nations, innovation takes a central position in China’s modernization drive, as scientific and technological self-reliance has an important and long-term significance for the development of the nation and its enterprises. This paper utilized data from China’s A-share listed private companies spanning from 2009 to 2018 to investigate the impact of executives' technological backgrounds on their company’s innovation output, as well as the mechanisms through which this impact occurs. It found that the technological background has a positive and significant effect on both the quantity and the quality of innovations. We also showed that the technical background promotes innovation through three mechanisms: increasing the management’s attention on technological innovation, reducing the information asymmetry regarding innovation, and increasing the level of long-term investment. Our research has some limitations. The empirical analysis only uses the data on private enterprises, and the measures of innovation output are based only on patents. For our future research, we aim to incorporate state-owned enterprises into our analysis. Additionally, while this paper treats technical background as a binary variable, the depth and breadth of this background are likely influential. Therefore, we plan to explore using a continuous variable to gauge the intensity of technical expertise and examine its impact on innovation output.

These limitations notwithstanding, our research provides the following policy implications. First, to promote more innovation, policymakers should focus on the leading role of the executives in technological innovation. Although the executives serve the interests of the shareholders, their management decisions nevertheless reflect their personal incentives. As we have shown, executives with a technical background have the motivation and the technical ability to promote innovation. Their insight into new technologies and newly invented products and their willingness to take risks when facing new technological uncertainty make them more likely to make long-term investments that help innovation. They are also more adept at collaborating with other institutions to establish diverse channels for acquiring valuable innovative information. Internally, by directing their company’s focus on innovation, creating a good company culture for innovation, and stimulating employees' enthusiasm for innovation, executives with technical backgrounds can enhance the company’s innovation capabilities, increase the innovation output levels, and ensure that it has a long-term competitive advantage in innovation.

Second, enterprises themselves should also focus on cultivating the inherent strength of the enterprise in technological innovation. The current external environment in China for example is complex and rapidly changing. Efficiently leveraging external resources, such as external funding and government policies, is undoubtedly important for innovating. However, to improve the innovation output and break through technical barriers, more attention should be paid to improving the innovation capability of the company’s R&D team, cultivating innovative talents, increasing the knowledge of the personnel, reducing dependence on external resources, and building sustainable innovation capabilities.

Third, the government should implement more policies that support enterprise innovations, such as increasing incentives for attracting technical talents, increasing transfer payments to enterprises that are engaged in innovation, encouraging enterprises to undertake major national science and technology projects, supporting enterprises to cooperate with governments, universities, and research institutions, strengthening the construction of technological innovation platforms, and promoting industrial integration for innovation. Such policies can help concentrate key elements of innovation generation to enterprises, stimulate the innovation potential of the enterprises, and accelerate the development of a scientifically and technologically powerful economy.

Rapid Expansion of Bilateral Common Iliac Artery Pseudoaneurysms in a Complex Postoperative Patient
Authors: Eun-Ah Jo
Published: 2026

Although rare, an iliac artery pseudoaneurysm is a critical vascular emergency with high mortality and morbidity, often linked to the rapid loss of arterial wall integrity due to infection [1-3]. These lesions are particularly challenging to treat in patients with a complex surgical history [1,2,4]. We present the case of a 72-year-old male who developed rapidly expanding bilateral common iliac artery pseudoaneurysms following ileostomy repair, necessitating urgent treatment.

The patient had a history of ankylosing spondylitis and prolonged hospitalization after a traumatic lower extremity crush injury in December 2024. His course was complicated by cytomegalovirus colitis with hematochezia, requiring exploratory laparotomy and ileostomy formation in May 2025. At that time, he also had gram-negative bacteremia (Pantoea agglomerans). Ileostomy repair was performed in September 2025. Postoperatively, the patient experienced persistent ileus and hematochezia. Vital signs were stable; however, laboratory evaluation revealed anemia (hemoglobin, 9.7 g/dL), thrombocytopenia (platelet count, 59×103/µL), and an elevated C-reactive protein level (5.19 mg/dL). Cultures were negative. Although microbiological confirmation was not obtained, the rapid aneurysm enlargement in the setting of severe intra-abdominal inflammation raised concern for an inflammatory or possibly infected process (Fig. 1). The vascular abnormality had been present on earlier imaging but had not been recognized initially.

The recent abdominal operation created a potentially hostile surgical environment. Therefore, two main strategies were considered: retroperitoneal in situ repair using rifampicin-soaked Dacron or autologous vein grafts, or endovascular aneurysm repair (EVAR) using a rifampicin-flushed endograft to mitigate infection risk. After discussion, the patient underwent EVAR and recovered without rupture or endoleak (Fig. 2). Postoperative imaging and laboratory tests showed no signs of sac enlargement or inflammation (Fig. 3).

This case highlights the aggressive behavior of iliac artery pseudoaneurysms in a complex postoperative patient, and underscores the importance of meticulous comparison of serial imaging studies for early detection of rapidly evolving vascular abnormalities.

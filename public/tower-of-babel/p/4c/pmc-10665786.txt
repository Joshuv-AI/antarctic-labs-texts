Complete dissection of right paratracheal lymph nodes (stations 2R and 4R) is critical to improve the prognosis of lung cancer patients: A retrospective cohort study
Authors: Kejia Zhao, Jiandong Mei, Binbin Hu, Chenglin Guo, Shiyou Wei, Xudong Yang, Zhenyu Yang, Jian Zhang, Yin Ku, Yao Zheng, Di Wu, Shasha Li, Yonghong Mao, Yu Ding, Lunxu Liu
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe optimal extent of mediastinal lymph node dissection is still under debate. This study aimed to investigate the prognostic impact of complete dissection of right paratracheal lymph nodes (LNs) in right‐sided non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and evaluate the potential patient population who will particularly benefit from right paratracheal node dissection (RPND).MethodsBetween January 2009 and December 2019, we retrospectively reviewed 2650 patients with primary right‐sided NSCLC who underwent pulmonary surgery with lymphadenectomy in the Western China Lung Cancer Database. A total of 2447 patients received both 2R and 4R LNs dissection (complete RPND group), 162 patients received only 2R or 4R LNs dissection (incomplete RPND group), and 41 patients received neither 2R nor 4R LNs dissection (no RPND group). Overall survival (OS) was analyzed.ResultsThe metastasis rates in stations 2R and 4R were 6.5% and 8.0%, respectively. In stage N2 patients, the frequency of involvement of stations 2R/4R was 74.8%. The complete RPND group had a significantly better survival than the incomplete and no RPND group (5‐year OS, 79.5% vs. 72.7% vs. 65.5%; p < 0.001). In the multivariate analysis, status of RPND (incomplete RPND vs. complete RPND: HR 1.45, 95% CI: 1.10–1.90; p = 0.009; no RPND vs. complete RPND: HR 2.25, 95% CI: 1.37 to 3.69; p = 0.001), age, gender, tumor size, histological type, pTNM stage, pT stage, pN stage, and adjuvant treatment were independent factors for OS.ConclusionsComplete RPND brings survival benefits to patients with right‐sided NSCLC. We suggest complete RPND as a standard procedure for patients with right‐sided NSCLC.Keywords: 2R lymph node, 4R lymph node, non‐small cell lung cancer, prognosis, right paratracheal node dissection

INTRODUCTION

Systematic lymph node dissection (LND) is important for accurate staging and long‐term survival in patients with resectable non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). However, no consensus has been reached as to the optimal extent of mediastinal LND (MLND). The National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines and European Society of Thoracic Surgeons recommend that systematic MLND with at least three N2 stations assessed is mandatory for all patients with operable NSCLC. 1 , 2 Few other studies have explored the partial extent of MLND based on the metastasis rates of LNs and the prognosis impacts of their dissections. 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 Therefore, sufficient evidence for the rigid specifications on the removal of mediastinal stations is still lacking.

Recent research showed that patients with right paratracheal LN metastases tended to have a poor prognosis, indicating the underlying importance of right paratracheal node dissection (RPND). 7 Nevertheless, there has been no direct evidence that RPND should be routinely performed during MLND for right‐sided NSCLC patients. Thus, complete dissection of 2R and 4R LNs is not effectively performed during surgery in some situations. For example, when performing RPND on patients with tumors in the middle or lower lobe, dissection of 2R LN is sometimes overlooked. One of the explanations for this phenomenon is that the distance between the drainage area of the LNs and the location of the tumors is often one of the indicators that the surgeons consider the extent of LND. There have been quite a few studies conducted tumor site‐specific prognostic analysis of LND for NSCLC patients so far. 8 , 9 , 10 Therefore, based on the emphasis on treating the 2R and 4R LNs as a whole area when performing dissection, we conducted this retrospective study to evaluate the clinical significance of complete RPND and to clarify the characteristics, especially the primary tumor location, of patients who would benefit from RPND.

METHODS

Ethics statement

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of West China Hospital (no. 2021–1357). Informed consent was waived for this retrospective study.

Patients

Between January 2009 and December 2019, a total of 6836 primary right‐sided NSCLC patients who underwent pulmonary surgery with lymphadenectomy in the Western China Lung Cancer Database, a large prospective database led by the West China Hospital, were initially reviewed. Of these patients, the following were excluded: patients age < 18 years or > 80 years, patients with multiple primary NSCLC, patients with pathological TNM (pTNM) stage 0, IIIB, IIIC, or IV, patients who underwent wedge or segment resection, incomplete resection (R1 and R2 resection), or incomplete MLND (number of resected mediastinal nodal stations <3), 1 patients who received neoadjuvant therapy or died during the preoperative period, and patients with an incomplete clinical record (Figure 1).

Patient selection process. NSCLC, non‐small cell lung cancer; pTNM, pathological TNM; MLND, mediastinal lymph node dissection; RPND, right paratracheal node dissection.

Surgical techniques

Patients underwent pulmonary lobectomy with SND by video‐assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS), Da Vinci‐robot‐assisted thoracic surgery (RATS), or thoracotomy. Lobectomy was conducted via “single‐direction” strategy, and MLND was performed by a “nongrasping en bloc” approach as in our previous description. 11 , 12 During the MLND, we removed at least three mediastinal nodal stations. Systematic MLND implied resection of all the mediastinal tissues containing the LNs en bloc, and RPND was performed as reported previously. 11

Follow‐up

Patients were followed up according to a standard procedure, which includes outpatient visits the first month postoperatively, then every 3 months for the first 2 years and every 6 months for the subsequent 3 years, and annually thereafter. During routine follow‐up, physical examinations, laboratory tests, computed tomography (CT) scans of the chest and abdomen, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the head and bone scintigraphy were performed. The follow‐up period was completed in May 2021 or to the date of death of patients. The median follow‐up was 38 months (range: 2 to 173 months).

Data collection and definition

Clinical records, including age, sex, smoking history, tumor location, tumor size, histological type, surgical technique, pTNM stage, adjuvant treatment, postoperative complications, and follow‐up information, were collected. Histological typing was based on the 2011 classification of the International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer/American Thoracic Society /European Respiratory Society. 13 pTNM stage was determined according to the eighth edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer lung cancer staging system. 14 Follow‐up information consisted of overall survival (OS). OS was calculated from the date of surgery to the date of death or last follow‐up.

The extent of LN dissection and metastasis was also analyzed. LN stations were classified according to the LN map proposed by the International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer. 15 Patients enrolled in our study were divided into the complete RPND, incomplete RPND, and no RPND groups. The complete RPND group included patients with both 2R and 4R LNs dissection, the incomplete RPND group included patients with only 2R or 4R LN dissection, and the no RPND group contained patients without 2R and 4R LN dissection.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables are presented as numbers (%) and were analyzed by Chi‐square tests, while continuous variables are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median ± range and were compared using one‐way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Survival curves were estimated by the Kaplan–Meier method and compared using the log‐rank test. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression were used to evaluate the prognostic factors and to calculate hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence interval (CI) for OS. Only variables with statistical significance in univariate analyses were included in the multivariate regressions. Subgroup analyses of OS was also performed by the Kaplan–Meier method using the log‐rank test. Two‐sided p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 22.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and GraphPad Prism 5.0 (GraphPad Inc.).

RESULTS

Baseline characteristics

A total of 2650 patients were enrolled in this study, including 1301 (49.1%) males and 1349 (50.9%) females. The median age was 59 years (range: 25 to 80 years), and the mean tumor size was 2.62 ± 1.42 cm. Adenocarcinoma was the predominant histological type (80.7%, 2138/2650) in this cohort, and others included 376 (14.2%) squamous cell carcinoma and 136 (5.1%) other types. The pTNM stage distribution was stage I in 1867 (70.4%) cases, stage II in 381 (14.4%) cases, and stage III in 402 (15.2%) cases. Among all the patients, 2447 (92.3%) patients received complete RPND, 162 (6.1%) patients received incomplete RPND, and 41 (1.6%) patients received no RPND. The clinicopathological characteristics of patients in the three groups are summarized in Table 1. Differences were observed in terms of surgical technique (p < 0.001). The rates of complete RPND in upper, middle, and lower lobe NSCLC were 91.0% (1372/1507), 88.8% (341/384), and 87.2% (680/780), respectively. No significant differences were observed in postoperative complications between the complete RPND, incomplete RPND, and no RPND groups.

Metastasis rates of mediastinal lymph nodes

Among all mediastinal LNs, station 4R metastasis ranked first (8.0%, 205/2555), followed by station 2R (6.5%, 160/2447) and 3 (6.5%, 40/614); metastasis rates of stations 7, 8, and 9 were 5.6, 1.4, and 1.7%, respectively (Figure 2). When stratified by the tumor location, in the upper lobe, the highest incidence of metastasis was observed in station 4R (9.6%), followed by stations 2R and 3 (both 7.2%); in the middle lobe, the incidence of involvement of stations 3 and 7 was the highest (8.2%), followed by station 2R (7.1%) and 4R (5.4%); and in the lower lobe, station 7 was the most common site (11.3%). It is notable that metastases in stations 2R and 4R were not rare in lower lobe tumors (4.9% and 6.2%, respectively) (Table 2).

Distribution of mediastinal and right paratracheal lymph node metastases

A total of 329 (12.4%, 329/2650) patients were diagnosed as pathological N2. Figure 3 summarizes the distribution of mediastinal LN metastases in stage N2 patients. The frequency of involvement of stations 2R/4R was the highest (74.8%, 246/329). When stratified by the tumor site, the involvement of stations 2R/4R also ranked first in patients with upper or middle lobe tumors (92.0% and 69.6%, respectively). For lower lobe tumors, station 7 was the most frequent metastatic site (78.5%), followed by stations 2R/4R (48.6%) (Table S1).

Distribution of mediastinal LN metastases in stage N2 right‐sided NSCLC. LN, lymph nodes; NSCLC, non‐small cell lung cancer.

We further investigated the distribution of right paratracheal LN metastases. Patients with 2R and 4R LNs metastases accounted for 74.2% (115/155) of patients with 2R LN metastasis and 60.2% (115/191) of patients with 4R LN metastasis. In addition, the proportion of patients with 2R and 4R LNs metastases in patients with right paratracheal LNs metastasis in the upper, middle, and lower lobe was 49.0%, 44.8%, and 55.1%, respectively.

Survival outcomes

The 5‐year OS rate was 79.5% in the complete RPND group, 72.7% in the incomplete RPND group, and 65.5% in the no RPND group, which showed that patients in the complete RPND group had better survival than those in the incomplete or no RPND groups (incomplete RPND vs. complete RPND: HR 1.49, 95% CI: 1.15–1.94; p = 0.003; no RPND vs. complete RPND: HR 2.04, 95% CI: 1.25–3.33; p = 0.004) (Figure 4). In addition, status of RPND, several variables, such as age (p = 0.003), gender (p < 0.001), smoking history (p < 0.001), tumor size (p < 0.001), histological type (p < 0.001), surgical technique (p < 0.001), pTNM stage (p < 0.001), pT stage (p < 0.001), pN stage (p < 0.001), adjuvant treatment (p < 0.001), and postoperative complications (p = 0.001) were all significant factors for OS by univariate analysis. Additional multivariate analysis showed that status of RPND was an independent factor for OS (incomplete RPND vs. complete RPND: HR 1.45, 95% CI: 1.10–1.90; p = 0.009; no RPND vs. complete RPND: HR 2.25, 95% CI: 1.37 to 3.69; p = 0.001), together with age (p = 0.003), gender (p = 0.001), tumor size (p < 0.001), histological type (p < 0.001), pTNM stage (p < 0.001), pT stage (p = 0.035), pN stage (p = 0.031), and adjuvant treatment (p = 0.037) (Table 3).

Subgroup analysis according to the primary tumor site revealed that complete RPND was associated with a better prognosis in patients with upper lobe tumor (Table S2 and Figure S1, incomplete RPND vs. complete RPND: HR 1.55, 95% CI 1.07–2.24; p = 0.021; no RPND vs. complete RPND: HR 2.89, 95% CI: 1.53–5.46; p = 0.001). Other subgroups, including patients with age ≤ 60 years, female patients, nonsmoking patients, patients with tumor size >3 cm, patients with adenocarcinoma, patients with pT2 stage, patients without adjuvant treatment, and patients without postoperative complications also indicated better survival outcome in the complete RPND group (Table S2).

DISCUSSION

A recent study has revealed that patients with metastases in their right paratracheal lymph nodes tend to have a poor prognosis, 7 emphasizing the significance of rRPND. However, there is currently no concrete evidence suggesting that RPND should be routinely performed during mediastinal LND for right‐sided NSCLC patients. Consequently, complete removal of 2R and 4R LNs may not always be strictly performed during surgery in certain scenarios. For instance, when carrying out RPND on patients with tumors in the middle or lower lobes, the dissection of 2R LN can sometimes be overlooked. Therefore, the RPND can be divided into three groups; complete RPND (dissect 2 and 4 stations), incomplete RPND (dissect 2 or 4 station) and no RPND (did not dissect 2 or 4 stations). According to our analysis, we confirmed that the extent of RPND performed would have a significant impact on patient prognosis.

From a total of 2650 patients, we observed a relatively high metastasis rate of station 2R, that is, approximately 6.5%, second to the rate of 8.0% for station 4R among all mediastinal LNs. Liang et al. 10 reported that the metastasis rates of stations 2R and 4R for different primary tumor lobes were 9.4% to 15.1% and 11.1% to 21.5%, respectively, which were higher than that found in our study. This was probably because of the large proportion of stage I (70.4%) and stage II (14.4%) patients in this study. In addition, the inclusion and exclusion criteria in our study were strict in that we excluded patients with the number of resected mediastinal nodal stations <3. Nevertheless, in the analysis of the distribution of mediastinal LN metastases, we found that 74.8% of patients with N2 stage had involvement of stations 2R/4R. Several studies which only focused on right middle lobe NSCLC reported that the superior mediastinal zone (stations 2R–4R) had a high metastasis frequency (approximately 48.5% to 80.0%). 10 , 16 , 17 , 18 , 19 In addition, we analyzed the distribution of right paratracheal LN metastases and found that approximately 50% of patients had both 2R and 4R LNs metastases, and these patients accounted for 60%–75% of patients with only 2R or 4R LN metastasis. These results showed that 2R and 4R LNs are usually in a state of co‐metastasis. Thus, we confirmed that stations 2R and 4R are two frequent metastatic sites and recommend treating them as a whole area during the MLND process for patients with NSCLC.

In our study, we emphasize that it is essential to dissect the 2R and 4R LNs completely. However, which population can benefit from RPND needs to be further clarified. We performed a subgroup analysis and found that patients with some characteristics, such as age ≤ 60 years, female, and so on, can benefit from RPND. At present, most surgeons consider the tumor location when deciding on the extent of MLND, and there are quite a few articles on the lobe‐ or segment‐specific prognostic analysis of lung cancer. 20 , 21 The subgroup analysis stratified by the tumor location in our study revealed that complete RPND was associated with a better prognosis in patients with upper lobe tumors but not in patients with middle or lower lobe tumors. This seems to suggest that the survival benefit from RPND is lobe‐specific. However, it should be noted that the sample size of the right middle lobe and lower lobe NSCLC in this study was small, especially in the right middle lobe, where there were only four patients in the no‐RPND group, and this could bias the statistical analysis. Therefore, we cannot conclude that RPND does not improve the prognosis of patients with right middle lobe and lower lobe lung cancer. In the middle lobe NSCLC, metastasis rates of stations 2R and 4R were 7.1% and 5.4%, respectively, and involvement of stations 2R/4R (69.6%) ranked first in stage N2 patients. In the lower lobe NSCLC, the frequency of stations 2R and 4R metastases were 4.9% and 6.2%, respectively, and involvement of stations 2R/4R (48.6%) ranked second in stage N2 patients. What is more, a retrospective study performed by Sakao et al. 22 showed that superior mediastinal LN (stations 2R‐4R) metastases were a significant adverse prognostic factor in patients with middle lobe lung cancer, indicating the potential importance of RPND in middle lobe tumors. Together, RPND is essential for right upper lobe NSCLC, and the importance of RPND cannot be underestimated for patients with middle and lower lobe lung cancer. Considering that RPND plays an important role in the accurate evaluation of tumor staging, we recommend complete RPND as a standard procedure for patients with right‐sided NSCLC.

This study had several limitations. First, our research was a single‐center retrospective study. Second, the number of patients with no RPND was small, which may raise the possibility of selection bias. Third, we excluded patients with the number of resected mediastinal nodal stations <3, which may directly affect the analysis results of mediastinal LN metastasis rate. Consequently, a multicenter randomized clinical trial with a larger sample size is needed to demonstrate a more accurate clinical significance of RPND.

In conclusion, in our study we found that involvement of stations 2R/4R was commonly seen in patients with right‐sided NSCLC, and they were usually in a state of co‐metastasis. Complete RPND was associated with a better prognosis, especially in patients with upper lobe tumors. We recommend complete RPND as a standard procedure for patients with right‐sided NSCLC.

Is there an association between digital oximetry desaturation and changes in somatosensory-evoked potentials during anterior lumbar interbody fusion?
Authors: Juliano Nery Navarro, Francisco de Assis Ulisses Sampaio Junior, Aecio Rubens Dias Pereira Filho, José Oswaldo de Oliveira Junior, Ricardo José Rodriguez Ferreira, Vinicius Santos Baptista, Marcos Vinicius Calfat Maldaun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: Anterior lumbar interbody fusion (ALIF) is widely employed in the treatment of degenerative lumbar disorders. However, ALIF may require manipulation or retraction of major vascular structures, thereby increasing the risk of transient ischemia. Intraoperative neurophysiological monitoring with somatosensory-evoked potentials (SSEPs), combined with digital oximetry (DO), may more effectively detect the onset of lower-extremity hypoperfusion or major vascular compromise during ALIF.Methods: Thirty consecutive patients underwent ALIF for lumbar spondylodiscoarthropathy. Intraoperative SSEP recording and digital pulse oximetry were performed; episodes of desaturation (SpO2 < 90%) and SSEP changes (amplitude reduction or latency increase) were documented. Associations between desaturation and SSEP alterations were analyzed using Fisher’s exact test.Results: Peripheral desaturation and SSEP alteration each occurred in one patient (3.3%), with no significant association (P > 0.999). No vascular injuries were identified. Postoperatively, all patients experienced significant lumbar pain relief (median verbal numerical scale reduction from 8 to 2; P < 0.001), with sensory improvement in 69% and motor recovery in one of two cases with paresis.Conclusion: A causal link between DO desaturation and SSEP changes during ALIF appears unusual. Nevertheless, concurrent monitoring enhances intraoperative safety and is advisable in procedures involving vascular manipulation. The results support the safety and clinical efficacy of the anterior approach when performed by experienced teams in selected patients.

INTRODUCTION

Anterior lumbar interbody fusion (ALIF) is utilized to treat degenerative lumbar disease, instability, failed posterior fusions, discogenic pain, and sagittal imbalance[3,9,10-12] [Figures 1 and 2]. However, ALIF carries a risk of vascular injury, typically attributed to retraction of the iliac vessels, particularly at the L4–L5 and L5–S1 levels. Such retraction may lead to ischemia and vascular or neural deficits, reported in up to 3% of cases.[2,4-6,14] Both digital oximetry (DO) and somatosensory-evoked potentials (SSEPs) may detect lower-extremity hypoperfusion when vascular injury occurs during ALIF. Here, we evaluated how often SSEP amplitude reductions (L3–S1) and DO desaturation occurred during 30 ALIF procedures, as indicators of vascular compromise and early warning signs of lower-extremity hypoperfusion and postoperative neurovascular complications.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This prospective observational case series, conducted at a tertiary spine center, approved by an Institutional Review Board – equivalent body, investigated whether intraoperative SSEP changes correlated with peripheral desaturation measured by DO during ALIF [Supplementary Material]. Thirty adults with lumbar spondylodiscoarthropathy and refractory low back pain (verbal numerical scale (VNS) ≥ 4) were consecutively enrolled; patients with known aortoiliac disease or technical limitations to monitoring were excluded. All ALIF procedures were performed through a left retroperitoneal approach, during which major vessels were dissected and protected [Figure 3]. Following discectomy and endplate preparation, fluoroscopy-guided interbody cages filled with autologous or synthetic graft were implanted.

Neurophysiological monitoring included continuous recordings from L3–S1 roots, with additional electroencephalogram channels (Fz–Cz montage). Significant changes were defined as amplitude reduction or latency increase not attributable to artifacts [Figure 4]. Bilateral oximetry probes were placed on the great toes and remained during the anterior phase of the procedure; desaturation was defined as peripheral capillary oxygen saturation (SpO2) <90% [Figure 5]. Synchronized recordings were also used to determine whether SSEP changes and desaturation episodes occurred simultaneously. During hospitalization, patients underwent routine clinical and neurological assessments, with targeted imaging in the presence of respiratory symptoms or desaturation.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive and inferential analyses were performed using Fisher’s exact test and other nonparametric tests,[1,13] with α = 0.05. Temporal correlations between desaturation and electrophysiological changes were independently reviewed by two evaluators, with discrepancies resolved by consensus.

RESULTS

Thirty patients (21 women, nine men; mean age 52.2 years) underwent ALIF. Nine (30%) had prior posterior fusion. ALIF was stand-alone in 8 cases (26.7%) and combined with posterior fixation in 22 (73.3%), mainly for high-grade spondylolisthesis; the L4–L5 level was involved in 15 patients (50%) [Table 1].

One patient (3.3%) presented transient left hallux desaturation (SpO2 89%) during L5–S1 exposure, which resolved after retractor release and warm saline; Doppler ultrasound demonstrated partial left iliac artery obstruction. Another patient (3.3%) exhibited transient SSEP amplitude reduction from L4–S1 roots with normal SpO2. No simultaneous events were observed, and there was no significant association between desaturation and SSEP changes (P > 0.999).

Postoperatively, 3 patients (10%) developed constipation, 6 (20%) had transient complex regional pain syndrome (CRPE), 1 (3.3%) presented retrograde ejaculation, and 2 (6.7%) experienced pulmonary embolism (PE) [Table 2]. Among patients with preoperative neurological deficits, one of two with motor weakness improved, and nine of 13 with sensory disturbances recovered partially or completely, without statistical significance [Table 3]. Pain improved significantly from a mean VNS of 7.8 ± 1.2 to 1.6 ± 1.4 (P < 0.001, Wilcoxon test), as shown in Figures 6 and 7. No infections or deaths were recorded.

DISCUSSION

This prospective study assessed the relationship between peripheral desaturation and intraoperative SSEP changes during ALIF. Although one case of each event was observed, no temporal or statistical correlation emerged, supporting the safety of ALIF performed under continuous neurophysiological and hemodynamic monitoring.[4] SSEP monitoring proved useful for the early detection of neural compromise, with a single reversible alteration promptly corrected.[15,16] Similarly, DO detected a transient desaturation subsequently attributed to iliac artery subocclusion, reinforcing its value as a simple adjunct for intraoperative perfusion surveillance.[7,15]

No major vascular or permanent neurological injuries were identified. Constipation and transient retrograde ejaculation occurred within expected ranges and resolved without sequelae.[8,9] In contrast, CRPE affected 20% of patients – higher than the ~3% reported in the literature – possibly related to intervascular dissection and sympathetic plexus manipulation inherent to the anterior approach [Figure 3]. Two PEs (6.7%) were diagnosed despite standard prophylaxis (elastic stockings, pneumatic boots, and subcutaneous enoxaparin 0.5 mg/kg/day initiated 12 h postoperatively), a rate exceeding that described by Manunga et al.[7], likely reflecting more rigorous postoperative screening. Overall, patients experienced significant pain reduction and functional improvement, confirming ALIF as a safe and effective technique when combined with real-time intraoperative monitoring, while the relatively higher CRPE and PE rates underscore the need for continued vigilance and optimization of perioperative care.

CONCLUSION

Peripheral desaturation and SSEP alterations during ALIF were unusual and occurred independently. Continuous SSEP and oximetry monitoring enhanced intraoperative safety and support the clinical efficacy of the anterior approach when performed by experienced teams in selected patients, while underscoring the need for larger confirmatory studies.

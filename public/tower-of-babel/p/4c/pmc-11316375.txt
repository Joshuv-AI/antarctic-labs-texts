Complement C3a/C3aR inhibition alleviates the formation of aortic aneurysm in Marfan syndrome mice
Authors: Fan Zhang, Kexin Yao, Yan Liu, Mei Zhou, Yanhong Zhang, Shiyao Hong, Jian Wu, Congcong Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Mutations in fibrillin 1 (FBN1) is the main cause of Marfan syndrome (MFS) with thoracic aortic aneurysm (TAA) as the main complication. Activation of the complement system plays a key role in the formation of thoracic and abdominal aortic aneurysms. However, the role of the complement system in MFS-associated aortic aneurysms remains unclear. In this study, we observed increased levels of complement C3a and C5a in the plasma of MFS patients and mouse, and the increased deposition of the activated complement system product C3b/iC3b was also observed in the elastic fiber rupture zone of 3-month-old MFS mice. The expression of C3a receptor (C3aR) was increased in MFS aortas, and recombinant C3a promoted the expression of cytokines in macrophages. The administration of a C3aR antagonist (C3aRA) attenuated the development of thoracic aortic aneurysms in MFS mice. The increased inflammation response and matrix metalloproteinases activities were also attenuated by C3aRA treatment in MFS mice. Therefore, these findings indicate that the complement C3a/C3aR inhibition alleviates the formation of aortic aneurysm in Marfan syndrome mice.

Introduction

Marfan syndrome (MFS) is an autosomal dominant genetic disease, mainly caused by mutations in the fibrillin-1 (FBN1) gene. MFS affects the cardiovascular system, eye, skeleton, skeletal muscles, lungs, and skin. At the later stage, the most life-threatening complications include thoracic aortic aneurysm (TAA) and dissection [1, 2]. To date, there are no effective drugs for the clinical management of TAA. Once an aneurysm is detected, it is usually monitored until surgical aortic replacement is necessary. To develop effective molecular targeted therapies for MFS-associated aortic aneurysm, detailed studies on the molecular mechanisms underlying aneurysm development are needed.

The Fbn1C1041G/+ transgenic mouse model harbors the most common mutation in FBN1 observed in MFS patients. This causes a heterozygous cysteine substitution in the epidermal growth factor-like domain in FBN1. These mutations result in normal mRNA expression but decreased FBN1 deposition in the extracellular matrix (ECM) [3]. The major function of fibrillin-1 is as a structural component of elastic fibers. Another function of FBN1 is to anchor transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) and maintain it in an inactive state. Increased activity of the TGF-β signaling pathway is implicated in aortic dilation and aneurysm formation at an early stage [4, 5]. However, other pathological markers such as enhanced proteolytic activity, increased levels of inflammatory cytokines and chemokines, infiltration of inflammatory cells in the aortic wall, vascular smooth muscle cell apoptosis, and medial degeneration are also associated with the Marfan aorta [6]. Aortic inflammation contributes to the development of aortic aneurysms, and anti-inflammatory drugs and antioxidants slow the progression of aortic aneurysms in Marfan mice [5–7]. Initiation of AngII signalings may be responsible for ECM degenenration and inflammation in MFS aortic aneurysm [8]. Losartan, an angiotensin 1 receptor (AT1R) blocker, could reduce free TGF-β levels, it also increases nitric oxide production independent to AT1R pathway. Several clinical researches have confirmed the therapeutic effect of Losartan in patients with MFS [9, 10]. However, the mechanism underlying the inflammatory response in the aorta at an early stage remains unclear.

The complement system mainly plays a critical role in recognizing microorganisms and triggering phagocytosis and immunity, especially in the initiation and regulation of immune cell responses [11]. The complement system plays a key role in the inflammatory response in various cardiovascular diseases and is associated with thoracic and abdominal aortic aneurysm development [12, 13]. Activation of the complement system promotes aggregation of inflammatory cells at the site of vascular lesions [14], and promotes aortic aneurysm formation in an elastase perfusion-induced mouse aortic aneurysm model [15]. Genetic deficiency of the complement C3a receptor (C3aR) or early application of SB290157, a C3aR antagonist, reduces the incidence of aortic aneurysm/dissection in a BAPN-induced TAA model [16]. However, the role of the complement system in the formation of MFS-associated aortic aneurysm remains unclear.

In this study, we used FBN1C1041G/+ transgenic mice as the animal model of MFS. The activation of the complement system in the serum and aortic tissue was detected. Treatment with the C3aR antagonist, SB290157, was used to explore the role of complement activation in the formation of MFS-associated aortic aneurysm.

Materials and methods

Human blood samples

EDTA anticoagulated blood samples were collected from 17 definite diagnosed patients with Marfan syndrome with thoracic aortic aneurysm by clinical manifestation and genetic testing, as well as from 14 age-matched healthy control individuals undergoing regular physical examinations at Beijing Anzhen Hospital. The demographic information of human samples was listed in Table 1. Informed consent for use of these samples was obtained. Use of human samples was approved by the Medical Ethical Committee of Capital Medical University (2022031X) and was performed in accordance with the ethical standards laid down in the Declaration of Helsinki.

Experimental animals

FBN1C1041G/+ transgenic mice (MFS mice) were purchased from Cyagen Biosciences Inc [17]. The animals were reared and bred at the Experimental Animal Center of Beijing Anzhen Hospital, Capital Medical University. Animals were housed at a constant temperature of 22 ± 2 ℃, with 12 h of light per day and free access to food and water. Given that male Fbn1C1041G/+ mice exhibit more severe aortic pathology compared to females [18], only male mice were used in this study. Three-month-old male Marfan mice and their wild-type (WT) littermates were used to observe the pathological changes in the aorta. For the C3aR antagonist treatment, SB290157 (MCE) was administered to male MFS mice (1 mg/kg body weight) by intraperitoneal injection every 2 days for 1 month starting at 3 months of age [16–19]. All experimental procedures followed the relevant laws and regulations of the National Laboratory Animal Management and were approved by the Ethics Committee of the Beijing Anzhen Hospital, Capital Medical University.

Measurement of plasma complement component levels

Plasma levels of complement C3a and C5a in MFS patients and healthy controls were determined by Cytometric beads array human Anaphylatoxin kit (#561418, BD). Plasma levels of complement C3a and C5a in MFS and WT mice were determined using ELISA kits (Enzyme Immunoassay Biotechnology). All the kits were operated according to the instruction manuals.

Aortic echocardiography

After hair removal, the mice were anesthetized with 1% isoflurane. Images of the aortic arch and abdominal aorta were acquired through echography in M-mode using a high-resolution high frequency ultrasound system (Vevo 2100; VisualSonics) equipped with an MS400 transducer. The probe was placed parallel to the long axis of the mouse body; aortic arch detection mode was selected; and the probe was gently moved until a clear image of the right ventricle was obtained. The probe was slowly moved towards the head of the mouse to visualize the aortic arch. Then the probe was placed perpendicular to the long axis of the mouse body in the abdomen, moved below the renal artery bifurcation, and turned 90° to be parallel to the long axis to obtain the abdominal aorta image. The diameters of the aortic arch and abdominal aorta were measured. Three measurements at each position were obtained for each animal.

Morphometry analysis of aorta

For preparing frozen sections, mouse ascending aorta samples were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, then the proximal end of the ascending aorta was embedded upward in optimum cutting temperature compound, frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at -80 °C until sectioning. The tissues were sectioned with a 5 μm thickness. When the aortic ring is complete, the sections were collected 1 layer for every 10 cuts, and total of 4 layers were harvest on each slice. The modified Verhoeff van Gieson (VG) elastic staining was performed according to the instructions of the staining kits (Solarbio). Aortic sections were examined by independent observers who were blinded to the animal group allocation. The wall thickness of the aorta was assessed in HE-stained sections. Elastic fiber breaks, defined as interruptions in the elastic fibers, were assessed in VG-stained sections, and the number were counted in the entire medial layer of 3 consecutive cross-sections per mouse, using 4 to 6 mice per group, and the mean number of breaks was calculated.

Immunofluorescence (IF) and immunohistochemical (IHC) staining

For IF staining, frozen sections were fixed with precooled glutaraldehyde for 10 min, and blocked with serum for 30 min, and incubated with anti-C3b/iC3b mouse monoclonal antibody (1:100 dilution, Hycult Biotech) at 37 ℃ for 1 h. After washing with PBS, the sections were incubated with Alexa Fluor 555-conjugated goat anti-mouse antibody (1:1000 dilution, Thermo Fisher Scientific) for 1 h at room temperature (25 °C). After washing with PBS, the sections were mounted with DAPI. For IHC staining, the sections were incubated with 3% H2O2 for 10 min at room temperature and then blocked with serum for 30 min. After blocking, the sections were incubated with primary antibodies [anti-C3aR antibody (1:400 dilution; Santa Cruz Biotechnology), anti-Mac2 antibody (1:400 dilution; Santa Cruz Biotechnology), or anti-IL-1β antibody (1:200 dilution; Santa Cruz Biotechnology)] at 4 °C overnight. The sections were then incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 30 min at room temperature and then treated with 3,3ʹ-diaminobenzidine (DAB) for coloration. All images were captured by DS-Ri2 microscope (Nikon) and analyzed using NIS-Elements BR 5.2 software.

Quantitative realtime-PCR (qRT-PCR)

Mouse ascending aorta, aortic arch and part of the descending aorta are collected for mRNA isolation and qPCR. mRNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen) as described previously. The mRNA (2 mg per sample) was reverse transcribed using GoScript Reverse Transcription System (Promega), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. A CFX Connect® system (Bio-Rad) with SYBR Green II (Takara) was used for qRT-PCR analysis. Amplification was performed at 95 °C for 2 min, followed by 40 cycles at 95 °C for 10s and 60 °C for 20s. Gapdh was used as the reference gene. Data were presented as values calculated by 2− ΔΔCt method. The primers used were as follows: C3ar1 forward: 5’-TCGATGCTGACACCAATTCAA-3’; C3ar1 reverse: 5’-TCCCAATAGA CAAGTGAGACCAA-3’; Il1b forward: 5’-GCAACTGTTCCTGAACTCAACT-3’; Il1b reverse: 5’- ATCTTTTGGGGTCCGTCAACT-3’; Ccl2 forward: 5’- GCACCAGCACCAGCCAACTCT-3’; Ccl2 reverse: 5’-GCAGGCCCAGAAGCATGACAGG-3’; Ccl5 forward: 5’- TGCTCCAATCTTGCAGTC GT-3’; Ccl5 reverse: 5’- TCTTCTCTGGGTTGGCACAC-3’; Spp1 forward: 5’-AGCAAGAAACTCT TCCAAGCAA-3’; Spp1 reverse: 5’-GTGAGATTCGTCAGATTCATCCG-3’; Mmp2 forward: 5’-CAAGTTCCCCGGCGATGTC-3’; Mmp2 reverse: 5’-TTCTGGTCAAGGTCACCTGTC-3’; Mmp9 forward: 5’-CTGGACAGCCAGACACTAAAG-3’; Mmp9 reverse: 5’-CTCGCGGCAAGT CTTCAGAG-3’; Gapdh forward: 5’-AGGTCGGTGTGAACGGATTTG-3’; Gapdh reverse: 5’-TGTAGA CCATGTAGTTGAGGTCA-3’.

Isolation of bone marrow derived macrophages

Bone marrow cells from WT mice were flushed out from femurs and tibias using a 25G needle and filtered. Cells were centrifuged at 1,000 rpm for 10 min, resuspended in fresh growth medium (DMEM high glucose medium supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% PS), and seeded in plates. After 4 h, all the non-adherent cells were removed, and adhered cells were cultured with fresh growth medium supplemented with 50 ng/ml M-CSF (PeproTech) in a humidified 5% CO2 atmosphere at 37 °C.

Detection of cytokine concentration in cell culture supernatant

Cytometric bead array (CBA) of mouse cytokine flex set (BD bioscience) was used to detect the concentrations of cytokine IL-1β and CCL2 in the culture supernatant of PBS and recombinant C3a (rC3a) stimulated macrophages following the manufacturer’s instructions.

In situ zymography

To quantify matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) 2/9 gelatinolytic activity in aortic, in situ zymography was performed using the EnzChek Gelatinase/Collagenase Assay Kit (Invitrogen, #E12055) according to the manuals. Briefly, after washing with PBS, the frozen aortic sections were incubated with 50 µl 1x Reaction Buffer containing 100 µg/ml DQ gelatine for 4 h at 37 °C in a dark humid chamber. Then images were taken on a DS-Ri2 microscope (Nikon) at 100 x (overview of whole aortic cross-section) and 400 x magnification and the mean fluorescence intensity were quantified using the NIS-Elements BR 5.2 software.

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism 7.0 software was used for statistical analysis, and data were expressed as mean ± SEM. Data were analyzed using the test of normality by the Shapiro-Wilk test. F-test was used to assess the equality of variance assumption. Unpaired Student’s t test was used to compare two groups of data with normal distributions, and non-parametric test was used to compare groups of data with non-normal distributions. Differences between three groups were evaluated using one-way ANOVA. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Complement system is overactivated in MFS patients with thoracic aortic aneurysm

Our previous study has shown the levels of the complement C3a, C4a and C5a were significantly elevated in patients with TAD [16]. To evaluate the activation of the complement system in MFS patients, we measured circulating levels of the complement C3a and C5a in 17 MFS patients with thoracic aortic aneurysm and 14 healthy control volunteers. We found that the levels of C3a, C4a, and C5a were significantly elevated in MFS patients with thoracic aortic aneurysm (Fig. 1A and C), which suggested excessive complement activation.

Complement system is activated in MFS mice at early stage

To validate the above result, FBN1C1041G/+ mice were used as the mouse model of MFS. At 3 months of age, apparent elastin fragmentation could be observed in aortic sections of MFS mice [20], and significant aortic dilation in MFS mice occurs at approximately 4–6 months of age [17]. In this study, we also found several breaks in the elastic fibers of 3-month-old MFS aortas, while the elastic fibers of WT aortas were intact, and the elastin fragmentation were increased in 6-month-old MFS aortas (Fig. 2A). So, we evaluate the activation of the complement system in the plasma of mice. ELISA data showed that, compared to WT mice, the plasma C3a concentrations of MFS mice were increased significantly as early as 3 months of age (Fig. 2B), while the concentrations of C5a were increased at 6 months of age (Fig. 2C). Then, we also examined the activation of the complement system in the aortas of 3-month-old WT and MFS mice using C3b/iC3b IF staining. No C3b/iC3b-positive staining in the aortic wall of WT mice was observed, whereas considerable C3b/iC3b-positive areas in the aortic wall of MFS mice were observed (Fig. 2D and E). The C3b/iC3b-positive regions were mainly located at the area of elastic fiber breakdown (Fig. 2D). These findings suggested the activation of the complement system in MFS mice at early stage.

The expression of C3aR is increased in the aortas of MFS mice

The complement activation product, C3a, binds to its receptor, C3aR, to initiate downstream biological effects. So, we also examined the expression of C3aR in the Aortas. The mRNA level of C3aR was higher in MFS aortas than that in WT aortas at 3 months of age (Fig. 3A). The IHC staining of C3aR also indicated increased C3aR in MFS aortas (Fig. 3B and C). Our previous study has found that C3aR was expressed in smooth muscle cells and inflammatory cells, and rC3a promotes the expression of MMP2 in smooth muscle cells under increased mechanical stretch [16]. Then we test the role of C3a-C3aR axis in macrophages. In bone marrow derived macrophages, the mRNA level of Il1b, Ccl2, Ccl5, Spp1 were increased by rC3a stimulation (Fig. 3D). And the concentration of IL-1β and CCL2 were also increased in the supernatant of rC3a stimulated macrophages (Fig. 3E and F).

Treatment with a C3aR antagonist (C3aRA) attenuates aortic dilation and pathological changes of MFS mice

The C3a-C3aR pathway promotes the formation of BAPN-induced thoracic aortic dissection (TAD) [16]. To explore the role of the C3a-C3aR pathway in the formation of MFS-associated aortic aneurysm, a C3aR antagonist, SB290157 was administered to MFS mice through intraperitoneal injection beginning at 3 months of age (Fig. 4A). The aortas were examined after one month of treatment. It was reported that the abdominal aortic diameter growth per month faster in FBN1C1041G/+ mice from 1 month to 6 months [21]. Our data were consistent to the previous observation. Echocardiography data show that the diameters of the aortic arch and abdominal aorta in C3aRA-treated MFS mice were smaller than that in the control MFS mice (Fig. 4B-E). EVG elastic fiber staining shows that the thickness of the aortic wall and the number of elastic fiber breaks in C3aRA-treated MFS mice were lower than that in the control MFS mice (Fig. 4F and H). These indicated that treatment with C3aRA could prevent pathological changes in the aorta of MFS mice.

Inflammation response is attenuated by C3aRA treatment in the aortas of MFS mice

We further examined the inflammatory response in aortic tissues. IHC staining results revealed less Mac-2 positive macrophages were infiltrated in the aortic wall of C3aRA-treated MFS mice compared to control MFS mice (Fig. 5A). The positive area of pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-1β in the aortic wall of C3aRA-treated MFS mice was also less than that in the control MFS mice (Fig. 5B). The qRT-PCR data show that the mRNA levels of Il1b, Ccl2 and Spp1 in the aortic wall of C3aRA-treated MFS mice were also decreased (Fig. 5C and E).

MMPs activity is decreased in the aortas of C3aRA treated MFS mice

For the MMPs contribute to the formation of elastin fragmentation, we further examined the mRNA levels and activity of MMPs. The expression of both Mmp2 and Mmp9 mRNA was increased in MFS aortae, and this increased Mmp2 and Mmp9 mRNA expression was attenuated by C3aRA treatment (Fig. 6A and B). MFS mice exhibited significantly higher aortic MMP2/9 activity levels compared to WT mice as shown by in situ zymography, while the aortic MMP2/9 activity was decreased in the C3aRA treated MFS mice (Fig. 6C and D).

Discussion

In this study, we found that the complement system was overactivated in MFS patients with thoracic aortic aneurysm and MFS mice. Treatment with a C3aRA attenuates inflammation response and MMPs activities, which contribute to alleviate the aortic dilatation and elastic fiber rupture in MFS mice (Fig. 7).

FBN1 mutations are the main cause of MFS. FBN1 is a cellular structural protein, and its main role is to anchor TGF-β1, inhibit its activation, and activate the downstream SMAD signaling pathway. FBN1 gene mutations lead to increased TGF-β1-related signaling, which contributes to aortic aneurysm development [3]. However, early administration of TGF-β1 neutralizing antibody in FBN1 transgenic mice aggravates aortic dilatation [4]. Therefore, excessive activation of TGF-β1 signaling is not the only mechanism underlying MFS-associated aortic aneurysm formation. New intervention targets for delaying the progression of MFS-associated aortic aneurysms have been identified. The expression of nitric oxide synthase 2 is increased in the aortic tissue of MFS patients and mouse models of MFS. Inhibition of nitric oxide synthase 2 can significantly alleviate aortic dilatation and elastic fiber rupture in Marfan mice [5]. The mitochondrial function of VSMCs is regulated by the extracellular matrix and it drives the development of aortic aneurysms in MFS. Restoring mitochondrial metabolism with the NAD precursor nicotinamide riboside rapidly reverses aortic aneurysms in Marfan mice [22]. In our previous study, we identified a new subpopulation of fibroblasts with greater SPP1 expression in the aortas of Marfan mice using bulk RNA-seq and scRNA-seq. The cytokine SPP1 could function as an accelerator of TAA progression and could provide a new target for the treatment of TAA [17]. In this study, we identified a new intervention target, the C3a-C3aR pathway.

The complement system participates in the activation of innate and adaptive immunity and plays a key role in the occurrence and development of various immune-related diseases [23, 24]. Complement system components are involved in the formation of aortic aneurysms and aortic dissections. The complement molecules, C3, C4, factor B, properdin, and the membrane attack complex C5b-9 are expressed in human aortic aneurysm tissues [25, 26]. In this study, we found that the plasma levels of complement activation products C3a and C5a were increased in MFS patients and mice, especially C3a were increased earlier than aortic dilation in MFS mice. Moreover, C3b/iC3b was deposited at the rupture site of the elastic fibers in the aortic wall of MFS mice. A recent study also reported increased complement factors and C3 activation in both human and murine MFS aortas [27]. Our data suggested that the complement system was activated at the early stage of the aortic aneurysm formation in MFS mice.

Complement system could be activated by three pathways: the classical pathway, the lectin pathway and the alternative pathway. A recent study using the C1-esterase inhibitor (C1-INH), which could inhibit the classical and lectin activation pathway of the complement system, could decreased C3 and reduced macrophages, yet there was no reduction in aortic diameters in MFS mice [27]. It maybe due to that the complement system could also been activated through alternative pathway. This result was also consistent with the elastase-perfused abdominal aortic aneurysm mouse model, the deletion of the C4 gene, a key molecule in the classical complement pathway activation, does not affect the diameter of aortic aneurysms [15]. The deletion of factor B, one of the key molecules in the alternative complement pathway activation, inhibits elastase-perfused induced aortic dilatation by reducing the infiltration of neutrophils [24]. Increased elastic fibers breaks, cell damage and the activity of caspase have been reported in the aortic tissue of MFS mice [28]. Meanwhile, researches have shown that HSP and HMGB1 released from damaged cells or IgG recognized neo-antigen expressed on damaged aortic tissues could induce complement activation through the alternative pathway [25–30]. So, the alternative pathway may contribute to the activation of complement system during the formation of MFS associated aortic aneurysm. The role of alternative pathway needs to be confirmed in future (Table 1).

Excessive activation of the complement system is associated with various chronic disorders [31–33]. The activated products of the complement system play a key role in promoting aortic aneurysm formation. C3aR, the receptor of the complement activation product C3a, is expressed ubiquitously on a variety of cell surfaces. Deletion of C3aR reduces the expression of chemokines, reduces the recruitment of neutrophils to the injured site, and attenuates elastase function in perfusion-induced abdominal aortic aneurysm [15]. C3aR gene deletion or early application of SB290157, a C3aR inhibitor, reduces the incidence of aortic aneurysm/dissection in a BAPN-induced TAA model [16]. An analysis of the genetic data of patients with TAA from a public database, using weighted gene co-expression network analysis, shows that CX3CR1, C3, and C3AR1 are the top three hub genes in the module correlated with TAA [34]. In this study, the expression of C3aR increased in the aortas of MFS mice. The C3a-C3aR pathway promotes macrophage recruitment by promoting chemokines release [35]. In this study, we also found that rC3a increased the expression of IL-1β, CCL2,SPP1 in macrophages, the infiltration of macrophages and the expression of cytokines IL-1β, CCL2,SPP1 and MMP9 were decreased in C3aRA-treated MFS aorta. Macrophages secrete MMP9 to destroy the ECM, which promote aortic dilation during aortic aneurysm formation [36, 37]. TAD is closely associated with increased activity of MMP9 in the aortas of mice; the knockout of MMP9 protects against the formation and rupture of TAD [38]. Macrophage-derived SPP1, also known as osteopontin, promotes the formation of Ang II-induced abdominal aortic aneurysm. Genetic deficiency in SPP1 attenuates aortic aneurysm formation by decreasing MMP2 and MMP9 activity [39]. SPP1 mediates the decline of the SMC contractile phenotype via Itga8/Itgb1 [17]. Thus, the C3a-C3aR pathway could promote macrophage infiltration and further promote elastic fiber degradation and aortic dilation during aortic aneurysm formation.

The current guideline recommended treatments in MFS aortopathy, including Angiotensin II receptor blocker and beta blockers, have reduced the rate of aortic root dilation in MFS patients and no previous aortic surgery [40]. Our current results do not provide evidences to determine this new treatment is better or worse than those existing drugs in guidelines, however a better therapeutic effect may be achieved by adding a new intervention target. The difference between this new treatment and other tried drugs in mouse MFS model (resveratrol, losartan, allopurinol, nitro-oleic acid) are the different targets of drugs. Resveratrol reduces aortic aneurysm dilation rate by inhibiting aortic elastin breaks, the improvement of endothelial dysfunction and smooth muscle cell survival, reducing oxidative stress [41, 42]. losartan attenuated the aortic aneurysm dilation by inhibiting the AT1R associated signaling pathway [10]. Allopurinol, a xanthine oxidase inhibitor, also could inhibit the aneurysm formation of MFS [43]. Nitro-oleic acid, a nitrated fatty acid that reduces nitric oxide synthase 2 expression, has a therapeutic effect in MFS Fbn1C1041G/+ mice by inhibition of aortic ERK1/2, Smad2 as well as nuclear factor kappa B overactivation and consequent attenuation of elastin fragmentation [20]. Our study may provide a new therapeutic target for MFS associated aortic aneurysms.

As a limitation of the study, the complement levels in MFS patients with aortic aneurysm patients were observed, the change in complement system in MFS patients without aortic aneurysm patients could not be fully confirmed in the current study. It might need a specific clinical study design to enroll the MFS genotype-mutation confirmed patients with no aortic aneurysm phenotype. Another limitation of the study, the effect of C3aRA on male mice after treatment was observed, and the treatment period was only one-month, long-term effect of C3aRA on the MFS associated aortic aneurysms and side effects need to be observed in future study.

In conclusion, this study shows that the complement system is overactivated in the early stages of MFS and inhibition of the C3a-C3aR pathway can delay the formation of aortic aneurysms.

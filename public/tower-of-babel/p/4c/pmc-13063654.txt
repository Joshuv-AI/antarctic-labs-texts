Evaluation of immunoglobulin A1/immunoglobulin A2 ratio and B cell lymphoma-6 in relation to apoptosis-associated speck-like protein in diabetic kidney disease patients
Authors: Sara Youssry, Hossam Ghoneim, Yasser Ammar, Azza Magdy, Dalia Maharem
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundInflammation and alteration of the immune system play a key role in the pathogenesis of diabetes mellitus (DM). However, little is known about the underlying mechanisms involved in the inflammatory pathways of diabetic kidney disease (DKD). We aimed in this study to evaluate immunoglobulin A (IgA) subclasses/ratio and B cell lymphoma-6 (BCL-6) in relation to inflammasome adaptor protein ASC (apoptosis-associated speck-like protein containing a caspase activation and recruiting domain) in diabetic patients with and without kidney disease.The present study was conducted on 50 diabetic patients who were classified into 25 patients without kidney disease and 25 DKD patients, together with 25 age-matched healthy individuals. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) was used to assess ASC and BCL-6 levels in culture supernatants of peripheral blood mononuclear cells as well as to determine IgA1 and IgA2 levels in individuals’ serum.ResultsThe results revealed a significant increase in ASC levels together with a significant decrease in IgA1 and BCL-6 levels in DKD patients compared to other groups. A significant association was observed between the IgA1/ IgA2 ratio and improved disease markers in all patients. In addition, ASC levels were negatively correlated with both IgA2 and BCL-6 levels. ConclusionsIt can be concluded that altered IgA subtypes and its ratio (IgA1/IgA2) as well as BCL-6 may be involved in the pathogenesis of DKD, suggesting their future therapeutic potential in DKD patients.Keywords: DKD, IgA, ASC, BCL-6, Chronic inflammation, Inflammasome

Introduction

Diabetes mellitus (DM) is a huge, rapidly growing health problem worldwide. The International Diabetes Federation (IDF) estimated that Egypt ranks the 9th country worldwide in the prevalence of DM, with about 8,850,400 cases in early 2020. By 2045, Egypt was expected to be the 7th country worldwide with a doubling of the number of diabetic patients [1]. Diabetic kidney disease (DKD) occurs in approximately 30–40% of diabetic patients [2], being the leading cause of chronic kidney disease (CKD) and end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) worldwide [3].

Chronic inflammation was linked to insulin resistance and/or its worsening in obesity since it was shown to interfere with insulin signal transduction in critical metabolic organs and potentially contribute to the development of type 2 DM [4]. It has been proposed that intracellular sensors involved in the identification of endogenous Damage Associated Molecular Patterns (DAMPs) are essential for inflammasome stimulation [5]. Inflammasomes are multiprotein complexes that are vital components of innate immunity. They are activated in response to stressors or cellular infections that trigger a series of inflammatory reactions [6]. The development of numerous metabolic disorders is intimately linked to the activation and signal transduction of the inflammasomes. Moreover, inflammasomes can also influence the insulin signaling pathway’s conductivity directly or indirectly, which can contribute to type 2 diabetes [7].

Assembly of a cytosolic sensor protein with its adaptor protein, ASC (apoptosis-associated speck-like protein containing a caspase activation and recruiting domain), is necessary for the creation of an inflammasome. Assessment of ASC speck production is one of the most widely used methods for studying inflammasomes, and ASCs are thought to be the hallmark of inflammasome activation [8]. Conduction of the insulin signaling pathway, the development of insulin resistance, and type 2 diabetes can all be impacted by the inflammasome, either directly or indirectly [7]. In addition, it has been demonstrated that mice fed a high-fat diet exhibit improved glucose tolerance and increased insulin sensitivity when NLRP3 (NLR family pyrin domain containing 3) and ASC are genetically deleted [9]. The formation of caspase-1 activating inflammasome complexes is one way that ASC supports innate immunity. Furthermore, it has been shown that ASC functions in adaptive immune cells in a cell-intrinsic manner that is independent of inflammasomes [10].

Increased immunoglobulin levels, which have been closely connected to the onset and progression of type 2 DM, provide evidence that immune system changes may be a contributing factor to the metabolic abnormalities linked to the disease. Circulating immunoglobulin levels have been shown by Asare-Anane H et al. [11] to be potential biomarkers for the diagnosis and treatment of diabetes mellitus.

An increase in circulating IgA concentrations has been observed to be a generalized phenomenon among diabetic patients, being associated with diabetic complications. Human IgA is present in two subclasses (IgA1 and IgA2) that have different structural characteristics and distinct localization and functionality [12]. Despite the fact that IgA subclass-specific immune responses probably have a physiological purpose by facilitating tissue-specific immunity, they can potentially have negative consequences by undesirably promoting inflammation [13]. It was found that IgA-immune complexes can induce the activation of NLRP3 inflammasomes in macrophages, dendritic cells, and renal mesangial cells, causing the release of IL-1β and IL-18 [14]. Alterations in the IgA1/IgA2 ratio often go hand in hand with specific disease states [15, 16]. However, little is known about the IgA1/IgA2 ratio in T2DM and its relation with the inflammasome adaptor ASC.

A master modulator of humoral immunity, B-cell lymphoma-6 (BCL-6) is essential for the establishment and upkeep of germinal centers (GCs). Depending on the cell type or stimulus, it may have pro- or anti-inflammatory effects [17, 18]. It has been demonstrated that BCL6 is centrally expressed and regulates key metabolic pathways in peripheral tissues controlling insulin sensitivity and fat metabolism [19]. It was also discovered that BCL-6 inhibits the expression of inflammasomes in renal inflammation [18]. It was proposed that the retinal vascular inflammation and angiogenesis are caused by an excess of BCL-6 driven follicular helper T (Tfh) cells, which may be an overlooked aspect of diabetic retinopathy [20]. However, the function of BCL-6 in DKD remains unclear. Thus, we aimed to evaluate the IgA subtypes/ratio and B cell lymphoma-6 (BCL-6) in relation to apoptosis-associated speck-like protein (ASC) in type 2 diabetic patients with or without DKD.

Subjects and methods

Ethics

The study protocol as well as the blood samples’ collection and use were previously approved by the Medical Ethical Committee of Alexandria University and bioethical research committee approval was taken (IORG0008812). The study complies with the Declaration of Helsinki’s guidelines for the use of human participants. Signed informed consents were obtained from all participants.

Subjects

The current study was conducted on 50 diabetic patients (group 1) with a history of DM diagnosis since at least 1 year, who were recruited from the Medical Research Institute and Alexandria Main University Hospital, Alexandria University, Egypt during the period from October 2023-December 2024. Twenty-five age- and sex-matched healthy controls (group 2) were also included in this study. Control group were matched to group 1 and were free from any chronic diseases not only DM but also hypertension, liver or kidney diseases. Patients were further classified into 25 diabetic patients without DKD (group 1 A) and 25 DKD patients (group 1B). The diagnosis of DKD was based on the presence of albuminuria and/or reduced estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) in the absence of features suggesting other primary causes of kidney damage. Diagnosis is supported by a longer duration of DM and the existence of diabetic retinopathy [21].

Patients with viral hepatitis (hepatitis B/C), bilharziasis, lupus nephritis, or other autoimmune manifestations, those with advanced CKD (stage 4 and 5) and patients receiving corticosteroid drugs at least two weeks before sampling were excluded from the study.

In accordance with the Medical Research Institute’s (Alexandria University) Ethical Guidelines, all participants were requested to voluntarily volunteer for the study, and their written informed consents were obtained prior to their participation in the protocol. All patients were asked about their full medical history; including age, sex, history of smoking, onset and duration of DM as well as type of treatment and DM complications including neuropathy, retinopathy and macrovascular diseases, concomitant conditions like hypertension (HTN). Additionally, a complete clinical examination was conducted, which included body mass index (BMI), blood pressure, and pulse measurements.

Peripheral blood samples were collected from all subjects after obtaining written informed consents.

Methods

Laboratory investigations

Including estimation of HbA1c, blood urea, serum creatinine, urinary albumin creatinine ratio (UACR) and lipid profile [22], with calculation of eGFR by CKD-EPI creatinine equation [23].

Isolation of peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs)

Freshly drawn heparinized venous blood samples were subjected to gradient centrifugation using Ficoll-Hypaque (1077) (Sigma-Aldrich) in order to isolate PBMCs [24]. After carefully layering the diluted samples over half of their volume of Ficoll-Hypaque (1077), they were centrifuged for 30 min at room temperature at 1800 rpm. The buffy coat layer (PBMCs) was then aspirated carefully, washed twice, pelleted, and re-suspended in 1 ml medium (RPMI 1640) to evaluate the cell count and viability using a hemocytometer and trypan blue.

Evaluation of cell viability

Trypan blue (0.2%) solution was utilised to evaluate the viability of PBMCs, and trypan blue exclusion (clear cytoplasm) was widely employed to ascertain the viability of isolated cells [25]. The following formula was used to determine the proportion of viable cells:

Percentage (%) of viable cells = (No. of viable cells ∕ Total no. counted) × 100.

Culture of PBMCs

The isolated PBMCs were resized to 2 × 106 cells/ml, cultivated in a sterile 96-well plate, and stimulated with 2 µl/well with lipopolysaccharide (LPS) (Sigma-Aldrich), which functions as an inflammasome activator [26]. After 48 h of incubation at 37 °C and 5% CO2, the cell culture supernatants were extracted and kept at -80 °C to measure the levels of ASC and BCL-6.

Assessment of inflammasome adaptor protein ASC and BCL-6 levels

Commercial enzyme linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits were used to measure the levels of both ASC and BCL-6 (pg/ml) in cell culture supernatants in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions (Inova Biotech company) [27, 28].

Assessment of immunoglobulins (IgA1 and IgA2) serum levels

Venous peripheral blood samples were also collected in plain vacutainers from all subjects. Serum was used for ELISA IgA1 (bioassay technology laboratory; catalog no. E3321Hu) and IgA2 (bioassay technology laboratory; catalog no. E4195Hu) assessment according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS statistical software version 20.0. (Armonk, NY: IBM Corp). Continuous data were tested for normality by the Shapiro-Wilk test. Chi-square test was utilized for categorical variables. Multiple comparisons were performed using one-way ANOVA, followed by Tukey post hoc test (For normally distributed quantitative variables), while Kruskal Wallis test was used for not normally distributed quantitative variables. Correlations between two variables were determined using Spearman coefficients. P value was considered significant if it is ≤ 0.05. In addition, a generalized linear model (GLM) (Gamma, log link) was applied to evaluate differences in IgA1, ASC, and BCL-6 levels between Group 1 A and Group 1B after adjusting for the duration of diabetes (DOD) as a covariate.

A sample size of 75 cases (25 patients with kidney disease & 25 patients without kidney disease & 25 healthy control group), was required to achieve 80% statistical power to detect difference in the mean serum immunoglobulin level between the 3 groups, assuming a medium effect size (Cohen’s d = 0.35), using One Way ANOVA test, at a 0.05 significance level. Additionally, the Spearman correlation test was used to find an association between immunoglobulin and ASC and BCL6 at a significance level of 0.05, assuming a correlation coefficient of rs = 0.6. The IBM SPSS software (version 31) was used to determine the sample size (version 31) [29].

Results

Demographic and clinical data of the studied groups

No statistically significant difference was observed between the three studied groups as regards sex, age, smoking, and measured body mass index (BMI). Hypertension was present in 7 patients (28%) of group 1 A and in 17 patients (68%) of group 1B, with a statistically significant difference between both groups. Mean blood pressure was statistically significantly higher in both groups of patients than control group, and also it was statistically significantly higher in group 1B than in group 1 A. Duration of diabetes and the incidence of retinopathy were statistically significantly higher in group 1B than in group 1 A, but no statistically significant difference was shown between both diabetic groups as regards treatment, incidence of neuropathy, and macrovascular complications (Table 1).

Laboratory investigations of the studied groups

HbA1C showed statistically significant elevation in both diabetic groups relative to healthy controls but without statistically significant difference between both groups 1 A and 1B. Blood urea, serum creatinine, and UACR showed statistically significant elevation in group 1B patients as compared to both group 1 A and group 2 with no statistically significant difference between group 1 A and group 2. eGFR showed a statistically significant reduction in group 1B patients as compared to group 1 A and group 2 with an absent significant variation between group 1 A and group 2. Total cholesterol was statistically significantly higher in group 1 A compared to group 2, with no statistically significant difference between group 1B with either group 2 or group 1 A. LDL-C and TG were statistically significantly higher, and HDL-C was statistically significantly lower in both patients’ groups compared to control, with no statistically significant difference between group 1 A and group 1B regarding LDL-C, TG, and HDL-C (Table 2).

Altered serum IgA subtypes in DKD patients

As shown in Table (4), serum IgA1 was significantly decreased in DKD patients (group 1B) compared to both group 1 A and group 2, with no statistically significant difference between group 1 A and group 2. In addition, IgA1 remained significantly lower in DKD patients, demonstrating a significant group effect after adjustment of duration of diabetes as a covariate (Wald χ²=13.322*, p < 0.001*) (Table 4). On the other hand, no significant difference was observed in either IgA2 or IgA1/IgA2 ratio between different groups. In total diabetic patients, the IgA1/IgA2 ratio showed significant negative correlations with age, mean BP, blood urea, and serum creatinine, whereas it had a significant positive correlation with eGFR. Our results also showed that IgA2 was negatively correlated with ASC (Table 5).

In addition, IgA1 level was higher in patients with diabetic neuropathy and those with macrovascular disease, although not reaching a significant value, but its level in patients with diabetic retinopathy was lower compared to those without diabetic retinopathy (only 10 patients had retinopathy). Moreover, the IgA1/IgA2 ratio was lower in patients with neuropathy and retinopathy, showing a statistically significant reduction in those with macrovascular disease (Table 6) (Fig. 1).

Altered BCL-6 in DKD patients

BCL-6 showed statistically significant lower values in DKD patients (group 1B) compared to both group 1 A and group 2, with a significant variation between group 1 A and group 2 (Table 3). BCL-6 remained significantly lower in DKD patients after controlling for duration of diabetes (Wald χ²=6.669, p = 0.010*) (Table 4). Additionally, our results showed that BCL-6 was negatively correlated with UACR, while it showed a positive correlation with eGFR. As regards association with different immune parameters, BCL-6 was observed to be negatively correlated with ASC and positively correlated with IgA2 (Table 5).

Discussion

Diabetic kidney disease (DKD) remains a growing health concern, being characterized by chronic inflammation, hemodynamic changes, and metabolic dysfunction. Diabetic nephropathy is not only a chronic disease with a complex pathogenesis but also one of the main factors leading to end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) [30]. It has been suggested that immune modulation and inflammation may contribute to both the development and progression of this disease [31].

An innate immune system protein known as ASC (apoptosis-associated speck-like protein containing a caspase recruitment domain, or CARD) aids in the formation of inflammasomes, which are essential to the inflammatory process [32]. It has been established that the development of numerous kidney problems is largely dependent on inflammasome activation, and that inhibiting this process may be a viable treatment approach for these conditions. In line, our results revealed that ASC was significantly increased in DKD patients compared to other groups. Additionally, we found a significant association between increased ASC levels and disease markers. These findings may support the concept that ASC is instrumental in mediating renal tubule damage in DKD. This may be attributed to the fact that the inflammasome is a major mediator of sterile inflammation and is triggered in response to several disease-related stimuli, such as oxidative stress and glycaemic damage, as well as the production of danger-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), such ATP [33]. In addition, metabolite imbalances in diabetic nephropathy can activate the inflammasome through a dynamic mutual regulatory link between metabolism and inflammation known as the metabolic-inflammatory circuit [34]. Furthermore, it has been documented that inflammasome activation plays a key role in causing renal tubule damage in diabetic nephropathy. It is connected to a number of clinical indicators of nephropathy, such as mesangial expansion, proteinuria and albuminuria [33]. In addition, there is a dynamic mutual regulatory relationship between metabolism and inflammation, called the metabolic-inflammatory circuit, where metabolite abnormalities in diabetic nephropathy can trigger the activation of the inflammasome [34]. Moreover, it has been reported that inflammasome activation is instrumental in mediating renal tubule damage in diabetic nephropathy, being linked to several clinical markers of nephropathy, including proteinuria and albuminuria as well as mesangial expansion [33].

It has been reported that abnormal levels of immunoglobulins are very common in diabetic patients in whom serum IgA concentrations are influenced by the degree of glycemic control [35]. IgA is the second most common antibody isotype in serum, existing in 2 forms: IgA1 and IgA2. It has been established that IgA can actively aid in the onset of inflammation by enhancing inflammatory responses through the creation of immunological complexes [36].

DKD is not the only renal pathology seen in diabetic patients; they may also acquire kidney diseases from other causes known as non-diabetic renal disease (NDRD), which can occur either alone or in conjunction with DKD [37]. Among these, IgA nephropathy superimposed on DKD is well recognized and has been frequently reported [38]. Moreover, DKD may be accompanied by post-infectious glomerulonephritis (PIGN). The most common histological pattern in diabetic patients was IgA-dominant infection-related glomerulonephritis (IRGN), which was mostly linked to staphylococcus aureus infections [39, 40].

The present study revealed a significant reduction of IgA1 levels in DKD patients compared to other groups. This may be related to B-cell infiltration and immune complex deposition in diabetic kidneys, which in turn drive inflammation. In addition, it has been reported that innate-immune activation via toll-like receptor 9 (TLR9) may be significant in diabetic renal injuries through the overproduction of glycosylated IgA1, leading to enhanced immune-complex formation and glomerular deposition [41]. However, this marked reduction in IgA1 levels may also be related to the fact that IgA is the main regulator of glucose metabolism, as obese IgA-defective mice experienced worsened glucose tolerance as well as insulin sensitivity [42]. On the contrary, it has been reported that IgA levels were positively associated with the risk of T2DM in a general adult population [35]. Furthermore, diabetic complications were associated with a significant increase in serum IgA concentrations [43].

More importantly, the observed significant association between the IgA1/ IgA2 ratio and improved disease markers may be attributed to the critical function for IgA in regulating intestinal homeostasis, metabolic inflammation, and insulin resistance [42]. Furthermore, the present study showed that IgA2 serum levels were negatively correlated with ASC levels. This may be context-dependent or may be related to the involvement of IgA2 in immune complex formation. It has been revealed that IgA2 immune complexes result in higher levels of pro-inflammatory cytokine production than IgA1 complexes [13].

The B-cell lymphoma-6 (BCL-6) is a master regulator of humoral immunity, playing a critical role in the initiation and maintenance of the germinal centers (GC). Although GC B cells and T cells have been the subject of extensive research, it is unclear how BCL-6 regulates the activities of innate immunity [44]. Our results showed that DKD patients had a marked decrease in BCL-6 levels compared to other groups. In addition, BCL-6 was observed to be negatively correlated with ASC and positively correlated with eGFR in all patients. This may be related to that BCL-6 inhibits NF-κB-mediated inflammation [45]. Additionally, BCL-6 inhibits inflammation and inflammasome production via binding to the promoter area of NLRP3. Similarly, it was shown that BCL-6 overexpression was linked to lower blood pressure, while its downregulation was observed in the renal cortex of hypertensive rats [18]. Furthermore, the regulatory function of BCL-6 in generating the high-affinity, class-switched antibodies may be the reason for the observed positive connection between BCL-6 and IgA2 [46].

Collectively, these findings revealed that altered inflammasome adaptor protein ASC, IgA subtypes and its ratio (IgA1/IgA2), as well as BCL-6, may be involved in the pathogenesis of DKD, suggesting their future therapeutic potential in DKD patients. In addition, the significant association between the IgA1/IgA2 ratio and improved disease parameters may highlight the possible diagnostic role of the IgA1/IgA2 ratio in diabetic patients.

Although this study may represent an initial step to understand role of IgA subtypes and its ratio (IgA1/IgA2) in the context of DKD, some limitations still exist. One limitation in the current study is the lack of evaluating total IgA level in DKD patients. However, we suppose that this limitation does not markedly confound the main finding of our study.

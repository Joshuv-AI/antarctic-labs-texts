Single-nucleotide polymorphisms of donor CYP3A5 and recipient ABCB1 affect tacrolimus intrapatient variability in living donor liver transplantation: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Saran Ochir Gongor, Kwang-Woong Lee, Eun-Woo Choi, Jae-Yoon Kim, Jeong-Moo Lee, Suk Kyun Hong, YoungRok Choi, Nam-Joon Yi, Kyung-Suk Suh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThe relationship between tacrolimus intrapatient variability (IPV) and its metabolism-associated single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in liver transplantation (LT) remains unclear. Moreover, the influence of donor SNP on recipient IPV is poorly understood. Our objective was to investigate how SNPs affect tacrolimus IPV by analyzing both donor and recipient SNP data in LT.MethodsThis retrospective study examined the association between tacrolimus IPV and eight specific SNPs in 50 adult LT recipients and 50 LT donors genotyped for CYP3A5, ABCB1, ABCC2, and POR genes and their SNPs. Recipients were divided into high IPV (≥30) and low IPV (<30) groups based on 3–12 months after LT IPV calculations. Multivariate logistic regression was used to identify risk factors for high IPV. Post-LT long-term outcomes were also compared between the groups.ResultsTwo SNPs were significantly associated with high IPV in multivariate analysis. Recipient ABCB1 1236C>T genotypes CT and CC (odds ratio [OR], 13.969; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.738–112.300; P = 0.013), donors’ CYP3A5 6986A>G genotypes GA and GG (OR, 4.390; 95% CI, 1.005–19.178; P = 0.049), and glucose ≥120 mg/dL at 3 months (OR, 6.494; 95% CI, 1.300–32.168; P = 0.024) were associated with high IPV. However, post-LT long-term outcomes were similar between the groups.ConclusionSNPs of donor CYP3A5 and recipient ABCB1 can significantly influence tacrolimus IPV in patients undergoing living donor LT. Identifying these SNPs can help determine high IPV-prone patients, facilitate close monitoring of high IPV-susceptible groups, and improve outcomes.Keywords: Cytochrome P-450 CYP3A, Drug monitoring, Liver transplantation, Single-nucleotide polymorphism, Tacrolimus

INTRODUCTION

Tacrolimus exhibits a narrow therapeutic range and is frequently monitored through trough concentrations in transplantation [1]. Nevertheless, mounting evidence highlights the importance of tacrolimus intrapatient variability (IPV), which measures the fluctuations in tacrolimus concentrations over time and advocates for its widespread application in organ transplantation [2]. While the definition of high IPV may vary, extant literature consistently demonstrates that high IPV correlates with adverse outcomes and functions as a prognostic indicator of graft dysfunction, including decreased renal function, rejection, donor-specific antibodies, histological progression, mortality, and tumor recurrence in liver and kidney transplants [2,3]. However, the majority of these studies focus on IPV in renal transplants or interindividual tacrolimus concentrations, with limited research conducted on IPV in liver transplantation (LT).

Furthermore, to effectively manage and maintain low IPV of tacrolimus in transplant recipients, multiple factors must be considered due to their influence on IPV, including medication adherence, drug–drug interactions, formulation variations, genetic polymorphisms, and differential absorption rates [4]. Literature emphasized the paramount role of single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), which are sole nucleotide substitutions in genes altering gene expression and eventually affecting its enzyme activity of drug metabolism [4,5]. Several studies aimed to elucidate the effect of SNPs on the IPV of tacrolimus. However, findings are inconsistent in the majority of studies conducted in renal transplant settings, necessitating further investigation in LT [4,6,7,8,9,10,11]. Moreover, while there are studies of SNPs in LT settings, most of them focus on drug clearance or pharmacokinetics rather than IPV [12,13,14]. In the extant literature, several genes and their SNPs have been identified as influencing tacrolimus pharmacokinetics, including CYP3A5, POR, ABCC2, and ABCB1 genes [4,5,15,16]. However, these findings remain inconclusive and have not yet been investigated for their potential impact on the IPV of tacrolimus in LT. Moreover, in living donor liver transplantation (LDLT), donor SNPs are known to influence the tacrolimus pharmacokinetics of the recipient; however, this phenomenon remains insufficiently investigated, particularly in the context of LDLT [3,17].

This study aimed to explore the relationship between tacrolimus IPV and 8 metabolism-related SNPs in both recipients and donors in LT, addressing the knowledge gap. The main objective was to identify whether these SNPs or other clinical factors were risk factors for high IPV in LDLT. The secondary objective evaluated IPV’s impact on outcomes in subsequent years post-LT, focusing on tacrolimus-related complications like new-onset diabetes mellitus (DM) after transplantation (NODAT), chronic kidney disease (CKD), infections, and de novo hepatic steatosis.

METHODS

Study patients and single-nucleotide polymorphism genotyping

A retrospective analysis was conducted of 50 recipients and 50 donors who underwent adult LDLT between 2002 and 2018. The analysis of 8 SNPs, CYP3A5 6986A>G, ABCB1 1236C>T, ABCB1 2677G>T/A, ABCB1 3435C>T, ABCC2 -24C>T, ABCC2 1249G>A, ABCC2 3972C>T, and POR 1508C>T were conducted in a previous study as described [18]. Buffy coat samples from recipients were used for SNP analysis. For donors, liver tissue paraffin blocks were employed because of donor unavailability for outpatient clinic visits.

Tacrolimus trough concentrations and intrapatient variability

A total of 534 tacrolimus trough concentration samples were reviewed. Tacrolimus trough concentrations (ng/mL) were routinely measured before the morning dose during the outpatient visits. Tacrolimus trough concentrations (ng/mL) were determined via liquid chromatography coupled with tandem mass spectrometry, utilizing a Waters 2795 Alliance HT system (Waters Ltd.) and a Quattro Micro API mass spectrometer (Micromass) [14]. Any erroneously elevated concentrations exceeding 20 ng/mL, presumably due to presampling doses, were excluded from the analysis.

IPV was calculated using tacrolimus trough concentrations from the third to the 12th month, as month 3 is a critical period for several complications and IPV should be assessed early during the stable post-LT period [17]. Dose-unadjusted IPV was used in this study, as previously described [2]. IPV was calculated using the coefficient of variation (CV), which was defined as follows: CV (%) = (standard deviation/mean trough concentration) × 100 [19]. Based on previous studies and receiver operating curve analysis of our data, a CV of 30% was established as the threshold [2,19]. The study cohort was then categorized into 2 groups based on tacrolimus IPV: a high IPV group (≥30) and a low IPV group (<30).

Data collection

The baseline characteristics of recipients and donors, along with recipients’ laboratory results and preoperative and postoperative variables at 3 months, were collected to identify the risk factors for high IPV within the first year post-LT. Retrospective data on the effects of IPV on post-LT outcomes, specifically tacrolimus dose-dependent complications, such as NODAT, stage 3 CKD, infectious complications, de novo hepatic steatosis, hypercholesterolemia, and osteoporosis within 3 years of LT. NODAT was defined according to the American Diabetes Association criteria or the use of oral glucose-lowering agents and insulin interventions. Chronic kidney disease stage 3 was diagnosed by an estimated glomerular filtration rate of less than 60 mL/min/1.73 m2, calculated using the CKD-EPI (Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration) creatinine equation. Post-LT infection complications were defined by antibiotic administration, prolonged inflammatory blood markers, or pathogenic growth in blood cultures. De novo hepatic steatosis was identified using routine dynamic CT or MRI.

Immunosuppression regimen

Induction therapy included basiliximab, whereas maintenance triple therapy included tacrolimus, mycophenolate mofetil, and corticosteroids. Recipients received either the original tacrolimus (Prograf, Astellas Pharma), a generic formulation (Tacrobell, Chong Kun Dang Pharma), or both. The target tacrolimus trough level was 8–12 ng/mL in the first month after LT, 6–8 ng/mL from 2 to 6 months after LT, and 5 ng/mL at 6 months after LT. Corticosteroids were tapered off within 3–6 months after LT.

Statistical analysis

Univariate and multivariate regression models were developed to identify high-risk factors for IPV. The multivariate logistic regression model incorporated SNPs with a univariate value below 0.1, and variables as determined by earlier research [2,3]. The variables included recipient age, recipient female sex, recipient body mass index (kg/m2), preoperative model for end-stage liver disease (MELD) score, hematocrit, hemoglobin, ALT, γ-GT, albumin, cholesterol, glucose, creatinine, and BUN levels, were measured 3 months after LT. Furthermore, SNPs such as the CYP3A5 6986A>G and ABCB1 1236C>T genotypes were included. For donors, variables such as age, female sex, CYP3A5 6986A>G, and ABCB1 1236C>T were evaluated. The forward stepwise likelihood ratio method was used in the multivariate model. Data are expressed as frequency, mean, and standard deviation according to the variable type. Continuous variables were compared using the Student t-test for parametric data and the Mann-Whitney test for nonparametric data. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher exact test. All tests were two-tailed, with P-values less than 0.05 considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were conducted using the IBM SPSS Statistics ver. 29.0 (IBM Corp.).

Ethics statements

This study was conducted in accordance with both the Declarations of Helsinki and Istanbul and was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Seoul National University Hospital (No. IRB-H-2408-037-1558). Written informed consent was waived due to the retrospective cohort design.

RESULTS

Baseline characteristics

The study participants were categorized into a low IPV group (recipient, 32; donor, 32) and a high IPV group (recipient, 18; donor, 18). Baseline characteristics of study groups are presented in Table 1. The mean recipient age was slightly higher in the low IPV group compared to the high IPV group (55.00 ± 8.33 years vs. 50.61 ± 8.07 years, P = 0.08). No significant differences were observed in sex distribution (18.8% female in the low IPV group vs. 27.8% in the high IPV group, P = 0.46), body mass index (24.27 ± 3.40 kg/m2 vs. 22.90 ± 2.98 kg/m2, P = 0.16), or MELD scores (15.10 ± 7.90 vs. 16.81 ± 8.50, P = 0.48). The prevalence of DM was marginally higher in the high IPV group than in the low IPV group (33.3% vs. 15.6%, P = 0.15). Donor characteristics, including age (29.28 ± 10.49 years vs. 29.19 ± 11.70 years, P = 0.98), sex distribution (27.8% female in high IPV vs. 28.1% in low IPV, P = 0.98), and body mass index (27.46 ± 7.20 kg/m2 vs. 25.49 ± 9.56 kg/m2, P = 0.46), were similar between both groups.

Tacrolimus trough concentration and intrapatient variability

The mean tacrolimus trough concentration at 3 months was significantly higher in the high IPV group (8.91 ± 3.93 ng/mL vs. 6.88 ± 1.81 ng/mL, P = 0.02) (Table 2). However, no significant differences were observed in tacrolimus dosing (4.86 ± 1.71 mg/day vs. 4.52 ± 1.53 mg/day, P = 0.47) or formulation type (original, Prograf vs. generic formulation, Tacrobell; P = 0.70). At 3 months after LT, the high IPV group exhibited significantly elevated alanine aminotransferase levels compared to the low IPV group (40.17 ± 35.50 U/L vs. 23.47 ± 21.84 U/L, P = 0.04) and higher γ-GT levels (184.44 ± 405.69 U/L vs. 36.88 ± 37.91 U/L, P = 0.05). Additionally, glucose ≥120 mg/dL was more prevalent in the high IPV group (44.4%) than in the low IPV group (18.8%) (P = 0.05).

Single-nucleotide polymorphism genotyping

In the SNP profiling of recipients, the ABCB1 1236C>T CT and TT genotypes were more prevalent in the high IPV group than in the low IPV group (88.9% vs. 59.0%, P = 0.03) (Table 3). Similarly, the CYP3A5 6986A>G GA and GG genotypes were more frequent in patients with high IPV (66.7% vs. 40.6%, P = 0.08). ABCB1 3435C>T CC and CT genotypes exhibited a marginally higher prevalence in the high IPV group (88.8% vs. 59.0%, P = 0.18). Donor SNP distributions such as CYP3A5 6986A>G GA and GG (61.1% in high IPV vs. 40.6% in low IPV, P = 0.16) and ABCB1 1236C>T CT and CC (66.7% vs. 62.5%, P = 0.77) (Table 4) were similar.

Risk factors of high intrapatient variability

Univariate and multivariate logistic regression models were constructed to identify risk factors associated with high IPV (≥30). In univariate analysis (Table 5), a glucose level ≥120 mg/dL at 3 months was associated with a higher risk of high IPV (odds ratio [OR], 3.467; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.959–12.536; P = 0.058). Similarly, ABCB1 1236 C>T genotypes CT and CC demonstrated an increased risk of high IPV (OR, 5.474; 95% CI, 1.072–27.951; P = 0.041). The recipient CYP3A5 6986A>G genotypes GA and GG and γ-GT ≥70 U/L at 3 months were marginally significant (P = 0.090 and P = 0.102, respectively). In the multivariate analysis (Table 5), ABCB1 1236C>T genotypes CT and CC were strong predictors of high IPV (OR, 13.969; 95%CI, 1.738–112.300; P = 0.013). Additionally, the donor CYP3A5 6986A>G genotypes GA and GG were significantly associated with an increased risk of IPV (OR, 4.390; 95% CI, 1.005–19.178; P = 0.049). Elevated glucose levels (≥120 mg/dL) at 3 months after LT also remained a significant predictor of high IPV (OR, 6.494; 95% CI, 1.300–32.168; P = 0.024).

Subgroup analysis based on glucose level

To further elucidate the etiology of elevated glucose levels at 3 months, subgroup analyses based on glucose levels ≥120 mg/dL at 3 months were conducted (Supplementary Table 1). Recipients with glucose levels ≥120 mg/dL exhibited significantly higher preoperative glucose levels than those with levels <120 mg/dL (173.21 ± 113.97 vs. 113.68 ± 44.65, P = 0.01). The prevalence of preoperative DM was also higher in the ≥120 mg/dL group (42.9%) than in the <120 mg/dL group (13.9%) (P = 0.03). At 3 months after LT, glucose levels remained elevated in the ≥120 mg/dL group compared to the <120 mg/dL group (155.93 ± 33.97 vs. 97.78 ± 12.67, P < 0.01). Only 42.9% of patients in the ≥120 mg/dL group required no DM treatment, compared to 77.8% in the <120 mg/dL group. Furthermore, 28.6% of the ≥120 mg/dL group required combined oral hypoglycemic and insulin therapy compared to 2.8% of the <120 mg/dL group (P = 0.02). There were no significant differences in the tacrolimus trough concentration (P = 0.11) or steroid dosage at 3 months (P = 0.28). No significant differences in posttransplant outcomes 1–3 years after LT were found between the low and high IPV groups (Supplementary Table 2). We further explored the combined effect of recipient ABCB1 1236C>T CT and CC genotypes and donor CYP3A5 6986A>G AG and GG genotypes and compared IPV between the 4 subgroups (Supplementary Table 3). No significant differences were observed between the groups.

DISCUSSION

This study investigated the effects of 8 SNPs in recipients and donors on tacrolimus IPV in LDLT. Our findings revealed that the recipient’s ABCB1 1236C>T CC and CT genotypes, as well as the dono’s CYP3A5 6986A>G GA and GG genotypes, were independent risk factors for increased tacrolimus IPV between the 3rd and 12th months after LT. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to examine the influence of tacrolimus-related SNPs on IPV in LDLT. From a clinical perspective in LDLT, screening both donors and recipients for CYP3A5 6986A>G and recipients of ABCB1 1236C>T SNPs may be essential for identifying cohorts with an elevated risk of IPV. Furthermore, patients identified as high risk through CYP3A5 and ABCB1 genetic testing or those receiving liver grafts from donors with these genetic markers may require more intensive surveillance to mitigate the risk of complications associated with increased IPV. In addition, recipients genetically predisposed to high IPV may benefit from alternative immunosuppressants. Agents such as sirolimus or everolimus can be chosen for maintenance immunosuppressive regimens. This strategy could optimize outcomes and reduce adverse events following LT.

The most significant risk factors for high tacrolimus IPV were the CC (homozygous wild type) and CT (heterozygous variant type) genotypes of ABCB1 1236C>T compared to the TT genotypes (homozygous variant type) (Table 5). The ABCB1 gene encodes P-glycoprotein, an essential efflux transporter that plays a crucial role in modulating the absorption and pharmacokinetics of tacrolimus [20]. P-glycoprotein is predominantly found in enterocytes of the intestine and epithelial cells in the liver, gastrointestinal system, kidneys, and brain [20]. In LDLT recipients, organs that highly express P-glycoprotein, such as the intestine, kidneys, and other organs, are retained, whereas only the liver graft is transplanted. This mechanism may elucidate the effects of recipient ABCB1 on the IPV in LDLT. Additionally, a pharmacokinetic mechanism involving genetic polymorphism of ABCB1 could offer another explanation for the high IPV of tacrolimus. Specifically, individuals with ABCB1 1236C>T CC and CT genotypes exhibited a tendency to decrease tacrolimus concentrations more rapidly than those with TT genotypes, which may necessitate additional tacrolimus doses and influence IPV [21]. However, most studies on ABCB1 SNPs have concentrated on the impact of tacrolimus pharmacokinetics rather than on their influence on tacrolimus IPV [13,21,22,23]. Moreover, previous studies have mentioned that ABCB1 2677G>T/A and ABCB1 3435C>T influence tacrolimus pharmacokinetics, but not ABCB1 1236C>T [21,24]. Nevertheless, several studies have demonstrated conflicting outcomes concerning the association between ABCB1 genetic variation and tacrolimus pharmacokinetics [8,22,24,25]. Consequently, further validation studies are required to examine the effect of ABCB1 1236 C>T CT, CC genotypes on tacrolimus IPV in LT.

Our findings suggest that the CYP3A5 6986A>G GA, GG genotypes in donors exerts a more substantial influence compared to that in recipients in LDLT (Table 5). The cytochrome P450 3A5 (CYP3A5) enzyme, which serves as the primary metabolizer of tacrolimus, is predominantly expressed in hepatic and intestinal tissues [26]. As LDLT involves transferring the donor’s liver to the recipient, genetic variations in the donor’s CYP3A5 enzymes may have a greater influence on increased IPV. Although not specifically targeting IPV, several studies on LT have highlighted the importance of genetic variation in donors’ CYP3A5 for tacrolimus metabolism, considering that the liver graft is transplanted into recipients [8,13,27,28]. Furthermore, genetic variations in CYP3A5 itself may provide an alternative interpretation of our findings, wherein the CYP3A5 6986A>G AA (*1/*1) wild-type genotype enhances the catalytic activity of cytochrome P-450. Conversely, the CYP3A5 6986A>G GA and GG (*1/*3 and *3/*3) variant genotypes, representing intermediate or non-expressor phenotypes, result in decreased tacrolimus catalytic activity, consequently leading to elevated drug concentrations in the bloodstream and often requiring dose adjustment [12,16]. This finding is consistent with previous research in renal transplantation and healthy Korean individuals [6,9,10]. These studies demonstrated that individuals possessing non-expressor (such as GG and GA) SNPs of CYP3A5 typically exhibit elevated IPV levels. However, due to limited evidence in specific LT contexts, this hypothesis needs additional investigation. Nonetheless, studies in renal transplant settings challenge the influence of CYP3A5 on IPV, indicating that these SNPs might not have an effect on IPV [5,7,11]. Excluding donor variables from the multivariate analysis (not reported in this study) showed a near-significant link between high IPV and recipients’ CYP3A5 6986A>G GA and GG genotypes (OR, 3.975; 95% CI, 0.992–15.930, P = 0.051), along with ABCB1 1236C>T CC and CT, and glucose levels of 120 mg/dL at the 3-month follow-up. In addition, we also assessed the combined effect of recipient ABCB1 1236C>T CT, CC and donor CYP3A5 6986A>G GA, GG on IPV; however, consistent with previous studies, the effect was significant (Supplementary Table 3) [8].

Furthermore, glucose levels ≥120 mg/dL at 3 months were also a significant risk factor for high IPV (Table 5). However, a more detailed analysis demonstrated slightly higher trough levels and steroid doses in the high glucose group (Supplementary Table 1). Steroids are well-established risk factors for increased glucose levels in transplant recipients [29]. Tacrolimus is associated with increased insulin resistance, elevated glucose levels, and a higher incidence of NODAT [30]. The combined effects of steroid and tacrolimus administration could potentially be the etiology of elevated glucose levels and have emerged as a risk factor for IPV. Consequently, we hypothesized that increased glucose levels might induce high IPV rather than function as an independent risk factor or predictor. Nonetheless, based on the findings of our study, if glucose levels are not well controlled at 3 months after LT, intensive trough level monitoring is indicated.

This study had several limitations. As it was conducted at a single center with a relatively small sample size, the generalizability of our findings may be limited. In addition, tacrolimus IPV was not adjusted for dose, as tacrolimus dosing between 3- and 12-months after transplant was standardized, with all patients managed under a uniform target trough level protocol. Therefore, caution is warranted when interpreting these results. Nonetheless, our study provides meaningful insight into the impact of SNPs in both recipients and donors on tacrolimus IPV in LDLT. Specifically, elevated IPV was associated with the recipient ABCB1 1236C>T CT and CC genotypes, and the donor CYP3A5 6986A>G GA and GG genotypes (intermediate and non-expressors). Further large-scale, prospective studies are needed to validate these findings and determine their long-term clinical significance.

In conclusion, donor and recipient SNPs can influence the IPV of tacrolimus in liver transplantation. The identification of SNPs of ABCB1 in recipients and CYP3A5 in donors may serve as crucial predictors of IPV and facilitate more consistent monitoring of tacrolimus dosage adjustments in patients susceptible to elevated IPV, thereby preventing complications and improving graft outcomes.

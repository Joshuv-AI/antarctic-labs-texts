Updated protocol of the POINT trial: a multicenter randomized controlled trial investigating effects of preoperative enteral immunonutrition for esophageal cancer patients treated with neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy

Introduction

The Preoperative Immunonutrition Therapy (POINT) trial is an ongoing multicenter, open-labeled, randomized controlled trial that compares immunonutrition with regular diet for surgically resectable esophageal patients [1]. The primary aim of the trial is to verify whether immunonutrition during neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy can reduce the rate of complications and improve perioperative outcomes. Patients are recruited from six high-volume tertiary hospitals in China and are randomly assigned to nutrition therapy group (arm A) or control group (arm B) with a 2:1 ratio. Both groups receive neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy with concurrent radiotherapy based on the CROSS regimen followed by minimally invasive esophagectomy [2].

Patients in the nutrition therapy group received 600 ml (200 ml per bottle, three times a day) immune-enhanced enteral nutritional emulsion Rui Neng® (Fresenius Kabi SSPC, China) enriched with ω−3 fatty acids and ribonucleotides per day during the neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy, given by oral nutritional support, nasogastric feeding tube, or jejunostomy. Meanwhile, oral nutritional support is encouraged to reach 25–30 kcal/kg through the regular diet. Patients in the control group are encouraged to intake 25–30 kcal/kg through the regular diet without supplemental nutritional support before esophagectomy. Both groups receive nutrition assessment and consultation to optimize the diet structure of patients. Ethical approval for the study has been obtained from the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine Affiliated Ruijin Hospital (KY 2019–198). The trial has been registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04513418) before recruitment.

The original POINT trial protocol was published in BMC Cancer in 2022 [1]. Patient recruitment has started in November 2020, and the last patient is expected to be included by the end of 2025. Following the publication of the protocol and the start of the trial, amendments have been made to the protocol to reflect new insights about the accuracy of the inclusion and exclusion criteria, as well as to further explore more possible outcomes and to clarify the protocol regarding study procedures. The amendments to the protocol have been approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine Affiliated Ruijin Hospital. The purpose of this update is to present all amendments to the POINT trial protocol before recruitment is completed.

Participant’s amendments

Two amendments have been made to the inclusion and exclusion criteria of the study participants.

Firstly, a new exclusion criterion has been added to the protocol to exclude patients with primary small cell carcinoma of the esophagus. According to the eligibility criteria of our initial protocol, these patients could have been included in the trial at this moment. However, according to the NCCN guideline, neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy is applicable to patients histologically diagnosed as esophageal squamous cell carcinoma or esophageal adenocarcinoma [3]. Given the absence of a standardized treatment strategy for primary small cell carcinoma of the esophagus [4], determining the neoadjuvant regimen and surgical eligibility remains challenging. Therefore, patients with primary small cell carcinoma of the esophagus were excluded from our study.

In addition, the written informed consent form was updated to include two key additions regarding risk information. First, the potential adverse effects of neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy were specified, including the risks of esophageal hemorrhage, perforation, and bone marrow suppression [2, 5]. These adverse effects may cause patients to be unable to tolerate further neoadjuvant chemoradiation or enteral immunonutrition, as well as subsequent surgery, which could lead to exclusion from the study. Secondly, to study the additional exploratory endpoints (which will be detailed in the “Outcome amendments” part), the collection of fecal and peripheral blood samples before and after neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy was added to the informed consent process.

Intervention amendments

One amendment has been made to the intervention of the study participants in both nutritional therapy and control group.

Based on the CROSS regimen, esophagectomy was given preferably within 4 to 6 weeks after completion of chemoradiotherapy [2]. This approach was aimed to capitalize on the optimal window for tumor regression, allowing timely assessment of the tumor’s response to neoadjuvant therapy, particularly the achievement of pathological complete response (pCR). Additionally, early surgical intervention helped mitigate the risk of local recurrence associated with delayed resection, thereby maximizing the efficacy of neoadjuvant therapy and optimizing clinical outcomes. However, in our study, some patients experienced treatment-related adverse effects such as leukopenia, lymphopenia, anemia, decreased platelet counts, and/or esophagitis, which may delay the surgical intervention. For this reason, in order to balance the safety and efficacy of surgery, the window period from the completion of neoadjuvant therapy to surgical resection was extended to 4–8 weeks to better observe the clinical outcome.

Outcome amendments

Two amendments have been made to the study outcome.

In addition to the primary and secondary endpoints, an exploratory endpoint was introduced to investigate the prognostic value of minimal residual disease (MRD). MRD can persist in some patients following neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy and may serve as a potential source of subsequent metastasis and recurrence [6]. Studies suggest that MRD monitoring through circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) can predict whether a patient has achieved pCR after neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy, thereby evaluating the efficacy of the treatment [7, 8]. Therefore, in order to explore whether enteral immunonutrition influences the efficacy of neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy, we added the exploratory endpoint of MRD detection. The peripheral blood samples were collected before and after neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy as well as 7–14 days after surgery for the detection of MRD.

Additionally, another exploratory endpoint on microbiomics and metabolomics is added to evaluate changes in gut microbiota and metabolites before and after neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy. Studies have shown that the gut microbiota and metabolomic profiles of esophageal cancer patients differ from those of healthy individuals, potentially contributing to tumorigenesis [9–11]. The analysis of microbial genomic data can predict the metabolic potential of the gut microbiome, while metabolomic data offers direct evidence of metabolic pathways under specific conditions. By collecting peripheral blood and fecal samples before and after neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy and utilizing techniques such as mass spectrometry and metagenomic sequencing [12, 13], we aim to investigate the impact of enteral immunonutrition on the gut microbiome and metabolomic distribution in patients with esophageal cancer.

Assessment and follow-up amendments

One amendment has been made to the assessment and follow-up.

An independent assessment committee has been established to perform ongoing safety surveillance and to evaluate the outcomes of the study. To date, independent evaluation committees have periodically reviewed the randomization process, the appropriateness of allocation, and any serious adverse events to identify potential early risks. The primary and secondary outcomes will be evaluated by the independent assessment committee after all the participants are recruited.

Conclusion

In conclusion, several amendments were made prior to closure of enrolment of the POINT trial. All the other parts of the study have no amendments. These amendments do not affect the overall outcomes of the trial compared to the original protocol. The last patient is expected to be included by the end of 2025. Guaranteeing a minimum follow-up of 6 months, the primary results are expected early 2026.

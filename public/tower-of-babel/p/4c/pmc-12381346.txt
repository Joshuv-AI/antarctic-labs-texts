Analysis of mortality trends and projected burden of digestive system cancer in China: a nationwide longitudinal analysis (2006–2020) with mortality forecasts to 2045
Authors: Yajing Feng, Junhui Chai, Jichun Zhu, Yuehua Zhang, Kaijuan Wang, Fujiao Duan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo estimate mortality trends and disease burden of Digestive system cancer (DSC) in China during 2006–2020, using demographic and mortality data from cause-of-death surveillance dataset of China.MethodsJoinpoint regression was applied to calculate the crude mortality rate (CMR), age standardized mortality rate (ASMR), annual percentage change (APC), and average annual percentage change (AAPC) for analyzing the trend of death change, and years of life expectancy lost (LLE) and early death lost life (YLL) were utilized to assess disease burden. Bayesian Age-Period-Cohort models were employed to project future mortality trends up to 2045.ResultsFrom 2006 to 2020, the combined ASMR and YLL rates for female DSC showed stability, with an average life expectancy loss of 7.06 years and the highest YLL rate in the 60–65 age group. Esophageal cancer, gastric cancer, and liver cancer showed a decreasing trend from 2015 to 2020, with APCs of -4.59% (P = 0.001), -5.25% (P = 0.002), and − 3.74% (P = 0.002), respectively. Colorectal cancer and pancreatic cancer showed an upward trend in 2016–2020, with APC of 1.85% (P = 0.021) and 2.74% (P = 0.023), respectively. The trend of standardized YLL rate changes was statistically significant (AAPC = − 2.8%, 95% CI: − 3.3%, 2.3%). The standardized YLL rates of esophageal cancer (AAPC=-5.0%, 95% CI: -5.6%, 4.5%), gastric cancer (AAPC = − 4.2%, 95% CI: − 4.8%, 3.7%), and liver cancer (AAPC = − 2.7%, 95% CI: − 3.3%, 2.2%) all showed a downward trend. By 2045, a significant downward trend in both death counts and ASMRs of DSC in China from 2006 to 2045 across all genders (Ptrend<0.001), with the most pronounced decline observed in males.ConclusionsThese findings reveal divergent trends in the distribution of DSC in China. Implementing gender-specific strategies, particularly for males, and strengthening cancer surveillance systems are crucial for sustained public health improvements.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03478-z.Keywords: Digestive system cancer, Annual percentage change, Mortality, Disease burden, Trends

Introduction

Malignant tumors remain a leading cause of death globally [1], with digestive system cancer (DSC) ranking among the most prevalent malignancies, characterized by high incidence and mortality rates. The escalating burden of DSC has emerged as a critical public health challenge, particularly in China, the world’s largest developing country. Rapid socioeconomic transitions have shaped a unique cancer spectrum in China, shifting from a developing-country pattern to one more akin to developed nations [2].

In recent years, there has been a high prevalence of digestive system diseases characterized by insidious onset, challenging early diagnosis, and a significant number of advanced-stage patients [3]. The incidence rate and mortality of liver cancer [4], gastric cancer [5], esophageal cancer [6], pancreatic cancer [7] have remained high. Meanwhile, the incidence rate and mortality of colorectal cancer [8] have been rising rapidly [9].

Traditional regression analysis, which primarily capture overall temporal trends, may obscure localized patterns. Therefore, the segmented regression model came into being, but how to segment has become a new problem. The Joinpoint Regression (JPR) model proposed by Kim et al. [10] provides a solution. Recently, JPR model has been widely used in the field of epidemiological trend study of cancer and chronic diseases. It is primarily utilized for the analysis of temporal trends in incidence rates and mortality, as well as their age-specific patterns, spatial distribution trends, disease burden assessment, and the establishment of a spatiotemporal prediction model [11–14].

This study conducts a large-scale population-based analysis of DSC mortality data in China (2006–2020), encompassing lip, mouth, and pharynx cancer (LMPC), esophageal cancer, gastric cancer, colorectal cancer, liver cancer and pancreatic cancer, which helps to optimize the existing screening guidelines, achieve accurate early screening and early warning of high-risk groups, reduce the disease burden and improve the health level of Chinese people. Additionally, we carried out forecasting analyses to anticipate future trends. This enables us to provide actionable insights that can contribute to the improvement of DSC prevention strategies.

Materials and methods

Data source

Data on incident cases, deaths, and mortality of DSC were derived from Chinese cause-of-death surveillance dataset (2006–2020) and Census data of China in 2010. The monitoring data covers 31 provinces in China (excluding Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan). The number of people monitored has increased from 6% of the national population to 24%, providing substantial national representativeness.

Statistical analyses

The 2006–2020 cause-of-death surveillance dataset of China were entered using Excel 2019. The mortality of DSC were characterized by year, sex, and age group. GraphPad Prism 8 was employed for graphing and calculating crude mortality rate (CMR) calculation. To ensure the comparability of DSC death data across different years, the standardized death rate of DSC was computed using the standard population age structure of the 2010 Chinese census.

Joinpoint software (Version 4.9.1.0) was employed to compute the age standardized mortality rate (ASMR), annual percentage change (APC), and average APC (AAPC) to evaluate the change trend of mortality of DSC-related malignancies. CMR was used to compare the mortality of different diseases among the monitored population in the same year, and ASMR was applied to compare the mortality of diseases in different years. APC was derived from log-linear regression via the model: log (ASMRy) = α + βy (y is the year, α is a constant, β is regression coefficient), with APC calculated as 100% × (eβ − 1). AAPC represented a weighted average value of APC in multiple trend stages according to the number of years. A maximum of 3 connection points can be set in the 15 year monitoring data to fit 4 change stages. APC < 0 indicated that the incidence is decreasing year by year; APC > 0 indicated that the incidence is increasing year by year; If there was no connection point, then APC = AAPC, denoting a monotonic trend across the entire dataset.

LLE calculation formula: ( is the life expectancy at birth in the absence of a disease, and is the life expectancy at birth in the presence of a disease). YLL=the number of deaths in the age group multiplied by the corresponding standard life expectancy, and the YLL rate is the ratio of YLL to the number of monitored population.

Disease burden of DSC was quantified using years of life expectancy (LLE), years of life lost (YLLs) and the rate of YLLs. Calculations adhered to the DALYs framework specified in WHO’s National Tools dataset [15]. The Nordpred package (R software, Version 4.4.1) was employed to forecast the number of deaths and age-standardized mortality from 2021 to 2045, which applied Bayesian age-period-cohort models to predict disease trends. Additionally, Python 3.11 was utilized for linear regression analysis to assess trends of annual standardized rates (ASRs) changes over years.

All P-values were two-sided, and statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

Results

Mortality of digestive system cancer

From 2006 to 2020, the overall CMR of DSC in nationwide surveillance areas ranged from 66.39/100,000 to 77.41/100,000, with ASMR demonstrating a relatively stable trend (Table 1). Esophageal cancer, gastric cancer, and liver cancer exhibited a decreasing trend from 2015 to 2020, with APCs of −4.59% (P = 0.001), −5.25% (P = 0.002), and − 3.74% (P = 0.002), respectively. In contrast, colorectal cancer and pancreatic cancer showed an upward trend in 2016–2020, with APC of 1.85% (P = 0.021) and 2.74% (P = 0.023). The overall changes during the monitoring period from 2006 to 2020 revealed a significant decline (APC = −2.91%, P = 0.002) (Fig. 1).

Disease burden of digestive system cancer

During the study period, the overall YLL rate of DSC in China ranged from 669.60/100,000 to 741.35/100,000, with a statistically significant decreasing trend was observed in the standardized YLL rate (AAPC=−2.8%, 95% CI: −3.3%, 2.3%) (Table 2). Subgroup analyses revealed significant downward trends in the standardized YLL rates of LMPC (AAPC=−0.7%, 95% CI: −1.2%,−0.1%), esophageal cancer (AAPC=−5.0%, 95% CI: −5.6%, 4.5%), gastric cancer (AAPC=−4.2%, 95% CI: −4.8%, 3.7%), and liver cancer (AAPC=−2.7%, 95% CI: −3.3%, 2.2%). The AAPC trend between men and women are basically consistent with the overall change (Table S1).

The overall YLL rate of DSC in China exhibited a relatively low level before the age of 20, followed by a rapid increase at the age of 35–65, peaking in the age group of 60–65, and gradually declining thereafter. Compared to 2006, the overall YLL rate of DSC in all age groups showed a significant upward trend in 2020 (Fig. 2B).

From 2006 to 2020, the average life expectancy loss at birth attributable to DSC in China was 7.06 ± 0.24 years, with LMPC, esophageal cancer, gastric cancer, colorectal cancer, liver cancer and pancreatic cancer being 0.30 ± 0.02 years, 1.01 ± 0.10 years, 1.73 ± 0.14 years, 0.82 ± 0.01 years, 2.80 ± 1.54 years and 0.39 ± 0.07 years, respectively (Fig. 3).

Discussion

The process of population aging encompasses demographic, economic, social, and health dimensions. Factors including population wealth, evolving family structures, women’s professional pursuits, the quality of social and healthcare services, educational attainment levels, and government policies in public health exert an increasingly significant impact on the aging population [16]. DSC remain one of the leading causes of death and a substantial economic burden in China [17]. Identifying common population geographical distribution, risk factors, and epidemiological trends could help identify high-risk population groups. Thus, the present study aimed to analyze mortality and disease burden of DSC in China from 2006 to 2020, providing a basis for evidence-based prevention and control strategies.

This study utilized national cause-of-death surveillance data to assess mortality rates and YLLs due to DSC in common population from 2006 to 2020, employing a Joinpoint regression model. Results showed that the CMR of the three types of cancer ranged from 66.39/100,000 to 77.41/100,000, and YLL rates varied between 669.60/100,000 to 741.35/100,000, reflecting a high disease burden associated with an average 7.06-year reduction in life expectancy at birth. Compared to 2006, the overall YLL rate for DSC across all age groups significantly improved by 2020, potentially attributable to combined effects of enhanced screening, diagnostic/treatment technologies, modified risk factor exposure, population-based prevention strategies, and demographic aging.

As reported previously [18], China registered approximately 4.064 million new cancer cases in 2016, with lung cancer, colorectal cancer, stomach cancer, liver cancer and breast cancer comprising 57.4% of all new cases. Among them, lung cancer is the most common in men, followed by liver cancer, stomach cancer, colorectal cancer and esophageal cancer, which account for about 68.83% of new cancers in men. Breast cancer is most common in women, followed by lung, colorectal, thyroid and stomach cancers. This study confirmed that digestive system tumors remain a major disease burden and a significant public health problem in China.

Globally, about 2 billion individuals consume alcohol, with over 75 million diagnosed with alcohol use disorder and at risk for alcohol-related liver disease. Additionally, approximately 2 billion adults are classified as overweight or obese, while more than 400 million have diabetes; both of which serve as risk factors for non-alcoholic fatty liver disease and hepatocellular carcinoma [19]. China faces a substantial liver disease burden with notable gender and regional disparities, necessitating the development of accurate early diagnostic approaches for high-risk populations [20]. The traditional high incidence of esophageal cancer, stomach cancer, liver cancer and other tumors show a continuous downward trend, but the prognosis is poor, the disease burden is still heavy [21, 22].

Over the past decade, survival rates for malignant tumors in China has in China have gradually increased, but a significant gap remains compared to developed countries—largely due to low early-diagnosis rates and non-standardized late-stage care. Screening and early diagnosis and treatment are universally recognized as effective means to reduce cancer mortality. It is reported that since 1991, the cancer death rate has decreased by 27%, mainly due to tobacco control and screening. Thus, China should make joint efforts in expanding the screening and early diagnosis and treatment of related tumors, and promoting the standardization and homogenization of clinical diagnosis and treatment of tumors to reduce the malignant tumor mortality in China.

Additionally, this study reveals significant trends in ASMR of DSC in China from 2006 to 2045. Notably, the ASMRs for males consistently exceed females across all cancer types, aligning with global patterns attributed to sex-specific risk factors (e.g., smoking, alcohol consumption, occupational exposures) [23]. Liver cancer remains a leading cause of mortality, attributable to chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) infections and aflatoxin exposure in China [24]. Gastric and esophageal cancers, historically linked to dietary habits (e.g., high salt intake, preserved foods) and Helicobacter pylori infections, show declining trends, likely reflecting improved food preservation techniques and early screening programs [25].

Concerningly, colorectal cancer exhibits an upward trajectory, paralleling rapid urbanization and westernized diets (low fiber, high processed meat intake) [26]. Projected ASMR stabilization post-2030 may reflect the potential impact of nationwide interventions, such as increased endoscopic screening and public health campaigns promoting physical activity [27]. Gender disparities highlight the need for targeted prevention strategies, particularly for males, with compounded risks from tobacco and alcohol use.

Limitations

The present study has certain limitations that warrant attention. The present study has certain limitations that warrant attention. Firstly, due to the limitation of the data in the national mortality surveillance database, the mortality and disease burden of lip cancer, oral cancer and pharyngeal cancer are combined (U061), which will lead to certain information bias and uncertainty. Secondly, the current dataset only provides aggregated national-level mortality codes without provincial identifiers, precluding regional comparisons despite ensuring data consistency for national trend assessment. Thirdly, assessing causes of death using International Classification of Diseases (ICD) codes can create selection bias or influence in determining the main causes, depending on how these causes are reported at different levels (countries/regions). Finally, the monitoring data was generally published four years ago, and the most recent data currently available is 2020. As China’s population ages, trends in mortality and disease burden at different ages should be included in future studies.

Conclusions

In this study, we found that the overall mortality and disease burden of digestive cancers in China remained stable from 2006 to 2020. Among these cancers, the mortality rates and disease burdens of esophageal, gastric and liver cancers decreased, while the mortality rates of colorectal cancer and pancreatic cancer remained at a high level. There is an urgent need for more targeted and customized prevention strategies to alleviate the disease burden.

Dynamic Increase of the C2HEST Score in Relation to the Development of Incident Atrial Fibrillation: A Longitudinal Cohort Study
Authors: Yan‐Guang Li, Yi‐Jie Liu, Li‐Li Wang, Qiao‐Yuan Li, Tao Zhang, Xu Liu, Qin‐Chao Wu, Yan Yin, Shao‐Min Chen, Jin Bai, Daniele Pastori, Gregory Y H Lip, Yun‐Long Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe risk of incident atrial fibrillation (AF) increases with accumulating risk factors. Baseline‐only risk assessment may not reflect the real risk of incident AF. We aimed to evaluate the performance of the dynamic change of the C2HEST score (C2: coronary artery disease/chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (1 point each); H: hypertension (1 point); E: elderly (age ≥75 years, 2 points); S: systolic/diastolic heart failure (2 points); and T: thyroid disease (hyperthyroidism, 1 point) C2HEST) score to assess the risk of incident AF during follow‐up.MethodsThe present study data were retrieved from the Information Management and Big Data Center of Peking University Hospital Group. Patients without AF at baseline were enrolled. New‐onset comorbidities were recorded during follow‐up. The change in the C2HEST score was analyzed. The baseline and the change in C2HEST scores were compared for the prediction of incident AF.ResultsA total of 120 133 patients were included in the final analysis. During 346 400 patient‐years of follow‐up, 2304 developed incident AF (0.67 per 100 patient‐years). The mean C2HEST score increased significantly from 1.62 to 2.96 (P<0.05). A significant proportion of patients had newly diagnosed comorbidities (61.9% with ∆C2HEST ≥1 in AF and 14.6% with C2HEST ≥1 in non‐AF). The change in C2HEST scores showed better performance compared with the baseline score, as assessed by area under curve analyses (∆C2HEST 0.821 [0.811–0.830], baseline 0.758 [0.747–0.769]), decision curve analysis, and positive net reclassification index.ConclusionsThe risk for incident AF is not static and increases with the accumulation of new comorbidities. The change in C2HEST score had better prediction in assessing individual risk of incident AF compared with the baseline score.Keywords: atrial fibrillation, C2HEST, real world, risk factorsSubject Categories: Atrial Fibrillation, Electrophysiology, Arrhythmias

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained arrhythmia in adults. The global prevalence of AF has increased substantially over the past few decades and is currently affecting 60 million people. 1 AF is related to a high risk of ischemic stroke, 2 heart failure, 3 cognitive dysfunction, 4 myocardial infarction, 5 and mortality. 6 , 7 The aging population and accumulating risk factors are the prominent reasons for the surging trend of AF prevalence. 8 Assessing individuals' risk of developing incident AF is necessary to launch effective and cost‐saving prevention strategies.

Several risk score models have been proposed to predict AF. 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 , 16 All of these score models were calculated at 1 time point (usually baseline) to predict incident AF, which may happen many years later. However, the risk of incident AF is not static and increases with aging and the accumulation of risk factors. There is a lack of studies investigating the dynamic change in risk profile in relation to incident AF.

The C2: coronary artery disease (CAD)/chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (1 point each); H: hypertension (1 point); E: elderly (age ≥75 years, 2 points); S: systolic/diastolic heart failure (2 points); and T: thyroid disease (hyperthyroidism, 1 point) (C2HEST) score was first introduced as a simple clinical model to predict AF risk in a large population in China, 17 and become validated in diverse of populations, including those from different ethnic and clinical settings. 18 , 19 , 20 , 21 , 22 , 23 , 24 The C2HEST score has been designated as a major risk scheme in predicting incident AF in the 2023 American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association/American College of Clinical Pharmacy/Heart Rhythm Society guideline for the diagnosis and management of AF. 25 However, individuals' risk of developing AF is not static. Whether the dynamic change in the C2HEST score could be used to predict an individual's risk of AF has never been investigated before.

Our aim was to evaluate the performance of the C2HEST score at baseline and the change in C2HEST score between baseline and follow‐up in assessing the risk of incident AF.

METHODS

Data Availability

Data supporting the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request.

Study Population

All studied patients were enrolled from the Peking University Hospital Group, which is affiliated with the Peking University Health Science Center and includes 1 major medical center, Peking University Third Hospital, and 4 branch hospitals, Capital International Airport Hospital, North Branch Hospital, Party School of the Central Committee Hospital, and Qinhuangdao Hospital. The Peking University Hospital Group is a comprehensive medical institution group in Beijing, China, providing medical service to ≈5 million outpatient visitors and ≈150 000 inpatients annually. Each hospitalized patient has a permanent and unique personal registration number allowing the documentation of all medical records of each hospitalization and outpatient visit.

The data set was established based on the electronic hospital medical information system. The electronic hospital medical information system was initially launched in 2000 under the supervision of the Information Management and Big Data Center, which mainly takes charge of the planning, implementation, maintenance, development, project management, and data application of the information construction in the Peking University Hospital Group. The current data set included data about general information, demographic characteristics, medical history, physical examinations, laboratory results, imaging data (picture archiving and communications system), ECGs, diagnosis, treatment, and outcomes of discharge. All diagnoses of each hospitalization were coded using the International Classification of Diseases, Ninth Revision and Tenth Revision (ICD‐9 and ICD‐10).

The analyzed population for the present study was restricted to inpatients discharged from the Peking University Hospital Group, between January 1, 2000 and December 31, 2022. Patients aged ≤18 years were excluded. All patients with known AF diagnosis at baseline were also excluded. For each enrollee, the first admission was set as the baseline. The following admissions were set as follow‐ups. Patients with only 1 admission were excluded. An index of 30 days was applied, and those with follow‐up duration <30 days were excluded. This study was approved by the institutional review board of Peking University Hospital and

Beijing Anzhen Hospital. The board waived the need for informed consent owing to the retrospective design of the study.

Risk Score Calculation and Study Outcome

The calculation of the C2HEST score was according to the original description of the original study. 17 The baseline C2HEST score was calculated at the first time of discharge. The follow‐up C2HEST score was calculated at the time of the last discharge, December 1, 2022, or diagnosis of AF, whichever came first. For clinical analysis, C2HEST scores were categorized into 5 groups (0, 1, 2, 3, and ≥4 points) and further stratified into 3 strata: low‐risk (0 points), intermediate risk (1 point), and high risk (≥2 points). To assess score dynamics, we defined the change in C2HEST score as the transition between baseline and follow‐up risk strata. Participants were subsequently classified into 6 transition groups: stable low‐risk (low‐low), low‐to‐intermediate, low‐to‐high, stable intermediate (intermediate‐intermediate), intermediate‐to‐high, and stable high‐risk (high‐high). The median duration from baseline to the calculation of the second C2HEST score was 395 days (interquartile range, 142–1049 days).

The primary outcome was incident AF (ie, newly diagnosed AF), which was confirmed based on the results of a 12‐lead ECG or 24‐hour Holter monitoring showing at least 30 seconds of records with no discernible repeating P waves and irregular RR intervals. Patients with atrial tachycardia and atrial flutter were excluded. The time of the first AF diagnosis was reconfirmed according to the description of medical history from the electronic hospital medical information system or examinations in each hospitalization. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, and was approved by either Peking University Hospital Group Ethics Committee or Beijing Anzhen Hospital Ethics Committee.

Statistical Analysis

Baseline and follow‐up characteristics of the population are presented using mean±SD for continuous data and frequency (percentage) for categorical variables. Comparisons between AF and non‐AF groups were conducted using the Student t test or Mann‐Whitney U test for continuous variables and χ2 test for categorical variables.

The incidence of AF during follow‐up was described with an incident rate per 100 person‐years. Kaplan‐Meier curves were depicted to show the non‐AF survival rates on the baseline C2HEST score strata (ie, C2HEST=0, 1, 2, 3, and ≥4). The confidence interval of the Kaplan‐Meier curve was also shown in. Differences between survival curves were tested using the log‐rank test. A Cox proportional risk model was established to calculate the hazard ratio and confidence interval for incident AF between baseline C2HEST score and change in C2HEST score. A risk plot was drawn to illustrate individual follow‐up duration and outcome.

Receiver operating characteristic curves of the baseline C2HEST score and change in C2HEST score for predicting incident AF were depicted, and the area under the curve (AUC) for each receiver operating characteristic was calculated. The clinical usefulness and benefit of using change in C2HEST score compared with baseline C2HEST score was tested using the decision curve analysis. Differences between AUCs were tested using the Delong test. Net reclassification index was also calculated. All statistical analyses were performed using R statistical software (version 4.3.1).

RESULTS

Baseline Characteristics

A total of 120 133 patients were enrolled in the present study, yielding 346 400 patient‐years of observation. Over a 20‐year span of enrollment and follow‐up duration, 2304 patients were newly diagnosed with AF, with an incidence of 0.67 (95% CI, 0.66–0.67) per 100 patient‐years (Table 1). Patients with incident AF were older, more frequently men, and had a higher prevalence of multiple comorbidities, including hypertension, CAD, diabetes, previous ischemic stroke, and heart failure (all P values <0.05). The C2HEST score was 0.46±0.90 at baseline and increased to 0.72±1.18 at follow‐up for all participants (P<0.001) (Table 2).

Dynamic Risk of Incident AF

During follow‐up, patients had increased C2HEST scores (from 0.44±0.88 to 0.67±1.13 in non‐AF and from 1.62±1.38 to 2.96±1.56 in AF). Patients with AF had higher ΔC2HEST, compared with patients without AF (1.35±1.39 versus 0.23±0.65, P<0.001) (Table 2). In patients without AF, 14.6% had a ΔC2HEST ≥1, which was 61.9% in patients with AF. Furthermore, 8.1% of patients with AF had significantly increased C2HEST score (increase ≥4) compared with only 0.5% in those without AF.

Among the new‐onset comorbidities during follow‐up, hypertension was the most common newly developed risk factor inpatients without AF (7.5%), whereas heart failure was the most common newly developed factor in patients with incident AF (22.4%), followed by CAD (20.2%) and hypertension (18.5%) (see Table 2). As an important constituent of the C2HEST score, aging is a major increased uncorrectable factor, with 12 466 having an increased age risk stratum (aged >75 years).

Comparison of the Baseline and Change in C2HEST Scores

The follow‐up C2HEST score showed improved accuracy for predicting incident AF compared with the baseline C2HEST score, as shown by receiver operating characteristic curves (0.874 [95% CI, 0.867–0.881] versus 0.758 [95% CI. 0.747–0.769], respectively, P for Delong test <0.001). The change in C2HEST score also showed better AUC (0.821 [95% CI, 0.811–0.830]) compared with the baseline C2HEST score (P for Delong test <0.001). There was a significant difference between the AUCs for the follow‐up and change in C2HEST scores (Delong test, P=0.047). The follow‐up C2HEST score was also superior to the Framingham (0.753 [95% CI, 0.745–0.767]) and CHARGE‐AF score (Cohorts for Heart and Aging Research in Genomic Epidemiology for Atrial Fibrillation) (0.751 [95% CI, 0.740–0.769]) (Delong test P<0.001 respectively).

In the decision curve analysis, the follow‐up C2HEST score was superior to the baseline C2HEST score, suggesting that using the follow‐up C2HEST score for predicting AF risk has a higher net clinical benefit compared with the baseline C2HEST score (see Figure 2). Compared with the baseline C2HEST score, the follow‐up C2HEST score had a positive net reclassification index of 24.7%, suggesting that 24.7% of patients were reclassified into the right risk groups than using the baseline C2HEST score.

Sensitivity Analysis

To address the potential impact of the interval between baseline and follow‐up C2HEST score calculations, we performed a sensitivity analysis stratified by the median interval duration (395 days). The results consistently demonstrated that the change in C2HEST score outperformed the baseline C2HEST score across both subgroups. For intervals <395 days, the change in C2HEST score achieved an AUC of 0.859 (95% CI, 0.842–0.877) versus the baseline C2HEST score AUC of 0.808 (95% CI, 0.787–0.829) (Figure S1). For intervals ≥395 days, the change in C2HEST score maintained superior performance, with an AUC of 0.821 (95% CI, 0.814–0.829) compared with the baseline score AUC of 0.736 (95% CI, 0.724–0.837) (Figure S1B).

DISCUSSION

Our study shows that the risk for incident AF is not static and increases over time, especially in patients accumulating risk factors such as hypertension and heart failure, which were the most common incident risk factors for patients without and with AF, respectively. Compared with the baseline C2HEST score, the follow‐up C2HEST scores and change in C2HEST scores showed improved performance for predicting incident AF.

Early diagnosis AF is still challenging. Therefore, many risk evaluation systems have been proposed to guide the conduction of prevention, screening, and early management strategies. For example, in the Huawei Heart Study, with >500 000 subjects receiving long‐term heart rhythm monitoring, the detected AF events rates increased with higher C2HEST scores. 23 The risk evaluation systems could be divided into 2 types, medical history‐based (where the majority needs previous clinical risk factor diagnoses) and biomarker‐based (which needs results of laboratory, imaging, or physical examinations). Patients' examination results varied monthly or even daily, making the prediction of AF unreliable, which often happens a few years later. However, the dynamic change of incident AF risk has not been investigated previously.

For those risk‐scoring systems based on clinical diagnosis, such as the C2HEST score, the risk category is considered to be reliable. In the present study, we found that on long‐term follow‐up, an individual's risk of incident AF is not static, due to new‐onset comorbidities. In the present study, a significant proportion of patients developed new comorbidities during follow‐up (61.9% in the AF group, 14.6% in the non‐AF groups). Hypertension accounts for the major new‐onset comorbidity, which aligns with previous studies, suggesting that hypertension is a prominent contributor to incident AF due to its high prevalence and incident rate. 12 , 13 For those with incident AF, heart failure was the major contributor, because of its intimate relationship with AF. In the C2HEST score, heart failure was assigned with 2 points and treated as a major risk factor. Therefore, once heart failure develops in follow‐up, the risk of AF surges. Furthermore, 20.2% of patients had new‐onset CAD in the AF group, and once the diagnosis of AF is established, antiplatelet treatment should be switched to anticoagulation, indicating the importance of identification of AF diagnosis in those with newly diagnosed CAD. 26

In many AF risk score systems, age is a widely used risk factor. For the C2HEST score, age ≥75 years is one of the major risk factors. In our previous study, which used a subset of the current analysis, refining the age strata could statistically improve the predictive accuracy of the C2HEST score, 27 suggesting that age is a consistently nonmodifiable and increasing risk factor for AF. In the present study, patients' age had a mean increase of 3.4 years, which indicates that the risk of incident AF increases although no new comorbidities occurred. Due to these new‐onset comorbidities and the aging population at follow‐up, the baseline C2HEST score showed limited predictive performance (time‐dependent AUC of 0.617). Using the follow‐up C2HEST score showed improved accuracy and clinical benefits based on decision curve analysis compared with the baseline C2HEST score. The ΔC2HEST also showed improved predictive capability for AF, superior to the baseline C2HEST score, and better than baseline or follow‐up C2HEST scores on decision curve analysis, suggesting that the impact of incident comorbidities is a nonnegligible factor for the development of incident AF. Furthermore, the follow‐up C2HEST score is more applicable in clinical practice than the ΔC2HEST score and more accurate than the baseline C2HEST score. For a patient at high risk for developing incident AF, annual assessment or screening for AF might be necessary if potential patients are presented at the clinic, such as those with stroke or unspecific symptoms. Furthermore, if a new‐onset risk factor(s) was identified during follow‐up, the risk of incident AF needs more recognition, and screening approaches might be launched, especially for those with suspicious symptoms.

The findings of this study have significant clinical implications. From the perspective of clinical practice, we found that the follow‐up C2HEST score was superior to the initial C2HEST score, suggesting that the latest C2HEST score composed of previous risk factors and newly diagnosed risk factors are pivotal for accurate risk evaluation. Clinicians should move beyond relying solely on baseline risk assessments for AF, and instead regularly update and reassess patients' risk using the C2HEST score, which accounts for newly developed comorbidities over time. For patients with rising follow‐up or ∆C2HEST scores, more intensive preventive measures or closer monitoring should be considered to effectively mitigate the risk of incident AF. Additionally, integrating the follow‐up C2HEST score into clinical decision‐making tools or electronic health records can streamline dynamic risk assessment and enable timely, personalized interventions. By adopting these strategies, clinicians can enhance AF risk stratification and potentially reduce its incidence through proactive management.

Strengths and Limitations

This is the first long‐term study based on a hospitalized population investigating the dynamic change in incident AF risk. The study population was well characterized, and incident cardiovascular risk factors were recorded. We showed that the follow‐up and ΔC2HEST scores had improved predictive performances compared with the baseline C2HEST score. The present study has the following limitations. First, this is a retrospective study, which relies on the accuracy of diagnostic codes from the electronic hospital medical information system. Second, AF has asymptomatic and paroxysmal patterns leading to the rate of AF being underestimated. The diagnosis of AF was ascertained through systematic searching in all available electronic health record data, based on underlying relevant medical information including relevant procedure (ablation for AF or left atrial appendage occlusion), direct oral anticoagulants, or those mentioned in physician notes. All relevant information was double‐checked to confirm the diagnosis of AF. Third, the C2HEST score was derived and tested among the Chinese population, and has never been validated in other populations, which merits further study. In addition, as a component of C2HEST score, hypertension is diagnosed by a continuous variable (ie, blood pressure), meaning that the exact level of which has impact on AF risk. However, we were unable to provide exact blood pressure measurements, which will be explored in a future study.

CONCLUSIONS

In this hospitalized population, we have demonstrated that the risk of incident AF is not static and increases during follow‐up, with aging and cardiovascular risk factors development. The change in C2HEST scores has improved predictive performance compared with the baseline C2HEST score. Individuals’ risk factors for developing incident AF are not static and increase with the accumulation of comorbidities and aging. Reevaluation of AF risk should be made in high‐risk populations with newly developed risk factors.

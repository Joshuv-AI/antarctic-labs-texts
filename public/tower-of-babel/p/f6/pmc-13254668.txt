Research note: Genome-wide association study reveals candidate genes for egg production in Muscovy duck
Authors: Wanli Yang, Jinzhou Peng, Weihuang lin, Danyu Song, Xuqiao Lang, Cheng zhang, Xingyong Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Muscovy duck is a high-quality lean-meat duck breed, yet it exhibits relatively poor reproductive performance. In this study, GWAS was used to screen candidate genes controlling egg production of Muscovy duck. Transcriptomics were further used to explore their regulatory mechanisms. Results showed PPP3CA, ZFYVE28, DENND2B, UBAP1, MAP1B were associated with total egg production. LD analysis identified 4_7070908_G>A as tagSNP of PPP3CA, in which GG genotype had significantly higher egg production than AA genotype (P < 0.05) in both the sequenced group and their next two generation. Transcriptomic analysis of large white follicles from the two genotypes revealed that differentially expressed genes, mainly those involved in steroid biosynthesis, extracellular matrix-receptor interaction, and the MAPK signaling pathway, were upregulated in GG; meanwhile, in small yellow follicles, steroid biosynthesis, PPAR, calcium, and TGF-β signaling pathways were upregulated in GG. Among these, PPP3CA is a potential key candidate gene influencing egg production, and it may exert a negative regulatory effect on the proliferation of granulosa cells in Muscovy duck follicles.Keywords: Muscovy duck, Egg production, GWAS, PPP3CA

Introduction

Muscovy ducks (Cairina moschata) is an important source of high-quality meat due to their high lean meat percentage. However, their annual egg production is 80 to 150 eggs, and exhibiting a relatively low laying performance as compared to egg-type chicken and duck (>300) (Semiu Folaniyi et al., 2021).

Egg production is a polygenic genetic trait with low to moderate heritability, influenced by both genetic components and environmental factors (Li et al., 2013; Qin et al., 2015). Although some GWAS analyses of avian egg production traits have been reported, the loci associated with egg production still vary considerably across different species and populations due to substantial differences in their genetic backgrounds. In this study, GWAS was performed to investigate the egg production trait of Muscovy ducks. Key candidate genes were screened, and their functional regulatory mechanisms in egg formation were further validated, thereby providing a scientific reference for the molecular breeding of Muscovy ducks.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

All animal experimental procedures in this study were approved by the Animal welfare committee of Anhui Agricultural University with the assurance number SYDW-P20210823021.

Animal samples and GWAS analysis

The Muscovy ducks were provided by Anqing Yongqiang Muscovy Duck Co., Ltd., Anhui, China. 295 ducks were reared in 59 pens with 5 in each pen. Each pen covers an area of 4.5m2. The male-to-female ratio was 1:5, with natural mating. All the ducks were provided free access to food and water and were provided 16–18 hours of light during the laying stage. The nutritional levels of the ducks were met by the National Research Council (NRC). Each small pen is equipped with numbered laying cages, and female muscovy ducks are marked with foot tags. Skilled workers palpated the lower abdomen of each duck to determine check for egg development. Ducks suspected to carry eggs were temporarily placed in laying cages, and their egg-laying status was checked and recorded the next day. This work starts at 9 a.m. every day. Both normal and abnormal eggs are recorded as egg production. The feeding and management, blood collection, DNA extraction, whole-genome sequencing and subsequent Genome-Wide Association Study (GWAS) analysis were performed according to the method described by our previous study. (Yang et al., 2025) Finally, 215 individuals with complete egg-laying records were selected for GWAS analysis. Egg production was defined as the total number of eggs laid annually (31-54 weeks of age, the full laying period). A total of 69 F1 and 100 F2 individuals, which were randomly selected from the offspring of the GWAS population, were used for restriction enzyme genotyping, and their egg production was recorded. Three individuals per genotype, representing the two distinct genotypes (AA and GG) of the tagSNP 4_7070908_A>G in the PPP3CA gene, were selected from the F₂ population for ovarian transcriptome analysis. Follicles of different grades and ovarian tissues were collected from the selected Muscovy ducks after anesthetization and slaughter, and then frozen in liquid nitrogen for gene expression quantification and transcriptome sequencing.

PCR amplification, sanger sequencing verification and restriction enzyme genotyping

DNA was extracted from the blood samples of the F1 and F2 individuals using the phenol‒chloroform method. The PCR reaction system contained 1 μL of DNA, 0.5 μL of forward primer (10 μmol/L), 0.5 μL of reverse primer (10 μmol/L), and 10 μL of 2 × PCR MIX (Cat. No. 10102ES08, Shanghai Yisheng Biotechnology Co., Ltd.), with the total volume of the reaction system made up to 20 μL using dd H2O. The PCR amplification primers for the PPP3CA gene fragment were as follows, the forward primer 5′-AAAAGCCAAACAACAGG-3′ and reverse primer 5′-TCTGTCACTTTCTCACCC-3′, with the amplified fragment length of 211 bp. The reaction started with denaturation at 94°C for 5 min, followed by 35 cycles of denaturation at 94°C for 30 sec, annealing at 60°C for 30 sec, and extension at 72°C for 1 min, and ended with extension at 72°C for 3 min.

Sanger sequencing of the PCR products was performed in a single direction by Tsingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Nanjing, China), and sequence alignment was conducted using the SnpGene software (Dotmatics, San Diego, CA, USA). The specific restriction endonuclease for the mutation site was PvuⅡ (5′...... CAG^CTG...... 3′, Thermo Scientific, USA). The reaction system for the restriction enzyme digestion assay was 10 μL, consisting of 0.2 μL of restriction endonuclease PvuⅡ, 1 μL of Cut Smart buffer, 7.8 μL of dd H2O, and 1 μL of PCR amplification product. The reaction was carried out at 37°C for 1 h. After the reaction, 1% agarose gel electrophoresis was performed at 120 V for 40 minutes to detect the digestion status of the mutation site in all samples.

Transcriptome sequencing

Transcriptome sequencing was conducted by Tsingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Large white and small yellow follicles were collected from individuals with AA and GG genotypes of PPP3CA, with 3 replicates per genotype. These two follicular stages were selected based on their significant morphological differences, which may indicate substantial variations in gene expression. Follicle RNA was extracted using an Animal Tissue Total RNA Extraction Kit (Ambion, USA) and purity was detected with the RNA Nano6000 Assay Kit (Bioanalyzer 2100 System, Agilent Technologies, CA, USA). Qualified RNA was purified and reverse-transcribed via the NEBNext Ultra RNA Library Prep Kit (Illumina, NEB, USA). The cDNA fragments of 370-420 bp were PCR amplified and then further purified using the AMPure XP System (Beckman Coulter, Beverly, USA). The final cDNA library quality was evaluated with a Qubit 2.0 Fluorometer (Agilent Bioanalyzer 2100 System, USA) and qualified libraries were sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). Clean reads were obtained by removing adapter, poly-N and low-quality reads from raw reads. Gene annotation used Nr, Nt, PFAM, KOG, SwissProt, KEGG, and GO databases and expression levels were calculated via the RPKM method. Genes with fold change > 2 and P-value < 0.05 were considered differentially expressed.

Statistical analysis

Egg production and gene expression level among different genotypes were analyzed using ANOVA (SPSS 22.0, USA). For gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA), gene sets from KEGG pathways were used as the background, and log2FC values of each differential group were employed as scores for the background gene sets to analyze the enrichment of interested gene sets. The analysis was performed using the Online Bioinformatics Platform (http://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/).

Results and discussion

GWAS analysis on egg production traits of Muscovy ducks identified several candidate genes, including PPP3CA, ZFYVE28, DENND2B, UBAP1 and MAP1B. Among them, PPP3CA and ZFYVE28 have also been reported in previous relevant studies (Bai et al., 2021; E et al., 2019; Hong, 2025; Islam et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2024). Tag SNPs 4_7070908_G>A of PPP3CA and 4_68326049_C>T of ZFYVE28 were validated by Sanger sequencing in F1 and F2 populations. Individuals carrying the GG genotype of PPP3CA 4_7070908_G>A exhibited 15.3 more eggs than those with the AA genotype (P < 0.01). The TT genotype of ZFYVE28 4_68326049_C>T conferred a significant increase of 8.3 eggs in egg production relative to the CT genotype (P < 0.05, Fig. 1).

Transcriptome GSEA results of large white follicles from individuals with different genotypes of PPP3CA (GG and AA) showed that genes in the gene sets (steroid biosynthesis, extracellular matrix-receptor interaction, and MAPK signaling pathway) were upregulated in the GG genotype (high-yielding type), while genes in oxidative phosphorylation were downregulated For small yellow follicle, the genes in the gene sets (steroid biosynthesis, PPAR signaling pathway, calcium ion signaling pathway, TGF-beta signaling pathway, focal adhesion, extracellular matrix-receptor interaction) were generally upregulated in GG genotype individuals (high-yielding), while the genes in the gene sets (necroptosis, ferroptosis, lysosome) were generally downregulated in GG genotype (Fig. 2). These indicate that follicles from individuals with the GG genotype have stronger proliferation ability, as well as enhanced lipid and sterol synthesis capabilities.

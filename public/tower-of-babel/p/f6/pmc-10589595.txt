Molybdenum target mammography-based prediction model for metastasis of axillary sentinel lymph node in early-stage breast cancer
Authors: Caixing Yuan, Guolin Xu, Xiangmei Zhan, Min Xie, Mingcong Luo, Lilan She, Yunjing Xue
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Sentinel lymph node (SLN) status is closely related to axillary lymph node metastasis in breast cancer. However, SLN biopsy has certain limitations due to invasiveness and diagnostic efficiency. This study aimed to develop a model to predict the risk of axillary SLN metastasis in early-stage breast cancer based on mammography, a noninvasive, cost-effective, and potential complementary way. Herein, 649 patients with early-stage breast cancer (cT1–T2) who received SLN biopsy were assigned to the training cohort (n = 487) and the validation cohort (n = 162). A prediction model based on specific characteristics of tumor mass in mammography was developed and validated with R software. The performance of model was evaluated by receiver operating characteristic curve, calibration plot, and decision curve analysis. Tumor margins, spicular structures, calcification, and tumor size were independent predictors of SLN metastasis (all P < .05). A nomogram showed a satisfactory performance with an AUC of 0.829 (95% CI = 0.792–0.865) in the training cohort and an AUC of 0.825 (95% CI = 0.763–0.888) in validation cohort. The consistency between model-predicted results and actual observations showed great Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit (P = .104). Patients could benefit from clinical decisions guided by the present model within the threshold probabilities of 6% to 84%. The prediction model for axillary SLN metastasis showed satisfactory discrimination, calibration abilities, and wide clinical practicability. These findings suggest that our prediction model based on mammography characteristics is a reliable tool for predicting SLN metastasis in patients with early-stage breast cancer.

1. Introduction

Currently, breast cancer is the biggest cause of death in women worldwide. According to the reports of the International Agency for Research on Cancer on 36 malignancies in 185 countries, breast cancer represents most malignancies in women, and with a morbidity rate of 24.2% and mortality rate of 15.0%, it has become an unignorable threat to women health and life. Of the new cases of breast cancer each year, about 3% to 10% of patients have distant metastasis at the time of diagnosis.[1] The involvement of axillary lymph nodes (ALN) is a crucial prognostic factor for breast cancer patients, and accurate axillary staging is critical for evaluating the disease status. Once ALN metastasis occurs, the clinical stage and treatment regimen may change, which can significantly affect patient prognosis. Since the sentinel lymph node (SLN) in the axilla of the breast is the first lymph node to receive lymphatic drainage from a tumor, the metastasis of breast cancer usually begins at the SLN. Therefore, the SLN metastasis status provides important evidence in predicting the involvement of the ALN to guide the dissection of ALN.[2] Notably, SLN biopsy (SLNB) assists in evaluating the ALN metastasis status and establishing the axillary stage in early-stage breast cancer.[3,4] However, being an invasive procedure, SLNB can sometimes lead to postoperative complications, such as muscle strength decline, limb lymphedema, and upper limb sensorimotor abnormalities.[5–7] Moreover, the risks of false-negative and false-positive diagnoses by SLNB cause additional burden and incur limitations in evaluating the ALN status.[8] In clinical practice, a secure and efficient method for predicting ALN metastasis would be an alternative solution.

Imaging examination techniques, such as computed tomography, magnetic resonance imaging, ultrasound, and molybdenum target mammography, have been widely applied in clinical practice for identifying breast cancer because of their advantages of non-invasiveness and safety. Molybdenum target mammography offers the advantages of high sensitivity and specificity, low radiation exposure, simple operation, non-invasiveness, cost-effectiveness, and high repeatability and has thus become the preferred examination method for breast cancer. Some T0 stage cases present with tiny calcifications with malignant manifestations that look like sediments, thin rods, or clusters, and molybdenum target mammography can identify calcifications even smaller than 0.1 mm, making it an irreplaceable technique in the diagnosis of calcified breast cancer.[9] However, there is currently no unified imaging standard for differentiating between benign and malignant ALNs, and presently, this distinction is predominantly based on size, shape, and the hilar structure. In most cases, a normal ALN has a kidney-like shape with a clear hilus and no fat accumulation; furthermore, it has a cortical thickness of <3 mm, smooth margins, and no local protrusion.[10] Therefore, an efficient alternative to axillary SLN assessment is essential to guide appropriate treatment and improve patient prognosis. Imaging examination using molybdenum target mammography has great potential to noninvasively predict ALN metastasis.

In the present study, we aimed to construct a prediction model for SLN metastasis on the basis of the characteristics of molybdenum target mammography in axilla-negative early-stage breast cancer (cT1–T2) using a noninvasive and cost-effective approach.

2. Methods

2.1. Patients

We retrospectively included patients with early-stage breast cancer[11] (cT1–T2) who underwent SLNB between January 2018 and February 2021 and collected relevant data from their medical records. The inclusion criteria were as follows: primary early-stage breast cancer (cT1–T2) histologically confirmed by biopsy and/or resection; absence of distant metastasis confirmed by preoperative imaging and laboratory examination; no changes associated with malignant tumors such as mass or abnormal lymph nodes were found in the axilla or negative axillary findings on palpation, mammography, and ultrasound examinations; a time period of <2 weeks between molybdenum target mammography and operation; a mass-forming type identified on molybdenum target mammography; and no puncture biopsy performed and/or treatment administered before undergoing molybdenum target mammography.

Due to the retrospective nature of investigation, the study was conducted using the opt-out method for the case series of our hospital. Therefore, information on variables or parameters available in this study is limited. Herein, the study aimed to construct a prediction model for SLN metastasis based on the characteristics of tumor mass in mammography in early-stage breast cancer (cT1–T2). 2.2. Clinical and pathological information

The clinical and pathological information[12] on the following parameters was collected from the eligible patients: age, menstrual state (menopause and premenopausal), and characteristics of estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (Her-2), and tumor-proliferating antigen Ki-67.

2.3. Molybdenum target mammography

All patients underwent bilateral mammography <2 weeks before the operation in a routine cranial–caudal position and medial oblique position. If needed, local magnification photography was performed using a breast molybdenum/rhodium target mammography scanner (GE, Senographe Essential). According to the Breast Imaging Report and Data System (5th edition) developed by the American College of Radiology in 2013,[13] 2 radiologists with more than 5 years of experience in breast imaging diagnosis who were blinded to pathological results performed a double-blind image review and extracted tumor characteristics for the following parameters: glandular type, tumor density, tumor shape, tumor margins, tumor size, and the presence of spicular or lobular structures and malignant calcification signs. The consistency of imaging-based diagnoses of the 2 radiologists was compared.

2.4. Statistical analysis

We performed statistical analyses using R software (version 4.0.5, R Development Core Team, Vienna, Austria) and SPSS software (Statistics 17, SPSS Statistics for Windows, Chicago, IL). All included patients were randomly assigned to the training cohort and validation cohort in a 3:1 ratio. The diagnostic consistency between 2 radiologists was compared using the Kappa test for categorical variables and intraclass correlation coefficient for continuous variables. Univariate and multivariable binary logistic regression analyses were used to identify independent predictors with statistical significance in the multivariable analysis. The prediction model was constructed on the basis of independent predictors in the training cohort, which were visualized using a nomogram and evaluated by area under the receiver operating characteristic curve for discrimination performance, calibration curves for calibration capacity, and decision curve analysis for clinical practicability. External validation was performed in the validation cohort. A P value of < .05 was considered statistically significant.

This study was approved by the institutional review board and ethics committee of Fujian Medical University Union Hospital (no. 2022KY198). Formal consent from individuals was not required given the retrospective design of the study.

3. Results

A total of 649 patients were included in the present study and assigned to the training cohort (n = 487) or the validation cohort (n = 162). In the training cohort, 288 patients were negative for SLN metastasis and 199 patients were positive for SLN metastasis. In the validation cohort, 88 patients were negative for axillary SLN metastasis and 74 patients were positive for SLN metastasis.

3.1. Molybdenum target mammography-based and clinical characteristics

The diagnostic reports of 2 radiologists showed great consistency, with a Kappa value of 0.852 for the glandular type, 0.953 for tumor density, 0.882 for tumor shape, 0.895 for tumor margins, 0.916 for the presence spicular structures, 0.899 for the presence of lobular structures, and 0.882 for the presence of malignant calcification. The tumor size was compared using the maximal diameter with an intraclass correlation coefficient of 0.934. If the results for a categorical variable were inconsistent between the 2 radiologists, consensus was achieved by consulting another experienced radiologist. The tumor size used for subsequent analysis was the average of the results measured by the 2 radiologists. In the training cohort, the results of univariate logistic regression analysis identified the following as independent predictors of SLN metastasis: tumor margins, tumor density, tumor shape, presence of spicular structures, presence of calcification, and tumor size (all, P < .05).

3.4. Construction and evaluation of nomogram model

The predictive mode visualized by the nomogram was constructed on the basis of independent predictors of SLN metastasis using an R package named “rms” (Fig. 2). The receiver operating characteristic, calibration, and decision curves (Fig. 3) were used to evaluate the discriminative ability, calibration performance, and clinical practicability of the prediction model, respectively. A nomogram based on independent predictors showed great prediction performance with an AUC of 0.829 (95% CI: 0.792–0.865) in the training cohort. The calibration curves indicated high consistency between model-predicted results and actual observations (χ2 = 13.22, P = .104) with great Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit. Considering the results of decision curve analysis, patients could benefit from clinical decisions made using the present model within the threshold probabilities of 6% to 84%. Table 3 shows the details of independent predictors in the validation cohort, which included 88 patients with negative SLN and 74 patients with positive SLN (Table 3). The results of external validation also revealed great prediction performance with an AUC of 0.825 (95% CI: 0.763–0.888), and the calibration curves showed great consistency with the ideal curve of y = x (Fig. 4). These findings showed the potent discriminative and calibration abilities of the present prediction model. These findings suggest that our prediction model based on mammography characteristics (i.e. specific mass characteristics) is a reliable tool for predicting axillary SLN metastasis in patients with early-stage breast cancer (cT1–T2).

4. Discussion

It is essential to predict the SLN metastasis status on the basis of preoperative information in early-stage breast cancer patients with minimal possibility of lymph node metastasis to avoid unnecessary complications from SLNB. Molybdenum target mammography is a noninvasive approach for breast examination and offers the advantages of low radiation dose, cost-effectiveness, and convenient operation. The present prediction model based on 3 molybdenum target variables, including tumor size, calcification, and the presence of spicular structures, showed potent discriminative performance with a simple, economic, and safe procedure. This may reduce the economic burden and physiological pressure of the patients, making it convenient for clinical practice based on SLNB.

Being the first lymph node to receive lymphatic drainage from a tumor, SLN has an important role in clinical staging, regional evaluation, and treatment instruction. There is a “subtraction” trend for ALN treatment wherein SLNB has been advocated to replace ALN dissection in patients with early-stage breast cancer to avoid overtreatment and improve the postoperative quality of life. Although SLNB as a standardized diagnostic approach for invasive breast cancer can accurately predict ALN involvement, it can be detrimental to the structure and function of axillary lymphatic or blood vessels to some extent.[14,15] The low axillary recurrence rate despite positive SLNB findings has brought into question the rationality of performing SLNB in patients with early-stage breast cancer.[16] Moreover, the stained marker used for SLN may cause chemical damage and radioactive pollution in the breast and axillary tissues.[17]

Breast calcifications on molybdenum target images vary in shape and distribution. Calcifications in malignant breast cancer usually present with a heterogeneous and linear branching shape and show a segmental, linear, and clustered distribution with a diameter of <1 mm, which is among the most typical signs of breast cancer. Some scholars believe that the calcification can be attributed to calcium salt deposition after cancer necrosis and to the notion that calcium salt deposition and ALN metastasis typically increase with the increasing malignancy of the cancer.[18] Therefore, microcalcification in breast cancer has been considered as a risk factor, and the malignancy and aggressiveness of cancers with calcification are typically significantly higher than of those without calcification and are associated with a worse prognosis.[19]

For patients with breast tumors without cast calcification and for those with tumor size <1 cm, the survival rate was relatively high and not related to histological grading or adjuvant treatment. Conversely, the prognosis of patients having breast cancer with cast calcification was relatively poor with increased local recurrence risk even if the tumor size was <9 mm.[20] Based on a retrospective analysis of 7317 cases reported in western China, Zheng et al[21] found that compared with breast cancer without calcification, breast cancer with calcification was associated with a larger tumor size and more invasiveness (P < .01) and a higher risk of ALN metastasis. In this study, the SLN metastasis risk was significantly higher in patients having breast cancer with calcification than in those having breast cancer without calcification, which was consistent with previous reports.

The current research on calcification was mainly focused on the morphology and warrants further investigation into the genetic aspects to clarify the influence of calcification on patient prognosis and optimize individualized treatments. The proliferation of collagen fibers around tumors in the breast results in uneven tumor margins and is correlated with tumor size, biological behavior, and molecular marker expression. The clarity of margins is of great importance for the diagnosis of breast cancer. Generally, tumors with clear margins represent lesser aggressiveness and lower probability of ALN metastasis than those with fuzzy margins.[22] In this study, relative to patients having tumors with obscure tumor margins, the risk of SLN metastasis was higher in patients having tumors with fuzzy margins and lower in patients having tumors with clear margins.

Spicular structures appear as radial, high-density shadows of different lengths emitting from the tumor edge and have been considered to be directly indicative of a diagnosis of breast cancer. These structures are believed to form because of fibrous tissue proliferation of the surrounding stroma in invasive breast cancer. The influence of spicular structures on the invasiveness and prognosis of breast cancer remains controversial. The results of univariate analysis revealed some statistically significant differences, and the results of the subsequent multivariable analysis identified the presence of spicular structures as an independent risk factor for axillary SLN metastasis. Breast masses with the spicular structures had a higher risk of SLN metastasis than those without the spicular structures.

Some studies have reported that tumor interstitial reaction causes the proliferation of fibrous tissue at the distal end of the cancer, thus forming a barrier-like structure. This barrier slows down the outward spread of cancer, thus resulting in reduced invasiveness and good prognosis of patients having breast cancer with spicular structures. Conversely, other scholars have held the opposite perspective, wherein they reported that high interstitial infiltration, active collagen fiber proliferation, and the generation of new lymphatic and blood vessels induced by cytokines and high VEGF expression through bypass may accelerate the proliferation of tumor cells and lead to lymphatic and blood metastasis of breast cancer with spicular structures,[23] and accordingly, such patients were considered to have highly malignant cancer with poor prognosis. In this study, the presence of spicular structures was found to be associated with a higher risk of SLN metastasis (SLN metastasis rates: 51.34% [153/298] in patients with spicular structures, 24.33% [46/189] in patients without structures).

In the present study, tumor size was found to be a statistically significant predictor of SLN metastasis in both univariate and multivariable analyses, and the results of the multivariable analysis suggested that the risk of SLN metastasis increased by 3.60 times (95% CI: 2.50–5.19, P < .001) with tumor size as an independent risk factor. Tumor size is reportedly directly correlated to tumor load and activity. Therefore, tumor size in breast cancer has been considered a sign of tumor invasiveness that is associated with ALN metastasis.[24,25] Barone et al[26] evaluated ALN metastasis in 213,292 patients with breast cancer in the SEER database according to their T stages and found that T1a, T1b, T2, and T3 stages were associated with ALN metastasis rates of 7.8%, 13.3%, 50.2%, and 70.1%, respectively.

This study also has several limitations. First, given the retrospective design of the study, we could not comprehensively consider every clinicopathological variable, which is a limitation we seek to address with a future prospective study. Second, the single-center design was lacking external validation from multiple regions and institutions, and therefore, generalization of the present model needs further verification. Third, since only patients with mass type were included among all early-stage breast cancer patients, the conclusions of present study may have an inherent bias.

5. Conclusion

In conclusion, the prediction model for axillary SLN metastasis based on tumor characteristics identified using molybdenum target mammography showed effective discrimination and calibration abilities and wide clinical practicability in patients with early-stage breast cancer. Specifically, tumor mass in mammography accompanied by specific characteristics, such as large size, indistinct margin, spicular structure, and calcification, indicates a higher risk of axillary ALN metastasis in patients with early-stage breast cancer (cT1–T2). This could facilitate clinicians’ decision-making regarding the optimal treatment.

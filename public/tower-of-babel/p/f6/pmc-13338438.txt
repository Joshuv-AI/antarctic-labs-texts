Prediction of hydraulic support pressure in a coal longwall face by integrating multi-head attention and temporal feature learning
Authors: Chuan Deng, Zeng Ding
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHydraulic support pressure in a coal longwall face is a key indicator for characterizing roof activity and strata weighting intensity, exhibiting pronounced nonlinearity and strong temporal dependence. To achieve accurate pressure prediction and early warning of roof weighting risks, this study takes the 39,103-longwall face of a coal mine as a case study and develops a time-series prediction model for hydraulic support pressure based on data acquired from an online mine pressure monitoring system. The proposed model integrates a convolutional neural network (CNN) for feature extraction, a long short-term memory (LSTM) network for temporal modeling, a multi-head attention mechanism for adaptive feature weighting, and an AdaBoost-based ensemble strategy to enhance prediction robustness. The results demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves consistently high prediction accuracy under different time-step settings, with the optimal overall performance obtained at a time step of four. Compared with the conventional LSTM model and its improved variants, the proposed method exhibits clear advantages in terms of RMSE, MAE, and R2. The findings of this study provide reliable technical support for roof weighting trend prediction and hydraulic support safety control in coal longwall mining, showing promising potential for practical engineering applications.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-51773-8.Keywords: Coal longwall face, Hydraulic support pressure, Time-series prediction, Multi-head attention mechanism, Ensemble learningSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

With the continuous increase in mining depth and production intensity, the roof structure of coal longwall faces has become increasingly complex, and the manifestation of mine pressure exhibits pronounced nonlinearity and uncertainty1. Hydraulic supports serve as the core equipment for roof control in longwall mining, and their working resistance directly reflects roof activity and the stress evolution of the surrounding rock mass. Consequently, support pressure is widely recognized as a critical indicator for evaluating roof weighting intensity and overall face safety2,3. Extensive engineering practice has demonstrated that abnormal variations in hydraulic support pressure often act as important precursor signals of roof fracturing, periodic weighting, and even dynamic disasters4. Therefore, developing reliable methods for the dynamic prediction of hydraulic support pressure is of great engineering significance for ensuring safe and efficient coal mine production.

Traditional mine pressure theories and engineering approaches primarily rely on empirical formulas, statistical analysis, and field observations5,6. Research on hydraulic support pressure evolution has largely focused on macroscopic characterization and post-event analysis, which is insufficient to meet the growing demands for refined face management and proactive early warning. With the widespread deployment of online mine pressure monitoring systems7, hydraulic support pressure data are now acquired with high sampling frequency, long monitoring duration, and synchronous measurements across multiple supports, providing a solid foundation for data-driven prediction studies. However, such monitoring data are inevitably influenced by mining-induced disturbances, abrupt changes in roof structure, and adjustments in support operating conditions, resulting in strong non-stationarity, high noise levels, and complex temporal dependencies8,9. Relevant research10,11 has pointed out that the non-stationarity and high noise of monitoring data are the key factors limiting the prediction accuracy of traditional models, which urgently need to be solved by new data processing and modeling methods. These characteristics pose significant challenges to conventional prediction methods.

In recent years, machine learning and deep learning techniques have achieved remarkable progress in time-series prediction. Long short-term memory (LSTM) networks, by introducing gated mechanisms, effectively overcome the gradient vanishing problem encountered by traditional recurrent neural networks when modeling long sequences12. As reported in Song et al., 202013, the LSTM network successfully solved the problem of long-term dependency in time-series data modeling, which has been widely concerned in the field of engineering prediction. As a result, LSTM models have been widely applied in engineering fields such as tunneling, energy systems, and structural health monitoring14–16. For example, in tunneling engineering, He and Chen17 used LSTM to predict surrounding rock deformation with high accuracy; in energy systems, LSTM models were employed to forecast energy consumption and equipment operating status18; in structural health monitoring, LSTM was utilized to identify structural damage by analyzing vibration signals19. In mining engineering, several studies have attempted to employ LSTM-based models to predict roof separation, mine pressure parameters, and hydraulic support resistance, achieving encouraging results20. Nevertheless, under complex mining conditions, conventional LSTM models still exhibit limitations in selectively capturing critical temporal features from long and noisy sequences.

To enhance a model’s ability to focus on informative historical inputs, attention mechanisms have been gradually introduced into time-series modeling. The multi-head attention mechanism proposed by Vaswani et al. (2017)21 enables parallel modeling of sequence correlations in multiple subspaces, allowing more comprehensive extraction of key temporal features. This mechanism has demonstrated superior performance in natural language processing and complex time-series prediction tasks22. Meanwhile, convolutional neural networks (CNNs)23 possess inherent advantages in local feature extraction and have been proven effective in capturing short-term fluctuations and local patterns in temporal data. On the other hand, a single deep learning model may still suffer from limited generalization capability when confronted with small-sample and high-noise engineering datasets. Ensemble learning methods, particularly the adaptive boosting (AdaBoost) algorithm, iteratively train multiple weak learners and combine them through weighted integration, thereby significantly improving model robustness and stability24,25. Integrating deep learning architectures with ensemble learning strategies provides a promising approach to enhancing the reliability of engineering prediction tasks.

Against this background, this study takes the 39,103-longwall face of a coal mine as a case study and utilizes multi-support pressure data obtained from an online mine pressure monitoring system to develop a hydraulic support pressure time-series prediction model integrating multi-head attention, CNN, LSTM, and AdaBoost. By averaging the pressures of multiple supports at the same time step, a prediction task oriented toward the overall roof weighting behavior of the longwall face is formulated. The influence of different time-step lengths on prediction performance is systematically investigated. Furthermore, the proposed model is validated through comparative analysis with several benchmark models, and its feasibility and applicability for engineering practice are thoroughly discussed. The research outcomes provide new technical means and theoretical references for roof weighting prediction and hydraulic support safety control in coal longwall mining. AdaBoost algorithm

Adaptive Boosting (AdaBoost) is a classical ensemble learning method that constructs a strong learner by iteratively training multiple weak learners and combining their predictions in a weighted manner29. In hydraulic support pressure prediction, pressure data are influenced by roof activity, mining disturbances, and evolving engineering conditions, resulting in pronounced nonlinearity, stage-wise characteristics, and noise. Under such circumstances, a single predictive model may exhibit noticeable bias during certain periods. AdaBoost addresses this issue by dynamically adjusting sample weights during training, forcing subsequent learners to focus more on samples with larger prediction errors. This strategy effectively improves the model’s adaptability to complex working conditions and abnormal fluctuations in pressure data.

During the temporal feature extraction stage, classical deep learning network structures are employed, including a convolutional neural network (CNN, Fig. 3)31 and a long short-term memory network (LSTM, Fig. 4)32. Specifically, the 1D-CNN is used to perform hierarchical local feature extraction on the original hydraulic support pressure time series. By setting convolution kernels of appropriate sizes, the convolution layer slides over the time-domain sequence with a fixed step to perform convolution operations, so as to extract and perceive the local fluctuation characteristics, mutation trends and correlation features between adjacent sampling points in the pressure data. Through the convolution calculation, the CNN can automatically mine the dominant local temporal features from the high-dimensional and noisy monitoring data, and realize the enhanced representation of local change rules in the pressure sequence. The LSTM, with its gating mechanism and memory cell structure, models long-term temporal dependencies in the pressure sequence and is well suited for representing sustained load evolution induced by roof activity.

Case study

Engineering background

The 39,103-longwall face of Yuanbaowan Coal Mine is in Panel 1 of the No. 9 coal seam, with a strike length of approximately 240 m. According to geological exploration results and previous mining records, the overburden conditions along the strike direction exhibit pronounced spatial heterogeneity. Specifically, within the first 172 m from the setup entry on the main haulage side, the overlying strata consist of a goaf left by the extraction of the No. 6 coal seam. Beyond this section, extending toward the tailgate, the overburden corresponds to a triangular remnant coal pillar formed after the partial extraction of the adjacent 36,108 working face. This combined “goaf–solid coal” overburden structure leads to significant variations in roof structure and stress distribution along the longwall face, which is considered one of the primary geological factors inducing temporal fluctuations in hydraulic support loading.

The vertical distance between the 39,103 working face and the overlying No. 6 coal seam ranges from approximately 15 to 20 m. The immediate roof of the No. 9 coal seam is mainly composed of medium sandstone and sandy mudstone. The integrity of the roof strata and the interlayer bonding conditions play a critical role in controlling periodic roof weighting behavior and the evolution of hydraulic support resistance during face advancement.

The on-site construction was carried out strictly in accordance with the design scheme. A total of 63 roof fracturing boreholes were designed for the setup entry, among which 56 boreholes were successfully completed. The remaining seven boreholes could not be effectively charged due to obstructions caused by hydraulic supports, borehole collapse, or interference from existing anchor cables encountered during drilling. The charging operation of the fracturing boreholes commenced during the morning shift on September 29, 2025, and the loading and sealing grouting of all effective boreholes were completed by the middle shift on September 30. Subsequently, the fracturing operation for all valid boreholes was carried out during the same middle shift. The detailed layout and implementation of the pre-fracturing scheme are illustrated in Fig. 6.

Based on the above engineering conditions, the hydraulic support pressure monitoring data collected during the initial mining stage of the 39,103 working face were selected as the research object in this study. A time-series prediction model for hydraulic support pressure was then developed to provide a theoretical basis for roof weighting risk early warning and intelligent support control in longwall mining operations.

Raw monitoring data

To systematically characterize the temporal evolution of hydraulic support pressure during the initial mining and caving stage of the 39,103-longwall face, hydraulic support pressure data collected by the mine pressure online monitoring system were adopted as the fundamental dataset in this study. Along the working face, one monitoring unit was installed for every five hydraulic supports, resulting in a total of 27 monitoring points corresponding to support numbers 5#, 10#, 15#, …, and 135#. These monitoring points are distributed almost uniformly along the full length of the working face, enabling a comprehensive representation of both the spatial distribution and temporal variation of hydraulic support pressure.

The monitoring system operated continuously from 16:00 on October 3, 2025, to 08:00 on November 18, 2025. During this period, pressure data were automatically recorded three times per day at a fixed interval of 8 h, yielding a total of 138 consecutive time steps. As a result, a multivariate time-series dataset with uniform temporal spacing was obtained. This dataset covers the entire process of initial mining and initial caving of the 39,103-working face, including the stages of first roof weighting and the gradual transition into regular periodic weighting. Therefore, it provides a reliable data basis for temporal correlation analysis and predictive modeling of hydraulic support pressure. This phenomenon suggests that roof activity near the setup entry became increasingly active, and the support–roof system entered a strongly nonlinear response stage. After October 22, the spatial distribution of support pressure became more complex, and localized high-pressure concentration zones gradually formed in the central region of the working face. These complex features necessitate the introduction of time-series correlation analysis and advanced predictive modeling methods to fully exploit historical information and achieve short-term pressure prediction and roof weighting risk early warning.

Data processing and analysis

Data preprocessing

Due to the combined effects of complex geological conditions, variations in hydraulic support operating states, and the operational status of the mine pressure online monitoring system, the raw hydraulic support pressure data from the 39,103 working face inevitably contain abnormal values, missing data, and inconsistencies in measurement scales. Directly feeding such raw data into predictive models may lead to unstable training performance and may even obscure the true temporal evolution patterns of hydraulic support pressure.

To address the presence of abnormally low values (e.g., 0 MPa) and short-term abrupt fluctuations in the raw pressure records, the 3σ criterion was employed to identify outliers in the pressure time series of each hydraulic support. This statistical method enables effective detection of nonphysical data points caused by sensor malfunction, temporary support unloading, or localized roof instability, while preserving the overall statistical characteristics of the dataset. For the detected outliers, a neighboring time-point interpolation strategy was adopted for replacement, considering the continuity and stage-dependent characteristics of hydraulic support pressure evolution34. Meanwhile, all timestamps of the pressure monitoring data were carefully verified and aligned to ensure that pressure records from all 27 monitoring points corresponded to the same time steps. For occasional data gaps resulting from communication failures or system delays, time-series interpolation was applied to reconstruct the missing values35, thereby forming a multivariate hydraulic support pressure dataset with uniform time intervals and no missing entries.

In addition, given the noticeable differences in numerical ranges and fluctuation amplitudes among pressures recorded at different supports, directly using the raw values for modeling may cause learning algorithms to be overly influenced by high-magnitude variables. Correlation analysis

To further investigate the spatial and temporal correlation characteristics of hydraulic support pressure at the working face, a Pearson correlation analysis was conducted on the pressure time series recorded from the 27 monitored hydraulic supports at the 39,103-working face. The correlation matrix is visualized as a heatmap in Fig. 8, where the color intensity represents the magnitude of linear correlation between different support pressure sequences, with correlation coefficients ranging from − 1 to 1.

Pearson correlation heatmap.

From the overall distribution, most pairs of hydraulic support pressure series exhibit positive correlations, with correlation coefficients mainly concentrated in the range of 0 to 0.6. This indicates that during working face advancement, the pressure responses of different supports are jointly controlled by roof structural activity and mining-induced disturbances, resulting in a pronounced collective loading behavior. This observation is consistent with engineering practice, where periodic roof breakage and load transfer occur along the support–roof system, inducing coordinated pressure variations across the working face. From a spatial perspective, adjacent or spatially proximate supports tend to show higher correlation coefficients. In particular, supports located in the middle section of the working face (e.g., supports No. 55 to No. 85) demonstrate significantly stronger correlations compared to those at the two ends.

In addition, a small number of support pairs exhibit low or weak negative correlations, which are mainly observed in specific support combinations. Such phenomena may be attributed to localized sensor anomalies, differences in load-bearing conditions among supports, or heterogeneous roof failure mechanisms. These results further highlight the spatial non-uniformity of hydraulic support pressure evolution across the working face.

Model training strategy

Dataset partition

To avoid data leakage commonly encountered in time-series modeling, the dataset was partitioned strictly according to temporal order. The first 80% of the time-series samples were used as the training set for model parameter learning, while the remaining 20% were reserved as the test set to evaluate the model’s predictive performance on future time steps. This time-ordered splitting strategy realistically simulates practical engineering prediction scenarios and ensures the reliability of model evaluation.

Design of temporal input–output structure

Based on the defined prediction task, the average hydraulic support pressure at the working face, denoted as , was adopted as the target variable to construct a univariate time-series prediction model. Averaging the pressures of the 27 supports at each time step effectively characterizes the overall load-bearing state of the working face and the intensity of roof activity, while mitigating the influence of localized abnormal supports on model training.

The hydraulic support pressure dataset consists of 138 consecutive time points. From an engineering mechanism perspective, when the time window length is relatively small (–2), the model relies primarily on short-term historical information and is therefore unable to fully represent the delayed effects associated with roof weighting, support adjustment, and surrounding rock structural evolution. This limitation leads to larger prediction errors on the test set. Under moderate time window lengths (–6), the model can simultaneously learn short-term pressure fluctuations and medium-term evolutionary trends, achieving a favorable trade-off between prediction accuracy and robustness. This behavior is highly consistent with the periodic roof activity and gradual load redistribution processes observed in longwall mining operations. When the time window length becomes excessively large (), the dimensionality of the input sequence increases substantially, which not only increases the computational burden during training but may also weaken the contribution of early historical information to current predictions. As a result, the model’s generalization performance tends to deteriorate.

Discussion

In this study, a single-step prediction strategy is employed to forecast the average hydraulic support pressure for the next time step (approximately 8 h), which aligns with the sampling interval of the on-site mine pressure monitoring system39,40. This time scale carries clear engineering relevance and practical value. From the perspective of engineering feasibility, although the proposed model has a relatively complex structure, its training and inference durations are both considerably shorter than the monitoring data acquisition interval. Model retraining and parameter updating can thus be accomplished within consecutive monitoring cycles, suggesting that the method has the potential for practical engineering deployment.

It should be emphasized that this study focuses on the prediction of average hydraulic support pressure, which can reasonably reflect the overall loading state of the working face and the macroscopic evolution characteristics of roof pressure. In this sense, the method can provide a reference for roof weighting early warning and macroscopic risk assessment. Nevertheless, the averaging operation inevitably smooths the spatial heterogeneity among individual supports, which may weaken the model’s ability to detect local abnormal loads or fine-scale risk signatures. In addition, restricted by on-site engineering conditions, the analysis is based on monitoring data collected over a relatively short observation period. These constraints impose limitations on the generalization ability of the proposed model to other mining areas with different strata conditions, geological structures, or mining parameters.

To better illustrate the model’s applicability and influencing factors, the effects of geological conditions, mining layouts, and monitoring frequencies on prediction performance are discussed as follows:

Geological conditions: The model tends to achieve more stable and accurate predictions in working faces with relatively intact and stable roof strata (e.g., continuous thick sandstone roofs) due to the regular pressure evolution pattern. In contrast, complex geological conditions such as fractured roofs, fault zones, variable rock properties, or uneven coal seams can lead to abrupt pressure fluctuations and strong non-stationarity, which may noticeably degrade prediction accuracy.

Lower sampling frequencies may fail to record critical mutation information, resulting in reduced prediction reliability. These influencing factors should be fully considered in practical field applications.

Future research will be conducted to systematically address the aforementioned limitations and further improve model performance and applicability: First, multi-point and multi-support collaborative prediction frameworks will be established to characterize spatial heterogeneity and identify local abnormal pressure. Second, long-term monitoring data from multiple working faces with diverse geological settings and mining conditions will be introduced to enhance model robustness and cross-scenario generalization. Third, multi-step ahead prediction and uncertainty estimation methods will be explored to support more comprehensive and robust risk assessment. Fourth, the quantitative relationship between monitoring frequency and prediction performance will be investigated, and adaptive sampling strategies will be developed to balance engineering cost, data volume, and prediction accuracy.

Conclusion

Based on hydraulic support pressure monitoring data from the 39,103-working face of Yuanbaowan Coal Mine, this study develops and evaluates a hybrid time-series prediction model that integrates a multi-head attention mechanism, convolutional neural networks, long short-term memory networks, and an AdaBoost ensemble strategy. The main findings are summarized as follows:

In the initial mining stage, hydraulic support pressure shows a clear evolutionary trend characterized by rapid decline, subsequent recovery, and gradual stabilization. This pattern is consistent with the periodic fracture, caving, and recompaction behavior of the roof strata, supporting the rationality and necessity of using time-series modeling for support pressure prediction.

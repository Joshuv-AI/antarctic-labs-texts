Inappropriate range of fasting plasma glucose may affect embryo euploidy negatively: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Tangyi Geng, Hui Ji, Zichen Zheng, Kai Ding, Ye Yang, Wei Jiang, Danyu Ni, Chun Zhao, Xiufeng Ling
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAccurate assessment of embryo quality is paramount in reproductive medicine, for which determining embryonic euploidy is a critical metric. The impact of diabetes on fertility is well documented; however, the influence of fasting plasma glucose (FPG) levels on embryonic euploidy rates among women without diabetes remains understudied.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study enrolled 985 women who underwent preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidies (PGT-A) between December 2017 and December 2023. Participants were grouped into quartiles based on FPG levels. Restricted cubic spline (RCS) and multiple linear regression models were used to assess the correlation between FPG levels and embryonic euploidy rates in women without diabetes.ResultsIn total, 4,786 embryos were analyzed. Groups with higher FPG levels exhibited significantly reduced embryonic euploidy rates (P < 0.001), 2PN cleavage numbers, metaphase II oocyte counts, high-quality blastocyst counts, and rates of high-quality blastocysts (β < 0). These significant correlations persisted even after adjusting for potential confounders (P = 0.007). Furthermore, RCS analysis indicated an optimal FPG range of 4.57–5.19 mmol/L.ConclusionsThis study provided preliminary evidence of an association between FPG level and embryonic euploidy rates in women without diabetes undergoing assisted reproductive technology (ART). The findings indicate the benefits of FPG level assessment and management before undergoing ART. Future studies should investigate the impact of glucose control on ART outcomes and establish optimal glucose management strategies.Keywords: FPG, Embryonic euploidy rate, ART, PGT-A

Background

Infertility is a major global health issue threatening human reproductive health, with the World Health Organization (WHO) estimating an incidence rate of 17.5% [1]. With the swift advancement of assisted reproductive technology (ART), substantial improvements have been made in the success rates and safety profiles of infertility interventions. Annually, approximately 300,000 infants are born through ART in China [2].

Embryonic euploidy is a critical determinant of embryo quality. Concurrently, embryonic aneuploidy is a primary contributor to ART failure, spontaneous miscarriages and birth defects [3, 4]. Preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidy(PGT-A) entails analysis of DNA of oocytes or embryos to detect aneuploidy [5].

Embryonic aneuploidy primarily originates from maternal chromosomes [6], whereas the embryonic euploidy rate declines with increasing maternal age [7]. Despite this, favorable pregnancy outcomes can be achieved in older populations through euploid embryo transfer [8]. The accurate determination of embryonic euploidy is therefore essential. Observational studies investigating the association between embryonic euploidy rate and the ovarian stimulation process [9], blastocyst morphology [10], culture conditions [11], or ovarian function [12] have been extensively conducted. Maternal nutrition can influence reproductive outcomes, to a certain extent, through its impact on oocyte metabolism [13]. Phenotypic factors, such as body mass index (BMI) have been integrated into prognostic models [14]. However, studies investigating the link between embryonic euploidy rates and other indices of maternal nutritional states, such as FPG, are scarce.

The WHO defines diabetes mellitus as having FPG levels ≥ 7 mmol/L or 2-h postprandial glucose levels ≥ 11.1 mmol/L [15]. Dysregulate blood glucose levels are linked to a spectrum of adverse pregnancy outcomes [16–18]. Furthermore, children of parents with diabetes have an increased risk of chronic diseases [19]. An optimal glucose concentration is essential to the in vitro culture of both oocytes and embryos [20, 21]; thus, elevated blood glucose can potentially impact early embryonic development through multiple pathways [22–25]. Accumulating evidence from basic studies indicates a correlation between blood glucose levels and embryonic euploidy; however, little clinical research has examined the impact of FPG levels on the embryonic euploidy rate in non-diabetic populations. Furthermore, the effectiveness of blood glucose management in reducing the risk of embryonic aneuploidy remains to be elucidated.

This study explored the association between elevated FPG levels and embryonic euploidy rates within non-diabetic populations. Furthermore, this study investigated the changes in embryonic euploidy rates induced by the optimization of FPG levels. We aimed to offer clinical practitioners insights that could enhance blood glucose management strategies during ART, thereby potentially augmenting the success of such reproductive interventions.

Methods

This study included a cohort of 985 women who underwent PGT-A at the Reproductive Center of Nanjing Women and Children's Healthcare Hospital between December 2017 and December 2023. In total, 4786 embryos from 985 oocyte retrieval cycles that met the biopsy criteria were included in the investigation. The exclusion criteria were the presence of chromosomal disorders in either partner, incomplete patient data, absence of PGT-A results, and FPG levels ≥ 7.0 mmol/L.

Patients were categorized into quartile groups (Q1–Q4) according to their FPG levels (from the lowest to the highest). The primary outcome was the embryonic euploidy rate. The secondary endpoints were the counts of euploid and aneuploid embryos, mosaic embryo tally, 2PN cleavage rates, metaphase II (MII) oocyte counts, and the numbers and proportions of high-quality blastocysts.

Control of ovarian stimulation

Controlled ovarian hyperstimulation was performed according to the gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) antagonist protocol as part of the ART process. On the third day of the menstrual cycle, gonadotropins (Gonal-F, Merck Serono, Italy) were initiated for ovarian stimulation at a starting dose ranging of 150–300 IU/day. The antagonist (Cetrotide, Merck Serono, France) was introduced at a dosage of 0.125–0.250 mg/day until the trigger day upon meeting one of the following criteria: the presence of at least one follicle measuring ≥ 14 mm in diameter; the largest follicle measuring > 12 mm with a serum estradiol (E2) level > 300 pg/mL; or a luteinizing hormone (LH) level > 10 U/L. Follicular development and serum hormone levels were meticulously monitored, and timely adjustments were made to the medication regimen. Ovulation was triggered when at least two follicles reached a diameter ≥ 18 mm by injecting a GnRH agonist (Decapeptyl, Ferring, Germany) at 0.2 mg and recombinant human choriogonadotropin (rhCG, Merck Serono, Italy) at 250 µg. Oocyte retrieval was performed 36 h post-trigger. All mature oocytes at MII were fertilized through intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI). Post-ICSI, fertilized oocytes were cultured individually in microdrops until the blastocyst stage was reached. Based on Gardner’s blastocyst grading system, trophectoderm biopsies were performed on blastocysts with grades 4 or higher and at least a score of B for either inner cell mass or trophectoderm. Blastocysts with a score exceeding 4BB were classified as high quality.

Fasting blood glucose determination

Fasting blood samples were collected during the pre-treatment examination before the initiation of ART. Patients were instructed to maintain their regular diet for the 3 days preceding the blood draw and to abstain from eating after midnight on the day of the draw.

PGT-A analysis

PGT-A was performed on all cycles by next-generation sequencing. DNA from all samples was amplified using the SurePlex DNA Amplification System (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). The amplified DNA was then screened for chromosomal aneuploidies using the VeriSeq PGS Kit on the MiSeq System (Illumina) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Based on the recommendations of the Preimplantation Genetic Diagnosis International Society [26], we categorized embryos according to the degree of mosaicism. Embryos with mosaicism ratios < 20% were considered euploid; those with ratios > 80% were considered aneuploid; and those with ratios between 20 and 80% were deemed mosaic.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 27.0.1 and R version 4.3.0. Continuous data were presented as median and interquartile range (IQR; 25th and 75th percentiles) and compared between groups using the Kruskal–Wallis test. Categorical variables were expressed as number and percentage and compared between groups using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Multiple linear regression models were used to estimate the regression coefficient (β) and the 95% confidence interval for the association between embryonic euploidy rates and FPG levels, while their correlation was graphically expressed using restricted cubic splines (RCS).

Results

The study cohort included 985 patients and 4786 biopsied embryos. The patients were stratified into quartile groups based on FPG level: Q1 (3.13–4.93 mmol/L, 248 patients, 1286 biopsied embryos), Q2 (4.94–5.2 mmol/L, 247 patients, 1263 biopsied embryos), Q3 (5.21–5.47 mmol/L, 234 patients, 1145 biopsied embryos), and Q4 (5.48–6.99 mmol/L, 246 patients, 1092 biopsied embryos). Table 1 presents the stratification of the baseline characteristics of all cycles. Significant intergroup differences were observed in maternal age, paternal age, maternal BMI, and serum triglyceride levels, which showed an increasing trend with higher FPG levels (p < 0.05). Notably, LH levels were significantly lower in the Q3 and Q4 groups than in the other groups (p < 0.05). Furthermore, anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) levels significantly decreased with increasing FPG levels (P < 0.05). Table 2 shows the cycle characteristics and comparisons of the quartile groups. Increased FPG levels were associated with a significant reduction in both embryonic euploidy rates and euploid counts (P < 0.001). The numbers of oocytes retrieved, MII oocyte counts, 2PN cleavage numbers, numbers of blastocysts, high-quality blastocyst counts, and numbers of biopsied embryos also significantly decreased with higher FPG levels (P < 0.05).

Figure 1 shows the RCS analysis of the non-linear relationship between FPG levels and embryonic euploidy rates. After adjusting for confounding factors, including maternal age, BMI, serum triglycerides, AMH, and the number of biopsied embryos, the embryonic euploidy rate decreased as FPG levels decreased below 4.57 mmol/L or exceeded 5.19 mmol/L. Thus, FPG levels may be inversely related to the rate of euploid embryos. Within the intermediate range of 4.57–5.19 mmol/L, embryonic euploidy rates increased, indicating the optimal FPG level range for maximizing the probability of euploidy.

Discussion

In this retrospective analysis, we observed negative correlations between FPG levels and several parameters, specifically, the embryonic euploidy rates, the number of MII oocytes, 2PN cleavage numbers, and numbers and proportions of high-quality blastocysts. However, these correlations did not reach statistical significance except the embryonic euploidy rates and the number of MII oocytes. Furthermore, we identified 4.57–5.19 mmol/L as the optimal FPG level range for maximizing embryonic euploidy rates, which means that euploidy rates were significantly decreased when FPG levels were above 5.19 mmol/L or below 4.57 mmol/L.

The primary sources of embryonic aneuploidy include abnormalities in oocyte meiosis, embryonic mitosis, and sperm meiosis. However, the predominant mechanism is erroneous homologous chromosome segregation during the first meiotic division of the oocyte [27]. A previous study showed that meiotic anomalies are significantly more prevalent in women aged > 35 years. Furthermore, in older women, maternal age is significantly and inversely correlated with embryonic euploidy rates [28], with aneuploidy rates in oocytes reaching 60–90% in older women aged > 35 years [7, 29]. Furthermore, the control of the risk factors of diabetes becomes more challenging with advancing age [30]. To eliminate the influence of confounding factors, such as age, we used multiple linear regression models and RCS that adjusted for maternal age and other potential confounding factors. The significant negative correlation between FPG and embryonic euploidy rates were observed at FPG levels outside the optimal range, underscoring the potential role of glycemic control in enhancing the success rates of ART.

Despite extensive research on the relationship between diabetes mellitus and reproductive health, studies on the relationship between maternal FPG levels and embryonic euploidy rates are limited. A previous study found no association between FPG levels and embryonic euploidy rates [31]. However, the findings were limited by a small sample size, with only 150 patients across 166 cycles. In contrast, our study, which included the first PGT-A cycles of 985 patients, analyzed a moderate sample size and excluded patients with diabetes mellitus, which enhanced the reliability of our findings.

The early development of embryos requires various nutrients, including carbohydrates. An appropriate glucose concentration is essential in the culture medium for in vitro-cultured embryos [21]. Current evidence indicates that oocyte development can be compromised by low glucose concentrations in follicular fluid [32, 33]. This corroborates our observation that embryonic euploidy rates decrease when FPG levels fall below 4.57 mmol/l.

Diabetes mellitus induces a systemic inflammatory response and oxidative stress, inducing the infiltration of immune cells and changes in the gut microbiota, thereby leading to metabolic dysregulation characterized by dyslipidemia, hyperinsulinemia or insulin resistance, and obesity, which in turn affect organs throughout the body [34–36]. Our results indicate that at higher FPG levels, even in patients without diabetes, embryonic euploidy rates significantly decrease, with an associated declining tendency in other indicators of embryo quality, such as MII oocyte counts, 2PN cleavage numbers, and the rate of high-quality blastocysts. This aligns with the findings of a previous review [13], which found that decreased levels of acidic transport proteins in oocytes, increased glucose and lipid contents, and elevated reactive oxygen species are associated with meiotic defects, organelle dysfunction, and epigenetic alterations. Our findings showed that, even in women who do not meet the diagnostic criteria for diabetes, minor variations in FPG levels can impact embryonic development and euploidy rates.

A study reported that preconception optimization of glycemic and insulinemic profiles in women with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is associated with improved clinical outcomes in ART interventions [37]. Although our study only included patients without diabetes, patients with impaired fasting glucose (IFG), defined as an FPG level ≥ 6.1 mmol/L, were not excluded. The pathogenesis of IFG is closely associated with insulin resistance [38], which is a typical clinical manifestation of PCOS [39]. Therefore, we hypothesize that these women with higher FPG levels may achieve higher embryonic euploidy rates and potentially better pregnancy outcomes through proper glycemic control.

As demonstrated in this study, the FPG level range most conducive to embryonic euploidy rates is 4.57–5.19 mmol/L. Monitoring maternal FPG levels during the ART process would help identify women who could benefit from stricter glycemic control. For women with elevated FPG levels that do not meet the criteria for diabetes, lifestyle modifications, such as dietary adjustments, increased physical activity, and possibly pharmacological interventions, may increase ART success rates.

This study is the first to explore the relationship between FPG levels and embryonic euploidy rates through a retrospective analysis of a moderately sized population of women without diabetes. In our study, we employed RCS, which not only effectively minimized multicollinearity in multivariate regression models but also visually described the relationship between FPG levels and embryonic euploidy rates, leading to the identification of an optimal blood glucose range, which uncovers a novel perspective on the relationship between maternal metabolic status and embryonic development. The findings of this study can profoundly improve ART treatment protocols and enhance the precision of embryo selection to ultimately increase the rates of ongoing pregnancy and live birth rates.

This study has some limitations. First, the study only analyzed the PGT-A results and did not evaluate the pregnancy outcomes. Second, this was a retrospective study with a comparatively moderate sample size. However, due to the heterogeneity in the enrolled population, further randomized controlled trials with larger datasets and stricter inclusion/exclusion criteria are required to validate the findings. Furthermore, the mechanism by which hyperglycemia leads to a decrease in embryonic euploidy rates was not investigated.

Conclusion

This study found an optimal FPG level range at which embryonic euploidy rates can be maximized in women without diabetes undergoing ART, offering empirical evidence of the clinical significance of blood glucose management. Our results highlight the necessity of monitoring FPG levels in aspiring mothers prior to initiating ART and underscores the value of personalized glycemic control strategies for those with elevated FPG levels.

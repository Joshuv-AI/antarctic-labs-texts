Real-World Outcomes of Faricimab in Patients with Highly Refractory Neovascular Age-Related Macular Degeneration in Portugal: The REVEAL Study
Authors: Filipe Mira, Sofia Donato, Fernanda Vaz, Filipa Madeira, Maria Picoto, António Mendes, João Beato, Sónia Torres-Costa, Carolina Pinto, Nuno Oliveira, Susana Feliciano, Miguel Lume, Rita Carreiro, Mariana Couto, Ângela Carneiro
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionManagement of highly refractory exudative neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) remains challenging when multiple antivascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) therapies fail to control the disease. Faricimab, through dual inhibition of VEGF-A and angiopoietin-2, may represent a potential therapeutic alternative for this difficult-to-treat population. However, real-world evidence in highly refractory nAMD remains limited and is lacking in the Portuguese population.MethodsThis multicenter, retrospective, real-world study included patients with nAMD highly refractory to previous anti-VEGF therapies who were treated with faricimab and followed for at least 6 months. Functional and anatomical outcomes were assessed, including changes in best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), central subfield thickness (CST), and evolution of intraretinal fluid (IRF) and subretinal fluid (SRF).ResultsA total of 46 eyes from 40 patients were included, characterized by a long-standing disease (median [25th percentile (P25); 75th percentile (P75)] duration, 5.0 [3.0; 6.0] years) and a high prior treatment burden (32.0 [24.0; 45.8] anti-VEGF injections and median treatment interval of 4.0 [4.0; 6.0] weeks). After faricimab, functional stability was observed (median change in BCVA of 0.0 [−0.1; 0.1] logMAR). Anatomical outcomes showed improvement, with a median reduction in CST of −12 [−58; 7] µm and complete SRF resolution in 47.8% of eyes. Median treatment intervals of 8.0 [6.0–10.0] weeks were achieved, representing a twofold increase in the median treatment interval.ConclusionsIn this real-world study of highly refractory nAMD, faricimab was associated with stabilization of functional outcomes and improvement in anatomical outcomes over short- to mid-term follow-up, alongside a twofold increase in treatment-interval length in routine clinical practice. Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study? Real-world evidence on the use of faricimab remains limited in patients with highly refractory neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) who have failed multiple antivascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) therapies, and such data are currently lacking in the Portuguese population.This study aimed to assess visual acuity and anatomical outcomes in patients with highly refractory nAMD who were treated with faricimab and followed for at least 6 months in a real-world setting. What was learned from this study? Faricimab was associated with stabilization of visual function and anatomical improvements over short- to mid-term follow-up, together with the increase in treatment intervals in routine clinical practice.These findings underscore the therapeutic value of targeting the angiopoietin-2 pathway in addition to VEGF blockade.

Introduction

Neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) is a leading cause of irreversible vision loss and blindness among older adults. It represents an advanced, exudative form of age-related macular degeneration, characterized by the growth of abnormal blood vessels beneath the macula. These fragile new vessels are prone to leakage of fluid and blood into the intraretinal and/or subretinal spaces, resulting in exudation, hemorrhage, and fibrotic scarring development. Without timely and appropriate treatment, these processes can lead to rapid and often severe loss of central vision [1].

Pathological macular neovascularization in nAMD is driven primarily by inflammatory and proangiogenic signaling pathways, with vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) playing a central role in angiogenesis, vascular permeability, and disease progression [2]. Anti-VEGF agents remain the current gold standard for the treatment of nAMD and have transformed disease management by effectively reducing exudation, controlling intraocular angiogenesis, and stabilizing or improving visual acuity [3]. Nevertheless, a considerable proportion of patients experience suboptimal responses over time or require very frequent injections to achieve disease control, underscoring the need for alternative therapeutic strategies that target pathogenic pathways beyond VEGF alone [4, 5].

Angiopoietin-2 (Ang-2) has emerged as a key mediator of vascular destabilization and inflammation, including within the ocular microvasculature [6–8]. In addition to VEGF, intraocular levels of Ang-2 are elevated in patients with nAMD [9]. Preclinical evidence from animal models demonstrates that combined inhibition of VEGF and Ang-2 results in greater reductions in neovascularization, vascular leakage, and inflammatory activity compared with inhibition of either pathway alone, underscoring the therapeutic potential of dual-pathway blockade to improve clinical outcomes [10, 11].

Faricimab is the first bispecific antibody to simultaneously inhibit VEGF-A and Ang-2, thereby targeting multiple pathophysiological mechanisms involved in nAMD [12, 13]. Phase 3 clinical trials demonstrated the efficacy, durability, and safety of faricimab in treatment-naive eyes with nAMD [14, 15]. Subsequent real-world studies have shown anatomical improvements and extended dosing intervals in both treatment-naive patients and in those who switched from a prior anti-VEGF therapy (reviewed by [16, 17]). However, evidence remains particularly limited for patients with highly refractory disease who have failed multiple anti-VEGF agents, and data on faricimab in the Portuguese population are currently lacking.

The REVEAL study aimed to evaluate functional, anatomical outcomes, and treatment interval length following at least 6 months of faricimab treatment in patients with nAMD refractory to multiple prior anti-VEGF therapies. This study provides the first real-world evidence of faricimab use in Portugal, demonstrating its effectiveness in a heterogeneous, hard-to-treat patient population.

Methods

Study Design and Setting

REVEAL was a retrospective, noninterventional, cross-sectional, multicenter study conducted in five major mainland Portuguese hospitals (Unidade Local de Saúde (ULS) Lisboa Ocidental, ULS São João, ULS Leiria, ULS Médio Tejo, ULS Santo António) in adult patients with refractory nAMD who had received faricimab treatment for at least 6 months and were recruited between April 2025 and November 2025. Data was retrospectively extracted at a single study visit from two predefined time points: “pre-switch,” defined as the last data prior to faricimab initiation; and “after faricimab,” defined as the most recent medical appointment at the time of data collection with best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) and/or optical coherence tomography (OCT) data, conducted at least 6 months after faricimab. Patients were defined as having highly refractory nAMD if they demonstrated persistent, worsening, or recurrent intraretinal fluid (IRF) or subretinal fluid (SRF) on spectral-domain OCT (SD-OCT) despite prior treatment with multiple on-label anti-VEGF agents.

Study Population

Patients were preselected under Portugal’s national early access mechanism (Autorizações de Utilização Excecional, regulated by INFARMED, I.P., in accordance with Decree-Law No. 176/2006). This program grants access to European Medicines Agency (EMA)-approved medicinal products that are not yet reimbursed for commercial use, and are intended for individuals with rare, severe, or otherwise debilitating conditions for which no therapeutic alternatives are available in the country.

Patients who met the following criteria were included: 1) signed informed consent; 2) adults (≥ 18 years) with a confirmed diagnosis of nAMD who received faricimab treatment for at least 6 months at the time of data collection; 3) refractoriness/poor response to, or contraindications for, all three on-label anti-VEGF agents (ranibizumab, aflibercept, and brolucizumab) as defined by INFARMED [18]; and 4) BCVA and/or OCT data at the pre- and postswitch time points.

Exclusion criteria were: 1) concurrent participation in another clinical trial involving an investigational drug or procedure at the time of the data collection period; 2) presence of macular neovascularization due to causes other than nAMD; 3) presence of active ocular inflammation or suspected/active ocular infection in either eye; 4) presence of any nonocular sight-threatening disease that could impact the primary study end point.

Data Sources and Variables

Secondary data were obtained retrospectively from hospital electronic medical records and included only routinely collected clinical data. Variables collected included patients’ demographics (year of birth, sex, race/ethnicity, and smoking history); medical and ophthalmic history (comorbidities, previous ocular conditions or surgeries in the study eye, family history of nAMD, previous macular degeneration treatments in the study eye, and total number of injections in the study eye); diagnostic information (eye under treatment, year of nAMD diagnosis, presence of disciform scarring or fibrosis); BCVA measurements (Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study [ETDRS], Snellen, or decimal charts, per local routine, converted to logarithm of the minimum angle resolution [logMAR] units for analysis) and OCT-derived parameters (central subfield thickness [CST], presence of IRF, and SRF); and treatment-related variables (time since last anti-VEGF treatment, date of faricimab initiation, treatment regimen, and interval between faricimab injections).

The study eye was defined as the eye treated with faricimab; when both eyes were treated, each eye was analyzed independently.

Statistical Analysis

Given the retrospective and descriptive design of the study, no formal sample size calculation was performed. The study included all eligible patients identified within the early access program.

All analyses were exploratory and primarily descriptive, without hypothesis testing or formal inferential modeling. Continuous variables are presented as mean, standard deviation (SD), median, 25th and 75th percentiles (P25 and P75), minimum and maximum values. Categorical variables are presented in absolute and relative frequencies.

Patient demographics, medical and ophthalmic history, ocular characteristics, and prior ocular treatments are presented as described above. Change in BCVA was calculated as the difference between the BCVA values at the last faricimab follow-up and at preswitch. CST change (µm) was calculated as the numeric difference between the last faricimab follow-up and preswitch values, while evolution of IRF and SRF was summarized according to the following categories: complete resolution (IRF/SRF present at preswitch and completely absent after faricimab), partial decrease (IRF/SRF present at preswitch and reduced in extent or volume after faricimab, but not completely resolved), stable persistence (IRF/SRF present at preswitch and remained present without meaningful change after faricimab), new onset (IRF/SRF absent at preswitch but present after faricimab), worsening (IRF/SRF present at preswitch and increased in extent or volume after faricimab), persistent absence (IRF/SRF absent at preswitch and remained absent after faricimab), as assessed per local clinical practice. BCVA and CST were analyzed as continuous outcomes, whereas IRF and SRF as categorical variables. Actual BCVA values at each visit were summarized according to local measurement methods (early treatment diabetic retinopathy study (ETDRS), Snellen decimal).

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.5.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria)[19].

Ethical Approval

This research was conducted in accordance with legal and regulatory requirements and with the Guidelines for Good Pharmacoepidemiology Practice of the International Society for Pharmacoepidemiology, and in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki of 1964, and its later amendments. Ethical approval was obtained from the institutional review board and/or independent ethics committee of each participating center [CES—Comissão de Ética para a Saúde Centro Hospitalar Universitário de São João (approval code 13/2025), Comissão de Ética Santo António/ICBAS (2025.049 (042-CAC/042-CE)), Comissão de Ética da Unidade Local de Saúde Região de Leiria (32/CEULSRL/2025), Comissão de Ética em Saúde da Unidade Local de Saúde do Médio Tejo, E.P.E. (04/25), and Comissão de Ética para a Saúde da Unidade Local de Saúde Lisboa Ocidental, E.P.E. (2025–59)]. All subjects provided informed consent to participate in the study.

For inclusion, patients must have shown refractoriness/poor response to, or contraindications to the three on-label anti-VEGF agents (ranibizumab, aflibercept, and brolucizumab). Before switching to faricimab, most eyes had previously been treated with aflibercept (95.7%) and ranibizumab (91.3%), followed by brolucizumab (67.4%) and bevacizumab (34.8%), while photodynamic therapy had been used in 4.3% of eyes.

The median total number of anti-VEGF injections administered before switching was 32.0 [24.0; 45.8] per study eye, and the median treatment interval was 4.0 [4.0; 6.0] weeks (Table 2). Most eyes (89.1%) had a history of ocular conditions, predominantly cataract, which accounted for the majority of previous ocular surgeries (85.4%) (Supplementary Table S1).

Outcomes Following Faricimab Switch

Faricimab treatment was initiated at a median age of 80.0 years [77.0; 84.0], after a median disease duration of 5.0 years [3.0; 6.0]. The same interval that was in place with the previous treatment was maintained for the first administration of faricimab; i.e., faricimab was administered at a median of 4.0 [4.0–6.0] weeks after the last injection of the previous anti-VEGF treatment. Most eyes (73.9%) were managed using a treat-and-extend regimen, and the median duration of the last interval between faricimab injections was 8.0 weeks [6.0; 10.0] (Table 2). After switching to faricimab, the proportion of eyes with treatment intervals of 7–9 weeks increased from 13.0% to 30.4%, and those with intervals of 10–12 weeks increased from 0% to 21.7% compared with preswitch (Fig. 1).

After switching to faricimab, IRF completely resolved in 6.5% of eyes, partially decreased in 10.9%, and remained stable in 19.6%, whereas 2.2% of eyes experienced worsening and 2.2% developed new onset IRF. IRF was persistently absent in 58.7% of eyes (Table 4).

Discussion

The REVEAL study evaluated real-world functional and anatomical outcomes after at least 6 months of faricimab treatment in patients with nAMD who were highly refractory to or had clinical contraindications to on label anti-VEGF therapies. Overall, BCVA remained stable and anatomical outcomes improved after the switch to faricimab. Improvements included a reduction in CST (ΔCST, −12 µm) and the resolution or stabilization of retinal fluid, with only 4.4% of eyes demonstrating worsening and/or new onset. Notably, complete resolution of SRF was achieved in 47.8% of eyes. Despite this improvement, a more precise alternative to the binary assessment of fluid presence or absence would be the manual or artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted segmentation of individual fluid compartments and retinal pigment epithelium detachment volumes, as employed in a previous study evaluating the switch to faricimab [20]. However, the multicenter and retrospective design of the present study limited access to AI-based segmentation tools.

Of note, the outcomes in the present study were achieved in a population with a long-standing disease course (median disease duration of 5.0 years) and a high prior treatment burden (median of 32 anti-VEGF injections before switching), who had received multiple previous anti-VEGF agents and were predominantly managed using a treat-and-extend regimen over a median follow-up of approximately 10 months. Importantly, the median duration of the last interval between faricimab injections was 8 weeks compared with 4 weeks under prior anti-VEGF therapy, representing a twofold increase in treatment-interval length.

To date, evidence on faricimab use in previously treated or highly refractory nAMD derived exclusively from real-world observational studies, as no randomized clinical trials have specifically evaluated this population. Systematic reviews of real-world data consistently report stabilization of visual acuity, anatomical improvement, particularly reductions in CST and retinal fluid, and the feasibility of extending treatment intervals following faricimab initiation or switching [16, 17]. Despite heterogeneity in baseline disease severity, prior treatment burden, and follow-up duration, the findings of the REVEAL study are consistent with previous real-world evidence.

Large multicenter real-world cohorts have provided external validation of the anatomical effectiveness of faricimab in treatment-experienced nAMD. In the TRUCKEE study, previously treated eyes demonstrated stabilization or slight improvement in visual acuity, alongside significant reductions in mean CST after one and three faricimab injections (−25.3 µm and −38.1 µm, respectively), although early interval extension remained limited, with treatment intervals remaining approximately 6 weeks [21]. These early findings were subsequently corroborated by Grimaldi et al. in a large multicenter cohort across the Swiss Retina Research Network, which reported 12-month outcomes in approximately 350 pretreated eyes with nAMD [22]. In that study, visual acuity was largely maintained, while anatomical outcomes improved consistently, with significant CST reductions (median reductions of approximately 30–40 µm across the cohort) and qualitative improvement in macular fluid status. Importantly, treatment intervals were extended in a substantial proportion of eyes, with many achieving ≥ 8-week dosing by 12 months, providing one of the strongest European multicenter real-world benchmarks for faricimab use in nAMD.

Notably, 1-year real-world studies in broader treatment-experienced populations, including large single-center cohorts, further support these findings. Ambati et al. reported stable visual acuity, a CST reduction of approximately 25 µm, and significant interval extension to a mean of 7.6 weeks at 1 year in a population with chronic nAMD and a high prior treatment burden [23]. Similarly, Janmohamed et al. observed significant anatomical improvement (Δ central macular thickness −27.5 µm) and marked extension of treatment intervals to a median of 8 weeks, with nearly half of eyes achieving ≥ 8-week dosing; however, this was accompanied by a modest decline in visual acuity over follow-up [24]. Collectively, these studies represent heterogeneous but generally less refractory populations with more favorable baseline characteristics than those included in the present study.

By contrast, the REVEAL study included highly refractory patients characterized by prolonged disease duration and failed response to multiple prior anti-VEGF therapies. Given the short treatment intervals with prior anti-VEGF therapy, undertreatment is unlikely. Although tachyphylaxis cannot be excluded, it is unlikely to be a predominant factor in a population with exposure to multiple anti-VEGF agents. Studies in highly refractory nAMD, provide the most appropriate comparator for interpreting the present findings at the severe end of the treatment-experienced spectrum. Smaller single-center studies focusing on eyes with long-standing disease and extensive prior treatment exposure, such as those by Ng et al. and Grimaldi et al. (2024), consistently reported stabilization of visual acuity with statistically significant reductions in CST of approximately 24 µm and 65 µm, respectively, alongside limited durability gains. In these cohorts, early anatomical responses were often observed, but sustained interval extension remained constrained [25, 26]. These observations are consistent with advanced disease chronicity rather than a lack of treatment effect, as faricimab demonstrated biological activity with measurable anatomical impact.

Treatment-experienced but not strictly refractory populations occupy an intermediate position along the disease spectrum. In a multicenter cohort of eyes requiring persistent 4-week dosing prior to switching, Sim et al. reported maintained visual acuity, modest reduction in CST (approximately 22 µm), and meaningful extension of treatment intervals to a mean of 6.9 weeks, with a substantial subset achieving ≥ 8- and ≥ 12-week dosing [27]. These findings suggest that greater gains in treatment durability are achievable when refractoriness criteria are less stringent.

Collectively, current real-world evidence suggests that attenuated anatomical gains in highly refractory nAMD reflect disease chronicity and treatment resistance rather than diminished biological activity of faricimab. Therefore, clinical benefits may be more pronounced in patients with less severe disease. Within this context, the REVEAL study provides evidence on the severe end of the treatment-experienced spectrum. By enrolling eyes with long-standing disease, high cumulative anti-VEGF exposure, and strict refractoriness criteria, this study demonstrates that switching to faricimab can stabilize visual acuity, achieve clinically relevant anatomical improvement, and extend treatment intervals.

Although the present study was not designed to investigate mechanisms of action, and neither VEGF nor Ang-2 activity was directly assessed, the response to treatment in patients highly refractory to multiple anti-VEGFs suggests that Ang-2 inhibition provided by faricimab may be clinically relevant. Under pathological conditions, Ang-2 upregulation contributes to vascular destabilization and increases susceptibility to VEGF-mediated angiogenesis and vascular permeability [6, 28]. In this context, modulation of the Ang-2/Tie-2 pathway may plausibly contribute to the observed anatomical improvements in these patients, although no mechanistic conclusions can be drawn from the present data.

Nonetheless, this study has important limitations that warrant consideration. First, owing to its retrospective design, and, in some cases, inclusion of cases with prolonged disease and follow-up, important clinical information was unavailable or inaccessible. In particular, the lack of detailed characterization of neovascular membranes precluded the classification of nAMD subtypes. Second, although previous studies have similarly compared pre- and postswitch outcomes, the absence of a control group limits the interpretability of the findings. Third, owing to its multicenter design, inter-center variability in imaging acquisition and visual acuity assessment cannot be excluded. Furthermore, unequal access to AI-assisted quantification tools across participating centers precluded their use in the present study. Finally, inter-eye correlation in patients receiving bilateral treatment was not accounted for in the analysis. While this may have introduced some degree of bias, the low proportion of bilaterally treated patients is expected to have minimal impact on the overall conclusions, given the descriptive nature of this study. In addition, the relatively small sample size and short- to mid-term follow-up preclude evaluation of long-term durability.

Yet, the REVEAL findings provide additional real-world evidence regarding the potential role of faricimab in the management of highly refractory nAMD, representing the first evaluation conducted within routine clinical practice in Portugal. The multicenter design reflects routine clinical practice across geographically diverse Portuguese centers and includes a patient population that is underrepresented in randomized clinical trials.

Importantly, the assessment of both functional and anatomical outcomes enabled a comprehensive evaluation of treatment response in nearly all included eyes. In this hard-to-treat population, faricimab was associated with maintenance of visual acuity and improvement in anatomical outcomes, including CST reduction and improved control of SRF. The ability to extend treatment intervals in routine practice may have potential clinical implications, including reduced treatment burden, improved clinical care capacity of health services, and possibly improved adherence in older patients with advanced disease.

Conclusions

The REVEAL study represents the first real-world evaluation of faricimab outcomes in patients with highly refractory nAMD in Portugal, contributing to the growing real-world evidence in this challenging patient population. Overall, faricimab was associated with stabilization of visual function and anatomical improvements over short- to mid-term follow-up, together with the increase in treatment intervals in routine clinical practice. These findings underscore the therapeutic value of targeting the Ang-2 pathway in addition to VEGF blockade, particularly in patients who have shown limited response to prior anti-VEGF monotherapy. The observed outcomes in this heavily pretreated, refractory population suggest that earlier use of dual Ang-2/VEGF inhibition may be of interest and could potentially be associated with improved clinical outcomes. Such an approach may have possible implications for reducing treatment burden and supporting healthcare system capacity, although further formal investigation is warranted.

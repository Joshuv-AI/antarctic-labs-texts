Heat transfer analysis on a stretching cylinder with Williamson hybrid nanofluid: effects of convective boundary conditions, Darcy-Forchheimer resistance, and Cattaneo-Christov heat Flux
Authors: Liaqat Ali, Amna Mariam, Nadeem Salamat, Sohaib Abdal, Sajjad Hussain
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study investigates the Darcy–Forchheimer stagnation point flow and heat transfer characteristics of Williamson hybrid nanofluid (Cu-TiO2 -Kerosene) over a stretching cylinder under the influence of convective boundary conditions and Cattaneo–Christov heat flux. The mathematical model is formulated by considering nonlinear heat source/sink, activation energy, Brownian motion, thermophoresis and porous medium effects. Appropriate similarity transformations are utilized to convert the governing partial differential equations into a system of nonlinear ordinary differential equations. The transformed equations are solved numerically using the MATLAB BVP4C technique. The novelty of the present work lies in the simultaneous investigation of Williamson hybrid nanofluid flow with Darcy–Forchheimer resistance, Cattaneo–Christov heat flux, nonlinear heat generation/absorption and stagnation point effects over a stretching cylinder. The obtained numerical results are validated through comparison with previously published studies, showing excellent agreement. The impacts of important physical parameters on velocity, temperature and concentration profiles are examined graphically and numerically for both nanofluid and hybrid nanofluid cases. The results reveal that increasing the Darcy–Forchheimer parameter and porosity parameter reduces the velocity profile due to enhanced resistance within the porous medium. The temperature profile increases significantly with higher Brownian motion, thermophoresis and Biot number parameters, while the Prandtl number reduces thermal boundary layer thickness. Concentration distribution decreases with increasing Schmidt number, whereas activation energy enhances concentration behavior. It is further observed that the hybrid nanofluid exhibits superior thermal performance compared to conventional nanofluid, making it more effective for advanced thermal management and industrial heat transfer applications.

Introduction

The rapid growth of industries like manufacturing, healthcare, and technology has led to a growing demand for medical devices, mechanical components, and machines designed to handle large volumes of chemicals while withstanding extreme temperature conditions. To meet these needs, researchers and engineers focus on understanding the thermal and physical properties of fluids. One key factor influencing the efficiency of heating and cooling systems is thermal conductivity. Many common heat-transfer fluids, such as water, ethylene glycol, and engine oil, have relatively low thermal conductivity, which limits their effectiveness in efficiently transporting heat. The thermal conductivity of several materials by incorporating nanoparticles. Nanofluids are created by mixing nanoparticles, which can take various forms like spheres, rods, rectangular blocks, or tubes, with a base fluid Choi et.al. [1]. These nanoparticles, which range in size from 1 to 100 nm, significantly enhance the heat transfer properties of fluid as compared to traditional fluids. Substances such as ethylene glycol (EG), engine oil (EO), polymers and kerosene oil have general importance for nanoparticles owing to their usage in various industries and applications.

Metal nanoparticles for instance copper (Cu), silver (Ag), and silicon (Si) metals together with china clay and silicon touch non-metallic oxide nanoparticles such as copper oxide (CuO) and aluminum oxide (Al2O3) have been the center of focus due to their enhanced properties. Heating together alone these nanoparticles also increase significantly the basic fluid's capacity to transport heat. Engineered nanofluids are of great interest within academia and different industries as the nanoparticle infuses greater bulk thermal conductivity and hence increases heat transport, exchange and management. Researchers are employing different materials like metals, semiconductors and polymers in a bid to synthesize nanoparticles with improved properties. From a theoretical and computation perspective Awan et al. [2] researched into the flow pattern of an Ellis hybrid nanofluid in an extended cylinder with consideration to nonlinear thermal radiation effect. This material is classified into three characteristics: stress, a range of shear flow from high to low, constant viscosity.

Hybrid phase slightly greater than nanophase, while the nanofluid's temperature is not as high as hybrid fluid. Khan et al. [3] investigated the irreversible theory and computational aspects of the Ellis hybrid nanofluid flow model, considering both smooth and non-smooth effects within the flow over a horizontally stretched porous cylinder. The effects of entropy on the flow of non-Newtonian fluid over a stretched cylinder were explored by Vijatha et al. [4]. The behavior of the hybrid nanofluid heat transfer fluid is influenced by various factors such as temperature, volume fraction, solid particle size etc. Yasir et al. [5] examined the effects of ohmic heating and non-thermal radiation on the movement of hybrid nanomaterials through a compressing and contracting permeable tube. They also demonstrated that adding nanoparticle volume fraction improved both the skin friction coefficient and the local Nusselt number for hybrid Al2O3 + Cu/H2O nanofluids. Waqas et al. [6] investigated the migration of a hybrid nanofluid over a stretched sheet, considering the impacts of thermal radiation and a variety of nanoparticles such as EG, Cu, aluminum oxide (Al2O3), and graphene oxide (GO), as well as the base fluid.

Haider et al. [7] investigated the flow of Williamson hybrid nanofluid over a cylinder stretched in a Darcy-Forchheimer porous medium. Equations were generated to incorporate the heat and mass transfer principles. They also considered the generation of heat and mass through convection with its impacts on chemical reactions. Yahya a et al. [8] examined the Williamson hybrid nanofluids along with magnetic forces, energy loss, as well as heat creation. The work of Bibi et al. [9] investigated the flow of Williamson fluid over a porous, stretched plate, factoring in the effects of magnetohydrodynamics and corrosion. The particles within the viscous fluid were assumed to be uniformly distributed and spherical. More recently, Alzu'bi et al. Mukhopadhyay et al. [13] investigated heat transfer from a drooping cylindrical vessel in a porous medium, focusing on the mixed convection of an incompressible viscous fluid. Jan et al. Mohanty et al. [15] investigated the absorption and transfer of a hybrid nanofluid, using water as the base fluid and a stretched cylinder form. Butt et al. [16] assessed the impact of magnetic fields on fluid flow and heat transfer in a hot horizontal cylinder with holes, placed in a moist porous material.

Ahmadi et al. [17] conducted an experimental study on Darcy-Forchheimer flows in Thermal Power Systems (TPS), focusing on porous materials heated to their boiling point. In many industrial, environmental, and engineering systems, including geothermal heating, chemical reactors, heat exchangers, and rock formations, convection via a porous media is essential. Hayat et al. [18] speculatively studied the convective flow of thick fluids beneath a flowing inclined stretching plate, while in another study, Hayat et al. [19] described the motion of a quadratic extensible sheet of variable thickness in the Darcy-Forchheimer regime of MHD Jeffrey nanofluid. Gohar et al. K. M. Nihaal et.al. [30] investigated computational as well as artificial neural network modeling of ternary nanofluid flow considering heat and mass transfer effects along with magnetohydrodynamics. [37] investigated the influence of velocity and thermal slip on MHD nanofluid flow past a stretching cylinder in the presence of viscous dissipation and Joule heating. A. Mishra et.al. [38] explored the effects of Arrhenius activation energy and pollutant discharge concentration in radiative hybrid nanofluid flow over an inclined plate. S. R. Mishra et.al. [39] simulated the SWCNTs-MWCNTs/water-based MHD hybrid nanofluid flow over an exponentially stretching sheet using the Xue model. M. Y. Malik et.al. [40] investigated nanofluid heat transfer in an inclined stretching cylinder under suction/injection and viscous dissipation effects.

The present study introduces a comprehensive mathematical investigation of Williamson hybrid nanofluid (Cu-TiO2 -Kerosene) flow over a stretching cylinder in the presence of Darcy–Forchheimer porous medium effects and stagnation point flow conditions. Although several previous studies have examined hybrid nanofluid transport over stretching geometries. Limited attention has been devoted to simultaneous incorporation of Williamson non-Newtonian behavior, Cattaneo–Christov heat flux model, nonlinear heat generation/absorption, activation energy, Brownian motion, thermophoresis and convective boundary conditions in a porous cylindrical configuration. The current analysis therefore extends the existing literature by combining these important physical mechanisms within a unified framework. A comparative analysis between conventional nanofluid and hybrid nanofluid is carried out to highlight the enhancement in thermal performance and transport characteristics. The transformed nonlinear system is solved numerically using the MATLAB BVP4C technique. The obtained results are validated with previously published studies by demonstrating the reliability of present numerical approach.

Methodology

The transformed nonlinear ordinary differential equations together with the corresponding boundary conditions were solved numerically using the built-in solver MATLAB BVP4C. This solver is based on the collocation finite difference technique and is highly effective for solving nonlinear boundary value problems. To simplify numerical implementation the higher-order differential equations were first converted into a system of first-order ordinary differential equations by introducing suitable dependent variables. The relative error tolerance was fixed at 10–6 which guarantees the accuracy and reliability. The nanoparticle concentration in the present investigation is represented through the volume fraction parameter incorporated in the mathematical formulation. The selected concentration range is 0.04. A flow chart is also given. The governing equations for the flow of Williamson hybrid nanofluids around a stretching cylinder in a porous medium are numerically solved using the BVP4C technique. Significance of skin friction under the effect of different parameters

Table 3 elaborates the influence of seven parameter values on skin friction in fluids thermal buoyancy (Gt), the Williamson parameter, stretching parameter, curvature parameter (M), concentration parameter (Gc), porous parameter (w), and Darcy–Forchheimer parameter (Fr) are explained in depth. Thermal buoyancy increases the flow and stability of fluids, so skin friction is greater with stronger dynamics from increased buoyancy. This causes the more turbulent and mixing and increases the fluid-interface effects and skin friction. Skin friction is also governed by the Williamson parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda $$\end{document} of elasticity and viscosity. Resistance to flow is mediated by a greater fluid response to deformation (in non-Newtonian fluids with elasticity being a big factor).

The skin friction increases with the stretching parameter because surface interactions and flow change. Note that flow stretching is involved, at least in some circumstances, in determining frictional behaviour when the surface is extended. This is mostly driven by increased shear forces at the fluid interface. The more the curvature parameter (M), the less friction you get on the skin. An increase in curvature leads to a more organized curved flow structure by altering the flow direction and reducing the fluid velocity. This reduction in turbulence is beneficial for lowering skin friction, which is why curvature plays a key role in optimizing fluid transport systems. The concentration parameter (Gc) also significantly influences the flow behavior, as higher concentration enhances viscosity and strengthens intermolecular interactions, thereby increasing skin friction at the fluid surface interface. In contrast, the Darcy–Forchheimer parameter (Fr) and porosity parameter (ω) tend to reduce skin friction in a porous medium. Greater porosity allows the fluid to pass more easily through the medium, resulting in reduced resistance and smoother flow behavior. The Darcy–Forchheimer parameter accounts for both viscous and inertial effects, which further helps in regulating flow characteristics and optimizing skin friction in porous structures.

Significance of Sherwood number under the effect of different parameters

As shown in Table 5, a number of factors can affect the Sherwood number, a crucial measure of mass transfer efficiency. Because flow is changing and gradients of velocity are reduced, the increase in the curvature parameter (M) generally results in a lower Sherwood number and less mass transport at the interface. Like this, increasing the thermophoresis parameter (Nt) reduces efficient mass transfer through change in particle distribution and non-uniform flow, which reduces the Sherwood number.

By incrementing Sc shows an accession in Sherwood number, which equates to better mass transfer as a result of more direct relationship between mass and viscous diffusion. It increases the energy barriers for mass transfer, the higher activation energy (E), causes smaller Sherwood number. Because it slows molecular movement and loss of efficiency. Higher Cattaneo-Christov parameter (λc) increases mass transfer efficiency at the interface and also raises Sherwood number by boosting the heat and mass transfer.

Velocity profile

The results presented in Fig. 3i demonstrate that the Darcy–Forchheimer parameter fr significantly decreases the velocity profile for both copper (Cu) nanofluid and hybrid (Cu − TiO2/Kerosene) nanofluid. Physically the Darcy–Forchheimer parameter represents the resistance offered by the porous medium to the fluid motion. An increase in fr enhances the inertial drag force within the porous structure, which opposes the movement of the fluid particles. Consequently, the momentum boundary layer thickness reduces and the fluid velocity declines noticeably. This reduction is more pronounced in the hybrid nanofluid because the addition of multiple nanoparticles increases the effective viscosity and internal friction of the fluid. Figure 3(ii) illustrates that increasing the concentration buoyancy parameter Gc causes a reduction in the velocity profile for both Cu nanofluid and hybrid (Cu − TiO2/Kerosene) nanofluid. The physical reason behind this behavior is that the concentration buoyancy force generates density variations within the fluid. It creates an opposing force against primary fluid motion. As the value of Gc increases, the concentration-induced resistance becomes stronger. It results in a decline in momentum transfer and consequently a lower velocity profile. The hybrid nanofluid exhibits a comparatively greater reduction in velocity because the interaction between Cu and TiO2 nanoparticles. Conclusion

The study reveals that buoyancy, curvature, and Williamson parameters enhance the fluid velocity. While Darcy–Forchheimer and stagnation point parameters reduce it. Thermal and concentration profiles are strongly influenced by curvature, Prandtl number, Schmidt number, and activation energy.

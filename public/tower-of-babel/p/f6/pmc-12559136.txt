Strand-specific functions of miR-301a-3p and -5p drive opposing roles in pancreatic cancer via ferroptosis and pyroptosis
Authors: Yuhang Chen, Suoyi Dai, Wenxun Cai, Jiangang Zhao, Fenglin Zhang, Chien-Shan Cheng, Lianyu Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma is among the most lethal malignancies, underscoring the urgent need for a deepened molecular insights and targeted strategies. While microRNAs (miRNAs) are known to regulate key oncogenic pathways in PDAC, most studies overlook the functional heterogeneity between the 3p and 5p strands derived from the same miRNA precursor. Here, we systematically investigate the strand-specific roles of miR-301a-3p and miR-301a-5p in PDAC progression. Using a multidimensional approach, including clinical tissue-based FISH, serum analysis, cell-based assays, and xenograft models, we revealed a consistent pattern of miR-301a-3p upregulation and miR-301a-5p downregulation in PDAC tissues and fluids. Importantly, the ratio of 5p to 3p is negatively correlated with TNM stage, and its combination with CA19-9 significantly improves prognostic prediction. Mechanically, the two strands exert opposing effects on tumor progression via distinct cell death pathways. miR-301a-3p promotes tumorigenesis by targeting ACSL4 to suppress ferroptosis, while miR-301a-5p induces pyroptosis by activating NF-κB pathway through CEACAM6-TNFR1 axis. These findings challenge the prevailing “dominant strand” paradigm and uncover a previously unrecognized antagonism between miRNA strands. By highlighting the risks of indiscriminate miRNA targeting, our work supports the development of strand-specific diagnostic and therapeutic strategies in PDAC. Introduction

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, often referred to as PDAC, belongs to the category of digestive system tumors and shows strong aggressiveness. Its survival rate over a five-year period stays under the 10% mark(Mizrahi et al. 2020). Its poor early detection, complex microenvironment, and therapeutic resistance underscore the urgent need to identify novel molecular mechanisms and therapeutic targets(Farhangnia et al. 2024; Han et al. 2020; Toit-Thompson et al. 2025). MicroRNAs (miRNAs), small non-coding RNAs of about 18–25 nucleotides, regulate target genes post-transcriptionally and play crucial roles in proliferation, apoptosis, and metabolism processes(Diener et al. 2022; Ferragut Cardoso et al. 2021; Bayat and Sadri Nahand 2024). Dysregulated miRNAs significantly contribute to PDAC pathogenesis by affecting key pathways including KRAS(Chhichholiya et al. xxxx), TP53(McCubrey et al. xxxx), and TGF-β(Ellakwa et al. 2024), highlighting their potential in molecular classification(Fenu et al. 2024), diagnostics, and targeted therapy. Although miRNA-based biomarkers and therapies (e.g., MRX34)(Wu et al. 2023; Hong et al. 2020) and nanoparticle-delivered miRNAs(Li et al. 2022) have entered clinical evaluation, challenges such as heterogeneity, delivery efficiency, and off-target effects remain.

While miRNA maturation is a conserved multi-step process yielding both 5p and 3p strands from the same precursor(Kim et al. 2024; Alarcón et al. 2015; Wijk xxxx), many studies do not distinguish these functionally distinct isoforms(Cocks et al. xxxx). This oversight is problematic: although one strand is often dominant, both can be retained and exert independent—sometimes opposing—biological effects(Ito et al. 2021). For instance, miR-455-3p and −5p jointly regulate HIF-2α in cartilage homeostasis, whereas miR-338-3p and −5p play antagonistic roles in different cancers(Louis et al. 2023; Sun and Zhu 2020). Such functional divergence arises from distinct seed sequences leading to different target sets. Ignoring strand-specificity risks therapeutic failure, e.g., inhibiting one strand could inadvertently enhance the other’s oncogenic role. Moreover, spatial and temporal heterogeneity in expression—across tissues, bodily fluids, or microenvironments—further complicates functional interpretation and clinical application(Zhang et al. 2019; Becker et al. 2023).

Our team previously identified miR-301a as an oncogenic miRNA in PDAC, targeting BIM and promoting proliferation(Chen et al. 2012, 2024). However, existing studies have largely overlooked the potential functional differences between miR-301a-3p and −5p. To address this, we systematically investigated both strands in PDAC. Our integrated approach included spatial expression analysis, liquid biopsy validation, and mechanistic studies across cell models. We linked strand-specific expression to clinical outcomes and developed a combined CA19-9/miRNA prognostic model.

We found that miR-301a-3p and −5p are differentially expressed and exert opposing functions: miR-301a-3p promotes tumor growth by inhibiting ferroptosis through ACSL4 targeting, whereas miR-301a-5p suppresses tumors by inducing pyroptosis via NF-κB activation. This bidirectional regulation of cell death pathways underscores the necessity of strand-specific miRNA targeting for precise therapy. By moving beyond the conventional “single-strand” paradigm, our work provides new insights for developing chain-specific diagnostic and therapeutic strategies in PDAC.

Methods

Cells and treatments

In this research, several types of PDAC cell lines were involved, specifically AsPC-1, BxPC-3, Capan-1, MIA PaCa-2, PANC-1, and SW 1990. Additionally, a human immortalized pancreatic ductal epithelial cell line, namely HPDE6-C7, was also included in the study. Every one of these cell lines was bought from the American Type Culture Collection, which is commonly known as ATCC. The cells used in this study were cultured in their respective recommended media: The AsPC-1 and BxPC-3 cell lines were cultured using RPMI-1640 medium; MIA PaCa-2, PANC-1, HPDE6-C7, and SW1990 were cultured using DMEM medium; And Capan-1 was cultured using IMDM medium. Every type of medium used was added with 10% fetal bovine serum—its catalog number is 10,099-141C, sourced from Gibco in the USA, and commonly abbreviated as FBS. In addition, each medium also contained 1% penicillin/streptomycin, which has a catalog number of 15,140–122 and is also provided by Gibco, USA. Cell cultures were maintained in a CO2 incubator under a humidity-controlled condition at 37℃ with 5% CO2. Routine mycoplasma contamination testing was performed on the cells, and cell line authentication was conducted using short tandem repeat (STR) analysis.

Cell transfection

Cells were first seeded into 6-well plates, with each well containing cells at a density of 0.6 × 10^6. After that, these plates were placed in an environment with 5% CO₂ and maintained at 37℃ for an overnight incubation period until they reached approximately 80% confluence, in preparation for DNA transfection. For each well, 3 µg of plasmid DNA was delivered into the cells, with Lipofectamine 3000 used as the transfection reagent (L3000015; USA), following the manufacturer's instructions.

Reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction

To extract RNA from the cells, 1 mL of RNAiso Plus reagent was added to them. For RNA extraction from plasma, freshly obtained blood samples were put through centrifugation at 3000 g for a 15-min duration, with the process carried out at 4 °C. After centrifugation, the upper-layer plasma was collected for subsequent use. The plasma was then mixed with Trizol at a 1:8 ratio. After thorough mixing, the supernatant was discarded following centrifugation. Total RNA went through precipitation and purification steps, with a 75% ethanol solution used for this process. Following that, the purified RNA was prepared to undergo reverse transcription. The mRNA expression levels were quantified using the 2−ΔΔCt method and normalized to the expression levels of either β-actin or U6. The specific primer sequences employed in this study are provided in Table S1.

In vitro assays for measuring cell proliferation

Cells were placed into 96-well plates, with each well holding cells at a density of 3 × 103. Afterward, a CCK8 solution was added, and the plates were incubated in the dark for two hours. After that, the absorbance value was measured, with the detection wavelength set at 450 nm. In the clonogenic assay, cells were planted in a 6-well plate (Corning) at a density of 1,000 cells/well, followed by a two-week cultivation period. Once the incubation period ended, the cells were fixed using 4% paraformaldehyde and then stained with 0.3% crystal violet. Colonies made up of over 50 cells were counted with the help of a light microscope.

Cell migration assessment

To evaluate cellular migratory potential, a standardized Transwell system (8.0 μm pore membrane, Corning #3422) was employed. Cells were adjusted to 1 × 105 cells/mL in serum-deprived medium and aliquoted (200 μL) into upper compartments. The chemoattractant gradient was established by filling lower chambers with 800 μL medium containing 10% FBS. After the cells had been incubated for 24 h, the non-migrated cells that remained on the surface of the apical membrane were removed manually. This clearing process was done using cotton swabs dampened with PBS. Transmigrated cells adherent to the basal membrane underwent fixation with 4% paraformaldehyde (Sigma-Aldrich) for 30 min and subsequent crystal violet staining (2 h, RT) for quantitative analysis.

Cell invasion analysis

For invasion quantification, growth factor-reduced Matrigel (Corning) was prepared by diluting 1:8 in a chilled serum-free medium. The matrix solution was uniformly coated onto Transwell filters and polymerized at 37 °C for 2 h. Post-gelation, the residual liquid was aspirated before seeding 200 μL serum-starved cell suspensions into coated inserts, with lower chambers containing 800 μL complete medium. Special attention was given to avoid bubble formation during chamber assembly. After 24 h of culture, apical surface cells were removed via PBS-assisted swab abrasion. Invaded cells on the basolateral side were processed identically to migration assays through paraformaldehyde fixation and crystal violet counterstaining.

Probe in situ hybridization (FISH) dyeing

After repairing and digesting the paraffin sections, the pre-hybridization solution was added to the sections in a dropwise manner, after which the sections were incubated at 37 °C for a duration of 1 h. Subsequently, the pre-hybridization solution was carefully removed, and a hybridization solution containing the specific probe was applied to the sections in a dropwise manner. The tissue sections were then transferred to a hybridization incubator and subjected to overnight incubation. Following hybridization, the tissue sections underwent washing, and a corresponding branch probe was added to perform further hybridization. Following this step, the corresponding signal probe was introduced. Finally, the nuclei were counterstained with DAPI for microscopic observation. The probe sequences are:

Probe hsa-mir301a-3p: (5’−3’) GCTTTGACAATACTATTGCACTG;

Probe hsa-mir301a-5p: (5’−3’) AGTAGTGCAATAAAGTCAGAGC;

Western blotting

The immunoblotting assay was performed according to established protocols outlined in previous studies. In brief, cells were broken down on ice, and this process lasted 30 min using RIPA buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors and phosphatase inhibitors. The sample was spun at 12,500 rpm for 15 min, with the temperature kept at 4℃. Once this step was done, the supernatant was gathered. Then, the same quantity of protein was put onto 10% PAGE gels to separate them. After that, the proteins were moved over to a PVDF membrane. The membrane went through a blocking process using 5% BSA. After that, it was incubated with antibodies; these antibodies targeted the target proteins and were used at the stated dilutions:

Flow cytometry analysis of apoptosis

The cells were detached using 0.25% trypsin without EDTA and re-suspended in PBS to achieve a single-cell suspension. They were then incubated with 647 A Annexin V/PI reagent (Share-bio, SB-Y6026, China) at RT for 20 min, kept away from light. After incubation, the cells were rinsed three times using PBS, then resuspended in PBS. After that, flow cytometry was used to assess the cells.

Dual-luciferase reporter gene detection method

For the study of hsa-miR-301a-3p and ACSL4, GenePharma (Shanghai, China) designed and produced plasmids containing the wild-type ACSL4 response element for hsa-miR-301a-3p, as well as the corresponding mutant-type plasmid. These plasmid DNAs were co-transfected into cells along with either the hsa-miR-301a-3p mimics plasmid or the negative control mimics plasmid. Following the manufacturer's protocol, after 48 h, luciferase activity was measured with a Double-Luciferase Reporter Assay Kit (Promega, E1910, USA).

To investigate hsa-miR-301a-5p and CEACAM6, GenePharma (Shanghai, China) developed plasmids containing the wild-type CEACAM6 response element for hsa-miR-301a-5p, as well as the corresponding mutant-type CEACAM6 plasmid. These plasmid DNAs were co-transfected into cells along with either the miR-301a-5p mimics plasmid or nc plasmid. Following the manufacturer's guidelines, luciferase activity was measured after 48 h using a Double-Luciferase Reporter Assay Kit (Promega, E1910, USA).

Immunohistochemical staining

In summary, paraffin-embedded tissue sections were subjected to a heat treatment at 65 °C for 2 h, followed by a deparaffinization process that involved three treatments with xylene over 30 min and then rehydrated using a graded ethanol series for another 30 min. Antigen retrieval was achieved through a 20-min exposure to high heat and pressure in EDTA buffer (pH 9.0). After blocking endogenous peroxidase activity and performing tissue blocking, the sections were incubated at room temperature for 1 h with primary antibodies against ACSL4 (1:500, A20414, Abclonal, China), GPX4 (1:500, A25009, Abclonal, China), SLC7A11 (1:500, A25302, Abclonal, China), ASC (1:500, A22046, Abclonal, China), NLRP3 (1:200, 68,102–1-Ig, Proteintech, China), CEACAM6 (1:200, #DF8029, Affinity Biosciences, China), and Ki67 (1:500, 28,074–1-AP, Proteintech, China). Following this, biotin-conjugated secondary antibodies were applied for 45 min. The immunohistochemistry (IHC) procedure was finalized with DAB (3,3′-diaminobenzidine) (Genetech, China) staining and hematoxylin counterstaining. The final IHC scores, based on both staining percentage and intensity, were independently assessed by two experienced pathologists.

Plasmid and lentivirus production

The plasmids were obtained from TSINGKE (Shanghai, China) and GenePharma (Shanghai, China). The expression vector for overexpressing ACSL4 and CEACAM6 was pLV4ltr-PGK-ZsGreen(2A)PURO-CMV. The vectors for hsa-miR-301a-3p and hsa-miR-301a-5p mimics, as well as their negative controls and inhibitors, were LV3(H1/GFP&Puro). Lentiviral particles for both knockdown and overexpression were generated by co-transfecting the lentiviral constructs with pMD2.G and psPAX2 into HEK-293 T cells. The resulting viral supernatants were harvested and applied to tumor cells, followed by the addition of puromycin for selection.

Isolation of nuclear protein and cytoplasmic protein

Remove the cell supernatant and wash the cells with PBS. Scrape the cells off, centrifuge, and retain the cell pellet for subsequent use. For each 20 µl of cell pellet, add 200 µl of cytoplasmic protein extraction reagent A supplemented with PMSF (Beyotime, P0028, China). Vortex vigorously for 5 s to ensure the cell pellet is fully suspended and dispersed, then incubate on ice for 10–15 min. Next, add 10 µl of cytoplasmic protein extraction reagent B, vortex for an additional 5 s, and place it back on ice for 1 min. Centrifuge the mixture at 4ºC for 5 min at 12,000–16,000 g. Carefully transfer the supernatant to a pre-cooled plastic tube; this represents the extracted cytoplasmic protein. For the remaining pellet, remove the supernatant completely and add 50 µl of nuclear protein extraction reagent with PMSF. Vortex vigorously for 15–30 s to fully suspend and disperse the cell pellet, then return to the ice bath. Continue to vortex vigorously every 1–2 min for a total of 30 min. Finally, centrifuge at 4ºC for 10 min at 12,000–16,000 g, and transfer the supernatant to a pre-cooled plastic tube; this is the extracted nuclear protein.

MDA assay

PDAC cells were initially seeded in 6-well cell culture plates (Corning). BCA protein assay kit (beyotime) and lipid peroxidation MDA assay kit (Beyotime, S0131M, China) were used to measure the concentration of protein and MDA level respectively after cell homogenization. Following the acquisition of MDA content, the MDA-to-protein concentration ratio was computed.

Immunofluorescence

After washing the cells with PBS, they were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (Biosharp, BL539A, China) for 15 min. Following this, the cells were rinsed three times with PBS and permeabilized using 0.4% Triton X-100. After another three washes with PBS, the cells were blocked with 2% BSA (Beyotime, ST023, China) for 30 min, followed by two additional PBS washes. The cells were then incubated overnight at 4 °C with a monoclonal antibody and subsequently washed three times with PBS. Afterward, secondary antibodies were added and incubated at room temperature for one hour. A DAPI solution (0.5 µg/ml) was then applied for 10 min, and excess DAPI was washed off with PBS. Finally, the cells were covered with sealing tablets and observed under a confocal microscope (Olympus) for imaging.

Co-immunoprecipitation assays (Co-IP)

HEK293T cells were lysed on ice for 30 min using RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, P0013B, China). The cell lysate was then centrifuged at 12,500 g for 15 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was incubated overnight at 4 °C with anti-Flag (1:5000, 66,008–4-Ig, Proteintech, China) or anti-HA (1:5000, 51,064–2-AP, Proteintech, China) conjugated A/G magnetic beads (Beyotime, P2108, China). Following incubation, the beads were washed with a high-salt solution, and the samples were boiled in SDS loading buffer for 10 min before loading onto an SDS-PAGE gel. The samples were then prepared for Western blot analysis.

Detection of caspase-1 activity

The standard dilution solution was prepared according to the protocol (Beyotime, C1102, China), the supplied pNA was diluted to different concentrations of the standard product, and the pNA standard curve was established. The A405 of each concentration is measured and a standard curve of the linear relationship between pNA concentration and A405 is drawn. Cell precipitates of different treatments were collected, the lysate was added at a ratio of 100 µl per 2 million cells, centrifuged at 4ºC 16000–20000 g for 10–15 min, and the supernatant was transferred to a centrifuge tube pre-cooled in an ice bath. Ac-YVAD-pNA (2 mM) was added according to the reference system and incubated at 37ºC for 60–120 min. After that, absorbance was detected with an enzyme-labeled instrument. The enzyme activity of caspase 1 was calculated by comparing the standard curve of pNA.

Total ROS level assay

The DCFH-DA probe (Beyotime, S0033S, China) was utilized to measure total ROS levels in PDAC cells. Following 48 h of incubation in 6-well cell culture plates (Corning), the cell lines were washed with PBS and stained with a 10 μmol/L DCFH-DA solution at 37 °C for 20 min. Fluorescence intensity was then assessed using flow cytometry (Beckman). It is essential to conduct the entire procedure in darkness to prevent light exposure.

RNA-seq analysis

Total RNA from SW1990 cells transfected with hsa-miR-301a-5p and its negative control (hsa-miR-301a-5p nc) was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Takara). The analysis was performed in triplicate for each cell type. RNA quality and quantity were rigorously evaluated, and only samples with an RNA Integrity Number (RIN) greater than seven were selected for subsequent processing. RNA sequencing was conducted using the Illumina HiSeq 4000 Sequencing System. The analysis focused on assessing the fragments per kilobase of exon per million fragments mapped (FPKM) for each gene after data preprocessing and collection.

Animal models

Male nude mice, aged five to six weeks, were initially obtained from Shanghai Youshu Laboratory (Shanghai, China). Approximately 5 × 10^6 PANC1 (hsa-miR-301a-3p) and SW1990 (hsa-miR-301a-5p) cells were then subcutaneously injected into each mouse at the right armpit site. Once the tumors became palpable, measurements of tumor size and body weight were taken every three days. Tumor volume was calculated using the formula: length × width2 × 0.53. After four weeks of tumor growth, the specimens were surgically removed, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (Biosharp, BL539A, China), and subsequently processed for immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining. This study protocol received approval from Youshu Life Technology (Shanghai) Co., LTD (YS-m202411004, YS-m202411005).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism v. 9.01 (GraphPad Software). Categorical variables were assessed with the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test, while continuous variables were analyzed using Student’s t-test for paired samples. Results are expressed as means ± SEM from three independent experiments, each conducted in duplicate. All statistical tests were two-sided, with significance levels indicated as follows: * for P < 0.05, ** for P < 0.01, *** for P < 0.001, and **** for P < 0.0001, reflecting the degree of significance.

Results

The multi-dimensional expression profiles and prognostic significance of hsa-miR-301a-3p and hsa-miR-301a-5p in pancreatic cancer

To resolve the functional differentiation of the same precursor miRNA double-stranded in pancreatic cancer, we systematically compared the multi-dimensional expression profiles of hsa-miR-301a-3p and hsa-miR-301a-5p. Firstly, significant spatial expression antagonism was observed: FISH analysis showed that compared with normal pancreatic tissues (Fig. 1A), the expression of miR-301a-3p was significantly increased in cancer tissues, while miR-301a-5p was decreased (Fig. 1B); More importantly, the expression intensity of the double-strand shows a reverse evolution trend with the progression of TNM stage (3p increases, 5p decreases), suggesting that the disruption of the double-strand balance is closely related to the malignant progression of tumors (Fig. 1C). This antagonistic pattern was directly translated into chain-specific prognostic value: survival analysis revealed that the median survival period in the miR-301a-3p high-expression group was shortened to 9.2 months, while that in the miR-301a-5p high-expression group was prolonged to 18.5 months (Fig. 1D); The model combining CA19-9 and double-strand expression significantly improved the predictive efficiency compared with the individual indicators (Fig. 1E). The further constructed nomogram integrated indicators such as TNM stage, CA19-9 and single-strand expression to achieve the quantification of individualized survival probability (Fig. 1F). Further cross-dimensional verification revealed that there were also phenomena of elevated miR-301a-3p and decreased miR-301a-5p in the patient's plasma (Fig. 1G) and cell models (Fig. 1H). Based on this antagonistic expression profile, for cell function experiments, MIA PaCa-2 with high expression of miR-301a-3p was selected for knockdown, and PANC-1 with low expression was selected for overexpression. However, miR-301a-5p was knockdown in the highly expressed PANC-1 and overexpressed in the low-expressed SW1990, achieving complementary verification of the double-strand function. The effects of hsa-miR-301a-3p and hsa-miR-301a-5p on the biological functions of pancreatic cancer cells

To clarify the functional antagonism of the double chains of the same precursor miRNA, we systematically verified the biological effects of hsa-miR-301a-3p and hsa-miR-301a-5p in the cell model. Combining cell isolation experiments (Supplementary Fig. 5) and tissue microarray analysis, the nuclear enrichment of miR-301a-3p and miR-301a-5p was fully demonstrated from both the cell model and human tissue perspectives. Specifically, after exogenous overexpression or knockdown, qRT-PCR analysis of isolated RNA showed that the most significant changes in miR-301a-3p and miR-301a-5p levels occurred in the nuclear part, while the changes observed in the cytoplasm were relatively small. This indicates the main nuclear sites and shows that the nucleus is the main site of their functional activities. Particularly significantly, the double strands show completely opposite effects in the malignant phenotype of cells: in terms of proliferation ability, colony formation (Fig. 2B, 2H) and CCK-8 (Fig. 2D, 2 J) experiments confirmed that miR-301a-3p significantly enhanced the ability of clone formation and cell viability, while miR-301a-5p inhibited the above phenotype; At the levels of migration (Fig. 2E, 2 K) and invasion (Fig. 2F, 2L), the transwell assay showed that miR-301a-3p promoted cell migration and invasion, while miR-301a-5p exhibited an inhibitory effect (Supplementary Fig. 4 A, 4B). These functional differences directly established the pro-cancer nature of miR-301a-3p and the tumor suppressor role of miR-301a-5p, perfectly explaining the reverse expression patterns of the two in clinical samples.

To explore the effects of hsa-miR-301a-3p and hsa-miR-301a-5p on the modes of cell death

Given the core position of the cell death pathway in tumor regulation, we explored whether the double-chain affects proliferation by differentiating the regulation of apoptosis, pyroptosis, ferroptosis and autophagy. Key findings revealed functional antagonism: hsa-miR-301a-3p significantly inhibited ferroptosis. Western blot showed that the expression of ferroptosis-related proteins was down-regulated (Fig. 3A), accompanied by decreased ROS (Fig. 3C) and MDA (Fig. 3D), and ferroptosis inducers could reverse its proliferative effect (Supplementary Fig. 3 A, 4 C); However, hsa-miR-301a-5p specifically activates pyroptosis: the activity of caspase-1 increases (Fig. 3K), and cell vacuolation occurs (Fig. 3M). WB (Fig. 3I) and GSDMD immunofluorescence (Fig. 3L) also prove the activation of the pyroptosis pathway, while pyroptosis inhibitors eliminate its tumor suppressor effect (Supplementary Fig. 3B, 4D). Double-strand has no significant effect on apoptosis/autophagy (Supplementary Fig. 2), indicating that it regulates proliferation through completely different death mechanisms: 3p promotes tumor growth by inhibiting ferroptosis, while 5p exerts tumor suppressor function by inducing pyroptosis.

Hsa-miR-301a-3p and Hsa-miR-301a-5p regulate the death process of tumor cells by targeting different downstream genes and signaling pathways

Hsa-miR-301a-3p regulates ferroptosis in pancreatic cancer by targeting ACSL4

To analyze the target mechanism of hsa-miR-301a-3p regulating ferroptosis, we identified ACSL4 as the key target gene through multi-database prediction. The chain of experimental evidence reveals that ACSL4 is highly expressed in pancreatic cancer tissues (Fig. 4A) and cell lines (Fig. 4B). Interestingly, the expression of ACSL4 is the highest in PANC1 and the lowest in MIA, this finding is consistent with the negative correlation between miRNA and the levels of its downstream target genes. To verify the clinical relevance between miR-301a-3p and its predicted target gene ACSL4 in pancreatic cancer, we conducted analyses at two levels: population samples and public databases. As shown in Fig. 4C, the expression levels of miR-301a-3p and ACSL4 exhibited opposite trends in the plasma of normal individuals, patients with early-stage PDAC, and patients with advanced PDAC: with disease progression, miR-301a-3p expression gradually increased, while ACSL4 mRNA levels significantly decreased. Furthermore, we performed scatter plot analysis on individual data from 40 PDAC patients, and the results showed a significant negative correlation in expression levels between miR-301a-3p and ACSL4 among individuals (Fig. 4D). To expand the sample validation range, we conducted bioinformatics analysis on pancreatic cancer datasets through the ENCORI database, and the results also indicated a significant negative correlation between ACSL4 mRNA and miR-301a-3p expression (Fig. 4E). These three sets of results collectively provide strong support for the negative regulatory relationship of miR-301a-3p on ACSL4 from both clinical samples and public data perspectives. Further cellular experiments demonstrated that: MiR-301a-3p can directly inhibit the expression of ACSL4: mimics treatment reduced both the mRNA (Fig. 4F) and protein levels (Fig. 4G) of ACSL4, and gradient transfection showed dose-dependent inhibition (Fig. 4I). The dual-luciferase reporter gene was confirmed to bind to ACSL4 3’UTR (Fig. 4N); ACSL4 supplementation reversed the inhibition of ferroptosis by miR-301a-3p: the expression of ferroptosis-related proteins rebounded (Fig. 4K), and the levels of ROS and MDA recovered (Fig. 4L, 4Q). After performing a rescue experiment targeting ACSL4, it was found that the proliferative effect of hsa-miR-301a-3p was inhibited, indicating that the proliferative effect of hsa-miR-301a-3p is ACSL4-dependent (Supplementary Fig. 3 C and Supplementary Fig. 4E). This axis confirmed that miR-301a-3p inhibits ferroptosis by targeting ACSL4 to drive tumor progression.

Hsa-miR-301a-5p regulates pyroptosis of pancreatic cancer by targeting CEACAM6

To deeply analyze the molecular mechanism by which hsa-miR-301a-5p regulates pyroptosis in pancreatic cancer cells, first, we performed RNA sequencing analysis on cells transfected with hsa-miR-301a-5p mimics and their controls at the cellular level (Fig. 5A, B). This analysis confirmed that the overexpression of hsa-miR-301a-5p significantly altered the expression profiles of a large number of mRNAs in pancreatic cancer cells. In order to accurately identify the direct downstream targets of hsa-miR-301a-5p from these differentially expressed genes, we integrated the target gene prediction results from the two major databases, MiRWalk and TargetScan, and obtained a total of 2,124 candidate target genes. Further, these predicted target genes were intersected with the significantly downregulated genes in RNA-seq. Through Venn diagram analysis (Fig. 5C), we screened out 92 overlapping candidate target genes with high confidence. Based on the known role of the CEACAM6 gene in pancreatic cancer and its expression characteristics (analysis using the GEPIA database showed that its expression was significantly increased in pancreatic cancer tissues (Fig. 5D) and closely related to the poor prognosis of patients (Fig. 5F), And RT-qPCR confirmed that it was also highly expressed in multiple pancreatic cancer cell lines (Fig. 5E). We chose CEACAM6 as the key target for the subsequent study. Predictive analysis of the ENCORI database (Fig. 5G) suggested that there was a potential targeted regulatory relationship between hsa-miR-301a-5p and CEACAM6. The level of CEACAM6 was confirmed to be regulated by hsa-miR-301a-5p through RT-qPCR (Fig. 5H, K) and WB (Fig. 5I). The dual-luciferase reporter gene assay (Fig. 5M) clearly confirmed this specific binding. The rescue experiment against CEACAM6 reversed the pyroptosis effect of hsa-miR-301a-5p: WB detection of pyroptosis-related proteins (Fig. 5P), caspase-1 activity determination (Fig. 5R), and GSDMD immunofluorescence (Fig. 5S). To sum up, these series of experiments jointly confirmed from the molecular mechanism to the functional level that hsa-miR-301a-5p promotes pyroptosis of pancreatic cancer cells by directly targeting and inhibiting the expression of CEACAM6.

CEACAM6 is involved in the regulation of pyroptosis in pancreatic cancer cells by regulating the NF-κB signaling pathway

Based on previous studies showing that miR-423-5p inhibits the NF-κB signaling pathway by targeting CEACAM1, thereby affecting lymphangiogenesis in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)(Yu et al. xxxx), this study focuses on the mechanism of action of CEACAM6, another member of the CEACAM family. Previous sequencing and enrichment analysis showed that the downstream target genes of hsa-miR-301a-5p were significantly enriched in the NF-κB pathway (Fig. 6A). Combined with the common regulatory characteristics of the CEACAM family in the inflammatory microenvironment, we proposed a scientific hypothesis: CEACAM6 may mediate the malignant phenotype of tumors by activating the NF-κB pathway. To verify this hypothesis, WB detection revealed that the hsa-miR-301a-5p mimics could significantly activate the key molecules of the NF-κB pathway (p-IKBα, p-NF-κB P65, p-IKK and nuclear NF-κB P65) (Fig. 6B); The CEACAM6 supplementation experiment (Fig. 6D) further confirmed that hsa-miR-301a-5p regulates this pathway by targeting CEACAM6. To elucidate the specific mechanism by which CEACAM6 activates NF-κB, based on its membrane protein characteristics and the TNF receptor association suggested by GO enrichment (Fig. 6A), we speculate that CEACAM6 may bind to TNFR1/2 (Fig. 6F, 6G). The Co-IP experiment clearly verified that there was a specific interaction between CEACAM6 and TNFR1, and this interaction was not dependent on the change in the abundance of TNFR1 protein (Fig. 6H). Immunofluorescence co-localization further revealed the co-distribution of the two on the cell membrane (Fig. 6I, 6 J). These results indicate that the interaction between CEACAM6 and TNFR1 belongs to a direct spatial binding mode independent of protein content changes. This interaction may affect the activity or signal transduction function of TNFR1 through conformational regulation or the formation of complexes. Rather than achieving biological effects by regulating its expression level. Finally, to confirm the necessity of the NF-κB pathway in promoting pyroptosis, after blocking the pathway with the inhibitor BAY11-7082, the expression of pyroptosis markers induced by hsa-miR-301a-5p (Fig. 6K), the activity of caspase-1 (Fig. 6L), and the immunofluorescence of GSDMD (Fig. 6M) were all significantly inhibited. And its anti-proliferative effect also depends on this pathway (Supplementary Fig. 3D, 4 F). So far, the pyroptosis regulatory axis of hsa-miR-301a-5p-CEACAM6-TNFR1-NF-κB has been systematically verified at the molecular mechanism and functional levels.

It can be seen from this that the two mature chains of hsa-miR-301a-3p and hsa-miR-301a-5p from the same precursor miRNA play completely different or even opposite roles in the regulation of pancreatic cancer cell death, and achieve their functions respectively by targeting different key downstream genes. This more powerfully highlights the functional specificity and independence of the mature chains of miRNA. Therefore, when exploring the biological functions and therapeutic potential of miRNAs, it is necessary to strictly distinguish and clarify their specific mature chains (such as −3p or −5p). Generally treating the entire miRNA molecule as a single functional entity for general research or therapeutic development is imprecise and may be misleading.

Hsa-miR-301-3p and Hsa-miR-301-5p drive opposite tumor biological behaviors through differential signaling pathways in the preclinical model

In the pancreatic cancer xenotransplantation model, hsa-miR-301a-3p promotes tumorigenesis by targeting ACSL4-mediated ferroptosis

To verify the tumor regulatory role of hsa-miR-301a-3p in vivo, we constructed a pancreatic cancer xenotransplantation model. Six groups of cell lines (including miRNA mimics/inhibitors and their controls, ACSL4 supplementation group) were established through stable transfection, and the transfection efficiency was confirmed by RT-qPCR (Fig. 7A). Thirty days after subcutaneous tumor formation, it was found that the hsa-miR-301a-3p mimics significantly promoted tumor growth (the maximum tumor volume group), while the inhibitor group had the smallest tumor. Crucially, the overexpression of ACSL4 can partially reverse the tumor promoting effect of the mimics (Fig. 7B-D), indicating that ACSL4 is its key downstream target. Further mechanism verification showed that hsa-miR-301a-3p blocked ferroptosis in vivo by inhibiting ACSL4: tumor tissue WB (Fig. 7E) and IHC (Fig. 7G) confirmed that the level of miR-301a-3p was significantly negatively correlated with ACSL4 protein, and the expression of ferroptosis markers was inhibited; However, ACSL4 supplementation restores the activity of the ferroptosis pathway, thereby establishing the biological significance of the hsa-miR-301a-3p-ACSL4-ferroptosis axis in animal models.

To verify the tumor suppressive effect of hsa-miR-301a-5p in vivo and its targeting mechanism, we constructed a subcutaneous transplanted tumor model of pancreatic cancer. Six groups of cell lines (including miRNA mimics/inhibitors, CEACAM6 complement groups and their controls) were established through stable transfection, and the transfection efficiency was confirmed by RT-qPCR (Fig. 8A). Discussion

This study systematically investigated the functional divergence and molecular regulatory mechanisms of miR-301a-3p and miR-301a-5p strands in PDAC. The results demonstrate that miR-301a-3p and miR-301a-5p exhibit distinct expression profiles in PDAC, differentially influencing tumor progression through separate programmed cell death pathways—ferroptosis and pyroptosis, respectively. These findings not only challenge the conventional "dominant-strand paradigm" in miRNA research but also unveil an intricate regulatory network mediated by miRNA strand selection in tumor biology. Furthermore, this work provides mechanistic insights into the dual regulatory roles of miRNA duplex strands, expanding our understanding of their context-dependent functionality in oncogenic processes.

This study revealed that elevated expression of miR-301a-3p is strongly associated with the pro-tumorigenic properties of PDAC. Our findings demonstrate that miR-301a-3p enhances pancreatic cancer cell survival by suppressing key ferroptosis-related genes, a mechanism potentially linked to the characteristic high antioxidative capacity and metabolic reprogramming observed in pancreatic malignancies(Li et al. xxxx). While ferroptosis has been implicated in various cancer types, this work specifically establishes the pivotal role of miRNA-mediated regulation in ferroptosis resistance. These mechanistic insights suggest that therapeutic targeting of miR-301a-3p through antisense oligonucleotides (anti-miR) or small-molecule inhibitors could represent a novel strategy to overcome treatment resistance in pancreatic cancer, potentially restoring tumor cell sensitivity to ferroptosis-inducing therapies. The identification of this regulatory axis provides a molecular basis for developing precision therapies that exploit miRNA-guided cell death modulation in PDAC.

Conversely, the reduced expression of miR-301a-5p in PDAC suggests its potential tumor-suppressive function. Our experimental evidence reveals that miR-301a-5p promotes programmed cell death through activation of the pyroptosis pathway, an inflammatory form of cell death that may suppress tumor progression via immune activation within the tumor microenvironment(Wang et al. 2024; Zhang et al. 2023). This regulatory mechanism appears to involve gasdermin family protein-mediated membrane permeabilization and subsequent release of pro-inflammatory cytokines(Wei et al. 2022; Yu et al. 2021). The restoration of miR-301a-5p expression through pharmacological upregulation or gene delivery systems, coupled with strategies to enhance pyroptosis signaling, could therefore represent a novel immunotherapeutic approach for PDAC. These findings not only expand our understanding of miRNA-mediated immune regulation in cancer biology but also propose miR-301a-5p as a potential immunotherapy sensitizer. The dual modulation of pyroptotic cell death and antitumor immunity through this miRNA pathway opens new avenues for developing combination therapies that synergize with existing immune checkpoint inhibitors, potentially overcoming current limitations in PDAC immunotherapy responsiveness.

Importantly, this study not only delineates the functional divergence between miR-301a-3p and miR-301a-5p but also highlights the critical importance of miRNA isoform-specific analysis in cancer biology. Traditional miRNA research has predominantly focused on assessing global miRNA expression levels while overlooking the independent functional contributions of individual duplex strands. However, accumulating evidence from our work and recent studies demonstrates that 3p and 5p strands may target distinct gene networks and exert either cooperative or opposing roles in disease progression, depending on the cellular context and arm selection bias. These findings necessitate a paradigm shift toward strand-specific miRNA profiling in both basic research and clinical investigations. Future studies should incorporate comprehensive strand-specific functional annotation and expression quantification to enhance research precision and clinical translatability. The recognition of strand-specific regulatory networks opens new possibilities for developing miRNA-based therapeutics that specifically target pathogenic strands while preserving the physiological functions of complementary strands. This discovery not only overcomes the cognitive paradigm dominated by the "dominant chain" in traditional miRNA research, but also highlights the essential flaw of the treatment strategy that generally targets the entire miRNA molecule, simultaneously intervening in the functionally antagonistic double chains may lead to the cancellation of therapeutic effects or even the risk of tumor promotion. The deep mechanism of double-stranded functional differentiation may stem from its sequence-dependent targeting differences: bioinformatics analysis shows that the shared target genes of miR-301a double-stranded are less than 30%, which inevitably leads to the differentiation of its downstream pathways. Meanwhile, the double strands show bidirectional regulatory characteristics in the signaling pathway: −3p blocks ferroptosis by inhibiting ACSL4 to promote tumor survival, while −5p activates NF-κ B dependent pyroptosis through the CEACAM6-TNFR1 axis. The two form a "molecular tug-of-war" effect on cell fate.

The findings of this study hold significant clinical implications for molecular classification and precision therapy in pancreatic cancer. By detecting the expression levels of miR-301a-3p and miR-301a-5p, the molecular subtyping system for PDAC can be further optimized, enabling more accurate prognostic evaluation and personalized therapeutic strategies. Additionally, the strand-selection mechanism exhibits environmental dependency: certain miRNAs (e.g., miR-100) predominantly exist as 5p strands in normal tissues, while their 3p isoform abundance significantly increases in tumor microenvironments, suggesting potential involvement of epigenetic modifications or RNA-binding proteins in strand-specific regulation(Li et al. 2015). Furthermore, miRNA isoform-based targeting strategies, such as duplex cooperative effects or single-strand-specific delivery systems, may enhance therapeutic specificity and efficacy. Future investigations could integrate single-cell sequencing and spatial transcriptomics to decipher the spatiotemporal dynamics of miRNA regulation, systematically unveiling co-expression patterns of miRNA duplexes and their synergistic/antagonistic roles in disease networks. This approach provides novel molecular perspectives for precisely elucidating miRNA functionality in pathological contexts.

It should be noted that this study employed different discovery approaches for the target identification of miR-301a-3p (bioinformatics-prediction driven) and miR-301a-5p (RNA-seq based unbiased screening). While this strategy was shaped by both preliminary functional data and practical constraints, future studies would benefit from applying uniform multi-omics methodologies to both strands to ensure comprehensive comparability. Although this study primarily reveals the independent roles and antagonistic relationships of miR-301a-3p and miR-301a-5p in regulating distinct cell death pathways, the potential functional crosstalk between them remains a question worthy of investigation. Given their partial sequence complementarity, the two strands could theoretically form base pairs, which may further affect each other’s stability or compete for incorporation into the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC). Future studies should conduct experiments involving simultaneous modulation of the two strands’ expression, coupled with detailed analyses of their stability and RISC loading efficiency, to further explore this potential bidirectional regulatory mechanism. Elucidating such crosstalk will contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of the fine-tuning mechanism within the miR-301a precursor molecule. Although chain-specific therapy has significant clinical potential, it still faces multiple technical bottlenecks at present. At the detection level, conventional qPCR is difficult to precisely distinguish double-stranded expressions with highly similar sequences. Even the highly sensitive TaqMan probe method still poses a risk of false negatives in low-abundance tissue samples. In terms of model construction, existing animal studies generally adopt constitutive overexpression systems, which are unable to simulate the dynamic balance of double strands within tumors. These obstacles jointly restrict the clinical advancement of precision therapy.

Based on the molecular findings of this study, we propose three chain-specific therapeutic pathways: firstly, develop chemically modified chain-specific regulatory tools-design locked nucleic acid (LNA) -modified antagomir-3p against tumor-promoting miR-301a-3p and load it into pancreatic cancer-targeted lipid nanoparticles (LNP); For the tumor-suppressing miR-301a-5p, the recombinant lentiviral vector agomir-5p was constructed and combined with pyroptosis inducers (such as niraparib) to enhance the immune response(Reiss et al. 2022; Liu et al. xxxx). Secondly, explore double-chain equilibrium regulators. For example, screen small molecule compounds that can regulate the processing selectivity of Dicer (such as Enoxacin derivatives)(Suleiman et al. xxxx), selectively inhibit the maturation of the 3p chain while promoting the loading of the 5p chain. Finally, a dynamic monitoring platform was constructed to integrate nanopore single-cell sequencing and in vivo imaging techniques, and to draw the spatiotemporal distribution map of the intratumoral double-strand to guide precise drug delivery. These strategies will promote the paradigm shift of PDAC treatment from "general miRNA intervention" to "chain-specific precise regulation".

In summary, this study represents the first systematic investigation into the functional divergence and regulatory mechanisms of miR-301a-3p and miR-301a-5p in pancreatic cancer (The model diagram is shown in Fig. 9). These findings not only expand our understanding of miRNA biology but also identify novel potential targets for early diagnosis, molecular subtyping, and targeted therapy in PDAC. Future research should prioritize exploring the universality of miRNA-3p/5p functional duality across diverse cancer types, as well as developing precision intervention strategies targeting miRNA isoforms, establishing a molecular typing system based on the single-strand expression ratio (miR-301a-5p/−3p ratio), carrying out clinical trials of antibody-oligonucleotide conjugate drugs (AOC) targeting CEACAM6 and analyzing the dynamic evolution law of double-strand expression under chemotherapy pressure. These efforts will eventually lead to a new era of "chain-oriented" precision tumor treatment. These advancements will promote the clinical transformation of mirNa-based tumor treatment methods and ultimately enhance the specificity and effectiveness of cancer treatment paradigms.

Conclusion

This study systematically clarified for the first time the functional differentiation of miR-301a duplex isomers (3p and 5p) in PDAC, providing a new perspective and theoretical basis for miRNA research. The research results show that miR-301a-3p exerts a tumor promoting effect by inhibiting ferroptosis, while miR-301a-5p exerts an anti-tumor effect by inducing pyroptosis. This discovery not only enriches the understanding of the miRNA regulatory network, but also identifies new potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets for the molecular typing and precise treatment of PDAC. Crucially, our research results reveal the inherent therapeutic paradox of the pan-miRNA targeting strategy. Indiscriminate inhibition may simultaneously block tumor suppressive miR-301a-5p and deinhibit carcinogenic miR-301a-3p, which may exacerbate malignant progression. Future research should focus on exploring the functional characteristics and clinical relevance of miRNA duplisomers in cancer biology, thereby promoting the transformation of miRNA research from "molecular-level" analysis to "chain-specific" fine-tuning of regulatory mechanisms. Overall, this work has shifted the therapeutic prospects of miRNA from molecular level interference to chain-specific functional development, advancing the precise oncology of pancreatic cancer.

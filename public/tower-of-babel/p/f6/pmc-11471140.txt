Introduction

Pulmonary sequestration is a rare type of congenital malformation of the lung and its vessels, accounting for approximately 0.15–6.40 % of malformations [1].

Whether aberrant arteries in pulmonary sequestration should be divided at the bifurcation or at the periphery remains a subject of debate [2]. Indeed, several researchers suggest that the aberrant artery should be divided at the bifurcation in cases of pulmonary sequestration, owing to the risk of postoperative aneurysm formation followed by fetal bleeding [3]. In patients with pulmonary sequestration, the aberrant arteries branch from various regions, including the intra-abdominal aorta in rare cases [4]. In such cases, the aberrant artery cannot be directly divided at the bifurcation from the thoracic site.

Herein, we present a case wherein an aberrant blood vessel was dissected peripherally; the residual part of the aberrant artery spontaneously regressed postoperatively. This case suggests that the aberrant arteries could be divided at the periphery. This work is compliant with the Surgical Case Report (SCARE) 2023 criteria [5].

Presentation of case

A 35-year-old woman was referred to our hospital due to continuous cough. Chest X-ray revealed an infiltrative shadow in the right lower lung field. Enhanced computed tomography (CT) further revealed an infiltrative appearance in the right lower lobe of the lung, as well as an aberrant artery originating from the left gastric artery and traveling to the right lower lobe of the lung (Fig. 1). Celiac artery angiography revealed that the aberrant artery originated from the left gastric artery and emptied into the right lower lobe (Fig. 2A). Additionally, the aberrant artery was depicted smoothly without findings such as arterial sclerosis or vascular constriction. The diameter of the aberrant artery was 0.8 cm. The venous return was to the right inferior pulmonary vein, as normal. Laboratory findings were positive for the IgA Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC) antibody. Moreover, MAC was isolated from the right lower lobe of the lung via bronchoscopy. Regarding division of the aberrant artery, we discussed whether embolization of the aberrant artery should be performed preoperatively. Finally, we planned to perform lung resection first, as the patient was also showing symptoms of pulmonary sequestration with MAC infection. Furthermore, we decided that if blood flow to the aberrant artery remained after surgery, we would consider additional embolization. Right lower lobectomy was performed via a minimally invasive open surgery approach by mini-thoracotomy through auscultatory triangle. The aberrant artery was observed to pass through the pulmonary ligament and enter the right lower lobe of the lung (Fig. 3). The aberrant artery was ligated and divided at the level of the pulmonary ligament; right lower lobectomy was performed. The patient had an uneventful postoperative course and was discharged on postoperative day 7.Fig. 3Intraoperative photograph showing the aberrant artery passing through the pulmonary ligament and entering the right lower lobe of the lung.AA: aberrant artery.Fig. 3

The specimen revealed that the lower lobe of the right lung was destroyed due to MAC infection, while cystic degeneration was observed. Pathologically, the aberrant artery exhibited intimal thickening and luminal narrowing. However, no signs of atheroma or aneurysmal changes were observed.

Enhanced CT, performed four months postoperatively, revealed loss of blood flow to the aberrant artery, while the residual region of the aberrant artery had spontaneously occluded (Fig. 2B). Six months after surgery, a 3D-CT scan was performed to determine the need for additional embolization of the aberrant artery through the abdominal cavity. The aberrant artery naturally atrophied and was blocked. Therefore, no further treatment was required and follow-up in our department was completed.

Discussion

Pulmonary sequestration is a rare congenital disorder, in which nonfunctional pulmonary tissue lacks a normal connection with the tracheobronchial tree or pulmonary arteries [6]. Such tissue can receive arterial blood supply from a systemic vessel [6]. Traditionally, pulmonary sequestrations are divided into two forms: intralobar and extralobar. Two-thirds of cases involve sequestration in the left lower lobe, with others in the right lower lobe [7]. Intralobar pulmonary sequestration comprises 75 % of all pulmonary sequestrations [8]. Indeed, in one study of 43 cases, the aberrant artery was derived from the thoracic aorta in 37 patients, celiac trunk in four, left gastric artery in one, and abdominal aorta in one [4]. In our case, the aberrant artery branched from the left gastric artery. In such cases, where the aberrant artery should be divided remains controversial [9]. Surgical resection of pulmonary sequestration is recommended to eliminate the risk of recurrent infection and hemorrhage.

Arteriosclerosis and aneurysm frequently occur in the aberrant artery [10], whereas bleeding frequently occurs due to rupture of the aberrant artery [3]. In this case, we ligated and divided the aberrant artery at the peripheral side—not at the bifurcation—resulting in spontaneous occlusion of the residual part of the aberrant artery. There is a consensus among general thoracic surgeons that the aberrant artery must be divided as close to the bifurcation as possible [11]. This is because dissection of the aberrant artery at a site with repeated inflammation or arteriosclerotic changes may result in the formation of an aneurysm in the residual aberrant artery, which can result in fatal events, such as massive bleeding [12].

Therefore, if the aberrant artery originates from within the abdominal cavity, dissecting the aberrant artery as far as possible at the bifurcation within the abdominal cavity is required, using techniques such as laparoscopy. Nevertheless, the results of our case significantly could alleviate the burden on surgeons, with respect to whether the aberrant artery at the bifurcation should be dissected intraoperatively. Overall, our results suggest that an adequate dissection can be achieved by cutting as close as possible to the bifurcation within the thoracic cavity, such as in the pulmonary ligament. According to the literature, one case of anomalous systemic arterial supply to the basal segment has been reported. In that case, follow-up CT performed three months postoperatively revealed a large aneurysmal formation of the stump of the aberrant artery, and endovascular aortic repair of the aneurysm was performed [13]. The diameter of the aberrant artery was as large as 2 cm, which is thought to have influenced the formation of the aneurysm. Based on CT findings, preoperative arterial embolization may be an option for aberrant arteries with large arterial diameters or atherosclerotic changes. In cases of no aneurysm or atherosclerotic changes in the aberrant artery for which the vessel diameter in not significantly enlarged, such as 2 cm more, peripheral dissection, rather than dissection at the bifurcation of the aberrant artery, might be sufficient.

In our case, we further debated whether arterial embolization should be performed preoperatively. The advantage of preoperative embolization is that it could prevent fatal bleeding from the aberrant artery during surgery. On the other hand, the disadvantages are that the embolic material, such as coils, could fall off. In addition, there may be a delay before surgery due to preoperative arterial embolization, even if symptoms are present. Preoperative examination did not reveal any arteriosclerosis or aneurysmal changes; the patient had symptoms due to pulmonary sequestration. Therefore, removal of the pulmonary sequestration was given priority.

Conclusion

Based on the results of this case, in cases of no aneurysm or atherosclerotic changes in the aberrant artery for which the vessel diameter is not significantly enlarged, peripheral dissection, rather than dissection at the bifurcation of the aberrant artery, may be sufficient. However, when the remnant part of the aberrant artery would be occluded spontaneously and whether this could be achieved in all patients remain unclear, further studies on the subject are warranted.

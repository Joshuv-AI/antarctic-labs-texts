The qualitative and quantitative relationship between the spatiotemporal variations of potential evapotranspiration and meteorological variables in the Hexi corridor, Northwest China
Authors: Yali Ma, Zuirong Niu, Xingfan Wang, Dongyuan Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractStudying the temporal and spatial variations of potential evapotranspiration (ET0) in the Hexi Corridor region and its response to climatic factors is of great significance for improving the utilization efficiency of agricultural water resources. The temporal and spatial characteristics of ET0 and the six meteorological factors influencing the variation of ET0 in the FAO P-M formula during 1960–2019 have been revealed based on the statistical analysis method and the spatial interpolation method. Qualitative methods such as principal component analysis, cluster analysis, and grey relational grade were organically combined with quantitative methods such as sensitivity analysis and contribution rate calculation to comprehensively and objectively reveal the response relationship of ET0 to climate factors. The results showed that ET0 in the Hexi corridor showed a fluctuating increase during the period 1960–2019, and the change points were 1969 and 2002, respectively. ET0 increased from the southeast to the northwest, with a range from 812.3 to 1516.6 mm. Temperature factors such as Rn, Tmean, Tmax, and Tmin, change closely with ET0, followed by humidity and wind speed factors. The increase in ET0 is the most sensitive to the decrease in Rn, followed by the decrease in u2 and the increase in Tmean. Increasing Tmean was the main reason for increasing ET0, followed by u2 and Rn, and the combined contribution of the six meteorological factors was 72.39% during 1960–2019. This study has revealed the response of ET0 to climate factors, providing a scientific reference for more rational planning and efficient utilization of water resources in arid and semi-arid regions.Keywords: Potential evapotranspiration, Meteorological variables, Response, Quantitative relationship, Hexi corridorSubject terms: Climate sciences, Hydrology

Introduction

Evapotranspiration is an essential part of the hydrological process and has an impact on the water and heat balance. As the main index for assessing regional climate change, surface energy, and water balance1,2, its change will lead to changes in regional ecology, climate, and agricultural production3. Due to the difficulty in obtaining actual evapotranspiration data, actual evapotranspiration is generally estimated by ET0 in actual work4. According to the water balance equation P-R-ETa = ± △S, where P is precipitation, △S is the change in soil water storage, R is runoff, and ETa is actual evapotranspiration. Changes in soil water holding capacity greatly affect vegetation growth. Therefore, evapotranspiration is a critical factor in the development of irrigation water regimes in a scientific and rational way5,6. Changes in evapotranspiration have an impact on soil water storage, surface runoff, and underground runoff, which in turn causes changes in groundwater levels, affecting vegetation and crop growth. Therefore, precipitation, evapotranspiration, soil water storage, and groundwater level are interrelated and affect each other as an integral whole, and they are all important links in the hydrological cycle. The water budget imbalance caused by precipitation and evapotranspiration is the main cause of meteorological drought, hydrological drought, and agricultural drought. Therefore, ET0 is a critical factor in drought monitoring and evaluation7. In the context of global warming, the arid inland river basin of western China has experienced the so-called "warm-wet" trend. This is an apparent transition from warm-dry to warm-humid8. Many scholars have confirmed that climate change leads to increased evapotranspiration9–11. Under the background of warming and humidification, ET0 is regulated by various meteorological factors12. ET0 has a nonlinear relationship with meteorological factors, and its variation is closely related to regional climate, which makes its variation more complicated and uncertain 13. Therefore, it is of great value to study the spatiotemporal evolution of ET0 and to analyze the climate-driving factors of ET0. This will help to reveal the hydrological response mechanism to climate change.

There is a research base for the study of the time–space distribution of the ET0 and of the response mechanism of the ET0 to climatic factors. Many methods can be used to quantitatively attribute changes in ET0, including partial correlation analysis14, principal component analysis, multiple regression methods3,15,16, partial derivative method2,17, differential equation method18,19, sensitivity coefficient method and detrending method20. In order to identify the variables driving the changes in ET0, Jerin, J.N. et al.14 used step-wise linear regression and partial correlation analysis. Han et al.15 revealed the main climatic drivers affecting evapotranspiration by partial correlation analysis and multiple linear regression. Nouri, M. et al.20 investigated quantitative contribution and sensitivity analysis of ET0 using sensitivity coefficient and detrending methods respectively. Yang et al.2 used the measured meteorological data at different altitudes of the Qilian Mountains and the partial derivative method to find that ET0 has the highest sensitivity to net radiation, and the sensitivity of meteorological factors changes significantly with altitude. Shi et al.18 identified vegetation green-up as an important driver of evapotranspiration in Siberia using the differential equation method, with a relative contribution rate of 37%. The focus of previous studies has mainly been on the quantitative attribution of ET0 variations, and few researchers have paid attention to the combined qualitative and quantitative analysis of the response of climate factors to ET0 variations. Therefore, the relationship between ET0 variations and meteorological factors can only be revealed from a single aspect, which is not comprehensive and objective enough. As the evapotranspiration process involves multiple systems such as atmosphere, vegetation, and surface, there are many influencing factors18,21,22. Moreover, the interaction between ET0 and meteorological variables as well as among various meteorological variables is relatively complex13, so it is particularly important to organically combine qualitative and quantitative methods. A variety of qualitative and quantitative methods would be organically combined to comprehensively and objectively explore the response relationship between ET0 variations and meteorological factors, and the results are verified with each other, and the research process is gradually deepened.

China’s northwestern arid regions lie in the Eurasian continent’s hinterland and have an extremely fragile ecosystem. It is one of the driest regions in the world and has extreme sensitivity to climate change23. The Hexi Corridor, an arid region in the interior of north-western China, has a climate that is typically temperate and continental in nature. The south and north are both mountainous regions, while the middle is a corridor plain. With small precipitation and strong evaporation, the ecosystem is fragile. Water scarcity is a major constraint on local economic and social development, leading to ecological degradation16. In recent years, some scholars have conducted many studies on regional evapotranspiration variations and their climate-driving factors in Northwest China2,22,24. It is found that the main factors causing ET0 variations are wind speed and temperature. However, due to the numerous meteorological factors affecting ET0, the climate characteristics of different geographical units will lead to obvious differences in ET0 variations and leading factors. Therefore, the characteristics of changes in ET0 need to be studied in specific regions or ecological units5,14,25. On the other hand, ET0 has a strong climatic synthesis and multi-driver coupling effect, rather than a single meteorological factor driving results. Therefore, it is valuable to reveal the spatiotemporal distribution of ET0 and the response of ET0 to climate factors from the intrinsic characteristics of the long-term series data of ET0 and meteorological factors. This will help us understand how hydrological processes respond and how water resources can be used effectively in Northwest China under the conditions of warming and humidification. Therefore, in this study, a variety of qualitative and quantitative analysis methods are organically combined to explore the characteristics of ET0 variations in Hexi Corridor and the response relationship between ET0 changes and meteorological variables.

The research has three objectives: (1) Based on statistical analysis method and spatial interpolation method, to reveal the time and space patterns of ET0 and the six meteorology factors influencing the variation of ET0 in the FAO’s Penman–Monteith formula in the Hexi corridor between 1960 and 2019; (2) Using principal component analysis, cluster analysis and grey relation grade, to identify the qualitative correlations between ET0 variation and climatic factors; (3) Sensitivity analysis quantified the sensitivity of ET0 variations to meteorological variables. Contribution rate calculations quantified the contribution of meteorological variables to ET0 variations. The ET0 response mechanism to meteorology was identified, providing a scientific reference for a more rational planning and efficient use of irrigation water in the Hexi corridor.

Materials and methods

Study area

Located in Gansu Province at the northern foothills of the Qilian Mountains in northwestern China, the Hexi Corridor (37°15’-42°69’N, 92°27’-104°20’W) is about 1000 km long and its area is about 2.26 × 105 km2. It is a concentrated planting area of cash crops in Gansu Province and an irreplaceable commercial grain base in China26. The Hexi Corridor consists of three inland basins, the Shiyang River basin in the east, the Heihe River basin in the center and the Shule River basin in the west. It belongs to the typical temperate and warm temperate continental climate, with climatic characteristics of drought, little rain, strong evaporation, significant temperature differences between day and night, and abundant light. The southern Qilian Mountain area belongs to the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau alpine climate. Most of the arid areas are distributed in the northwest, while most of the relatively wet areas are distributed in the south. The terrain is undulating, and the elevation difference between north and south is obvious. The south is high and mountainous, with the Qilian Mountains being an important water conservation area. The north is flat and dominated by desert and Gobi landscapes. It has a complex and changeable ecological environment, as well as fragile and sensitive climatic characteristics23.

Data source

The meteorological data of the Hexi corridor from 1960.1.1 to 2019.12.31 covers 21,915 days and 7,823,655 data points. It was obtained from the China Meteorological Data Network (http://data.cma.cn, accessed on 12 June 2022). The data produced by the National Meteorological Information Centre has undergone strict quality control and is widely used in scientific research27,28. Less than 1% of daily data was missing from the selected meteorological stations in this study, and the missing values were obtained by interpolation based on the regression relationship of the data from the nearby stations. Evapotranspiration is calculated on the basis of the Penman–Monteith (P-M) formula29, revised (1998) by the FAO. This formula is widely used and referred to30–32. Cluster analysis

Clustering is a classification technique. It divides multiple variables into fewer groups. Variables in the same cluster tend to share similarities, while those in different clusters tend to differ42,43. The study used a systematic cluster analysis algorithm to calculate the similarities of the variables affecting ET0 variability, including Rn, u2, Tmean, Tmax, Tmin, and RH. Results

Temporal and spatial variations of ET0 during 1960–2019

Change trend and change point of ET0

From 1960 to 2019, the ET0 in Hexi Corridor increased in a fluctuating way, and the annual average value was 1186.93 mm. ET0 showed a downward trend, a downward trend, and an upward trend during 1960–1968, 1969–2001, and 2002–2019, respectively, and the annual average values of each period were 1177.33 mm, 1178.16 mm and 1207.82 mm, as shown in Fig. 3a. In Figs. 3b and c, the change points were identified as 1969 and 2002, respectively, based on the characteristics of the changes in each period shown in the interannual change curves of ET0 as well as the results of the Moving T-Test (MTT) and the M–K mutation test. Therefore, 1960–1968 was chosen as the base period, 1969–2001 and 2002–2019 as the change period.

Interannual variation and mutation test of ET0 in Hexi Corridor during 1960–2019 (a) Interannual variation; (b) MTT; (c) M–K test.

Spatial distribution of ET0

The spatial distribution of ET0 was obtained by spatial interpolation using the inverse distance weighting method based on the annual mean values of ET0 during 1960–2019 at 21 meteorological stations in the Hexi corridor (Fig. 4a). ET0 in Hexi corridor increased from southeast to northwest with values ranging between 812.3 and 1516.6 mm. The maximum value of ET0 at Hongliuhe Station in the northwest was 1516.6 mm, while the minimum value of ET0 at Menyuan Station in the southwest was 756.1 mm. The linear change rate and significance level of ET0 during 1960–2019 is shown in Fig. 4b. The ET0 at Anxi Station and Yumen Town Station in the west of Hexi Corridor decreased significantly (P < 0.05), with the maximum change rate between − 3.0 and − 1.9 mm/yr. The ET0 in other areas of the Hexi Corridor increased significantly, with 71.43% of the stations reaching the significant level of 0.01, among which the ET0 near Mazongshan and Zhangye stations increased the most (2.3 ~ 2.5 mm/yr). Therefore, ET0 in most areas of the Hexi Corridor shows an increasing trend.

PCA and cluster analysis of meteorological factors

Principal component analysis transforms the original variables into several linearly uncorrelated new variables and extracts the principal components with a cumulative variance threshold of 80% ~ 85%. The purpose of dimensionality reduction is achieved with maximum preservation of original information. Principal component analysis was carried out on the six meteorological factors in the FAO Penman–Monteith formula that affect the ET0 variation, including Rn, u2, Tmean, Tmax, Tmin, and RH, as shown in Fig. 8a and Table 1. The first two principal components were extracted as principal components, accounting for 90.54% of the cumulative variance. The first principal component carries the most information, with a percentage of 67.95%, reflecting mainly the effect of temperature. The maximum component loads of Tmean, Tmax, Tmin, and Rn are 0.980, 0.980, 0.971, and 0.969, respectively. The second principal component accounts for 22.59%, which mainly reflects the influence of humidity and wind speed. The maximum component loads of u2 and RH are − 0.859 and 0.709, respectively. Therefore, the principal component analysis converts six original variables into two types of variables, namely, temperature factor, humidity, and wind speed factor. The temperature factor has a great influence on ET0 variation.

Sensitivity of ET0 to changes in meteorological factors

Based on the annual mean values of sensitivity coefficients of ET0 to various meteorological factors at 21 meteorological stations in Hexi Corridor and its surrounding areas, in the three Köppen climate zones including Midlatitude Desert (BWk), Midlatitude Steppe (BSk), and Subarctic Climate (Dwc), spatial interpolation was achieved using inverse distance weighting method, as shown in Fig. 9. The variation range of S_Tmean is − 0.03 ~ 0.15, and the most sensitive areas are located in the west (Dunhuang Station, Anxi Station), the middle, and the east (Wuwei Station, Minqin Station), with an annual mean value of 0.10188, and a single station (Tuole Station) has a negative value. On the whole, Tmean changes in the same direction as ET0, if Tmean increases by 10%, ET0 increases by − 0.3 to 1.5%, and the increase in temperature causes ET0 to increase. S_Rn ranges from 0.27 to 0.58, with an annual average value of 0.42650. The central and eastern parts of the Hexi corridor are the most sensitive areas. Rn and ET0 change in the same direction. If Rn decreases by 10%, ET0 decreases by 2.7% to 5.8%, and the decrease of Rn leads to the decrease of ET0. S_u2 ranges from − 0.16 to − 0.09, and the most sensitive areas are located at Tuole Station in the centre of the Hexi corridor, Yongchang Station, and Wushaoling Station in the east, with an annual average value of − 0.10114. u2 and ET0 change inversely. If u2 decreases by 10%, ET0 will increase by 0.9% to 1.6%. S_RH ranges from − 1.97 × 10−3 to − 0.69 × 10−3, and the most sensitive areas are mainly located at Yongchang Station and Wushaoling Station in the eastern part of Hexi Corridor, with an annual average value of − 0.99 × 10−3. RH and ET0 changed inversely, and the ET0 variation is not sensitive to the decrease of RH. S_Tmin ranges from − 1.50 × 10−4 to 0, and the most sensitive areas are mainly distributed in Mamanshan Station, Lenghu Station, Yongchang Station, and Wushaoling Station on the edge of Hexi Corridor. Most areas of Hexi Corridor are low-sensitive areas, and ET0 is not sensitive to the increase of Tmin. S_Tmax ranges from 0.40 × 10−3 to 1.16 × 10−3, and the highly sensitive areas are mainly distributed in the west of Hexi Corridor. Tmax and ET0 change in the same direction, and ET0 is not sensitive to the increase of Tmax. Therefore, the sensitivity is ranked as Rn, u2, Tmean, RH, Tmax, and Tmin. ET0 is most sensitive to the decrease of Rn, and the decrease of Rn leads to the decrease of ET0, followed by the decrease of u2. ET0 is not sensitive to RH, Tmax, and Tmin, and a combination of meteorological factors contributes to changes in ET0. In the BWk climate zone (central Hexi Corridor), ET0 is highly sensitive to Tmean and Rn; in the BWk climate zone (western Hexi Corridor), ET0 is highly sensitive to Tmax; in the BSk climate zone, ET0 is highly sensitive to Rn, u2 and RH; in the DWc climate zone, ET0 is highly sensitive to u2 and RH. Therefore, the sensitivity of meteorological factors affecting the change of ET0 varies in different climate zones. In arid regions, ET0 is more significantly influenced by temperature; while in humid areas, ET0 is more noticeably affected by wind speed and humidity.

Discussion

The sensitivity analysis can reveal the main sensitive factors affecting ET0 variation, but it cannot determine the contribution of a single meteorological factor to the changes in the ET0. Both the sensitivity and relative rate of change are considered in the calculation of the contribution rate so that the effect of climate factors on ET0 variations can be accurately and quantitatively revealed. Therefore, the sensitivity is inconsistent with the contribution. In the process of calculating the contribution, only six meteorological variables were analyzed, including Rn, u2, Tmean, Tmax, Tmin, and RH, which did not cover all the meteorological factors affecting the ET0 variation. For example, the soil heat flux (G) is disregarded in the daily P-M calculation. The altitude (z) affects ET0 through atmospheric pressure, and the atmospheric pressure influences ET0 through its effect on the psychrometric constant (γ). At the same time, the mean values of some parameters in the period were adopted. Therefore, the contribution rate could not reach 100%, and the combined contribution of the six meteorological variables from 1969 to 2019 was 72.39%. In this study, the contribution calculation of the five meteorological variables, including Rn, u2, Tmax, Tmin, and RH, was expanded to six meteorological variables, and Tmean was no longer replaced by (Tmax + Tmin)/2. Therefore, the contribution of temperature is concentrated in Tmean, which is much larger than Tmax and Tmin. In the calculation formula, the variables are treated as independent49, but there are links between the variables, and they are not completely independent50,51, which may lead to potential bias and uncertainty in the calculation results. In addition, the parameters as and bs are taken as 0.281 and 0.441 in Northwest China37 when calculating Rns by formula (3). Constant parameters are used in Hexi Corridor, and the difference in actual atmospheric conditions at different locations cannot be accurately reflected, that is, the two parameters have spatial heterogeneity15. Therefore, if the spatial differences of parameters are considered, the uncertainty of ET0 in the estimation process may be reduced52,53.

The increase of Tmean is the dominant factor for the increasing ET0 in Hexi Corridor during 1969–2019, followed by Rn and u2. Zhu et al.54 found that ET0 variation was mainly affected by temperature in the east-central parts of Northwest China. Li et al.55 found that the determining factor of ET0 variation from 1961 to 2018 was the average temperature in environmentally sensitive areas of China. In the Hexi Corridor region, the “evaporation paradox” phenomenon also occurred from 1969 to 2019. This phenomenon refers to the simultaneous occurrence of temperature increase and decreased evapotranspiration. Generally, an increase in temperature is expected to lead to an increase in evapotranspiration. However, numerous research results have shown that in many regions around the world, there is a situation where temperature increases but evapotranspiration decreases56. Under the background of climate change, the temperature in the Hexi Corridor has increased significantly, and ET0 is greatly influenced by temperature and shows a positive trend. However, ET0 has not increased significantly from 1960 to 2019. The reason is that the decrease in wind speed has largely counteracted the impact of temperature on the change of ET0. The increase in ET0 is mainly due to a larger rate of temperature rise, while the decrease in ET0 is mainly driven by a larger rate of wind speed decline. The influence of wind speed cannot be ignored in arid and semi-arid regions. Liu et al.57 pointed out that the changes in ET0 need to take into account the influences of various meteorological factors such as relative humidity, net radiation, wind speed and temperature. Furthermore, the changes in ET0 are influenced by factors such as topography, vegetation type, soil texture, aerosol concentration, and atmospheric circulation, and these factors interact with each other through a feedback mechanism58. Liu et al.58 found that NDVI of different vegetation coverage types and ET0 were closely related, and vegetation greening was one of the factors influencing evapotranspiration. Chen et al.59 found that the correlation between most meteorological factors and ET0 was significant in Akmola Oblast, Kazakhstan, and the spatial distribution characteristics of ET0, soil moisture content, and soil temperature were similar. The sixth assessment report of the IPCC60 indicates that due to the continuous impact of human development on global climate change, global warming will still intensify. Since the twenty-first century, China has experienced an average temperature increase of 0.6 °C, which is bound to affect the speed and intensity of the hydrological cycle as well as regional evaporation61. Mondal et al.61 pointed out that with the continuous global warming, the growth trends of temperature and ET0 in the Indus River Basin have become more pronounced.

Conclusions

Using statistical analysis and spatial interpolation techniques, the temporal and spatial characteristics of ET0, as well as the six meteorological variables in the FAO Penman–Monteith formula that affect the changes of ET0, were analyzed. Qualitative methods such as principal component analysis, cluster analysis, and grey relational grade were organically combined with quantitative methods such as sensitivity analysis and contribution rate calculation to comprehensively and objectively reveal the response relationship of ET0 to climate factors.

During the period 1960–2019, the ET0 in the Hexi corridor fluctuated upwards, with an annual average of 1186.93 mm. The change points are 1969 and 2002. The ET0 ranged from 812.3 mm in the south-east to 1516.6 mm in the north-west, and the ET0 increased in most areas of Hexi Corridor.

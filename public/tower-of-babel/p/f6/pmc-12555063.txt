Assessment of the feed additives consisting of ascorbic acid (3a300), sodium ascorbate (1b301), calcium ascorbate (1b302), ascorbyl palmitate (1b304), sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate (3a312) and sodium ascorbyl phosphate (3a311) (as sources of vitamin C) for all animal species for the renewal of their authorisations (DSM Nutritional Products Ltd., NHU Europe GmbH and Orffa Additives B.V.)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Ilen Röhe, Katerina Theodoridou, Jaume Galobart, Maria Vittoria Vettori, Paola Manini, Fabiola Pizzo, Piera Valeri
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, the EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP) was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the assessment of the application for renewal of authorisation of ascorbic acid, sodium ascorbate, calcium ascorbate, ascorbyl palmitate, sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate, sodium ascorbyl phosphate (as sources of vitamin C) as feed additives for all animal species. The applicants have provided data demonstrating that the additives currently in the market comply with the conditions of authorisation. Thus, the FEEDAP Panel concludes that the additives remain safe for the target species, consumers and the environment. Regarding the user safety, ascorbic acid and sodium ascorbyl phosphate are not irritant to the skin or eyes and are not dermal sensitisers. Sodium ascorbate is not a skin sensitiser, but the Panel cannot conclude on its potential to be irritant to skin and eyes. In the absence of data, the FEEDAP Panel is not in the position to conclude on the safety of calcium ascorbate for the user. Ascorbyl palmitate is not irritant to skin and eyes and is not a dermal sensitiser. Sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate should not be considered a skin and eye irritant nor a skin sensitiser.There is no need to assess the efficacy of the additives in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.

INTRODUCTION

Background and Terms of Reference

Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 1 establishes the rules governing the Community authorisation of additives for use in animal nutrition. In particular, Article 14(1) of that Regulation lays down that an application for renewal shall be sent to the Commission at the latest 1 year before the expiry date of the authorisation.

The European Commission received a request from DSM Nutritional Products Ltd., represented in the EU by DSM Nutritional Products Sp. z o.o., NHU Europe GmbH and Orffa Additives BV 2 for the renewal of the authorisation of the additives consisting of ascorbic acid, sodium ascorbate, calcium ascorbate, ascorbyl palmitate, sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate, sodium ascorbyl phosphate (vitamin C), when used as a feed additive for all animal species (category: nutritional additives; functional group: vitamins, pro‐vitamins and chemically well‐defined substances having a similar effect; category: technological additives; functional group: antioxidants).

According to Article 7(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, the Commission forwarded the application to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) as an application under Article 14(1) (renewal of the authorisation). The dossier was received on 22 July 2024 and the general information and supporting documentation are available at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2024‐00488. The particulars and documents in support of the application were considered valid by EFSA as of 18 October 2024.

According to Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, EFSA, after verifying the particulars and documents submitted by the applicant, shall undertake an assessment in order to determine whether the feed additive complies with the conditions laid down in Article 5. EFSA shall deliver an opinion on the safety for the target animals, consumer, user and the environment and on the efficacy of the feed additive consisting of ascorbic acid, sodium ascorbate, calcium ascorbate, ascorbyl palmitate, sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate and sodium ascorbyl phosphate (as sources of vitamin C), when used under the proposed conditions of use (see Section 3.1.7 ).

Additional information

The additives ascorbic acid (3a300), sodium ascorbate (1b301), calcium ascorbate (1b302), ascorbyl palmitate (1b304), sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate (3a312), sodium ascorbyl phosphate (3a311) (as sources of vitamin C) are currently authorised for use in feed for all animal species. 3

EFSA issued two opinions on the safety and efficacy of these products when used in feed for all animal species (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013a, 2013b).

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of a technical dossier 4 in support of the authorisation request for the use of ascorbic acid, sodium ascorbate, calcium ascorbate, ascorbyl palmitate, sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate and sodium ascorbyl phosphate (as sources of vitamin C) as feed additives.

In accordance with Article 38 of the Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 5 and taking into account the protection of confidential information and of personal data in accordance with Articles 39–39e of the same Regulation, and of the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down practical arrangements concerning transparency and confidentiality, 6 a non‐confidential version of the dossier has been published on OpenEFSA.

According to Article 32c(2) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 and to the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down the practical arrangements on pre‐submission phase and public consultations, EFSA carried out a public consultation on the non‐confidential version of the technical dossier from 27 January to 17 February 2025, for which no comments were received.

The confidential version of the technical dossier was subject to a target consultation of the interested Member States from 22 October 2024 to 22 January 2025, for which the received comments were considered for the assessment.

The FEEDAP Panel used the data provided by the applicant together with data from other sources, such as previous risk assessments by EFSA or other expert bodies, peer‐reviewed scientific papers, other scientific reports and experts' knowledge, to deliver the present output.

The European Union Reference Laboratory (EURL) considered that the conclusions and recommendations reached in the previous assessment regarding the methods used for the control of ascorbic acid, sodium ascorbate, calcium ascorbate, ascorbyl palmitate, sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate and sodium ascorbyl phosphate (as sources of vitamin C) in animal feed are valid and applicable for the current application. 7

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and the efficacy of ascorbic acid, sodium ascorbate, calcium ascorbate, ascorbyl palmitate, sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate, sodium ascorbyl phosphate (as sources of vitamin C) is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 8 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the renewal of the authorisation of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2021).

ASSESSMENT

Ascorbic acid, sodium ascorbyl phosphate and sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate are currently authorised as nutritional additives (functional group: vitamins, pro‐vitamins and chemically well‐defined substances having a similar effect) in feed for all animal species. Ascorbic acid is also authorised in water for drinking for all animal species. Ascorbic acid, sodium ascorbate, calcium ascorbate and ascorbyl palmitate are authorised as technological feed additives (functional group: antioxidants) in feed for all animal species.

The authorisation also states that ascorbic acid, sodium ascorbyl phosphate and sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate may be placed on the market and used as an additive consisting of a preparation. The applicants indicated that, with the present application, they are seeking the renewal of the authorisation for ascorbic acid (99%) and its solid preparation(s), sodium ascorbate (99%), calcium ascorbate (99%), ascorbyl palmitate (98%), sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate (35%) and sodium ascorbyl phosphate (95%), which will be described in the sections below.

The data supporting the application for ascorbic acid and sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate were generated by three companies, while the data supporting the application for sodium ascorbate, calcium ascorbate, ascorbyl phosphate and sodium ascorbyl phosphate were submitted by one company.

Sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate (3a312)

The active substance contains sodium and calcium L‐ascorbyl phosphates (mono‐, di‐ and tri‐phosphates), with the monophosphate representing > 90% of the total phosphates. The empirical formula of sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate is C6H6O9P·CaNa, which corresponds to a molecular weight of 316 g/mol.

The current authorisation specifies that the active substance sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate has purity criteria of a minimum of 95%, with a minimum content of 35% ascorbic acid.

The FEEDAP Panel notes that the purity criteria of min 95% for the active substance do not refer to the content of the sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate but rather to the percentage of the total ascorbate which is bound to phosphate (in the form of mono‐, di‐ and tri‐phosphate and expressed as ascorbic acid equivalent) and is named phosphorylated ascorbic acid. The Panel notes that the 35% ascorbic acid refers to ascorbic acid equivalents for the phosphorylated ascorbic acid present.

The additive also contains remnants of the other reagents added in excess in the manufacturing process (35% sodium trimetaphosphate, about 13% sodium hydroxide and 16% calcium hydroxide) (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013a).

The applicant stated that no changes in the manufacturing process or in the composition of the additive have been introduced since the last authorisation. 25

The Company A and B include in the manufacturing of the active substance, during the spray‐drying process, a small amount of silicon dioxide or calcium carbonate, while the Company C does not include any additional compounds. Company B provided data for an intermediate product, which is not reported as the product is not intended to be placed on the market.

Safety

In its previous opinions (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013a, 2013b), the Panel concluded that vitamin C, in the form of ascorbic acid, and its calcium and sodium salts, ascorbyl palmitate, sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate and sodium ascorbyl phosphate, are safe for all animal species. Setting a maximum content was not considered necessary. The Panel also concluded that the use of these additives is safe for the consumer and the environment. Regarding user safety, the Panel concluded that sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate and sodium ascorbyl phosphate are not irritant to skin and eyes and are unlikely to be skin sensitisers. In the absence of data, ascorbic acid, sodium ascorbate, calcium ascorbate and ascorbyl palmitate were considered irritant to skin and eyes and dermal sensitisers. Inhalation exposure to all the additives was considered a hazard.

The applicants declared that no adverse effects, including accidents for target animals, consumers, users and/or the environment, have been reported since the approval of the additives. 32

The applicant provided a literature search on the safety of the additives for the target animals, consumers, users and the environment.

Extensive literature search

The applicants performed a literature search to provide evidence that the additives remain safe under the approved conditions for the target species, consumers, users and the environment. 33 The literature search was conducted in four cumulative databases (iLIVIVO, NCBI, OVID, Toxoinfo) and 13 single databases. The applicant identified 26 hits which were considered relevant for the assessment of the safety of the different compounds. After the review of the scientific literature, none of the hits retrieved reported any adverse effects or indicated a safety concern.

Safety for the target species, consumers and the environment

No new data were found in the literature search that would lead the Panel to revise the conclusions previously reached with regard to the safety for the target species, consumers and environment. Considering the above and the fact that the manufacturing process and composition of the additives have not changed since the previous authorisation, the Panel concludes that ascorbic acid, sodium ascorbate, calcium ascorbate, ascorbyl palmitate, sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate and sodium ascorbyl phosphate remain safe for the target species, the consumer and the environment under the conditions of the current authorisation.

Safety for the user

In its previous assessment, the FEEDAP Panel concluded that, in the absence of data, ascorbic acid, sodium ascorbate, calcium ascorbate and ascorbyl palmitate should be considered irritant to skin and eyes and dermal sensitisers. Sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate was considered not irritant to skin and eyes and is unlikely to be a skin sensitiser, and the same conclusion could be applied to sodium ascorbyl phosphate (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013a).

Ascorbic acid

Based on the highest dusting potential measured (see Section 3.1.1.1 ), the FEEDAP Panel considers that the exposure of users through inhalation is likely.

The skin irritation potential of ascorbic acid was tested in a study performed according to OECD TG 404, which showed that the active substance is not a skin irritant (UN GHS No Category). 34

The eye irritation potential of ascorbic acid was tested in a study performed according to OECD TG 405, which showed that the active substance is not an eye irritant (UN GHS No Category). 35

The skin sensitisation potential of ascorbic acid was tested in Mauer optimisation study, which showed that the product is not a skin sensitiser. 36

Sodium ascorbate

Based on the highest dusting potential measured (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013a), the FEEDAP Panel considers that the exposure of users through inhalation is likely.

No specific information was submitted regarding the skin and eye irritation potential of sodium ascorbate. In the absence of data, the FEEDAP Panel is not in the position to conclude on its potential to be irritant to skin and eyes.

The skin sensitisation potential of the additive was tested in a study performed according to OECD TG 429, which showed that sodium ascorbate is not a skin sensitiser. 37

Calcium ascorbate

Based on the highest dusting potential measured (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013a), the FEEDAP Panel considers that the exposure of users through inhalation is likely.

No specific information was submitted. In the absence of data, the FEEDAP Panel is not in the position to conclude on the safety of calcium ascorbate for the user.

Ascorbyl palmitate

Based on the highest dusting potential measured (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013a), the FEEDAP Panel considers that the exposure of users through inhalation is likely.

The applicant provided one publication (Johnson et al., 2022), which indicates that ascorbyl palmitate is not irritant to skin and eyes, and is not a dermal sensitiser.

Sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate

Based on the highest dusting potential measured (see Section 3.1.5 ), the FEEDAP Panel considers that the exposure of users through inhalation is likely.

As no new studies were provided, the previous conclusion would still apply and, therefore, sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate should not be considered a skin and eye irritant nor a skin sensitiser.

Sodium ascorbyl phosphate

Based on the highest dusting potential measured (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2013a), the FEEDAP Panel considers that the exposure of users through inhalation is likely.

The skin irritation potential of sodium ascorbyl phosphate was tested in a study performed according to OECD TG 404, which showed that the active substance is not a skin irritant (UN GHS No Category). 38

The eye irritation potential of sodium ascorbyl phosphate was tested in a study performed according to OECD TG 405, which showed that the active substance is not an eye irritant (UN GHS No Category). 39

The skin sensitisation potential of sodium ascorbyl phosphate was tested in a study performed according to OECD TG 406, which showed that the active substance is not a skin sensitiser. 40

Conclusions on safety for the user

Ascorbic acid and sodium ascorbyl phosphate are not irritants to the skin or eyes and are not dermal sensitisers. Sodium ascorbate is not a skin sensitiser, but the Panel cannot conclude on its potential to be irritant to skin and eyes. In the absence of data, the FEEDAP Panel is not in the position to conclude on the safety of calcium ascorbate for the user. Ascorbyl palmitate is not irritant to skin and eyes and is not a dermal sensitiser. Sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate should not be considered a skin and eye irritant nor a skin sensitiser.

Efficacy

The present application for renewal of the authorisation does not include a proposal for amending or supplementing the conditions of the original authorisation that would have an impact on the efficacy of the additives. Therefore, there is no need for assessing the efficacy of the additives in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.

Post‐market monitoring

The FEEDAP Panel considers that there is no need for specific requirements for a post‐market monitoring plan other than those established in the Feed Hygiene Regulation 41 and good manufacturing practice.

CONCLUSIONS

The applicants have provided evidence that the additives currently in the market comply with the existing conditions of authorisation.

The use of ascorbic acid, sodium ascorbate, calcium ascorbate, ascorbyl palmitate, sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate and sodium ascorbyl phosphate remains safe for the target species, the consumer and the environment under the conditions of the current authorisation.

Ascorbic acid and sodium ascorbyl phosphate are not irritants to the skin or eyes and are not dermal sensitisers. Sodium ascorbate is not a skin sensitiser, but the Panel cannot conclude on its potential to be irritant to skin and eyes. In the absence of data, the FEEDAP Panel is not in the position to conclude on the safety of calcium ascorbate for the user. Ascorbyl palmitate is not irritant to skin and eyes and is not a dermal sensitiser. Sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate should not be considered a skin and eye irritant nor a skin sensitiser.

There is no need to assess the efficacy of the additives in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.

RECOMMENDATIONS

The FEEDAP Panel recommends amending the purity criteria for sodium calcium ascorbyl phosphate as follows: purity criteria: min. content of 35% of ascorbic acid equivalent, with min. 95% of ascorbic acid bound to phosphate.

The FEEDAP Panel recommends amending the purity criteria for sodium ascorbyl phosphate as follows: purity criteria: min. 95% of sodium ascorbyl phosphate with a min. content of 45% ascorbic acid equivalent.

The FEEDAP Panel also recommends amending the identity of the active substance of calcium ascorbate to calcium L‐ascorbate dihydrate.

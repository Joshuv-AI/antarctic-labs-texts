Doctors paying forward
Authors: Rebecca E Barchas
Published: 2026

I still recall the precise words from a conversation with my oldest brother in the early 1980’s, which was likely the most consequential conversation of my life. I had just started my first job after Psychiatry Residency, earning a higher salary than I had previously, and I asked him earnestly what I should do with the money I earn. “Give it to causes you believe in,” he said. “And you might want to consider establishing an Endowed Chair at a university.” I knew immediately that I would do just that. Over the next 40 years, I saved persistently with quiet determination towards that single goal. In 2021, I established an Endowed Chair at Case Western Reserve University to support translational psychiatry.

For decades, it felt simply like the right thing to do. Today it feels more urgent than ever before. We are living through a challenging time in which science, truth, research, and education are too often dismissed or trivialized. Research funding from once reliable sources is being slashed. Researchers are feeling an unprecedented level of job insecurity, and we are watching a brain drain unfold in real time, with talented scientists hesitating to come to the United States to study or work at all. There is not a medical school or university in this country that is not hurting. It is utterly disheartening and discouraging. But those of us who care must do more than curse under our breath. Those of us who have benefitted from a medical education must look into our hearts and pocketbooks and ask: “What can I do to make this a little better?”

We cannot replace everything that has been cut. But collectively we can have more impact than we might ever imagine. We can fund research and causes we know matter. We can put our money where our convictions are because all those projects need it and they will otherwise wither. Some hesitate to contribute financially because they believe their gift is too small. Yet no amount is too small, just as no amount is too large. Philanthropy is not defined by the amount of money. Rather, philanthropy is defined by its purpose. The consequences to healthcare, both in our country and globally, will be devastating if those of us who value science, research, and education do not act. We only lose the battle if we do nothing. And if not us, then who?

There is one more reason to give, which is how it benefits the giver. Generosity in service of something larger than yourself matters and is meaningful, purposeful, and quietly empowering. It is the gift that keeps giving as it becomes a positive feedback loop that benefits everyone it touches. I never imagined my seventies would be the best decade of my life, but this has happened because I can see what my giving has built and who it will reach long after I am gone.

Sometimes we need only to be reminded of what matters for a seed to be planted that leads to action. My brother planted that seed in my mind 45 years ago. I am determined to keep paying it forward.

The selective autophagic degradation of avian metapneumovirus subgroup C M2-2 protein via SQSTM1 suppresses viral replication
Authors: Lei Hou, Jinshuo Guo, Jianwei Zhou, Jue Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAvian metapneumovirus subgroup C (aMPV/C) is an emerging pathogen that causes acute respiratory infection in chickens and turkeys. Sequestosome 1 (SQSTM1), a selective autophagy receptor, regulates cellular activity or viral replication by recognizing ubiquitinated substrates. Here, we found that SQSTM1 expression inhibits aMPV/C replication through selective autophagy. In particular, SQSTM1 interacts with the aMPV/C M2-2 protein via its PB1 domain, and by recognizing a ubiquitinated lysine at position 67 of viral M2-2 protein. This recognition leads to the autophagic degradation of the aMPV/C M2-2 protein, suppressing viral replication.KEYWORDS: aMPV/C, SQSTM1, M2-2 protein, degradation, selective autophagy

Autophagy is involved in metabolism, reutilization of intracellular contents and maintains cellular homeostasis. The selective autophagy receptors (SARs) are key to selectively degrading specific cargo proteins, protein complexes and organelles. Sequestosome 1 (SQSTM1/p62), a SARs, interacts with microtubule-associated protein 1 light chain 3 (LC3) through its LC3-interacting region (LIR) and drives the formation of autophagosome and degradation of vesicle contents. Despite the role of SQSTM1 in autophagy being well documented, the functions of this protein in response to viral infections remain to be comprehensively explored. Our previous study has shown that SQSTM1 expression is substantially reduced in cells infected with aMPV/C. However, the regulatory and molecular relationship between SQSTM1 and aMPV/C replication were unknown.

We found that SQSTM1 expression negatively impact aMPV/C production in monkey Vero cells and conversely, the increase of viral replication mediated by siRNA targeting SQSTM1 (siSQSTM1) is reversed by overexpression of SQSTM1. To explore whether SQSTM1-mediated inhibitory role in aMPV/C replication is associated with autophagy, we knocked down autophagy-related gene 7 (ATG7), a gene essential for autophagy. This depletion impaired the antiviral effect of SQSTM1, suggesting that the autophagy is involved in the inhibitory effect of SQSTM1 on aMPV/C replication. Confocal microscopy-based screen of viral proteins possibly regulated by SQSTM1 revealed that aMPV/C M2-2, an important viral protein involving in aMPV/C replication, co-localized with SQSTM1 in the cytoplasm, suggesting that M2-2 may interact with SQSTM1. This hypothesis was confirmed by forward and reverse co-immunoprecipitation (co-IP) experiments, in which a specific band was detected in cells co-expressing M2-2 and SQSTM1. Additionally, we explored the effect of autophagy on viral M2-2 expression. Rapamycin, an autophagy inducer, promotes M2-2 degradation, while treatment with chloroquine, a lysosomal inhibitor, diminishes the reduction in M2-2 expression trigger by rapamycin. To exclude the non-specific effects of these compounds, siATG5- or siATG7-based knockdowns were used to inhibit autophagy, a treatment that reduced the M2-2 degradation stimulated by rapamycin, suggesting that autophagy is responsible for M2-2 turnover in autophagy-inducing conditions. The inhibition of viral replication by SQSTM1 overexpression through autophagy may be attributed to degrading viral proteins. To clarify the relationship between M2-2 degradation and SQSTM1 during autophagy activation, the co-IP experiment revealed an interaction between M2-2 and SQSTM1 in the cells co-expressing these two proteins and this interaction was enhanced by rapamycin treatment. We thus explored the effect of SQSTM1 on aMPV/C M2-2 expression. We found that viral M2-2 expression gradually decreased in concomitance with an increase in SQSTM1 expression. Moreover, rapamycin-triggered reduction of viral M2-2 was specifically attenuated by knockdown of SQSTM1, confirming that SQSTM1 is responsible for M2-2 turnover upon autophagy induction. Importantly, the knockout or the knockdown of ATG7 significantly disrupted the degradation of viral M2-2 by SQSTM1, and the confocal microscopy and co-IP experiments further showed that co-localization and interaction between M2-2 and LC3 is enhanced in the presence of SQSTM1, indicating that SQSTM1 plays a crucial role in autophagy-mediated M2-2 degradation.

We screened the structural domains of SQSTM1 responsible for the degradation of M2-2 and found that the deletion of SQSTM1 Phox and Bem1 (PB1) domain lost the ability of binding and degrading to aMPV/C M2-2. Subsequently, the effect of SQSTM1 lacking PB1 domain on viral replication was further explored. We found that the deletion of SQSTM1 PB1 domain restored the expression of viral proteins and viral titers compared to that in the cells expressing SQSTM1, indicating that the importance of PB1 domain of SQSTM1 in inhibiting aMPV/C replication. The recognition of ubiquitinated substrates by SQSTM1 is central in the degradation of substrates targeted by selective autophagy. We found that M2-2 was ubiquitinated with K63 ubiquitin chains in the cells overexpressing SQSTM1 and screen of the potential ubiquitinated sites reveled that the modified residue is the lysine at amino acid position 67. Its mutation into an arginine hampered M2-2 ubiquitination and degraded in the cells overexpressing SQSTM1. Importantly, the K67R mutation in M2-2 disrupted the interaction between M2-2 and SQSTM1, thereby inhibiting SQSTM1-mediated autophagic degradation of M2-2.

The close genetic and antigenic relatedness between aMPV/C and human metapneumovirus (hMPV) argues that viral M2-2 proteins between these two viruses have similar characteristics. Thus, we carried out co-IP and confocal microscopy experiments, which showed that hMPV M2-2 also interacts and co-localizes with SQSTM1. Moreover, the reduction of hMPV M2-2 gradually increased with the increase of SQSTM1 and this reduction was reversed by knockdown of ATG7, suggesting that SQSTM1 induced the degradation of hMPV M2-2 via selective autophagy.

In conclusion, our study revealed that SQSTM1 probably recognizes the ubiquitin appended to K67 in aMPV/C M2-2, which in turn recruit LC3 and possible other ATG components to mediate the sequestration of M2-2 into autophagosomes and subsequent degradation in lysosomes. The turnover of M2-2 by selective autophagy results in the inhibition of aMPV/C replication (Figure 1).

SQSTM1 suppresses aMPV/C replication through selective autophagy-mediated degradation of viral M2-2 protein[1]. SQSTM1 recognizes K67 in aMPV/C M2-2 and recruit LC3 and possible other ATG components, to trigger the sequestration of M2-2 into autophagoomes and its lysosomal degradation. Thus SQSTM1-depepndent selective autophagy has an antiviral function against aMPV/C.

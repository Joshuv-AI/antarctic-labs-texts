Gene association study between polycystic ovary syndrome and metabolic syndrome: a transcriptomic analysis and machine learning approach
Authors: Hongmei Xu, Lihua Mao, Wujian Huang, Qiuxiang Huang, Li Li, Yun Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPatients with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) often experience a range of metabolic comorbidities, suggesting a potential association between PCOS and metabolic syndrome (MetS). However, this potential link has not yet been fully elucidated.MethodsThis study employed transcriptomic analysis and machine learning techniques to identify key genes and signaling pathways associated with both PCOS and MetS. Background

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common endocrine disorder that affects about 5–10% of women of reproductive age, characterized by a combination of symptoms including irregular menstrual cycles, hyperandrogenism, and polycystic ovaries [1–3]. This condition not only impacts fertility but is also associated with various metabolic abnormalities, such as insulin resistance and obesity, which can lead to long-term health complications, including type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular diseases [4, 5]. The underlying mechanisms of PCOS involve genetic, hormonal, and environmental factors, with studies indicating that specific genes related to insulin signaling and steroidogenesis may play crucial roles in its pathophysiology [6, 7]. Furthermore, the dysregulation of metabolic pathways in PCOS patients highlights the importance of early diagnosis and intervention to mitigate associated risks [8].

Metabolic Syndrome (MetS) is a cluster of metabolic disorders that significantly increases the risk of cardiovascular diseases and type 2 diabetes, affecting approximately 25% of the adult population globally [9, 10]. It is characterized by a combination of abdominal obesity, hypertension, dyslipidemia, and insulin resistance, which can lead to severe health consequences if left unmanaged [11]. The phenotype manifestations of MetS are primarily driven by lifestyle factors, including poor diet and physical inactivity, which contribute to the development of insulin resistance and subsequent metabolic dysregulation [12]. The relationship between PCOS and MetS is particularly noteworthy, as women with PCOS are at a higher risk of developing MetS due to shared psychophysiology mechanisms, such as insulin resistance and chronic inflammation [13]. Understanding the interplay between these conditions is essential for developing effective prevention and treatment strategies.

PCOS is intrinsically linked to metabolic dysregulation, with insulin resistance serving as a central feature that drives both endocrine and metabolic disturbances in affected individuals [14]. Elevated anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) levels, a hallmark of PCOS pathophysiology, correlate with follicular dysregulation and may exacerbate hyperandrogenism, creating a vicious cycle that amplifies metabolic dysfunction [15, 16]. The syndrome’s metabolic phenotype manifests as dyslipidemia, visceral adiposity, and impaired glucose homeostasis, mirroring core components of metabolic syndrome (MetS) through shared mechanisms involving adipose tissue dysfunction and chronic low-grade inflammation [16, 17]. This metabolic overlap is particularly pronounced in PCOS patients with obesity, where adipokine dysregulation and ectopic lipid deposition potentiate both ovarian dysfunction and systemic insulin resistance [18, 19]. Crucially, the bidirectional interaction between PCOS and MetS extends beyond simple comorbidity, with emerging evidence suggesting that hyperinsulinemia directly stimulates ovarian androgen production while simultaneously promoting atherogenic lipid profiles [14, 20]. These intertwined pathways create a distinct metabolic-endocrine axis that predisposes women with PCOS to accelerated cardiovascular risk trajectories, necessitating integrated diagnostic and therapeutic approaches that address both reproductive and metabolic dimensions of the syndrome [14, 20].

This study aims to utilize transcriptomic analysis and machine learning techniques to identify key genes and signaling pathways associated with both PCOS and MetS. By integrating data from multiple gene expression datasets, we seek to uncover potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets that could facilitate early diagnosis and intervention for these conditions. The application of advanced computational methods will enhance our understanding of the molecular underpinnings of PCOS and MetS, ultimately contributing to improved clinical outcomes for affected individuals.

Methods

Data processing and computational analysis

All data used in this study are publicly available and were primarily sourced from the GEO (Gene Expression Omnibus, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) database. The whole-genome expression profiles and clinical data for PCOS were downloaded from the GEO database using the R package “GEOquery.”

The dataset GSE168404 was generated using the GPL16791 Illumina HiSeq 2500 (Homo sapiens) platform and includes a total of 40 samples, of which 30 are non-RNA-seq samples and 10 are RNA-seq samples. Among these, 5 granulosa cell samples from women with PCOS and 5 matched control granulosa cell samples were used for subsequent analysis. The dataset GSE138518 was generated using the GPL11154 Illumina HiSeq 2000 (Homo sapiens) platform and includes 3 granulosa cell samples from women with PCOS and 3 granulosa cell samples from normal controls. The dataset GSE193123 was generated using the GPL24676 Illumina NovaSeq 6000 (Homo sapiens) platform and includes a total of 6 samples, comprising 3 granulosa cell samples from women with PCOS and 3 granulosa cell samples from normal controls. All three datasets—GSE168404, GSE138518, and GSE193123—were included in the current study to enhance the analysis.

Whole-genome expression profiles and clinical data for MetS were downloaded from the GEO database using the R package “GEOquery.” Dataset GSE98895, generated with the GPL6947 Illumina HumanHT-12 V3.0 expression beadchip platform, includes 40 samples: 20 from healthy individuals and 20 from patients with metabolic syndrome.

In this analysis, we merged three datasets—GSE168404 (5 PCOS granulosa cell samples and 5 controls), GSE138518 (3 PCOS samples and 3 controls), and GSE193123 (3 PCOS samples and 3 controls)—to create a new dataset with 11 PCOS samples and 11 control samples. The merged dataset was utilized for all analyses in this study. We corrected batch effects from non-biological technical variations during the merging process using the ComBat method from the R package “sva” [PMID: 22257669]. We assessed the degree of correction using principal component analysis (PCA) (Figure S1).

The dataset GSE98895 (20 samples from normal subjects and 20 samples from patients with metabolic syndrome) was also used for all analyses in this study.

This study followed the data access policies of each database, and since the data came from publicly available datasets, no additional ethical approval was necessary.

Differential expression analysis

Differential expression analysis was conducted using the R package “limma” (version 3.50.0) [PMID: 25605792] to identify the key targets involved in the progression of PCOS. We identified differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the control group and the PCOS group using the following criteria: |log2Fold Change| >0.5 and a BH-adjusted p-value < 0.05. The identified genes are believed to play significant roles in the development of PCOS.

To identify the key targets involved in the progression of MetS, we also conducted differential expression analysis using the R package “limma” (version 3.50.0) [PMID: 25605792]. DEGs between the control group and the MetS group were identified using the same thresholds: |log2Fold Change| >0.5 and a BH-adjusted p-value < 0.05. The identified genes were considered to play significant roles in the development of MetS.

Finally, we extracted the intersection of the DEGs identified in both analyses. These intersecting genes were considered the core targets involved in the progression of both PCOS and MetS.

GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis

We performed Gene Ontology (GO) [PMID: 25428369] enrichment analysis to determine the biological significance of the intersecting genes. The analysis covered three categories: Biological Process (BP), Molecular Function (MF), and Cellular Component (CC). Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) [PMID: 10592173] is a bioinformatics resource used to identify enriched and significantly altered metabolic pathways within a specified gene list.

We conducted GO and KEGG enrichment analyses of the intersecting genes using the R package “clusterProfiler” (version 4.2.2) [PMID: 22455463]. The analyses set a significance threshold of p-value < 0.05.

Machine learning algorithms for screening hub genes

Machine learning algorithms were used to identify key genes in the PCOS cohort and the overlapping genes. We employed SVM-RFE (Support Vector Machine-Recursive Feature Elimination) to train subsets of features from various categories, gradually refining the feature set to identify the most predictive ones.

LASSO regression was performed using the R package “glmnet” (version 2.25.0) to compute and select linear models while preserving important variables. Binomial distribution variables were used for LASSO classification, and the model was established by selecting the minimum error.

Following LASSO regression, we conducted random forest analysis using the “RandomForest” function. We selected the mtry node value based on the smallest error and determined the ntree value at the point where the graph stabilized. The top 10 intersecting genes were selected as important genes based on the Mean Decrease Accuracy (MDA) and Mean Decrease Gini (MDG) scores from the random forest analysis.

Finally, the most significant feature genes were identified as hub genes by integrating the results from SVM-RFE, LASSO regression, and random forest analysis. These hub genes were considered to play critical roles in the progression of PCOS and were used for further analysis in this study.

Expression, diagnosis, and validation of hub genes

This study utilized the Wilcoxon rank sum test to analyze the differential expression of hub genes between the disease and control groups in both the PCOS and MetS cohorts. Next, the Pearson correlation method was used to assess the relationship between hub genes in the PCOS and MetS cohorts. Finally, we evaluated the diagnostic performance of hub genes in the PCOS and MetS cohorts using single-gene Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) analysis.

PPI network construction, genemania, and enrichment analysis

STRING database and PPI network construction

The STRING database (https://string-db.org) provides known and predicted protein-protein interactions (PPIs). These interactions encompass direct (physical) and indirect (functional) associations. They are derived from computational predictions, knowledge transfer between organisms, and data aggregated from other primary databases. This study constructed a PPI network using intersecting genes from the STRING database, which was subsequently visualized and annotated by coloring nodes according to PCOS DEGs, MetS DEGs, intersecting genes, and hub genes (Figure S2).

GeneMANIA and hub gene interaction network

GeneMANIA (http://genemania.org) is a web-based tool designed to predict relationships among functionally similar genes and central genes. It identifies various interactions, including protein-protein and protein-DNA interactions, pathways, physiological and biochemical reactions, co-expression, and co-localization [PMID: 20576703]. We constructed a PPI network for the hub genes using GeneMANIA to explore their functional relationships and interactions.

GO and KEGG enrichment analysis

The genes in the PPI network underwent Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses using the R package ‘clusterProfiler’ (version 4.2.2) [PMID: 22455463]. The GO enrichment analysis covered three categories: Biological Processes (BP), Molecular Functions (MF), and Cellular Components (CC). Meanwhile, the KEGG analysis identified significantly enriched pathways. A p-value < 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant for both analyses.

Enrichment analysis and correlation exploration of HALLMARK pathways

Single-sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA) [PMID: 36090900] extends Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) by calculating enrichment scores for each sample and gene set separately. Each ssGSEA enrichment score indicates how much the genes in a specific gene set are either upregulated or downregulated in that sample.

In this study, we used the msigdbr package (version 7.4.1) in R to download 50 hallmark gene sets from the MSigDB database (http://software.broadinstitute.org/gsea/msigdb) as reference gene sets. We used the ssGSEA function from the GSVA package (version 1.42.0) to calculate GSVA scores for each gene set in both the PCOS and MetS cohorts. The Wilcoxon rank sum test method was applied to compare the GSVA scores of different gene sets between the control group and the disease group. We visualized the results of the GSVA score comparisons using box plots.

Subsequently, the Pearson correlation method was used to calculate the correlation between hub genes and the 50 hallmark gene sets. The results were visualized using the ggplot2 package (version 3.4.0) in R. This analysis provided insights into the relationship between hub genes and hallmark pathways, highlighting potential mechanisms underlying PCOS and MetS.

Construction of the CeRNA network

Since the role of competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) in PCOS and MetS is still unclear, we created a ceRNA network to investigate potential regulatory interactions.

First, we identified the microRNAs (miRNAs) that target the hub genes by using reverse prediction with the miRTarBase database (https://mirtarbase.cuhk.edu.cn/~miRTarBase/miRTarBase_2022/php/index.php) [PMID: 34850920], starBase 2.0 (https://starbase.sysu.edu.cn/starbase2/index.php) [PMID: 24297251], and miRDB databases (https://mirdb.org/index.html). Next, we predicted the long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) associated with the miRNAs that target the hub genes.

Finally, we constructed the ceRNA network by integrating the relationships among lncRNAs, miRNAs, and hub genes, which offers insights into the potential regulatory mechanisms of ceRNA involved in PCOS and MetS.

Construction of the RBP-mRNA network

This study utilized the open-source platform StarBase (https://starbase.sysu.edu.cn/tutorialAPI.php#RBPTarget) to analyze ncRNA interactions. We investigated the associations between mRNA and RNA-binding proteins (RBPs) using CLIP-seq, degradome-seq, and RNA-RNA interaction data.

To identify key mRNA-RBP pairs related to disease, we applied the following criteria: p-value < 0.05, clusterNum ≥ 5, and clipExpNum ≥ 5. These criteria selected significant and reliable mRNA-RBP interactions.

Finally, the RBP-mRNA network was constructed and visualized using Cytoscape, providing insights into the regulatory roles of RBPs in the disease mechanisms.

Construction of the TF-mRNA network

Transcription factors (TFs) regulate transcription by recognizing specific DNA sequences, playing crucial roles in various biological processes. To explore the regulatory relationships between TFs and mRNAs, we utilized the hTFtarget database (http://bioinfo.life.hust.edu.cn/hTFtarget#!). This database offers a comprehensive collection of TF-target regulatory data from large-scale human transcription factor ChIP-Seq experiments, including 659 transcription factors, 7,190 samples, and 569 conditions (399 cell lines, 129 tissues and cell types, and 141 treatments).

We predicted shared transcription factors that regulate multiple genes using the hTFtarget database. We used these results to construct the TF-mRNA network, which sheds light on the transcriptional regulatory mechanisms underlying the biological processes of interest.

Screening hub gene-related drugs and molecular docking

This study aimed to identify potential drugs targeting hub genes by utilizing the DGIdb database (https://www.dgidb.org) [PMID: 37953380], which includes more than 10,000 genes, 20,000 drugs, and nearly 70,000 drug-gene interactions. We retrieved drugs that interact with hub genes from the DGIdb database and constructed a gene-drug interaction network to visualize the relationships between hub genes and their associated drugs.

To further evaluate the binding energy and interaction patterns between the drugs identified from the DGIdb database and the proteins encoded by hub genes, we employed AutoDock Vina 1.1.2 [PMID: 33674590], a computational protein-ligand docking software. First, we downloaded the SDF format files of the drugs from the PubChem database and then converted these files into mol2 format using OpenBabel (version 2.4.1). The mol2 files were then imported into AutoDockTools 4.2.6, where torsions were set, and the files were exported in pdbqt format. For the protein structures, the PDB files of the hub gene-encoded proteins were downloaded from the PDB database. If the PDB files were unavailable, the protein structures were retrieved from the AlphaFoldDB database. The downloaded protein structures were processed in PyMOL (version 2.5.0) to remove water molecules and ligands, followed by hydrogenation in AutoDockTools 4.2.6, and exported as pdbqt format files.

After preparing the drugs and proteins, molecular docking was performed using Autodock Vina 1.1.2, and the binding energy was calculated to evaluate the interaction between the receptor (protein) and the ligand (drug). A lower binding energy indicates stronger interactions and greater stability of the receptor-ligand complex. Finally, the docking results were visualized using Discovery Studio, which provided detailed insights into the interaction patterns, including hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic interactions, and other binding modes.

By integrating the gene-drug interaction network from the DGIdb database with the molecular docking results, this study comprehensively evaluated the potential therapeutic targets and mechanisms of action for hub gene-related drugs, providing valuable insights for further drug development and mechanistic research.

Statistical analysis

This study utilized R software version 4.1.2 for statistical analyses, employing the Pearson correlation test to assess the relationship between two parameters and the Wilcoxon rank sum test to compare differences between two groups, considering a two-sided p-value of less than 0.05 statistically significant.

A comparison between the PCOS group and the Control group identified 373 DEGs that exhibited statistically significant differences (|log2Fold Change| >0.5, p-value < 0.05). Screening hub genes using multiple machine learning algorithms

We used several machine learning algorithms, including LASSO regression, random forest, and SVM-RFE, to identify core target genes from the 14 overlapping genes.

Using LASSO regression analysis, we identified 8 hub genes (Fig. 3A and B). Using the random forest algorithm, we selected the top 10 genes based on their feature importance scores. These scores include Mean Decrease in Accuracy (MDA) and Mean Decrease in Gini (MDG). By combining the results from LASSO regression and random forest analyses, we identified 7 hub genes (Fig. 3C and D). Through the SVM-RFE method, we identified 13 key overlapping genes (Fig. 3E and F).

Construction of the hub gene regulatory network

To clarify how the hub genes function at the molecular level, we built an mRNA–miRNA–lncRNA interaction network. The analysis identified 4 hub genes (FOS, SCD, JDP2, and TRIB1) as target mRNAs. The mRNA–miRNA–lncRNA network we constructed includes 71 nodes: four mRNAs, 27 miRNAs, and their associated lncRNAs. It contains 293 edges that represent the interactions among these molecules. Discussion

PCOS is often associated with metabolic dysregulation, reflecting not only an endocrine disorder but also a significant comorbidity with MetS. The interplay between these two conditions substantially increases the risk of metabolic complications, notably cardiovascular diseases and type 2 diabetes. Studies indicate that approximately 50% of women with PCOS may display characteristics of MetS, which include insulin resistance, obesity, and dyslipidemia. This concurrent manifestation not only leads to more complex clinical presentations but also exacerbates the overall health burden for affected individuals, significantly raising the likelihood of long-term complications. Therefore, investigating the relationship between PCOS and MetS, and identifying potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets, is crucial for improving patient quality of life. In the following sections, we will explore the interactions between these two disorders and their clinical implications.

In this study, we identified hub genes that play crucial roles in the development and progression of both PCOS and MetS. Our integrated analysis identified 14 conserved genes at the intersection of 373 PCOS-specific and 516 MetS-associated DEGs, revealing molecular convergence between these syndromes. The stringent overlap highlights shared pathophysiology, particularly through apoptosis and TNF-α signaling. These pathways mechanistically link ovarian dysfunction with metabolic dysregulation via three key mechanisms: (1) TNFRSF1A-mediated NF-κB activation driving insulin resistance; (2) BAX/CASP3 overexpression inducing granulosa cell apoptosis; (3) SCD-regulated lipid droplet formation promoting visceral adiposity. The multi-algorithm consensus (LASSO/RF/SVM-RFE intersection) prioritized six hub genes showing cross-tissue dysregulation: FOS (AP-1 complex, inflammatory priming), SCD (Δ9-desaturase, lipid remodeling), and ZNF331 (zinc finger regulator, AUC = 0.86) exhibited the strongest diagnostic potential, outperforming conventional biomarkers like AMH (AUC = 0.68) in ROC analysis. Multiple studies have shown that the TNF and IL-17 signaling pathways play significant roles in the pathogenesis of PCOS. Our GO and KEGG enrichment analyses also revealed significant enrichment of these pathways, highlighting their critical position in the immune and inflammatory disorders associated with PCOS. Relevant research further confirms that through a systematic analysis of the granulosa cell transcriptome, the TNF and IL-17 pathways are crucial regulatory players in the occurrence and development of PCOS [21, 22].

Moreover, the application of machine learning algorithms to filter hub genes has provided a robust framework for identifying key players in the molecular landscape of PCOS and MetS. The six hub genes (DPYSL4, FOS, JDP2, SCD, TRIB1, ZNF331) not only exhibited significant differential expression but also demonstrated strong correlations with various signaling pathways, suggesting their potential as biomarkers for early diagnosis. The ROC curve analysis further validated their diagnostic efficacy, with AUC values exceeding 0.7, indicating a strong discriminatory power.

The six hub genes identified in this study—DPYSL4, FOS, JDP2, SCD, TRIB1, and ZNF331— form a coordinated network driving PCOS-MetS comorbidity through distinct yet interconnected mechanisms. DPYSL4 modulates ovarian follicular atresia via ERK1/2-mediated granulosa cell apoptosis, while simultaneously exacerbating hepatic steatosis through PPARγ-dependent lipid accumulation [23]. FOS, a core AP-1 component, exhibits tissue-specific duality: ovarian upregulation activates IL-6/STAT3 signaling to promote chronic inflammation, whereas adipose-specific suppression disrupts adiponectin secretion, directly linking reproductive dysfunction to metabolic derangement [24]. The IL-6/JAK/STAT3 signaling pathway plays a crucial role in the pathogenesis of PCOS, and recent studies have further confirmed this. In the work by Berenji et al. [25], which systematically analyzed the transcriptome of granulosa cells and the ceRNA network in polycystic ovary syndrome, the IL-6/STAT3 signaling pathway was significantly enriched. This finding further substantiates its key regulatory role in the inflammatory response and disease progression of PCOS. JDP2 emerges as a master epigenetic regulator, with ChIP-seq validation showing enhanced binding at promoters of lipogenic genes and cell cycle inhibitors, reconciling hyperandrogenism with impaired folliculogenesis [26]. SCD (stearoyl-CoA desaturase), the most therapeutically promising target, demonstrates dual-phase lipid remodeling: ovarian SCD suppression elevates saturated fatty acids, inducing ER stress, while hepatic overexpression drives VLDL overproduction [27, 28]. TRIB1 pseudokinase activity coordinates glucose-lipid crosstalk via AKT/FOXO1 axis modulation, explaining concurrent insulin resistance and β-cell dysfunction [29]. Lastly, ZNF331 stands out as a multi-compartment biomarker, with serum levels correlating with both ovarian volume and HOMA-IR, outperforming traditional markers like SHBG [29–32]. The findings from this study not only elucidate the roles of these hub genes in the pathophysiology of PCOS and MetS but also pave the way for future research aimed at developing targeted therapies that could mitigate the impact of these conditions on women’s health.

Recent transcriptomic and multi-omics studies have revealed the complex molecular mechanisms underlying PCOS and its metabolic comorbidities. Numerous studies indicate the central roles of pro-inflammatory signaling pathways (such as TNF-α and IL-17), steroid hormone biosynthesis, and lipid metabolism abnormalities in PCOS. Research by Zanjirband et al. demonstrated that the transcriptomic features of granulosa cells in PCOS patients show significant enrichment in pathways related to TNF-α, IL-17, and cytochrome P450-mediated steroid synthesis, suggesting a close association between inflammatory stress and metabolic disturbances [22]. Additionally, dysregulation of the granulosa cell apoptosis pathway has been experimentally validated, with the typical upregulation of the p53 pathway and its downstream factors leading to abnormal follicular development and fertility issues [33].

Furthermore, ceRNA networks (such as XIST-related networks) and non-coding RNAs play significant regulatory roles in TNF-α/NF-κB-mediated inflammation and metabolic reprogramming. The study by Berenji et al. indicated that the XIST-mediated ceRNA axis impacts both inflammatory signaling and immune metabolic networks in PCOS [25]. Systematic reviews emphasize that abnormal expression of lipid metabolism factors (such as SREBP1) directly contributes to the increased risk of metabolic syndrome and long-term complications associated with PCOS [34]. These findings further reinforce the central role of lipid/cholesterol metabolism and the apoptosis-inflammation loop in the pathogenesis of PCOS.The interplay between hub genes and the signaling pathways associated with PCOS and MetS reveals critical insights into the underlying immune mechanisms involved in these conditions. Our study identified 14 intersecting DEGs between PCOS and MetS, which were significantly enriched in pathways related to immune responses, such as the TNF signaling pathway and IL-17 signaling pathway. These findings suggest that the dysregulation of these pathways may contribute to the chronic inflammation observed in both syndromes, highlighting the role of immune system alterations in their pathogenesis. The identification of hub genes, including DPYSL4, FOS, and JDP2, further emphasizes their potential as biomarkers for diagnosing and understanding the immunological aspects of PCOS and MetS. The significant expression differences of these hub genes between affected individuals and controls underscore their relevance in the immune response and metabolic dysregulation associated with these disorders. This research not only enhances our understanding of the immunological landscape in PCOS and MetS but also opens avenues for targeted therapeutic interventions aimed at modulating immune responses, potentially leading to improved management strategies for patients suffering from these interconnected conditions. The implications of these findings are profound, as they suggest that addressing immune dysregulation could be a pivotal strategy in mitigating the adverse effects of PCOS and MetS on women’s health, particularly in preventing long-term complications such as type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular diseases [35–37].

Previous analyses have suggested the potential therapeutic effects of these targets for the comorbidities of PCOS and MetS. To further explore this, we utilized the DGIDB database to identify major drugs interacting with these targets. The results indicated the potential binding of five small-molecule drugs to FOS and SCD. Molecular validation confirmed the strong binding affinity of these targets to the identified drugs. The docking results demonstrate that both FOS and SCD proteins exhibit strong binding affinities with their respective small molecule compounds (COMPOUND (R)-26 and TIOCARLIDE), as indicated by their binding energies (-4.1 kcal/mol and − 8.4 kcal/mol, respectively).

The identification of druggable targets among the hub genes, particularly FOS and SCD, presents a promising avenue for therapeutic intervention in PCOS and MetS. FOS, a member of the immediate early gene family, is known to play a pivotal role in cellular proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis. The compound COMPOUND (R)-26, which demonstrated the strongest correlation with FOS, has been previously reported to exhibit anti-inflammatory and anti-cancer properties, suggesting its potential utility in modulating the pathological processes associated with PCOS and MetS. The docking simulation results, indicating a binding energy of -4.1 kcal/mol, further support the feasibility of this interaction, suggesting that COMPOUND (R)-26 may effectively target FOS to influence downstream signaling pathways involved in these conditions. This interaction warrants further investigation to elucidate the specific mechanisms through which COMPOUND (R)-26 may exert its effects on FOS-mediated pathways.

Similarly, SCD, which is integral to lipid metabolism, was found to correlate strongly with TIOCARLIDE, a compound recognized for its role in modulating lipid profiles and exhibiting anti-diabetic effects. The binding energy of -8.4 kcal/mol observed in the docking simulations indicates a robust interaction between SCD and TIOCARLIDE, suggesting that this compound may serve as a therapeutic agent to ameliorate metabolic dysregulation in PCOS and MetS. Given the established link between lipid metabolism and the pathophysiology of these syndromes, TIOCARLIDE’s potential to influence SCD activity could provide a novel approach to managing the metabolic aspects of PCOS and MetS.

This study has certain limitations. Firstly, the sample size is relatively limited because it relies on publicly available database resources. This limitation may affect the generalizability and applicability of the research conclusions. Additionally, the study lacked a separate validation cohort, as all models were established and evaluated using the training cohort. This limitation may restrict the applicability of the findings to other populations or sample sets. The lack of an independent validation cohort is mainly due to the limited availability of publicly accessible transcriptomic data samples. Additionally, differences in sample characteristics and sequencing platforms across databases make it difficult to construct a fully independent and representative validation set. In the future, we plan to collect larger multi-center clinical samples and establish an independent validation cohort to externally verify the models and key genes identified, thus improving the robustness and clinical relevance of our findings. Moreover, this study lacks direct validation of the key genes and their functions through in vitro or animal experiments. Future work will focus on experimental research to clarify their biological mechanisms. Lastly, potential batch effects from data integration may affect the accuracy of some analytical results.

Conclusions

Despite these limitations, our analysis successfully identified six key hub genes (DPYSL4, FOS, JDP2, SCD, TRIB1, ZNF331) that exhibit significant differential expression between PCOS and MetS compared to control groups. These findings suggest that these hub genes may play crucial roles in the pathogenesis of both conditions and could serve as potential biomarkers for diagnosis and therapeutic targets. Future studies should focus on validating these results in larger, more diverse populations and exploring the clinical implications of these hub genes in the management of PCOS and MetS.

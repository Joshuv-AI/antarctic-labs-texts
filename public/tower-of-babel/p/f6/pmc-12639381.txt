Evaluation of genes and molecular pathways involved in ferroptosis in breast cancer: A systems biology and bioinformatics approach
Authors: Arezu Heydari, Javad Yaghmoorian Khojini, Mohammad Javad Hayati, Fatemeh Khara, Seyed Mohammad Sadra Abootorabi, Hamed Manoochehri, Mohammad Javad Mousavi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBreast cancer (BC) is a leading cause of cancer mortality worldwide. Ferroptosis, an unique form of iron-dependent cell death, has emerged as a promising therapeutic target to overcome drug resistance. This study aimed to identify key ferroptosis-related genes and their regulatory pathways that are dysregulated in BC.MethodsThrough a systems biology approach, we analyzed gene expression data from two independent GEO datasets. We identified differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in tumor versus normal tissues and intersected them with a curated database of ferroptosis regulators to define a high-confidence list of Ferroptosis-related DEGs (FeffDEGs). Subsequently, we performed functional enrichment, protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis to identify hub genes, and explored their potential regulatory miRNAs and transcription factors (TFs).ResultsWe identified 73 FeffDEGs significantly enriched in pathways related to oxidative stress and lipid metabolism. Seven hub genes were pinpointed, including the upregulated oncogene EZH2 and the downregulated tumor suppressor PTEN. Further analysis revealed that these hub genes are potentially regulated by key molecules such as hsa-miR-137, hsa-miR-429 and the transcription factor AR.ConclusionsOur findings highlight that hub genes like EZH2 and PTEN are potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets for modulating ferroptosis in breast cancer. These results provide a strong foundation for future experimental validation to translate computational insights into clinical applications.

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) is one of the most common and lethal malignancies affecting women globally [1]. Despite significant advances in treatment, challenges such as therapeutic resistance persist, highlighting the need for novel strategies to target the disease's complex molecular underpinnings [2]. In recent years, ferroptosis, a distinct form of regulated cell death characterized by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation, has emerged as a prominent area of focus in cancer biology [3]. Inducing ferroptosis in cancer cells is now recognized as a promising therapeutic approach, especially for overcoming resistance to conventional therapies [4].

The susceptibility of cancer cells to this process is controlled by a network of ferroptosis-related genes (FRGs) that govern iron metabolism, oxidative stress, and lipid biology [5,6]. Elucidating how these genes and their associated pathways are dysregulated in breast cancer is critical for identifying new therapeutic vulnerabilities [7,8]. While individual FRGs have been studied, a comprehensive, systems-level investigation to identify the most critical genes and networks driving ferroptosis in BC has been lacking.

This study addresses this gap by employing a systems biology and bioinformatics approach to analyze large-scale gene expression data to investigate the intricate molecular underpinnings of breast cancer. Our objective was to identify a robust signature of differentially expressed FRGs in BC, characterize their associated biological functions and protein-protein interaction networks, and pinpoint central hub genes that could serve as high-priority candidates for future validation as biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

Materials and methods

Data selection

In this study, in order to analyze gene expression in breast cancer, two microarray datasets including 121 samples of GSE42568 and 433 samples of GSE54002 were extracted from a database called Gene Expression Omnibus or GEO (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). The selection of two datasets from independent studies was a deliberate choice to ensure that the identified common gene signature is robust and generalizable across different patient cohorts. For further analysis, 784 genes related to ferroptosis were downloaded from the FerrDb website (http://www.zhounan.org/ferrdb/). This website is as a database of markers and regulators of ferroptosis, and also as the first ferroptosis-disease associations. These ferroptosis-related genes are classified into three different subgroups of suppressors, markers and drivers.

Screening of DEGs

To screen for DEGs, GEO2R web-based online software for each of the datasets was employed to detect the genes that showed differential expression between normal and cancerous samples. Cutoff criteria (|log fold-change (logFC)| greater than 1.0 and adjusted p-value less than 0.05) were considered to ascertain the DEGs between tumor tissues and non-tumor tissues.

This independent analysis of each dataset was a deliberate strategy to mitigate potential confounding batch effects prior to identifying the common differentially expressed genes. The lists of DEGs from both datasets (GSE42568 and GSE54002) were then intersected with the list of 784 ferroptosis-related genes. The overlapping genes common to all three lists were identified via Venn diagram analysis as the final set of Ferroptosis-related DEGs (FeffDEGs) used for subsequent analyses.

Functional enrichment analysis of common genes

Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis was performed for the overlapping genes obtained using David (https://david.ncifcrf.gov/) online software. GO terms and KEGG pathways were statistically significant with p-values <0.05.

Identifying hub genes by protein-protein interactions (PPI) network analysis

Also, in this study, STRING (https://string-db.org) an online biological database dedicated to PPI prediction, was analyzed to identify the network between the proteins of its 73 common Ferroptosis-Related DEGs (FeffDEGs). This PPI network was constructed from interactions with a medium confidence score of 0.4 that were considered statistically significant. Using a powerful bioinformatics platform called Cytoscape software, the extracted PPI network of the STRING were visually analyzed. After that, the CytoHaba plugin was used to identify hub genes using the degree, closeness, and betweenness centrality methods, and the intersection of hub genes among these methods was determined as hub gene.

Validation of hub genes

In this step, in order to the confirm expression level of hub genes was used from UALCAN (https://ualcan.path.uab.edu/) software and the mRNA expression patterns of the hub genes were analyzed across normal and patient groups. UALCAN is a interactive and comprehensive web resource for analyzing cancer OMICS data (from TCGA and the GTEx projects). The information available in this software and relative clinical pathological parameters are used to study the transcriptional expression of potential genes and compare the transcriptional expression of normal samples and tumors.

Evaluation of associations between the hub genes with miRNAs and TFs

In order to analyze the microRNAs (miRNAs) that presumably target BC-related hub genes, the Enrichr dataset-linked miRTarBase (https://maayanlab.cloud/Enrichr/) was used. miRTarBase provides information about experimentally-validated miRNA-target interactions (MTIs). Based on adjusted p-values <0.05, the top 5 miRNAs targeting of hub genes related to BC were ranked and selected.

Moreover, the TRRUST web server (https://maayanlab.cloud/Enrichr/) available in the Enrichr software was used to search for transcription factors (TFs) that may potentially regulation the expression of hub genes related to BC (ranked based on adjusted p-values <0.05).

Results

Identification ferroptosis-related DEGs in BC

In the current investigation, two gene expression datasets, including GSE42568 and GSE54002, associated with BC to identify DEGs, from the GEO database were selected. Differential analysis to identify DEGs from each microarray datasets was performed by using the GEO2R tool, with the selection criteria |log fold-change (logFC)| > 1.0 and an adjusted p-value <0.05. After differential analysis and filtering, 4015 and 2529 DEGs from GSE42568 and GSE54002 datasets (Supplementary File), respectively, for subsequent analysis, with 1893 and 830 up-regulated and 2122 and 1699 down-regulated genes were identified and are presented as volcano plots of the up and downregulated genes (Fig. 1(A and B)). The “up-regulated” or “down-regulated” polarity of genes in this study is described as tumor vs. normal. In our analyses, we used the FerrDb website to extract 784 well-defined genes related to ferroptosis. In order to identification of FeffDEGs in microarray datasets with differentially expressed between BC samples and normal samples, online venn software were used to screened out intersection of the DEGs with ferroptosis-related gene, which a total of 73 FeffDEGs were identified (Fig. 1(C)). To elucidate the collective biological functions of the 73 FeffDEGs, we performed GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses. The GO analysis confirmed that these genes are significantly involved in processes central to ferroptosis. The most prominent Biological Process terms were related to the regulation of programmed cell death, cellular response to stress, and response to lipids. Specifically, EZH2 expression was significantly higher in TNBC and HER2+ tumors compared to normal and Luminal subtypes (p < 0.0001). PTEN, JUN, EGR1, and EGFR were significantly downregulated across all cancer subtypes compared to normal tissues (p < 0.0001 in most comparisons), with JUN and EGR1 showing the most dramatic reduction. PTGS2 also showed significantly reduced expression in cancer subtypes, particularly in Luminal and HER2-positive tumors (p < 0.001). In contrast, LOX expression did not differ significantly between most subtypes, except for a mild but significant reduction between Luminal and TNBC (p = 0.015).

These results indicate that the expression of several hub genes is subtype-specific and statistically significant, highlighting their potential as biomarkers for molecular classification of breast cancer.

Discussion

Despite advancements in detection and management, breast cancer remains a major health concern globally. The rising occurrences of this disease emphasize the importance of improving diagnostic procedures and treatment approaches [9]. In order to improve diagnoses accuracy and treatment results for patients with breast cancer; it is essential to gain an understanding of the mechanisms involved. Gene expression profiling serves as an technique that shows great promise in deciphering the intricate nature of this illness and offers substantial benefits for practical use, in clinical settings [10].

As we sought to uncover new potential therapeutic targets, we conducted a thorough bioinformatics analysis centered on ferroptosis-related differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in breast cancer. Using two microarray datasets (GSE42568 and GSE54002) from the GEO database, we successfully identified 73 DEGs associated with ferroptosis. The differences in expression between BC tissues and normal tissues provide us with important insights into the molecular mechanisms that drive the development of BC and highlight possible new avenues for treatment.

The increasing number of cases of this illness highlights the importance of treatment approaches that focus on its molecular details [11]. One of the approaches being considered is ferroptosis. A controlled cell death process marked by the buildup of iron and lipid peroxidation. Which has been identified as an area, for cancer study advancement in times [12]. Exploring the significance of ferroptosis in breast cancer could lead to treatment options and enhanced results, for patients [13]. Our detailed analysis, in bioinformatics uncovered that 73 genes related to ferroptosis show expression in breast cancer tissues and play roles in important biological functions like regulating oxidative stress and lipid metabolism. This irregularity is essential for the development of breast cancer as it changes the conditions and promotes the advancement of cancer. By exploring into the processes linked to ferroptosis it is possible to discover targets, for treatment that target cancer cells effectively while preserving normal tissues to minimize side effects [14]. Central to our findings are key ferroptosis-related DEGs that play vital roles in the modulation of tumor cell survival and death. Notable among these are hub genes such as EZH2, PTEN, JUN, LOX, EGR1, PTGS2, and EGFR. Our analysis of the protein-protein interaction (PPI) network revealed how these genes interact within the tumor microenvironment, establishing a complex regulatory network that influences ferroptosis and cancer progression. Specifically, the upregulation of EZH2 in BC tissues suggests its role in promoting cancer cell survival and proliferation [15], while downregulated genes like PTEN and JUN may impair apoptosis and cell cycle regulation [16].

A notable insight from our network analysis is that the identified hub genes likely modulate ferroptosis sensitivity through both upstream signaling control and downstream effector mechanisms, rather than acting as isolated markers. For example, loss of the tumor suppressor PTEN has been shown to activate PI3K–AKT signaling, inhibit GSK3β and increase NRF2 activity, thereby upregulating the cystine transporter SLC7A11/xCT and conferring resistance to ferroptosis in multiple tumor contexts [17,18]. Similarly, oncogenic receptor signaling such as EGFR has been reported to interact with or support membrane localization/stability of SLC7A11 and to engage MAPK/AKT-linked pathways that can promote SLC7A11-mediated cystine uptake, providing a plausible mechanism for ferroptosis resistance downstream of EGFR activation [19]. At the transcriptional level, AP-1 family member JUN (and AP-1 activity more broadly) has been reported to repress GPX4 transcription in certain settings, thereby modulating cellular antioxidant capacity and ferroptotic susceptibility [20]. Finally, PTGS2/COX2 is consistently observed as a downstream marker/effector induced in cells undergoing GPX4-dependent lipid peroxidation, and is widely used as a readout of ferroptotic lipid peroxidation in experimental models [18,21]. Collectively, these literature-supported links, together with the pathway and PPI context presented in our manuscript, are consistent with a model in which our hub genes form an upstream–downstream regulatory network that converges on canonical ferroptosis regulators (e.g., SLC7A11, GPX4) and thereby fine-tunes breast cancer cell sensitivity to ferroptosis. We emphasize that these mechanistic inferences are based on prior functional studies and our transcriptomic/network analyses; direct causal validation in breast cancer models (e.g., targeted perturbation of individual hubs with readouts for SLC7A11/GPX4 expression and ferroptotic susceptibility) will be important next steps.

Our investigation into the molecular subtypes of breast cancer further refines the clinical relevance of our findings. The observation that EZH2 is significantly overexpressed in TNBC and HER2-positive subtypes is particularly noteworthy, as these are historically more aggressive and difficult-to-treat forms of the disease [22,23]. This suggests that the ferroptosis resistance conferred by EZH2 may be a key mechanism contributing to the aggressive phenotype of these subtypes. Consequently, therapeutic strategies that inhibit EZH2 to induce ferroptosis could represent a promising and highly targeted approach for these specific patient populations, particularly for TNBC, which currently lacks broadly effective targeted therapy options [24]. The consistent downregulation of tumor suppressors like PTEN across all subtypes further solidifies their fundamental role in breast carcinogenesis [25], potentially creating a cellular environment that is broadly susceptible to ferroptotic modulation if other key nodes, such as EZH2, are targeted.

The practical consequences of these findings are considerable. Specifically, targeting ferroptosis-related DEGs presents a new treatment strategy for breast cancer, especially for the cases with no response to standard therapy [26]. The intricate interactions between ferroptosis-related genes and other molecular players, as illustrated by the PPI network, reveal potential drug targets that could enhance the efficacy of existing treatments. For instance, modulation of the PPAR signaling pathway and MAPK signaling pathway, both implicated in ferroptosis, could sensitize BC cells to ferroptotic agents, improving treatment outcomes [27]. Moreover, the expression patterns of ferroptosis-related genes could serve as diagnostic markers, providing insights into disease progression and treatment response [28]. The validation of hub genes like EZH2 and PTEN in clinical samples supports their potential use in clinical settings as biomarkers [29,30]. For example, high EZH2 levels may indicate aggressive tumor behavior, suggesting that therapies aimed at inducing ferroptosis could be particularly effective in specific breast cancer subtypes [23]. Despite our efforts establishing groundwork for therapies that rely on ferroptosis, it is apparent that there are several challenges that live to overcome. The intricate nature of ferroptosis, as well as its crosstalk with other forms of cell death such as apoptosis or necroptosis, makes it difficult to understand the complete picture without further studies. The discovery of ferroptosis inducers that are particularly directed towards cancer cells and do not affect normal tissues is critical for translating these findings into clinical practice. Moreover, investigating the crosstalk between hub genes and microRNAs, transcription factors will help to elucidate the complexity of regulatory interaction in control of ferroptosis. Further studies should also focus on strategies combining targeting of ferroptosis with conceptual approaches targeting other pathways of cancer initiation and progression. Combining ferroptosis inducers with standard chemotherapy or immunotherapy may help overcome resitance mechanisms to help advance breast cancer therapeutics [[31], [32], [33]]. Furthermore, understanding the tumor microenvironment's role in regulating ferroptosis especially the influence of immune cells and extracellular matrix components could refine our therapeutic strategies [34].

Despite the robustness of our bioinformatic approach, it is important to acknowledge the inherent limitations of this study. Our findings are based on in silico analyses of publicly available transcriptomic data and, while they generate strong hypotheses, they do not establish causal relationships. The mechanistic connections we have proposed, along with the regulatory roles of the identified hub genes, miRNAs, and TFs, require rigorous experimental validation in breast cancer cell lines and patient-derived models. Future wet-lab studies should focus on confirming these findings, for instance, by using siRNA to modulate the expression of hub genes like EZH2 and assessing the subsequent changes in cellular sensitivity to ferroptosis inducers like Erastin. Such functional studies will be essential to translate these computational insights into clinically viable therapeutic strategies.

Our study offers novel insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying ferroptosis in breast cancer, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target. By restoring ferroptosis through the modulation of specific DEGs, miRNAs, and TFs, we may develop innovative strategies for treating breast cancer, particularly in overcoming therapeutic resistance. Continued experimental validation and clinical studies will be essential in establishing ferroptosis-based therapies as viable options for improving patient outcomes in breast cancer treatment.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our systems biology analysis has successfully identified a high-confidence signature of ferroptosis-related hub genes and examining the pathways activated by hub genes can be useful and effective in prognostic prediction, designing appropriate treatment strategies and targeting therapy in breast cancer. The identification of key hub genes, such as EZH2 and PTEN, and their association with critical signaling pathways like MAPK and PPAR, provides valuable new insights into the molecular mechanisms governing ferroptosis in this disease. Our findings suggest that these hub genes may serve as potential biomarkers for diagnosis and prognosis. Furthermore, they represent promising therapeutic targets for developing novel strategies aimed at modulating ferroptosis to overcome treatment resistance. It is crucial, however, to emphasize that these results are based on computational analyses. Therefore, future experimental studies are essential to functionally validate these findings and to explore the full therapeutic potential of targeting these pathways in a clinical setting.

Pembrolizumab combined with nab-paclitaxel and platinum in recurrent/metastatic HNSCC: efficacy, safety, and survival predictive model☆
Authors: L Tang, R Gao, J Yao, W Zhang, Y Zhang, Y Zhang, H Lu, C An, L Gui
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRecurrent/metastatic head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (R/M HNSCC) remains a therapeutic challenge. This study evaluated the triplet combination of pembrolizumab, nab-paclitaxel, and platinum in R/M HNSCC and developed a practical predictive model for treatment stratification.Patients and methodsIn this single-arm phase 2 study (NCT04857164), treatment-naïve patients with R/M HNSCC received pembrolizumab (200 mg), nab-paclitaxel (260 mg/m2), and cisplatin (75 mg/m2) [or carboplatin area under the curve (AUC) 5] once every 3 weeks for six cycles, followed by pembrolizumab maintenance. Additionally, we developed a clinical parameter-based random forest predictive model and conducted exploratory flow cytometry analysis and plasma proteomic profiling.ResultsFrom April 2021 to February 2025, a total of 148 patients with R/M HNSCC were enrolled. At a median follow-up of 23.0 months [95% confidence interval (CI) 19.4–29.6], objective response rate was 64.2% (95/148) and disease control rate was 93.2% (138/148). Median progression-free survival (PFS) was 12.7 months [95% confidence interval (CI) 10.2–14.7) and median overall survival (OS) was 21.8 months (95% CI 18.9–35.7). Hypothyroidism was the most common immune-related adverse event (27%, 40/148), with 39 grade 1-2 and one grade 3 case. The predictive model based on seven clinical parameters achieved excellent predictive accuracy, with time-dependent AUCs of 0.826-0.906 (training) and 0.753-0.919 (test) for PFS prediction, and 0.682-0.799 (training) and 0.784-0.908 (test) for OS. Higher model scores delineated a high-risk phenotype marked by nutritional depletion and chronic inflammation activation, mechanistically explaining the associated poor outcomes.ConclusionsThis study demonstrated encouraging efficacy and a manageable safety profile of pembrolizumab–nab-paclitaxel–platinum combination in R/M HNSCC. Furthermore, we established a predictive model using routine clinically accessible parameters, offering a practical and cost-effective tool for treatment stratification.Key words: biomarker, chemoimmunotherapy, immune checkpoint blockade, predictive model, recurrent or metastatic head and neck squamous cell carcinoma

Introduction

Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC), encompassing cancers of the oral cavity, oropharynx, hypopharynx, and larynx, represents the seventh most prevalent malignant cancer worldwide, with China bearing the highest burden.1,2 Over 60% of patients with HNSCC present with advanced-stage disease at diagnosis.3 Despite therapeutic advances, the prognosis for recurrent or metastatic (R/M) HNSCC remains dismal, with a 5-year overall survival (OS) rate of merely 5%-10% under conventional chemotherapy regimens.4,5

Immunotherapy, particularly immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), has reshaped the therapeutic landscape for R/M HNSCC. Landmark trials such as KEYNOTE-048 demonstrated that pembrolizumab monotherapy extended median OS from 10.7 to 14.9 months in patients with programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) combined positive score (CPS) ≥20. Furthermore, pembrolizumabin in combination with a platinum and 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) achieved a 5-year OS rate of 16.0% compared with 5.2% with the EXTREME regimen.4,5 Similarly, CheckMate 141 reported a survival benefit with nivolumab in platinum-refractory patients.6 Given the challenges of continuous 5-FU infusion and its toxicity, exploration of alternative non-5-FU-based chemotherapy regimens is clinically warranted.7 The KEYNOTE-B10 study established the efficacy of paclitaxel combined with pembrolizumab and carboplatin in R/M HNSCC.8 Emerging evidence further suggests that nab-paclitaxel may potentiate ICI efficacy more effectively than conventional paclitaxel through tumor immune microenvironment remodeling and enhanced antitumor immune responses.9

Clinical responses to ICIs exhibit heterogeneity. Although PD-L1 expression represents the most extensively validated predictive biomarker to date, its clinical application faces substantial limitations. PD-L1 immunohistochemistry (IHC) demonstrates methodological variability due to different antibody clones and scoring systems. Furthermore, its reliability is compromised by significant intertumoral heterogeneity.10 Moreover, PD-L1 exhibits limited predictive accuracy in combined immunochemotherapy. Currently, the field lacks robust, clinically practical predictors for effective patient stratification, underscoring the critical need to identify more reliable predictive tools for R/M HNSCC management.

Based on this evidence, we initiated a single-center, phase II trial evaluating first-line pembrolizumab combined with nab-paclitaxel and platinum in patients with R/M HNSCC. Preliminary analysis of 67 enrolled patients revealed promising efficacy and manageable safety profile with this triplet regimen.11 We assessed the triplet regimen in an expanded cohort of 147 patients with R/M HNSCC. Beyond evaluating efficacy and safety, we developed practical predictive tools from clinically accessible parameters for patient stratification. Through integrated lymphocyte subsets analysis and proteomic profiling, we further elucidated the underlying mechanisms. These findings advance both treatment approaches and biomarker discovery in HNSCC immunochemotherapy.

Patients and methods

Patient specimens and clinical characteristics

This single-arm, prospective phase II study (ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT04857164) was conducted at the National Cancer Center/National Clinical Research Center for Cancer/Cancer Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences (CHCAMS) between 30 April 2021 and 27 February 2025. By the last follow-up date, 27 February 2025, the median follow-up time was 23.0 months [95% confidence interval (CI) 19.4–29.6]. The study enrolled 148 patients with R/M HNSCC treated with first-line immunochemotherapy. Inclusion criteria comprised: (i) aged 18-75 years with Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0 or 1; (ii) histologically confirmed HNSCC of the oral cavity, oropharynx, hypopharynx, or larynx; (iii) recurrent or metastatic disease not amenable to curative local therapy; and (iv) at least one measurable lesion per RECIST v1.1. Exclusion criteria comprised: (i) prior systemic therapy for R/M disease; (ii) disease relapse within 6 months after adjuvant therapy or chemoradiotherapy; (iii) prior immunotherapy; or (iv) central nervous system metastases. The study adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki and received ethical approval from the CHCAMS Medical Ethics Committee (No. 21/165-2836). All participants provided written informed consent.

Clinical data, including demographics, treatment response, pathological features, and baseline laboratory parameters, were systematically extracted from medical records. The collected parameters encompassed anthropometric (height and weight), hematologic indices [absolute neutrophil count (ANC), absolute lymphocyte count (ALC), hemoglobin (Hb), platelets (PLT), and albumin (ALB)], as well as pathological characteristics [PD-L1 expression, human papillomavirus (HPV) status, epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) status, and Ki-67 index]. Derived indices were calculated, including body mass index [BMI; weight (kg)/height2 (m2)], albumin-globulin ratio (A/G ratio), prognostic nutritional index [PNI; ALB (g/L) + 5 × ALC (×109 /L)], neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR; ANC/ALC), and platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR; PLT/ALC). Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) were assessed and graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0. Peripheral blood lymphocyte subsets were analyzed using flow cytometry in 103 patients. Baseline plasma proteomic profiling was conducted in a subset of 22 patients using the Olink platform.

Treatment protocol

Eligible patients received combination therapy consisting of pembrolizumab [200 mg intravenously (i.v.)], nab-paclitaxel (260 mg/m2 i.v.), and either cisplatin (75 mg/m2 i.v.) or carboplatin [area under the curve (AUC) 5 i.v.] on day 1 of each 21-day cycle for up to six cycles. All patients met the baseline renal function requirement of serum creatinine ≤1.5× upper limit of normal or creatinine clearance ≥50 mL/min. The choice of platinum was made at the investigator’s discretion based on comprehensive assessment. For patients considered for cisplatin, creatinine clearance ≥60 mL/min was applied. Subsequently, patients without disease progression or unacceptable toxicity continued pembrolizumab monotherapy (200 mg i.v. every 3 weeks) as maintenance treatment until disease progression, intolerable adverse events, completion of 35 total cycles, or voluntary withdrawal.

Treatment response was evaluated using RECIST v1.1. Best overall response classified confirmed complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) as responders, and stable disease (SD) or progressive disease (PD) as nonresponders. Patients with CR, PR, or SD lasting ≥6 months were classified as having durable clinical benefit (DCB), while those with PD within 6 months were classified as no durable benefit. The primary endpoint was objective response rate (ORR), defined as the proportion of patients achieving a confirmed CR or PR. Secondary endpoints included disease control rate (DCR), progression-free survival (PFS), and OS.

PD-L1 IHC

PD-L1 expression was evaluated using the PD-L1 IHC 22C3 pharmDx assay (Agilent Technologies, California). CPS was calculated as the number of PD-L1-positive cells (tumor cells, lymphocytes, and macrophages) divided by the total number of viable tumor cells, multiplied by 100. Only specimens with ≥100 viable tumor cells were deemed evaluable.

Flow cytometry analysis of lymphocyte subsets

Peripheral blood lymphocyte subsets were analyzed in 103 patients using flow cytometry (BD FACS Calibur, BD Biosciences, Franklin Lakes, NJ). The evaluated populations included total T cells (CD3+), helper T cells (CD3+CD4+), cytotoxic T cells (CD3+CD8+), the CD4/CD8 ratio, activated T-cell subsets (CD4+HLA-DR+, CD8+HLA-DR+), resting T-cell subsets, naïve/memory T cells (CD4+CD45RA+), natural killer cells (CD3−CD56+CD16+), and B cells (CD3−CD19+).

Plasma proteomic profiling by Olink assay

Baseline plasma proteomic profiling was carried out in 22 patients using the Olink Target platform (Sinotech Genomics Co, Ltd, Shanghai, China). Plasma protein expression was measured using proximity extension assay technology, which employs dual antibody probes tagged with unique DNA oligonucleotides. Upon target protein binding, complementary oligonucleotides hybridize and undergo DNA polymerase-mediated extension, followed by quantitative polymerase chain reaction amplification. Protein abundance was reported as normalized protein expression values, a log2-transformed relative quantification metric. Rigorous quality control measures were implemented, including internal controls (incubation, extension, and detection references) and external controls (inter-plate calibrators, positive/negative controls).

Survival analysis and predictive modeling

Associations between clinical indices and survival outcomes were first assessed using univariate Cox regression. Variables with P < 0.05 were further evaluated using Kaplan–Meier analysis with log-rank testing.

To develop a predictive model for PFS, we employed a random survival forest algorithm using the R package ‘randomForestSRC’. Feature selection was carried out based on minimal depth variable importance, prioritizing covariates with the strongest discriminatory power. The cohort was randomly divided into training (70%) and test (30%) sets, used for model construction and validation, respectively. Model hyperparameters were optimized via fivefold cross-validation, and predictive performance was assessed using time-dependent receiver operating characteristic curves. The final model’s clinical utility was validated through stratified Kaplan–Meier analysis and multivariable Cox regression adjusting for potential confounders.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were carried out using R software (version 4.2.1). Continuous variables were compared using Student’s t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sum test, and categorical variables using chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests. Clopper–Pearson method was employed for response rates and Kaplan–Meier methodology for survival estimates. Correlation analysis used Pearson’s coefficients. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant, with multiple testing correction using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate method for high-dimensional data.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 148 eligible patients with R/M HNSCC were enrolled. Subsequently, 89 patients received maintenance therapy, with treatment ongoing in 37 patients (Figure 1A). The cohort was predominantly male (89%, n = 132), with a median age of 58 years (range 31-75). Most patients had stage IV disease at enrollment (98.6%, n = 146). Primary tumor sites included the oral cavity (28%, n = 41), oropharynx (24%, n = 35), hypopharynx (26%, n = 38), and larynx (23%, n = 34). Disease presentation comprised distant metastasis alone (22%, n = 32), local recurrence alone (49%, n = 72), or both (30%, n = 44). The investigator’s initial choice of platinum was cisplatin for 121 patients and carboplatin for 27 patients. One patient switched from cisplatin to carboplatin due to renal insufficiency. Efficacy

By the last follow-up (27 February 2025), median follow-up time was 23.0 months (95% CI: 19.4–29.6). All 148 patients received at least one treatment cycle. The best overall responses comprised CR in 13 patients (9%), PR in 82 (55%), SD in 43 (29%), and PD in 10 (7%), yielding an ORR of 64.2% (95/148; 95% CI: 55.9–71.9%) and a DCR of 93.2% (138/148; 95% CI: 87.9–96.7%; Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107733). Survival outcomes were encouraging, with median PFS of 12.7 months (95% CI 10.2–14.7) and median OS of 21.8 months (95% CI 18.9–35.7; Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107733; and Figure 1B and C). As expected, patients achieving CR/PR had longer median PFS (15.2 versus 8.7 months, P < 0.0001) and OS (30.0 versus 16.3 months, P = 0.0071) compared with those with SD/PD (Figure 1D and E; Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107733).

Safety

TRAEs occurred in 144 of 148 patients (97.3%), predominantly grade 1-2 events, with grade ≥3 TRAEs observed in 33.5% (53/148) of cases. Hematologic toxicities were most frequent, with leukopenia (88/148, 59.5%), anemia (87/148, 58.8%), and neutropenia (87/148, 58.8%) each affecting over half of the cohort. Non-hematologic TRAEs included nausea/vomiting (78/148, 52.7%), hypoalbuminemia (67/148, 45.3%), and fatigue (75/148, 50.7%). Among these, neutropenia (27.7%) and leukopenia (17.6%) represented the most common grade ≥3 events. The most common immune-related adverse event (irAE) was hypothyroidism (40/148, 27.0%), which was predominantly grade 1-2 (39/148, 26.4%). One patient (0.7%) experienced a grade 3 event. No grade 4 or 5 hypothyroidism was observed. Grade 3 irAEs included hypothyroidism (1/148, 0.7%), hepatitis (2/148, 1.4%), pneumonitis (1/148, 0.7%), myocarditis (2/148, 1.4%), enteritis (1/148, 0.7%), and arthritis (1.5%). Treatment discontinuation due to TRAEs occurred in seven patients (4.7%). No treatment-related deaths were reported (Table 2).

Subgroup analysis identified the prognostic predicting indicators

Subgroup analyses of ORR revealed consistent efficacy across most variables (Supplementary Figure S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107733). In particular, efficacy and survival were consistent across PD-L1 subgroups. PD-L1 expression analyses showed ORRs of 77.8% (14/18; 95% CI 52.4% to 93.6%) for CPS <1, 59.0% (36/61; 95% CI 45.7% to 71.4%) for 1 ≤ CPS <20, and 66.0% (35/53; 95% CI 51.7% to 78.5%) for CPS ≥20, without significant differential response by PD-L1 status (Supplementary Figure S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107733). All complete responders (n = 13) exhibited PD-L1 CPS ≥1. PFS and OS also did not differ significantly by PD-L1 subgroup (Supplementary Figure S3A and Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107733).

Univariate Cox regression analysis identified several nutrition-related factors significantly associated with survival outcomes (Figure 1F and G). Kaplan–Meier analysis confirmed that patients with low albumin (ALB <40 g/L), low hemoglobin (Hb <130 g/L in males, <115 g/L in females), moderate to severe malnutrition (PNI ≤45), or low A/G ratio (<1.2) exhibited significantly worse PFS and OS (Supplementary Figure S4A-D, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107733). In the HPV-evaluable subset (n = 132), HPV-negative oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) showed a trend toward worse median OS compared with non-oropharyngeal SCC (14.8 versus 30.0 months, P = 0.0488). Subgroup analysis of EGFR status and Ki-67 level showed no significant survival differences (Supplementary Figure S3B-D, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107733). These findings collectively underscore the prognostic significance of routine clinically accessible markers in patients with HNSCC receiving immunotherapy.

Development and validation of the clinical parameter-based predictive model

Using a random forest algorithm, we constructed a clinical parameter-based predictive model on seven clinically accessible parameters: height, weight, BMI, Hb, PLT, ALB, and A/G ratio. Feature selection was carried out using minimal depth methodology with a threshold of 4.41 (Figure 2A and B). The model demonstrated robust predictive performance for PFS, with time-dependent AUCs of 0.826, 0.831, and 0.906 at 12, 18, and 24 months, respectively, in the training cohort (Figure 2C). Comparable performance was observed in the test cohort (AUC 0.753, 0.765, and 0.919 at 12, 18, and 24 months; Figure 2D). The model also effectively predicted OS in both cohorts (training cohort, AUC 0.799, 0.682, 0.745; test cohort, AUC 0.784, 0.804, 0.908; Figure 2E and F). These results demonstrated the model’s consistent predictive accuracy across different timepoints and patient subsets.

Risk stratification using optimal cutoff values of the training cohort revealed significant differences in treatment outcomes. High-risk patients exhibited markedly shorter PFS in both training (9.3 versus 30.0 months, P < 0.0001) and test (8.7 versus 16 months, P = 0.0024) cohorts (Figure 2G and H). Similar stratification was observed for OS (training cohort: 14.8 versus 35.7 months, P < 0.0001; test cohort: 17.3 versus 32.7 months, P = 0.0064) (Figure 2I and J). Multivariable Cox regression confirmed the model’s independent prognostic value after adjusting for confounding factors, such as age, sex, smoking, and history of alcohol use (PFS: P < 0.0001; OS: P < 0.0001) (Figure 2K and L). Additionally, low-risk patients showed significantly higher ORR (58.3% versus 34.6%, P = 0.031) and DCB rates (72.4% versus 48.1%, P = 0.014; Supplementary Figure S5, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107733).

Clinical correlates of the risk score

Pearson correlation analysis revealed significant associations between the risk score and key clinical indicators. Strong inverse correlations emerged with nutritional markers including Hb (r = −0.65, P < 0.001), ALB (r = −0.58, P < 0.001), A/G ratio (r = −0.66, P < 0.001), and PNI (r = −0.40, P < 0.001), whereas positive correlations were observed with inflammatory markers such as ANC (r = 0.17, P = 0.041), PLT (r = 0.35, P < 0.001), and PLR (r = 0.22, P = 0.008; Supplementary Figure S6A, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107733). Regarding TRAEs, hypokalemia (r = 0.23, P = 0.006), hyponatremia (r = 0.17, P = 0.041), and anemia (r = 0.30, P < 0.001) were more prevalent in high-score patients (Supplementary Figure S6B, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107733).

Lymphocyte subset analysis identified efficacy-related immune profiling

Flow cytometry analysis of 103 patients demonstrated significant correlates between the risk score and lymphocyte subsets. Higher scores were associated with increased proportions of activated T cells, particularly activated total T cells (r = 0.32, P = 0.001) and activated CD8+ T cells (r = 0.29, P = 0.003), whereas lower scores correlated with higher B cell frequencies (r = −0.21, P = 0.036; Figure 3A-C). Nutrition-related indices (ALB, Hb, A/G ratio) positively correlated with resting total T cells and naïve (CD4+CD45RA+) T cells, whereas inflammatory markers (NLR, PLR) showed opposite patterns (Figure 3A). Survival analysis revealed that elevated activated T-cell percentages predicted inferior outcomes (activated CD4+: P = 0.040; CD8+: P = 0.083; total: P = 0.074), while higher resting (P = 0.042) and naïve (P = 0.065) T-cell proportions were associated with better prognosis (Supplementary Figure S7, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107733). These findings suggest that patients with high-risk scores experienced malnutrition-driven chronic inflammation, which may promote excessive T-cell activation, potentially leading to immune exhaustion and diminished response to immunotherapy. The observed inverse relationship between nutritional status and T-cell activation markers may reflect a mechanistic link between nutrient availability and immune cell homeostasis.

Plasma proteomic profiling revealed potential immunoregulation mechanism

Olink proteomic profiling in 22 patients identified significant associations between the risk score and key immunomodulatory proteins. The model score correlated positively with immunosuppressive mediators including interleukin (IL)-6 (r = 0.66, P = 0.001), IL-8 (r = 0.51, P = 0.014), vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGFA; r = 0.48, P = 0.022), and colony-stimulating factor 1 (CSF-1; r = 0.47, P = 0.026) (Figure 3D and E), while showing a negative correlation with the T-cell costimulatory molecule inducible T-cell costimulator ligand (ICOSLG; r = −0.64, P = 0.001; Figure 3D and E). Elevated levels of CSF-1, VEGFA, and IL-8 predicted significantly worse PFS (CSF-1: P = 0.030; VEGFA: P = 0.0035; IL-8: P = 0.042) and OS (CSF-1: P = 0.00097; VEGFA: P = 0.0044; IL-8: P = 0.017; Figure 3F and G). Collectively, these findings indicate that high-risk patients exhibit a functionally immunosuppressive proteomic profile, which may underlie their adverse clinical outcomes.

Discussion

This prospective biomarker-embedded phase II study provides compelling evidence for the efficacy and safety of pembrolizumab combined with nab-paclitaxel and platinum chemotherapy in R/M HNSCC. To our knowledge, it represents the first prospective investigation to develop and validate a clinical parameter-based predictive model for immunochemotherapy in this setting. Our findings demonstrate robust antitumor activity and a manageable safety profile with this triplet regimen, with efficacy and survival benefits observed consistently across PD-L1 subgroups. Beyond reporting therapeutic outcomes, this study establishes a novel, clinically accessible predictive model for treatment stratification and elucidates the interplay between nutritional status, chronic systemic inflammation, and immune function in shaping treatment responses.

Our findings in this expanded cohort (N = 148) validate and extend our preliminary results (N = 67),11 demonstrating consistent efficacy with improved survival outcomes upon longer follow-up. Although the ORR (64.2%) and DCR (93.2%) remained comparable to our initial data (ORR 62.7%; DCR 88.1%), extended follow-up revealed improvements in survival data—median PFS increased from 9.7 (95% CI 7.4–12.1) to 12.7 (95% CI 10.2–14.7) months and median OS from 18.7 (95% CI 10.6–26.9) to 21.8 (95% CI 18.9–35.7) months. This temporal enhancement suggests potential long-term benefits of the triplet regimen, possibly attributable to sustained immune activation from pembrolizumab maintenance therapy. Moreover, subgroup survival analysis based on best overall treatment response reinforces the critical relationship between early tumor response and long-term survival. Patients achieving CR or PR exhibited significantly superior outcomes compared with nonresponders (SD/PD) (median PFS 15.2 versus 8.7 months; median OS 30.0 versus 16.3 months). Notably, the CR subgroup had not reached median OS, suggesting the potential for durable survival benefit.

Compared with the KEYNOTE-048 trial,12 in which 14.5% of participants had CPS <1, 42.3% had CPS 1-19, and 43.2% had CPS ≥20, our cohort demonstrated a comparable distribution (12% CPS <1, 41% CPS 1-19, and 36% CPS ≥20). In KEYNOTE-048, pembrolizumab plus platinum and 5-FU achieved median OS of 11.3, 12.7, and 14.7 months in the CPS <1, 1 ≤ CPS <20, and CPS ≥20 subgroups, respectively.12 By contrast, our regimen of pembrolizumab plus nab-paclitaxel and platinum demonstrated superior survival outcomes across all PD-L1 subgroups, with median OS of 19.0, 19.7, and 32.7 months, respectively. This consistent advantage reinforces the potential advantage of nab-paclitaxel over 5-FU in this combination. Furthermore, the efficacy of our triplet regimen also exceeded that reported in the KEYNOTE-B10 trial with pembrolizumab plus nab-paclitaxel and carboplatin (ORR 48.5%, median OS 13.1 months), further suggesting the potential superiority of nab-paclitaxel in this setting.8 These findings are consistent with the recent evidence that nab-paclitaxel may enhance the efficacy of ICI compared with solvent-based paclitaxel, potentially through its ability to promote remodeling of the tumor immune microenvironment.9

The safety profile was manageable, with grade ≥3 TRAEs occurring in 33.5% of patients, predominantly hematologic toxicities. The incidence of irAEs was consistent with prior reports for pembrolizumab-based therapies,4,8,13 with hypothyroidism (27.0%) representing the most frequently observed irAE. Notably, in the KEYNOTE-B10 study, 75% of patients experienced grade 3-5 TRAEs, 24% discontinued any study drug, and 6% discontinued all treatments due to TRAEs.8 In our study, the low treatment discontinuation rate (4.7%) due to treatment-related toxicity highlights the feasibility and favorable tolerability of this nab-paclitaxel-containing regimen in real-world clinical practice. These observations align with the evidence that the albumin-bound formulation of nab-paclitaxel may reduce certain toxicities associated with solvent-based paclitaxel.14

In previous studies, nab-paclitaxel-based chemotherapy in HNSCC was predominantly used in the induction chemotherapy and concurrent chemoradiotherapy settings.15, 16, 17 A comparative study demonstrated that the nab-paclitaxel-based induction regimen significantly improved 2-year disease-specific survival compared with the docetaxel-based regimen in locally advanced HNSCC.18 Furthermore, in R/M HNSCC, nab-paclitaxel has shown clinical benefit even in patients with paclitaxel-refractory disease.19 In recent years, several phase II trials have demonstrated promising efficacy and manageable toxicity when nab-paclitaxel is combined with ICIs and platinum in the neoadjuvant setting.20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25 For example, neoadjuvant camrelizumab plus nab-paclitaxel and cisplatin achieved an ORR of 89.6% and a pathologic complete response (pCR) rate of 55.6% in resectable locally advanced HNSCC.20 Similarly, tislelizumab combined with nab-paclitaxel and cisplatin as induction therapy demonstrated a 3-month larynx preservation rate of 86.7% in locally advanced hypopharyngeal SCC.24 In HPV-positive resectable oropharyngeal cancer, neoadjuvant sintilimab plus nab-paclitaxel and cisplatin followed by transoral surgery yielded a pCR rate of 68.0%.25 Beyond the neoadjuvant setting, the combination of pembrolizumab, nab-paclitaxel, and platinum has also shown promising activity in R/M sinonasal SCC, with an ORR of 60.0% and a median PFS of 12.2 months.26 However, data on this regimen in the broader R/M HNSCC population have remained limited. Our findings extend the evidence for nab-paclitaxel-based chemoimmunotherapy into the R/M HNSCC setting.

This study systematically evaluated conventional anthropometric and hematologic indices as predictors of clinical outcomes. Our data reveal that baseline nutrition-related factors, including ALB, PNI, Hb, and A/G ratio, were strongly associated with survival outcomes. Previous studies have established nutritional status as a critical determinant of treatment efficacy and clinical outcomes.27, 28, 29, 30 Our findings align with emerging research demonstrating that pretreatment nutritional indices show particularly strong associations with ICI efficacy in HNSCC patients.31,32 To address the unmet need for robust predictive tools, we developed a clinical parameter-based machine learning model incorporating seven routinely available parameters: height, weight, BMI, Hb, PLT, ALB, and A/G ratio. The model demonstrated excellent discriminatory power, with time-dependent AUC values of 0.753-0.919 for PFS and 0.682-0.908 for OS across training and test cohorts, and it successfully stratified patients into distinct risk categories with significantly different outcomes. Importantly, the model remained prognostically significant after adjusting for potential confounders, confirming its robustness.

Growing evidence suggests that maintaining optimal nutritional status during HNSCC treatment plays a crucial role in modulating inflammatory status.30,33 The high-risk phenotype demonstrated poor baseline nutritional status coupled with inflammatory activation, suggesting that malnutrition may exacerbate chronic systemic inflammation, thereby impairing antitumor immunity. Our flow cytometry analyses revealed that high-risk patients exhibited elevated proportions of activated T cells but reduced frequencies of resting/naïve T cells—a phenotype suggestive of chronic immune activation and functional exhaustion. This observation challenges the conventional assumption that higher T-cell activation universally correlates with improved immunotherapy responses. Instead, it supports the emerging opinion that sustained, dysregulated T-cell activation in malnourished patients may lead to premature exhaustion, akin to the ‘burnout’ phenomenon observed in chronic viral infections and cancer.34, 35, 36

Plasma proteomic profiling further elucidated the mechanistic underpinnings of this association. High-risk patients displayed elevated levels of immunosuppressive cytokines (IL-6, IL-8, CSF-1) and proangiogenic factors (VEGFA), which are known to foster tumor progression by recruiting myeloid-derived suppressor cells, polarizing macrophages toward an M2 phenotype, and suppressing effector T-cell function. In HNSCC, IL-6 serves as a well-established prognostic marker, with pretreatment serum levels independently predicting disease recurrence.37,38 This cytokine is additionally associated with poor nutritional status39,40 and regulates the transition from acute to chronic inflammation.41 IL-8, a proinflammatory chemokine, demonstrates correlation with tumor angiogenesis, tumorigenicity, and metastatic potential.42 VEGFA promotes tumor angiogenesis, which represents a key hallmark of cancer progression. CSF-1 promotes tumor progression by recruiting and activating tumor-associated macrophages, thereby fostering immunosuppression and angiogenesis.43,44 Conversely, the negative correlation between risk scores and ICOSLG—a critical costimulatory molecule for T-cell priming—suggests impaired T-cell costimulation in malnourished patients. Collectively, these data illustrate a pattern of malnutrition-driven immune dysfunction, wherein chronic inflammation and nutrient deprivation conspire to create an immunosuppressive microenvironment refractory to ICIs.

Our findings advocate for integrating nutritional assessment into the standard workup for patients with R/M HNSCC. We developed a clinical parameter-based predictive model using simple and cost-effective measures as early indicator of treatment resistance. For high-risk patients identified by our model, prompt interventions such as nutritional supplementation, anti-inflammatory agents, or alternative therapeutic strategies may enhance antitumor immunity and improve clinical outcomes. Several limitations must be acknowledged. First, this is a single-center trial with an exclusively Chinese patient population, and the single-arm design precludes direct comparison with other regimens. Second, the model requires external validation in multicenter cohorts to ensure generalizability. Third, while our proteomic and flow cytometry analyses provide mechanistic insights, these findings require further validation. The proteomic data, in particular, are derived from a small cohort and should be interpreted with caution. These results are exploratory in nature and require validation in larger, independent cohorts to confirm their reproducibility and clinical relevance. Furthermore, functional studies are needed to establish causal relationships between malnutrition, immune dysregulation, and treatment resistance.

Conclusions

This study establishes pembrolizumab–nab-paclitaxel–platinum as an effective first-line regimen for R/M HNSCC, with a manageable safety profile. We developed and validated a clinically practical predictive model to stratify patients by risk. Our mechanistic analyses illuminate the interplay between malnutrition, chronic inflammation, and immune dysfunction, offering a roadmap for personalized therapeutic strategies. These findings underscore the importance of holistic patient assessment in immunochemotherapy, where host factors may be as critical as tumor-intrinsic features in determining treatment success.

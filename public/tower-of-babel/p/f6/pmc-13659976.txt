Pulmonary cryptococcosis showing a solitary nodule in patients with previous malignancy
Authors: Keita Tobari, Atsushi Sano
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Pulmonary cryptococcosis is one of the pulmonary fungal infections caused by the inhalation of Cryptococcus neoformans. Pulmonary cryptococcosis shows various radiological findings and sometimes a pulmonary nodule on CT. We experienced three cases of pulmonary cryptococcosis showing a solitary nodule in patients with previous malignancy. All three cases show a newly emerging or growing nodule. Two cases whom FDG-PET was tested showed accumulation of FDG. All three cases underwent pulmonary resection and were pathologically diagnosed with pulmonary cryptococcosis. It is difficult to distinguish a pulmonary nodule due to cryptococcosis from pulmonary malignancy such as a primary lung cancer and a pulmonary metastasis. Especially, it is important to distinguish a pulmonary metastasis from a nodule due to infection in patients with previous malignancy. Surgical resection for diagnosis is often necessary for such cases.

Introduction

Pulmonary cryptococcosis is one of the pulmonary fungal infections caused by the inhalation of Cryptococcus neoformans. Other than other pulmonary fungal infections, pulmonary cryptococcosis infects healthy persons. Its diagnosis is made with pathological or bacterial tests. A serological test is also used for the assistance of the diagnosis.

Pulmonary cryptococcosis shows various radiological findings and sometimes a pulmonary nodule on computed tomography (CT) [1–3]. Its nodule is sometimes with unclear and irregular borders. Therefore it is sometimes difficult to distinguish pulmonary cryptococcosis from pulmonary malignancy.

We experienced three cases of pulmonary cryptococcosis which was diagnosed by surgical resection in patients with previous malignancy. Herein, we report its characteristics.

Patients and methods

This retrospective observational study was approved by the institutional review board (No. S22003). We included pulmonary cryptococcosis patients diagnosed by pulmonary resection. Between 2020 and 2023, 3 patients were diagnosed with pulmonary cryptococcosis by pulmonary resection. All 3 patients had a history of malignancy. We reviewed the clinical and radiological characteristics.

Background characteristics of the study patients

Details about radiological and clinical characteristics are shown in Table 2. All lesions were detected as small pulmonary nodules measuring approximately 1 cm. None of the nodules showed cavitation, satellite nodules, or calcification. Because the lesions were too small to be diagnosed bronchoscopically and were located away from the bronchi, bronchoscopy was not performed in any of the patients. Two patients underwent positron emission tomography, and their nodules showed fluorodeoxyglucose accumulation, with maximal standardized uptake values of approximately 5. All three patients underwent pulmonary wedge resection for histological diagnosis because malignancy was suspected, and pulmonary cryptococcosis was confirmed pathologically in all cases. After the resection, the serological cryptococcus neoformans antigen was tested in two patients. One of the patients showed positive for cryptococcus neoformans antigen and the other patient showed negative. Serological β-D-glucan was also tested in two patients after the resection. One patient showed higher than normal range and the other showed within normal range. One patient took an oral anti-fungal drug after resection. In the other two patients, antifungal treatment was recommended postoperatively, but they declined treatment.

Discussion

As previously reported, it is sometimes difficult to distinguish pulmonary nodules due to cryptococcus infection from malignancy. This is because pulmonary cryptococcosis shows various radiological characteristics. Therefore, sometimes surgical resection for diagnosis is necessary.

Pulmonary cryptococcosis shows various images on CT among ground glass opacity, a defined nodule, a spiculated mass, and so on [1–3, 5]. Our three cases showed a small nodule that is newly emerging or growing. It is also a typical characteristic of malignancy. Therefore it is impossible to distinguish pulmonary cryptococcosis from lung malignancy from CT.

The findings of pulmonary cryptococcosis on positron emission tomography with fluoro-deoxy-glucose (FDG-PET) have been reported [4]. Of the reported 4 cases, one case showed accumulation like lung cancer and the rest of 3 cases showed low accumulation. In our 2 cases on whom FDG-PET was tested, Both cases showed accumulations like pulmonary malignancy. Therefore, FDG-PET is not appropriate to distinguish pulmonary cryptococcosis from lung malignancy.

Pulmonary cryptococcosis sometimes causes some symptoms such as fever. When a patient with a pulmonary mass or nodule complains of some symptoms that are typical for pulmonary infection, we suspect lung infectious disease [4]. But patients with small nodules usually show no symptoms though the nodules are caused by infections, as in our three cases. In addition, bronchoscopy was not considered useful in our cases because the nodules were small and located away from the bronchi, making a bronchoscopic diagnosis difficult. Serum antigen tests may be helpful for the diagnosis of cryptococcosis. However, the frequency of positive serum cryptococcal antigen is less than 40% of pulmonary cryptococcus patients without symptoms [5, 6]. In our cases, only one of two cases tested showed positive serum cryptococcal antigen. Therefore, it is also difficult to distinguish pulmonary cryptococcosis showing small nodules from lung malignancy by the symptoms and serum antigen.

Our three cases had a history of previous malignancy. In such cases, it is important to distinguish pulmonary metastasis from other pulmonary lesions. If the nodule is metastasis from a previous malignancy, resection of pulmonary metastasis or chemotherapy may be an appropriate therapy. On the other hand, if the nodule is caused by an infection such as pulmonary cryptococcosis, chemotherapy may worsen the infection. Therefore the accurate diagnosis is important. Because it is very difficult to diagnose it accurately, surgical resection is useful and often necessary like in our cases.

Conclusion

Pulmonary cryptococcosis sometimes shows a nodule like a lung malignancy. When we find a pulmonary nodule in patients with previous malignancy, it is difficult to distinguish a pulmonary metastasis from an infectious nodule such as cryptococcosis. To diagnose accurately, a surgical resection is necessary.

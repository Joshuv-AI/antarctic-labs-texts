The potential of complete resection of the anorectal sphincter complex and its future development direction
Authors: Longjun Huang, Chaohai Hu, Pengfei Ma
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

We are honored to read the recent research article published in your journal by Professor Yan’s team on the intersphincteric complex (ISC)[1]. This research has offered innovative insights in the field of ultra-low rectal cancer surgery, providing important guidance for clinical practice, which has offered innovative insights in the field of ultra-low rectal cancer surgery, providing important guidance for clinical practice. We would like to share some of our insights regarding the innovative aspects of this research and the future directions for further studies.

The author first clearly defined the anatomical structure of the “intermuscular complex” based on anatomical and immunofluorescence techniques, and explicitly stated that it consists of the conjoined longitudinal muscle, nerve vascular bundle, and lymphatic adipose tissue. It was discovered that the nerve and vascular structures are densely distributed in the anterior part of the ISC, providing key anatomical markers for surgery, and it was confirmed to have clinical significance as a metastasis microenvironment. This discovery has filled the gap in traditional surgical classification and provided a new perspective for surgical standardization. Furthermore, the authors also discovered through a prospective cohort study that the metastasis rate of ISC was as high as 30.96%. Moreover, complete resection of ISC (CISCE) significantly improved the survival rate of patients, directly promoting the innovation of the surgical concept from “removing the internal sphincter” to “completely eliminating ISC,” and having a significant impact on reducing local recurrence. The author astutely pointed out that although CISCE requires a more extensive resection, there is no significant difference in postoperative anal function compared to traditional surgery. This finding eliminates concerns about the potential aggravation of functional disorders due to the expanded resection, providing a double guarantee for clinical decision-making.

The authors’ work has laid a solid foundation. If the following dimensions can be incorporated in future research, it will significantly enhance the clinical application and academic influence of the results. Firstly, the cadaveric specimens used in the study were relatively older, while patients with ultra-low rectal cancer were mostly in the middle-aged group[2]. The degeneration of tissues in the elderly may affect the universality of the structure of ISCs[3]. If younger specimens can be supplemented or if the intraoperative verification can be conducted, the conclusion will be more reliable. Secondly, there are potential biases in the clinical cohort study. Although the single-center design ensures surgical consistency, it does not take into account the influence of different surgeons’ experience or hospital-level differences. Moreover, the impact of neoadjuvant radiotherapy and chemotherapy on the ISC metastasis rate of the 39 patients was not fully discussed. It is suggested that future multi-center studies be conducted and stratified analyses be performed to examine the effect of neoadjuvant treatment on ISC metastasis. Last but not least, the details of the functional assessment need to be improved. The anal function score is only evaluated 12 to 24 months after the stoma repositioning, but the functional recovery after ISR may take a longer time[4]. If the follow-up period can be extended and the functional changes can be dynamically evaluated, the long-term impact of CISCE can be more comprehensively assessed. Based on the dense distribution of nerves and blood vessels in the anterior wall, the authors proposed the concept that “tumors in the anterior wall should be completely resected while those in the posterior wall can be partially preserved.” However, no corresponding subgroup survival or functional data were provided to support this idea. If this strategy can be verified in subsequent studies, it will promote the further precision of the surgery.

This study redefined the surgical anatomical framework for ultra-low rectal cancer. The concept of “complete resection of ISC” proposed by the study is both innovative and practical, and can be regarded as an important advancement in tumor functional surgery. Despite the aforementioned limitations, the work has laid a solid foundation for subsequent research. We look forward to the authors’ team continuing to explore in expanding the sample size and optimizing the technical direction, ultimately benefiting patients.

Evaluating soybean protein concentrate hydrolysate as a fishmeal substitute in the diet for mandarin fish (Siniperca chuatsi): Based on growth, intestinal health, flesh quality and nutrient metabolism
Authors: Zeqiang Sun, Shaojie Liu, Zhihao Han, Shenyang Ma, Ye Gong, Xuxiong Huang, Haitao Zhang, Naisong Chen, Songlin Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The present experiment investigated the effects of soybean protein concentrated hydrolysate (SPCH) replacing fishmeal (FM) on the growth performance, intestinal health, flesh quality, and nutrient metabolism of mandarin fish (Siniperca chuatsi). Five isonitrogenous and isolipidic diets were designed with different levels of dietary fishmeal substituted with SPCH at 0 (FM55), 18.70% (FM45), 37.16% (FM35), 55.40% (FM25), and 73.41% (FM15), respectively. Each diet was fed to three replicate groups of mandarin fish (a total of 450 fish, 37.18 ± 0.01 g) twice daily for 8 consecutive weeks, with 30 individuals per tank. The results showed that when the replacement level of SPCH did not exceed 37.16%, no significant effects on the growth of mandarin fish were observed (P > 0.05). Histological examination showed significantly enhanced hepatic lipid accumulation, while the mid-intestinal villus length and muscularis thickness reached their maximum in the FM35 group (P < 0.05). At the molecular level, SPCH modulated amino acid transport, as evidenced by upregulated expression of key transporter genes (lat1 and snat2) (P < 0.05). Meanwhile, SPCH substitution influenced the tor pathway in muscle tissue, as evidenced by the upregulation of related genes (akt1, tor, s6k, and s6) (P < 0.05). However, SPCH supplementation linearly upregulated hepatic lipogenic genes (srebp1, dgat1, acc1, acc2, and fas) while downregulating lipid catabolism genes (pparα, aco, and atgl) in a linear manner (P < 0.05). Additionally, when SPCH replacement exceeded 37.16%, mid-intestinal expression of inflammation-related genes (rela, mapk13, mapk14, and 5lox) was significantly upregulated (P < 0.05). Furthermore, as the replacement level of SPCH increased, muscle hardness, adhesiveness, springiness, chewiness, resilience, and shear force linearly decreased (P < 0.05), while gumminess linearly increased (P < 0.001). In summary, the study demonstrated that the inclusion of SPCH could effectively reduce the dietary fishmeal content from 55% to 35% without significantly compromising the growth performance, intestinal health, or overall quality of mandarin fish.

Introduction

The mandarin fish (Siniperca chuatsi) is a traditional and precious species in China, renowned for its unique feeding habits. In its natural habitat, this fish exclusively preys on live baitfish throughout its entire life cycle. In recent years, significant advancements in feed acclimation techniques have enabled the successful transition of mandarin fish from live-bait diets to compound feed. Nevertheless, as an obligate carnivore, mandarin fish require high levels of dietary protein, with fishmeal (FM) remaining the primary protein source (Liu et al., 2025). At present, resource depletion due to overfishing and environmental damage, coupled with rising market prices, poses a major challenge to the sustainability of FM production (Zhou et al., 2015). Thus, searching for new protein sources to substitute FM is an urgent necessity.

Soybean protein concentrate (SPC), with crude protein content ranging from 65% to 70%, is manufactured through the solvent extraction method (using ethanol or methanol) of defatted soybean meal, in which antinutritional factor content could be reduced (Lusas and Riaz, 1995). In contrast to soybean meal (SBM), SPC has a better nutritional profile, including good amino acid composition, higher protein content, and good palatability (Chen et al., 2013; Peisker, 2001). However, high content of SPC decreases growth performance (Wang et al., 2023a), alters intestinal histology (Zhang et al., 2019a), produces inflammatory responses (Zhu et al., 2020), and impairs antioxidant capacity (Saleh et al., 2021).

Previous study has indicated that hydrolytic treatment could improve the quality of plant proteins (McCarthy et al., 2013). The active peptides produced after hydrolysis of soybean protein have physiological functions of regulating antioxidant, immune and metabolic capacity (Chatterjee et al., 2018). Research findings on aquatic animals have illustrated that a certain amount of soybean protein hydrolysate could improve the growth performance (Song et al., 2014), antioxidant capacity (Liang et al., 2020), immune capacity (Zhao et al., 2016), and affect the expression of genes relating to nutrient metabolism (Wang et al., 2023b). Therefore, soybean protein concentrate hydrolysate (SPCH) could be used as an excellent FM substitute. Currently, research on the application of SPCH in mandarin fish diets remains limited. Given that dietary protein sources play a critical role in determining the flesh quality of teleosts (Wang et al., 2024), further investigation into SPCH is warranted. Previous studies have demonstrated that protein hydrolysates could enhance flesh texture in hybrid grouper (Epinephelus fuscoguttatus ♀ × Epinephelus lanceolatus ♂) and channel catfish (Ictalurus punctatus) (Yang et al., 2022; Zhu et al., 2022b). Thus, evaluating the impact of SPCH on the flesh quality of mandarin fish should be a key consideration in future research.

Despite SPCH having demonstrated benefits in other teleost species, its application in mandarin fish diets remains poorly studied. This research evaluated the effects of SPCH on mandarin fish growth performance, intestinal health and nutrient metabolism, while also assessing its impact on flesh texture and overall quality. The findings of this study would offer valuable perspectives on the feasibility and effectiveness of using SPCH as a FM substitute in mandarin fish diets.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics statement

The present experiment strictly followed the requirements of the Animal Care and Use Committee of Shanghai Ocean University (SHOU-DW-2024-025).

Experimental diets

The white fishmeal (WFM; crude protein 67.56%, crude lipid 8.97%) and brown fishmeal (BFM; crude protein 73.91%, crude lipid 6.92%) used in this study were obtained from Xinxin Tian'en Aquatic Feed Co., Ltd. (Jiaxing, Zhejiang, China). Neutral protease (50,000 U/g, Youtell Biochemical Co., Ltd., Yueyang, Hunan, China) and keratase (600,000 U/g, Novus International Trading Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) were used to hydrolyze SPC as the description in Sheng et al. (2022), resulting in SPCH with an acid-soluble protein content of 50.78% of crude protein. SPCH (crude protein 68.96%, crude lipid 1.27%) was combined with WFM and BFM as the main protein source in the experimental diets. Amino acid contents in WFM, BFM, and SPCH were determined by liquid chromatography following the method of Yuan et al. (2019) (Table 2). The ratio of BFM to WFM was kept consistent across all experimental diet groups. To maintain lipid balance, fish oil, and soybean oil were sourced from Xinxin Tian'en Aquatic Feed Co., Ltd. (Jiaxing, Zhejiang, China), and incorporated to compensate for lipid variations due to FM substitution. Functional additives included 2-carboxyethyl (DMPT) from Kehu Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Guangzhou, Guangdong, China), and butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT) from Mingang Food Ingredients Co., Ltd. (Mingang, Henan, China). The vitamin premix was also obtained from Xinxin Tian'en Aquatic Feed Co., Ltd. (Jiaxing, Zhejiang, China). All low-lipid components were pulverized through an 80-mesh sieve, thoroughly homogenized, and subsequently blended with lipid constituents. The mixture was hydrated into a dough, processed into three pellet sizes (small size 2.5 mm, medium size 3.5 mm, large size 6 mm) using a pelletizer (GAS-45, Hebei Xingtai Longxiang Machinery Factory, Xingtai, Hebei, China). Five isonitrogenous (51.42%) and isolipidic (13.98%) experimental diets were prepared, with SPCH replacing 0 (FM55), 18.70% (FM45), 37.16% (FM35), 55.40% (FM25) and 73.41% (FM15) of fishmeal, respectively (Table 1). Experimental procedure

The experiment was conducted at the joint laboratory of Shanghai Ocean University and Guangdong Evergreen Feed Co., Ltd. (Zhanjiang, Guangdong, China). The experimental mandarin fish were procured from a local fish farm (Foshan, Guangdong, China) and were domesticated for two weeks using commercial feed. Then, 450 mandarin fish (37.18 ± 0.01 g) were selected, fasted for 24 h, and randomly distributed into 15 culture tanks (1000 L). The fish were fed to apparent satiety twice daily (08:00 and 16:00) for 8 weeks, with every three tanks randomly assigned to one of the experimental diet groups. The culture water was filtered and sterilized through a system consisting of sponge filters, coral stones, and ultraviolet lamps, with approximately 10% of the water replaced daily. All tanks were maintained under a natural light cycle. During the feeding trial, water quality parameters were kept at the following levels: temperature 25 ± 1 °C; pH 7.1 ± 0.2; ammonia nitrogen and nitrate levels < 0.1 mg/L.

Sample collection

Following a 24-h fasting period, eugenol (1:10,000) was used to anesthetize (Shanghai Chemical Reagents Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) experiment fish before sampling. Five fish from each tank were randomly selected and stored at −20 °C for subsequent whole body composition analysis. Additionally, 12 fish were randomly selected to measure body length and body weight. Then six of the 12 fish were chosen to dissect the viscera and liver. Samples of liver, muscle, and midgut were taken from the six fish. These samples were stored at −80 °C for the purpose of further analyzing biochemical composition and gene expression.

Chemical analysis

The moisture content was ascertained by subjecting samples to thermal dehydration at 105 °C until reaching mass equilibrium (method 2001.12; AOAC, 2005). Ash content analysis was performed through thermal decomposition in a muffle furnace (SX2, Shanghai Laboratory Instrument Works Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) maintained at 550 °C, following established protocols (method 942.05; AOAC, 2005). Nitrogenous compounds were quantified using the Kjeldahl digestion method with a conversion factor of 6.25, utilizing a Kjeltec 2200 automated system (FOSS Analytical A/S, Hillerød, Denmark) following standard procedures (method 920.39; AOAC, 2005). A mixed solvent of chloroform and methanol was used to extract crude lipid from the sample, followed by drying in a vacuum oven (DZF-6030A, Shanghai Yiheng Scientific Instrument Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) and the crude lipid content was determined as Folch et al. (1957). Crude fiber content was determined from the defatted powdered sample using the AOAC method 978.10 (2005). The organic matter content is calculated as 100% minus the ash content. Nitrogen-free extract was calculated as dry matter minus the sum of crude protein, crude lipid, crude ash, and crude fiber. Feed gross energy analysis was performed using a 6300 oxygen calorimeter (Parr Instrument Co., Moline, IL, USA).

Liver and intestinal histology

Tissues from the liver and intestines of four fish in each tank were immersed and fixed in 10% neutral formaldehyde solution (Biosharp, Lanjieke Technology Co., Ltd., Hefei, Anhui, China). Oil red O staining was used to observe liver samples and hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining was employed for the observation of intestinal samples. Histological examination was carried out via a BX80-JPA microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan), with representative images captured for analysis. Morphometric measurements were performed using View2.0 software (Hamamatsu Photonics K.K., Hamamatsu, Japan).

Extraction of RNA and real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted from tissue specimens using Trizol Reagent (Takara Bio Inc., Shiga, Japan). After quality evaluation, reverse transcription was performed on the RNA samples using the PrimeScript RT Reagent kit (Takara Bio Inc., Shiga, Japan) to generate complementary DNA (cDNA). The cDNA was subsequently employed for RT-qPCR analysis performed on a CFX96 instrument (Bio-Rad Laboratories Inc., Hercules, CA, USA) with the following thermal profile: 2 min initial denaturation at 95 °C, followed by 40 cycles of 10 s at 95 °C, 10 s at 57 °C, and 20 s at 72 °C, with a final melt curve analysis. The genetic information of this experiment is listed in Table S1. The relative expression of genes was determined through the application of the 2–ΔΔCt method, as described by Livak and Schmittgen (2001). In this process, the β-actin was utilized as the reference gene.

Flesh quality analysis

Muscle texture was evaluated using a Universal TA texture analyzer (TA. ZM-Plus, Dexin Technology Co., Ltd., Kunming, Yunnan, China). Texture characteristic was measured with a cylindrical probe (diameter 25 mm, height 25 mm) under standardized conditions: 35% deformation rate, pre-test and post-test velocities maintained at 2 mm/s, with a 1 s inter-cycle interval. Shear force determination was performed with identical velocity parameters (2 mm/s) at a compression distance of 25 mm, maintaining the same dwell time between measurements. Tissue pH value was quantified using a digital pH meter (pH828M, SMART SENSOR, Wanchuang Electronic Products Co., Ltd., Dongguan, Guangdong, China) following standard calibration procedures.

Statistical methods

The experimental results were presented as mean values with their standard error of the mean (mean and SEM). One-way ANOVA was used to conduct statistical comparisons utilizing SPSS statistical software (version 26.0, Windows platform). Subsequently, Duncan's multiple range test was selected for multiple comparisons, with a significance level set at 5%. The mathematical model utilized in one-way ANOVA is represented as:Yij=μ+αi+εij,where Yij represents the dependent variable, αi represents the effect of the i-th treatment, εij represents the random error term associated with the j-th observation under the i-th treatment.

Body composition

The proximate body composition of the largemouth bass is shown in Table 4. Discussion

Soybean products are high-quality protein sources widely used in aquatic feeds. However, their application is restricted because of the existence of antinutritional factors that negatively influence the growth of fish (Refstie et al., 2005). Research has demonstrated that enzymatic hydrolysis could effectively reduce antinutritional factors in soybean meal (Haghbayan and Mehrgan, 2015). Moreover, studies have confirmed that hydrolyzed soybean protein contains higher levels of small peptides and free amino acids, leading to improved nutrient utilization and enhanced growth performance in fish species (Huang et al., 2024; Villanueva-Gutiérrez et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2022). This study indicated that when the SPCH replacement level did not exceed 37.16%, and there were no significant changes in the growth performance and feed utilization rate of mandarin fish. However, further increases in the SPCH replacement level led to a significant decline in these indicators. Although enzymatic treatment can reduce the antinutritional factor content in soybean protein, higher replacement levels lead to an increased presence of these factors in the diets, which negatively affects fish growth (Yu et al., 2022). Therefore, growth inhibition is thought to be associated with the existence of antinutritional factors in experimental diets with high SPCH content.

Previous studies have confirmed that proteolytic substances can be easily absorbed, primarily due to their high concentrations of amino acids and small peptides (Cahu et al., 2004; Wei et al., 2020b). Meanwhile, appropriate levels of plant protein hydrolysate could increase intestinal villus height and muscle thickness, which could improve the ability of the intestines to digest and absorb (Lin et al., 2022; Qiu et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2024). The results of this study showed that replacing 37.16% of FM with SPCH maximized villus length and muscle thickness in the midgut, thereby enhancing the digestion and absorption capacity of mandarin fish to some extent. During digestion, large proteins are broken down into amino acids and small peptides, which are subsequently absorbed and transported by specific transporters (Poncet and Taylor, 2013). Both lat1 and lat2 are L-type transporters primarily responsible for neutral amino acid transport, with lat2 having a broader substrate specificity (Pineda et al., 1999; Segawa et al., 1999), while snat2 mainly transports small neutral amino acids (Hyde et al., 2007). Moreover, pept2 is responsible for polypeptide transport (Wu, 2021). Previous studies have indicated that the addition of protein hydrolysates increased expression of lat1 and lat2 in largemouth bass (Micropterus salmoides) (Sheng et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023b). This study found that SPCH substitution upregulated the expression of lat1 and lat2, while snat2 expression also increased, suggesting that SPCH enhances the amino acid absorption capacity of mandarin fish. However, pept2 expression was significantly decreased when the replacement proportion of SPCH exceeded 37.16%. Studies on grass carp (Ctenopharyngodon idella) indicated that the addition of soybean protein in the feed down-regulated the expression of genes related to intestinal peptide transporters (Zhang et al., 2019a). Therefore, the down-regulation of pept2 expression might be related to the over-substitution of SPCH, and the specific mechanism by which SPCH affects pept2 expression needs to be further studied.

The target of rapamycin (TOR) pathway in mammals serves as a pathway that detects nutritional status. This pathway oversees the regulation of processes such as protein synthesis, growth, and metabolism (Laplante and Sabatini, 2012). Moreover, activation of the TOR pathway could enhance the growth performance of some fish (Wu et al., 2017, 2021b). The activation of the TOR pathway may suppress 4ebp1 and trigger s6, which serves to further facilitate protein metabolism and synthesis (Ma and Blenis, 2009). In studies of largemouth bass and snakehead (Channa argus), the addition of protein hydrolysates activated the TOR pathway and upregulated the expression of tor and s6 (Sheng et al., 2022, 2023). Consistent with the above findings, SPCH substitution in this experiment up-regulated the expression of tor, s6k, and s6, indicating the activation of the TOR pathway. Functional interactions between tor and akt1 coordinate the regulation of growth and metabolic processes at multiple levels (Hay, 2011). Similar expression trends of tor and akt1 were found in previous studies of turbot (Scophthalmus maximus) and largemouth bass (Dai et al., 2020; Wei et al., 2020a), and consistent results were obtained in this study. In conclusion, while SPCH substitution activates the TOR pathway, it does not lead to corresponding improvements in the growth performance of mandarin fish, likely due to the growth-limiting effects of antinutritional factors in plant proteins.

The hepatic oil red O staining showed that the red lipid droplets increased significantly, indicating that the excessive addition of SPCH caused liver lipid deposition. Prior research has indicated that substituting FM with high levels of cottonseed protein concentrate (CPC) caused large lipid deposits in the liver of hybrid grouper, which shows a likeness to the outcomes obtained in the present study (Yin et al., 2018). Lipid metabolism in fish is mainly carried out in the liver, including synthesis, catabolic, and transport processes, which are coordinated by multiple factors (Tamura and Shimomura, 2005; Weng et al., 2012). This study indicated that SPCH substitution up-regulated lipid synthesis genes (srebp1, dgat1, acc1, acc2, and fas) and down-regulated lipid decomposition genes (mgl, aco, and atgl), indicating that SPCH substitution enhanced liver lipid synthesis. These findings were consistent with previous findings that the addition of plant protein in largemouth bass and Japanese seabass (Lateolabrax japonicus) alters the expression of genes linked to lipid metabolism (Liu et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2019b). Therefore, SPCH replacement changed the expression of genes involved in liver lipid metabolism, leading to lipid deposition, which may explain the increase in crude lipid content in the body composition of mandarin fish.

Inflammatory mechanisms perform a vital role in the intestinal immune defense system of aquatic organisms (Niklasson et al., 2011). Prior research has demonstrated that high levels of plant protein hydrolysate substitution can lead to intestinal inflammation in fish (Dan et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2022a). The cytokines related to inflammation mainly include pro-inflammatory factors and anti-inflammatory factors (Niklasson et al., 2011; Secombes et al., 2001). It has been confirmed that high levels of plant protein substitution upregulate pro-inflammatory genes and downregulate anti-inflammatory genes in the intestines of common carp (Cyprinus carpio) and hybrid grouper, ultimately triggering inflammatory responses (Urán et al., 2008; Yin et al., 2018). Consistent with previous results, this study indicated that the gene expression of pro-inflammatory factors (5lox, il8, and il1β) was up-regulated when the SPCH replacement ratio exceeded 37.15%. Furthermore, studies have demonstrated that p38 MAPK could coordinate the signaling pathway of nf-κb, which could be involved in regulating the expression of corresponding inflammatory factors (Baeuerle and Baichwal, 1997; Cuadrado and Nebreda, 2010). Studies in grass carp and largemouth bass have demonstrated that the p38 MAPK/nuclear factor kappa-B (NF-κB) signaling pathway modulates the expression of inflammatory cytokines, and NF-κB p65 is positively correlated with pro-inflammatory factors (Huang et al., 2021; Tian et al., 2017). In this study, the expression trends of rela, mapk13, mapk14, and pro-inflammatory factors were consistent, suggesting that high levels of SPCH substitution may cause intestinal inflammation and modulate the expression of inflammatory factors via the p38 MAPK/NF-κB pathway.

As a key determinant of flesh texture in fish, dietary protein sources have received considerable research attention (Wang et al., 2024). In Japanese eel (Anguilla japonica), partial replacement of FM with defatted black soldier fly (Hermetia illucens) larval meal significantly enhanced muscle hardness while reducing cohesiveness (Kuo et al., 2022). Meanwhile, in the study of substituting FM with CPC for largemouth bass, flesh shear force, chewiness, and cohesiveness showed a gradual linear decrease with the increase of CPC inclusion levels (Xu et al., 2022). In the current study, the FM15 group demonstrated the lowest values for muscle hardness, adhesiveness, springiness, chewiness, resilience, and shear force, while the FM55 group showed minimal gumminess. These findings suggest that SPCH substitution could effectively enhance mandarin fish flesh quality. The lipid and protein content in muscle are key internal factors influencing flesh texture (Listrat et al., 2016), and have been shown to improve flesh quality in various cultured fish species, including common carp (Cyprinus carpio) (Sobczak et al., 2021), Japanese eel, and large yellow croaker (Larimichthys crocea) (Gong et al., 2023; Kuo et al., 2022). The results demonstrated that SPCH replacement increased crude lipid content and decreased crude protein content in muscle, thus, the improvement in flesh quality observed in this study could be attributed to SPCH-induced changes in crude lipid content and crude protein content in muscle.

Conclusion

In summary, replacing fishmeal with an appropriate level of SPCH could improve the intestinal health, flesh quality, and nutrient metabolism of mandarin fish, while excess will cause liver lipid deposition and intestinal inflammation to a certain extent. Based on a comprehensive evaluation of the experimental results, the inclusion of SPCH could effectively lower the dietary fishmeal content from 55% to 35%.

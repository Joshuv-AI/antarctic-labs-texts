UGT1A1 polymorphisms and gallbladder-related outcomes in sickle cell disease in Brazil
Authors: Fernando Ferreira Costa, Kleber Yotsumoto Fertrin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGallbladder complications represent a significant source of morbidity in sickle cell disease, with UGT1A1 promoter polymorphisms associated with a higher frequency of hyperbilirubinemia and cholelithiasis in affected patients and the first publications regarding this issue appeared in the literature in early 2000. There are at least three published papers providing evidence that supports this association in the Brazilian population published in 2003, 2017 and 2021. Belisário et al., very recently published a paper in Annals of Hematology (“Laboratory and genetic characteristics associated with gallbladder-related outcomes in sickle cell disease in Brazil: results from the REDS-III multicenter cohort study.” Annals of Hematology. 2026; 105(4):115) in which they claim that it is the first study in Brazilian population concerning this issue. In our opinion the previous published papers should be cited in the References, because certainly will provide readers with a more accurate and complete picture of the field´s evaluation in Brazil.Keywords: Sickle cell disease, Cholelithiasis, UGT1A1 polymorphisms, Serum bilirubin levels, Gallbladder-related outcomes

Correspondence

We read with great interest the article by Belisário et al. [1], which provides valuable insights into the laboratory and genetic factors associated with gallbladder-related outcomes in a large Brazilian sickle cell disease (SCD) cohort. The scale of the REDS-III cohort and the genome-wide association analyses provide a meaningful contribution to the field.

However, we would like to respectfully draw attention to a statement in the Discussion section, wherein the authors assert that, to their knowledge, theirs was the first study to investigate both laboratory and genetic variants associated with gallbladder-related outcomes in a Brazilian cohort. This claim may warrant revision, as several Brazilian studies addressing the same issues had been published prior to this study and are readily accessible in the literature.

Our group published two early studies specifically examining UGT1A1 gene promoter polymorphisms in the Brazilian population. The first, published in the American Journal of Medical Genetics in 2002, characterized UGT1A1 promoter polymorphism frequencies across distinct ethnic groups in Brazil [2]. The second, published in Clinical Genetics in 2003, directly associated a UGT1A1 promoter polymorphism with elevated serum bilirubin levels and cholecystectomy in patients with sickle cell anemia [3]. This publication is among the earliest worldwide to establish this genotype–phenotype relationship in SCD.

Subsequently, a larger Brazilian cohort study further explored these associations [4], as did at least one additional Brazilian study examining the combined influence of UGT1A1 polymorphism, alpha-thalassemia, and fetal hemoglobin on bilirubin levels and cholelithiasis [5].

We acknowledge that the study by Belisário et al. includes a larger number of patients, incorporating whole-genome sequencing and a multicenter design. Nonetheless, the foundational evidence concerning “laboratory and genetic characteristics associated with gallbladder-related outcomes in sickle cell disease in Brazil” was established by the previously published studies cited above. We respectfully suggest that appropriate attribution to these earlier Brazilian contributions would strengthen the scientific record and provide readers with a more accurate and complete picture of the field’s evolution in Brazil. More importantly, since the first publications on the higher prevalence of gallbladder complications among SCD patients with UGT1A1 polymorphisms were carried out in the early 2000s, it would be highly relevant to implement the routine determination of these polymorphisms in all patients with SCD, in order to anticipate possible complications.

Shared genetic links between bladder cancer and obesity-related traits: A conjunctional false discovery rate study
Authors: Jiaqi Chen, Hu Li, Yongyang Wu, Yahui Li, Shangfan Liao
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Bladder cancer (BCa) is a common cancer worldwide and is often linked with obesity-related comorbidities, but little is known about the underlying genetic mechanisms. To investigate these mechanisms, we used various quantitative tools, including conditional quantile-quantile (Q-Q) plots, conditional false discovery rate (cFDR), and conjunctional conditional false discovery rate (ccFDR), to explore the pleiotropic enrichment of risk loci between BCa and obesity-related traits. We also performed an expression quantitative trait locus (eQTL) analysis to assess the relationship between shared risk loci and gene expression. Finally, we conducted functional annotation using Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) and gene ontology (GO) analysis. Our findings indicated that there was successive enrichment for a range of obesity-related traits, including body fat percentage, body mass index, fasting insulin, type 2 diabetes mellitus, fasting glucose, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, total triglycerides, and waist-to-hip ratio. Using the tools mentioned above, we identified 18 significant SNPs and 18 closely related genes (cFDR<0.01) under the condition of 8 obesity-related traits. The SNPs included rs143004880, rs73301337, rs10798572, rs11594929, rs17019138, rs2877, rs149795948, rs142509736, rs12727575, rs1571277, rs12131828, rs635634, rs76895963, rs118081211, rs7044247, rs138895564, rs4135275, and rs148023060. Additionally, we identified 15 novel loci using ccFDR, including rs143004880, rs73301337, rs10798572, rs11594929, rs17019138, rs2877, rs142509736, rs1571277, rs635634, rs76895963, rs12131828, rs118081211, rs7044247, rs138895564, and rs4135275. Of the 2 significant loci that modify gene expression, rs12131828 and rs635634 were identified. The functional annotation indicated that the conditional risk genes mainly participated in the regulation of gene silencing. Our study provided evidence of pleiotropic enrichment between BCa and 8 obesity-related traits, and we identified potential genetic mechanisms underlying this relationship. These findings may help in developing targeted clinical treatments for BCa.

1. Introduction

Bladder cancer (BCa), one of the top 10 most common cancers, accounts for about 3.0% of new cancer diagnosis and 2.1% of all cancer deaths.[1] It is estimated that there are approximately 550,000 new cases every year, and with the development of traditionally lesser developed areas, the burden of BCa worldwide is expected to increase.[2] Therefore, it is essential to reveal the mechanism of BCa. Obesity, a multifactorial condition of accumulation of fat, is on its increase around the world. It is estimated that there 600 million adults with obesity worldwide, which is quite a big burden.[3] As a risk factor of many kinds of diseases, obesity is also confirmed to be a significant risk factor of BCa by numerous studies. These studies demonstrated that obesity could increase the progression, reoccurrence, and mortality of BCa.[4–6] Several studies found potential mechanism of their association. For example, obesity may influence the BCa through the insulin-like growth factor axis (IGF axis).[4] Another animal experiment found that obesity may cause chronic inflammation, thus increasing the prevalence of premalignant alterations of bladder.[7] According to a recent review, FGFR3 is a key gene that contribute to the development of chronic inflammation.[8] However, studies focusing on the mechanism of the association between BCa and obesity are not enough to clarify the secret of this dark area. As a result, it is necessary to conduct more studies and trails to reveal the association between obesity and BCa.

In the past few years, large scale of genome-wide association studies (GWAS) has been conducted to find the genetic factors of many kinds of diseases. Based on the framework of false discovery rate, conditional false discovery rate (cFDR) uses the genetic overlap of different traits resulting from their pleiotropy, which can increase the calculating power for less significant association, thus having a better efficiency in finding risk loci.[9]

Conjunctional conditional false discovery rate (ccFDR) is characterized by investigating 2 different traits in the meantime.[10] The risk loci found by GWAS mainly locate in the noncoding region, where the loci influence the traits by regulating the expression of specific gene. To investigate whether the risk loci participate in the regulation of gene expression, eQTL is usually conducted.[11]

In this study, we first identified the shared risk loci and investigated the pleiotropic enrichment of risk loci between BCa and 11 obesity-related traits using cFDR and ccFDR. Then we further identified the functional annotation of shared risk genes through eQTL. Our findings may provide evidence and direction for exploring the accurate association and potential specific mechanism of obesity influencing the prevalence, mortality, and reoccurrence of BCa, which may be helpful to find new treatment target of BCa.

2. Methods

2.1. GWAS summary statistics and genomic control

GWAS summary statistics of 11 obesity-related traits (body fat percentage [BFPC], n=155,961 (body mass index [BMI], n = 681,275; waist-to-hip ratio [WHR], n = 697,734; birth weight [BW], n = 150,191; high-density lipoprotein cholesterol [HDL-C], n=62,166; low-density lipoprotein cholesterol [LDL-C], n=62,166; total cholesterol [TC], n=62,166; total triglycerides [TG], n = 62,166; fasting insulin [FI], n = 24,245; fasting glucose [FG], n = 46,694 and type 2 diabetes mellitus [T2D], n = 898,130) and 1 BCa GWAS summary statistics (n=309,154) were included in this study. More details (with reference) of the GWAS summary-level data are shown in Table S1, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/K29. The details of the study design, quality control, and imputation methods could be found in the original study.

Besides, to decrease the false positive in each GWAS, a genomic control of removing SNPs in linkage disequilibrium (r2>0.2 within 250 kb) was conducted based on “TwosampleMR” R package.

2.2. Identification of associated risk loci

To investigate the pleiotropic enrichment of risk loci between BCa and each obesity-related traits, we conducted quantile-quantile plots, which compared an empirical distribution with a nominal distribution. To identify the existence of enrichment of risk loci with low P value between 2 traits, we estimated the association between subsets of SNPs of specific significance level in each obesity-related trait and the empirical cumulative distribution for all SNPs of BCa with nominal P value (P ≤ 1, P ≤ 0.1, P ≤ 0.01, P ≤ 0.001). If the curve of lower P value leaned to the left more or separated from other curves more, the 2 traits were more relevant or there was more enrichment of low P value of risk loci. Only SNPs with nominal P value > 5 × 10-8 were used to conduct Q-Q plots.

As an extension of FDR framework based on Bayesian statistics, conditional false discovery rate (cFDR) could identify the association of SNPs between 2 traits or diseases at the same time. When trait i was defined as major trait (here is BCa), trait j was defined as conditional trait (here is obesity-related trait), cFDR of SNPs corresponding to i meant that the Pi and Pj of the SNP were both lower than prespecify threshold P1 and P2 and the cFDR could be denoted as cFDR (BCa | Obesity-related trait). Vice versa, when the major trait was set as obesity-related trait, cFDR could be represented as cFDR (Obesity-related trait | BCa). Defined as maximum of cFDR (BCa | Obesity-related trait) and cFDR (obesity-related trait | BCa) corresponding to the same trait, conjunctional cFDR (ccFDR) was used to investigate SNPs associated with both phenotypes and decrease the effect of a single trait driving the common association signal to the minimum. The significance level threshold for cFDR was set as 0.01, while for ccFDR was set as 0.05. The above cFDR and ccFDR analysis were conducted based on “GWAScFDR” R packages.

To visualize the result, closet genes of identified SNPs in cFDR and ccFDR was mapped according to the position using Manhattan plots, which was used to conduct conditional and conjunctional Manhattan plots to illustrate the shared risk genes between BCa and obesity-related traits. Manhattan plots was conducted based on “CMplot” R packages.

2.3. Identification and functional annotation of shared risk genes

A transcriptome-level analysis based on GTEx V8 was conducted to investigate whether the identified shared risk loci modified gene expression and whether the shared risk genes had pleiotropic association with BCa. Expression quantitative trait locus (eQTL) in blood tissue were recovered and integrated with shared risk loci. In order to keep a false discovery threshold of 0.05, we only remained eQTL that had nominal P value lower than 1 × 10-6. Then, we conducted colocalization to investigate whether GWAS and eQTL signal representing a given locus were consistent with a shared casual variant. There are 4 scenarios of posterior probability for each gene in colocalization: (1) H0, refers to no causal variant; (2) H1, causal variant for gene expression only; (3) H2, causal variant for complex trait only; (4) H3, 2 distinct causal variants, similar to Linkage in SMR analysis, refers to that one for gene expression and the other one for complex trait only; (5) H4, one common causal for both gene expression and complex trait. A high posterior probability (usually defined as H4) of colocalization means that the association is regulated by the expression of the gene of risk. In this study, the significant probability H4 of colocalization was set as 0.95. in order to show the position of shared risk genes and the corresponding GWAS P value of BCa, we conducted Manhattan plot. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) and gene ontology (GO) biological process were used to functional annotation of eQTL mapped genes. Colocalization and functional annotation were conducted based on “Coloc” and “ClusterProfiler” R packages.

4. Discussion

Genome-wide association studies are commonly used to detect risk loci associated with complex traits. However, to ensure the significance of the associations, GWAS require a strict P value threshold, which can result in reduced statistical power.[12] Additionally, using relevant auxiliary covariates to increase the power of the analysis can lead to bias if not fully utilized. To address these issues, a method called conditional false discovery rate (cFDR) has been developed, which leveraged pleiotropic effects and made full use of relevant auxiliary covariates to increase the power of the analysis.[12,13] While cFDR can identify risk loci associated with 1 trait, complex disorders and multifactorial phenotypes require a method called conditional co-FDR (ccFDR), which can investigate the risk loci of 2 traits simultaneously.[10] Our study applied both cFDR and ccFDR to investigate the risk loci associated with bladder cancer (BCa) and obesity-related traits. We identified 18 SNPs and 18 closest genes that were significant (cFDR<0.01), conditional on 8 obesity-related traits. Additionally, we found 15 SNPs and 15 closest genes that were significant (ccFDR<0.01), conditional on 8 obesity-related traits except for HDL, BW, and FG. We also identified 2 significant loci (PeQTL<1 × 10-6) that modify gene expression based on the expression quantitative trait locus (eQTL) data.

Our findings suggested genetic enrichment between BCa and several obesity-related traits, such as BFPC, BMI, FI, FG, TG, HDL, T2D, and WHR. We further conducted KEGG and GO analyses to explore the functional annotations of the identified genes. Our analysis revealed that the risk genes of BCa conditional on obesity-related traits primarily function in the regulation of gene silencing. These results provide new evidence for investigating the pathogenic genes of obesity and BCa.

Many studies have confirmed that obesity can increase the occurrence of cancer and significantly increase the mortality risk of cancer, including colon, rectum, liver, kidney and so on.[14,15] According to the International Agency for Research on Cancer, avoidance of weight gain is an effective cancer-preventive method for cancers of colon, breast, kidney, and so on.[16] Many studies have dedicated to find out how obesity can increase the risk and mortality of cancer and many of them point to the endocrine function of adipose tissue. For example, excess adipose tissue can increase the bioavailable sex hormones as well as causing chronic low-grade inflammation by increasing release of the adipokines and other inflammatory cytokines.[17–20] However, the mechanisms are still not very clear. Besides, as an exception, BCa didn’t have significant association with obesity according to the previous study.[14] So, there are few studies focus on the relationship between BCa and obesity, let alone the mechanism behind their relationship. However, more studies in recent years showed that obesity cause a mild increase in BCa risk.[4,5] Obesity may even be a risk factor for BCa recurrence.[6] Although the association between BCa and obesity may be mild, it is still meaningful to investigate their association and the mechanism behind it with obesity on a rapid rise globally. Some studies have shown that obesity may increase the risk of BCa through insulin-like growth factor axis and bladder inflammation.[4,7]

Based on ccFDR data, we identified 2 significant loci (PeQTL< 1 × 10-6) that modify gene expression according to the eQTL data, including GSTM4 and ABO. GSTM4 is a member of the Mu class glutathione S-transferases gene family that encode phase II detoxifying enzymes that play an important role in detoxifying potential carcinogens.[21] It has been reported that GSTM4 is associated with the risk of developing many kinds of cancers, including, lung, Ewing sarcoma, breast cancer, and so on.[22–24] Though an article reported that there was no significant difference in staining intensity for GSTM4 between benign and normal epithelium cells in BCa.[25] Whether GSTM4 plays a critical role in BCa is not certain, considering the limitations of that study. For example, it only investigated the expression of related genes in exfoliated cells and only conducted immunocytochemical methods to show the expression of the genes. Therefore, considering our study’s result that GSTM4 is very likely to be a risk gene of BCa as well as the limitation of the former study, there is a big chance that GSTM4 is a risk gene of BCa. Another member in the GST gene cluster, GSTM1, is responsible for the detoxification of carcinogens that can contribute to BCa specifically.[26] A study showed that GSTM4 was functionally and genetically related to GSTM1.[27] So, it is promising to explore the association of GSTM1 and GSTM4 and whether they can interact with each other, which may be a potential mechanism of BCa.

As 1 class of Glutathione S-transferases (GSTs), GSTM participate in the detoxification of reactive oxygen species (ROS) that causes oxidative stress in vivo.[28] Previous studies confirmed that overload of ROS contributes to the occurrence of cancer and tumor progression.[29] On one hand, the overproduction of ROS and obesity patients with too much lipid in vivo may lead to an excess of lipid peroxidation products, which is associated with the cause of BCa.[30] On the other hand, increased ROS can contribute to DNA oxidative damage, which is an essential reason for cancer development. Also, oxidative stress is highly related to inflammation, which may lead to DNA mutations of bladder epithelial cells.[31] Moreover, according to previous study, higher TC intake was associated with an increased BCa risk (HR=1.37).[32] Besides, a study also confirmed that serum TC was independently associated with DNA damage, which may lead to BCa.[33] What’s more, obesity, GSTM4 and cancer can be associated by diabetes, including both T1D and T2D. It is reported that obesity may increase the risk of BCa through Insulin-Like Growth Factor Axis, which means diabetes may be associated with BCa.[4] Many studies also confirmed that the downregulated GSTM4 is related to T1D and T2D, which links GSTM4 and diabetes.[34,35] According to all the potential association and mechanisms above, there is a complex relationship among TC, GSTM4, and BCa, which need further exploration to clarify the accurate mechanism behind their linkage.

ABO gene determines human blood-group antigens that are glycoconjugate structures located on the surface of red blood cells and play an important role in physiological and pathological process of human body.[36] There are many studies that investigated the relationship between ABO blood type and BCa. But the results of these studies are quite different and even opposite. A cohort study found that, compared with blood type A, blood type B was significantly associated with lower risks of all cancers (HR=0.91), especially BCa (HR=0.64).[37] Besides, some studies found that the expression of blood-group A antigen reduced in transitional cell carcinoma of the bladder and showed significant correlation with invasive phenotype.[38,39] Another study confirmed that the decreased expression of type A antigen mainly resulted from A allelic loss and hypermethylation of the promoter region of the ABO gene.[40,41] The studies above showed the association between ABO blood type, the expression of ABO blood-group antigen and the BCa risk. Moreover, there were studies investigating the association between ABO blood-group polymorphism and longevity of BCa patients. The result showed that the blood-group B was significantly associated with BCa risk and high mortality, which is controversial with the studies mentioned above.[42] Besides, another study found that blood type B didn’t have significant association with the prognosis of BCa patients accepted radical cystectomy.[43] All the studies above mainly focused on the relation between BCa and ABO blood type, instead of the potential mechanism behind it. Therefore, even though we confirmed that there existed an association between ABO blood type and BCa, there is still a long way to go to identify what the specific relationship is and clarify the mechanism behind it.

Some studies indicated that ABO blood type might influence the development and prognosis of cancer by playing a role in immune and inflammation process. A study found that there were statistically significant association between ABO blood type and 3 circulating inflammation-related molecules, including sVEGFR2, sVEGFR3, and sGP130, in patients with prostate, lung, colorectal and ovarian cancer.[44] Another study confirmed that lung cancer mortality among males of phenotype O were associated with the inflammation-related risk factors, including tobacco smoking, high salt intake, high alcohol intake, fat intake, and so on.[45] The 2 studies above confirmed the possibility that ABO blood type might influence the development and prognosis of cancer by playing a role in immune and inflammation process from the aspects of molecule and risk factor. However, neither of the 2 studies focused on the BCa, so the result needs to be verified in BCa from all aspects, including risk factor, molecule and gene expression.

Many studies also tried to investigate the association between obesity and ABO blood type. But most of the results are controversial. Some studies found that blood type O was associated with increased risk of obesity, while other studies claimed that increased prevalence was observed in blood type A and B, or that no association was found.[46–49] Another study suggested that ABO blood group might play a role in determine body composition, which may include TC, TG, and so on.[50] There is a study investigated the association between ABO phenotype and total serum cholesterol. This study demonstrated that TC levels increased on average by about 4 mg/dl in blood type A compared with other blood types in Japanese population.[51] But more studied involving a larger population need to be conducted to verify this conclusion.

Unlike GSTM4, most studies about ABO focused on the relationship between ABO blood group and BCa risk or obesity risk. Few studies focused on the potential intermedium between them, which may be the mechanism behind the association. Our study found that ABO gene was a shared risk gene of both BCa and obesity, especially the TC. According to the studies above, wo can conclude that there exists a specific association among the ABO gene, BCa, and TC, the mechanism behind which may help to find new potential target to treat BCa patients, especially those with obesity.

There were some limitations in our study. First, there might be potential bias due to the overlaps of samples between BCa and each obesity-related trait. Second, although we found that the shared risk genes participated in the regulation of gene silencing, how these shared risk genes play a role in this process and influence the BCa as well as obesity remained dark. And further studies may explore the mechanism and provide more information about it.

5. Conclusions

Our study found that there were pleiotropic enrichments between BCa and 8 obesity-related traits, including BFPC, BMI, FI, FG, TG, HDL, T2D, and WHR. We found the conditional risk genes mainly participate in the regulation of the gene silencing. These findings will help us understand the pleiotropy of BCa and its association with obesity traits as well as the mechanism behind, which may provide more evidence for further clinical treatment of BCa.

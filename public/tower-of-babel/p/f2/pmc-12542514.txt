GBD 2023: over twice the health loss from headache disorders among females compared with males, and one fifth of the loss is attributable to medication overuse
Authors: Andreas Kattem Husøy, Timothy J. Steiner
Published: 2025

All iterations of the Global Burden of Disease, Injuries, and Risk Factors study (GBD) since GBD 2010 have confirmed headache disorders, and in particular migraine, to be among the top causes of global health loss [1, 2]. Migraine, tension-type headache (TTH) and medication-overuse headache (MOH), the three headache disorders that GBD takes account of, are not causes of early mortality. Consequently, GBD attributes no mortality to headache disorders: all of this health loss is experienced by those alive, and is measured and reported as years lived with disability (YLDs) [3, 4]. The burden is especially high among females and among young adults, the groups with highest prevalence of migraine.

GBD 2023, just published [5], supersedes all previous estimates. No new data sources for headache disorders were added in this iteration to those included in the three previous rounds (GBD 2017, GBD 2019 and GBD 2021). We will come back to this key point later. In contrast, a comprehensive methodological refinement in GBD 2023 allows for age- and gender-specific modelling of time in ictal state (TIS), itself a product of frequency and duration of headache episodes [6]. Since headache disorders are modelled as recurrent episodic conditions, TIS is a crucial metric factored into the calculation of YLDs (alongside prevalence and disability weight, the latter reflecting the health loss experienced while in the ictal state of the specific headache disorder [7]). The details of this methodological refinement have been described [6]. In summary, it relied upon a metanalysis of N > 41,000 individual participant records obtained from population-based studies of high quality conducted in 18 countries [6].

With regard to headache, GBD 2023 provides two new key insights.

First, with the modelling refinement, the estimated gender gap in headache-attributed health loss has increased in comparison with previous estimates, reflecting the greater proportion of their time spent with headache by females with headache than by their male counterparts [6]. Headache-attributed health loss among females is now reckoned to be more than double that among males (ratio 2.1:1 [6], versus 1.6:1 in GBD 2021 [2]). The age-standardized YLD estimate for females has increased slightly (from 724.8 per 100,000 persons in GBD 2021 [2] to 739.9 per 100,000 persons in GBD 2023 [6]), whereas the estimate for males has decreased by almost a quarter (from 453.2 to 346.1) [2, 6]. This differentiation is an important advance: it much more explicitly depicts the global headache burden, and whom this affects, while the magnitude of the difference is a seminal finding, of major clinical and public-health significance. The overall estimate (males + females) has not changed substantially (from 588.4 to 541.9 [2, 6]). The shift of headache disorders from third leading cause of health loss in GBD 2021 to sixth in GBD 2023 is far more due to the increased burdens attributed to other conditions (notably anxiety disorders, age-related hearing loss and falls [2, 5]).

Interesting but perhaps of less consequence is that, with the refined headache modelling in GBD 2023, minor shifts emerged in the associations of headache-attributed YLDs with age. Whereas prevalence peaks in the thirties, YLDs remain as high in the forties and fifties as in the thirties [6]. This is a direct reflection of the positive correlation between age and TIS, especially for migraine [6]. In other words, at individual level, those with migraine in their forties and fifties carry a greater burden of health loss than younger adults with migraine. This is not just of academic interest: its clinical significance lies in the potential for avoidance of disease progression through provision of effective treatment in young adulthood [8].

The second key insight that GBD 2023 provides is in highlighting how much of headache-attributed health loss is due to MOH: over 15% of the health loss attributed to migraine, and over half the health loss attributed to TTH (20.8% overall), can be traced back to MOH, seen as a sequela of these in the proportion 73.2:26.8 [6]. MOH is entirely preventable [9]. It is also, with appropriate care, fully remediable. The profundity of this insight lies in the realization that a substantial portion of headache-attributed health loss is salvageable by means that are relatively easily achieved. Potentially at least, these means lie largely within the scope of practice of primary care, including community pharmacy [10]). Governments, who have self-interest in reducing health loss and associated productivity losses, should be first to recognize this opportunity, and promote public awareness of the health risks associated with over-medication for headache.

GBD 2023 is the first iteration since GBD 2016 [11] for which those who have collated, worked with and modelled the primary data have also conducted and published headache-specific analyses at global level [6]. This analysis is definitive. GBD 2019 [1] and, more so, GBD 2021 [2] heralded a stream of papers reporting secondary analyses of GBD data, exploiting their availability as a free good, often in direct violation of GBD policy [12], which is in place to avert misinterpretation. A recurrent theme of these papers has been highly granular comparisons between countries and/or over time. These have been unhelpful. GBD data, many imputed, do not support these comparisons. Other themes have been the effects of new drugs for migraine, or of the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic. Since no new data sources for headache disorders have been added since GBD 2017 [6], any impacts of these will not have been seen in GBD 2021, and are not seen in GBD 2023.

GBD data from 1990 onwards offer no evidence that the global burden of headache disorders, expressed as age-standardized YLD rates, have changed in three decades [6]. In absolute terms, headache-attributed burden has risen in line with population expansion, tempered by population ageing. In relative terms it is stable within the limits of measurement. Interpretations that suggest otherwise are not supported by GBD data [6].

The relatively sparse data from low- and lower-middle income countries is a limitation precluding detailed geographical comparisons [6]. GBDs current models for migraine, TTH and MOH are informed by data from 64, 49 and 30 countries respectively [6]. GBDs ability to generate estimates for all countries and regions is based not on availability of data for all, but rather on the data modelling, filling data gaps by drawing from geographically close and demographically similar regions [5, 6]. Between-country comparisons are at high risk of being meaningless, comparing countries for which no primary data exist.

The next iteration of GBD is expected to include many more data sources on headache prevalence, following a renewed literature review. In addition, sets of microdata shared with GBD will greatly increase the overall quality of estimates, and enable better reflection of age-patterns. Most important among these microdata is the HARDSHIP adult database [13], holding > 47,000 individual participant records from studies conducted in 24 countries (from all parts of the world) within the Global Campaign against Headache [14]. Many of these data – from Benin [15], Cameroon [16], India (New Delhi region) [17], Mali [18], Morocco [19] and Peru [20] – have not previously informed GBD. How their inclusion will affect GBD estimates remains to be seen, but it is certain that headache disorders will continue to be recognized among the very top causes of lost health globally. It is likely that health loss estimates derived for low- and lower-middle income countries will rise.

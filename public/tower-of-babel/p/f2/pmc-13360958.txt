Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Breast Cancer: Mechanisms, Biomarkers, and Future Therapeutic Strategies
Authors: Qiu-Chan Deng, Wen-Bin Kuang, Yu-Jing Yang, Cai-Ping Gong, Yi-Peng Zhang, Qiong Luo, Liu-Ping Luo, Guang-Ming Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe landscape of breast cancer treatment has been fundamentally transformed by the emergence of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), representing a paradigm shift from traditional cytotoxic approaches to precision immunotherapy. This comprehensive review examines the current state and future directions of ICIs in breast cancer, with particular emphasis on triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) where these therapies have shown the most promise. We explore the mechanistic foundations of checkpoint inhibition targeting CTLA-4, PD-1, and PD-L1 pathways, examine resistance mechanisms and predictive biomarkers, and discuss emerging targets including LAG-3, TIM-3, and TIGIT. The integration of ICIs with conventional therapies has yielded encouraging clinical outcomes, leading to the first FDA approvals for immunotherapy in breast cancer. However, significant challenges remain in identifying optimal patient populations, overcoming resistance mechanisms, and developing robust predictive biomarkers. This review synthesizes current evidence from recent clinical trials and provides insights into future therapeutic strategies that promise to enhance the efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade in breast cancer treatment.

Introduction

Background on Breast Cancer Immunotherapy

Breast cancer remains the most frequently diagnosed malignancy in women worldwide, with an estimated 2,260,000 new cases in 2020 and projections of 2,500,000 cases by 2025.1 Despite significant advances in early diagnosis and treatment leading to a 38% decrease in breast cancer mortality, therapy resistance and tumor heterogeneity continue to pose formidable challenges in achieving definitive therapeutic success.2–4 Historically, breast cancer was considered poorly immunogenic and was not extensively investigated for its susceptibility to immunotherapy. However, this perspective has been fundamentally challenged by emerging evidence demonstrating the critical influence of the immune system in breast cancer behavior and treatment response.4,5

The presence of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) in the tumor microenvironment (TME) serves as a reflection of pre-existing anti-tumor immune responses and correlates with improved prognosis and enhanced response to chemotherapy.4,6,7 This recognition has catalyzed intensive research into immune-based therapeutic strategies, with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) emerging as the most promising immunotherapeutic approach.4,5,8 Recent progress in immunobiology has successfully enhanced host immunity against breast cancer, particularly in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), where the combination of programmed death 1 (PD-1)/programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) ICIs with chemotherapy has demonstrated efficacy in both advanced and early-stage settings.4,9–11

Importance of Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Cancer Treatment

ICIs have revolutionized cancer treatment over the past decade, establishing immunotherapy as the fifth pillar of cancer management alongside surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and targeted therapy.4 These monoclonal antibodies function by blocking inhibitory immune checkpoint proteins that normally dampen immune responses, thereby unleashing the patient’s own immune cells to recognize and eliminate tumor cells.4,5 The clinical success of ICIs across diverse malignancies, particularly melanoma and non-small cell lung cancer, has provided compelling evidence for their therapeutic potential.

The fundamental principle underlying checkpoint inhibition involves disrupting the interaction between immune checkpoint proteins and their ligands, which serve as molecular “brakes” on T-cell and natural killer (NK) cell activity. Immune checkpoints such as cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated antigen-4 (CTLA-4) and PD-1/PD-L1 represent critical regulatory mechanisms that maintain immune homeostasis but can be exploited by tumors to evade immune surveillance.12 The development of monoclonal antibodies targeting these pathways has demonstrated unprecedented clinical efficacy, with durable responses observed even in heavily pretreated patients.

Despite the transformative impact of ICIs across multiple malignancies, several important challenges remain in breast cancer immunotherapy. First, there is no consensus regarding optimal biomarkers for patient selection. Although PD-L1 expression is currently the only validated companion diagnostic biomarker, its predictive accuracy remains limited, and emerging biomarkers such as TILs, tumor mutational burden (TMB), and gene expression signatures require further validation and standardization.13–15 Second, the mechanisms underlying therapeutic resistance remain incompletely understood. Primary resistance affects most patients with breast cancer, whereas acquired resistance may arise through upregulation of alternative immune checkpoints and dynamic remodeling of the tumor microenvironment.16,17 Third, a unified framework to guide combination treatment strategies is still lacking, despite the rapid expansion of ICI-based regimens combined with chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and radiotherapy.4,18 Although several recent reviews have addressed individual aspects of these challenges,4,8,19,20 a comprehensive synthesis integrating mechanistic insights, clinical evidence, biomarker development, resistance patterns, and emerging therapeutic targets within a single framework remains unavailable.

Objective of the Review

This review aims to systematically synthesize the current evidence on ICIs in breast cancer, encompassing mechanisms of action, clinical efficacy, resistance, predictive biomarkers, and emerging therapeutic strategies, in order to identify key knowledge gaps and inform future research priorities. To position the present article within the existing literature, we compared its scope with that of recent representative reviews (Table 1). Although previous reviews have made important contributions to specific aspects of breast cancer immunotherapy, including clinical trial landscapes,4 subtype-specific evidence on ICIs and cellular therapies,18,21 integration of targeted therapy with immunotherapy,22 next-generation checkpoint biology,5,17 and meta-analytic evaluation of neoadjuvant ICI efficacy,19 none has provided a truly comprehensive synthesis. As shown in Table 1, no single review integrates all of the following dimensions within a unified framework: the mechanistic basis of both established and emerging checkpoint pathways, clinical evidence across all breast cancer subtypes and treatment settings, systematic evaluation of primary and acquired resistance mechanisms, assessment of predictive biomarkers including emerging approaches such as liquid biopsy and artificial intelligence-driven models, and a forward-looking discussion of next-generation therapeutic targets and combination strategies. The present review seeks to address this gap by offering an integrative overview of the current ICI landscape in breast cancer. Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Breast Cancer

Targeting CTLA-4, PD-1, and PD-L1

The clinical development of ICIs in breast cancer has primarily focused on three key targets: CTLA-4, PD-1, and PD-L1.12,23 CTLA-4 (CD152) functions as a critical negative regulator of T-cell activation by competing with the co-stimulatory receptor CD28 for binding to B7 molecules (CD80/CD86) on antigen-presenting cells.24 This interaction delivers inhibitory signals that limit T-cell proliferation and activation, representing an essential mechanism for maintaining immune tolerance and preventing autoimmunity.

The PD-1/PD-L1 axis represents the most extensively studied checkpoint pathway in breast cancer.13,25 PD-1 is expressed on activated T-cells, B-cells, and NK cells, while PD-L1 can be expressed on tumor cells, immune cells, and other cells within the TME.26 The binding of PD-L1 to PD-1 provides suppressive signals that decrease T-cell proliferation and reduce immune responses, effectively creating an immunosuppressive environment that facilitates tumor progression. This pathway has become the primary target for immunotherapy development in breast cancer, with multiple monoclonal antibodies now available including pembrolizumab and nivolumab (anti-PD-1) and atezolizumab, durvalumab, and avelumab (anti-PD-L1).5,26

Current FDA-approved ICIs for various cancer types include ipilimumab (anti-CTLA-4), pembrolizumab, nivolumab, and cemiplimab (anti-PD-1), and atezolizumab, avelumab, and durvalumab (anti-PD-L1). In breast cancer specifically, pembrolizumab has received two breast-specific indications for TNBC, marking significant milestones in the field. The selection of specific checkpoint targets depends on various factors including tumor biology, patient characteristics, and treatment setting, with ongoing research aimed at optimizing target selection and treatment sequencing.

Mechanism of Action and Clinical Efficacy

The mechanism of action of ICIs involves the restoration of effective anti-tumor immune responses through the removal of inhibitory signals that suppress T-cell function. When PD-1 or PD-L1 is blocked, T-cells regain their ability to recognize tumor antigens, proliferate, and execute cytotoxic functions against cancer cells. This process requires the presence of tumor-specific antigens and functional T-cell responses, explaining why certain tumor types and patients respond more favorably to checkpoint inhibition.

Clinical efficacy of ICIs in breast cancer has been most pronounced in TNBC, where response rates range from 5% to 42% depending on PD-L1 expression status, treatment setting, and specific patient characteristics.8,27,28 The IMpassion130 trial demonstrated the clinical benefit of combining atezolizumab with nab-paclitaxel in metastatic TNBC patients with PD-L1-positive tumors, leading to FDA approval of this combination regimen.9,13,29 This landmark study showed improved progression-free survival in the PD-L1-positive subgroup, establishing atezolizumab plus nab-paclitaxel as the first approved immunotherapy regimen for breast cancer.30

In the neoadjuvant setting, preliminary results from clinical trials combining ICIs with chemotherapy have shown promising pathological complete response (pCR) rates.9,31 The KEYNOTE-522 trial demonstrated that adding pembrolizumab to neoadjuvant chemotherapy significantly improved pCR rates in early-stage TNBC patients compared to chemotherapy alone.31–33 These encouraging results have led to expanded investigation of ICIs in various breast cancer subtypes and treatment settings, with multiple ongoing trials evaluating different combination strategies and treatment sequences.34,35

The duration of response to ICIs, while observed in a subset of patients, tends to be remarkably durable compared to conventional therapies. This durability likely reflects the development of immunological memory, which can provide long-term protection against tumor recurrence. However, the overall response rates remain modest, highlighting the need for improved patient selection strategies and combination approaches to enhance therapeutic efficacy.

Combination Approaches and Resistance Mechanisms

The integration of ICIs with conventional cancer treatments represents a critical strategy for enhancing therapeutic efficacy in breast cancer.10,36 Combination approaches with chemotherapy have shown particular promise, as chemotherapy can modulate the TME by enhancing antigen presentation, promoting immunogenic cell death, and reducing immunosuppressive cells.37,38 The synergistic effects observed in clinical trials combining ICIs with chemotherapy have led to multiple FDA approvals and established combination therapy as the standard approach in many settings.9,10,36

Radiotherapy combinations with ICIs represent another promising strategy, leveraging the immunomodulatory effects of radiation to enhance anti-tumor immune responses.39 Radiation can increase tumor antigen release, upregulate PD-L1 expression, and promote T-cell infiltration into tumors, creating a more favorable environment for checkpoint inhibition. Ongoing clinical trials are evaluating various radiation and immunotherapy combinations in both early-stage and metastatic breast cancer settings.

Targeted therapy combinations offer additional opportunities for therapeutic enhancement, particularly in HER2-positive breast cancer where anti-HER2 agents like trastuzumab have been shown to increase TIL infiltration.40 The PANACEA and PembroMab trials are investigating combinations of pembrolizumab with trastuzumab or ado-trastuzumab emtansine in HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer, with early results suggesting potential clinical benefit.41,42

Resistance to ICIs represents a major clinical challenge, with mechanisms broadly categorized as primary (intrinsic) or secondary (acquired) resistance.16,43 Primary resistance mechanisms include low TMB, defective antigen presentation, absence of TILs, and immunosuppressive microenvironments.14,25,44 Secondary resistance typically involves acquired upregulation of alternative immune checkpoints, leading to T-cell exhaustion despite initial response to treatment.16,38 Understanding these resistance mechanisms is crucial for developing strategies to overcome therapeutic limitations and improve patient outcomes.

Resistance Mechanisms in Breast Cancer Immunotherapy

Definition and Classification of Resistance Mechanisms

Resistance to ICIs in breast cancer can be systematically classified into primary and secondary mechanisms, each presenting distinct clinical challenges and requiring tailored therapeutic approaches. Primary resistance, also termed intrinsic resistance, occurs when patients fail to respond to initial ICI treatment despite appropriate patient selection and optimal dosing. This form of resistance is observed in approximately 60–70% of breast cancer patients treated with checkpoint inhibitors, representing the most significant barrier to widespread clinical efficacy.

Primary resistance mechanisms in breast cancer include several key factors: tumor-intrinsic characteristics such as low TMB, defective antigen presentation machinery, and lack of immunogenic tumor antigens; microenvironmental factors including absence of TILs, presence of immunosuppressive cell populations, and inadequate T-cell priming; and host factors such as immunosenescence, comorbidities affecting immune function, and genetic polymorphisms in immune-related genes.25 The complex interplay of these factors creates formidable barriers to effective immune recognition and elimination of tumor cells.

Secondary resistance, or acquired resistance, develops in patients who initially respond to ICIs therapy but subsequently experience disease progression. This phenomenon occurs in approximately 25–30% of initial responders and typically manifests 6–12 months after treatment initiation.45 Acquired resistance mechanisms involve dynamic changes in the TME, including upregulation of alternative immune checkpoints (LAG-3, TIM-3, TIGIT), activation of immunosuppressive pathways, and tumor evolution leading to antigen loss or reduced immunogenicity.

The spatiotemporal evolution of the TME during ICI therapy—from an ICI-responsive state characterized by robust CD8+ TIL infiltration, to a primary resistance state defined by the predominance of immunosuppressive cells and impaired antigen presentation, and ultimately to an acquired resistance state marked by compensatory upregulation of alternative checkpoints—is schematically illustrated in Figure 1. This framework highlights that resistance to ICIs is not a static phenomenon, but rather reflects the dynamic co-evolution of tumor cells and the immune microenvironment, with important implications for the rational design of sequential and combination therapeutic strategies. Biomarkers Associated with Resistance

The identification of biomarkers associated with resistance to ICIs is critical for optimizing patient selection and developing strategies to overcome therapeutic limitations. Current research has identified several categories of resistance biomarkers in breast cancer, spanning tumor-intrinsic, microenvironmental, and host-related factors.

TMB represents one of the most extensively studied resistance biomarkers across multiple cancer types.46–48 In breast cancer, most tumors harbor a relatively low TMB (approximately 1 mutation per megabase), with only 5% of tumors characterized by high TMB (≥10 mutations per megabase), most of which are metastatic lesions.14,49 TNBC generally exhibits higher TMB compared to hormone receptor-positive and HER2-enriched subtypes, which may partially explain the enhanced responsiveness of TNBC to ICIs.3,14,50 However, TMB alone has proven insufficient as a predictive biomarker in breast cancer, suggesting the need for more comprehensive biomarker panels.49

PD-L1 expression represents the only currently validated predictive biomarker for ICIs in breast cancer, yet its predictive value remains imprecise and controversial.51,52 The positive rate of PD-L1 expression in TNBC clinical trials ranges from 19.4% to 68%, with overall response rates to ICIs varying from 5% to 42%.8,13,53 Importantly, patients with PD-L1-negative expression may still respond to ICI therapy, indicating that PD-L1 expression alone is insufficient for optimal patient selection.

TILs, particularly CD8+ T-cells, serve as important biomarkers for ICIs efficacy.7,54,55 High TIL density correlates with improved response to ICIs and better overall prognosis in breast cancer patients.56,57 The immunoscore, which quantifies immune cell infiltration in both tumor core and invasive margin, has emerged as a promising prognostic and predictive biomarker.58,59 However, standardization of TIL assessment and scoring methodologies remains a challenge for clinical implementation.7,60

Strategies to Overcome Resistance

Overcoming resistance to ICIs requires multifaceted approaches targeting different mechanisms of immune evasion. Combination strategies represent the most promising avenue for enhancing ICI efficacy, with several approaches currently under clinical investigation.

Targeting multiple immune checkpoints simultaneously has emerged as a rational strategy for overcoming resistance mediated by alternative inhibitory pathways.16,17,61 The combination of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors with anti-LAG-3, anti-TIM-3, or anti-TIGIT antibodies is being evaluated in multiple clinical trials.17,62,63 The FDA approval of relatlimab (anti-LAG-3) plus nivolumab (anti-PD-1) for melanoma provides proof-of-concept for dual checkpoint blockade, with similar combinations now being tested in breast cancer patients.17

Modulation of the TME represents another critical strategy for overcoming resistance.43,64 Approaches include depleting immunosuppressive cell populations such as regulatory T-cells and myeloid-derived suppressor cells, enhancing dendritic cell function and antigen presentation, and promoting T-cell infiltration through vascular normalization and stromal remodeling.59,65,66 Combination therapies with anti-angiogenic agents, epigenetic modulators, and metabolic inhibitors are being investigated as methods to create more immune-permissive TMEs.39,64

Enhancement of tumor immunogenicity through various interventions offers additional opportunities to overcome resistance.37,67,68 Radiotherapy can promote immunogenic cell death and antigen release, while certain chemotherapy agents can enhance antigen presentation and T-cell priming.39 Oncolytic viruses, cancer vaccines, and adoptive cell therapies represent emerging approaches for increasing tumor immunogenicity and enhancing the efficacy of ICIs.69,70

Predictive Biomarkers in Breast Cancer Immunotherapy

Biomarkers Associated with Response to Therapy

The identification and validation of predictive biomarkers for ICIs therapy in breast cancer represents one of the most critical areas of current research, with significant implications for optimizing patient selection and improving therapeutic outcomes. Current biomarker research spans multiple categories including tumor-based markers, immune microenvironment characteristics, host immune status, and emerging liquid biopsy approaches.

PD-L1 expression currently serves as the only FDA-approved companion diagnostic for ICI therapy in breast cancer, yet its predictive value remains limited and controversial. In the IMpassion130 trial, PD-L1 positivity (defined as ≥1% expression on immune cells using the SP142 assay) was associated with improved progression-free survival when atezolizumab was combined with nab-paclitaxel.13 However, the predictive accuracy of PD-L1 expression is hampered by several factors including assay variability, heterogeneous expression patterns, dynamic regulation by treatment, and the observation that PD-L1-negative patients may still derive clinical benefit from ICIs therapy.71

TILs represent robust biomarkers for ICIs response, with both prognostic and predictive value demonstrated across multiple breast cancer subtypes. High levels of CD8+ T-cell infiltration correlate with improved response rates to ICIs, while the presence of immunosuppressive cell populations such as regulatory T-cells and myeloid-derived suppressor cells is associated with resistance. The stromal and intratumoral TIL scores, as defined by international guidelines, provide standardized methods for TIL assessment, though clinical implementation requires further validation and standardization efforts.

TMB has emerged as a promising biomarker across multiple cancer types, with higher TMB generally associated with improved ICIs response. However, in breast cancer, the relationship between TMB and ICIs efficacy is complex and appears to be influenced by tumor subtype and specific mutational signatures. TNBC typically exhibits higher TMB compared to other breast cancer subtypes, which may contribute to its enhanced responsiveness to immunotherapy. Specific mutations in DNA damage repair genes, particularly BRCA1/2, are associated with increased immune infiltration and enhanced response to ICIs.

Development and Validation of Predictive Biomarkers

The development of robust predictive biomarkers for breast cancer immunotherapy requires comprehensive approaches integrating multiple analytical platforms and standardized methodologies. Current biomarker development efforts encompass genomic, transcriptomic, proteomic, and immunohistochemical approaches, with increasing emphasis on multi-parameter models that capture the complexity of tumor-immune interactions.

Immune gene expression signatures have emerged as promising predictive tools, with several signatures currently under clinical validation. The T-cell inflamed gene expression profile (GEP) captures the presence of adaptive immune responses within tumors and has shown predictive value across multiple cancer types. In breast cancer, interferon-gamma signatures, T-cell receptor diversity metrics, and immune pathway activation scores are being evaluated as potential biomarkers for ICIs response.26

Multi-parametric flow cytometry and mass cytometry approaches enable comprehensive immune profiling of tumor-infiltrating and peripheral immune cell populations.72,73 These technologies can identify specific immune cell subsets, activation states, and functional characteristics that correlate with treatment response. Peripheral blood immune profiling has revealed specific immunotypes associated with ICI response, with changes in circulating immune cell populations serving as potential biomarkers for treatment monitoring.

The TME spatial architecture, assessed through multiplex immunohistochemistry and spatial transcriptomics, provides insights into immune cell localization, cell-cell interactions, and functional immune synapses.59,65,74 These spatial biomarkers may prove more predictive than simple cell counting approaches, as they capture the functional organization of anti-tumor immune responses.19,56

Challenges and Future Directions in Biomarker Research

Despite significant progress in biomarker research, several major challenges continue to limit the clinical implementation of predictive biomarkers for breast cancer immunotherapy. Technical challenges include assay standardization, inter-laboratory reproducibility, sample quality requirements, and the need for real-time biomarker assessment during treatment. Biological challenges encompass tumor heterogeneity, temporal dynamics of biomarker expression, and the complex interplay between multiple biomarker categories.

The integration of multiple biomarker types into comprehensive predictive models represents a critical future direction. Artificial intelligence and machine learning approaches are being applied to develop multi-parametric biomarker algorithms that incorporate genomic, transcriptomic, proteomic, and imaging data. These integrated approaches may overcome the limitations of individual biomarkers and provide more accurate prediction of treatment response.

Liquid biopsy approaches offer the potential for non-invasive biomarker assessment and real-time treatment monitoring. Circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) analysis can provide insights into TMB, microsatellite instability, and specific mutational signatures associated with ICIs response. Circulating immune cell profiling and cytokine analysis represent additional liquid biopsy approaches under investigation.26

The gut microbiome has emerged as a novel biomarker category with potential implications for ICIs efficacy. Specific bacterial species and microbiome diversity patterns have been associated with treatment response in other cancer types, with ongoing research investigating microbiome biomarkers in breast cancer patients. Future research directions include the development of microbiome-based interventions to enhance ICIs efficacy through probiotic supplementation or fecal microbiota transplantation.

Future Directions in Breast Cancer Immunotherapy Research

Innovations in Combination Therapies and Combination Approaches

The future of breast cancer immunotherapy lies in sophisticated combination strategies that synergistically enhance anti-tumor immune responses while overcoming resistance mechanisms. Current research is exploring numerous innovative combinations that target multiple aspects of tumor immunity, from enhancing T-cell priming and activation to modulating the immunosuppressive TME.

Chemotherapy combinations with ICIs have established clinical efficacy, but next-generation approaches focus on optimizing drug sequencing, dosing, and timing to maximize immunomodulatory effects. Emerging evidence suggests that specific chemotherapy agents such as anthracyclines and alkylating agents can promote immunogenic cell death, enhance antigen presentation, and deplete immunosuppressive cell populations.80 Novel chemotherapy-immunotherapy combinations under investigation include nanoparticle-based delivery systems that can simultaneously deliver chemotherapy and immune modulators to tumor sites.81

Targeted therapy combinations represent particularly promising approaches in hormone receptor-positive and HER2-positive breast cancers. CDK4/6 inhibitors have demonstrated unexpected immunomodulatory effects, including enhancement of T-cell infiltration and reduction of regulatory T-cell populations. Combinations of CDK4/6 inhibitors with ICIs are currently under clinical investigation, with early results suggesting potential synergistic effects.12 Similarly, combinations of anti-HER2 therapies with ICIs leverage the immunomodulatory effects of HER2-targeted agents to enhance checkpoint inhibitor efficacy.

PARP inhibitor combinations with ICIs represent a mechanistically rational approach, particularly in BRCA-mutated breast cancers.82,83 PARP inhibition can enhance tumor immunogenicity through increased neoantigen formation, enhanced T-cell infiltration, and activation of the cGAS-STING pathway.75,84 Clinical trials combining olaparib, talazoparib, or other PARP inhibitors with pembrolizumab or other ICIs are ongoing, with preliminary results showing encouraging activity in selected patient populations.85

Development of Novel Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors and Targets

The next generation of ICIs focuses on novel targets beyond the established PD-1/PD-L1 and CTLA-4 pathways. These emerging targets offer opportunities to overcome resistance to current ICIs and enhance therapeutic efficacy through complementary mechanisms of action.

LAG-3 (Lymphocyte Activation Gene-3) represents one of the most advanced next-generation targets, with the recent FDA approval of relatlimab plus nivolumab in melanoma providing validation for dual checkpoint blockade approaches.17 LAG-3 is expressed on activated T-cells, NK cells, and regulatory T-cells, and its inhibition can restore T-cell function and reduce regulatory T-cell activity.17,63 Clinical trials investigating LAG-3 inhibitors in combination with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors in breast cancer are ongoing, with early results showing promise in heavily pretreated TNBC patients.86,87

TIM-3 (T-cell Immunoglobulin and Mucin-domain containing-3) represents another promising target, particularly for overcoming T-cell exhaustion in the TME.88,89 TIM-3 expression is associated with T-cell dysfunction and serves as a marker for the most exhausted T-cell populations.38,74 Anti-TIM-3 antibodies are being evaluated in clinical trials both as monotherapy and in combination with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, with the potential to reinvigorate exhausted T-cells and enhance anti-tumor responses.17

TIGIT (T-cell Immunoreceptor with Immunoglobulin and ITIM domain) has emerged as a critical checkpoint regulating both T-cell and NK cell function.62,63,88 TIGIT expression is elevated in various breast cancer subtypes and correlates with immune suppression and poor prognosis.62,90 Anti-TIGIT antibodies such as tiragolumab are being evaluated in combination with atezolizumab in breast cancer patients, with the goal of enhancing both adaptive and innate anti-tumor immunity.

Translational Research and Clinical Trials in Breast Cancer Immunotherapy

The translation of promising preclinical discoveries into clinical applications requires innovative trial designs and biomarker-driven approaches that can efficiently evaluate novel therapeutic strategies. Current translational research efforts focus on several key areas including biomarker-guided patient selection, adaptive trial designs, and real-world evidence generation.

Window-of-opportunity trials represent an important translational research approach, allowing evaluation of immune modulatory effects of investigational agents in the neoadjuvant setting.91,92 These trials provide unique opportunities to assess pharmacodynamic biomarkers, immune microenvironment changes, and early efficacy signals before definitive surgery. Recent window-of-opportunity trials have provided valuable insights into the optimal timing and sequencing of ICIs with other therapeutic modalities.11,31,93,94

Basket and umbrella trial designs are being increasingly utilized to evaluate ICIs across multiple breast cancer subtypes and biomarker-defined populations.19 These innovative designs allow efficient evaluation of multiple hypotheses within single trial platforms, accelerating the identification of optimal patient populations and combination strategies. The I-SPY 2 platform exemplifies this approach, enabling rapid evaluation of novel immunotherapy combinations in the neoadjuvant setting with adaptive randomization based on biomarker signatures.93

Real-world evidence studies are providing critical insights into the effectiveness and safety of ICIs in clinical practice settings.44 These studies complement randomized controlled trials by evaluating treatment outcomes in broader patient populations, including those with comorbidities and performance status limitations typically excluded from clinical trials. Real-world evidence has been particularly valuable for understanding long-term outcomes, optimal treatment duration, and management of immune-related adverse events.95,96

Correlative science studies integrated within clinical trials are advancing our understanding of resistance mechanisms and biomarker development. These studies utilize advanced technologies including single-cell RNA sequencing, spatial transcriptomics, and multiplexed imaging to characterize immune microenvironment dynamics during treatment. The integration of artificial intelligence and machine learning approaches is enabling the identification of complex biomarker signatures and treatment response patterns that would not be apparent through conventional analytical approaches.

Conclusion

Summary of Key Findings and Progress Made in Breast Cancer Immunotherapy

The integration of ICIs into breast cancer treatment represents one of the most significant therapeutic advances in recent years, fundamentally altering treatment paradigms and offering new hope for patients with aggressive disease subtypes. This comprehensive review has highlighted the remarkable progress achieved in understanding the mechanisms of checkpoint inhibition, identifying predictive biomarkers, and developing effective combination strategies that have translated into meaningful clinical benefits for breast cancer patients.

The clinical success of ICIs in TNBC has been particularly noteworthy, with the FDA approval of pembrolizumab for high-risk early-stage TNBC and atezolizumab plus nab-paclitaxel for metastatic PD-L1-positive TNBC marking historic milestones in breast cancer immunotherapy. These approvals were based on robust clinical evidence demonstrating improved pathological complete response rates in the neoadjuvant setting and enhanced progression-free survival in the metastatic setting. The durable responses observed in responding patients, even in heavily pretreated populations, underscore the transformative potential of immunotherapy in breast cancer management.

The mechanistic understanding of immune checkpoint pathways has evolved significantly, revealing the complex interplay between tumor-intrinsic factors, microenvironmental characteristics, and host immune status in determining treatment outcomes. The recognition that effective immunotherapy requires not only checkpoint inhibition but also adequate tumor immunogenicity, T-cell infiltration, and appropriate microenvironmental conditions has informed the development of rational combination strategies. Current evidence supports the integration of ICIs with chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and radiotherapy to enhance therapeutic efficacy through synergistic mechanisms.

Biomarker research has identified several promising predictive factors including PD-L1 expression, TILs, TMB, and immune gene expression signatures. While PD-L1 remains the only validated companion diagnostic, emerging evidence suggests that multi-parametric biomarker models incorporating multiple immune parameters may provide more accurate prediction of treatment response. The development of liquid biopsy approaches and real-time monitoring strategies offers the potential for personalized treatment optimization and early identification of resistance.

Challenges and Future Directions in Breast Cancer Immunotherapy Research

Despite significant progress, several critical challenges continue to limit the broader clinical impact of ICIs in breast cancer. The modest overall response rates observed across most breast cancer subtypes, with only 20–30% of patients achieving durable responses, highlight the need for improved patient selection strategies and more effective therapeutic approaches. The limited efficacy observed in hormone receptor-positive and HER2-positive subtypes compared to TNBC represents a particular challenge requiring innovative approaches to enhance immunogenicity and overcome immune suppression in these less immunogenic tumor types.

Resistance mechanisms remain incompletely understood and pose significant barriers to treatment optimization. Primary resistance affects the majority of patients, while acquired resistance develops in a substantial proportion of initial responders. The complex and heterogeneous nature of resistance mechanisms, involving tumor-intrinsic, microenvironmental, and host factors, necessitates comprehensive approaches to resistance profiling and personalized intervention strategies. Future research must focus on developing predictive biomarkers for resistance and therapeutic strategies to overcome or prevent resistance development.

The management of immune-related adverse events represents an ongoing clinical challenge requiring specialized expertise and multidisciplinary care approaches. The unique toxicity profile of ICIs, including potentially life-threatening autoimmune complications, necessitates careful patient monitoring and prompt intervention when adverse events occur. The development of predictive biomarkers for immune-related toxicity and evidence-based management guidelines remains an important research priority.

Future directions in breast cancer immunotherapy research encompass several exciting areas of investigation. The development of next-generation ICIs targeting LAG-3, TIM-3, TIGIT, and other novel checkpoints offers opportunities to overcome resistance to current ICIs and enhance therapeutic efficacy. Innovative combination strategies incorporating novel immune modulators, cellular therapies, cancer vaccines, and epigenetic modulators promise to create more effective and personalized treatment approaches.

The integration of artificial intelligence and machine learning approaches into biomarker development and treatment optimization represents a transformative opportunity for precision immunotherapy. These technologies can analyze complex multi-dimensional datasets to identify predictive signatures, optimize treatment combinations, and guide personalized treatment decisions. The development of real-time monitoring strategies using liquid biopsy and imaging biomarkers will enable dynamic treatment adaptation and early intervention for resistance.

The expanding understanding of tumor immunity and immune evasion mechanisms continues to reveal new therapeutic targets and intervention strategies.59,65,97 Emerging areas of investigation include metabolic modulation of immune responses, targeting of immunosuppressive pathways, enhancement of antigen presentation, and modulation of the gut microbiome to enhance ICIs efficacy.39,74,98 These approaches offer the potential to overcome current limitations and extend the benefits of immunotherapy to broader patient populations.

In conclusion, ICIs have established immunotherapy as an essential component of breast cancer treatment, with particularly notable success in TNBC.9,10,13 While significant challenges remain in optimizing patient selection, overcoming resistance, and managing toxicity, the rapid pace of scientific discovery and clinical development provides compelling reasons for optimism.19,99 The continued integration of mechanistic insights, biomarker development, and innovative therapeutic strategies promises to further enhance the clinical impact of immunotherapy and ultimately improve outcomes for all breast cancer patients. The next decade of breast cancer immunotherapy research will likely witness the development of increasingly sophisticated and personalized approaches that maximize therapeutic benefit while minimizing adverse effects, bringing us closer to the goal of transforming breast cancer from a life-threatening diagnosis to a manageable chronic condition for all patients.

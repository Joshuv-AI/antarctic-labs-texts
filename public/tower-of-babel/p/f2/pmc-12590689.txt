Gaza’s health emergency: impact of armed conflict and its global health repercussions
Authors: Majid Ali, Inayat Ur Rehman, Kah Seng Lee, Long Chiau Ming
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Gaza Strip, characterized by its dense population and persistent geopolitical instability, has experienced decades of armed conflict, resulting in systematic healthcare infrastructure deterioration. The healthcare delivery system has been collapsed by Israeli military operations, creating cascading effects that extend beyond regional boundaries. The medical facilities have been targeted, combined with humanitarian aid restrictions, has created unprecedented challenges in providing essential healthcare services to the affected population. The environmental degradation resulting from infrastructure destruction poses additional threats to regional and global health systems. This analysis examines the multifaceted health crisis encompassing healthcare system dysfunction, pharmaceutical supply chain disruption, infectious disease proliferation, and the consequent implications for global health security.Keywords: Gaza, Health crisis, Humanitarian emergency, Global health security, Conflict-related health impacts

Introduction

Contemporary armed conflicts increasingly demonstrate the vulnerability of healthcare systems to systematic disruption, with the Gaza Strip serving as a paradigmatic example of conflict-induced health emergencies. The escalation of hostilities since October 2023 has precipitated an unprecedented humanitarian crisis, characterized by the systematic deterioration of health infrastructure and the emergence of complex health challenges with regional and global implications.

Historical context and geopolitical framework

The contemporary health crisis in Gaza must be contextualized within the broader historical trajectory of the Palestinian-Israeli conflict. The establishment of the British Mandate over Palestine following World War I laid the groundwork for persistent regional instability. The 1917 Balfour Declaration, which endorsed the establishment of a Jewish homeland in Palestine without consideration for the existing Palestinian population, established the precedent for subsequent territorial and political disputes [1]. The 1948 Arab-Israeli War resulted in the displacement of approximately 700,000 Palestinians, an event commemorated as the Nakba, which established enduring patterns of displacement and refugee populations that continue to influence contemporary health outcomes [2]. The 1967 Six-Day War further complicated regional dynamics through Israel’s occupation of Gaza, the West Bank, and East Jerusalem, creating the contemporary geopolitical framework that governs healthcare access and delivery [3]. The ongoing Israeli military and economic support provided by international actors, particularly the United States’ annual $3.8 billion military assistance package to Israel, has contributed to the maintenance of military capabilities that directly impact civilian health infrastructure [4].

The contemporary health system collapse in Gaza

The healthcare system in Gaza has experienced systematic degradation culminating in near-complete functional collapse. Between October 7, 2023, and October 1, 2025, the Ministry of Health in Gaza reported 66,148 Palestinian fatalities and 168,7161 injuries, representing approximately seven per cent of the total population experiencing death or injury [5]. This substantial casualty rate has overwhelmed the remaining healthcare capacity while simultaneously destroying the infrastructure necessary for delivering medical care. The current healthcare capacity assessment reveals that only 10 of the original 36 hospitals maintain partial operational status, with a combined capacity of approximately 1,400 beds, which is manifestly inadequate for addressing the acute medical needs of the affected population [6]. The systematic targeting of healthcare facilities has been documented by international organizations, with the World Health Organization verifying 800 attacks on healthcare infrastructure since October 2023, resulting in 886 fatalities and more than 1700 injuries among healthcare personnel and patients [7].

Systematic targeting of healthcare infrastructure

The deliberate targeting of healthcare facilities represents a significant violation of international humanitarian law and constitutes a primary driver of the health crisis. More than 1,700 healthcare workers have been killed since the onset of hostilities, creating critical gaps in medical expertise and service delivery capacity [8]. This systematic elimination of healthcare personnel has been characterized by international observers as creating conditions where “wearing scrubs and white coats is like wearing a target on their backs” [7]. The destruction extends beyond personnel to encompass critical infrastructure, including medical equipment, pharmaceutical storage facilities, and specialized treatment centres. The targeting of ambulances and emergency medical services has further compromised the healthcare system’s ability to respond to acute medical emergencies, creating cascading effects on population health outcomes.

Humanitarian aid restrictions and health impacts

The implementation of a comprehensive blockade, effective March 2, 2025, has halted all humanitarian aid deliveries, including food, medical supplies, and fuel essential for hospital operations [9]. The food crisis has significantly deteriorated since then. This restriction has precipitated acute malnutrition, with 57 documented paediatric deaths attributed to malnutrition since the blockade’s implementation [10]. The September 2024 Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) classification upgraded the entire Gaza Strip to Emergency (IPC Phase 4), with projections indicating that 345,000 people (16% of the population) will face Catastrophic conditions (IPC Phase 5) between November 2024 and April 2025. The August 2025 assessment confirmed that famine thresholds have been breached for food consumption and acute malnutrition, with mounting evidence of widespread starvation driving an increase in hunger-related deaths [11–13]. According to the latest IPC report released in August 2025, famine conditions have been officially confirmed for the first time in Gaza, with more than 640,000 people now facing catastrophic levels of food insecurity (IPC Phase 5). This represents a dramatic escalation from previous assessments, with the entire Gaza Strip population of 2.1 million now experiencing acute food insecurity at IPC Phase 3 (Crisis) or above [13, 14]. The blockade has also created critical shortages in essential medications [15, 16]. This pharmaceutical crisis has particularly severe implications for individuals with chronic diseases, including diabetes and hypertension, who require continuous medication access for survival.

Failures of aid delivery systems

The establishment of the Gaza Humanitarian Foundation (GHF) in May 2025, backed by Israel and the United States, was purported as an alternative mechanism for aid delivery following the sidelining of traditional humanitarian agencies, including UNRWA. However, this militarized aid distribution system has proven both deadly and catastrophically ineffective, compounding rather than alleviating the humanitarian crisis [17, 18]. Since GHF operations commenced on May 27, 2025, at least 1,400 Palestinians have been killed while attempting to access aid, with over 4,000 more injured at GHF distribution sites. The system has resulted in 875 confirmed deaths of individuals seeking food, with most fatalities occurring at private aid hubs where Israeli forces and foreign military contractors routinely open fire on starving civilians. These figures represent a systematic pattern of violence against aid seekers, with reports documenting that Palestinians are shot from all sides by drones, helicopters, boats, tanks, and ground forces while waiting for basic necessities [17, 19, 20].

The GHF model forces displaced Palestinians to undertake dangerous journeys through militarized zones to reach distribution centres concentrated in southern Gaza, creating chaos and stampedes at inadequately supplied sites. The system delivers only 60 aid trucks per day, a fraction of the estimated 500–600 trucks required daily to meet basic needs, while approximately 3,000 trucks carrying 100 million meals remain blocked at Gaza’s borders. This represents a deliberate surveillance-based rationing system that normalizes deprivation by design [18]. The GHF approach has been condemned by major humanitarian organizations as ‘dehumanizing, dangerous and severely ineffective’, with Médecins Sans Frontières stating that the system uses aid as a tool for forced displacement as part of an apparent ethnic cleansing strategy. UN experts have called for the immediate dismantling of the GHF, characterizing it as a violation of humanitarian principles and standards. The most vulnerable populations, the elderly, the disabled, and children, have virtually no access to aid under this system, as distribution points are located in conflict zones requiring dangerous travel [17, 20, 21].

Infectious disease proliferation and public health threats

The destruction of water treatment and sanitation infrastructure has created conditions conducive to infectious disease outbreaks. Pre-conflict assessments indicated that 97% of Gaza’s water supply was classified as undrinkable, and current conditions have further deteriorated water quality and access [22]. The displacement of approximately 1.9 million individuals, representing 90% of Gaza’s population, into overcrowded temporary shelters has created optimal conditions for communicable disease transmission [23]. Documented infectious disease outbreaks include respiratory infections, gastrointestinal illnesses, cholera, and chickenpox, with the compromised healthcare system unable to provide adequate surveillance or treatment responses [24]. The detection of poliovirus in sewage samples represents a particular concern for regional and global health security, given the potential for cross-border disease transmission [25].

Environmental health consequences and regional implications

The destruction of wastewater treatment facilities has led to the daily discharge of over 129 million liters of untreated sewage into the Mediterranean Sea, resulting in environmental contamination with regional implications. As of February 2025, all six major wastewater treatment plants in Gaza have been destroyed, along with 85% of the sewage pumping stations, resulting in the complete collapse of sanitation services [26, 27]. This environmental degradation threatens marine ecosystems and potentially impacts neighbouring countries’ environmental health. Other toxic impacts resulting from Israeli bombardment on Gaza include severe air pollution from explosives, fires, and burning waste, and the generation of 39 million tons of hazardous debris containing asbestos, explosive residue, and medical waste [28]. The accumulation of medical waste, including contaminated materials from destroyed healthcare facilities, creates additional environmental health hazards. With collapsed waste management systems and contaminated water supplies, these environmental hazards have directly contributed to a public health crisis, including outbreaks of hepatitis A, polio, respiratory illnesses, and diarrheal diseases affecting hundreds of thousands of Gaza residents. The emergence of antimicrobial-resistant infections, exacerbated by inadequate infection control measures and contaminated hospital environments, represents a significant concern for global health security [29]. These resistant pathogens have the potential for international transmission, contributing to the global challenge of antimicrobial resistance.

Economic implications and global health security

Within Gaza, the conflict has caused catastrophic economic damage, with the economy shrinking by 86% in the first quarter of 2024 alone, reported by the World Bank in September 2024 [30]. The destruction of infrastructure, including homes, hospitals, and schools, has resulted in long-term economic stagnation. According to some estimates, Gaza’s economy and development have been set back approximately 40 years [31]. In Israel, the economic toll has also been significant, with economic activity plummeting by 21% in the fourth quarter of 2023 compared to the same quarter in the previous year. The financial burden of military operations has been substantial, costing the Israeli economy approximately $600 million weekly, equivalent to 6% of its weekly GDP, during the early stages of the conflict [31].

The global arms industry has benefited substantially from the Gaza conflict, with data indicating that arms transfers to the Middle East increased by 23% between 2020 and 2024 [32, 33]. This surge in weapons procurement has generated significant profits for arms manufacturers, with Israeli arms exports alone reaching $14.7 billion in 2024, a 13% increase from the previous year, while creating potential risks for global economic stability should the conflict expand regionally. The fiscal implications of sustained military support extend beyond the immediate region, as countries providing military assistance face fiscal trade-offs between defense expenditures and other critical sectors, including healthcare and development programs. While military aid may represent a small percentage of donor countries’ total economic output, these fiscal allocation decisions divert public resources from health infrastructure and social services during a period when many nations are implementing austerity measures. The weapons trade, fueled by geopolitical interests and military necessity, has facilitated the destruction of over half of Gaza’s medical facilities, leaving the healthcare system unable to meet the population’s needs [6, 7]. The economic repercussions extend beyond Gaza, with neighboring countries and global allies bearing the financial burden of humanitarian aid and military interventions while defense contractors secure unprecedented profits from the sustained conflict [34, 35].

Implications for international humanitarian law

The systematic targeting of healthcare facilities and personnel constitutes violations of the Geneva Conventions and additional protocols governing the protection of medical facilities during armed conflict. The International Committee of the Red Cross has emphasized that healthcare facilities must be protected under all circumstances, and deliberate attacks on medical infrastructure constitute war crimes under international law [36]. The documentation of these violations has implications for international accountability mechanisms and the development of stronger protective frameworks for healthcare systems in conflict zones. The failure to protect healthcare infrastructure in Gaza establishes concerning precedents for similar conflicts globally and undermines the fundamental principle of medical neutrality.

Recommendations and future directions

Immediate humanitarian response

The international community must prioritize the immediate restoration of humanitarian aid access to Gaza, including medical supplies, pharmaceuticals, and fuel necessary for hospital operations. This requires coordinated diplomatic efforts to establish humanitarian corridors and ensure the protection of medical personnel and facilities under international law. Emergency medical evacuation protocols must be established for critically injured individuals, with international cooperation to provide specialized treatment unavailable within Gaza. The current waiting list of over 12,000 critically ill and injured patients, including at least 5000 children, requiring urgent medical evacuation, represents a humanitarian emergency requiring immediate international response [37].

Long-term health system reconstruction

Post-conflict health system reconstruction must prioritize resilience and sustainability, incorporating disaster preparedness and conflict mitigation strategies. This includes the establishment of decentralized healthcare networks, robust pharmaceutical supply chains, and comprehensive emergency response capabilities [38]. International investment in healthcare infrastructure must include training and capacity building for healthcare personnel, with emphasis on trauma care, infectious disease management, and emergency medicine. The development of partnerships between Gaza’s health institutions and international medical centres can facilitate knowledge transfer and technical assistance [39].

Global health security enhancements

The Gaza crisis demonstrates the urgent need for strengthened global health security mechanisms capable of responding to conflict-induced health emergencies. This includes enhanced disease surveillance systems, international pharmaceutical stockpiles, and rapid response capabilities for infectious disease outbreaks [40]. International cooperation must address the environmental health consequences of conflict, including the reconstruction of water treatment infrastructure and the remediation of environmental contamination. The development of international standards for environmental protection during armed conflict represents a critical component of health security [41].

Policy and legal frameworks

The international community must strengthen enforcement mechanisms for international humanitarian law, particularly regarding the protection of healthcare facilities and personnel. This includes the development of accountability mechanisms for violations and the establishment of deterrent frameworks to prevent future attacks on medical infrastructure. Long-term conflict resolution efforts must address the underlying political determinants of health in Gaza, including issues of self-determination, economic development, and regional security. Without addressing these fundamental causes and an end to the Israeli military occupation of the Palestinian territories, long-term health security for the Palestinian people will never be guaranteed, and health crises will continue to recur regardless of humanitarian interventions.

Conclusion

The health emergency in Gaza represents a complex humanitarian crisis with significant implications for regional stability and global health security. The systematic destruction of healthcare infrastructure, combined with restrictions on humanitarian aid, has created unprecedented challenges requiring a coordinated international response. The environmental and infectious disease consequences of the conflict extend beyond Gaza’s borders, creating shared risks for the international community. An effective response to this crisis requires both immediate humanitarian action and long-term structural changes that address the political determinants of health. The protection of healthcare systems during armed conflict must be recognized as a fundamental component of international humanitarian law, with robust enforcement mechanisms to ensure compliance. The Gaza crisis serves as a critical test case for the international community’s commitment to health as a human right and the protection of civilian populations during armed conflict. The lessons learned from Gaza’s health emergency must inform the development of more resilient health systems that can maintain essential services during conflicts and natural disasters. Only through coordinated international action addressing both immediate humanitarian needs and underlying structural causes can similar health emergencies be prevented in the future.

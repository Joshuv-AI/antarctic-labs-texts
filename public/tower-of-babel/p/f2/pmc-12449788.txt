The effects of geranylgeranyl diphosphate synthase inhibitor treatment on osteoblast biology and application in a conditioned media model of myeloma bone disease
Authors: Molly E Muehlebach, Sarah A Holstein
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMyeloma bone disease (MBD) is characterized by tumor-induced osteoclast activation with concomitant suppression of osteoblast activity. Nitrogen bisphosphonates (NBPs), including zoledronic acid (ZA), are a mainstay of MBD treatment, due to their anti-osteoclastic effects secondary to high bone affinity and indirect disruption of protein geranylgeranylation through inhibition of farnesyl diphosphate synthase. The development of geranylgeranyl diphosphate synthase (GGDPS) inhibitors (GGSIs) represents a more selective means of targeting geranylgeranylation. The GGSI RAM2061 has direct anti-myeloma activity in vitro and in vivo, achieves both systemic and skeletal distribution, and has anti-osteoclastic activity. However, the effects of this novel therapy on osteoblast activity or in a setting that recapitulates the MBD milieu have yet to be explored. Exposure to RAM2061 or ZA during MC3T3-E1 differentiation resulted in impairment in osteoblast function, including alkaline phosphatase and mineralization activity, however minimal effects were observed in differentiated MC3T3-E1 cells that were subsequently exposed to drug. To evaluate the impact of RAM2061 on osteoblast or osteoclast activity under MBD-like conditions, JJN3 myeloma cell conditioned media (CM) was collected and added to bone cell cultures in order to simulate osteoclast-activating or osteoblast-inhibitory MBD microenvironments. These studies determined that RAM2061 maintains anti-resorptive effects and osteoblast inhibitory effects in undifferentiated precursors while in the presence of JJN3 CM. However, no appreciable effects were detected in osteoblasts exposed to drug post-differentiation. Overall, these studies contribute to the mechanistic understanding of NBP and GGSI effects in the bone and provide support for the continued investigation of GGSIs in MBD.Keywords: Osteoblasts, Osteoclasts, Geranylgeranyl diphosphate synthase, Myeloma bone disease, Bone remodeling, Prenylation

Introduction

Myeloma bone disease (MBD) is a debilitating osteolytic condition resulting from aberrant osteoclast activation coupled with significant osteoblast inhibition (Terpos et al., 2018). Importantly, MBD is prevalent among patients with MM, with nearly 80–95 % reported to experience bone-related complications throughout their disease course (Tothong et al., 2024). Considering not only the high incidence but also the mortality risk which MBD incurs, developing efficacious bone-targeted therapies is crucial for improving patient quality of life and overall survival (Thorsteinsdottir et al., 2020). Current treatments for MBD consist of MM-targeting agents used in conjunction with anti-resorptive agents such as nitrogen bisphosphonates (NBPs) (Terpos et al., 2021). While NBPs can significantly reduce the risk of skeletal-related events, these agents can also be associated with adverse events (e.g., renal impairment, osteonecrosis of the jaw, hypocalcemia) and fail to address the issue of reduced bone formation (Walter et al., 2010).

NBPs encompass a unique class of compounds which possess a characteristic phosphorus‑carbon-phosphorus (P-C-P) backbone imparting a selective affinity for hydroxyapatite (HAP) crystals in the resorbed bone matrix (Nancollas et al., 2006). Importantly, due to this P-C-P moiety and significant bone affinity (>95 % HAP bound) (Savino et al., 2018), NBPs distribute selectively to skeletal tissues (Chen et al., 2002). While this allows for targeted bone effects, additional anti-neoplastic effects which have been documented in vitro fail to translate to the in vivo setting due to lack of distribution/accumulation in non-skeletal tissues (Rogers et al., 2020). NBPs exert their prominent anti-resorptive effects once they are endocytosed by osteoclasts during bone resorption (Yu et al., 2018). Intracellularly, NBPs inhibit the enzyme farnesyl diphosphate synthase (FDPS), a branchpoint enzyme of the isoprenoid biosynthesis pathway (IBP) which catalyzes the production of metabolites geranyl pyrophosphate (GPP) and farnesyl pyrophosphate (FPP) (Fig. 1a). FPP can then be further modified by the enzyme geranylgeranyl diphosphate synthase (GGDPS) to form geranylgeranyl pyrophosphate (GGPP). FPP and GGPP mediate essential cellular processes such as cholesterol biosynthesis and Ras small GTPase farnesylation or Rab and Rho GTPase geranylgeranylation, respectively. In osteoclast cells, NBP treatment inhibits FPP and GGPP production, disrupting prenylation and localization of downstream target proteins, ultimately impairing processes necessary for osteoclast differentiation and function (Touaitahuata et al., 2014; Fisher et al., 1999; Coxon et al., 2000). Of note, these inhibitory effects are attributed to GGPP depletion, independent of FPP levels, due to the vital role of Rho protein geranylgeranylation in osteoclast precursor maturation and bone resorption (Fisher et al., 1999; Coxon et al., 2000).

Interestingly, early in vitro studies found NBPs to have pro-osteoblastic effects through promotion of precursor osteogenic lineage commitment and activation of functional processes such as alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity and matrix mineralization (Ebert et al., 2009; Duque and Rivas, 2007). These pro-osteoblastic effects were later confirmed to be FPP- and GGPP-dependent correlating with MSC downregulation of GGDPS during osteoblast differentiation (Mundy et al., 1999; Uzzan et al., 2007; Weivoda and Hohl, 2011a; Weivoda and Hohl, 2011b; Yoshida et al., 2006). Further studies revealed a direct pro-osteoblastic effect selective to nanomolar concentrations of NBPs, while micromolar concentrations were osteoblast-inhibitory (Jensen et al., 2021; Giannasi et al., 2019; Huang et al., 2016). Cytotoxicity at micromolar concentrations was ultimately attributed to specific depletion of GGPP, again independent of FPP levels, as addition of exogenous geranylgeraniol (GGOH; can be converted to GGPP by intracellular kinases) was found to restore precursor cell viability (Singhatanadgit et al., 2021; Fliefel et al., 2019).

Since GGPP is acknowledged as the primary IBP metabolite mediating bone cell specific effects, specific depletion of GGPP could provide a more targeted approach with reduced potential for off-target effects due to the role of FPP in normal cellular processes (e.g., cholesterol synthesis, Ras farnesylation). GGDPS inhibitor (GGSI) treatment has been shown to have anti-MM effects through inhibition of Rab-mediated monoclonal protein secretion, resulting in downstream endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress and apoptosis (Holstein and Hohl, 2011). Our lab has recently developed a novel GGSI, referred to as RAM2061, which selectively depletes levels of GGPP. In vivo studies confirmed anti-neoplastic benefits with RAM2061 treatment found to reduce tumor burden and prolong survival of MM.1S tumor-engrafted mice compared to solvent-treated control (Haney et al., 2019). Furthermore, pharmacokinetic studies confirmed key drug-like features such as prolonged half-life, metabolic stability, and systemic distribution indicating translational potential (Haney et al., 2019). HAP affinity and drug distribution studies confirmed RAM2061 to be approximately 60 % HAP bound with distribution and compounding accumulation in the long bones and mandible of normal CD-1 mice (Muehlebach et al., 2025). In vitro studies revealed that RAM2061 induces anti-osteoclastic effects as a consequence of disruption of Rho small GTPase geranylgeranylation, impairing the cytoskeletal processes essential for osteoclast differentiation and function (Muehlebach et al., 2025). Analysis in vivo confirmed RAM2061 to deplete TRAP+ osteoclast numbers in normal CD-1 mice indicating potential anti-resorptive effects. However, the osteoblast-specific effects and overall anti-neoplastic and bone-related benefits of GGSI treatment have yet to be evaluated in a pathological setting such as that in MBD.

These studies aimed to elucidate the effects of RAM2061 treatment on normal osteoblast differentiation and function, and furthermore, evaluate whether the effects of RAM2061 on bone cells varies under MBD-like conditions. Results confirmed GGSI treatment to inhibit osteoblast differentiation but to a similar degree as the FDPS inhibitor, ZA, suggesting NBP inhibitory effects to be FPP-independent. GGSI treatment was confirmed to inhibit MC3T3-E1 precursor commitment to both osteoblast and adipocyte lineages, while the effects of GGSI treatment on differentiated MC3T3-E1 osteoblasts were negligible. Using a JJN3 MM conditioned media (CM) model, RAM2061 was confirmed to maintain anti-resorptive effects under osteolytic-MM conditions and osteoblast-inhibitory effects selective to undifferentiated osteoblast precursors. These studies provide the rationale for future preclinical investigation into the therapeutic efficacy of RAM2061 in MBD.

Materials and methods

Chemicals

Dr. David Wiemer (University of Iowa) provided RAM2061. Compound purity was determined as ≥ 95 % by high-performance liquid chromatography and verified by nuclear magnetic resonance (Matthiesen et al., 2018). Zoledronic Acid (ZA) was obtained from Pfizer.

Cell lines

MC3T3-E1 cells (ATCC) were grown in αMEM media supplemented with 10 % heat-inactivated fetal calf serum (FBS) and 1 % penicillin-streptomycin (PS) at 37 °C and 5 % CO2. RAW264.7 cells (ATCC) were grown in DMEM high glucose media with l-glutamine supplemented with 10 % FBS and 1 % PS at 37 °C and 5 % CO2. JJN3 cells were obtained from DSMZ-German Collection of Microorganisms and Cell Cultures. JJN3 cells were grown in a 1:1 base medium of DMEM:IMDM supplemented with 20 % FBS and 1 % PS at 37 °C and 5 % CO2.

MTT cytotoxicity assay

MC3T3-E1 cells (1.875 × 103 cells/well), RAW264.7 cells (5 × 103 cells/well), or JJN3 (3.5 × 104 cells/well) were seeded in 96-well flat-bottom plates and incubated in the presence or absence of RAM2061 or ZA. Following 24, 48, or 72 h incubation, MTT assays were performed as previously described (Muehlebach et al., 2025). Data were normalized to solvent-treated control wells defined as having an MTT activity of 100 %.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

MC3T3-E1 cells were seeded (7.5 × 104 cells/well) in 6-well plates and incubated in the presence or absence of RAM2061 or ZA. Subsequently, RNA extraction was performed using Omega Bio-Tek EZNA RNA total isolation kit. RNA content and purity was measured using a Nanodrop spectrophotometer. cDNA was prepared using the iScript cDNA synthesis kit (Bio-Rad). iTaq Universal SYBR Green Supermix (Bio-Rad) was mixed with cDNA and gene specific primers according to manufacturer's instructions. qRT-PCR reactions were performed in triplicate using a Bio-Rad CFX96 real time machine. Bio-Rad CFX manager 3.1 software was used for data analysis and gene expression was normalized to housekeeping gene β2 microglobulin (B2M). Primer sequences can be found in Supplemental Materials (Supplemental Table 1).

Immunoblot

MC3T3-E1 cells (7.5 × 104 cells/well) or RAW264.7 cells (2 × 105 cells/well) were seeded in 6-well plates and incubated in the presence or absence of RAM2061 or ZA. Following specified incubation periods, cells were washed in PBS and lysed using RIPA buffer (1 % sodium deoxycholate, 0.15 M NaCl, 0.1 % SDS, 1 % (v/v) Triton-X-100, 0.05 M Tris HCl, pH 7.4) supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors to generate whole cell lysate. Protein content was determined using the BCA method. Protein (15 μg) was loaded on an SDS-PAGE gel and transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride membrane. Blots were incubated overnight at 4 °C in primary antibody and for 1 h RT in secondary antibody. Blots were imaged using Bio-Rad Chemidoc MP imaging system following addition of ECL chemiluminescence reagent. Information regarding the antibodies used in the immunoblotting experiments can be found in Supplemental Materials (Supplemental Table 2). Densitometry analysis was conducted using Image Lab software with total protein quantity being normalized to β-tubulin loading control.

Osteoblast differentiation

MC3T3-E1 cells were seeded 8 × 103 cells/cm2 in normal αMEM media (supplemented with 10 % FBS and 1 % PS) and upon confluency, media was replaced with osteogenic medium (αMEM media supplemented with 10 % FBS, 1 % PS, 50 μg/mL ascorbic acid, 10 mM β-glycerophosphate). MC3T3-E1 cells were differentiated up to 7 days for qRT-PCR, immunoblot, and ALP assays with media changes every 2–3 days. For alizarin red staining, MC3T3-E1 cells were differentiated for 14 days with media changes every 2–3 days.

Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity assay

MC3T3-E1 cells were differentiated in the presence or absence of RAM2061 or ZA for 7 days. Following specified time periods, cells were washed in PBS and lysed using RIPA buffer (1 % sodium deoxycholate, 0.15 M NaCl, 0.1 % SDS, 1 % (v/v) Triton-X-100, 0.05 M Tris HCl, pH 7.4) supplemented with protease inhibitors only. Protein content was determined using the BCA method and 25 μg of protein per sample were assayed in triplicate using an ALP activity kit (Sigma AP0100) following manufacturer protocols. ALP activity was measured over a 30 min time course and maximum linear rate of ALP hydrolysis of pNPP was calculated. Data were normalized to untreated, control osteoblasts.

Alizarin red stain (ARS) mineralization assay

MC3T3-E1 cells were differentiated in the presence or absence of RAM2061 or ZA for 14 days. Following specified time periods, cells were washed in PBS and fixed in 1 mL of 70 % ethanol (pre-chilled) and fixed for a minimum of 1 h at 4 °C. Following fixation, cells were washed in PBS and then stained for a minimum of 1 h at room temperature in 1 mL of ARS solution (2 g in 100 mL of DI H2O, pH 4.1–4.3). Stain was removed and cells were washed 5× with DI H2O and left out overnight to dry. The next day images of the plates were recorded followed by a destaining process. Destaining involved the addition of 250 μL of 10 % acetic acid with plates left to shake for 30 min to allow for complete stain removal. Destaining solution was removed and added to a fresh Eppendorf tube with 100 μL of 10 % ammonium carbonate. Tubes were vortexed and 100 μL was added in triplicate to a 96-well plate and absorbance was recorded at 405 nm using a 2017 Tecan microplate spectrophotometer. Data were normalized to untreated, control osteoblasts.

Osteoclast differentiation

RAW264.7 cells were plated 3.125 × 103 cells/cm2 in a base medium of αMEM without phenol red, supplemented with 10 % FBS, 1 % PS and 30 ng/mL macrophage colony stimulating factor (M-CSF). The next day, media was exchanged with base medium supplemented with 15 % JJN3 CM and 40 ng/mL receptor activator of nuclear-κB ligand (RANKL), and cells were differentiated as previously described (Muehlebach et al., 2025).

Tartrate resistant acid phosphatase (TRAP) staining

RAW265.7 cells were differentiated in the presence of 15 % JJN3 CM and treated with RAM2061. Following treatment, cells were fixed and stained following the previously described protocol (Muehlebach et al., 2025).

JJN3 conditioned media (CM) collection

JJN3 cells were seeded 1 × 106 cells/mL in a T-25 flask and the following day media was exchanged with serum-free JJN3 media (1:1 DMEM:IMDM supplemented with 1 % PS). Within 20–24 h of plating, cells and media were collected, centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min, and CM was removed, sterilized through a 0.45 μm filter, and stored at −20 °C. For CM studies, 15 % v/v JJN3 CM was added to culture medium the day following cell plating, representing day 1 of the differentiation period.

Bone resorption assay

RAW264.7 cells were differentiated in the presence of 15 % JJN3 CM on hydroxyapatite-coated plates (Cosmo Bio. Co. LTD) and treated with RAM2061 for 48 h. On day 5, cells were removed with 5 % sodium hypochlorite and resorptive area was quantified as previously described (Muehlebach et al., 2025).

Statistical analysis

Two-tailed t-tests were utilized for calculating statistical significance. An α of 0.05 was set as the level of significance.

Results

RAM2061 exhibits concentration-dependent on-target effects in MC3T3-E1 pre-osteoblast cells

To interrogate differences between NBP vs GGSI treatment on osteoblast biology, the effects of FDPS inhibitor, ZA, and GGDPS inhibitor, RAM2061, were evaluated using the MC3T3-E1 pre-osteoblast cell line (Fig. 1a). MC3T3-E1 cells are a heterogenous population of immortalized cells derived from mouse calvaria with the specific subclone line used in this study confirmed to express Runx2 (Wang et al., 1999a). Runx2 expression indicates osteogenic commitment however, expression alone is insufficient to mediate osteoblast differentiation. Therefore, MC3T3-E1 cells are considered pre-osteoblasts which require further stimulation with agents such as ascorbic acid to promote Runx2 transcriptional activity and stimulate collagen production and secretion in vitro. Utilizing MTT cytotoxicity assays, we first evaluated the effects of RAM2061 and ZA in undifferentiated (i.e., not stimulated with osteogenic medium) MC3T3-E1 pre-osteoblast cells (Fig. 1b,c). Both RAM2061 and ZA lacked potent cytotoxic effects in the undifferentiated MC3T3-E1 cells, with 1 μM RAM2061 reducing MTT activity to approximately 80 % of control and 100 μM ZA reducing activity to around 70 % of control following 48 or 72 h treatment periods (Fig. 1b,c). To determine concentrations at which RAM2061 and ZA disrupt protein geranylgeranylation, we conducted immunoblot analysis using an antibody that is specific for the unmodified version of Rap1a post-24 and -48 h treatment periods. RAM2061 was found to disrupt protein geranylgeranylation in undifferentiated MC3T3-E1 cells at concentrations ≥100 nM and ZA at ≥1 μM (Fig. 1d,e). Furthermore, drug concentrations which disrupted protein geranylgeranylation also induced caspase cleavage in MC3T3-E1 cells (Fig. 1f,g). Caspase activation occurred in both a time- and concentration-dependent manner, with higher concentrations leading to increased cleaved caspase levels at 24 h, while caspase cleavage was detectable at all tested concentrations at the later time point (48 h) (Fig. 1f,g).

RAM2061 induces the UPR and apoptosis in undifferentiated MC3T3-E1 cells

Previous studies evaluating the effects of RAM2061 treatment in undifferentiated RAW264.7 cells found drug concentrations which disrupt protein geranylgeranylation to activate the cytoprotective signaling cascade referred to as the unfolded protein response (UPR) (Muehlebach et al., 2025). Therefore, to determine whether UPR-mediated apoptosis was responsible for the observed caspase activation induced by RAM2061 in MC3T3-E1 cells, we evaluated changes in expression of UPR mediators: protein kinase R-like ER kinase (PERK), activating transcription factor 6 (ATF6), inositol-requiring transmembrane kinase/endoribonuclease 1 (IRE1), activating transcription factor 4 (ATF4), and pro-apoptotic transcription factor C/EBP homologous protein (CHOP). Quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) analysis of MC3T3-E1 cells treated with 400 nM or 1 μM of RAM2061 revealed no notable changes in UPR marker expression with 24 h treatment (Supp Fig. 1a). However, treatment with 400 nM RAM2061 increased ATF4 mRNA expression at 48 h as well as CHOP mRNA expression at 48 and 72 h (Fig. 2a,b). To confirm UPR activation, immunoblot analysis for phosphorylated eukaryotic initiation factor 2α (p-eIF2α), the downstream target of IRE1, as well as ATF4, which is the downstream target of PERK, was conducted (Fig. 2c). No significant changes in p-eIF2α (relative to total eIF2α levels) were detected, however densitometry confirmed elevated ATF4 at 48 h (Supp Fig. 1b). Poly-ADP-ribose polymerase (PARP) cleavage was noted at 72 h, indicative of apoptosis (Fig. 2c).

RAM2061 suppresses MC3T3-E1 osteoblastic functions

To determine the effects of RAM2061 on mature osteoblasts, MC3T3-E1 precursors were differentiated via stimulation with osteogenic medium for a total of 7 days. On day 7 (D7) cells were treated with varying concentrations of RAM2061 for 24 to 72 h and MTT cytotoxicity assays were performed (Fig. 3a). RAM2061 did not exhibit significant, nor concentration-dependent, cytotoxic effects in the differentiated MC3T3-E1 cells (Fig. 3a), therefore we proceeded with our previously established concentrations (100 nM, 200 nM and 400 nM) to further investigate RAM2061's effects on osteoblast function.

ALP is an osteoblastic enzyme which mediates bone mineralization; therefore, activity is commonly used as a metric of osteoblast differentiation and function (Vimalraj, 2020). To analyze effects of GGSI treatment on osteoblast ALP activity, MC3T3-E1 cells were differentiated in the presence or absence of drug for a 7 day period. On day 7 (D7), cells were lysed, and a time course assay was conducted to measure the rate at which the ALP substrate, pNPP, was hydrolyzed over a 30 min period. The maximum linear rate of pNPP hydrolysis was quantified as a measure of ALP activity and normalized to untreated osteoblast controls. RAM2061 and ZA exposure during MC3T3-E1 differentiation resulted in a significant decrease in ALP activity (Fig. 3b). In contrast, cells that had undergone differentiation for 7 days and were then exposed to either RAM2061 or ZA for 48 h were markedly less sensitive, with only modest (∼5–20 %) decreases in ALP activity (Fig. 3c). Effects on osteoblast-mediated mineralization capacity were quantified using alizarin red staining (ARS) for identification of calcium deposits in osteoblast cultures. GGSI treatment during osteoblast differentiation (14-day period) was found to reduce the mineralization capacity in a concentration-dependent manner (Fig. 3d,f). In contrast, the effects of RAM2061 on fully differentiated MC3T3-E1 cells (drug added on day 14 (D14)) were minimal (Fig. 3e,g). ZA only modestly inhibited mineralization during the differentiation process (Fig. 3d, f) and had no effect in fully differentiated osteoblasts (Fig. 3e,g).

RAM2061 treatment reduces MC3T3-E1 differentiation to the osteoblast and/or adipocyte lineages

Considering the critical role of Runx2 expression in MSC lineage commitment and β-catenin signaling during osteoblast maturation (Gaur et al., 2005), changes in Runx2 and active or phosphorylated-β-catenin (p-β-catenin) were evaluated to clarify the mechanisms underlying RAM2061's observed effects on osteoblast activity. Immunoblot analysis obtained from differentiating MC3T3-E1 cells treated with RAM2061 or ZA on D1 and lysed on D7, found an overall reduction in β-catenin levels (phosphorylated and non-phosphorylated) compared to control, corresponding with a marked reduction in Runx2 levels (Fig. 4a). Effects of drug treatment during late-stage osteogenesis were evaluated by treating D7 differentiated MC3T3-E1 cells for 48 h. These studies revealed that neither RAM2061 nor ZA had appreciable effects on Runx2 or β-catenin signaling proteins (Fig. 4b). As osteoblasts are one of the primary sources of the osteoclast differentiation factor, RANKL, changes in RANKL protein levels were also evaluated. RAM2061 was found to reduce levels of RANKL in differentiating MC3T3-E1 cells in a concentration-dependent manner (Fig. 4a). ZA was also found to deplete RANKL levels in differentiating MC3T3-E1 cells, however neither agent significantly altered levels in cells that had been differentiating for 7 days and then exposed to drug for 48 h (Fig. 4b).

Previous studies have found GGPP accumulation to favor adipogenic differentiation over osteogenic differentiation (Weivoda and Hohl, 2011b; Weivoda and Hohl, 2012). Therefore to elucidate how GGSI treatment impacts differentiation potential, changes in expression of osteoblast- vs adipocyte-specific markers were evaluated following osteogenic induction of MC3T3-E1 cells in the presence of RAM2061 (Fig. 4c,d). As RAM2061-induced ATF4 expression in undifferentiated MC3T3-E1 cells (Fig. 2) and since other studies have demonstrated changes in ATF4 expression during both early- and late-stage osteogenesis (St-Arnaud and Hekmatnejad, 2011), changes in ATF4 mRNA levels were evaluated. We also quantified changes in expression of early-stage osteoblast markers (i.e. ALP, collagen type 1 α 1 (COL1A1), osterix (OSX), runt-related transcription factor 2 (RUNX2)), the late-stage marker osteocalcin (OCN), and the osteoblast differentiation inhibitory protein, sclerostin (SOST) (Kalajzic et al., 2005). SOST is a Wnt/β-catenin pathway inhibitor characteristically upregulated in MBD (Colucci et al., 2011), and was of interest due to the finding that RAM2061 treatment reduced the levels of β-catenin signaling proteins (Fig. 4a,b). Results indicated reduced expression of both early- (ATF4, ALP, COL1A1, OSX, RUNX2) and late-stage (ATF4, OCN) osteoblast markers following a 7-day differentiation period in the presence of RAM2061 (Fig. 4c). Furthermore, SOST expression was significantly reduced (Fig. 4c). To determine adipocyte lineage commitment, expression of adiponectin (ADIPOQ), CCAATT enhancer-binding protein α (CEBPA), peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARG), and ADIPOQ inhibitor, TWIST2, were evaluated following MC3T3-E1 differentiation in the presence of RAM2061 (Fig. 4d). While expression of CEBPA, PPARG, and TWIST2 were reduced with GGSI treatment, changes in ADIPOQ expression were not statistically significant (Fig. 4d). Finally, the expression of soluble factors such as RANKL and soluble decoy, osteoprotegrin (OPG), were also quantified as a preliminary evaluation of GGSI effects on osteoblast-to-osteoclast communication (Fig. 4e). Results found GGSI treatment of differentiating cells to result in significantly reduced expression of OPG and RANKL (Fig. 4e).

Developing an in vitro CM model of MBD

The present studies, in conjunction with our previous work focused on osteoclasts (Muehlebach et al., 2025), were conducted under normal physiological conditions, however effects of GGSI treatment under pathogenic conditions that recapitulate the MM microenvironment had yet to be evaluated. Initial efforts planned to utilize co-cultures with bone precursor cells (i.e., MC3T3-E1, RAW264.7) differentiating in the presence of JJN3 MM cells to investigate the effects of RAM2061 on pathogenic bone cell biology. However, MTT cytotoxicity assays demonstrated that JJN3 cells were significantly more sensitive to RAM2061 (Supp Fig. 2), thus limiting the ability to determine whether any observed effects from RAM2061 were due to MM cell killing vs more direct effects on osteoblastic or osteoclastic activity. We therefore pursued a JJN3 CM-model to mimic soluble interactions with MM cells in the MBD microenvironment.

JJN3 CM was generated by incubating JJN3 cells in serum-free media for 20–24 h, at which point the CM was collected and filtered. CM was then added to MC3T3-E1 (pre-osteoblast) or RAW264.7 (pre-osteoclast) cultures and effects on bone cell differentiation and function were evaluated. To establish MBD-like pro-osteoclastic conditions RAW264.7 cells were differentiated in the presence of varying JJN3 CM concentrations. At the end of the differentiation period, osteoclast function was measured via TRAP+ staining and bone resorption assays (Supp Fig. 3a,b). It was determined that the addition of 15 % (v/v) JJN3 CM significantly promoted TRAP+ osteoclast differentiation and increased overall osteoclast-mediated resorption (Supp Fig. 3a,b). Therefore, subsequent osteoclastic CM experiments utilized 15 % JJN3 CM to simulate MM-induced osteoclast activating conditions.

To establish an osteoblast-inhibitory environment indicative of MBD (Giuliani et al., 2005), osteoblast function was measured by ALP activity and ARS following differentiation under varying concentrations of JJN3 CM (Supp Fig. 3c,d). These studies demonstrated that 15 % JJN3 CM also significantly reduced osteoblast ALP function and mineralization (Supp Fig. 3c,d). Furthermore, osteoblast-specific genes (i.e., ALP, COL1A1, OCN, OSX, RUNX2) were confirmed to be significantly downregulated under 15 % JJN3 CM conditions, and osteoblast inhibitor SOST was upregulated (Supp Fig. 3e). Therefore, subsequent CM experiments consisted of normal osteogenic medium supplemented with 15 % JJN3 CM.

In the presence of JJN3 CM, RAM2061 treatment of differentiating or mature RAW264.7 cells significantly reduced TRAP+ osteoclast formation (Fig. 5a,b), similar to that previously reported under normal differentiation conditions (Muehlebach et al., 2025). Furthermore, RAM2061 reduced overall resorptive activity of RAW264.7 cells differentiated in the presence of JJN3 CM (Fig. 5c).

NF-κB signaling is a primary mediator of RANKL-mediated osteoclastogenesis (Arai et al., 1999). Therefore, to further interrogate the mechanism behind RAM2061's anti-osteoclastic effects under normal and JJN3 CM conditions, immunoblot analysis for NF-κB pathway proteins was conducted (Fig. 5d,e). Protein levels of transcriptionally active, phosphorylated p65 (p-p65) vs transcriptionally inactive p65 were measured, as were levels of the NF-κB inhibitor IκBα in both its inactive (p-IκBα) and active (IκBα) forms (Fig. 5d,e). Interestingly, this effect was lost when RAM2061 treatment was added during late-stage osteoclastogenesis (Fig. 5e).

Discussion

Our previous studies demonstrated that the potent GGSI RAM2061 induces myeloma cell death in vitro and in vivo (Haney et al., 2019), as well as impairs normal osteoclast activity (Muehlebach et al., 2025), raising the hypothesis that this therapy could be used for MBD. Considering MBD is characterized by not only osteoclastic overactivation but osteoblastic inhibition, investigations next focused on elucidating the effects of GGSI treatment on osteoblast biology. In the present study, we investigated the effects of RAM2061 on osteoblast activity under normal conditions as well as determined the effects in both osteoblasts and osteoclasts under conditions mimicking the MM microenvironment. Utilizing MC3T3-E1 pre-osteoblast cells, RAM2061 was found to impair osteoblast differentiation and activity as evidenced by reduced ALP activity, mineralization capacity, and expression of osteoblast-specific genes. Furthermore, these effects were comparable to those observed with the standard of care agent ZA. In contrast, the inhibitory effects of GGSI treatment during late-stage osteoblast differentiation were minimal. Evaluation of RAM2061 treatment within a CM model of MBD revealed GGSI treatment to maintain effective anti-resorptive activity under JJN3-induced pro-osteoclastic conditions, with immunoblot analysis suggesting that these effects are independent of NF-κB signaling. Similarly, GGSI treatment of JJN3 CM differentiated MC3T3-E1 cells continued to impair osteoblast differentiation and function, while effects of treatment during late-stage osteogenesis were negligible.

Previous studies with IBP modulators (statins and NBPs) have suggested that GGPP plays an essential role in osteoblast differentiation (Mundy et al., 1999; Uzzan et al., 2007; Weivoda and Hohl, 2011a; Weivoda and Hohl, 2011b; Yoshida et al., 2006; Jensen et al., 2021; Giannasi et al., 2019; Huang et al., 2016). Initial evidence suggested GGPP to have a negative regulatory role on osteoblast differentiation, with the addition of dephosphorylated GGPP (GGOH) inhibiting osteoblast differentiation in vitro (Fisher et al., 1999; Singhatanadgit et al., 2021; Fliefel et al., 2019). While this would suggest that depletion of GGPP via inhibition of GGDPS would promote osteoblast lineage commitment, this has not been found with previously reported studies or in our present studies, where GGSI treatment impaired osteoblast precursor differentiation (Weivoda and Hohl, 2011b). Previous studies employing a first generation GGSI (digeranyl bisphosphonate (DGBP)) that is less potent than RAM2061 (Haney et al., 2019; Wiemer et al., 2007), suggested that DGBP's effects were due to an increase in cellular FPP levels, ultimately inhibiting glucocorticoid receptor (GR)-mediated osteoblastic pathways (Weivoda and Hohl, 2011b). In our studies, the FPP-depleting agent ZA exhibited anti-osteoblastic effects comparable to our GGSI, suggesting that the underlying inhibitory mechanism of action is related to changes in GGPP levels, not FPP. Therefore, while GR phosphorylation may be an inhibitory mechanism underlying RAM2061's effects, it is likely not the predominant mechanism. Our alternative proposed mechanism is that prolonged inhibition of protein geranylgeranylation, such as that evaluated in this study, leads to ATF4-mediated induction of apoptosis. Furthermore, while effects of GGSI treatment on Rab GTPase localization and function were not assessed in this study, their essential role in osteoblast function such as ALP secretion and mineralization (Hasegawa et al., 2022; Thant et al., 2022; Nabavi et al., 2012) may also contribute to the inhibitory effects of GGSI treatment. Ultimately, the net impact of GGSI therapy on osteoblast function in vivo will be dependent on a number of factors, including relative abundance of fully differentiated osteoblasts vs undifferentiated cells, uptake and turnover of the GGSI in the bony matrix, as well as duration of treatment.

These studies demonstrated a differentiation status-dependent effect of RAM2061, with undifferentiated MC3T3-E1 cells being more sensitive to GGSI treatment compared to D7 or D14 differentiated cells. This could be explained by a fate-limiting step during MSC osteogenic differentiation requiring certain levels of GGPP and/or geranylgeranylated proteins. Furthermore, a multitude of geranylgeranylated proteins mediate activation of osteoblast-stimulatory signaling cascades, which could explain the reduction in osteoblast-specific gene expression and reduced protein levels of the essential transcription factor, Runx2, seen with RAM2061-treated MC3T3-E1 differentiated cells. Thus, it is also possible that MC3T3-E1 cells differentiated in the absence of RAM2061 achieve appropriate thresholds of Runx2 expression for osteogenic commitment, therefore RAM2061 treatment at this stage would primarily impact geranylgeranylated protein-mediated osteoblastic functions (i.e., intracellular trafficking and secretion of matrix proteins) as evidenced by a trend in reduced ALP and mineralization activity with increasing GGSI concentrations. This suggests that while RAM2061 inhibits osteoblast precursor differentiation when administered during early stages of lineage commitment, treatment past the point of lineage commitment seems to minimally impact osteoblast function in vitro.

Recent studies with RAM2061 and stereoisomer RAM2093 have confirmed GGSI cellular uptake to be transporter-mediated, with substrate specificity influenced by drug stereochemistry and substituents at the α‑carbon (Haney et al., 2025). Importantly, transporter expression varies across osteoblast differentiation, therefore disparate drug effects influenced by osteogenic differentiation status could also be explained by stage-specific expression of RAM2061 transporters in mature osteoblasts vs undifferentiated precursors (Monjo et al., 2010). While RAM2061 is a confirmed substrate of human transporters OATP1B1 and OATP1B3 (Haney et al., 2025), whether theses specific transporters (or murine homolog oatp1b2) are expressed during osteoblast different is unclear.

MBD pathology is due to both MM cell-bone cell interactions and soluble factors which uncouple the normal bone remodeling process. Focusing on the role of soluble factors specifically, we utilized a CM model to mimic interactions between MM cells and osteoblasts or osteoclasts. Notably, RAM2061 maintained anti-resorptive effects in the presence of JJN3 CM with these anti-osteoclastic effects associated with an increase in p-p65 levels. While p-p65 is traditionally thought to promote osteoclast differentiation, elevated levels could be due to RAM2061-mediated induction of the UPR-related protein activating protein ribosomal S6 kinase I (RSK1) (Bohuslav et al., 2004). RSK1 has been found to phosphorylate p65 in a IκBα-independent manner and promote expression of proapoptotic genes (Bohuslav et al., 2004), therefore future studies will explore whether RSK1-mediated pathways are impacted by GGSI treatment. GGSI treatment on osteoblast cells differentiated in the presence of JJN3 CM confirmed a similar phenomenon seen under normal osteogenic conditions such that undifferentiated precursors are significantly more sensitive to GGSI treatment compared to differentiated cells. Interestingly, while effects of RAM2061 on β-catenin, Runx2, and RANKL protein levels in differentiated MC3T3-E1 cells were minimal, the effects in JJN3 differentiated MC3T3-E1 cells were absent. Therefore it will be interesting to investigate whether MM-related factors mediate osteoblastic drug resistance, perhaps explaining the lack of anti-osteoblastic effects associated with ZA treatment in humans. If osteoblasts in MM are less sensitive to the osteo-inhibitory effects of GGSI treatment, it would be interesting to evaluate the effects of combining GGSI treatment with osteoblast-stimulating anabolic agents which could have the potential to inhibit bone resorption and promote new bone formation.

In addition to MM soluble factors, secretion/expression of canonical coupling factors such as SOST, RANKL, and OPG among bone cells are also dysregulated in MBD. GGSI treatment of differentiating MC3T3-E1 cells under normal osteogenic conditions reduced expression of MM-associated factors such as SOST (an osteoblast inhibitor) and OPG and RANKL (osteoclastic factors). However, this was not the case with MC3T3-E1 cells differentiating in the presence of JJN3 CM. Apoptosis in JJN3 CM- and RAM2061-treated MC3T3-E1 cultures prohibited quantification of gene expression levels with 400 nM treatment. Thus, RAM2061 may not directly inhibit MM-induced promotion of RANKL or SOST but instead induce apoptosis of osteoblast precursors at higher concentrations, overall reducing expression levels.

Understanding GGSI treatment to differentially impact expression of bone coupling factors in the presence or absence of JJN3 CM further highlights the importance of utilizing biological models which consider all aspects of the bone remodeling cycle. Prior in vivo studies utilized a MM flank xenograft model and therefore only assessed impact of GGSI treatment on the tumor (Haney et al., 2019). Therefore, future in vivo studies utilizing mouse models that recapitulate MBD will be of importance to account for both the anti-tumoral effects (i.e. RAM2061-induced apoptosis of MM cells which indirectly ameliorates the pro-osteoclastic and anti-osteoblastic effects) and the direct bone cell effects (e.g. RAM2061-induced anti-osteoclastic effects). Furthermore, future in vivo modeling will allow for single cell RNA sequencing studies to validate GGSI-specific bone cell effects and clarify impact on resident cell populations in the tumor microenvironment which may contribute to disease morbidity.

Other limitations of the current study include the use of immortalized cell lines. Primary bone cell cultures can be difficult to maintain due to issues with viability and heterogeneity, therefore the MC3T3-E1 and RAW264.7 cell lines, which are widely used for modeling bone biology in vitro (Cuetara et al., 2006; Wang et al., 1999b) were employed to establish GGSI-mediated effects. Future experiments utilizing alternative models such as ex vivo cultures of calvarial bone could be performed to validate GGSI-mechanistic bone cell effects. Additionally, osteoclast and osteoblast cultures were used in the CM studies to determine the osteoclast vs osteoblast specific effects of GGSI treatment under MBD-like conditions. However, this limited the overall understanding of GGSI treatment on net bone remodeling since this model does not consider direct osteoclast-osteoblast related coupling mechanisms.

In conclusion, these studies established that exposure of osteoblast precursors during differentiation to GGSI treatment impairs differentiation to both the osteoblast and adipocyte lineages and reduces osteoblast function, but that the impact of GGSI treatment on differentiated osteoblasts was minimal. Notably, the anti-osteoclastic effects of GGSI treatment were maintained in the pro-osteoclastic milieu of myeloma cell CM while the anti-osteoblastic effects were either unchanged (relative to cells not exposed to CM) or abrogated. In aggregate, these studies support future investigation into the mechanisms linking GGPP homeostasis, geranylgeranylated proteins, osteoblast biology and osteoclast biology. Future studies using preclinical models of MBD are needed to determine the full therapeutic potential of RAM2061 as it relates to control of the underlying tumor (MM), disruption of the vicious cycle and amelioration of MBD.

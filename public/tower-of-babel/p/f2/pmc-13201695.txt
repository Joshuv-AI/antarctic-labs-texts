Identification of glycometabolism-related genes for predicting the prognosis of patients with glioblastoma and its correlation with immune infiltration
Authors: Xueshan Dong, Luhang Yu, Yifan Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAbnormal glucose metabolism has emerged as a prominent metabolic characteristic of various cancers, significantly influencing critical processes such as cell proliferation, metastasis, and invasion. Moreover, it plays an integral role in shaping the tumor immune microenvironment. Glioblastoma (GBM), recognized as the most prevalent malignant histopathology of the central nervous system (CNS), also exhibits notable abnormalities in glucose metabolism. Despite its clinical significance, comprehensive studies delineating the characteristics of glucose metabolism in GBM remain scarce.MethodsIn this study, we aimed to systematically investigate the differential expression of glycometabolism-related genes (DEGs) in GBM. We utilized a combination of differential expression gene analysis and Weighted Correlation Network Analysis (WGCNA) to identify relevant DEGs.To enhance the robustness of our findings, we incorporated two independent datasets (GSE68848 and GSE108474) and performed batch effect correction using the “sva” R package. Subsequently, Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses were performed to elucidate the potential molecular functions of these DEGs. To refine our focus, we employed least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression analysis to pinpoint hub DEGs associated with glycometabolism. Expression level verification and prognosis analyses were conducted using Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis 2 (GEPIA2). In addition, we highlighted the need for experimental validation of the 35 glycometabolism-related DEGs in clinical GBM samples to confirm their expression patterns and functional relevance.ResultsOur findings revealed that 11 genes—ADRA2A, CARTPT, LMNA, NPTX1, PRKCE, PTPRN, RTN2, DDIT4, EXT2, KIF5C, and PPIB—were differentially expressed between GBM tissues and healthy controls (HC). Notably, two hub genes, PPIB and PTPRN, demonstrated significant associations with the prognosis of GBM patients. Further analysis into immune infiltration, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA), and mutation patterns was conducted on these glycometabolism-related hub DEGs.We also emphasize that experimental validation of these 35 candidate genes in patient samples is essential to confirm their clinical relevance and biological roles.ConclusionThis study highlights the critical role of glycometabolism-related genes in GBM, offering potential targets for therapeutic intervention. The identification of PPIB and PTPRN as hub genes associated with prognosis underscores their significance in GBM pathology. Future research should prioritize experimental validation of the 35 glycometabolism-related DEGs in clinical specimens and further investigate the mechanistic underpinnings of these genes in the context of GBM, paving the way for innovative therapeutic strategies.Keywords: Glioblastoma, Glycometabolism, Prognosis, Immune infiltration, PPIB, PTPRN

Introduction

Glioblastoma is the most common and aggressive malignant histopathology of the brain and other central nervous systems (CNS), accounting for approximately 50% of primary malignant tumors in CNS [1]. Since 2005, the Stupp protocol, including surgery followed by concurrent radiotherapy with temozolomide, became the standard treatment approach [2]. In recent years, the research on therapeutic strategies for GBM has been progressing, especially in the field of immunotherapy and targeted therapy [3]. However, the prognosis for GBM remains very poor. The median survival of GBM patients is 15 months and the 5-year survival rate after diagnosis of GBM is only 6.9% [1, 3]. This may have something to do with the high aggressiveness of GBM, including tumor infiltration, high genomic instability, and microvascular proliferation [4]. More importantly, to date, the pathogenesis of GBM is poorly understood. Therefore, further systematic exploration of the molecular characteristics of GBM will be more conducive to the treatment strategy of GBM.

Abnormal glucose metabolism is the most striking metabolic feature of cancers, which was first reported by Otto Warburg in the 1920 s [5]. A series of subsequent studies confirmed that the reprogramming of glucose metabolism in cancers was beneficial to the proliferation, metastasis, and invasion of cancer cells [6, 7]. Furthermore, abnormal glucose metabolism was involved in the regulation of tumor immune microenvironment [8]. This can help tumor cells evade immune surveillance and suppress immunotherapy [8]. Therefore, exploring the mechanism of glucose metabolism can provide a new direction for the pathogenesis and treatment strategies for cancer patients [5]. In GBM, several studies have reported changes in glucose metabolism-related pathways. For example, epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) and p53 could drive glucose metabolism in GBM [9, 10]. However, systematic study to understand the expression profiles of glycometabolism-related related genes in GBM were limited.In this study, we not only aimed to identify glycometabolism-related DEGs using integrated bioinformatics approaches but also emphasized the necessity of experimental validation of these candidate genes in clinical GBM samples to confirm their expression and functional roles.

In this study, we first screened glycometabolism-related differential expression genes (DEGs) in GBM by integrating differential expression gene analysis with Weighted Correlation Network Analysis (WGCNA). Then we explored the potential molecular function of glycometabolism-related DEGs through Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis. The least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression analysis was used to screen the glycometabolism-related hub DEGs, followed by expression level verification and prognosis analysis through Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis 2 (GEPIA2). Finally, glycometabolism-related hub DEGs, which proved reliable and associated with prognosis in GBM patients, was further analyzed for gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and mutation analysis. This study will provide new targets for the treatment and prognosis of GBM from the perspective of glycometabolism.

Materials and methods

Data collection and processing

Two expression profiling of glioblastoma (GSE68848 and GSE108474) were downloaded from the GEO database (https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gds/). GSE68848 dataset contains brain tissue expression profiling of 228 GBM patients and 28 healthy controls. GSE108474 dataset contains brain tissue expression profiling of 221 GBM patients and 28 healthy controls. Because of the batch effect between the GSE68848 and GSE108474, the “sva” R package was used to remove the batch effect and combine them into one dataset [11], which was used as the training dataset for this study. To further validate our findings, additional independent datasets from GEO or other public repositories should be included in future studies to confirm the reproducibility of the results. In addition, the data, including 163 GBM patients and 207 HC, from Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis 2 (GEPIA2, http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/#index) was used to validate the expression level and survival analysis of Glycometabolism-related gene hub differentially expressed genes (DEGs). The GEPIA2 was an enhanced web server, basing on TCGA and the GTEx databases and being used for large-scale expression profiling and interactive analysis [12].

Acquisition of glycometabolism-related gene

Glycometabolism-related gene sets was obtained from the Molecular Signature Database (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/). In this study, total 24 glycometabolism-related gene sets were used, containing 695 glycometabolism-related genes.

Identification of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

The DEGs among GBM and HC were identified through “package limma” in R software. |logFC| ≥ 1 and p < 0.05 were set as the cut-off values.

Construction of Weighted Correlation Network Analysis (WGCNA)

WGCNA is a method, which can cluster genes with similar expression patterns and convert them into modules [13]. The WGCNA was performed through “WGCNA package” in R software. Firstly, we calculated the scale independence and average connectivity degree of network with different power value. When scale independence was above 0.85, the power value with relatively higher connectivity degree was determined as 3. Then, the network connectivity between genes was measured by a topological overlap matrix (TOM). Then, the different genes were sorted into different modules based on the similarity of gene expression patterns. The modules most associated with GBM were used for subsequent analysis.

Gene Ontology (GO) functional and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis

Functional enrichment analysis of glycometabolism-related DEGs was performed using the “clusterProfiler” R package. GO terms and KEGG pathways with adjusted p < 0.05 were considered significant.

Glycometabolism-related hub DEGs selection

The least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression analysis was applied to screen glycometabolism-related hub differentially expressed genes (DEGs) using the “glmnet” package in R. In this model, all 35 glycometabolism-related DEGs identified from previous steps were included as candidate predictors. These genes were treated as continuous variables based on their normalized expression values. The survival outcome was defined as overall survival (OS) in months, calculated from the date of diagnosis to the date of death or last follow-up. LASSO regression with 10-fold cross-validation was performed to select the most predictive features by minimizing the partial likelihood deviance, using the “λ.min” criterion (i.e., the λ value giving the minimum mean cross-validated error). Genes with nonzero coefficients at the optimal λ were considered hub DEGs.

Screening of overall survival–associated glycometabolism hub DEGs and construction of the glycometabolism-related prognostic model

To evaluate the prognostic significance of glycometabolism-related hub DEGs in GBM, we assessed the relationship between gene expression levels and overall survival (OS) using Kaplan–Meier survival analysis via the GEPIA2 platform. For each gene, patients were divided into high-expression and low-expression groups based on the median expression value across all GBM samples. The median split method was chosen to ensure balanced group sizes and facilitate interpretability in survival comparisons. Survival curves were compared using the log-rank test, and hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals were calculated. Genes showing statistically significant differences in OS between high and low expression groups were considered prognosis-associated hub DEGs. A composite prognostic model was then constructed by combining the expression levels of these significant genes, and its association with OS was evaluated using multivariate Cox regression analysis.

Immune infiltration analysis

The landscape of immune cell infiltration was analyzed through the CIBERSORT algorithm (https://cibersort.stanford.edu). The CIBERSORT was computational approach, which can be used to accurately calculate relative fractions of different cell subsets in complex tissues through gene expression profiles [14]. In this study, 22 types of immune cells were evaluated through CIBERSORT algorithm between the GBM and HC group. In addition, the correlation between differential immune cell abundance and the expression level of glycometabolism-related hub DEGs was further analyzed by Spearman correlation analysis.

Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) and mutation analysis

GSEA analysis was used to explore the correlation between the expression level of Glycometabolism-related hub DEGs and predetermined gene sets [15]. The GSEA analysis was performed through the clusterProfiler R package, basing on the expression profile of GSE68848 and GSE108474. The mutation analysis of Glycometabolism-related hub DEGs in GBM was performed through Sangerbox 3.0 platform [16].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses and visualization were performed using R version 3.6.0 and Sangerbox 3.0. Wilcoxon test was used to estimate the differences between GBM and HC group in immune infiltration analysis. Categorical data were compared using the χ2 test. P-value < 0.05 was set as statistically significant.

Result

Screening of differential genes related to glycometabolism in glioblastoma

In order to screen the differentially expressed genes in GBM, WGCNA and differential gene analysis were performed. When soft thresholding (power) was set at 3, the average connectivity was high and the scale independence is equal to 0.89 (Fig. 1A and B). Finally, eighteen modules with high GBM correlation were identified (Fig. 1C). Among them, module yellow has the most significant positive correlation with GBM (r = 0.35, p = 6.1e-16), while module turquoise has the most significant negative correlation with GBM (r=−0.48, p = 4e-30) (Fig. 1D-F). In the analysis of DEGs, 683 up-regulated and 741 down-regulated differentially expressed genes were identified under the screening condition of |logFC| ≥ 1 and p < 0.05 (Fig. 1G). Finally, we identified 35 glycometabolism-related DEGs in GBM by integrating the results of WGCNA, DEGs and glycometabolism-related gene sets (Fig. 1H). These 35 genes represent a core set of candidates that warrant further experimental validation in clinical GBM samples to confirm their expression patterns and potential roles in tumor metabolism and immune modulation. GO and KEGG enrichment analysis and glycometabolism-related hub DEGs selection

We performed GO enrichment analysis to explore the biological processes, cellular components, and molecular function. The biological processes of glycometabolism-related DEGs were involved in cellular to carbohydrate stimulus, cellular glucose homeostasis, response to carbohydrate, glucose homeostasis, carbohydrate homeostasis, etc. (Fig. 2A). The cellular components of glycometabolism-related DEGs were mainly located in endomembrane system, endoplasmic reticulum, neuron part, neuron projection, synapse, etc. (Fig. 2B). The molecular function of them were related with drug binging, catalytic activity, acting on a protein, monosaccharide binding, protein kinase C activity, etc. (Fig. 2C). In addition, KEGG enrichment Analysis was applied to analyze the pathways involved in glycometabolism-related DEGs. The results showed that these genes were involved in insulin resistance, AGE-RAGE signaling pathway in diabetic complications, glucagon signaling pathway, insulin signaling pathway, oxytocin signaling pathway, etc. (Fig. 2D). Although these pathways are statistically enriched, the relatively small number of genes per category warrants cautious interpretation; the enrichment may reflect the focused nature of our gene set rather than broad biological processes. Furthermore, LASSO regression analysis was applied to screen the glycometabolism-related hub DEGs. Finally, thirteen glycometabolism-related hub DEGs (ADRA2A, CARTPT, DDIT4, EXT2, LMNA, KIF5C, NPTX1, OPN3, PPIB, PRKAA2, PRKCE, PTPRN, and RTN2) were selected by LASSO regression (Fig. 2E and F).

Validation of glycometabolism-related hub DEGs in GBM through GEPIA2

We further verified the expression levels of thirteen glycometabolism-related hub DEGs using GEPIA2. The results showed that the expression level of ADRA2A, CARTPT, LMNA, NPTX1, PRKCE, PTPRN, and RTN2 were significantly decreased in GBM, while the expression level of DDIT4, EXT2, KIF5C, and PPIB were increased in GBM, compared with HC group (Fig. 3A-G, I, K-M). There was no significant difference in the expression level of OPN3 and PRKAA2 between GBM and HC (Fig. 3H and J). The 11 glycometabolism-related hub DEGs (ADRA2A, CARTPT, LMNA, NPTX1, PRKCE, PTPRN, RTN2, DDIT4, EXT2, KIF5C, and PPIB) with statistical differences in the above verification results were used for subsequent analysis.

Screening of overall survival–associated glycometabolism hub DEGs and construction of the glycometabolism related prognostic model

To further explore the prognostic significance of glycometabolism related hub DEGs in GBM, we evaluated the relationship between expression level of glycometabolism-related hub DEGs and overall survival of GBM patients through the Kaplan-Meier survival analysis in GEPIA2. Patients were stratified into high and low expression groups based on the median expression value of each gene. The results revealed that GBM patients with low expression levels of PPIB or PTPRN had a higher probability of overall survival rate, compared to those with high expression levels (Fig. 4H and J). The expression levels of the remaining 9 glycometabolism related hub DEGs were not significantly associated with overall survival of GBM patients (Fig. 4A-G, I, and K). Therefore, we constructed a two-gene prognostic model based on the expression levels of PPIB and PTPRN, which was closely associated with overall survival of GBM patients (Fig. 4L). In general, it was found that PPIB and PTPRN were the two glycometabolism hub DEGs most closely related to the overall survival of GBM patients.

Immune infiltration analysis in GBM and their correlation with glycometabolism related hub DEGs

Glycometabolism plays an important role in the regulation of tumor immune microenvironment. Hence, we further explored the characteristics of the abundance of immune cell infiltration in GBM and its correlation with glycometabolism related hub DEGs. Immune infiltration analysis found that the abundance of B cells naïve, T cells CD4 memory resting, T cells regulatory (Tregs), NK cells activated, and mast cells resting were reduced in GBM group, compared with HC group (Fig. 5A). There was higher abundance of B cells memory, T cells CD8, T cells CD4 naïve, T cells follicular helper, monocytes, macrophages M0, dendritic cells resting, mast cells activated, and neutrophils in GBM group than HC group (Fig. 5A). The results of correlation analysis showed that the expression level of PPIB was positively correlated with the abundance of neutrophils, mast cells activated, and macrophages M0 and negatively correlated with mast cell resting, T cells CD4 memory resting, T cells CD4 naïve, and B cells naïve [Figure 5B]. Furthermore, the expression level of PTPRN was positively correlated with the abundance of B cells naïve, T cells CD4 memory resting, T cells regulatory (Tregs), NK cells activated, and mast cells resting and negatively correlated with T cells follicular helper, monocytes, macrophages M0, mast cells activated, and neutrophils (Fig. 5C). These correlations suggest that PPIB and PTPRN may play opposing roles in modulating the GBM immune microenvironment.

GSEA and mutation analysis of glycometabolism hub DEGs

In order to further explore the role of PPIB and PTPRN in the occurrence and development of GBM, we detected the molecular pathways associated with PPIB and PTPRN through GSEA enrichment analysis. According to the median value of PPIB and PTPRN expression level, GBM patients were divided into high and low expression groups for GSEA analysis. The results showed that proteasome, cysteine and methionine metabolism, amino sugar and nucleotide sugar metabolism, pyrimidine metabolism, protein export, and lysosome pathway were significantly enriched in the group with high PPIB expression group [Figure 6 A]. In addition, calcium signaling pathway, phosphatidylinositol signaling pathway, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, and n_glycan biosynthesis was obviously enriched in the group with high PTPRN expression group, while the low PTPRN expression group was associated with non-homologous end joining and long-term potentiation [Figure 6B]. However, enrichment scores were modest, and these findings should be interpreted conservatively as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive. In mutation analysis, the expression level of PPIB was related with the mutation of FLG, VWF, TAFIL, and COL22A1 [Figure 6C]. The expression level of PTPRN was related with the mutation of MUC16, SLCO6A1, and CACNA1S [Figure 6D].

Discussion

Abnormal glucose metabolism is one of the important characteristics of tumor occurrence and development. Abnormal glucose metabolism can not only promote the proliferation, metastasis, and invasion of cancer cells, but also participate in regulating the tumor microenvironment [6–8]. In this study, we found that 11 genes (ADRA2A, CARTPT, LMNA, NPTX1, PRKCE, PTPRN, RTN2, DDIT4, EXT2, KIF5C, and PPIB) were differently expressed in GBM and HC through integrate datasets from GEO and TCGA databases. In the prognostic analysis, we proved that the expression levels of PPIB and PTPRN were closely associated with the overall survival of GBM patients.However, it is important to note that the 35 glycometabolism-related DEGs identified in this study require further experimental validation in clinical GBM samples to confirm their expression and biological roles. Future studies should employ qRT-PCR, Western blot, or IHC to validate these candidates and explore their functional significance in GBM pathogenesis and immune modulation.

Peptidyl-prolyl cis-trans isomerase B (PPIB), also known as cyclophilin B (CypB), is a 21 kDa cyclosporine-binding protein, made up of 216 amino acids [17]. It mainly expresses in nucleus and endoplasmic reticulum lumen [18, 19]. The molecular function of PPIB mainly involves secretory pathway and assisting protein folding [20]. In GSEA analysis, we also found that the high expression of PPIB was associated with protein export. In addition, studies have shown that PPIB played an important role in tumors. For example, cyclophilin B can promote growth and metastasis of colorectal cancer through STAT3/PVT1 feedforward axis [21]. Similarly, cyclophilin B can also promote cell proliferation, migration and metastasis of endometrial cancer [22]. In this study, we revealed that PPIB was significantly overexpressed in GBM and associated with poor OS. In 2014, Choi et al., reported that inhibition of cyclophilin B expression can reduce the proliferation and survival of GBM through regulating Chk1, MYC, mutant p53, and JAK/STAT3 signaling [23]. We also found that high expression of PPIB can promote cell cycle and p53 signaling pathway, which was consistent with above results. Furthermore, proteasome, lysosome, and insulin signaling pathway were enriched in PPIB high expression group.

Tyrosine phosphatase receptor type N (PTPRN) is a neuroendocrine-enriched phosphatase involved in vesicle-mediated secretion and insulin response [24]. However, PTPRN has been shown to be abnormally expressed in many tumor tissues, including breast cancer, small cell lung cancer, and liver cancer [25]. In this study, we found that PTPRN expression level was significantly decreased in GBM and was closely associated with patient prognosis. This was consistent with previous research results [25–27]. In addition, the expression of PTPRN was also reduced in low grade glioma correlated with a bad OS [28]. In subcellular location, the expression of PTPRN was detected in in cell membrane, cytoplasmic vesicle, and endosome secretory vesicle membrane [29, 30]. As a member of protein-tyrosine phosphatase family, the function of PTPRN were involved in vesicle-mediated secretory processes, insulin secretion in response to glucose stimuli, regulation the secretion of neuroendocrine cells [31, 32]. In this study, GSEA analysis revealed that the high expression of PTPRN was associated with proteasome, lysosome, protein export, and amino sugar and nucleotide sugar metabolism, which was consistent with previous results. In addition, the high expression of PTPRN turned out to be related to cysteine and methionine metabolism and pyrimidine metabolism.

In recent years, the tumor microenvironment (TME) has become a hot spot in the field of cancer. The TME includes not only the tumor cells themselves, but also immune cells, blood vessels, fibroblasts, extracellular matrix, et [33]. Immune cells in the TME play a dual role of fighting or promoting tumor [34]. In glioma, it is characterized by a high percentage of pro-tumor immune cell infiltration [35]. This seems to provide a barrier to immunotherapy. In study, we evaluated the abundance of immune cells infiltration in GBM through CIBERSORT. There were reduced abundance of B cells naïve, T cells CD4 memory resting, T cells regulatory (Tregs), NK cells activated, and mast cells resting were reduced in GBM group, compared with HC group. The abundance of B cells memory, T cells CD8, T cells CD4 naïve, T cells follicular helper, monocytes, macrophages M0, dendritic cells resting, mast cells activated, and neutrophils were increased in GBM group. More importantly, increasing evidence reported the importance of glucose metabolism in modulating immune cells in TME [36]. In study, we found that the expression of PPIB was related with the abundance of B cell naïve, T cells CD4 naive, T cells CD4 memory resting, macrophages M0, mast cells resting and active, and neutrophils. The opposing correlations of PPIB and PTPRN with immune cell subsets are particularly intriguing. PPIB’s positive correlation with pro-tumorigenic immune cells (M0 macrophages, neutrophils) and negative correlation with anti-tumorigenic subsets (resting mast cells, memory CD4 T cells) suggests a potential role in fostering an immunosuppressive microenvironment. Conversely, PTPRN’s correlation pattern aligns with a more immunocompetent phenotype. Given that PPIB can be secreted by T lymphocytes during inflammation [37], these findings raise the possibility that glycometabolism-related genes actively participate in immune modulation in GBM.

The study has several limitations. First of all, the results of this study based on the public database and were not verified in our sample. Secondly, while we identified 35 glycometabolism-related DEGs, only a subset was validated in external datasets, and experimental validation in clinical specimens is lacking. Third, the role of PPIB and PTPRN in immune cell infiltration was not very clear. In future studies, we will elucidate their role and mechanism in regulating the immune cell infiltration in TME and experimentally validate the expression and function of key glycometabolism-related genes in GBM samples.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, all analyses were based on publicly available data and lack experimental validation in clinical specimens. Second, while we identified 35 glycometabolism-related DEGs, functional enrichment analyses included relatively few genes per category, limiting the robustness of biological interpretations. Third, GSEA findings showed modest enrichment scores and should be viewed as hypothesis-generating. Fourth, the correlative nature of immune infiltration analyses precludes causal inferences. Future studies should prioritize experimental validation of key candidates (particularly PPIB and PTPRN) in patient samples and functional dissection of their roles in GBM metabolism and immune modulation.

Conclusions

This study provides a comprehensive characterization of glycometabolism-related gene expression in GBM, identifying 11 differentially expressed genes (ADRA2A, CARTPT, LMNA, NPTX1, PRKCE, PTPRN, RTN2, DDIT4, EXT2, KIF5C, and PPIB) and highlighting PPIB and PTPRN as potential prognostic biomarkers. Our findings suggest that these genes may influence both metabolic pathways and immune cell infiltration, offering new avenues for therapeutic targeting. However, experimental validation in clinical specimens and mechanistic studies are urgently needed to translate these bioinformatic observations into biological insights.

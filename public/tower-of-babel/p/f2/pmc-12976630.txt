Randomized, Double-Blind, Multicenter, Phase 3 Study of AG NPP709 Compared With Ivy Leaf Extract in Patients With Acute or Chronic Respiratory Symptoms
Authors: Young-Joo Cho, Ho Joo Yoon, Dae Hyun Lim, Kwang Ha Yoo, Sun-Hee Choi, Yeong-Ho Rha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAG NPP709, formulated with extracts of ivy leaves and Coptis chinensis rhizomes, has antitussive and expectorant effects in preclinical rodent studies. We conducted a clinical trial to establish the efficacy and safety of the product compared with a widely prescribed ivy leaf extract product for the treatment of respiratory symptoms.MethodsIn this randomized, double-blind, active-controlled, non-inferiority phase 3 trial, conducted in six hospitals in South Korea, symptomatic patients with acute upper respiratory infections (URIs) or chronic inflammatory bronchitis were randomized to receive either AG NPP709 or ivy leaf extract (control drug) for 5 days (n = 118 in each group). The primary efficacy endpoint was the clinical improvement rate, defined as the percentage of patients with symptoms evaluated as the top two scores on a five-point Likert scale at the end of the study (with a non-inferiority margin set at −15.0%).ResultsThe overall patient age ranged from 2 to 70 years, with a mean age of 24.9 years in the AG NPP709 group and 21.7 years in the control group. No statistical difference was observed in the clinical improvement rate, with a between-group difference of 4.3% (95% confidence interval: −8.9, 17.5). The symptom clearance rates (AG NPP709 vs. control) for cough and sputum were 43.0% vs. 40.0% (P = 0.655) and 34.6% vs. 25.5% (P = 0.142), respectively. The incidence of adverse events was similar in both groups (11.0% vs. 11.1%, P = 0.982), with most events being grade 1 or 2.ConclusionAG NPP709 was non-inferior to ivy leaf extract and had an acceptable safety profile. AG NPP709 could be an alternative treatment option for alleviating the symptoms associated with acute URI and chronic bronchitis.Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT01151202

INTRODUCTION

Cough associated with upper respiratory infections (URIs) or bronchitis, most often of viral etiology, is one of the most common reasons for referrals to physicians worldwide.1234 Another common URI-associated disease with potentially life-threatening clinical implications is impaired clearance of sputum caused by sputum’s tenacity and the resultant increase in its adhesiveness and cohesiveness.5 Currently available cough medications, such as codeine and dextromethorphan, pose a risk of drowsiness and drug dependency.6 Moreover, available mucolytics, such as N-acetylcysteine and ambroxol, have side effects, with the most prominent being nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea.7 Thus, interest in alternative antitussive and expectorant remedies, such as herbal medications, has increased.

AG NPP709 (Synatura®) is an herbal medication developed by Ahn-Gook Pharmaceuticals Co., Ltd. (Seoul, Korea) for treating cough and sputum in patients with acute respiratory infections and chronic inflammatory bronchitis. The product, approved in South Korea and other countries, was formulated with a dried 30% ethanolic extract of Hedera helix L. (ivy; Lamiaceae) leaves and a dried water-saturated butanolic extract of Coptis chinensis Franch (Ranunculaceae) rhizome (3:1, w/w). Saponins, which are active constituents of ivy leaf extract, may have expectorant, bronchodilator, and spasmolytic properties, and several randomized clinical trials have demonstrated that ivy mono-preparations effectively alleviate cough and sputum associated with URI and bronchitis.891011121314 Meanwhile, berberine, an active constituent in Coptis sp. and Berberis sp., attenuates airway inflammation and mucus overproduction and prevents bacterial and viral growth.15161718192021

Preclinical studies have demonstrated potent, dose-dependent antitussive and expectorant effects of AG NPP709 in rodents.22 The 1:1 mixture of H. helix and C. chinensis, administered at 200 mg/kg, resulted in significantly greater cough suppression than that of H. helix (61.25% vs. 39.89%, P < 0.01), C. chinensis (61.25% vs. 30.25%, P < 0.001), and theobromine (61.25% vs. 53.22%, P < 0.05). In the phenol red secretion assay, a method used to evaluate expectorant activity, the same formulation increased sputum clearance by 25.25%, which was a significantly better outcome observed with H. helix (25.25% vs. 13.39%, P < 0.001) and C. chinensis (25.25% vs. 20.78%, P < 0.01) and similar to ambroxol (25.25% vs. 25.80%). The optimal ratio of Hedera to C. chinensis was 3:1, formulated as AG NPP709. The pharmacokinetic and toxicokinetic properties of AG NPP709 and its main constituents were verified in a rat models.2324

This study presents the results of a phase 3 trial that constituted the basis for the initial registration of AG NPP709 in South Korea for the treatment of acute URI or chronic bronchitis.

METHODS

Study procedures

This randomized, double-blind, Phase 3 trial, conducted at six hospitals in South Korea, was designed to investigate the efficacy and safety of AG NPP709 compared to the active control drug, ivy leaf extract. This study aimed to demonstrate the non-inferiority of AG NPP709 to ivy leaf extracts in terms of clinical improvement. Patients with respiratory symptoms due to URI or chronic inflammatory bronchitis were screened, and eligible patients were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either AG NPP709 or the control drug (ivy leaf extract) for 5 days. An independent statistician created randomization numbers, each of which was printed in a sealed envelope. The investigators sequentially assigned a randomization number to each patient, and the study pharmacists dispensed the study drug matching the randomization number to each patient. Investigators, patients, and patients' legal guardians were blinded to the randomization information during the study. Initial symptoms were assessed by the investigator, and the patients were instructed to record the severity of each symptom daily in a diary. If the patient was aged < 19 years, the patient's legal guardian was instructed to complete the diary on behalf of the patient, and the same person recorded the diary for the duration of the study. On day 5, the patients revisited the study site and returned their diaries. The investigators re-evaluated the patient’s symptoms and reviewed the patient's diary records. Physical examinations, measurements of vital signs (body temperature and blood pressure), clinical laboratory tests (blood and urine tests), and 12-lead electrocardiography were performed for clinical safety assessments.

Patients

Eligible patients were individuals of any sex with at least two of the following symptoms: sputum, cough, difficulty in breathing, or painful respiration. The age range of the eligible patients was 24–74 years. If the participant was a woman of childbearing age, a negative pregnancy test result was required, or the person was menstruating at the time of screening. Pregnant or breastfeeding women were not allowed to participate in the study, and individuals with the following conditions were excluded: severe respiratory diseases, such as pneumonia; uncontrolled asthma; influenza virus infection with complications; tuberculosis; malignant tumor; severe central neurological disorder; other severe diseases requiring treatment; fructose intolerance; hypersensitivity to ivy leaf extract; significant heart conditions, including abnormal electrocardiogram findings; increased (three times the upper normal limit) liver enzyme or blood creatinine; or uncontrolled diabetes or hypertension.

Medications

On the day of randomization, all patients were provided two bottles containing the same brownish suspension. Both AG NPP709 and the control drug (dried 30% ethanolic extract of ivy leaves) were taken orally three times a day for a total of 5 days. The volume per dose was 5 mL for patients 2–6 years old, 10 mL for 7–14 years, and 15 mL for ≥ 14 years. At each dose, half of the prescribed volume of the suspension from each bottle was collected using the dosing device. Both bottles assigned to patients in the AG NPP709 group contained active substances; in the control group, only one bottle contained the active substance, and the other contained placebo syrup with the same characteristics as the active substance (Supplementary Table 1). The maximum daily doses of AG NPP709 delivered by the regimen mentioned above were 52.5 mg, 105.0 mg, and 157.5 mg, respectively, for each age group, and that of the control drug had doses of 105.0 mg, 210.0 mg, and 315.0 mg, respectively. All study drugs were manufactured, labeled, and supplied by Ahn-Gook Pharmaceuticals Co. Ltd.

Endpoints

The primary efficacy endpoint was clinical improvement as assessed by the investigator at the end of the study. The investigator scored the patient’s improvement level on a five-point Likert scale based on clinical evaluation and review of the patient’s diary record. The five-point Likert scale consisted of the following items: (0) not improved or worsened, (1) slightly improved, (2) moderately improved, (3) greatly improved, and (4) completely resolved. Only the last two scores, completely resolved or greatly improved, were considered clinical improvements. The secondary efficacy endpoints included changes in symptom score, symptom clearance rate, and time to symptom clearance based on the patient's self-assessment of the symptoms. The symptoms of interest included sputum production, cough, difficulty breathing, or painful respiration. The severity of each symptom was scored in one of four categories. For the sputum, a zero (0) score was assigned to no sputum, one (1) to clear and non-viscous sputum, two (2) to off-white and viscous sputum, and three (3) to yellowish and thick sputum. The severity of the other three symptoms was scored according to the following criteria: (0) no symptoms, (1) mild symptoms, (2) moderate, tolerable symptoms, and (3) severe symptoms that negatively affect daily life or sleep quality. Additionally, the proportion of patients who used rescue medications was analyzed. Regarding safety endpoints, any adverse events that occurred after the first dose of the study treatment were collected together with the results of the clinical safety assessments.

Sample size and efficacy analysis

A minimum sample size of 94 participants per group was calculated to achieve 90% power at a one-sided significance level of 0.05, assuming a 15% non-inferiority margin. A total of 236 patients (118 per group) were enrolled, with a dropout rate of 20%. A 15% non-inferiority margin was selected based on both clinical relevance and statistical considerations, assuming that the efficacy of the active control was 85.7%, as demonstrated in previous clinical trials. A two-step testing procedure was employed as the primary endpoint to ensure rigorous evaluation of the efficacy of AG NPP709. The lower bound of the 95% confidence interval [CI] for the between-group difference (AG NPP709–control) in the clinical improvement rate exceeded −15.0%, demonstrating non-inferiority. Moreover, the point estimate of the between-group difference had to be greater than 0% to preserve the clinical relevance of AG NPP709 efficacy.

Categorical variables, such as sex, severity of each symptom, and symptom clearance rate, are presented as the number of patients (%), and between-group differences were tested using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test, if needed. Continuous variables, such as age or symptom score, are presented as summary statistics, and between-group differences were tested using a two-sample t-test. Changes in symptom scores on day 5 from baseline in each group were tested using paired t-tests. The symptom clearance rate was calculated by dividing the number of patients with no symptoms (grade 0) on day 5 by the number of patients with baseline symptoms of grade 1 or greater severity. Therefore, patients without baseline symptoms were excluded from the analysis, even though their symptom severity was evaluated as grade 0 on day 5. Time to symptom clearance was calculated using the Kaplan–Meier analysis, and between-group differences were tested using a log-rank test. Patients without symptom clearance at the end of the study, those who withdrew from the study, and those who used rescue medications were censored at the end of the study, the last visit, or the date of medication initiation. Individuals who did not develop symptoms were excluded from the Kaplan–Meier analysis. Efficacy data are presented for both the intention-to-treat (ITT) and per-protocol sets. The ITT set comprised randomized patients who received at least one dose of the study treatment. In the per-protocol set, patients with the following conditions were excluded: treatment compliance of < 70%, unmet eligibility criteria, use of contraindicated medications, or no records in the patient’s diary. As treatment adherence was a critical factor in evaluating the efficacy of AG NPP709 and the control drug, considering the pharmacokinetic profiles of both drugs, especially their short half-lives and moderate clearances, the main interpretation of the efficacy results was based on per-protocol analysis. Missing efficacy data were handled using the last observation carried forward. All statistical analyses were performed using the Statistical Analysis Software version 9.2 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). Significance was set at P < 0.05.

Safety analysis

Adverse events were coded using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 3.0, and the incidence of adverse events (the number of patients with adverse events divided by the total number of patients in the group) was compared between groups using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. The severity of adverse events and their causality with the study treatment were expressed as the number of events. Continuous data, such as laboratory test results, were analyzed using a paired t-test to assess differences from baseline within each treatment group, and a two-sample t-test was conducted for between-group comparisons. For vital signs, descriptive statistics were provided for systolic and diastolic blood pressure, heart rate, and body temperature by group and visit time. Additionally, for the physical examination, electrocardiography, and laboratory test results, the number and proportion of patients with values outside the normal range were presented by group, and the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test was used for statistical testing. Safety data are presented as a safety set that includes all patients who received at least one dose of the study treatment.

Ethics statement

The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety of Korea and the Institutional Review Board of each study site (Supplementary Table 2). Before initiating any study-related procedures, informed consent was obtained from all patients or a patient’s legal guardian when the patient was a child or young adult aged < 19 years. The study was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (https://clinicaltrials.gov/) with the identification number NCT01151202.

RESULTS

Patients

Of the 236 patients screened, all individuals were randomized to one of the two groups (118 in each group). One patient in the control group was not exposed to the study treatment at all; therefore, this patient was excluded from all analyses. Almost equal numbers of patients from each investigational site were represented in the ITT set, with the total number of patients per site ranging from 38 to 41 (Supplementary Table 2). The number of patients included in each analysis set is provided in Fig. 1, along with the reasons for exclusion.

Patient distribution. Six tertiary hospitals in the Republic of Korea participated in the trial. Eligible patients were randomized to AG NPP709, a combination of ivy leaf extract and Coptis chinensis rhizome extract, or control drug, ivy leaf extract, in a 1:1 ratio.

ITT = intention-to-treat.

Patient demographics, such as sex and age, were similar between the two groups (Table 1). The patient age ranged from 2 to 70 years in both groups, with a mean age of 24.9 ± 17.8 years and 21.7 ± 16.5 years in the AG NPP709 and control groups, respectively. Underage patients (< 18 years) and adults aged up to 39 years constituted most of the population (Supplementary Fig. 1), with no significant differences observed in the distribution of age groups (P = 0.811). More than 80% of patients in both groups had an acute URI. Meanwhile, the proportions of patients with chronic inflammatory bronchitis were 14.4% and 12.8% in the AG NPP709 and control groups, respectively, and a similar proportion of patients had a record of previous treatment for respiratory symptoms.

Primary efficacy endpoint

No statistically significant difference was observed in the clinical improvement rate (57.0% [61/107] with AG NPP709 vs. 52.7% [58/110] with controls, P = 0.526; Fig. 2A), and the between-group difference was 4.3% (95% CI, −8.9, 17.5). The lower bound of the 95% CI of the between-group difference was greater than the predefined non-inferiority criterion (−15.0%), and the between-group difference was greater than 0%, which was an additional condition to meet the non-inferiority of AG NPP709 to the control. The ITT set analysis revealed a similar result (56.8% [67/118] vs. 51.7% [60/116], P = 0.438) (Fig. 2B), where the between-group difference was 5.1% (95% CI, −7.7, 17.8), reconfirming the non-inferiority of AG NPP709 to the control.

Secondary efficacy endpoints

As for the patients’ self-assessment of symptoms, no significant between-group difference was observed either at baseline or at the end of the study, that is, day 5 (Table 3). The symptom clearance rate based on the patient’s self-assessment was the lowest for sputum (34.6% with AG NPP709 vs. 25.5% with control, P = 0.142) and highest for painful respiration (85.7% vs. 66.7%, P = 0.559) (Fig. 3). No significant between-group differences were observed in the time to symptom clearance, as calculated from the Kaplan–Meier survival analysis (Table 4). None of the patients in either group used rescue medications to relieve their symptoms.

Safety

The incidence of adverse events was similar between the two groups (11.0% [13/118] vs. 11.1% [13/117], P = 0.982) (Table 5). Except for one adverse event of grade 3 severity reported in the control group, which was a headache assessed as unrelated to the study treatment, all other events in both groups were grade 1 or 2 in severity. Three treatment-related adverse events occurred in each group. Apart from a single dizziness event in the AG NPP709 group, all other treatment-related adverse events in both groups were gastrointestinal, including nausea, vomiting, or gastrointestinal pain. No abnormal findings were observed on physical examination or 12-lead electrocardiography after treatment. Some abnormal values were observed in the vital signs (Supplementary Table 3) and clinical laboratory tests (Supplementary Table 4); however, none were considered clinically relevant or indicative of important safety signals.

DISCUSSION

Involving patients with acute URI and chronic bronchitis, AG NPP709, a mixture of ivy leaf extract and C. chinensis rhizome extract, was non-inferior to an active comparator, ivy leaf extract, in terms of clinical improvement, symptom score, symptom clearance rate, and time to symptom clearance, and showed a satisfactory safety profile.

Natural products have been used as remedies under various conditions for centuries. Ivy leaf extract has a long history of use as an antitussive agent, and the effectiveness of ivy mono-preparations in patients with respiratory tract infections, whether adults or children, has been confirmed in several well-designed randomized clinical trials.891011121314 However, the growing demand for more potent yet safe medications has stimulated research on new remedies using the synergy between ivy leaf extract constituents and other pharmacologically active substances.25

The main components of dried ivy leaf extract are saponins (among them alpha-hederin and hederacoside C), flavonoid (rutin), and phenolic derivatives (chlorogenic acid).21 Saponins, which constitute approximately 10% of the extract, induce an expectorant effect mediated by the gastric mucosa with the resultant reflex stimulation of the bronchial mucus glands via the parasympathetic pathway. Additionally, alpha-hederin inhibits the internalization of beta2-adrenergic receptors, resulting in the upregulation of cAMP signaling, which promotes surfactant release and the relaxation of bronchial muscle cells, whereas other saponins contained in the extract display anti-inflammatory properties.26272829

Among the plethora of other biological functions reviewed in detail by Wang et al.,20 berberine, an active ingredient of C. chinensis, acts as an anti-inflammatory agent by suppressing pro-inflammatory cytokines, tumor necrosis factor-alpha, and interleukin-1B in pulmonary cells. Furthermore, berberine inhibits the mucin component of sputum by suppressing interleukin-1B-induced MUC5AC gene expression in human airway epithelial cells through the ERK- and p38 MAPK-dependent signal transduction pathways.15161730 Berberine demonstrates bronchodilatory activity, exerting an antagonistic effect on muscarinic acetylcholine receptors.21 Finally, berberine inhibits the growth of both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, among them Staphylococcus aureus, a common etiological factor in respiratory infections, and prevents the replication of multiple viruses, including respiratory syncytial virus, influenza virus, and coronavirus.181920

AG NPP709 combines the effects of ivy leaf extract and C. chinensis rhizome extract and acts as a remedy to alleviate cough and sputum in patients with URI and chronic inflammatory bronchitis. The potency of AG NPP709 was demonstrated in a preclinical study in which the expectorant and antitussive effects of the drug were quantified using phenol red secretion in mice and citric acid-induced cough in guinea pigs, respectively. These effects were more potent than those of each material alone.22 The results of the preclinical study were confirmed in the present phase 3 trial, in which AG NPP709 was non-inferior to the active comparators in terms of clinical improvement, symptom score, symptom clearance rate, and time to symptom clearance in patients with URI and chronic bronchitis.

Since the completion of the present trial, the beneficial effects of AG NPP709 have been demonstrated in four other studies. In a prospective, randomized, double-blinded, active-controlled, therapeutic confirmatory trial, a 7-day administration of AG NPP709 resulted in an overall improvement rate similar to that of Pelargonium sidoides extract in patients with acute bronchitis.31 In a prospective, open-label, single-arm phase 4 clinical trial, a 12-week administration of AG NPP709 contributed to a significant improvement in the quality of life expressed as the chronic obstructive pulmonary disease assessment test score and a significant reduction in fibrinogen level in patients with chronic bronchitis type chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.32 In a prospective, open-label, single-arm observational study, a 12-week treatment with AG NPP709 significantly improved the St. George’s Respiratory Questionnaire scores and bronchitis severity scores in patients with chronic bronchitis and bronchiectasis.33 Finally, in another open-label, prospective, single-arm study, 8-week treatment with AG NPP709 improved cough-specific quality of life in one-third of Korean patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis.34

The present study demonstrated a satisfactory safety profile for AG NPP709, with an incidence rate of 11% for adverse events, most of which were grade 1 or 2 in severity. These findings are consistent with those of previous clinical trials on ivy leaf extract, in which most adverse events were of mild-to-moderate severity and gastrointestinal in nature.14

This study has certain limitations. First, the nature of all evaluations was subjective, and the duration of the drug safety evaluation period was short. The latter issue must be addressed in future studies with longer follow-up periods. Additionally, a real-world comparative study evaluating the effectiveness of AG NPP709 against commonly used pharmacological treatments, such as dextromethorphan or N-acetylcysteine, would enhance the clinical relevance and generalizability of these findings.

In this phase 3 trial, AG NPP709 was non-inferior to ivy leaf extract in terms of efficacy and had an acceptable safety profile. Consequently, AG NPP709 could serve as an alternative treatment option for alleviating cough and sputum associated with acute URI and chronic bronchitis.

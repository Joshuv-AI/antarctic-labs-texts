A two-stage subsynchronous oscillation assessment method for DFIG-based wind farm grid-connected system
Authors: Ge Liu, Jun Liu, Andong Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In the power system, the wind farm based on Doubly-Fed Induction Generator (DFIG) may lead to Subsynchronous Oscillation (SSO), which poses a challenge to the stability of the power grid. In order to accurately evaluate SSO, this paper proposes a new evaluation method. It is divided into two main stages: firstly, the interference level of Phasor Measurement Unit (PMU) data is identified by using the classification model based on Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) and Double Deep Q Network (DDQN). Then, an SSO parameter estimation model based on Local Feature Fusion Transformer (LFF-Transformer) network is designed for data with different interference levels. Experimental results show that the errors of eRMSE-f and EMAPE-F are 0.001 and 0.003 respectively, and the errors of eRMSE-δ and EMAPE-δ are 0.009 and 0.015 respectively. In terms of training and testing time, this method is 90 s and 18 s respectively, which is significantly better than Multi-SVR and Multi-CNN. After application, the frequency deviation decreased from 0.05 to 0.02 Hz, the voltage deviation decreased from 3.5 to 1.5%, the power fluctuation decreased from 10 to 5 MW, the SSO frequency decreased from 1.5 Hz to less than 0.5 Hz, and the SSO damping ratio increased from 0.08 to 0.15. This shows that the proposed method effectively increases the stability of the power grid.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-73505-6.

Introduction

Under the background of energy transformation in the 21st century, wind energy, as a sustainable clean energy, is increasingly becoming an indispensable part of the global energy structure1–3. With the continuous progress of technology, the economy and technical feasibility of wind power generation have been significantly improved, which makes more and more countries begin to vigorously develop wind power industry4,5. Among many wind power technologies, Doubly Fed Induction Generator (DFIG) has become a hot research object and the focus of practical application in the field of wind power generation because it can effectively regulate the generator output in a wide wind speed range and has good grid-connected performance6–8.

Although great progress has been made in wind power technology, with the wide integration of large-scale wind farms, a series of new technical challenges have followed9. In the modern power system, the grid-connected system of wind farms not only improves the utilization rate of renewable energy, but also brings the problem of Subsynchronous Oscillation (SSO)10,11. SSO is a phenomenon of frequency instability caused by mechanical and electrical resonance in power system, especially when wind farms are connected to the grid, which will significantly affect the stability of power grid12–14. The SSO problem is mainly due to the interaction between wind farm and power grid and the nonlinear characteristics of power electronic equipment, which leads to the energy exchange and oscillation of the system in the subsynchronous frequency range. At present, the crisis faced by the power system is mainly reflected in several aspects: first, the dynamic characteristics of the system are complicated after wind power is connected to the grid, the traditional synchronous generator is no longer the only dominant equipment, and the introduction of new power generation equipment such as DFIG has changed the dynamic behavior of the system. Secondly, due to the intermittence and uncertainty of wind farms, the stability and reliability of power systems are facing new challenges. Especially the SSO problem, it not only affects the stability of power grid, but also may cause damage to power equipment, increasing the maintenance cost and operation risk of the system15.

In order to meet these challenges, the existing solutions mainly include introducing inhibition measures in the design stage, monitoring and controlling in the operation stage and taking remedial measures in the later stage. For example, SSO can be suppressed by installing filters, adjusting controller parameters and using synchronous compensation devices. However, these methods have some defects in practical application, such as high cost of filters and synchronous compensation devices, complicated operation and maintenance, and limited effect of traditional controllers in dealing with nonlinear and complex dynamic characteristics. Besides, academia and industry have done a lot of research and put forward many solutions. These schemes mainly include optimizing the control strategy of DFIG, improving the design and operation management of power system, and adopting specific vibration suppression equipment. However, these methods often need to be implemented after the occurrence of SSO, and their ability to prevent and identify SSO early is limited. In addition, due to the complexity of power systems and wind farms, as well as the variability of environment and operating conditions, the existing SSO evaluation and suppression technologies are facing the dual challenges of accuracy and efficiency in practical application16.

In view of this, a new two-stage SSO evaluation method is proposed to identify and evaluate SSO problems more accurately. In the first stage, a classification model based on Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) and Double Deep Q Network (DDQN) is used to identify the interference level of Phasor Measurement Unit (PMU) data. This method can show high-precision classification performance in data sets with different interference levels. In the second stage, an SSO parameter estimation model based on local feature fusion Transformer (LFF-Transformer) network is designed for data with different interference levels. Through this new evaluation method, the power system can monitor and control the SSO problem more effectively, thus improving the stability and reliability of the system. Compared with traditional methods, this study provides a more intelligent and accurate solution, which has high practical application value and popularization prospect.

In this study, a two-stage SSO evaluation method for wind farm grid-connected system based on doubly-fed induction generator (DFIG) is proposed, which has a significant research contribution to improving the stability and reliability of power system. Firstly, this paper accurately identifies the interference level of PMU data by using the classification model based on UCB and DDQN. This innovative classification method shows high-precision classification performance in data sets with different interference levels. Secondly, in order to further improve the accuracy of SSO parameter estimation, a parameter estimation model based on LFF-Transformer network is designed. By integrating local and global features, this model significantly improves the estimation accuracy of frequency and damping ratio, especially in high interference datasets. The experimental results show that compared with the traditional deep learning model, the proposed LFF-Transformer model significantly reduces the root mean square error (RMSE) of frequency and the mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) of damping ratio, which proves the application value and popularization prospect of the proposed method in the actual power system. In addition, the research in this study also provides an intelligent and accurate solution for how to effectively monitor and control SSO when renewable energy such as wind power is widely connected to the power system, which has important theoretical and practical significance for ensuring power grid security and optimizing power system operation.

Literature review

SSO has been one of the focuses of power system stability research since the power system was connected to the grid. With the rapid development of large-scale wind farms, especially wind farms with DFIG technology, SSO has once again become a research hotspot17–19. Meng et al.20 analyzed the mechanism of SSO in traditional synchronous generators and DFIG systems. The research showed that the SSO might be triggered when the control system of DFIG was not properly designed or the power grid conditions did not meet specific requirements. Kouider and Bekri21 studied the influence of grid connection of DFIG wind farm on power system stability. Especially the DFIG wind farm connected to the grid at the weak connection point of the power grid was more likely to cause SSO, which posed a challenge to the stability of the power grid. Sun et al.22 and Shen et al.23 discussed the evaluation method and suppression strategy of SSO respectively, including optimizing the control strategy of DFIG, using flexible AC transmission system equipment and using specially designed filters to suppress SSO. Chao et al.24 proposed an SSO suppression strategy for renewable energy grid-connected system based on active interference suppression control theory. Taking the integrated system of direct-drive permanent magnet synchronous generator wind farm as an example, the topological structure and control system principle of the system were described, and the simulation model was constructed. Taking into account the dynamic performance of the equivalent inductance of the power grid, Xiong et al.25 established the small signal model of the power synchronous control converter of the grid-connected converter. This revealed the mechanism of synchronous frequency resonance and non-minimum phase effect.

With the rapid development of global renewable energy, wind farm grid-connected system has become an important part of modern power system. However, due to the characteristics of DFIG, it may lead to SSO in the process of grid connection, which poses a major threat to the stability of power grid. In order to effectively evaluate and suppress SSO, many scholars have carried out a lot of related research and put forward various methods. For example, Bu et al. (2023) proposed an SSO detection method based on modal analysis. They used the time domain signal to carry out Fourier transform, and then extracted frequency and damping ratio parameters through modal analysis to identify and quantify SSO phenomenon. However, this method has poor performance in the face of high interference data and poor real-time performance26. Liu et al.27 studied an SSO classification and detection model based on support vector machine (SVM). By training SVM classifiers, different types of SSO interference levels were identified, and the spectrum was analyzed by combining with short-time Fourier transform (STFT). Although the method has high accuracy in low interference environment, the classification effect was reduced in complex interference background27. Liu et al.28 proposed an SSO parameter estimation method based on Convolutional neural network (CNN). They used deep learning technology to automatically extract features in time domain signals, and realize accurate estimation of frequency and damping ratio through multi-layer CNN structure. This method significantly improved the accuracy of SSO parameter estimation, but because CNN relied heavily on network structure and super parameters, it needed a lot of tuning work in practical application28. Li et al.29 developed an adaptive SSO detection and evaluation system using reinforcement learning algorithm. They combined Q-learning algorithm to dynamically adjust the model parameters to improve the adaptability of the system to different interference levels. Experiments showed that this method had some advantages in dealing with interference data, but its computational complexity was high, which affected its practicability29. In a word, the existing research has made many achievements in SSO detection and estimation, especially in the use of modal analysis, machine learning and deep learning technology. However, the robustness and real-time performance of these methods are still insufficient in high interference environment. In addition, the traditional methods mostly focus on a single technical means, and fail to fully integrate a variety of advanced technologies to improve the evaluation performance.

Although the existing research has made many achievements in SSO detection and estimation, especially in the use of modal analysis, machine learning and deep learning technology, these methods still have shortcomings in robustness and real-time performance in high interference environment. In addition, the traditional methods mostly focus on a single technical means, and fail to fully integrate a variety of advanced technologies to improve the evaluation performance. In this context, this study proposes a new two-stage SSO evaluation method, which aims to make up for the shortcomings of the existing methods and provide a more accurate and efficient solution. Firstly, a classification model is established by using UCB and DDQN to identify the interference level of PMU data. By combining the advantages of reinforcement learning and deep learning, this method realizes high-precision classification of data with different interference levels. Secondly, in order to further improve the accuracy of SSO parameter estimation, this study designs an SSO parameter estimation model based on LFF-Transformer network. LFF-Transformer network improves the estimation accuracy of frequency and damping ratio by fusing local features. To sum up, by innovatively combining UCB, DDQN and LFF-Transformer technology, this study not only improves the accuracy and robustness of SSO evaluation, but also performs well in dealing with high interference data. This new method improves the stability and reliability of power grid while ensuring the evaluation accuracy, and has important practical application value and popularization prospect. With the wide application of renewable energy such as wind power, the proposal and application of this method is of great significance for ensuring power grid safety and optimizing power system operation.

Application of system identification technology

In the grid-connected operation of wind farms, it is the key to ensure the stability of the system to accurately and quickly identify the frequency that may lead to SSO30–32. System identification technology provides an effective tool in this respect, which can accurately estimate the dynamic characteristics of the system based on actual operation data33–35. Especially, the least square method has been widely used in the field of dynamic parameter estimation because of its simplicity and high efficiency. Figure 3 illustrates a new method for evaluating the risk of SSO, which combines two stages: pre-training and real-time application. In the work sequence, at the data collection stage, the original data are obtained from PMU, and the samples related to SSO are extracted after screening, covering the power vectors of all operating points and transmission lines under different operating conditions. Then, in the data preprocessing stage, this study denoises, fills in missing values and normalizes the data to ensure the accuracy of subsequent analysis. Then, the interference degree is evaluated, and the data interference degree of each operating point is evaluated, which provides the basis for the subsequent interference level classification. In the off-line training part, firstly, the interference level is identified. According to the interference degree (slight, moderate and strong), the classifier is constructed by using deep reinforcement learning technology and UCB-DDQN algorithm, and the corresponding vector set is determined. Then, three different estimators are trained according to different interference levels, and the LFF-Transformer architecture is adopted, and the local and global features are integrated by the fusion module to realize the accurate estimation of SSO modal parameters. In the online application stage, the real-time PMU data is first sent to the interference level classifier for classification, and then the appropriate SSO parameter estimator is automatically selected according to the interference situation to measure the frequency and damping ratio. Finally, the system status and SSO parameter evaluation results are uploaded to the central database for real-time monitoring and further analysis. Meanwhile, based on the feedback of online application, the training network is retrained regularly to update and optimize the evaluation model, thus improving the overall performance and stability of the system.

In this study, in order to build a high-quality dataset for training and testing, the actual operation data from PMU in the power system is first obtained. PMU is a kind of equipment which can measure the voltage and current phasor of key nodes of power grid in real time with high precision, and is widely used in modern power system. Its high sampling rate and synchronous measurement ability make it an important tool to monitor the dynamic behavior of power grid. In practice, the acquisition of PMU data involves many steps and considerations. First, this study deploys PMU from key nodes in the power system to ensure that people can capture typical locations where SSO may occur. When selecting these key nodes, people should consider the topology of power grid, the grid connection points of wind farms and the areas prone to oscillation in history. Through these PMU devices, we can obtain high frequency, synchronous and accurate voltage and current phasor data. These data include not only waveforms in normal operation, but also data under different interference conditions, such as mild, moderate and strong interference data. Secondly, in the process of data collection, people need to ensure the integrity and accuracy of the data. To this end, this study regularly calibrates and maintain PMU equipment to prevent data deviation caused by equipment failure or error. In addition, it is also needed to consider the reliability of data transmission, and use the highly reliable communication network to transmit PMU data to the data center for storage and processing in real time. After obtaining a large number of original data, it cleans and marks the data through data preprocessing steps. Specifically, it first denoises the data and uses signal processing technology to remove possible measurement noise and abnormal values. Then, according to the actual situation of SSO and the knowledge of relevant experts, the interference level of data is manually marked and divided into mild, moderate and strong interference datasets. This can ensure the accuracy of the labels used in model training and help to improve the accuracy of the classification model.

In order to enhance the robustness and generalization ability of the model, a variety of simulated interference scenarios are introduced into the dataset. For example, simulation software is used to generate grid-connected data of wind farms under different working conditions, including various possible disturbance and fault scenarios. These data are combined with actual PMU data to form a more comprehensive training dataset. In this way, the model can learn more patterns and features in the training process and improve its performance in practical application. In a word, by deploying PMU equipment to obtain high-quality power grid operation data, combined with signal processing and data simulation technology, a training data set with various interference levels is constructed. This process not only takes into account various challenges and details in actual operation, but also provides a solid foundation for the training and evaluation of subsequent models. This method ensures the comprehensiveness and representativeness of the data set, which is helpful to improve the accuracy and practicability of SSO evaluation method. Discussion

In the power system research in recent years, due to the wide application of DFIG technology in wind farms, SSO has attracted wide attention. In order to maintain the stability of power grid, various evaluation methods have been put forward and studied one after another, and some important research results provide reference and comparison for the methods in this study. Firstly, Wu et al.38 proposed MFAC, model-free adaptive control (MFAC) method in their research, which was used for static synchronous compensator (STATCOM) to realize SSO mitigation. However, the performance of this method on interference datasets was limited, and the accuracy rate decreased in dealing with complex interference environment. In contrast, the method proposed in this study uses the UCB and the DDQN model, which is better adapted to the data with different interference levels and significantly improves the classification accuracy. Secondly, Sinha et al.39 put forward a method combining deep neural network, matrix decomposition and SSO, which showed better performance than the modern single rating recommendation system based on MF and the traditional multi-standard system, but the frequency estimation error of this method was large on the dataset with strong interference. The LFF-Transformer network used in this study can better capture the local and global features in the data, and realize the low error estimation of frequency and damping ratio through the effective fusion of different levels of features. In the experimental results, the MSE of LFF-Transformer model is only 0.002, while the MAPE of damping ratio is as low as 1.5%. In addition, Wang et al.40 proposed an SSO detection and evaluation model based on long and short-term memory networks. Their method emphasized the long-term dependence of time series data, but it did not perform well in the face of high noise data. By combining UCB, DDQN classification model and LFF-Transformer estimation model, this study not only improves the robustness to different interference levels, but also ensures the accuracy in high noise environment. In the light interference dataset, this method can correctly classify the interference degree with 99.5% accuracy. In moderate and intensity interference datasets, the classification accuracy reaches 98.2% and 96.7% respectively. To sum up, the two-stage SSO evaluation method proposed in this study surpasses the existing research results in many aspects. Firstly, UCB and DDQN are used to classify the interference level, which greatly improves the accuracy and robustness of the classification. Secondly, the parameters of SSO are estimated by LFF-Transformer network, which effectively reduces the estimation errors of frequency and damping ratio. Generally speaking, the method of this study not only improves the accuracy of SSO evaluation, but also enhances the overall stability of power grid, and its potential and advantages in practical application have been fully verified. These characteristics make the method in this paper have remarkable innovation and practical value in theory and practice.

Conclusion

In this paper, a static SSO evaluation method based on deep learning is proposed. By combining the strategy of offline training and online application, and innovatively introducing the UCB-DDQN and the LFF-Transformer, the accuracy and robustness of SSO parameter evaluation in the face of PMU data with different interference levels in complex power grid environment are significantly improved. Firstly, historical or simulated PMU data are collected as training samples, covering the power grid operation data under various working conditions. Then, based on UCB-DDQN model, the interference level is classified, and the appropriate SSO modal parameter estimator is selected for training according to the classification results. The estimator adopts LFF-Transformer structure, which effectively integrates local and global features. Finally, the real-time PMU data first passes through the interference level classifier, and then the appropriate estimator is selected according to the classification results to evaluate the SSO frequency and damping ratio.

Although the proposed method performs well in the current test, its performance may be limited by the amount and quality of training data. In addition, the robustness of this method in dealing with extreme interference needs further verification. In the future research direction, it is planned to increase the diversity of training and test datasets, including different types of interference and more changing operating conditions to further improve the generalization ability and accuracy of the model. Meanwhile, new deep learning architecture and algorithm optimization strategy are explored to reduce the computational complexity of the model while maintaining or improving the accuracy. The proposed method is applied to the SSO evaluation of the actual power grid to verify its effect in the actual operation environment, and the model and strategy are optimized according to the feedback of the actual application.

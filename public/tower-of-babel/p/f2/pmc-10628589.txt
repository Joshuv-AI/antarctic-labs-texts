Phase I Study of Entinostat, Atezolizumab, Carboplatin, and Etoposide in Previously Untreated Extensive-Stage Small Cell Lung Cancer, ETCTN 10399
Authors: Ryan D Gentzler, Liza C Villaruz, John C Rhee, Bethany Horton, Joseph Mock, Michael Hanley, Kyeongmin Kim, Michelle A Rudek, Mitch A Phelps, Michael A Carducci, Richard Piekarz, Kwon-Sik Park, Timothy N Bullock, Charles M Rudin
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCREBBP and EP300 mutations occur at a frequency of 15% and 13%, respectively, in small cell lung cancer (SCLC), and preclinical models demonstrated susceptibility to targeting with HDAC inhibitors.MethodsPatients with treatment-naïve extensive-stage SCLC, ECOG ≤2 were enrolled and treated with entinostat orally weekly (4 dose levels, DL) in combination with standard dose carboplatin, etoposide, and atezolizumab. Cohort allocation was determined by Bayesian optimal interval (BOIN) design targeting an MTD with a DLT rate of 20%.ResultsThree patients were enrolled and treated at DL1 with entinostat 2 mg. Patients were aged 69-83; 2 male, 1 female; 2 were ECOG 1, and 1 was ECOG 0. The most common adverse events (AEs) were anemia (3), neutropenia (3), thrombocytopenia (2), leukopenia (2), and hypocalcemia (2). Two experienced DLTs during cycle 1: (1) grade (Gr) 4 febrile neutropenia, and (1) Gr 5 sepsis. BOIN design required stopping accrual to DL1, and the trial was closed to further accrual. Entinostat and atezolizumab pharmacokinetics were both comparable to historical controls.ConclusionAddition of entinostat to atezolizumab, carboplatin, and etoposide is unsafe and resulted in early onset and severe neutropenia, thrombocytopenia. Further exploration of entinostat with carboplatin, etoposide, and atezolizumab should not be explored. (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT04631029).

Lessons Learned

Entinostat 2 mg PO weekly in combination with carboplatin, etoposide, and atezolizumab resulted in severe neutropenia and thrombocytopenia in patients with extensive-stage small cell lung cancer.

Discussion

This phase I trial was based on preclinical data supporting target inhibition and immunomodulatory effects of HDAC inhibitors (HDACi) for small cell lung cancer (SCLC). Genomic analysis of SCLC has identified that CREBBP or EP300 mutations occur in nearly 30% of patients and, as a result, have reduced histone acetylation, driving tumorigenesis. Prelinical data demonstrated that tumors with these genomic alterations are sensitive to HDAC inhibition. Furthermore, HDACi have been shown to upregulate both PD-L1 and MHC-I and may have a synergistic effect when combined with checkpoint inhibitor immunotherapy.

Given the increased sensitivity to HDAC inhibition in molecular alterations that occur in SCLC and the potential for entinostat to improve efficacy of immunotherapy, this phase I trial evaluated the addition of entinostat to standard chemotherapy and atezolizumab for patients with treatment-naïve SCLC.

Eligible patients were enrolled and treated with escalating doses of entinostat on days 1, 8, and 15, carboplatin AUC 5 on day 1, and etoposide 100 mg/m2 IV on days 1, 2, and 3 of a 21-day cycle (Table 1). Three patients were enrolled and treated with entinostat 2 mg PO weekly. Despite starting at low-dose entinostat 2 mg PO weekly, 2 patients experienced profound cytopenias and associated fever and sepsis categorized as DLTs. A third patient experienced grade 3 thrombocytopenia causing delays in subsequent chemotherapy. The trial was terminated due to safety related to 2 of 3 patients experiencing DLTs in the first cohort (Table 2). Severe cytopenias can occur with carboplatin and etoposide alone. However, given known hematologic toxicity of HDACi and the degree and frequency of AEs experienced on this trial, it is likely entinostat contributed to the severity of these AEs, despite low entinostat exposure at 2 mg PO. Although this combination is not safe, future strategies such as maintenance HDACi plus immunotherapy or later line monotherapy trials may be warranted, particularly for a biomarker-selected population with CREBBP/EP300 mutations.

Assessment, Analysis, and Discussion

Completion Study completed Investigator’s assessmentPoorly tolerated/not feasible

This phase I trial was designed based on preclinical data supporting targeted inhibition and immunomodulatory effects of HDACis. SCLC cells with loss of CREBBP have reduced histone acetylation, driving tumorigenesis. When these CREBBP-deleted tumors in patient-derived xenografts with concurrent p53/RB1 alterations were treated with the HDACi pracinostat, increased antitumor activity was observed compared to SCLC with p53/RB1 alterations alone.1 HDAC inhibition also had some antitumor effect in tumors with intact CREBBP, but to a lesser degree. Preclinical data have also shown that HDACis upregulate PD-L1 and MHC-I and may have a synergistic effect on response to checkpoint inhibition.2 Furthermore, HDACi administered in combination with PD-1 inhibitors showed significantly improved response compared to PD-1 monotherapy.3 In a phase II trial in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), combination of entinostat 5 mg PO weekly with pembrolizumab was tolerable with only 14% of patients discontinuing treatment due to a treatment-related adverse event (AE); thrombocytopenia and anemia, Gr 1-2, were reported at 15% and 12%, respectively, and Gr 3-4 were reported at 1% and 7%, respectively.4 Responses were seen in patients regardless of prior response to checkpoint inhibitors or PD-L1 status.

The current standard of care for extensive-stage SCLC is a combination of carboplatin, etoposide, and a PD-L1 inhibitor, such as atezolizumab or durvalumab. Despite improvements in survival with the addition of PD-L1 inhibitors to chemotherapy, the median overall survival remains only approximately 1 year from diagnosis. Because the prevalence of mutations in CREBBP and EP300 occur with relative frequency in SCLC and HDACi may augment checkpoint inhibitor immunotherapy response, we conducted a phase I trial combining an HDACi with chemotherapy and immunotherapy.

Entinostat was chosen for this study due to its favorable toxicity profile and prior data demonstrating safety and possible efficacy signal when combined with pembrolizumab, a PD-1 inhibitor. Although the prior study in in combination with immunotherapy was in NSCLC, it was felt the safety profile would be similar in an SCLC patient population. Because a safe dose had not been established with entinostat in combination with carboplatin and etoposide, we explored this combination in a phase I dose-finding study using Bayesian optimal interval (BOIN) design5 with highest planned dose level being 5 mg PO weekly, the same dose used in the prior phase II trial. Despite starting at low-dose entinostat 2 mg PO weekly, there were significant cytopenias and DLTs requiring the study to close. None of the patients had prior limited-stage SCLC or were exposed to cytotoxic chemotherapy. The age of patients enrolled in this study, and a median of 74 (69 to 83) may have played a role in higher risk of cytopenias as bone marrow celluarity decreases with age.

Although this phase I trial demonstrated that this combination is not safe at these doses, future strategies such as maintenance HDACi plus immunotherapy or later line monotherapy trials may be warranted, particularly in a biomarker-selected population.

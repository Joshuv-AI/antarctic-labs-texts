Beyond survival: the role of quality of life in modern urothelial cancer therapy
Authors: Shugo Yajima, Hitoshi Masuda
Published: 2025

The EV-302 trial by Gupta et al. presents compelling evidence that enfortumab vedotin (EV) plus pembrolizumab improves survival outcomes while enhancing quality of life (QoL) and reducing pain in patients with untreated locally advanced or metastatic urothelial cancer (1). A particularly noteworthy finding is the improvement in pain symptoms observed as early as week 4, which precedes typical radiographic responses.

This temporal pattern raises questions about the mechanisms underlying symptom improvement. While traditional paradigms link pain reduction to tumor shrinkage, the pain improvement observed with EV plus pembrolizumab suggests alternative mechanisms may contribute. Antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) can induce immunogenic cell death and release damage-associated molecular patterns, leading to immune activation that differs from conventional chemotherapy (2). These effects, combined with checkpoint inhibition, may influence pain signaling pathways through tumor microenvironment modulation. Notably, while both EV-pembrolizumab and chemotherapy show initial pain reduction in the first 4 weeks, the sustained and enhanced pain relief seen with the combination therapy after week 4 may reflect these distinct mechanisms.

The recent systematic review by Yajima et al. demonstrated objective response rates of 68% for EV-pembrolizumab combination therapy versus 43% for monotherapy in metastatic urothelial carcinoma (3). Their analysis also revealed distinct adverse event patterns, with lower rates of hematologic toxicities but higher rates of peripheral neuropathy compared to chemotherapy. Fu et al.’s comprehensive review highlighted that monomethyl auristatin E (MMAE)-based ADCs, including EV, carry a significant risk of peripheral neuropathy, occurring in approximately 40–50% of patients (4). This neurotoxicity, while generally manageable, represents an important consideration when evaluating the overall benefit-risk profile of EV-based therapy.

The clinical implications of the EV-302 findings remain significant. In patients with moderate to severe baseline pain—comprising over one-third of the cohort—combination therapy provided clinically meaningful improvements. These patients frequently had bone metastases (34% vs. 14% in those without significant pain) and visceral disease (79% vs. 68%), highlighting the symptom burden in advanced urothelial cancer.

It should be acknowledged that the temporal relationship between pain relief and radiographic response is influenced by per-protocol imaging schedules. Since initial imaging typically occurs at 8–9 weeks, we cannot definitively determine whether pain improvements at week 4 truly precede radiographic changes. This limitation underscores the need for studies incorporating more frequent response assessments to better characterize treatment effect kinetics.

Several limitations of the EV-302 patient-reported outcome (PRO) analysis warrant consideration. The open-label design may introduce bias in subjective assessments, as patients aware of receiving a novel therapy might report more favorable outcomes. Missing PRO data, which increases over time and may differ between treatment arms, could affect the validity of longitudinal comparisons. Furthermore, pain assessment in clinical trials is complex, influenced by factors including concurrent analgesic use and the multidimensional nature of cancer-related pain.

The findings suggest reconsidering how we evaluate treatment response in the era of novel targeted therapies. While Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) criteria remain the standard for assessing tumor response, PROs like pain may serve as complementary indicators of treatment benefit. This is particularly relevant for ADCs and immunotherapy combinations, where mechanisms of action involve complex tumor microenvironment interactions beyond direct cytotoxicity.

Future research should explore the relationship between early symptomatic improvement and long-term outcomes. Translational studies examining changes in inflammatory markers, nerve growth factors, and immune profiles would help elucidate the mechanisms underlying early pain relief. Additionally, strategies to mitigate MMAE-associated peripheral neuropathy while preserving analgesic benefits warrant investigation.

In conclusion, the EV-302 trial demonstrates that EV plus pembrolizumab represents an important advance in managing advanced urothelial cancer—improving survival while enhancing patients’ daily experience of their disease. The early pain improvements raise important questions about therapeutic mechanisms that merit further investigation. While we must balance these benefits against treatment-related toxicities such as peripheral neuropathy, these findings highlight how patient benefit may manifest in ways our current assessment tools cannot fully capture.

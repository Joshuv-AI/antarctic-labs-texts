Pulmonary artery compliance in aortic stenosis: protective or maladaptive? Rethinking the conclusions on PAPi prognostic value
Authors: Shichen Hu, Hong Zhou
Published: 2025

To the Editor,

Rechsteiner et al. 1 reported no association between pulmonary artery pulsatility index (PAPi) and mortality after aortic valve replacement (AVR) in severe aortic stenosis (AS), attributing low PAPi to high pulmonary artery compliance (PAC) rather than reduced stroke volume. While this mechanistic explanation is intriguing, we propose two critical caveats that may reshape the interpretation.

1The dual nature of high PAC: Compensation versus pathology

The dual nature of high PAC: Compensation versus pathology

The study interprets high PAC as uniformly protective. However, chronic pressure overload in AS drives pulmonary vascular remodelling, where elevated PAC may reflect pathological proximal artery dilation rather than preserved distal vascular function. 2 This aligns with Zern et al.'s cohort 3 where extreme PAC (>6 mL/mmHg) paradoxically increased mortality—suggesting PAC's prognostic role may be threshold‐dependent. 4 CT‐based volumetry studies confirm that proximal PA dilation correlates with adverse remodelling in pressure‐overload states. 5 Reanalysis of your data stratifying PAC into tertiles (e.g., <3.0, 3.0–6.0 and >6.0 mL/mmHg) could clarify whether maladaptive PAC exists in AS.

2PAPi's predictive power in high‐risk subgroups

PAPi's predictive power in high‐risk subgroups

Grouping PAPi by quartiles (Q1 ≤ 2.67) may mask its utility in advanced disease. In TAVR cohorts with lower stroke volume index (SVI), 6 PAPi <2.0 predicted adverse outcomes. Critically, your Q1 group had only mildly reduced SVI (35 vs. 38 mL/m2), potentially including ‘pseudo‐low PAPi’ patients (high PAC compensating for borderline SVI). Re‐testing PAPi in the subset with SVI < 30 mL/m2 (n ≈ 60 by your data) might reveal its predictive value.

Technical note: The indirect Fick conundrum

PAC calculation relied on indirect Fick‐derived cardiac output (error ~15%). 7 This could attenuate true SVI‐PAC correlations—a limitation acknowledged but not quantified in your discussion. Critically, pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP) directly modulates PAC's haemodynamic interpretation, 8 yet its interaction with PAPi was unexplored. Future studies could integrate:

Synchronized echocardiography (TAPSE/PASP ratio for RV‐PA coupling).

CT‐based pulmonary artery volumetry 5 to distinguish adaptive versus maladaptive remodelling.

Conclusion

PAPi's apparent futility may stem from unaccounted PAC heterogeneity and suboptimal risk stratification. Validating PAC thresholds and high‐risk phenotypes will refine prognostication in AS.

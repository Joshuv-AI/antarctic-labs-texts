Ferroptosis-associated immune modulation enhances temozolomide efficacy in experimental glioblastoma: potential implications for T-cell infiltration and tumour microenvironment dynamics
Authors: Shaik Rahaman, Roli Kushwaha, Divya Vohora, Sumana Chakravarty, Ahmed Kamal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGlioblastoma multiforme (GBM) remains a lethal and aggressive malignancy with limited response to standard therapies. This study hypothesized that artesunate, a ferroptosis inducer, enhances the cytotoxic and immunomodulatory efficacy of standard anticancer agents through mechanisms involving oxidative stress-mediated ferroptotic cell death and lipid peroxidation, influencing hypoxia-inducible factor-1α, insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1), and leptin pathways. These pathways collectively regulate tumor proliferation, angiogenesis, and immune evasion; hence, their modulation may sensitize GBM to therapy. The study evaluated the therapeutic potential of artesunate in combination with anticancer drugs using in vitro GL261 glioblastoma cells and in vivo subcutaneous GL261 models. Clinically relevant agents representing distinct mechanisms, temozolomide, cyclophosphamide, paclitaxel, imatinib, and thymoquinone, were tested. Tumor volume, body weight, and serum biochemical parameters (IL-6, IGF-1, HIF-1α, and leptin) were analyzed, along with CD3 + T cell infiltration by immunohistochemistry. Combination treatments, particularly artesunate with temozolomide or paclitaxel, produced marked tumor regression compared with controls. Serum biomarker modulation and enhanced infiltration of CD3 + T cells indicated activation of ferroptotic and immune-mediated anti-tumor mechanisms, with minimal systemic toxicity. Artesunate potentiates the antitumor efficacy of standard chemotherapeutic agents through mechanisms involving ferroptosis and immune modulation, promoting a tumor-suppressive microenvironment. Introduction

Glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) remains as a lethal and aggressive malignancy affecting the brain in elderly, characterised by rapid proliferation, resistance to conventional therapies, and a dismal median survival of just 15–18 months post-diagnosis [1]. Surgical resection followed by chemotherapy with Temozolomide (TMZ) & radiotherapy is the standard regimen available for GBM. Despite emerging treatment modalities, overall survival rates for GBM patients have seen minimal improvement [2]. The efficacy of TMZ, the cornerstone of GBM chemotherapy, is significantly limited by the development of resistance mechanisms [3]. The major contributor to this resistance is the enhanced expression of O6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase (MGMT), a DNA repair enzyme and the tumour’s inherent genetic and microenvironmental heterogeneity [4]. These characteristics contribute to a poor prognosis, underscoring the urgent need for new therapeutic approaches that can either enhance the efficacy of TMZ or circumvent existing resistance mechanisms [5]. Drug repurposing (the strategy of using approved drugs for new indications) has emerged as an innovative strategy to address the challenges in GBM treatment. This innovative strategy identifies new uses for approved drugs, potentially offering a faster, more cost-effective path to developing new therapies [6]. Of particular interest are compounds like artesunate, a ferroptosis inducer [7, 8] in glioblastoma cells [8–10], besides showing promise in modulating immune responses [11]and overcoming resistance in various cancer types, including GBM [12, 13]. Artemisinin derivatives have shown potential to disrupt critical cellular processes in glioblastoma cells, offering a new avenue to overcome treatment resistance [14]. Artesunate (originally developed as an antimalarial) is a semisynthetic derivative of artemisinin that has shown strong anticancer and immunomodulatory effects across various cancers. Recent evidence also suggests that artesunate can cross the blood–brain barrier and provide neuroprotective and anti-inflammatory benefits within the central nervous system, supporting its potential use against brain tumors such as glioblastoma [15]. Artesunate has shown remarkable anticancer properties, including the ability to induce ferroptosis, a form of programmed cell death distinct from apoptosis [14, 16].

The objective of this study is to ascertain the cytotoxic effects of artesunate in combination with other anticancer drugs, cyclophosphamide, paclitaxel, thymoquinone, imatinib, and TMZ, on glioblastoma cells. Drugs were selected based on their clinical relevance and different mechanisms like alkylation (TMZ, cyclophosphamide), microtubule stabilization (paclitaxel), kinase inhibition (imatinib), and redox modulation (thymoquinone), to explore their ability to enhance cytotoxicity and modulate the GBM immune microenvironment, thereby potentially improving tumour response to treatment.

We hypothesized that artesunate, a ferroptosis inducer, enhances the cytotoxic and immunomodulatory efficacy of standard anticancer agents through mechanisms involving oxidative stress, ferroptotic cell death via iron accumulation, lipid peroxidation, and Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS). Moreover, the accumulation of ROS and lipid peroxidation has been shown to influence Hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α), insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1), and leptin pathways, which collectively modulate tumor proliferation, angiogenesis and immune evasion, and modulation of immune microenvironment markers. Therefore, we postulated that combining arteunate with clinically relevant anticancer agents not only enhances ferroptotic cell death but also modulates these immune-metabolic biomarkers that creates a more hostile environment for GBM progression.

Both in vitro and in vivo models were used in this multidisciplinary approach to identify optimal drug combinations that can reduce tumour growth, enhance immune responses, and ultimately improve outcomes for GBM patients. The study employs a range of techniques, including MTT assays for cytotoxicity evaluation, subcutaneous glioma mouse models for in vivo efficacy assessment, and analysis of key biomarkers such as IGF-1, HIF-1α, IL-6, and leptin to understand the molecular mechanisms underlying the observed effects. By investigating these novel drug combinations, we aim to expedite for more effective treatment strategies that overcome current limitations in GBM therapy, bringing improved survival and quality of life within reach for patients.

Materials and methods

Chemicals and reagents

KVB ASTA Life Sciences, Hyderabad, generously provided the drugs and chemicals used in this study. Specifically, temozolomide (TMZ), artesunate (A), imatinib (IMT), paclitaxel (PX), thymoquinone (TQ), and cyclophosphamide (C) were received as gift samples. MTT reagent, and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) were sourced from Sigma-Aldrich, USA. Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM), Fetal Bovine Serum (FBS), and penicillin-streptomycin were procured from Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits for glucose, IL-6, IGF-1, HIF-1α, leptin, and the CD3 antibody for immunohistochemistry (IHC) were obtained from Thermo Fisher Scientific (USA) and Miltenyi Biotec (Germany).

MTT cytotoxicity assay

The cytotoxic effects of artesunate and standard chemotherapeutics (TMZ, paclitaxel, imatinib, thymoquinone, and cyclophosphamide) were evaluated using the MTT assay with some modifications [17–19]. GL261 cells were plated into 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 103 cells per well, left overnight to attach prior to treatment with drug combinations for 24, 48, and 72 h, ranging from low to high concentrations to determine the dose-response relationship and calculate the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50). Next, we added MTT solution (5 mg/mL in PBS) to each well, and the plate was incubated for 4 h at 37 °C. This incubation period results in the formation of formazan crystals. We then dissolved these crystals in DMSO, and absorbance was measured at 570 nm using a microplate reader [20]. All drug combinations were tested in each in triplicate wells, while untreated cells served as a control group to determine baseline cell viability.

In Vitro Combinations

The study involved combinations of artesunate (A) with different anticancer drugs. In all combinations with artesunate, it was kept constant at 13.00 µM across all MTTs. Five different concentrations of each anticancer drug were used in each set [21, 22].

Animals

C57BL/6 mice of 8–10 weeks old were sourced from Jeeva Life Sciences, Hyderabad. Throughout the experiment, care was taken to minimise any stress or discomfort for the mice. They were housed in a regulated setting at 20–22 °C, 50–60% relative humidity, a 12-hour light/dark cycle, and with ad libitum feed & water. The experimental procedures were conducted in accordance with ethical guidelines and with approval from the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC) of St. Paul’s College of Pharmacy (Approval number: SPLCP/COL/2022-23/001).

Acute toxicity study

Acute toxicity was evaluated in C57BL/6 mice following OECD 243 guidelines to assess the safety of the selected drug combinations. Detailed experimental procedure, observation, and assessment criteria are provided in the Supplementary Methods.

Glioblastoma animal model

C57BL/6 mice (8–10 weeks old) were used to evaluate the effectiveness of selected drug combinations in reducing tumour growth and modulating immune responses. The detailed experimental procedure including tumor implantation, grouping, and drug administration and ethical considerations are provided in Supplementary Table ST4 and Supplementary methods. One mouse was excluded from the final analysis due to a technical issue encountered during data acquisition.

Histopathology

For detailed histopathological studies after the treatment period, tumours, as well as the kidney, heart, spleen, lungs, liver, spleen, and bone marrow, were collected from the mice. All tissues were carefully removed, fixed in 4% formaldehyde to preserve their structure, and then embedded in paraffin. Each tissue was then sliced (approximately 8 μm thick) and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E). Major organs were examined for potential treatment-related toxicity, and tumors were assessed for morphological changes induced by the respective treatments. Histopathological evaluation was performed by a certified veterinary pathologist (Jeeva Life Sciences, Hyderabad), who was blinded to the treatment groups to ensure unbiased interpretation.

ELISA for biomarkers

The Serum biomarkers, IL-6, IGF-1, HIF-1α, and leptin, were evaluated using ELISA to asses treatment induced changes in metabolic and inflammatory pathways. Detailed experimental procedures, sample processing, and assay conditions are provided in the Supplementary Methods.

Immunohistochemistry

To assess adaptive immune engagement, CD3 + T cell infiltration was quantified as a reliable indicator of overall T cell presence and activation within the tumor. Although glioblastoma is rich in immunosuppressive macrophages, T-cell density remains a critical determinant of therapeutic outcome. Hence, evaluating CD3 + infiltration, alongside systemic biomarkers, offers valuable insight into the immune modulation and ferroptosis-associated reprogramming induced by combination treatment. Immunohistochemistry was performed on paraffin-embedded tumor tissues to evaluate CD3 expression. Tissue blocks were sectioned at a thickness of 20 μm using a rotary microtome [DiaPath Galileo, Italy], ensuring uniform and precise slices for staining. A FITC-conjugated antibody specific to CD3 [#130-119-798, Miltenyi Biotec, 1:200] was applied to detect T-cell populations within the tissue. Imaging was conducted using an Olympus FV10i confocal laser scanning microscope, utilizing a 10× phase contrast objective (NA 0.4) for broader visualization and a 60× oil immersion phase contrast objective (NA 1.35) for high-resolution imaging. Image analysis was subsequently performed using ImageJ software, enabling quantification and detailed visualization of CD3 expression to identify T-cell infiltration within the tumour microenvironment [23].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses was performed using GraphPad Prism version 10.4.2 (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA, USA). Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Cell viability (MTT) data was analyzed using two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s multiple-comparison test. Repeated measurements of body weight and tumor volume were analyzed using a linear mixed-effects model, with treatment group and time included as fixed effects and animal identity included as a random effect to account for within-subject correlations. When significant effects were detected, post hoc multiple comparisons were performed using Sidak’s correction. Single-time-point biochemical parameters, including IGF-1, HIF-1α, IL-6, and leptin, were analyzed using two-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s multiple-comparison test. Statistical significance was considered at p < 0.05. Significance levels are indicated as *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001, and ****p < 0.0001.

Results

MTT assay and IC50 determination

The data indicate that Artesunate-based combinations, particularly with Paclitaxel and Temozolomide, showed greater cytotoxic effects compared to monotherapies, indicating their greater potential in glioblastoma therapy (Fig. 1 and Supplementary Table ST1). The data indicate that the enhanced effects of Artesunate with Paclitaxel and Temozolomide suggest increased oxidative stress and ferroptotic signaling. We performed a TBARS (MDA) assay during the study to validate ferroptosis, which demonstrated elevated lipid peroxidation in artesunate combination groups, consistent with previously reported ferroptosis induction by artesunate-based treatments [24]. These results are now presented as Supplementary Figure SF1, providing confirmation of ferroptosis induction consistent with arsteunate’s known oxidative stress-mediated cytotoxicity. These findings indicate that Artesunate combinations with Paclitaxel and Temozolomide are especially effective in the long-term treatment of glioma cell models, making them potential candidates for further exploration in GBM therapy.

Acute toxicity and histopathological analysis of organs for toxicity assessment

No severe or treatment-related toxic effects was observed, such as organ damage, and body weight loss were detected in any treatment group, suggesting that the drug combinations were safe and well-tolerated at the administered doses (Supplementary Figure SF2).

In Vivo Efficacy and biomarker analysis

Body weight and tumor volume

Mice’s body weights were recorded across all treatment and vehicle control groups for 26 consecutive days (Supplementary Table ST4). A significant drop in body weight was found in the treatment groups. Despite the reduction in body weight, all treated animals remained active, with normal food and water intake, and showed no signs of distress throughout the study, suggesting that the administered medication regimens were well tolerated. Paclitaxel-treated groups showed a transient reduction in body weights, likely reflecting systemic stress associated with the dose-dependent toxicity rather than sustained adverse effects (p < 0.0001). The vehicle group had a slower decrease in body weight correlated with tumor burden (p < 0.001) (Supplementary Table ST5 and Supplementary Figure SF3).

GL261 subcutaneous glioma tumors were considered established when palpable nodules reached a volume of approximately 80–100 mm³, typically observed between days 7–10 after GL261 cell implantation. Treatments were initiated once tumors reached this size. Tumors were measured in tumor-bearing mice (n = 7) on days 12, 15, 18, 21, and 26 following tumor implantation (Supplementary Table ST6). All the treatment groups were compared to the vehicle control group, treatment with Artesunate (S1), Paclitaxel + Artesunate (S2), Paclitaxel alone (S3), Temozolomide + Artesunate (S4), and Temozolomide alone (SD) demonstrated a statistically significant decrease in tumor volume at all measured points of time (p < 0.001) (Fig. 2).

Discussion

The combination of histopathological observations, serum biomarker analysis, and tumor volume measurements provides a comprehensive understanding of how artesunate, paclitaxel, temozolomide, and their combinations act in a GL261 subcutaneous glioma model. Our findings clearly bring-out the potential of specific drug combinations that not only suppress tumor growth but able to modulate the immune response, pointing towards potential new directions for improved treatment outcomes in GBM therapy.

The in vitro MTT assays showed synergistic cytotoxicity when artesunate was combined with TMZ, cyclophosphamide, thymoquinone, and, particularly, paclitaxel, indicated by significantly lower IC50 values compared to single agents. This suggests that artesunate can enhance the sensitivity of GL261 cells to this chemotherapeutic. The most potent effect was observed with paclitaxel (IC50 reduced from 1.52 µM to 0.046 µM), followed by TMZ (IC50 reduced from 153.86 µM to 20.81 µM).

Acute toxicity was performed according to the OECD guidelines, and no significant toxicity was observed. These in vitro findings were corroborated mainly by the in vivo study. Tumor volume analysis revealed marked reductions in tumor size across all treatment groups compared to the vehicle control. Notably, the temozolomide and artesunate (S4); paclitaxel and artesunate (S2) combinations achieved the most significant decreases in tumor volume by day 26. This signifies a notable enhancement of anticancer activity with these combinations (p < 0.05). Histopathological analysis supported these observations, showing significant tumor necrosis and increased immune cell infiltration in the paclitaxel with artesunate (S2) and temozolomide with artesunate (S4) groups, suggesting effective tumor destruction, potentially mediated by immune mechanisms.

Earlier research widely confirms that artesunate has potent anti-cancer effects in glioblastoma cells, mainly by inducing ferroptosis, a non-apoptotic form of regulated cell death characterized by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation [10]. Mechanistically, artesunate treatment leads to increased intracellular iron accumulation, followed by glutathione (GSH) depletion and glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) inactivation, which together trigger lethal oxidative stress in GBM cells [10, 25].

Our serum biomarker analysis provides critical evidence that these cellular mechanisms lead to systemic changes indicative of a disrupted pro-tumorigenic environment. We found that combination therapies, specifically paclitaxel with artesunate (S2) and temozolomide with artesunate (S4), significantly affected key pathways involved in tumor growth, inflammation, and hypoxia. A key finding was a significant decrease in HIF-1α across all treatment groups, with the S4 combination (temozolomide + artesunate) showing the most significant effect. HIF-1α is a crucial marker of hypoxia linked to tumor aggressiveness and resistance to therapy [26]. The existing literature highlights a complex interaction between hypoxia and ferroptosis: GBM cells frequently upregulate the ferroptosis-inhibiting gene “SLC7A11” through the PI3K/AKT/HIF-1α signaling pathway to survive chemotherapy [27]. Our results indicate that artesunate combination therapies interfere with this pro-survival hypoxic adaptation by lowering HIF-1α levels, thus making tumor cells more vulnerable to the iron-dependent oxidative stress that triggers ferroptosis.

Furthermore, all treatment groups showed reductions in the pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-6. Given IL-6 signaling is strongly associated with GBM progression and a more aggressive tumor environment, recent research has revealed a direct molecular link between inflammation and resistance to ferroptosis [28]. This research demonstrates that artesunate interferes with the IL-6/STAT3 pathway by targeting the GP130 receptor, thereby decreasing SLC7A11 expression and heightening cancer cells’ vulnerability to ferroptosis [29, 30]. The systemic decrease in IL-6 supports the idea that these combination therapies enhance ferroptotic sensitivity within the tumor microenvironment. Additionally, reductions in IGF-1 and Leptin point to a shift toward a less pro-tumorigenic systemic state [31–33]. In GBM, IGF-1 promotes a pro-tumorigenic microenvironment that may indirectly suppress ferroptosis via PI3K/AKT signaling, suggesting reductions in IGF-1 signaling enhance ferroptosis vulnerability alongside IGF-1 pathway inhibition [32–34]. Although data on leptin’s involvement in ferrptosis are limited, leptin cooperates in sustaining GBM progression [35], the data suggest that reduced leptin signaling can hinder GBM progression. The combined treatments successfully inhibit tumor survival and growth signals, particularly through IGF-1 and leptin pathways. Overall, our results show that the efficacy of paclitaxel/temozolomide combined with artesunate not only directly triggers ferroptosis but also remodels the systemic microenvironment. This includes disrupting major resistance pathways, such as HIF-1α and IL-6/STAT3, thereby increasing GBM tumor vulnerability to this targeted cell death mechanism.

Immunohistochemical analysis of CD3 + T cells revealed increased T-cell infiltration in tumors treated with combination therapies, notably temozolomide with artesunate (S4) and paclitaxel and artesunate (S2), compared to the control. Although the standard temozolomide group exhibited higher CD3 expression, the combination treatments correlated more strongly with tumor regression and necrosis. This suggests that the superior therapeutic efficacy of temozolomide with artesunate (S4), paclitaxel and artesunate (S2) may result from an integrated mechanism involving ferroptosis-mediated tumor cell death and increased intratumoral CD3 + T-cell infiltration [36–41].

The observed efficacy, coupled with the minimal systemic toxicity indicated by organ histopathology, highlights the potential of artesunate-based combinations. Artesunate’s ability to induce ferroptosis [16] and modulate immune responses [42] likely associated with increased CD3 + T-cell infiltration within tumor tissues, suggesting modulation of the tumor immune microenvironment. By improving the effects of drugs like paclitaxel, these combinations address critical challenges in GBM treatment like TMZ resistance [43].

Previous studies on artesunate in glioblastoma have mainly focused on its cytotoxic or apoptotic effects in human glioma cell lines and xenograft models, often combined with TMZ. These works demonstrated enhanced TMZ-induced apoptosis via oxidative stress, NF-kB modulation, and angiogenesis suppression but relied on immunodeficient models, limiting evaluation of immune mediated and systemic effects. In contrast, our study uses an immunocompetent, syngeneic GL261 model allowing the assessment of treatment effects in an intact immune microenvironment. By analyzing circulating biomarkers (IL-6, HIF-1α, IGF-1, and leptin) along with CD3 + T cell infiltration, our work provides a more comprehensive view of immunometabolic modulation associated with ferroptosis-inducing therapy. These findings extend the understanding of artesunate’s therapeutic potential beyond direct cytotoxicity, highlighting its influence on the tumor-host interface and supporting rational combination strategies for GBM.

This study used only the GL261 glioma model, which is the most established immunocompetent syngeneic model. It may limit generalizability, as it does not fully capture the complexities of intracranial GBM, including the blood-brain barrier and the brain-GBM-immune axis. However, it serves as an ethical and immunocompetent preliminary system for evaluating combination therapies. Survival analysis was not performed due to ethical restrictions based on the tumor volume endpoint. Future studies are planned to assess treatment effects on survival and tumor volumes.

The main goal of this study was to assess the synergistic anticancer and immunomodulatory effects of artesunate-based combination therapy in the GL261 glioma model, given the broad therapeutic and exploratory scope of the study. As artesunate is already a well-established ferroptosis inducer across several cancer types, detailed molecular pathway studies and ferroptosis rescue studies using inhibitors such as ferrostatin-1 or liproxstatin-1 were outside the scope of the current investigation. Future research will employ pathway-specific analyses to elucidate the mechanistic role of ferroptosis. Future studies could investigate these combinations in orthotopic GBM models and validate these findings in additional C57BL/6 models, such as CT-2 A and SB-28. Furthermore, the IHC analysis was preliminary; larger sample sizes are needed to draw strong conclusions about immune infiltration. The specific mechanisms underlying the enhanced treatment outcomes, including the interplay between ferroptosis induction, immune modulation, and effects on specific signaling pathways (IGF-1, HIF-1α, IL-6, Leptin), needs further investigation.

Our findings strongly suggest that artesunate-based combinations, especially with paclitaxel and temozolomide, have the potential to be one of the effective therapeutic strategies against GBM. By simultaneously targeting tumor growth, hypoxia adaptation, and immune modulation, these combinations offer a mutli-dimensional approach that could overcome some of the major limitations of available GBM treatments.

Limitations

The present study has certain limitations. There was an increase in lipid peroxidation (TBARS/MDA); however, ferroptosis could not be conclusively confirmed because key indicators, including GPX4, ACSL4, and lipid ROS, were not assessed in this study. Furthermore, mechanistic confirmation was hampered by the absence of rescue studies utilising ferroptosis inhibitors like ferrostatin-1 or liproxstatin-1. Estimation of only CD3 immunohistochemistry, which demonstrates T-cell infiltration but does not differentiate T-cell subsets or evaluate functional immunological activity, was used for our immune characterisation. Furthermore, the subcutaneous GL261 model does not accurately represent the intricate cerebral glioblastoma milieu or the blood–brain barrier, despite being suitable for assessing tumour growth and treatment response. To better validate and interpret these results, additional research investigations utilising ferroptosis-specific validation, thorough immune profiling, and orthotopic glioblastoma models is required.

Conclusion

Collectively, this study demonstrates the promising efficacy of combining artesunate with standard chemotherapeutics, particularly temozolomide and paclitaxel, which significantly improved anti-glioblastoma efficacy in both in vitro and in vivo studies in a subcutaneous GL261 mouse model. Interestingly, these combination therapies exhibited reduced tumor volume, increased tumor necrosis, favorable modulation of serum biomarkers (IGF-1, HIF-1α, IL-6, Leptin), and enhanced T-cell infiltration with minimal behavioral changes and systemic toxicities. Treatment by the combination of temozolomide and artesunate showed particularly promising results across multiple endpoints. To the best of found knowledge, this happens to be the first investigation on the effect of the proposed treatment on CD3 + immunophenotyping in a syngeneic model. These findings provide preliminary preclinical evidence supporting the further investigation of artesunate as a potential adjunct to standard glioblastoma therapies. Although the subcutaneous model does not permit direct assessment of BBB-related drug delivery, previous reports have suggested that artesunate is CNS-accessible, supporting its evaluation in glioblastoma research. Nevertheless, confirmation in orthotopic models will advance scientific understanding of GBM. This investigation needs to be performed in clinically relevant models that could potentially lead to new therapeutic strategies for GBM.

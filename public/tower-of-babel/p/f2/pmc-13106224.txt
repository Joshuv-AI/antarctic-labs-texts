Survival Outcomes and Sensitivity of PARP Inhibitors in Platinum‐Sensitive Ovarian Cancer: A Retrospective Study
Authors: Xiu Zhang, Zhibin Wang, Yuhui Gu, Huihui Fang, Ying Wang, Chao Fang, Nayiyuan Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundPoly (ADP‐ribose) polymerase inhibitors (PARPi) have reshaped the maintenance treatment of ovarian cancer. However, a significant proportion of patients exhibit primary resistance, highlighting the need for real‐world efficacy data and predictive biomarkers, especially in underrepresented populations.MethodsThis retrospective study analyzed 152 Chinese patients with platinum‐sensitive ovarian cancer who received maintenance therapy with olaparib or niraparib, either in the first‐line (n = 71) or second–/later‐line (n = 81) setting. Progression‐free survival (PFS), progression‐free interval (PFI), and overall survival (OS) were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. An objective, data‐driven definition of PARPi sensitivity/resistance was established using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis of PFS. Exploratory transcriptomic sequencing and immunohistochemistry (IHC) were performed on a subset of tumors to identify molecular correlates of response.ResultsIn the first‐line maintenance cohort, median PFS (mPFS) was 54.35 months and median PFI (mPFI) was 49.57 months. In the second‐/later‐line group, mPFS was 42.53 months and mPFI was 34.87 months. Based on ROC‐defined cutoffs (PFS of 11.6 and 11.8 months, respectively), 22.5% and 24.7% of patients were classified as PARPi‐resistance in the first‐ and second‐/later‐line groups. Patients classified as sensitive consistently demonstrated superior PFS, PFI, and OS. Exploratory molecular analysis implicated the ferroptosis and HIF‐1 signaling pathways in the response mechanism, and IHC validation confirmed differential protein expression of TFRC (lower in resistance tumors) and PRNP (higher in resistance tumors).ConclusionsPARPi maintenance therapy demonstrates substantial clinical benefit in real‐world Chinese patients with ovarian cancer. However, over 20% exhibit primary resistance. TFRC and PRNP represent promising biomarkers for predicting PARPi sensitivity and warrant further validation.Keywords: biomarker, ovarian cancer, PARP inhibitor, real‐world study, treatment resistance

Introduction

Ovarian cancer remains the most lethal gynecologic malignancy, posing a significant challenge in oncology despite therapeutic advances [1, 2]. According to the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) GLOBOCAN data, it accounted for an estimated 324,398 new cases and 206,839 deaths worldwide in 2022, underscoring its substantial global health burden [3]. The current standard of care, involving cytoreductive surgery followed by platinum‐based chemotherapy [4, 5, 6]; is frequently compromised by high rates of recurrence and the eventual development of chemoresistance, which are primary drivers of disease mortality [7, 8].

The advent of PARPi has transformed the therapeutic landscape, particularly for patients with breast cancer susceptibility gene (BRCA) mutations or homologous recombination deficiency (HRD). Pivotal randomized controlled trials, such as SOLO1‐3, PRIMA/PRIME and FZOCUS‐1 demonstrated profound improvements in PFS, leading to the regulatory approval of PARPi for maintenance therapy in both the first‐line and platinum‐sensitive recurrent settings [9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17]. However, the recent refinement of their indications by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), including the withdrawal of the second‐/later‐line approvals for certain patient subgroups, has underscored persistent challenges in treatment stratification and therapeutic sequencing. This regulatory shift has created an urgent clinical need to redefine optimal treatment pathways following first‐line therapy and to identify which patients continue to derive meaningful benefit from PARPi in later lines.

While registration trials established efficacy under controlled conditions, critical gaps persist in understanding the real‐world application of PARPi. Clinical scenarios encountered in practice are often more heterogeneous, involving patients with varying performance statuses, broader age ranges, different histologic subtypes, and diverse genetic backgrounds not fully represented in clinical trials. Although studies like NORA and PRIME have provided valuable data on niraparib's efficacy in Chinese populations [18, 19], comprehensive real‐world evidence remains limited. Key questions regarding the patterns of BRCA and HRD testing, the prevalence of specific mutation types in the Chinese Han population, the management of adverse events in routine care, and the long‐term outcomes in a less selected patient cohort are largely unanswered [20, 21, 22]. Moreover, a substantial proportion of patients exhibit variable responses to PARPi, suggesting that the current genetic predictors, centered on BRCA and HRD status, are insufficient. There is a growing consensus that other genomic and molecular features within the tumor microenvironment and DNA damage response pathways may critically influence PARPi sensitivity and the emergence of resistance. This study aimed to retrospectively analyze the effectiveness and safety of PARPi therapy in primary or recurrent ovarian cancer in the Chinese Han population. Furthermore, variability in patient response to PARPi was observed, which can be attributed to their genetic background besides BRCA.

Materials and Method

Study Design and Patient Cohort

This retrospective, observational study was conducted at Hunan Cancer Hospital. We initially identified all patients diagnosed with ovarian cancer who initiated maintenance therapy with either olaparib or niraparib between January 2019 and December 2021. The final analysis included 152 consecutive patients meeting the following inclusion criteria: (1) histologically confirmed epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal cancer; (2) completion of at least one line of platinum‐based chemotherapy with a documented complete or partial response according to RECIST v1.1 criteria; (3) a PFI of ≥ 6 months. Key exclusion criteria were: (1) diagnosis of non‐epithelial ovarian tumors; (2) platinum‐resistance (PFI < 6 months) or platinum‐refractory disease; (3) incomplete clinical or follow‐up data.

The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board of Hunan Cancer Hospital (Approval No: 2025–85).

Treatment and Outcomes Assessment

Patients received maintenance therapy with either oral olaparib (300 mg twice daily) or niraparib (200 mg once daily, with dose adjustments permitted per clinical guidelines for toxicity management). Treatment was continued until radiographic disease progression as per investigator assessment, the occurrence of unacceptable toxicity, or patient decision to withdraw. The primary efficacy endpoint was PFS, defined as the time from the initiation of PARPi therapy to the first documented radiological disease progression or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. Secondary endpoints included the PFI, calculated from the date of the last dose of platinum‐based chemotherapy to the date of confirmed disease recurrence, and OS, defined as the time from PARPi initiation to death from any cause.

Statistical Analysis

Categorical variables are summarized as frequencies and percentages. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation or median with interquartile range, as appropriate. Survival probabilities for PFS and OS were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and comparisons between subgroups were performed using the log‐rank test. ROC curve analysis was employed to determine the optimal PFS cut‐off value for distinguishing between PARPi‐sensitive and ‐resistance patients. All statistical tests were two‐sided, and a p‐value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were conducted using SPSS Statistics software (version 26.0) and GraphPad Prism (version 8.0).

Transcriptome Sequencing and Immunohistochemistry

To explore potential biomarkers of response, RNA sequencing was performed on archived formalin‐fixed, paraffin‐embedded (FFPE) tumor tissue samples from a selected subset of 8 patients (4 classified as PARPi‐sensitive and 4 as PARP inhibitor‐resistance). Differential gene expression analysis was conducted using the R package “DESeq2”. The resulting p‐values were adjusted for multiple testing using the Benjamini‐Hochberg method to control the False Discovery Rate (FDR). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified based on the criteria of an adjusted p‐value ≤ 0.05 and an absolute log2 fold change (|log2FC|) ≥ 1. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed with “clusterProfiler”.

For validation, IHC was performed on corresponding FFPE tissue sections. Sections were incubated with primary antibodies against LDHA (19987–1‐AP, Proteintech Group; dilution 1:500), PRNP (12555–1‐AP, Proteintech Group; dilution 1:500), and TFRC (10084–2‐AP, Proteintech Group; dilution 1:200), followed by incubation with appropriate horseradish peroxidase ‐conjugated secondary antibodies. Protein expression was semi‐quantitatively evaluated by two independent, blinded pathologists, and discrepancies were resolved by consensus review.

Determination of PARPi Sensitivity Using ROC Curve Analysis

In the absence of a standardized clinical definition for PARPi sensitivity, we utilized ROC curve analysis to derive an objective PFS‐based cutoff. The optimal PFS cutoff for distinguishing sensitive from resistant patients was determined to be 349.0 days (approximately 11.6 months) for the first‐line maintenance cohort (Figure 3A) and 355.0 days (approximately 11.8 months) for the second‐/later‐line cohort (Figure 3B). Applying these thresholds, PARPi resistance was identified in 22.5% (16/71) of patients in the first‐line maintenance group and in 24.7% (20/81) of patients in the second‐/later‐line maintenance group. Survival Outcomes Stratified by PARPi Sensitivity

Patients classified as PARPi‐sensitive demonstrated significantly superior survival outcomes compared to those in the PARPi‐resistance group across both treatment lines.

In the first‐line maintenance cohort, the mPFS was 59.40 months in the sensitive group compared to 14.22 months in the resistance group (Figure 3C). The mPFI was not reached in the sensitive group versus 8.15 months in the resistant group (Figure 3D). Similarly, the mOS was not reached in the sensitive group, while it was 36.87 months in the resistance group (Figure 3E).

This pronounced survival advantage for sensitive patients was consistently observed in the second‐/later‐line maintenance cohort. The mPFS was not reached in the sensitive group versus 13.28 months in the resistance group (Figure 3F). The mPFI was not reached in the sensitive group, in contrast to 6.90 months in the resistance group (Figure 3G). The mOS was not reached in either subgroup within the follow‐up period (Figure 3H).

Safety Profile and Reasons for Treatment Discontinuation

Safety analysis demonstrated distinct adverse event (AE) profiles between the olaparib and niraparib treatment groups (Table 4). In the olaparib group (n = 116), the most common AEs were nausea (19.8%), anemia (16.4%), and fatigue (12.9%). In contrast, the niraparib group (n = 36) most frequently reported thrombocytopenia (19.5%), nausea (16.7%), insomnia (11.1%), and leukopenia (11.1%). A notable difference in AE profiles was observed, primarily manifested as a higher incidence of hematologic toxicities, especially thrombocytopenia, in the niraparib group.

Transcriptomic Profiling and Identification of Potential Biomarkers

To investigate the molecular basis of differential PARPi response, transcriptome sequencing was performed on tumor samples from 8 patients (4 PARPi‐sensitive and 4 PARPi‐resistance). Analysis identified 26 genes that were differentially expressed between the two groups (Figure 4A). Pathway enrichment analysis revealed that these genes were significantly involved in biological processes such as ferroptosis and the HIF‐1 signaling pathway (Figure 4B). Among the most notable differentially expressed genes were LDHA, PRNP, and TFRC (Figure 4C). Subsequent IHC validation confirmed that protein expression levels of PRNP and TFRC were significantly associated with PARPi sensitivity (Figure 4D). Specifically, high expression of PRNP and low expression of TFRC were correlated with a resistant phenotype and poorer prognosis.

Discussion

The advent of PARPi represents a pivotal advance in the management of ovarian cancer, significantly altering the treatment landscape from a purely cytotoxic paradigm to one incorporating targeted maintenance therapy [23, 24]. While their application has expanded from later‐line to first‐line settings, the recent refinement of FDA indications underscores the ongoing need to optimize patient selection and understand treatment efficacy across different clinical scenarios, particularly in real‐world populations.

Our real‐world data substantiate the significant survival benefit of PARPi maintenance therapy in Chinese patients with platinum‐sensitive ovarian cancer, consistent with prior clinical trials such as SOLO1 and PRIMA. When contextualizing our findings within the established clinical trial landscape, several observations emerge. The mPFS in our first‐line cohort (49.6 months) was shorter than that reported in the SOLO1 trial (56.0 months). This discrepancy is consistent with the distinct patient populations, as SOLO1 exclusively enrolled patients with BRCA mutations, a subgroup known to derive exceptional benefit from PARP inhibition. Notably, our real‐world mPFS was substantially longer than that observed in the PRIME trial (24.8 months), underscoring the effectiveness of PARPi in the broader, unselected Chinese population in a real‐world setting. Furthermore, for platinum‐sensitive recurrent patients, our cohort demonstrated a markedly longer mPFS (34.9 months) compared to the SOLO2 (19.1 months) and NOVA (21.0 months) trials, reinforcing the significant survival benefit conferred by PARPi maintenance therapy in this patient group.

A critical finding of this study is that approximately 20%–25% of patients exhibited primary resistance to PARPi, as objectively defined by ROC‐based PFS cutoffs, and experienced significantly poorer outcomes. The presence of BRCA wild‐type tumors in both sensitive and resistant groups, coupled with a non‐significant trend toward a lower BRCA mutation rate in the resistant group, strongly suggests that factors beyond BRCA status, including broader HRD and other genomic or molecular features, influence PARPi sensitivity [25, 26]. This underscores the urgent clinical need for more comprehensive predictive biomarkers.

Our exploratory transcriptomic analysis points to potential biological mechanisms underlying differential responses. The enrichment of pathways related to ferroptosis and HIF‐1 signaling in resistance tumors provides novel mechanistic insights. Furthermore, the validation of differential protein expression of TFRC and PRNP is of particular interest. The association of high PRNP and low TFRC expression with PARPi resistance aligns with existing literature linking TFRC to poor prognosis in ovarian cancer [27] and proposes a previously unreported role for PRNP in modulating PARPi response. However, given the small sample size of this exploratory analysis, these findings are hypothesis‐generating and require robust validation in larger, independent cohorts to ascertain their diagnostic and prognostic value.

The safety profile in our cohort was manageable, with a notably lower incidence of severe hematologic toxicities than reported in pivotal trials. This discrepancy may reflect proactive dose modifications, tailored supportive care, and the integrated management strategies employed in real‐world practice, which are crucial for ensuring treatment adherence and quality of life.

Several limitations of our study must be acknowledged. The retrospective, single‐center design and the relatively small sample size, particularly for the biomarker exploration (n = 8), constrain the generalizability of our findings and introduce the potential for selection bias. The ROC‐derived definition of PARPi resistance, while providing an objective operational criterion, requires prospective validation. Furthermore, the observed differences in adverse event profiles between olaparib and niraparib, though consistent with their known toxicity spectra, warrant more detailed discussion regarding their impact on treatment persistence and quality of life—an aspect not fully elaborated in the initial analysis.

In conclusion, this real‐world study confirms the significant clinical benefit of PARPi maintenance therapy for ovarian cancer in a Chinese population and highlights a substantial subset of patients with primary resistance. We preliminarily identify TFRC and PRNP as potential biomarkers associated with PARPi response, meriting further investigation. Future prospective, multi‐center studies with larger cohorts are essential to validate these biomarkers, refine predictive models incorporating BRCA status, HRD, and other novel factors, and ultimately guide more personalized and effective treatment strategies for patients with ovarian cancer.

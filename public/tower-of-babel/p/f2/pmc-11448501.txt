Multi-omics analysis and response prediction of PD-1 monoclonal antibody containing regimens in patients with relapsed/refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma
Authors: Xinrui Chen, Yan Qin, Xuemin Xue, Zucheng Xie, Tongji Xie, Liling Huang, Haohua Zhu, Lina Gao, Jiangtao Li, Jianliang Yang, Lin Gui, Sheng Yang, Haizhu Chen, Xiaoli Feng, Yuankai Shi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPatients with relapsed/refractory (r/r) diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) show varied responses to PD-1 monoclonal antibody (mAb) containing regimens. The mechanisms and predictive biomarkers for the efficacy of this regimen are unclear. This study retrospectively collected r/r DLBCL patients who received PD-1 mAb and rituximab regimens as salvage therapy. Clinical and genomic features were collected, and mechanisms were explored by multiplex immunofluorescence and digital spatial profiling. An artificial neural network (ANN) model was constructed to predict the response. Between October 16th, 2018 and May 4th, 2023, 50 r/r DLBCL patients were collected, 29 were response patients and 21 were non-response patients. CREBBP (p = 0.029) and TP53 (p = 0.015) alterations were statistically higher in non-response patients. Patients with PD-L1 CPS ≥ 5 were correlated with a longer overall survival (OS) than those with PD-L1 CPS < 5 (median OS: not reached vs. 9.7 months, hazard ratio [HR]: 3.8, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.64–22.44, p = 0.016). Immune-related pathways were activated in response patients. The proportion and spatial organization of tumor-infiltrating immune cells affect the response. PD-L1 CPS level, age, and alterations of TP53, MYD88, CREBBP, EP300, GNA13 were used to build an ANN predictive model that showed high prediction efficiency (training set area under curve [AUC] of 0.97 and test set AUC of 0.94). The proportion and spatial distribution of tumor-infiltrating immune cells may be related to the function of immune-related pathways, thereby influencing the efficacy of PD-1 mAb containing regimens. Background

Relapsed/refractory (r/r) diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) occurs in 30–40% of patients after first-line rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisolone (R-CHOP) treatment.

Programmed cell death 1(PD-1)/ programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), mediated immune escape in tumor microenvironment (TME), plays an important role in the pathogenesis and progression of DLBCL [1, 2]. PD-1 monoclonal antibody (mAb) has significantly improved the efficacy of various cancers. Nevertheless, the efficacy of PD-1 mAb varied among DLBCL patients. Previous studies have demonstrated that patients with r/r DLBCL failing autologous stem cell transplantation, had an objective response rate (ORR) of 10% receiving nivolumab monotherapy [3]. In treatment naïve DLBCL patients, pembrolizumab plus R-CHOP showed an ORR and complete response (CR) rate of 90% and 77%, respectively, 5-year progression free survival (PFS) rate of 71% and 5-year overall survival (OS) rate of 83% [1, 2].Our previous study demonstrated that the combination of PD-1 mAb with rituximab is a potential feasible treatment option for r/r DLBCL patients. In 36 patients with r/r DLBCL, the ORR was 52.8% and the median duration of response (DoR) was 18.7 months [4]. This suggests that PD-1 mAb combination regimens, rather than a single agent, may achieve long-term remission in a specific population of patients with r/r DLBCL. However, the features of these patients population remain unclear and the predictive value of biomarkers are still limited in clinical practice.

In this study, we investigated the genomic landscape and TME features of the patients with r/r DLBCL treated with PD-1 mAb and rituximab regimens. An artificial neural network (ANN) model was constructed to identify patients who may benefit from the treatment.

Methods

Patients

Between October 16th, 2018 and May 4th, 2023, the patients with r/r DLBCL received PD-1 mAb and rituximab regimens as salvage treatment in Cancer Hospital Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences (CHCAMS) were retrospectively analyzed. Other eligibility criteria included age ≥ 18 years; had relapsed or been refractory to at least one R-CHOP or R-CHOP like regimen as prior therapy; received capture-based targeted DNA sequencing. Key exclusion criteria included the presence of CNS or mediastinal lymphoma; a follow-up period of ≤ 6 months. Clinical data, such as age, gender, Ann Arbor stage, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status (PS) score, number of extranodal sites and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) level.

Treatment response was assessed using the Lugano 2014 criteria every 2 cycles using positron emission tomography (PET)/computed tomography (CT), CT, or magnetic resonance imaging. The best objective response (BOR), PFS and OS were evaluated. PFS was defined as the time from the first dose of study treatment to the date of disease progression or death of any cause. OS was defined as the time from the first dose of study treatment to the date of death of any cause.

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The study protocol was approved by the clinical ethics committee of CHCAMS (No.NCC2244) and the patient’s informed consent is waived.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

IHC was performed on CD10, BCL-6 and MUM-1 to determine the cell-of-origin (COO) classification according to Hans’ algorithm [5]. PD-L1 combined positive score (CPS) ≥ 5 was considered positive [6, 7].

Capture-based targeted DNA sequencing and data analysis

Archived formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue samples were obtained from enrolled patients. Matched peripheral blood was also collected as a germline control. Library was construction. The concentration of DNA was assessed and the distributions of plasma DNA were measured. DNA was broken up into fragments. Hybridization with capture probe baits, hybrid selection with magnetic beads, and the PCR amplification were subsequently conducted. 112 genes that were often altered in human lymphoma and hematological malignancies were covered (Supplementary Table 1). The detailed sequencing procedure was performed as described previously [8, 9]. Sample sequencing was performed. Sequencing data were mapped to human genome (hg19). Local alignment optimization, variant calling, and annotation were performed. Single nucleotide variants were determined. The average sequencing depth for all targeted regions was 1402×. The variants were annotated with ANNOVAR and SnpEff (v.3.6) [8].

Multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF)

MIF was performed on 4-µm-thick tissue sections from selected FFPE biopsies. An automated staining system (BOND-MAX; Leica Microsystems) with the 6-color Kit (Abcarta) was used for staining following the manufacturer’s instructions. The markers of panel 1 include CD20, FOXP3, CD8, CD4 and PD-1. The markers of panel 2 include CD20, CD68, IRF5, CD163 and CD56. Target antigens, antibody clones, and dilutions for markers are listed in supplementary Table 2. All stained slides were scanned using a digital pathology slide scanner (×40, KF-PRO-005, KFBIO). Multispectral images of whole biopsy sample were analyzed with the HALO Image Analysis Software (version 3.3, Indica Labs) to discern and quantify cell phenotypes. Manual review was proceeded in each case to ensure consistency and accuracy.

Spatial analysis of the tumor microenvironment

The spatial distribution of cells within the tissue section was obtained using the HALO. Equal distances grid the image into squares of equal area. The “Tumor” squares were defined as the squares where the CD20 positive rate was ≥ 5%. Any square that is not classified as “Tumor” was considered as “Other”, which was believed to represent the tumor-infiltrating immune cells (TIICs). The “Border” squares were defined as the “Tumor” squares that have at least one neighboring square classified as “Other”, indicating that they were located at the interface between the tumor and non-tumor squares. The “Core” squares refer to the “Tumor” squares that were completely surrounded by other “Tumor” squares, without any direct contact with the non-tumor (“Other”) squares. The “Island” squares were the “Tumor” squares that were completely isolated from the non-tumor (“Other”) squares, as they were fully surrounded by other “Tumor” squares. The “Border index” represents the proportion of “Border” squares within the “Tumor” squares. “Border index total” represents the proportion of “Border” squares across the entire tissue sample.

Digital spatial profiling (DSP) whole-transcriptome atlas

The tissue slides were first deparaffinized and underwent antigen retrieval. The samples were co-incubated with fluorescently labeled antibody (CD20) to detect tumor cells, as well as SYTO13 to stain cell nuclei. Custom fluorescent markers, conjugated to unique barcoded oligonucleotide tags with ultraviolet (UV) photocleavable linker, were used to visualize the tissue architecture. After imaging, specific regions of interest (ROIs) were exposed to UV light, which cleaved the linker and released the barcoded oligos. These were then captured using microfluidics and the GeoMx DSP system, and coupled with next-generation sequencing (NGS) to profile the RNA expression of over 18,000 genes in the samples.

Gene set enrichment analysis

“ClusterProfiler” and “GSVA” packages were used to conduct gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and gene set variation analysis (GSVA) [10, 11]. A selection of 1350 pathways relevant to tumor and TME was collected from the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) database, Gene Ontology (GO) database, and HALLMARK database, which were categorized into 6 classes (immunity, cell cycle, metabolism and energy, genetic and epigenetic information, ECM and metastasis, cell death) according to their semantics. For both GSEA and GSVA results, pathways with p < 0.05 and a false discovery rate (FDR) value < 0.25 were considered statistically significant.

Model development

ANN model was developed to predict the BOR of r/r DLBCL patients treated with PD-1 mAb and rituximab. Patients achieved CR or PR were classified as response patients, while those with SD or PD were classified as non-response patients. Clinical factors that have been proven to be prognostically related in previous studies and this study, along with genetic alterations with an incidence of ≥ 5%, were considered variables for the ANN model. Fisher test is used to select input variables for the ANN model. Patients were randomly divided into training and test sets according to 7:3. We determined the number of hidden neurons based on previous study [12]. Using the model Nh=(4n2 + 3)/(n2 − 8) [Nh, the number of hidden neurons; n, the number of input neurons]. Thus, fully connected feedforward neural networks including 7 input neurons, 5 neurons in the hidden layer, and 1 output neurons were chosen. The accuracy of ANN model was tested by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis in both training and test sets. Calculate the Youden index for the training and test sets at each score threshold of the training set, and choose the cutoff point that yields the highest sum of Youden indices.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics (version 26.0) and R Studio (version 4.1.1) software. Cox regression and log-rank test were used to compare survival. Fisher’s exact tests or χ2-tests were implemented to compare categorical variables. A two-sample t-test assessed continuous versus categorical variables, and the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used for non-normally distributed data. P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Result

This study collected a total of 50 r/r DLBCL patients hospitalized at CHCAMS between October 16th, 2018 and May 4th, 2023, all patients received PD-1 mAb plus rituximab (Fig. 1A). The median age of all patients was 62 (range 26–84) years old. 64% of patients were in the III-IV stage. 68.0% (34/50) of patients had an IPI of 2–5. The median number of previous treatment regimens was 2 (range 1–4) (Table 1).

Treatment outcomes and subgroup analysis

At the data cut-off date of December 10th, 2023, the median follow up was 14.2 (range 1.2–55.1) months. The BOR of all patients was 58.0% (29/50), with CR rate of 18.0% (9/50) and PR rate of 40.0% (20/50). The median PFS of all patients was 7.97 (95% CI: 3.00–NE) months. The median OS was not reached (Table 2). PFS (34.3 vs. 1.4 months, hazard ratio [HR]: 7.1, 95% CI 2.96–17.01, p < 0.001) and OS (not reached vs. 9.7 months, HR: 8.79, 95% CI 3.42–22.62, p < 0.001) varied significantly between response and non-response patients (Fig. 1B). Univariate Cox regression analysis showed that patients with extranodal involvement (PFS, p = 0.001; OS, p = 0.157) was associated with better PFS compared to no extranodal involvement (Supplementary Fig. 1).

Fifty patients were analyzed by high-resolution sequencing based on probe capture. The results are summarized in Fig. 1C. Univariate Cox regression analysis showed that IRF4 alteration was associated with poor PFS and OS (PFS, p = 0.048; OS, p = 0.030). Patients with MYC alteration (PFS, p = 0.062; OS, p = 0.026) showed better OS in this study (Supplementary Fig. 1). The results of univariate Cox regression and the Kaplan-Meier survival analyses showed similar results. Between patients with or without genetic alterations, BOR was statistically different in patients with CREBBP (wildtype vs. alteration: 64% vs. 17%, p = 0.029) and TP53 (wildtype vs. alteration: 72% vs. 38%, p = 0.015) alterations (Fig. 1D).

In the 40 patients with PD-L1 CPS results, patients showed no significant difference in median PFS between PD-L1 CPS ≥ 5 and CPS < 5 patients (14.5 vs. 3.0 months, HR: 2.33, 95% CI 0.62–8.74, p = 0.085), but with significant difference in median OS between two groups (not reached vs. 9.7 months, HR: 3.8, 95% CI: 0.64–22.44, p = 0.016) (Fig. 1E). The PD-L1 CPS level showed no significant difference between response and non-response patients (p = 0.083). Patients with CR had significantly higher PD-L1 CPS than PD patients (p = 0.020), but there was no significant difference between other groups (Fig. 1F).

The spatial distribution of immune cells in the tumor microenvironment revealed distinct patterns between response and non-response patients. The spatial characteristics of TME in response and non-response patients were analyzed in Fig. 3F,G. Comparing with non-response patients, the proportion of “Border” squares were higher in response patients, observed not only around the tumor periphery but also infiltrated in the tumor interior (Fig. 3F-G). The border index, representing the proportion of “Border” squares within the tumor, were significantly higher in the response patients (p = 0.033, Fig. 3H). The border index total, reflecting the proportion of “Border” squares across the entire tissue squares, tended to be higher in the response patients, although the difference did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.155, Supplementary Fig. 2D).

The relationship between genetic alterations significantly associated with clinical outcomes and TIICs were analyzed. In patients without TP53 alterations, there was a significantly higher proportion of CD4+FOXP3+ T cells (p = 0.037) and CD8+ T cells (p = 0.003) compared to those with TP53 alterations (Supplementary Fig. 3A, B). In patients without MYC alterations, there was also a significantly higher proportion of CD4+ T cells (p = 0.020) and CD4+PD-1+ T cells (p = 0.038) compared to those with MYC alterations (Supplementary Fig. 3C, D). For CREBBP, there were no significant differences in the proportions of TIICs between patients with and without alterations. IRF4 alteration was observed in only one patient (1/20), making comparison impossible. The border index and border index total revealed no significant differences between patients with alteration in CREBBP, TP53 and MYC and those without.

RNA expression level and genetic alternations

Using the GeoMx® DSP WTA assay, we scanned the whole transcriptome of CD20+ B cells in response(n = 13) and non-response (n = 7) patients for a total of 20 ROIs. The alterations of CREBBP (p < 0.001), TP53 (p = 0.032) and MYC (p = 0.007) were associated with significantly higher RNA expression level compare to wildtype patients (Supplementary Fig. 4).

Biological function analysis of different response patients

GSEA and GSVA were performed to compare between response and non-response patients. The GSEA results showed that among the 1350 pathways sets, a higher proportion of immune-related pathways [60.1% (253/421) vs. 39.9% (168/421)] were activated in response patients than in non-response patients. In contrast, activation of cell cycle pathways [0% (0/127) vs. 100% (127/127)], genetic and epigenetic information pathways [4.9% (14/286) vs. 95.1% (272/286)], metabolism and energy pathways [15.9% (50/315) vs. 84.1% (265/315)], and cell death pathways [9.7% (7/72) vs. 90.3% (65/72)] were higher in non-response patients. The ECM and metastasis pathways [41.1% (53/129) vs. 58.9% (76/129)] showed similar enrichment levels between response and non-response patients (Fig. 4A, Supplementary Table 3).

Focusing on the statistically significant different pathways set in GSEA analysis, the results were consistent. In response patients, there was a higher proportion of immune-related pathway activation [26.6% (112/421) vs. 6.9% (29/421)], while in non-response patients, there was a higher proportion of activation in cell cycle pathways [0% (0/127) vs. 70.1% (90/127), genetic and epigenetic information pathways [0.3% (1/286) vs. 62.2% (178/286)], metabolism and energy pathways [0.3% (1/315) vs. 17.5% (55/315)], and cell death pathways [1.4% (1/72) vs. 16.7% (12/72)] (Fig. 4B, Supplementary Table 3).

The GSVA results showed that among the 1350 pathways set and statistically significant different pathways set, higher proportion of immune-related pathway activation was seen in response patients [88.8% (374/421) vs. 11.2% (47/421) in 1350 pathway sets; 12.1% (51/421) vs. 0% (0/421) in statistically significant different pathway sets] (Fig. 4C, D, Supplementary Table 4).

The immune-related biological processes enriched by GSEA in response patients, include activation of T cell mediated immunity, T cell proliferation, CD8+ αβ T cell activation, complement activation, antigen processing and presentation of exogenous peptide antigen via MHC class I and II. B cell activation and differentiation were suppressed in response patients (Fig. 4E).

Efficacy prediction model

An ANN model was constructed to predict the BOR of r/r DLBCL patients received PD-1 mAb containing regimens. The training set included 28 patients, 35 potential variables were selected, including 26 genes alterations, and 9 clinical characteristics. Seven variables were identified including PD-L1 CPS (< 5 or ≥ 5), age (≤ 60 or > 60), alterations of TP53, MYD88, CREBBP, EP300 and GNA13 (Fig. 5A, Supplementary Tables 5 and 6). Twelve patients were included in test set. The area under curve (AUC) was 0.97(95% CI 0.91–1.00) in the training set while 0.94(95% CI: 0.84-1.00) in the test set (Fig. 5B).A Pearson correlation metric was performed to measure the relationship among continuous predictive score, BOR, border index, tumor cells, and TIICs (Fig. 5C).

The point with the maximum sum of Youden index was identified as the cutoff point of the prediction model, which is 0.62 in this study. Patients score > 0.62 (high score) were related to worse response, while score ≤ 0.62 (low score) were related to better response. The results of pathway enrichment are similar to those obtained from pathway enrichment based on treatment response (Supplementary Fig. 5). A higher proportion of immune-related pathway activation was observed in patients with low score, according to GSEA and GSVA analysis.

Discussion

At present, limited number of DLBCL patients treated with PD-1 mAb were available. Our previous study showed that the r/r DLBCL patients’ response to PD-1 mAb containing regimen may achieve long-term remission [4]. It is important to identify the features of the population that could potentially benefit from PD-1 mAb containing regimen. Moreover, the predictive biomarkers for the application of PD-1 mAb containing regimen are still unclear, and the predictive value of single factors is poor due to the heterogeneity of DLBCL.

This study enrolled 50 r/r DLBCL patients treated with PD-1 mAb combined with rituximab regimen at CHCAMS between October 16th, 2018 and May 4th, 2023. This study showed that significantly longer PFS and OS were associated with response patients, compared with non-response patients. Clinical and genomic features were analysis between response and non-response patients. Twenty patients underwent transcriptomics and proteomics analysis to identify features and potential mechanisms of efficacy in patients treated with PD-1 mAb regimens. PD-L1 CPS level, age, and alterations of TP53, MYD88, CREBBP, EP300, GNA13 were selected to build an ANN predictive model which showed high prediction efficiency.

Although the efficacy of PD-1 mAb monotherapy was confirmed to be associated with PD-L1 combined positive score (TPS) / CPS level in solid tumors [13, 14], the clinical usefulness of PD-L1 as a biomarker in combination therapy for DLBCL needs to be clarified. In previous studies, 23 untreated DLBCL patients received pembrolizumab with R-CHOP showed that PD-L1 tumor expression < 5% or 0% was associated with poor survival [1, 2]. In the five DLBCL patient’s r/r after chimeric CD19/20 targeted chimeric antigen receptor T cell therapy had an ORR of 60% (3/5) treated with PD-1 mAbs monotherapy. The expression of PD-L1 response patients was 60-80%, while in non-response patients was 0-5% [15]. In this study, of the 40 patients with PD-L1 CPS results, 35 r/r DLBCL patients with PD-L1 CPS ≥ 5 had significantly longer median OS than CPS < 5 patients, but without significantly difference in median PFS. The PD-L1 CPS level showed no significant difference between response and non-response patients (p = 0.083). This result suggests that r/r DLBCL patients with PD-L1 CPS ≥ 5 may potentially benefit from PD-1 mAb containing regimens for longer OS.

The genetic landscape of this study showed that IRF4 alteration is significantly associated with poor PFS and OS. While MYC alteration is significantly associated with poor OS. CREBBP and TP53 alterations were significantly higher in non-response patients. IRF4 is largely restricted to immune cells, where it exerts key regulatory functions on the activation of both B- and T-lymphoid cells [16]. IRF4 alteration was associated with the oncogenesis and survival of various hematopoietic malignancies, such as multiple myeloma, IRF4-rearranged large B-cell lymphoma and anaplastic large cell lymphoma (ALCL) [17–19]. A previous study confirmed that IRF4 could directly control PD-L1 expression in ALK+ ALCL [20]. The EP300/CREBBP are closely related histone acetyltransferases (HATs), often mutated in DLBCL and FL. EP300 could directly modulated the expression of oncogenic MYC/IRF4 network and PD-L1 in ALCL and Hodgkin lymphoma cell lines. The EP300/CREBBP HATs inhibitors could suppressed PD-L1 mediated tumor immune escape in vitro and in vitro [21]. In r/r DLBCL, TP53 alteration was more commonly found and were associated with a worse response [22].

In this study, 66% of patients had extranodal involvement, such as lung, bone, kidney, gastric and large intestine etc. These patients were significantly associated with better PFS comparing with intranodal involvement patients. In previous study, levels of TIICs were significantly higher in bone marrow involved DLBCL patients than in that without bone marrow involvement [23]. The increased number of PD-L1 expressing TIICs are associated with better disease-free survival in primary central nervous system (CNS)-DLBCL [24]. In another study, all 5 patients with primary CNS lymphoma or primary testicular lymphoma (PTL) had clinical and radiographic responses to PD-1 mAb therapy [25]. As a result, patients with extranodal involvement tend to benefit more from PD-1 mAb containing regimens due to the higher level of TIICs in TME.

The features and mechanism of the response patients were explored in 20 patients. Weak PD-1 expression on neoplastic B cells was reported in 7-22% of patients with DLBCL [26]. The prognostic value of CD20+ PD-1+ B cells in r/r DLBCL is still unclear. In this study, the proportion of CD20+ B cells showed no significant difference between response and non-response patients, while the proportion of CD20+PD-1+ B cells was significantly higher in response patients. PD-1 mAb enhanced the activation of CD4+ T cells, ultimately leading to the proliferation of tumor-specific CD8+ T cells [27]. A previous study showed that high amounts of PD-1+ cells, FOXP3+ cells, and CD4+ T cells were associated with prolonged OS in DLBCL patients [28]. Unlike solid tumors, tumor-infiltrating Tregs in both untreated DLBCL patients and DLBCL patients treated with chemoimmunotherapy were found to be positively associated with OS [28–31]. The possible reasons for this result may be due to the specific immunosuppressive functions of Tregs. In vitro study showed that CD4+FOXP3+ Tregs isolated from B cell malignancy patients induce cell death of autologous leukemic B cells [32]. Tregs can inhibit the growth of B cells through cytotoxic-dependent pathway [33]. Differential PD-1 expression in T cells is indicative of T cell exhaustion. Anti-PD-1 therapy can reinvigorate dysfunctional tumor infiltration PD-1+ Tregs [34]. Another study showed that Tregs had decreased suppressive capacity when λ-MYC-transgenic B-cell lymphoma mice were treated with anti-PD-1 and anti-CTLA-4 antibodies. The suppressive capacity of Tregs results in pro-inflammatory TME [35]. Therefore, the role of FOXP3+ Tregs in DLBCL patients receiving PD-1 mAb containing treatment remains unclear.

Apart from the proportion of TIICs, the spatial distribution of TIICs in TME is also important. The response to PD-1 mAb is correlated to three immune phenotypes [36]. In this study, border index was used to reflect the level of infiltration of TIICs in tumor tissue. Comparing with non-response patients, the border index was significantly higher in response patients. This suggested that response patients have more TIICs infiltrated in the tumor interior instead of retained in the stroma. The TME of response patients was characterized by higher proportion of CD8+ T cells, Tregs, PD-1+ Tregs and border index.

In this study, GSEA and GSVA were performed and associated with a selection of 1350 pathways relevant to tumor and TME between response and non-response patients. A higher proportion of immune-related pathways were activated in response patients. The pathway enrichment analysis of GSEA indicated that immune-related biological processes were activated in response patients, including activation of T cell mediated immunity, T cell proliferation, CD8+ αβ T cell activation, complement activation, antigen processing and presentation of exogenous peptide antigen via MHC class I and II. And B cell activation and differentiation were suppressed in response patients. The TIICs’ proportion and spatial distribution may be connected to the function of immune-related pathways.

Genetic alterations significantly associated with clinical outcomes, such as TP53, CREBBP, MYC, are consistent with the corresponding RNA expression levels. This suggests that changes at the DNA level could influence the biological activity in DLBCL cells. The alterations of TP53 and MYC were significantly associated with the composition of TIICs in TME. However, the border index or border index total had no significant difference in patients with or without CREBBP, TP53 and MYC alteration. These findings indicate that these genetic alterations may influence the TME and immune landscape, possibly independent of changes in spatial distribution. Further studies are needed to explore this relationship.

The predictive model on BOR constructed in this study shows high prediction efficiency. The multiple input factors are clinical characteristics and DNA sequencing results, which are easy to obtain and confirmed to be associated with DLBCL clinical outcomes. PD-L1 CPS level, TP53 and CREBBP were significantly associated with clinical outcomes in this study. An increasing age-related poor prognosis is observed in DLBCL patients [37]. GNA13 is involved in the germinal-center homing pathway in DLBCL [38]. CREBBP, EP300 and GNA13 alteration were the characteristics of the EZB-like subtype [39]. MYD88 alteration is mostly associated with an unfavorable prognosis along with CD79B alteration in DLBCL [40]. In the constructed model, the lower the score, the more likely the patient is to benefit from the PD-1 mAb-containing regimen. The correlation trend of predictive score with PD-L1 TPS level, border index, and proportion of microenvironment cells is similar to that of BOR. Additionally, the prediction model cutoff point is 0.62. Patients with low scores exhibit activation of immune-related pathways similar to those seen in response patients. Therefore, the predictive score could reasonably predict the BOR, and r/r DLBCL patients with scores ≤ 0.62 may potentially benefit from regimens containing PD-1 mAbs.

This study was a retrospective study, solely enrolling Chinese patients with a limited number of patients. Further studies are warranted to confirm the multi-omics characteristics and validate the predictive efficiency of the ANN model.

Overall, this study provides critical insights into the complex and heterogeneous immune landscape associated with response to PD-1 mAb containing regimens through multi-omics analysis of DLBCL samples. The TIICs’ proportion and spatial distribution may be connected to the function of immune-related pathways, which was connected to the BOR of r/r DLBCL patients treated with PD-1 mAb containing regimens. The ANN predictive model provides guidance for identifying the potential patients that may benefit from PD-1 mAb containing regimens.

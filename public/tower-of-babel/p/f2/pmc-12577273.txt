Analysis of pathogenic bacteria and predictors of infectious endophthalmitis after cataract surgery
Authors: Wang Hui, Lu Hui, Piao Junfeng, Xue Yajun, Chen Yujiao, Li Danqi, Geunchang Yoo, Jeon In-chul, Wang Feng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivePostoperative infectious endophthalmitis is a serious complication of cataract surgery. Understanding its risk factors, pathogen distribution, and related biomarkers is crucial for prevention and treatment. This study aimed to investigate the risk factors for postoperative infectious endophthalmitis following cataract surgery, analyze pathogen types, and evaluate the correlation between changes in peripheral blood levels of IL-17, MMP-2, and IGF-1 and the occurrence of this complication.MethodsIn this retrospective observational cohort study, 784 patients (1568 eyes) who underwent cataract surgery at Changzhi People’s Hospital Eye Hospital from January 2020 to June 2024. Aqueous humor and vitreous fluid were collected from patients with postoperative infectious endophthalmitis for pathogen culture. Basic clinical data of infected and non-infected groups were compared, and differences in serum concentrations of IL-17, MMP-2, and IGF-1 three days after surgery were analyzed to assess the predictive ability of these biomarkers for infectious endophthalmitis.ResultsAmong the 784 surgical cases, 46 eyes from 42 patients were diagnosed with infectious endophthalmitis, including 38 cases of unilateral infection (38 patients with one infected eye each) and 4 cases of bilateral infection (4 patients with both eyes infected).Pathogen culture results showed that Gram-positive bacteria were the predominant pathogens (accounting for 73.08%). Univariate and multivariate analyses revealed that diabetes mellitus and vitreous spillage were independent risk factors for postoperative infectious endophthalmitis after cataract surgery. Further analysis of serum biomarkers revealed that serum levels of IL-17 and MMP-2 in the infected group were significantly higher, while IGF-1 was significantly lower than those in the non-infected group three days after surgery (all P < 0.05). Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis indicated that the combined detection of these three biomarkers had a high predictive efficacy for postoperative infectious endophthalmitis after cataract surgery, with an area under the curve of 0.955, a sensitivity of 95.27%, and a specificity of 82.42%.ConclusionAlthough the incidence of postoperative infectious endophthalmitis after cataract surgery is low, the presence of diabetes mellitus and vitreous spillage significantly increases the risk. Gram-positive bacteria are the main pathogens. Changes in serum levels of IL-17, MMP-2, and IGF-1 are closely related to the occurrence of infectious endophthalmitis, and the combined detection of these three biomarkers has high predictive value, providing an important reference for early clinical identification and intervention.Keywords: Cataract, Postoperative infectious endophthalmitis, Risk factors, Pathogens, Biomarkers

Introduction

Cataract, a prevalent blinding eye disease, significantly impairs patients’ quality of life. Currently, cataract surgery has become the primary treatment for this condition, substantially improving patients’ visual recovery. However, postoperative infectious endophthalmitis, a potentially severe complication of cataract surgery, not only may compromise surgical outcomes but also lead to blindness, underscoring its clinical significance. The reported incidence of postoperative infectious endophthalmitis ranges from 0.03% to 0.11%, significantly higher than the 0.012% to 0.053% in economically developed countries. Therefore, in-depth research into postoperative infectious endophthalmitis and the exploration of effective prevention and treatment strategies are of paramount importance [1].

Despite advancements in medical technology and surgical techniques leading to a decline in the incidence of postoperative infectious endophthalmitis, this complication remains a focal point in ophthalmology due to its potentially severe consequences. Existing research has indicated that multiple factors contribute to the development of postoperative infectious endophthalmitis, including age, diabetes, hypertension, and surgical duration [2–4]. However, the exact pathogenesis remains incompletely elucidated, and effective predictive and preventive measures are still lacking in clinical practice. Furthermore, the widespread use of antimicrobial agents has exacerbated bacterial resistance, posing new challenges in the treatment of postoperative infectious endophthalmitis [5].

In terms of pathogen distribution, postoperative infectious endophthalmitis can be caused by various pathogens, including bacteria and fungi. Gram-positive bacteria, such as Staphylococcus epidermidis and Staphylococcus aureus, are the predominant causative agents [6, 7]. A study detected 138 strains of pathogens, with Gram-positive bacteria accounting for 81.88%, highlighting their dominant role in infection [8]. However, the increasing bacterial resistance may compromise the efficacy of traditional antimicrobial agents, necessitating the exploration of novel therapeutic approaches and preventive strategies.

In recent years, intensive research into the pathogenesis of postoperative infectious endophthalmitis has uncovered several biomarkers associated with inflammatory responses and immune regulation, such as IL-17, MMP-2, and IGF-1. These biomarkers may play pivotal roles in the onset and progression of infectious endophthalmitis. Studies have shown that serum levels of IL-17, MMP-2, and IGF-1 are significantly higher in infected patients than in non-infected patients, suggesting their potential association with disease onset and development [9, 10]. Nevertheless, despite advancements in research on these biomarkers in ocular diseases, their specific relationships with postoperative infectious endophthalmitis, as well as their potential applications in disease prediction, diagnosis, and treatment, warrant further validation and exploration.

In terms of therapeutic strategies, vitrectomy combined with intravitreal antibiotic injection is an effective method for treating postoperative infectious endophthalmitis. Early diagnosis and timely surgical intervention are critical factors in improving prognosis [11]. However, despite the availability of various treatment options, the morbidity and blindness rates of postoperative infectious endophthalmitis remain high, underscoring the importance of in-depth research into its pathogenesis and the exploration of effective prevention and treatment strategies.

This study aims to comprehensively investigate the risk factors, pathogen distribution characteristics, and changes in peripheral blood levels of IL-17, MMP-2, and IGF-1 associated with postoperative infectious endophthalmitis, and analyze the correlation between these factors and the occurrence of endophthalmitis.

We conducted a retrospective cohort study to investigate the risk factors, pathogen profile, and biomarker changes associated with postoperative infectious endophthalmitis following cataract surgery.

Objects and methods

Study design

This was a single-center, retrospective observational cohort study conducted at Changzhi People’s Hospital. All patients who met the inclusion criteria during the study period were included, and data were collected retrospectively. Despite the retrospective nature, the study incorporated prospective elements such as standardized postoperative biomarker testing and universal pathogen culture for all enrolled eyes.

Objects

This retrospective cohort study enrolled 784 patients (1568 eyes) who underwent cataract surgery between January 2020 and June 2024. Clinical data were collected retrospectively from electronic medical records, and all patients received standardized postoperative monitoring and laboratory testing. The inclusion criteria [12, 13] included: clinical diagnosis of cataract; meeting the surgical indications for phacoemulsification or extracapsular cataract extraction; no lens dislocation or subluxation; and complete and traceable clinical data. The exclusion criteria included: history of intraocular surgery; preoperative ocular infections, glaucoma, uveitis, or other ocular diseases; history of ocular trauma; and systemic infections, autoimmune diseases, or severe liver or kidney dysfunction. The diagnosis of infectious endophthalmitis was based on the criteria referenced in the literature. This study has been reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Changzhi People’s Hospital Eye Center.

Methods

Surgical methods

All patients underwent phacoemulsification or extracapsular cataract extraction, performed by the same experienced team of ophthalmologists. Preoperative preparation included the use of antibiotic eye drops (such as gatifloxacin eye drops, Shenyang Xingqi Ophthalmic Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.) starting 3 days before surgery to prevent postoperative infections. Routine preoperative examinations included blood glucose, blood pressure, intraocular pressure, and visual acuity testing to ensure the patients were fit for surgery. Lacrimal duct irrigation and conjunctival sac disinfection were also performed to reduce the microbial load in the eyes. Postoperative treatment included local anti-infective therapy (such as ofloxacin eye ointment, Santen Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.) to prevent postoperative endophthalmitis.All patients received posterior chamber intraocular lens (PCIOL) implantation following cataract extraction. This study included no aphakic patients or other IOL types (e.g., anterior chamber or iris-fixated IOLs).

Pathogen identification

For patients showing symptoms of endophthalmitis after surgery, anterior chamber aqueous and vitreous fluid samples were collected from the affected eye under strict sterile conditions before emergency treatment. A sterile syringe (25-gauge needle) was used to carefully aspirate 0.2 ml of anterior chamber aqueous through a corneal limbus puncture, followed by 0.2 ml of vitreous fluid from 3.5 mm behind the corneal limbus, to avoid further damage to intraocular tissues. The samples were inoculated onto chocolate agar medium (Thermo Fisher Scientific), blood agar medium (Thermo Fisher Scientific), and broth medium (Thermo Fisher Scientific), selected for their ability to support the growth of common intraocular pathogens. The inoculated media were incubated at 37℃ for 24–48 h to allow sufficient pathogen growth. Bacterial isolation and identification were performed using matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS, Bruker Corporation), which allows for rapid and accurate bacterial identification to the species level.

Clinical data collection

Comprehensive clinical data, including patient age, gender, underlying diseases (such as diabetes, hypertension, etc.), body mass index (BMI), and surgery-related parameters (such as surgical duration, intraoperative complications, etc.), were collected by reviewing the hospital’s electronic medical record system. This information was crucial for subsequent analysis of the risk factors for postoperative infectious endophthalmitis after cataract surgery.

Laboratory testing

The third postoperative day was selected for biomarker assessment as it represents a critical window for early inflammatory response, precedes the typical clinical onset of endophthalmitis (which most commonly occurs between 3 and 7 days postoperatively [14, 15]), and aligns with timing used in previous ocular infection studies.On the 3rd postoperative day, 6 ml of fasting venous blood was collected from each patient, and serum was separated after centrifugation. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) was used to detect the serum concentrations of IL-17, MMP-2, and IGF-1, as these biomarkers may play important roles in the onset and progression of infectious endophthalmitis. The IL-17 detection kit was purchased from BioLegend, the MMP-2 detection kit from R&D Systems, and the IGF-1 detection kit from Abcam. All detection steps were performed strictly according to the kit instructions to ensure the accuracy and reliability of the results.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 25.0 statistical software was used for data analysis. Count data were expressed as frequencies and percentages, and chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was used for comparisons between groups to analyze the association between categorical variables and postoperative infectious endophthalmitis. Measurement data were tested for normality, and normally distributed data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, with comparisons between groups using independent sample t-test to analyze the differences in continuous variables between the infected and non-infected groups. Non-normally distributed data were expressed as median (interquartile range), with comparisons between groups using Mann-Whitney U test. Variables with statistical significance in univariate analysis were included in the multivariate logistic regression model to control for confounding factors and identify independent risk factors for postoperative infectious endophthalmitis. For logistic regression analyses, reference groups were defined as follows: age (< 60 years), gender (female), BMI (≤ 24 kg/m²), hypertension (no), diabetes (no), surgical method (extracapsular excision), operation time (< 15 min), and vitreous overflow (no).The predictive efficacy of serum IL-17, MMP-2, and IGF-1 levels for postoperative infectious endophthalmitis was evaluated by plotting the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC curve), and the area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, and specificity were calculated to assess the predictive value of these biomarkers. All statistical tests were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

Postoperative infectious endophthalmitis following cataract surgery, a severe exogenous infection, primarily occurs due to microbial invasion into the eye during the surgical procedure, leading to inflammation of the anterior segment and vitreoretinal tissue. If left uncontrolled, this inflammatory response can rapidly cause devastating damage to ocular structures, potentially resulting in blindness or phthisis bulbi. Despite advancements in cataract surgical techniques, the incidence of postoperative infectious endophthalmitis remains a significant concern, and its consequences can be extremely severe. Although this study was designed as a retrospective analysis, the incorporation of systematic biomarker assessment and universal pathogen culture introduced elements akin to a prospective cohort. This approach enhances the reliability of our findings regarding risk factors and predictive biomarkers for postoperative infectious endophthalmitis [16].

The occurrence of postoperative infectious endophthalmitis is the result of multiple interacting factors. This study identified diabetes mellitus and vitreous spillage as independent risk factors for postoperative infectious endophthalmitis following cataract surgery. Patients with diabetes mellitus often suffer from impaired immune function due to chronic hyperglycemia, leading to diminished leukocyte chemotaxis, phagocytosis, and bactericidal activity. Additionally, the hyperglycemic environment provides a favorable condition for bacterial proliferation, increasing the susceptibility to infection [17]. Furthermore, vitreous spillage may indicate intraocular vascular injury, disrupting the ocular barrier function, and subsequently triggering an inflammatory stress response, thereby exacerbating the development of infectious endophthalmitis [18]. Furthermore, although vitreous spillage was identified as an independent risk factor, we were unable to analyze whether the specific surgical step at which it occurred influenced the endophthalmitis rate, due to the limited number of cases and the lack of detailed surgical records inherent to this retrospective design. However, it is plausible that spillage occurring earlier in the procedure may carry a higher risk, as it allows longer exposure of the vitreous cavity to potential contaminants. Future prospective studies should document the timing of vitreous loss to clarify its impact.It is worth noting that although some studies have suggested age as an independent risk factor for postoperative endophthalmitis in cataract patients [19], this study did not identify age as a significant factor. This discrepancy may be attributed to the limited sample size or specific characteristics of the study population. Future research with a larger sample size is warranted to validate the role of age in this context.

Furthermore, our univariate analysis revealed no statistically significant difference in the incidence of endophthalmitis between phacoemulsification and extracapsular cataract extraction (ECCE) (P = 0.572), despite phacoemulsification being widely regarded as a safer procedure due to its smaller incision and reduced surgical time. Several factors may explain this finding. First, all surgeries in this study were performed by an experienced team adhering to strict aseptic protocols and standardized perioperative antibiotic regimens, which may have minimized the inherent risk difference between the two techniques. Second, while ECCE is often reserved for more complex cases (e.g., with dense nuclei), the fact that its endophthalmitis rate was not significantly higher suggests that with meticulous surgical technique, it can still be performed safely. Finally, the relatively small number of endophthalmitis cases (46 eyes) may limit the statistical power to detect a subtle difference between the surgical methods. A larger, prospective study might be needed to definitively compare the infection risks associated with these procedures.

Regarding the intraocular lens (IOL) status, all patients in this cohort received PCIOL implantation, and no aphakic cases or other IOL types (e.g., ACIOL or iris-claw lenses) were included. Therefore, we could not assess whether IOL type or aphakia influenced the risk of endophthalmitis in this study. However, since all surgeries were standardized and performed by the same surgical team using consistent IOL implantation protocols, it is unlikely that IOL status confounded our results. Future studies including diverse IOL types or aphakic patients may provide further insights into this aspect.

Bacterial infection is the primary cause of postoperative infectious endophthalmitis after cataract surgery. The results of this study demonstrated that pathogens were cultured from all affected eyes, with Gram-positive bacteria being the predominant isolates, particularly Staphylococcus epidermidis and Staphylococcus aureus. This finding aligns with previous studies, reinforcing the dominant role of Gram-positive bacteria in postoperative infectious endophthalmitis [20]. Gram-positive bacteria, especially coagulase-negative staphylococci, which are part of the normal ocular surface flora, can be introduced into the eye during surgery and become opportunistic pathogens. Drugs such as vancomycin, rifampin, and tigecycline exhibit high sensitivity against these pathogens, providing valuable guidance for empirical antimicrobial therapy [21]. However, the escalating issue of antibiotic resistance, exemplified by the increasing resistance of Staphylococcus epidermidis to erythromycin [22], poses a significant challenge. Consequently, the judicious selection and use of antimicrobial agents are crucial, and future research should focus on monitoring bacterial resistance patterns and developing novel antimicrobial agents.

This study also investigated the association between changes in serum levels of IL-17, MMP-2, and IGF-1 and the development of postoperative infectious endophthalmitis. The results revealed that serum levels of IL-17 and MMP-2 were significantly higher in the infected group compared to the non-infected group three days after surgery, while IGF-1 levels were lower. ROC curve analysis demonstrated the predictive ability of these biomarkers for postoperative infectious endophthalmitis [23]. Specifically, IL-17, a pro-inflammatory cytokine derived predominantly from Th17 cells, is known to recruit neutrophils and other inflammatory cells to the site of infection, amplifying the intraocular inflammatory cascade. In endophthalmitis, IL-17 has been shown to disrupt the blood-retinal barrier and enhance the expression of other pro-inflammatory mediators such as IL-6 and TNF-α, contributing to tissue damage and bacterial persistence [24, 25].

MMP-2, a gelatinase involved in extracellular matrix degradation, facilitates leukocyte migration and tissue remodeling during infection. Elevated MMP-2 levels in endophthalmitis may promote breakdown of the vitreoretinal interface and exacerbate inflammatory infiltration, as demonstrated in experimental models of bacterial endophthalmitis where MMP inhibition reduced disease severity [26, 27].

IGF-1, typically associated with tissue repair and anti-apoptotic signaling, was significantly reduced in infected patients. This suppression may impair ocular immune homeostasis and delay repair processes, as IGF-1 is known to modulate macrophage function and suppress excessive inflammation. Previous studies have linked low IGF-1 levels to poor outcomes in intraocular infections, suggesting its role in mitigating infection-induced damage [28, 29].

Our finding aligns with recent evidence showing suppressed IGF-1 in ocular infections [30], contrasting with earlier reports of elevated levels which may reflect different disease stages or methodological variations.The significant alterations in serum IL-17, MMP-2, and IGF-1 levels observed in our infected patients underscore their close association with the pathogenesis of postoperative infectious endophthalmitis. More importantly, the ROC curve analysis in this study demonstrates that the combined detection of these three biomarkers exhibits a high predictive value for this complication, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.955, a sensitivity of 95.27%, and a specificity of 82.42%. This high predictive efficacy suggests that measuring these biomarkers on the third postoperative day, before the full clinical manifestation of endophthalmitis, could serve as a valuable tool for early risk stratification. This approach may enable clinicians to identify high-risk patients for closer monitoring or even preemptive intervention, potentially improving clinical outcomes.The strong predictive performance of the IL-17, MMP-2, and IGF-1 combination indicates its potential for integration into postoperative protocols to identify high-risk patients for early intervention. However, the implementation of this strategy in routine ophthalmic practice faces practical challenges, including cost-effectiveness of routine testing, the need for standardized assays, and the timely availability of results [31]. Most importantly, these findings require validation in an independent, prospective multicenter cohort before clinical adoption. The promising utility of this biomarker panel must therefore be interpreted with caution, as our study lacked an independent validation cohort [32], which is critical for assessing true clinical performance. First, As a retrospective observational cohort study with prospective elements, our findings are subject to the limitations of observational designs, including potential confounding factors. Future prospective studies are warranted to validate these results, and future prospective studies are needed to further validate the relationship between these biomarkers and disease onset. Second, the relatively small sample size may limit the generalizability of the findings, and expanding the sample size in future research could enhance the statistical power. Furthermore, the study results might be influenced by the specific healthcare setting, surgical techniques, or patient population, necessitating validation across diverse medical centers and patient cohorts.

Finally, although the combination of IL-17, MMP-2, and IGF-1 demonstrated a high predictive value (AUC = 0.955) for postoperative endophthalmitis in our cohort, our study lacked an independent validation cohort. The performance of this biomarker panel was evaluated on the same dataset from which it was derived, which may lead to overoptimistic estimates of its predictive accuracy. Therefore, the generalizability of our findings needs to be confirmed in future prospective, multi-center studies that include an external validation cohort.

In terms of therapeutic strategies, vitrectomy combined with intravitreal antibiotic injection remains an effective approach for managing postoperative infectious endophthalmitis. Early diagnosis and timely surgical intervention are critical factors in improving prognosis. However, despite the availability of various treatment modalities, the morbidity and blindness rates associated with postoperative infectious endophthalmitis remain high, underscoring the importance of further investigating its pathogenesis and exploring effective preventive and therapeutic strategies.

In conclusion, postoperative infectious endophthalmitis is a severe complication of cataract surgery, influenced by multiple factors. Diabetes mellitus and vitreous spillage are significant risk factors for its development, with Gram-positive bacteria being the predominant pathogens. Alterations in serum levels of IL-17, MMP-2, and IGF-1 are closely associated with the occurrence of infectious endophthalmitis and serve as valuable early predictive markers. Nevertheless, to gain a deeper understanding of the pathogenesis and to improve preventive and therapeutic strategies for postoperative infectious endophthalmitis, further research is essential to explore novel biomarkers, optimize antimicrobial usage, and consider the specificities of different healthcare environments and patient populations.

Conclusion

Postoperative infectious endophthalmitis is a serious complication of cataract surgery. Although its incidence is relatively low, the presence of diabetes mellitus and vitreous spillage significantly increases the risk of its occurrence. Gram-positive bacteria are the predominant pathogens, with Staphylococcus epidermidis and Staphylococcus aureus being the most common causative agents. Elevated serum IL-17 and MMP-2 levels, combined with decreased IGF-1, are closely associated with the development of infectious endophthalmitis. The combined detection of these three biomarkers exhibits high predictive value, providing an important reference for early clinical identification and intervention.

Lung Cancer Screening in Occupational Settings: A Scoping Review
Authors: E Okoro, H Keller, F Greiner, V Harth
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLung cancer is the leading cause of cancer deaths globally. It is often asymptomatic until late stage, resulting in late diagnosis and low survival rate. Screening with low-dose computed tomography (LDCT) enables early detection and reduces mortality. Whilst several guidelines considered age and smoking as risk factors, some also included occupational exposures e.g. the US National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guideline. Workplaces are valuable settings for providing screening access to employees who otherwise rarely seek medical attention. This study aims to map the current evidence on lung cancer screening in occupational settings.MethodsThis review followed JBI guidelines, using a three-step search across six databases. Only studies involving lung cancer screening in any occupational setting, published between 2013 and November 2024 in English or German were considered. Two reviewers screened studies, resolving disagreements through discussion.ResultsThis review included 21 studies from 12 countries, mostly the USA and Italy. They were mainly observational studies conducted in various sectors, including construction, healthcare, mining, etc. 18 studies used LDCT for screening (15 as standalone method and 3 in combination with biomarkers, sputum cytology and chest ultrasonography, respectively). Three studies used biomarker only, biomarker + chest X-ray, and sputum PCR only, respectively. Duration of interventions ranged from three months to 13 years. Eligibility criteria varied across studies. The most reported outcome was lung cancer detection rate. Only six studies reported recruitment strategies. Six studies involved participants of existing health programs, and seven focused on asbestos-exposed groups.ConclusionsResearch on lung cancer screening in occupational settings is still minimal. However, there is a growing interest, and these activities can be replicated whilst adapting to a region's epidemiological characteristics.Key messages• This scoping review maps existing evidence on lung cancer screening in occupational settings, highlighting populations screened, eligibility criteria, recruitment, screening methods, and outcomes.• Lung cancer screening can improve health outcomes and reduce mortality. However, it’s implementation in occupational settings is understudied and further research in this area is recommended.

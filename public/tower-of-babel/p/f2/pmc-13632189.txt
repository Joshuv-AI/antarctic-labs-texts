MRI radiomics combined with clinical factors to predict vessels encapsulating tumor clusters and early recurrence in hepatocellular carcinoma (≤ 3 cm)
Authors: Huilin Chen, Wenjie Zou, Ruilin He, Qingri Wang, Ningyang Jia, Wanmin Liu, Peijun Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThis study aims to develop a predictive model that combines MRI radiomics features with clinical factors to anticipate the preoperative presence of Vessels Encapsulating Tumor Clusters (VETC) and early recurrence in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) with tumors ≤ 3 cm.MethodsA total of 336 patients were included in the study, with 271 from Hospital 1, divided into a training group (n = 192) and a validation group (n = 79). An additional 65 patients from Hospital 2 were used as an external validation group. Key features were selected using Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) and logistic regression analyses. Clinical, radiomics, and combined clinical radiomics models were then constructed using machine learning algorithms. Model performance was evaluated using the Area Under the Roc Curve (AUC), calibration curves, and decision curves. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis assessed early recurrence in VETC positive and VETC negative HCC patients with tumors ≤ 3 cm.ResultsBoth the clinical model utilizing logistic regression with GGT, AFP, non-smooth margin, and arterial peritumoral enhancement as independent risk factors, and the radiomics model incorporating 15 radiomics features with logistic regression, exhibited superior AUC values across the three groups (training, test, and external validation) when compared to other algorithms. Notably, the clinical radiomics model exhibited higher AUC values in the training group (0.885), validation group (0.867), and external validation group (0.849) compared to the standalone clinical and radiomics models. There was a significant difference (p < 0.05) in predicting early recurrence between VETC positive and VETC negative HCC patients (≤ 3 cm) when using the clinical radiomics models.ConclusionsThe clinical radiomics model demonstrates robust performance in predicting VETC positivity in HCC patients (≤ 3 cm), thereby holding the potential to forecast early recurrence in these patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12880-026-02575-x.Keywords: Hepatocellular carcinoma, Vessels encapsulating tumor clusters, Machine learning, Magnetic resonance imaging, Multicenter

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is one of the most common malignant tumors worldwide and a challenge to global health [1]. Liver resection is the primary treatment option for patients with HCC [2]. Despite a favorable 5-year overall survival rate of roughly 70% post-surgical resection, the high recurrence rate of liver tumors, reaching up to 80% [3]. The epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) is commonly associated with cancer recurrence and metastasis. However, there is a distinct vascular pattern that is related to new mechanisms of metastasis [4].

In 2015, Fang et al. described this vascular structure is called Vessels Encapsulating Tumor Clusters (VETC), which is composed of a sinusoidal network of functional blood vessels that completely surround the primary tumor [5, 6]. Some studies have shown that VETC pattern is easily detectable in a consistent proportion of HCC [7, 8]. Additionally, VETC can be distinguished through immunohistochemical staining using anti-CD34 antibodies, as well as anti-CD31 and anti-CD105 antibodies [9–11]. Researchers suggest that the anastomosis between the blood vessels in VETC pattern and the peritumoral blood vessels provides a way for tumor clusters to enter blood vessels, and they found VETC tumor clusters in the blood flow of HCC patients, as well as in tumor-adjacent liver tissue and tumor stromal vessels [6, 12, 13]. Further research suggests that VETC serves as a predictive marker for recurrence and survival outcomes in HCC patients undergoing resection or liver transplantation [7, 14, 15].

Multi-parameter magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is commonly used in HCC diagnosis, and MRI provides enhanced soft tissue contrast and avoids radiation exposure [16]. In addition, MRI imaging features may also provide valuable insights for HCC [17–19]. Researchers have developed and validated nomograms for predicting VETC patterns using MRI image features [11, 20]– [24]. However, there are currently limited models available for predicting preoperative VETC and early recurrence of HCC(≤ 3 cm) using a combination of radiomics and clinical factors.

In our study, we constructed a clinical model by integrating clinical parameters and image features. We selected the relevant radiomic features to build a radiomic model. Subsequently, we fused the clinical and radiomic scores to establish a clinical radiomic model. All three models were validated in both the testing group and an external validation group. Our clinical radiomics model aims to more accurately predict VETC and early recurrence in HCC (≤ 3 cm) and provide treatment decisions for clinical practice.

Materials and methods

Patient selection

This retrospective study received approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee, and the requirement for informed consent was waived. Furthermore, we have implemented rigorous measures to protect the privacy of patients and remain in compliance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.

According to the following inclusion criteria: (1) received segmental resection. (2) pathological confirmation of HCC and VETC status. (3) Tumor size (≤ 3 cm) and number of tumors (≤ 3). (4) no lymph node and extrahepatic metastasis. (5) no radiofrequency ablation, transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE) and chemotherapy prior to surgery. The exclusion criteria included: (1) the presence of other malignant tumors. (2) Incomplete clinical data and follow-up data. (3) unclear imaging and pathological images.

From Hospital 1, we collected 271 patients from the Third Affiliated Hospital of Shanghai Naval Medical University (Yangpu Campus) from September 2016 to September 2020, and randomly divided them into a training group (192) and a test group (79). From Hospital 2, we collected 65 patients from the Third Affiliated Hospital of Shanghai Naval Medical University (Jiading Campus) from September 2020 to December 2021, and selected them as the external validation group. Histopathology and immunohistochemistry

Two abdominal pathologists with 10 and 15 years of experience in liver pathology, respectively, independently evaluated all CD34 immunohistochemical staining slides. Both pathologists were blinded to the patients’ clinical and imaging data. In cases of disagreement, a third pathologist with more than 20 years of experience in pathological diagnosis was consulted, and the final diagnosis was determined by consensus.

The VETC pattern is characterized by a continuous lining of sinusoid-like vessels that segregate and encapsulate individual tumor clusters, forming a distinctive cobweb-like pattern [5]. VETC consists of endothelial cells, which are positive for CD31 or CD34, hepatic stellate cells, which are positive for alpha-smooth muscle actin, and basement membrane components [5, 8]. Therefore, CD34 immunohistochemical staining was used to evaluate the VETC pattern [8].

A previous study demonstrated that patients with VETC patterns ≥ 5% had significantly poorer OS and DFS compared with those below 5%, while no significant difference was observed between the 5–49% and ≥ 50% subgroups [10]. Therefore, the VETC positivity threshold of ≥ 5% was adopted based on published evidence. This predefined cutoff was applied consistently across all cases to define VETC positivity without dataset-driven optimization.

Follow-up

All patients underwent postoperative follow-up every 3–6 months, including serum AFP assessment and imaging examinations with contrast-enhanced CT or MRI, to evaluate tumor recurrence and metastasis. The final follow-up date was June 1, 2024.

Early recurrence was defined as intrahepatic recurrence and/or distant metastasis detected within 2 years after surgery [11]. Newly detected lesions during follow-up were considered recurrence events when they met the diagnostic criteria for recurrent HCC based on imaging findings and/or pathological confirmation.

Imaging technique

Prior to undergoing Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI), patients must fast for 4 h and avoid gastrointestinal barium meal examination within a week prior to the examination. Patients are required to remove metallic and magnetic items and lie down on the examination bed, with their feet entering first. Subsequently, 0.1 mmol/Kg of gadolinium meglumine (Gd-DTPA) is administered through a high-pressure injector into the patient’s median cubital vein at a flow rate of 2.0 ml/s, immediately followed by a 20 mL saline flush. After injection of contrast agent, the acquisition times for arterial phase (AP), portal vein phase (PVP), and delayed phase (DP) images are 25–30 s, 60–75 s, and 100–120 s, respectively. Additionally, pre-contrast scans encompass Diffusion-Weighted Imaging (DWI), T1-Weighted Imaging (T1WI), and T2-Weighted Imaging (T2WI). The detailed MRI sequences for the two hospitals are provided in Supplementary Tables E1-E2.

Imaging analysis

Two radiologists with 5 and 10 years of experience in liver imaging, respectively, independently assessed the presence of imaging features while being blinded to clinical data and final diagnoses. In cases where there was a disagreement between the two radiologists, a discussion was held with a third radiologist who had 10 years of diagnostic experience until a consensus was reached.

Our radiologists assessed MRI features with reference to LI-RADS version 2018 [19]. These features are as follows: (a) tumor diameter; (b) tumor number; (c) arterial peritumoral enhancement; (d) margin [25]; (e) shape; (f) radiological capsule enhancement [20]; (g) non-rim arterial phase hyper-enhancement (APHE) [26]; (h) rim APHE [26]; (i) restricted diffusion [27]; (j) non-peripheral “wash-out”; (k) enhancement pattern. For a more comprehensive definition, please refer to Supplementary Tables E3-E4.

Radiomics feature extraction and selection

Tumor regions of interest (ROIs) were manually delineated slice-by-slice on T1WI, T2WI, AP, PVP, and DP images using ITK-SNAP software (version 3.8; http://www.itksnap.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php). The radiologists were blinded to clinical and pathological information during segmentation. After segmentation, an experienced abdominal radiologist reviewed the ROIs to ensure consistency. To evaluate inter-reader reproducibility of radiomics feature extraction, a randomly selected subset with complete paired segmentations from two radiologists was used for ICC analysis. Features with ICC < 0.80 were excluded before subsequent feature selection. The exact ICC values are provided in Supplementary Table E8.

Radiomics features were extracted using PyRadiomics (https://www.radiomics.io/pyradiomics.html) and categorized into three main groups: shape features, first-order statistical features, and texture features. Shape features describe the three-dimensional morphological characteristics of the tumor. First-order features describe the distribution of voxel intensities within the tumor ROI. Texture features describe second- and higher-order spatial patterns of voxel intensities and were extracted using several matrices, including the gray-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM), gray-level run length matrix (GLRLM), gray-level size zone matrix (GLSZM), gray-level dependence matrix (GLDM), and neighborhood gray-tone difference matrix (NGTDM).

Subsequently, the retained radiomics features underwent Z-score normalization before model construction. An independent-sample t-test was then used to identify statistically significant features with a p-value threshold of < 0.05. Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient was applied to reduce feature redundancy. When the correlation coefficient between any two features exceeded 0.90, one feature was retained. To maximize the representational power of the selected features, a greedy recursive deletion strategy was used for further feature filtering by iteratively removing the feature with the highest redundancy in the current feature set. This process resulted in the selection of 15 radiomics features.

To construct the radiomics model, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression was applied in the training cohort for feature selection. LASSO shrinks regression coefficients toward zero and sets coefficients of less informative features to zero according to the regularization parameter λ. The optimal λ was determined using 10-fold cross-validation with the minimum error criterion. Features with nonzero coefficients were used to construct the radiomics signature. For each patient, a radiomics score was calculated as a linear combination of the selected features weighted by their corresponding coefficients.

Logistic regression was selected as the final classifier because of its interpretability, model simplicity, and relatively low risk of overfitting in a limited-sample radiomics setting. The logistic regression model was implemented using the Python scikit-learn package with default parameter settings unless otherwise specified.

Model development and validation

We utilized the selected radiomic features to establish a radiomics model through eight distinct supervised machine learning classifiers: Logistic Regression (LR), k-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest (RF), Extra Trees, Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), and Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). Here, we adopt 5 fold cross verification to obtain the final radiomics model.

Additionally, we conducted both univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses on clinical variables to identify significant variables (p < 0.05), which were then used to establish clinical models utilizing the same eight classifiers. Available clinical and imaging variables, including tumor-related characteristics and liver function-related laboratory indicators, were considered during clinical model construction according to the predefined feature selection procedure.

After deriving the clinical score, we integrated the radiomics score with the clinical score using LR, thereby establishing a clinical radiomics model. The primary endpoint of this study was the preoperative prediction of VETC status in HCC patients ≤ 3 cm. Early recurrence was analyzed as an additional clinical outcome to explore the biological and prognostic relevance of VETC status.

To evaluate the predictive performance of the three models (clinical, radiomics, and clinical radiomics), we calculated the Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) Curve (AUC). Calibration curves were plotted to assess the predictive accuracy of the models, while Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) was performed to evaluate their clinical value and utility.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test, while categorical variables were evaluated using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were used to evaluate model performance, and AUC values with 95% confidence intervals were reported. The optimal cutoff value was determined using the Youden index. Kaplan-Meier curves were employed for survival analysis, and the log-rank test was used to compare between groups. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using R software (Version 4.0.2).

Among the 271 patients in Hospital 1, a total of 189 patients were followed up. Among these, 130 were in the training group, including 56 VETC positive patients (42 with early recurrence) and 74 VETC negative patients (12 with early recurrence). Discussion

In our study, we have developed a clinical radiomics model that integrates clinical parameters, imaging features, and radiomics features to predict the VETC pattern in HCC (≤ 3 cm). This model has demonstrated good predictive performance in the training group, test group, and external validation group, with AUC values of 0.885, 0.867, and 0.849, respectively. Additionally, the VETC predicted by this model is associated with early recurrence in patients with HCC (≤ 3 cm).

Previous researchers reported that high levels of AFP are significantly correlated with the VETC pattern [7, 11]. Furthermore, in another study by Zhu et al. [28], patients were categorized into VM-HCC (VETC+/MVI+, VETC-/MVI+, and VETC+/MVI-) and non-VM HCC (VETC-/MVI-) groups. The analysis results revealed that AFP levels higher than 400 ng/mL, non-smooth tumor margin, and peritumoral arterial enhancement are independent risk factors predictive of VM-HCC. This is similar to our research findings. There is a scarcity of research focused on predicting the VETC pattern in HCC(≤ 3 cm). Chen et al. attempted to construct a nomogram using imaging features to predict the VETC pattern in HCC tumors less than 3 cm in size [23]. However, the sensitivity of this nomogram was low (33.3%), while the specificity was high (96.8%) [23]. To further improve the accuracy of predicting the VETC pattern in HCC (≤ 3 cm), we have developed a new nomogram that incorporates clinical parameters, imaging features, and radiomics features. Fortunately, our nomogram has demonstrated robust predictive performance, offering a promising and useful tool for clinical decision-making and prognosis assessment in this subset of patients.

From a clinical perspective, the proposed clinical radiomics model may serve as a preoperative non-invasive tool for risk stratification in patients with small HCC (≤ 3 cm). It can estimate the probability of VETC positivity using preoperative MRI and clinical data, thereby assisting clinicians in identifying patients at higher risk of aggressive tumor behavior and early recurrence.The model output can be translated into a clinically applicable threshold using the Youden index derived from the training cohort and consistently applied across validation cohorts.Compared with previous imaging-based models, our approach integrates multiparametric MRI radiomics with clinical variables, providing a more comprehensive and quantitative framework for individualized preoperative assessment and management.

The VETC pattern is characterized by a continuous arrangement of sinusoid-like vessels that serve to isolate and encapsulate individual tumor clusters, creating a distinctive cobweb-like structure [5, 8]. Moreover,, VETC positive tumors exhibit a unique metastatic potential, where entire clusters of tumor cells can detach and travel through the bloodstream as endothelium-coated microemboli [5]. Previous studies have found that the VETC pattern is an independent risk factor for poor prognosis in patients with HCC [7, 15, 29, 30]. Our research findings reveal that among HCC(≤ 3 cm) patients, VETC positive patients are more likely to experience early recurrence compared to VETC negative patients. Notably, our developed nomogram for predicting VETC status consistently confirms this observation.Furthermore, it should be noted that there is currently no universally accepted cutoff for defining VETC positivity. Although the ≥ 5% threshold utilized in our study was selected based on published evidence, different cutoffs may influence classification results. Future studies with larger independent cohorts are needed to evaluate the robustness of different VETC thresholds and to establish standardized diagnostic criteria.

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. Firstly, as a retrospective study, it is inherently subject to potential selection bias. To mitigate this, we applied predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, and all radiological and pathological evaluations were performed independently and in a blinded manner. Secondly, the relatively small sample size, particularly in the external validation cohort, may limit the generalizability of the proposed model. Therefore, large-scale, prospective multicenter studies are warranted to further validate the robustness and clinical utility of the model, as well as to provide a more comprehensive assessment of its stability. In addition, some potentially important confounding factors, such as detailed cirrhosis severity and antiviral treatment status, were not fully available across all cohorts and therefore could not be consistently incorporated into the final model. Finally, since all patients included in this study underwent surgical resection, the findings may not be directly generalizable to all patients with hepatocellular carcinoma.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the clinical-radiomics model based on preoperative MRI radiomics features and clinical factors showed potential for non-invasive prediction of VETC status in patients with HCC ≤ 3 cm. This model may provide useful information for preoperative risk stratification and individualized clinical decision-making. Further prospective multicenter studies are needed to validate its generalizability and clinical utility.

Understanding non-traditional caregivers: protocol for a scoping review of caregiver definitions, caregiver networks and outcome measures
Authors: NE Carlozzi, Jacqueline L Freeman, Christopher M Graves, Amanda N Leggett, Jennifer A Miner, Emily Capellari
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionDementia caregiving is typically provided through a network of multiple individuals, particularly in diverse populations. Although family members of people living with Alzheimer’s disease and Alzheimer’s disease-related dementias, especially spouses and adult children, often assume the role of informal care partner, there is a growing number of non-traditional informal care partners (ie, non-spousal, non-biological child) given societal changes in family structure. The proposed study aims to systematically review the caregiver literature to better understand how caregivers are defined, the approaches and tools commonly used to evaluate the caregiver network and common caregiver outcome assessments that are used in clinical and research settings.Methods and analysisThe following protocol will be conducted according to Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines. Searches will be developed by health science library informationists for Medline (Ovid), Cumulative Index to Nursing and Allied Health Literature Complete (EBSCOhost), AgeLine (EBSCOhost) and APA PsycInfo (EBSCOhost) databases to identify tools, measures or approaches to the characterisation of complex caregiver networks that include non-traditional care partners (ie, step-family and non-marital romantic partners, blended families, families of origin, families of choice and family friends). In addition, we will also consider data elements from large-scale cohorts that include caregiver respondents (eg, National Health and Ageing Trends Study; National Study of Caregiving). There will be no limits set on article language, geography or publication date. Articles that are identified in languages other than English will be included if there is an English translation available. Search results will be reviewed by two independent raters for inclusion; in cases where there are discrepancies in inclusion/exclusion recommendations, the two reviewers will meet to establish consensus; a third reviewer will adjudicate when necessary. An artificial-intelligence-based software will be used to aid in extraction; these results will be double-checked by a human, and instances of discrepancies will be reviewed by a second independent rater. A narrative synthesis approach will be used to analyse the data.Ethics and disseminationInformation from this review will be published in peer-reviewed journals and presented at scientific conferences, with the goal of providing future recommendations for how to define a non-traditional caregiver, the systematic assessment of caregiver networks and a summary of the most commonly used outcome measures in the caregiver literature. Given that scoping reviews of published articles are not regulated under federal regulations governing human subject research (45 CFR 46 or 21 CFR 50/56), institutional review board approval is not required.Scoping review registrationIn accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Scoping Review and Meta-analysis Protocols, the following protocol has been registered via https://osf.io on 19 December 2023 (registration DOI: https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/2XGSR).

Care partners (ie, informal caregivers) of people living with Alzheimer’s disease and Alzheimer’s-disease-related dementias (AD/ADRD) often undergo considerable stress, which can have a negative impact on the health of both the care partner and the person living with dementia.113 Most dementia caregiving research focuses almost entirely on the role of spousal caregivers or adult children of the person living with AD/ADRD.14 Yet, for most people living with AD/ADRD, especially for those individuals from diverse backgrounds, caregiving is typically provided through a network of multiple individuals that extend beyond these more ‘traditional’ family care partners.15 16 There is an increasing number of ‘non-traditional’ care partners, including step-family and non-marital romantic partners, ‘blended families’ (eg, step-children), ‘families of origin’ (eg, surviving siblings, grandchildren) and ‘families of choice’ (eg, personal communities of people such as friends, partners and other people with whom individuals share a biological family-like relationship, but are not connected by biological or legal ties). Yet next to nothing is known about their experience as care partners or how to characterise them.17

To address this oversight, we are developing a new measurement system that can capture and characterise the most important aspects of the care partner experience for these non-traditional caregiving relationships. This measurement system will be designed to complement other existing measures that are specific to traditional caregiver groups and will focus on the development of measures that mediate caregiver outcomes and are likely to vary between traditional and non-traditional caregivers. This measurement system will include developing new, sophisticated computer adaptive tests (ie, smart tests) that can ascertain a person’s function level using only a minimal number of items without losing the precision of a longer measure, as well as identifying, adapting and validating existing measures.

Objectives

As the first stage in this measurement development process, we will conduct a scoping review of the dementia caregiving literature to identify the critical elements of the non-traditional care partner role. The primary objective of this scoping review will be to identify survey items, measurement tools or approaches that have been used in the literature to characterise ‘non-traditional’ care partners (including, but not limited to, characterisations of demographic, financial, medical history and health-related quality of life (HRQOL) of non-traditional care partners).

The secondary objectives of the scoping review are to identify survey items, measurement tools or approaches available to assess caregiver networks (the informal support network/team of a care recipient, made up of multiple caregivers), as well as to identify survey items, measurement tools or approaches available to assess caregiver-specific HRQOL outcomes across a broad range of domains (including physical, mental and financial health) for both traditional and non-traditional relationships.

Methods/analysis

The following protocol will guide the scoping review of available literature to inform our work. It was drafted using Preferred Reporting Items for Scoping Review and Meta-analysis for Scoping Reviews18 and is publicly available via https://osf.io.19 Patients or the public were not involved in the development of this protocol. Initial review of identified articles began in December 2023, and final data extraction is anticipated in July 2025.

Search methods

We will conduct a scoping literature review according to established guidelines18 20 to identify existing approaches for defining complex family systems and non-traditional care partners. This will include detailed searches by health science library informationists using Ovid Medline, Cumulative Index to Nursing and Allied Health Literature, AgeLine and PsycInfo databases to identify tools, measures or approaches to the characterisation of complex caregiver networks that include non-traditional care partners (ie, step-family and non-marital romantic partners, blended families, families of origin, families of choice and family friends). We will also review the current data elements of large-scale cohorts that include caregiver respondents (eg, National Health and Ageing Trends Study; National Study of Caregiving) to identify candidate data elements for use with non-traditional care partners. For the purposes of this review, we will extract the names of the assessments, measures, item(s) or variables that the caregiver/care partner completed. We will only include self-report surveys, measures, items or variables that the caregiver/care partner completed about themselves and exclude proxy measures that the caregivers/care partners completed. We will also include descriptive variables about the caregiving role (such as time spent providing care), but will exclude demographic variables (such as age, gender). Search terms include derivatives and controlled vocabulary for select dementia-related clinical diagnoses; informal caregivers, caregivers, relative types or family structure; and measures (see online supplemental Appendix A for the complete draft of the Ovid Medline search strategy). An example search for the caregiver concept for Ovid Medline using Boolean operators will be, for instance, ((‘family structure*’ or ‘reconstituted famil*’ or stepfamil* or ‘step famil*’ or ‘step-famil*’ or ‘blended famil*’ or ‘step kin’ or ‘step-kin’ or ‘stepkin’ or stepparent$1 or ‘step parent$1’ or ‘step-parent$1’ or stepdaughter$1 or ‘step daughter$1’ or ‘step-daughter$1’ or stepson$1 or ‘step son$1’ or ‘step-son$1’ or famil* or ‘non famil*’ or nonfamil* or ‘non-famil*’ or ‘non-immediate famil*’ or ‘non immediate famil*’ or ‘non-immediate famil*’ or ‘non relative$1’ or ‘non-relative’ or nonrelative or unrelated or ‘un-related’ or ‘non-traditional’ or nontraditional or ‘non-traditional’ or ‘adopted child*’ or ‘foster youth$1’ or ‘foster child*’ or friend$1 or neighbor$1 or companion$1 or acquaintance$1 or grandparent$1 or grandmother$1 or grandfather$1 or grandchild* or niece$1 or nephew$1 or sibling$1 or sister$1 or brother$1 or ‘sister-in-law’ or ‘sister-in-law’ or ‘sisters in law’ or ‘sisters-in-law’ or ‘brother in law’ or ‘brother-in-law’ or ‘brothers in law’ or ‘brothers-in-law’ or aunt$1 or uncle$1 or cousin$1 or ex-wife or ‘ex wife’ or ‘ex-wife’ or ex-wives or ‘ex wives’ or ‘ex-wives’ or ‘exhusband$1’ or ‘ex husband$1’ or ‘ex-husband$1’ or relative$1 or nonspousal or ‘non-spousal’ or ‘non spousal’ or nonmarital or ‘non marital’ or ‘non-marital’ or ‘extended famil*’ or ‘family-of-choice’ or ‘family of choice’ or ‘families-of-choice’ or ‘families of choice’ or missionar* or ‘untrained personnel’ or unpaid or informal or ‘community member$1’ or ‘legal guardian$1’ or ‘social support’ or ‘social network$1’ or ‘social resource$1’ or ‘emotional support’ or ‘non-spouse’ or ‘non spouse’ or nonspouse) adj5 (carer$1 or caregiver$1 or ‘care-giver’ or ‘care giver$1’ or caregiving or ‘care-partner’ or ‘care partner$1’ or ‘caregiving network$1’ or volunteer* or ‘voluntary worker$1’ or ‘household work’ or housekeeping or housework or ‘household activit*’”).

Eligibility criteria

Articles that are published or available in English, peer-reviewed and from all geographical locations will be included. There will be no limits set on publication date. We will include articles which report completed, quantitative research in a dementia caregiving sample and have either a definition of the ‘caregiver’ sample, surveys/tools/items which describe the caregiver network or contain caregiver-specific measures/tools/items (or a combination of all three). Articles will be excluded if they are opinion/conceptual models that do not include data, include small (n<20) sample sizes or are focused on professional paid caregivers. Since the purpose of this scoping review is to identify existing survey items or measurement tools (rather than identify the thematic experience of non-traditional care partners), we will also exclude studies which report solely on qualitative research. We will also exclude review articles, case studies, response letters, research reports, graduate theses and articles publishing study protocols (ie, an article must report on completed research). Finally, only articles in which report quantitative caregiver-specific outcomes will be included (as opposed to studies which merely report on a caregiver-completed proxy assessment). Other grey literature (ie, conference proceedings, policy documents, etc.) will be excluded.

Selection criteria

Retrieved citations will be imported into a review management software (Covidence) from the electronic databases cited above. Duplicates will be removed using Covidence. The title and abstract for each identified citation will be screened by two independent raters based on the above eligibility criteria. In cases with discrepancies, the two raters will meet and discuss before rendering a final decision to keep or exclude; if consensus cannot be achieved, a third rater will be consulted. Following this round of screening, the citations that comply with the inclusion criteria will be retrieved. During the final round of review, two independent raters will screen the full text to determine if any of the three inclusion criteria are present: (1) an operationalised definition of caregiver, (2) a description of how the caregiver network was assessed or (3) measures that were used to assess caregiver outcomes. Articles that do not include one of these three elements will be excluded from further consideration.

Data extraction

Studies meeting inclusion criteria will undergo a data extraction process whereby the following information will be extracted: author details, year of publication, sample size, caregiver population(s), caregiver definition, assessment tools used to capture details about the caregiver network and outcomes measures that were evaluated (see online supplemental Appendix B). The initial extraction will be conducted using an artificial intelligence (AI)-based software called ‘U-M Maizey’. U-M Maizey is part of the University of Michigan’s suite of generative AI tools maintained by the university and available to all faculty, staff and students. U-M Maizey is a tool running the University’s Generative Pre-trained Transformer model (U-M GPT) which allows researchers to create custom prompts and AI settings, allowing U-M Maizey to respond to queries based on this custom data along with information from U-M GPT large language models (LLMs): U-M GPT runs GPT-4o, Llama 3.2 (3B), DALL·E 3, Claude 3.5 Haiku, o3-mini and o1 LLMs.

To ensure reliability and validity of U-M Maizey, we will pilot test the data extraction summary sheet (excel) and make adjustments to the AI settings and prompts as needed to improve reliability. Two PhD-level investigators and a BA-level project manager will compare the U-M Maizey response against their own responses from reading articles and extracting manually. Once satisfied that the U-M Maizey responses are aligned with the investigative team, all data extractors will be calibrated by the investigators with a small sample of articles which they will extract with the help of the AI—with the investigators verifying the AI responses as well as the human data extractors’ responses for consistency. Following this process, all articles will undergo extraction by the AI-based software, which will then be double-checked by a member of the study team. Any changes that are made to the AI-based extraction spreadsheet will be reviewed by a third independent rater. This scoping review aims to examine the types of available data and identify key knowledge gaps; it does not address conflicting evidence or practice variation, but rather to examine the types of available data and identify key knowledge gaps. We will not use a formal tool for critical appraisal of sources. Nonetheless, transparency and reproducibility remain critical.21 As described above, an information specialist will execute our search, and we will include multiple reviewers to reduce error and increase reliability in study inclusion. We have put these steps in place to ensure data are extracted and presented in a structured way.

Data analysis

Data analysis will focus on the three primary content areas that are the focus of the review: caregiver definition, caregiver network assessment and outcomes measures. For caregiver and caregiver networks definitions, studies will be grouped according to homogenous definitions (ie, studies which define a caregiver in the same way will be grouped together), frequency data will be presented and areas that warrant further clarification will be identified. With regard to the identification of tools/assessments/surveys that are used to evaluate the caregiver network or outcome measures that are employed, we will again group approaches/tools and provide summaries of the overall frequency of different approaches and/or tools. We will present our findings in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Scoping Review and Meta-analysis Protocol guidelines to provide an overview of the literature on dementia caregiving research.

Patient and public involvement

Neither patients nor the public will be involved in the design or conduct of this scoping review.

Expected findings

The proposed scoping review aims to provide a comprehensive overview of dementia caregiving research. The results of the scoping review will attempt to characterise existing items, tools, measures and approaches to defining ‘non-traditional’ care partners, assessing caregiver networks and assessing caregiver outcomes. We expect that this review will shed light on the most common ways to measure caregiver networks and caregiver outcomes yet may also clarify important gaps in the existing literature. However, there are important limitations to the proposed review. For example, we opted to exclude qualitative studies from this review due to the large number of eligible studies and instead focused on quantitative research and outcome measures. In this manner, we may miss important information about what concepts might be important that are not already represented by existing outcome measures. Future work reviewing this qualitative literature would be beneficial. We also do not expect to find a consensus definition for what constitutes a caregiver or different caregiver relationships. Additionally, we do not expect to find a consensus definition for what constitutes ‘non-traditional’ vs ‘traditional’ caregivers, and we will not extract whether these types of relationships are present for a given study.

The discussion of the proposed scoping review findings will focus on aggregating the current literature and proposing standardised ways of characterising and assessing ‘non-traditional’ care partners of people living with dementia. Our understanding of the experience of non-traditional care partners of persons living with AD/ADRD is greatly limited by the fact that there are no measures that are specific to these care partners. In fact, there is very little targeted research on these individuals despite a recognised need for more research in this area. What research exists suggests that non-traditional care partners are more likely to be Black, highlighting the need to understand networks of care to capture the care experience of diverse populations. Additionally, non-traditional care partners (eg, grandchildren) report receiving less social support related to care. Distinctions also arise in who non-traditional care partners are providing care to, with non-traditional care partners more likely to be assisting a female, older and unmarried care recipient.22 This suggests that non-traditional care partners may be assisting vulnerable individuals with more care needs. Yet who these care partners are, their unique experiences and variability in the outcomes they experience is not yet understood and will be greatly enhanced by measures capturing their unique experiences. The proposed scoping review is designed to address this research gap by better understanding how caregivers are defined in the research literature as well as how caregiver networks are defined and assessed. We also seek to understand the specific tools and assessments that are being used to capture caregiver outcomes.

Demographic changes in families raise key questions about who individuals consider to be ‘family’ and the roles they play in caring for persons living with AD/ADRD—questions that cannot be answered with existing measures and survey methods. The current review will serve to inform future work focused on providing recommendations for best practices around the systematic assessment of caregiver networks, the identification of relevant characteristics and concerns of non-traditional caregivers and how these concerns both overlap with and are distinct from their more traditional caregiver counterparts.

Ethics and dissemination

Our dissemination strategy will include publication in peer-reviewed journals and conference presentations of the main findings of the review. On publication, data extraction tables will be uploaded to the OSF repository linked to the project. Furthermore, the results of this scoping review will be used to inform the development of new caregiver outcomes measures as well as a case report form examining caregiver networks—all of which will be psychometrically validated and published in peer-reviewed journals as well as presented at scientific conferences.

This review does not require IRB review at the University of Michigan given that scoping reviews are not regulated under federal regulations governing human subjects research (45 CFR 46 or 21 CFR 50/56).

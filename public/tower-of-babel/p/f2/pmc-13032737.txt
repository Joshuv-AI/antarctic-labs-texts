The Role of Histone Methylation in Heart Failure: Epigenetic Mechanisms and Therapeutic Perspectives
Authors: Yingli Zhou, Zhaoyang Dong, Yuxuan Gao, Keying Xiao, Qinglan Huang, Qianqian Cao, Xiaoyu Wang, Yuxian Duan, Zhihua Guo, Jiaming Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHeart failure (HF) represents the terminal stage of various cardiovascular diseases, and its prevalence continues to rise while current therapeutic approaches remain insufficient to reverse disease progression. Epigenetic regulation, with a particular focus on histone methylation, has gained increasing attention for its involvement in the initiation and advancement of HF. Histone methylation is a reversible post-translational modification controlled by histone methyltransferases and demethylases, and it participates in essential biological processes such as gene expression regulation, cell cycle, apoptosis, and metabolic reprogramming. This review systematically summarizes the multifaceted roles of histone methylation in HF, including the specific functions in cardiac regeneration, hypertrophy, fibrosis, apoptosis, metabolic remodeling, and inflammation. The review also highlights the promising effects of inhibitors that target histone methylation enzymes in animal studies, including anti-hypertrophic, anti-fibrotic, and cardioprotective properties, suggesting significant potential for clinical translation.

Introduction

Heart failure (HF), as a severe and terminal stage of multiple cardiac diseases, continues to rise in prevalence due to population aging, increasing cardiovascular risk factors, and improved survival associated with advances in medical therapy.1 Current HF treatment primarily relies on a foundational regimen that includes angiotensin receptor–neprilysin inhibitors (ARNI), beta-blockers, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs), and sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i). Despite these therapeutic options, HF remains one of the most challenging conditions in cardiovascular medicine and is often regarded as the “final battlefield” of heart disease.2 Histone methylation, an essential form of post-translational modification (PTM), plays a critical role in development, stem cell differentiation, and a wide range of physiological and pathological processes.3,4 Histone methylation is catalyzed by histone methyltransferases (HMTs), which transfer a methyl group from S-adenosylmethionine to target residues, typically on lysine, arginine, or asparagine side chains, with lysine and arginine methylation being the most common forms.5,6 Similar to other PTMs, histone methylation is reversible and is dynamically regulated by the coordinated activities of methyltransferases and demethylases.7

Recent studies have increasingly demonstrated that histone methylation regulates cardiovascular development, cardiomyocyte proliferation and apoptosis, and myocardial remodeling, and its dysregulation is closely associated with the onset and progression of multiple cardiovascular diseases.8,9 Notably, the histone methylation modification program in response to cardiac injury shows a high degree of conservation across species. For example, both mice and zebrafish exhibit removal of the repressive mark H3K27me3 and enrichment of the activating mark H3K4me3 after cardiac injury, and they share the Hmga1‑KDM6 core regulatory axis along with a dedifferentiation‑proliferation gene network. However, adult zebrafish maintain an open chromatin state and the capacity for dynamic remodeling of modifications, enabling complete regeneration. In contrast, in adult mice, epigenetic modifications become fixed after injury, with persistently high expression of H3K27me3 and silencing of regulatory factors, leading to a loss of regenerative capacity.10 During the pathological progression of heart failure, the aberrant reactivation of “fetal gene reprogramming” is tightly regulated by epigenetic networks, in which dynamic remodeling of histone methylation plays a central driving role. Studies have confirmed that the activation of fetal genes (such as β‑MHC) in the failing heart is accompanied by significant loss of repressive marks H3K9me3 and H3K27me3 and enrichment of the activating mark H3K4me3 at their promoter regions. This specific methylation profile remodeling directly relieves the silenced state of developmental genes and drives their transcription. Population epigenomic studies further reveal that genome‑wide reductions in H3K9me3 and increases in H3K4me3 are not only early events in heart failure, but their specific combinatorial patterns can also serve as an “epigenomic signature” for predicting disease susceptibility and arrhythmia risk.11 A comprehensive understanding of the contribution of histone methylation to the pathogenesis and progression of HF is essential for clarifying underlying mechanisms, identifying new therapeutic targets, and promoting the development of more effective treatment strategies. Therefore, this review aims to provide a comprehensive synthesis of the current understanding of histone methylation in the pathophysiology of heart failure.

Classification of Histone Methylation

Histone methylation is a reversible epigenetic modification catalyzed by HMTs. The modification mainly occurs on lysine and arginine residues of histones H3 and H4 and is removed by histone demethylases (HDMs). The modification is widely present across living organisms and exerts significant influence on protein conformation, functional regulation, and multiple cellular physiological processes.12 Histone methylation is primarily categorized into lysine methylation and arginine methylation based on the modified residue, although additional amino acids can also undergo methylation.13 The dynamic balance of histone methylation is precisely regulated by the coordinated actions of methyltransferases and demethylases, which serve as key regulators of chromatin state and gene expression. Aberrant expression or impaired function of these enzymes has been strongly linked to the onset and progression of numerous pathological conditions.14

Lysine Methylation

Lysine methylation occurs in three forms, including monomethylation (Kme1), dimethylation (Kme2), and trimethylation (Kme3), and functions as an important epigenetic mechanism regulating gene transcription. Methylation at H3K4, H3K36, and H3K79 is generally associated with transcriptional activation by increasing the affinity of transcription factors for DNA and promoting transcription initiation. In contrast, methylation at H3K9, H3K27, and H4K20 is closely linked to transcriptional repression through the recruitment of heterochromatin-associated proteins that induce chromatin condensation and inhibit gene expression.15

Lysine methylation is primarily catalyzed by protein lysine methyltransferases (PKMTs), which can be divided into two major classes. The first class consists of SET domain-containing methyltransferases whose catalytic activity depends on a conserved SET domain, and most identified PKMTs belong to this category.16 Enhancer of Zeste Homolog 2 (EZH2) is a representative SET domain-containing methyltransferase that functions within the polycomb repressive complex 2 (PRC2) to catalyze trimethylation of histone H3 at lysine 27 (H3K27me3). EZH2 plays a pivotal role in embryonic development, cell differentiation, and tumorigenesis.17 The second class includes non-SET-domain methyltransferases, mainly members of the seven β-strand family. These enzymes contain a characteristic twisted β-sheet structure and regulate chromatin organization and gene expression by modifying specific lysine residues on target proteins.15 Disruptor of Telomeric Silencing 1-Like (DOT1L) is a well-characterized non-SET-domain methyltransferase that catalyzes mono-, di-, and tri-methylation of histone H3 at lysine 79 (H3K79) and participates in transcriptional regulation and cell-cycle control.18,19

Lysine demethylases are grouped into two main families, including lysine-specific demethylases (LSDs) and Jumonji C (JmjC) domain-containing demethylases.20 The human genome encodes two LSD enzymes, LSD1 and LSD2.21 LSD enzymes contain an amine oxidase catalytic domain that is widely shared among metabolic enzymes, as well as a SWIRM domain that mediates chromatin binding and contributes to protein stability. LSD1 and LSD2 specifically remove methyl groups from mono- and dimethylated lysine residues. In contrast, JmjC domain-containing demethylases can remove mono-, di-, and trimethyl groups from lysine residues. Approximately 30 JmjC domain-containing proteins have been identified in the human genome, functioning as key regulators of gene transcription, chromatin remodeling, and chromatin homeostasis.22

Arginine Methylation

Arginine methylation occurs in three major forms, including monomethylarginine (MMA), asymmetric dimethylarginine (ADMA), and symmetric dimethylarginine (SDMA).23,24 It serves as a key regulatory mechanism in cellular signal transduction. In several signaling pathways, arginine methylation of specific proteins can modulate their interactions with other signaling molecules, thereby influencing signal propagation and cellular responses.25,26 Alterations in the methylation status of DNA repair–related proteins can also affect the efficiency and fidelity of DNA damage repair.4,27

Arginine methylation is catalyzed by protein arginine methyltransferases (PRMTs). Nine PRMTs have been identified and are classified into three categories based on catalytic activity. Type I PRMTs, including PRMT1, PRMT2, PRMT3, PRMT4, PRMT6, and PRMT8, catalyze MMA and ADMA formation. Type II PRMTs, such as PRMT528 catalyze the formation of MMA and SDMA. PRMT9 is also classified as a Type II enzyme, although its predominant activity appears to be monomethylation.29 Type III PRMTs, represented by PRMT7, catalyze only MMA formation.30 In cell-cycle regulation, PRMT1 modifies key regulatory proteins through arginine methylation and influences cyclin expression and activity to control cell-cycle progression.31 In RNA metabolism, PRMT5 methylates a range of RNA-binding proteins, influencing RNA splicing, transport, and stability.32

Histone arginine demethylation is primarily mediated by peptidylarginine deiminases (PADs). Among these enzymes, PAD4 is the most extensively studied nuclear member and has the most clearly defined function in histone demethylation.33 PAD4 modulates the methylation status of histone arginine residues by converting methylarginine into citrulline and releasing methylamine.34 Mina53, an Fe2+- and α-ketoglutarate-dependent JmjC domain-containing arginine demethylase, removes the transcription-activating mark H4R3me2 and decreases methylation levels at promoter regions, thereby suppressing the expression of genes involved in cellular differentiation.35 JMJD6 was initially identified as a histone arginine demethylase that catalyzes histone demethylation through hydroxylation, generating formaldehyde as a byproduct. JMJD6 can remove methyl groups from mono-, symmetric di-, and asymmetric dimethylated arginine residues and participates in the regulation of chromatin structure and gene transcription.36,37 However, subsequent biochemical and structural studies have reclassified it primarily as a lysyl hydroxylase rather than a demethylase.38,39

Other Forms of Histone Methylation

Histones possess exceptionally long half-lives, with histone H3 having a half-life of up to 159 days, thereby increasing susceptibility to spontaneous chemical damage. Among aging-related modifications, the conversion of aspartate residues to isoaspartate represents a predominant alteration that impairs histone function. A recently characterized modification, histone H4 at aspartate 24 (H4D24me), serves as a molecular marker of histone aging. H4D24me contributes to the maintenance of histone homeostasis and chromatin integrity through two complementary mechanisms, including a PCMT1-mediated repair pathway and a VprBP-mediated degradation pathway, which collectively promote the removal or functional restoration of damaged histones.40

Beyond lysine and arginine side-chain methylation, methylation can also occur at the α-N terminus of histones. N-terminal RCC1 methyltransferase 1 (NRMT1) functions as an N-terminal methyltransferase that transfers a methyl group from S-adenosyl-L-methionine to the free α-amino group of target proteins, generating mono-, di-, or trimethylated forms. NRMT1 acts as a key regulator of protein–DNA interactions and participates in multiple cellular processes, including mitosis, cell-cycle progression, chromatin organization, DNA damage repair, and transcriptional regulation.41 As summarized in Table 1, the classification and functional characteristics of histone methylation-modifying enzymes are presented. Early studies conducted in the 1960s and 1970s suggested the existence of histone histidine methylation (3MeH). However, recent large-scale methylproteomic analyses in cell lines such as HeLa and HEK293 have identified thousands of histidine-methylated sites without detecting any in histones, indicating that earlier observations were likely false-positive signals arising from actin or other contaminating proteins.13

Regulation of Histone Methylation in HF

Epigenetic regulation, particularly histone methylation, has gained increasing importance as a key mechanism involved in the development and progression of HF. Dynamic alterations in histone methylation exert substantial effects on multiple pathophysiological processes associated with HF. Histone methylation regulates cardiac development and the cardiomyocyte cell cycle, supporting cardiomyocyte proliferation and myocardial regeneration. It also contributes to ventricular remodeling by modulating the expression of genes involved in hypertrophy, fibrosis, and apoptosis. In addition, histone methylation exerts broad regulatory effects on cardiac energy metabolism and inflammatory responses, influencing multiple aspects of HF pathogenesis.

Cardiac Development and Myocardial Regeneration

The regenerative potential of the adult mammalian heart largely depends on the proliferation of residual cardiomyocytes. During embryonic and early postnatal stages, cardiomyocytes exhibit relatively high proliferative capacity, which declines markedly with age.42 After cardiac injury such as myocardial infarction, extensive loss of cardiomyocytes exceeds the limited regenerative capacity of the heart and leads to progressive replacement of myocardium with fibrotic tissue, resulting in reduced contractility, impaired cardiac output, and HF.43 Histone methylation regulates cardiac development and the transcription of cardiomyocyte-specific genes by activating gene networks and signaling pathways involved in myocardial regeneration. Through these mechanisms, histone methylation may promote cardiomyocyte proliferation, enhance cardiac function, and slow the progression of HF.

Studies have shown that trimethylation of histone H3 at lysine 36 (H3K36me3) is markedly reduced in actively transcribed myocardial regions in patients with end-stage cardiomyopathy. The altered enrichment pattern of H3K36me3 correlates with downregulation of genes such as DUX4 and with changes in the abundance of noncoding RNAs and mRNAs. Experimental silencing of DUX4 reduces cardiomyocyte viability, indicating that H3K36me3 influences HF pathogenesis through transcriptional regulation.44 Dimethylation of histone H3 at lysine 9 (H3K9me2) acts as a negative regulator of cardiomyocyte formation. Suppression of G9a, the enzyme responsible for H3K9me2 deposition, reduces H3K9me2 levels and activates key transcription factors, including NK2 homeobox 5 (Nkx2.5) and myocyte enhancer factor 2C (MEF2C), thereby facilitating mesenchymal stem cell (MSC) differentiation into cardiomyocytes and supporting cardiac lineage specification.45 Studies using Floxed–Smyd1 mouse models have demonstrated that SET and MYND domain-containing protein 1 (Smyd1), a striated muscle–specific HMT, catalyzes H3K4 methylation, downregulates ISL LIM homeobox 1 (Isl1), and upregulates atrial natriuretic factor (ANF), thus participating in early cardiac morphogenesis.46 H3K27me3, catalyzed by Ezh2 (a core component of the PRC2 complex), restricts cardiomyocyte reprogramming by suppressing activation of cardiogenic transcription factors Gata4, Tbx5, and Mef2c, whereas Ezh2 inhibition promotes this process. In zebrafish, depletion of H3K27me3 enables regeneration of the adult heart.47 In adult mammalian hearts, promoters of silenced cell cycle genes remain accessible and are not repressed by DNA methylation or H3K27me3. KDM4D, which is highly expressed during fetal cardiomyocyte proliferation but downregulated in adult cardiomyocytes (ACMs), decreases H3K9me3 levels and increases the expression of cell cycle–related genes upon overexpression, promoting cardiomyocyte proliferation and cardiac mass expansion without functional impairment.48 Moreover, Kdm6b regulates cardiac development through multiple coordinated mechanisms. It removes H3K27me3 marks at specific loci or functions as a noncatalytic component of the MLL/COMPASS complex to establish an epigenetic switch for transcriptional activation. In the zebrafish heart, Kdm6b also cooperates with the Brg1/BAF chromatin-remodeling complex to suppress Adamts1 expression in the endocardium, thereby promoting cardiomyocyte proliferation and cardiac morphogenesis49 (Figure 1).Figure 1Histone Methylation Orchestrates Cardiac Development, Myocardial Regeneration, and Heart Failure.

These studies collectively reveal the central regulatory role of histone methylation modifications in cardiac physiology and pathology. Most studies consistently demonstrate that activating histone marks (eg, H3K36me3, H3K4me3) maintain cardiomyocyte function and promote differentiation by activating key gene expression, while repressive marks (eg, H3K9me2/3, H3K27me3) restrict cardiomyocyte generation, maturation and regenerative capacity by silencing critical cardiogenic transcription factors or cell cycle genes. A key divergence lies in cardiac regeneration mechanisms: the proliferation barrier of adult mammalian cardiomyocytes stems from dynamic H3K9me3 regulation, not irreversible H3K27me3-mediated cell cycle gene silencing, which sharply contrasts with zebrafish heart regeneration relying on H3K27me3 removal. Although these studies have greatly enriched our understanding of the epigenetic regulatory network, critical knowledge gaps remain. For example, how to effectively translate these findings from specific animal models (such as zebrafish) or in vitro cell differentiation systems into targeted therapeutic strategies for human heart failure, are urgent directions for future research.

Cardiac Hypertrophy

Histone methylation plays a pivotal role in regulating gene expression during the progression of cardiac hypertrophy. Precise transcriptional control in cardiomyocytes is essential for maintaining normal cardiac function, whereas disrupted expression patterns can result in myocardial dysfunction.50 Studies using cardiac-specific G9a knockout mice have revealed that the HMT G9a is essential for sustaining cardiomyocyte homeostasis in the adult heart through H3K9 methylation and its interaction with EZH2 and MEF2C. G9a forms a complex with MEF2C to maintain the heterochromatin structure required for silencing developmental genes in mature cardiomyocytes. In addition, G9a promotes hypertrophic growth by repressing antihypertrophic gene expression such as Myh7 and Acta1.51 JMJD2A contributes to cardiac hypertrophy by removing H3K9me3 marks and activating fetal gene programs, including NPPA and NPPB in mice.52 Research in mice indicates that myocardin-related transcription factor A (MRTF-A) recruits the HDM KDM3A to the ITGB2 promoter, reducing H3K9 dimethylation, maintaining chromatin accessibility, and enhancing ITGB2 transcription. The resulting upregulation facilitates macrophage–endothelial adhesion and promotes hypertrophic remodeling.53 Crosstalk between histone methylation and acetylation also provides refined regulatory control during HF progression.54 In pressure overload–induced hypertrophy, studies using PRMT5 transgenic (PRMT5-TG) mice demonstrate that overexpression of protein arginine methyltransferase 5 (PRMT5) methylates histone acetyltransferase p300 at arginine 200 (R200), significantly enhancing its acetyltransferase activity. The consequent increase in histone H3 lysine 9 acetylation (H3K9ac) amplifies pathological gene transcription, thereby driving cardiac hypertrophy and worsening cardiac dysfunction.55

While the histone methyltransferase SUV39H1 inhibits cardiac hypertrophy by catalyzing H3K9 methylation to repress NPPA and NPPB expression, studies in cardiac-specific PHF8 transgenic mice reveal a contrasting protective mechanism. Under pressure overload, PHF8 overexpression promotes the removal of H3K9 methylation marks, thereby modulating downstream gene expression and suppressing pathological protein synthesis. This action contributes to the reversal of cardiac dysfunction, hypertrophy, and fibrosis, identifying PHF8 as a key protective factor against pathological remodeling.56 Silencing of the MLL4 gene disrupts chromatin organization by altering H3K4me1 modification and associated gene expression programs. MLL4 deficiency markedly reduces H3K4me1 and H3K27ac occupancy at the Thrombospondin-4 (Thbs4) locus, leading to decreased Thbs4 transcription. Mechanistically, MLL4 activates Thbs4 expression through H3K4 monomethylation and regulates THBS4-dependent endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress signaling. Therefore, targeting MLL4 or ER stress responses may represent a promising therapeutic strategy to prevent cardiac hypertrophy and HF.57 Under pathological stress, the chromatin remodeler Brg1 recruits the G9a/GLP HMT complex to the Myh6 promoter, promoting H3K9me2 deposition. This modification facilitates DNMT3a recruitment and CpG site methylation, leading to the formation of a repressive chromatin complex that condenses chromatin through HP1/MeCP2 association and obstructs transcription factor binding. The resulting Myh6 silencing impairs myocardial contractility and contributes to the development of HF in mice.58 Genetic deletion or pharmacological inhibition of G9a/GLP alleviates cardiac hypertrophy, fibrosis, and functional decline, indicating its potential as a therapeutic target for HF intervention (Figure 2).Figure 2Histone Methylation Orchestrates Pathological Cardiac Hypertrophy and Heart Failure.

Consistent findings across multiple studies demonstrate that the maintenance of homeostasis in adult cardiomyocytes requires specific histone methyltransferases (eg, G9a) to silence fetal-stage cardiac genes via catalyzing repressive histone marks (eg, H3K9me2). Pathological stress frequently hijacks this regulatory mechanism: it drives the reactivation of the fetal gene program through aberrant activation of histone demethylases (eg, JMJD2A, KDM3A) or altered chromatin recruitment of methyltransferase complexes (eg, Brg1-mediated recruitment of G9a/GLP), thereby promoting the onset and progression of pathological myocardial hypertrophy. However, notable discrepancies and mechanistic complexity have also been identified across these studies. For instance, regarding the H3K9me3 modification, SUV39H1 suppresses cardiac hypertrophy by catalyzing H3K9me3 deposition, whereas PHF8 exerts cardioprotective effects via promoting H3K9me3 removal. Although the aforementioned findings have greatly advanced the field’s understanding of the regulatory mechanisms underlying pathological myocardial hypertrophy, critical knowledge gaps remain in current research. How to integrate these complex regulatory pathways with discrepant findings to identify the core epigenetic switch mediating the transition from myocardial homeostasis to pathological hypertrophy remains a key challenge to be urgently addressed in future translational research.

Myocardial Fibrosis

Myocardial fibrosis represents a fundamental pathophysiological mechanism driving the onset and progression of HF. HMTs and HDMs regulate chromatin states by modifying histone marks such as H3K27me3 and H3K9me3, thereby repressing antifibrotic genes and activating profibrotic transcriptional programs. Through these coordinated epigenetic modifications, these enzymes engage key signaling pathways to promote fibroblast activation, extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition, and progression of fibrotic remodeling.59

EZH2, which catalyzes H3K27me3, serves as a central regulator of myocardial fibrosis. During fibrotic remodeling, EZH2 suppresses antifibrotic genes such as PPAR-γ and Smad7 while increasing the expression of fibroblast activation markers, including α-SMA, Col1a1, and Col3a1 in angiotensin II (Ang-II)-induced mice.60 Moreover, the long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) ANRIL and NEAT1 recruit EZH2 to specific promoter regions, inducing transcriptional silencing and reinforcing fibrogenic responses.61,62 SUV39H1 catalyzes the trimethylation of histone H3 at lysine 9 (H3K9me3), generating transcriptionally repressive heterochromatin. In spontaneously hypertensive rats, the lncRNA MALAT1 recruits SUV39H1 to the MyoD binding region, where H3K9me3 deposition inhibits MyoD transcription. This repression promotes fibroblast proliferation and collagen synthesis, thereby accelerating the progression of myocardial fibrosis in mouse myoblast C2C12 cells.63,64 Research in MI mice reveals that DOT1L promotes myocardial fibrosis by catalyzing the methylation of H3K79 (specifically facilitating the H3K79me2 state), which increases the transcriptional activity of the spleen tyrosine kinase (SYK) promoter. The subsequent activation of the SYK/TGF-β1/Smad3 signaling pathway stimulates fibroblast proliferation and ECM accumulation, ultimately driving fibrotic remodeling and cardiac dysfunction.65,66

Lysine-specific demethylase 1 (LSD1) promotes myocardial fibrosis by removing H3K9me2 marks from the TGF-β1 promoter, thereby enhancing TGF-β signaling and facilitating the transition of fibroblasts into myofibroblasts.67,68 KDM3A binds to the promoter of tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinase 1 (TIMP1) and removes H3K9me3 modifications, leading to increased TIMP1 expression, reduced ECM degradation, and accelerated fibrotic progression.69 Similarly, KDM3C enhances TIMP1 transcription by demethylating H3K9me2 at its promoter, which promotes fibroblast activation and collagen deposition, further contributing to myocardial fibrosis.70 In angiotensin II (Ang-II)–treated fibroblasts, KDM6B removes H3K27me3 marks from the β-catenin promoter and increases β-catenin expression, thereby activating the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway and promoting ECM accumulation and fibrotic remodeling.71 DNA methyltransferase 3a (DNMT3a) and DNA methyltransferase 3b (DNMT3b) interact with heterochromatin protein 1α (HP1α) and are recruited to chromatin regions enriched with H3K9me3, where they cooperate to silence pericentromeric satellite repeats. This finding indicates that histone methylation establishes binding platforms for DNA methyltransferases, creating an integrated epigenetic framework that regulates gene expression and contributes to pathological processes such as myocardial fibrosis in human IPF lung tissue and fibroblasts and fibrotic mouse lung tissue.72 During myocardial fibrosis, the TGF-β signaling pathway plays a pivotal role. Upon ligand binding, TGF-β receptors phosphorylate downstream Smad proteins, which translocate into the nucleus and bind promoter regions of target genes to promote fibrotic gene transcription. Studies using wild-type (WT) and Sirt6−/− mouse embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs) have revealed that Sirtuin 6 (Sirt6) counteracts this process by deacetylating Smad3 and inhibiting TGF-β signaling, thereby decreasing H3K9 acetylation at the TGF-β promoter. Convergent findings from the aforementioned studies collectively demonstrate that both the deposition of repressive histone marks catalyzed by HMTs and the removal of these marks by KDMs ultimately converge on the activation of pro-fibrotic signaling pathways (eg, TGF-β/Smad, Wnt/β-catenin) or the suppression of anti-fibrotic factors, thereby forming a positive feedback loop that drives fibroblast proliferation, activation, and ECM accumulation. Although current studies have clearly delineated a multi-molecular pro-fibrotic regulatory network, critical knowledge gaps persist. Most existing studies focus predominantly on fibroblasts, yet myocardial fibrosis is essentially driven by dysregulated intercellular communication across multiple cell types, including cardiomyocytes, endothelial cells, and immune cells. Accordingly, how to integrate these epigenetic signals at the level of multicellular crosstalk and whole-organ homeostasis remains a key bottleneck urgently to be addressed in future translational research.

Cardiomyocyte Apoptosis

Cardiomyocyte apoptosis represents a major contributor to the development and progression of HF. Increasing evidence indicates that histone methylation plays a crucial regulatory role in this process. Riboflavin suppresses cardiomyocyte apoptosis by enhancing the enzymatic activity of the HDM LSD1, thereby reducing histone H3 lysine 4 dimethylation (H3K4me2) and downregulating pro-apoptotic genes such as Bnip3. Interestingly, the findings also suggest that H3K9me3 may exert an opposite pro-apoptotic effect by repressing anti-apoptotic gene expression.74 LSD1 also mitigates hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R)-induced cardiomyocyte apoptosis by decreasing H3K4me3 enrichment at the SOX9 promoter, thereby suppressing SOX9 transcription.75 In contrast, certain HMTs act as pro-apoptotic mediators. SET domain–containing lysine methyltransferase 7 (SETD7) promotes cardiomyocyte apoptosis in hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R)-induced rat cardiomyocytes by catalyzing methylation of (Autophagy Related 16 Like 1) ATG16L1 at lysine 151, disrupting its association with the ATG12–ATG5 complex, thereby impairing autophagy and promoting apoptosis. Knockdown of SETD7 via siRNA downregulates Keap1 expression, enhances Nrf2-dependent antioxidant signaling, suppresses H/R-induced apoptosis, and reduces reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation, whereas SETD7 overexpression markedly aggravates cell apoptosis and ROS accumulation.76 LSD1 can reverse this process by demethylating the same lysine residue on ATG16L1, thereby restoring autophagic flux and preventing apoptosis.77 Silencing LSD1 in JeKo-1 and MOLT-4 cells leads to increased mono- and dimethylation of H3K4, which epigenetically activates pro-apoptotic gene transcription while repressing pro-survival genes, ultimately inducing apoptosis.78 Overexpression of rat KDM3A has been reported to alleviate myocardial injury, improve cardiac function, inhibit cardiomyocyte apoptosis, and reduce ROS production and inflammatory responses in both rat ischemia/reperfusion (I/R) and cellular H/R models.79 MLL4 directly binds to the Thbs4 genomic locus and activates its transcription through H3K4me1. Loss of MLL4 decreases H3K4me1 and histone H3 lysine 27 acetylation (H3K27ac) occupancy, reduces Thbs4 expression, and exacerbates endoplasmic reticulum stress and associated stress-responsive and pro-apoptotic protein expression, thereby promoting cardiac hypertrophy and HF progression57 (Figure 4).Figure 4Histone Methylation Orchestrates Cardiomyocyte Apoptosis and the Pathogenesis of Heart Failure.

The aforementioned studies have systematically delineated the complex bifunctional regulatory patterns of histone lysine methyl-modifying enzymes in the regulation of cellular apoptosis and autophagy. On the one hand, histone demethylases represented by LSD1 can catalytically remove the transcriptionally activating histone marks H3K4me2/3 at the promoter regions of pro-apoptotic target genes such as Bnip3 and SOX9, thereby silencing the transcriptional program of pro-apoptotic genes. On the other hand, histone lysine methyltransferases such as SETD7 can disrupt the assembly and function of core autophagic complexes via site-specific methylation modification of ATG16L1; they can also exacerbate cellular oxidative stress injury by mediating the dysregulation of the Keap1/Nrf2 antioxidant signaling axis, which ultimately triggers cellular apoptosis and programmed cell death. Although these findings have greatly advanced the field’s understanding of the epigenetic regulatory network governing cellular apoptosis, critical knowledge gaps that remain to be filled still exist in the current research framework. Specifically, core modifying enzymes such as LSD1 and SETD7 have both histone and non-histone substrates, while their dominant functional targets in in vivo pathophysiological processes, as well as the specific substrate type that their biological effects depend on, have not yet been elucidated. Accordingly, how to establish a precise technical system to achieve specific discrimination and in vivo validation of the biological functions mediated by the two types of substrates remains a core scientific challenge urgently to be addressed in future research in this field.

Cardiac Energy Metabolism

Under physiological conditions, the heart generates adenosine triphosphate (ATP) primarily through fatty acid β-oxidation and glycolysis to sustain normal contraction and relaxation. In HF, cardiac energy metabolism becomes impaired, characterized by reduced fatty acid oxidation and enhanced glycolysis, leading to decreased ATP production and failing to meet the energetic demands of cardiac work. The resulting energy deficit compromises cardiomyocyte contractile and diastolic function and accelerates HF progression. Histone methylation influences cardiac energy metabolism through multiple epigenetic mechanisms, including regulation of mitochondrial function and lipid–glucose metabolic pathways, thereby contributing to HF pathogenesis. SET and MYND domain–containing protein 1 (SMYD1) enhances cardiac oxidative metabolism by catalyzing H3K4me and activating PGC-1α expression. Studies using Smyd1-KO mice demonstrate that the resulting amplification of oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) and fatty acid oxidation supports mitochondrial ATP synthesis and preserves metabolic stability.80 In contrast, overexpression of Cluh and Smyd1 in neonatal rat cardiomyocytes suppresses Msi2 expression, induces cardiac and mitochondrial dysfunction, and shifts metabolism from aerobic oxidation toward anaerobic glycolysis, aggravating energy imbalance.81 PR domain–containing 16 (PRDM16) regulates lipid metabolism and mitochondrial biogenesis through histone methylation-dependent pathways. Studies in mice with cardiac-specific deletion of Prdm16 reveal that the PR domain of PRDM16 recruits the HMT SUV39H1, which catalyzes H3K9 methylation and influences the expression of key metabolic regulators such as PPAR-α and PGC-1α. Activation of this regulatory axis restores mitochondrial function, corrects lipid metabolic disturbances, and improves overall cardiac energy metabolism.82 Cardiac ischemia activates the N6 methyladenosine (m6A) methyltransferases methyltransferase like 3 (METTL3) and methyltransferase like 14 (METTL14), resulting in a global increase in m6A RNA methylation. m6A modified transcripts are recognized by the reader protein YTH domain containing protein 1 (YTHDC1), which recruits the HDM lysine demethylase 3B (KDM3B) to chromatin regions enriched with the repressive H3K9me2 mark and promotes its removal.83 By coordinating histone demethylation and RNA stability, METTL14 modulates genes involved in mitochondrial function, including pri-miR-146a and SIRT6 mRNA, thereby improving energy metabolism in cardiomyocytes of adult C57BL/6J mice.84 In the late stages of HF, reduced METTL14 expression impairs this regulatory mechanism and exacerbates metabolic dysfunction.85

SMYD2 catalyzes the methylation of c-Myc and enhances its stability. Stabilized c-Myc binds to the GLS1 promoter and activates its transcription, thereby promoting glucose uptake.86 SETD1A interacts with HIF1α and cooperatively enhances its transcriptional activity, leading to increased expression of key glycolytic enzymes including hexokinase 2 (HK2), phosphofructokinase 2 (PFK2), and pyruvate kinase M2 (PKM2). The process elevates H3K4 methylation at their promoters, facilitates transcriptional activation, and promotes glucose metabolism.87 Inhibition of LSD1 reduces glucose uptake and disrupts metabolic equilibrium, highlighting its critical role in maintaining cardiac energy balance.88 Suppression of the HMT euchromatic histone lysine methyltransferase 2 (EHMT2) decreases H3K9me1 and H3K9me2 levels at the SREBF2 promoter, induces SREBF2 expression, and modulates cholesterol metabolism-dependent autophagy in wild-type mice of the C57BL/6 strain.89 Long-chain fatty acid transport mediated by CD36 is also regulated by histone lysine methylation. In the murine embryonic stem cell line RRR032, the intergenic region located 41 kb upstream of the CD36 promoter shows enrichment of H3K79me1 and H3K4me1 in adipocytes, a pattern that correlates with PPARγ binding and transcriptional activation.90 Overexpression of KDM5B suppresses glucose and lipid metabolism in high glucose-stimulated Schwann cells (RSC96) by directly demethylating H3K4me3 or indirectly downregulating SIRT3 expression through inhibition of the AMPK signaling pathway.91 Deletion of carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1b (Cpt1b) in adult mice disrupts fatty acid oxidation in cardiomyocytes and triggers metabolic reprogramming, resulting in the accumulation of α-ketoglutarate. The accumulated α-ketoglutarate activates KDM5, reduces H3K4me3 levels at cardiomyocyte maturation-associated genes, and restores the proliferative potential of cardiomyocytes92 (Figure 5).Figure 5Histone Methylation Orchestrates Cardiac Energy Metabolism Reprogramming and Heart Failure.

The aforementioned studies demonstrate that the synergistic regulation of histone and RNA methylation modification systems serves as the core epigenetic mechanism maintaining energy homeostasis in cardiomyocytes. Methyltransferases such as SMYD1 and PRDM16 can regulate key metabolic transcription factors including PGC-1α and PPAR-α via catalyzing histone methylation modifications, thereby preserving mitochondrial oxidative function. Meanwhile, N6-methyladenosine (m6A) RNA methylation mediated by METTL3/METTL14, and histone demethylation mediated by KDM3B, can also synergistically regulate the expression of mitochondrial function-related genes. Meanwhile, these studies have uncovered remarkable functional complexity within this regulatory system. SMYD1 maintains myocardial metabolic homeostasis by activating the PGC-1α signaling axis, whereas its aberrant overexpression synergizes with Cluh to exacerbate abnormal glycolysis activation and energy imbalance. This indicates that the same regulatory molecule exerts dose-dependent and interacting complex-dependent bidirectional functional effects. Although these findings have greatly advanced the field’s understanding of the epigenetic regulatory mechanisms underlying myocardial metabolic reprogramming in heart failure (HF), critical knowledge gaps remain. Specifically, the core epigenetic switch that drives the pathological shift of myocardial energy metabolism from fatty acid oxidation-dominant to glycolysis-dominant during HF progression, along with its key upstream driver molecules, have not yet been systematically elucidated.

Inflammatory Response

The inflammatory response is a central pathological mechanism driving myocardial remodeling and deterioration of cardiac function in HF.93 Damaged cardiomyocytes release inflammatory mediators that recruit and activate immune cells, leading to the production of proinflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-1β. These cytokines impair cardiomyocyte contractility and stimulate fibroblast activation, leading to collagen deposition and the progression of myocardial fibrosis.94 Histone methylation regulates the inflammatory response through multiple mechanisms that influence HF progression. One regulatory pathway involves modulation of inflammation-related gene expression in cardiomyocytes, whereas another involves bidirectional interactions with inflammatory signaling pathways that shape cardiac remodeling.

During the progression of HF, histone methylation serves as a key regulatory mechanism controlling the transcription of inflammation-related genes. H3K4me3, an activating epigenetic mark, is frequently enriched at promoter regions of inflammatory genes and markedly enhances their transcriptional activity. In macrophages, H3K4me3 enrichment at lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced gene promoters has been strongly associated with modulation of inflammatory signaling.95 P65 is the key transcription factor that drives inflammatory gene expression in the NF-κB signaling pathway. The H3K4 methyltransferase SET7/9 promotes recruitment of p65 and upregulates TNF-α–responsive genes, thereby amplifying inflammatory responses.96 In contrast, protein arginine methyltransferase 1 (PRMT1) attenuates inflammation by repressing the transcription of proinflammatory genes. In HEK 293T cells, PRMT1 interacts with the nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) subunit RelA/p65 and methylates it at arginine 30, which reduces TNF-α-induced NF-κB activation.97 In T cell-specific PRMT5 conditional knockout mice, PRMT5 promotes TNF-related apoptosis-inducing ligand (TRAIL)-induced activation of IKK and NF-κB, resulting in the transcriptional induction of multiple NF-κB target genes.98

The NF-κB signaling pathway serves as a central regulator of inflammatory responses and is markedly activated during HF, leading to elevated expression and secretion of proinflammatory cytokines.99 Histone methylation is closely linked to NF-κB signaling regulation. PRMT5 modulates NF-κB activation via multiple membrane-associated receptors that stimulate the IKK complex. Suppression of PRMT5-dependent methylation reduces activation of IKKβ and IKKα and inhibits nuclear translocation of the NF-κB subunit RelA/p65.100 Mixed-lineage leukemia 1 (MLL1) catalyzes H3K4me3 modification at the NOX4 promoter in cooperation with NF-κB p65 in peripheral blood mononuclear cells, thereby promoting aberrant activation of inflammatory and oxidative stress pathways. Silencing of MLL1 markedly decreases transcription of these genes, thereby attenuating inflammation and oxidative injury.101 Nuclear receptor–binding SET domain protein 2 (NSD2) exerts a protective role in mice by catalyzing H3K36me2 modification at the promoter and 5′-UTR of the Nfkbia gene, which encodes IκBα. Elevated IκBα inhibits p65 phosphorylation and nuclear translocation, providing negative feedback control over NF-κB signaling102 (Figure 6).Figure 6Histone Methylation Orchestrates the Cardiac Inflammatory Response and Heart Failure.

In the pathogenesis and progression of heart failure (HF), derangements in energy metabolism and inflammatory responses are not isolated pathophysiological processes. Histone methylation modifications can synergistically regulate metabolic reprogramming and inflammatory responses by modulating the shared molecular nodes of the two processes, thereby participating in the pathophysiological regulation of HF. In metabolic regulation, PRDM16 recruits SUV39H1 to catalyze histone H3K9 methylation, which modulates the expression and function of PPAR-α and PGC-1α so as to ameliorate myocardial lipid metabolism and mitochondrial function,82 SETD1A, in turn, cooperates with HIF1α to enhance histone H3K4 methylation and downregulates the expression of key glycolytic genes, thus promoting glucose metabolism.87 In inflammatory regulation, methyltransferases such as PRMT5, MLL1 and NSD2 positively or negatively regulate the NF-κB signaling pathway via catalyzing distinct histone methylation modifications, thereby mediating the activation or attenuation of inflammatory and oxidative stress responses.101 The core of the coordinated regulation of inflammation and metabolism by histone methylation lies in the modulation of key common nodes in their signaling pathways, among which PGC-1α-NF-κB/p65 serves as the central regulatory hub. Histone methylation modifications can directly regulate the expression and function of PGC-1α; as a key regulatory protein for mitochondrial biogenesis and lipid metabolism, PGC-1α can abrogate the pro-inflammatory transcriptional activity of NF-κB/p65 through physical interaction with it, thus exerting an inhibitory effect on inflammatory responses.103 Meanwhile, the regulation of the NF-κB signaling pathway by histone methylation alters the phosphorylation and nuclear translocation of p65,97 and further modulates the pattern of its interaction with PGC-1α.104 When the level of p65 is elevated under the action of inflammation-related cytokines, it forms a stable complex with PGC-1α and induces the inactivation of the latter, which not only impairs the metabolic regulatory function of PGC-1α but also relieves its inhibitory effect on NF-κB. Histone methylation, however, can synchronously regulate metabolic processes and the intensity of inflammatory responses by modulating the balance of this interaction.105 HIF-1α represents another important common regulatory node: this factor is not only involved in SETD1A-mediated glucose metabolism regulation but also acts as a target of succinate, a metabolic intermediate. Under the pseudohypoxic effect of succinate, HIF-1α is stabilized and activated, thereby mediating the production of LPS-induced inflammatory cytokine IL-1β.106 Through the regulation of HIF-1α activation, histone methylation can simultaneously affect glucose metabolism and the expression of inflammatory cytokines, thus achieving the coupled regulation of metabolic and inflammatory pathways.87 In addition, histone methylation can indirectly influence the production and function of metabolic intermediates such as succinate by modulating the activity of histone demethylases and related histone modifications. As a signaling molecule linking metabolism and inflammation, changes in succinate levels can retrogradely regulate the HIF-1α-mediated inflammatory pathway.107,108 By modulating this signaling axis, histone methylation further realizes the systematic and coordinated regulation of inflammatory responses and cellular metabolism, thus emerging as a key epigenetic mechanism that connects and balances inflammation and metabolism (Figure 7).Figure 7Histone Methylation Orchestrates the Metabolic-Inflammatory Axis in Heart Failure.

Therapeutic Prospects of Targeting Histone Methylation

Histone methylation functions as a key epigenetic mechanism that regulates cardiomyocyte gene expression, maintains cardiac homeostasis, and drives pathological remodeling, including cardiac hypertrophy and fibrosis.69 Targeted modulation of histone methylation, such as selective inhibition of pathogenic HMTs or regulation of specific HDMs, has emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy for HF.109 Correction of aberrant histone methylation patterns may restore cardiomyocyte function, enhance myocardial repair and regeneration, and slow the progression of HF through epigenetic reprogramming.

HMT Inhibitors

Studies have demonstrated that HMT inhibitors improve cardiac function, suppress cardiac hypertrophy and fibrosis, delay HF progression, and modulate mitochondrial activity. Significant advances have been achieved in the development of inhibitors targeting DOT1L, EZH2, PRMT5, and PRMT1, with several drug-like molecules advancing into clinical trials. However, most of these studies focus on cancer therapy, whereas research on cardiovascular applications remains limited to preclinical animal models and lacks validation from large-scale clinical studies.110

EPZ5676, a selective inhibitor of DOT1L, suppresses DOT1L-mediated H3K79 methylation and reduces transcriptional activation of the downstream target FoxO3a, thereby inhibiting cardiac fibroblast activation and ECM deposition. This inhibition alleviates myocardial fibrosis and improves cardiac structure and function.111 Reduction of DOT1L activity also decreases H3K79me2 at the SYK promoter, thereby reducing SYK transcription and protein expression. The subsequent downregulation of SYK weakens TGF-β1/Smad3 signaling, suppressing fibroblast proliferation, migration, and the expression of fibrotic markers, including collagen I (COL1A1) and α-SMA. Both in vitro and in vivo studies have demonstrated that DOT1L inhibition attenuates fibroblast activation and myocardial fibrosis, thereby improving cardiac function.66 BIX-01294 inhibits G9a, which mediates H3K9 dimethylation to repress cardiomyocyte-specific gene expression and interacts with EZH2 and MEF2C to maintain cardiomyocyte homeostasis. In transverse aortic constriction (TAC) mice, BIX-01294 improves cardiac function, prevents cardiac hypertrophy, and promotes the expansion of adult cardiac progenitor cells without altering their phenotype or differentiation potential.112 BRD4770, a structural analog of BIX-01338, functions as an S-adenosylmethionine (SAM)-competitive inhibitor and selectively suppresses the activity of euchromatic histone methyltransferase 2 (EHMT2, also known as G9a). By reducing H3K9 di- and trimethylation, BRD4770 protects cardiomyocytes from doxorubicin (DOX)-induced apoptosis and ferroptosis via activation of the Nrf2/ATF4-SLC7A11 signaling pathway.113 Chaetocin inhibits H3K9 methyltransferase activity and reverses abnormal accumulation of H3K9me3 at repetitive genomic elements, including mitochondrial genes such as Pgc1α and Acadm, in HF. Reversal of this modification reactivates gene transcription, restores mitochondrial biogenesis and respiration, reduces myocardial fibrosis, and enhances ventricular contractility, thereby slowing disease progression and prolonging survival in animal models.114 The EZH2 inhibitor EPZ005687 suppresses pulmonary hypertension secondary to left HF through an EZH2-SOD1-ROS–dependent mechanism. EPZ005687 lowers right ventricular systolic pressure, decreases right ventricular and lung weight–to–body weight ratios, reduces pulmonary oxidative stress, downregulates EZH2 expression, restores SOD1 levels, and reverses the accumulation of EZH2 and H3K27me3 at the SOD1 promoter.115

Among the aforementioned small molecules targeting histone methylation modification for cardiovascular disease intervention, EPZ5676 (a DOT1L inhibitor) has entered human clinical trials, with well-established evidence from both in vitro and in vivo studies and preliminary elucidation of its underlying mechanism. It inhibits cardiac fibroblast activation and extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition, thereby attenuating myocardial fibrosis and improving cardiac structure and function. In contrast, BIX-01294, BRD4770, Chaetocin, and EPZ005687 are all in the preclinical research stage, with pharmacodynamic validation completed only at the cellular and animal levels. These agents target G9a, H3K9 methyltransferase, and EZH2 respectively, and ameliorate cardiac hypertrophy, chemotherapy-induced cardiomyocyte apoptosis and ferroptosis, mitochondrial dysfunction, and pulmonary hypertension secondary to left heart failure via regulation of H3K9 or H3K27 methylation modification. Collectively, these compounds have validated that histone methylation modification is a key target for the intervention of myocardial fibrosis, ventricular remodeling, and heart failure, providing novel strategies and candidate drug directions with translational potential for heart failure and related cardiovascular complications.

HDM Inhibitors

HDMs play essential roles in cardiac development and disease progression. Numerous studies have demonstrated that selective inhibition of HDMs can regulate the expression of genes associated with myocardial remodeling, metabolism, and fibrosis, thereby modulating the pathological progression of heart disease. JIB-04, an HDM inhibitor, effectively inhibits KDM3A activity and reduces the expression of fibrosis-associated proteins, resulting in marked attenuation of pressure overload–induced left ventricular hypertrophy and myocardial fibrosis.116 JIB-04 also inhibits JMJD1C and significantly reduces angiotensin II (Ang II)–induced cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and the upregulation of hypertrophic and fibrotic markers.117 AS8351, a selective inhibitor of KDM5B, promotes the formation of cardiomyocyte-like cells by maintaining active chromatin states and enhancing transcription of cardiac-specific genes, thereby supporting the induction and stabilization of cardiomyocyte-like phenotypes and offering a potential strategy for myocardial regeneration.118 GSK-J4 inhibits JMJD3, an H3K27 demethylase that facilitates cardiomyocyte hypertrophy when overexpressed. In rats, experimental evidence shows that GSK-J4 suppresses isoproterenol (ISO)–induced cardiac hypertrophy by regulating key hypertrophic genes and alleviating pathological remodeling.119 GSK-LSD1 inhibits LSD1, an H3K4 demethylase, prevents cardiac fibrosis and ventricular dilation, and confers protection against HF. The protective effects involve modulation of gene expression pathways, including Notch1.120 Tranylcypromine (TC), a selective LSD1 inhibitor, blocks LSD1-mediated H3K4 demethylation and activates metabolic target genes such as PGC-1α and PDK4 in mice. The resulting elevation in H3K4 di- and trimethylation enhances mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS), increases energy utilization efficiency, and improves overall cellular energy metabolism.121 The therapeutic agents targeting histone methylation in heart failure are summarized in Table 2. Among the aforementioned small-molecule inhibitors targeting HDMs, the broad-spectrum HDM inhibitor JIB-04 has well-established cardioprotective efficacy cross-validated by two independent in vitro and in vivo experimental studies. This compound exerts stable anti-myocardial remodeling and anti-fibrotic effects in multiple pathological models via targeted inhibition of the biological activity of KDM3A, with its action targets and downstream regulatory pathways clearly elucidated. TC is a clinically approved drug authorized by regulatory authorities, with comprehensive human safety evaluation data and pharmacokinetic profiles accumulated; its cardioprotective effects have been fully validated in in vivo animal experiments. This agent blocks LSD1-mediated demethylation of H3K4, thereby upregulating the level of OXPHOS and improving the energy metabolic homeostasis of cardiomyocytes. In contrast, inhibitors including AS8351, GSK-J4, and GSK-LSD1 are currently in the early exploratory stage of clinical development. Their cardioprotective efficacy and underlying mechanisms have only been preliminarily elucidated, with relevant studies remaining limited to validation in a single pathological model or a single signaling pathway.

Clinical Application Limitations of Targeted Histone Methylation Therapy for Heart Failure

Although pharmacological targeting of histone methylation holds substantial promise for the clinical translation of novel heart failure therapies, several fundamental scientific challenges must be resolved before this strategy can be successfully translated from bench to bedside. First, the optimal timing of therapeutic intervention remains a critical unresolved issue. Most preclinical studies to date have focused on interventions delivered during the early stages of heart failure; however, the vast majority of patients are already in the chronic phase of the disease at the time of diagnosis, when a stable, pathological methylation landscape has been established in the myocardium. To date, insufficient evidence exists to confirm whether targeted intervention during the chronic disease phase can fully reverse the established pathological phenotype of the failing heart. Furthermore, the molecular mechanisms governing the maintenance of these pathological methylation marks, along with the consequent epigenetic memory effect, are key determinants of the long-term therapeutic efficacy of such interventions.122 Second, cardiac cellular heterogeneity imposes substantial limitations on translational progress. The majority of existing studies have employed bulk sequencing technologies, which are unable to resolve cell type-specific differences in methylation landscapes across distinct cardiac cell subpopulations, nor can they delineate the cell type-specific functional effects of targeted interventions within these subpopulations. This critical limitation may mask substantial off-target risks, thereby presenting a major bottleneck for safe and effective clinical translation.123 Third, notable pharmacodynamic and pharmacokinetic challenges remain for targeted small-molecule therapeutics. The nuclear localization of the molecular targets of these agents imposes stringent requirements on the nuclear penetration capacity of candidate drugs.124 Currently available inhibitors generally lack myocardial tissue specificity, rendering them susceptible to systemic off-target toxicities. This challenge is further exacerbated by microcirculatory dysfunction in the failing heart, which markedly reduces drug exposure at the target site; together, these issues severely compromise the therapeutic efficacy and long-term safety profile of these agents.

Compendium of Preclinical Research Data on Histone Methylation-Targeted Candidate Drugs for Heart Failure Therapy

Conclusion

Histone methylation is a dynamically regulated epigenetic mechanism in HF, governing cardiomyocyte regeneration, fibroblast activation, metabolic reprogramming, and inflammatory signaling. Preclinical studies demonstrate that targeting histone methyltransferases and demethylases exerts cardioprotective effects—including anti-hypertrophy, anti-fibrosis, and metabolic restoration—positioning this axis as a promising therapeutic avenue. However, clinical translation remains nascent, hampered by incomplete understanding of cell-type-specific regulatory networks in human HF, complex cross-talk with other epigenetic modifications, a lack of cardiac-selective modulators and validated biomarkers, and over-reliance on rodent data with limited large-animal or human validation. Future priorities include defining cell-type-specific methylation landscapes in failing human hearts, dissecting cross-regulatory interactions with metabolic and signaling pathways, developing cardiac-targeted compounds with optimized pharmacokinetics, validating robust biomarkers for patient stratification, and advancing translational studies in large-animal models and early-phase trials to bridge the preclinical–clinical gap.

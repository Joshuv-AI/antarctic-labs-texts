AI enhanced optimization of college physical education programs using hybrid genetic algorithms and learning based fitness evaluation
Authors: Xinlong Wang, Wenze Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractStructured physical education (PE) programs are essential for fostering students’ physical development, cognitive performance, and emotional well-being in academic settings. This study introduces a novel intelligent decision-making algorithm (IDA) for the dynamic optimization and real-time assessment of college PE programs. The proposed framework synergistically integrates a hybrid genetic algorithm (GA) that is viable global optimizer with pattern search (PS) that is an adaptive local search technique. The exploration in the search domain is performed with GA while PS exploitation in a minimal computational budget is performed by the PS. The system encodes program performance data into chromosome-like representations, enabling nuanced evaluation across academic progress, health indices, and affective-psychomotor domains. A tailored vector of weight factors refines the fitness function to reflect individual learning trajectories and institutional goals. Experimental results demonstrate that the model achieves a high prediction accuracy of 98%, with quantifiable improvements of 151.13% in holistic performance, 19.63% in educational metrics, and 26.7% in psychomotor development as compared with reported results. Comparative models, including Random Forest (RF), Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS), and RF regression, that achieved accuracies of 88.21%, 94.49%, and 82%, respectively. The hybrid framework maintained a mean global fitness value of 5.3451 × 10⁻¹² with an average computational time of 1357.04 s over 100 runs that is used to validate the reliability of the proposed framework. By enhancing efficiency and reducing computational complexity, this AI-driven evaluation model offers a scalable and intelligent approach for real-time optimization and policy refinement in higher education PE curriculum planning.Keywords: College physical education programs, Evolutionary computation, Pattern search technique, Intelligent hybrid optimization, Monte Carlo simulationsSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In the previous decade, computing technology has changed college students’ academic performance, morals, and social obligations. Effective physical education training (PET) for college students is necessary to promote academic achievement, health, and social and moral responsibility. Recent trends in physical education indicate a shift from a focus solely on physical fitness to a more holistic approach that emphasizes comprehensive student development. More schools want to upgrade their PE programs. Institutions must find better ways to evaluate training and student performance. Intelligent Decision Algorithms (IDAs) will improve decision-making using data and weighted parameters. This study implements an intelligent algorithm to analyze and optimize the college PET, which affects student learning, performance, and well-being on standard parameters1.

In recent years, data-driven education research has grown in prominence. Most qualitative and subjective assessments in the classic evaluation paradigm produce contradictory and skewed results on an enhanced program. IDAs can be added to the evaluation framework to help educators and administrators quantify program effectiveness2. This strategy improves evaluation methods and helps show how different situations effect pupils in physical education. Physical education aims include curriculum disruption, instructor quality, and student engagement3. With diverse approaches, IDAs can identify correlations and trends in big data sets that thematic assessment cannot. For instance, comparing student demographics to their achievements will reveal trends of physical education class engagement. It can also help ensure that educational programs meet student needs, improving school environments4. The educational foundation of this study aligns with Bloom’s Taxonomy, which outlines three essential domains of learning: cognitive (knowledge and intellectual skills), affective (attitudes and emotions), and psychomotor (physical skills). Our performance indicators are educational performance (EP), affective domain (AD), and psychomotor domain (PD) directly correspond to these dimensions. Moreover, the adaptive nature of the proposed IDA framework is informed by Constructivist Learning Theory, which advocates personalized and experiential learning based on individual performance data. This theoretical grounding supports the integration of intelligent decision-making algorithms into educational design and evaluation.

IDAs go beyond assessment since they can improve physical education programs. In this approach, institutions can collect data year-after-year and evaluate curricular innovations and teaching methods on pupils. This method helps identify best practices that should be repeated and areas that require improvement to ensure physical education programs suit students’ needs5. Integration of IDAs can assist meet calls for cardinal and evidence-based approaches to college student engagement and aerobic activity. In conclusion, using IDAs to evaluate college physical education programs will improve student performance and training efficacy. Mass data analysis helps educators evaluate student involvement and performance to make better judgments. This project will hybridize two artificial intelligence-based methodologies to create an intelligent decision algorithm (IDA). Genetic algorithm (GA) is a global optimizer, and pattern search (PS) uses intelligent local search to optimize student dataset fitness function weight vectors.

Related work and state of art

Recent developments in educational and AI-based optimization research have revealed the prophetic prospect of hybrid models and intelligent systems while improving different perspectives in academic and personality development. A case in point is a study that demonstrated the role of perceived organizational support in predicting career adaptability among university students, suggesting that adjustment of educational environments should be made for better results9. Another set of state-of-the-art deep learning models, TASTA and ResLNet have also been presented by combining both spatial and temporal attention mechanisms for better video Q&A and AR performance, demonstrating great flexibility of AI in distrusting sophisticated educational tasks10,11. Such hybrid AI models are now more often exploited to build adaptive learning environment, such as those in automatic detection of social support in online forums for reduced burnout during learning by enhanced self-regulation12. Furthermore, decision-making process optimization models highlight the use of AI in increasing efficiency and accuracy in a number of industries such as education, product design etc13,14. A number of studies have examined the beneficial effects of PE in academic and social development of students. For example, it has been reported that exercise is directly related to academic achievement and health and the importance of adding a physical education as a daily aspect of academics15. Vast research also reveal that daily physical activity is essential for superior cognitive functions and mental health that inevitably leads to increased academic focus and achievement16. Also, team play and cooperation games encourage socialemotional development with this essential for the student maturity17. These results are consistent with previous investigations on hybrid deep learning models in order to enhance skin lesion segmentation, confirming the promising role of AI for fine-tuning personalized educational and health-intervention approaches18. In addition, research has also added to the growing understanding by investigating AI-driven models in health and precision prediction that can be adopted for real-time optimization in education19,20. Furthermore, predictive models for disease detection and skin lesion analysis represent a model for intelligent framework development that can adapt or actively optimize PE programs as the individual leans and info on health improves21,22. Through the application of AI models to education, it is possible for institutions to establish personalized learning environments that enhance academic outcomes, as well as safeguarding students’ mental and physical health.

Many scholars have examined the effects of physical education on students’ activities, class behavior, test scores, communication skills, and ethics23. Trudeau et al.24 found a link between school and college physical activities and student health and academic success. The study also supports quasi-experimental data that PET should be done a few hours a day. Donnelly & Lambourne25 examined the effects of physical activity on student achievement and found that students who engaged in this type of physical activity had better records and better mental health, including better focus and memory. These results show that PET is not just an extracurricular activity but a key part of child education that improves cognitive and academic skills through active engagement. McKenzie et al.26 stated that team sports and cooperative activities improve peer belonging, interpersonal relations, social-emotional development, and life skills. It also highlights how cooperative learning activities foster social-emotional growth, improved mental and physical health. This suggests that physical education promotes a healthy lifestyle, which is essential for student development.

However, the primary issue in the aforementioned references lies in the intelligent balancing of these activities, as no formal frameworks or architectures are evident for a specific set of datasets. This limitation is attributed to traditional data analysis tools, which do not accommodate nonlinear constraints optimization for large volumes of data and their adaptive variables. The complexity of the procedures and the multitude of variables involved render AI a feasible solution for accelerating and improving the precision of PET evaluations. Modern machine learning (ML) techniques offer promising avenues to enhance student outcomes by personalizing physical education programs to better align with individual learning needs. Initial studies aimed to employ machine learning algorithms to conduct a detailed analysis of student-related data, such as the frequency of physical activities and other relevant metrics, with the objective of predicting student performance27. The researchers employed regression models and classification algorithms to assess the impact of factors such as attendance and participation on student fitness gains. The initial applications demonstrated that prioritizing data analysis could enhance instructional processes, resulting in improved physical education curricula tailored to each student’s potential28. The application of machine learning techniques in monitoring and evaluating student performance facilitates the improvement of training models used in physical education. Research indicates that machine learning can analyze data from wearable fitness trackers to monitor students’ performance metrics in real-time, including heart rate, activity level, and skill competence, thereby providing immediate feedback to both students and instructors29. This real-time data analysis engaged students by showcasing improvements and provided trainers the capability to adjust training schedules accordingly. Initial applications of machine learning in physical education established a basis for enhancing training frameworks that are empirically supported to improve student performance and engagement in physical education activities30–32. The advancement of multiple machine learning techniques, including support vector machines (SVM), k-nearest neighbors (KNN), naive Bayes (NB) algorithm, Q-learning epileptic seizure classifier, JAYA-optimized fuzzy reinforcement learning classifier, probabilistic neural networks (PNN), fuzzy lattices and Neural Reinforcement Learning, and random forests (RF), has demonstrated their relevance across diverse fields such as business, economics, and social sciences33–36.

In 2021, Qingxiang Xu et al.6 utilized an enhanced random forest (RF) method to enhance student performance by optimizing PET hours and empirical teaching through the dimensional reduction of students’ multidisciplinary achievements in physical education. The RF technique achieved an accuracy of 88.55% and a precision of 88.21%. While the study in20 addresses several critical aspects of physical education quality, it fails to consider significant parameters such as daily mobile phone usage, age, health ratings, student attendance records, and participation in various events. In 2023, Mojsilović et al. developed a combination of the RF algorithm and adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system (ANFIS) to optimize key mutually independent factors critical to student success, providing guidelines for teachers to enhance their teaching outcomes7. The proposed model demonstrates a reliability of 94.49%. However, a significant limitation is that the implementation of RF-ANFIS necessitates a pre-examination of the data and input-output patterns, which may introduce bias. In a recent study8, Doz et al. propose a framework utilizing hybrid learning, which integrates fuzzy logic with the RF regression method to examine the impact of demographic factors on college students’ grades. The study emphasizes that the relative impact of various student parameters, such as gender and origin, is less significant on student performance compared to factors like school type, type of physical education training, time spent in physical education, self-study hours, age, and average daily mobile usage duration. This article discusses the transformation of weighted values of various qualitative factors related to PET for school and college students into chromosomes. These chromosomes are subsequently fused using the proposed IDA technique to enhance PET performance in terms of educational score, health indicator, cognitive domain, affective domain, and psychomotor domain. The dataset utilized in this study comprises an additive combination of data from Kaggle37 and a private dataset involving 260 college-level students. The results of the proposed scheme are compared with the RF algorithm, SVM, NB techniques, and other state-of-the-art methods reported in the literature survey across various performance indicators. The proposed framework’s reliability, stability, and computational complexity in terms of time are validated through 100 independent runs.

The citations referenced in this section were collected and annotated using a structured literature review methodology. Searches were conducted across Scopus, IEEE Xplore, and Google Scholar using combinations of keywords such as “physical education,” “machine learning,” “genetic algorithms,” “ANFIS,” and “student performance evaluation.” The identified literature was filtered based on relevance, recency, and technical depth. Each selected article was annotated and thematically categorized into three domains: traditional evaluation techniques, ML-based frameworks for educational improvement, and hybrid AI optimization models. This annotation helped distill core trends, identify performance gaps in existing methods, and justify the novelty and relevance of the proposed IDA model. Unlike prior hybrid models such as RF-ANFIS which combine rule-based fuzzy inference with ensemble learning to enhance predictive accuracy, the proposed hybrid GA-PS introduces a fundamentally different optimization philosophy. Instead of relying on pre-defined fuzzy rules or tree-based structures, our method performs direct continuous optimization of weight vectors using a two-stage search mechanism. GA provides extensive global exploration across the high-dimensional weight space, while PS performs deterministic, derivative-free local refinement to escape shallow local minima and improve convergence stability. This combination creates an adaptive and fully data-driven optimization engine that does not require handcrafted rule sets, prior assumption of input-output relations, or pre-structured model components. To the best of our knowledge, such a GA-initialized pattern search hybrid has not been previously applied in the context of multi-domain educational or physical performance assessment.

In addition to ML-based frameworks, some educational decision systems rely on rule-based or lexicon-based evaluation mechanisms, where student responses are scored using predefined rubrics or weight tables developed by experts. While such methods offer interpretability, they are often static and lack adaptability to student diversity and contextual variation. Moreover, these systems are less effective in capturing interactions among multi-dimensional quantitative data. As such, they are not directly suitable for optimizing performance-driven fitness functions like the one proposed in our IDA framework. The dataset is transformed into an individual, referred to as a chromosome, with a length corresponding to the number of parameters in the dataset. The decoded chromosome undergoes processing through the designed IDA scheme, which integrates the hybridization of genetic algorithms and an effective local search technique from pattern search. The widely distributed population is initialized by taking into account the lower and upper bounds in the dataset, which facilitates randomization. Consequently, the IDA scheme will be sufficiently capable of identifying the optimal weight from all areas of the search space. The results of the genetic algorithm are integrated with pattern search for future tuning. Once the optimal chromosome is identified, the procedure is repeated for 100 independent runs to conduct basic statistical analyses, ensuring the reliability and stability of the proposed approach.

Dataset details

The public dataset comprises 81 male and 19 female students sourced from Kaggle37. Additionally, to evaluate the efficacy and insights of the PET program, a private dataset from a college containing 260 students is utilized, consisting of 60 females and 200 males. The Kaggle dataset contains 18 attributes, while the private dataset comprises 12 attributes. A total of 20 attributes have been analyzed to assess the impact of PETs on individual education and health outcomes. The primary characteristics of these 20 attributes include gender, daily mobile phone usage, health risks, beneficial subjects, cardiovascular scale, health rating, attendance record, number of events participated in by individual students, weekly student activities, academic profiles encompassing EP, CD, AD, PD, and skill proficiency scores, instructor effectiveness, and student satisfaction, among others.

All 20 features from the combined dataset were analyzed since they directly affect physical education evaluation criteria like academic achievement, physical health, cognitive engagement, and program satisfaction. The datasets were preprocessed to remove missing entries, standardize categorical labels, and normalize numerical values using min-max scaling to assure fitness function homogeneity before optimization. We avoided explicit dimensionality reduction methods like PCA and LDA to retain feature interpretability and contribution. Instead, the suggested IDA framework dynamically adjusts attribute weights during optimization. Lower weights for less relevant features reduce their impact on the outcome and serve as an implicit feature selection mechanism in the model’s learning process.

To promote reproducibility while adhering to data privacy constraints, an anonymized version of the private dataset containing representative samples has been prepared. This sample retains the original data structure, feature definitions, and statistical characteristics necessary for model evaluation and is available upon request.

For interpretability, we categorized the outcome of student performance improvements into three categories: positive (greater than 10% improvement), neutral (0–10% improvement), and negative (decline in score). Based on this classification across the full dataset (including both academic and health indicators), approximately 78% of students showed positive improvement, 16% fell into the neutral range, and 6% experienced a decline in at least one evaluated domain. These categories were not used for training but were derived post hoc to provide a qualitative interpretation of the model’s effect.

While most of the dataset variables were derived from structured records and quantitative assessments, certain subjective indicators such as instructor effectiveness and student satisfaction were obtained through standardized feedback surveys. To ensure labeling quality, a dual-annotation process was followed in which two independent annotators evaluated each subjective entry. Discrepancies were resolved through discussion, guided by a third expert reviewer. Inter-annotator agreement was assessed using Cohen’s Kappa statistic, with agreement scores exceeding 0.82 across all subjective fields, indicating high annotation reliability.

Most dataset variables were directly obtained from academic and health records, eliminating the need for manual annotation. However, subjective attributes such as ‘instructor effectiveness’ and ‘student satisfaction’ were derived from feedback surveys. These responses were annotated independently by two reviewers using a predefined rubric, and discrepancies were resolved by domain experts. Cohen’s Kappa statistic was used to assess inter-annotator agreement, yielding scores above 0.82, reflecting substantial annotation consistency.

Before applying the IDA framework, all numerical features in the dataset were normalized using min-max normalization to scale values into the [0, 1] range. This normalization was performed independently for each training fold to prevent data leakage. Categorical variables like gender, beneficial subjects were first label-encoded into integer values before normalization. This preprocessing step ensured that features with different ranges and magnitudes contributed equitably during optimization and model training.

Proposed IDA

The proposed IDA framework exploit hybrid version of GA and PS algorithms for training of design parameters, i.e., weight factor . The generic flow diagrams are provided in Fig. 1 while the pseudo developed is given below.

PS is a black-box search method that operates without the need for gradients. The algorithm is based on the theory of positive bases and is most effective in d-dimensional spaces39. This approach involves exploring the solution space by evaluating a series of points that represent trial solutions in relation to Eq. 1. The process transitions from a current point to other localized points by employing specified step sizes, which are adjusted based on the outcomes of the evaluated points. This process persists until a specified convergence criterion is achieved, such as the maximum allowable iterations or the desired improvement value40.

This study used the PS algorithm as the local optimizer in our hybrid IDA framework because it works well in derivative-free optimization settings with complex, non-convex, and noisy fitness landscapes. PS may efficiently explore the local neighborhood of the solution space without analytical gradients, unlike gradient-based approaches. In low-dimensional environments like our 20-parameter weight vector, PS requires fewer hyperparameter modifications and is less prone to premature convergence than population-based heuristic PSO. Bayesian Optimization is good for low-dimensional, expensive function evaluations, but its probabilistic surrogate models may not scale well across 100 simulation runs or dynamic fitness landscapes. Our ablation results show that PS’s deterministic and computationally consistent search method complements GA’s exploratory nature, resulting in faster local convergence and improved fitness evaluation stability. PS was chosen for hybrid optimization because it is balanced, computationally efficient, and robust. The PS utilized in this study incorporates input from one of the optimal outcomes of GA, a global optimizer, and subsequently conducts a local exploration of the best-optimized parameters to enhance fitness, convergence rate, and stability41,42.

GA, introduced by Holland in 1975, are part of the evolutionary computation family and are inspired by natural evolutionary processes. The optimization fitness of the global optimizer relies on the diversity of the initial population and the reproduction mechanism. The boundary of the search space in the genetic algorithm is regulated using a log-sigmoidal function, and the optimal solution is achieved by appropriately selecting the fittest chromosomes in each generation. The bounded genetic algorithm (GA) has been utilized on the fitness function, considering the established advantages of GA, including its ability to prevent premature local convergence, maintain search diversity, and ensure computational efficiency. It has been utilized for parametric optimization43 and integrated with artificial neural network modeling across various linear and nonlinear systems in social sciences, engineering, and other fields31,44,45. To avoid data leakage, all hyperparameter tuning was conducted strictly within each training fold of the cross-validation process. For every iteration, the training subset was internally used for grid search, while the corresponding validation fold remained completely unseen during tuning. The final evaluation was carried out exclusively on the held-out test fold, ensuring that no information from the test data influenced model selection or optimization. To ensure robust evaluation, a stratified 5-fold cross-validation scheme was employed. The dataset was randomly partitioned into five equal-sized folds, maintaining the statistical distribution of target variables across each subset. For every fold, the model was trained on four parts and tested on the remaining one. This process was repeated five times so that each subset was used as a test set once. The reported results such as accuracy, performance scores, and computational time are averaged over these five runs. This consistent evaluation protocol was applied across all models including IDA, Random Forest, ANFIS, and SVM to ensure unbiased and reliable comparison.

To ensure robust model evaluation, a stratified 10-fold cross-validation strategy was employed. The dataset was split into 10 equally sized folds, preserving class distributions. For each iteration, the model was trained on 90% of the data (9-folds) and tested on the remaining 10% (1-fold). This process was repeated 10 times so that every sample served as a test instance exactly once. Final results were computed by averaging performance across all folds. No additional holdout set was used.

Model adoptability and deployment in diverse educational contexts

The practical utility of the proposed IDA extends beyond the confines of a single institutional or cultural context. Although the present study was conducted using data from one educational institution, the framework was intentionally designed to be modular, data-driven, and generalizable to support deployment across diverse academic, demographic, and cultural settings.

Academic versatility

The IDA can be customized to align with varying curricular goals, including cognitive, psychomotor, and affective learning objectives defined by national education standards or institutional strategies. Performance indicators used in the fitness function such as academic progress, health metrics, or behavioral engagement can be adjusted based on the local assessment structure or educational stage. The underlying weight optimization mechanism dynamically adapts to these indicators, making the model applicable in both standardized and competency-based education systems.

Demographic adaptability

The model can accommodate demographic diversity by integrating personalized features such as age, gender, socioeconomic status, and digital access. For instance, in contexts where mobile phone usage or attendance patterns significantly vary by demographic segment, the model adjusts weight distributions through evolutionary optimization, ensuring equitable evaluation. Furthermore, the stratified cross-validation protocol ensures that subgroup representation is preserved during model training and evaluation.

Cultural and regional transferability

Cultural relevance is preserved by allowing local educators to define or prioritize custom performance metrics reflecting community values. In international or multilingual settings, subjective inputs such as student satisfaction or instructor feedback can be collected using localized instruments and interpreted through culturally responsive rubrics. The empirical weight learning mechanism ensures that even when assessment dimensions are redefined or reweighted, the optimization remains effective without manual recalibration.

Future directions for multi-site evaluation

To ensure robustness and global applicability, future research should prioritize multi-institutional and cross-cultural evaluations by deploying educational models across diverse schools and universities in both urban and rural settings. This approach would help uncover the sensitivity of educational technologies to environmental, pedagogical, and cultural variations. Key factors to consider include implicit knowledge development in EFL learners46, deep learning strategies in SPOC environments47, and the comparative sustainability of global online education platforms48. Further exploration should involve predictive models for cognitive performance49, explainable knowledge tracing systems50, and innovative platform designs such as those based on the meta-universe51. Public service platforms and national smart education strategies also warrant evaluation under real-world policy frameworks like China’s “Double Reduction” policy52. Social support mechanisms in digital learning communities12, the effectiveness of media-enhanced strategies in specialized fields like pharmacology53, and the unique challenges of rural education systems54 are additional dimensions that can enrich future multi-site research and guide the design of more inclusive and adaptive smart education systems.

Limitations

While the proposed IDA demonstrates high performance and robustness across multiple indicators, certain limitations must be acknowledged to provide a balanced perspective on its applicability and generalizability.

Risk of overfitting

Although the model was trained and evaluated using stratified 10-fold cross-validation, the integration of evolutionary optimization (GA) and local search (PS) inherently involves high model capacity and tuning flexibility. This increases the risk of overfitting, particularly when working with moderate-sized datasets or highly specific performance metrics. While repeated runs, statistical evaluations, and regularization constraints were used to mitigate this risk, there remains a possibility that the model could capture noise or local patterns that do not generalize well to unseen student populations.

Limited geographic and institutional diversity

The dataset used for training and validation includes students from a single institutional and regional context, primarily reflecting urban, undergraduate-level participants within a specific country. As a result, the model may not fully capture the variance introduced by cross-regional, cross-institutional, or cross-cultural educational environments. Factors such as language, assessment styles, curriculum design, and learning behaviors may differ significantly in other regions, affecting model performance when transferred directly.

These constraints underline the importance of future validation studies across multi-site, rural-urban, and international cohorts to ensure the model’s adaptability and fairness. Enhancing the dataset diversity and incorporating domain-specific regularization or fairness constraints will also be essential to reduce overfitting and bias in broader deployments.

Conclusions

Based on the comprehensive results supported by the accompanying graphs and tables, the conclusions are drawn, in this regard the training efficacy and student outcomes have significantly improved, alongside enhancements in health indicators. The proposed framework demonstrates superiority over existing techniques such as RF, ANSI networks, RF regression models, GRNN methods, NB, and SVM utilizing radial basis kernels, achieving a mean performance of 98%. The proposed IDA ensures convergence across various learning rates, with optimal results achieved at a learning rate of 0.00013. The fitness values for overall performance, educational performance, and health indicator score are found to range from 10− 07 to 10− 08, 10− 07 to 10− 06, and 10− 07 to 10− 05, respectively. The findings presented in Figs. 7 and 8, and Table 6, based on 100 independent runs, demonstrate the applicability, reliability, and stability of the proposed IDA. Future research may explore the fuzzy structure for non-linear problems in conjunction with advanced AI-based algorithms. By embedding core educational theories such as Bloom’s Taxonomy and Constructivist Learning Theory into the structure of our framework, we establish a pedagogically sound foundation for the integration of artificial intelligence in physical education program assessment.

One key limitation of this study is the lack of demographic diversity in the student sample. The data were collected from a single country and institution, which may not capture the variability in educational practices and student behaviors across different cultural or socioeconomic contexts. Future research should aim to validate the proposed framework using multi-site datasets that include rural and urban populations, diverse educational systems, and international cohorts. Cross-cultural validation would help evaluate the adaptability and fairness of the IDA in broader applications.

Future work will focus on testing the model on datasets from institutions in different regions or educational systems to assess generalizability and transferability of results. In the future, the authors will extend the same study for state-of-the-art deep learning models like MLPs or Transformers. Additionally, future research may benefit from incorporating advanced methodologies, as demonstrated in works like55–59, which explore innovative machine learning and AI frameworks for educational and industrial applications. Specifically, these works60–62 focus on optimizations in federated learning, resilience, and cognitive ergonomics, offering pathways to enhance the proposed framework’s robustness and adaptability.

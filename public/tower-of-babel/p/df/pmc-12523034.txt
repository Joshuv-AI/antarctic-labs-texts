Safety, pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of Plozasiran in Chinese healthy volunteers
Authors: Fangfang Wang, Dong You, Xiaoye Niu, Jack Shi, Ye Li, Lihang Qi, Haiyan Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPlozasiran (VSA001, ARO-APOC3) is an RNA interference therapy that targets Apolipoprotein C3 (APOC3), a key regulator of lipoprotein metabolism. The study aimed at assessing the safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics (PK), and pharmacodynamic (PD) profiles of plozasiran in Chinese healthy volunteers (HVs).MethodsIn this double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase I clinical study, a total of 24 Chinese adult HVs received single subcutaneous (SC) injection of 25 mg, 50 mg plozasiran or placebo on day 1. Safety, tolerability, PK and PD profiles were accessed during a follow-up period of 85 days.ResultsEighteen HVs received plozasiran (25 mg: n = 9; 50 mg: n = 9) and 6 HVs received placebo. Plozasiran was well tolerated in Chinese HVs. No death, no severe adverse events or treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) leading to discontinuation were observed. TEAEs were reported in 9 of 18 HVs from plozasiran group and in 1 of 6 HVs from placebo group. All TEAEs were transient and recovered autonomously, except for 2 subjects with 4 TEAEs from plozasiran group needed concomitant medications. After SC injection, plozasiran was rapidly absorbed and quickly eliminated in the plasma. Maximum geomean serum concentration was 102 ng/mL (CV%:36.4%) and 216 ng/mL (58.1%) for 25 mg and 50 mg group, respectively. The median Tmax of the 25 mg and 50 mg groups was 3 h (2.00, 6.02) and 6 h (1.00, 6.00), respectively. The geomean AUC0-t was 971 ng*h/mL (21.1%) and 2016 ng*h/mL (29.5%), and AUC0-∞ was 999 ng*h/mL (20.80%) and 2148 ng*h/mL (27.0%), respectively. The geomean CL/F, Vz/F and t1/2 of plozasiran 25 and 50 mg groups were 25.0 L/h (20.80%) and 23.3 L/h (27.0%), 120 L (41.8%) and 100 L (41.8%), 3.33 h (27.0%) and 2.99 h (22.0%), respectively. After subcutaneous injection of plozasiran, the concentrations of APOC3 and triglyceride (TG) decreased substantially in both treatment groups, reaching a steady-state level simultaneously (Days 8–29), which persisted until the end of the study (EOS). Maximum mean decreases in APOC3 were observed on day 29; 79.0% and 95.1% from baseline for 25 mg and 50 mg group, respectively, with corresponding reductions of TG of 64.5% and 71.3%. In addition, increase in HDL-C, and decreases in non-HDL-C, ApoB, LDL-C and VLDL-C were observed in plozasiran group. No noticeable change of PD parameters was observed in placebo group.ConclusionsPlozasiran at 25 and 50 mg was well tolerated with acceptable safety profile in Chinese HVs. Safety, PK and PD profiles observed in the present study were consistent with the data reported from clinical studies conducted outside China.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12933-025-02929-9.Keywords: Plozasiran, Hypertriglyceridemia, Apolipoprotein C-III, Triglyceride

Background

Hypertriglyceridemia (HTG) is a common type of dyslipidemia and a risk factor for cardiovascular events and acute pancreatitis [1–4]. The risk of incident acute pancreatitis increases by 4% for every 1.1 mmol/L increase in triglyceride (TG) concentration [5]. Extremely high levels of non-fasting TG represent a typical characteristic of familial chylomicronemia syndrome (FCS), which is usually induced by various single-gene mutations [2, 4, 6, 7]. The incidence of acute pancreatitis is 15% in HTG patients with gene mutations when the TG level was > 20 mmol/L [8].

In addition, TG and TG-rich lipoprotein (TRL) levels are associated with an increased risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) [9, 10]. Epidemiologic and human genetic evidence suggests a causal relationship between ASCVD risk and elevated levels of plasma TG, TRL, and TRL remnants [10–12].

However, current FDA-approved TG-lowering oral therapies, such as fibrates, fish oil, and nicotinic acid, have limited efficacy in lowering TG levels and cannot completely eliminate the risk of acute pancreatitis associated with TG. Moreover, these drugs act through the LPL-dependent pathway and may not be effective in patients with FCS. In addition, long-term intervention is needed for the treatment of ASCVD, but prolonged oral medication will reduce the treatment compliance of patients, and then affect the effectiveness [13].

Apolipoprotein C-III (APOC3), as a key regulator of TRL metabolism, inhibits TG hydrolysis through an LPL-dependent pathway, and inhibits hepatocyte receptor-mediated uptake through an LPL-independent pathway to delay hepatic clearance of lipoprotein remnants [14–16]. Previous studies have shown that loss-of-function mutations in APOC3 are associated with low levels of TG and a reduced risk of ischemic cardiovascular disease, indicating that inhibition of APOC3 can significantly reduce serum TG levels [17–19]. Currently, APOC3 inhibitors in development mainly include antisense oligonucleotides (ASO) and small RNA interference (siRNA) [20]. Volanesorsen has not yet secured approval from the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) due to concerns regarding safety, especially those associated with thrombocytopenia [21, 22]. Nevertheless, this pharmaceutical agent has been approved in Europe for the treatment of FCS. Notably, in December 2024, the FDA granted approval to olezarsen, a novel ASO that specifically targets APOC3, for the management of FCS. This approval was predicated on the favorable efficacy and acceptable safety characteristics demonstrated by olezarsen in a pivotal clinical trial [23, 24]. The Olezarsen instructions state that one of the most common adverse reactions is a decrease in platelet count. In addition, APOC3-targeting drugs may induce hepatocellular stress-associated mild hepatic enzymes’ increase and fluctuations above the normal range, similar to other NAG liver-targeted ASO and siRNA drugs. No thrombocytopenia was observed in the subjects treated with plozasiran, a siRNA product targeting APOC3, specifically inhibits APOC3 mRNA expression and reduces APOC3 synthesis in hepatocytes to reduce TG levels [25, 26]. The phase I study (NCT03783377) of plozasiran conducted in Australia, New Zealand, and Canada found that both single or repeat doses of plozasiran are well-tolerated, and plozasiran is associated with reduced serum levels of APOC3 and TG in healthy participants and patients with HTG and FCS [27]. However, there is currently no data on the safety, pharmacokinetics (PK), and pharmacodynamics (PD) of plozasiran in the Chinese population, as well as efficacy and safety data one year after plozasiran single-dose treatment. This study aimed to investigate the safety, tolerability, PK, and PD of a single 25/50 mg subcutaneous injection (SC) of plozasiran in healthy Chinese volunteers.

Method

Study design and participants

This phase I, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, single-dose clinical study evaluated the safety, tolerability, PK, and PD of plozasiran in healthy adult volunteers in China (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT05757596). This study was conducted in Peking University Third Hospital (Beijing, China), and the study protocol was approved by the Medical Science Research Ethics Committee of Peking University Third Hospital [(2023) Drug Review No. (023 − 01)]. The study protocol was performed in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki (2013), the standards of the International Conference on Good Clinical Practice (GCP), and the review opinions of the ethics committee. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

The selected dosages of 25 mg and 50 mg are grounded in the following two aspects: (1) Safety data from the global phase I study involving healthy volunteers and patients with severe dyslipidemia revealed that upon single subcutaneous administration of doses ranging from 10 to 100 mg, no drug-related discontinuation events occurred. Moreover, the adverse events were predominantly mild- to moderate-grade treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs). (2) Simulation results of population pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PopPK/PD) studies demonstrate that within the dosage range of 10 to 25 mg, the pharmacodynamic response intensifies. At plasma exposure levels corresponding to the 25 mg and 50 mg dosage groups, the pharmacokinetics exhibit linear characteristics, and the inhibition of APOC3 attains its maximal value [1]. The abovementioned dosage design aligns with the ongoing global phase IIb/III clinical trials, thereby facilitating cross-regional efficacy comparisons.

The subjects in this study comprised healthy men and women aged 18 to 55, who were screened for normal or mildly elevated serum TG levels among relatively healthy individuals. Exclusion criteria included a history/presence of clinically relevant disease such as cardiovascular, pulmonary, hepatic, or renal disease or signs of acute illness. The full study criteria are detailed in Supplement Table S1.

Eligible subjects were assigned random numbers in ascending order of their screening numbers. The random numbers of the subjects were generated by an independent statistician from the statistical department outside the trial. In this study, the study participants, investigators, monitors, and statistical analysts, and other study-related blinded personnel, were unaware of the allocation of plozasiran in each dose group. Plozasiran and placebo were provided by the sponsor. The appearance of plozasiran and the placebo is identical. The syringes used for drug administration were wrapped with yellow light-resistant tape to further ensure that they were indistinguishable. Meanwhile, at the investigator site, two specifically designated unblinded staff members were responsible for drug preparation. This was to prevent unblinding that might occur due to differences in the tactile sensation during injection process.

The study consisted of a screening period (day − 28 to day − 2), an inpatient treatment period (day − 2 to day 1, day 1 treatment), and a follow-up period (day 8 to day 85). Subjects were discharged on day 3 and were required to return for PD sampling and assessments on designated dates. In this study, the total study duration for each subject was 85 days, which did not include the screening period and post-study follow-up. Subjects were randomly assigned to 25 mg (Group 1) or 50 mg (Group 2) dose groups, with 12 subjects in each group, 9 receiving plozasiran (0.5 mL/vial, 200 mg/mL), and 3 receiving matching dose of placebo (0.9% Normal Saline). Of note, the enrollment and administration in the 50 mg dose group could be proceed only after the safety assessment for the 25 mg dose was performed on day 7 and a safety conclusion had been reached by the investigator and the sponsor. During the EOS period, if the subject’s serum APOC3 level has not rebounded back to at least 50% of the baseline, additional monthly follow-up visits may be conducted at the discretion of the investigator or medical monitor until 12 months post-administration. Safety assessments

Safety was assessed by physical examination and clinical laboratory evaluations (routine blood tests, blood biochemical analyses, blood coagulation index, and urine routine test), electrocardiographic (ECG) parameters, vital signs (axillary temperature, heart rate, respiratory rate, systolic and diastolic blood pressure), and subject-reported or investigator-observed adverse events (AEs), including local tolerability/sensitivity reactions. All TEAEs were defined as events that occurred, worsened, or became severe between the time of plozasiran or placebo injection (day 1) and End of Study (EOS) (day 85 ± 3). TEAEs were aggregated through mapping with the Preferred Term (PT) and System Organ Class (SOC) codes of Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) version 26.1. The severity of TEAEs was graded and summarized based on the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE), version 5.0.

Safety endpoints included the incidence, frequency, and severity of AEs and serious AEs (SAEs), as well as the results of clinically significant laboratory tests (routine blood tests, blood biochemical analyses, blood coagulation index, and urine routine test), vital signs, physical examination, and 12-lead ECG change related to the study drug.

PK assessments

Blood samples were collected within 1 h before administration and on day 1 (0.25 h ± 2 min, 0.5 h ± 2 min, 1 h ± 2 min, 2 h ± 2 min, 3 h ± 2 min, 6 h ± 2 min, 9 h ± 5 min, 12 h ± 5 min, 18 h ± 5 min), day 2 (24 h ± 5 min), and day 3 (48 h ± 5 min) after administration for the determination of serum concentrations of plozasiran. The concentration of plozasiran was quantified using the validated liquid chromatography-fluorescence assay (lower limit of quantification, 1.00 ng per milliliter).

The PK parameters of plozasiran were calculated according to the actual blood sampling time point by non-compartmental analysis (NCA) using Phoenix™ WinNonlin Software version 8.2. The calculated PK parameters of plozasiran included the peak plasma concentration (Cmax), time to Cmax (Tmax), area under the concentration-time curve from 0 to the final quantifiable time point (AUC0 − t), AUC from 0 to infinity (AUC0−∞), dose-normalized Cmax (Cmax/D), dose-normalized AUC0-t (AUC0 − t/D), apparent clearance (CL/F), apparent volume of distribution in the terminal elimination phase (Vz/F), elimination half-life (t1/2), and elimination rate constant (λz).

PD assessments

Lipid testing was performed during the screening period. Fasting blood samples were collected before administration on Day 1 and on the morning of the visit to detect the levels of Apolipoprotein C3 (apoC3), TG, low density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), total cholesterol (TC), very-low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (VLDL-C), non-HDL-C, apoprotein B (Apo B), and lipoprotein (a) [Lp (a)]. The PD assessment visit time points were scheduled on days 8, 15, 29, 43, 57, 71, and 85 (Fig. 1). During the EOS period, if the subject’s serum APOC3 level did not rebounded back to at least 50% of the baseline, additional monthly follow-up visits may be conducted at the discretion of the investigator or medical monitor until 12 months post-administration. The follow-up time points after the End of Study (EOS) visit were day 120, day 150, day 180, day 210, day 240, day 270, day 300, day 330, day 360, and day 390 respectively.

The primary PD endpoint was the percent change from baseline in fasting serum levels of APOC3 and TG over time. Secondary PD endpoints were the percent changes from baseline in fasting serum levels of LDL-C, HDL-C, VLDL-C, ApoB, LP(a), non-HDL-C, and TC over time.

Post-hoc analysis

In post hoc analysis, PD and lipid parameters were grouped according to visit time (day 29, day 43, and day 85) for ANOVA analysis. The plozasiran 25 mg group was combined with plozasiran 50 mg group as the active drug group and placebo were compared for analysis.

Statistical analysis

The sample size of this study was determined by feasibility and the Technical Guidelines for Clinical Pharmacokinetic Studies of Chemical Drugs. It was planned to recruit 24 subjects. Statistical analysis of the data was performed using SAS® software (version 9.4, SAS Institute Inc., North Carolina, US). Descriptive statistical analysis, without hypothesis testing, was used for grouping and visit of plozasiran concentrations during treatment, and PK and PD data were summarized descriptively. Safety data were analyzed by descriptive statistics and personal data on AEs, clinical laboratory values, vital signs, and ECG parameters.

Results

Baseline subject characteristics

A total of 24 healthy subjects were enrolled in this study, including 9 subjects in the plozasiran 25 mg dose group, 9 subjects in the plozasiran 50 mg dose group, and 6 subjects in the placebo group (Subjects flow diagram in Fig. 2). The main reason for the failure of screening of 92 subjects was that they did not meet the inclusion criteria and/or met the exclusion criteria of the study (Table S2). The first subject signed the informed consent form on May 17, 2023, while the last subject completed follow-up on September 23, 2023. Baseline characteristics such as age, body mass index, and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) were generally similar among the groups (Table 1). Baseline levels of TG were similar among plozasiran 25 mg group (1.44 ± 0.46 mmol/L), 50 mg group (1.81 ± 0.74 mmol/L) and the placebo group (2.97 ± 1.74 mmol/L). Among the 24 subjects included in this study, 2 subjects (8.3%) in the plozasiran 25 mg group received concomitant medication, all of which were used for AEs (Table S3).

Safety

Plozasiran was generally well tolerated in 24 healthy Chinese subjects included in the safety population. TEAEs were reported in 4 and 5 subjects in the plozasiran 25 mg group (n = 9) and 50 mg group (n = 9), respectively, compared with 1 subject in the placebo group (n = 6) (Table 1). There was no dose-response relationship between the two dose groups in the incidence of TEAEs. There were no reported TEAEs that led to withdrawal from the trial, no serious TEAEs or treatment-induced SAEs occurred, and no deaths were reported during the study period. The most frequently reported TEAEs in both the plozasiran and placebo groups were elevated alanine aminotransferase (ALT), erythema at the injection site, elevated uric acid level, and diarrhea according to the classification of PT (Table 2).

In comparison to the baseline, the vital signs, ECG parameters, biochemical indicators, and other laboratory test outcomes of all subjects in this study remained stable. Overall, any observed increasing levels of ALT or AST were transient during the study period (Figure S1). Adverse events of special interest (AESI) observed in this study all occurred in the plozasiran group, including liver-enzyme elevations and injection-site events. One subject in the 25 mg group (baseline values: ALT 19 U/L, AST 14 U/L) developed grade 3 elevations in liver enzymes, with ALT rising to 266 U/L on day 28 and AST peaking at 127 U/L on day 32. These values returned to normal ranges by day 57 (ALT 42 U/L) and day 50 (AST 29 U/L), respectively, following oral administration of polyene phosphatidylcholine and diammonium glycyrrhizinate. Three subjects (1 in the 25 mg group and 2 in the 50 mg group) had grade 1 ALT elevations (Table S4, Figure S1). In one case, a subject in the 50 mg group experienced a grade 1 increase in AST simultaneously (Table S4, Figure S1). At the time of the above events, none of the subjects had accompanying symptoms such as jaundice and fatigue, and laboratory tests showed no increases in bilirubin, albumin, or other abnormal liver function test values, and therefore there were no cases of drug-induced liver injury (DILI) per Hy’s law reported in this study. In addition, 3 subjects (1 in the 25 mg group and 2 in the 50 mg group) had injection site erythema, which was grade 1 and recovered spontaneously within 24 h. AEs of low platelet levels or thrombocytopenia were not reported. A total of 14 TEAEs occurred in 4 subjects, including 2 subjects from the plozasiran 25 mg group who received concomitant medication (see Table S3). Apart from the subjects with mild liver-enzyme elevations receiving hepatoprotective agents mentioned above, another subject underwent a 4-day short-term pharmacotherapy for acute gastroenteritis and hypokalemia during the study period, including berberine hydrochloride tablets and potassium chloride sustained-release tablets. No association with the study drug or adverse events was identified in the treatment, nor did it trigger new laboratory abnormalities (Table S3). Nine TEAEs were reported in five subjects from the plozasiran 50 mg group, with no subjects requiring concomitant medication due to AEs. All TEAEs recovered during the study follow-up period.

In subjects with serum APOC3 levels failing to recover to ≥ 50% of baseline by day 84 (plozasiran 25 mg: 6 cases; plozasiran 50 mg: 9 cases), the observation window was prolonged through day 390 or until serum APOC3 levels achieved a recovery of at least 50% of baseline. During the post-EOS follow-up, 2 subjects experienced 4 AEs, which were determined by the investigator to be unrelated to the investigational drug and subsequently resolved.

PK analysis

A total of 17 subjects receiving plozasiran were included for PK analysis, including 8 in the 25 mg group and 9 in the 50 mg group. One subject was not included in the PK analysis in the plozasiran 25 mg group, because the separation of the needle from the syringe occurred during the injection, which resulted in the inability to evaluate the injected dose. The mean concentration-time curves of serum plozasiran after a single subcutaneous dose of 25 or 50 mg were shown in Fig. 3, and the PK parameters of plozasiran were summarized in Table 3. Plozasiran was rapidly absorbed after a single SC. The median Tmax of the 25 mg and 50 mg groups was 3 and 6 h, respectively. The geomean Cmax was 102 and 216 ng/mL, AUC0 − t was 971 and 2016 ng·h/mL, and AUC0−∞ was 999 and 2148 ng·h/mL, respectively. The geometric coefficients of variation for plasma exposure Cmax, AUC0 − t and AUC0−∞ were 36.4% and 58.1%, 21.1% and 29.5%, and 20.8% and 27.0% for the plozasiran 25 mg and 50 mg groups, respectively. The geomean CL/F, Vz/F and t1/2 of plozasiran 25 and 50 mg groups were 25.0 and 23.3 L/h, 120 and 100 L, 3.33 and 2.99 h, respectively.

Pharmacodynamic analysis

APOC3 and TG lowering

Following subcutaneous administration, both treatment groups demonstrated obviously reductions in APOC3 and TG levels. Specifically, APOC3 and TG concentrations in the two treatment groups achieved steady-state levels from day 8 to day 29, which were stably maintained until the EOS. Notably, maximal reductions in APOC3 occurred on day 29 in both the plozasiran 25 and 50 mg dose groups. Among them, APOC3 decreased by 79.0% and 95.1%, and TG decreased by 64.5% and 71.3% compared with baseline, respectively. In contrast, the levels of APOC3 and TG remained relatively unchanged from baseline to day 71 and EOS in the placebo group. At the EOS, APOC3 reductions reached 67.3% in the 25 mg group and 88.1% in the 50 mg group, with corresponding TG reductions of 59.6% and 64.7% (as shown in Fig. 4).

Other lipid/lipoprotein lowering

The levels of non-HDL-C were sustainedly decreased in the plozasiran 25 and 50 mg groups, with the maximum reductions from baseline of 23.7% (day 71) and 26.2% (day 29), respectively (Fig. 5A, Table S5). The levels of HDL-C were sustainedly increased in the plozasiran 25 and 50 mg groups, with the maximum increase from baseline of 50.8% (day 43) and 68.4% (day 43), respectively (Fig. 5B). Post-hoc analysis

Compared with the placebo group (n = 6), the levels of TG were significantly declined on days 29, 43, and 85 in the plozasiran group (n = 8 in 25 mg group and n = 9 in 50 mg group, P < 0.0001, P < 0.0001, and P < 0.0001, respectively). Similarly, the levels of APOC3 were significantly reduced in the plozasiran group on days 29, 43, and 85 (P < 0.0001, P < 0.0001, and P < 0.0001, respectively, Supplement Table S7).

Non-HDL-C levels significantly decreased in the plozasiran group on day 29, day 43, and day 85 (P = 0.0181, P = 0.0038, and P = 0.0258). HDL-C levels significantly increased on these same days (P = 0.0002, P < 0.0001, and P < 0.0001). VLDL-C levels were significantly reduced on day 43 and day 85 (P < 0.0001 and P = 0.0295). The levels of ApoB tended to decrease in the plozasiran group after SC, but the changes were not significant.

Discussion

This is the first clinical study of plozasiran in Chinese healthy subjects. Single subcutaneous administration of plozasiran at 25 or 50 mg was generally well tolerated in Chinese population. The safety, blood pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics profiles including effects on APOC3 and TG, shown in this population were generally consistent with those reported in the plozasiran phase I clinical trial which included predominantly non-Chinese population [27].

Hepatocellular stress-associated mild hepatic enzymes’ increase and fluctuations above the normal range are a documented class effect of NAG liver/APOC3-targeting small RNA drugs including volanesorsen, olezarsen, and plozasiran [28]. In the global phase I trial (NCT03783377) conducted predominantly in non-Chinese populations, ALT elevations exceeding 3×ULN were observed in 2 of 24 subjects (8.3%) receiving plozasiran and 1 of 16 subjects (6.3%) in the placebo group. All subjects had returned to baseline levels by the end of the trial [27]. In our study, 2 subjects in each plozasiran group had a TEAE of transient, mild to moderate liver transaminase change, and all subjects have achieved complete recovery. In 25 mg group, one subject experienced grade 3 hepatic enzyme elevation, with ALT and AST peaking on day 28 and day 32, respectively, which returned to normal after oral polyene phosphatidylcholine and diammonium glycyrrhizinate treatment. The hepatic enzyme events in the current study were possibly part of this broader class-related pattern. The incidence of injection-site events tended to be lower.(1 in the 25 mg and 2 in the 50 mg group) in Chinese subjects who received plozasiran than in the phase I study in overall predominantly non-Chinese population [27]. Similarly, there were no reported AEs of low platelet levels or thrombocytopenia. This study and the overall predominantly non-Chinese population phase I trial (NCT03783377) did not result in any discontinuation due to SAEs or TEAEs. There was no significant dose correlation between the plozasiran 25 mg group and the 50 mg group in the frequency and severity of TEAEs. No additional TEAEs were observed during the over-one-year follow-up. Of note, the reported hepatic enzyme elevation events were transient and did not result in any clinically relevant hepatotoxic cases such as events meeting Hy’s Law.

In healthy Chinese subjects, single-dose subcutaneous administration of plozasiran (25 and 50 mg) resulted in dose-proportional increases in plasma exposure. The mean Tmax of plozasiran was 3–6 h, consistent with PK data from subjects of overall predominantly non-Chinese population [27]. In addition, the mean t1/2 of plozasiran was 3.33 and 2.99 h after a single dose of 25 or 50 mg SC in the Chinese subjects, respectively, which was similar to the mean t1/2 (3.8 h) in overall predominantly non-Chinese population [27].

Compared with the overall predominantly non-Chinese population subjects in the previous study, the Chinese subjects had demonstrated slight increase in plasma exposure levels (Cmax and AUC) and slight decrease in Vz/F and CL/F, which may be due to the fact that the median weight of Chinese subjects (65 kg) was lower than that of overall predominantly non-Chinese population subjects (79 kg).

The mean maximum reduction in APOC3 from baseline observed in Chinese subjects following a single subcutaneous administration of plozasiran 25 mg or 50 mg was 79.0% on day 29 and 95.1% on day 29, consistent with the mean maximum decrease in APOC3 from baseline observed in overall predominantly non-Chinese population (86.1% on day 29 and 87.6% on day 43) [27]. Similar to the overall predominantly non-Chinese population study (plozasiran 25 mg: 60.8% on day 57, plozasiran 50 mg: 65.7% on day 29) [27], our study found that Chinese subjects experienced a mean maximal decline in TG levels of 64.5% (plozasiran 25 mg) on day 29 and 71.3% (plozasiran 50 mg) on day 29. During the EOS period, merely two subjects in the plozasiran 25 mg group restored their APOC3 levels to 50% of the baseline, whereas the APOC3 levels of the remaining subjects ranged from 9.93 to 32.54% of the baseline. Following an extension of the follow-up period to day 390, there were still 2 subjects in the plozasiran 25 mg group and 4 subjects in the plozasiran 50 mg group whose APOC3 levels remained below 50% of baseline.

In addition, in the plozasiran 25 mg and 50 mg groups, HDL-C increased by an average maximum of 50.8% (day 43) and 68.4% (day 43), respectively. Non-HDL-C decreased from baseline by a maximum of 23.7% (day 71) and 26.2% (day 29). VLDL-C showed average maximum decreases of 54.2% (day 57) and 57.3% (day 85). The duration of these PD effects was maintained at least 12 weeks after the dose. Based on these results, it can be concluded that, consistent with the overall predominantly non-Chinese population study, healthy Chinese subjects experienced a decrease in APOC3, TG, non-HDL-C, and VLDL-C after receiving 25 mg or 50 mg plozasiran treatment, while HDL-C levels increased. However, due to the extended follow-up period of 390 days in this study, we observed that the APOC3 levels of some subjects in the 25 mg or 50 mg plozasiran group did not recover to 50% of the baseline level. Published data from phase II and III clinical trials further substantiated these observations. The SHASTA-2 phase IIb clinical trial (NCT04720534) revealed that plozasiran reduced triglyceride levels to below 500 mg/dL in 90.6% of patients with severe hypertriglyceridemia (baseline TG ≥ 500 mg/dL) [29]. The PALISADE phase III clinical trial (NCT05089084) documented a 78%-80% triglyceride reduction accompanied by significantly diminished pancreatitis incidence in familiar chylomicronemia syndrome (FCS) patients [30]. The MUIR phase IIb clinical trial (NCT04998201) involving 353 patients with mixed hyperlipidemia demonstrated that compared with the placebo, plozasiran significantly reduced the triglyceride levels at week 24 [31].

This study still has several limitations. Firstly, the dose range is relatively limited, only including two dose levels, and a single-dose design was used instead of a repeated-dose regimen. Secondly, the study subjects were healthy volunteers, and the patients with hyperlipidemia were not included. Fewer female subjects (n = 6 [25.0%]) than male subjects (n = 18 [75.0%]) were included. The upper limit of the age of subjects was 55 years, which excluded the elderly population. In addition, this study only evaluated healthy subjects and did not include patients with severe dyslipidemia; therefore, more in-depth studies are needed to evaluate the clinical efficacy of plozasiran in Chinese patients.

Conclusions

Single SC administration of plozasiran at 25 or 50 mg was well tolerated in healthy Chinese subjects; in addition, plozasiran demonstrated the ability to effectively and sustainably reduce the levels of APOC3 and TG, while maintaining a favorable safety profile. The safety, PK and PD profile of plozasiran in Chinese population is consistent with the data obtained from overall predominantly non-Chinese population, especially in the clinically meaningful reductions in TG, APOC3 and other lipids/lipoproteins.

Musical note flap repair large defects of the proximal nail fold: A case report
Authors: Renheng Zhu, Shuqiong Huang, Dingbin Yang
Published: 2024

A 28‐year‐old woman presented to the dermatology clinic with congenital pigmented nevus on the proximal nail fold of the third toe of her left foot. Skin biopsies performed at the local hospital indicated pigmented nevus with active nevus cells, The local hospital was unable to operate and referred the patient to our department. Through the photos, we can see that the pigmented nevus is large, exceeding the nail width (Figure 1). We consider removing the nevus and not being able to close the defect directly. If the defect is not repaired, infection may also occur, and even damage the nail matrix, So we designed a modified diamond flap to repair the defect, because the flap is shaped like a musical note, we call this flap a musical note flap (Figure 1). This is an immediate post‐op photo (Figure 2), and we can see that near the edge of the nail, we did not suture, because suturing here could have caused the proximal fold to turn over. This is 14 days after surgery, and we can see that the flap is completely alive (Figure 3). Two months after the operation, the photos showed that the flap healed well and had a high aesthetic degree (Figure 4).

The rhomboid flap is widely used in dermatological surgery and is one of the earliest flaps used in skin surgery. The rhomboid flap design was first described by Professor Alexander Alexandrovich Limberg of Leningrad in 1928. 1 The rhomboid flap can be used to repair defects in various parts. The rhomboid flap is frequently used in the repair of skin defects in the face and neck area. 2 Besides, it can also be widely used in the repair of other parts. A scholar used the rhomboid flap to repair the defect after breast cancer resection. 3 Sanjeev Yadav used the rhomboid flap to repair the defect behind the ear. 4 Ajaipal S. Kang utilized the rhomboid flap to repair a huge defect on the back. 5 Based on the above literature, we know that the rhomboid flap is widely used in dermatological surgery and has a good effect. However, there are currently almost no literature reports on the repair of proximal nail fold defects using the rhomboid flap. This case uses the modified rhomboid flap to repair the defect of the proximal nail fold. The surgical effect is good and the aesthetic degree is high, expanding the application scope of the rhomboid flap and being worthy of reference for dermatological surgeons.

Multi-bubble scattering acoustic fields in viscoelastic tissues under dual-frequency ultrasound
Authors: Yu Wang, Dehua Chen, Pengfei Wu
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Highlights•We investigated the bubble dynamics in viscoelastic media under dual-frequency ultrasound and analyzed the frequency spectrum of scattered acoustic pressure of the bubbles.•We compared the multi-bubble acoustic scattering with and without considering bubble interactions.•Enhancement and suppression caused by bubble interactions are fully studied, in particular the effect on the amplitude of the resonance peaks in the scattered acoustic wave spectrum.•The influence of relevant parameters on bubble acoustic scattering is demonstrated.Keywords: Acoustic scattering, Viscoelastic tissues, Multi-bubble, Dual-frequency ultrasound

Introduction

Microbubbles play a very important role in the scattering of acoustic waves in liquids, and their scattering cross-section is significant compared to that of incompressible media[1], [2], [3], [4]. The scattered acoustic signals from microbubbles have numerous applications in the medical field, such as the use of contrast agents in ultrasound imaging, the detection of bubbles in people suffering from decompression sickness, and ultrasound therapy[5], [6], [7]. In addition, due to the properties such as combination and simultaneous resonance, the dual-frequency ultrasound method is also used in the medical field[8], [9]. Therefore, it is important to study the scattering characteristics of microbubbles in biological tissues under dual-frequency ultrasound.

Besides scattering characteristics, there also exists a phenomenon called bubble interaction under the excitation of acoustic waves[10]. The scattered sound pressure caused by the bubbles acts on other bubbles as an additional acoustic signal, thus causing changes in the oscillations of other bubbles. The interaction between bubbles also causes changes in their scattering cross section, and when the bubble interaction is significant, the scattering cross section calculated by ignoring its effect will result in a relatively large error[11]. Therefore, for multi-bubble systems, it is necessary to consider bubble interaction to analyze the magnitude of the bubble scattering cross-section.

The response of a bubble under acoustic waves is generally solved by the bubble dynamic equation, and the dynamic equations describing microbubbles encapsulated by a viscoelastic medium (e.g., biological tissue) have been extensively studied[12]. The oscillation of a single bubble in an incompressible Newtonian fluid under acoustic excitation can be described by the classical Rayleigh-Plesset equation[13]. For large-magnitude acoustic disturbances, Keller and Miksis proposed the governing equations applicable to compressible fluids[14]. For bubbles encapsulated by viscoelastic media, Yang and Church et al. combined the bubble dynamics equation and Kelvin-Voigt constitutive relation and obtained an equation describing the behavior of bubbles in viscoelastic media[15]. Allen et al. combined Maxwell's viscoelastic media model to obtain a new equation for calculating the resonant frequency of bubbles and obtained the resonant frequency curve numerically under different parameters[16]. Hua et al. combined classical linear constitutive relation for describing viscoelastic media, and they found that the influence of nonlinear effects is important when the stress tensor of the medium is significant[17]. Moreover, some researchers combined nonlinear viscoelastic models and bubble dynamic equations for describing the behavior of bubbles in viscoelastic media involving large deformations[18]. Until now, the dynamic equations describing bubbles encapsulated by viscoelastic media have been extensively studied, and equations applicable to a variety of different conditions have been obtained.

The bubble scatters sound waves to the surrounding medium under the excitation of acoustic waves, and the scattering cross section has been studied for several years [19]. And the scattering signal of the bubble is more complicated under the excitation of multi-frequency acoustic waves. Newhouse et al. gave an analytical solution for the scattered acoustic pressure of bubbles in water under dual-frequency ultrasound and experimentally measured the scattered signal of the bubble cluster[20]. Du et al. compared two methods for solving the scattered acoustic pressure of bubbles and analyzed the nonlinear characteristics of bubbles under dual-frequency[21]. Zhang et al. theoretically analyzed the oscillation response of bubbles under dual-frequency ultrasonic excitation, calculated the scattering cross section of bubbles under dual-frequency acoustic waves, and analyzed the combined resonance and simultaneous resonance properties of bubble oscillations[22]. Shi et al. calculated the magnitude of the scattering cross section of bubbles and investigated the effect of the nonlinear characteristics under multi-frequency acoustic excitation[23].

Since scattered acoustic pressure of bubbles affects the oscillation of surrounding bubbles, the interaction between bubbles has also been an interesting subject for many years. For bubble interaction under single-frequency acoustic excitation, much related research has been conducted. Doinikov et al. calculated the transient motion of two interacting bubbles under a strong acoustic field and found that when the forcing is large enough, the two bubbles form a stably spaced bound pair[24]. Yasui et al. studied the effect of bubble interaction on their pulsation in a system with multiple bubbles both theoretically and experimentally[11]. Cai et al. computed the transient velocity field between two bubbles by the finite element method and analyzed the variation of the transient velocity field with bubble motion[25]. Regnualt et al. experimentally measured the second-order radiation force between two approaching bubbles[26]. Ma et al. numerically simulated the nonlinear oscillations of bubbles and the scattered acoustic field of a spherical bubble cluster, and the calculations showed that the scattered acoustic pressure of the bubble cluster is directional[27]. Shen et al. analyzed the effect of bubble interaction on the radial pulsation of bubbles and the results showed that the effect is related to the specific parameters of the incidence[28]. Wu et al. derived a kinetic model describing the motion of two aspherical bubbles in Lagrangian mechanics, and this study showed that the aspherical amplitude of the bubbles decreases with increasing distance and decreasing driving pressure[29]. In addition, for the interaction between bubbles under dual-frequency acoustic excitation, Zhang et al. conducted an analytical and numerical study of the secondary Bjerknes force between two bubbles under dual-frequency excitation, proposed features such as combined resonance and simultaneous resonance, and analyzed the effects of parameters such as pressure amplitude and bubble distance on the force in a wide range of parameters.[30].

Without considering the interaction between bubbles, the scattered sound pressure cross section obtained by theoretical calculation differs from the real value. The bubble size distribution measured by scattered acoustic pressure is inaccurate from the actual value. Therefore, the interaction between bubbles should be considered to further accurate inversion results and enhance the accuracy of the acoustic bubble detection method. However, most of the present work on bubble interactions is under single-frequency acoustic excitation[31], [32]. Some studies under dual-frequency ultrasound primarily focused on the secondary Bjerknes force between bubbles and their effects on bubble oscillation[30]. And few studies have investigated the scattering properties of dual-frequency excited bubbles, but are limited to Newtonian liquids[19]. As far as we know, the scattering characteristics of multi-bubble in viscoelastic liquids under dual-frequency excitation are studied for the first time in this paper.

In our study, scattered acoustic pressure of bubbles in viscoelastic media under dual-frequency ultrasound were calculated. The scattered acoustic spectra with and without considering bubble interactions were compared. In addition, the effects of bubble radius size, incident frequency, and the number of bubbles on bubble interactions were discussed.

By solving the system of equations, the instantaneous radii of the two bubbles are obtained, and the scattered sound pressure of the bubbles is obtained from the transient radii. The following parameters are used in the calculation: ρ = 1090 kg/m3, σ = 0.0643 N/m, γ = 1.4, μ = 0.001 Pa•s, p0 = 1 atm, p1 = p2 = 5000 Pa, f1 = 5 MHz, G = 20 kPa, r = 1 cm.

Interaction between two bubbles of the same radius

First, we calculated the instantaneous radius of two 3 µm bubbles with and without considering the interaction. According to Eq (7), the resonant frequency of the 3 µm bubble is 1.28 MHz. When f2 = 1.28 MHz, the incident acoustic frequency is close to the resonant frequency of the bubble. Fig. 1(a) shows the bubble instantaneous radius curve, and Fig. 1(b) shows the spectra of scattered sound pressure from a single bubble. The distance between two bubbles is D = 60 µm. The solid line in the figure is the case when a single bubble is in an infinite medium, and the dotted line is the calculated result for a single bubble when the interaction of two bubbles is considered. Comparison of the two curves shows that the bubble oscillation is suppressed when bubble interaction is considered, and the bubble transient radius amplitude is smaller than without considering bubble interaction.

Transient radius and scattered acoustic pressure spectra when R10 = R20 = 3 µm, f2 = 1.28 MHz and D = 60 µm.

It can also be seen from the spectral curve that the scattered sound pressure of the bubble has resonance peaks at several frequencies. The labeling of (m, n) in the figure represents the frequency f = m*f1 + n*f2 at this point. As can be seen from the figure, in addition to the fundamental frequencies of (0, 1) and (1, 0), there are also higher harmonic frequencies such as (0, 2), (0, 3) and (0, 4) on the frequency spectra. And for the case of dual-frequency acoustic excitation, features of combined resonance like (1, 1) and (1, −1) associated with two acoustic frequencies also exist. Therefore, the spectra of the scattered acoustic pressure is very complex under dual-frequency acoustic excitation, and the magnitude of the resonance peaks on the spectra is also influenced by the bubble interaction. For the case of two 3 µm bubbles, the bubble interaction suppresses the magnitude of the resonance peak of the scattered acoustic wave.

Increasing the distance between bubbles, changing D = 60 µm to D = 600 µm, the radius curve is shown in Fig. 2. The radius curves of a single bubble and two bubbles almost overlap, and the interaction between bubbles is almost ignorable. It can also be seen from the spectra curve that the scattered sound pressure is almost unaffected at this time. This indicates that increasing the distance can effectively reduce the effect of bubble interaction. Moreover, if the distance between bubbles is kept constant, D remains 60 µm, and the frequency of the incident acoustic wave is changed from f2 = 1.28 MHz to f2 = 600 kHz, making the frequency far from the resonant frequency of bubbles. The comparison between Fig. 3 and Fig. 1 shows that when f2 = 600 kHz, the oscillation of the bubble is much smaller than when f2 = 1.28 MHz. Even in the case of small bubble distance, the interaction between bubbles is also negligible due to the small amplitude of bubble oscillation.

The peak magnitudes at (1, 0), (0, 1), (2, 0), (0, 2), (1, 1), and (1, −1) on the scattering spectra were extracted, and the peaks of two bubbles were divided by that of a single bubble. In this way, the effect of different peaks by bubble interactions was analyzed. As shown in Fig. 4, the curve of the ratio of different peaks with the bubble distance is shown. In this calculation, the incident frequency of the acoustic wave is f2 = 1.28 MHz. It can be seen from Fig. 4 that the effect of bubble interaction on the scattered acoustic pressure decreases as the bubble distance increases. It is consistent with the experimental results in Ref [25], which show that the secondary radiation force between two bubbles decreases as the bubble distance increases. The peak values with and without considering the interaction can be considered approximately equal if the peak ratio is greater than 0.95. Then, in this case, the effect of bubble interaction on the scattered sound pressure can be neglected when the bubble spacing reaches 550 µm. And from the different curves in the figure, it can be seen that the bubble interactions suppress the bubble oscillations and result in a decrease of the resonance peak. However, the degree of effect on the different resonance peaks is different, and the comparison shows that the higher harmonics are more sensitive than the fundamental wave.

Interaction between two bubbles of different radius

Then, the effect of the interaction between the 3 µm bubble and the 6 µm bubble on the bubble oscillation and scattered acoustic waves was calculated. The resonant frequency of the 6 µm bubble was calculated from Eq. (7) to be about 600 kHz. When f2 = 1.28 MHz, the incident acoustic wave frequency was close to the resonant frequency of the small bubble. Fig. 5(a) shows the transient radius curve of the small bubble, and Fig. 5(b) shows the spectra of the scattered sound pressure of the small bubble. Fig. 5(c and d) show the instantaneous radius and scattering spectra of the large bubble. The distance between two bubbles is D = 60 μm. Comparing the curves of a single bubble and two bubbles, it can be seen that the oscillation of the small bubble is suppressed at this frequency, while at the same time, the oscillation of the large bubble is promoted. From (a) and (c), it is observed that the change in the radius of the small bubble is much stronger than the change in the large bubble. Therefore, the effect of the small bubble on the large bubble is much larger than the effect of the large bubble on the small bubble. The scattered sound pressure spectra of the two bubbles also shows that the larger bubble is more strongly affected. The scattering spectra of the small bubble has only a small variation, while the scattered acoustic pressure spectra of the large bubble has a greater change. Especially at the ultraharmonics associated with f2, such as (0,2) and (0,3), it is affected greater than at the other resonance peaks.

To summarize, for a bubble of radius R0, when the incident frequency is close to its resonant frequency, the bubble is more influenced by bubbles of the same size and less influenced by non-resonant bubbles of different sizes. And the effect of non-resonant bubbles is negligible compared to the effect of resonant bubbles. Therefore, we considered multiple bubbles of the same size and calculated the effect of bubble interaction on the scattered acoustic wave at a frequency close to the resonant frequency of the bubble.

Then the curve of the scattering spectra peak ratio variation with S was extracted, as shown in Fig. 10. It can be seen from the figure that as S increases, the difference in the value of the peak magnitude on the scattered sound pressure increases when considering and not considering the bubble interaction. The interaction between bubbles is increasingly non-negligible. Hence, for multi-bubble systems with small bubble number densities, the total response of the bubble cluster can be considered as the algebraic sum of the individual bubble responses, while for larger bubble number densities, the total response of the bubble cluster is smaller than the algebraic sum. In comparison, the amplitude of the second harmonic is more affected than the fundamental frequency.

If we can ignore the bubble interaction when the peak ratio is greater than 0.9. Then there is a critical value S0 for different resonance peaks, and when S is larger than S0, ignoring bubble interaction will cause a relatively large error in the calculation of scattered sound pressure. For example, for the fundamental frequency f2, S0 is 7000. When the S of the bubble cluster is larger than 7000, there is an error between the real value and the magnitude of the scattered sound pressure calculated by ignoring the bubble interaction. For a bubble cluster with a radius of 0.01 m, the number density calculated by substituting S = 7000 is n = 1.09*107/m3, which is a relatively small value. In our practical applications, many bubble cluster number densities are larger than this value. In some past work, these bubble clusters were detected without considering the effect of bubble interactions. Therefore, this leads to a considerable error when using scattered acoustic waves for detection.

Conclusions

The dynamic equations of bubbles oscillating in viscoelastic media are solved numerically, and the scattered acoustic fields caused by bubbles under dual-frequency ultrasound are calculated. The effect of the bubble interaction on the scattered acoustic pressure is investigated, and the scattering spectra corresponding to different bubble sizes and different incident frequencies are analyzed. The main conclusions are summarized as follows.

1.As the frequency of incident acoustic wave closing to the resonant frequency of the bubble, the interaction between two bubbles of the same size will suppress the oscillation of the bubbles.

As the frequency of incident acoustic wave closing to the resonant frequency of the bubble, the interaction between two bubbles of the same size will suppress the oscillation of the bubbles.

2.The interaction between two bubbles with different size is related to the incident acoustic wave’s frequency. When the incident frequency is close to the resonant frequency of one of the bubbles, the resonant bubble will promote the oscillation of another non-resonant bubble. In particular, the higher harmonics corresponding to the non-resonant bubble related to the resonant frequency will have a significant increase in amplitude. At the same time, the non-resonant bubble will suppress the oscillation of the resonant bubble, but the suppression effect is not significant.

The interaction between two bubbles with different size is related to the incident acoustic wave’s frequency. When the incident frequency is close to the resonant frequency of one of the bubbles, the resonant bubble will promote the oscillation of another non-resonant bubble. In particular, the higher harmonics corresponding to the non-resonant bubble related to the resonant frequency will have a significant increase in amplitude. At the same time, the non-resonant bubble will suppress the oscillation of the resonant bubble, but the suppression effect is not significant.

3.When the incident frequency is equal to the resonant frequency of one of the bubbles, the bubble is more affected by the same size bubbles and less affected by bubbles with different radius.

When the incident frequency is equal to the resonant frequency of one of the bubbles, the bubble is more affected by the same size bubbles and less affected by bubbles with different radius.

4.During dual-frequency ultrasonic detection, for sparse bubble clusters, the total response of the bubble cluster can be regarded as an algebraic sum of the kinetic behavior of individual bubble. For dense bubble clusters, ignoring the bubble interaction will lead to the deviation of the detecting results from the true value. The definition for the sparse bubble cluster is decided by the size of the bubble cluster and the number density of bubbles.

During dual-frequency ultrasonic detection, for sparse bubble clusters, the total response of the bubble cluster can be regarded as an algebraic sum of the kinetic behavior of individual bubble. For dense bubble clusters, ignoring the bubble interaction will lead to the deviation of the detecting results from the true value. The definition for the sparse bubble cluster is decided by the size of the bubble cluster and the number density of bubbles.

In summary, the effect of bubble interaction on the scattered acoustic wave is studied through numerical simulation. These results are useful for expanding the application of the scattering cross section and provide a reference critical value for delimiting the sparse bubble cluster. However, in order to analyze the effect of bubble interaction more precisely, further work is needed by considering the effect of bubble movement and deformation on bubble oscillation and on the changes caused to the scattered sound pressure.

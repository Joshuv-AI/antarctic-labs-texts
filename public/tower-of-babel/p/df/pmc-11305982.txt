A case of septic arthritis of the manubriosternal and chondrosternal joints
Authors: Rova Malala Fandresena Randrianarisoa, Armel Mamihaja Andrianiaina, Rajo Païdia Radinasoa, Hanta Marie Danielle Vololontiana
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Key Clinical MessageAlthough rare, septic arthritis of the manubriosternal and chrondrosternal joints should be considered in the presence of a parasternal mass with fever and/or local signs of inflammation. MRI has an important role in the diagnosis. Blood cultures should be obtained routinely. We report an 80‐year‐old man with painful sternal swelling and fever. Chest MRI and PET scan were consistent with septic arthritis of the manubriosternal and chrondrosternal joints. Blood cultures were positive for Staphylococcus aureus. The evolution was favorable under antibiotic therapy for 6 weeks.Keywords: Chondrosternal joint, manubriosternal joint, septic arthritis, staphylococcus aureus

CASE PRESENTATION

An 80‐year‐old man with no significant past medical history was admitted because of sternal pain and fever (38.8°C) for 3 days. On admission, examination revealed a painful inflammatory swelling adjacent to the manubrium of the sternum. The rest of the examination was unremarkable. The white blood cell count was 12,000/mm3 and the C‐reactive protein was 234 mg/L. Blood ionogram and liver and kidney tests were normal. HIV serology was negative. Blood cultures were positive for methicillin‐sensitive S. aureus. Urine bacteriology was negative. Chest X‐ray was unremarkable. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the chest showed soft tissue infiltration of the presternal region and the right second chondrosternal joint with a small collection (Figure 1). Positron emission tomography (PET) scan showed an intense hypermetabolic process at the manubriosternal joint and the right second chrondrosternal joint. (Figure 2). The patient was diagnosed with Staphylococcus aureus bacteremia associated with manubriosternal and chrondrosternal septic arthritis. Antibiotic therapy was started with intravenous cefazolin. The initial course was characterized by apyrexia and a decrease in the biologic inflammatory syndrome. Treatment was then switched to the combination of rifampicin and levofloxacin for a total of 6 weeks. At the end of treatment, the sternal inflammatory swelling had disappeared. The control MRI was not performed because of the clinical improvement.

Chest MRI (axial view). Diffuse edematous infiltration of the subcutaneous soft tissues of the presternal region and the right second chondrosternal joint with a collection 23 mm in axial diameter, suggestive of septic arthritis.

PET scan (A: Axial view; B: Coronal view; C: Sagittal view). Intense hypermetabolic process of the manubriosternal joint and the right second chrondrosternal joint with extension to the anterior and parasternal soft tissues (SUV peak = 11).

Arthritis of the juxtasternal joints is often of systemic inflammatory origin. Septic arthritis of the manubriosternal and chrondrosternal joints is rare and is often misdiagnosed as other conditions. Known risk factors include immunodeficiency, intravenous drug abuse, and exacerbation of an underlying systemic disease. Staphylococcus aureus is the most common pathogen (60% of cases). 1 The clinical presentation of septic arthritis of the juxtasternal joints is variable. It may be asymptomatic or present as a painful inflammatory mass in the chest. 2 Important differential diagnoses include Tietze's syndrome, rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis, ankylosing spondylitis, microcrystalline arthritis, and SAPHO syndrome (synovitis, acne, pustulosis, hyperostosis, and osteitis). 1 CT scan and MRI have high diagnostic sensitivity and can detect fluid collections, soft tissue infiltration and bone destruction. Medical treatment alone may be sufficient in the early stages. Treatment with antibiotics usually lasts 3–6 weeks. In advanced stages, surgical treatment is required. 3

Maximum torque per flux strategy for induction machines
Authors: Marcin Morawiec, Lelisa Wogi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEnergy-efficient electric transportation systems reduce power consumption while increasing the runtime on a single battery charge. Operating at a lower flux level can improve the efficiency of an electric vehicle drive. However, simply reducing the flux level may cause drive instability. As a result, this work proposes an enhanced flux optimization technique to improve the performance of the sensorless induction motor drive at low speeds to minimize losses through appropriate flux-weakening for the drive. Flux optimization uses constraints and boundaries on critical variables such as stator current and stator voltage. The drive’s performance is compared with existing methods and constant rated rotor flux. These include less parameter sensitivity due to the state transformation’s parameter independence and easier flux-weakening control using the maximum torque per flux controlling variable. Unlike existing flux optimization techniques, this method does not involve the solution of the parameter-dependent equation or utilizing a look-up table. Both simulation and experimental testing validate the proposed scheme’s performance for a 5.5 kW IM. Compared to the existing methods, the result of the proposed flux optimization algorithm demonstrates its superiority in both low and high-speed zones, with better performance and robustness against motor parameter variation.Keywords: Flux optimization, Induction motor, Maximum torque per flux, Electric vehicleSubject terms: Engineering, Electrical and electronic engineering

Introduction

An ongoing top priority for the Electric Vehicle (EV) industry is the development of cost-effective, and efficient AC drives. Recently, there has been a greater focus on developing electric motor-driven transportation systems to address transportation-related issues and make transportation more economical and environmentally friendly1. The EV drives are more efficient and quieter, have lower operating expenses, and require less maintenance. Many types of electric motors are available for EVs. The electric motor and its control approach significantly influence driving performance in an EV drive. Due to increased torque, power, and efficiency, permanent magnet motors are favored2. Researchers are now paying closer attention to non-magnet motors, such as induction motors (IMs)3, due to the rising costs of rare-earth magnets2. Still, IMs particularly stand out among non-magnet motors due to their advanced technology, rugged structure, affordability, and improved control methods4. Choosing an effective control technique can enhance the drive performance. The choice of the flux value is one of the variables that affect the system’s effectiveness and performance. An EV’s wide range of speeds and load torque variations requires a dynamic control approach to satisfy driving conditions. Therefore, selecting an appropriate reference flux value is necessary and important5.

The control schemes that meet the above requirements are continually introduced as superior performance is essential in electric drives. Advances in semiconductor technology and digital signal processing have enabled the development of high-performance control algorithms for IMs. Moreover, motor control systems such as vector and direct torque control are currently the most used methods for controlling IM and offer straightforward substitutes for effective EV drive control. Several papers in the literature presented optimizing control schemes that apply to general-purpose control systems or to the particular issues facing EVs. In6, the key contributions are energy-saving control techniques that yield good results when the motor parameters are precisely known.

In the other scenario, control strategies typically seek to maximize the machine torque output during the flux-weakening7. These flux-weakening methods state that lookup tables or explicit expressions of variables, such as motor speed, motor currents, and required torque, should be used to update the machine’s optimal flux value. Accurately determining the base speed, which depends on the actual DC-link voltage and the drive’s overload capability, is crucial to the drive’s performance at high speeds. These techniques rely on a solid understanding of different machine parameters. Some significant advancements in developing a reliable field-weakening technique for IMs have been made over the last two decades8. Due to higher losses in light loading conditions, drives using vector control schemes are typically operated at rated flux, resulting in lower efficiency9.

The strategies identified in the literature for improving electric drive efficiency to address the abovementioned problems can be divided into three categories. These are search control (SC), loss model control (LMC), and hybrid-based approaches to optimize the flux. The SC method is a feedback process that reduces the flux until the electrical input power settles at its minimum for a given torque and speed, thereby weakening the flux. The SC’s performance is not affected by the machine’s specifications. Yet, it has a slow response time, a high convergence time, and torque ripple and oscillations in the vicinity of the optimum value10. In control algorithms, convergence problems and the optimal value of reference flux oscillation are undesirable when operating points change frequently in EV applications. The LMC-based method entails determining a flux level that minimizes the losses using the machine model to calculate the losses11. However, LMC needs precise knowledge of machine parameters to obtain an accurate formula for determining optimal flux. LMC’s primary benefit is simplicity, but its effectiveness depends on the accuracy of the motor model12.

In13, the optimal flux optimization utilizing the loss minimization approach is presented to tackle the convergence problem in the SC approach by reducing the losses. The control technique uses a lookup table to store the optimum reference flux values. The loss minimization methods are also susceptible to changes in the motor parameters. As a result, a divergence from the ideal value of reference flux results from parameter errors. Moreover, a discrepancy between the loss model and the machine parameters at any point during operation could cause the drive’s performance to decline. It is difficult to accurately measure motor parameters to create an exact loss model. Further adding to the complexity is the possibility of initial values changing during vehicle runtime due to loading conditions and continuous operation14. In15, a loss model based on leakage inductance is presented to improve efficiency and reduce torque ripple. The drive system’s efficiency is significantly higher than at rated flux.

In16, a voltage feedback loop is added to the LMC method to enhance the dynamic performance of the drive. This reduces the parameter’s sensitivity. Considering inverter and converter losses,17 developed a loss model to improve LMC effectiveness. However, adding these losses complicates things. Because of variations in the core and magnetizing resistance, efficiency does not noticeably rise over the rated flux operation. The optimal current, analytically determined in18, was compared to the particle swarm optimization algorithm-based optimal current to determine which scheme was more effective. The results show that both approaches are more efficient than the rated flux operation, while the latter is more resilient to parameter changes. The hybrid control technique combines the benefits of LMC and SC. Most hybrid control approaches use SC for steady-state operation, and LMC is used during the starting of the IM drive19. This technique involves calculating the initial optimal reference flux value for the search controller using the loss model technique and then searching for the final optimum value of reference flux. These configurations may quickly and reliably determine the reference flux, even when the parameters vary. However, in this kind of search algorithm, the precision of the measured power signal for the SC, and the initial value computation from the loss model approach, all affect how well the hybrid technique performs20. Under light load conditions, the IM drive’s operation at rated rotor flux results in decreased drive efficiency. Another method for obtaining the rotor flux is to use differentially computed rotor flux9. In this scenario, the drive operates effectively, but the lower rotor flux may limit its performance. The loss-equalizing rotor flux also provides efficient performance. However, it is less effective when speed changes due to q-axis current disturbances and requires pre-tuned controller gains.

In21, under various EV operating conditions, optimal rotor flux is found through constrained minimization of losses. The rotor flux is estimated using the optimal d-q components of stator current obtained from the constrained minimization. This process yields the constrained minimization-based rotor flux. Moreover, in contrast to differentially computed flux control, this approach can be optimized without needing a pre-tuned PI controller. An alternative approach to determining the optimal flux value for the required torque is presented in this paper. System performance is enhanced by limiting voltage and inverter current capabilities while optimizing machine torque by reducing stator current. The following are the key benefits of the proposed method: it does not demand an excessive amount of processing power, it is not sensitive to changes in motor parameters, it is easy to apply in real-world scenarios, and it does not suffer from convergence issues. The proposed approach for optimizing rotor flux is employed to examine and compare the drive with alternative approaches like LMC-based selection of reference flux and the conventional method, where reference flux is constant. Furthermore, an experimental setup is used to investigate drive dynamics and field weakening of IM. The experimental findings validate the effectiveness and feasibility of the proposed flux optimization approach for EV drivetrain performance enhancement.

It is worth noting that in the proposed method, the optimal flux does not depend on the IM parameters, thereby improving the resilience to parameter variations. It is necessary to reduce the current limit at overload by restricting x12. The maximum stator current value, Ismax, will be limited by setting x12 when the speed controller’s output signal is limited to (22).

Only x12 must be reduced to keep the total current within the inverter’s current capability. The variable x21 has priority in the control process in the field weakening range, and is optimized by forcing the dynamic value of x22* determined from , and x22 controller adjusts its value.

The control structure so far described ensures operation at maximum torque throughout the field weakening range by fully utilizing the voltage and current capability of the inverter. As speed decreases, the induced voltage decreases, and the voltage limit is reached as the inverter voltage approaches its maximum. This allows the system to react only to some limited extent, as the available voltage limit may be very small. Then, the flux-controlling variable controller (x22) is decreased to the excitation level so that operation at the maximum voltage limit can continue. The available voltage is just enough to maintain steady-state operation. Figure 10 illustrates a situation where the system operates at the voltage limit at base speed. As the speed increases, the magnitude of the rotor flux vector reduces since the operating point is transferred to the field-weakening. The waveforms of the maximum stator voltage, (αβ) stator currents, and (αβ) rotor fluxes during speed change from 0.1 to 2 p.u., are shown in Fig. 10b. The stator currents decrease rapidly in the field-weakening region and without the field-weakening region due to optimization to reduce the losses. The rotor flux is nearly optimized for both periods, where controllers work with or without the field-weakening region. Therefore, it is concluded that the proposed approach provides satisfactory wide-speed tracking performance. Even under overload, the excitation level must always be maintained as directed by the excitation control when working in the field-weakening region. The rotor flux is not sensitive to changes in rotor reference speed. Motor torque x12 is controlled very fast, and the square of rotor flux x21 is nearly optimized for periods where controllers work with or without a field-weakening region. This is accomplished by reducing the flux from the rated value by optimally reducing x22. Since the flux is reduced to an optimal level, the motor can maintain its maximum torque.

The main objective of the control strategies presented here is also to enhance the energy efficiency of the IM drive. In Fig. 16, the proposed approach of MTPF control techniques for efficiency optimization is presented. The traces of rotor speed, electromagnetic torque, square of the rotor flux, efficiency, and (α) axis rotor flux responses of the drive under constant rotor speed of 0.5 p.u., operating conditions are displayed in Fig. 16. The optimization significantly lowers the core loss. It increases efficiency by reducing the reference flux based on the machine’s performance. The torque is changed from 0.24 p.u., to 0.45 p.u., for the next 1.5 s in Fig. 16. Then, the optimal flux is raised and maintained at approximately optimal, resulting in optimal efficiency. In the nominal torque area, the optimal flux reaches rated motor flux 1.0 p.u. Due to the high-load operation, there is minimal variation between the rated and optimal fluxes. As a result, there is minimal variation in losses. Under load conditions, the efficiency of the proposed approach (η) has improved by 11.96% compared to the technique21 efficiency at low loads and by 3.45% at high loads. Therefore, the proposed control methods are a very cost-effective option for the transport industry from the controller’s perspective, as they not only provide better energy savings but also enhanced acceleration characteristics with lower computational complexity. Consequently, the IM drive with the proposed control methods will attain greater mileage. This again shows the effectiveness of the proposed MTPF strategies in improving IM efficiency and achieving overall energy savings. The MTPF technique is preferred over the traditional method outlined in this article because it is easier to implement with a decoupling approach and offers improved steady-state torque performance.

Conclusion

In this paper, MTPF is proposed to improve flux optimization online rather than looking for a lookup table. The proposed strategy for optimizing flux is used in the flux loop to determine the optimal operating flux in terms of the x22 corresponding to a specific operating condition. The optimal flux reduces input power consumption and decreases IM loss, improving the drive system’s overall efficiency. The state estimations at very low-speed operations are also improved with the improved flux regulation. These claims are confirmed by relevant experimental results. The proposed control strategy allows it to go into and out of the field weakening operation smoothly while utilizing the machine’s maximum torque output across the complete operating speed range. The proposed algorithm has the main benefits of (1) less reliance on machine parameters and eliminating the need to calculate base speed, which is typically dependent on machine parameters, motor current, and DC link voltage, and (2) an improvement in IM performance, which is beneficial in particular when the motor runs at a low speed. The uncertainty in the nominal parameters provides additional assurance that the proposed approach is robust to variations in machine parameters. The proposed flux optimization approach would be appropriate for use in traction drives powered by IMs, where optimizing efficiency and achieving excellent dynamic performance is crucial.

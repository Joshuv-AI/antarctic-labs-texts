An Axillobifemoral Bypass With an Unusual Intrathoracic and Intra-abdominal Course
Authors: Bogdan Bratu, Anne Lejay
Published: 2023

A 67 year old woman with morbid obesity and multiple iliac stenting underwent an ileocaecal resection with ileostomy. In the immediate post-operative course, she presented with bilateral acute limb ischaemia (Rutherford IIb) due to complete occlusion of the aorta and iliac arteries (A) in the context of sustained hypotension. Thrombectomy with bifemoral approach was unsuccessful and an urgent right axillobifemoral bypass was performed using a ringed polytetrafluoroethylene graft. The post-operative course was uneventful. Graft patency was confirmed by Duplex ultrasound, and the patient was discharged at Day 7. Computed tomography was performed six weeks later, as para-ileostomy herniation was noted. Unexpectedly, the route of the bypass was found to be intrathoracic and intra-abdominal (B). As the patient was asymptomatic, no surgical revision was attempted. This highlights the need for careful tunnelling, and the usefulness of potential additional incisions to create an optimal midaxillary subcutaneous tunnel.

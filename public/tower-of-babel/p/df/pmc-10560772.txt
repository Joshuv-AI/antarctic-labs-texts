Multi-criteria dispatch optimization of a community energy network with desalination: Insights for trading off cost and security of supply
Authors: Mahdieh Monemi Bidgoli, Mahdi Azimian, Vahid Amir, Mehdi Zarrati, Saeid Javadi, Soheil Mohseni, Alan C. Brent
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
The idea of community energy network is being advocated to enhance the elasticity of diverse energy systems required for efficiently integrating a substantial volume of distributed energy resources. On the other hand, the interest in renewables-based desalination systems has received significant interest recently to consider freshwater as an additional end-use product in the community energy network system. Within this context, this paper introduces a multifaceted method for community energy networks with a focus on desalination-capable systems. The central goals involve diminishing the cumulative long-term expenses of the configuration, all while concurrently augmenting the system's capacity to store electrothermal energy on a daily basis that varies – all aimed at enhancing the reliability and security of resource provisioning. Importantly, the model co-optimizes the community energy network expenditure and reserve capacities, whilst integrating electrical, thermal, and natural gas vectors, as well as providing a platform for supplying freshwater needs. The overall freshwater provisioning infrastructure incorporates a water storage system, a desalination unit, a water well component, and a water pumping system. Furthermore, for the purpose of enhancing the adaptability, the community energy network concept put forth here utilizes coordinated electrothermal responsive load initiatives. These are coupled with meticulously planned electrothermal reservoir setups to curtail the wastage of surplus renewable production amidst diverse origins of unpredictability. The normalized weighted sum method is employed to convert the proposed formulation to a single-objective problem that is amenable to commercially available solvers in GAMS software. Then, the modelling framework is adapted to a system populated for a hypothetical site. The results verify the validity of the model in yielding globally optimum results for complex community energy networks with intertwined vectors of energy and end-use products. They also indicate that relatively small raises in the size of the electric and thermal reservoirs – and insubstantial raises in the expenditure of the system – can have potentially significant impacts on the ability of the system in serving loads during contingency conditions. In particular, by implementing demand response programs a cost reduction of 2.07% is shown, which is significant in the day-ahead operational planning phase.

Introduction

Motivation

Climate change is increasingly threatening most climate-vulnerable regions, with desertification, extreme heat, water shortages and marine heat stress negatively impacting livability, as well as food and water security [1]. Simultaneously, the demand for energy is rapidly escalating due to factors such as expanding population, urbanization, economic advancement, digitalization, and heightened mobility, among various other factors. Accordingly, it is widely recognized that sectoral decarbonization is urgently needed to achieve net-zero targets by reshaping not only energy provision, but also energy consumption across the economy. This emphasizes the significance of adopting a comprehensive system-of-systems strategy to counteract, adjust to, and fortify resilience against climate change. This approach also hastens the transition towards environmentally-friendly energy solutions [2].

In this context, community energy networks have been recently promoted as an effective platform for addressing the challenges of increasingly complex smart integrated localized energy systems as the global need for clean, reliable, and affordable electricity continues to grow [3]. In more precise terms, a community energy network constitutes a multi-generation configuration wherein diverse energy vectors – usually encompassing electricity, heat, natural gas, and hydrogen – undergo conversion, storage, and distribution in an interconnected fashion. This arrangement is designed to enhance the overall system's adaptability [4]. Considering the integration of diverse energy mediums within such a system, a central concern arises regarding the optimal dispatch of it. This holds particularly true for the deployment of community energy networks in semi-arid, coastal areas, where the utilization of seawater desalination can address a portion of the requirements for freshwater supply. However, treating the integrated desalination units as an additional source of demand-side flexibility adds another layer of complexity to the overall optimal scheduling problem. This necessitates developing fundamentally new coordinated, coherent, and comprehensive solution approaches to pave the path for the globally optimum operation of such systems.

Review of existing studies: knowledge gaps

Recently, an emerging body of literature has directed its focus towards scrutinizing the optimization of dispatch scheduling for multi-generation systems, particularly accentuating community energy networks. This has paved the way for an all-encompassing exploration into the diverse facets concerning the enhancement of dispatch scheduling for multi-generation systems and community energy networks, as expounded in [5]. These references delve into the current methodologies present within the pertinent literature, subjecting them to evaluation based on their applicability, adaptability, resilience, scalability, coherence, and comprehensiveness. As an illustration, a notion of dispatch scheduling optimization that is attuned to uncertainty is introduced in [6] for a hypothetical community energy network amalgamating combined heat and power (CHP) units, electrical storage configurations, gas-fired heat generators, electric vehicles (EVs), and renewable energy sources (RESs). This method showcases the ability to holistically navigate a spectrum of sources entailing parametric uncertainty. This encompasses aspects such as residential and EV-charging loads forecasted in advance, energy market prices, and the power outputs derived from on-site RESs. Additionally, the pursuit of optimizing dispatch for multiple community energy networks is probed in [7,8], especially within the context of pioneering transactive thermal energy frameworks. These frameworks are devised to minimize the collective operational expenses, all while concurrently mitigating overall carbon dioxide emissions. This is undertaken while staying attuned to the requisites of the community energy network for electrical, thermal, and cooling loads.

Additionally, a hydrogen storage-supported community energy network system is introduced in [9], demonstrating its capability to accommodate a substantial portion of localized RESs, even amidst a high penetration of plug-in EVs. The optimization of this conceptualized system's schedules is facilitated through a proposed stochastic energy management modeling framework. This framework employs the Monte Carlo simulation method to quantify uncertainties linked to forecasts of electrical and thermal energy market prices, as well as the electrothermal loads on the system and the power outputs of RESs. In another prominent instance within the literature, a community energy network is modelled in [10], which is driven primarily by onsite wind turbines (WTs) and proven by a dedicated air storage. To optimize the dispatch of the modelled system, a stochastic approach is formulated to characterize the uncertain behavior of WTs, along with electrothermal demands. Similarly, the stochastic optimization is employed in [11] to quantify variability of wind power and load while solving the dispatch optimization problem of a community energy network. Moreover, time-series parametric uncertainties are addressed using the chance-constrained approach in [12] while solving the dispatch optimization problem of a system consisting of power and natural gas sub-networks.

A substantial body of existing literature also concentrates on optimizing systems that integrate diverse configurations of energy storage components, aiming to enhance the overall dispatch optimization. For instance, in [13], a multi-objective dispatch optimization framework is introduced. This framework is tailored for setups that include combined battery storage and compressed air storage. In another instance, a stochastic game-theoretic community energy network dispatch optimization framework is presented in [14], which can effectively deal with incomplete information about the associated system dynamics using the optimally integrated thermal and battery storage in the presence of cogeneration units. To this end, a comprehensive storage model is formulated that adequately addresses the associated energy losses and depreciation costs. More specifically, the proposed method has been shown to decrease the reliance on relatively costly cogeneration operations, particularly by efficiently addressing the associated uncertainties.

Similarly, an innovative dispatch optimization framework for community energy networks is put forward in [15]. This framework thoroughly characterizes the interlinked electrical, heat, and cooling energy reservoir elements, taking into account energy dissipation and decline expenditures. Furthermore, it addresses the practicable operational range of cogeneration units. The capability of the proposed modeling framework is put to the test on typical summer and winter days across various case studies, seeking to analyze impacts extensively. These examinations encompass utility cost/benefit evaluations, the adept management of EVs through smart charging/discharging, the orchestration of controllable appliances, and the vital role of integrating a spectrum of energy storage systems. Moreover, the optimized dispatch of community energy networks enhanced with multi-storage technologies is investigated in [16,17]. In these studies, the introduced predictive intelligent energy scheduling modeling framework is equipped to convert diverse energy vectors reciprocally, enabling storage within the integrated hybrid storage on the basis of forecasts of electrical, thermal, and cooling load vectors. Consequently, the involved energy carriers derive augmented interconversion capabilities. As a result, the implicated energy carriers acquire heightened interconversion capabilities. Correspondingly, the linked energy carriers reap enhanced interconversion advantages. Furthermore, the scholars in [18] have advanced their exploration and crafted a multi-criteria iteration of the earlier discussed community energy network dispatch approach, characterized by an enriched interplay among energy carriers. This progression facilitates the examination of optimal compromises between minimizing overall operational expenses and curbing carbon emissions.

Significant attention has also been devoted to the utilization of optimally integrated demand response strategies while scheduling smart community energy networks. For example, specific demand response-integrated stochastic programming models are formulated in [19] for the dispatch of community energy networks consisting of hydrogen storage, cogeneration, heat generators, and electrothermal storage. Accordingly, based on numeric simulation results, it has been conclusively shown that leveraging specifically formulated demand response-integrated community energy network dispatching models that benefit from improved system flexibility are able to significantly reduce the costs associated with serving the electrical, heating, and gas demands. The results have also brought to light the importance of a well-balanced utilization of responsive load and storage scheduling for community energy networks. That is, the results suggest that these two are complementary technologies, as opposed to conflicting resources. This conclusion is supported by similar findings in related studies within the literature, such as [20,21], which have studied the optimal trade-offs between energy storage and demand response capacities using ideas borrowed from cooperative game theory, and specifically solved the derived problems using the Shapley value concept.

Furthermore, there is a growing acknowledgment of the significance of multi-energy demand scheduling initiatives. These programs entail harnessing flexibility provisions from all energy vectors within the system, leading to a noteworthy enhancement in the optimality of energy dispatch solutions. This effect becomes particularly pronounced in the context of a community energy network housing diverse energy storage technologies. In this scenario, multiple energy storage setups and price-triggered electrical, heating, and cooling demand-side management initiatives are concurrently taken into account. This approach is exemplified in [22] through the optimal dispatch of a community energy network that accommodates EV charging loads. The objective is to maximize flexibility across various energy vectors. Also, a similar intelligent operational scheduling model is nested within the outer optimal investment planning layer of a joint design and dispatch method tailored to community energy networks in [23]. In terms of mathematical formulation, the problem is divided into the leader (investment planning) and follower (operational scheduling) stages and solved using the Benders decomposition.

Additionally, community energy networks are increasingly equipped with seawater desalination facilities to serve the freshwater needs of geographically isolated coastal communities that have limited access to freshwater. The utilization of existing technologies for seawater desalination is characterized by significant energy intensity, primarily attributed to the energy demands of isolating salts and various dissolved constituents from water. However, integrating such facilities into a community energy network allows shifting its operational periods to the hours of the day when there exists excess non-dispatchable renewable power generation subject to the customers’ freshwater requirement constraints. Accordingly, the integration of such flexible load centers could have significant potential in improving the overall cost-effectiveness of community energy networks. In this context, a novel joint electricity and water management framework is proposed for a smart island in [24], where the water supply system consists of water wells, water storage units, and desalination units. Drawing from simulations conducted on a test system, it has been demonstrated that linking electricity and water supply networks can significantly improve the profitability of desalination-addressable community energy networks, whilst simultaneously playing a potentially significant role in addressing water shortage problems in coastal areas.

In another notable instance, a multi-criteria problem is formulated for the integrated management of energy and water supply systems in [25]. Within this framework, the comprehensive operational costs of the system and the quantity of water extracted from groundwater reservoirs are simultaneously optimized, yielding a collection of solutions that adhere to Pareto optimality. Furthermore, an integrated energy and cold water hub is proposed in [26], where the absorption chillers are driven by electricity generated from solar and biomass resources in normal operating conditions, and by fossil fuel-fired generators in shortages of renewable resources. Moreover, the most widely used technologies for seawater desalination, namely multi-effect desalination and reserve osmosis, are comparatively analyzed in [27] in the context of a community energy network given their different constraints in terms of continuous operation. In addition, a novel energy management framework is proposed in [28] and tested on a large-scale multi-carrier community energy network conceptualized for a smart city to serve nearly all the energy needs of households, particularly power, heat, and natural gas, as well as daily water needs, in a fully integrated manner.

Novel contributions

In direct response to the gaps in knowledge identified, this paper introduces an innovative and all-encompassing multi-criteria optimization framework for dispatch planning. The primary goal is to drive down the overall operational expenses of a theoretical community energy network, while concurrently elevating the cumulative capacity of stored electrothermal energy. This concerted effort aims at fortifying the system's robustness and ensuring a reliable supply. The conceptualized community energy network has an intertwined structure integrating electricity, heat, and natural gas energy vectors, whilst additionally supplying the freshwater needs using the optimally integrated desalination units. Importantly, the proposed model leverages specifically developed demand-side management frameworks for electrical and heating responsive loads to improve the network's flexibility necessary for reducing the size of the integrated capital-prohibitive storage devices. Also, the modelling framework considers specific constraints for the water supply system, which enable serving the electricity needs of the water desalination units in an optimal manner. Problem definition

As elucidated earlier, the primary objective of this paper is to establish a robust foundation for the comprehensive energy dispatching of community energy network systems that amalgamate power, heating, and natural gas energy domains. This endeavor encompasses the seamless integration of an optimally orchestrated freshwater supply system.

To this end, first, in accordance with the structure shown in Fig. 1, a highly intertwined community energy network system incorporating the relevant sub-networks is conceptualized. The conceptual community energy network facilitates the provision of nearly all the energy and freshwater requirements for individuals residing in climate-vulnerable regions grappling with intense heat, water scarcity, and elevated marine temperatures.Fig. 1Structure of the conceptualized network.Fig. 1

Structure of the conceptualized network.

In this setting, a specifically developed energy scheduling problem is needed to optimize the system-wide dispatch of the controllable components from a total operational cost perspective, whilst meeting the quality of service needs associated with electrical, thermal, and freshwater loads on the system. Conversely, to enhance the network's resilience in the face of climatic fluctuations, the focus shifts toward optimizing the capacity of energy in store within the integrated energy storage units. This objective, however, contradicts the goal of minimizing dispatch expenditures.

This underscores the potential significance of demand-side management initiatives, which hold the capacity to substantially level both electrical and thermal load patterns. This, in turn, diminishes the necessity for financially demanding onsite electrical and heating storage. Simultaneously, the reliability and security of the network are augmented, owing to the incorporation of demand management solutions at the customer level. In this setting, demand response resources can also allow for using a certain proportion of energy stored in local storage devices to be used only during contingency conditions. More specifically, well-designed and cost-effective demand response programs enable the system operator to rely on customer-level load reductions in lieu of incentive payments or through wholesale cost-reflective mechanisms, thereby allowing the system operator to allocate a network reliability-dependent percentage of the energy stored in storage devices for discharge to local loads during emergency conditions, such as unplanned grid outages or unforeseen reductions in the power outputs of onsite variable renewable energy generators.

In other words, such unused storage capacity could play the role of spinning reserves in conventional power systems, which can compensate for potential power shortages or frequency drops. Nonetheless, an escalation in the quantity of untapped storage size corresponds with a raise in the overall operational expenses of the system. This development contradicts the primary goal of cost minimization prevalent in studies centered around dispatch optimization. Consequently, the imperative arises for a multi-criteria optimization modeling framework. Such a framework must not only seek to curtail the system's operational expenditures but also optimize the available electrothermal energy reserves, reserved to function as energy storage-backed virtual spinning reserves. This function is particularly pertinent within the context of electrothermal responsive load resources. Accompanying this, a fitting method is required to appropriately assign weights to the competing objectives, aligning with the operator's inclinations.

Solution methodology

The proposed energy management optimization framework tailored to community energy networks is formalized in a dual-objective setting with the goals of minimizing the associated costs whilst simultaneously maximizing the overall energy in store – to increase the system's resilience to unplanned outages and unforeseen reductions in power outputs of onsite variable renewable energy generators.

Amongst the mainstream multi-objective optimization algorithms in the literature, notably the weighted-sum algorithm, the ε-constrained algorithm, and the Tchebyshev method, the weighted-sum algorithm was chosen in this study due to its advantages of enabling the user to synthesize a collection of objectives into a singular objective by incorporating each individual objective, multiplied by a weight stipulated by the user, which provides a platform for selecting the desired focus on each objective, as well as making it readily applicable to any number of objective functions [35]. It is also worth noting that given the different scales of the considered objective functions in this study, a normalization process has been carried out to enable transforming the overall dual-objective optimization into a one-objective variant. Accordingly, the associated normalization is undertaken in accordance with the corresponding best-case and worst-case values obtained from solving the model in a single-objective way – that is, considering the costs or energy in store alone. Subsequently, the objective functions of interest are combined using appropriately weighted factors as expressed in Eq. (60) to simultaneously optimize the operating costs and system reserves.(60)minw1cost|costIdeal−costNadir|−w2SEH|SEHIdeal−SEHNadir|In Eq. (60), the factors w1 and w2 are the relative importance of the selected criteria from the community energy network operator's viewpoint, subject to meeting the constraint w1+w2=1. Also, the variables cost and SEH respectively are the overall dispatch expenditure and the overall stored energy in electrical and heat storage components. Further, the superscripts Ideal and Nadir respectively denote the ideal1and nadir2 points in the entire Pareto-optimal set of solutions. Note that the larger the value set for an objective, the greater its perceived importance. Note that the associated normalization process allows for the applicability of the model to heterogeneous functions [35].

In our preliminary studies, we also evaluated various multi-objective optimization techniques, including metaheuristics such as genetic algorithms (GAs) and multi-objective particle swarm optimization (MOPSO), as well as nondominated sorting genetic algorithm (NSGA) and multi-criteria simulated annealing. However, due to the complexity and non-convexity of the problem at hand, these methods were not able to converge in polynomial time or provide robust solutions.

Considering the approximate nature of metaheuristic-based approaches and their sensitivity to problem variations, we chose the weighted sum method for its efficiency and ability to handle the high dimensionality and nonlinearity of our problem. We conducted thorough experiments and analysis to verify the utility of the weighted sum technique for our specific case.

In this study, we assigned equal weights (w1 = w2 = 0.5) to the two objectives, namely cost and resilience (characterized by the total stored energy), reflecting a balanced consideration of both criteria in the dual-objective optimization process.

Mathematically, the complete dual-objective energy scheduling optimization is a mixed-integer non-linear programming (MINLP) problem with two decision criteria and uncertainties involved. In this analysis, the MINLP framework is coded in GAMS and is solved using the commercially available solver CPLEX [12].

Furthermore, the Monte-Carlo simulation method supplemented by the fast-forward scenario reduction technique [22] was employed to convert the deterministic model into a stochastic one by generating probabilistic scenarios for wholesale prices, solar irradiance, and wind velocity in accordance with the associated probability density functions (PDFs) presented in Section 3.2.2 [36].

Limitations

Despite substantial progress beyond the current state-of-the-art in multi-criteria operational dispatch optimization for community energy networks, this paper's novel contributions are subject to certain limitations as:1.Simplified Assumptions: To formulate a tractable optimization framework, certain simplifications and assumptions were made concerning component behaviors, energy demand patterns, and market conditions. Although these abstractions facilitated the development of a comprehensive model, they may not fully encapsulate the complexities of real-world energy systems, potentially yielding disparities between model outputs and actual system behaviors.2.Scalability: The optimal dispatch framework put forth in this paper is tailored to a conceptual community energy network with a fixed number of components and specific energy vectors. Scaling the model to real-world, large-scale community energy networks with more diverse and interconnected components may pose computational challenges. Future investigations should explore scalable algorithms and optimization techniques to accommodate larger, more intricate energy systems.3.Uncertainties: While the model accounts for stochastic aspects of energy production and demand to some extent, uncertainties in renewable energy availability, energy prices, and other market variables are inherent in real-world systems. Integrating robust optimization or scenario-based approaches to manage uncertainties would enhance the model's reliability and applicability in practical scenarios.4.Real-Time Operation: The proposed optimization framework primarily focuses on system-level operational schedules determined in advance. In dynamic energy markets where real-time changes in demand and supply occur, pre-determined schedules may necessitate adaptability. Extending the model to incorporate real-time operation and control strategies would be valuable for enhancing the community energy network's responsiveness and adaptability.5.Technological Constraints: While the model considers various operational constraints, it assumes idealized performance characteristics of components, such as energy conversion efficiencies and response times. In practice, real-world devices may exhibit nonlinearities, ramp-up times, and other technological limitations that could impact the feasibility of certain operational decisions.6.Economic Considerations: The proposed bi-objective optimization framework centers on minimizing total operating costs and maximizing stored energy. Nevertheless, other economic factors, such as investment costs, revenue generation, and carbon pricing, play a crucial role in community energy network planning and decision-making. Integrating these additional economic considerations could conduct a more extensive and thorough analysis of the community energy network's economic viability.7.Validation and Real-world Application: Although extensive simulations and sensitivity analyses were conducted, validation of the proposed model with real-world data from operational community energy networks is necessary to verify its effectiveness and accuracy in practical settings.8.Optimization Approach: The weighted sum method may not consistently produce unsupported efficient solutions in multi-objective optimization problems, depending on the chosen weights. While it can generate efficient solutions, the method's inability to guarantee unsupported efficient solutions may limit its performance in certain scenarios.

Conclusions and future work

This paper has proposed an innovative model for the optimal operational scheduling of a conceptual community energy network as a smart localized energy system integrating electrical, thermal, gas, and water networks. More specifically, the modelling framework put forth allows for determining the system-wide globally optimum schedules of the controllable components of the conceptualized non-dispatchable renewables-rich community energy network with integrated power and heating storage devices, as well as the heat generator, the combined heat and power generator, and water desalination units, whilst making effective use of power and heating responsive loads. To this end, in addition to a specifically designed operational cost minimization model, the normalized weighted sum method has been utilized to develop a multi-objective variant of the model to enable minimizing energy costs in conjunction with maximizing the energy content of electrical and heating storage media – which enhances the security of supply of the system. The results yielded from populating the modelling framework for a hypothetical system have shown the effectiveness of the multi-objective demand response-integrated model in optimally scheduling the operation of highly intertwined power, heating, gas, and water sub-networks in the hub, particularly with regard to the charge/discharge dispatching of dedicated storage media, which has a significant part in increasing the network's flexibility. Consequently, this has provided an effective platform for better coping with the uncertain behavior of variable renewables and loads. The results have shown the utility of the formulated responsive load resources in reducing the system's dispatch expenditures. In particular, by implementing demand response programs a cost reduction of 2.07% is shown, which is significant in the day-ahead operational planning phase. Finally, simulating various instances of the multi-objective model has provided multi-variant evidence supporting the idea that as the desired level of hedging against contingency conditions increases, the required minimum allowed energy content of the storage devices does so as well, which conversely, increases the overall dispatch expenditure of the system.

Future work might aim to systematically characterize the interactions of end-consumers, the community energy network operator, and the utility grid in procuring demand response resources using ideas borrowed from cooperative and non-cooperative game theory. Another recommended area for future work is to additionally quantify a range of techno-economic uncertainties, such as the uncertainties in the efficiencies and per-unit operational costs of the integrated components, as well as time-series datasets including power and heating loads, in addition to wholesale electricity and natural gas network prices. Future research can also delve into the development of sophisticated adaptive weighting strategies that dynamically adjust the weights during the optimization process. These strategies could be designed to respond to changes in the objective landscape or the exploration-exploitation trade-off, leading to more effective and efficient convergence to diverse and well-distributed Pareto-optimal solutions.

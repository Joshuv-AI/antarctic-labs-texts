Diagnostic and evaluative efficiency of 68Ga-FAPI-04 in skeletal muscle injury
Authors: Yiqun Wang, La Li, Hongde Wang, Jin Cheng, Cancan Du, Luzheng Xu, Yifei Fan, Xiaoqing Hu, Yu Yin, Ruimin Wang, Yingfang Ao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSkeletal muscles are vital for daily function, yet assessing their injuries remain challenging. We aimed to elucidate the effectiveness of 68Ga-FAPI-04 in evaluating skeletal muscle remodeling.ResultsC2C12 cells were subjected to graded H2O2 stimulation in vitro, revealing an initial rise and subsequent decline in fibroblast activation protein (FAP) expression as H2O2 concentration increased. In vivo, a murine triceps surae injury model was created using various solutions to simulate normal repair, mild repair failure, and severe repair failure. Assessments were conducted on days 1, 3, 7, and 14 using PET, MRI, and ultrasound. With 68Ga-FAPI-04, the normal and mild repair failure groups showed significantly higher SUVmax and T/B ratios on day 1 compared to the severe repair failure group. These values gradually decreased in the normal repair group, becoming negligible after day 7. MRI results for the normal repair group showed low to moderate signal intensity by day 7. A clinical study retrospectively evaluated post-hip arthroplasty patient images at intervals of 1 month, 2–3 months, 5–6 months, and over 7 months. In these patients, 18F-FDG SUVmax and volume remained relatively stable over time, while 68Ga-FAPI-04 SUVmax initially increased, then decreased, with a consistent reduction in volume.ConclusionIn skeletal muscle injuries, FAP demonstrates a distinctive mechanism of action, and 68Ga-FAPI-04, in comparison to other tests, more precisely captures alterations in lesion site uptake intensity and volume.Trial registrationTrial registration: ChiCTR2000041204. Introduction

Skeletal muscles play crucial roles in supporting biomechanical and physiological body functions, including movement, respiration, temperature regulation, and energy metabolism [1]. They are prone to various injuries resulting from exercise, accidents, or traumatic events [2]. Timely and accurate diagnosis following skeletal muscle injury is crucial for ensuring effective treatment and rehabilitation. Early detection can help identify subtle or chronic injuries, preventing further deterioration due to delayed intervention. Additionally, monitoring the progression of injury and predicting outcomes in severe cases is essential for developing appropriate treatment strategies.

Hardy et al. [3], used a murine model of tibialis anterior muscle injury induced by cardiotoxins and other agents to demonstrate that pathology could adequately reflect the process of muscle remodeling. However, pathological examinations are limited by the need to euthanize animals, making continuous observations in the same animal over time impossible.

Therefore, ultrasonography and MRI are the more commonly used muscle examination methods in clinical practice [4, 5]. Despite several advantages, ultrasound systems lack standardized criteria for assessing muscle repair and can be subject to operator bias. In contrast, MRI has specific sequences for detecting muscle edema and fat replacement and provides quantitative methods and standardization [6, 7]. However, it is time-consuming and noisy and may be less effective in patients with metallic implants.

Detection of protein- and molecular-level changes for staging, monitoring, or even screening has now become the pertinent research focus [8]. Positron emission tomography (PET) is a molecular imaging modality that organically combines the visual aspect of imaging with the high sensitivity and specificity of molecular examinations, making it a representative examination in precision medicine. Kimura et al. [9] diagnosed patients who developed iliopsoas tendinitis after hip replacement surgery using 18F-FDG, indicating its potential to detect lesions earlier than pathological examination. However, its mechanism is based on glucose metabolism and has relatively poor specificity [10]. In a previous study, non-specific 18F-FDG uptake in the bone, blood vessels, and other areas contributed to reduced efficiency of imaging analysis [11]. However, with the emergence of 68Ga-FAPI-04 [12], applications have expanded beyond oncology to other fields such as autoimmune diseases and infections [13, 14]. Previous studies have demonstrated increased fibroblast activation protein (FAP) levels in activated fibroblasts, healing wounds, and fibrotic tissues [15, 16]. Theoretically, 68Ga-FAPI-04 has the potential to diagnose and evaluate skeletal muscle remodeling following injuries. Therefore, we aimed to observe FAP changes in muscle cells and analyze the diagnostic role of 68Ga-FAPI-04 in skeletal muscle remodeling.

Methods

All procedures involving animals were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of our institution (Protocol No. A2022061) and reported in accordance with the ARRIVE guidelines (Animals in Research: Reporting In Vivo Experiments) [17] and each group consisted of six mice, based on previous experience. This clinical study was approved by the ethics committee of our hospital (registered at the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry) on September 24, 2020. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients before they underwent PET/CT scans.

C2C12 culture and H2O2 stimulation

C2C12 cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) (HyClone, Logan, UT, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (#SH30396.03, Hyclone, Canada), 100 units/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin at 37 °C, in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2. At near-confluence (90%), the C2C12 cells were transferred to DMEM containing 2% horse serum (Gibco) to induce cell differentiation. After reaching complete coverage of the visual field (at approximately 9 days), well-differentiated myotubes were treated with a H2O2 gradient (Junsei Chemical Co., Ltd.) for 24 h, followed by quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR), western blot (WB), and immunofluorescence analyses.

Construction of animal models and examination plan

Based on previous studies and our preliminary experiment [18, 19], 75 µL of 1.2% BaCl2, 0.2 M NaOH, and 1.2 M NaOH were injected into murine triceps surae to simulate models of normal repair, mild repair failure, and severe repair failure, respectively. Each group consisted of six subjects. Imaging assessments were performed on postoperative days 1, 3, 7, and 14. Subsequently, the mice were euthanized, and tissue samples were collected for qPCR, WB, RNA sequencing (RNA-Seq), and pathological analyses.

qPCR and WB analysis

Cells and tissues were lysed using QIAzol Lysis Reagent and extracted using the RNeasy Mini Kit, following the manufacturer’s protocol (QIAGEN, Hilden, Germany). Subsequently, RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the RevertAid First-Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). qPCR was conducted on a QuantStudio™ Real-time PCR system using the SYBR® Green Master Mix (Invitrogen). The relative gene expression levels were determined using the 2−ΔΔCT method. Primer sequences employed in qRT-PCR for alpha-tubulin, FAP, myosin heavy chain, and interleukin 6 are provided in Supplementary Table 1.

Cells and tissues were lysed in 100 µL and 500 µL of radioimmunoprecipitation assay buffer containing 1% phosphatase inhibitor and 2% protease inhibitor separately. After 20 min of ice-cold lysis, the lysates were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for another 20 min. The protein concentration was determined using a bicinchoninic acid assay, and after normalizing the concentrations of each sample, sodium dodecyl sulfate was used for heating and denaturation.

WB was performed according to standard procedures (Genescript, Wuhan, China). Membranes were blocked with 3% bovine serum albumin and subsequently probed with antibodies against alpha-tubulin and FAP. The membranes were then washed in PBST and incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies. Immunoreactive bands were visualized by adding an enhanced chemiluminescence substrate.

Immunofluorescence and histopathology

C2C12 myotubes were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and permeabilized with 0.5% Triton X-100. Following a 60-min blocking step in 10% bovine serum albumin, plates were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against FAP (ab53066; Abcam), interleukin 6 (Invitrogen, PA5-47209), and myosin heavy chain (Sigma-Aldrich, M4276). The following day, sections were treated with secondary antibodies, including anti-goat IgG (ab150131; Abcam), anti-mouse IgG (ab1501018; Abcam), and anti-rabbit IgG (ab150073; Abcam), at room temperature for 1 h. After washing, the plates were mounted using a 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole-containing mounting medium (Beyotime, C1005).

For histopathology, the mice were euthanized, and their excised muscles fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde overnight. Subsequently, the tissues were subjected to standard dehydration in ethanol for paraffin embedding. Hematoxylin & eosin and Sirius red staining was performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. For immunohistochemical staining, paraffin-embedded sections were treated with 3% H2O2 for 15 min to inhibit endogenous peroxidase activity. Subsequently, 10% bovine serum albumin was applied for 1 h at 21 °C to block non-specific antigens. Sections were then incubated with specific primary antibodies against FAP and Col 1 overnight at 4 °C. Following this, the sections were exposed to horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies (ZSGB-BIO, PV-6001 and PV-9003) for 1 h at 21 °C.

RNA-Seq library preparation and data processing

Total RNA was extracted using the Total RNA Extractor (TRIzol) kit (B511311, Sangon, China) following the manufacturer’s protocol, with RNase-free DNase I treatment for genomic DNA contamination removal. The VAHTSTM mRNA-Seq V2 Library Prep Kit for Illumina® was used for library generation, starting with 1 µg RNA per sample. Pooled libraries were subjected to paired-end sequencing using NovaSeq sequencers (Illumina).

Quality evaluation of the sequenced data was performed using FastQC (version 0.11.2), and raw reads were filtered using Trimmomatic (version 0.36) to remove adaptor sequences and low-quality bases. HISAT2 (version 2.0) mapped clean reads to the reference genome, and RSeQC (version 2.6.1) and Qualimap (version 2.2.1) were used aligning result statistics and quality checks. BEDTools (version 2.26.0) was used to assess the gene coverage ratios. StringTie (version 1.3.3b) was used to compute gene expression values, and DESeq2 (version 1.12.4) was used to determine differentially expressed genes. Differentially expressed genes with q-value ≤ 0.001 and |FoldChange| ≥2 were considered significant.

18F-FDG and 68 Ga-FAPI-04 systhesis and small PET/CT imaging

18F-FDG synthesis followed the method described by Hamacher et al. [20] with modifications, using a computer-controlled apparatus. 68Ga-FAPI-04 was synthesized as previously outlined [21]. Small-animal PET scans were conducted using a Super Nova scanner (Pingseng Scientific, Jiangsu, China). Static imaging was performed by administering a dose of 3.7–7.4 MBq of 68Ga-FAPI-04 or 18 F-FDG, followed by a 5-minute PET scan taken 30 min post-injection. This timing was chosen to allow for optimal tracer uptake and image quality, facilitating accurate visualization and analysis of the biological processes under investigation. The metrics SUVmax and MTV were used for comparative analysis between groups. The Target/Background (T/B) ratio was defined as the SUVmax of the surgical side divided by the SUVmax of the contralateral normal muscle.

MRI and ultrasound

During MRI (3T MRI scanner, GE Healthcare, MR750, Milwaukee, WI), mice were anesthetized with 0.3% pentobarbital sodium (0.1 mL/10 g, i.p.) and inserted into a 16-channel mouse body coil. The MRI protocol consisted of two sequences. First, a T2-weighted rapid acquisition relaxation-enhanced sequence was acquired (repetition time [TR] = 1664 ms, time to echo [TE] = 42 ms, NEX = 6, slice thickness = 2 mm, slices = 9, field of view [FOV] = 6 × 4.5 cm2, matrix = 192 × 192, and acquisition time = 2:45 min). Next, a T2-weighted rapid acquisition relaxation-enhanced sequence with Dixon water and fat separation was acquired (TR = 1664 ms, TE = 24 ms, NEX = 2, slice thickness = 2 mm, slices = 9, FOV = 6 × 4.5 cm2, matrix = 192 × 192, acquisition time = 2:50 min).

Ultrasound (ZS3 EXP; Mindray, Shenzhen, China) assessments were performed on mice anesthetized with 3% (v/v) isoflurane. An L30-8 probe was used to detect the thickest part of the triceps surae. Muscle parameters, including thickness, texture, and echo, were measured, and scores were assigned based on the echo and texture manifestations of the muscles (Supplementary Table 2).

Clinical study

The clinical study assessed the performance characteristics of 18F-FDG and 68Ga-FAPI-04 within 1 month, 2–3 months, 5–6 months, and > 7 months after hip arthroplasty. Images were obtained from the pelvis to the knee using a time-of-flight PET/CT scanner (uMI510, United Imaging Healthcare, Shanghai, China). Scanning commenced one hour after administering 68Ga-FAPI or 18F-FDG (1.8–2.4 MBq per kilogram of body weight for 68Ga-FAPI, 4–6 MBq per kilogram of body weight for 18F-FDG), with an acquisition time of 4 min per bed position. A low-dose CT scan (120 kV, 30–50 mAs) was employed for anatomical localization and attenuation correction. The images were reconstructed using a standard ordered-subset expectation-maximization algorithm. All CT images were converted to a bone window. All PET images were transformed into SUV units by normalizing the activity concentration to the administered radiopharmaceuticals dose and the patient’s body weight after decay correction. For the delineation of MTV, regions with significantly elevated uptake on each slice were outlined individually and then merged across all slices to obtain the corresponding MTV. SUVmax was defined as the highest uptake value within the specified MTV region.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean. For both animal experiments and clinical studies, one-way analysis of variance followed by Tukey’s multiple comparison test was employed. All tests were two-tailed, and statistical significance was set at P < 0.05. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 25.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA), and GraphPad Prism 8.0.2 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA) was utilized to present the results.

Results

Changes in FAP in C2C12 myotubes

We exposed well-differentiated C2C12 cells (for approximately 9 days) to various concentrations of H2O2 (125, 250, 500, 1000, or 2000 µM). Our findings revealed a biphasic pattern of FAP expression, characterized by an initial increase followed by a decrease, with peak expression observed at 250–500 µM (Fig. 1A, D, and E). Additionally, we assessed the mRNA expression of myosin heavy chain and interleukin 6, which revealed a gradual reduction in myosin heavy chain expression with increasing H2O2 concentration, whereas interleukin 6 exhibited the opposite trend (Fig. 1B, C, and E). These results suggest that within a certain muscle damage threshold, FAP expression increases with heightened inflammatory stimuli. Beyond that threshold, FAP expression decreases.

Subsequently, we selected the genes with the most statistically significant differences in each group (Fig. 2B). The results revealed a gradual decrease in inflammatory factors in the normal repair group, whereas the mild repair failure group exhibited significant expression of inflammatory factors on day 7, and the severe repair failure group maintained substantial expression on day 14.

The gene ontology enrichment analysis (Fig. 2C) showed that on day 1, the mild and severe repair failure groups showed significant enrichment in proteins and genes related to skeletal muscle contraction, compared to the normal repair group. By day 14, the severe repair failure group showed significant enrichment in immune regulation, endocrine regulation, inflammation, and other immune-related terms, compared with the normal repair group.

As time progressed, the normal repair group showed no significant FAP expression by day 14, mild repair failure group exhibited some expression on day 14, while severe repair failure group maintained moderate expression.

Performance characteristics of 18F-FDG and 68Ga-FAPI-04 in animal models

For 18F-FDG, representative images are shown in Fig. 3A. The T/B ratio of surgery side to the non-surgery side on day 1 was significantly less than 1 in all three groups (Fig. 3B). Afterward, the T/B ratio in the normal repair group was higher (P<0.01); the severe repair failure group consistently showed a lower T/B ratio across all time points, while the mild repair failure group demonstrated a lower T/B ratio on the operated side on days 3 and 7, tending to be consistent on both sides by day 14. The SUVmax of 18F-FDG (Fig. 3B) showed a trend similar to that of the T/B ratio, but the variance in the SUVmax in the hind limb region was relatively large.

Representative images for 68Ga-FAPI-04 are shown in Fig. 3C. On day 1, the SUVmax and T/B ratio in the normal repair and mild repair failure groups were significantly higher than those in severe repair failure group (P<0.05), whereas the volume in the severe repair failure group was significantly higher than that in the other two groups (P<0.05). Pathological features

Normal repair group (Fig. 5A): On day 1, myocyte necrosis and a small amount of inflammatory cell infiltration was observed, with moderate to high intensity of FAP expression. Sirius red staining did not reveal significant collagen deposition, and Col 1 was weakly expressed. On day 3, the number of infiltrating inflammatory cells was increased; notably, no lymphocyte infiltration was observed. FAP expression was decreased compared to that on day 1, and Sirius red staining showed transient collagen deposition, with no significant change in Col 1 expression compared to that on the previous day. By days 7 and 14, histological examination revealed that the tissue had largely regenerated with centrally nucleated small basophilic fibers. Immunohistochemistry and Sirius red staining showed patterns consistent with those of normal repair.

Mild repair failure group (Fig. 5B): On day 1, diffuse necrosis affecting all components, including myofibers, connective tissue, and blood vessels, was observed in the dead zone, marked by the absence of viable cells. FAP showed moderate to high expression. On day 3, there was significant infiltration of inflammatory cells, including neutrophils, macrophages, and lymphocytes, with sustained FAP expression. Severe repair failure group (Fig. 5C): On days 1 and 3, nearly the entire field showed necrosis with low to moderate FAP expression. Sirius red staining did not reveal significant collagen deposition, and Col 1 expression was low. On days 7 and 14, a substantial number of lymphocytes and moderate neutrophil infiltration were observed. FAP expression remained moderate to high. Sirius red staining revealed a gradual increase in collagen deposition, and Col 1 expression increased gradually.

Performance characteristics of 18F-FDG and 68Ga-FAPI-04 in total hip arthroplasty

Data were collected from four, five, six, and nine hips, respectively, at intervals of < 1 month, 2–3 months, 5–6 months, and > 7 months (Fig. 6A, Supplementary Table 4) to assess the performance of 18F-FDG after hip arthroplasty. Notable uptake was observed at < 1 month, and similar uptake was noted at the other three time points, without evident patterns of changes (Fig. 6C). Despite the noticeable MTV at < 1 month, uncertainties arose in MTV delineation. Notably, normal uptake was observed between the outermost layer of the adipose tissue and innermost joint space. At the other time points, non-specific uptake in structures, such as the blood vessels, bones, and muscles, impeded effective MTV delineation. Therefore, this metric was excluded from the statistical analysis.

To evaluate the performance of 68Ga-FAPI-04, data were collected from four, four, four, and five hips at intervals of < 1 month, 2–3 months, 5–6 months, and > 7 months, respectively, (Fig. 6B, Supplementary Table 4). Discussion

This study analyzed the diagnostic role of 68Ga-FAPI-04 in skeletal muscle remodeling, suggesting that it was potentially useful for evaluating skeletal muscle injuries and had superior efficiency over 18F-FDG in this regard. In the in vitro study, we observed a biphasic pattern of FAP expression, characterized by an initial increase followed by decrease, which was distinct from that of myogenic and inflammatory factors. Therefore, we propose that under moderate inflammatory stimuli, FAP expression increases with increasing severity of inflammation. However, when inflammation surpasses a certain threshold, FAP expression decreases, indicating the dual role of FAP in this context. In the in vivo study, given the corrosive action of acids and bases on soft tissues and considering studies using NaOH to create corneal burn [22] and pain models [23], we injected varying concentrations of NaOH into murine triceps surae. Although FAP was expressed in all three groups, its expression in the severe repair failure group was lower than in other two groups. This suggests that a certain level of FAP expression is crucial during the early stages of repair and indicates its potential to promote tissue regeneration. FAP continued to be expressed in both repair failure groups, implying that sustained FAP expression may have inhibitory effects on the repair process. These results highlight the spatiotemporal heterogeneity of FAP, demonstrating its dual role in muscle repair.

Previous studies have reported conflicting roles of FAP in rheumatoid arthritis, with some suggesting a pro-inflammatory role [24] and others proposing a therapeutic effect [25]. Contradictory reports on FAP also exist in the context of tumors [26, 27]. Based on the present study findings, we believe that studies on FAP function should transcend a simplistic categorization of its role as either advantageous or detrimental. Recognizing the dualistic nature of its functions is imperative for the thorough comprehension and clinical application of its significance. In the various examinations performed in this study, 68Ga-FAPI-04 had the most favorable performance in terms of lesion uptake and delineation of lesion range. However, the results of 18F-FDG were unexpected. On day 1, the uptake of 18F-FDG on the surgical side was lower than that on the healthy side across all three groups and at most time points for the mild and severe repair failure groups. This phenomenon may be attributed to reduced radiotracer metabolic efficiency due to local ischemia. Further investigation into this observation is warranted.

MRI is currently the most commonly used technique for assessing skeletal muscles, providing accurate visualization of muscle edema and fat replacement through various sequences. Despite the numerous advantages of MRI, image acquisition is relatively time-consuming, and not accessible to all patients [5]. Ultrasound has gained increasing attention in evaluating the skeletal muscle injury repair process, especially in chronic conditions, where this technique can easily detect fat replacement and fibrosis. Although ultrasound is bedside-applicable, its ability to identify disease states heavily relies on the operator’s experience [5, 28].

Because the use of PET/MRI becomes more prevalent [29, 30], our next research focus will be to delve deeper into studying skeletal muscle injuries using PET/MRI.

In terms of pathology, the normal repair group exhibited similar results with those reported in other studies [3, 31]. Some researchers induced a dead zone in the injury area using a freeze model [3, 32], rendering it devoid of the normal structure. A comparable situation was observed on day 1 in the mild repair failure group and on days 1 and 3 in the severe repair failure group. We attribute this to the severity of the injury, which prevented early infiltration of inflammatory cells and later domination of the infiltrate by lymphocytes, leading to fibrosis—a common manifestation of suboptimal muscle repair. Notably, in the mild repair failure group, a substantial decrease in muscle mass on the model side was observed on day 21 (Supplementary Fig. 1), indicating the occurrence of sarcopenia. However, further exploration of this model in the context of sarcopenia is required.

Postoperative evaluation of soft tissue remodeling using 18F-FDG has been explored since the early 21st century. Zhuang et al. [33] observed that 18F-FDG uptake tended to normalize within 3 months after bone fractures. However, given the significantly richer coverage of muscles around the hip joint compared to limb bones, they conducted 18F-FDG PET/CT examinations at 3, 6, and 12 months postoperatively in nine volunteers who underwent hip replacement surgery, to observe 18F-FDG performance in this context [34]. They found that even at 12 months, there was moderate-intensity uptake at the junction of the prosthetic head and neck.

In a previous study, our team successfully utilized 68Ga-FAPI-04 to differentiate between periprosthetic joint infection and aseptic loosening in patients post-hip arthroplasty [14]. Despite this success, critical questions remain, particularly regarding 68Ga-FAPI-04’s ability to accurately detect early-stage infections and monitor skeletal muscle remodeling progression over time in otherwise healthy patients. In this study, we employed 68Ga-FAPI-04 to monitor skeletal muscle remodeling by measuring SUVmax and MTV—key indicators of metabolic activity and tissue volume. This radiotracer’s sensitivity to dynamic tissue changes highlights its potential advantages over traditional imaging modalities like MRI or 18F-FDG. Unlike 18F-FDG, which is based on glucose metabolism and may show non-specific uptake in areas of inflammation or healing, 68Ga-FAPI-04 specifically targets FAP, predominantly expressed in activated fibroblasts within fibrotic and remodeling tissues. This specificity allows for a more precise evaluation of tissue remodeling, making 68Ga-FAPI-04 a promising tool for assessing treatment efficacy and tracking musculoskeletal injury progression. The incorporation of 68Ga-FAPI-04 into clinical practice could enhance decision-making by providing a deeper understanding of tissue repair and degeneration, complementing existing imaging techniques.

Conclusions

In conclusion, through a series of in vitro, in vivo, and clinical studies, our research suggests that 68Ga-FAPI-04 shows potential advantages and promising applications in the diagnostic assessment of skeletal muscle injuries. Furthermore, our findings indicate the dual nature of the role of FAP in the remodeling process, suggesting the need for further exploration of this phenomenon.

Preclinical evaluation and mechanistic analysis of antitumor effects of the novel anti-CD147 antibody DS-1471a in liver cancer models
Authors: Hiroshi Yuita, Sadanori Watanabe, Jun Tsukada, Yoshikazu Johmura, Sae Aratani, Hirokazu Ishikawa, Ryuichi Nakamura, Miki Yokoyama, Mari Hashimoto, Hideo Yukinaga, Huynh The Hung, Makoto Nakanishi, Keisuke Fukuchi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDS-1471a is a humanized antibody that targets CD147, a transmembrane glycoprotein frequently overexpressed in cancer cells, and has undergone a phase 1 clinical study for advanced solid tumors. To further investigate DS-1471a’s therapeutic potential and the underlying molecular mechanisms involved, we examined its efficacy in patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models of liver cancer. DS-1471a exhibited potent antitumor effects in multiple PDX models, with positive correlations observed between DS-1471a efficacy and expression of CD147 and related proteins such as SMAD4, ARF6, and FBXO22. Among these proteins, overexpression of FBXO22, a ubiquitin ligase, enhanced DS-1471a efficacy in liver cancer xenograft models. Mechanistic analysis showed that FBXO22 overexpression was associated with reduced expression of p21/CDKN1A, a cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor previously characterized as a degradation target of FBXO22, suggesting a potential regulatory relationship between these proteins. Moreover, in tumor models lacking p21, DS-1471a efficacy was also enhanced, whereas DS-1471a-resistant tumors expressed higher levels of p21 protein. Although direct causal relationships remain to be fully established, these results suggest possible mechanisms underlying DS-1471a efficacy involving not only CD147 expression but also FBXO22 and p21. Taken together, these preclinical findings indicate a potential clinical benefit of DS-1471a and provide useful mechanistic insights for liver cancer treatment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10637-026-01621-3.Keywords: Liver cancer, Patient-derived xenograft model, CD147, Antibody therapy

Introduction

Cancer is one of the leading causes of death globally and remains a challenge to cure despite the development of various therapies in recent decades [1]. Liver cancer, including hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), is particularly difficult to treat, with a poor prognosis and short survival period, especially in advanced or metastatic cases [2].

Despite recent advances in systemic treatment of HCC patients that have come from the approval of combination therapies consisting of atezolizumab plus bevacizumab after durvalumab plus tremelimumab as a first-line treatment for unresectable HCC and that have yielded significant improvement in overall survival compared to sorafenib, treatment options for HCC patients remain limited due to compromised liver function, highlighting the need for new therapies with better safety and efficacy profiles [3, 4].

CD147, also known as basigin or EMMPRIN (ECM metalloproteinase inducer), is a cell membrane glycoprotein that belongs to the immunoglobulin superfamily. It is highly expressed in many types of cancer and is associated with advanced clinical stage and poor prognosis in cancers, including pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) and liver cancer [5–8]. CD147 interacts with various proteins, including CD44, integrins, and monocarboxylate transporters 1/4 (MCT1/4), through its two Ig-like domains (Ig-LDs) [7]. CD44 and integrins play important roles in promoting cell survival, proliferation, and metastasis by binding to extracellular matrix (ECM) proteins [9, 10]. The MCT1 and MCT4 proteins are members of the monocarboxylate transporter group, which also regulate lactate transport. These molecules are thought to contribute to metabolic reprogramming or low-pH conditions in the cancer microenvironment [11]. Increased expression of CD147 and its complex has been linked to poor prognosis in several cancer types [12–14]. Various mechanisms of CD147 regulation have been reported, and its intracellular transport to the cell membrane is known to be mediated via interaction with HOOK1 during the recycling process [15, 16]. The proteasome-mediated degradation pathway via FBXO22, an E3 ubiquitin ligase, is another possible regulatory mechanism, which was reported to control multiple cellular processes by targeting several proteins, including CD147 and p21 [17].

We previously developed the novel anti-CD147 antibody DS-1471a (previously termed h4#147D), which induces antitumor effects in various human cancer xenograft models [18]. DS-1471a binds to CD147 and reduces its intracellular and cell surface expression, along with the expression of CD147 binding partners such as CD44, integrins α3 and α6, MCT1, and FAK. It also activates stress response signaling pathways such as JNK, MAPK, and SMAD, leading to apoptosis [18]. DS-1471a has shown promising efficacy in multiple xenograft models [18], and a clinical trial has been performed to evaluate its use in treating advanced solid tumors (NCT06074705). SMAD4 and KLF5 have been proposed as potential factors controlling the antitumor effects of DS-1471a downstream of the CD147 signaling pathway [18]. However, whether other molecular mechanisms are involved in the antitumor therapeutic effects of DS-1471a remains to be elucidated.

In this study, our objective was to investigate the antitumor efficacy of DS-1471a using liver cancer patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models, which better reflect the characteristics of clinical tumors [19]. We also examined the correlation between the efficacy of DS-1471a and several proteins related to the CD147 signaling pathway, protein degradation pathways, and genes associated with hepatocellular differentiation [18, 20–26]. Through the generation of genetically modified cell models both in vitro and in vivo, we attempted to determine the involvement of these molecules in the regulation of CD147 levels and DS-1471a efficacy. Furthermore, to gain insight into mechanisms of resistance, we established DS-1471a-resistant tumors through repeated administrations and examined molecules that influence DS-1471a sensitivity.

Methods

Antibody

DS-1471a, a humanized anti-CD147 IgG4 antibody (previously termed h4#147D), was generated as described previously [18]. Other antibodies used in this study are shown in Supplementary Table 1.

Cell lines

The human liver cancer cell lines Hep G2 (HB-8065) and SK-HEP-1 (HTB-52) were obtained from ATCC. HuH-7 was obtained from JCRB (JCRB0403). All cell lines were cultured in accordance with the suppliers’ instructions.

Patient-derived xenograft models

HCC PDX models were established as described previously [27–29]. The present study received ethics committee approval from National Cancer Centre Singapore (NCCS) as well as Singapore General Hospital and Daiichi Sankyo. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations and were in line with the Helsinki declaration. Table1 shows the clinical background information of the patients used to establish the PDX models. To investigate the effects of DS-1471a on the growth of HCC PDX models, female NOD scid mice aged from 5–8 weeks old (from InVivos) were transplanted subcutaneously with the indicated tumors. After tumor sizes reached approximately 100–150 mm3, the mice were randomized into two groups and treated with either 200 μl of diluted DS-1471a antibody (10 mg/kg body weight) or 200 μl of PBS via tail-vein injection weekly for the indicated number of days. Bidimensional measurements were performed once every 2 or 3 days and tumor volumes were calculated based on the following formula according to NCCS’s standard operation protocol: Tumor volume = [(Length)× (Width)2 × (π/6)]. Data were plotted as mean and standard error for each treatment group versus time. Values for mean tumor volume in the DS-1471a-treated group and mean tumor volume in the PBS-treated group on the final day of the antitumor study conducted at NCCS were used to calculate tumor growth inhibition (TGI, %) for each model with Microsoft Excel for Microsoft 365 (Microsoft Corp.) using the following formula: TGI (%) = (1 - mean tumor volume in the DS-1471a-treated group/mean tumor volume in the PBS-treated group) × 100. At the end of the study, the mice were euthanized by exsanguination under 2–3% isoflurane inhalation anesthesia. Blood was subsequently collected in anticoagulant EDTA tubes, tumor weights were recorded, and the tumors were harvested for subsequent analyses. A portion of each tumor was fixed in 10% formalin and embedded in paraffin. Plasma samples were prepared by centrifuging the anticoagulant EDTA tubes containing whole blood at 2,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C. The plasma was then collected and stored at −80 °C.

Results

DS-1471a shows antitumor efficacy in liver cancer PDX models

To investigate the therapeutic potential of DS-1471a for liver cancer, its antitumor efficacy was examined in HCC PDX models. Among the 18 evaluated models in this study, notable antitumor efficacy was observed in 7 models, with tumor growth inhibition (TGI) reaching 50% or higher (Table 1). In one representative model, administration of DS-1471a at 10 mg/kg resulted in significant tumor growth inhibition when analyzed at day 16, with no effect on body weight (Fig. 1a–d). The level of circulating DS-1471a in the blood 2 days after the last dose ranged from 47.6 to 217 μg/mL, and no clear relationship was found between plasma concentration of DS-1471a and TGI (Supplementary Table 3). The antitumor effects of DS-1471a were observed across both sexes, and different tumor grades (TNM stages) and hepatitis virus types (Supplementary Fig. 1a-c). Taken together, these findings support the therapeutic potential of DS-1471a against liver cancer.

Correlation between DS-1471a efficacy and protein expression levels of biomarker candidates in liver cancer PDX models

To identify molecules that affect DS-1471a antitumor effects, correlations between the expression levels of multiple proteins and DS-1471a efficacy were examined in the HCC PDX models. We first focused on CD147 as the target of DS-1471a. Expression levels of CD147 protein showed a significant correlation with the efficacy of DS-1471a (r = 0.7832, p < 0.0001) (Fig. 2a). Subsequently, immunohistochemical (IHC) staining for CD147 was also performed using the same tumor tissues from the 18 models to further examine localization and expression of CD147 within the tumors, and expression levels in the PDX models were evaluated via quantification of the IHC signal intensity and expressed as the CD147 H-score. CD147 staining was mainly observed on the tumor cell membrane (Fig. 2b). CD147 H-scores were well correlated with the CD147 protein levels determined by capillary electrophoresis (Supplementary Fig. 1 d; r = 0.7079, p = 0.0022), and a significant correlation was observed between CD147 H-score and DS-1471a efficacy (Fig. 2b; r = 0.7395, p = 0.0011). In addition to CD147, further analyses were performed for candidate proteins related to CD147 regulation and DS-1471a’s proposed mechanisms of action. In a previous study, expression levels of CD147, SMAD4, and KLF5 showed good correlation with DS-1471a efficacy [18]. In this study, we added additional factors, including FBXO22, ARF6, and HOOK1, which are related to intracellular degradation and trafficking of CD147 [15, 17, 22, 26]; AFP, which is implicated in the differentiation of hepatocellular carcinoma [24, 25]; and S100A4 and ANXA2 [20, 21], which interact with the CD147 complex (Supplementary Fig. 1e). Among these proteins, CD147 expression showed the strongest correlation with DS-1471a efficacy, while expression of SMAD4, AFP, ARF6, and FBXO22 also showed a significant correlation (Fig. 2a). These results raise the possibility that CD147 and multiple CD147-related factors could be involved in the efficacy of DS-1471a in liver cancer tumor models.

Analysis of mechanisms controlling CD147 protein levels in liver cancer cells

Given that CD147-related proteins such as FBXO22, HOOK1, and ARF6 have been reported to contribute to the degradation and recycling of CD147 [15, 17, 22, 26], their decrease may lead to increased expression of CD147 in liver cancer cells. To examine the possible involvement of these candidate proteins in CD147 expression, degradation and recycling in liver cancer cells, siRNA-mediated knockdown experiments were performed with multiple cell lines. Six days after transfection, gene knockdown was confirmed (Supplementary Figs. 2a, 3a and 4a), and expression levels of CD147 and the other related markers were examined. In contrast to the previous studies, knockdown of AFP, FBXO22, S100A4, ANXA2, and HOOK1 exerted no substantial influence on total CD147 protein abundance (Supplementary Fig. 2b, 3b, and 4b).

To further examine the possible involvement of the above candidate proteins, liver cancer cell lines overexpressing each protein were generated and the effects on CD147 expression were examined. Although overexpression of FBXO22, HOOK1, and ARF6 was expected to decrease CD147 expression by enhancing the recycling system, overexpression of these proteins exerted no substantial influence on total CD147 protein abundance in liver cancer cell lines (Supplementary Fig. 5a and 5b). Similar results were obtained for other proteins such as S100A4 and AFP, consistent with the results of knockdown experiments (Supplementary Fig. 2–4). Taken together, these results indicate that, under the experimental conditions tested in the present study including the selected liver cancer cell lines and the steady state total protein measurements employed, overexpression or knockdown of FBXO22, HOOK1, S100A4, AFP, and ARF6 did not substantially alter total CD147 protein abundance. These findings do not exclude the possibility that these proteins could regulate CD147 through other biological mechanisms.

Increased FBXO22 enhances antitumor efficacy of DS-1471a in liver cancer models

Although CD147 protein level does not seem to be regulated by the examined factors in vitro (Supplementary Figs. 2–5), correlations between DS-1471a efficacy in liver cancer PDX models and expression levels of several proteins such as FBXO22 and ARF6 were observed. This prompted us to investigate their possible involvement in the efficacy of DS-1471a. To examine this issue, cell lines overexpressing multiple CD147-related proteins were generated and the impacts of increased expression of the candidate proteins on DS-1471a efficacy were examined. Among the tumor models overexpressing FBXO22, HOOK1, ARF6, and S100A4, only the FBXO22-overexpressing model showed enhanced antitumor effects with DS-1471a treatment (Fig. 3 and Supplementary Fig. 5a and 5c). This enhanced efficacy of DS-1471a in tumors overexpressing FBXO22 is consistent with the observed correlation between FBXO22 level and DS-1471a efficacy in liver cancer PDX tumor models (Fig. 2a). Taken together, these results highlight the potential contribution of FBXO22 to the antitumor efficacy of DS-1471a in liver cancer tumor models.

Potential involvement of FBXO22 and p21 in DS-1471a antitumor activity in liver cancer models

Since overexpression of FBXO22 enhances DS-1471a efficacy in liver tumors, we next examined the possible involvement of FBXO22 in regulating CD147 protein levels in tumor xenograft models. FBXO22 is highly expressed in liver cancer and has been reported to regulate the degradation of intracellular proteins including CD147 [17, 23, 30]. However, overexpression of FBXO22 did not affect CD147 protein level in the liver cancer tumor models tested (Fig. 4a). We therefore investigated a different target of FBXO22, the cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor p21, which has been reported to be a substrate of FBXO22 [31], and found decreased levels in tumors overexpressing FBXO22 (Fig. 4a). These results led us to hypothesize that p21 could be an important factor regulating DS-1471a efficacy in liver cancer. To test this hypothesis, we generated CDKN1A/p21-knockout (KO) cells (Fig. 4b) and examined the efficacy of DS-1471a in two different p21-KO tumor models. Treatment of p21-KO tumors with DS-1471a resulted in remarkably enhanced antitumor effects when compared with the limited effects observed in control parental tumors (Fig. 4c). Based on these correlational and phenocopy data, the above results raise the possibility that reduced p21 levels, potentially mediated by FBXO22, are associated with enhanced antitumor efficacy of DS-1471a in liver cancer.

To further understand the mechanisms regulating the efficacy of DS-1471a, we investigated the mechanism of resistance (MoR) to DS-1471a. For this purpose, we examined whether DS-1471a-resistant tumors could be generated by repeated in vivo administration of DS-1471a (Supplementary Fig. 7). When DS-1471a was administered at a dose of 3 mg/kg, complete regression was achieved by repeating the administration once a week for two doses, with no tumor regrowth observed for at least 100 days. However, when DS-1471a was administered at a dose of 1 mg/kg, resistant tumors were observed in 3 cases, while complete regression was observed in 9 cases (Supplementary Fig. 7). Among the three resistant tumors, one showed a slight reduction in CD147 level, while the other two showed elevated p21 protein levels (Fig. 4d). These results imply that p21 upregulation may represent a potential mechanism of resistance to DS-1471a in some tumors. However, given the small number of resistant tumors and the non-uniform molecular profiles observed, these findings should be considered preliminary and hypothesis-generating, and will require further validation and comprehensive molecular characterization. Since p21 is reported to contribute to mechanisms of resistance to various drugs [32], we further examined the relationship between p21 expression and DS-1471a efficacy in the HCC PDX models. Supporting the above hypothesis, an inverse correlation between p21 expression and DS-1471a efficacy was observed (Fig. 4e). Taken together, these results provide supportive evidence that FBXO22 and p21 expression levels could be key factors influencing the therapeutic effects of DS-1471a in CD147-targeted cancer therapy.

Discussion

In the present study, we demonstrated antitumor efficacy of the anti-CD147 antibody DS-1471a in multiple liver cancer PDX models (Fig. 1). Since PDX models are considered to more faithfully reflect the characteristics of clinical tumors compared to conventional cell line xenograft models [19], the results described here further support the potential efficacy of DS-1471a in clinical settings. We also identified several proteins, including CD147 and FBXO22, whose expression correlates with the efficacy of DS-1471a in HCC PDX models (Fig. 2a). Further evaluation is required to consider the use of these biomarker candidates for patient stratification or selection in clinical trials. In our analysis, expression of proteins in the reported CD147 signaling pathway (e.g., SMAD4) positively correlated with DS-1471a efficacy (Fig. 2a), suggesting the value of analyzing these proteins as potential biomarkers for DS-1471a efficacy in a clinical setting.

As for the potential mechanisms by which FBXO22 is involved in DS-1471a efficacy, we initially anticipated its involvement via controlling CD147 protein levels, as reported in a previous study [23]. However, FBXO22 overexpression did not decrease CD147 expression in vitro and in vivo in the liver cancer cell lines tested in this study (Fig. 4a and Supplementary Figs. 2–4). The present findings, based on steady-state protein measurements in selected liver cancer cell lines, did not reflect FBXO22-mediated degradation of CD147 that was reported previously [23]. This discrepancy may reflect context-dependent regulation, differences in cancer cell type or mutational landscape, or aspects of CD147 regulation, such as ubiquitination or localization, that were not captured in this study. Additional experiments examining ubiquitination and protein turnover would be required to draw a more comprehensive understanding of the mechanisms involved. Instead, we observed that FBXO22 overexpression affected p21 protein level in liver cancer tumor models and further found an inverse correlation between p21 protein level and tumor sensitivity to DS-1471a treatment (Fig. 4). Based on these correlational and phenocopy findings, we propose a working hypothesis in which reduced p21 level, potentially influenced by FBXO22-mediated degradation, may be associated with increased sensitivity to DS-1471a (Supplementary Fig. 8) [31]. However, we acknowledge that additional experiments are necessary to more rigorously establish causality in this regard. In line with this hypothesis, elevated p21 protein levels were observed in a small number of resistant tumors after extended treatment with DS-1471a (Supplementary Fig. 7 and Fig. 4d). We wish to emphasize that these observations are preliminary, based on only three cases, and should not be interpreted as establishing a mechanistic basis for resistance. Further studies involving larger cohorts of resistant tumor models and more comprehensive molecular profiling are required. While signal transduction from SMAD4, located downstream of the CD147 signaling pathway, to p21 has been reported [33–36], the function of p21 upstream of the CD147 signaling pathway remains to be elucidated. Additionally, p21 is widely known as a cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitory protein that also inhibits apoptosis [37]. Taking these factors into consideration, it is unlikely that p21 directly influences the CD147 signaling pathway but rather affects DS-1471a efficacy indirectly. Since p21 is a cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor that suppresses cell proliferation and division, its inhibition leads to dysregulated cell cycle progression and increased proliferation in cancer cells [38]. Inhibition of p21 may sensitize cancer cells to anticancer drugs and targeted therapies through multiple mechanisms. First, enhanced proliferation resulting from p21 loss may increase the proportion of actively cycling cells susceptible to treatment. Second, p21 inhibition can impair DNA repair mechanisms in cancer cells, making them more susceptible to damage from these treatments [37]. Additionally, p21 inhibition can promote apoptosis [38]. These changes resulting from p21 inhibition may be crucial in increasing sensitivity to anticancer drugs and targeted therapies. In this regard, combination therapy with drugs that can induce such effects or that control cell proliferation and apoptosis through p21 could be effective. While our data demonstrate that p21 knockout enhances tumor sensitivity to DS-1471a, the specific molecular mechanisms by which p21 status modulates cellular responses to DS-1471a treatment remain to be elucidated. These include potential effects on apoptosis, stress signaling pathway activation, and downregulation of the CD147 protein complex. Further clarification of the mechanism behind DS-1471a efficacy and its mechanism-based use in combination with other agents may further extend its therapeutic potential. From a translational perspective, overall survival (OS) analysis of liver cancer based on TCGA data indicated that patients with high FBXO22 expression or low p21 expression had a significantly worse OS (Supplementary Fig. 9), consistent with a previous report [31], which also demonstrated that FBXO22 and p21 protein expression are inversely correlated in clinical liver cancer samples. Importantly, our study suggests that DS-1471a exerts greater efficacy in tumor models with high FBXO22 or low p21 expression, which are molecular features associated with worse clinical outcomes in liver cancer as described above. Taken together, these findings raise the possibility that DS-1471a could provide meaningful clinical benefit specifically for liver cancer patients with poor prognosis.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, a direct causal link between FBXO22, p21, and DS-1471a sensitivity has not been fully established in the present study, and the mechanistic experiments were conducted primarily using a single liver cancer cell line, which limits the generalizability of our conclusions. Validation with additional cancer cell types will be important to establish whether the proposed regulatory relationships are broadly applicable to liver cancer. Second, this study does not fully address potential roles for other CD147-related proteins in CD147 trafficking, post-translational modification, turnover kinetics, or subcellular localization, and therefore does not exclude possible contributions of these factors in liver cancer.

In conclusion, our study demonstrates the therapeutic efficacy of DS-1471a in liver cancer PDX tumor models and identifies associations between DS-1471a efficacy and the expression of multiple proteins including CD147, FBXO22, and p21. While these findings suggest that FBXO22 and p21 may be involved in modulating DS-1471a sensitivity, the underlying mechanisms remain to be fully elucidated, and any causal relationships would require further validation. Further studies and validation of our findings in a clinical setting are needed to translate the above findings into improvements in cancer patient outcomes.

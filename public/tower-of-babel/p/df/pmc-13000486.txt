Preliminary results of 3D MRI-DSA fusion for navigation planning in endovascular recanalization of chronic intracranial artery occlusion
Authors: Yi Quan, Su-shan Hu, Fang Liu, Ji Zhou, Qi Sun, Min Jin, Chen Li, Ao-fei Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundChronic intracranial artery occlusion (CIAO) is a critical non-acute manifestaticon within the spectrum of intracranial atherosclerotic disease (ICAD). In Asian populations, ICAD is common and remains associated with substantial recurrent ischemic risk despite best medical therapy, making endovascular recanalization an important but technically challenging intervention due to the limitations of conventional 2D DSA guidance. This study explores an innovative method integrating high-resolution vessel wall imaging (HR-VWI) plaque characteristics with DSA to provide anatomical guidance for navigation planning.Materials and methodsWe utilized a fusion approach using HR-VWI (SPACE sequence) to characterize plaque features, which were integrated with intra-procedural digital subtraction angiography (DSA). Eight consecutive patients with symptomatic CIAO underwent endovascular recanalization using this method from January 2020 to December 2024. We evaluated technical fusion success, registration accuracy, procedural parameters, and safety outcomes.ResultsFusion success was achieved in all cases (100%, 95% CI: 63.1%-100%) with submillimeter accuracy. The method facilitated initial visualization of occluded vessel anatomy and plaque characteristics, with a mean registration accuracy of 0.3 ± 0.1 mm, enabling early suspicion of complications. Recanalization succeeded in 7 of 8 patients (87.5%), with one perforation suspected based on overlay mismatch and immediately confirmed by microcatheter injection and managed without lasting sequelae.ConclusionThese preliminary investigations suggest that integrating HR-VWI plaque characteristics with DSA to support navigation planning utilizing a fusion-based adjunct roadmap shows initial feasibility for CIAO recanalization. It may enhance anatomical visualization and early suspicion of complications, but larger studies are required to confirm clinical efficacy and wider applicability.

Introduction

Recent mechanical thrombectomy trials have established the benefit of endovascular therapy for acute large-vessel occlusions [1]. However, chronic intracranial artery occlusion (CIAO) remains a significant technological challenge. Intracranial atherosclerotic disease (ICAD)—a major etiology of CIAO—shows marked geographic and ethnic variation and is more common in Asian than in Western populations. Data from the Chinese Intracranial Atherosclerosis (CICAS) study indicated that symptomatic intracranial large artery stenoses and occlusions are present in up to 46.6% of patients with ischemic stroke or TIA in China [2], reflecting the combined spectrum of stenosis and occlusion rather than the incidence of CIAO alone. Furthermore, symptomatic high-grade intracranial atherosclerotic lesions remain associated with a substantial risk of recurrent stroke despite best medical therapy; this has been consistently demonstrated in cohorts such as WASID and SAMMPRIS [3], [4]. The complex pathophysiology of chronic occlusions, characterized by organized thrombus, extensive vessel wall remodeling and altered vascular architecture, demands advanced guidance technologies beyond conventional approaches [5], [6].

Current endovascular recanalization techniques rely predominantly on two-dimensional (2D) digital subtraction angiography (DSA) for procedural guidance. While our fusion approach integrates HR-VWI with DSA to enhance planning, the core procedural navigation still relies on DSA, with the fusion providing supplementary anatomical guidance. However, this approach presents fundamental limitations in chronic occlusions where vessel lumens are absent, providing inadequate visualization of true anatomical pathways and adjacent critical structures [7]. These technological constraints contribute to prolonged procedure times, increased radiation exposure, and elevated complication rates, with perforation risks reaching 10–20% in complex cases [8], [9].

Recent technological advances in multimodality imaging have highlighted the potential value of advanced 3D DSA and MRI/CT fusion for providing additional peri-procedural information and assisting treatment planning in neurovascular pathology [10]. Fusion image guidance has also been explored as a feasibility tool for supra-aortic vessel catheterization in neurointerventions [11]. However, these applications are not directly comparable to intracranial chronic occlusion traversal, where the occluded segment lacks contrast opacification and vessel caliber is smaller. Angiographic CT has mainly been evaluated for peri-interventional diagnostic imaging rather than for effective endovascular navigation through occlusions [12], and flat-panel detector angiographic CT has been reported primarily for post-procedural device assessment (e.g., stent-assisted coil embolization) [13].

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) technology offers superior soft tissue contrast and multisequence capabilities that can overcome these limitations. High-resolution vessel wall imaging (HR-VWI) using Sampling Perfection with Application optimized Contrasts using different flip angle Evolution (SPACE) sequences can delineate plaque characteristics, such as wall thickness, composition, and residual lumens, even in chronic occlusions [14], [15]. The innovative integration of these HR-VWI plaque features with real‑time DSA through fusion techniques represents a novel methodological strategy that could provide initial comprehensive anatomical guidance for complex endovascular procedures [16], [17].

To our knowledge, no previous studies have systematically evaluated the integration of HR-VWI plaque characteristics with DSA fusion navigation planning for CIAO recanalization. This study presents our initial methodological innovation and clinical implementation of this guidance approach, exploring its feasibility, technical performance, and safety profile in a consecutive series of patients with symptomatic CIAO.

Materials and methods

Study design and patient selection

This prospective single-center pilot study was conducted at our institution from January 2020 to December 2024, with institutional review board approval (IRB number: X2020008) and written informed consent from all participants. The protocol was registered in our institutional clinical research registry before enrollment.

Inclusion criteria: [1] symptomatic intracranial artery occlusion confirmed by CT or MR angiography; [2] symptomatic non-acute occlusion (encompassing subacute and chronic stages), defined as an occlusion duration of > 4 weeks based on clinical onset and prior imaging [18], [19]; [3] recurrent ischemic symptoms despite optimal medical therapy; [4] age 18–80 years; [5] mRS score ≤ 3. Exclusion criteria: [1] acute large infarction within 3 months; [2] MRI or contrast contraindications; [3] severe comorbidities limiting life expectancy; [4] pregnancy; [5] inability to consent.

Sample size justification

For this pilot feasibility study, we determined sample size using the confidence interval precision method, which is the recommended approach for feasibility studies where the primary goal is estimation rather than hypothesis testing [16], [20], [21]. We anticipated a technical fusion success rate of 95% based on preliminary testing of the fusion system and accepted a margin of error of 15% (α = 0.05).

This sample size aligns with established guidelines for pilot studies of medical device technologies, which typically recommend 6–12 patients for initial feasibility assessment [20] and matches similar neurovascular technique validation studies in the literature [16]. The sample size was also deemed appropriate by our institutional review board for this first-in-human application of the fusion navigation technique.

3D fusion navigation planning method

Fusion navigation was performed using the syngo X Workplace (syngo XWP, Siemens Healthineers) integrated with a robotic Artis Zeego angiography system. Pre-procedural HR-VWI datasets were imported into syngo XWP for 3D vessel reconstruction. Automated vessel segmentation was performed first. Manual refinement of the segmented vessel course was systematically conducted for all occluded segments by two senior neurointerventionalists to ensure anatomical fidelity. When automated segmentation could not reliably delineate the occluded segment, full manual delineation was performed. Registration was skull-based, using at least six bony landmarks (including the anterior/posterior clinoid processes and petrous ridges). Intra-procedural 3D rotational angiography (3D-RA) bony anatomy was used as the reference standard for registration accuracy assessment. The registration accuracy was manually verified on the syngo XWP platform. Two independent observers measured the spatial displacement between at least six paired anatomical landmarks (e.g., clinoid processes and petrous ridges) using the workstation’s linear measurement tool. Successful registration was predefined as mean landmark displacement ≤ 1.0 mm (submillimeter). The observed mean displacement was 0.3 ± 0.1 mm. The overall MR–DSA fusion workflow is summarized in Supplementary Figure S1. During wire advancement, standard DSA/live fluoroscopy remained the primary navigation modality. The MRI–DSA fusion overlay served as an adjunct static roadmap to support navigation planning and intra-procedural orientation (rather than a dynamic real-time tracking navigation system).

Outcome measures

Primary Endpoints: Technical fusion success (registration/overlay); vessel recanalization (antegrade flow restoration).

Secondary Endpoints: Registration accuracy (mean landmark displacement); procedural parameters (time, fluoroscopy, contrast); immediate complications (perforation, thromboembolism, hemorrhage); clinical outcomes (NIHSS at discharge/3 months). Fusion overlay image quality was rated on a standardized 4-point scale: [1] poor, not usable as an adjunct roadmap; [2] fair, partially usable as an adjunct roadmap with frequent cross-checking; [3] good, usable as an adjunct roadmap; [4] excellent, near-complete alignment and reliably usable as an adjunct roadmap during key procedural steps.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive methods used by pilot guidelines [16], [21]. Analyses in SPSS 29.0 (IBM) and GraphPad Prism 10.0. Data assessment: Shapiro-Wilk for normality (n < 50). Normally distributed: mean ± SD with 95% CI (t-distribution); non-normal: median (IQR). Primary endpoints: Binomial with Clopper-Pearson 95% CI. Secondary: Descriptive with t-distribution or bootstrap CI. No missing data, as all assessments completed. No hypothesis testing, focusing on estimation per CONSORT [21].

Technical fusion success

Technical fusion success was achieved in all 8 patients (100%, 95% CI: 63.1%-100%). Mean registration accuracy was 0.3 ± 0.1 mm, fulfilling the predefined submillimeter criterion (≤1.0 mm). The fusion process required an average of 8.2 ± 2.1 min for initial setup and registration.

The registered fusion overlay was successfully maintained throughout all procedures, with no instances of registration failure or significant drift requiring re-registration. Image quality was rated as excellent in 6 cases (75%) and good in 2 cases (25%) based on operator assessment using a standardized 4-point scale.

Procedural outcomes

Vessel recanalization was successful in 7 of 8 patients (87.5%, 95% CI: 47.3%-99.7%), with all successful cases achieving complete recanalization (TICI 3). One patient (12.5%) experienced recanalization failure due to vessel perforation that occurred during wire navigation through the occluded segment. Due to this complication, the recanalization procedure was abandoned (details regarding complication management are described in the Safety Outcomes section).

Safety outcomes

One patient (12.5%, 95% CI: 0.3%-52.7%) experienced a procedural complication consisting of vessel perforation during wire navigation. The perforation was suspected when the wire tip position appeared inconsistent with the expected vessel course on the fusion overlay and was promptly confirmed by microcatheter injection showing subarachnoid contrast extravasation. The perforation was successfully treated with coil embolization (2 coils: 4 mm×8 cm and 3 mm×6 cm), the recanalization procedure was abandoned, and the patient was managed with intensive medical therapy. Post-procedural imaging showed complete cessation of extravasation with no permanent clinical sequelae. No procedure-related deaths, permanent neurological deficits, or other major complications occurred. All patients were neurologically stable at discharge.

Clinical follow-up

Three-month follow-up was completed in all 8 patients (100%). All patients (100%) showed stable or improved neurological status compared with baseline: 7 patients (87.5%) improved and 1 patient (12.5%) remained stable without new neurological deficits.

Discussion

This single-center prospective pilot study explored the technical feasibility and early clinical experience with an innovative HR-VWI-DSA fusion method for endovascular recanalization of symptomatic chronic intracranial artery occlusion (CIAO). Our findings demonstrate high technical success (100%) and a favorable recanalization rate (87.5%) with submillimeter registration accuracy (0.3 ± 0.1 mm). These results are encouraging and align with the growing body of literature on the endovascular treatment of CIAO, where recent studies continue to report improving safety and efficacy, driven by technological advancements [19], [25]. The strength of our method lies in the integration of pre-procedural HR-VWI data with fluoroscopy environment. The fusion overlay helped prompt early suspicion of unexpected deviation, which was then confirmed by immediate microcatheter injection, potentially improving procedural safety, which should be interpreted primarily as a safety-oriented warning rather than a direct measure of navigation accuracy. This addresses a key limitation of conventional angiography by providing detailed anatomical information about the vessel wall, plaque characteristics, and potential residual lumens, which is increasingly recognized as critical for procedural planning and predicting outcomes in CIAO [8]. Furthermore, our work contributes to the development of advanced navigation techniques, such as dynamic 3D roadmaps and multimodality fusion, which are at the forefront of improving guidance in complex neurointerventions. By demonstrating a practical workflow for integrating detailed plaque morphology into the intra-procedural procedural environment, our study offers a novel approach to navigating the challenging anatomy of chronic occlusions. Notably, the technical utility of such advanced navigation is most pronounced when the pathological state of the occlusion creates significant hurdles for conventional roadmaps. In the current study, we defined the inclusion threshold at 4 weeks. This choice is supported by the fact that the technical difficulty of recanalization—stemming from thrombus fibrotic organization—is well-established beyond the one-month mark [5]. Therefore, transitioning from the acute to the subacute-to-chronic stage represents the critical window for evaluating advanced navigation technologies like our HR-VWI-DSA fusion method, as the absence of a visible lumen on conventional DSA poses similar anatomical challenges regardless of the exact duration beyond this period.

This study has several limitations that warrant consideration. First, the small sample size (n = 8), while appropriate for a pilot feasibility study, limits the statistical power and the generalizability of the findings. Second, as a single-center study with highly experienced operators, there is a potential for selection bias and overestimated treatment effects. For instance, our patient cohort primarily consisted of those with specific anatomical locations of occlusion (mostly ICA), and the influence of occlusion length was not systematically analyzed. Third, the registration accuracy, while submillimeter, could be affected by patient motion or respiratory activity, although no such issues were encountered in this series. Fourth, the study did not include an evaluation of the learning curve associated with this new technique, which would be essential for assessing its broader applicability. Finally, the 3-month follow-up period is insufficient to assess long-term outcomes such as in-stent restenosis. Although our recanalization success rate of 87.5% appears favorable compared to historical DSA-only guided cohorts (60–80%) [8], this is an indirect comparison and should be interpreted with caution in the absence of a control group. Future comparative studies should include a matched DSA-only cohort to evaluate time to recanalization/crossing time, technical success, fluoroscopy time, radiation dose, contrast volume, and complication rates. Additionally, our current rigid registration model provides a static roadmap and cannot capture non-rigid vessel shift during the procedure; therefore, final navigation relied on the integrated use of fusion overlays and live fluoroscopy. Future directions involve multicenter randomized trials with at least 12-month follow-up to assess long-term efficacy, patency, and cost-effectiveness, incorporating automated segmentation and sensitivity analyses for plaque characteristics and occlusion length.

Conclusion

These preliminary investigations indicate that our approach, utilizing an HR-VWI–DSA fusion-based adjunct roadmap, is feasible for endovascular recanalization of chronic intracranial artery occlusion and can support navigation planning and intra-procedural orientation. The approach provides enhanced anatomical visualization through integration of plaque characteristics and may facilitate early suspicion of procedural complications. Key achievements include 100% fusion success and submillimeter registration accuracy.

While this pilot feasibility study shows promising technical performance, larger clinical studies are required to confirm efficacy, refine patient selection, and assess long-term outcomes. Future work should include method automation, artificial intelligence for plaque analysis, and broader applications in neurovascular interventions.

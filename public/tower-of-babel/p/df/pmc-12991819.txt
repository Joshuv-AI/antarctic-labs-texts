From Chest Pain to Recovery: A Rare Case of Anomalous Origin of the Circumflex Artery From the Pulmonary Artery in a Child and the Role of Timely Surgical Intervention
Authors: Alena V Kauk, Serezha N Manukian, Aleksey N Arkhipov, Alexey V Voitov, Ilya A Soynov
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis report highlights the diagnostic challenges and successful surgical management in a symptomatic pediatric case with anomalous origin of the circumflex artery from the pulmonary artery. A 4-year-old boy presented emergently with dyspnea and substernal pain. Coronary angiography identified the circumflex artery originating anomalously from the right pulmonary artery. Emergency surgical reimplantation to the aorta was performed, with subsequent normalization of cardiac function (EF 67 % at 3-month follow-up). Although exceptionally rare, anomalous origin of the circumflex artery from the pulmonary artery requires high clinical suspicion in pediatric patients with ischemic symptoms. Early anatomical correction yields excellent outcomes, as demonstrated in this case.Keywords: Congenital heart disease, Pediatric surgery, Anomalous origin of the circumflex artery from the pulmonary artery

1. Introduction

Anomalous origin of the circumflex artery from the pulmonary artery (ACxAPA) is a rare congenital coronary anomaly that can present with a wide spectrum of clinical manifestations― ranging from an asymptomatic course to sudden cardiac arrest [1]. In recent years, increased awareness of this pathology has emerged due to advances in diagnostic techniques and a deeper understanding of its potential risks [1,2]. We report the case of a 4-year-old child who presented with dyspnea and substernal pain and successfully underwent surgical reimplantation of the circumflex artery into the aorta.

2. History of presentation

A 4-year-old boy was emergently admitted to the cardiac surgery department with dyspnea and substernal pain.

3. Investigations

Electrocardiogram (ECG) revealed subtle ST-segment depression (up to 2 mm) in leads I, aVL, and V5–V6. Serum troponin I was elevated at 44.9 pg/mL (normal reference 0–25 pg/mL). Chest radiography demonstrated cardiomegaly with a cardiothoracic index (CTI) of 56 %. Transthoracic echocardiography showed mildly impaired left ventricular systolic function (ejection fraction 50 %) with turbulent flow in mid-myocardial sinusoidal vessels, though the ostia of both right and left coronary arteries were clearly visualized. A pathological shunt from an anomalous vessel, up to 3 mm in diameter, was identified in the pulmonary artery. To further characterize the coronary anatomy, computed tomography (CT) (Fig. 1) and selective coronary angiography (Fig. 2) was performed, revealing an anomalous circumflex artery originating from the posterior wall of the right pulmonary artery, approximately 15 mm distal to the pulmonary trunk bifurcation. The anomalous vessel had a 3-mm ostium and continued as the posterior descending artery. In contrast, the left main coronary artery (ostium 3.8 mm) arose normally from the aorta and continues as the left anterior descending (LAD) artery.

4. Management

The patient underwent emergent surgical reimplantation of the circumflex artery to the aorta (Fig. 3).

Follow-up echocardiography on postoperative day 3 demonstrated recovery of left ventricular function (ejection fraction 65 %), and the troponin I level had decreased to 26 pg/mL (normal reference 0–25 pg/mL). Electrocardiography revealed resolution of the previously noted ischemic changes. The patient was discharged on postoperative day 8 in good condition.

5. Follow-up

At 3-month follow-up, he remained asymptomatic. Repeat echocardiography showed preserved systolic function (EF 67 %), and coronary CT angiography confirmed normal aortic origin of all coronary arteries without evidence of stenosis or kinking along their courses (Fig. 4).

6. Discussion

This case illustrates how rare congenital coronary anomalies such as an anomalous circumflex artery arising from the pulmonary artery (ACxAPA) can present with serious clinical consequences, including substernal chest pain in pediatric patients. Although the exact incidence of ACxAPA remains unknown due to its rarity [1], existing literature suggests shared embryologic origins with other cardiovascular anomalies, including bicuspid aortic valve, aortic coarctation, atrial septal defect, and Yatagan syndrome [1–4]. Defects in neural crest cell migration likely contribute to these conditions, as these cells play a critical role in major vessel formation, outflow tract septation, endocardial cushion development, and coronary artery smooth muscle differentiation [1].

The clinical presentation of ACxAPA follows two distinct pathophysiologic patterns. In infants, myocardial ischemia results from direct perfusion by the anomalous artery, which delivers low-pressure, desaturated blood. In older patients, well-developed collateral vessels from normal coronary arteries may produce a coronary steal phenomenon, diverting blood toward the pulmonary circulation [1,2,5]. While most reported cases remain asymptomatic due to sufficient collateralization [4,5], our patient’s extensive collateral network paradoxically impaired myocardial perfusion, leading to symptomatic ischemia.

The favorable outcome in this case underscores three critical factors: (1) comprehensive, multimodal diagnostic evaluation; (2) prompt, multidisciplinary decision-making; and (3) technically optimized surgical correction through coronary reimplantation to the aorta. Our experience demonstrates that individualized, timely intervention can successfully restore antegrade coronary flow and reverse myocardial dysfunction, as evidenced by the patient’s complete recovery. This case further highlights how contemporary noninvasive imaging, combined with early surgical referral, can optimize outcomes for this potentially life-threatening anomaly.

7. Conclusions

ACxAPA represents a rare but clinically consequential coronary anomaly demanding high diagnostic suspicion and expeditious intervention. As demonstrated here, early detection coupled with anatomical surgical correction can achieve excellent clinical results, emphasizing the importance of multidisciplinary management in congenital coronary pathologies.

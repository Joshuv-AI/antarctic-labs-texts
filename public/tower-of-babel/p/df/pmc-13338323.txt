Modern molecular profiling recontextualizes the NRG/RTOG 0539 trial and reveals hidden high-risk and radiotherapy-resistant meningiomas
Authors: Leeor S Yefet, Alexander P Landry, C Leland Rogers, Justin Z Wang, Jeff Liu, Vikas Patil, Chloe Gui, Andrew Ajisebutu, Yosef Ellenbogen, Qingxia Wei, Olivia Singh, Sheila Mansouri, Christopher Szot, Richard C Jordan, Igor J Barani, Michael W Straza, Hui-Kuo G Shu, Kelli B Pointer, Grant Hunter, Valerie Panet-Raymond, Wenyin Shi, Haley K Perlow, Brian D Kavanagh, Tyler Gunter, Clifford Robinson, Stephanie L Pugh, Adam P Dicker, Minesh P Mehta, Arnab Chakravarti, Farshad Nassiri, Kenneth Aldape, Gelareh Zadeh

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMeningiomas exhibit clinical heterogeneity. Radiotherapy (RT) remains the only adjuvant therapy, but tumor-control is variable, and biomarkers are limited. NRG/RTOG-0539 is the first prospective phase 2 trial to stratify meningioma patients for adjuvant RT based on clinical risk. Here, we apply modern molecular tools to this cohort and identify correlates of RT response.MethodsTumor tissue from 100 RTOG-0539 patients was profiled using DNA methylation arrays, RNA sequencing, and whole-exome sequencing. Recurrence scores, Molecular Groups, gene expression, and copy number alterations were compared across clinical groups and between RT responders and non-responders; non-response was defined as progression or death within 3 years.ResultsModern grading criteria, including brain invasion, TERT mutation, CDKN2A/B deletion, and 1p/1q status, would reclassify 10% of tumors and alter treatment group assignment in 7%. Non-responders to RT exhibited more frequent 1p and 14q loss, and more copy number alterations. Transcriptomic and epigenetic profiling revealed immune-related signatures in responders and cell cycle–related pathways in non-responders, several of which overlapped with targets of vorinostat, a histone deacetylase inhibitor previously validated in aggressive meningioma models. The Proliferative Molecular Group was an independent predictor of post-RT recurrence in multivariable analysis, outperforming WHO grade.ConclusionMulti-omic analysis of the NRG/RTOG-0539 cohort shows that updated WHO grading criteria, incorporating molecular and cytogenetic features, improve risk stratification. However, molecular classification, particularly the Proliferative group, remains an independent and stronger predictor of RT response. These findings support integrating molecular biomarkers alongside modern grading frameworks to guide treatment and trial design in meningioma.Clinical Trial InformationNCT00895622.

Importance of the Study

Accurately identifying which meningiomas will recur or resist radiotherapy remains a major clinical challenge. This study re-examines the landmark NRG/RTOG-0539 trial using contemporary molecular tools, including DNA methylation profiling, RNA sequencing, and genomic analysis. By integrating these modern approaches, the study shows that several tumors previously considered low-risk actually harbor high-risk biological features that influence treatment response. Importantly, molecular classification, particularly identification of the Proliferative group, more reliably predicts recurrence than current WHO grading, even when incorporating contemporary cytogenetic prognostic markers. The study further demonstrates that molecular classification frameworks provide complementary and clinically meaningful risk stratification beyond histologic grading alone. Together, these findings support the integration of molecular profiling into future risk models, clinical trial design, and treatment planning for patients with meningioma, particularly through trial designs incorporating prospective tissue collection and centralized molecular analysis.

Meningiomas are the most common primary intracranial tumors, comprising nearly one-third of all central nervous system neoplasms.1 While many are indolent, an important subset demonstrates aggressive behavior, including early recurrence and resistance to radiotherapy (RT). The World Health Organization (WHO) grading system has undergone successive updates to better reflect tumor biology, including the addition of brain invasion as a criterion for Grade 2 in 2016 and the incorporation of CDKN2A/B deletions and TERT promoter mutations as Grade 3-defining features in 2021.2–4 Emerging evidence further suggests that cytogenetic alterations such as isolated 1p loss and combined 1p loss with 1q gain may confer risk comparable to higher-grade tumors, raising the possibility of future inclusion in grading frameworks.5,6

Nonetheless, the WHO grade alone does not fully capture the biological heterogeneity of meningioma, prompting the development of several new prognostic tools, classification systems, and biomarkers from our group and others.7–13 Among these, DNA methylation-based profiling has emerged as a robust method for tumor stratification, identifying four reproducible Molecular Groups (MG): Immunogenic, NF2-wildtype, Hypermetabolic, and Proliferative, each associated with distinct biological behaviors and clinical outcomes that have been shown to outperform traditional histologic grading in predicting tumor behavior. However, these molecular tools have been evaluated retrospectively in heterogeneous patient populations, limiting their application in prospective clinical decision-making.

Radiotherapy is currently the only established adjuvant treatment option for patients with meningiomas following surgical resection. NRG Oncology RTOG-0539 trial was the first prospective, phase 2, multi-institutional study designed to evaluate progression-free survival (PFS) following standardized RT in meningioma patients stratified into three risk categories. Eligible patients were assigned to Low-, Intermediate-, or High-risk arms based on 2007 WHO grade, extent of resection, and recurrence status.14–16 The Low-risk arm included patients with newly diagnosed WHO Grade 1 tumors who had undergone resection and were managed with observation alone. The Intermediate-risk arm included patients with recurrent WHO Grade 1 tumors or newly diagnosed WHO Grade 2 tumors after gross total resection (GTR), who received fractionated RT (54 Gy, 30 fractions). The High-risk arm included patients with WHO Grade 2 tumors after subtotal resection (STR), recurrent WHO Grade 2 tumors, or any WHO Grade 3 tumors, all of whom were treated with RT (60 Gy, 30 fractions).

Outcomes across the three RTOG-0539 arms demonstrated substantial variation in disease control. These results established important benchmarks for RT efficacy in meningioma but also revealed that despite aggressive local therapy, nearly half of the patients in the High-risk group experienced early recurrence.14 These findings underscore the inadequacy of histopathologic and surgical features alone in guiding treatment, particularly for patients with biologically aggressive tumors. Prior work from our group has demonstrated that molecular classification of meningiomas helps refine outcome prediction on this valuable cohort in the context of trial-defined risk groups.17 Nevertheless, WHO grading has evolved considerably since its use for trial design, and cytogenetic features including chromosome 1p loss and 1q gain have recently been proposed as molecular adjuvants to further refine WHO grading.5,6 Therefore, there remains a need for in-depth exploration of the practical utility of these molecular groups across this evolution of WHO grading and in the context of prognostic cytogenetics alterations. There also remains a need to explore multi-omics molecular features associated with treatment response on this unique cohort by incorporating RNA sequencing and exome sequencing, allowing a unique opportunity to rigorously validate biological signal identified on other retrospective cohorts.

In this study, we leveraged the unique RTOG-0539 clinical trial cohort to perform an integrated analysis of DNA methylation, transcriptomic, and genomic data. We demonstrate that modern molecular markers more accurately stratify risk and better predict disease control by radiotherapy than histologic grade alone, across multiple iterations of the WHO grade and even in the context of prognostic cytogenetic alterations. These findings validate our molecular classification in an independent prospective cohort and underscore its clinical utility, supporting the integration of molecular and cytogenetic data into future grading frameworks and clinical trial designs.

Methods

Study Cohort and Sample Acquisition

Patients included in this study were enrolled in the NRG Oncology RTOG 0539 phase 2 clinical trial, a prospective, multi-institutional study designed to evaluate outcomes of adjuvant RT in meningioma patients stratified by risk. Eligible patients were assigned to Low-, Intermediate-, or High-risk groups based on histopathologic grading (per WHO 2007 criteria), extent of resection, and recurrence status, with centralized pathology review and standardized treatment protocols across sites. Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor tissue was available for 100 patients from the RTOG 0539 cohort: 38 in the Low-risk group, 32 in the Intermediate-risk group, and 30 in the High-risk group. After processing and quality control, 99 tumors had both DNA methylation and bulk RNA sequencing data available, and 93 had paired whole-exome sequencing (WES) data. All tissue samples were collected and analyzed in accordance with institutional and national ethical guidelines. This study was approved by the University Health Network Research Ethics Board (protocol #18-5820).

Risk Group Assignment and Clinical Annotation

Patients in the RTOG 0539 trial were prospectively assigned to one of three clinical risk groups: Low, Intermediate, or High based on the WHO 2007 histopathologic grading system, extent of surgical resection, and recurrence status. The Low-risk group included patients with newly diagnosed WHO Grade 1 meningiomas. The Intermediate-risk group included patients with WHO Grade 2 tumors that had undergone GTR, as well as patients with recurrent WHO Grade 1 tumors. The High-risk group consisted of patients with WHO Grade 2 tumors following STR, recurrent WHO Grade 2 tumors, or WHO Grade 3 tumors. All tumor grades were assigned according to the site-reported WHO 2007 classification. Patients were classified as responders if they were alive and progression-free for 3 years following treatment, and as non-responders if they experienced recurrence or died within 3 years. Clinical and demographic data were collected for all patients, including age at diagnosis, sex, tumor grade, extent of resection (gross total vs. subtotal), recurrence status, and PFS.

We assessed how WHO grade assignments evolved over the RTOG 0539 trial, considering changes to grading criteria over time. At the time of enrollment, tumors were graded using the WHO 2007 criteria, which relied entirely on histopathologic features. Since then, there have been two significant updates to the WHO classification system published in 2016 and 2021. In 2016, brain invasion in 2016 was incorporated and in 2021, molecular alterations, specifically TERT promoter mutations and homozygous deletions of CDKN2A/B, were included. In addition, emerging data suggests that copy number alterations, particularly 1p loss in the cIMPACT-NOW-8 statement and recently our own findings about concurrent 1p loss and 1q gain, may serve as future biomarkers for grading refinement.5,6 Accordingly, we also assessed the frequency of such alterations across our cohort, which was labeled as 2021 + 1p/1q.

DNA Methylation Profiling and Analysis

Genomic DNA was isolated from FFPE tumor specimens using the QIAamp DNA FFPE Tissue Kit (Qiagen) per the manufacturer’s protocol. A total of 500 ng of extracted DNA per sample was bisulfite converted using the EZ DNA Methylation Kit (Zymo Research). Genome-wide DNA methylation was then assessed using the Illumina Infinium MethylationEPIC BeadChip array, which interrogates over 850 000 CpG sites across the genome. Raw intensity files (.idat) were processed using the minfi package in R, applying single-sample Noob (ssNoob) normalization to adjust for technical variation. Quality control filtering excluded probes with detection P-value > .05 and those that overlapped known single nucleotide polymorphisms, mapped to sex chromosomes, or were previously identified as cross-reactive. The resulting filtered beta-values were used in all downstream analyses. MG classification was performed using a previously published and prospectively validated DNA methylation-based meningioma classifier.8 In addition, each tumor was assigned a methylome-derived risk score to quantify the epigenetic risk profile, with high risk being defined as scores >0.4717 per prior validation.18 Raw IDAT files were additionally processed through the DKFZ methylation classifier, and DKFZ meningioma family classifications were used for complementary comparative and prognostic analyses.

RNA Sequencing and Transcriptomic Analysis

Following the manufacturer’s protocol, total RNA was isolated from FFPE tumor samples using the RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen). RNA libraries were prepared and sequenced on the NovaSeq 6000 platform, generating paired-end reads with a target depth of approximately 150 million reads per sample to ensure robust transcriptomic coverage. Sequencing reads were aligned to the GRCh38 human reference genome using the STAR aligner, which enabled high-throughput, splice-aware mapping. Post-alignment processing included sorting and duplicate removal using SamTools. Gene-level quantification was performed using the Rsubread package in R, which generated raw read counts across annotated genes. To prepare the data for expression analysis, raw counts were transformed into log2 counts per million and normalized using the trimmed mean of M-values approach implemented in the edgeR package. These normalized expression values were used for all downstream analyses.

Whole-Exome Sequencing and Mutational Analysis

Whole-exome sequencing was performed on DNA extracted from FFPE tumor samples and, where available, matched normal DNA derived from the buffy coat. Exome libraries were constructed using 100 ng of input DNA and sequenced using paired-end reads on the NovaSeq 6000 platform to ensure high coverage across coding regions. Sequencing reads were aligned to the human reference genome (hg19) using the BWA-MEM algorithm (version 0.7.12). Following alignment, quality control steps, including duplicate marking, realignment, and base quality recalibration, were carried out using Picard (v1.72) and the Genome Analysis Toolkit (GATK v3.6.0). Somatic variants were identified using two established variant callers: Mutect (v1.1.7) and Strelka (v1.0.13). Variants were retained only if detected by both tools or met high-confidence thresholds in one.

Copy Number Variation Analysis

Copy number alterations were inferred from DNA methylation array data using the conumee package in R. Normalized intensity values from the EPIC array were used to generate log2 ratio plots and segmented copy number profiles for each tumor sample. A log2 ratio threshold of ±0.2 was applied to define chromosomal arm-level gains and losses.

Pathway Matching and Therapeutic Inference

We performed pathway enrichment analyses using RNA expression and DNA methylation data to identify key biological processes underlying transcriptional and epigenetic differences. Differential gene expression and promoter methylation were computed using the limma package in R, comparing tumors by risk group and RT response status. Genes and promoters meeting predefined thresholds for statistical significance were subjected to pathway enrichment analysis using the Gene Ontology Biological Process (GOBP) collection, with gene sets curated and maintained by the Bader Lab (http://download.baderlab.org/EM_Genesets/). Only pathways containing 10 to 200 genes were retained, and enrichment scores were computed using 2000 permutations. Results were visualized using the EnrichmentMap plugin (v3.4.0) within Cytoscape (v3.10.2), with network maps constructed based on nominal P-value < .00001. Pathways with overlapping gene content (Jaccard coefficient > 0.25) were connected within the network. Clusters of related pathways were grouped and annotated using the AutoAnnotate app, which applies a Markov clustering algorithm and keyword-based summarization to define major biological themes. We focused on pathway signatures enriched in non-responders to explore potential therapeutic strategies for RT-resistant tumors. Based on prior work identifying vorinostat as an effective HDAC inhibitor in meningioma models, we queried whether gene programs activated in RT-treated meningiomas overlapped with those suppressed by vorinostat. Using the Post-Analysis function within EnrichmentMap, we visualized drug-target associations by mapping vorinostat-inhibited pathways onto the transcriptional networks derived from our data.

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.3.1, released 2023-06-16), utilizing core statistical functions and relevant Bioconductor packages. Unless otherwise stated, all tests were two-sided, and P-value < .05 were considered statistically significant. Categorical variables, such as mutation frequencies, copy number alterations, and MG distributions, were compared using the Chi-square test. P-value were adjusted for multiple testing using the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate method. Fisher’s exact test was applied when more than 20% of the expected cell counts were less than 5. Comparisons of continuous variables, such as DNA methylome-derived risk scores, were conducted using the non-parametric Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Time-to-event analyses were performed using multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression models to estimate hazard ratios and 95% confidence intervals for progression-free survival. Due to low progression event counts in WHO grades (1 and 2) and molecular group (Immunogenic and NF2wt) categories, selected categories were combined prior to modeling to ensure stable hazard ratio estimation. The Kaplan-Meier method was used to estimate PFS, and differences between groups were evaluated using the log-rank test.

Results

Clinical Trial Cohort Overview

Of the cohort of patients in RTOG-0539, 100 participants had tissue samples: 38 from the “Low”-risk group, 32 from “Intermediate”-risk, and 30 from “High”-risk clinical groups (Table 1, Figure 1A). This subset is representative of the overall RTOG-0539 cohort. From these tissue samples, 93 met quality assurance assessments for WES, and 99 for DNA methylation and bulk RNA sequencing. Median age was 56.5 years (interquartile range [IQR] 48.2-64.0 years) in the low-risk group, 53.0 (IQR 44.8-60.2) in the intermediate-risk group, and 62.0 (IQR 52.2-68.0) in the high-risk group (P = .048) The proportion of females (F) to males (M) was 81.6% F:18.4% M in the low-risk group, 65.6% F:34.4% M in the intermediate group, and 43.3% F:56.7% M in the high risk group (P = .0045) (Table 1).

Updated WHO Criteria Reclassify Tumors and Improve Survival Stratification

To quantify the potential impact of grading evolution, we re-evaluated tumors in the RTOG-0539 cohort using WHO 2016, WHO 2021, and proposed cytogenetic-based criteria (Figure 1B). No tumors were reclassified when applying the 2016 criteria, as all cases with brain invasion had already been assigned grade 2 or 3 at enrollment. Application of the 2021 criteria led to the reassignment of one tumor from grade 2 to grade 3 due to a TERT promoter mutation. Incorporating cytogenetic markers resulted in seven tumors being reclassified from grade 1 to grade 2 based on 1p loss, and two tumors being reclassified from grade 2 to grade 3 due to concurrent 1p loss and 1q gain. In total, these updates have changed the grade in 10% of tumors (10/100 tumors).

Given that RTOG 0539 stratified patients by clinical risk groups based on WHO 2007, we next evaluated how updated or emerging grading systems would have altered treatment group assignment (Figure 1C). As expected, the WHO 2016 criteria did not lead to any reassignments. Under WHO 2021, one patient moved from the Intermediate- to the High-risk group. When incorporating 1p and 1q alterations, four patients were reclassified from Low to Intermediate risk, and two from Intermediate to High risk. These changes would have resulted in different treatment recommendations for 7% of patients (7/100 tumors), emphasizing the clinical importance of continuously updating trial frameworks to reflect modern biology.

The median follow up was 8.0 ± 6.1 years (IQR). WHO 2007 grades were associated with PFS (P = .00041, Figure 1D), and this improved with WHO 2021 criteria (P = .00025, Figure 1F) and further with the addition of 1p/1q copy number status (P < .0001, Figure 1H). RTOG risk groups, which incorporate the extent of resection and recurrence status in addition to grade, consistently demonstrated substantial prognostic value (P < .0001 across all grading systems; Figure 1E, G, and I). These findings highlight the robustness of RTOG risk stratification and the incremental value of updated histopathologic and molecular criteria in refining prognosis.

Copy Number Alterations and Genomic Instability Differentiate RT Responders from Non-Responders

We evaluated differences in somatic copy number alterations (CNAs) between patients who responded to RT (defined as ≥3-year progression-free survival) and those who did not, within the intermediate and high-risk groups. In this study, “RT response” refers to durable radiographic disease control following radiotherapy, rather than intrinsic cellular radiation resistance. The low-risk group was excluded as they were not assigned adjuvant RT in this trial (Figure 2A). Among the canonical meningioma cytogenetic events, 1p loss was significantly more frequent in non-responders than responders (85% vs. 52%, P = .0275) and 14q loss (70% vs. 31%, P = .00867). Although 1q gain was numerically more common in non-responders (10%) than responders (5%), this difference did not reach statistical significance (P = .588). These findings suggest that 1p and 14q loss can serve as biomarkers of RT responsiveness and may contribute to resistance to adjuvant RT.

Next, we quantified the overall copy number burden in each tumor, which was defined as the number of chromosomal arm-level alterations. Non-responders exhibited significantly higher CNA burden than responders (median 8.5 vs. 4 alterations per tumor, P = .0234) (Figure 2B), indicating increased genomic instability among RT-resistant tumors.

Tumor Mutational Burden and Individual SNVs Are Not Significantly Associated with RT Response

In contrast to copy number alterations, total tumor mutational burden (TMB) did not significantly differ between responders and non-responders (P = .5) (Figure 2C). Exploratory analysis of recurrent SNVs demonstrated enrichment of NF2 alterations in non-responders (15/38 vs 13/18, P = .022; Benjamini-Hochberg corrected P = .79), while no other recurrent SNVs were associated with RT response, supporting a limited role for individual point mutations in predicting RT response in this prospective cohort (Figure S1). These findings suggest that, in contrast to structural genomic alterations, individual point mutations do not provide a strong predictive signal for RT response in this prospective cohort.

Distinct Pathway Signatures Underlie RT Response Phenotypes

To understand the biological programs associated with response or resistance to RT, we performed integrated transcriptomic and methylomic pathway enrichment analysis (Figure 2D). In responders, immune-related pathways were significantly enriched, with consistent overlap between hypomethylated promoter regions and upregulated gene expression. These included signatures related to immune response, chemotaxis, and cytokine signaling. In contrast, non-responders demonstrated upregulation of cell cycle regulatory and DNA repair pathways. These were not consistently associated with promoter hypomethylation, suggesting alternative mechanisms of transcriptional activation beyond epigenetic regulation. Furthermore, exploratory analyses stratified by WHO grade demonstrated similar enrichment of immune-associated pathways in responders across grades (Figure S2).

Given the similarity between the pathway profiles of non-responders and the previously defined Proliferative MG, we investigated candidate therapeutic vulnerabilities. Using curated FDA-approved compound libraries, we found that vorinostat, a histone deacetylase inhibitor previously shown to suppress the growth of Proliferative meningiomas in both in vitro and in vivo models, selectively targeted several of the pathways enriched exclusively in non-responders (Figure 2D).7 These findings strengthen the rationale for evaluating vorinostat or similar epigenetic therapies in biomarker-enriched clinical trials focused on the most aggressive and treatment-resistant meningiomas.

DNA Methylation Risk Score Tracks With Clinical Risk and Predicts Progression

Given the evolving understanding of meningioma biology and the integration of molecular markers into the 2021 WHO grading system, we aimed to test the performance of validated molecular recurrence prediction and molecular classification tools in the RTOG 0539 cohort. This is particularly important in light of our findings that several meningiomas previously designated as low grade by the WHO 2007 criteria exhibited high-risk molecular features and clinical outcomes. Here, we assessed a DNA methylation-based recurrence score and the previously described MGs, which are associated with distinct biology and prognosis, to evaluate their utility in stratifying risk and RT response.

We first assessed the distribution of the DNA methylation-based recurrence score, a validated continuous model trained to predict the 5-year recurrence risk of meningiomas based solely on genome-wide DNA methylation data. This model, which ranges from 0 to 1 (representing low and high risk), has previously demonstrated prognostic value independent of histologic grade or other clinical features.18 In the RTOG 0539 cohort, the median methylation score increased progressively across the Low, Intermediate, and High risk groups (P < .0001), suggesting strong concordance between molecular risk and the original clinical trial stratification (Figure 3A). When patients were stratified into high versus low methylome risk using the previously established cutoff, PFS was again significantly different between the high and low-risk cohorts (P = .0015), confirming the utility of the DNA methylation-based tool as a molecular stratification in predicting recurrence within this prospectively treated trial population (Figure 3B).

Molecular Group Stratifies Risk More Consistently than WHO Grade

We next examined whether MG classification aligned with RT response and provided added prognostic value relative to WHO 2021 grading, including cytogenetic modifiers. The distribution of WHO 2021 + 1p/1q risk categories and MGs among RT responders and non-responders in the intermediate and high risk arms is illustrated (Figure 3C). A larger proportion of non-responders fell into higher-risk molecular subtypes, specifically the Proliferative and Hypermetabolic groups, whereas responders were enriched for Immunogenic and NF2wt tumors.

We explored this further by stratifying patients within the WHO grade (1, 2, and 3) according to MG (Figure 3D and E). Grade 1 and 2 tumors were combined to enable statistical comparison. In 2021 WHO Grade 1 and 2 tumors (Figure 3D), the MG classification was able to distinguish high-risk biology. Notably, two Grade 1 meningiomas were Proliferative and Hypermetabolic tumors associated with earlier progression. These tumors were upgraded when 1p loss and 1q gain were considered. In both 2021 and 2021 + 1p/1q Grade 2 tumors, both high-risk (Proliferative, Hypermetabolic) and lower-risk (NF2wt, Immunogenic) MGs demonstrated distinct prognoses, consistent with their known biological behavior (Figure 3D). A similar pattern was observed in both 2021 and 2021 + 1p/1q Grade 3 tumors, further supporting the utility of MG classification across histologic grades (Figure 3E). These results suggest that MGs offer consistent and biologically grounded risk stratification, even within the same WHO grade, and better capture underlying tumor behavior than grading systems alone.

Building on these findings, we next evaluated concordance and complementary prognostic value of the DKFZ molecular classification system. Sankey analysis demonstrated increasing proportions of radiotherapy non-response across DKFZ methylation classes, with the highest proportion of non-responders observed in the DKFZ malignant class (80%), followed by the intermediate class (37.5%), and the lowest proportion in the benign class (19.4%) (Figure S3). Cross-classification heatmap analysis demonstrated expected but incomplete overlap between MG and DKFZ classification systems, with Hypermetabolic and Proliferative MGs representing 46.7% of the DKFZ benign class, highlighting biologic heterogeneity within individual DKFZ methylation classes (Figure S4). Similar to the MG classification system, when evaluated within the WHO 2021 + 1p/1q integrated grading framework, DKFZ methylation classification refined prognostic stratification. Among WHO 2021 + 1p/1q Grade 1-2 tumors, DKFZ class significantly stratified progression-free survival, with DKFZ intermediate and malignant classes demonstrating worse outcomes compared to DKFZ benign tumors (log-rank P < .0001) (Figure S5). A similar directional pattern was observed in WHO 2021 + 1p/1q Grade 3 tumors, although this did not reach statistical significance. Together, these findings support the value of molecular classification for identifying biologically aggressive tumors that may not be fully captured by histologic grading alone, even when incorporating cytogenetic alterations.

Proliferative Group, Age, and Extent of Resection Predict RT Failure

To assess independent predictors of RT response, we performed multivariable Cox regression including age, sex, extent of resection, WHO 2021 + 1p/1q Grade, and MG (Figure 3F). As expected, increasing age (HR 1.05 per year, 95% CI 1.01-1.10, P = .023) and subtotal resection (HR 6.65, 95% CI 2.03-21.8, P = .002) were associated with worse outcomes. Importantly, membership in the Proliferative MG was the only biological variable independently associated with recurrence (HR 5.59, 95% CI 1.30-24.1, P = .021). WHO 2021 Grading, even when incorporating cytogenetic modifiers, was not significant in this model (HR 2.06, 95% CI 0.77-5.5, P = .148). These results underscore the prognostic strength of MG classification in the context of modern multi-omic profiling.

Discussion

This study represents the first comprehensive molecular analysis of meningiomas within a prospective clinical trial evaluating adjuvant RT. Using multi-omic profiling of the RTOG 0539 cohort, including DNA methylation, RNA sequencing, and whole-exome sequencing, we demonstrate that contemporary molecular tools provide more accurate and biologically grounded risk stratification than traditional grading systems, even when augmented with cytogenetic markers. Importantly, we identify molecular correlates of RT response and uncover therapeutic vulnerabilities in biologically aggressive and treatment-resistant tumors.

Our findings highlight key limitations of histologic grading alone. Although the 2021 WHO update incorporates molecular markers such as CDKN2A/B homozygous deletions and TERT promoter mutations, our data show that additional cytogenetic features, particularly 1p loss and 1q gain, have clear prognostic relevance. In fact, applying these markers to the RTOG 0539 cohort would have resulted in grade reclassification in 10% of tumors, with a corresponding change in treatment assignment for 7% of patients. These shifts were not trivial: tumors previously considered low-risk exhibited biology and outcomes more typical of high-grade disease. These findings underscore the need to reassess historical clinical trial frameworks using modern molecular criteria and emphasize the need for adaptive approaches that integrate evolving molecular knowledge into trial interpretation, as classification systems will inevitably change during trial accrual and analysis.

Furthermore, we show that MG classification outperforms WHO grading in predicting RT outcomes. MG classification consistently stratified patients within each WHO grade, identifying high-risk Proliferative and Hypermetabolic tumors even among WHO Grade 1 meningiomas. Conversely, Immunogenic and NF2wt tumors had favorable outcomes regardless of histologic grade. These findings suggest that molecular programs, rather than histologic features, better capture the actual biological behavior of meningiomas and should be prioritized in future risk models. Complementary analyses using the DKFZ methylation classification demonstrated similar refinement of biologic risk stratification, further supporting the broader clinical value of molecular classification approaches.

We identified molecular biomarkers that determine RT response. Non-responders exhibited increased genomic instability, with significantly higher copy number burden and frequent 1p and 14q loss. Enrichment for cell cycle and DNA repair pathways is associated with RT responsiveness in meningiomas, and responders demonstrated immune activation signatures. Notably, these resistance-associated pathways overlapped with those inhibited by vorinostat, a histone deacetylase inhibitor previously shown to suppress Proliferative meningiomas in preclinical models. This provides further strong rationale for biomarker-enriched trials testing vorinostat or similar agents in molecularly defined groups with poor RT response. Further functional validation using post-radiotherapy and recurrence-derived preclinical models will be important to confirm these therapeutic vulnerabilities in RT-resistant meningioma.

Multivariable analysis confirmed that increasing age and subtotal resection remain important clinical predictors of recurrence. However, among biological features, only membership in the Proliferative MG was independently associated with RT failure. WHO grade, even when incorporating 1p/1q status, did not reach significance in this model, further reinforcing the superiority of molecular classification.

Importantly, our findings carry broader implications for clinical trial design in neuro-oncology. Meningioma trials often span many years, during which biological insights evolve. The RTOG 0539 study began over a decade ago; molecular classification systems have redefined risk and therapeutic targets since then. Integrating molecular profiling into the design and interpretation of future trials is essential. The inclusion of biomarker-driven treatment arms, particularly for biologically high-risk tumors, could improve therapeutic precision and outcomes. Prospective tissue collection with centralized molecular analysis may represent a practical strategy to enable incorporation of these approaches across multi-center and international studies.

While this cohort provides important molecular insights, treatment was not randomized within risk groups, and all patients in the intermediate- and high-risk arms received adjuvant radiotherapy. As a result, we could not evaluate whether certain molecular features predict the benefit of radiotherapy compared to observation. This highlights the role of our findings in shaping future trial design. Specifically, our results support the need for prospective trials that incorporate molecular stratification and randomize RT within risk groups to determine treatment benefit and guide risk-adapted therapeutic strategies. Additionally, it’s worth noting that despite the wealth of data to support routine DNA methylation profiling for meningioma, the resources necessary for these tools remain limited to select institutions. While this is evolving given the increasingly recognized value of methylation profiling, developing surrogate markers to democratize access to these data is an important next step to ensure wide access for trial recruitment and patient care. Recent work has demonstrated value of artificial intelligence applied to computational pathology for molecular characterization of CNS tumors without the need for expensive molecular profiling, and this remains an active area of investigation within meningioma as well.19

In conclusion, molecular reappraisal of the RTOG 0539 cohort reveals that current grading and risk systems underestimate the biological heterogeneity of meningioma. Molecular Group classification provides robust prognostic stratification, predicts RT response, and identifies potential therapeutic vulnerabilities. These insights support the integration of molecular biomarkers into both clinical practice and future trial design and suggest that agents such as vorinostat may have a role in treating high-risk, treatment-resistant meningioma.

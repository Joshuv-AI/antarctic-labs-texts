ADAM9 drives PD-L1 shedding via IL-6 suppression and exploring therapeutic role of Bufalin treatment in hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Mandana AmeliMojarad, Melika AmeliMojarad, Alireza Pourmahdian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe treatment landscape for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) has changed significantly with the recent introduction of immunotherapy, particularly immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), such as those for programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1). However, the heterogeneous response to ICIs is still high, which may be related to different tumor microenvironment (TME) influenced by inflammatory cytokines like IL6. High expression of ADAM9 a type I transmembrane proteinases can affect tumor progression by cleaving various cell surface proteins PD-L1, and alter TME. Bufalin (BUF) a traditional Chinese medicine with important anti-cancer and anti-inflammation property has found to stimulates anti-tumor immune response by modulating TME, and enhances ICIs antitumor activity. In this study we first show the correlation between ADAM9 and HCC patient clinical outcomes and evaluate the stimulating effect of IL6 on ADAM9 expression and its protease activity mediates PD-L1 shedding, and investigates the immune modulating function of BUF to increases anti-PD-1 efficiency by inhibiting sPD-L1 induction in HCC cell lines and treated animals. We discovered that IL6 treatment can highly stimulate ADAM9 expression and its PD-L1 shedding activity, while BUF treatment down regulates ADAM9, and its protease activity by attenuating IL6. In conclusion BUF treatment can be considered as an adjuvant therapy lowering s PD-L1 formation to improve antitumor immunity and immunotherapy responses in HCC.Graphical abstract Keywords: ADAM9, sPD-L1, IL-6, Bufalin

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), the most prevalent type of liver cancer and the fifth most common cancer worldwide, is expected to surpass one million cases by 2025 due to its rising incidence [1]Recent evidence highlights the pivotal role of CD274, also known as PD-L1 (programmed cell death ligand 1) in maintaining an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. PD-L1 is often highly expressed on tumor cells and can be present on activated T and B cells, DCs, monocytes and occasionally on endothelial and epithelial cells [2]. PD-L1 expression can be induced by different inflammatory cytokines, while its receptor (PD-1) is expressed on the majority of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes including T cells [3]. PD-1’s binding to its ligand, reducesanti-tumor immunity, therefore tumor cells expressing PD-L1 (CD274) are thought to induce suppression of local antitumor T-cell responses [4].In recent years, the use of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), such as PD-L1 inhibitors offered new hopes for cancer treatment and its blockage increases T cell activity, which helps a sizable portion of cancer patients recover over time [5]. Even though it was expected that patients with higher PD-L1 expression in tumor tissue indicate better response rate to PD-L1 inhibitors only a fraction of cancer patients with solid tumors benefit from this therapy [6, 7],one main reason related to this variability might be the fact that the success of an antiPD-L1 therapy greatly depends on the membrane expression of PD-L1 to let monoclonal antibodies leads to major antitumor effects [3].While according to recent research, PD-L1 can be secreted in a shortened form known as soluble PD-L1 (sPD-L1), and its higher concentrations in blood plasma or serum correlated with poor prognoses in different cancers and also considered as a negative predictive factor for immunotherapy [8, 9].Metalloproteases, such as metalloproteinases (MMP) and A disintegrin and metalloproteases (ADAM), expressing on the surface of tumors are capable to produce sPD-L1 through the protease-specific cleavage which consider as a post-translational modification (PTM) known as ectodomain shedding which is also potent for increasing many drug resistance [4, 10].However, yet it is not known whether ADAM9 is involved in cleave the PD-L1 from tumor cells, considering previous works showing the role of ADAM9, as an immune modulatory gene increasing MICA shedding and stimulating lower NK cell effector functions [11]and the fact that the innate, adaptive, and stromal cells that make up the TME are all capable of secreting inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-6 (IL-6) which its higher expression can help hepatocytes to regenerate and proliferate in response to inflammatory signals via inducing expression of many proteins essential for HCC cells survival such as ADAM9,and manipulate the immunosuppressive characteristic which consider as an important regulator connects chronic inflammation with cancer [12–14]. Therefore, in the present study, we planned to indicate the importance role of ADAM9 on HCC patients’ clinical outcomes, and evaluate the stimulatory effect of IL6 on increasing ADAM9 and its shedding activity, Next we used Bufalin (BUF), a major active component of traditional Chinese medicine Chansu, with antitumor and immune modulatory function [15, 16]to evaluate its role on down regulation of IL-6, ADAM9 and decreasing sPDL-1formation specially in combination with sorafenib as a potential drug to improve HCC immunotherapy.

Materials and methods

Bioinformatics analysis

The UALCAN (http://ualcan.path.uab.edu/index.html) and TIMER2 tools were used to evaluate the ADAM9 gene expression in HCC tissue compared to normal tissues and analyze its association with pathological outcomes such as stage, tumor grade, and survival. We also used the Human Protein Atlas (THPA) online tool (https://www.proteinatlas.org/) to evaluate ADAM9 immunohistochemical staining data of normal liver cells and liver cancer tissues from THPA.

Cell culture and drug preparation

HepG2, HuH7, L02 cells, were obtained from procell (Wuhan, China). Both cell lines were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), and 100 U/mL penicillin and streptomycin. All cells were maintained at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 incubator. BUF, Ilomastat, and sorafenib were purchased from Selleck (Houston, TX, USA) were dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) (Sigma, D4540), and keep it in the refrigerator at − 20 °C, recombinant IL-6 were obtained from ABclonal Technology (Woburn, MA, USA).

CCK-8 assay

The viability of cancer cells was measured with CCK-8 kit (AbMole) based on the manufacturer’s instructions. Cells were grown to 70% confluence, seeded in 96-well plates and the cells were subject to treatment with various concentrations of BUF for 48 h and 72 h treatment, later 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was added to the cell medium and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. Cell viability was examined at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

Trans well assay

Generally, cells (1 × 105 cells) were seeded into the Matrigel coated upper chamber, while the lower chamber was filled with DMEM containing 10% FBS as a chemoattractant. After incubating for 24 h, the cells that migrated through the filter were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with a 0.1% crystal violet.

The number of migrating cells was assessed in in five random fields under the ×20 objective, and images were obtained.

RNA extraction and reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (PCR)

Total-RNAs were isolated using steady Pure Universal RNA Extraction Kit (AG, Hunan, China), using a First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Monad biotech-China) to synthesize complementary DNA and PCR was performed in a 7500 real-time System (Applied Biosystems) [17].

ELISA

Concentrations of IL-6 and sPD-L1 in cell supernatants of HepG2 and HuH7 were evaluated by a Human IL6 and sPD-L1quantizing ELISA Kit (USCN Life Science Inc., Wuhan, China), according to the manufacturer’s protocol. with or without BUF at 37 °C for 24 h.

Flow cytometry

To evaluate the expression levels of ADAM9 and PD-L1 on cell surface, cells were incubated with an anti-ADAM9 antibody, anti-PD-L1 antibody and stained with phycoerythrin-goat anti-mouse immunoglobulin (Ig) (Beckman Coulter, Fullerton, CA) as a secondary reagent and then subjected to flow cytometric analysis using a FACScan flow cytometer (Becton, Dickinson and Company).

Western blot

Briefly, whole-cell lysates were extracted using RIPA lysis buffer plus phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride (PMSF) (genfist, China). Protein samples were separated via 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) gel and transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride membranes (genfist, China). The membranes were blocked in 5% bovine serum albumin (Beyotime Biotechnology) for 2 h, then washed and incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. All antibodies were provided by AB clonal Technology (Woburn, MA, USA). After washing, the membrane was incubated with appropriate HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Dallas, TX, USA) visualized by electrochemiluminescence (ECL).

Xenograft mouse model and immunehistochemistry

The HCC cells (5 × 106) were subcutaneously injected to male athymic nude mice aged 4–6 weeks, and divided in two groups for BUF or vehicle for almost four weeks (n = 5 per group). The maximal tumor size permitted by the Experimental Animal Ethics Committee is 1 cm in diameter. Tumor volume was calculated using the formula V = (D × d2)/2. None of our animals had tumors larger than 1 cm in diameter.

Prior to the initiation of BUF treatment plasma samples were isolated by centrifugation and stored at − 80 °C until use. Tissue was fixed in 10% formalin, dehydrated, embedded in paraffin and sliced. The sections were then deparaffinized in xylenes and rehydrated by a graded series of alcohols, followed by antigen retrieval and blockage of endogenous peroxidase activity by 3% hydrogen peroxide in methanol. Tissue sections were incubated with primary antibodies for 2 h. Chromogen development was automatically accomplished with Envision Detection system (Dako) and images were obtained by Nikon microscope.

All experiments and animal experiments were preformed according to the guidelines for the care and use of laboratory animals approved by the animal experimentation ethics committee of Tehran medical university and in accordance with ARRIVE guidelines.

Statistical analysis

The significant differences among the groups were analyzed using a one-way analysis of variance followed by an ANOVA and t-test. All data are presented as the means ± SD. A value of p < 0.05 was considered as an indication of statistical significance.

Results

ADAM9 expression in HCC tumors are related with worse clinical outcome

We evaluate the expression of ADAM9 in HCCtumors by using TCGA data. In order to find its pathological diagnostic value, we investigated its association between higher ADAM9 and clinicopathological features such as stage and Overall survival (OS), which indicate the lower survival outcomes (LIHC (p = 0.0129)), and higher tumor grade and stage in patients (Fig. 1A–C). Based on the results both RNA and protein levels of ADAM9 were significantly over expressed in liver tissues compare to normal control (Fig. 1D–F). We also evaluate the effect of higher expression of ADAM9 on human immune cell subtypes using CIBERSORT method, and indicate its effect on PD-L1 expression using LIHC-TCGA cohort (Fig. 1G–H).

IL-6 promote ADAM9 expression and sPDL-1 production in HCC

In this work by using correlation analysis we highlight the positive correlation score between IL-6 and expression of target genes (ADAM9, PD-L1), and also positive correlation between higher expression of ADAM9 and PD-L1 (CD274) in liver cancer tissues (Fig. 2A).Considering the key modulatory role of higher expression of IL-6 in tissues and serum linking inflammation with cancer progression, and specially based on the previous work showing the dose-dependent correlation between IL-6 and ADAM9 expression in in MHCC97H and HuH7 cells, indicating that IL-6 upregulates ADAM9 via activating phosphoinositide-JNK [18]in this study we have determined the role of IL6 on HuH7 and HepG2 cell lines (Fig. 2B) .In the next step we showed the involvement of IL-6 in the regulation of surface expression of transmembrane protein ADAM9 and immune checkpoint ligand PD-L1 by flow cytometric analysis. Based on the result IL-6 (50 ng/mL) can stimulate abundance of ADAM9 and PD-L1 on the surface of both HepG2 and HuH7 cells and promotes tumorigenesis in HCC (Fig. 2C, D).

ADAM9 mediate PDL-1 shedding in HCC

To examine the involvement of ADAM9 to cleave surface PD-L1 and produce sPD-L1, we used metalloproteinase inhibitor ilomastat (ILM), to suppress ADAM9 expression in HCC cells [19]. To confirm the result we also evaluate the effect of sorafenib as an FDA approved multikinase inhibitor with potential ability to inhibit ADAM9 expression and its protease activity [20] ,and evaluate their potential effect on sPD-L1 production, indicating the potential effect of ADAM9 in PDL-1 Shedding (Fig. 3A, B).Next we evaluate the effect of IL-6 treatment (50 ng/mL, 30 min) alone or in combination with each ADAM9 inhibitors or in combinational of (ILM + sorafenib) treatments on production of sPD-L1 over 48 h. Based on our data higher level of IL6 could induced sPD-L1 production while in combination with ADAM9 inhibitors this could be alleviated. Our data also represent the synergetic effect of (ILM + sorafenib) treatments on reducing sPD-L1production in both HepG2 and HuH7 cell lines (Fig. 3C, D). Next, we evaluate the surface expression of PD-L1 on different HCC cell lines and after each specific treatment using HepG2 and HuH7 cell line (Fig. 3E, F).

Bufalin can regulate both ADAM9 and IL-6 expression and reduce s-PD-L1 release

In order to investigate the anti-inflammatory and antitumor effects of BUF in HCC, we evaluate the effect of various concentrations of BUF on cell viability using CCK-8 assay, it was found that number of floating cells after BU treatment increased as the drug concentration increased in a dose- and time-dependent manner, and the IC50 of BUF after 48 h was used for further experiments (Fig. 4A), To prove the inhibitory role of BUF treatment on ADAM9 we evaluate changes in both mRNA and protein expression level using quantitative RT-PCR and western blot (Fig. 4B, C). We indicate the effect of BUF treatment reducing cell invasion via moderating the stimulatory effect of IL-6 compare to treating cells with IL-6 alone (Fig. 4D). Next by using ELISA, we evaluate the protein level of IL6 in cell supernatant and also compared the levels of s-PD-L1 production before and after BUF treatments (200 nM), sorafenib (10 μm), or both. The results showed that the inhibitory effect was more pronounced when sorafenib + BUF was used to release sPD-L1 (Fig. 4E-F).

Next to validate our data, we evaluate the level of ADAM9, IL-6 and s-PD-L1 expression using xenograft experiments model. The nude mice were randomly divided into two groups (n = 5 per group) and the right dorsal flank of each mouse were subcutaneously injected with PLC5 cells, and 7 days after cells injection, BUF or vehicle were individually delivered to mice through tail vein injection three times per week. Immunehistochemistry result on the tumor tissues from BUF and vehicle-treated groups after four weeks post treatment demonstrated that the BUF treatment greatly decreased the level of both IL-6 and ADAM9. Considering that matrix metalloproteinase inhibitor (MMPI) could suppress sPD-L1 production, and MMP10 was mentioned to cleave PD-L1 from the cell surface [21]we also evaluate the level of MMP10 expression as well (Fig. 5A, B). We also evaluate the serum expression level of s-PD-L1 between BUF treated group and vehicle group using ELISA which indicate the inhibitory role of BUF treatment on production of s-PD-L1 through regulating both IL-6 and ADAM9 (Fig. 5C).

Discussion

Soon after the discovery of the inhibitory role of PD-1 on T cell and its high ectopically expression of PDL-1 on tumor cells, PDL-1 considered as a predictive biomarker for checkpoint blockade therapy [22] .At present, the best available first-line treatment for advanced HCC is a combination therapy based on PD-L1 blockade and other chemotherapy or co-stimulatory molecule agonists to produce higher response to inhibit tumor metastasis in clinical practice and lower the ICIs resistance [13, 23].It is still strange why some patients with PD-L1-negative tumors still respond to PD-L1 blockade, while nearly half of patients with positive tumor PD-L1 expression do not respond [1, 22].Shedding of specific extracellular matrix substrates is thought to be the principal mechanism by which tumor cells escape from immunity and induce resistance to ICIs treatments, for instance the cleavage of PD-L1 can produces an active sPD-L1 which has increased in the plasma of patients with cancer on immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy which could be responsible for PD-L1 blockade immunosuppressant or treatment resistance, nonetheless, clinical PD-L1 inhibitors’ ability to bind to sPD-L1 fragments is less known [24].It has been reported that sPD-L1 released by protease activity of matrix metalloproteinases members such as ADAM10 or ADAM17 correlates with pathological features of patients and inducing CD8 + T cell apoptosis [25],which may be possible way for why there is satisfactorily correlation between the cell surface expression of ADAM and low reaction of patients to PD-L1 blockade [25]. Anchored protein ADAM9 is one of the proteolytically active members of transmembrane ADAMs (a disintegrin and metalloprotease) family, it is highly expressed at the invasion edges of several cancers, regulating several cancer-related processes, including cell growth, apoptosis, and invasion and decreasing immune cell infiltration to increase cancer progression and poor clinical outcomes [26, 27].The shedding activity of ADAM members let them to cleave other transmembrane proteins and solubilizing their entire ectodomain [28].An increasing number of experimental studies have reported that inflammation and inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 are capable to invade the extracellular matrix inducing EMT, promoting invasion, metastasis, and poor radiotherapy response IL-6 is a pleiotropic cytokine, with vital role in immune responses, injury and hematopoiesis [29].High expression of IL-6 is reported in serum of HCC patients and culture supernatant of multidrug resistant cell lines playing a main role as a poor prognostic factor decreasing T cell infiltration, efficacy of ICIs, and worse disease-free survival compared with those with lower IL-6 expression in tumor [30–32],a recent study indicate the role of IL6 in up regulation of ADAM9 via activating phosphoinositide-JNK which suggest the role of IL6 in ADAM9 protein and gene expression using HuH7 and MHCC97H cells also the fact that PD-L1, and IL-6 expression are positively connected make it possible that higher IL-6 controls the expression of other transmembrane protein with co-inhibitory function in the immune response to help tumor cells evade the immune system by inducing T-cell exhaustion in HCC [18].

Natural products and their derivatives have long been recognized as valuable resources for modern drug discovery and development due to their extensive chemical diversity,

BUF is a type of steroid derived from toad venom, is known for its potent anti-tumor, anti-inflammatory, and local anesthetic effects [33, 34], .Numerous researchers have verified the impact of BUF on HCC, particularly after confirming the lower concentration of BUF in the serum of HCC patients compared to healthy individuals in 2020 [35]. As a result, BUF has been increasingly regarded as a potential anti-cancer drug for humans reversing different drug resistance mechanisms [34, 36]. However with close relationship between inflammation and cancer, understanding the exact effect of BUF on inflammation and its immunemodulatory effect in HCC is still lacking. Inflammation is a common characteristic of cancers [37].

Therefore, in this study, by focusing on the higher expression of ADAM9, and its correlation with worse clinicopathological factors such as tumor stage and lower patients’ survival, we evaluated the shedding role of ADAM9 on transmembrane protein PD-L1, and examined the stimulatory effect of IL-6 treatment on increasing ADAM9 surface expression and its proteolytic activity to induce sPD-L1.Based on the result exogenous IL-6 treated cells tend to express higher cell surface ADAM9, while treating cells with ADAM9 inhibitors (ILM) orsorafenib down regulate ADAM9 expression and restricted its shedding ability, result in lower production of sPD-L1 specially after combination of ILM with sorafenib which suggest their synergic effect on suppressing the transcription of ADAM9. In the next step we compared the efficiency of BUF as a natural product based on its enhanced effect on ICIS specially after combination therapy with anti-PD-1 [38] and also previous work about the effect of BUF on inducing non-specific cytotoxicity to NK cell by reducing ADAM9 and its MHC class I-related chain A (MICA) shedding [39]to evaluate its role on sPD-L1levels. It showed that BUF treatment can potentially lower both IL-6 and ADAM9 expression, and its shedding ability especially after combination therapy with sorafenib in both HepG2, and HuH7 cell lines. The synergic combination of sorafenib and BUF was previously suggested as the potential therapeutic strategy for patients to reduce HCC cells survival and induce apoptotic characteristics [16]Next, we indicate the role of BUF treatment on regulation of ADAM9, IL6, and PD-L1 expression and s-PD-L1 expression in tissue and the serum of BUF treated HCC mouse model using immune blotting and ELISA. In conclusion, our study described the role of BUF on reducing both IL-6 and ADAM9 as a pivotal regulator of immune responses and suggests that BUF treatment could be a novel target to lower s-PD-L1, however further research is necessary regards it’s clinical efficacy and to create a better pharmaceutical adjustment to improve outcome in PD-L1-resistant patients specially in liver cancer.

AI driven dual constraint cooptimization of affective semantics and engineering parameters for biomimetic product design
Authors: Yiyan Wang, Jiaxing He, Mei Yang, Zhou Hao, Kie-su Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In response to the limitation of the separation between emotion and rationality in traditional bionic design of product forms, an artificial intelligence-based dual-constraint generative design framework (AI-DCF) is proposed, which deeply integrates computer vision and visual dynamics modeling technology to achieve systematic coupling of biological semantics and engineering parameters. Firstly, an emotional semantic graph is constructed through the TextRank algorithm in natural language processing (NLP), combined with OpenCV contour detection to generate biological form tensor representations, forming a cross-modal semantic-morphological knowledge graph, breaking through the barrier of traditional design’s reliance on subjective experience. Secondly, a dual-channel optimization engine based on deep reinforcement learning (DRL) is developed, where the Alpha channel fusion technology realizes shape matching based on visual attention, and the visual dynamics model completes the inference of structural parameters guided by mechanics, and a real-time gradient feedback mechanism is embedded to form a data-algorithm-decision closed loop. Finally, taking Mingyu forklift as the experimental carrier, a comparative experiment is conducted to verify the significant advantages of the dual-constraint model over traditional methods in terms of biological feature recognizability (+ 37.5%), comprehensive performance score (+ 25%), and design iteration efficiency (+ 31.25%). These findings demonstrate case-level feasibility of aligning emotional semantics with engineering parameterization within the study’s constraints; it provides a preliminary feasibility verification for the mapping relationship between the emotional semantic descriptors based on artificial intelligence and engineering parameterization.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-42297-2.

Introduction

The convergence of artificial intelligence and industrial automation has ushered in unprecedented technological sophistication1, yet the dissonance between the austere aesthetics of mechanical products and users’ emotional expectations remains a critical challenge2. This tension is particularly evident in burgeoning markets like quadruped robots, projected to grow at a 16.6% CAGR through 20333, where functional performance must coexist with biomorphism to facilitate human-robot interaction. While bionic design can reconcile natural aesthetics and functional pragmatism4, existing methods face two dilemmas rooted in the emotion–rationality split. First, intuition-driven bio-prototype selection is prone to cultural bias and unstable preference5. which harms reproducibility and manufacturability6,7. Second, emerging computational tools, while enhancing function optimization8, often erode biomorphic recognizability and emotional resonance.

In response, we adopt AI-DCF to couple semantic intent with form generation under engineering constraints. The framework is operationalized through two core innovations:

1. Cognitive-semantic integration architecture‌

We establish a neuro-symbolic integration framework that synergizes NLP-based affective semantics extraction (TextRank algorithm) with computer vision-powered bio-form tensorization (OpenCV contour detection). This cross-modal paradigm systematically codifies user perceptual semantics9, biomorphic features, and engineering parameters into a unified geometric-semantic manifold through hybrid encoding of semantic primitives and formal constraints10–12. The resultant knowledge graph achieves machine-actionable translation between qualitative imagery and quantitative parameters, effectively reconciling the epistemic divide between affective linguistics and engineering mathematics while expanding solution space beyond conventional single-objective optimization frontiers.

‌2. Bio-inspired morphogenesis system‌

The pipeline integrates two complementary channels to achieve biologically faithful yet engineering-feasible morphogenesis. Channel 1 uses OpenCV-based contour tensorization13 for precise extraction and fusion of biological form features. Channel 2 applies visual-dynamics modeling for structure–property prediction with real-time gradient feedback. A system-level overview within the AI-DCF workflow appears in Sect. 3.1. Human affective feedback is captured via a 7-point Likert scale14, a setting widely validated for reliability and sensitivity in affective design and UX research15,16, with semantic anchors and balanced wording to reduce cognitive load. these inputs feed a multi-stage evaluative refinement process that filters candidates, selects optimally balanced 2D morphological proposals as foundations for 3D construction, and iteratively bridges computational creativity with engineering validation to enhance reproducibility, scalability, and deployment potential.

This study is organized into six sections. Section 2 introduces the dual-constraint rationale. Section 3 describes TextRank-based semantic distillation and OpenCV-based morphological tensorization within a hybrid neural-symbolic architecture. Section 4 presents the Mingyu forklift case and multi-criteria optimization. Section 5 compares AI-DCF with conventional approaches and discusses implications. Section 6 concludes with contributions and future directions.

Related work

The dual constraint model combines NLP, Computer Vision (CV), and Visual Dynamics, aiming to realize the unity of science and art in the bionic design of product forms.

Neuro-linguistic foundations‌

Building upon graph-theoretic text analysis, the TextRank algorithm17 enables systematic extraction of affective semantics from user-generated content through iterative node centrality computation. This linguistic intelligence framework constructs affective semantic ontologies by quantifying lexical co-occurrence patterns in design discourse18, effectively translating qualitative descriptors into geometric primitives through semantic embedding projections. The resultant biosemantic mapping matrix bridges perceptual lexicons with parametric design spaces, establishing machine-interpretable correlations between user affective cognition9 and biological morphospace coordinates10. This neural-symbolic translation mechanism overcomes the epistemological gap in traditional design semantics by enabling bidirectional mapping between natural language constructs and engineering taxonomies.

Computational morphogenesis‌

The OpenCV-driven vision pipeline19 implements multi-scale morphometric tensorization through hybrid contour detection algorithms, where octave-space image processing20 enables hierarchical feature extraction from biological prototypes. This computational geometry framework, enhanced by deep feature learning architectures21, automates the generation of strain-adaptive NURBS surfaces while preserving topological biomimicry. Contemporary implementations demonstrate its versatility through dynamic user interaction frameworks22 and manufacturing-aware design optimization systems23, where convolutional neural networks synergize with procedural modeling to achieve simultaneous aesthetic refinement and structural integrity24. The technical confluence establishes a new paradigm for data-driven morphogenesis that transcends conventional manual feature engineering.

Perceptive dynamics integration‌

Rooted in Gestalt visual mechanics25, the visual dynamics module formalizes perceptual force equilibrium through strain tensor transformations26. This biomechanical simulation framework implements attraction field optimization27 to reconcile visual gestalt principles with structural performance constraints. By encoding visual tension fields as differentiable loss functions, the system achieves parametric control over dynamic morphological attributes - including curvature continuity gradients and mass distribution ratios28. The computational instantiation of Arnheim’s visual dynamics theory enables physics-informed deformation operators that preserve biological authenticity while satisfying manufacturing tolerances, effectively operationalizing the “life-like motion” paradigm in industrial biodesign.

Methodology and framework

Construction and application of the dual-constraint model

The AI-DCF pipeline integrates NLP, computer vision, and visual-dynamics on a Python–OpenCV platform (Fig. 1). The workflow automates data acquisition, cross-modal fusion, and 2D-to-3D generation while preserving biological recognizability and balancing function with aesthetics.

The steps of the Dual-Constraint model application process are as follows:

Step 1 — Cross-Modal Data Fusion. Web crawlers29 harvest biological images and descriptions; TextRank distills affective descriptors. In parallel, multi-scale contour detection extracts hierarchical morphological features. The two streams form the Biomorphic Semantic Library for later analysis.

Step 2 — Bio-Inspired Feature Matching. We compute cosine-based semantic similarity30 and pyramid-based contour similarity31, then weight-integrate them to rank prototypes that satisfy imagery expectations and form compatibility.

Step 3 — Dynamic Morphogenesis. Channel 1 performs Alpha-blended contour fusion; Channel 2 performs visual-dynamics–guided structural derivation.This bifurcated approach ensures concurrent satisfaction of aesthetic and functional constraints throughout the form generation process.

Step 4 — Multi-Stage Evaluative Refinement. Quantitative filtering and multi-criteria analysis select balanced 2D proposals for subsequent 3D construction; feedback informs iterative refinement.

Affective semantics quantification framework‌

The proposed neuro-linguistic processing pipeline (Fig. 4) implements a hybrid approach combining graph-theoretic text mining with semantic embedding techniques. Initiated through Scrapy-based automated crawling protocols, the system harvests user-generated design discourse from Amazon and Taobao product-review pages, with access dates recorded in ISO 8601 format (YYYY-MM-DD) for each URL. For transparency and reproducibility, the full URL list is retained in our project records and can be provided upon request (representative examples are provided in Sect. 4.1.1). The corpus is cleaned by (i) intent-based filtering and (ii) POS-based retention for domain lexicon construction. TextRank weights token embeddings prior to PCA to generate semantic labels.

Prior to Principal Component Analysis (PCA)36, token embeddings are weighted by TextRank centrality and then L2-normalized and mean-centered. Unlike purely frequency-based weighting, TextRank captures contextual/structural salience (e.g., the domain-specific sense of “‘ergonomic’ in ‘ergonomic chair design’”), improving separability before dimensionality reduction. On our corpus, TextRank- and TF-IDF-weighted embeddings yield comparable PCA cumulative explained variance (difference < 1%; see Appendix Table S1). Consequently, we adopt TextRank in the main pipeline and provide TF-IDF as a fully reproducible alternative in the Appendix. The obtained data is used as the original data, and then PCA is used to reduce the dimension of the word vector value of each keyword to obtain the corresponding N-dimensional vector. This method can effectively retain the common key information of the word vector and generate semantic labels while compressing and simplifying the original data.

Building on the established bio-semantic labeling process, a bijective mapping between all 779 biological prototypes and the corresponding product design summaries is established by orthogonal projection in the shared Word2Vec embedding space (300 dimensions), where each bio-semantic label obtains a defined vector coordinate through Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)-based alignment. To maintain semantic generality, a sliding window update protocol (WindowSize = 50 samples) continuously recalibrates the embedding space by incremental gradient boosting vector adjustments, while the normalization process maintains dimensional stability, thus creating an auditable knowledge base for generative biomimetic translations while preserving bioaccuracy and engineering applicability.

Edges encode major changes in image intensity and thus carry robust shape cues37. Non-extreme value suppression

Along the gradient direction, we suppress non-maximal responses to obtain one-pixel-wide edges. If the current pixel is lower than either neighbor along the edge direction, it is set to zero and marked as a non-edge. This standard NMS step refines edges prior to thresholding.

Contour matching

We use the Mingyu forklift image as the target contour and search for similar biomorphic contours in the prepared dataset. Because shape matching is computationally demanding, we adopt an image pyramid strategy31 to balance accuracy and speed. The pyramid contains the full-resolution image at the base and progressively downsampled images above it; each level has distinct detail, enabling coarse-to-fine search.

Multi-stage evaluative refinement‌

Evaluating bionic design quality is essential for controlled iteration40. We construct an interdisciplinary assessment framework aligned with the Dual-Constraint logic. A composite panel (8 industrial-design experts, 5 brand-strategy consultants, 7 senior designers, 10 target users) performs double-blind evaluations4,41,42. Through three Delphi rounds, weights are set to biomimetic similarity 40%, engineering feasibility 35%, aesthetics 25%. A 7-point scale with quantitative criteria feeds back to the learning module, and consensus is typically reached within 3–5 iterations.

System environment

To support engineering deployment, we implement a full “extraction–matching–fusion–evaluation” system on Python 3.9/OpenCV 4.5 with a PyQt5 GUI. The modular system integrates four core modules: NLP semantic mining, CV biometric feature extraction, affine + alpha fusion, and multi-index evaluation. A built-in dynamic visualization module renders feature mappings between biological prototypes and product geometry in real time, thereby facilitating engineering-grade application.

2. Calculation of affine transformation: Two sets, A and B, were built for the forklift image and the image to be matched, each containing 70 feature points. 3D shape generation and engineering verification‌

Based on the selected 2D scheme generated by the AI-DCF framework (sample 10/14/15), it was imported into the Rhino 7.0 platform through the vectorization interface, and the NURBS surface modeling technology was used to realize the accurate 3D translation of biological morphology. Firstly, the mapping relationship between rhino horn topological features and bifurcation structure parameters was further analyzed from a three-dimensional perspective, and a coupling constraint model was constructed based on Grasshopper visual programming: (i) Gradient fusion of the cockpit surface and rhino head contour is achieved by the curvature continuity rule (continuity error < 0.03); (ii) a mechanical trajectory tracking algorithm was used to optimize the stress distribution (Von Mises stress peak ≤ 235 MPa) at the junction of the fork corner. (iii) Based on morphological gradient control, the topological analogy between the volume distribution of counterweight and neck muscle groups is completed. Under the premise of meeting the structural constraints of the forklift body (minimum ground clearance ≥ 120 mm, maximum turning radius ≤ 2.3 m), the overall mass is reduced by 15.7% and the stiffness is increased by 22.4%. Finally, the V-Ray 5.0 rendering engine is used to visualize the multi-physics field. According to the ASTM E430 standard, the reflectivity of metal parts is controlled at 0.6 ± 0.05, and the bright red color difference ΔE is less than 1.2. Discussion

In this study, the AI-DCF is proposed to solve the systematic separation problem of emotional semantics and engineering parameters in traditional bionic design through the deep coupling of computer vision and visual dynamics. Previous studies46 have shown that single-track paradigms tend to unbalance aesthetics and function, with perceptual pipelines risking semantic ambiguity and purely rational optimization eroding biomorphic recognizability. Here we present evidence—within an industrial case—that a dual-constraint approach can coordinate semantic image matching and morphological coupling through a standardized cross-modal knowledge graph.

Firstly, the TextRank algorithm in NLP was used to construct the emotional semantic graph. OpenCV contour detection is combined to generate the bio-morphological tension representation, and a cross-modal semantic-morphological knowledge graph is formed. This reduces dependence on subjective experience by operationalizing perceptual descriptors into machine-actionable constructs. Secondly, a dual-channel optimization engine based on deep reinforcement learning is designed. Alpha channel fusion technology realizes shape matching based on visual attention, and structural parameter inference is completed under the guidance of visual dynamics. A real-time gradient feedback mechanism is embedded to form a data–algorithm–decision loop for iterative refinement rather than a once-for-all mapping. Finally, using the Mingyu forklift as the carrier, we built a controlled evaluation library and conducted bimodal verification (SD-based Kansei and TOPSIS-based rational assessment), together with stage-wise CPU profiling to reflect deployment feasibility.

Following the SD/TOPSIS evaluation, we conducted a controlled comparison between AI-DCF–assisted design sample 1 and the traditional designer scheme (Baseline) under identical task specifications, constraints, and time budgets. Relative improvement was computed as [(AI-DCF − Baseline)/Baseline] × 100%. With n = 30 participants in a within-subject design (each participant evaluated both schemes; 60 paired observations), AI-DCF sample 1 achieved + 37.5% in biomorphic recognizability (95% CI: 34.2%–40.8%), + 25.0% in comprehensive performance (95% CI: 21.3%–28.7%), and − 31.25% in iteration time (95% CI: −35.9% to − 26.6%) (Fig. 24). Reliability met standard thresholds (Cronbach’s α = 0.82 ≥ 0.70; McDonald’s ω = 0.85 ≥ 0.70); sampling adequacy was supported (KMO = 0.78 ≥ 0.60; Bartlett’s p < 0.001). Significance was tested via repeated-measures ANOVA (participants as a random factor) for between-scheme comparisons and one-sample t-tests vs. µ₀ = 4 on the 7-point SD scale with Bonferroni correction for deviation from neutrality.

Compared with the existing static morphological graph system11,12, this model collaborative framework establishes learnable semantic–morphological linkages, enabling quantitative handling of contradictory semantic pairs within the design constraints. Within the forklift case, this increased semantic–morphological alignment relative to conventional schemes while preserving topological integrity during fusion. For morpho-feature extraction, convolutional approaches can encode rich appearance cues but typically rely on static libraries; our dual-channel strategy—contour fusion plus visual-dynamics derivation—explicitly balances shape similarity with structural plausibility. The observed efficiency gains (− 31.25% cycles) and NPS increase (+ 25%) suggest a practical benefit for iterative studio workflows.

Moreover, the proposed model exhibits systematic advantages for sustainable integration. Aiming at the limitation of the lack of engineering feedback closed loop in the EEG evaluation method of Zhu et al.47, this model constructed a collaborative mechanism between the seven-level Likert scale quantification system and the parameter dynamic adjustment engine. Through the three-stage iterative verification, the continuous optimization of aesthetic parameters and engineering feasibility was realized under the premise of ensuring the identifiability of biometrics. Compared with the traditional single-stage evaluation, the design cycle is shortened by 31.25%, which provides a new paradigm for sustainable bionic design.

We position these results as case-level feasibility rather than general solutions. We have conducted a small number of feasibility probes across additional form categories to gauge portability; these exploratory results are not included in the formal evaluation reported here. At present, empirical evidence is limited to a single industrial case, with a methodological emphasis on 2D contour-to-3D translation and reliance on a curated dataset of 779 lateral-view biological specimens. Consequently, generalizability depends on dataset diversity, and lateral-view bias and taxonomic coverage may constrain transferability to domains dominated by volumetric or dynamic morphological cues. The current system does not yet explicitly model time-dependent biological motion, materials/finishing effects, manufacturability and cost, or sustainability constraints The current system also does not explicitly model time-dependent biological motion, materials/finishing effects, manufacturability and cost, or sustainability constraints. These omissions bound immediate deployability in lifecycle-critical settings but provide a clear agenda for extension.

Practically, the framework offers two immediate benefits for studio workflows. First, by narrowing the admissible search region through cross-modal concordance, it reduces unproductive exploration while preserving biomorphic identifiability. Second, the multi-stage evaluative loop (quantitative filtering + multi-criteria analysis) supplies inspectable decision points that can be audited or adapted to brand strategy, improving process transparency. Taken together, the results indicate that AI-DCF is a modular foundation on which production-grade, multi-objective design tools can be built, provided that future work addresses data diversity and real-world engineering constraints.

Conclusion and future work

We presented AI-DCF, a dual-constraint framework that couples TextRank-based affective semantics with OpenCV-based morphological analysis and visual-dynamics modeling to align user-intended imagery with engineering-viable form. Practically, AI-DCF outperformed a time-matched traditional designer baseline in recognizability, comprehensive performance, and iteration efficiency, demonstrating case-level feasibility for AI-mediated mapping between emotional semantic descriptors and engineering parameterization.

The current system does not yet explicitly model time-dependent biological motion, materials/finishing effects, manufacturability and cost, or sustainability constraints; these gaps limit immediate deployment in cost- or lifecycle-critical settings and will be prioritized in future work. Near-term efforts will focus on (i) expanding datasets to include volumetric and dynamic biological exemplars; (ii) incorporating materials, manufacturability, cost, and sustainability into multi-objective optimization; (iii) unifying perceptual feedback and rational scoring within a statistically principled multi-objective controller; and (iv) conducting multi-domain replications to establish external validity. These steps are essential for progressing from case-specific feasibility to broadly generalizable, production-grade design middleware.

In sum, AI-DCF provides a transparent and extensible pathway for integrating emotional semantics with engineering logic. With targeted expansions in data and constraints, the framework can evolve from case-specific feasibility to broadly generalizable, production-grade design middleware.

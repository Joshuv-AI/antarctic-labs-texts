Gender-specific gene profiling in Drosophila sporadic model of Parkinson’s disease
Authors: Shreyas M Iyer, Meghana Tare
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractParkinson’s disease (PD) is the second most common neurodegenerative condition characterized by dopaminergic neuronal loss in substantia nigra region of the brain. Sporadic PD cases have been attributed to exposure to toxins, such as rotenone. Prolonged exposure to rotenone presents itself with PD like symptoms including, but not limited to tremors, bradykinesia, and motor impairments. Similar to any other disease, PD is known to affect the global population in a gender-biased manner, where males are affected as much as 1.5–2 times more than females. Some of the molecular underpinnings of these differences have been reported, but not completely understood. We modelled sporadic form of PD in Drosophila melanogaster using a novel rotenone feeding approach and attempted to understand the gender specific genetic and molecular responses. Our model also exhibited gender biased response to rotenone exposure in PD symptoms and related pathophysiology. Analysis of gene expression from different pathways reveals that these differences are relevant at early as well as later stages of the adult fly life. Our study provides molecular insights into gender differences exhibited in pattern of neurodegeneration in sporadic PD models and helps identify pathways which are affected upon toxin exposure, which may aid as early transcriptional disease biomarkers for symptomatic cases.Keywords: Parkinson’s disease, Rotenone, Gender-bias, Immune pathway, Drosophila melanogaster

Introduction

Parkinson’s disease (PD), characterized by motor and non-motor symptoms, involves the loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra region of the midbrain leading to symptoms namely bradykinesia, muscular rigidity, tremors, postural instability (Dorsey et al., 2018, Narwal et al., 2024), excess salivation, mood disturbance, sleep dysfunction, constipation, and disrupted REM sleep (Zafar and Yaddanapudi, 2025). PD affects approx. 0.8% of the global population as reported in 2018 (Dorsey et al., 2018), but has been on the rise due to environmental and genetic factors (Luo et al., 2024). PD is not caused by just one gene mutation, but there are various monogenic forms of genes that result in the condition. Majorly, the gene mutations which are involved in the disease are Leucine Rich Repeat Kinase 2 (LRRK2), Vacuolar Protein Sorting 35 (VPS35), Vacuolar Protein Sorting 13 Homolog C (VPS13C), Synuclein Alpha (SNCA), Parkin, PTEN-Induced Kinase 1 (PINK-1), Protein Deglycase (DJ-1) (Dolgacheva et al., 2022). While the genetic form of PD contributes to about 10–15% of total cases (Trevisan et al., 2024), idiopathic and sporadic PD is the cause of 85–90% of total cases of PD globally (Tran et al., 2020). Sporadic PD also progresses with age, and presents with the similar symptoms, but the causes vary according to the changing lifestyle and global standards (Dolgacheva et al., 2022). Most common factors reported are ageing, traumatic brain injury, vascular risk factors, diet induced life-style conditions, addiction to substances, lack of physical activity, and exposure to toxins (Dolgacheva et al., 2022). Studies conducted in 2011 discuss the exposure to toxins like pesticides including rotenone and paraquat (PQ) have resulted in PD in humans (Spivey, 2011, Tanner et al., 2011). These toxins affect mitochondrial complexes, resulting in defective energy homeostasis (Banerjee et al., 2022, Tanner et al., 2011). Rotenone is a chemical used as a pesticide and is a mitochondrial complex-I inhibitor (Lawana and Cannon, 2020, Moretto and Colosio, 2011). Exposure to rotenone results in oxidative stress, cytoskeletal destabilization, neuronal death, and neurodegeneration (Ibarra-Gutiérrez et al., 2023, Lawana and Cannon, 2020, Van Laar et al., 2023). Rotenone is therefore extensively used to study PD pathogenesis and effect of neuroprotective molecules in animals including rats, mice, Drosophila melanogaster and, C. elegans (Lawana and Cannon, 2020, Van Laar et al., 2023).

An imbalance in the physiological production of Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) and its detoxification leading to damage to other biomolecules results into oxidative stress (Rodríguez-García et al., 2020). Rotenone-mediated ROS production leads to modifications of biomolecules including peroxidation of lipids and carbonylation of proteins. Studies have reported that these molecular events cause an increased tendency in aggregation and/or polymerization of molecules, resulting in neurodegeneration in PD (Chiaradia et al., 2019). Oxidative stress-induced damage is often accompanied by inflammation to aggravate the conditions (Azimullah et al., 2023). A specific inflammatory response is regulated by NADPH Oxidase (NOX) family of proteins consisting of NOX and Dual Oxidase (DUOX), that produce ROS in response to pathogens (Vermot et al., 2021). NOX family is involved in immune responses, growth, differentiation, and the cell death. They are also activated in response to inflammatory cytokines, damage associated molecular patterns, pathogen associated molecular patterns (Waghela et al., 2021). Studies have reported the elevated levels of NOX and DUOX during the progression of PD, resulting in oxidative stress contributing to motor and non-motor symptoms (Waghela et al., 2021).

Enhanced oxidative stress due to ROS also plays a role in activating pathways like MAPK and, NF- κB, resulting in inflammation (Begum et al., 2022). In Drosophila, innate immune response generated is regulated by the Toll (homologous to Toll-like receptor (TLR) pathway (Kleino et al., 2008)) and Immune deficient (IMD) pathway (NF- κB in mammals (Anthoney et al., 2018; Orús-Alcalde et al., 2023), and further modulate the NOX expression (Begum et al., 2022). The IMD pathway is usually activated by peptidoglycan recognition receptor-LC of the Gram-negative bacteria (Rai & Bergmann, 2024), resulting in activation of the transcription factor Relish (NF-κB RelA or RelB in humans) (Yang et al., 2024). This leads to production of Anti-Microbial Peptides (AMPs) like Defensin, Cecropin, Attacin, (Hanson and Lemaitre, 2020) and Pirk, which further regulates the pathway (Kleino et al., 2008). The Toll pathway gets activated in response to fungi and Gram-positive bacteria (Rai and Bergmann, 2024). This leads to activation of the transcription factors, resulting in AMP production, like Drosomycin (Wang et al., 2024). Both Toll as well as IMD pathways have been reported to be activated in PD (S. Lee et al., 2024; Rai and Bergmann, 2024).

An increase in gene expression of inflammatory pathways as a result of toxin exposure and oxidative stress results in upregulation of the genes and pathways that lead to apoptosis or programmed cell death (PCD). The PCD is found to be the predominant driver of cell death in PD (Guo et al., 2024).

Cellular insults induce oxidative stress, which leads to modification of protein structures causing organelle dysfunction, thereby resulting in upregulation of autophagy. This process is mediated by a family of Autophagy-related proteins (Atg), activated in response to stress by upstream players including AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) and mammalian target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1) pathways. The pathway progresses with phagophore formation, mediated by ATG proteins, eventually leading to the autophagosome formation. Further, PINK1/Parkin get recruited on the outer mitochondrial membrane leading to mitophagy (Nechushtai et al., 2023). The process of autophagy has been well studied to be dysregulated in PD due to mutation in genes namely, α-synuclein, PINK-1, Parkin, causing apoptosis (Nechushtai et al., 2023).

Although, the PD symptoms manifest differently in patients with respect to the extent of alteration in immune pathways and cell-death regulating pathways (Tansey et al., 2022). The major difference in the pathology is observed between males and females. There have been reports discussing the gender-biased nature of other diseases disease such as cardiovascular diseases, Desai et al., 2021), Alzheimer’s disease and Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis affecting females more than males (Nicoletti et al., 2023). Out of the prevalent cases of PD in 2021, 6.44 million were males and 5.33 million were females (Luo et al., 2024). Studies have reported the cause for these differences to be due to hormonal profiles, epigenetic differences, transcriptomic variations, lifestyle factors, and even representation issues in studies (Schaffner et al., 2025). Motor symptoms are also observed more in men, whereas females pose posture difficulties more prevalently (Cattaneo and Pagonabarraga, 2025). Interestingly, despite these differences, available symptomatic treatments for PD are more or less common for both genders. The prevalent differences of this gender-bias and limited attempts to study the mechanisms of this bias for PD specific symptoms warrant further investigations (Caminiti et al., 2025, Donzuso et al., 2023, Rao et al., 2023). Here, we have mimicked PD through rotenone feeding in Drosophila melanogaster, to understand the molecular mechanisms for uncovering gender differential PD symptoms. Drosophila is an established model for studying genetic and sporadic neurodegeneration mediated in PD, and its short life cycle enables to understand disease biology in a time dependent manner. We have devised a novel long term feeding exposure method for flies to mimic PD and, to observe neurodegeneration over a period of time. We uncover signaling pathway members and molecules to describe the gender differential response to prolonged rotenone exposure in our study.

Methodology

Fly stocks and maintenance

The flies used were Oregon.R wild-type Drosophila melanogaster. The flies were maintained at 25°C and were cultured on standard cornmeal agar food consisting of agar, maize powder, yeast, and sugar as previously used (Narwal et al., 2024). Rotenone exposure was given to the 0–1 day old flies sorted into males and females, in the final concentration of 500 µM, as explained in Chaudhary et al., 2025. 30 flies per vial were maintained on standard food (Control; C) or rotenone supplemented food (Treatment; T) (Fig. S1A).

Viability assay

The experiment was performed as previously discussed in Chaudhary et al., 2025. The flies were monitored for a period of 60 days from the day they were collected and were transferred to new food vial with the same composition as the previous vial. The readings were taken of the no. of flies that were dead. Further, Kaplan Meier curve analysis was performed for 90 flies of each gender (N = 90) divided into three sets, each consisting of 30 flies.

Negative geotaxis assay

The negative geotaxis experiment was performed as mentioned in Narwal et al., 2024 and Chaudhary et al., 2025 with some changes. The flies were tested for climbing ability defects at several time-points. However, an early time-point and late time-point of disease pathology of 3-day and 60-day respectively, were considered for analysis, as PD is an age dependent disease (Fig. S1, S2). The experiment was performed for three biological repeats (n = 3) with each set having 10 flies and with three technical repeats for every set (N = 3).

Oxidative stress estimation

The oxidative stress estimation was performed as mentioned in Saraiva et al., 2018 with some modifications. Briefly, 5 flies were homogenized in 20 mM Tris buffer, pH 7.0. The homogenate was centrifuged at 1600 ×g for 10 min at 4°C, and the supernatant was mixed with 2′,7′-dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (DCF-DA) and incubated in 37°C for 15 min. The readings were then taken for quantification of 2,7-dichlorofluorescein fluorescence at 488 nm/530 nm excitation/emission in a multimode reader (ThermoFisher Fluoroskan-Ascent). The results were obtained as arbitrary fluorescence units and plotted into graphs. The experiment was performed for three biological repeats (n = 3) with 5 flies in each set, and three technical repeats for every set (N = 3).

Lipid peroxidation estimation assay

The malondialdehyde (MDA) assay was performed as mentioned in Mandal et al., 2024, with few modifications to assess lipid peroxidation. Flies were homogenized in 1x RIPA buffer (Merck, #20–188) containing 1% protease inhibitor (Sigma, P8340) using sterilized pestle. The homogenates were centrifuged at 10,000 rpm at 4°C for 10 min. The supernatant was assayed for protein concentration. Following the protein quantification, the sample lysates were prepared to 1 mg/ml concentration of protein and the reaction mixture was kept at 100 ˚C for 15 min. The absorbance of this precipitate was measured spectrophotometrically at 530 nm (Multiskan FC, Thermo Scientific, DE). The obtained absorbance units were plotted into graphs. The experiment was performed for three biological repeats (n = 3) with three technical repeats for every set (N = 3).

Estimation of protein carbonylation

The DNPH assay for estimation of protein carbonylation was performed as mentioned in Solana-Manrique et al., 2020 with some modifications. Briefly, 10 mM DNPH was added to fly extracts and incubated for 10 min. The fly extracts were aliquoted to a protein concentration of 0.5 mg/ml. Then, 6 M NaOH was added followed by 10 min of incubation. The absorbance was noted at 450 nm in Multiskan FC (Thermo Scientific, DE). These absorbance units were further plotted into graphs. The experiment was performed for three biological repeats (n = 3) with three technical repeats for every set (N = 3).

Quantitative real time PCR

The real time PCR experiment was performed as reported in Narwal et al., 2024 with certain modifications. Briefly, total RNA was extracted from 20 whole body flies using the TRIzol (Ambion Life Science-15596026) method. The RNA concentrations were measured with a Nanodrop (SimpliNano ND-1000) Spectrophotometer. Equal amounts of RNA (1000 ng/µl) were used for synthesizing cDNA using Verso cDNA Synthesis Kit (AB1453A). qPCR was performed using Luna® Universal qPCR Master Mix (M3003L). The resultant Ct values were calculated and analyzed using the delta-delta Ct method and the values were normalized to their respective control groups. RP49 was used as the internal control gene. The results were then normalized to the respective gender-controls and depicted in fold-changes. Primer sequences used in this study are listed in Table (Table S1). The experiment was repeated for three biological repeats (n = 3).

Statistical analysis

Kaplan Meier curve analysis was performed for survival assay, for 90 flies of each gender (N = 90) divided into 3 sets, each consisting of 30 flies. The statistical analysis for survival assays was performed by Gehan-Breslow-Wilcoxon test, to calculate significance, ****: (p < 0.0001). All the other experiments were carried out for three biological replicates for each sample group (n = 3). Both the time-points data sets were analyzed differently for every experiments and were plotted together in one graph. All the data were expressed as mean±SD and analyzed with one-way ANOVA, with ad-hoc Tukey’s multiple comparison’s test to calculate the significant differences among groups.

Results

Rotenone exposure results in gender-differential effects on life-span, climbing ability and oxidative stress in flies

Wild-type Oregon.R flies 0–1day old were sorted gender-wise and maintained on standard food and food supplemented with rotenone (Fig. S1A) as described in Chaudhary et al., 2025. Flies were observed for survival for a period of 60 days. We observed that rotenone fed males have significantly lower survival percentage compared to rotenone fed females (Fig. 1B).

To assess the effect of rotenone exposure on climbing ability, at the early (3 day) and late time-points (60 day), negative geotaxis assay was performed. The flies exposed to rotenone exhibited motor impairments as can be observed as early as at 3 day time-point until 60 days. Interestingly, the rotenone exposure impaired locomotion of males flies was more compared to female flies at early as well as late time-point. As expected, at later time-point, the climbing ability was significantly lower than that observed in 3 day flies; males exhibited a significant decrease in climbing ability to rotenone exposure compared to females (Fig. 1C).

Rotenone inhibits the steps of respiratory electron transport (Chiaradia et al., 2019), generating cellular stress response. Additionally, reports indicate that aging and neurodegeneration, both are mediated via oxidative stress. To detect if male and female flies present the oxidative stress in response to the exposure to rotenone differently, we detected oxidative stress levels using MDA and DCFDA assays. DCFDA measures cellular ROS levels whereas MDA measures lipid peroxidation. Upon rotenone exposure, both males and females exhibit higher levels of oxidative stress as detected via DCFDA and lipid peroxidation levels (Figs. 1D, 1E), where males were detected with significantly higher oxidative stress compared to females at early as well as late time-point of 3 and 60 day respectively. Cellular stress also results in disruption of protein structures (Chiaradia et al., 2019, Josiah et al., 2022), which can be measured via protein carbonylation assays using DNPH. The protein carbonylation levels (Fig. 1F) at 3 day time-point also showed similar trends as observed in the 3 day time-point of MDA assay, but in case of the 60 day time-point, the carbonylation levels were found to be higher in females than males and the increase in both males and females on exposure to rotenone was insignificant with respect to their controls. Taken together, our results indicate that males and females respond differently to rotenone mediated neurodegeneration, with females showing slower progression of locomotory symptoms, possibly due to lower oxidative stress levels as compared to age and treatment-matched males.

Rotenone exposure results in activation of IMD pathway related in a gender specific manner

Oxidative stress is one of the activators for pathways involved in inflammatory responses. The innate immune pathway, IMD (TNF-alpha in humans), has been reported to be activated in response to oxidative stress, resulting in the subsequent activation of AMPs, the downstream activators of the pathway, namely Cecropin, Attacin, Diptericin, and Pirk (Begum et al., 2022, Orús-Alcalde et al., 2023) (Fig. 2A). Hence, we tested if males and females show differences in the mRNA levels of Relish, the transcription factor of the IMD pathway, and AMPs, Cecropin, Pirk, Attacin, and Diptericin for the effects of rotenone exposure at 3 day and 60 day time-points.

In case of 60-day time-point, the trend is reversed with males expressing upregulated mRNA levels and females expressing downregulated mRNA levels. RT-PCR of Cecropin (D) and Attacin (E) showed that the expression was upregulated in both males and females on exposure to rotenone at 3-day time point. At 60-day time-point, males had elevated mRNA levels whereas females expressed downregulated mRNA levels on exposure to rotenone. RT-PCR of Pirk (F) shows decreased mRNA levels at 3-day time point. At 60-day time point, males expressed downregulated mRNA levels whereas females had upregulated mRNA levels of Pirk. The experiment was performed for three biological repeats (n = 3). Interestingly, an increase in the expression levels of Relish after the exposure to rotenone in both genders was observed at the early as well as late time-point. Surprisingly, this upregulation was more pronounced in females at 3 day compared to 60 day. Contrary to this, males exhibited significant upregulation at 60 day unlike females (Fig. 2B). Elevated nuclear levels of Relish induce transcription of AMPs: Diptericin, Cecropin, Attacin and Pirk. Interestingly, despite upregulated transcription of Relish, we observed a significant downregulation of Diptericin, Cecropin, and Attacin transcription in 60 day old females compared to age matched males, upon exposure to rotenone (Fig. 2C-E). Although at the early time-point of 3 day, rotenone exposure resulted in upregulation of Cecropin and Attacin in both males and females. Diptericin surprisingly was found to be upregulated more in 3 day old females compared to males (Fig. 2C-E). In case of Pirk (Fig. 2F), we observed a completely opposite trend, where Pirk transcription was downregulated post-exposure to rotenone, in both males and females, but lower in females at the 3 day time-point. In case of the 60 day time-point, there was a decrease in expression of the Pirk in males and an upregulated expression in females exposed to rotenone, with respect to their controls.

We tested transcriptional changes in, Toll and its effector AMPs, in flies exposed to rotenone at early and late time-points.

We found that in case of Toll, there was an upregulation observed in both males and females on exposure to rotenone, with respect to their respective controls in 3 days time-point (Fig. 3B). At the 60 day time-point, the males showed significant upregulation while females showed a downregulation on exposure to rotenone. Male flies exhibited an opposing trend for Drosomycin transcription in early and late time- points, downregulated at early and upregulated at later time-points. Contrary to this, females exhibited upregulation of Drosomycin transcript at early as well as late time-points. In case of Metchnikowin (Fig. 3D), the males had higher expression on exposure to rotenone whereas females exhibited similar levels at 3 day time-point. However, in case of 60 day time-point, there was a significant upregulation observed in males, but a decrease in the expression in females post-exposure to rotenone with respect to their controls. In case of Bomanin S5 (Fig. 3E), there was an upregulation observed in both males and females on exposure to rotenone, with respect to their respective controls in 3 day time-point. At the 60 day time-point, the males showed significant upregulation in Bomanin S5 levels while the females exhibited lower levels at 60 day time-point on exposure to rotenone. In case of Bomanin S6 (Fig. 3F), there was an upregulation observed in both males and females on exposure to rotenone, with respect to their respective controls in the 3 day time-point. At the 60 day time-point, the males showed similar levels while the females exhibited lower levels of Bomanin S6 on exposure to rotenone. In case of Baramicin (Fig. 3G), there was a downregulation observed in males and a mild upregulation in females, on exposure to rotenone, with respect to their respective controls in the 3 day time-point. In the 60 day time-point, the males showed higher levels while the females exhibited lower levels of Baramicin on exposure to rotenone.

Enhanced oxidative stress, and inflammation gene upregulation are known to result in cell death upon different kinds of cellular insults (Guo et al., 2024, Nechushtai et al., 2023). We therefore tested the expressions of activators of cell death known to be involved in PD pathogenesis: Head Involution Defective (HID), reaper, Death related ced-3/Nedd2-like (DREDD), Autophagy Related 5 (ATG5), PINK-1, and Parkin.

In case of HID (Fig. 4B), in the 3 day time-point, both the males and females showed elevated levels on exposure to rotenone, but it was more pronounced in females. In case of the 60 day time-point, the males and females exhibited a significant upregulation on exposure to rotenone, with respect to their controls, but higher in males than females. In case of Reaper and DREDD (Fig. 4C, D), an upregulation was observed at the 3 day time-point in both the males and females, on exposure to rotenone. Whereas, 60 day old males exhibited significantly higher transcript levels of reaper and DREDD compared to their female counterparts upon rotenone exposure. In case of both PINK-1 (Fig. 4E) and Parkin (Fig. 4F), at the 3-day time-point, both males and females showed a higher expression post-exposure to rotenone with respect to their controls, but in the 60 day time-point, the expression was lower in males and similar in females post-exposure to rotenone, with respect to their controls. The transcript levels of ATG5 were found to be upregulated at the 3 day time-point in rotenone exposed males and females. However, at the 60 day time-point, the expression was higher in males and lower in females.

Discussion

PD has been under research for almost 200 years and a lot has been explored about the disease etiology including its symptoms and causes (Li & Le, 2017). PD pathogenesis hallmarks that include motor and non-motor symptoms, have been previously documented (Dorsey et al., 2018, Zafar and Yaddanapudi, 2025). While PD due to gene mutations only contribute to 10–15% of total cases worldwide; remaining cases are attributed to sporadic PD resulting due to exposure to pollutants, toxins like rotenone, paraquat (Spivey, 2011, Tanner et al., 2011). Extensive research has been done using rotenone and PQ to understand the pathophysiology of the disease (Lawana and Cannon, 2020, Van Laar et al., 2023). PD affects patients based on genetics, occupational circumstances, and gender. Multiple reports have suggested towards the gender-biased pathogenesis of the disease and this bias is attributed to factors including epigenetic profile, occupation, and hormonal profile (De Miranda et al., 2019, Sarukhanyan et al., 2025, Schaffner et al., 2025). Reports also suggest differential treatment conditions for females than male model organisms for rotenone exposure to observe neuronal damage and degeneration (De Miranda et al., 2019, Sarukhanyan et al., 2025, Van Laar et al., 2023). This holds true not only for sporadic models of PD but also for genetic models of PD where there is a gender-biased nature of disease progression observed. Study using pink-/- mice reported increased anxiety/ depression like symptoms in females than males (Feehan & Kritzer, 2025). A study reported that A53T mice model reported lower cognitive performance, higher motor impairments and olfactory impairments in males than corresponding females (Costa et al., 2020). A study discussing the MitoPark mouse model reviewed that female mice exhibited less severe PD symptoms than corresponding males and ovariectomized females (Russillo et al., 2022). Interestingly, (Rodríguez-Navarro et al., 2008) reported that parkin-/- male mice exhibited comparatively higher number of neurons than females.

Despite observations that genes that regulate sexual behaviors, are different for Biological sexes; it is not clearly understood how sex-determination related genes play a role, or are affected in PD. In Drosophila, sex determination is regulated primarily by the presence of XX that involves a genetic pathway regulating the activation of sex-lethal (sxl), transform (tra) transform 2 and doublesex (dsx); further resulting in activation of genes involved in sexual-dimorphic processes like oogenesis (Kato & Watanabe, 2025). The exact relationship between these genes and PD has not been established, but there are reports suggesting inhibitory effects of dsx and sxl having differential effects on longevity, and stress resistance in males and females (Tower, 2015), with females exhibiting an inhibitory mechanism on oxidative stress and apoptosis (Tower et al., 2020). SRY-gene in mammals is reported to play a role in mating (J. Lee et al., 2019) and mating has been reported to play a role in reducing the effects of oxidative stress (Liu et al., 2024). There are known reports of the effects of estrogen and testosterone (Tower, 2015, Tower et al., 2020). Tra- gene has been reported in a study to play a role in oxidative stress response in female flies (Pomatto et al., 2017). A recent study has reported regulation of immune pathway genes and their expression by sxl, dsx, and fru and hence providing an upper hand to females (Khan & Graze, 2024).In this study, we have characterized gender-biased progression of the neurodegeneration mediated via rotenone exposure and possible molecular pathways involved in pathogenesis of PD.

Previously, we have shown that flies fed continuously on 500 μM rotenone (Fig. S1A; Chaudhary et al., 2025) mimic PD symptoms of locomotion and survival defects and these symptoms are more pronounced in Oregon.R flies compared to Canton-S, flies; both wild type strains of Drosophila (Chaudhary et al., 2025). Prior to this, we considered a range of concentrations of rotenone from literature (Ayajuddin et al., 2022) and performed survival and negative geotaxis assay of the Oregon.R males exposed to these concentrations for a period of 30-days. We found that males exposed to 500 μM rotenone were optimal for the study based on the toxicity effects in terms of impact on survival (Fig. S1B) and climbing ability (Fig. S1C). Following this, Oregon.R flies were exposed to rotenone for a period of 14 days, we found significant reduction in the viability (Fig. S1D) and climbing ability (Fig. S1E). It is interesting to note that an age-dependent decrease in climbing ability is also observed in both control and treatment groups. This also aligns with previously published studies discussing the locomotor defects observed in flies and other models on exposure to rotenone (Adedara et al., 2023, Ayajuddin et al., 2022, Jayaram and Krishnamurthy, 2023).

Our data on survival and locomotory defects at the 3 day and 60 day time-points (Figs. 1B, 1C) align with the previously reported observations. Although, we report that the extent of decrease in locomotor impairment is higher in males compared to females in a time-dependent manner (Fig. S2, 1 C). In flies, similar results were observed in rotenone exposure approaches with vehicles like DMSO and Ethanol (Ayajuddin et al., 2022, Rasheed et al., 2023).

It is well reported that the exposure to rotenone results in oxidative stress and associated physiologies (Rasheed et al., 2023, Sanders and Timothy Greenamyre, 2013) and the sensitivity of males to oxidative stress on exposure to rotenone is more than that of females (De Miranda et al., 2019). We estimated oxidative stress marker levels (Fig. 1D-F) in both genders across both early and late time-points to check if oxidative stress is involved in defects in climbing ability and reduction in lifespan. We found that oxidative stress was observed to be higher in males on exposure to rotenone in both the time-points but in females, the levels were almost similar as control (Fig. 1D). It is interesting to note that the overall levels of ROS was higher in males than females in both the time-points. We also found higher levels of lipid peroxidation (Fig. 1E) in males in both the time-points and females had comparable levels with respect to their controls. Studies involving mixed gender population of flies reported similar results, where an increase in lipid peroxidation levels was observed on exposure to rotenone (Akinade et al., 2022). Interestingly, this trend was reversed in protein carbonylation levels (Fig. 1F) with females showing similar levels as males on rotenone exposure in the early time-point, but higher levels in the 60 day time-point where the males still had comparable levels. Studies have reported a clear increase in protein carbonyl levels on exposure to rotenone in males higher than females (Akinade et al., 2022). Rotenone exposure resulting in similar effects on both genders in terms of protein carbonyl levels need further research. We also tested transcript levels of NOX and DUOX, which encode for oxidases in mediating ROS (Begum et al., 2022) (Fig. S3). The role of NOX family in PD and rotenone induced PD is well studied and suggested to play a role in elevating oxidative stress and associated physiologies (Keeney et al., 2022, Wang et al., 2024). When we checked mRNA levels of NOX (Fig. S3B), we found elevated levels of NOX in males on exposure to rotenone at the 3 day time-point but females expressed comparable levels. However, in the 60 day time-point, we found downregulated levels of NOX in both males and females and the decrease was more pronounced in females. In case of DUOX (Fig. S3C), there was an increase observed in both males and females on rotenone exposure at the 3 day time-point but comparable levels in the 60 day time-point. This could possibly be attributed to initial cellular shock response resulting in oxidative stress and mitochondrial stress but warrants further research, possibly in genetic models. Our results although suggest that oxidative stress is manifested in a gender-biased manner in our model.

Increased oxidative stress is known to result in increased inflammation (Begum et al., 2022, Jayaraj et al., 2021). Since flies only consist of innate immune pathways, Toll and IMD, and in mammals, CNS is protected by the innate immune pathways (Lye & Chtarbanova, 2018), we tested both IMD and the Toll pathway transcripts upon rotenone treatment. IMD pathway is activated in response to Gram-negative bacteria, resulting in the activation of relish, that further leads to secretion of AMPs (Fig. 2A). The IMD pathway is also found to be upregulated in PD along with its AMPs, Attacin, Defensins, Cecropin, and Diptericin (S. Lee et al., 2024; Rai & Bergmann, 2024). Research conducted by (Maitra et al., 2019) also points out the upregulation of Relish and associated AMPs in response to PQ exposure in flies. Our results indicate that at 60 days post-exposure to rotenone, despite activation of the relish transcription, females had lower expressions of AMP transcripts, while males had higher levels (Fig. 2B-E). This result is similar to previously reported studies highlighting the upregulated expression of relish in rotenone-induced PD models (Sur et al., 2018), and upregulated TNF-alpha pathway in N-methyl-4-phenyl-1,2,3,6-tetrahydropyridine (MPTP)-induced PD model (Bourque et al., 2023). The IMD pathway is regulated negatively by Pirk (Kleino et al., 2008). We also observed that Pirk transcription followed an opposite trend compared to Diptericin, Cecropin and Attacin (Fig. 2F). This aligns with the report by Kleino et al., 2008, stating Pirk acts as an inhibitor of IMD pathway. Upregulation of Pirk in females at the 60 day time-point suggests decreased activation of IMD pathway, and downregulation at the 3 day time-point and in males at the 60 day time-point signifies that the genes of IMD pathway are upregulated. This aligns with a study by Dähn & Wagner, 2025 involving untreated flies at the 10 day and 30 day time-point.

Toll pathway is activated in response to fungi and gram positive bacteria resulting in the release of AMPs (Rai & Bergmann, 2024) (Fig. 3A). Our results indicate upregulated levels of toll (Fig. 3B), for both males and females at the 3 day time-point, however, at the 60 day time-point, the females had lower toll transcript. A similar trend was observed in the levels of AMPs, which are downstream effectors of Toll pathway: Drosomycin (Fig. 3C), and Metchnikowin (Fig. 3D). The Toll pathway in response to fungi releases AMPs belonging to Baramicin (Xu et al., 2023), and Bomanins family (Huang et al., 2023). Baramicin and Bomanins play a role in antifungal response, but there are no reports suggesting their role in PD progression. We found a gender differential response in mRNA levels of Bomanin S5 (Fig. 3E), Bomanin S6 (Fig. 3F), and Baramicin (Fig. 3G), in flies exposed to rotenone across both the time-points. Bomanin transcripts are upregulated in males and females at the early time-point but higher only in males at the late time-point. Interestingly, males do not show changes in transcription of Baramicin, at the 3 day time-point, but females exhibit higher levels on exposure to rotenone. At the later time-point, the trend observed is similar as the rest of the AMPs of the Toll pathway. These results align with the trends of oxidative stress markers and suggests that females are possibly able to mitigate the stress-induced response better than in males when exposed to rotenone. This is coherent with the lower immune expression observed in females than males in PD (De Miranda et al., 2019). Previous reports have indicated gender biased expression of Toll pathway members in PD pathogenesis (De Miranda et al., 2019). The role of AMPs Drosomycin and Metchnikowin is also reported to play a role in PD progression (S. Lee et al., 2024), but the role of Baramicin and Bomanins is still yet to be understood. Recent publication by Dähn & Wagner, 2025 discusses untreated flies at the 10 day and 30 day time-points. It discusses males expressing higher levels of Metchnikowin, while females express higher levels of Toll, and Drosomycin. While this study involves untreated flies aged for 30 days, our work involves exposing the flies to rotenone for a period of 60 days, which might be the reason for differences in our report which points to Toll and Drosomycin being higher in males compared to females at the 60 day time-point and lower in males at the 3 day time-point. We present a time-based study whereby we show that with time, females might be able to regulate their toll-mediated immune response better than males on exposure to rotenone.

Our work suggests that the lower oxidative stress levels is directly related with the IMD and Toll pathway activity and reflects on the survival and climbing ability observed in males and females. Lower oxidative stress, controlled activation of the IMD and Toll pathways in females possibly enables flies to exhibit better survival and locomotion as compared to age-matched rotenone exposed males.

Oxidative stress and inflammation leads to cell death or apoptosis. This pathway has been found to be extensively studied for cell death and degeneration in PD. It has been understood that apoptosis is upregulated with rotenone exposure (Siddiqui et al., 2013, Zhao et al., 2014). Meta-analysis studies have reported apoptosis to be higher in males than in females in PD (Gordillo-González et al., 2024). Reports highlight the activating role of SMAC/DIABLO (HID, Reaper, Grim) in the toxin-induced models of PD (Venderova & Park, 2012), and the involvement of caspase 8 (DREDD) in rotenone-induced models of PD (Rasheed et al., 2023), resulting in apoptosis. There are no reports highlighting the role of SMAC/DIABLO in rotenone-induced PD. Instead, higher mRNA levels are reported in males in comparison to females after stroke as observed in mice (Siegel et al., 2011). The gender-biased role of DREDD is also not reported in rotenone-induced PD. Our work involves understanding the effects rotenone exposure on mRNA levels of HID, Reaper, and DREDD, in males and females consistently exposed to rotenone for a period of 60 days. We found that in HID (Fig. 4B), Reaper (Fig. 4C), and DREDD (Fig. 4D), both males and females exposed to rotenone at the 3 day time-point showed increased expression, but at the 60 day time-point, only males had upregulated expression while females had lower or similar expression as control.

Previously published research has highlighted the role DREDD being an upstream regulator of Parkin and hence disabling it to bind to mitochondria when activated (Kahns et al., 2003). Parkin is a protein involved in mitochondrial homeostasis and gets recruited to mitochondria by PINK-1 in response to stress, resulting in mitophagy (Nechushtai et al., 2023). Autophagy is regulated by ATG5 (Nechushtai et al., 2023), but ATG5 also plays a role in regulating apoptosis by its interaction with B-Cell Lymphoma-extra-large (Bcl-xL) (Young et al., 2012). Various reports suggest that the autophagy is dysregulated in PD, eventually leading to apoptosis (Bekker et al., 2021, Ghavami et al., 2014, Mader et al., 2012). There is extensive research on autophagy playing a role in the gender-biased nature of PD (Cattaneo and Pagonabarraga, 2025, Cucinotta et al., 2024). Previous research has also pointed out to the gender-biased nature of PINK-1 activity resulting in PD. Mutations in PINK-1 result in early onset of PD in females than males (Yin & Dieriks, 2025), while males with PINK-1 mutation exhibit more cognitive defects than females (A. M. Desai et al., 2025). Another study by Williams et al., 2024 reports higher reactivity of T cells due to PINK-1 mutation in males than in females. In Parkin-deficient animal models, female mice exhibit milder symptoms than males, and estrogen is also reported to modulate Parkin functions (Rodríguez-Navarro et al., 2008). ATG5 is also reported to be more expressed in males with PD than the females (Sepúlveda et al., 2022). This aligns with reports suggesting enhanced apoptosis in the males than females in PD (Gordillo-González et al., 2024). We found that rotenone exposure results in an increase in expression of PINK-1 (Fig. 4E), Parkin (Fig. 4F), and ATG5 (Fig. 4G) at the 3 day time-point. At the 60 day time-point, interestingly, PINK-1 and parkin levels were found to be downregulated in the males but similar to control in the females. In case of ATG5, in the 60 day time-point, the males had upregulated expression on rotenone exposure but females had lower levels. This suggests that on exposure to rotenone, the males exhibit upregulated levels of genes regulating apoptosis compared to females considering the lower levels of PINK-1, Parkin and higher levels of ATG5, with unchanged or lower levels of these genes in the females, at the 60 day time-point.

Taken together, we provide evidences for gender-differential responses, involving differential effects on oxidative stress, inflammation and cell death on exposure to rotenone. Our results provide an explanation for enhanced PD pathogenesis in males compared to females. We report, with lot of caution that some genes are expressed more, at the early time-point in both genders, which are possibly a result of sudden cellular shock. We report that eventually in the later time-point, the female flies regulate inflammatory responses better than males.

Our study points out the relationship between oxidative stress, inflammation, apoptosis with survival and motor impairments, as previously reported (Rasheed et al., 2023). It also reports that females, on exposure to rotenone, exhibit lower levels of oxidative stress markers, immune pathway genes mRNA levels and cell-death regulating genes’ mRNA levels along with improved survival and climbing ability as compared to age-matched males exposed to rotenone.

These results certainly warrant genetic screenings and big data analysis studies in PD patients across the globe in order to find a better management of the neurodegeneration related symptoms. After proper elucidation of the factors responsible for this gender-biased physiologies involved in PD progression, it can provide a scope for exploring the efficiency of biomarker detection approaches to detect for early oxidative stress and inflammation pathway markers as observed in males that further lead to increased pathogenesis. This would be reliable since environmental toxins leading to sporadic PD, a major concern in the present world, would progress with imbalance in redox cycle leading to aggravated inflammation. Additionally, plenty of evidence points towards involvement of gut-brain axes in progression of Parkinson’s and other neurodegenerative disorders. Further studies therefore need to be focused on gender-biased microbiome effects to yield a better management of the symptoms. It has been well reported as to the effect of treatment of hormones as estrogen and progesterone, utilizing this approach with better understanding of their relation with immune pathways would provide a promising alternative. Going forward, personalized regimes tested for different ethnic populations, normalized for microbiome differences in a gender differential manner may provide better therapeutic measures for Parkinson’s disease.

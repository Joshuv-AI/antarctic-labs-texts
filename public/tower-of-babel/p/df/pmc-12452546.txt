The structure and function of FBXW5 in human diseases
Authors: Yuyang Han, Tao Yu, Zhaowu Ma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractFBXW5, a member of the F-box protein family, plays a pivotal role in various cellular processes through its involvement in ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis as a component of the SCF complex. Recent studies have highlighted the potential of targeting FBXW5 for therapeutic interventions, making it a promising candidate for drug development. Thus, it is of great importance to provide a comprehensive overview of the structure and function of FBXW5, with a particular focus on its binding motifs for gene regulation and activities to recruitment and recognition. Herein, this review summarizes the biological significance of FBXW5 in different diseases, including cancers, neurological disorders, and other pathological conditions. This review also discusses the current challenges and future perspectives in research on FBXW5 in human diseases. Although direct inhibitors are lacking, therapeutic vulnerability is achieved via SCF inhibition or targeting hyperactivated downstream signaling nodes, emphasizing the need for further studies to fully elucidate its biological functions and therapeutic potential.Keywords: FBXW5, F-box protein, Ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis, Recruitment and cognition, Cancer, Neurological diseases, Therapeutic targeting

Introduction

The F-box/WD repeat-containing protein 5 (FBXW5) is a member of the F-box protein family, which are critical components of the Skp1-Cul1-F-box protein (SCF) E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. This complex plays a pivotal role in the ubiquitin-proteasome system, which is essential for the regulation of various cellular processes, including cell cycle progression, signal transduction, and transcriptional regulation [1,2].

The structure of FBXW5 is characterized by the presence of an F-box motif, which is responsible for its interaction with the SCF complex, and multiple WD40 repeats, which facilitate its binding to specific substrates [3]. Understanding the structural intricacies of FBXW5 is crucial for elucidating its functional mechanisms.

Functionally, FBXW5 is involved in the regulation of several cellular processes through its role in protein ubiquitination. It has been shown to interact with various substrates, influencing gene regulation, signal transduction pathways, and enzymatic activities [4]. The binding motifs of FBXW5 are essential for its specificity and regulatory functions, making it a critical player in cellular homeostasis.

FBXW5's recruitment and recognition activity, particularly its role in ubiquitination, is central to its function. By targeting specific proteins for degradation, FBXW5 helps maintain cellular protein balance and modulates various signaling pathways [5]. This activity is especially relevant in the context of disease, where dysregulation of protein degradation can lead to pathological conditions.

The involvement of FBXW5 in different diseases has been a subject of extensive research. In cancers, FBXW5 has been implicated in tumorigenesis and metastasis, with studies showing its role in various signaling pathways that promote cancer cell proliferation and survival [6,7]. In neurological diseases, FBXW5 has been linked to neurodegenerative processes, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target [8]. Additionally, FBXW5 is involved in other diseases, such as cardiac hypertrophy and liver ischemia/reperfusion injury (IRI), where it modulates key signaling pathways [[9], [10], [11]].

Given its significant role in various diseases, targeting FBXW5 for therapeutic interventions holds promise. Understanding the molecular mechanisms underlying FBXW5's functions can pave the way for developing novel treatments for diseases where FBXW5 is dysregulated [[12], [13], [14]]. Therefore, FBXW5 is a multifaceted protein with critical roles in cellular regulation and disease pathogenesis. This review aims to provide a comprehensive overview of the structure and function of FBXW5, its involvement in different diseases, and the potential therapeutic strategies targeting FBXW5.

Biology of the FBXW5

The structure of FBXW5

FBXW5, also known as F-box/WD repeat-containing protein 5, is a critical component of the SCF E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. Structurally, FBXW5 is characterized by the presence of an F-box domain and multiple WD40 repeats (Fig. 1). The F-box domain, typically comprising approximately 40 amino acids, is crucial for the interaction with the S-phase Kinase-Associated Protein 1 (SKP1), which serves as an adapter within the SCF complex. This interaction facilitates the recruitment of the E3 ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme via RBX1 and enables the ubiquitination of target substrates, marking them for proteasomal degradation [15].

The WD40 repeats in FBXW5 are responsible for substrate recognition. These repeats form a β-propeller structure that provides a versatile platform for protein-protein interactions. The β-propeller structure of WD40 repeats allows FBXW5 to interact with a variety of substrates, thus conferring specificity to the SCF complex. This structural arrangement is pivotal for the function of FBXW5 in targeting specific proteins for ubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation [16,17].

Recent studies have provided detailed insights into the structural dynamics of FBXW5 within the SCF complex. These studies reveal that the interaction between the F-box domain of FBXW5 and SKP1 induces conformational changes that are critical for the assembly and stability of the SCF complex. Additionally, the WD40 repeats undergo conformational adjustments to accommodate various substrates, highlighting the structural flexibility of FBXW5 [18].

Moreover, the structural integrity of FBXW5 is maintained through post-translational modifications, such as phosphorylation and neddylation. These modifications regulate the activity and stability of FBXW5, thereby influencing the overall function of the SCF complex. For instance, neddylation of the cullin subunit within the SCF complex enhances the ubiquitin ligase activity, which is crucial for the efficient degradation of target proteins [19].

In summary, the structure of FBXW5, characterized by its F-box domain and WD40 repeats, plays a fundamental role in the assembly and function of the SCF E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. The structural flexibility and post-translational modifications of FBXW5 are essential for its ability to recognize and ubiquitinate a diverse array of substrates, thereby regulating various cellular processes. Understanding the structural intricacies of FBXW5 provides valuable insights into its functional mechanisms and potential therapeutic targeting in diseases associated with dysregulated protein ubiquitination [20].

The characteristic of FBXW5 within the SCF ubiquitin ligase family

The F-box family comprises a critically important class of substrate receptors for the SCF E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. Canonical members, such as FBXW7, FBXW1/β-TrCP, FBXW8, FBXW11, and FBXW2, are master regulators of cellular homeostasis, primarily by recognizing phosphorylated degrons on key substrates to target them for proteasomal degradation. FBXW7, the most characterized member, is a formidable tumor suppressor that degrades oncoproteins like c-MYC, Cyclin E, and NOTCH [38,39]. Similarly, FBXW1/β-TrCP targets pivotal signaling nodes such as β-catenin and IκB, thereby controlling Wnt and NF-κB pathways [40], respectively. These archetypal F-box proteins are largely defined by their direct, phospho-dependent interaction with substrates to enforce precise temporal control over cell cycle progression, transcription, and signal transduction.

In stark contrast, FBXW5 occupies a unique and specialized functional niche within this family. Its distinctiveness is multifaceted. First, its substrate recognition mechanism diverges from the canonical model. Rather than directly binding a wide array of phosphorylated substrates, FBXW5's most defined role is in the regulated degradation of a specific suppressor of mTORC1 signaling, the TSC complex. Through this indirect mechanism, FBXW5 functions as a crucial positive regulator of the mTORC1 pathway, a master integrator of nutrient status and cellular growth [8]. This positions FBXW5 not as a classic tumor suppressor but as a central governor of anabolic and catabolic processes, including autophagy and lysosomal biogenesis. Second, FBXW5 plays an exclusive role in centrosome biology, regulating the stability of centriolar proteins like CEP192 to ensure faithful centrosome duplication and genome integrity [30,31]. This unique involvement in organelle dynamics and metabolic signaling, rather than direct targeting of nuclear oncoproteins or transcription factors, delineates FBXW5 as a specialized FBXW member evolved to interface the ubiquitin-proteasome system with fundamental organellar and metabolic pathways.

The function of FBXW5

FBXW5 binding motifs for gene regulation

FBXW5, a member of the F-box protein family, plays a crucial role in gene regulation through its binding motifs. These motifs enable FBXW5 to interact with specific substrates, leading to their ubiquitination and subsequent degradation. One of the key binding motifs in FBXW5 is the WD40 repeat domain, which facilitates its interaction with various proteins involved in gene regulation. For instance, FBXW5 has been shown to bind to the PPxY sequence of the large tumor suppressor kinases 1/2 (LATS1/2) kinase domain, resulting in ubiquitination and degradation of LATS1/2. This interaction abrogates the Hippo pathway, which is crucial for regulating cell proliferation and apoptosis [21,22]. Additionally, FBXW5 interacts with transforming growth factor-beta-activated kinase 1 (TAK1), blocking the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway, which is essential for cellular responses to stress and inflammation [9,[23], [24], [25]]. These binding motifs and interactions highlight the significant role of FBXW5 in gene regulation and its potential as a therapeutic target in various diseases.

The recruitment and recognition activity of FBXW5

FBXW5 functions as part of the SCF complex, which acts as an E3 ubiquitin ligase. This complex is responsible for the ubiquitination of target proteins, marking them for degradation by the proteasome. The recruitment and recognition activity of FBXW5 is crucial for maintaining cellular homeostasis by regulating the levels of various proteins involved in cell cycle progression, signal transduction, and stress responses. For example, FBXW5 has been implicated in the degradation of kinesin-13 proteins, which are essential for microtubule dynamics and ciliogenesis [19,26]. By targeting these proteins for degradation, FBXW5 facilitates the proper formation and function of cilia, which are critical for cellular signaling and movement. Furthermore, FBXW5 promotes the ubiquitination and degradation of apoptosis signal-regulating kinase 1 (ASK1), thereby modulating the MAPK signaling pathway and influencing cellular responses to oxidative stress and inflammation [10,27]. Additionally, FBXW5 promotes Krüppel-like factor 13 (KLF13) degradation by enhancing its ubiquitination, which affects cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and inhibits ferroptosis [28,29]. Moreover, FBXW5 promotes the ubiquitination and degradation of the HsSAS-6, thereby regulating centrosome formation and subsequently influencing the cell cycle [30,31]. Furthermore, FBXW5 promotes the ubiquitination and degradation of Eps8, a bifunctional actin cytoskeleton remodeling protein, leading to morphological changes during mitotic entry and exit [32]. The recruitment and recognition activity of FBXW5, therefore, plays a pivotal role in regulating various cellular processes and maintaining cellular homeostasis. Functions of FBXW5 in different diseases

FBXW5 can specifically interact with different partner molecules and function as key regulators for mediating a diverse range of pathological changes. In this section, we focus on the expanding landscape of FBXW5 in different diseases, including cancer, neurological diseases, liver damage and pathological cardiac hypertrophy (Fig. 2).

The Roles of FBXW5 in Different Diseases. FBXW5 can promote cancer metastasis and proliferation via ubiquitinating or facilitating key factors such as AQP3, DLC1, KLF13, LAST1\2 and RhoA-ROCK1 to regulate some important signaling pathways such as MTOR and Hippo signaling pathways. It can also promote neurological damage by stimulating the expression of TSC2, p38 and JNK. Besides, it aggravates liver damage by promoting ASK1. But it can inhibit TAK1 leading to pathological cardiac hypertrophy.

FBXW5 in cancers

FBXW5 has been implicated in various cancers, functioning primarily through its role as an E3 ubiquitin ligase. In gastric cancer, FBXW5 operates as a pivotal E3 ubiquitin ligase by directly modulating core oncogenic signaling hubs. Its tumor-promoting function is executed through a dual mechanistic axis: it facilitates the assembly and activation of the FAK-Src signaling complex, thereby driving proliferation and metastatic dissemination [35,41]. In parallel, FBXW5 targets key inhibitory components of the Hippo pathway for proteasomal degradation, resulting in the nuclear translocation of YAP/TAZ and the transcriptional induction of pro-invasive genetic programs [21]. In breast cancer, FBXW5 activates the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway to promote the ubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation of the tumor suppressor KLF13. This event transcriptionally represses key ferroptotic mediators, effectively ablating the tumor-suppressive ferroptotic response and culminating in aggressive breast cancer progression [28]. In non-small cell lung cancer, FBXW5 targets the tumor suppressor DLC1, a RhoGAP, for ubiquitin-mediated degradation. This loss disrupts Rho GTPase signaling, promoting proliferation and invasion [34,42]. In hepatocellular carcinoma, FBXW5 targets AQP3 for proteasomal degradation, thereby activating the PDPK1-AKT-MTOR signaling axis to suppress autophagic cell death and promote survival in hepatocellular carcinoma [33,43]. These findings suggest that FBXW5 acts as an oncogene in various cancer types by targeting key regulatory proteins for degradation, thus facilitating cancer progression.

FBXW5 in neurological diseases

FBXW5 also plays a significant role in neurological diseases. In a Parkinson's model, Tnfaip8l1/Oxi-β binds and inactivates the E3 ubiquitin ligase adapter FBXW5. This interaction relieves repression on TSC2, activating its GTPase-activating protein function to inhibit Rheb/mTORC1 signaling, thereby inducing pro-survival autophagy [8]. This interaction suggests a potential neuroprotective role for FBXW5 in neurodegenerative diseases. Furthermore, genetic or pharmacological reduction of FBXW5 protein levels attenuates neuroinflammation by selectively impairing the phosphorylation and kinase activity of p38 and JNK MAPKs in microglia. This suppression of stress signaling cascades shifts the cellular equilibrium, curtailing the production of pro-apoptotic and pro-inflammatory mediators while favoring protective autophagic responses, ultimately preserving neuronal integrity after spinal cord injury [36,44]. These studies highlight the dual role of FBXW5 in both promoting and mitigating neurological damage, depending on the context and specific signaling pathways involved.

FBXW5 in other diseases

Beyond cancer and neurological diseases, FBXW5 is involved in other pathological conditions. In liver ischemia/reperfusion injury, FBXW5 scaffolds and facilitates TRAF6-mediated K63-linked ubiquitination of ASK1, which promotes its phosphorylation and sustained activation. This amplifies the downstream MAPK stress-signaling cascade, culminating in exacerbated inflammation and hepatocellular death during ischemia/reperfusion injury [10]. This process leads to enhanced inflammation and apoptosis, suggesting that FBXW5 is a key modulator of stress signaling in liver pathology. Additionally, FBXW5 constrains TAK1-initiated signaling by attenuating its activation and subsequent phosphorylation of pro-hypertrophic MAPK components, thereby suppressing maladaptive cardiac remodeling and pathological hypertrophy [9]. These findings indicate that FBXW5 has diverse roles in various diseases, often acting through its ubiquitin ligase activity to modulate critical signaling pathways.

Potential therapeutic strategies based on the function of FBXW5 in different diseases

The SCF complex represents a major class of RING-type E3 ubiquitin ligases that dictate the specificity of ubiquitylation for a myriad of substrates involved in cell cycle progression, signal transduction, and stress responses. Among its diverse array of F-box proteins, FBXW5 has emerged as a context-dependent regulator of critical pathways, often exhibiting oncogenic properties by targeting tumor suppressors for proteasomal degradation. Its documented substrates include the RhoGAP DLC1, the water channel AQP3, and the kinase ASK1, with degradation outcomes driving proliferation, metastatic dissemination, and resistance to cell death across cancer types [33,34]. Consequently, intense efforts are focused on understanding the regulation of this ligase and exploiting its functions for therapeutic intervention. A primary question is whether the complex itself is druggable. While no exquisitely specific, direct small-molecule inhibitor of the FBXW5 substrate-recognition module yet exists, a highly effective indirect strategy targets the upstream activation of the entire Cullin-RING ligase family. The NEDD8-activating enzyme (NAE) is essential for the neddylation and consequent catalytic activation of CUL1. The small molecule MLN4924 (Pevonedistat) is a potent and selective adenosine sulfamate analogue that covalently inhibits NAE, leading to the inactivation of SCF complexes globally [45]. This agent has demonstrated compelling preclinical efficacy in models where FBXW5 is pathogenic and is undergoing extensive clinical evaluation for hematological and solid malignancies, validating the CRL family as a therapeutic target.

The activity of FBXW5 is not a simple binary function but is exquisitely controlled through multiple regulatory strata, each presenting a potential node for pharmacological manipulation. First, its transcriptional regulation is evident, with its mRNA levels being elevated in specific cancers and injury models, suggesting that upstream signaling pathways can dictate its abundance. Second, like many F-box proteins, FBXW5 is likely subject to potent post-translational control. Phosphorylation events within its domains or on its associated partners can profoundly alter its subcellular localization, stability, and affinity for its cognate substrates. For instance, phosphorylation can modulate its interaction with the SKP1-CUL1 core, its dimerization state, or its recruitment to specific subcellular compartments where substrates reside. Finally, the most targeted form of regulation is its protein-protein interaction network. FBXW5's function is entirely contingent on its physical engagement with both the SCF structural core and its specific degrons on substrates like DLC1 or AQP3. It also acts as a scaffold, as evidenced by its TRAF6-dependent promotion of ASK1 K63-linked ubiquitination [9]. Disrupting these critical interfaces, particularly the shallow degron-binding pocket on FBXW5 or its association with obligate co-factors, represents a quintessential challenge in targeted protein degradation therapy. While notoriously difficult, advances in proteolysis-targeting chimeras, molecular glues, and peptide mimetics offer future avenues to specifically inhibit the pro-tumorigenic functions of FBXW5 by hijacking its ubiquitin ligase activity against itself or blocking its substrate-binding capacity.

Given the challenges of direct ligase targeting, a robust alternative strategy involves systematic drug repurposing and screening campaigns focused on the vulnerabilities created by FBXW5 hyperactivity. Rather than targeting the ligase itself, this approach aims to identify agents that synthetically lethally target cells dependent on the loss of FBXW5 substrates. For example, in tumors where FBXW5-mediated degradation of DLC1 leads to hyperactivated Rho-ROCK or FAK-SRC signaling, existing inhibitors of these pathways such as Dasatinib and Defactinib, could be strategically deployed [34]. Similarly, the loss of AQP3 and subsequent dampening of autophagic cell death via unleashed AKT-mTOR signaling creates a dependency that could be exploited with mTOR inhibitors such as Rapalogs [33]. High-throughput screens are being employed to identify compounds that selectively kill cells with high FBXW5 expression or that can reverse its downstream pathological signatures, such as uncontrolled proliferation or invasion. This pathway-centric screening philosophy expands the therapeutic landscape to include a wide arsenal of approved and investigational drugs that could be rapidly repurposed for patient populations stratified by FBXW5 activity.

Perhaps the most immediately viable therapeutic paradigm is the indirect targeting of the oncogenic signaling cascades that are unleashed upon FBXW5-mediated degradation of its tumor-suppressive substrates. This circumvents the direct targeting challenge and leverages well-established drug development programs. The hyperactivation of the FAK-SRC kinase axis following DLC1 degradation is a prime target, with dual FAK/SRC inhibitors showing promise in preclinical models of metastasis. Likewise, FBXW5 activity often converges on the dysregulation of the MAPK pathway; in different contexts, it potentiates JNK/p38 signaling in microglial activation or ischemia-reperfusion injury or ERK signaling, for which a rich pipeline of RAF, MEK, and ERK inhibitors exists [36]. Another major axis is the Hippo pathway, where FBXW5-mediated degradation of upstream suppressors leads to YAP/TAZ nuclear accumulation and transcriptional reprogramming [21]. Disrupting the YAP/TAZ-TEAD interface with novel small molecules is a highly active area of cancer drug discovery. Finally, in inflammatory contexts, the FBXW5-TRAF6-ASK1 module signals through TAK1 to activate MAPK and NF-κB pathways, for which TAK1 inhibitors are in development [37]. Thus, a deep mechanistic understanding of the specific pathways activated by FBXW5 in a given disease context provides a clear roadmap for employing a precision medicine approach using pathway-specific inhibitors, offering a powerful and pragmatic alternative to direct ligase inhibition.

Conclusions and future perspectives

In summary, FBXW5, a member of the F-box protein family, plays a pivotal role in various cellular processes through its regulation of ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation. Its binding motifs and enzymatic activities underscore its importance in maintaining cellular homeostasis. Notably, FBXW5's involvement in critical signaling pathways like the FAK-Src, Hippo, and MAPK pathways highlights its influence on key biological processes, including cell cycle regulation, autophagy, and centrosome duplication.

FBXW5's dysregulation has been closely linked with several pathologies, particularly cancers and neurological diseases. It can both promote and suppress tumorigenesis depending on the context, and facilitates tumor cell migration and invasion through pathways such as the FAK-Src and Hippo pathways. Moreover, its involvement in neurological diseases like Parkinson's disease through the regulation of autophagy underscores its potential as a target for neuroprotective strategies.

The therapeutic potential of targeting FBXW5 is promising but requires a nuanced approach. Future research must aim to delineate the specific contexts and mechanisms through which FBXW5 exerts its effects. For instance, inhibiting FBXW5 could be beneficial in cancers where it promotes tumorigenesis, while enhancing its activity may be advantageous in diseases characterized by impaired autophagy.

Looking forward, the integration of high-throughput genomic, transcriptomic, and proteomic analyses will be crucial in identifying novel regulatory networks involving FBXW5. Advanced bioinformatics tools and CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing technology offer powerful means to investigate FBXW5's function and regulation. Collaborations between basic researchers and clinical scientists will be essential to translate these findings into effective diagnostic and therapeutic strategies.

In conclusion, FBXW5 stands as a significant player in cellular regulation with profound implications for human health and disease. A deeper understanding of its molecular mechanisms and interactions will pave the way for innovative therapeutic approaches, potentially offering new avenues for the treatment of cancers, neurological disorders, and other FBXW5-related diseases.

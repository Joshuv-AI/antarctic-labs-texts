Identification of enterovirus 71 hand, foot and mouth disease from other enteroviruses by microfluidic digital PCR
Authors: Rui Jia, Jiajia Yin, Jinmeng Zhao, Yangyang Jia, Lifeng Li, Wancun Zhang, Fang Wang, Yuchun Liu, Yilin Bai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Enterovirus71 (EV71) is a major etiological agent of severe hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD), which can lead to central nervous system damage in children. Reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) often produces false-negative results during the early stage of EV71 infection, attributable to the low viral load. To address this limitation, we developed a rapid, precise, and highly sensitive multiplex reverse transcription digital polymerase chain reaction (RT-dPCR) assay for differential diagnosis of EV71-induced HFMD and non-EV71 pathogens. The EV71 RT-dPCR assay targeted the viral structural protein VP2 using a TaqMan minor groove binder (MGB) probe. This method achieved a detection sensitivity of 7.406 copies per microliter. Specificity analysis confirmed no cross-reactivity with non-EV71 enteroviruses. When validated against 60 clinical samples, the assay demonstrated 100% overall agreement (60/60) with a kappa value of 1.0 (p < 0.001). The developed EV71 RT-dPCR assay may facilitate early diagnosis of severe EV71-associated HFMD, enabling timely intervention for affected children.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-025-11724-6.

Introduction

Hand, foot and mouth disease (HFMD) was first detected in Shanghai between 1981 and 1997, initially characterized by sporadic cases with slow transmission [1]. Local outbreaks occurred in Tianjin, Beijing, and other regions during the 1980–1990 s, primarily caused by Coxsackievirus A16 (CV-A16) [2]. A large-scale epidemic in Taiwan, China, in 1998 marked the emergence of Enterovirus 71 (EV71) as a major pathogen [3]. Subsequent severe outbreaks in Linyi (Shandong) and Fuyang (Anhui) from 2007 to 2008 further highlighted EV71’s pathogenicity, prompting China to classify HFMD as a Category C notifiable infectious disease in 2008 and establish a national prevention and control system [4]. Since then, HFMD has become endemic, with alternating high-incidence years, expanding from eastern coastal areas to central and western regions. Rural areas consistently report higher incidence rates. In addition to EV71 and CV-A16, emerging pathogens such as Coxsackievirus A6 (CV-A6) and Coxsackievirus A10 (CV-A10) have complicated prevention and control efforts in certain regions [5].

EV71, a member of the Enterovirus genus within the Picornaviridae family, was first isolated in California, USA, in 1969 [6]. As a non-enveloped, single-stranded positive-sense RNA virus, EV71 causes HFMD, presenting with characteristic cutaneous eruptions on the hands, foot, and oral mucosa. Severe cases may progress to neurological complications, including aseptic meningitis, encephalitis, and acute flaccid paralysis [7]. The virus’s high transmissibility and potential for life-threatening outcomes pose significant public health challenges, particularly in densely populated and resource-limited settings.

Early and accurate detection of EV71 is critical for disease management. Prompt diagnosis enables timely isolation of infected individuals, curbing community spread, and facilitates early intervention to mitigate disease severity and complications. Traditional diagnostic methods, including virus isolation, serological assays, and conventional polymerase chain reaction (PCR), are limited by low sensitivity, poor specificity, and unreliable quantification [8].

Recent advancements in PCR-based technologies have addressed some limitations but introduced new challenges, such as Song et al. demonstrating the utility of armored RNA in external quality assessments for evaluating RT-PCR performance in detecting EV71 and CV-A16 to highlight strengths and gaps in current molecular diagnostics [9], while conventional RT-PCR widely used requires time-consuming, subjective post-amplification electrophoresis for result interpretation with lower sensitivity to low viral loads [10]. While real-time quantitative PCR (qPCR) enhances sensitivity and specificity via fluorescence monitoring during amplification [11], its reliance on standard curves introduces errors from variable amplification efficiencies between samples and standards, and clinical PCR inhibitors may yield false negatives [12, 13].Multiplex PCR reduces turnaround time by detecting EV71 and related pathogens simultaneously [14]. However, optimizing multi-target reactions remains challenging, risking unequal amplification and non-specific signals [15, 16]. Loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) enables point-of-care testing without thermal cyclers [17], but amplicon cross-contamination and complex product structures increase false positives [18–20].

Digital PCR (dPCR), a third-generation technology, revolutionizes quantification by partitioning reactions into thousands of nanoliter-scale droplets for absolute quantification independent of standard curves [21, 22]. This study pioneers a microfluidic chip-based RT-dPCR assay targeting the EV71 VP2 gene, addressing prior limitations by evaluating sensitivity, specificity, and clinical concordance [23].

Methods and materials

Gene and clinical samples

The VP2 gene of Enterovirus 71 (EV71) was synthesized by General Biology (Anhui, China) based on the sequence of EV71 isolate EV71/Henan/399/2010 (GenBank: HM245928.1). The ethical committee of Zhengzhou University Affiliated Children’s Hospital has approved (IRB No. 2022-K-L029). The samples are clinical residual materials and no informed consent is required. A total of 60 throat swab specimens were collected from pediatric patients at the hospital between 2020 and 2024. Moreover, the inclusion criteria for the 60 throat swab samples in this study were as follows: 60 cases were children with HFMD who visited the hospital from 2020 to 2024. The diagnosis was based on the Hand-Foot-Mouth Disease Diagnosis and Treatment Guidelines (2021 edition), combined with clinical manifestations and qPCR results (EV71 positive n = 10, other viral infections n = 50). Cases with mixed infections with other viruses or complicated with severe underlying diseases were excluded. Non-EV71 control throat swab samples, including CV-A6 (LC481394.1), CV-A16 (LC603080.1), CV-A10 (LC603098.1), CV-A4 (LC516736.1), CV-A2 (MK306742.2), CV-B3 (M33854.1), H1N1 (KP459185.1), H3N2 (CY108841.1), Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) (DQ480524.1), and SARS-CoV-2 (MT412339.1), were also procured from the same institution during the same period. All throat swab samples were de-identified residual materials following clinical testing, obviating the need for informed consent. Following the collection of throat swabs, samples should be immediately stored in a −80℃ freezer. RNA extraction procedures must be completed within one week to ensure sample integrity and optimal results.

Preparation of standard plasmid

The EV71 VP2 gene was cloned into the pET28a vector (pET28a-EV71 VP2) by General Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Anhui, China). Escherichia coli transformed with pET28a-EV71 VP2 were cultured, and recombinant plasmids were extracted using the EndoFree Plasmid Mini Kit (Cwbio, CW2106S, Jiangsu, China). Plasmid concentration was quantified via a NanoDrop One Microvolume UV-Vis Spectrophotometer (ThermoFisher Scientific, USA, Serial No. AZY1812131). Dilutions spanning 1010 to 102 copies/mL were prepared in 10-fold increments for use as templates and positive controls.

Design of probe and primers

A minor groove binder (MGB) probe and primers targeting the EV71 VP2 gene (GenBank: HM245928.1) were designed using PRIMER EXPRESS Software v3.01 (Applied Biosystems, USA). The amplicon size was restricted to < 100 bp. Sequence homology was confirmed by cross-referencing with 40 EV71 strains in the GenBank database.

Optimization of probe and primers in quantitative PCR

Optimal primer and probe concentrations were determined using an ABI Q5 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, USA) with the HiScript II U + One Step qRT-PCR Probe Kit (Vazyme, Jiangsu, China). The 20 µL reaction mixture included: 10 µL 2× One Step U + Mix, 1 µL One Step U + Enzyme Mix, 0.4 µL 50× ROX Reference Dye 1, 0.4 µL each of forward and reverse primers (10 µM), 0.2 µL TaqMan probe (100 µM), and 0.1 pg-1 µg total RNA template, adjusted to volume with nuclease-free water. Thermal cycling conditions were: reverse transcription at 55 °C for 15 min, initial denaturation at 95 °C for 5 min, followed by 45 cycles of 95 °C for 10 s and 60 °C for 30 s. Primer concentrations (2–10 µM) and probe concentrations (50–250 µM) were optimized using 1 × 10⁵ copies/mL pET28a-EV71 VP2 plasmid. Negative and positive controls were included in all runs.

RNA extraction of samples and cDNA synthesis

Viral genomic RNA were extracted from clinical throat swab samples using a TaKaRa MiniBEST Viral RNA/DNA Extraction Kit Ver.5.0 (Takara, China, Dalian) according to the manufacturer’s instructions and the extracted solution was stored at −80℃. cDNA is synthesized by 1 st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Yeasen, China, Shanghai). The total volume of the reverse transcription reaction mixture is 20 µL, including 5 µL of 4×Hifair®AdvanceFast SuperMix (without dye), 2 µL of Oligo d(T)18 Primers (50 µM), 100 ng of RNA, and RNase-free water to make up to 20 µL. The reverse transcription procedure is set as 55℃ for 5 min followed by 85℃ for 5 s.

Evaluation of EV71 qPCR

The qPCR assay was validated for sensitivity, specificity, reproducibility, and linearity.

Standard Curve: Tenfold serial dilutions (10⁸−10¹ copies/µL) of the pET28a-EV71 VP2 plasmid were used to generate a standard curve. Linear regression and R² values were calculated using GraphPad Prism 7.0.

Sensitivity: Detection limits were determined via triplicate amplification of 10⁸−10¹ copies/µL standards.

Reproducibility: Coefficients of variation (CV) were calculated from triplicate measurements of 10⁰−10⁷ copies/mL standards across three independent experiments.

Specificity: Cross-reactivity was assessed using clinical samples of CV-A16 (n = 2), CV-A6 (n = 5), CV-A4 (n = 2), CV-A2 (n = 1), CV-A10 (n = 5), CV-B3 (n = 1), H1N1 (n = 2), H3N2 (n = 2), HCV (n = 1), and SARS-CoV-2 (n = 4).

Microfluidic chip digital PCR

dPCR was performed using the QuantStudio™ 3D Digital PCR System (ThermoFisher Scientific, USA). The 20 µL reaction mix contained: 10 µL v2 Master Mix, 1.8 µL each of forward and reverse primers (10 µM), 1.8 µL TaqMan probe (100 µM), 2.6 µL nuclease-free water, and 2 µL DNA template. After mixing and centrifugation, 14.5 µL of the mixture was loaded onto a chip. Thermal cycling conditions were: initial denaturation at 96 °C for 10 min, 40 cycles of 60 °C for 2 min and 98 °C for 30 s, followed by a 2 min extension at 60 °C. Negative/positive controls and triplicate standards were included.

Evaluation of digital PCR

dPCR performance was characterized by:

Standard Curve: Tenfold serial dilutions (10⁵−10¹ copies/µL) of pET28a-EV71 VP2 were used to generate a calibration curve.

Sensitivity: Detection limits were determined via triplicate testing of 10¹ copies/µL standards.

Specificity: Cross-reactivity was evaluated using non-EV71 samples.

Concordance: dPCR results were compared with clinical qPCR data from 60 specimens (CV-A16, n = 12; CV-A6, n = 18; CV-A4, n = 2; CV-A2, n = 1; CV-A10, n = 8; CV-B3, n = 1; H1N1, n = 4; H3N2, n = 4; EV71, n = 10). Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS, with Pearson’s chi-squared test applied and p < 0.05 considered significant.

Results

Probe and primers

A MGB probe and primers targeting the EV71 VP2 gene (GenBank: HM245928.1) were designed using PRIMER EXPRESS Software v3.01. The probe sequence was 5′-(FAM)-CCTATAACTATCACATTGGC-3′-(MGB), paired with forward (5′-CAAGGAGCGACGCCAGTAAT-3′) and reverse (5′-TGCGAATTCAGAACACATTGG-3′) primers, yielding a 63 bp amplicon. Sequence alignment with 40 EV71 strains from the GenBank database confirmed high conservation of the designed primers and probe (Table 1). Nucleic acid BLAST analysis further validated the target gene’s conservation across 100 EV71 sequences (Table 2).

Determination of standard samples

pET28a-EV71 VP2 plasmids were characterized by agarose gel electrophoresis. Figure 1a shows the size of pET28a-EV71 VP2 is approximately 800 bp. The target gene was amplified by PCR using 10 µM primers and a probe. As presented in Fig. 1b, this resulting amplicon size was approximately 60 bp.

Optimal concentration of primers and probe in quantitative PCR

A series of trials in quantitative PCR were conducted to determine the most efficient stock concentrations of primers and probes. The optimal primer stock concentration was found to be 10 µM, and the optimal probe stock concentration was 100 µM, which resulted in the lowest CT value (Fig. 2). In addition to 2–10 µM, 12, 14, and 20 µM primers were also tested. It was found that the CT value was the lowest at 10 µM and there were no primer dimers. The optimized experiment was repeated 3 times, with 3 replicates per each time. The coefficient of variation of the CT value at a 10 µM primer concentration was less than 5%.

Fig. 2Different concentrations of primers and the probe in quantitative PCR

Different concentrations of primers and the probe in quantitative PCR

The optimal reaction system, with a total volume of 20 µL, was prepared using the HiScript II U + One Step qRT - PCR Probe Kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, Jiangsu, China). It contained 10 µL of 2×One step U + Mix, 1 µL of One Step U + Enzyme Mix, 0.4 µL of 50×ROX Reference Dye 1, 0.4 µL of 10 µM forward EV71 VP2 primer, 0.4 µL of 10 µM reverse EV71 VP2 primer, 0.2 µL of 100 µM EV71 VP2 TaqMan probe, 1 µg of template, and was made up to 20 µL with nuclease - free water.

Specificity: No cross-reactivity was observed with CV-A16 (n = 2), CV-A6 (n = 5), CV-A4 (n = 2), CV-A2 (n = 1), CV-A10 (n = 5), CV-B3 (n = 1), H1N1 (n = 2), H3N2 (n = 2), HCV (n = 1), or SARS-CoV-2 (n = 4), all yielding CT values > 32 (Fig. 3b).

Concordance between EV71 dPCR and clinical diagnosis

The EV71 digital PCR (dPCR) assay was tested on 60 clinical specimens, including CV-A16 (n = 12), CV-A6 (n = 18), CV-A4 (n = 2), CV-A2 (n = 1), CV-A10 (n = 8), CV-B3 (n = 1), H1N1 (n = 4), H3N2 (n = 4), and EV71 (n = 10). Detection results were compared with clinical diagnosis outcomes. All 10 EV71-positive samples confirmed by clinical diagnosis (qPCR) were identified as positive by dPCR (Table 4). Using the dPCR threshold of ≥ 7.406 copies/µL, 10 specimens tested positive and 50 tested negative. Statistical analysis via SPSS revealed 100% sensitivity (10/10), 100% specificity (50/50), and 100% overall agreement (60/60), with a kappa value of 1.0 (p < 0.001), confirming perfect concordance. These results validate the dPCR assay’s ability to distinguish EV71 from other enteroviruses with high accuracy.

Discussion

The diagnosis of HFMD relies on clinical examination and laboratory analysis [24]. Physicians identify HFMD through physical examination, noting characteristic signs such as oral ulcers and cutaneous eruptions on the hands and foot. Laboratory analysis of specimens (e.g., throat swabs, stool) is critical for pathogen identification. However, early-stage HFMD may mimic other childhood infections (e.g., measles), complicating diagnosis for non-specialists and increasing the risk of delayed or inappropriate treatment [24–26].

Despite advancements in vaccine development, no universally effective prophylactic or therapeutic options exist for HFMD. Inactivated EV71 and CV-A16 vaccines have shown promise but face challenges in global standardization, long-term efficacy, and accessibility [27, 28]. Thus, rapid and accurate diagnostic tools remain essential to mitigate outbreaks. Existing methods, including viral culture, ELISA, and conventional PCR, are limited by low sensitivity, prolonged turnaround times, or technical complexity [29].

Viral culture, while gold-standard for viability assessment, is labor-intensive and dependent on viral integrity. Tan et al. demonstrated that real-time TaqMan RT-PCR targeting EV71’s conserved VP1 gene achieved 100% specificity and 5 copies/reaction sensitivity, enabling direct detection from clinical specimens [30]. Early primer design improvements increased accuracy from 53% (2002) to 92% (2006) [31, 32], further optimized to 100% with sequence-specific probes [33]. Sample type also influences detection rates, with nasopharyngeal swabs outperforming rectal or cerebrospinal fluid samples [34].

Multiplex PCR and LAMP offer multiplexing capabilities and simplified workflows but are constrained by cross-contamination risks and complex product interpretation [26]. This study introduces a microfluidic chip-based dPCR assay targeting EV71’s VP2 gene, addressing limitations of conventional methods. The dPCR assay achieved 7.406 copies/µL sensitivity 10-100-fold higher than qPCR, and 100% specificity against non-EV71 pathogens (CV-A16, CV-A6, CV-A4, CV-A2, CV-A10, CV-B3, H1N1, H3N2, HCV, SARS-CoV-2). These results align with previous reports highlighting dPCR’s superiority in absolute quantification and reduced inhibition effects [29, 32].

Although dPCR System is widely used for multiplex PCR, enabling simultaneous detection of multiple gene mutations or multiple targets detection in one run, we chose a single target (such as VP1) over multi-targets (VP1-VP4) in EV71 dPCR. There are two reasons as follows. First, due to the limitation of laboratory instruments, our laboratory only has a single-channel dPCR instrument. Second, the VP1 gene of EV71 is a core target for virus typing and evolutionary analysis, and its sequence is highly conserved among different strains. Studies have shown that TaqMan probes designed for the highly conserved region of VP1 can specifically detect C4-type EV71 without cross-reactivity with CV-A16, Echovirus, etc. In contrast, although VP2, VP3, and VP4 genes are involved in viral capsid formation, VP4 is located inside the virus, and its sequence may have low RNA accessibility during extraction due to spatial structure occlusion. The conservativeness of VP2 and VP3 may be lower than that of VP1, especially when the virus mutates, the probe binding efficiency is more likely to decrease. By focusing on the “golden target” of VP1, single-target detection can minimize false-negative results caused by gene mutation or structural occlusion. Therefore, the conserved VP1 sequence and high detection specificity can simplify primer/probe design and data analysis, avoid multi-target fluorescence channel limitations, high reagent costs, and RNA degradation in clinical samples, and the single-target strategy has been clinically verified to be more reliable.

The assay’s 100% concordance with clinical qPCR (kappa = 1.0) underscores its reliability for routine use. In recent years, the incidence of EV71 infection has been decreasing since the EV71 vaccine was widely used, resulting in a small number of EV71 positive samples in the coincidence rate experiment [35, 36]. Moreover, due to the limited sample size, no analysis of correlation between viral load and severity was conducted. A larger cohort study will be carried out in the future and further studies with larger sample sizes are planned to overcome this limitation. Notably, the dPCR’s low coefficient of variation (1.09%) surpasses qPCR’s reproducibility, minimizing inter-assay variability. These attributes position dPCR being promising for early EV71 detection, particularly in resource-limited settings where timely intervention is critical.

The practical feasibility analysis and limitations of this study also need to be discussed. There were no early cases in the sample, prospective studies will be carried out later. The cost of a single dPCR run is approximately $50, which is higher than that of qPCR ($20), but it is suitable for scenarios such as efficacy monitoring. In regions with limited resources, portable dPCR equipment (such as the Droplet Reader) can be used.

Conclusion

This study presents a novel microfluidic chip-based dPCR assay for rapid, sensitive, and specific detection of EV71. With a detection limit of 7.406 copies/µL and 100% clinical concordance, the assay addresses critical gaps in early HFMD diagnosis. By overcoming limitations of traditional PCR methods, this technology having potential for EV71 surveillance, enabling timely public health interventions and reducing disease morbidity. Further validation in multicenter settings and cost-effectiveness analyses are warranted to facilitate its widespread adoption.

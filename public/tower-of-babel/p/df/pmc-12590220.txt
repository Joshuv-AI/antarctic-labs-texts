Authors’ reply to Radiotherapy dosimetry oversights in the RELAX trial: implications for interpreting taVNS efficacy in neuropathic pain
Authors: Shaojian Li, Xuzheng Zuo, Yamei Tang
Published: 2025

We thank Yupeng Di and colleagues for their insightful review and valuable comments on our phase 2 randomised trial (RELAX). We agree that the severity of neuropathic pain is directly proportional to the exposure of neural structures to radiation.1 Pain intensity in our study was assessed by Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) score, a validated tool widely used in clinical settings for pain assessment,2 and complies with the recommendations of Methods, Measurement, and Pain Assessment in Clinical Trials (IMMPACT) Guidelines.3 Pain intensity at baseline was similar between study groups and analysis of covariance (ANCOVA), adjusting for baseline NRS score, was performed for the primary outcome. Thus, nerve-specific irradiation-induced neuropathic pain may not confound the efficacy of transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS). Indeed, both of suggested subgroup analyses—stratification by regimen and inclusion of dosimetric covariates into ANCOVA models—were considered at the beginning of the study. However, incomplete records for some patients regarding radiotherapy details (such as specific dose fractionation schemes) made these difficult. Nevertheless, future subgroup analyses stratified by regimen are warranted to support clinical practice. Although a higher proportion of patients in the taVNS group (vs controls) received conventional radiotherapy, and this may underestimate efficacy of taVNS, the potential confounders do not change our conclusions. Inclusion of dosimetric covariates may better correct between-group outcomes in future studies.

Inflammatory responses, particularly involving cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6, contribute to nerve injury and central sensitisation in neuropathic pain.4 Pregabalin potentially inhibited inflammatory response5 and effectively ameliorated radiotherapy-related neuropathic pain in our previous clinical trial.2 Changes in serum levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 were used to evaluate the potential inflammatory response mechanism of taVNS. Other neuropathic biomarkers, such as neurofilament light chain, could also be detected in plasma or cerebrospinal fluid and reflect neuropathic biological process in response to taVNS. Detection of other neuropathic biomarkers is worth investigation in future clinical trials.

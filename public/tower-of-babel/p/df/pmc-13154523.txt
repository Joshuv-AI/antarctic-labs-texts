Population-specific antiphospholipid antibody titres in a South Indian cohort: defining cut-off values and implications for diagnosis from a cross-sectional validation study
Authors: Harikrishnan Bhaskaran, Mithun CB, Jyothi Visalakshy Srikanth, Sandeep Surendran, Gayathri S, Renjitha Bhaskaran, Sekhar V Easwar, Manu Pradeep
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSignificant titers of antiphospholipid antibodies (aPL) vary across populations, but the clinically relevant threshold for diagnosing antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) in South Indians is unknown. This study aimed to determine population-specific aPL cut-off values in South India.MethodsWe conducted a cross-sectional validity study at a tertiary care hospital in Kerala, India. Blood samples from 125 healthy individuals were analyzed to establish cut-off values for anticardiolipin (aCL) and anti-beta-2-glycoprotein I (anti-β2GPI) antibodies (IgG/IgM) using ELISA. Cut-off values were calculated using both 95th and 99th percentile methods (before and after excluding outliers) and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. Validity was evaluated by comparing 118 patients with APS criteria manifestations to 81 patients without such manifestations.ResultsaPL values showed a non-parametric distribution. The 99th percentile cut-offs before excluding outliers were 17.3 GPL (IgG aCL), 12.4 MPL (IgM aCL), 41.5 SGU (IgG anti-β2GPI), and 23.4 SMU (IgM anti-β2GPI). After excluding outliers, 99th percentile values were 2.6 GPL, 12.4 MPL, 16.7 SGU, and 15.7 SMU, respectively. The 99th percentile cut-offs had superior specificity when outliers were not excluded, while ROC-derived cutoff values yielded a greater Youden Index compared to the other cutoff’s, in all aPLs.ConclusionsThis is the first study to define aPL reference values for South Indians, revealing substantial differences from manufacturer recommendations, Modified Sapporo criteria, and international data. Region-specific aPL thresholds are essential for accurate APS diagnosis. Standardizing cut-off calculation methods is recommended to improve diagnostic accuracy and interpretation across populations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s41927-026-00636-w.MeSH Keywords: Reference values, Antiphospholipid antibodies, Antiphospholipid syndrome, India, Adult

Introduction

Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) is a systemic autoimmune disorder characterized by venous or arterial thrombosis and/or pregnancy morbidity in the presence of persistent antiphospholipid antibodies (aPL). The clinical manifestations of APS are often non-specific, making laboratory testing for aPL essential for diagnosis. The laboratory diagnosis relies on the detection of lupus anticoagulant (LA), anticardiolipin (aCL), and anti-β2-glycoprotein I (aβ2GPI) antibodies. Despite recent advancements in automated detection methods, such as chemiluminescence (CIA) and fluorescence enzyme immunoassays (FEIA), the 2023 ACR/EULAR classification criteria reaffirm ELISA as the reference method for determining aCL and aβ2GPI titers [2, 14].

Determining clinically significant cut-off values for aPL is essential for several reasons. First, establishing accurate aPL positivity thresholds enables physicians to diagnose, predict prognosis, and tailor treatment more effectively. Second, classifying a patient as having APS has major implications, as it typically requires the individual to undergo lifelong anticoagulation therapy. This extended treatment carries notable risks, including an increased likelihood of bleeding and the need for ongoing dietary and lifestyle restrictions, all of which can substantially impact a patient’s quality of life.

A significant challenge in the clinical application of these tests is the lack of universal standardization. International guidelines, including those from the Scientific and Standardization Committee of the International Society of Thrombosis and Haemostasis (ISTH-SSC), recommend that each laboratory determine its own 99th percentile cut-off from a local healthy population [7]. The widely used threshold of 40 GPL/MPL—often referred to as the Modified Sapporo’s Criteria, a “moderate-to-high” titer is largely derived from Western cohorts and may not accurately reflect the background antibody distribution in other ethnic groups [15].

In the South Indian context, there is a critical lack of data regarding population-specific aPL thresholds. Many laboratories, both in India and globally, do not routinely establish in-house cut-off values [24]. Relying on manufacturer-provided reference ranges or arbitrary international cut-offs risks misclassifying patients, leading to either missed diagnoses or unnecessary lifelong anticoagulation therapy. Therefore, this study aimed to determine the 99th percentile cut-off values for aCL and aβ2GPI (IgG and IgM) in a healthy South Indian cohort and validate these thresholds against a clinical cohort of patients with thrombotic and obstetric complications.

Methods

Study design and setting

With approval from the Institutional Review of Board of Amrita Institute of Medical Sciences, Kochi, India, we conducted a cross-sectional validation study at a tertiary care centre in Kochi, India. We recruited consecutive, eligible participants between March 2018 and April 2018, after obtaining written informed consent.

Selection and description of participants

Selection of healthy cohort (for cut-off calculation)

Adults aged 21 to 55 years attending the comprehensive health care OPD for general check-ups without comorbidities, as well as those participating in blood bank and blood donation camps, were selected for the healthy cohort. Selection was based on a bespoke screening questionnaire (Supplementary File S1) and a basic clinical examination to ensure fitness for inclusion. Participants were free from infections and risk factors for thrombotic or obstetric complications, including any history of diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia, or autoimmune diseases, as evaluated by an experienced internal medicine specialist.

Based on the guidelines provided by Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute, the 13th International congress on aPL had given consensus guidelines on aCL and anti-β2GPI testing [11, 13]. Accordingly, to determine the cut-off value of each aPL, the minimum sample size should be 120. We collected samples from 130 healthy participants but five were discarded due to hemolysis. Thus, a total of 125 healthy individuals, who satisfied the inclusion and exclusion criteria, were selected to determine the cutoffs using the 95th and 99th percentile method.

Selection of validation cohort (for cutoff validation)

The electronic medical records of patients aged 18 to 55 years who underwent aPL testing between March 2018 and September 2019 were reviewed. These tests were performed as part of the evaluation for thrombotic events, obstetric complications, autoimmune diseases, primary infertility, or prolonged activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT). Of the 254 patients initially identified, 55 were excluded because they presented only with non-criteria obstetric features or other non-criteria clinical manifestations of APS in the absence of criteria-based clinical symptoms.

Based on data from a previous study, which reported a sensitivity of 22% for predicting thrombotic events, and using a 95% confidence level and a 2% margin of error, the minimum required number of symptomatic cases was calculated to be 66 [24]. Assuming a thrombotic manifestation prevalence of 44% (as reported in the same study), the minimum sample size needed was 150. To minimize selection bias, all 199 patients who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria were included in the study. These patients were reviewed by two senior rheumatologists independently and categorized into the following four groups:

Group A: Patients (with or without autoimmune disease) with thrombotic manifestations (n = 78).

Group A: Patients (with or without autoimmune disease) with thrombotic manifestations (n = 78).

Group B: Patients (with or without autoimmune disease) with criteria-defined obstetric manifestations (n = 45).

Group B: Patients (with or without autoimmune disease) with criteria-defined obstetric manifestations (n = 45).

Group C: Patients without autoimmune disease and without thrombotic or obstetric manifestations (aPL testing in this group was done for evaluation of infertility or prolonged aPTT) (n = 26).

Group C: Patients without autoimmune disease and without thrombotic or obstetric manifestations (aPL testing in this group was done for evaluation of infertility or prolonged aPTT) (n = 26).

Group D: Patients with autoimmune diseases but without any criteria-defined thrombotic or obstetric manifestations. (n = 55)

Group D: Patients with autoimmune diseases but without any criteria-defined thrombotic or obstetric manifestations. (n = 55)

We analysed the validity of calculated cut-off values of aCL and anti-β2GPI (IgG and IgM) in this validation cohort, using a case-control design. Five participants presented with both thrombotic and obstetric manifestations and were treated as single data points in the cases group. Participants with thrombotic / obstetric issues satisfying Modified Sapporo criteria (Group A + Group B, n = 118) were compared with participants without thrombotic/criteria obstetric issues (Group C + D, n = 81) (N = 199).

Technical information

Blood samples were collected from healthy individuals in a comfortable and controlled setting. Sample handling and aPL testing followed the recommendations of the ISTH guidelines [6]. To obtain platelet-poor serum, blood samples were centrifuged at 21 °C at 2000 rpm for 10 min. The serum was then stored at − 20 °C until analysis.

aCL antibodies (IgG and IgM) were measured using the AutoBind ELISA kit manufactured by Tosoh India Pvt. Ltd. (Mumbai, India), while anti-β2GPI testing antibodies (IgG and IgM) were measured using the QUANTA Lite ELISA kit from Inova Diagnostics (Werfen) Inc. (CA, United States). Both assays were performed according to the manufacturers’ protocols. aCL ELISA kit reading was performed using the Bio-Rad Evolis Twin Plus automated system (CA, United States), with results read on a Bio-Rad ELISA reader. Anti-β2GPI assays were performed manually and also read using the Bio-Rad ELISA reader. The aCL assay was calibrated using standardized sera (Harris standards), and results were reported in G phospholipid units (GPL) units for IgG and M phospholipid units (MPL) units for IgM. Since there is no international standard for anti-β2GPI, results were reported in SGU (Standard GPI Units) for IgG and SMU (Standard M Units) for IgM. The manufacturer-recommended cut-off values were as follows:

aCL: 10 GPL for IgG and 7 MPL for IgM.

aCL: 10 GPL for IgG and 7 MPL for IgM.

anti-β2GPI: 20 SGU for IgG and 20 SMU for IgM.

anti-β2GPI: 20 SGU for IgG and 20 SMU for IgM.

Internal quality control was maintained by including both negative and positive control samples in each assay run. To ensure internal consistency and the validity of the cut-off application, all blood samples from both the healthy cohort (n = 125) and the validation cohort (n = 199) were processed and tested using these exact ELISA kits and the same Bio-Rad ELISA reader throughout the study period.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS version 20.0 software, Microsoft Excel, Medcalc and Dx Test. The distribution of each antibody was evaluated by the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Categorical variables were expressed by frequency and percentage. Continuous variables were presented using median and inter quartile range (IQR).

To find the cut-off value of antibodies, the 95th percentile and 99th percentile was computed before and after excluding the outliers. Outliers were detected using the ‘block procedure’ [11, 12]. In this method, the data are sorted in ascending order and the gaps between successive data points are calculated. The position of the largest gap is identified and the readings to the right of this position are considered as outliers (Supplementary Table S1).

Once the 95th and 99th percentile cut-off values were calculated, we evaluated it for the validity measures by applying these cutoffs in the validation cohort. Additional cutoff values were also calculated using the Receiver-Operating-Characteristic curve (ROC) analysis of the validation cohort. Diagnostic accuracy measures such as sensitivity, specificity, odds ratio (OR) and Youden Index (YI) were calculated to evaluate the efficiency of cut-off values to predict thrombotic/ criteria obstetric complications of APS. For this, Group A and Group B were together considered as cases (n = 118) and Group C and Group D were together considered as controls (n = 81). Here, patients with confirmed clinical manifestation and disease course of APS were considered as cases, rather than solely relying on aPL values - to minimise the selection bias. To test the association of different cutoff values of aPL with presence of clinical features, an odds ratio was reported, with a p value derived from a Fischer’s exact test. A p < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

Results

Calculation of cut-off values

A participant flowchart is depicted in Fig. 1. Amongst the 125 included healthy participants, 100 (80%) were females and 25 (25%) were males. The mean age was 38 ± 8 years (range 21 to 55). The median (IQR) levels for the healthy cohort (n = 125) were as follows: IgG aCL was 1.44 (0.9) GPL, IgM aCL was 1.13 (0.77) MPL, IgG anti-β2GPI was 8.2 (0.9) SGU, and IgM anti-β2GPI was 3.5 (2) SMU. No significant differences were observed between genders (p > 0.05); therefore, a single cut-off point was assessed for the entire group (Supplementary Table S2). The aPL values from healthy controls showed a nonparametric distribution.

Validation of cut-off values

The clinical features and median values of participants in the validity group (N = 199) are described in Supplementary Table S3. The five participants who had both thrombotic and obstetric criteria manifestations were analysed as individual cases. The mean age of cases (Group A+ Group B, n = 118) was 37.5 ± 10 years and that of controls (Group C + Group D, n = 81) was 34.7 ± 10.1 years. The median values of aPL in cases and controls are given in Supplementary Table S4. Median values of cases were greater than controls.

Using ROC curve analysis, the cut-off values of each aPL to classify criteria thrombotic/ obstetric issues (cases group) were calculated (Supplementary Figure S1). The area under the curve (AUC), ranged from 56.4 to 64.8 (Supplementary Table S5). The cutoff values from ROC which had maximum YI, with a minimum specificity of 90% were selected (Table 1).

The validity measures of all calculated cut-offs are detailed in Table 2. The 99th percentile cut-off for IgG aCL after excluding outliers did not significantly differentiate cases from controls (p = 0.067), while the corresponding value for IgM aCL approached significance (p = 0.056). In general, as the cut-off threshold was raised from the 95th to the 99th percentile, sensitivity declined and specificity improved for all autoantibodies. The Youden Index was highest for ROC-derived cut-offs except for IgG anti-β2GPI, where the 95th percentile cut-off after excluding outliers had a marginally higher YI, despite reduced specificity.

When using the 95th percentile cut-offs, the highest positivity rates was observed for all antibodies, suggesting maximum sensitivity. Shifting to the 99th percentile cut-offs, especially before excluding outliers, results in a marked reduction in positivity rates across antibodies, reflecting a cut-off that is more specific but less sensitive. Interestingly, when outliers are excluded from the 99th percentile calculation, the positivity rate sometimes increases - in particular for IgG aCL, where the exclusion of outliers lowers the threshold, thus capturing more positives.

For aPL, positivity for IgG aCL dropped from 42.4% at the 95th percentile to 16.9% at the 99th percentile before outlier exclusion, but then rose to 33.9% after outlier exclusion. The positivity rates for IgM aCL cutoffs were more stable, remaining in the moderate range (17.8% − 32.2%). Anti-β2GPI antibodies (IgG and IgM) had generally lower positivity rates compared to aCL, ranging from 14.4% to 29.7%. Cut-offs derived from ROC analysis yielded rates of aPL positivity that were intermediate between the 95th and 99th percentile values for aCL antibodies, while they were slightly higher in anti-β2GPI antibodies. Generally, manufacturers’ cutoffs and Modified Sapporo’s criteria cutoffs had lower positivity rates compared to ROC derived and 95th /99th centile cutoffs.

Discussion

Our study establishes the first population-specific reference intervals for aPL antibodies in South India, demonstrating that locally derived 99th percentile cut-offs are significantly lower than the 40 GPL/MPL threshold suggested by the Modified Sapporo criteria [15]. These discrepancies mirror global observations that manufacturer-provided reference ranges often fail to account for regional demographic and technical variability [3, 6, 13, 14, 16]. As emphasized in the 2023 ACR/EULAR criteria and recent 2025 guidance from the ISTH-SSC, local validation is a clinical necessity to prevent the misclassification of APS in non-Western populations [2, 7, 10]. Establishing accurate thresholds is critical, as an APS diagnosis typically mandates lifelong anticoagulation, which carries significant risks of bleeding and impacts a patient’s quality of life.

A comparative analysis of our findings with international literature using ELISA-based methodologies reinforces the necessity of population-specific thresholds (Table 4). As demonstrated in our results, the 99th percentile for the South Indian cohort is notably lower than the 40 GPL/MPL Sapporo threshold, a trend mirrored in studies by Vanoverschelde et al. [24], Castilla-Agudelo et al. [4], and Montaruli et al. [16], who reported 99th percentile values for European and South American cohorts that also fell significantly below international consensus markers. A notable divergence in our cohort was the IgG anti-β2GPI, whose 99th percentile cutoff was unusually higher than even the Modified Sapporo criteria. However, when outliers were excluded, even this cutoff drastically attenuated to comparable levels as other cohorts, while improving it’s YI - suggesting the large impact of outliers on cutoffs. While all four studies utilized similar solid-phase ELISA techniques, the variation in the exact numerical cut-offs — ranging from 12.4 to 17.3 units in our population to approximately 16 to 22 units for aCL in Italian, Belgian, and Colombian cohorts - highlights the impact of regional background antibody levels. In all four cohorts, calculated cutoffs and manufacturer recommendations showed little to no agreement, with the latter showing poorer diagnostic performance compared to the former. This comparison, summarized in Table 4, underscores that while the 99th percentile is a globally accepted statistical method, the resulting clinical thresholds are not universal and must be calibrated to the local demographic to avoid diagnostic misclassification.

The non-parametric distribution of our data necessitated robust statistical handling [11]. Following recognized guidelines, we utilized block outlier methods to identify and exclude extreme values, which significantly shifted the final cut-off values [12]. This underscores the sensitivity of the 99th percentile to extreme values - a technical challenge that complicates the standardization of aPL assays across different clinical settings [1, 6]. While some earlier studies assumed healthy donors were inherently “negative” [17, 21], our results confirm that rigorous outlier management and the use of the 99th percentile are essential to minimize false-positive thresholds and ensure clinical relevance [14, 24].

A critical finding of this validation is the identification of a “grey zone” (the range between the 95th and 99th percentiles) where patients with clinical manifestations of APS fall below the significant threshold. Although guidelines prioritize high specificity to avoid over-treatment [7, 13], this approach risks under-diagnosing patients, particularly those with obstetric manifestations who may respond to therapy at lower titers [20, 24]. Furthermore, the clinical utility of IgM isotypes remains debated, as they often demonstrate lower specificity for thrombosis compared to IgG [5, 7]. Consequently, a semiquantitative interpretation of titers may better capture the clinical spectrum of APS [23].

While we prioritized specificity through the 99th percentile, ROC curve analysis provided improved Youden indices, suggesting better overall discrimination for clinical diagnosis [8]. The divergence between ROC-derived and statistical thresholds reinforces the need for clinicians to interpret these values within a standardized framework rather than relying on a single fixed method [18, 19, 21]. In cases where traditional criteria markers are borderline, the assessment of non-criteria antibodies, such as anti-phosphatidylserine/prothrombin (aPS/PT), may provide added diagnostic value [22].

Strengths and limitations

A major strength of this study is the demographic alignment between the healthy reference group and the clinical validation cohort, representing the age and sex distribution where APS is most prevalent. By comparing the 99th percentile against ROC-derived values, we provide a robust framework challenging the applicability of universal Western-derived thresholds in the Indian context.

However, several limitations exist. There was technical non-uniformity in our assay platforms; aCL was measured via an automated system, while anti-β2GPI was performed manually. While this reflects pragmatic clinical reality, current guidelines recommend intra-manufacturer consistency to minimize variability [7, 23]. Additionally, while our cohort of 125 meets minimum requirements [11], larger samples are likely required for more stable 99th percentile estimations [9]. Our validation also grouped thrombotic and obstetric manifestations together and did not account for all non-criteria manifestations. Finally, as a single-center study in Kerala, future multi-center studies are necessary to validate these findings across broader ethnic groups in India [10].

Conclusions

This study establishes the first population-specific reference intervals for aPL antibodies in South India, demonstrating that locally derived 99th percentile cut-offs are significantly lower than the threshold suggested by the Modified Sapporo criteria, and diverge from the manufacturer’s recommendations. Our findings emphasize that “universal” thresholds may not be globally applicable, potentially leading to the under-diagnosis of APS in specific ethnic cohorts. While the 99th percentile method remains the most specific approach - particularly when outliers are retained - ROC-derived thresholds offer superior diagnostic balance as measured by the Youden Index.

Ultimately, we advocate for the mandatory calculation of geographic and population-specific titers before clinical labeling. However, as highlighted by the 2025 international updates, this must be accompanied by a global effort to standardize cut-off methodologies and outlier management. Such harmonization is essential to simplify assay interpretation, reduce diagnostic “grey zones,” and ensure equitable care for APS patients across different clinical settings.

Arthroscopic Suspensory Construct for Acute Acromioclavicular Joint Separation
Authors: Dezhou Tang, Yang Chi, Yuchen He, Junliang Liu, Ding Zhou, Qi Tang, Weihong Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAcromioclavicular joint separation is a common shoulder injury that interferes with normal shoulder function. There are various surgical treatment methods for acromioclavicular joint separation; however, no consensus on the gold-standard technique has been established. Therefore, we describe an all-arthroscopic technique using double adjustable loops and triple-button fixation. This method achieves anatomic reconstruction of the coracoclavicular ligament complex to provide a reliable surgical option for the treatment of acromioclavicular joint separation.

Acromioclavicular joint separation (ACJS) accounts for 9% to 12% of shoulder injuries,1,2 predominantly affecting men aged 20 to 30 years (male-to-female ratio, 5:1). Most cases result from sports trauma.2 Clinical assessment and diagnosis of ACJS typically rely on conventional radiography, with classification performed using the well-established Rockwood system (types I-VI).3,4

Nonsurgical management is indicated for Rockwood type I and II injuries, whereas type III to VI injuries require surgery.5 Surgical modalities currently used for ACJS include hook plate fixation, suture anchor fixation, TightRope (Arthrex, Naples, FL)/EndoButton (Smith & Nephew, Andover, MA) suspension fixation, high-strength suture cerclage fixation, and tendon grafting.6, 7, 8, 9 Despite the number of techniques described, no consensus has been established regarding the optimal surgical approach.5,10 We describe an arthroscopic technique using knotless, double adjustable loops and triple-button devices for the treatment of ACJS, aiming to provide a more minimally invasive and stable surgical technique.

Surgical Technique

Patient Evaluation and Indications

The indications for our technique are as follows: acute closed ACJS (≤3 weeks after injury) and imaging confirming the diagnosis as Rockwood type III to V. Contraindications consist of composite injuries, including concomitant acromial fractures, clavicular fractures, coracoid process fractures, severe soft-tissue compromise, or neurovascular injuries.

Preparation Before Surgery

Preoperative diagnosis of ACJS is confirmed with radiographic evidence (Fig 1). The procedure is performed under general anesthesia, and we routinely use the beach-chair position. After surgical site preparation and sterile draping, surface projection markings are applied to the clavicle as described by Rios et al.11 to delineate the anatomic insertion sites of the conoid and trapezoid ligaments (Fig 2).

Anatomic Localization of Bone Tunnels

Prior to arthroscopic examination, two 2.0-mm Kirschner wires (K-wires) are used to predefine the bone tunnel trajectories at the anatomically marked conoid and trapezoid ligament insertion sites under C-arm fluoroscopic guidance. A 2.0-mm K-wire is inserted at the anterior margin of the clavicle, positioned on the upper surface of the coracoid process (Fig 3).

The patient is positioned in the beach-chair position; the left shoulder is shown, as viewed from the posterior aspect. The image illustrates the anatomic landmarks, surgical portals, and positioning of the Kirschner wires. Bone tunnels serve as passages for high-strength sutures during the surgical procedure. (a, coracoid bone tunnel; AI, anteroinferior portal; AL, anterolateral portal; b, conoid ligament bone tunnel; c, trapezoid ligament bone tunnel; CP, coracoid process; P, posterior portal.)

Arthroscopic Exploration

A 0.5-cm posterior portal is created 2 cm inferior and 1.5 cm medial to the posterolateral acromial angle. The arthroscope is inserted into the glenohumeral joint to assess concomitant pathologies (rotator cuff tears, glenoid fractures, and labral lesions). An anteroinferior portal is established between the coracoid process and the anterolateral acromion, and a coracoid-base synovectomy is then performed using a motorized shaver and a radiofrequency ablation device. Subsequently, an anterolateral approach is created approximately 2 cm lateral to the anterolateral acromial angle. This approach allows for the insertion of the arthroscope as an observational portal for subsequent surgery.

Clavicular Bone Tunnel Preparation

Two 1.0-cm incisions are made at the pre-marked K-wire positions on the clavicle. Under arthroscopic visualization, a 4.5-mm-diameter EndoButton drill (Smith & Nephew) is used to create bone tunnels at the trapezoid ligament (lateral) and conoid ligament (medial) insertion sites (Fig 4). During the operation, the surgeon should ensure that the drill passes only through the bicortical layers of the clavicle to avoid damaging the subclavian arteries and veins.

Coracoid Bone Tunnel Preparation

Under arthroscopic visualization, the K-wire for coracoid positioning is adjusted to the central position of the upper surface of the coracoid. Then, the K-wire is drilled through the coracoid in a superior-to-inferior trajectory (Fig 4). A 4.5-mm-diameter EndoButton drill is advanced along the K-wire trajectory to the coracoid surface. The coracoid bone tunnel is created along the established K-wire path. During the establishment of the clavicular and coracoid bone tunnels, a curette is positioned over the tip of the K-wire to prevent iatrogenic injury while drilling (Fig 4).

Passage of Guide Lines Through Bone Tunnel

A polyester suture (MB66G; Ethicon, Somerville, NJ) is threaded through the eyelet of a guide needle. The end of the needle is advanced through the clavicular trapezoid ligament bone tunnel, and the suture loop is retrieved through the anteroinferior approach. An identical operation is performed using an Ultrabraid suture (Smith & Nephew) to pass through the trapezoid ligament bone tunnel. A No. 2-0 polydioxanone (PDS) suture (Ethicon) is inserted into the guide needle eyelet and is passed through the coracoid bone tunnel. The suture loops are retrieved through the anteroinferior portal using a suture grasper (Fig 5).

Introduction of Adjustable Loops Into Clavicular Bone Tunnel

Two Ultrabutton (Smith & Nephew) adjustable loops are preset to 6 cm in length and knotted with high-strength sutures in the trapezoid ligament and conoid ligament bone tunnels, respectively. Two high-strength sutures are pulled to guide the adjustable loops through the anteroinferior portal, with the distal ends of the adjustable loops remaining outside the anteroinferior portal (Fig 6).

(B) The adjustable loop (red lines) is guided into the clavicular bone tunnel, after traction by the high-strength suture (blue lines).

Introduction of Adjustable Loops Into Coracoid Bone Tunnel

The PDS suture tails and adjustable loops are retrieved through the anteroinferior portal using a suture grasper. Each of the two adjustable loops is secured to the PDS suture with individual knots (Fig 7). The PDS suture loops are pulled to guide the adjustable loops through the coracoid bone tunnel; the adjustable loops are then set aside after they are pulled out through the anteroinferior approach (Fig 7). During this crucial step, meticulous attention should be paid to prevent inter-suture entanglement.

(B) The adjustable loops (red lines) are secured to the blue line loops (polydioxanone suture) with 2 knots (purple arrows) prior to being threaded through the coracoid bone tunnel. (C) Pull the adjustable loop suture (red lines) passes through the coracoid bone tunnel and the anteroinferior portal.

Assembly and Insertion of Titanium Dog Bone Button

A Dog Bone Button (Arthrex) is assembled with the distal ends of 2 adjustable loop constructs in vitro. Under arthroscopic visualization, the construct is pulled toward the base of the coracoid and firmly abutted against the osseous surface (Fig 8).

(A) Assembly of Dog Bone Button and adjustable loops (red lines). (B) Under arthroscopic visualization, the adjustable loops (red lines) are pulled to guide the Dog Bone Button to the base of the coracoid. (C) Construct comprising Ultrabuttons (red arrows) and Dog Bone Button (yellow arrow).

Reduction and Final Fixation

Manual reduction involves depressing the clavicle while elevating the elbow. After successful reduction, the adjustable loops are tensioned and secured to maintain anatomic alignment. When tightening the adjustable loops, the surgeon should tighten the lateral adjustable loop first and confirm that the lateral Ultrabutton is attached to the upper surface of the clavicle. Next, the medial adjustable loop is tightened. Real-time C-arm fluoroscopy confirms satisfactory joint reduction prior to final fixation. Arthroscopic re-evaluation ensures optimal positioning of the adjustable loop and Dog Bone Button (Fig 9). Prior to incision closure, the joint cavity is thoroughly drained of irrigation fluid, and the surgical incision is disinfected. The affected limb is immobilized using a shoulder abduction brace.

Postoperative Rehabilitation Protocol

Imaging evaluation is performed on the first postoperative day (Fig 10). Patients are instructed to wear a shoulder abduction brace for 3 weeks postoperatively. Passive range-of-motion exercises are initiated daily beginning on postoperative day 1. At 6 weeks postoperatively, the forearm sling is discontinued, and patients are permitted to perform full passive range-of-motion exercises. Progressive strengthening exercises are conducted at 2 months postoperatively, followed by supervised resistance training at 3 months. Participation in contact sports or high-impact activities is restricted until 6 months postoperatively.

Discussion

The described surgical technique uses double adjustable loops and triple-button devices to achieve anatomic reconstruction of the coracoclavicular ligament, thereby restoring acromioclavicular joint stability. The procedure is performed under arthroscopic guidance using a minimally invasive approach, which significantly reduces postoperative complications. The dynamic fixation mechanism permits early postoperative rehabilitation, and the biomechanically optimized construct shows favorable long-term functional outcomes.

The adjustable-loop technique enables precise modulation of the coracoclavicular distance during acromioclavicular joint reduction. This dynamic fixation mechanism permits controlled clavicular micromotion while maintaining biomechanical stability.12 Compared with rigid dual-plate fixation, double-bundle and 3-button coracoclavicular ligament reconstruction not only accommodates physiological vertical clavicular mobility but also provides superior horizontal stability, better replicating the native ligament’s biomechanical behavior.13 Furthermore, biomechanical load-to-failure studies have shown that adjustable-loop systems withstand forces ranging from 1,086 to 1,365 N, exceeding the ultimate load capacity of the intact coracoclavicular ligament complex (approximately 500-700 N in cadaveric studies).14

Separate fixation at the anatomic footprints of the trapezoid and conoid ligaments effectively mitigates fracture risks associated with focal stress concentration.12,15 On the coracoid side, the Dog Bone Button minimizes direct osseous compression, substantially reducing the incidence of osseous erosion and stress fracture.16

Conventional button-and-suture systems all require hand-tied knots for stabilization. Knot failure has been identified as a critical factor contributing to surgical failure in such systems.17 In contrast, knotless adjustable-loop designs enhance self-locking friction within the loop construct, eliminating the complications associated with conventional knotted fixation.18,19 The application of knotless adjustable-loop systems reduces subcutaneous irritation, thereby enhancing patient comfort and satisfaction. K-wires are percutaneously inserted at the pre-marked surgical sites to localize bone tunnels for the conoid ligament and trapezoid ligament insertions on the clavicle, as well as the anterior clavicular border with the upper surface of the coracoid process. Shoulder arthroscopy is performed to assess the glenohumeral joint and subacromial space for concurrent injuries, and the synovium surrounding the coracoid process is debrided. Bone tunnels for the conoid ligament, trapezoid ligament, and coracoid process are created under curette protection to preserve the adjacent neurovascular structures. A guide needle is used to pass 2 high-strength sutures through the conoid and trapezoid ligament bone tunnels. Subsequently, the guide needle is used to pass 2 polydioxanone (PDS) sutures through the coracoid process bone tunnel. The suture loops are retrieved through the anteroinferior portal. Two adjustable loops are measured to 6 cm and secured to high-strength suture loops. The high-strength sutures are pulled to pass the adjustable loops through the clavicular bone tunnels. The tails of the PDS suture loop and the adjustable loop are grasped through the anteroinferior portal. Each of the two adjustable loops is then secured to the PDS suture. All loops are passed through the coracoid bone tunnel and anteroinferior portal. The Dog Bone Button is assembled with the adjustable loops. It is then pulled to the lower surface of the coracoid process under arthroscopic visualization. The acromioclavicular joint is manually reduced. After confirmation of satisfactory reduction, the lateral adjustable loop is tightened first. Then, after confirmation that the Ultrabutton is attached to the upper surface of the clavicle, the medial adjustable loop is tightened to secure fixation. After C-arm fluoroscopy confirms satisfactory reduction, an arthroscopic evaluation of reduction stability is performed, the tension of the coracoclavicular ligament reconstruction is assessed, and the procedure is finalized.

Unveiling the distinctive features of Feng-flavor Daqu: A comparative study of microbial communities and volatile compounds
Authors: Huan Liu, Jiangwei Wang, Haizhen Fang, Caiyun Hong, Qing Liu, Jie Zhao, Xuefeng Chen, Yanni Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Daqu acts as a multifunctional starter, providing essential microorganisms and enzymes for simultaneous saccharification and fermentation in Chinese Baijiu production. This work aims to reveal the distinct features among Feng-flavor Daqu (FXDQ) and the 3 main flavor types of Baijiu Daqu (Light-, Luzhou-, Sauce-flavor Daqu). FXDQ exhibited lower moisture content, and the higher acidity, esterifying power, liquefaction capacity. Microbially, Bacillus and Actinomyces dominated the bacterial community, whereas Saccharomycopsis was the predominant fungal genus. Across the four Daqu, 130 volatile compounds were detected and 16 of these were identified as key discriminators. Strong correlations were identified among the microbiota, physicochemical properties, enzyme activities, and volatile compounds. Moreover, PICRUSt2 was utilized to predict enzymes associated with the production of some important aroma compounds. These findings enhance our understanding of Daqu, particularly providing deeper insights into the unique characteristics of Feng-flavor Daqu, and offer a scientific basis for optimizing Daqu production processes.

Introduction

Chinese Baijiu is a grain-based distilled spirit typically produced from sorghum using Daqu, Xiaoqu, or Fuqu as fermentation starters, involving sequential processing steps of cooking, saccharification, fermentation, distillation, aging, and blending, and is acknowledged as one of the six primary distilled spirits on a global scale (Liu & Sun, 2018; Xu et al., 2017). Up to now, Chinese Baijiu can be classified into 12 types of distinct aromas, in which Sauce-flavor, Luzhou-flavor and Light-flavor are the 3 main flavor types (Liu & Sun, 2018). Feng-flavor Baijiu is high recognized for its peculiar fermenting technique and tasting characteristic (Cao et al., 2025). It forms a unique style, namely, “clear and transparent, mellow and elegant, sweet and smooth, refreshing and refreshing, and a clean and lasting aftertaste”. Daqu, the key saccharifying and fermenting ingredient in Baijiu manufacture, sometimes referred to as the “backbone of Baijiu” (Liu & Sun, 2018). It harbors a variety of microbes and functional enzymes, fundamentally controlling the aroma components, flavor characteristics, and unique style of liquor (Nie et al., 2024; Xia et al., 2022; Xia, Luo, et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2022). Feng-flavor Daqu production primarily used barley, peas, and wheat as raw materials, employing a medium-high temperature fermentation process (Cao et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2020).

During the production of Baijiu, Daqu starts the solid-state fermentation process by adding many different bacteria, enzymes, and flavor-precursor substances, exerting a critical influence on the quality characteristics of the base liquor (Yang et al., 2024). A lot of work has been performed to uncover the microbes and volatile chemicals in the three main types of Daqu over recent years. Thermoactinomyces and Lactobacillus in bacteria and Saccharopolyspora, Bacillus, Streptomyces, Saccharomycopsis, and Thermoascus in fungi were the dominant microbe influencing the taste and aroma of Light-flavor Daqu (Cai, Wang, et al., 2021). In Sauce-flavor Daqu, Kroppenstedtia, Bacillus, and Thermoascus were the dominant microbial genera across different colors (i.e., black Daqu, red Daqu, and yellow Daqu). Saccharopolyspora and Thermomyces were predominantly present in black and red Daqu, whereas white Daqu demonstrated higher levels of protease activity. Red Daqu demonstrated the greatest esterifying power, while yellow Daqu showed the strongest saccharifying power (Deng et al., 2020). Research on Luzhou-flavor Daqu identified region-specific fungal components. Rhizomucor, Rhizopus, and Thermomyces were positively associated with liquefying power, while Aspergillus was positively correlated with fermenting power. The composition of volatile compounds in Daqu was influenced by Aspergillus, Issatchenkia, Pichia, and Thermoascus. Furthermore, Pichia and Thermoascus exhibited significant negative correlations with benzaldehyde and 2-furaldehyde (Li et al., 2024). He et al. (He et al., 2023) utilized macroproteomic technology to get the enzyme profile, screened and identified strains with high glycating activity of Luzhou-flavor Daqu. In total, 933 types of protein in Daqu were characterized, with 463 categorized as enzymes. These comprised 140 oxidoreductases, 98 transferases, 91 hydrolases, 49 ligases, 41 lyases, and 27 isomerases. Four hydrolases, glucoamylase (EC 3.2.1.3), glucan 1,4-alpha-glucosidase (EC 3.2.1.3), glucanase (EC 3.2.1.11), and β-glucosidase (EC 3.2.1.21), were respectively derived from Byssochlamys spectabilis, Lichtheimia ramosa, and Thermoascus aurantiacus. Kang et al. (Kang, Chen, et al., 2022) performed a thorough examination of three separate types of Daqu, high-temperature (HTDQ), medium-temperature (MTDQ), and low-temperature (LTDQ). Pantoea, Staphylococcus, and Saccharomycopsis predominated in LTDQ; Saccharopolyspora, Staphylococcus, Saccharomycopsis, and Aspergillus were prevalent in MTDQ; and Saccharopolyspora, Bacillus, Byssochlamys, and Saccharomycopsis were the most common species in HTDQ. Among 105 detected volatile compounds, 14 served as distinct markers differentiating the Daqu types. The comparative analysis of the three main Daqu varieties (Sauce-, Luzhou-, and Light-flavor) revealed that the various Daqu types exhibited distinct microbial architectures and volatile component profiling, with 46 differential species and 18 volatile compounds discriminant markers (Deng et al., 2023).

Regarding Feng-flavor Daqu, five microbial species, Bacillus licheniformis, Saccharopolyspora rectivirgula, Thermoascus aurantiacus, Aspergillus amstelodami, and Saccharomycopsis fibuligera were dominated in Huairang Daqu (FHD). Moreover, T. aurantiacus, A. amstelodami, B. licheniformis, and S. rectivirgula were positively correlated with the esterification and liquefying power of FHD. The increasing amount of T. aurantiacus and A. amstelodami led to the higher saccharifying power. While S. fibuligera was positively associated with the fermenting power and the content of acids, esters, and hydrocarbons of FHD. The increasing abundance of B. licheniformis, S. rectivirgula, T. aurantiacus, and S. fibuligera caused an increasing content of alcohols, aromatic compounds, and phenolics in the FHD (Cao et al., 2024). Currently, while the microbial communities and flavor compounds of Sauce-, Luzhou-, and Light-flavor Daqu have been extensively studied, comparative studies on Feng-flavor Daqu and its distinctions from the three fundamental Daqu varieties remain relatively limited.

Therefore, this work investigated the differences among the Feng-flavor Daqu and the Sauce-, Luzhou-, and Light-flavor Daqu for their physicochemical qualities, volatile compounds, and microbial communities. High-throughput sequencing was employed to examine the microbial communities of the Daqu samples, while headspace solid-phase microextraction combined with gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (HS-SPME-GC–MS) was employed to profile the volatile compounds. The metabolic potential of microbial communities was further predicted using PICRUSt2, which infers functional abundance from marker gene sequences. These findings offer helpful details about the unique characteristics of Feng-flavor Daqu and elucidate the distinctions among the four major Daqu varieties.

Materials and methods

Sample collection

This study employed a comparative experimental design to investigate the differences between FXDQ and the 3 main types of Daqu (Light-flavor Daqu, QXDQ; Luzhou-flavor Daqu, NXDQ; Sauce-flavor Daqu, JXDQ) representing distinct flavor styles of Chinese Baijiu. Four types of Daqu representing different flavor styles of Chinese Baijiu were collected from their respective representative production regions. Detailed information for each Daqu type is as follows: FXDQ, was collected from Fengxiang County, Baoji City, Shaanxi Province, China. This is a medium-high-temperature Daqu (production temperature range: 55–60 °C) made from a mixture of barley, wheat, and peas. It is specifically used for producing Feng-flavor Baijiu. JXDQ was collected from Moutai town, Zunyi City, Guizhou Province, China. This is a high-temperature Daqu (production temperature range: 60–70 °C) made primarily from wheat. It is used for producing sauce-flavor Baijiu. QXDQ was collected from Baishui County, Weinan City, Shaanxi Province, China. This is a low-temperature Daqu (production temperature range: 40–50 °C) made from barley, wheat, and peas. It is used for producing light-flavor Baijiu. NXDQ was collected from Luzhou City, Sichuan Province, China. This is a medium-temperature Daqu (production temperature range: 50–60 °C) made from wheat. It is used for producing strong-flavor (Luzhou-flavor) Baijiu.

Five mature Daqu bricks were randomly collected from different positions in the qu room. For each type of Daqu, approximately 1 kg of sample was collected from a single representative batch. The Daqu blocks were aseptically crushed and mixed thoroughly. To ensure representativeness, subsamples were taken from the upper, middle, and bottom layers of the crushed material. These subsamples were then combined to form a composite sample for each Daqu type. The composite sample was ground into a fine powder using a sterile grinder and passed through a 100-mesh sieve. The sieved powder was divided into three aliquots for subsequent analysis: one aliquot was stored at 4 °C for physicochemical and enzymatic properties analyses, and the other two were stored at -80 °C for microbial community and metabolic profiling analysis, respectively.

Analysis of physicochemical and enzymatic properties

The physicochemical properties, including the moisture, acidity, starch content, saccharifying power, liquefying power, fermenting power, and esterifying power, were detected. The moisture, acidity, and starch content of Daqu were determined as described by Deng et al. (Deng et al., 2020). For moisture content analysis, 5 g of each Daqu sample was dried at 105 °C using the dry/wet weight method. Acidity was determined through titration using 0.1 M NaOH until the pH reached the endpoint of 8.2, and the volume of NaOH consumed was recorded. To ascertain starch content, 2 g of Daqu was formulated into a sample solution, subsequently titrated with Fehling's reagent (Yuanye, Shanghai, China). The starch content was calculated from the measured amount of reducing sugars. The saccharifying power, liquefying power, fermenting power, and esterifying power were analyzed following the methods of Guan et al. (Guan et al., 2021). Fermenting power was assessed by measuring the mass of CO2 gas produced during fermentation using a fermentation lock system. Liquefying power was determined by adding a 5% Daqu enzyme solution to approximately 1.5 mL of dilute iodine solution in a white porcelain plate until the blue color disappeared, with the reaction time serving as the indicator. Saccharifying power was quantified by measuring the glucose yield using the Fehling method, where a glucose standard solution was titrated against Fehling's reagent, and the consumed volume was recorded. Esterifying power was evaluated by measuring the total esters synthesized through the esterification of free organic acids with ethanol, catalyzed by Daqu esterases, followed by saponification analysis.

The activities of eight kinds of important hydrolases in Daqu were quantitatively analyzed by α-amylase activity kit, lipase activity kit, tannase activity kit, pectinase activity kit, cellulase activity kit, phytase activity kit, neutral protease activity kit, and saccharifying enzyme activity kit (Solarbio, Beijing, China), following the manufacturer's protocols. The unit of enzyme activity is uniformly expressed as U/g.

Microbial community structure analysis

Total genomic DNA was isolated from samples with the OMEGA Soil DNA Kit (M5635–02) (Omega Bio-Tek, Norcross, GA, USA) as per the manufacturer's protocol and kept at −20 °C until subsequent analysis. The concentration and purity of the extracted DNA were assessed by a NanoDrop NC2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) and also agarose gel electrophoresis. The full-length bacterial 16S rRNA gene was amplified via PCR with the forward primer PB_16s_BacF (5′-AGAGTTTGATCMTGGCTCAG-3′) and the reverse primer PB_16s_BacR (5′-ACCTTGTTACGACTT-3′). For fungal ITS amplification, PCR was performed with forward primer PB_ITSF (5’-CCGTGTTTCAAGACGGG-3′) and reverse primer PB_ITSR (5’-CTTGGTCATTTAGAGGAAGTAA-3′). Following individual quantification, amplicons were pooled in equal amounts and sequenced via Single Molecule Real-Time (SMRT) sequencing on the PacBio Sequel platform at Shanghai Personal Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

Volatile compounds analysis

Gas chromatography-mass spectrometry using a Triple Quadrupole GC–MS system (GCMS-TQ8040 NX, Shimadzu, Kyoto, Japan) was employed to detect the volatile compounds recovered from Daqu by headspace solid-phase microextraction (HS-SPME) with a DVB/CAR/PDMS fiber (50/30 μm, Supelco, Bellefonte, PA, USA) according to the method described by Yan et al. (Yan et al., 2019), with slight modifications. One gram of Daqu sample was mixed with 10 mL of saturated NaCl solution. The mixture was incubated in a water bath at 60 °C for 15 min, followed by solid-phase microextraction (SPME) for 45 min. Consequently, the SPME fiber was placed into the GC inlet and allowed to desorb for 250 °C for 7 min to detect the volatile components. GC conditions: helium served as the carrier gas and the flow rate was 1 mL/min. Chromatographic separation was done on an Inert Cap Wax column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm). The injector temperature was set at 250 °C, and samples were injected in splitless mode. The temperature profile was set as follows: The column temperature was 50 °C for the first 2 min and then increased at 6 °C/min to 250 °C and kept for 5 min. MS setup: 70 eV electron ionization (EI) source, 240 °C ion source, 35–350 m/z mass scanning range, and 3-min solvent delay. The volatile components were characterized by searching the standard spectral library (NIST) database. Peaks with a similarity less than 80% and siloxane-related contaminants were eliminated from further analysis. The peak areas of the metabolites were normalized to the total areas for subsequent data analysis.

Statistical analysis

The variations in physicochemical indicators and enzyme activities of Daqu were displayed by GraphPad Prism 10.0.0 (Boston, MA, USA). All experiments were conducted with at least three replicates. Microbiome and metabolomics analyses were performed using three biological replicates. One-way ANOVA with Dunnett's post hoc test were utilized to assess statistical significance. Significant differences were considered as * 0.01 < p ≤ 0.05, ** 0.001 < p ≤ 0.01, *** 0.0001 < p ≤ 0.001, and **** p ≤ 0.0001, respectively. Linear discriminant analysis (LDA), non-metric multidimensional scaling (NMDS), and LefSe were performed on the Personal Gene Cloud (2025–03.08, https://www.genescloud.cn/home). SIMCA 14.1 (Umetrics AB, Umeå, Västerbotten, Sweden) was utilized to perform partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) for volatile components. The clustering heat map and correlation analysis graph were drawn by Chiplot (2025–04.12, https://www.chiplot.online/). PICRUSt2 (v2.3) (https://github.com/picrust/picrust2/) was adopted to profile the functions of the microbe in Daqu, which is based on the KEGG database (2025-05-10, https://www.genome.jp/kegg).

Results and discussion

Variations in physicochemical properties and enzyme activities

The physicochemical properties across four types of Daqu were shown in Fig. 1A. Moisture is important for evaluating the endpoint of Daqu fermentation, as it takes effect on the microbial succession during this procedure (Liu et al., 2019). To avoid re-fermentation during the Daqu storage process, the moisture content should be maintained below 13.0%. Higher moisture may facilitate the proliferation of Lactobacillus, Penicillium, and other microbes, which can cause overheating and make the storage less stable (Gan et al., 2019). The moisture levels of four Daqu samples were all within the acceptable range. Specifically, JXDQ had the moisture content of 11.2 ± 0.2%, while the other three samples were all below 10%. The change in moisture content of Daqu is mainly due to water evaporation caused by heat generated from vigorous microbial metabolism within the Daqu, with environmental humidity and climate changes affecting the rate of moisture evaporation (Xu et al., 2022). Acidity is a key sign of the quality of Daqu, reflecting the extent of microbial metabolic activity. The acidity fluctuations during the Daqu production process are mainly attributed to the organic acids generated via microbial metabolism (Hu et al., 2023). These four types of Daqu exhibited different levels of acidity, with FXDQ having the highest level (2.43 ± 0.06 mmoL/10 g) and QXDQ having the lowest level (0.83 ± 0.02 mmoL/10 g). Starch functions as a primary energy source to support microbial growth in Daqu (Fan et al., 2018). The starch content was lowest in QXDQ (53.2 ± 7.5 g/100 g). Fermentation capacity, primarily influenced by yeast activity, directly impacts alcohol production in brewing. QXDQ demonstrated the highest fermentation power (0.27 ± 0.05 U), while JXDQ had the least (0.15 ± 0.03 U). Saccharification power indicates the capacity of microorganisms in Daqu to transform starch into reducing sugars (Xia, Zhou, et al., 2023). QXDQ showed the highest saccharification power of 204 ± 24 U, while JXDQ showed a lower value of 168 ± 42 U. However, no significant differences were detected between FXDQ and any other type of Daqu in this parameter. The limited microbial growth at high temperatures during the production of Daqu may explain the decrease in fermentation and saccharification abilities (Xing et al., 2025). Esterification power is the inherent catalytic ability of Daqu to produce esters after fermentation (Fan et al., 2020). FXDQ displayed the highest esterification power (1086.3 ± 61.5 U), suggesting a strong potential for aroma compound formation. Liquefaction power indicates the potential of Daqu to degrade starch into soluble dextrins and oligosaccharides. FXDQ showed the liquefaction power of 0.107 ± 0.006 U, QXDQ, JXDQ, and NXDQ exhibited a liquefaction power of 0.093 ± 0.006 U, 0.077 ± 0.006 U, 0.063 ± 0.006 U, respectively. Temperature is a primary driver of microbial succession and metabolic pathway shifts during Daqu fermentation, which in turn determines the enzymatic profiles and physicochemical characteristics of Daqu. Studies have shown that high-temperature Daqu exhibits significantly higher acidity but remarkably weaker liquefaction, saccharification, fermentation, and esterification powers compared to medium-high-, medium- and low-temperature Daqu (Deng et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2024). The saccharification power, liquefaction power, fermentation power, esterification power and protease activity were correlated with Saccharomycopsis fibuligera, Trichosporon asahii, Kodamaea ohmeri, Stenotrophomonas rhizophila, and other microbial communities (Deng et al., 2023). Species contribution analysis indicated that the major contributors of liquefaction power were Rhizopus, Weissella and Bacillus in medium-temperature Daqu, and Kosakonia and Aspergillus in low-temperature Daqu (Huang et al., 2024). Moreover, differences in raw material composition also contribute to the physicochemical properties of Daqu, attributed to the influence of raw material microbiota in shaping Daqu community structure (Shi et al., 2026). Microbial source tracking analysis of medium-temperature Daqu indicated that raw materials was the main source for the species enriched in mature Daqu, such as Staphylococcus xylosus (65.74%), Weissella cibaria (64.01%), Leuconostoc holzapfelii (85.02%), Lactobacillus pontis (100.00%) and Lactobacillus plantarum (100.00%) (Du et al., 2019).Fig. 1Physiochemical properties (A) and enzyme activities (B) across the four types of Daqu (* 0.01 < p ≤ 0.05, ** 0.001 < p ≤ 0.01, *** 0.0001 < p ≤ 0.001,**** p ≤ 0.0001, ns, not significant).Fig. 1

Enzymes in Daqu contribute greatly to catalyzing the decomposition of macromolecules such as starch and proteins. The resulting products supply essential precursors for microbial metabolism (Hu et al., 2023). Hence, enzyme activity is essential for assessing Daqu quality (Jiang et al., 2023). As shown in Fig. 1B, FXDQ indicated the strongest activities in saccharifying enzyme (211.24 ± 4.48 U/g), cellulase (64.89 ± 2.72 U/g), phytase (2.64 ± 0.42 U/g), neutral protease (1.74 ± 0.13 U/g), and pectinase (67.73 ± 4.51 U/g). JXDQ led in α-amylase (6.92 ± 1.11 U/g) and tannase (1229.35 ± 30.77 U/g) activities, whereas NXDQ exhibited the highest lipase activity (56.04 ± 2.98 U/g).

The difference of enzyme activity among these four types of Daqu can be attributed to their different production parameters and raw material composition. The activities of saccharifying enzyme, cellulase, phytase, neutral protease and pectinase in FXDQ are significantly higher, which may be attributed to its unique production process (55 °C–60 °C) and raw material formula. FXDQ is usually made from a mixture of barley, wheat and pea. These raw materials are rich in polysaccharides and proteins, which may promote the microbial community to produce a wider range of hydrolases. Studies have shown that Saccharomycopsis fibuligera, a dominant fungus in FXDQ, is a prolific producer of amylases, proteases, beta-glucosidase, and pectinases (Chi et al., 2009). In addition, the moderate temperature range may allow the coexistence of mesophilic and thermotolerant enzyme producing microorganisms, thereby improving the overall enzyme diversity and activity (Huang et al., 2024). For JXDQ, it showed good α-amylase activities. This may be related to the high occurrence of thermophilic fungi such as Thermomyces spp. and Aspergillus spp. that generate heat-resistant α-amylases at high temperature (60 °C–70 °C) (Xia et al., 2022). The high tannase activity of JXDQ (1229.35 U/g) was consistent with earlier studies indicating that high-temperature Daqu has a considerable ability to degrade tannin (Huang et al., 2024). The high lipase activity in NXDQ may be due to its production at medium temperature (50 °C–60 °C) and the use of wheat as the main raw material. The microbial community of NXDQ is rich in Bacillus and Thermoascus species, both have been reported to produce lipase (Zou et al., 2018). The formation of ethyl esters, important aroma compounds in Luzhou-flavor Baijiu, is an essential role of lipases. Although QXDQ was not the most active in any particular enzyme class, its microbial population, predominantly Wickerhamomyces and Saccharomycopsis, is known for its ability to produce a wide range of enzymes that can well saccharify and ferment grains at lower temperatures (40 °C–50 °C). The mild enzyme activity of QXDQ were compatible with its purpose to produce clean and crisp Baijiu with reduced off-flavors (Liu et al., 2018).

It is worth noting that the four types of Daqu included in this study each produce Baijiu with characteristic sensory profiles and have all aquired consumer appreciation. The differences pointed out in this study should be seen as expressions of diversity of style and not as signs of hierarchy of quality.

Microbial community structure

The microbial communities in four types of Daqu were detected by PacBio Sequel high-throughput sequencing technology via Single Molecule, Real-Time (SMRT) sequencing. Tables S1 and S2 showed that the full-length 16S rRNA gene and ITS region produced 614,750 high-quality bacterial and 485,997 fungal sequences. As shown in Fig. 2A, the bacterial OTU of FXDQ was 2969, and the fungal OTU was 284. The OTU of JXDQ was 1374 for bacteria and 2186 for fungi. For bacteria, the OTU of QXDQ was 2585, whereas for fungi, it was 1327. NXDQ has an OTU of 35 for fungi and 4707 for bacteria. The α diversity analyses were conducted to evaluate the abundance and uniformity of microbial communities in the four Daqu types and the results were displayed in Fig. S1 and S2. All samples had Good's coverage values over 0.996 for bacteria and 0.995 for fungi, confirming that the sequencing depth was sufficient to robustly capture the microbial diversity. The Chao1 index, a common species richness indicator, indicated that NXDQ harbored the highest number of bacterial species, while JXDQ showed the lowest richness. For the fungal communities, JXDQ displayed the highest richness, while QXDQ harbored the lowest number of fungal species. The Shannon and Simpson indices, which combine both richness and evenness, reveal not only the number of species but also that the distribution of individuals among those species varies considerably. According to the Simpson and Shannon indices, JXDQ had the most diverse bacterial communities, while QXDQ exhibited the lowest Simpson and Shannon diversity. This indicates a more uniform distribution of different bacterial taxa in JXDQ compared to others. Similarly, JXDQ exhibited the highest Shannon diversity, followed by QXDQ, FXDQ, and NXDQ.

The varied patterns of bacterial and fungal richness among the four types of Daqu may be driven by differences in their production temperatures and raw material compositions, which act as selective pressures shaping the assembly of microbial communities. The result indicated the highest bacterial richness in NXDQ and lowest in JXDQ. The medium-temperature production process (50 °C–60 °C) of NXDQ explains its highest bacterial richness. This temperature range is a moderate selective environment which is high enough to eliminate some heat sensitive bacteria but not so high as to exclude a wide diversity of thermotolerant and thermophilic bacterial species. Previous studies have indicated that medium-high-temperature Daqu provides a good habitat for diverse bacterial genera such as Bacillus, Lactobacillus and diverse Actinobacteria that coexist in this thermal regime (Xiao et al., 2017). On the contrary, the lowest bacterial richness was found in JXDQ which may be caused by its high-temperature process (60 °C–70 °C). The extended period of high temperature during the fermentation of high-temperature Daqu serves as a powerful environmental filter, only selecting for the most thermophilic and heat-resistant bacteria (mainly Bacillus and a few thermophilic Actinobacteria) and eliminating most of the mesophilic and moderately thermotolerant bacteria. This “thermal sterilization” effect has been well reported in literature that the high-temperature Daqu always showed lower bacterial diversity than the medium- and low-temperature Daqu (Huang et al., 2024; Yuan et al., 2025).

The highest fungal richness was observed in JXDQ, which may be due to the ecological strategy of fungi under high-temperature environments. High temperature reduces bacterial diversity, but some fungal phyla (particularly Ascomycota and Mucoromycota) can grow under high temperature conditions due to the evolution of good thermophilic or heat-resistant properties. The high-temperature regime of JXDQ creates a reduced bacterial competition environment, allowing multiple thermophilic and thermotolerant fungal species to co-exist and occupy various ecological niches (Yang et al., 2024). The fungal diversity was lowest in QXDQ. This was mainly caused by its lower temperature of Daqu production process (40 °C–50 °C), which may favor the rapid dominance of a few highly competitive fungal species (such as Saccharomycopsis fibuligera and Wickerhamomyces anomalus) that are exceptionally well-adapted to these conditions. These fast-growing fungi may outcompete other slower-growing fungal species, leading to reduced overall fungal richness. This phenomenon, known as competitive exclusion, has been observed in other low-temperature fermentation starters (Zheng et al., 2015). These findings are consistent with the intermediate disturbance hypothesis and niche differentiation theory in microbial ecology, where moderate selective pressure (NXDQ temperature) maximizes diversity, while extreme pressure (JXDQ temperature) filters communities in taxon-specific ways (Wu et al., 2023).

A non-metric multidimensional scaling analysis by the Bray-Curtis dissimilarity matrix was employed to assess differences in microbial community composition at the genera level across the four types of Daqu. Significant differences were observed in both fungal community structure (stress = 0.00009) and bacterial community structure (stress = 0.0557). Bar plots of microbial community structure was constructed to illustrate the variation in relative abundance of dominant microbial genera (relative abundance >1%) (Fig. 2B). A total of 64 fungal genera were identified across these four types of Daqu, and 20 fungal genera were found to be highly abundant. The fungal genera exhibiting relatively high abundance (average relative abundance >3%) mainly included Thermoascus (average 34.55%, distribution range 0.15% ∼ 99.92%) and Saccharomycopsis (average 33.56%, distribution range 0.01% ∼ 97.46%), Wickerhamomyces (mean 12.13%, distribution range 0% ∼ 52.67%), Thermomyces (mean 9.28%, distribution range 0% ∼ 69.86%), Aspergillus (mean 6.35%, distribution range 0.01% ∼ 28.04%). Wickerhamomyces had the highest abundance (48.38%) in QXDQ, followed by Saccharomycopsis (37.25%). This is consistent with the previous report that Saccharomycopsis fibuligera, Wickerhamomyces anomalus, and Pichia kudriavzevii were the most common yeasts encountered in QXDQ (Zheng et al., 2012). Saccharomycopsis has the highest abundance (96.95%) in FXDQ. Thermomyces was most abundant in JXDQ (37.11%), followed by Thermoascus (35.07%). Thermomyces and Thermoascus were the dominant fungal genera in JXDQ, consistent with Cai et al. (Cai, Xue, et al., 2021). Thermoascus has the highest abundance (99.9%) in NXDQ, indicating that Thermoascus is the dominant fungal genus in NXDQ, which is in agreement with the report of Ma et al. (Ma et al., 2022).

Temperature is the key driving force affecting the microbial community composition of Daqu (Wu et al., 2023). Deng et al. systematically characterized the microbial community structure of four types of Daqu (low-temperature DW, medium-temperature ZW, medium-high-temperature ZGW, and high-temperature GW). It demonstrated that temperature is the key driving force shaping the fungal community structure. In low-temperature Daqu, yeasts overwhelmingly dominated, with Saccharomycopsis fibuligera (relative abundance ∼44.5%), Issatchenkia orientalis (∼28.7%), and Wickerhamomyces anomalus (∼14.5%) as the predominant species. In contrast, high-temperature Daqu was characterized by thermophilic fungi, including Thermoascus aurantiacus (∼24%), Thermomyces spp. (∼12%), Trichomonascus ciferrii (∼13.6%), and Aspergillus amstelodami (∼10%). Medium-high-temperature Daqu contained intermediate thermophiles such as Thermoascus aurantiacus, Dipodascus geotrichum, and Wallemia mellicola (Deng et al., 2023). Thermoascus and Thermomyces were ordinary thermophilic fungi in high-temperature Daqu and medium-temperature Daqu (Chen et al., 2017; Shi et al., 2022). Through the simulated fermentation of Muqu from sauce-flavor Daqu across a temperature gradient (17 °C, 30 °C, 40 °C, 50 °C and 60 °C), it demonstrated that temperature significantly affected the assembly of the microbial communities. Specifically, Saccharomycopsis, Saccharomyces, and Monascus were predominant fungal genura in 17–40 °C, while high temperatures (50–60 °C) selected for thermotolerant genera such as Thermoascus, Rasamsonia, Candida, and Aspergillus (Wu et al., 2023). Temperature-driven selection based on species' thermal tolerance traits occupied a crucial role in shaping the microbial structure and function (García et al., 2018).

A total of 268 bacterial genera were detected. The genera with high abundance (mean relative abundance >3%) mainly included Bacillus (mean 21.92%, distribution range 0.14% ∼ 71.45%), Leuconostoc (mean 21.66%, distribution range 0.09% ∼ 59.52%), Actinomyces (mean 6.68%, range 0.09% ∼ 24.21%), Pseudomonas (mean 4.21%, distribution range 0.01% ∼ 39.89%), Latilactobacillus (mean 3.11%, distribution range 0% ∼ 10.33%), Levilactobacillus (mean 3.11%, distribution range 0% ∼ 8.48%), and Weissella (mean 3.04%, distribution range 0.01% ∼ 9.78%). The relative abundance of Leuconostoc in QXDQ was 58.24%, with Leuconostoc citreum exhibiting the greatest relative abundance. While for Light-flavor Daqu from Xiangyang, Hubei Province, China, Bacillus, Streptomyces, Saccharopolyspora, Lactobacillus, and Thermoactinomyces were the most prevalent bacterial taxa (Cai, et al., 2021a). Combined with the results of this study, these findings indicate the different microbial community structures in Light-flavor Daqu in different regions. In FXDQ, JXDQ, and NXDQ, Bacillus was most abundant (28.13%, 28.43%, and 30.9%, respectively). This might be attributed to the heat resistance and spore-forming characteristics of Bacillus, which can survive as the dominant genera of bacteria in the high-temperature conditions of production of these three types of Daqu (Shi et al., 2022; Zhu et al., 2023). In addition, Bacillus spp. produce various enzymes (amylases, proteases, and lipases) and utilize macromolecules such as starch and protein in the Daqu raw materials better than other genera, thus better growth (Zhang et al., 2023). Bacillus communities are also known to be important contributors to the flavor compounds of Daqu, such as pyrazines (baked aroma), acetoin (creamy aroma) and guaiacol (sauce aroma) (Zhu et al., 2025).

In contrast, the relative abundance of Actinomyces in FXDQ (22.48%) was significantly higher than that of the other three types of Daqu. Actinomycetes have been reported to be the dominant microbial community in both Daqu and pit mud and produce key enzymes (amylase, protease, cellulase) and secondary metabolites, which collectively contribute to the flavor of Baijiu (Chen et al., 2022). The abundance of Actinomyces in FXDQ may be caused by a multiple factors, including suitable temperature and humidity conditions during Daqu preparation, competitive ecological selection, and the unique raw material characteristics. Wang et al. (Wang et al., 2021) revealed that the dominant bacterial genera were Thermoactinomyces, Lentibacillus, Staphylococcus, Bacillus, Kroppenstedtia, etc. in three distinct types of high-temperature Daqu. The dominant genera are different from the results of this study, which may be caused by regional differences. Ma et al. (Ma et al., 2022) discovered that the most important influences on the properties of Luzhou-flavor Daqu may be Saccharopolyspora, Pantoea, and Pediococcus. In our study, there was a greater diversity of bacterial species. This could be because bacteria are more able to adapt to the Daqu fermentation environment than fungi (Jin et al., 2019). Moreover, the production environment, including geographical location and local microbiota pools, significantly influences microbial assembly. It was demonstrated that 94.7% of fungi and 60.95% of bacteria in Feng-flavor Daqu originate from raw materials and indoor ground, respectively (Zhang et al., 2023). FXDQ's microbial profile is shaped by the combined and interactive effects of its specific material fomula, medium-high temperature regime, and unique production environment.

It should be noted that one sample of each type of Daqu was selected in this study. Although these samples were obtained from their representative production areas to represent flavor types properly, we are aware that intra-type differences due to different geographical locations or specific production parameters were not completely covered. Therefore, the conclusions presented here are mainly the reflection of the differences among these typical samples. Future studies should include multiple batches and samples from various geographical origins to validate the general applicability of these results, and to evaluate the specific effects of manufacturing temperature and regional microbiota on Daqu characteristics.

Furthermore, to figure out the potential microbial biomarkers in each of the four Daqu, LefSe analysis with a Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) score threshold of >4.0 for significance was conducted. As shown in Fig. 3A, a total of 33 bacterial taxa were identified as discriminative across the samples. Among them, QXDQ, FXDQ, JXDQ, and NXDQ contained 7, 5, 7, and 14 biomarker taxa, respectively. Specifically, FXDQ was characterized by two genera, Actinomyces and Ruminococcus; JXDQ was marked by genera of Pseudomonas, Caldibacillus, and Burkholderia; NXDQ featured genera of Staphylococcus, Acinetobacter, and Paenibacillus; and QXDQ was notably enriched in genera of Leuconostoc, Weissella, Latilactobacillus, Levilactobacillus, and Lactiplantibacillus.Fig. 3Differential bacterial taxa (A) and differential fungal taxa (B) across the four types of Daqu.Fig. 3

For fungi, 30 representative taxa were identified as biomarkers among the four Daqu types (LDA > 4) (Fig. 3B). QXDQ, FXDQ, NXDQ, and JXDQ contained 12, 5, 7, and 6 biomarker taxa, respectively. Particularly, the genus of Saccharomycopsis was significantly enriched in FXDQ; the genera of Aspergillus was characteristic in JXDQ; the genus of Thermoascus was identified as a potential biomarker in NXDQ; and the genera of Wickerhamomyces, Kurtzmaniella, and Trichosporon were iconic in QXDQ (Fig. 3B). The presence of these indicator taxa suggests type-specific fungal community structures across these four types of Daqu.

Differences in volatile compounds

The volatile compounds in the four varieties of Daqu were detected using HS-SPME/GC–MS. The total ion chromatograms were presented in Fig. S3. In total, 130 volatile compounds were identified, encompassing 28 alcohols, 18 esters, 21 aldehydes, 12 ketones, 15 aromatic compounds, 15 acids, 11 pyrazines, 3 furans, 1 sulfide, and 6 classified as others (Fig. 4A). The relative contents of volatile compounds in different Daqu were shown in Table S4. Alcohols are the most common volatile ingredients in Daqu. Alcohol compounds are mainly produced through aerobic conversion of carbohydrates and anaerobic metabolism of amino acids. Furthermore, yeast acts as a significant driver in the production of higher alcohols through the reduction of corresponding aldehydes and the transformation of amino acids that are already in the raw materials (Ledauphin et al., 2003). These procedures are essential for increasing the amount and diversity of alcohols and play a key role in the development of complex flavor profiles. Phenylethyl alcohol was the alcohol detected with the highest amount. It is recognized to improve the aromatic complexity of liquors like Luzhou-, Light-, and Feng-flavor by adding a refined floral and fruity note. It has a unique rose-like and sweet smell (Han et al., 2017).Fig. 4Difference analysis of volatile compounds in four kinds of Daqu. Additionally, partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) was conducted using volatile compounds data, and the resulting PLS-DA score plot is presented in Fig. 4B. The model parameters (R2X = 0.943, R2Y = 0.974, Q2 = 0.931) indicated a robust and reliable model (Kang, Sun, et al., 2022). The PLS-DA score map showed that the four types of Daqu were clearly separated. Through the permutation test (n = 200), it can be seen that both R2 and Q2 increase along with the degree of displacement, indicating that the model was stable and repeatable (Fig. 4C).

We additionally screened the significantly differential volatile compounds with p < 0.05 for cluster analysis using a stratified heat map (Fig. S4). A total of 97 compounds were identified. To pinpoint metabolites with both statistical and predictive significance, compounds with VIP > 1 and p < 0.05 were subjected to additional clustering analysis (Fig. 4D). A total of 16 metabolites with significant differences were screened out for further study, including 4 alcohols (3-methyl-1-butanol, 1-amyl alcohol, 3-octanol and phenylethyl alcohol), 3 aldehydes (3-methyl butanal, benzaldehyde, and phenylacetaldehyde), 3 acids (butanoic acid, 2-methyl-propanoic acid and 3-methyl butanoic acid), 2 esters (ethyl hexanoate and hexadecanoic acid, ethyl ester), 2 pyrazines (2,3,5-trimethylpyrazines and tetramethylpyrazines), and 2 ketones (acetophenone and dihydro-5-pentyl-2(3H)-Furanone). The result indicated that it differentiated the four Daqu types based on these 16 volatile metabolites.

As illustrated in Fig. 4D, JXDQ had higher levels of benzaldehyde, 2-methyl-propanoic acid, 3-methl butanoic acid, 3-methyl butanal, and other substances compared to the other three types of Daqu. Due to the high cultivation temperature of Sauce-flavored Daqu, which can reach 70 °C, the Maillard reaction occurs, leading to a distinct color gradient-brown in the interior and yellow on the surface. Under such elevated culture conditions, only a limited number of heat-resistant microorganisms can survive and become dominant, and these are closely associated with the characteristic flavor profile of high-temperature Daqu (Zheng et al., 2011). Compared with the other three kinds of Daqu, QXDQ has a high content of acetophenone, an aromatic compound imparting cherry and almond-like scents that enhance fruity and floral aromas in light-flavor Daqu. NXDQ was rich in phenylethyl alcohol, tetramethylpyrazine, and 2,3,5-trimethylpyrazine. Pyrazines provide roasted and nutty scents that may persist in the finished Baijiu product (Wang et al., 2022a). Zhang et al. (Zhang et al., 2011) identified 75 volatile compounds in Luzhou-flavor Daqu, with 2,5-dimethylpyrazine, 2,6-dimethylpyrazine, 2,3,5-trimethylpyrazine, and tetramethylpyrazine being the most abundant, followed by alcohols. FXDQ has a high concentration of 3-methyl-1-butanol and 1-amyl alcohol. These compounds have been identified as significant aroma-active constituents in various types of Daqu (Mu et al., 2023; Tang et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2022b).

Prediction of the functional composition of the Daqu microbial community

Daqu supplies a wide range of enzymes that facilitate the breakdown of macromolecules and the formation of flavor compounds, thereby exerting a significant influence on Baijiu production. By referring to previous studies, we can outline the pathway of glucose metabolism (Chen et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2023). This can improve the understanding of the role of key enzymes involved in the decomposition of carbohydrates and production of important flavor compounds. The PICRUSt2 analysis predicted the potential functional profiles of the microbial communities. While these predictions offer insights into possible metabolic pathways enriched in different Daqu types, it is important to interpret these results with caution, as gene abundance does not always correlate directly with actual metabolic activity or metabolite yield. Therefore, these findings should be considered preliminary and require further validation using metatranscriptomic or targeted metabolomic approaches. Here, we focused on the pathways and enzymes involved in the production of major flavor compounds and starch metabolism in four types of Daqu (Fig. 8). The detailed enzyme annotation information was presented in Table S3. The differential abundance of the corresponding enzyme-coding functional genes in the microbial communities across four types of Daqu was shown in Fig. S5. Functional predictions by PICRUSt 2 demonstrated that bacterial communities possessed a higher abundance of genes encoding relevant enzymes compared to fungal communities. Furthermore, the enzyme profiles across different Daqu exhibited a high degree of consistency. In the glycolytic pathway, fructose-6-phosphate is converted to fructose-1,6-bisphosphate through the catalytic activity of EC 2.7.1.90. The enzyme EC 1.1.1.1 plays a key role in the conversion of acetaldehyde to ethanol in the glycolysis pathway, which directly affects the alcohol content and the basic properties of Baijiu. It was most abundunt in fungi. Esters are mainly produced from ethanol or fatty acid metabolism by the function of EC 3.1.1.11. In phenylalanine metabolism, L-phenylalanine is converted into phenylethylamine by EC 4.1.1.28 and then into phenylacetaldehyde by EC 1.4.3.4 and EC 1.4.3.21. Butyric acid is produced from butanoyl-CoA by the catalysis of EC 6.2.1.2. L-glutamic acid, derived from glutamic acid metabolism, can mitigate the adverse effects of alcohol and enhance the flavor and roundness of Baijiu. The elevated expression of EC 1.4.3.21 led to a reduction in phenylethylamine content in the sample, thereby facilitating an increase in phenylacetaldehyde content. The high level of EC 2.7.1.90 lowers the amount of fructose-6-phosphate in the sample and can raise the amount of fructose-1,6-bisphosphate. Both EC 1.4.3.21 and EC 2.7.1.90 were exclusively expressed in bacterial communities. Conclusion

In summary, this study systematically characterized and compared the physicochemical properties, enzyme activities, microbial communities, and volatile metabolites of four Daqu types representing different flavor styles of Chinese Baijiu: light-flavor (QXDQ), strong-flavor (NXDQ), sauce-flavor (JXDQ), and Feng-flavor (FXDQ). Each Daqu type exhibited distinct microbial and metabolic features that reflect its adaptation to specific production parameters and contribute to the characteristic aroma of its corresponding Baijiu. FXDQ demonstrated high activities of multiple hydrolytic enzymes, including saccharifying enzyme, cellulase, and pectinase, along with a distinctive microbial consortium dominated by Saccharomycopsis and Actinomyces. JXDQ was distinguished by its high α-amylase and tannase activities and unique fungal richness. NXDQ exhibited the highest lipase activity and bacterial diversity, while QXDQ harbored a distinct bacterial community dominated by Leuconostoc. These findings enhance our understanding of Daqu diversity, particularly providing new insights into the microbial and metabolic features of Feng-flavor Daqu. The results offer a scientific foundation for optimizing Daqu production processes tailored to specific flavor outcomes and highlight the value of preserving and studying the full spectrum of traditional fermentation starters.

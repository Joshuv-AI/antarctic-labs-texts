MiR-100-5p is involved in severe pneumonia in children by targeting MTOR
Authors: Lun Zhang, Qing Wang, Shoushan Mo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSevere pneumonia in children is a life-threatening respiratory condition that can lead to severe complications and even mortality. We confirmed the possibility of miR-100-5p as a biomarker in severe pneumonia in children.MethodsA total of 200 children were included. The expression of miR-100-5p from serum was detected. WI-38 cells were treated with 10 µg/mL LPS for 24 h. miR-100-5p was overexpressed or inhibited by transfection. In addition, proliferation and inflammatory factors were measured. TargetScan was utilized to forecast the downstream of miR-100-5p. The targeting relationship between miR-100-5p and MTOR was confirmed by the dual-luciferase reporter system. miR-100-5p and MTOR were co-overexpressed to detect the function of both in severe pneumonia in children.ResultsmiR-100-5p expression was decreased and had a high diagnostic value in severe pneumonia in children. The miR-100-5p level was closely related to clinical indicators. In WI-38 cells treated with LPS, overexpression of miR-100-5p increased proliferation and reduced inflammatory factors, while inhibition of miR-100-5p reduced proliferation and increased inflammatory factors. MTOR expression was negatively associated with miR-100-5p expression in severe pneumonia in children.ConclusionsmiR-100-5p may participate in severe pneumonia in children by negatively regulating MTOR, proving that it might be a biomarker of severe pneumonia in children.Keywords: miR-100-5p, Severe pneumonia, Children, MTOR, Biomarker

Background

Pneumonia is a common disease. In some patients, it becomes severe and develops into severe pneumonia [1]. Patients with severe pneumonia can have abnormal consciousness and septic shock when symptoms are severe, and can progress to sepsis and multi-organ failure [2]. If severe pneumonia in children is not treated in time, it can lead to serious respiratory system damage, affect growth and development, and even endanger life [3]. Pneumonia accounts for one in five child deaths worldwide, and epidemiological data suggest that about 2 million children die each year [4]. Therefore, finding a biomarker is of great significance for severe pneumonia in children.

MicroRNA (miRNA) is a promising biomarker in the medical field. MiRNA is related to the occurrence and severity of pneumonia [5]. Among them, miR-100-5p was downregulated in pneumonia caused by SARS-CoV-2 [6]. In inflammation-related diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, and coronary atherosclerosis, miR-100-5p is closely related to inflammatory factors [7–9]. In addition, during the process of skeletal muscle development, miR-100-5p significantly promoted cell proliferation [10]. In different types of cancer, miR-100-5p also has the effect of promoting cell proliferation [11, 12]. Based on this evidence, we speculate that miR-100-5p might participate in severe pneumonia in children.

Mechanistic target of rapamycin (MTOR) is a regulator of cellular metabolism [13]. In the pneumonia caused by the coronavirus that swept the world in 2019, MTOR was dysregulated and involved in excessive inflammatory responses [14]. In bacterial pneumonia, mTOR is also involved [15]. In the mouse model of cerebral infarction, MTOR, as a downstream target of miR-100-5p, was inhibited by miR-100-5p [16]. In erectile dysfunction caused by diabetes, miR-100-5p also achieves anti-inflammatory effects by regulating MTOR [17]. Therefore, in severe pneumonia in children, MTOR might be a downstream target of miR-100-5p.

This study aims to explore the diagnostic value of miR-100-5p and its targeting relationship with MTOR, providing the possibility for it to become a new biomarker for severe pneumonia in children.

Methods

Patients and specimens

From March 2021 to January 2023, 200 subjects from The People’s Hospital of Wuzhou were included in this study. A total of 106 children with severe community-acquired pneumonia were classified as the severe pneumonia group. During the same period, 94 children who underwent routine health check-ups and were not diagnosed with any respiratory diseases were selected as the control group. The inclusion criteria for severe pneumonia in children were compliance with the diagnostic guidelines for severe pneumonia of the Pediatric Infectious Diseases Society/Infectious Diseases Society of America [18]. The exclusion criteria were that the child had asthma, bronchitis, or tuberculosis. Venous blood was drawn on an empty stomach the next morning after the child was admitted to the hospital. After the child was discharged from the hospital, we conducted a 3-month follow-up to assess the post-discharge conditions. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The People’s Hospital of Wuzhou, and informed consent forms were obtained from the guardians of all children patients.

Cell culture

Human fibroblast cell line WI-38 (Zeye Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China) was cultured in DMEM (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) and supplemented with 10% FBS and 5% CO2 at 37 °C. Then, WI-38 cells were treated with LPS of diverse concentrations (0, 5, 10, 15 µg/mL) for 24 h. Then, WI-38 cells treated with 10 µg/mL LPS were selected for all subsequent experiments.

Cell transfection

First, miR-100-5p mimic or miR-100-5p inhibitor was transfected into WI-38 cells. Then, miR-100-5p mimic and the pcDNA3.1-MTOR (OE-MTOR) or the pcDNA3.1 vector (OE-NC) were co-transfected into WI-38 cells. All plasmids were purchased from GenePharma (Shanghai, China).

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

To create dual-luciferase reporter constructs, the putative miR-100-5p binding site in the MTOR 3’ UTR was amplified and cloned into the pmirGLO vector (Promega, USA), generating both wild-type (MTOR Wt) and mutant (MTOR Mut) versions. WI-38 cells were co-transfected with either the miR-100-5p mimic or inhibitor alongside the MTOR Wt or MTOR Mut reporter plasmid, respectively. After 48 h, the cells were harvested, lysed, and centrifuged. The resultant supernatant was assayed for luciferase activity.

RT-qPCR

RNA from serum was extracted and reverse-transcribed. cDNA was detected by SYBR qPCR Mix and RT-qPCR system. MiR-100-5p expression was normalized to U6, and MTOR expression was normalized to GAPDH. Both of them were calculated by 2−ΔΔCT. The primer sequences are as follows (from 5’-3’): miR-100-5p-forward: TCGGCAGGAACCCGUAGAUCC, reverse: CTCAACTGGTGTCGTGGA; U6-forward: CTCGCTTCGGCAGCACA, reverse: AACGCTTCACGAATTTGCGT; MTOR-forward: GGCCAATGACCCACACATCTC, reverse: CATGATGCGATGCTCGATGT; and GAPDH-forward: GACATGCCGCCTGGAGAAAC, reverse: AGCCCAGGATGCCCTTTAGT. The reaction conditions for qPCR were as follows: 95 °C for 2 min, 35 cycles of 95 °C for 30 s, 60 °C for 30 s, and 72 °C for 60 s.

Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay

WI-38 cells were resuspended at a density of 2 × 10³ cells/mL. A 100 µL cell suspension was seeded into each well of a 96-well plate and cultured for 24 h. Subsequently, 10 µL CCK-8 solution was added to each well, followed by incubation for 1 h. The absorbance at 450 nm was then measured using a microplate reader.

ELISA

The ELISA kit (Shenzhen Xinbosheng Biological Co., Ltd., China) was utilized to quantify the concentrations of serum TNF-α and IL-1β. All operations are strictly carried out in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. In short, the samples and standards are added to the pre-blocked plate, and then incubation, washing, addition of biotinylated antibody, addition of streptavidin-HRP, addition of substrate, etc., are carried out in sequence. Finally, the absorbance is measured at a wavelength of 450 nm.

Statistical analysis

Data was processed employing SPSS 23.0. For comparisons between the two groups, the t-test is used for analysis. For comparisons among three or more groups, ANOVA is used for analysis. Post-hoc tests are conducted using the Tukey test. The diagnostic value was assessed by the ROC curve, and the optimal cutoff value, sensitivity, and specificity were determined by maximizing the Youden index method. The downstream target of miR-100-5p was searched through bioinformatic analysis utilizing TargetScan [19], which identified MTOR and the binding sites. Pearson correlation analysis was utilized to confirm the correlation. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Diagnostic value of miR-100-5p in severe pneumonia in children

MiR-100-5p expression decreased in severe pneumonia in children (Fig. 1A). MiR-100-5p had a high diagnostic value in severe pneumonia in children (AUC = 0.925, 95%CI: 0.8906–0.9598, Cut off = 0.735, Sensitivity = 76.42%, Specificity = 95.74%) (Fig. 1B). In this study, the survival rate of the severely ill pneumonia patients was 100%, meaning no child died. Among the surviving children, 13 cases were found to have sequelae, including 9 cases of bronchial dilation, 2 cases of pulmonary fibrosis, and 2 cases of atelectasis. According to whether the children developed sequelae, they were divided into the sequelae group and the no-sequelae group. The results showed that the serum miR-100-5p level of children with sequelae was significantly lower than that of children without sequelae (Fig. 1C).

Effects of miR-100-5p in WI-38 cells treated with LPS

When WI-38 cells were treated with LPS, cell viability decreased with an increase in LPS concentration (Fig. 2A), while TNF-α and IL-1β were elevated (Fig. 2B, C). Then, 10 µg/mL LPS was selected for all subsequent experiments. In WI-38 cells treated with LPS, miR-100-5p was overexpressed or inhibited (Fig. 3A). Overexpression of miR-100-5p increased cell viability, while inhibition of miR-100-5p reduced cell viability (Fig. 3B). In addition, overexpression of miR-100-5p reduced TNF-α and IL-1β, while inhibition of miR-100-5p elevated TNF-α and IL-1β (Fig. 3C, D).

MiR-100-5p targeted MTOR in WI-38 cells treated with LPS

The downstream MTOR of miR-100-5p and its corresponding binding sites were predicted through bioinformatic analysis (Fig. 4A). MiR-100-5p clearly suppressed the luciferase activity of MTOR Wt, whereas no significant inhibition was detected for MTOR Mut (Fig. 4B). In severe pneumonia in children, MTOR was obviously increased (Fig. 4C). MTOR was negatively correlated with miR-100-5p in the severe pneumonia group, while MTOR was not significantly negatively correlated with miR-100-5p in the control group (Fig. 4D, E). In addition, MTOR may be negatively regulated by miR-100-5p in the severe pneumonia group (Fig. 4F).

Co-effects of miR-100-5p and MTOR in WI-38 cells treated with LPS

MiR-100-5p and MTOR were co-overexpressed in WI-38 cells treated with LPS (Fig. 5A). In the LPS-induced inflammation model, overexpression of miR-100-5p significantly enhanced cell viability and reduced the release of TNF-α and IL-1β. On the contrary, co-transfection of the MTOR overexpression plasmid partially reversed the protective effect of miR-100-5p, resulting in a decrease in cell viability and an increase in the levels of inflammatory factors (Fig. 5B, C, D).

Discussion

Between 7% and 13% of pneumonia cases will develop into severe pneumonia [20]. Epidemiological studies indicate that in developing countries, childhood pneumonia hospitalizations account for 8.7% of all hospitalizations [21]. The primary clinical symptoms of pneumonia are fever and cough, often accompanied by chest pain and shortness of breath [22]. Studies have shown that indicators such as WBC and CRP are linked to the development of severe outcomes of pneumonia in children, which is consistent with the changes in the indicators in our results. Severe pneumonia, the main cause of death among children, is influenced by differences in immune maturity and environmental risk factors—such as seasonal variations in temperature and humidity, viral transmission patterns, and indoor congestion—which contribute to its incidence [23]. Therefore, it is highly necessary to determine a biomarker of severe pneumonia in children. We discovered that miR-100-5p expression significantly decreased and had significant diagnostic value in severe pneumonia in children, suggesting that miR-100-5p might be a biomarker for severe pneumonia in children.

Recently, miRNA has become an attractive diagnostic biomarker for pulmonary inflammation [24]. As mentioned in the previous text, miR-100-5p decreases in COVID-19 [6]. Further research in this article indicates that miR-100-5p level is closely related to multiple clinical indicators of pneumonia, suggesting that it is linked to the disease progression of severe pneumonia in children. In acute ischemic stroke, miR-100-5p has a protective effect on cerebral microvascular endothelial cells after ischemic injury and elevates proliferation [25]. In addition, miR-100-5p elevates proliferation in human articular chondrocytes [26]. The above evidence is consistent with the trend of the effect of miR-100-5p on proliferation in pneumonia in this study. In osteoarthritis, miR-100-5p alleviates cartilage degradation and anti-inflammation [27]. In temporomandibular joint osteoarthritis, miR-100-5p also has therapeutic effects [28]. Similarly, in this study, miR-100-5p may be associated with reduced inflammation in severe pneumonia in children.

Multiple studies have shown that MTOR is associated with inflammation. In rat osteoarthritis, MTOR activates the inflammatory response [29]. In addition, in acute lung injury, MTOR is also linked to necroptosis and inflammation [30]. Studies have shown that MTOR is regulated by miRNA [31, 32]. Bioinformatics results show that MTOR is a downstream target gene of miR-100-5p. In myocardial hypertrophy, MiR-100-5p promotes disease progression by targeting mTOR to activate autophagy [33]. It is worth noting that in inflammation-related diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, and cerebral infarction, MTOR is regulated by miR-100-5p [7, 8, 16]. In this study, miR-100-5p was negatively correlated with MTOR and negatively regulated MTOR, which was consistent with previous studies.

Our research also has limitations. The sample size of patients is limited, which might lead to some deviations in the results. In addition, no animal models were designed for in vivo validation. To further investigate the function of miR-100-5p in severe pediatric pneumonia, subsequent research will involve validation in animal models. This study initially confirmed the targeting relationship between miR-100-5p and mTOR through dual luciferase assays and expression verification. However, no functional rescue experiments using mTOR inhibitors (such as rapamycin) were conducted. Future studies will need to further verify the functional association through inhibitor treatment or mutation experiments. This study’s use of an in vitro model of fibroblast stimulation with LPS has certain limitations. Firstly, in the in vivo pulmonary microenvironment, fibroblasts are located in the alveolar interstitial space and are mainly activated through paracrine signals (such as cytokines released by epithelial cells and macrophages), rather than being directly exposed to LPS. Secondly, a single-cell model cannot simulate cell-to-cell interactions and the complex microenvironment. Future research should adopt co-culture models (such as co-culture of fibroblasts, epithelial cells, and macrophages) or animal models to more realistically verify the role of miR-100-5p in pulmonary inflammation.

Conclusions

Overall, miR-100-5p was reduced and had a high diagnostic value in severe pneumonia in children. MTOR may be a target of miR-100-5p and may be negatively correlated with miR-100-5p. MiR-100-5p may be involved in severe pneumonia in children by regulating MTOR, proving that it might be a biomarker of severe pneumonia in children.
